Arc 10 Chapter 10: "Crybaby"

—Felix Argyle.

Subaru knew that was the true name of the person standing before him now.
He'd heard it secondhand before, and, more than that, the man himself had just now given it. Despite that dainty appearance, Subaru had once thought the name itself carried a certain manliness to it.
But even faced with the man actually giving that name for himself, the fact and the reality of it wouldn't line up properly inside Subaru.

That was how thoroughly his way of living had been built around the image conjured by the nickname "Ferris."

"Dismissed... from knighthood? That means—"

"It means I quit. Well, but there's no helping it, ne? Once I decided to take the hand the 'Divine Dragon Church' offered—the one that could save Crusch-sama—I was already braced for it to come to this. —Ah, please don't go feeling responsible for it, Emilia-sama."

"Ferris..."

"Honestly, having you all come was so reassuring back then. Knowing there were people besides me and old Vil, besides Crusch-sama's own allies, who were glad she was safe—it let me believe what I'd done wasn't wrong."

Ferris folded down the cat ears atop his head and answered with the corners of his eyes softening.
At that answer, Emilia—who had stopped mid-stride on her way to rush to him—raised and lowered her hand, at a loss for how to respond.
Bewilderment and confusion; Emilia's reaction was only natural. —But Subaru's answer, faced with Ferris standing there in his tailcoat, calmly announcing he'd quit being a knight, was something else entirely.

"Wait! Subaru-kun, where do you think you're going?"

"—Hah, isn't it obvious? To Crusch-san's place. This is way too much."

Grinding his teeth, Subaru had already turned on his heel when the voice called him back, and he raised his own in a low growl.
He could guess the circumstances. The 'Dragon's Blood' that had invaded Crusch's body—to remove that agony, they'd borrowed the hand of the 'Divine Dragon Church,' and as a result, the standing of the Crusch camp in the Royal Selection had been put in jeopardy.
And that had been Ferris's decision, made in place of a Crusch whose mind had been lost to delirium.

By Ferris's judgment, Crusch had been saved—but victory in the Royal Selection had become that much harder to reach. It was surely a decision Crusch, who'd had such a powerful motive to become queen, would never have wanted made.

"But that was all something you did *for* Crusch-san, wasn't it! And now they're firing you for it...? That's just wrong!"

"Subaru!"

Unable to bear his own indignation, Subaru shook off the hand Beatrice had been holding, and she cried out.
But Subaru didn't stop. He meant to run straight back to the Karsten estate and confront Crusch himself if he had to, to make her take back what she'd done to Ferris.
But the moment he tried to take that first step, he was stopped by someone spreading their arms wide to block his path—Rem.

"Rem, don't stop me. I don't want you all seeing me screaming like a complete emotional wreck."

"If you're aware you're getting excessively emotional, then stop right there. Also, before you go and rage off like that, why not actually look at the other person's face first?"

"The other person's... face?"

Rem, halting Subaru's fit of momentum before it could fully build, nodded and jerked her chin. Following that gesture, Subaru turned and took in Emilia and Beatrice looking at him with worry, and then, beyond them, Ferris looking down at Subaru from the entrance of the mausoleum.
Ferris—the very source of Subaru's fury—looked exasperated at Subaru's outburst, and turned first toward Rem.

"Thanks, Rem-chan... This is the first time I've seen you standing and walking around like this, but you're a much better girl than I expected. Maybe it's just your taste in boys that's off?"

"...I've heard that while I was asleep, Ferris-san took care of me as well. Since you're someone I owe a debt to, I'll answer modestly, but... that's a terrible misunderstanding."

"So. If that's how Rem-chan sees it, fine by me. But I'm glad you're up and about."

Across the Subaru caught in the middle, Rem and Ferris traded those words. Once that exchange was done and Rem pursed her lips with a complicated look, Ferris switched gears with an "Anyway."
Narrowing his eyes as he looked down at Subaru, he said,

"You know, Subaru-kun, you really are arrogant, ne?"

"Arro—gant...?!"

"It's not quite the same as being self-centered, I think, so I'm choosing my words carefully here, okay? You're not doing all this for yourself—you're doing it for someone else, but it's spinning its wheels so hard, or maybe I should say it's such an unwanted kindness—"

"You—don't talk like that—"

"—So, if Subaru-kun is arrogant, then I guess I'm arrogant too, ne."

The instant Subaru saw that smile, he felt like he'd been doused in cold water.

"——"

He caught his breath, staring back at Ferris, who had just spoken as ill of himself as he had of Subaru a moment ago. Ferris's air hadn't changed at all since he'd first turned his attention to them at the mausoleum—unreadable, or rather, to Subaru it felt almost too composed, too serene.
If he had to put it into words, it was like a calm, still sea. —But that couldn't possibly be right. Given everything that had happened to Ferris, this ought to be the middle of a raging storm, not a calm sea at all.

Subaru narrowed his eyes, searching Ferris's expression for some sign of that storm. But Ferris himself, seeming to pay it no mind, closed one eye, then let out an "Ah," and said,

"That's right. If you'd like, would Emilia-sama and the rest of you care to pay your respects too, while you're here?"

"Pay your respects... you mean, that mausoleum behind you? Can we even go in?"

"Yes, I've properly been entrusted with the key by the caretaker, and besides, I hold the qualification of a proxy visitor, so I actually come here fairly often."

"A proxy visitor...?"

"Wow, Subaru-kun really doesn't know anything, does he. Being First Knight without knowing that—you're going to embarrass Emilia-sama."

Ferris gave a small laugh and stuck out his tongue, and Subaru fell silent.
It wasn't so much being called ignorant that stung, as the fact that the words "First Knight" coming out of Ferris's mouth felt, given the current situation, like an extremely delicate topic to bring up.
Of course, Ferris, with that mischievous look in his eye, had probably said it on purpose.

Toying with them like that all the while, Ferris gently laid a hand on the half-closed door of the mausoleum he himself had just come out of.

"Let's talk about everything that's happened, inside. —I was just thinking His Highness might get lonely if it's only me visiting."

Within the royal cemetery, the mausoleum's presence clearly set it apart from everything else.
Built up from pure white marble handpicked from across the kingdom, adorned throughout with the dragon motifs symbolic of the Dragon-kin Kingdom, the building carried a dignity that overwhelmed anyone who merely looked upon it. Its outer walls, gleaming as they reflected the sunlight, radiated a divine air that seemed to reject the entry of anything wicked, and the dragon carvings that completed the closed doors like a single painted image seemed to go on eternally guarding the dignity of the deceased royals.

Stepping inside, the tombstones of the sleeping royal family lined the white marble corridor at every turn. The stone coffins, arranged in orderly rows, were each carved and adorned in ways befitting the royal they belonged to—simply looking at them seemed to conjure the living form of the deceased.
The tomb of the Fourth Prince, their destination, made one feel this especially strongly.

"This is His Highness's... Fourier Lugunica-sama's grave."

On the polished floor, Ferris, who had led Subaru and the others with the click of his footsteps, came to a stop and gestured with his hand toward a tombstone that stood out above all the rest—the stone coffin of Fourier Lugunica.
Within the stern, hushed order that governed the mausoleum, that principle wavered ever so slightly here.
For the Fourth Prince's tomb was, for a place where the dead lay at rest, far too vividly colorful.

"Are these... offerings? Letters, knitted things, and carved wooden animals..."

"None of them really feel like offerings for a royal, do they, ne. Actually, the ones who asked me to place these things here were kids pretty far removed from that kind of upper-class etiquette, so it can't be helped."

"Far removed people—you don't mean these are gifts from ordinary citizens? Don't things like that get inspected, or confiscated, before they're allowed in?"

"Of course, if it were some kind of prank, it wouldn't be let through. But I confirm that it isn't, myself, when I receive it as the proxy visitor."

Crouching before the stone coffin, Ferris answered while narrowing his eyes at the various items laid there.
The offerings placed here were surely things prepared, each with their own feelings, by people who had adored the Fourth Prince resting here, regardless of their station.
A proxy visitor was someone who, in place of those who couldn't come to this place directly, held—by special exception and with the cemetery caretaker's permission—the right to bring such offerings here.
And Ferris had been entrusted with people's feelings as this Fourth Prince's proxy visitor.

"Originally, the Lugunica royals were, well, mostly good-natured people—kind and good at being spoiled, that kind of type—but His Highness Fourier was exceptionally bright and cheerful, a really fun person to be around. So he'd sneak out of the castle all the time and go play, mixing in with the people of the royal capital, ne."

"...Wow. That's the kind of episode you often hear about in manga. But if you actually did that in real life, it'd cause a huge problem, wouldn't it?"

"Well, it would, ne. They'd mobilize the royal guard to go search for the missing prince and everything. And in the end, His Highness would be found mixed in among the poor district's soup kitchen, and since that soup kitchen happened to belong to the 'Divine Dragon Church,' it nearly caused friction between the castle and the church—a huge fuss."

"That's the kind of thing that's hard to laugh off as a joke, even now."

Past Ferris's rounded back, Subaru gazed at the tombstone of Fourier, beloved by so many.
In Ferris's reminiscence, laced with laughter, there was an affection for Fourier—the subject of the story—so plain it couldn't be hidden, and even from that fragment of an anecdote, one could sense what a person beloved by everyone he'd been.

"...Did I ever mention His Highness to you before, Subaru-kun?"

"...Yeah, that there was someone like that, who was close with you and Crusch-san. Didn't you also say something about him and me being a little alike?"

"Huh? Why would His Highness and Subaru-kun be alike? Want me to kill you?"

"You said pretty much the exact same thing back then too!!"

Since her voice had taken on a genuinely serious edge, Subaru hastily explained himself.
In fact, that was about the extent of what Subaru had been told about Fourier. Of course, even Subaru, dull as he could be, understood that this person's death was not unrelated to Crusch's reason for entering the Royal Selection.

"So then, did Crusch-san enter the Royal Selection for Prince Fourier's sake?"

"...As expected of Emilia-sama. You go straight at the hard-to-ask questions, ne."

"Ah, sorry. But you were trying to tell us about it, weren't you, and that's why you invited us to visit the grave? I wanted to hear it before deciding what I'm going to do next."

"What you're going to do next, is it? What do you mean by that?"

"Yes—well, Subaru stole a bit of my thunder just now, but I was thinking of going to ask Crusch-san to reconsider too. Subaru and I will talk to her together, with everything we've got. What do you think?"

"I think it's a great idea. ...Ow ow ow ow!"

"Please don't just say things because you're swept up in the moment. Do you understand where we are right now?"

The moment he agreed with Emilia's idea, Rem pinched his side and he writhed. Watching the two of them, Ferris let out a wry laugh and stood up, saying,

"Like I said before, I'd really rather you not go say all sorts of things to Crusch-sama. I did this fully aware from the start that it would come to this."

"But...!"

"I've already accepted it. I said I was dismissed, but it was me who told her I no longer had the right to stay by her side. And besides..."

Pressing a finger to his own lips, Ferris paused a beat between words, then pointed that raised finger first at Emilia, then in turn at Subaru.

"If, say, I—or the 'Council of Wise Men,' or whoever, really, anyone from outside—told you that you should reconsider your master-servant relationship with Subaru-kun, what would you do?"

"Eh? What Subaru and I have is something I thought long and hard about and decided for myself. So if someone told me something like that, I'd feel really—unsettled, and think, what business is it of theirs... ah."

"That's exactly it. What Emilia-sama is trying to do right now."

An unnecessary, oversized meddling from an outsider. Explained so plainly by Ferris, Emilia visibly slumped her shoulders in dejection. Faced with that reaction, there was little joy to be had in the fact that Emilia herself would have answered that she didn't want anyone butting into her relationship with Subaru.
More than anything, this argument was effective not just on Emilia, but on Subaru too.

Ferris, surely aware of that, shrugged past a dejected Emilia toward Subaru as well and said,

"As for the answer to your earlier question—Crusch-sama isn't so narrow-minded. So Prince Fourier is just one part of why she entered the Royal Selection."

"...But it's not a part small enough to just ignore, is it."

"Wow, look at you nitpicking so skillfully. You've really lost all your charm, Subaru-kun."

Even so, it didn't seem like he'd been struck in a sore spot, and Ferris went on.

"I won't deny it. His Highness's passing was, for Crusch-sama—for me too—a truly huge thing. I was hurt deep down, cried enough to last a lifetime. Well, I'm a crybaby, so I cried plenty more after that too."

"——"

"Crusch-sama, too, didn't show it outwardly, but she was hurt, hurt so much, and suffered. —But the moment Crusch-sama was hurt the most was after His Highness passed away."

"What...?"

"His Highness, and the other royals, one after another, passed into hiding, and everyone was thrown into chaos. Crusch-sama, as Duke Karsten, attended the castle council as one of the upper nobility... and that's where she realized it. That everyone attending that council was more consumed with worry over whether the covenant with the 'Divine Dragon' might be severed, than over the deaths of His Highness and the others."

His tone was quiet, deliberately careful not to let emotion into it. But that very effort only threw the grotesqueness of what Ferris was describing into sharper relief.
How warm the character of the Lugunica royals had been, how beloved—one could tell just by looking at this mausoleum, where every tombstone besides Fourier Lugunica's bore some offering praying for the peace of the departed.

Even the deaths of people so beloved were, before the covenant with the 'Dragon,' treated as of little concern.

"So that's why," Emilia murmured, the words spilling out like a breath—a realization she'd reached along with Subaru. She pressed a hand to her chest, her violet eyes narrowing painfully.

"So that's why Crusch-san decided that when she became queen, instead of a country that relied entirely on its covenant with the 'Dragon,' she had to make it a stronger country."

"—That's not the only reason, but, yes, that's part of it, ne."

"Not the only reason, meaning...?"

"Crusch-sama really hated it, you know. ...Hated that if she hadn't grown close with Prince Fourier, she probably would have ended up thinking the exact same way as everyone else."

That was something that couldn't be known unless it had actually happened, and something that could never actually have happened otherwise—so it gave Crusch no real reason to blame herself.
However she struggled against it, Crusch had met Fourier, and arrived at those convictions because of it. Hating some nonexistent version of herself who never met him was nothing but pointless suffering.

Still, even as he thought that, Subaru realized that the situation Crusch found herself in wasn't merely a matter of having made a choice that contradicted her platform in the Royal Selection.

Crusch, who had declared she would end the covenant with the 'Dragon,' had ended up saved by the power of a sacrament belonging to none other than the 'Divine Dragon Church,' which worshipped that very 'Dragon.' —And that wasn't all.

"Crusch-san had sworn to restore an environment where the Lugunica royal family—where Prince Fourier—would be truly mourned, and she was trying to fulfill that, but now she can't."

"...And what's more, the 'Divine Dragon Church' didn't save His Highness. Even though Crusch-sama was the one who saved them."

"—!"

Ferris's hoarse words struck Subaru and the others to the very core, in the truest sense.
Until just moments ago, Subaru had been fully prepared to go confront Crusch, who had dismissed Ferris from knighthood, and argue that it was too much even accounting for the poor state of her mind and body.
But now, knowing that the resolve and conviction Crusch had carried, the ideals and vow she'd raised up, had been shattered in about the worst way imaginable—what could Subaru possibly say to her?

If only Subaru could offer up an arm, a leg, anything, and take on Crusch's curse himself instead—

"See, this is exactly why you're arrogant, Subaru-kun."

"Wha—"

"Anyone can tell just by looking at your face. No wonder Beatrice-chan can't let go of your hand. And even if it might help a little, you understand it wouldn't actually solve anything, don't you?"

Tilting his head, Ferris spoke in a manner almost like scolding a child, chiding the shallowness of Subaru's thinking without letting emotion into it.
Subaru knew it. His current line of thinking was armchair theorizing, and just as Ferris said, it was meaningless.
Even if the one being saved changed from the 'Divine Dragon Church' to Natsuki Subaru, Emilia's knight, it wouldn't erase the demerit of Crusch having relied on a rival camp to solve her own problem. And in a Royal Selection already past its midpoint, that was far too great a disadvantage.

"Dumping the solution to your own problems on someone outside your camp is a problem before you're even fit to be king, huh..."

"—. That's harsh, ne. But that's exactly it."

"...I'm borrowing that."

That, too, was borrowed—borrowed from none other than Crusch's own mouth.
Once, when the Witch Cult had targeted Emilia, when Subaru had gone to Crusch begging for help with nothing to bargain with, Crusch had cut down Subaru's ugly pleading with dignified sharpness.
That had been a lesson Subaru needed to learn. So he bore Crusch no grudge over it. And yet, for that very thing to come around and flip back on him like this was almost too ironic.

"Hey, Ferris, I'm not sure if I should even ask this, but..."

In place of Subaru, who had lowered his head, lost for words, Emilia spoke up to Ferris.
She stepped forward beside Ferris, stood before Fourier Lugunica's gravestone, gazed at the many offerings placed there, then clasped the mana crystal at her chest and closed her eyes.
Looking back, behind Emilia, Beatrice and Rem had likewise lined up, each offering their own prayer for the dead in their own way.
Once that was finished, Emilia gazed at one especially vivid bouquet of flowers—the one Ferris, crouching, had just adjusted the position of, surely the one he himself had brought—and asked,

"Were Crusch-san and Prince Fourier in love with each other?"

"——"

At that question, Ferris's eyes widened faintly for a moment, and then he gave a small laugh.
And then—

"That's something I can't say, coming from my mouth. —It'd be far too crude."

The mausoleum's doors closed with a heavy sound, and Subaru's eyes met the dragon carved into the double doors like a single painted image.
He understood it was likely meant to represent the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica, the same figure carved onto the coin. But now, having heard the tale of the tragic love surrounding Crusch and Prince Fourier, his feelings toward that 'Divine Dragon' had grown even more complicated than before.

"We ran into the real thing up on the tower too, but with that empty-headed state it was in, calling it the kingdom's covenant partner or the object of the 'Divine Dragon Church's' faith doesn't exactly carry much weight..."

"Maybe it's the type that only pulls itself together when it's really needed. When it's not, it might be running on energy-saving mode, I suppose—like Otto when he doesn't have any work to do."

"Even for an Otto with no work to do, calling him empty-headed is going too far. ...No, wait, if the comparison is the 'Divine Dragon,' maybe that's an honor from the kingdom's perspective?"

At Beatrice's proposal, Subaru tried simulating a mental-Otto treated as the 'Divine Dragon' too, but since off-duty Otto was too giddy with the relief of finishing his work to be of much use as a reference, he didn't get much of an opinion worth hearing out of it. Moderation in heavy drinking, and all that.
In any case—

"Ferris, what are you going to do after this?"

"After this, you mean?"

"Yes. I mean, you can't go back to Crusch-san's place right now—it'd be hard to go back, wouldn't it? You've been living in the same mansion this whole time, so, do you have somewhere to go?"

On their way to return the mausoleum key to the cemetery caretaker, Emilia asked that, and Ferris, brow furrowed, scratched his cheek with a finger, at a loss for how to answer.
Judging by that reaction, Emilia's remark must have struck Ferris right at the critical point.
It was probably safe to say that Wilhelm telling Subaru and the others where to find Ferris had likewise been a bitter decision made out of concern for his future.

Whatever the circumstances, Crusch and Ferris had dissolved their master-servant relationship. In that situation, Wilhelm, as Crusch's retainer, couldn't take action against his master's wishes.
Having someone trustworthy check in on what became of Ferris afterward was the greatest consideration Wilhelm could offer in that moment.
And if they were to answer that consideration of Wilhelm's as fully as possible—

"What Emilia-tan's saying is, why not come stay with us for a while, for now?"

"Yes, exactly! What do you think? Of course, I don't mean forever. Parting ways just like this would be sad, and I think some things really do need time. It took me about a hundred years to accept that I'd frozen everyone back home."

"I do think that's an unusually long time, even so."

"By the way, it took Betty about four hundred years to accept Subaru's love, I suppose."

"I do think that's a bit too pitiable for this man, even so."

Because of the misleading remarks that came flying out of Emilia and Beatrice, the long-lived pair, Rem started looking at Subaru with a slightly pitying gaze. He'd have to properly clear up that misunderstanding later, but at the very least, the invitation being extended to Ferris right now was sincere.
And yet, hearing it, Ferris put a hand to his own forehead with a "Ta-haa," and said,

"I had a feeling I might get told that, and, well, I really did. Guess it makes sense there'd be a bidding war over me, seeing as I'm such a capable, skilled healer."

"Mm, I'm not saying it as a joke..."

"Yes, right, of course, I know Emilia-sama's always serious about these things. And I really am happy you're inviting me like this. Really, I am, okay?"

"...But the way you're saying that..."

"—Right. As much as I appreciate the invitation, I can't quite say yes to it."

Shaking his head slowly, Ferris turned down Emilia's proposal with a wry smile. There was a strong urge in Subaru to push back against that attitude, but he could also understand the feeling behind not being able to say yes.
Because—

"Even if we end up apart, I still cherish Crusch-sama. Even if my own decision has made the Royal Selection basically hopeless for her, I can't just brush that off and decide to cheer for someone other than Crusch-sama instead."

"I—it's fine even if you hate me. I just want you to make sure you don't wander off somewhere on your own—that you let us know you're really okay."

"I wouldn't hate you. —Ah, that's a lie. I really do hate you. Emilia-sama, I've hated you, from the very start, this whole, whole, whole time."

"Eh! Really!?"

Emilia, who had pushed back with even an ill-fitting bargaining chip like that, was shocked. Nodding "Yep" to her, Ferris gestured at Subaru and the others with the mausoleum key and said,

"It's not just Emilia-sama. I hated all the other Royal Selection candidates besides Crusch-sama too, and their knights as well. I hate Beatrice-chan too, and even though this is basically the first time I've met Rem-chan, I hate her too. Subaru-kun, I really hate."

"The way you say it makes it sound like I'm the only one who's genuinely hated..."

"Why would you think you're not genuinely hated?"

Countered like that, Subaru had nothing left to say, and was thoroughly shut down.

"...Are you really, truly not going to change your mind?"

Even Emilia could tell that Ferris's "hate" just now wasn't sincere. That Ferris had used liking and disliking as an excuse to refuse Emilia's proposal. And that no matter what was said to him, no matter what treatment he received, his feeling of supporting Crusch would never change.

"Please don't worry that much. I'm not sure if I'll keep up with the Royal Guard... I'll talk to the captain about that, but between clinics and everything else, I've got plenty of places that'll take me."

"...You're not going to do anything reckless, are you."

"Reckless? You mean like, growing weary of the world and ending it all... that sort of thing? Me, who hates death more than anyone else in this world?"

"—. Sorry about that."

With not a trace of joking left in his voice for just that one line, Ferris stated it clearly, and Subaru apologized.
Even if he left Crusch's side, Ferris wouldn't do anything that would bend who he was—the 'Blue,' a healer. Subaru would believe that.

"Aah, but still! This sucks! I can't go say anything to Crusch-san, and even offering to take Ferris in for a while got turned down—there's nothing I can do...!"

"Isn't that the very thing Ferris-san scolded you for, calling it arrogance?"

"Oh, right, right, just like Rem-chan says."

"But, Rem..."

"—. No matter what, there are problems that only the people directly involved can solve."

Watching a tragedy of two people missing each other, yet having no right to set it straight.
To Subaru, voicing that frustration, Rem lowered her eyes and let out a small sigh.

"Just as an outsider shouldn't meddle in your master-servant relationship with Emilia-san, or your contractual relationship with Beatrice-chan. Same with you and me..."

"Rem?"

"...What relationship is there, between you and me, anyway? What am I to you?"

"Don't suddenly come back to reality like that! You—me—important!"

"That goes for us too! We—Rem—so, so important!"

"—. Thank you, Emilia-san."

"What about me!?"

Rem elegantly deflected the entirety of Subaru's existence, and his voice cracked with the shock of it.
Behind that exchange between Subaru and the others, Beatrice called out, "Hey," to Ferris. Still pinching the hem of Subaru's clothes, she said,

"Betty isn't as worried about your future as Subaru and the others are, I suppose. But Betty does owe you an enormous debt, I suppose."

"Beatrice-chan-sama owes me something? Did I ever lend anything to a Great Spirit-sama?"

"You saved Subaru back when his Gate was on the verge of breaking from all his reckless overexertion, I suppose. Unfortunately, Subaru ignored your warning and broke his Gate anyway, but it still bought some time, I suppose. If it weren't for your power, there would have been no contract between Betty and Subaru at all, I suppose."

"Um, that's actually more gratifying than I expected."

"Savor it all you like, I suppose. And Betty is the sort who always pays back her debts, I suppose. So if anything happens to you, Betty will lend her strength anytime, I suppose."

"Even if Subaru-kun's about to fall off a cliff?"

"Betty would come running the instant she's picked Subaru back up, I suppose."

Hands on her hips, giving a self-satisfied huff, Beatrice answered, and Ferris's eyes widened slightly at that before he put a hand to his mouth and laughed, "Aha."
That impulse to laugh only grew stronger, and before long, tears welled up at the corners of Ferris's eyes.

"Ahh, no, don't. Don't make me cry, Beatrice-chan-sama."

"Just as self-declared—a crybaby, I suppose."

At those words from Beatrice, Ferris's smile broke apart into something even smaller, even more helpless.

"If you ever change your mind, come visit anytime, okay? I'll make sure everyone knows to properly welcome you whenever you come! It's a promise! Can you keep a promise, Ferris?"

"A stray shot just hit me!"

"Fair enough."

Right up until the very last moment of parting, Emilia and the others had done their best to keep inviting him.
Ferris knew perfectly well there was no malice or scheme behind it, and turning them down again and again, each time watching Emilia's face cloud over a little more, weighed on him with guilt too. But there was no helping it.

"It's not that I have any complaints about Emilia-sama and the others, ne."

Even having relinquished the right himself, Ferris had been Crusch's knight.
It was unthinkable for him, even temporarily, to take shelter under the umbrella of any Royal Selection candidate other than Crusch. If anyone found out, it wouldn't reflect well on Crusch, and more than anything, what frightened him was what Crusch herself would think of it.

"...In that regard, His Highness was amazing. Wasn't he ever scared?"

Standing before the gravestone and the stone coffin in the mausoleum, Ferris had meant to make his confession for as long as time allowed. And yet, no sooner had a little while passed than the lingering attachment, wanting to be heard again, welled right back up.
On and on, over and over, even switching his outward guise to that of a man, the womanish streak at his core refused to peel away in the slightest. He truly did hate himself for it.

The one thing he could believe he'd judged correctly—

"I'm glad I was the one to bring it up myself."

Just as he'd told Emilia and the others, the dismissal from knighthood had been Ferris's own idea.
The moment he'd decided to take the hand offered by the 'Divine Dragon Church' and rely on the power of Filore's sacrament, he'd already resigned himself to losing the right to stay by Crusch's side. And yet, Ferris's soul couldn't bear to sit and wait for those words to come from Crusch's own mouth.
So he'd spoken first, to spare Crusch from having to be the one to cut Ferris loose herself. —And there it was; even as he tried to praise himself for making the right call, it turned out to be just another expression of that hateful womanishness.

"I really do nothing but lie, don't I..."

He'd thought he'd made the right judgment. A lie.
He'd asked whether Fourier hadn't been scared. A lie.
He'd told Emilia and the others he had no complaints about them. A lie.

The dismissal from knighthood had been to protect his own fragile heart, and even that Fourier, he knew, had needed to gather all his courage every time he invited Crusch anywhere.
And there was no way Ferris felt nothing, watching Emilia and the others' meteoric rise—having built up achievement after achievement from an overwhelmingly disadvantageous starting position, giving the upper nobility no end of trouble over her half-elf birth—especially the accomplishments of Subaru, who stood in the same position as a knight.

In fact, Ferris hadn't asked Subaru about the circumstances of his ungloved right hand.
That Subaru himself hadn't brought it up meant it was probably some method not easily put to use in treating Crusch. But a guess was just a guess. He hadn't made it certain. The reason for that was plain—he'd fled from the possibility that there might have been an answer other than relying on the 'Divine Dragon Church.'

It almost felt as if everything he did was simply prioritizing his own escape from pain.

"Am I really... someone who cherishes anyone, I wonder...?"

If he'd truly cherished that person, shouldn't he have cherished the very conviction that person refused to give up? In a position where he knew the vow Crusch had sworn before Fourier's grave, shouldn't he have searched for a path where that wish never had to be abandoned, even if it meant enduring unhealed suffering forever?
Wasn't it precisely the anger of confronting an unacceptable reality that would have reminded Crusch of Fourier, and made her—of all people—realize the cruelty of what Ferris had done?

If that confusion in Crusch's 'memory,' when she recalled Fourier Lugunica, had been called up by anger at Ferris's having trampled on that vow—
Then Ferris might be hated not only by Crusch, but by Fourier as well.

"—! Stop, stop, this is pointless."

He noticed himself, once again, under the guise of self-reproach, searching for words to comfort himself.
If he had time to waste on something so pointless, he needed to settle where he stood from here on, so as not to keep worrying Emilia and the others—and Wilhelm, whom he'd put in the position of being caught between him and Crusch.

In truth, he'd lost his reason to remain a knight. His title of 'Blue' was still intact, so if he went to a clinic or a magical research institute, he'd surely be welcomed with open arms. Securing a place to work would be easy—but simply drifting through the days meant nothing.
With these healing hands, continuing to save people was a given; beyond that, there were other things he wished for too.

Even if the right to wish for them was something Ferris—no, Felix Argyle—had thrown away himself.

"—Ah, good, I'm relieved. I was worried we might miss each other."

Suddenly, on the way back from the cemetery, Ferris—who had been glaring down at the cobblestones of the noble district—felt his ears twitch, reacting to a voice speaking directly to him.
He stopped and turned to face forward. Ahead, in the middle of the road, stood a single figure. —Someone he recognized.

A man with dusky golden hair and deep blue eyes. His tall, slender frame was wrapped in a well-tailored suit, his hair and beard neatly groomed, carrying an air of refined elegance. Given the man's profession, none of that was strange in the least.
After all, the man was—

"—Russell Fellow, was it. From the Commerce Guild, right?"

"Yes, that's right. I'm deeply honored that you remember me. And might I address you as Ferris-sama? Or would you prefer Felix-sama?"

"...Whichever you like."

"Then, if I may take you up on that, Felix-sama it is. Given my profession, addressing someone by their formal name rather than a nickname tends to avoid all sorts of inconvenience."

"Hmm."

Ferris closed one eye at the man—Russell Fellow—who bowed his head with polished courtesy.
The man was a shrewd operator who served as a representative of the royal capital's Commerce Guild, famous even among the capital's battle-hardened merchants as exceptionally capable. In fact, they'd borrowed his help with supply logistics and delivery during the Subjugation of the White Whale, so he wasn't an unrelated figure to the Crusch camp either.

"Though, in my current position, I can't jump into any profitable deals, you know? Sorry to disappoint..."

"Your master-servant contract with Duke Karsten has been dissolved. Yes, I'm aware."

"...Your ears are frighteningly fast."

Having said it himself, Ferris reflected that he'd brought it up in a rather foolish way.
Because Russell had clearly been lying in wait for him. Something that had never once happened before—if it was happening today, of all days, that could only mean some reason had arisen for him to take that kind of action.
And there was one more foolish thing Ferris was slow to notice, on top of that.

"...This street—it's still evening, isn't it? Why isn't anyone passing through?"

"——"

Russell stood in the middle of the road, about ten meters from Ferris. Around Russell, and around Ferris too, not a single presence drew near on the street in the noble district.
If this were the dead of night or early dawn, perhaps, but at an hour when the sun hadn't even fully set, it was unthinkable. And for the unthinkable to happen, there was always a reason.
In this case, the one causing that reason was—

"Felix-sama, there is something I would very much like to discuss with you, and I wonder if you might allow me to invite you to dinner at my house. If you haven't yet decided where you're headed."

"Trying to recruit me the moment my collar's off? Sorry, but that offer—"

"—No, this isn't a recruitment. In fact, this conversation is not optional."

Slowly, as if lamenting it, Russell shook his head. —In that instant, Ferris found a cold blade pressed to the slender line of his own throat, wielded by someone who had appeared right behind him.

"—!"

Ferris caught his breath, his body sealed in place, unable to move. There was nothing he could do in that sudden instant. All he could tell was that the blade pressed to him was a thin, deeply curved sword, and that whoever stood behind him was considerably taller than he was. —No, and one more thing—

"...You're not just an ordinary merchant, are you, ne?"

"I wonder. I'd like to think that I could be, if it came to that."

Closing the distance step by step, Russell approached; with the blade held to his throat, unable to do anything, Ferris at least tried not to let his eyes waver—and hated himself for it all over again.
Why had he ever assumed a person capable of such cold eyes was just an ordinary civilian?

Biting his lip tightly, he held back so that no weakness, no groan, would slip free. That, he told himself, was the most he could manage right now.
Watching Ferris up close like that, Russell, his eyes still cold, delivered his statement.

"Now that you're no longer the Duke's knight, I have no hesitation about interrogation. You will talk. —About the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King,' passed down within House Argyle."
