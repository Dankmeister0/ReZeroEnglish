Arc 1 Chapter 6: "The End of the Beginning"

The search phase had entered the slums, and things looked set to remain as rough going as ever—or so it seemed, until an unexpected turn of events here made Subaru useful.

"For some reason, everyone around me is being unnaturally nice. What's going on... don't tell me my popularity phase has finally arrived!? For the first time since kindergarten!"

As a small child, Subaru had been quite the cute-looking kid, and with his long hair, he'd often been mistaken for a girl. —And yet, more than a decade later, things had turned out like this, which was no laughing matter.

"It's just a guess, so take it with a 'probably' attached, but..."

"I'm listening! If there's a scientific... no, wait, this is a fantasy world, so magical, huh!? If there's a magical basis for this popularity phase, I want to hear it."

"I don't think it's the answer you're hoping for, but it's probably your appearance. You're grimy and there's still blood on you, and the people here look like they've had a hard time of it themselves, so maybe they just can't stand to watch and look away."

"Well, that clears things right up, damn it!"

So that explained why everyone was being so oddly kind.

There was even an old woman who held out something like a small dried fruit, saying, "Eat this and live strong." Trying it while nervously chewing, he found a faint sweetness within the hard texture. And then an overpowering strange smell surged out. He writhed in agony at the sensation of his nose being assaulted from the inside.

"Fwoaaah! I thought it was kindness, but it was poison! Poison! My whole body feels like it's burning up! This is bad! I might actually die! Or I might strip naked and die socially instead!"

"Oh, I wondered what you'd been given—that's a Bokko Fruit. Eating it stimulates the Mana inside your body and speeds up wound healing. The effect varies from person to person, and for most people it's basically just a placebo, but..."

Watching Subaru, feverish, sweating, and breathing hard, Satella pressed a finger to her lips. "Hmm."

"From the look of it, your Mana circulation seems to be pretty high, Subaru. You might die from an overdose."

"I kind of wish you'd told me that before I ate it! What am I supposed to do!?"

"Right... Puck."

In response to the call, Puck emerged from within her silver hair. His movements were terribly sluggish, showing almost none of the liveliness from before. In short, he looked incredibly sleepy.

Seemingly having barely listened to the conversation, Puck rubbed his big eyes with a paw.

"Nyaa? What is it? I'm about at my limit here..."

"Fill your stomach before you sleep. Look, there's a midnight snack right in front of you."

Feeling the thorn in her tone, Subaru noticed Puck's black eyes turn to look at him. And then those sleepy eyes suddenly gleamed as brightly as if presented with a favorite treat.

"Can I eat him?"

"P-please don't make it hurt."

Surely he wasn't about to be bitten starting from the head, Subaru thought, even as he struck a coy pose in reply.

Puck answered with a vague "aye-aye," stretching his small body out firmly on Satella's shoulder.

"Well then, itadakimasu~"

The instant after, Subaru felt something unidentifiable that had been rampaging inside his body get pulled cleanly out.

It was a sensation hard to describe as coming from any particular place. If he had to put it into words, it felt like the contents of his body were being ripped out root and all through every pore in his skin.

"Thanks for the meal."

Puck, mindful of his manners, pressed his paws together. Subaru was about to respond with something like "Glad you enjoyed it," when he suddenly felt chills bearing down on his entire body.

After the heat came the cold. And it wasn't just a chilly feeling on the skin, but a cold that seemed to come from deep within his core.

Seeing Subaru instinctively hug his own shoulders, Satella looked at Puck reproachfully.

"Puck."

"Sorry, sorry, it's been a while so I got the amount wrong. But Subaru's Gate feels strange. It hasn't been used much, yet it's so obedient. That's why I ended up sucking up a bit too much."

Puck gave a bashful "tehe" and rapped his own head, but for Subaru, the one who'd been sucked dry, it was no laughing matter.

More unfamiliar words like "Mana" and "Gate" had come up again, but—

"It's probably something like MP... and it got Aspir'd out of me, that's why I'm in rough shape."

Even here, his highly game-literate brain proved capable of grasping the concept.

The question was how much importance this world placed on MP. If MP recovery items were set to be ridiculously expensive, then this could be considered quite a serious offense, but—

"I'm sorry. I'll make sure to have a proper talk with him about it later."

"I'm reflecting on it. But I don't regret it. It was delicious."

Judging from Puck's attitude—like the protagonist of some manga—and the way Satella was content to just lightly scold him for it, it didn't seem to be that serious a problem after all.

In that case, it would look pretty uncool to keep shivering resentfully forever.

"Don't worry about it, at least the whole burning-to-death thing is over now. More importantly, let's go track down the culprit whose whereabouts were revealed thanks to my achievement. Thanks to my achievement."

"I get that you're happy you were useful, but saying that part twice is sooo uncool."

He'd meant to change the subject, but he'd ended up sounding like some small-time guy bragging about his one accomplishment.

Satella wore the look of someone who felt cheated out of having reconsidered her opinion of him, while Subaru gave a wry laugh. It was just as the two of them were resetting their minds to head deeper into the slums again that—

"Sorry, I'm really at my limit now."

Saying that, Puck, on Satella's shoulder, leaned weakly against her neck.

His gray fur took on a faint glow, trembling as fragile and faint as if he might vanish at any moment.

"Somehow that looks like the way something dies."

"Well, I've been pushing myself pretty hard. Since I use Mana to manifest a body, when I disappear, I scatter into mist. ...Sorry, could you get the orb, please?"

"Got it. Sorry for making you overdo it, Puck. Rest well."

What Satella drew from within her robe was a green crystal about the size that would fit in her palm. It had a different feel from what one would call a gem, and by Subaru's reckoning, "crystal" was the closest match.

Puck made his way along her arm from her shoulder to reach the crystal, and once his small body was wrapped around it, he turned back to look at Satella.

"I'm sure you already know this, but please, whatever you do, don't push yourself too far. If it comes down to it, use your Od to manifest me into this world."

"I know. I'm not a child—I understand my own limits."

"I wonder about that. My daughter's pretty shaky on that front. I'm counting on you, Subaru."

With a smile heavy with compassion, he shifted his gaze from the discontented Satella over to Subaru.

Watching the two of them, thinking it looked just like an exchange between a parent and child, Subaru—now put on the spot—thumped his chest.

"All right, leave it to me. Just count on my chicken-sense. The moment I think 'danger's dangerously dangerous, DANGER!' I'll turn right back around."

"I don't really get about half of what you just said, but I'm counting on you.
—Well then, good night. Be careful."

Looking once more at Satella one last time, Puck's form vanished from the world—for good this time.

Just as he'd said, his shape broke apart into fragments of light and dispersed into nothing. It was a sight that could never, ever happen in Subaru's original world, and a shiver ran naturally through his entire body at witnessing it.

Once Puck was gone, Satella gently stroked the crystal in her palm as if cherishing it, then carefully tucked it away inside her robe.

Given the flow of the conversation, something like the core of Puck's spirit body was probably inside that crystal now.

"It's just the two of us now, but... don't get any funny ideas. I can still use magic, you know."

Unaware of what was actually going on in Subaru's head, and apparently thinking he'd been peering right into her thoughts, Satella made this wary remark. In response to her attitude, Subaru couldn't help raising his hands and shaking his head.

"No way! Being alone with a girl is a situation I haven't been in since elementary school. There's absolutely no way I could do anything. Haven't you been watching my sheer force of character this whole time?"

"Somehow that's incredibly lame and yet incredibly convincing. ...Fine, let's move on. But since we don't have Puck keeping watch anymore, we'll need to be even more careful than before."

Perhaps deflated by Subaru puffing out his chest, Satella retied the cord of her robe and stepped forward. Keeping her usual position, she turned only her head to look back.

"I'll take the front, and you keep watch behind me. If anything happens, call for me right away. Don't go thinking you should try to do something yourself. It's not that I want to hurt your feelings, but... you are weak, after all."

"It's because you tack on that little preface that I can't bring myself to hate you for it..."

If she wanted to maintain a cold, distancing image, the part before "you are weak, after all" was entirely unnecessary.

The fact that she couldn't hide her true feelings in that part just showed that, at heart, she really was soft. Soft enough to melt.

Urging on Satella, who looked as though she had more to say, the two of them began their search alone together.

That said, there wasn't much difference in what they were doing. They simply found residents of the slums, described the features of the person they were looking for, and asked around if anyone recognized the description.

Subaru took on the role of asking questions, and the more times he did it, the more he grew used to the part.

"Might be that Felt kid. A quick-fingered little blonde girl, right?"

He stumbled onto this promising lead from the fourteenth man he'd questioned since starting his rounds—a man Subaru had greeted with a familiar, "Yo, brother, how's business?"

Taking pity on Subaru's filthy appearance, the "brother" spoke with a deeply sympathetic look.

"If it's that Felt girl, whatever she stole should be sitting in the Loot House by now. You tag it and leave it with the warehouse, and later the warehouse master sells it all off wholesale in some market elsewhere."

"That's a weird system... What happens if this 'warehouse master' guy just runs off with the whole lot?"

"He's the warehouse master precisely because he's trusted not to do that. Though, well, even if you tell him something was stolen, he's not gonna just say 'ah, I see' and hand it back. You'll need to negotiate and buy it back properly."

The man laughed, pressing the rule of the slums on Subaru as though it were only natural—kindly, but firm on that one point: that it was the victim's own fault for getting robbed in the first place.

Having gotten the location of the Loot House out of him, it seemed a reunion with the stolen goods was within reach before long.

However, another problem came surfacing. Namely, the fact that the two of them were flat broke.

"Buy it back, huh, but how? Since we're the ones at a disadvantage here, I've got a real strong 'gonna get ripped off' vibe about this whole event."

"We're only asking to get back something that was stolen from us in the first place—why should we have to pay money for that..."

Once the problem tilted toward the matter of funds, it was Satella who suddenly looked troubled.

The murmur that slipped from her was certainly the reasonable, correct position, but it was also true that the people they were dealing with weren't the type such reasoning would work on. To settle things peacefully—and reliably—it would be wise to follow the man's advice.

Still—

"Does that stolen insignia of yours look expensive at a glance? I mean, even if we're gonna get ripped off, I don't even know what the going rate would be."

"...There's a small gem set in the center. I don't know exactly how much it'd be worth in money either, but I'm sure it's not cheap."

"A gem, huh... yeah, that's trouble."

Even someone with no knowledge at all could tell at a glance that a gem was something valuable—it was that kind of convenient item.

This world didn't seem to have the technology to produce imitations, so anything that looked like a gem was almost certainly a real one. Which meant the price it would fetch was naturally going to be high.

While there was nothing at all to be relieved about, Subaru also found himself bothered by something odd in Satella's remark. The insignia should have been her own possession, and yet she'd said she didn't know its worth.

It was entirely possible it had been a gift from someone, but he couldn't help feeling a slight snag of doubt about it.

"Anyway, let's think about it once we actually get to this Loot House place. Depending on how the negotiation goes, maybe we can get it for a decent price..."

In the worst case, it might come at a painful cost to Subaru, but he did have a means.

Satella furrowed her brow at his trailing-off answer, but Subaru only waved a hand and said "It's nothing," in response to her confusion.

After about ten minutes of walking while mulling over how to manage the funding problem—
—they arrived in front of the building that seemed to be called the Loot House, and the two of them exchanged a look.

"Huh, it's bigger than I expected."

"Now I get why they call it a 'house' and not a 'shack.' ...If everything inside really is stolen goods, just as the name says, that's kind of hopeless."

Of course, since the goods were apparently sold off periodically, it probably wasn't literally overflowing with nothing but stolen items.

While Subaru thought this to himself, the Loot House stood before them, its appearance not at all matching its crude name, waiting for their visit.

Going by Subaru's frame of reference, the building's size was roughly equivalent to a convenience store. And not just the building itself—the convenience store including its parking lot.

Though it was only a single story, it had space enough to easily fit twenty cars. Around it, none of the houses that had lined the streets so far—small, filthy ruins and shack-like structures—were to be seen; with a tall outer wall at its back, it stood, quite literally, at the very deepest point of the slums.

"That tall wall over there is..."

"Probably the royal capital's outer wall. Seems like we've made it all the way to the edge without realizing it."

At Satella's words, Subaru vaguely pictured a map of the royal capital he was currently in.

It was probably a perfect square shape, enclosed on all four sides by walls like this one. Somewhere inside, either at the center or at the far north, sat the castle, and this slum was positioned some distance away from it.

Since he had the sense that two or three hours had passed since the search began, the royal capital seemed to be of a fairly considerable size. Though, compared to Japan, which had no such clear-cut boundary, there wasn't really anything to compare its scale against.

"Well, if the rumors are right, there's probably a warehouse master inside managing all the stolen goods... so, how should we play this from our position?"

"We'll go in honestly. Tell them we have something stolen, and ask to search inside, and if we find it, to give it back."

He'd insisted over and over that such straightforward reasoning wouldn't fly, but Satella wouldn't listen.

At her core, she was fundamentally straightforward. She couldn't easily convince herself to accept some crooked method as simply one valid option among others.

But then again, it was precisely because she was like that, that she'd helped Subaru even though it brought her no benefit of her own.

"Ah, all right. Then leave this part to me."

Since the odds of things going sideways were far too high, Subaru had no choice but to volunteer himself.

His last resort—it was coming into play a bit too early to feel like much of a trump card, but missing the timing to play it and letting the situation get more complicated was also a problem. When it came to this kind of decision, Subaru didn't hesitate.

Meanwhile, it was Satella who blinked in surprise at his offer.

She eyed the somehow confident-looking Subaru with a suspicious look, and said,

"All right. I'll leave it to you, Subaru."

"Yeah, I get that it's not easy to just nod along with that. I know full well I haven't exactly done anything up to this point to earn your trust. But I've got a plan, so just trust me and— wait, whaaaaat!?"

"W-why are you so surprised?"

"Because that whole exchange was totally supposed to be a 'we argue about it first' pattern!? I was fully braced for something like, 'Leave it to a freeloader with zero people skills like you? That's laughable enough to make my navel boil tea and my nose burst out laughing—a dog would do better'!?"

"I would never say something that awful!"

Satella was thoroughly irritated at Subaru's stream of paranoid delusions. Still, she cleared her throat once and fixed Subaru with a straight look from her amethyst eyes.

"It's true that, so far, you haven't been useful in the slightest, and there's been nothing in your actions or words that would make me reconsider my opinion of you, but..."

"Wow, what an evaluation of myself. Though I can't exactly argue against it!"

"But I don't think you're thoughtless, and I don't think you're the type of person who'd lie."

Grabbing him by the scruff of the neck, verbally speaking, just as he was about to dive into more self-deprecation, Satella caught her breath and continued, "So—"

"I'll try trusting you, Subaru. ...With about the attitude that if it works out, it's a bonus."

"For that second half, if instead of your real feelings, you'd said something like 'do your best for my sake,' it'd get me way more motivated, you know?"

"I can't force myself to say something like that. But—do your best."

In every sense of the word, she was a girl who couldn't tell a lie.

Subaru broke into a grin as his answer to her request, and headed toward the entrance of the Loot House.

His steps were by no means light, but he felt as though her expectation and that faint trust were pushing him forward from behind. If he didn't bring back at least one accomplishment, he'd end up being a genuinely useless case.

As he walked, Subaru looked down at what was in his hand—a plastic bag that had not once come up in conversation until now.

Inside it were the belongings he'd brought over from the modern world—the only things he could really call assets. If it came down to it, maybe one of them could be used to strike a barter deal.

He had no idea how valuable the insignia was, but there was almost certainly nothing in this world with rarity value exceeding that of a cell phone.

It would come at a cost to Subaru, but if he used some of his belongings as bargaining material, closing the deal itself wasn't impossible.

That was the last resort he hadn't told her about. To begin with, it was the kind of card that was useless in this world anyway. If there was a chance to use it, playing it without hesitation was the right call.

"Uhh, is anybody home?"

Standing in front of the door, he knocked on the wooden surface for the time being. A duller, lower sound than expected echoed back muffled, but there was no reaction from inside.

Feeling uneasy, he put his hand on the handle, and the door opened easily, as if there had never been a lock to begin with.

With absolutely no light source inside, the room was pitch dark in the night, leaving him to grope entirely blind. What was with this defenselessness—not even posting a lookout, for a place where stolen goods were supposedly gathered?

"I guess there's a chance he's just on a bathroom break, but... um, is anyone there?"

He poked just his head in to peer inside, but with not even moonlight reaching in, the interior was total darkness. Greeted by stagnant air and a stale, rank smell, Subaru steeled himself and stepped inside.

And then, before searching the interior, he turned back to Satella behind him.

"It'd be bad if we both went in and got mistaken for thieves, so could you stay outside?"

"Are you sure? Wouldn't it be better if I went in instead..."

"I've called out plenty already, so it's not like someone's just gonna jump me with a sword out of nowhere. It'd be more trouble if the warehouse master came back from outside and got the wrong idea, so please, I'm asking you."

At Subaru bowing his head, Satella thought it over briefly, then reached a hand into her robe. What she pulled out next was a white mineral stone.

She held the stone out in front of Subaru.

"At least take a light with you. Call for me whether there's no one there, or whether there is."

"I know. Be careful, right? ...So how do I make this thing light up?"

"You don't even know how to use Lagmite? You really are hopelessly clueless, aren't you, Subaru."

Speaking in a tone tinged with both exasperation and amusement, she gave the stone in her hand a light tap against the wall.

At once, the white stone began to give off a faint glow. The light was weak, dimly illuminating an area of at most a few meters, but it was enough light to check for danger underfoot and at hand.

"These days it's mostly been replaced by magic lamps that run on Mana, but this kind is more convenient, and I find it handy. If it goes out, just do the same thing and it'll light up again."

"I see... handy little mechanism. Well then, I'll go take a quick look inside."

With the faintly glowing Lagmite in one hand, Subaru tentatively stepped inside, hearing Satella's voice behind him say "Don't push yourself," and raising one hand in reply as he went.

Within the dimly secured field of view, what lay before Subaru as he passed through the entrance was a small counter. The Loot House might originally have been a tavern or some such building. Beyond the counter sat a cracked wooden crate, and he could guess that the warehouse master or someone had probably been sitting there.

On the counter, which seemed to have served as a reception desk, several small boxes, jars, and blades of various kinds were carelessly laid out—perhaps the merchandise.

Since he wasn't well-lit in either the figurative or literal sense—lacking both knowledge and actual light—he couldn't gauge their worth, but even to an amateur's eye, none of it looked especially valuable.

"Well, obviously, the pricier-looking stuff's probably further in."

Next to each stolen item lay a wooden tag, carved with what looked like letters cut by a blade. Going by what the man who'd told him this location had said, these were probably the names of the thieves who'd supplied the goods. If he just gathered up all these tags and handed them over to the guards, it sounded like that alone could round up the whole lot in one sweep.

"Though I don't know how much of a concept 'fake names' even is around here. And besides, in a case like this, the standard plot is that the shady underside of the government's corrupt and in cahoots with these kinds of shady types anyway."

Where the stolen goods that got sold off eventually ended up was suspicious in its own right too.

Entertaining the kind of unpleasant speculation he'd normally skip past in a novel or manga, Subaru's feet carried him further into the building.

There was still no sign of anyone, but even by the meager light, he could tell that the size and value of the stolen goods increased the further in he went.

If it was an insignia with a gem set in it, there was no doubt it would be somewhere near the very back.

Thinking this, his footsteps quickened unconsciously. —And it was just then.

"Hm?"

Subaru stopped at a sudden odd sensation under the sole of his shoe.

It wasn't the kind of sensation like stepping on something hard. Rather, it was the opposite—something sticky, as though the ground he'd stepped on were pulling back at his foot.

He lifted his foot and touched the sole of his sneaker with his fingertip, finding a liquid stuck thickly to it. Strangely viscous, it stretched slightly under his fingertip, stirring up an instinctive sense of revulsion.

"What is this?"

He brought his finger close to his nose to smell it, but since the air inside was already stagnant to begin with, he couldn't quite make it out. Needless to say, he had no courage at all to try tasting it with his tongue.

Wiping the unpleasant substance off on the wall, Subaru, driven by the discomfort welling up in him, pushed the light forward. The cause of it lay just ahead of where he'd been trying to go.

"...Ah?"

An involuntary, dumbstruck sound escaped him as Subaru finally recognized *it* for what it was.

Within the faint reach of the light, the first thing he saw was an "arm" lying carelessly on the ground. Its fingers were spread as if reaching for something, and strangely, there was nothing above the elbow.

He moved the light, searching for where it connected to, and found a leg cast further beyond. Fortunately, the leg was properly connected to a torso, and the torso had the other parts that should have been attached to it as well.

—The corpse of a large, elderly man, his throat slashed wide open, missing one arm.

"Hh—"

The instant he registered the corpse, a meaningless breath of air escaped Subaru's mouth.

In that moment, what dominated Subaru's mind was neither fear, nor despair, nor shock. An overwhelming blankness took over his brain, stripping every last bit of "thought" out from within it.

A blankness with no room even for choices like "flee" or "stay" to surface.

A useless, empty span that simply left him standing rigid, waiting for his brain to fully absorb the scene in front of him.

And that blankness brought a fatal outcome down upon Subaru's fate.

"—Ah, so you found it after all. Well, then, it can't be helped. Yes, it simply can't be helped."

He thought it had been a woman's voice.

He thought he'd heard a woman's voice—low, cold, and somehow taking pleasure in it.

"Guh—aah!"

There was no time to turn around.

The instant he tried to turn his face toward the source of the voice, Subaru's body was sent flying by a sudden impact.

Slammed back-first into the wall, the Lagmite knocked from his hand, his vision was swallowed in darkness.

But Subaru's consciousness wasn't focused on any of that. What consumed his awareness was—

"Guhh... ah, it's hot—"

—an overwhelming "heat" that gripped his entire body.

*—This is genuinely bad.*

Tasting the hard feel of the ground against his face, Subaru realized he'd collapsed facedown.

He couldn't put strength into any part of his body, and feeling had already left his fingertips.

Only the heat, bad enough to make him want to claw at his own throat, ruled the very center of his body.

*—Hot, hot, hot, hot, hot, hot, hot.*

The instant he opened his mouth to scream, what spilled out wasn't a shriek but a clot of blood.

Coughing, he vomited up, without restraint, the very wellspring of his life welling up from his throat. Blood gurgled out in a hemorrhage thick enough to froth pink foam at the corners of his mouth. In his blurred vision, he could see the ground dyed a deep crimson red.

*—Ah, so all of this... it's all my blood, huh.*

Enough blood loss that his fallen body was soaking in it. He'd heard that human blood made up about 8% of total body weight, and that losing a third of it was life-threatening—but this... hadn't he already lost all of it?

The bleeding from his mouth had run its course, but whatever was causing the searing "heat" that felt like it was burning through his body was still at work. His hand, barely able to move, reached toward his stomach, and upon finding an impossible sensation there, understanding dawned.

*—Oh, I see. My stomach's been torn open, huh.*

No wonder it felt hot. He seemed to be mistaking "pain" for "heat."

The sharp gash cut clean through his torso, nearly splitting him in two, held together only by a single layer of skin at the waist.

In short, it seemed he'd come face-to-face with what you'd call a "checkmate" on life.

The moment he understood this, his consciousness rapidly began to fade.

Even the "heat" that had forced him to writhe just moments ago vanished somewhere, and the unpleasant feel of blood, the sensation of his hand touching his own organs—all of it was carried off as an escort for his receding consciousness.

Left behind was only the flesh, denied the company of its own "soul."

With one last effort from his fading consciousness, he moved that flesh just a little. He turned his head upward.

Before his eyes, a pair of black shoes trod across the floor carpeted in fresh blood, sending ripples across it.

Someone was there. And that someone had, most likely, killed him.

Strangely, he felt no urge to look upon that person's face.

He had no memory of ever being the kind of opportunist who'd resign himself to just watching, even against someone who'd killed him—yet his heart couldn't muster a shred of interest in who that person even was.

All he wished for was just this—that she be safe.

"—baru?"

He thought he heard a voice like the ringing of a bell.

In a state where he couldn't even tell where his ears or nose were anymore, it was more likely just his imagination.

And yet, even if it was only a reconstruction pulled from memory, that voice stirred his emotions with an intense, pleasant ache.

So—

"—!"

A short scream rang out, and the carpet of blood welcomed a new participant.

The body that collapsed fell right beside him, and right there lay his own arm, limply outstretched.

That pale hand, fallen without strength, entwined with his own blood-soaked hand.

It was probably all just coincidence.

He thought he felt those faintly moving fingertips grip his hand back.

"...stay alive."

He grabbed his receding consciousness by the scruff of its neck and forced it to turn back around, buying himself a little more time.

Both the "pain" and the "heat" were all distant now, nothing but the futile howl of a defeated dog.

But even so—,

"I will, without fail—"

*—I will save you.*

The next moment, Natsuki Subaru lost his life.
