Arc 9 Chapter 4: "Dunce"


"—Madelyn?"

Spotting Madelyn Eschart striding boldly through the fortress, Emilia called out her name, eyes rounding in surprise.

At the call, the girl stopped, turning her black-horned head this way, twisting her lovely face as though she'd just bitten into something sour.

At that reaction, Emilia held up her hands to show there was no one else around her.

"You don't have to make that face. It's just me over here."

"...That's not reassuring at all-cha. The dragon's making this face because it's you-cha."

"Eh, is that so? Why?"

"Have you forgotten how much snow you made fall, how much you traded blows with the dragon and Mezoreia both!? There's no way the dragon could have a good impression of you-cha!"

Madelyn's golden eyes sharpened as her voice rose. At the bite in her words, Emilia murmured, "Oh, right..." and gave a small nod, recalling the history between them.

Indeed, Emilia had clashed with Madelyn twice — once at the walled city, once in the decisive battle for the capital. Both of them had been in the capital during the fight against the Great Disaster, too, though they simply hadn't crossed paths there. Come to think of it, this was the first time the two of them had exchanged words so calmly.

"So the last time was when I froze you solid, then?"

"—! That was you after all-cha!? The dragon couldn't move a muscle, and it was so cold, so cold, the dragon nearly... you're an outrageous one-cha!"

"Ugh, I'm sorry. I meant to thaw you out properly afterward, but you ran off with Mezoreia, so..."

"Don't go blaming the dragon for that, Human!"

Madelyn bared her sharp fangs, looking ready to snap at her with a bite. Emilia, a little startled by her lively energy, felt relieved at the same time.

After all, she'd heard that for a time, Madelyn had joined Mezoreia and become one of the Great Disaster's allies.

"But in the end, you sided with us properly, didn't you? I heard from Garfiel."

"...That tiger-human half-beast-cha?"

"That's right. He's my dependable ally, like a little brother to me. He's so-o-o strong."

"——"

In front of Emilia, who puffed out her chest with a proud little "hmph," Madelyn fell silent with a difficult expression. It wasn't that she doubted Emilia's words were a lie — the silence held some other meaning entirely.

Something closer, perhaps, to frustration, or embarrassment.

"—Madelyn, you're going to stay on as one of the Empire's Generals, aren't you?"

"—! You got a complaint about that-cha?"

"No, not at all. It's just, I heard Mezoreia, who was with you, went back, so I was thinking Madelyn chose differently."

Turning her gaze away from Madelyn, who stood facing her, Emilia looked out at the sky beyond the fortress.

Madelyn, an extremely rare Dragonkin, and the Cloud Dragon Mezoreia — Emilia didn't know the details of the bond between the two of them. But if they cared for each other so deeply, she thought their relationship might be something close to that between a Spirit and a Spiritual Arts user.

If that were so, then Mezoreia was to Madelyn what Puck was to Emilia. Choosing to part ways with someone like that couldn't have been easy.

"I've felt the same thing before, too. In my case, I wasn't even given the choice of whether to go with him or not... so I think Madelyn, who chose for herself, is so-o-o brave."

"Naturally-cha. —Don't go lumping the dragon together with a human-cha."

"Maybe so. But maybe not, right? You're Dragonkin, but are you tough enough that even the pain of parting from someone precious doesn't touch you? Is that really true?"

"You're an annoying woman-cha...!"

Said with a bitter face, the words made Emilia press a hand to her chest with a small "ugh."

She'd never been called "an annoying woman" as an insult before, and the sting of it felt strangely fresh. Lately, she'd been called "Half-Devil" less and less, and even when it happened, it no longer wounded her the way it once had. —Only that the one who had so boldly called her "Half-Devil" was no longer anywhere to be found.

"——"

"Don't go clamming up all of a sudden-cha. That's disrespectful in front of the dragon, you-cha."

"Mm, you're right. I'm sorry for making you feel lonely."

"—!!"

Stopped just as she was about to sink into thought, Emilia apologized, and Madelyn's face went red.

For a while she stayed red-faced, seeming to search for something to say, but then she let out a long breath, drew one in, and glared at Emilia again.

"Talking to humans is tiring-cha, but the dragon feels that especially with you-cha."

"That's probably because talking to me makes people nervous. Subaru told me that once."

It was something Subaru had told her, and it frustrated Emilia that there was nothing she herself could do about it, but apparently she had a way of making the people she met nervous. Subaru covered for the reason with jokes like "E.M.T." and other excuses, but she suspected the real cause was being a Royal Selection candidate, and being a Half-Elf.

"So, to keep that from happening, I try to keep the muscles in my cheeks relaxed as much as I can."

Her standing as a Royal Selection candidate, her Half-Elf birth, her silver hair and violet eyes that called the Witch of Envy to mind — none of it could be changed, so she wanted at least to soften her expression.

With that thought in mind, Emilia tried it out and asked Madelyn, "How is it?" Madelyn let out another long breath.

"Talking to you makes the dragon start to feel like deciding to stay here was a mistake-cha."

"Um, it's fine? We're leaving the Empire in a few more days, so..."

"Whether you're here! Or not! Has nothing to do with it-cha!"

Shouted at in short, clipped bursts, Emilia's eyes went round. Before her, Madelyn planted her hands on her hips and, as if returning the favor from before, turned her eyes to the sky outside the fortress.

"This country is a place the dragon's beloved... Balleroy, risked his life to protect-cha."

"—. Yes."

At Madelyn's whisper-like words, Emilia gave a small nod.

She'd heard that an Undead by the name of Balleroy had fought hard for their sake. That when the sky had been wrapped in that tremendous light, he'd been up there among the clouds covering the capital.

Emilia had been fighting Sphinx, who looked just like Echidna, and hadn't been able to spare any attention for that. So she had no idea at all what kind of person Balleroy had been.

"It's so-o-o disappointing that I can't say thank you to him."

"Don't go making eyes at the dragon's beloved-cha."

"Making eyes...?"

"Even without any thanks, the dragon's beloved fulfilled his duty-cha. ...Right up to the very end, he never once let the dragon join him in what he wanted to do-cha."

Hearing the thin, lonesome note in Madelyn's voice, Emilia narrowed her violet eyes.

The heavy, clouded feeling packed into Madelyn's small body — Emilia felt she understood it. No, she did understand it.

After all, Subaru, too, often decided things on his own without consulting her.

"But that's not because he thinks I'm unreliable or dislikes me — it's because he cares about me, and that's what makes it so-o-o hard to deal with."

"You..."

"It's because I'm cared for, after all. That makes it hard to get angry about, which is a little unfair, really."

If she weren't cared for — if she were thought unreliable or disliked — she could work hard to change how she was seen, for the better. But when she was already seen in a good light, wanting to do something more about it was very difficult.

"So — it's unfair. What are we supposed to do, the two of us?"

"...Unfair."

At Emilia's words, Madelyn lowered her head as though lost in thought.

She didn't know Balleroy well, but Emilia felt, somehow, that the frustration Madelyn held for him was close to what she herself felt for Subaru.

But even if their troubles were close, the answer Madelyn had arrived at was one far removed from Emilia's own.

Because—

"—I can't stay behind in this country that Priscilla protected."

If it came to risking one's life, then without question, one of the people who had done so was Priscilla — even now, days later, her death still didn't feel real to Emilia, who thought of her as a "friend."

"——"

She wished they could have become the kind of friends who called each other that. But Priscilla had disappeared before that could come to pass, and so now it was only Emilia, one-sidedly, who wanted to call her that.

Still, just before she vanished, Priscilla had appeared while Emilia and Anastasia were talking, and for a brief, short while, laughter had passed between them — Emilia would go on lingering over that, longing for it, for a very long time.

She couldn't help dreaming of the unfulfilled hope that they could have gone on having more of that calm, gentle time — so much more, so much longer, so much deeper — endlessly, from here on.

And so, as Emilia pictured the far side of that vanished dream—

"—Eh?"

Her arm was suddenly pulled, and Emilia, unable to plant her feet at all, was tugged off balance. Just as she was about to topple over, her head was caught, softly, on Madelyn's shoulder.

With the difference in their height, it left Emilia resting her head against Madelyn's shoulder. Had she been off by just a little more, Madelyn's horn would have jabbed right into her face.

"Um, Madelyn? That startled me all of a sudden, so..."

Thinking she shouldn't let this go on, Emilia tried to gently scold her.

But—

"Don't cry-cha."

"——"

At that single line, Emilia blinked, and then noticed drops of water soaking into the shoulder she'd rested her head against. For a moment she didn't know what it was, but Madelyn's words caught her up. —Tears.

They were tears whose cause and whose object were both perfectly clear.

"I thought we could become friends."

"——"

"I got along well with Anastasia-san... and I could talk properly with Felt-chan and Crusch-san too, so I thought Priscilla was the most stubborn one of all... and then finally, I was able to talk with her, too, and I thought there'd be so much more, from here on..."

"——"

"I thought I could come to like Priscilla. I wanted to like her."

As if chasing after the tears that had once begun to fall, words and feelings came spilling out.

Always so proud and imposing, sometimes very mean-spirited, wearing a face that claimed to know everything by logic Emilia couldn't follow — and yet, underneath, someone with a truly gentle heart.

Priscilla, who had shown her so many faces, good and bad alike — Emilia had wanted to cherish her even more. She had wanted to take a long time and nurture that feeling.

To Emilia, from whom such feelings — regret, or perhaps longing — came tumbling out—

"The dragon—"

"Eh?"

"The dragon will protect it-cha."

Madelyn said that, gently resting a hand on the head of the Emilia crying tears onto her shoulder. At those words, Emilia, her eyes wet, was startled.

In their position, with their faces pressed almost cheek to cheek, neither could see the other's expression. Emilia's face was a crying one, but what kind of face was Madelyn making?

Without ever showing that face, Madelyn went on.

"The Empire is the country Balleroy protected-cha. The dragon has reason to protect this place-cha. —And the reason for your tears lived and died for this country too, cha. So then—"

"...So then?"

"The dragon will protect it too, alongside you-cha. Unlike a human, that much is nothing at all once it's in the dragon's hands-cha."

Emilia had always believed that important talks should be had while looking someone in the eye — that this was an absolute, essential rule for when a conversation truly mattered.

But this time, Emilia thought it was for the best that neither of them could see the other's face — her own tear-streaked one, and the face of Madelyn, who had declared those words so boldly.

Even words that were terribly important, truly precious — surely there were some that couldn't be said while looking someone in the face.

And surely there were words that could only be given precisely because you weren't looking at the other's face. Madelyn's words just now seemed, to Emilia, to be exactly that kind of word — sayable only because of that.

"—Mm. Thank you, so-o-o much."

"It's just incidental-cha. The dragon didn't do it to be thanked-cha."

"I know. But I want to say it. Thank you, Madelyn."

At Emilia's repeated thanks, she heard Madelyn give a small snort.

Whether it was one of genuine annoyance, or one hiding embarrassment, she couldn't tell without seeing her face. She realized there was a kind of fun to be had, too, in conversations where you couldn't see the other's face.

She had, it seemed, opportunities like this to cherish such trivial, foolish little things.

And then—

"—Ah! Look, Madelyn!"

"Guah!?"

It happened just as she'd tucked that deep, precious feeling away in her heart.

Spotting a small figure walking along the passage directly across the fortress, Emilia, in her surprise, grabbed hold of Madelyn's horn and wrenched her head around to look the same way.

Paying no mind to Madelyn's pained groan, Emilia's attention was drawn entirely to the figure. —A small boy with peach-colored hair; it was Schult, who had been Priscilla's attendant.

"Schult-kun..."

The moment she caught sight of him, a pain like something wringing tight around her chest struck Emilia.

Priscilla's death had shaken Emilia deeply, who had been picturing a future together with her. But the grief of those who had deeply adored Priscilla was on an entirely different scale.

Even though she knew that comparing griefs, comparing misfortunes, only ever left one feeling lonelier.

"—!"

"Ah! You!"

The instant the thought struck her, Emilia kicked off the floor and broke into a run toward the passage where she'd seen Schult. Madelyn's flustered voice called after her back, but her feet didn't stop.

Racing headlong through the fortress, Emilia rounded the corner into the opposite passage.

"Schult-kun!"

"—Emily-sama, is that you?"

At the force of Emilia's voice, Schult's small, thin shoulders trembled, and he turned around.

Facing the young boy head-on, Emilia caught her breath softly. His fluffy peach-colored hair, his crisp, clean clothes — everything about him was proper. But even so, he couldn't hide the redness around his eyes or the exhaustion on his tired face, and his color looked somehow poor.

Still, rather than sobbing away in his room, he had come outside.

"...You came outside. That's so-o-o..."

Emilia hesitated over whether to follow that with words like "wonderful" or "admirable."

She'd rushed over unable to sit still, but she hadn't chosen her words at all. Whether what Schult needed right now was comfort, or encouragement, or—

"There's no need to apologize to me, Emily-sama."

"—Ah."

"Because Emily-sama, you kept your promise, after all."

At Schult, who murmured that with lowered eyes, his gaze cast down, Emilia regretted her own manner.

Schult had seen through her hesitation, had seen that she'd wavered over whether to voice an apology. —Before the battle with the Great Disaster, Emilia had made Schult a promise: that she would surely bring Priscilla back, and let him see her again.

That, Emilia had not been able to fulfill. The one who had fulfilled it was Priscilla herself.

But she mustn't apologize for that. If she did, everything Schult had endured up to now, every word Priscilla had spoken to him, would all become a lie.

"Ee, don't be so down."

"...Utakata-chan, were you with Schult-kun?"

Emilia noticed the girl who had popped her face up beside her, peering up from below — Utakata.

Around the same age as Schult and an important friend of his, she seemed to have stayed with him instead of moving with the rest of the People of Shudrak.

At Emilia's words, Utakata gave a small nod, a little "Nn."

"Mii and Taa busy too. Suu and Aa look rough too. Only Uu can stay with Shuu."

"Yes, that's right. Thank you for not leaving Schult-kun alone."

"Ee's thanks, received."

Utakata thrust her fist out at her with a little jerk; sensing something of Subaru in the gesture, Emilia tapped her own fist lightly against the one offered.

Then Emilia turned back to face Schult once more.

"Are you sure you don't need to still be resting in your room?"

"...I've already rested plenty. To begin with, I'm small and weak, so I hardly do any work at all... so right now, there's a lot I want to do."

"Shuu try hard, lots. If do that, Puu's soul surely feel at ease too."

"That's right, it is."

"——"

Emilia was surprised that Utakata had said that so naturally, and that Schult had nodded along with it.

At Emilia's surprise, Utakata tilted her head. "What wrong?"

"Life go around. Prey, human, enemy, ally, all same. That how Uu's people think. Uu's mother too, Shuu's Puu too, go around. Surely go around."

"...And when it goes around and around like that, what happens?"

"Born again. In different shape. Prey, human, enemy, ally, all maybe different. But still so."

That the souls of the dead circled round, and came back once more.

It was a way of thinking that didn't quite click for Emilia. Still, it felt better than the idea that someone who had died was simply dead, and that everything ended there.

When a person died, the Od that should have been in their body slipped away, dissolving whole into the Mana that filled the world. Even if that was, in fact, what happened — if some shape, some trace of that person, still remained within what had dissolved—

"—Is that what the Book of the Dead is?"

Murmuring that, Emilia remembered she'd once personally felt something close to proof of what Utakata was saying.

Inside the Pleiades Watchtower, in the archive of the Book of the Dead where the living memories of the dead were kept, the traces of countless people from this world remained, firmly intact. That such a thing could be preserved seemed, in itself, like proof that time had been set aside to copy something from the soul of a person who had died into a book.

"——"

For the first time, Emilia felt a strong attachment — an obsession, almost — toward the Book of the Dead.

It was different from the time back in that tower, when Subaru's "memories" had once been lost — this was an attachment to the Book of the Dead in its true, original sense.

Emilia, too, had people whose deaths she could never forget — Fortuna, Juice, people she could never meet again even after the freeze over the Elior Forest had thawed.

But her parting with Fortuna and the others, sad as it was, had reached its resolution.

So what stirred this attachment in Emilia was a grief she had not yet overcome.

If it could be granted, she wanted to know what Priscilla had thought in her final moment — whether, beyond that laughing conversation, she had wanted to see the same thing Emilia had. —Had she not spoken with Madelyn, that is.

Had there been no time spent with someone who worried alongside her over how to settle what she couldn't fully digest — her parting from Priscilla — surely...

"I was taken in by Priscilla-sama. Priscilla-sama chose me."

"Schult-kun..."

Before Emilia, who had fallen silent, Schult tensed his cheeks tight and slowly lifted his lowered face, saying that haltingly, but with unmistakable heat behind it.

Though his round eyes welled with tears, the boy went on.

"Priscilla-sama loved beautiful things, and magnificent things. And... Priscilla-sama chose me... if I'm no good, hopeless, I'll be tarnishing Priscilla-sama's name...!"

"——"

"That is something I cannot allow."

At Schult, answering with all the strength he could muster, Emilia's eyes widened.

It might have been an illusion, some trick of the eye. But for just an instant, the briefest instant, she felt as though a dazzling light had dwelt within Schult as he declared that.

And that was none other than the very same thing Priscilla, who had lived like a flame, had always made her feel.

—Emilia sensed that this was the moment Schult had determined the course of his own life.

In the life ahead of him, countless hardships would surely rain down on Schult. But he would never break under them again, never shrink back in fear.

The reason he wouldn't: Schult had taken the flame he'd watched so closely, so near at hand, and let it dwell within his own chest — deciding, in that moment, never to let the firewood of his own vitality run out.

"There's still so little I'm able to do on my own. I'm studying as hard as I can, but there's nothing but shortcomings."

"Emily-sama?"

"Even now, and surely still on into the future, I'm someone whose shortcomings just get filled in, propped up, and helped along by everyone around me. I know it's so-o-o presumptuous of someone like that to say this. But let me say it anyway."

"——"

"I'll be there for you, Schult-kun, from here on too. I promise, I'll be someone you can rely on."

Placing a hand over her own chest, Emilia said it straight to the small boy before her.

It was a decision of Emilia's that cast aside every other reason — whether it was right or wrong given the broken promise to bring Priscilla back, or pity for a boy left in such a helpless position after losing someone so precious to him.

It was, instead, the expression of a simple wish: that she wanted to support Schult, just as he was, in the stance he'd taken.

"...Thank you very much, Emily-sama."

Taking a while to let Emilia's words sink in, Schult finally loosened his lips just slightly and answered so.

At his reply, Emilia thought that she'd made a promise all on her own, and that she'd have to apologize to Roswaal and Otto for it later — but she also thought it had been good to say it.

"If you're saying that, then I'm glad. I need to talk about this with the others, too... Are Al, and Reinhard's father, around?"

In practice, now that Priscilla was gone, what would become of the Barielle domain in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica was an immediate concern. Even so, while still in the Vollachia Empire, it would surely be difficult to discuss the matter fully.

Al, in particular, was in the same position as Subaru — someone who had watched Priscilla disappear with his own eyes.

"—Subaru."

It frustrated Emilia terribly, speaking Subaru's name, that she had her hands so full with herself.

Before Emilia's group had reunited in the Empire, Subaru and Rem had already made contact with Priscilla, spending time together that Emilia and the others knew nothing about — and for that, they'd been wounded even more deeply than Emilia herself.

If things were as they should be, she'd have wanted to be by their side, even while bearing her own pain and grief.

"But now that I've finally gotten ready, once I've talked about this here..."

She could go talk to Subaru and Rem, and the other people carrying grief in their hearts.

Before Emilia as she resolved this, Schult continued, "Um—"

"Al-sama has stayed shut up in his room the whole time. Heinkel-sama, I was just about to bring him some water..."

Saying so, Schult glanced beside him at Utakata, who was holding an empty water jug. It seemed the plan had been to draw water and head straight for Heinkel afterward.

Realizing she'd held them up with quite the long conversation, Emilia opened her mouth, meaning to go along with their original plan—

"—Oh, ah."

Suddenly, a low, groaning voice came from deeper in the passage, and Emilia looked up. Her eyes met an unexpected face, and she blinked in surprise.

Standing there was none other than the very person who had just come up in conversation—

"Heinkel-sama! You came outside!"

Schult's voice rose as he called out to Heinkel, his red hair unkempt and his face shadowed with stubble. He likely hadn't taken care of his appearance since that time, and he looked even more worn down now than he had when the battle against the Great Disaster had ended.

The bags under his eyes were terrible, plainly showing he hadn't been sleeping. And yet those sunken blue eyes gleamed with an eerie intensity, fixed on them — no, on Emilia.

"I'll bring you water right away, so..."

"Emilia-sama...!"

Even as he said it, Schult bravely tried to support Heinkel's swaying body. But Heinkel paid him no mind at all, calling out Emilia's name instead.

Frowning at his manner, Emilia nodded, "Yes?"

"Listen, you look terrible. Like Schult-kun said, let's get you some water and have you rest. If you haven't been eating, I'll go get you something..."

"No, no, that doesn't matter... I mean, it really doesn't matter. More than that, Emilia-sama, there's something I want to discuss!"

"—!"

Emilia's hand, reaching out to touch his shoulder and calm him, was seized by Heinkel instead. Startled by the strength of his grip, her throat catching, Heinkel brought his face in close.

And then, his blue eyes — the same blue as Reinhard's — trembling violently, he said:

"Let me... no, allow me, to serve you, Emilia-sama. I swear it! I swear I'll prove useful to your Royal Selection...!"

"Wh-what are you talking about? So suddenly... you're Priscilla's attendant, aren't you?"

"I can be useful! I'll be a deterrent! To win the Royal Selection, there's someone you absolutely have to force to back off, isn't there? As long as he's there, no one stands a chance. But with me there, even Reinhard won't be able to lift a hand—"

"—!"

At the ghastly desperation in Heinkel's manner, the words caught in Emilia's throat.

Emilia, who had lately been coming to think that acting to solve a problem before she even finished thinking it through was one of her strengths, was silenced by the obsessive fixation in Heinkel's manner.

His self-promotion was so grim, so utterly shameless — if this was Heinkel's own way of facing Priscilla's disappearance, then—

"I'm so-o-o sad..."

"I understand, but it's the truth! You can't beat Reinhard. Not without me. That's why Lady Priscilla took me in, too. That's right, the fact that Lady Priscilla did that is proof. Emilia-sama! Let's avenge Lady Priscilla's regrets together!"

"——"

Had there been even a shred of grief for Priscilla in that, Emilia might have lent him her ear.

But in Heinkel's plea, she could feel nothing of that kind of feeling for Priscilla. Had it been there, he surely would have properly seen the sorrow on Schult's face.

And so Emilia bit down on her lip toward Heinkel—

"That's en—"

"Silence-cha."

The next instant, a violent punch cut in front of Emilia, and Heinkel's chin, right there before her, was slammed upward from below with tremendous force. With a short, strangled "Guh," Heinkel's eyes rolled white and he collapsed flat on the spot.

It was force enough that an ordinary person's neck and head parting ways would have been no surprise at all.

"M-Madelyn!?"

"What a contemptible fool-cha. And you — don't go feeling sorry for a guy like that-cha."

Shaking out the hand she'd used to punch Heinkel, Madelyn spat the words out, sullen.

After Emilia had rushed off to Schult's side, Madelyn had caught up slowly behind her and watched the situation in silence — but here, it seemed, her patience had reached its limit.

"Heinkel-sama! Heinkel-sama! Are you all right!?"

"Don't go shrieking-cha. It galls the dragon, but this is a human who didn't die even when the dragon punched him for real-cha. The dragon only knocked him out, is all-cha."

Madelyn said this to Schult, who had thrown himself at the utterly flattened Heinkel, worried over him. But even so, given the force of that blow, worrying was only natural.

Still, it wasn't as though Emilia couldn't understand why Madelyn had done it.

"Madelyn, I..."

"The dragon protects the Empire-cha. But the dragon has no intention of protecting anything beyond that-cha. Take that worthless human back with you right away-cha. There won't be a next time-cha."

"...Mm, understood."

Emilia understood that Madelyn's words, her anger held in check, were her own way of settling the matter. With that understanding, Emilia crouched down beside Schult and looked at the fallen Heinkel.

She gently held out a hand over him, knocked out completely by a blow of that force to the jaw.

"I'm not very good at it, but just some first aid before we get him to the healing room..."

She activated her healing Magic, anxious that no lasting damage remain from Madelyn's blow. Beside her as she worked, Schult, still worried about Heinkel, said:

"Um, Emily-sama... Heinkel-sama, he was, um, very tired."

"Schult-kun..."

"Heinkel-sama is properly sad about Priscilla-sama too, it is. He seems to have had some promise with Priscilla-sama, and that's just... it's got him overwhelmed, is all..."

"Mm, it's all right. It's all right."

"...It is."

At Schult, bowing his head and looking down like that, Emilia smiled at him as best she could manage. If it could be even a small comfort or encouragement to him, that would be enough.

And so, as Emilia continued casting her healing Magic on Heinkel, Utakata, the water jug balanced atop her head, watched her with round eyes and said:

"Ee, admirable."

At Utakata's words, murmured offhandedly, without a second thought, Emilia caught her breath softly. It was nothing more than the unfiltered impression of a young girl spilling out — and yet it made Emilia realize something.

That she, too, was no longer merely one more person grieving Priscilla's death — she had earned the right to be someone who stood beside others grieving that same death.

And that was something she had wanted so badly, thinking of Subaru and Rem, that she could have reached out and grabbed it with her bare hands—

"—Priscilla, you dunce."

—and yet it also marked the end of a time when she could simply think of her terribly mean-spirited friend, and nothing more. That thought alone was lonely and fleeting enough to make her body tremble from the inside.

<notes>
- Title "オタンコナス" (a colloquial word for "fool/blockhead") rendered as "Dunce" to match its use in the closing line; a substitution rather than literal transliteration.
- Madelyn's sentence-final tic "っちゃ" has no direct English equivalent; rendered as an appended "-cha" throughout to preserve her distinctive dialect, alongside her third-person self-reference "the dragon."
- Schult addresses Emilia as "エミリー様" (Emily-sama), distinct from the correct "エミリア様" (Emilia-sama) that others like Heinkel use — this contrast appears intentional in the source and was preserved.
