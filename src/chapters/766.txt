Arc 10 Chapter 8: "The Result of the Choice"

"—We are endlessly grateful for your repeated concern for this household."

"No, no, that's only natural. It's nothing you need to thank us for."

Saying so while thoroughly abashed, Subaru flailed Beatrice's hands—she was sitting on his lap—about in fluster, in response to the words of the person serving the tea: Wilhelm.

Beatrice shot him a flat look at that gesture, but she could probably feel his nervousness through the heartbeat against her back. With a resigned little sigh, she kept right on serving as Subaru's puppet.

—Having just finished their audience with Filore, who was at that very moment busy plunging the Royal Selection into chaos, Subaru and the others, unable to sit still after hearing at the Divine Dragon Church's chapel that Crusch had awoken, had come racing straight here, to the Karsten mansion in the noble quarter.

Naturally, proper etiquette demanded making an appointment before visiting. But this had been a visit so socially inept that they'd forgotten even that much—and still, they'd been welcomed in without complaint.

Shown into the parlor by Wilhelm, Subaru and the others sat lined up on the sofa—four of them, with Beatrice on Subaru's lap—waiting with bated breath for that moment to arrive.

Perhaps noticing how impatient Subaru was, Wilhelm bowed deeply.

"Please allow a little more time for Lady Crusch to make herself ready. I apologize."

"No, no, we're the ones who barged in unannounced. If anything, we're just grateful we weren't turned away."

"We would never turn you away. Lady Crusch herself was overjoyed to hear that you and Lady Emilia had come to visit."

"No, no, no, that's—if anything it might go the other way, since seeing Crusch-san awake might actually make me cry..."

It was a pathetic thing to admit, but there was a genuine, non-joking chance of that happening, so he wanted Wilhelm and Emilia and the others to brace themselves in advance.

For now, as if to hold back his impatience, all he could do was flail Beatrice's arms wildly in time with each "no, no" he gave in response to Wilhelm.

"—Hold on, that is far too much flapping of Betty's hands, I suppose! Honestly, my shoulders and arms are starting to ache!"

"Ngh... sorry, my bad, I'm sorry, I just can't calm down. I mean..."

Beatrice, who had been dutifully serving as Subaru's emotional-support puppet, barked out at having reached the end of her patience. Poking her cheek, Subaru paused there for a beat, cutting his words short.

He felt the frustration, the urge to hurry. But it was worth enduring.

"I mean, Crusch-san..."

"—Yes. That much is certain."

To Subaru's sigh, heavy with a thousand feelings, Wilhelm nodded, just as deeply moved.—No, to say "just as" was far too presumptuous.

Wilhelm's relief had to be many times, dozens of times greater than Subaru's. The recovery of the person to whom he'd sworn his loyalty was that momentous an event.

"I've already said it so many times, but, Wilhelm-san, I'm really so glad."

The one offering Wilhelm renewed words of comfort was Emilia, seated beside Subaru.

Having been present when Filore used the Church's sacred rite to heal Crusch, Emilia had already offered Wilhelm and the others her comfort that very day. Even so, Crusch's consciousness hadn't returned afterward, so now, with the chance to speak to a waking Crusch right in front of her, Emilia's violet eyes shone with anticipation as well.

"Honestly, at this rate I'm frightened of what blunder the two of them might commit, I suppose. Rem, if Betty and you don't keep our wits about us here, things could turn dreadful, I suspect."

"Indeed. Please leave it to Rem if things go too far. Rem has been getting used to handling the iron ball and its weight, little by little."

With Subaru and Emilia so restless, it was reassuring to have Beatrice and Rem—the two of them keeping the composure proper to visitors—ready for when it truly mattered. Though the faint clink of chains mixed into Rem's slightest shift of posture was both frightening and, admittedly, a little thrilling.

Whenever he asked Rem to back him up, Subaru figured he'd need a fair amount of resolve.

"Oh right, speaking of backup, where's Ferris? Helping Crusch-san get changed? There's a bunch of stuff I want to talk to him about too."

Suddenly noticing that a face that ought to be here was missing, Subaru voiced the question.

Subaru and the others, and Wilhelm too, no doubt felt joy beyond hope at Crusch's recovery. But the one who felt it most keenly of all should be none other than Crusch's foremost knight.

Of course, the fact that Crusch's salvation had come by way of the Divine Dragon Church must have stung bitterly for him, a healer himself. But Subaru had no doubt that he was the kind of person who could rejoice in Crusch's safety more than he blamed himself out of helplessness.

And yet—

"..."

"—? Wilhelm-san?"

At the sight of Wilhelm's cheek stiffening for just an instant, Subaru frowned in puzzlement. But Wilhelm quickly erased that stiffness with an "My apologies," and said,

"As for Ferris... the truth is, he's fallen a bit ill. It seems that right after the matter of Lady Crusch's body, all the strain he'd been under came bursting out at once."

"Oh no, something like that happened after all that? ...I'm worried about Ferris. Even after Crusch-san finally woke up, if he ends up collapsing himself, he'll blame himself for it."

"Yeah, that's right. Wilhelm-san, is Ferris doing badly?"

"...No, rest should set him right. But for now, I'd ask that you refrain from visiting him. I believe he himself wouldn't want to intrude on the feelings of those visiting Lady Crusch."

"I wouldn't call it intruding, but... all right, understood. I'll come another time."

It wasn't quite a case of misfortunes piling on misfortunes, but given such circumstances, Emilia nodded at Wilhelm's suggestion, and Subaru agreed as well.

Subaru and the others planned to stay in the royal capital a while longer yet. Even if they missed today, there would be other chances to console Ferris. For now, they ought to respect his own wishes.—Just as Subaru and the others let out a breath at that thought—

"—My apologies for the long wait."

Hearing that and seeing who had opened the parlor door, Subaru found himself on his feet before he knew it.

At the entrance of the room, the figure who appeared was a beautiful woman in a dark-colored nightgown with a pink stole draped over her shoulders, her slender frame and long green hair unmistakable—Crusch Karsten herself.

"..."

The last time he'd seen her, Crusch's breathing had been ragged and pained, and she'd barely been able to raise herself from the bed. Now, seeing her standing on her own two feet, showing herself before them like this, joy and emotion beyond all hope welled up in him.

Still, as she looked at Subaru with a fragile smile, half of her face remained wrapped in bandages covering her left eye, and that was inevitably the first thing to catch his eye—

"Please, don't worry. This bandage is only because one eye is still a little hard to see out of—the greater part of the affliction... no, the affliction itself has been removed."

"Ah..."

"So please, don't let your face cloud over like that."

Hearing that, Subaru reflected ruefully that he'd come to comfort her, and instead had made her worry about him.

He was startled that the bandage still covered part of her face, but far more worth noting was the fact that Crusch had come to meet them on her own two feet.

Her condition had been serious enough that such a thing should have seemed an impossible feat.

"..."

"—Subaru-sama?"

Noticing Subaru had fallen silent, Crusch, still smiling, narrowed her amber eyes. Gazed at by that single eye now, Subaru chose his words with care.

After all, if he got even one word, one breath wrong, the tears he was holding back seemed liable to spill over in an instant.

"...Um, listen, Crusch-san. There's a lot—really a lot—I wanted to say, and most of it was stuff like 'sorry' or 'my fault,' all kinds of apologies."

"—. Yes."

"But now that I'm actually meeting you face to face like this, all that's coming out is stuff like 'thank goodness' or 'what a relief.' I really, really am relieved..."

He should have had several model answers prepared in advance, as a proper visitor.

On the way here to the mansion, and during this waiting time too, he'd desperately run simulations in his head of the best possible words to say, and thought he'd picked out the finest answer.

And yet the moment he met the real Crusch, all of it had been blown away, and what came out was nothing but an immature, formless relief, just like a child's.

"..."

At the words wrung out from Subaru's pathetic self-awareness, Crusch's eyes widened for a moment before a soft smile returned to her lips.

"Hearing you say that puts me at ease as well. I've shown you my unsightly side so many times, Subaru-sama, that I half expected you'd have given up on me by now."

"No way! Not at all, never, there's no way! I mean, anyway, you shouldn't be standing around talking right after recovering from illness. Um, please, sit down."

"Fufu, then I'll do just that."

Flustered and thrown off his pace, Subaru gestured toward the chair across from him, and Crusch, laughing with a hand at her mouth, accepted his escort. Somehow it was Subaru orchestrating things, but this was her house, so of course it was laughable.

Unable to help how awkward the conversation had become, Subaru watched Crusch take a seat with Wilhelm's assistance, then shot a resentful glance at Emilia and the others right beside him.

"Somehow it's just me flailing around while everyone else is calm—isn't that unfair? Why am I the one being made to dance around here?"

"Well, you were the one who was most worried about Crusch-san, weren't you, Subaru? So I figured you'd be the one who most wanted to talk to her first."

"Rem is in no position to push herself forward here right now. Besides, there was the matter of Beatrice-chan as well."

"The matter of Bea-ko...? Wait, huh? Why's Bea-ko on Rem's lap? Weren't you sitting on my lap?"

"I was, but I got sent flying by the force of you standing up, I suppose! If Rem hadn't caught me nicely, I'd be sprawled spread-eagle in the middle of the room right now!"

"O-oh, is that right, my bad. Literally didn't even see you there..."

"That is a terrible way to put it, I suppose!"

Puffing out her cheeks, Beatrice clung to Rem as if out of spite. Leaving her in the arms of Rem, who was fully ready to receive her, Subaru reflected once more that he'd been far too oblivious to his surroundings.

At this exchange between Subaru and the others, Crusch too deepened her smile with a soft "fufu." At her reaction, Emilia narrowed her violet eyes and said,

"I'm sorry everyone's being so noisy, even though this is supposed to be a visit."

"No, I don't dislike a bit of noise. And besides, the fact that you all came to visit as soon as you heard the news... that made me happy."

"Well, that's good then. ...Ah, but I'll make sure they don't get too carried away."

Mindful of Crusch's condition, Emilia added one last, characteristically Emilia-like remark.

In it could be seen consideration, and the will to choose her words properly—and even in a scene like this, one could feel Emilia's visible growth.

Of course, taking Crusch's words too much at face value wouldn't do either, but—

"How are you feeling?"

"Yes. I've become considerably more comfortable. It will likely take a little more time for my strength to fully recover, but I don't believe there's any major issue."

So she answered, adjusting the stole draped over her shoulders. Being freshly recovered, her complexion was still a bit pale, but her responses were natural, and there was no shadow over her intelligence.

From what he could see, even her way of walking was proper, and aside from the bandage over her left eye, no trace of that day seemed to remain on her. For the moment, at least, it could be called a relief.

Still—

"I've received a report on what happened while I was bedridden as well. ...Including the news that Lady Priscilla has passed away."

If her intelligence remained intact, then Crusch also understood, all too well, that this was not a situation where one could simply rejoice, unreservedly, over her physical recovery.

"..."

Even knowing it was an unavoidable topic, one that seemed inappropriate to bring up so soon, it was Subaru and the others whose cheeks stiffened at having it raised by none other than Crusch herself.

As though she had no interest in the small comfort of celebrating Crusch's recovery here and saving the deeper matters of the Royal Selection for another time.

"As far as I can recall, I hardly ever exchanged words with Lady Priscilla. But there's no doubting that she too, in her own way and by her own convictions, took part in the battle at Priestella... and by extension, the Royal Selection. Which is precisely why we must carry on the Royal Selection for her sake as well."

"—. Yes, I feel exactly the same way. For Priscilla's sake too, I don't think we can afford to stay discouraged forever."

"Just so. I'm glad. That you feel the same way, Lady Emilia."

Letting out a breath of relief before the nodding Emilia, Crusch brought the teacup to her lips. Having wetted her tongue and lips with it, she fixed them with the amber gleam of her one remaining eye, and continued, "In that case—"

"I believe you'll agree that what we should prioritize now is the stability of the kingdom. Within the Royal Selection, the Witch Cult's actions were certainly a bitter blow, but spending too much time on countermeasures and dealing with them would be misguided, out of keeping with the true purpose of the Royal Selection itself."

"Mm, I understand that too. The Royal Selection is meant to decide the kingdom's future, not to deal with the Witch Cult. That's what you mean, right?"

"Yes, exactly. Of course, dealing with those people is a task rulers must take up—but to place it first would be exactly what plays into their hands. We must remember the true duty of the Royal Selection, its very essence."

"In that case, Felt-chan is in the royal capital right now too. It seems Anastasia-san hasn't been able to make it back here yet, but if we can talk things over, I'd gladly—"

"—And what's more," Crusch cut in, interrupting Emilia partway and leaning slightly forward. For an instant, at a sensation like being pressed by a bared blade, Emilia's eyes widened slightly.

Paying no mind to Emilia's reaction, Crusch only intensified the light in her amber eyes further.

"I've heard. That you've opened a path to the tower of the 'Sage'... the Pleiades Watchtower. I'm most impressed—truly a remarkable achievement. If that tower holds the power rumor claims it does, it could well serve as considerable leverage against other nations. Its handling must be carefully deliberated with the Council of Wise Men."

"Crusch-san..."

"And the crucial matter of the Rite of Dragon's Vessel... the deadline for the vote to decide the next monarch is now less than a year and a half away. From here on, the merits of each camp's platform will be scrutinized all the more. We must, more than ever before, look to the faces of those around us and listen to their voices."

"Wait, Crusch-san, I do want to talk about this. But please don't rush so much. Right now—"

"—I!"

"—!"

The instant Emilia tried, with sheer feeling, to hold back the muddy torrent of Crusch's words, which had come pouring out all at once, unstoppable, a sharp cry tore through the parlor.

The teacup in Crusch's hand was set back down on its saucer with force, spilling tea with a sharp clatter. Wilhelm instantly drew Crusch's hand back, laid a handkerchief over his mistress's hand where the tea had splashed, and swiftly withdrew the cup.

Throughout, Crusch, letting herself be handled, never once took her eyes off Subaru and the others, not for a single moment.

Without looking away, her thin lips trembled, and she said,

"Am I... wrong?"

"..."

The composed, intelligent tone she'd held until just a moment before broke down, and the question came out wet with tears.

In truth, there were no tears in Crusch's eyes. But her voice was weeping. The sheer gap between her reason and her emotion cut into Subaru's chest with a fierce, cutting pain.

Crusch understood, of course.

She was intelligent, a woman of superior insight, a hard worker who had continued to face down countless responsibilities and challenges—so it made no difference whether Subaru and the others chose their words carefully or failed to.

She understood that the manner of her own salvation had not been befitting of the king she'd aspired to become.

"...There's no way that's true."

Looking back at the conviction that had risen up within himself, Subaru ground his back teeth.

He had to call it absurd, truly absurd. To say the manner of her salvation was somehow bad—that would mean it would have been better for Crusch to keep suffering under the malice of "Lust" even now.

There was no way that could be true. Why should someone as admirable as Crusch have her dreams and goals cut short by the doings of ridiculous people like the Witch Cult?

It had been an unreasonable suffering. An absurdity she had no reason to have to endure.

So what if she'd been saved from it by someone's kindness? Thank you for saving me. I won't forget this gratitude, and I'll keep doing my best from here on.—Was that not enough?

"—You're not wrong. Not at all."

There was a feeling welling up inside his chest, together with an anger he couldn't do anything about.

As if pushed by it, Subaru found himself on his feet again before he knew it, and had taken hold of Crusch's hand across the low table, gripping it tight.

On Crusch's pale white fingers, where the handkerchief had fallen away, that ugly black mottled pattern was nowhere to be seen. This was her true hand—one with no reason to be defiled by anyone.

"Subaru... sama."

"You're not wrong. There's no way you're wrong. Let's do the Royal Selection right. Let's talk it through properly, think it through properly, and compete properly, all of us together. I'll do whatever it takes."

It was a declaration made ready to be scolded, cursed at, for making yet another rash promise.

This was a rival camp. Surely everyone would think it would be a great help to Emilia if Crusch, a formidable rival, simply dropped out of the Royal Selection as things stood.

"But no. That's not it, is it. The Royal Selection that all of us decided—that we wanted, that we'd try, to make our own person king—it shouldn't be decided that way."

"..."

"So, Crusch-san, you're not wrong at all."

He wasn't confident, even to himself, that it had all properly connected into a "so" that followed logically.

Still, though slightly overwhelmed by the force of Subaru's overflowing feelings, Crusch let out a breath, as if something taut within her had loosened somewhere.

"Honestly, Subaru went and burst out with it all, so most of what I was going to say got said before I could. That's not fair."

Perhaps sensing that shift in Crusch's mood, Emilia, still seated on the sofa, pursed her lips and scolded Subaru's behavior like that.

At once, realizing he alone had gotten ahead of himself and worked up, Subaru felt heat rise all the way to his ears.

Then, thinking no, no, this won't do, Subaru hastily tried to return to his original seat—

"—. Subaru-sama, that hand..."

"Huh? Hand?"

Pointed out, Subaru looked down at his own hand, which had just been gripping Crusch's.

He'd thought, now that it was mentioned, that he was being called out for some tremendous breach of manners, but that wasn't it. What Crusch's amber eyes had noticed wasn't Subaru's rudeness, but—

"Your hand, Subaru-sama. It had become the same as my body was... don't tell me your hand too received the power of the 'Divine Dragon Church'?"

"Ah, no, this was more... violent, I guess, the result of a big bang, sort of thing, but even I never really figured out the exact method, so it's kind of a quietly scary story."

In truth, Subaru's hand had regained its original, flesh-colored human skin by now, but the process by which that had happened was fairly violent, not something he could recommend anyone else try.

To put it plainly: it was burned off, blown apart, and grown back. —As things stood, the same could be said of his leg, but he had no intention of testing whether burning it off and blowing it apart would make it grow back too.

"—...i"

"..."

While opening and closing his hand, reflecting on that extreme treatment method, Subaru suddenly thought he heard a faint whisper, and instinctively held his breath.

Blinking, he looked at Crusch in front of him, with a feeling of surely-not, there's-no-way.

"Crusch-san, first, please rest your body slowly. After that, I intend to do whatever I can too, just like Subaru said before me."

"...Thank you. You might come to regret that, you know?"

"The me now, compared to the me back then, thinks she can tell the difference between the kind of regret that's all right to have and the kind that isn't."

But the sense of unease and doubt that had crept into Subaru was pushed aside by the exchange between Emilia and Crusch, passing right over his head, in a conversation befitting fellow Royal Selection candidates.

In truth, Subaru's and Emilia's proposal was nothing other than throwing the enemy a lifeline, but avoiding that and winning against Crusch would never let them rejoice from the bottom of their hearts.

So this proposal, this declaration of resolve, was exactly what the Emilia Camp wished for.

"—Phew."

Having exchanged all that, Crusch let out a breath tinged with fatigue.

With her strength still not fully returned after her illness, there had also been that moment of emotional outburst. It could be said this had already become quite a long stretch for Crusch's stamina.

"Let's take our leave soon, Emilia-san."

"Right. Thank you for meeting with us even when you're so, so tired and it must be hard."

"No, if anything, I should apologize for not being able to entertain you properly. Wilhelm, would you see everyone out?"

Prompted by Rem, before Emilia, who was drawing the visit to a close, Crusch smiled and said as much to Wilhelm at her side. Wilhelm bowed and accepted the task, and as Subaru and the others prepared to take their leave, Crusch too rose to see them off.

And then—

"Thank you, truly, for today. I was glad to see you all."

"..."

Apologizing for the discourtesy of seeing them off from where she stood, Crusch offered Subaru and the others that parting word.

For a moment, Subaru hesitated over whether to say something more—

"—Subaru-dono, allow me to see you out."

As though sensing that impulse, Wilhelm cut him off before he could.

—

"I must apologize, for a moment ago."

On the way from the parlor, being escorted to the mansion's entrance, Wilhelm, walking ahead, opened with those words. At them, Subaru, holding Beatrice's hand, widened his eyes with a thought of "I knew it," and said,

"I noticed you stopped me there at the end. ...But I don't even know myself what I was about to say."

"Even so, I overstepped by interrupting a guest's words. It was done purely out of concern for Lady Crusch, but I apologize for the discourtesy."

"...You've noticed too, haven't you, Wilhelm-san. The way Crusch-san was, back there."

"Of course. She's far from her usual self, and will need further rest yet a while. But even once her body and heart return to their proper state..."

Lowering his eyes, Wilhelm didn't voice what came after. But Subaru could more or less guess what he'd left unsaid.

No one there had dared to say it clearly. But precisely because Crusch herself felt it most keenly of all, such an intensely painful feeling had come spilling out.

And besides—

"Ferris must feel frustrated too, I bet. Right when he should want to support Crusch-san at her side more than anyone, and his body won't cooperate."

Glancing back toward the parlor they'd just left, Emilia let that worried remark slip as well. At her words, Wilhelm's throat made a faint sound as he walked ahead.

Then, without stopping, he said, in a solemn tone, "Lady Emilia—"

"A moment ago, I told you a lie."

"Eh? A lie?"

"Yes. —About Ferris."

At the word and the name he'd conveyed, Subaru and the others all exchanged glances at once.

A lie concerning Ferris, Wilhelm had said. By his earlier explanation, Ferris had fallen ill and was in the midst of resting, but—

"Wait, that's weird. Isn't Ferris the type who doesn't get sick in the first place?"

"Surely not to that extent... I mean, he's not Reinhard-san, after all."

"Sure, Reinhard's off the charts too, but in that sense Ferris is one of the off-the-charts ones as well, kind of."

Setting aside Reinhard, that globally astonishing human who could scoop up a Dragon Carriage and leap clean across the land, Ferris was a healer—and moreover, the holder of the title "Blue," said to be among the strongest in the kingdom.

Once before, Subaru had witnessed Ferris get caught up in a Witch Cultist's suicide attack, the Dragon Carriage smashed to bits, and come strolling back from the scene without so much as a scratch.

The so-called super-healer, familiar from manga and anime—that was Ferris.

Of course, given that he'd run out of Mana and poured everything he had into Crusch, it couldn't simply be said that the same held true for him now.

"As Subaru-dono has noticed, Ferris is not laid up in bed. Right now, he isn't in this mansion at all."

"Not in the mansion? That means..."

"—Please, go to the Royal Cemetery."

Rather than answer the question that had unexpectedly arisen, Wilhelm, sparing of words, only gave that place name and led Subaru and the others to the mansion's entrance.

The Royal Cemetery—just hearing the name, it seemed like a place whose purpose was self-evident.

But Wilhelm's true intent in telling Subaru and the others about that place—

"However much shame I heap upon myself, I am, at present, one who serves Lady Crusch. I cannot act against my mistress's wishes. —So please, I ask that you lend an ear to what he has to say."

Bowing deeply at that, Wilhelm sent Subaru and the others on their way.

The color of anguish etched into that deeply lined face felt like a prayer—the prayer of a man who, even as the "Sword Devil," could not resolve this problem by the sword, yet still wished, even at the cost of bitter suffering, to see it resolved.

—The Royal Cemetery was located at the very innermost part of the noble quarter of the royal capital of Lugunica.

Just as its sound, and the characters one could sense from that sound, suggested, the place was a cemetery—that is, a resting place for those whose souls were prayed to find peace, lined with numerous gravestones raised to mourn the dead.

"There was the Subjugation of the White Whale, right? Apparently the graves of the people who took part in that battle and lost their lives are here too."

"I see. Man, not having come here before makes me a real heartless jerk. I didn't bring flowers today, so next time I've gotta come properly prepared."

"That's right. We need to say a big thank-you, and tell them they did well."

Passing through the black iron main gate, not a speck of rust on it, what spread out before them was a stone-paved approach laid in geometric patterns, flanked on both sides by rows of gravestones—and not a single one of the stones lined up there could be called a mere memorial slab.

Some were crowned with a knight's statue displaying martial glories won in life; others were built like small shrines, adorned with intricate carvings telling of a family's history. Banners bearing carved family crests fluttered in the wind, and the colorful bouquets offered by mourners were the only splash of life in that world of gray stone.

"Guess nobles are vain even about their gravestones. ...Minus the flashiness, this is kind of close to the impression I got of graveyards from foreign movies and TV shows, I think."

Well-tended lawns and stone-paved paths spread out, centered around a gentle hill. It was the kind of space through which a cool, lonesome wind blew, with none of the Japanese-style gravestones, wooden grave tablets, or shrine-and-temple buildings that were familiar to Subaru anywhere around.

"Come to think of it, I never really thought about it before... but over here, do they cremate the dead, or bury them?"

"Cremation, basically, I suppose. When a living creature loses its life, its Soul—its Od—slips out of the body. A body that's lost its Od can only crumble away, formlessly, and it's not something one can bear to look at, I suppose. So before that happens, one shows the body too, just like the Od, that its role has come to an end, I suppose."

"I see, so it's not about disease prevention or anything—it's more about how existence itself is structured."

Thanks to Beatrice answering the question right away, the mechanism made sense to him now.

No wonder, then, that during the "Great Disaster" that occurred in Vollachia, the many zombies that were revived hadn't come back with rotted bodies or missing limbs, incomplete like that. In their case, the bodies had been shaped out of clods of earth, based on the form of the souls used to revive them.

That was why those zombies had come back to the present world looking just as they had in life.

"So that means the place where souls end up... the Cradle of Od Lagna, or whatever it's called, must be a pretty precious sort of place. Like the River Sanzu, or the far edge of time itself."

"Do not go muttering suddenly terrifying things like that, I suppose. The Cradle of Od Lagna isn't something to speak of even in jest, I suspect. Word has it that if you were careless enough to lock eyes with it, it would rob you of your sanity, I suppose."

"No, I wasn't joking, I meant it seriously... but then again, that was just something Rui said on her own, so who knows how reliable it actually is?"

Chided by a frowning Beatrice, Subaru found himself recalling that pure white space, where he'd spent a stretch of time that could be called intense, for better or worse.

Rui Arneb, playing gatekeeper of that place, had called it the "Corridor of Memories," but in truth, while it did seem to lean that way, what exactly that place had been remained unclear. And now, with Rui's own consciousness having vanished somewhere and her having been reborn as Spica, there was no way left to confirm it.

"If I had to say something, maybe I could go find Reid's book again and brace myself for something awful and peek inside, but..."

"—Um, I know you're in the middle of talking, but is it all right if Rem asks something too?"

"Eh?"

"You came here to look for someone, didn't you? Ferris-san, was it?"

Tugged sharply at the sleeve, Subaru turned around, and Rem asked him just that.

Rem, her eyes colored with deep confusion, naturally didn't know the first thing about what kind of person Ferris was. Being brought along to a cemetery like this, she must indeed have felt quite at loose ends.

"Oh, my bad, my bad! Um, so the person we're looking for now has cat ears and a real bubbly kind of vibe. Got a tail too, and is pretty cute, so I think you'll know when you see."

"...Rem would like a bit more useful information than that, if possible."

"No, I feel like I gave a decent number of hints... ah, and when I say cute, I don't mean cute like Bea-ko, more like cute in the Petra or Meili sense. Height's about the same as mine."

"I see, so there is a difference in the type of cuteness there."

Holding the lifted-up Beatrice out in front of her face and studying it carefully, Rem nodded as if satisfied with Subaru's explanation. Beatrice herself, meanwhile, only looked more puzzled by that satisfaction, but her being unaware of the finer details of her own cuteness could be called one of her charm points.

In truth, cuteness came in endless varieties—beauty leaning cute, cuteness leaning beauty, and so on. That said, now that they'd managed to share Ferris's features—

"I wonder what that attitude of Wilhelm-san's was all about. I'm worried."

"—And him knowing where Ferris was, and still wanting us to be the ones to go talk to him, yeah."

"Mm, right. Being consulted, being relied on, makes me happy, but I always felt like neither Crusch-san nor Wilhelm-san showed that side of themselves much."

There was something in Emilia's sense of it that Subaru could nod along to as well.

What was clear was something like this: some kind of problem had arisen within the Crusch Camp, and Wilhelm couldn't move to resolve it on his own.

Of course, Subaru could think of what might have triggered such a problem. But even so, the bond within the Crusch Camp was strong. If anything, compared to any other Royal Selection candidate, these were the two bound by the longest, firmest bond of all.

The depth of that bond of feeling hadn't vanished even when Crusch's "memory" had been lost—at least, that was what Subaru had felt, and had believed.

However—

"..."

Staring down intently at his own right hand, Subaru glared at its lifeline. What crossed his mind was the moment when he'd rudely grasped Crusch's hand, and she'd looked back at his in turn.

Seeing Subaru's hand, learning that it had been freed of that black pattern, and hearing that it hadn't come from borrowing the power of the Divine Dragon Church's sacred rite—

'—Unfair.'

That was what she had said, in a voice so faint it seemed on the verge of vanishing.

"That wasn't me mishearing... right..."

He wished with all his heart that it had been, but Subaru didn't possess ears—or a self-image—convenient enough for that. That had unmistakably been a dark, true feeling that had slipped out from Crusch's lips.

Honestly, it had been a shock. But to wish that Crusch shouldn't even be allowed to give voice to it—that would be sheer arrogance. More than anything, Subaru understood, painfully well, exactly why Crusch would let something like that slip out.

"...There's no way I can bring that up with Crusch-san."

Crusch was the one at the very center of it all, and now that her body had only just recovered, she'd taken on the possibility of bearing countless wounds to her heart instead. Something he couldn't directly press her about—did that mean he could ask Ferris, her knight, instead? That wasn't a certain thing either.

Still, he knew that if he slunk back to his companions as things stood, all he'd do was go on endlessly carrying an anguish with no answer in sight.

"Hmm, no sign of Ferris."

Lost in thought like that, Subaru and the others found they'd already made a full circuit of the cemetery.

It was by no means a small plot of land, but being a cemetery placed within the noble quarter, most of the dead sleeping here were seen as people who had carried the kingdom's weighty responsibilities, and their treatment even after death was correspondingly reverent. The farther in one went, the larger and more meticulously kept each individual gravestone became, and correspondingly, the fewer mourners' faces there were to distinguish—so it seemed unlikely they could have overlooked Ferris.

If only they knew even just whose grave he'd come to visit, that would be a different matter.

"Rem has looked all around, but there's no sign of a woman matching the description Rem was given."

"Strictly speaking, he's not a girl—well, but that doesn't matter for appearances, so never mind that part."

"—? As for the other conspicuous spot, would it be that building?"

Puzzled by Subaru skipping over a detailed explanation, Rem gestured with her hand toward what she meant: a white building seated on the central hill, backed by the neatly arranged gravestones across the vast grounds—what was known as a mausoleum.

Wrapped in a solemn, sacred atmosphere, commanding the gravestones that adorned its perimeter as though they were retainers sworn in loyalty to a lord, its very bearing proved that this was the resting place of the chosen.

If this was the Royal Cemetery, and that was the resting place of the dead given special treatment within it—

"—Seems like a mausoleum where members of the royal family are laid to rest."

"Then, the deceased royals are all inside there..."

The royal family—the trigger for the start of the Royal Selection, bearers of the great role of carrying the long-continuing Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's rightful bloodline down to the present day, and who had met such an anticlimactic end.

Standing before the mausoleum where such people had been laid to rest, Subaru finally, belatedly understood.—The solemn presence drifting through this place: in a sense, this was a microcosm of the royal capital itself. If the royal castle was a symbol of authority for the living, then the mausoleum was the same, for the dead—

"...I guess visiting the kings' graves casually isn't really a thing you can just do, huh."

"By ordinary reasoning, a royal mausoleum would surely be under tight guard, I suppose. There is no shortage of people who would try to do mischief with the ashes of the powerful, I suspect. Lamentable, I'd say."

"Still, if we could just ask who's inside, at least... ah, perfect timing."

Though the mausoleum was the only place they hadn't checked, it wasn't somewhere one could simply walk into either.

That made the odds of Ferris being there all the lower, but having been entrusted with this by Wilhelm, Subaru didn't want to go back with a half-finished job.

Just as he was thinking that, Emilia pointed sharply at the mausoleum's entrance. And sure enough, just as she'd said, a figure was stepping out from inside the mausoleum at that very moment.

It was a slender figure, dressed in something like a dark navy tailcoat. From the beauty of that figure's bearing, back turned to them, one could strongly sense reverence and care toward the mausoleum.

Since the figure had been coming and going from the royal mausoleum, Subaru guessed it might be the cemetery or mausoleum's caretaker, or something of the sort, and followed after Emilia's back as she took off at a hurried pace.

"Um, excuse me, do you have a moment? It's about the building you just came out of—I'd like to ask whether the person we're looking for was inside."

Emilia, hurrying up, called out to the figure without any hesitation. Such boldness might well have provoked wariness in the other party, but for what it was worth, guards were stationed at the cemetery's entrance, and identities were confirmed when passing through the main gate, so the assumption was that there were no suspicious persons within the grounds.

Subaru sincerely hoped that intent would carry across to the other party as well—

"—Lady Emilia?"

Saying so in a voice tinged with surprise, the figure in the tailcoat turned around and looked at Emilia. Seeing them face-on, Emilia let out an "eh" of surprise, her feet coming to a stop.

And Subaru and the others too, past the now-stopped Emilia, laid eyes on that figure.

There, standing before them, was—

"...Aw, geez, you caught me somewhere weird, huh. Was it Vil-jii?"

So he said, with a wry, bitter little smile that could have been either exasperation or self-mockery—Ferris, his slender frame wrapped, unmistakably, in a man's tailcoat, shrugging his shoulders at Subaru and the others.

"..."

At the sight of Ferris, standing with his back to the mausoleum's entrance, Subaru found himself at a loss for words.

There had certainly been times before when Ferris had dressed like this—putting on the Royal Guard's uniform, dressed as a man. But that had come with the pretext of being the knight order's official uniform, and even then, Subaru thought, there had been touches of Ferris's own flair for how to present himself worked in.

But in the tailcoat-clad figure before him now, there was no such flair, no such artifice at all.

What that meant was—

"...Ferris, what... happened?"

"Don't make a face and voice like it's the end of the world. It's nothing much to worry about, for you guys. Just—"

Still wearing that wry smile, brows drooping, Ferris started to tilt his head to one side—then stopped himself. It looked almost as if that gesture itself meant a farewell to such mannerisms.

Without betraying that impression, Ferris went on.

It was—

"—Just that I got dismissed as Lady Crusch's knight. So, Feri-chan is finished now, and I've gone back to being Felix Argyle."
