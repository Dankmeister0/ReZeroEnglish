"—So, she didn't actually go and conjure up a whole separate dimension with Yin magic, then?"

"If she could do that, Mother could create worlds however she pleased. Even Mother, no matter how much Mana she had, wouldn't have enough to manage that, I suppose."

"It wouldn't be strange if she could... that's how amazing Mother was, I suppose."

Walking through the ruined castle, Beatrice spoke proudly of her outrageous mother's power.
Thinking it might be an old wound, they hadn't dug much into her past until now — but as if to make up for that, Beatrice's bragging about her parent Dona showed no sign of running dry.
No doubt it helped that this was her first homecoming in four hundred years, actually walking the halls of Kugelschreiber Castle — even if what surrounded them was the castle's former glory, mercilessly ruined.

"But hearing you're thought of like that, Bea-ko, I feel kind of jealous."

"Honestly, you and Mother aren't even competing in the same category, I suppose. Mother is the unshakable, permanent number one in the family rankings, and Subaru is the unshakable, permanent number one in the Betty rankings."

"Huh, what's that, I'm really happy. Does that mean I beat Puck?"

"You and Nii-cha are tied for first, I suppose."

"Curse that fluffball... he keeps standing in my way, whether it's being number one to Emilia-tan or number one to Bea-ko...!"

The image of the cat spirit stroking his whiskers with a smug "nyahaha" rose up in Subaru's mind, and he gnashed his teeth at the height of the rival he had to overcome. He had to be careful, after all — he himself was hopelessly weak to that fluffy coat.
And so, while they went back and forth like that—

"—It's here, I suppose."

At Beatrice, who had stopped dead in her tracks, Subaru and the others tilted their heads. "Here?"
After all, the spot she'd turned to face was nothing but an unremarkable wall in the passage — one wall, in a corridor they'd been carefully picking their way through, mindful of the crumbled footing.
But Beatrice wagged a raised finger side to side with a "tut-tut-tut, I suppose," and said—

"The owner of this castle is a Witch, I suppose. It'd be downright laughable for the records that Witch left behind to just be sitting out in some ordinary room, I suppose. So—"

So saying, Beatrice gently pressed the finger she'd been wagging against the wall.
Nothing happened. Still holding her finger to the wall, Beatrice reached out her other hand and called softly, "Subaru, Subaru." He hurried to take her hand.
Mana flowed from Subaru into Beatrice, and light welled up from the tip of the finger she'd pressed to the wall. It formed a door of light in what had been a blank wall a moment before—

"That's the same thing I saw once already, at the castle entrance."

"There's no reason to bother making something different when I've already got the best version, I suppose. Now, take a good look, I suppose. —This is the Witch Echidna's private laboratory!"

Beyond the now-passable wall, what greeted Subaru and the others as they stepped through, fully braced for it, was an air even heavier than what they'd felt on first entering the castle, the scent of wood that had steeped in a long silence, and a room whose contents were almost too perfectly "Witch-like."

"This is... quite something, indeed."

"Kuhahaha, the duchess sure picks her words gracefully and modestly! This place is creepy! Tasteless! Gloomy as hell! You'd be doing her a favor just saying it straight out! Oh, but wait, she's already dead, isn't she—?"

"B-Black Dog! How can you say something like that! Librarian-san, I'm so sorry!"

At Black Dog's outburst as he spun around, surveying the room, White Sheep hastily bowed her head. But Beatrice, magnanimous, held up an open palm and said, "It's fine, I suppose."

"Being feared and held in dread is something a Witch needs, too... if anything, this reaction is exactly what Mother would've wanted, I suppose. There's no need to apologize, I suppose."

"Man, your feelings toward your own parent are seriously twisted up..."

"Huh, coming from you, Long-Tongue-kun..."

Ferris voiced the plain, obvious question to Rachins, whose lips twisted.
In truth, Rachins had deliberately left a wealthy household to run around with a gang of thugs. Even without saying "parents" outright, the odds were strong that something had gone wrong in his family relationships.
Either way—

"Black Dog's manner of speaking is a bit much to stomach, but there's no denying it's a fresh sight."

Such was the scene unfolding — even Russell affirming Black Dog's characterization.
The laboratory was a space large enough to be called a great hall, with a high ceiling held up by numerous black pillars. Rows of pedestals resembling workbenches lined the room, but what caught the eye were the countless crystals atop them, of every size and every color—

"...This really does feel like a Witch's laboratory."

Peering closely into the nearest crystal, Subaru murmured that quietly.
Right in front of him, sealed inside a palm-sized crystal, was a butterfly with its wings still spread wide. Next to it, a flowering plant preserved not just with its petals but with the roots that stretched into the soil. Next to that, a small lizard-like creature curled into a ball.
Every one of them was a specimen preserved without the slightest loss of color or shape, looking as though it might spring back to life at any moment.

"It's not just small things — there are Witchbeasts here too. Take a look. That one there, for instance, some kind of large... what was it called."

"Now that's a surprise. That's a Guiltyrau. To think a Witchbeast could be preserved in a form like this."

Among the group making their way past the crystal specimens, Crusch and Russell, having gone on ahead, marveled aloud. Before them stood a crystal as large as a Dragon Carriage, with a massive Witchbeast sealed inside.
Beyond that, the specimens ranged widely — fish, Birds, Witchbeasts none of them had ever seen before.

"For what purpose would you need so many living things... hyaa!"

"Oi oi, you numbskull! Don't go touching things on your own! Talk about careless and clumsy!"

"S-sorry! But, um, look at this!"

Having let out a small shriek and been scolded by Black Dog, White Sheep — who'd apparently poked the crystal — pointed at it. Sealed inside was a crimson-furred vermilion deer, but it wasn't simply trapped there. Light welled up within the crystal, and the deer's body turned translucent, revealing its organs and bones, and even countless lines of light running through it.

"Wait, don't tell me — that's not just the inside of its body, that's its Gate you're seeing?"

Witnessing it firsthand, Ferris murmured in a voice that made no effort to hide his shudder of dread. His yellow eyes went wide as he touched the crystal beside him to confirm the same phenomenon.

"Well, Ferris? What can you tell?"

"...The little ones inside aren't alive. But, this is amazing. Once something dies, its Od gradually disperses — but this, this has the exact shape its Od held while it was alive, copied and preserved as-is."

"Copying the Od... you mean the shape of its Soul has been recorded?"

"That's right. ...At the very least, I think it's something close to that."

Whether the shock had simply been too great, Ferris didn't even seem to notice Crusch speaking to him, trembling at this glimpse into the Witch's research.
Subaru, too, turned to look at Beatrice at that exchange.

"Seems like calling this a Witch's laboratory isn't false advertising after all. You never heard anything about what she was doing here, Bea-ko?"

"...Mother never wanted to show her research to others, I suppose. Even the knowledge she gave out, she chose carefully, I suppose. Probably, there was some unpleasant memory behind it, I suppose."

"An unpleasant memory... well, I guess if you're smart or powerful, there's bound to be a lot going on there."

Come to think of it, there was a real gulf in outlook between the young Dona — who used to boast about others coming to her for advice — and the parent Dona that Beatrice described. It made him sense the passage of time: that young Dona had gone through something unpleasant, her values shifted, and the result was the parent Dona standing before them now. But—

"These experimental tools are certainly interesting, but ideally we'd find some kind of material we can actually read and decipher here. Librarian, do you have any idea where such a thing might be?"

"There's a small room in the back I was told never to go into, I suppose."

"A small room, huh — this one?"

At Russell's urging that staring at experimental equipment all day wasn't getting them anywhere, Beatrice pointed to a corner of the laboratory. Subaru put his hand to the door there and flung it open. A thick, musty scent of old books came rushing out, and he found himself waving a hand in front of his face.
And then—

"Oi oi, this is... where the heck do we even start with this? This'll be brutal."

From behind the murmuring Subaru, the others peering in just as he had let out groans of their own.
The room Beatrice had called a "small room" might indeed be modest compared to the great-hall-sized laboratory, but it was still about the size of the dining hall at Roswaal Manor.
And crammed into a room of that size, in complete disarray, were books, bundles of paper, and scrolls, packed in wherever they'd fit.

"I did warn you, I suppose. —Mother wasn't exactly great at keeping things tidy, I suppose."

"I wish there'd been some limit to that. Think of future generations."

He picked up two books that had fallen right by his feet near the door, only to find both were untitled and, from what he could tell, had no relation or continuity between them whatsoever — a discovery that left him feeling utterly hopeless.

"First the Forbidden Library, then the Pleiades Watchtower..."

Natsuki Subaru had never once had a good memory involving someone else's library, and this looked set to join that same category — a truly high-maintenance archive if ever there was one.

—In the end, sorting through the Witch's archive took several hours.

Tackled by all eight of them, the work went on in a string of small but exacting tasks — righting fallen bookshelves, gathering scattered books, collecting scattered sheets of paper back into bundles, carefully retrieving torn scrolls.
And after doing all that, they'd only just reached the starting line for actually sorting anything — proof enough that cleaning up someone else's library was not something to take on lightly. The one small mercy was that, at the very least, there was no way for them to miss whatever it was they needed.

"—This one, and this. And this document too, into the box. Let's take them back and go over them."

So saying, Russell studied the recovered materials — sorted as best they could — and picked out the valuable ones using his Divine Protection of Appraisal, deciding which documents to take with them.

"That last book was just an endless flower observation journal or something... is that actually worth anything?"

For two people who kept getting dragged into each other's important business, the longer time went on, the more it looked like friction was building between them instead of any warming up.
But—

"Ahh, ugh! It's over, it's done! What an exhausting, grueling day! I've decided! From now on, anyone who looks like they'll read a ton of books gets killed before they can buy a ton of books!"

"The one-night time limit's still ticking, and just because this is a separate dimension doesn't mean time moves slower in here than outside, right? No perk like that going on?"

"Not as far as Betty knows, I suppose. If there were a perk like that, this castle would've been left in a somewhat better state... though, because of Melancholy, even that's doubtful, I suppose."

Some of it was surely the effect of long neglect, but most of the castle's ruin came from the power of the Warlock of Melancholy, who was said to have rampaged through here. In all likelihood, this had been a clash between a Witch and a Warlock on a scale that "just a family spat" could never begin to capture.

As she answered Russell, Beatrice glanced briefly his way, and Subaru nodded back.
If the theory held that Echidna hadn't wanted Beatrice to see any books relating to Souls, then the archive she'd been forbidden from entering must surely be exactly where those forbidden books were hidden.
If that much was certain, then all that was left was to bring their haul back—

At Ferris's question, asked with his head tilted, Subaru frowned.

But before Subaru could say anything, Ferris bent at the waist to meet Beatrice's eyes and said—

To Subaru and Ferris peering into her face, Beatrice waved a flustered hand. It was an adorable little resistance, but—

Narrowing the eye not covered by her eyepatch, Crusch — whose gift let her see through lies — exposed her without hesitation.

"It's nothing much, I suppose. It's just — I remembered. When I fled the castle chased by Melancholy, there wasn't any time to pack slowly, I suppose. Something I couldn't take with me back then might still be left in this castle, in Betty's room... I suppose."

Casting her eyes down, Beatrice finished with that touching admission.
Moved by the sight of her, Subaru also tasted a bit of frustration — at his own inadequacy, having let Ferris be the one to notice Beatrice's lingering regret first.
Still, it would be foolish to sit around sulking over it.

"Russell, mind if we go grab something Bea-ko left behind four hundred years ago? I'm well aware it's got nothing to do with the mission, but..."

To Subaru, pushed back by sound reasoning, it was once again Ferris who lent him a hand. Russell turned a slightly sharp gaze on him for voicing an opinion so favorable to Beatrice, and said—

"...Surely you don't mean this as payback, Blue Snake?"

"As if. But could you not forget something? Grey Star-kun and Librarian-chan aren't your subordinates. And yet, even with people they'd rather be seeing, the two of them have gone along with you for two whole months. Wouldn't it be nice to show a little... sincerity?"

"You..."

Ferris's tongue was as sharp as ever, but when its point was aimed at someone other than himself, it apparently felt this reassuring — Subaru felt both moved and bewildered all at once.
He couldn't understand why Ferris was going this far to look out for them. Of course, no matter how foul his mouth, Subaru trusted the good heart underneath in someone who'd mastered the healing arts to such a degree.

"Don't look at me like that. It's not a big deal — if there's a lost thing you can still get back, isn't it better to go get it?"

"Well... yeah. Sorry. You really are a good guy after all."

"...I hate that about you, Subaru-kun."

With Ferris turning away from him with a huff, Subaru scratched his cheek and smiled wryly. Meanwhile, faced with Ferris's request, Russell spent a moment touching his chin with a troubled expression, but—

"Um, Silver Fox, I'd like to ask you too, please. We don't know when we might be able to come back here again, do we? That would be too sad for Librarian-san."

"White Sheep, you as well?"

"The circumstances for me and Black Dog are different from Grey Star and the others. Even you can't force them to do something against their will. You already understand that much, don't you?"

Tensing her cheeks with resolve, even White Sheep offered Subaru and the others covering fire.
Compassionate as she was, it must have taken considerable courage to speak up against her superior, Russell. It wasn't as though her stance moved him to some deep admiration, but—

"—Don't linger. Retrieve what you're after and rejoin us promptly."

After a moment with his eyes closed, they'd succeeded in wringing a concession out of Russell.

"I'll prioritize packing up the materials. Gather at the entrance."

"Just so we're clear, I'm not coming either. Like hell I'm babysitting some kid's little trip down memory lane."

And so, leaving behind Russell, who prioritized the work, and Rachins, who was no fun to be around, the rest of the group set off through Kugelschreiber Castle toward Beatrice's private chambers.
Unexpectedly many of them had offered to come along with Subaru's group, leaving Rachins — stuck alone with Russell — to see them off with a look on his face that said this hadn't been part of the plan.

"But seriously, you're coming along too?"

"Kuhahaha, it's simply that this beats helping carry boxes, that's alllll there is to it. Besides, if our clumsy, dim-witted White Sheep trips and falls down some hole by accident, that'd be a real problem, wouldn't it?"

"Y-you don't need to worry about that! I'm not that clumsy— kyah!"

"See, look at that, laugh, scold her! The words weren't even out of my mouth and here she is!"

Laughing loudly at that, Black Dog grabbed White Sheep by the scruff of her collar as she nearly fell and hauled her back up.
White Sheep played her expected role so faithfully it drew a wry laugh, but for all his griping, Black Dog was actually quite overprotective of her.
Normally it was White Sheep who came across as the more protective one, but right now the roles seemed reversed.

"You two were together even before joining the Six Tongues, right... you were both Beloved Children?"

"...I was, yes. Black Dog probably was too."

"Probably? That part's uncertain?"

At White Sheep's uncertain answer, Subaru frowned, puzzled. He'd simply assumed White Sheep and Black Dog had ended up in the Six Tongues under the same circumstances.

"Oh-ho, lies or truth, truth or lies! Behind that saintly little face, the truth is she's a rotten, lying woman who loooves deceiving you all!"

"A wind of falsehood is blowing. Your words ring false, sir — White Sheep's words ring true."

"Gaaah! Boring, dull, buzzkill!"

Black Dog's little jab, meant to stir the pot, was cut down flat by Crusch's Divine Protection. Even so, it seemed just as much like a cover to draw attention away from what White Sheep had said.
In fact, Crusch herself had just vouched that White Sheep's words were true.

"Honestly, I'm not entirely sure of my relationship with Black Dog myself. That he's someone close to me... that much should be certain, but I can't pin down exactly how."

"For the record, I haven't the foggiest clue either! For me it's more like 'I noticed I was already there,' and for her it's more like 'she just so happened to be there too.'"

"What is that, doesn't that make zero sense?"

"Yes, it makes zero sense. —And I think that, right there, is my reason."

Clenching her pale fists, White Sheep said this with something like resolve on her face.
Having brought the subject up himself, Subaru felt a small pang of hesitation over whether it had been right to hear it. Once again, he'd gone and stomped into someone's heart with muddy boots, rudely, without asking.

"No matter how much 'stomping into people's hearts uninvited' is a family motto of ours, even the stomper's got manners to keep — so when it happens without meaning to, it throws me off."

"What are you mumbling on about, I suppose. Come on, this way — don't go getting yourself lost, I suppose."

Beatrice tugged the arm of Subaru, lost in thought, and he followed after her as she led the group.
At first, Beatrice had felt somewhat sheepish about having everyone tag along just to search for something she'd left behind, but walking through the family home she knew so well, her stiffness gradually eased.
Now her eyes shone with anticipation for the reunion soon to come, with the keepsake she was about to reclaim.

"By the way, what was it — Echidna's, I mean, Mother's — present, anyway?"

"Mm, that's a treat you'll enjoy once you see the real thing, I suppose. Wanting to know everything in such a hurry isn't just a bad habit of yours, Subaru — it's a bad habit of all you Humans, I suppose."

"Well, of course, when you're being compared to someone with a four-hundred-year waiting track record like you, Bea-ko."

Subaru smiled wryly, faintly amused, at Beatrice puffing her chest out proudly and leading the way with small, quick strides. Watching her drill-shaped twintails sway as her steps bounced along, he could only find it endearing.
Whether everyone else felt the same way or not, no one tried to dampen Beatrice's mood as she brimmed with anticipation of finally holding a present she'd waited four hundred years to receive.

"And yet here it is, getting in our way bit by bit..."

Subaru's grumbling was a complaint aimed at the Warlock of Melancholy, whose destructive handiwork lingered thick no matter which way through the castle they went.
Every time they came across some spot where Melancholy's rampage had brought down a ceiling, snapped a pillar, or cracked open a floor, they had to detour around the impassable path and take the long way around toward Beatrice's room.
Even now, cutting across a half-collapsed room and taking a shortcut through a crushed corridor, they ran into a spot up ahead where a single thick pillar lay toppled sideways, blocking the passage.

"Oh, come on, another detour?"

"...No, wait, look, I suppose. It looks like we can slip past the side of the pillar, I suppose."

The pillar looked too thick for an adult to wrap both arms around, but just as Beatrice pointed out, there was a gap between it and the right-hand wall wide enough for one person at a time to squeeze through.

"Still, not being able to see the other side is a little unnerving. I'll go first here. Bea-ko, wait your turn for now."

"Betty's more compact and cute, so I'd get through without any trouble, I suppose."

"Hey, my body's not so bad in the flexibility department either, you know. I do the pigeon pose every night."

Faced with this display of yogic spirit, Beatrice yielded the role of first penguin to Subaru.
Subaru ducked his head, twisted his body into the gap by the pillar, and slipped straight through to the other side. Luckily, none of the snags he'd feared, nor a trapdoor waiting right past the gap, awaited him — the corridor seemed to continue on ahead and wasn't a dead end either.

"Okay, we're good. It's passable. Just be careful not to snag your hair or dress."

Brushing the knee of his tracksuit clean where it had touched the floor, Subaru called out to Beatrice on the other side of the pillar. He crouched right there, waiting for Beatrice to come slipping through the gap—

"—Bea-ko?"

Beatrice was small. She should have been able to slip through the gap far more easily than he had. And yet no matter how long he waited, she never came through to his side. Peering impatiently into the gap by the pillar, there was no sign of Beatrice there — no, that wasn't quite it.

"—! Hey, you've gotta be kidding me?"

Doubting his own eyes, Subaru ducked his head again and squeezed back through the gap in the opposite direction. Frustrated, scraping his head against the pillar, he came out into the corridor on the other side and looked up.
And then—

"—No one's here?"

Standing up in the corridor where he'd been walking with everyone just moments ago, Subaru looked around in a daze. Beatrice, who should have been there — Crusch, Ferris, White Sheep, Black Dog — none of them were there.
Not a single one of the people who'd come with him remained. Not a trace, not a shadow of them was left.

"No way, that's insane... Beatrice! Bea-ko! Answer me! Bea-koooo!!"

Raising his voice, Subaru shouted Beatrice's name at the top of his lungs. His voice echoed hollowly through the aged castle, but the answer he sought never came back.
A surge of panic drenched Subaru's back in cold sweat in an instant.

"Crusch-san! Ferris!! White Sheep-chan! Black Dog! Where are you?! Bea-ko! Somebody! Anybody! Answer me!!"

Unable to sit still, Subaru called out his companions' names one after another. But no one answered. Not just Beatrice — not Crusch or any of the other four either. It was an abnormality far too obvious to ignore.

"Just what is going on... don't tell me — Echidna?!"

From the moment they'd discovered this hidden archive — or rather, research room — in the castle of the Witch of Greed, he'd had an unpleasant premonition that some kind of trick might be waiting.
But given that Beatrice hadn't mentioned anything of the sort, and that up until now, whether with the Trials of the Tomb or any other test, there had always been some trigger before anything happened—
his guard had gradually worn thin—

"Am I an idiot? No — I am an idiot... letting my guard down against Echidna, of all people!"

He bit down hard on his lip, using the pain to discipline himself. There was no time to spend on drawn-out self-recrimination. He had to search for the vanished Beatrice and the others right away — or else regroup with Russell and the rest.
And then, he had to figure out whatever trick this castle held—

"—Ah?"

It was the very instant after he'd resolved to do just that.
Just as he took his first step to double back the way he'd come, it happened. Without a hint of warning, the footing beneath him gave way with no resistance at all, and his body tipped.
By the time he thought *this is bad*, it was already too late. —The next instant, his body was flung out into open air.

"—!"

The floor had given out and he'd been blown clear out of the corridor. The instant he realized it, Subaru yanked the Guiltywhip from behind his waist and, in a desperate frenzy, cracked it toward the corridor he'd just fallen out of. —By sheer luck, the tip caught in some crack or fissure that had opened in the passage.
"Guohh!" A violent jolt slammed through his right shoulder, the one holding the whip, and Subaru's body jerked to a halt, caught.

"Wh-wh-what..."

He had no idea at all what had just happened.
No sign, no omen, no warning, no trigger of any kind — the floor had simply dropped away. Flung outside the corridor like that, Subaru cast his eyes around and felt a chill run through him.

—There was nothing. It was an empty sky, as though creation itself had never taken place there.

"———"

Fall in there, and there's absolutely no coming back — that's what Beatrice had said at the very start. Now, half-fallen himself, Subaru understood exactly what she'd meant.
It was true — this was impossible. Fall in here, and there was no way back. No means of returning. None to be found.
So—

"Somehow, I've gotta get back up..."

It was a flimsy lifeline at best, just a whip caught in a crack in the floor.
So that it wouldn't slip loose, he wound the whip around his wrist, and little by little — hurrying all the while — carefully hauled himself up, hauled himself up, hauled himself up—

"Eh."

The snap of impact came without warning.
If it made a sound, it would've been a snap. But he heard no sound. He was in no state to hear anything.

"—Ah."

Mercilessly, the lifeline holding Subaru's body up lost its taut tension, and as if mocking a life struggling desperately to survive, it flung Subaru's body out into empty air.

"Ah, aaaaahhhh—!!"

Screaming, Subaru's body spun round and round, tumbling end over end and backward, until up and down lost all meaning, hurtling away all at once from the corridor — no, from Kugelschreiber Castle itself.
Farther and farther and farther away, he fell. Into a literal, pitch-black Abyss.

"I... saw..."

Battered all over by a raging wind, tossed about by tremendous force, a voice spilled out of him.
He'd seen it. Seen it with his own eyes. In the instant the whip came loose and he was flung out, he'd seen it. —The snap: the tip of the whip that had come free from the corridor, its end laid bare with a fresh, sharp-edged cut.

Someone had cut Subaru's whip. Someone had severed his lifeline.
Severed, he fell, and fell, and fell, falling, falling, falling, falling, falling, fall, fall, fall, fall, fall, fall, fa, fa, fa, fa, fa, fa, fa, fa, fa, fa, fa—

"———"

It doesn't come.

Gritting his teeth hard, Subaru braced his entire body for the impact of the ground that was sure to come.
He tried to search for some way out, only to realize at once there was nothing he could do. Beatrice wasn't here. There was nowhere for his whip to reach. He was already dozens, hundreds of meters away from the castle.
There was no way back, no means of survival. So all that was left was to brace for the "death" that was sure to come — and, if nothing else, to share this desperate plight with everyone. He steeled his resolve for it.

*Come. Come anytime. Come. Come now. Come this instant. Come. Hurry, now, right now, everyone's in danger. Come right now. Come. Come. I said come. Come on. Come!*

"———"

—It doesn't come.

"———"

He realized it. After steeling himself, he realized it.
He should have already understood this himself just a moment ago. This was a world where creation had never taken place. No matter how far he fell, there was no ground anywhere to crash into, none to smash Subaru's body to pieces.
Even if he kept falling like this, kept falling on and on and on without end, he would never arrive anywhere.

"———"

He tried desperately to deny the terrifying possibility that had risen in his mind.
But the greatest ally to that denial — a collision with the ground — never came. Still, wind and fall kept flinging Subaru's body about, and his speed only continued to climb.

Eventually, his vision blacked out.
The spinning of his body threw his circulation into chaos, sending blood surging too much to his head and to the tips of his limbs. A ferocious pain began to race through his entire body, and just like that, Subaru's consciousness—


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


"———"

It doesn't end. There is no end to this fall.

How much time it took him to accept that fact, Subaru didn't know.
Only that his ceaselessly falling body never stopped its spinning, and the resulting chaos in his blood flow made him lose consciousness again and again, then come back to it for no reason at all, forced each time to recognize this nightmare situation as reality.

His stomach cried out with hunger, the lower half of his body — soiled where he'd lost control of himself — had gone numb, and his eyes had long since stopped being of any use.

And even so, Natsuki Subaru was alive. Alive — he'd gone and stayed alive.

"—Aaaaaah"

Groaning low like that, Subaru cursed the slowness of his own judgment, his own foolishness.
His body, endlessly falling, endlessly tossed by the wind, had lost all freedom; his limbs no longer moved as he willed them. If his hands could still move, he might have been able to strangle himself with his own hands.
But he couldn't anymore. There was no strength left anywhere in him for that. In the same way, he didn't even have the strength left to bite through his own tongue — every means of leading himself to death had already been lost.

"—Aaaaaah"

Even now, even as Subaru went on falling pointlessly, Beatrice and the others were in danger.
Inside a castle he could no longer return to, Beatrice and the others might even now be exposed to some threat. —And yet he couldn't go back for them. He couldn't even die.

"—Aaaaaah"

No matter how much Subaru longed for an end, his lungs craved air, his heart kept pumping blood through a body whose circulation had gone haywire, and the bare minimum activities of a living creature carried on regardless.
Time in which he could do nothing, could not even die, stretched on and on, idle and meaningless, drawn out endlessly.

"—Aaaaaah"

His throat was parched.
Having screamed, having wept, having wet himself, having drained every drop of moisture from his body, he was bone-dry.

His body had gone completely cold.
His circulation had gone haywire; through the dizzying stretches of time he'd been battered by the wind without ever once moving in any way that generated heat.

His Soul was screaming.
Crying out for Beatrice, for everyone in the castle, for Emilia, for his companions torn away from him.

"—Aaaaaah"

He kept letting out a voice — not a scream, not a wail, but something like a groan.
From deep within his body, from the very depths beneath the depths beneath the depths, wringing it out from there, he kept letting the voice out.

Hurry — even a second sooner — to wring it all out completely, so that he could die.

There was nothing else he could do. —No, there was. Something beyond groaning. To decide. To swear a hard oath in his heart, to decide, and to see it through without fail.

*I will not let go of her hand. I will never, ever let go. I will not let go of Beatrice.*

"—Aaaaaah"

*No matter what happens, I will absolutely never let go.*

"—Aaaaaah"

Swearing that hard oath in his heart, Natsuki Subaru kept on crying it out.
Down to the very last drop, wringing himself dry to the final drop—


"—Aaaaaah"


This particular way of dying ranks pretty high up there among the ones I hate.
