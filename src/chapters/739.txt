Arc 9 Chapter 43: "True Name"

---

—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never play with children.

△▼△▼△▼△

"For example, if the Witch Factor that Clind-niisama has could be used by someone else... by me, then I could 'Teleport' as much as I wanted without paying the price?"

That was the first misuse Petra had thought of, when Clind told her about the existence of the "Melancholy" Witch Factor and its relationship to the Authority of "Compression."
Clind wasn't compatible with the Witch Factor, and as a result, he explained, using the Authority of "Compression" required a price—a cost to be paid. At the same time, he told her that the "Melancholy" Witch Factor was a one-off factor made for one specific person, and would never be compatible with Petra.

For that reason, the special-feeling tag team of the Warlock Natsuki Subaru, who held the Authority of "Return by Death," and the Witch Petra Leyte, who would cooperate with him, dissolved before it could even form—and while she was relieved, if she was honest, she'd also felt a bit disappointed.

With that, the matter of the "Melancholy" Witch Factor had been set outside the scope of discussion, settled with the plan that Clind would use "Compression" to bring their allies together and to plunge them into the decisive battle against the enemy—but then, with the discovery of Al's Authority of "Time Regression," and as they hammered out a plan to reliably stop Al's group, the spotlight fell on the Authority of "Melancholy" once again.

Clind had told Petra and the others that, as a result of his incompatibility with the Witch Factor, it was impossible to use the Authority without paying a price. But turn that around, and—

"—If someone could pay the price, then someone other than Clind-niisama could use 'Compression' too, right?"

At Petra's confirmation, Clind's expression didn't change.
That, if anything, only convinced Petra that her guess was right. If he'd genuinely wanted to hide it, Clind should have acted surprised and said "I never even considered that."

"To deal with Al and the others, it's absolutely essential that we split everyone up and scatter them. Other than Al, the toughest opponent is the Divine Dragon... but Clind-niisama's going to handle that somehow, right?"

"I can vouch for that point myself. Clind is a key person who could well become the trump card against the Divine Dragon Volcanica—or so I'd sa-a-ay. Did I use that right?"

"—...You did."

In the course of the war council, it was Clind who had boasted that he could suppress the Divine Dragon.
Honestly, between that and the matter of the "Melancholy" Witch Factor, she couldn't help feeling exasperated at just how many secrets he'd been keeping together with Roswaal—but there was nothing wrong with having someone so dependable. Still, being dependable didn't mean she could lean on Clind for absolutely everything.

"But if Clind-niisama is going to be fully tied up with the Divine Dragon, he won't be able to move people in and out with 'Compression' anymore, right?"

"I won't deny it. Affirmed. Of course, I will use everything in my power to see that everyone is delivered to the battlefield without fail, but..."

"No. If it's just that, I think it kills the whole strength of 'Compression.' The best part about 'Compression' is that, like a teleport, it lets you move fighting forces in and out, swap them around."

The words Clind had sworn, with a quiet expression but unmistakable heat behind them, were surely no lie.
He could reliably deliver their greatest fighting force into enemy territory. That alone would be an extraordinary feat. But if they could hope for more than that, this was also a battle where they should hope for as much as they possibly could.

"And so it comes down to talk of handing over the Authority, does it. Before we go and consider whether it's actually workable, I'd like to make certain of it first."

"—...As for that. Hesitation."

"Clind-niisama, please lay everything out on the table."

"——"

"Right now, I think this is the moment for us to talk about what cards everyone's holding, and how much we can use each one for. So, no secrets."

To Clind, who hesitated over his words, Petra made that demand clearly.
She understood his reasons for not wanting to talk, and his reasons for agonizing over whether to withhold information. She understood, but this discussion was a council meant to save the kingdom, the world—Natsuki Subaru and the others.
That Meili was glaring at Petra for declaring "no secrets" was just part of the charm.

"...If we're talking about whether it's possible or not, it is possible. Approved. The 'Melancholy' Witch Factor isn't compatible with me either—I'm merely in possession of it. Responsibility."

"If that's the case, then it's just a matter of changing owners? Hah! What a convenient thing to handle. If Clind ever misplaced it, that'd be a disaster."

"Misplacing it is one thing, but it's frightening to think of it being stolen, or taken from him by someone. ...In that sense too, it makes sense Clind was the one entrusted with it."

Apparently the "Melancholy" Witch Factor had been managed in a more precarious state than expected.
Petra agreed with the idea that it made sense Clind had been entrusted with managing it. Always brisk and efficient, handling everything without a misstep, Clind was probably the perfect custodian.

However—

"Even so, this time it might be better to hand that duty over to someone else."

"Someone else... but Witch Factors are a rea-a-ally dangerous thing, you know. I've seen one before myself, at the Sanctuary. It wasn't something that could be handled safely, not by any means."

"...There's definitely a risk. But if it's used well..."

"—! Petra-chan, I'm against this, you know!"

At the voice that sharpened and objected the most emotionally of all, Petra's brow drooped.
She knew Meili had been worrying about her all along, and it made her happy. After all, back when she'd first met Meili, Petra had been trying to make Meili fall for her. She'd figured that affection would be the best weapon for blunting killing intent. And that had turned out to be a huge hit.

"Although maybe it worked a little too well."

*"I'll tell you now, I'm with Meili on this one. You never let me hear about it, but Petra's already paying Clind-san something. I can't budge past that."*

The phantom voice, unheard by anyone else, gave Petra the real feeling of being cherished.
She would state it plainly: Imaginary Subaru was Petra's own convenient delusion, but she was certain the reproduction was accurate enough that the real Subaru, without a doubt, would have said the exact same thing.

"It's a good thing Frederica-neesama and Beatrice-chan aren't here."

Meili alone had already managed to shake Petra's resolve considerably.
If Frederica and Beatrice's double punch had been added on top of that, even Petra might have been knocked clean out. What would it have been like if Garfiel and Otto had been there? Garfiel would probably get angry and shout, but she had a feeling Otto would understand.
Besides, Otto was already the one pushing himself the most recklessly out of anyone in the camp right now, so she didn't think he had any right to nag her about anything.

Holding onto that awareness of being a happy, well-loved girl, Petra—

"—Let's properly divide up the roles. So that everyone can do what they do best."

—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never visit a place of memories.

△▼△▼△▼△

Tightly, Petra checked the feel of the square box tucked into her breast pocket, aware of the "Melancholy" Witch Factor she had succeeded—by talking her way around everyone—into having entrusted to her.

"—I need to concentrate."

By putting it into words, Petra sternly cautioned herself to keep hold of her sanity. —Honestly, up until today, Petra had thought of herself as someone with an unshakably strong will.
She hadn't broken her mind even after reading the "Book of the Dead," and even though the boy she loved wouldn't so much as glance her way, she'd never once thought of giving up on making him turn around. For her age, she knew she'd been through more than her share of harrowing scrapes and cutthroat situations.

Even so, even for a Petra like that, the destructive sense of omnipotence a Witch Factor brought left her head spinning.

"No, no—I mustn't... Subaru, cheer me on."

*"Petra, hang in there! Don't lose! If you go and gamble on the very thing I told you not to, and then lose to it on top of that—that's it, I'll end up hating you!"*

"Then, I absolutely won't lose..."

Getting encouragement from the boy she loved inside her own head, and rallying her spirits that way, felt like a fairly hopeless state to be in—but since it actually worked, the power of feelings was mighty indeed.
A Witch Factor was nothing before the power of a crush.
Since the struggle against a Witch Factor was, in the end, ultimately a matter of mentality, it made perfect sense that the power of feelings would serve as a miracle cure. —And the potency of that medicine was something Petra, who had stopped being able to remain a mere village girl because of it, understood better than anyone.

And so—

"—Fire's eight, Noble! Flower's three, Warrior!"

At the hoarse voice striking hard against her eardrums, Petra's eyes went wide, and she clenched her back teeth.
"Compression" activated, and inside Petra's mind, onto the designated position of the battlefield—divided into an eight-by-eight grid, laid out the same way as a Shatranj board—she inserted the designated personnel.

Sure enough, in the blink of an eye, two members of the Felt Camp were brought onto the battlefield—Felt's attendant Gaston, and Doltero, the grim-faced Pig Human known as the "Pig King"—and they swung their mighty arms, sending "Gluttony" and Heinkel, who had each been about to launch an attack, flying.

"Ehh? For real? Does that kind of thing happen?"

Roy, having deflected the shock of the punch with acrobat-like movements, lifted his infuriating face, tearing his gaze away from Ram—whom he'd been fixated on the whole time—to look toward Petra.
Toward Petra, who kept her distance from Ram and Meili, and from Flam and Grassis, who were fighting directly, positioning herself where she could grasp the whole battlefield—and old man Rom, riding perched on her shoulder.

"—Petra-chan, since when did you become a Sin Archbishop?"

Perhaps it was only to be expected of a Sin Archbishop, that he could sense the whereabouts of a Witch Factor—no, that wasn't it; if that were true he'd have caught on to this trick much sooner. Probably he'd guessed that "Compression" was an Authority, then worked out who was in the best position to use it most effectively. Not impressive at all, really.

"That makes me sound terrible. If you're going to call me something that makes me sound bad anyway, then call me this instead."

Without letting even a hint of her wariness toward Roy's insight show, Petra put a hand to her chest, checking the feel of the small black box entrusted to her by Clind, stuck out her tongue with a "bleh," and said—

"—Call me the 'Witch of Melancholy,' Petra Leyte."

Claiming that title was, in most cases, not something to be welcomed.
Naming yourself that was one thing, but having someone else call you that was out of the question. Regardless of whether one had the resolve for it, it was a title like a curse, loathed and despised by every single person across the whole world.
And yet, Petra had declared it proudly, as though it were something to boast of.

After all, the mistress Petra served was the "Witch of Ice," and the boy she loved was a kind, splendid knight who had given his heart to just such a Witch.

—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never pick a beautiful flower.
—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never eat my favorite food.
—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never return to my hometown.

—"Witch of Melancholy," Petra Leyte.

At Petra's declaration, naming herself thus, Roy Alphard felt a rush of ecstasy. —Surprise, that she would go that far, and joy, that she had gone that far for him.
To incorporate a Witch Factor and, of her own will, become a possessor of Authority—a being called a "Witch" or a "Warlock," or perhaps a Sin Archbishop—didn't seem like the act of a sane mind. And in the fact that she'd been able to make such an unhinged decision, Roy couldn't help but feel, strongly, deeply, hugely, hotly, the thoroughness that chose no means—which was to say, the spirit of hospitality: love itself.

"Absolutely the best...! I'm so glad I came out here!"

Spreading both arms wide, Roy, choking with delight, gave thanks for this hospitality and this meeting.
Now that she'd named herself the "Witch of Melancholy," Roy's guess that his opponent's Authority derived from "Pride" had missed the mark. Still, Roy had once been told by the monster who called herself his mother that the "Melancholy" Witch Factor was something special, a defective piece compatible with no one. The "Melancholy" Witch Factor was one of the things that mother of his wanted, so if she heard where it was, she'd surely be delighted.

Of course, he had no intention of giving his mother so much as a single bite of such a special feast.

"It's just a hungry child's cute little selfishness, so surely she'll go easy on me for it, riiight?"

She was the very embodiment of the craving for approval, a mother who loved nothing more than being respected.
If she learned that Roy had prioritized himself over her, it was plain to see what kind of tantrum she'd throw.
Once, that selfishness of his mother's had been nothing but a nuisance to Roy, but now he found himself feeling just a little differently than before. —A feeling that could almost esteem even his mother's selfish, self-satisfied, pathological self-love as an irreplaceable trait all her own.

"Earth's six, Queen! Sun's six, Hunter! Shadow's five, Noble!"

In response to the giant elder's instructions, a faint light settled into Petra's eyes.
It wasn't as though they actually glowed. It was more that, as a result of pouring her attention into using an Authority she wasn't used to, the moment she strained showed through. Normally it was a trivial habit not worth worrying about, but in the middle of a battle where an instant decided victory or defeat, even that became information directly tied to life and death.

In Roy's field of view, the figures of Ram, the Pig Human, and Meili astride the Hungry Horse King vanished. —And the instant they vanished, they reappeared surrounding Roy from three directions, launching their attacks.
That timing, Roy could tell as plainly as if he'd reached out and touched it, from the light in Petra's eyes.

"Yes, yes, pardon the intrusion!"

An instant before the attacks landed, Roy pressed his hands together in front of his chest. —In that moment, the earth surged upward like a mound of sand squeezed hard from both sides, cutting off Ram's and the Pig Human's line of sight to Roy. Leaving those two to deal with that, Roy focused his attention on the noisiest crying baby.

Without even turning around, the view from the back of his head leapt into focus—there, the Hungry Horse King's bone lance carrying Meili, and, launched right alongside it as a set, the monstrous arm of Guiltilau came swinging.
Tilting his body sharply, dodge after dodge after dodge, Roy licked his lips.

The "Compactor" Figdol had been a burial specialist assassin who used his peculiar ability to crush everything from the edges to the center of his own field of view together, and the "Saintess of a Thousand Visages" Ielna had possessed both a wide field of vision and a heart that, three hundred sixty degrees in every direction, never missed the tears of a grieving weakling. —But the ability of the "Witchbeast Queen" Meili was, if anything, every bit their equal, if not their better.

"——!!"

A pack of Ulgarm, the Hungry Horse King of the sand sea, and Guiltilau, renowned as the "Black King of the Forest"—at the skill with which Meili summoned Witchbeasts one after another, Roy couldn't even catch his breath from emotion. He'd already studied Meili's method of commanding Witchbeasts whose horns hadn't been broken, through "Memories," but at the Pleiades Watchtower, actually experiencing its true worth firsthand had been put on hold—so seeing the real thing had him tremendously excited.

"Being born with a Divine Protection like that, your life must've been quite the roller coaster, huh? Tell us how you've lived, won't you, Meiliii!"

"...Tell you how I've lived—Subaru-oniisan and Emilia-oneesan asked me that before too."

"Ohh? And? And?"

Nimbly dodging the Hungry Horse King's bone lance and Guiltilau's sharp claws, glaring at Roy as he slurped up his drool, Meili, her face full of hostility and disgust, stuck her tongue out at him and said,

"Just like that talk about love from earlier—in the end, what matters isn't what's said, it's who says it, you know!"

At that truth, which left him nothing to grab onto, Roy could only manage a "Tahaa!"—no words to answer with.
Behind Roy, the Pig Human's wall of earth was shattered, and from overhead Ram fell, having vanished and reappeared once again. Front, behind, overhead—surrounded three-dimensionally, the space beneath Roy's legs and to both his sides warped, and the sorcery of the "Magic Manus Magister" Yardley—buried in the shadow of history, and at last devoured—activated: spears of fire, ice, and earth conjured by Magic, taking their aim in an instant.
His targets were Meili, the Pig Human, and Ram—but they weren't there.

"Losing sight of Ram again and again. Why don't you just throw those eyes away?"

—There she was. At a distance so close it was almost an embrace, Ram's slender frame appeared.

—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never cry in front of others.
—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never wipe away anyone's tears.

"The 'Witch of Melancholy'...!"

—"Compactor" × "Saintess of a Thousand Visages" × "Magic Manus Magister."

Outmatched even by the combined power of these new Outer-World-defying figures he'd multiplied together, by the three-pronged, multi-angled assault—and knowing whose hand lay behind it, Roy's throat let out a high sound.
It wasn't anger that made his voice shake. It was elation. His heart wouldn't stop pounding with exhilaration.

Giving himself over to that pounding, Roy re-aimed the three-elemental spears he'd already aimed once, smashing them into one another, and from the resulting blast and steam, Ram and the others, unable to bear it, moved to reset the scene—

"—Wha—"

"Did you think I'd 'Teleport' just to avoid getting hurt?"

The hot steam that should have covered her vision and forced a "Teleport" via the Authority of "Melancholy" was torn apart by sheer force, and from beyond it, the owner of those pale pink eyes came bursting through.
At the sight of her diving headfirst into a heat wave certain to burn her, Roy—who had erased the option of deliberately taking damage from his mind entirely—was caught off guard. Immediately, the option arose to use the "Leaper's" "Teleport" and force a reset on his own terms. —But,

"—Absolutely the best!"

The instant he acknowledged his enemy's splendor, Roy Alphard's "Omnivore" awakened.
A cry of triumph burst inside his head, and countless options branched out and shone bright within Roy's mind.
He threw open the treasury of stored "Memories," seeking, among the innumerable treasures tossed in without order, the one that shone—the birth-cry of some sleeping fortune, waiting impatiently for its cue to appear in this very instant.

—At present, the biggest focus was the true identity of the hospitality that had made his "Corrosion" fail.

"——"

Roy had failed at "Corrosion" against Ram, the one whose soul he'd reached for, meaning to devour her "Memories."
But she was, without a doubt, Ram. That much was clear even from the freshly devoured "Memories"—the source of those "Memories" called her Ram, everyone else around her called her Ram too, and for that matter she herself even used "Ram" as her own first-person pronoun; she was thoroughly, completely Ram.

And yet, the activation condition for "Corrosion" hadn't been met under the name Ram. In this case, the thing most likely to have caused "Corrosion" to fail was—

"—A change of true name!"

What "Corrosion" required was the preparation to snatch the target from the roster of souls managed by Od Lagna. Therefore, the target's name had to be exactly the one Od Lagna had recorded as such.
In most cases, that was the vow made at the naming ceremony at birth—when that prayer reached him, the true name would be recorded by Od Lagna, who might be called a great hollow head.
As a rule, once a true name was set, it would not change no matter how the person themselves self-identified. The few exceptions where Od Lagna would recognize such a change were—

"—Ram Mathers."

"—!"

Roy reached her first—Ram, who had broken through the steam and was moving to land a blow.
The most common reason for a change of true name was "marriage." —And Ram had had Meili call her "wife." From the "Memories" too, there was no room to doubt who it was Ram had feelings for.

Convinced this was it, Roy activated "Corrosion" on the soul of Ram—no, Ram Mathers—whose pale pink eyes went wide, meaning to devour her "Memories" and understand her down to the very last drop, saying—

"Itadakima—guh."

In that instant, with the sensation of having filth stuffed into his open mouth, Roy's eyes bulged.
That was the proof of "Corrosion's" failure—his whole body shook with violent revulsion, and Roy, whose readiness to welcome a feast had been torn apart from within, found his thoughts overrun with senseless questions.

Why, how come, understanding, marriage, Ram, true name, dotes on his little sister, bad taste, margrave, devoted, mean-spirited, capable, dependable, only his taste is bad, mean-spirited too, marriage, passion—.

"—Did you peek at Otto's 'Memories'? How stupid."

"Ah—?"

"Using an emergency like this as an excuse, did you think Ram would settle down as Roswaal-sama's spouse? —If you truly knew Ram, that's a mistake you wouldn't have made."

At the mocking smile that lightly curved Ram's lips, Roy understood.
Having Meili deliberately call her "wife" had been a misdirection meant to lead him, relying on a family member's "Memories," to the wrong conclusion—and in fact, Roy had skipped the low-risk option of the "Leaper" and, convinced he could feast on it, extended his tongue toward the high-return soul of Ram.
A judgment he never would have made, had he not devoured the "Memories" of Otto Suwen.

"That one's for Otto's sake, more or less."

So Ram declared, and her wind-wrapped fist, the Pig Human's rock-like knuckles, and on top of that the burning bone lance wreathed in searing heat and the ferocious beast's mighty arm all struck Roy Alphard at once, driving home simultaneously.
