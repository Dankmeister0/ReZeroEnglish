Arc 9 Interlude: "Yell"

—For Petra Leyte, the Witch of Melancholy, there had only ever been one ending worth aiming for, worth arriving at, worth reaching. That had been decided from the very start.

It began from the moment Petra Leyte—back when she was just a village girl, before she became the Witch of Melancholy—read something as outrageous as Natsuki Subaru's Book of the Dead.
It began from the moment she learned that Natsuki Subaru had kept on fighting, trading his own "deaths" to change the fates of so many people.
It began from the moment that, through sharing quiet, ordinary days together, she came to love him for who he was—what made him happy, what made him sad, what brought him joy and what brought him sorrow.

—Gather up all those various reasons, bundle them together, wrap them up, and the answer had already been decided.

"Because, either way, Subaru wants to do something about Al-san too, doesn't he?"

Honestly, from Petra's perspective, it was an outrageous thing to even consider.
If you tried to count off everything Al had done, one finger at a time, all the fingers on your hands and feet wouldn't be enough—you'd have to call in the Unseen Hand just to have more fingers left to fold down. That's how many irreversible things Al had done. —But even so, she didn't think that made him some villain to the bone, doing it all while sneering at everything.

"Maybe this is Subaru's book poisoning me too……"

It felt strange to say about herself, but Petra was a realist, and the range of things she could take on was narrow. Or rather, it would be more accurate to say she deliberately kept it narrow.
After all, if she used every last bit of the capacity she had without holding back, Petra was confident she had a vessel big enough to hold quite a lot. That much she wasn't shy about admitting.
But she couldn't bring herself to use every corner of that capacity and try to cherish everything she could possibly cram into it.
And yet——

"Subaru's so greedy……"

But that's what she liked about him. —No, that's wrong. That, too, was something she liked about him.
And because she couldn't change a Natsuki Subaru like that, Petra Leyte would do it herself.
It was a decision that hurt—like being cut to the bone, like having her heart torn apart without mercy.

—Send Natsuki Subaru back through Return by Death, and reset everything that had happened.

From the moment she left the Pleiades Watchtower and decided to go on together with Imaginary Subaru, Petra Leyte had been pressing forward toward that ending.

She'd insisted, adamantly, on becoming the holder of the Melancholy Witch Factor. She'd consolidated the price of using its Authority onto herself alone, save for a few exceptions. She'd played every negotiating card she had without holding back, and offered up every piece of information she could share—and in doing all of that, she'd shaped the policy of the Aldebusters.

"If it's because I read the book that the Witch of Envy is rampaging—then that, too, can be put to use, right?"

From the moment Roy Alphard, the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony, was brought out of the Prison Tower, and Petra came to grasp the effect of his Authority, a question had lodged itself inside her: if her memories—Petra's, who had read the Book of the Dead—were taken from her, what judgment would the Witch of Envy render?
And, following that same thread—wasn't this exactly the plan Al, who'd said he had no intention of going so far as to destroy the world, had devised for driving off the Witch of Envy?
So when the Aldebusters, with everything they had, brought down Roy Alphard, she bound him as he lay at death's door, barely breathing, and made him a promise.

"If you want to eat me, I'll make you the chance."

A promise she fully intended to break without mercy, of course, if she didn't have to keep it.
Under Natsuki Subaru's bad influence, Petra had become an outlaw who didn't think twice about breaking a promise made to someone she disliked.
And besides, just in case it did come to pass, she'd already secured a reassuring ally to prepare for it.

"Rem-neesama is amazing."

That was her quiet, heartfelt thought, and not merely the favoritism she'd picked up so heavily from Natsuki Subaru's influence.
Rem had figured out Subaru's Return by Death—at first she'd called it "time regression"—and even though it was Rom-jii's unraveling the true nature of Al's Authority that had sparked it, she'd reached that realization on her own.
On her own, Rem had realized that Natsuki Subaru was someone who had kept fighting at the cost of his own life. —And while that made Petra glad, she couldn't help but feel frustrated by it too.

"But that's exactly why I can entrust it to Rem-neesama. ……No, it has to be Rem-neesama. No one else will do."

No matter how many good things happened along the way, no matter how many feelings she came to treasure and hate to part with, Petra Leyte had decided to overturn all of it—to make it as though none of it had ever happened.
And that, quite precisely, meant that even the moment of welcoming back Rem—who had finally regained her name and her memories, who had truly come home in every sense of the word—would itself vanish and cease to be.

Petra, who meant to go through with it knowing all that, had a duty to bring Rem into it.
As fellow comrades who both knew they would be erased just the same; as two women who, even so, wished from the bottom of their hearts that the person they loved most wouldn't lose a single thing.

This was something she could only entrust to someone who believed, with utter conviction, that there was no other way—someone willing to throw everything away for love.
She couldn't leave a task this cruel to someone who would keep desperately searching, right up to the very last possible moment, for some better answer than this.
So this was something Petra and Rem would carry out, the two of them.

"Subaru, Emilia-neesama—you must both be so tired. I'm sorry. And on top of that, now I'm becoming a burden too. I'm sorry. I wanted to keep thanking you, over and over, forever, since it would never be enough……"

She'd wanted to say thank you—a real, true thank you.
As Petra Leyte, who had read the Book of the Dead and knew everything, she'd wanted to cheer on the road ahead: Emilia walking her thorny path, and Natsuki Subaru, who had chosen to support her at the cost of working himself to the bone.
That wish wouldn't be granted. Petra Leyte would do everything in her power, but in the end she'd go back to being a village girl who knew nothing.
That was frustrating, and sad, and no amount of thank-yous or sorries would ever be enough.
Even so——

"……I wonder if I did everything I could."

Within the limits of the situation, hadn't she done absolutely everything within her power?
Al, after all, must have assembled his own all-star lineup to keep himself from losing, while she'd only had one single shot at trial and error to push back against it—there was a limit to how unfair that was.
But she'd done it. She'd pulled it off. Along the way, Otto joining the fight despite having no memory of any of this had, admittedly, made her recoil a little—but that too, all of it, was thanks to everyone pitching in together.

"Fufu, that's pretty impressive, right? Falling for me yet?"

"……Yeah. I might just fall for you."

She tilted her head slightly, greeting him with the cutest face she could possibly stage herself.
At Petra's adorable little gesture and question, Imaginary Subaru answered with just that touch of bashfulness. Was that Petra's own wishful fabrication, putting words in his mouth? Or was it convincing enough that the real Subaru might have said the same thing?

"Well, since I'm going to disappear soon anyway, I might as well take it however suits me best."

She gently reached out, laying her hand over a hand she couldn't actually touch, lacing their fingers together. Surely a small phantom perk like this could be forgiven. She had, after all, worked so very hard.

"Come to think of it, can I ask you something? Well, it's kind of weird for me to be the one asking, but…"

"Hmm~? What is it?"

"Before you got the Witch Factor handed over from Clind-san, you paid some kind of price once, for the trip there… but you never told me what it was. I was just wondering what that was about."

"I didn't tell you on purpose, so why don't you just leave it unasked?"

"That's cruel."

Even his pitiful expression looked cute to her, and Petra let out a small burst of laughter.
But even so—Imaginary or not, Subaru was Subaru. Whatever Petra hid because she didn't want it seen, didn't want it known, he wouldn't pry into, even as a resident of her own head.
Relieved by that, Petra stuck out her tongue and said,

"It's a se~cret!"

That made Imaginary Subaru pull a sour face, but as far as Petra was concerned, she wanted to keep it hidden.
It was fine to have at least one maiden's secret she wouldn't show even to Imaginary Subaru. And this particular secret had just a little bit of a maiden's-heart sneakiness to it.

Back then, Petra had offered up a certain possibility of her own to Clind, as payment.
It was——

"——The possibility that I become Subaru's bride."

Clind, who knew of Petra's feelings, had been considerably startled at the time.
Clind, who apparently bore the terribly heavy history of being one of the Dragonkin, had looked at Petra so devotedly offering up her own possibility with eyes overwhelmed with emotion—a reaction Petra wouldn't soon forget.
Which was exactly why she felt sorry about it. —Because that price had given Petra a certainty.

"If I'm able to offer that up as a price, then that future possibility must really, truly exist for me."

Given the nature of the Melancholy Witch Factor, what she'd actually offered up, precisely speaking, was her influence on the world in the form of "all the various efforts Petra would make to obtain a future in which she marries Subaru."
But such fine details didn't amount to much for Petra in that moment.

What mattered was believing in a future where Petra Leyte became Natsuki Subaru's bride.
And that was proven, by none other than a Witch Factor that touched the very core of the world itself.
Because she had that certainty, she'd been able to resolve to see it through, and run all the way to the very end.

So——

"Do your best, me. ——Whether you can seize the chance is entirely up to how hard you try!"

That was the yell—from the village girl who had become the Witch of Melancholy, who had used her Witch Factor for her own happiness, to the future version of herself who would never become the Witch of Melancholy at all.

—It was the yell of a tough, shrewd girl from another world. A somewhat heavy-handed yell of love.
