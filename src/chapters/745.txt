Arc 9 Chapter 49: "The Sorcerer"

—Bursts of violet lightning flashed and danced as Clind bounded across the battlefield sky, springing every which way, utterly unconstrained by direction.

'—!!'

Chasing the light of that thunderous burst, dragon claws lashed out like a storm, raking at the empty sky, and each swipe tore open nothingness itself, the resulting vacuum waves sealing off the Dragonkin's avenues of escape.
Even as his options were whittled away, Clind kept the Dragon's aim fixed squarely on himself, maneuvering through the heavens with careful boldness so that not even the aftershocks would reach the ground.

"So you've exceeded my expectations. Astonishment."

Dodging, deflecting, and meeting head-on the Dragon's violent might—power capable of reshaping the terrain with every single blow—Clind could not help but marvel at how the Usurper handled the Dragonhusk.

As already noted, the Usurper had not fully mastered the Dragonhusk of the Divine Dragon. It was, after all, like handing a newborn babe the body of the Sword Saint—an impossible ask from the very start.
And so Clind was impressed not that the Usurper had unleashed the Divine Dragon's true potential, but that he treated even the Dragonhusk as merely one more tool for victory—a greedy resolve Clind respected as yet another facet of the strength that weak humans could possess.
But as the battle dragged on far longer than expected, he noticed something—that the Usurper, too, was adapting to the Dragonhusk far beyond anything Clind had anticipated, and growing all the while.

"I had heard he'd already crossed swords with both the Sword Saint and the Sword Devil. Understanding."

Through repeated battles against powerful foes whom even the Divine Dragon's Dragonhusk could not easily overwhelm, the Usurper had drawn out the Dragon's potential to a considerable degree. That much was more than evident in the brilliance of the dragon scales and dragon halo guarding the Heart Core.

"The potential of humankind, is it... Reverence."

How absurd, that the Dragons should boast of being a perfected form of life.
Why had the Dragons been able to stride the earth as though they owned it, the strongest life-form of all? Clind believed the answer lay in that great, presumptuous footfall itself. Their presence was so overwhelming that no one could look away from it, could not simply leave it be. That was what let the Dragons wear the biggest face on the earth.
And so, once the Dragons had no place left on this earth, they vanished beyond the Great Waterfall.

"—"

Even knowing full well that such sentiment was out of place, memories of the past stirred within the Dragonkin's breast.
Countless rifts split open the sky, boulders and shards of ice caught up in the roaring, howling wind flew through the air, and as he tore through shockwave after shockwave—ten, then twenty—Clind's mind conjured a scene from four hundred years past: his kin, driven from the earth, taking flight and vanishing.

It's said that on that day, only strays from the herd and oddities remained on the earth, while the Dragons possessed of sound thought and judgment departed beyond the Great Waterfall—but to Clind's mind, it was the other way around.
The Dragons had been able to stand as the apex life-form because their very lives were designed to be the strongest. If so, then the Dragons ought to have kept learning and growing without pause, so as never to relinquish that seat.
Was not the very birth of the Dragonkin proof of what a Dragon ought to be?

"Above all, is it that they simply cannot see it? —Derision."

Turning the violet lightning wreathing him into rolling thunder, shattering the hundreds of boulders and ice-blocks that filled the sky, Clind—drawing the Dragon's full attention onto himself—looked down at the earth.
There—

"—Dragon scales and dragon halo. So that's the trick behind how the Divine Dragon neutralized your trump card."

"'Neutralized' is a rather unwelcome choice of words. I'd prefer either 'nullified' or 'rendered harmless,' if you plea-ease."

"Is now really the time to quibble over trivialities!? ...Simultaneous activation of different attributes—is that an application of the Dual-Attribute Cross Formula? But if you mix three types together, how do you control the convergence pressure between fire and wind?"

"It's simple, really. You set the convergence pressure itself as the core. As for the imbalance the formula develops from mutual interference—if the third attribute is water, you correct it by aligning it with fire; if earth, with wind."

"You mean you're exploiting the attributes' spiral interference!? Impossible—clash attributes of opposing phase against each other and the formulas cancel each other out, easily exceeding the Gate's critical deviation and... Polar Compression!"

"Oh-ho, arriving there on your own—you're better than I expected. From what I can see, it's a novel formula construction, and you seem to be self-taught, too... no, wait, looking closer, isn't that the foundational formula left behind by my house's forebear from two generations back?"

"Nngh...!"

"These days, the mainstream approach is the 'Equilibrium Circulation,' which treats the mana circulating through the body as a closed loop—this one should have been discarded long ago as worthless... The records were lost quite some time back. Don't tell me it was you?"

"T-to master the path of magic requires resolve, great or small! In any case, the treatise was gathering dust on a market stall! From it I derived the 'Unified Circulation,' and dramatically improved the efficiency of mana usage in the activation of magic!"

"Quite the shameless thief, stealing someone else's the-e-eory."

"I admit it was the spark for the idea, but improvement and refinement are the constant nature of progress! Are you telling me, then, that you never received guidance or teaching from anyone at all!?"

"—"

"Don't go silent on me all of a sudden! In the first place, even conceding every point, the credit belongs not to you but to the great achievement of your predecessor's predecessor, the lady Roswaal J. Mathers!"

"What I mean is, she's more or less me as well. —Let's get back on track. I'd never once considered the 'Unified Circulation.' If you understand the flaw in the Dual-Attribute Cross Formula, need I even explain why 'Tripod Center-of-Gravity Control' is ill-suited to the Multi-Attribute Cross Formula?"

"Are you mocking me? Add just one more attribute to those activated simultaneously, and the disorder of spiral interference multiplies. With 'Tripod Center-of-Gravity Control' you can manage simultaneous activation up to three attributes, but beyond that... no, wait—just now you achieved simultaneous control of five attributes. Just how did you manage that?"

"I see. You want to hear the answer. Very well, I'll reveal it. I'd have thought the true joy of magic lay in testing one's own spirit of inquiry against the profound essence of the arcane—but if that's the extent of your passion—"

"Wait, wait, wait, wait, be quiet...! Five attributes, spiral interference, Polar Compression... no, his formula runs on 'Equilibrium Circulation'... that's it! A layered parallel run of 'Tripod Center-of-Gravity Control'!"

"—!"

"In other words, you ran two strands of three-attribute simultaneous activation under 'Tripod Center-of-Gravity Control,' layered on top of each other! Within that second strand's three attributes, you used the activated Yang attribute to reinforce your own Gate, and by setting the convergence pressure born of spiral interference as the core, you achieved Polar Compression between three attributes and two—realizing that very feat..."

"Yes, exactly! That is the breakthrough proving the existence of the 'Fivefold Resonance Point'!"

"Are you insane!? One move, one instant, one hair's breadth off, and it all ends in a spiral collapse! You'd create a second Agzadd Canyon centered right on yourself!?"

"But it didn't happen that way. —That is the lofty, distant summit of the path of magic."

"—"

"Are you frightened, Ezzo Cadner-kun?"

"Ah, terrified indeed. Terrified of the part of myself that longs for it, Roswaal L. Mathers."

—Yes, there on the ground, two sorcerers were clashing their hard-won mastery against each other, honing one another in turn.

"What a lively face you're wearing. Delight."

He had originally been entrusted with a role of his own. That was why fighting fiercely at the very front of the battle against the Dragon, wrapped in violet lightning, was something Clind had chosen to take on of his own will.
But even had that role never been entrusted to him, Clind—following his own convictions—would surely have chosen, just as now, to cross violet lightning with what had once been his own Dragonhusk.

Human striving against human, mutual polishing between them, was on the verge of birthing something new.

That, precisely, was the wish of Clind, Dragonkin of the Divine Dragon Volcanica—who, deemed a heretic by kin unwilling to accept new values, had chosen the path of being left behind on this earth.
For it was the sturdiest pillar of the spirit that had never once been consumed by the Witch Factor of Melancholy, which demands the closing off of possibility as its price.

Roswaal knew almost nothing about Ezzo Cadner, who now stood at his side.

He'd heard the name. A new addition to the Felt Camp, a domestic-affairs officer who, thanks to his competence, had swiftly become one of the camp's central figures—that was the extent of his understanding.
There had also been word that he was a sorcerer, but Roswaal had taken him for some self-styled hedge-wizard of unknown pedigree and teacher—and given that Ezzo was in fact self-taught, that impression hadn't been far off the mark.
But even that impression of him as a mere pretender to sorcery was overturned the moment Roswaal faced the real Ezzo and laid eyes on the theory behind his magical constructions. —He was the genuine article.

And between two men who each recognized the other as genuine, many words were unnecessary.

"Rest a while and catch your breath. I'll need you to push yourself past reason at least once more."

Having said that, Ezzo leapt down from the platform he'd raised to bring his eyes level with Roswaal's, stepped one pace further into the battlefield, and looked up at the sky.

"—"

Under the barrage of Great Magic and Super Magic, layered atop the clashes between Dragon and Dragonkin, the once-blue sky had been battered by the dramatic effects of pooling mana and transformed into a shimmering, rainbow-hued freak of weather. There, countless bursts of violet lightning still chained one after another, as the Dragonkin's lone battle across the heavens continued.
Even after Roswaal, his strength spent, had collapsed, Clind had gone on fulfilling his role—

"Why is he fighting alone? You're laboring under a misapprehension."

"...What did you say?"

"Do you think a sorcerer cannot join with others? No—harmony is the very essence of the art!"

Before Roswaal's widening eyes, countless orbs of water rose up around Ezzo as he spoke.
The water orbs had drawn in numerous shattered chunks of rock, and carrying them within, shot upward all at once—plunging into the intensifying battlefield where Clind and the Divine Dragon clashed.

"That's—"

Reckless—no, pointless, was Roswaal's first thought; he couldn't grasp Ezzo's intent.
Even granting that it was single-attribute control of water, the skill to so casually handle a number approaching a hundred was impressive—but water orbs of that size, even flung together with their rocks, wouldn't so much as catch the Dragon's attention.
If anything, it might well collapse the tactics Clind was using to face the Divine Dragon single-handedly.

"I'll say it again, Roswaal. —You are laboring under a misapprehension."

In the instant right after Ezzo's declaration, Roswaal caught his breath at what he saw.
The countless water orbs he'd raised weren't meant to attack the Divine Dragon at all. —Bursting violet lightning detonated the water orbs and their stones, and with footing gained, Clind's speed as he wheeled through the sky climbed another notch.
The water orbs spread wide across the sky as if to guide the accelerating Clind's path, and with launch pads granted him, the Dragonkin's blows bit into the Divine Dragon's scales sharper, heavier, and more vivid than before.
The roar and shockwaves reached all the way down to the ground, and as the wind buffeted him, Ezzo went on speaking to Roswaal.

"Certainly, he has the capacity to fight aerially on his own. But if I judge rightly, that magic light's original purpose isn't offense—it's a jaw-dropping defensive capability. Because he's a seasoned, powerful fighter, he can make do fighting it in a different way, but strip away the dissipation of power, and its true might isn't even comparable."

"—. Even so. Preparing footholds in the sky would, if anything, make it harder to scatter one's aim. Wouldn't it risk killing Clind's strength—the freedom to kick off the air at will?"

"Do you take him for some unskilled novice, incapable of switching between the two as the need arises? If so, then what you lack isn't only consideration for others—it's also an eye for people."

"—"

"Roswaal, fighting alongside someone means trusting them—it isn't the same as refusing to give any thought to anything at all. It means entrusting what should be entrusted, and supplementing what should be supplemented. Your way might turn a hundred and a hundred into two hundred that merely don't get in each other's way, but it will never turn a hundred and a hundred into a thousand that elevate one another."

Even as he laid out this theory of his, Ezzo never slackened his support for the battle overhead.
It was a rocky wasteland, with no shortage of stone to lift. The water orbs multiplied fast enough to fill the sky, and even as Clind consumed them as footing at a speed too quick to follow, the rate at which they replenished overwhelmed the rate of loss.

'Tch, thought something felt clever—it's the Professor, is it!'

Having traced the source of the footholds being used against it, the Divine Dragon spotted Ezzo and bellowed savagely. Its dragon claws shattered a hundred water orbs in a single stroke, and then, aiming at the sorcerers below, unleashed its breath—

"Looking away isn't advisable—see for yourself. Proof."

Clind's leaping feet slammed straight up into the Dragon's jaw from directly below. The breath it couldn't release exploded within its own mouth, and its fangs could be seen bursting off from the impact.

'Y-you...!'

The Divine Dragon, reeling from its own self-inflicted blast, twisted and warped the air around itself as an unstable but undeniably immense magic light expanded—the Dragon was attempting to invoke a Great Magic it had picked up merely by watching.
Clind, who could turn violet lightning into a barrier, and the Divine Dragon, who could rely on its own dragon halo for defense, might withstand it—but should it fall to the ground, Roswaal and the others wouldn't stand a chance.

"—In that case, all that's left is to make it explode in the sky."

Fortunately, there was mana scattered at random, and, right beside him, countless magical water orbs built on the foundation of the 'Unified Circulation'—which he'd had ample chance to observe up close. —He cut into that magic's construction and rewrote its performance.

"That's my magic, you know!?"

"Magic belongs to no one. The path is always open to all. —So my teacher taught me."

In an instant, he bundled together the water orbs whose control he'd seized, drew in the surrounding mana, and triggered Polar Compression—forcing his own interpretation into the Great Magic the Divine Dragon had been trying to construct, and deliberately botching its completion.
What followed was the spiral collapse Ezzo had feared—a colossal explosion of polar light, the sky that had already broken so many times in this battle, breaking apart once more.
Searing heat and bitter cold raced through in alternation; the rocks hanging in the sky melted, froze, and shattered apart. Bolts of lightning burned away even their remains, while the blast of wind hurled ash and dust several kilometers away—a catastrophe of the first order.

"Are you out of your mind!? You very nearly blew away your own ally along with it!?"

"But you protected him. —This is what you meant by harmony, is-s-sn't it?"

Closing his yellow eye in a wink, Roswaal silenced Ezzo, whose face had gone pale.
In truth, it had been Ezzo's own skill that had encircled Clind, caught up in the great blast overhead, with layer upon layer of banded water, suppressing that ruinous firepower to the utmost and getting protection through to him in time.
Roswaal had trusted that Ezzo would sense his intent and act on it. Had their roles been reversed, the gap in skill and consideration would have made such perfect division of labor absolutely impossible.
He was, after all, an inhuman villain. —Just one with room left for improvement and refinement, for progress.

"Even so, this deed makes me question my master's sanity. Fiendish."

So, at least, one could almost hear the grievance of Clind, who'd only escaped a fatal fall thanks to Ezzo's quick thinking—but any scolding delivered while facing down that cold countenance of his would all have to wait until after the battle was over.
And then—

"Ezzo Cadner-kun."

"—. What is it, Roswaal L. Mathers."

It was almost funny, the way Ezzo, called by name, had shot the same treatment right back, as if in competition. Hiding those thoughts behind makeup that blood and sweat had all but ruined, Roswaal let out a breath.
Then, once more, he fixed his gaze on Ezzo Cadner of the Gray—

"—I want your help. In bringing about a new dawn of magic, the likes of which has never once occurred in this world."

Even he had to admit it was an underhanded thing to say, Roswaal thought, brazening it out regardless. —After all, there was no way someone besotted with the path of magic could ever resist a temptation like that.

—In the midst of constructing his formula, Ezzo Cadner found himself, without realizing it, on the verge of tears.

"Fool, fool, fool—you absurd tear ducts, you. Don't go working now. Slack off."

Scolding his own body's functions like that, he tightened his cheeks and dammed up the tears threatening to spill.

Ezzo hated to slack off. That was because the Dwarf Tribe was blessed with neither exceptional physical prowess nor an outstanding Gate, and most of his kin had to strive harder than other races just to reach their goals. The one racial trait the Dwarf Tribe did have going for it was an ease of receiving Divine Protections—but unlike, say, Camberley of the same Felt Camp, Ezzo had missed out on that lottery too, plainly one of the have-nots.

Ezzo hated to slack off. Not because he'd been forced into a harsher life than others—no. It was because being looked down on with an "ah, of course" on account of his Dwarven blood was, somehow, something he simply couldn't stand.
Even if Ezzo failed to reach a goal he'd set for himself, it wouldn't be because he was a dwarf.
He understood all too well that in life, the moment you allow yourself one excuse, laziness and negligence come storming in without delay, remaking you into a sluggish, slovenly, soft touch of a person.
For that matter, that was exactly what had happened to his older brothers and sisters. Ezzo was the youngest of ten siblings, but every one of them besides himself had been granted a Divine Protection, and they were living it up in a thoroughly slack, self-indulgent way of life leaning on that gift.
He wouldn't call that a bad thing. It was his brothers' and sisters' lives to live. He wanted to call it bad, but he wouldn't. He was holding it in.

Still, if that were ever to become his own life, it would be unbearable.
That was why, when Ezzo Cadner discovered that dust-covered treatise on magic tucked away at the back of a box on a market stall, he dedicated his life to the emotion it stirred in him and swore he would never make an excuse again.

"You're the same, aren't you, Roswaal. ...I can tell just by looking."

Roswaal, who held the position of the Kingdom's Court Mage and stood in a position to be granted the titles of the "Colored," for those who had mastered a magical attribute, was nonetheless famous for never teaching or guiding others, never mingling with them. Capable as a Margrave, sure—but his standing as a sorcerer had been bought with pedigree and political power. That was how Ezzo had once doubted and challenged Roswaal—a shameful chapter of his past he'd rather forget.

But when he actually witnessed how utterly without waste Roswaal's magical constructions unfolded, the beauty of the formulas he drew out, Ezzo was ashamed of himself to the very depths of his heart.

Ezzo and Roswaal were of the same generation.
And Ezzo had been arrogant enough to believe that no one his own age had worked harder than he had. He'd poured every moment of his living time into it, and told himself he'd only just brushed against the barest edge of what magic truly was. But not even close. —Between Roswaal and Ezzo, in the pursuit and refinement of magic, it felt as though a gap of more than a hundred years separated them.
That was an absolute truth that admitted no excuse of talent, of race, of birth—and that was why Ezzo Cadner was so happy he nearly wept.

"—"

He was glad—glad that the one standing in the domain he'd yearned for was a man who had striven harder than he himself ever had.
That understanding, that admiration, that gratitude, sent Ezzo's heart surging, and for the very first time in his life, made him—who despised slacking off more than anything—order his own tear ducts to slack off instead.

With such feelings in his breast, Ezzo, too, focused his mind, determined to show the depth of his own accumulated inquiry.
Magic demands that one vividly imagine the result to be brought about. That act is tantamount to laying bare everything about oneself—what one thinks, what one wants, and what shape one gives to one's own wishes.
That is why magic contains no lies. —See a person's magic, and you see everything about them.

"—Hey, you understand, don't you, Roswaal."

Was that, in truth, spoken aloud? Or was it merely an illusion conjured by a Gate pushed harder than ever before, and a concentration sharp enough to shave away at his very life?

"...Ah, of course I understand, Ezzo Cadner-kun."

And yet, contrary to the part of him that suspected it was mere hallucination or auditory illusion, an answer came.
The scenery had gone white with the extreme surge of mana clashing against mana; he couldn't even hear the rolling thunder of the violet lightning fighting so hard to protect them—and yet each man's presence felt close enough that even their heartbeats might have reached the other.
Through the magic being woven together, the consciousnesses of the two sorcerers, each laying himself bare, intertwined.

The days Roswaal had piled one atop another, the fruit of his single-minded pursuit, the long stretch of time he'd walked on alone toward the summit with no one ever catching up to him—all of it came through.
Going too far, without meaning to, leaves a person alone. Roswaal, too, had remained alone all this while.
But by the tears he had refused to shed, Ezzo had to say this to Roswaal.

"How far you mean to go, all on your own—I don't know, not as I am now. But no matter how many people try to place you far out of reach, I alone will be different."

If effort and hard-won mastery had brought him this far, then Ezzo could do the same—no, more than that.

"However much you may prefer solitude, however much you choose to die alone, however far you try to go to a place no one else can reach—I alone will sink my teeth in and hold on."

He couldn't understand the urge to give up, but the urge to grieve—that much Ezzo understood.
You do everything within your power to the fullest, and before you know it, there's no one left around you. —No one wants to think that's their own fault.

"The titles granted those who master an attribute—of the three colors 'Red,' 'Green,' and 'Yellow,' you were named. And yet you ceded the seat of 'Blue' to Master Felix Argyle, and to this day leave the seats of 'White' and 'Black' vacant. —Do you understand what that means?"

"—"

"Do you know what they call me? Ezzo Cadner of the Gray. What colors do you think make up gray? —Roswaal, I am something that reaches you."

And so, Ezzo Cadner offers up to the great sorcerer the very same gratitude he himself once tasted.


"—You will never again walk this path alone."

The color had already vanished from the sky.
Blue had drained from what had been a clear azure heaven, leaving only a faded curtain of ashen white spread wide, as though the very worth of the atmosphere itself had thinned and were fading away into nothing.

A flash of light tore through that faded sky. Clind, streaking through it like lightning, trailing violet electricity like a ribbon behind him—it was no longer a matter of "leaping"; the sheer force of it was more like kicking the sky itself apart.
Facing him was a colossal silhouette, its Dragonhusk glowing faintly as it soared through the sky, protected by dragon scales and dragon halo—every flash of its claws split the void, and fault-lines of vacuum surged toward the Dragonkin amid a deafening roar.

"—"

There was no room left to spare for words. Only the crackle of violet lightning.
Next came the beating dragon wings, and countless bolts of light, loosed amid gusts of wind, flashed across the sky. They scattered like stardust across the gray heavens and drew savage arcs as they closed in on Clind—closer, closer, closer. —Thunder flickered, hundreds of streaking beams of light were pierced through, and countless glimmers colored the desolate sky. The aftershocks of the blast dispersed skewed upward, so that not so much as a fragment of star reached the two sorcerers on the ground.

'—'

In the next instant, everyone truly saw a star in the heavens flicker.
Falling from far beyond the faded sky, drawn in by some overwhelming gravity, was a shaft of starlight plunging in a straight line toward the earth—the hesitation before it lasted less than the blink of an eye.

"—!"

An especially fierce burst of violet lightning surged forth, and a chain of thunderous detonations carried the Dragonkin upward, into the sky where the star was falling.

'——!!'

The Divine Dragon's breath, the torrent it unleashed, blazed white-hot and seared through the sky—yet, turning its point neither toward the Dragonkin above nor the sorcerers below, it flew off toward some distant land.
Bursting violet lightning and the Dragon's roar mingled together, and the star, shattered at the far end of the sky, blazed brighter and brighter, casting a dazzling light across the faded heaven and earth—

"—Al Sextet."

The incantation shook the horizon, and the heavens themselves seemed to hold their breath.

Every mana forming the world was assigned one of six differing colored roles, and the powers set loose crossed and fused together. The interference that should ordinarily have collapsed in an instant instead birthed a single unified rhythm, and using the world itself, stripped of color, as its medium, formed a beautiful, unbroken circle.
Before long, the six-colored torrent converged into one, becoming a pillar that severed the boundary between heaven and earth.
Favoring none of the six colors, the pillar chose as its own hue only the polar light in which the very root that is "magic" had manifested itself, and the pillar of light reduced itself down to a power that swallowed everything whole.

—At the center of that pillar of light, the Divine Dragon's colossal form was caught fast.

Its resisting claws, its wings, its breath—all alike were swallowed by the void, and the Dragon's very power became fuel for the magic outright, the pillar of light growing thicker still, its brilliance ever stronger.
The light stretching skyward pierced the cloudless heavens, casting its pale glow over the far upper reaches of the atmosphere and beyond the horizon. In that instant, the very laws governing heaven and earth were, for a single moment, rewritten.

—It was an absolute miracle, one that not even the "First Sorcerer," nor even the "Witch of Greed," had ever achieved since the system called magic first began.

'—Ah.'

In a world stripped of sound, that sound had echoed far too faintly, far too small a note of praise for the event it accompanied.
But it was enough to reach certainty. —Here, the Divine Dragon's dragon halo had been shattered.
And then—

"—Seal-Shackle, Third Release."

In the very midst of a world where six attributes held their balance and became a pillar, splitting the sky and searing the earth, a single bolt of violet lightning, alone bearing color, pierced through that whirl of polar light and plummeted from the heavens.
Like a meteor iron falling to shatter a star, his body plunged in from the far reaches of the sky, wreathed in flashing lightning. Detonating the very air beneath his kicking feet, he made of himself a single supreme arrow meant to pierce one point and one point alone.

His target was the reverse scale—the single vital point etched into the Divine Dragon's throat, the one chink in an armor that boasted absolute defense. It was the last wall standing before the Heart Core of a Dragon that had been swallowed by the pillar of light and stripped of its dragon halo.

"My thanks. —Gratitude."

Like a comet trailing its tail, the violet lightning tore through the sound barrier and the protection of the dragon scales alike, and a fist slammed home.
The pillar's radiance and the torrent of violet lightning overlapped, the impact shaking the void, and amid a shattering roar, bands of light raced across the Dragon's entire body.
That all-out blow, joined to the very apex of an unprecedented miracle called magic, now drove straight through to the deepest depths of the Divine Dragon's Dragonhusk, and pierced the Heart Core slumbering there.

'—'

Pierced through by that decisive blow, the Divine Dragon, wings spread at the zenith of the sky, slowly turned its gaze down to the earth. There, recognizing a tiny, truly gasping presence, its claw sliced through empty air near its own head—as though, out of old habit, reaching to touch the metal fittings of a helmet it had thought it was still wearing.

'Tch, pathetic... After all that handicap I was given, I can't even blame it on the stars.'

So it cursed, bitterly, in words utterly mundane and utterly unlike the solemn dignity one might expect of a Divine Dragon, and the absolute presence that had ruled the heavens was drawn down by gravity, falling to the earth.
Trailing the flickering remnants of light behind it, splitting the void, it fell amid a deafening roar.

—And so it was decided, this reckoning with the Usurper who had stolen the Dragonhusk of the Divine Dragon Volcanica.
