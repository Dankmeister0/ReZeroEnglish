—A howling, body-slicing wind wrapped around the entire Everfrost.

"——"

Even bundled in heavy cold-weather gear, feeling the piercing chill of the air, Subaru forced open eyes that threatened to fog over with white breath, and looked ahead.
If the cold alone were the enemy, there would have been any number of ways to bundle up further. But the true foe to be faced here was not the biting wind or the snow.
It was a threat that, far more plainly, held a blade to their very lives.

"—Are you scared?"

Hearing a voice ask that, Subaru became aware of the soft feel of the hand beside him. The warmth felt in his left hand, never once let go, belonged to the finest partner there was—one who, however dire the straits, would never abandon him, made of nothing but trust and affection.

"Nah. There's also protectiveness, and cuteness, and passion, and just about every positive feeling you can cram in there... I don't have enough words to sing your praises."

"Fufun, naturally, I suppose. Those insufficient words of yours—much as I'd love to listen to as many as time allows... right now, there's a bit of an interloper, I suppose."

"Yeah. —Damn, I knew it was coming, but I really wish I could've been spared this."

Agreeing with his adorable partner's words, Subaru shrugged while his gaze stayed fixed ahead—trading light banter without ever letting his guard down for even a moment against the being standing there.
Honestly, no matter how much tension, how much vigilance he poured into it, it could never be called enough against this opponent.

"I still think it's questionable that this works as a yardstick, but... compared to how scary you are, Regulus is downright cute... nah, maybe not cute. More like a flea next to you."

"—I shall take that as a special evaluation from you, and accept it as such."

Catching the aside with hellishly good hearing and dutifully responding to it, the man closed one eye at him.
A relaxed stance, a magnificent bearing over a well-trained tall frame, eyes that showed a will pushed through with terrifying strength, and skin darkened as if faintly sun/snow-burned. Upon that skin faintly floated a strange tattoo resembling Sanskrit characters.
A man whose appearance screamed, at a glance, that he was no ordinary person—and indeed, he was nothing of the sort. At least, looking across the entire world as it stood now, there was likely no one who could beat him.

—People called him the "Mad Prince."
That was the formidable enemy they now faced, here on the Everfrost, where a frigid gale howled and swirled.

"Facing off, huh. ...Why is it we have to fight each other, I wonder."

"A question to yourself? If it's a genuine question, then I must question the very attitude of questioning it. As for the why, our lord and I have already exchanged more than enough words with the two of you. And upon that, we broke off. Hence, this. —For our part too, it is far from what we wished."

"Not what you wished? How's that, then?"

"We hold neither resentment nor hatred toward you. The reason our lord and you must face each other thus has nothing to do with mutual liking or loathing. Only—"

Saying that, the "Mad Prince" paused once, narrowed his eyes, and lowered his gaze slightly. From that, it was clear his gaze had shifted from Subaru to his partner beside him.
In that instant, a thick, viscous, ineradicable hatred leaked out from him.

That was, without a doubt, an emotion he never directed at Subaru, the one he faced now. And it was, on top of that, precisely the reason he and Subaru could never see eye to eye.
Letting that undisguised hatred pour out freely, the "Mad Prince" declared:

"—Over that filth, our lord and you cannot see eye to eye."

"First off, I flat-out can't understand the aesthetic sense that lets you look at my ridiculously cute partner and call her filth. That's why you and I can't be friends."

"Most, most regrettable. Regrettable, and so regrettable I cannot help it. Regretfully, though, we shall not withdraw our hand."

Slowly shaking his head from side to side, the "Mad Prince" sighed as if lamenting his failed persuasion. But if that was meant as an attempt at persuasion, the gulf in their values would never be bridged.

"Truly a man impossible to talk to, I suppose. ...Cecilus was still more conversable, I daresay."

"Comparing him to that is unfair to Cess. Cess could talk just fine, actually... it's just that somehow, the conclusion of the conversation would land wherever he liked. Kind of like the logic of the strong."

"But we cannot let that slide, I suppose. —You understand that much, don't you?"

"—Yeah, no need to say it. Though hearing it does fire me up more."

Squeezing his joined hand a little tighter, they exchanged a glance and traded smiles.
Before the clash with a formidable foe, their sense of solidarity as partners was more than enough. —And then, the reason they had chosen this place as the battlefield, the reason they'd spent so long in conversation, arrived without warning.
It was a prism of eternal ice, glimpsed glinting for just an instant at the edge of their vision—

"「—E·M·M!!」"

In that instant, the overlapping chanted voices activated the spell, and an absolute defensive magic wrapped around Subaru and his partner, nullifying all interference by shifting their existence half a step out of this world.
Passing straight through their now-blurred, unreal bodies and out the other side was a white light that had been trying to blast mercilessly through their knees. Had they not blocked it, standing there side by side, they would have both had their legs shot through together, rendered unfit for combat.

And that same light had, likewise, been aimed at the "Mad Prince" as well—but—

"—Full power? Or do you wish to anger us? If this is provocation, you should expect to pay for it, though. It is us who shall make you pay, and you who shall pay."

So saying, the "Mad Prince" caught the white light between two raised fingers, and stopped it cold.
The light slowly lost its brilliance at the man's fingertips, and eventually revealed the body of a single long needle, which then, too, scattered and dispersed into mist. —A needle constructed of magic, fired at ultra-high speed: this attack was called—

"Hell's Snipe... which means—"

"—You're that person again? You never learn. It's the same no matter how many times you come."

"——"

Startled by that voice suddenly falling from above, Subaru caught his breath without meaning to.
The wind was still howling as fiercely as ever. And yet, somehow—why, even amid such wind, could this voice shake his eardrums so clearly?
Or perhaps the logic of the strong held power even to bend and force obedience out of the very laws of nature.
If that were true, then it stood to reason that the voice could slice through the blizzard and reach him.
For, appearing serenely in the sky over this Everfrost, was—

"—It has been a while, 'Witch of Melancholy.' Are you keeping well?"

"Would you stop talking to me like we're acquainted? I'm not your friend, or a casual acquaintance, or anything at all. Whether I'm doing well or not is none of your business."

"Not entirely so, and not entirely otherwise. After all, at present, you and this fellow here are both certain obstacles to the achievement of our purpose. Should there be a season of poor health, we would very much like to take advantage of it."

"Oh, is that so? Then if I spread a lie that I'm in terrible shape, will you come flying in believing it? Wow, how great. Go die."

Clapping her hands lightly before her chest, the woman floating in that frigid sky laughed cruelly.

"——"

She was a beautiful woman.
Her long, reddish-brown hair was tied up with a ribbon, and she wore a dress with a peculiar pattern mixing several colors—pink, pale blue, and gold. Her outfit, exposing white, slender shoulders, was unbelievably light for the frigid Everfrost, but for a woman whose very breath did not fog white, it seemed to pose no hindrance at all.
Just as the roar of the blizzard could not drown out the beautiful melody that slipped from her lips, neither heat nor cold nor foul weather could stand in the way of what she wanted to do, or wear.

"—She's here, I suppose."

That call drew Subaru's attention back from the moment when he'd stiffened, holding his breath.
His partner, gazing up at the same figure and surely having reached the same feelings, was nevertheless holding back her own overflowing emotions out of consideration for his. Not thinking that being considered for was pathetic, nor that it was something to apologize for—only, resolving not to squander such thoughtfulness carelessly, Subaru drew a deep breath.
And then—

"—We finally meet again, ■■■!"

"——"

He called out, raising his voice, to the woman beyond the whitened, misted wind. Unfortunately, unlike her and the "Mad Prince," he had no technique to bend the wind to his will.
And besides, there was no way in hell he was going to repeat himself just because a voice he'd spent so long building up to this moment hadn't reached her—that would be way too uncool.

Just how long had he waited for this moment?
How much helplessness had he swallowed down, how much had he clawed his way over, to arrive here?

Seeking the culmination of all of it, Subaru drew a breath—and Natsuki Subaru gazed at ■■■.
Gazing at her, he said:

"I'm taking you back, ■■■. —Bring it on, Fate!!"

That was the culmination of the long time it had taken Natsuki Subaru to set foot on this frigid land, and a declaration settling the accounts of the days in which he had decided to give everything.

—Dark. Dark. Dark. He drifted on and on in a dark abyss.

Hazy as a doze, uncertain as eternity, scattered as a mire.
He drifted through a haze, an uncertainty, a scatteredness, an unending darkness—like remaining forever on the border between the pages of dream and reality in a single book.

Just how long had he been like this?
Just how much longer would he go on being like this?
Just how far did this endlessly continuing instant stretch on?

Even such natural thoughts, questions, propositions as should exist as a matter of course—the darkness stretched them thin.
Stretched that thin, they became like flimsy sheets of paper, tearing easily at the slightest firm poke, their seams becoming indistinguishable, until eventually they strayed from their original shape.

Surely, he had repeated this many times already.
Scraping together his scattering consciousness, gathering it, stitching it together, only to have it crushed easily by the smallest exertion of force, scattered again into fragments and dust.

He might as well stop trying to hold together something broken beyond counting. —And yet, he could not do that.

—Dark. Dark. Dark. He kept drifting on in a dark abyss.

Hazy as a doze, uncertain as eternity, scattered as a mire—but he kept drifting on in a darkness that was, without question, never solitary.

Not alone. He would not become alone.
The reason that he was not alone held back the drawn-out instant, the repeating failures, the ever-drifting darkness, and would not permit them to vanish into nothing.

Even with his self-definition incomplete, even with the grounds for not being alone shrouded in black fog, still, he could not throw it away, let it go, or give up.

Giving up—he could not. —Because giving up did not suit him.

So—

"——"

The instant his field of vision opened and he was flung out into the outside world, his mind was thrown into a grand tumult by the flood of information pressing in, and his body writhed violently.

"—!"

His vision flickered chaotically, red and black contrasts violating the world alternately.
His outstretched limbs went rigid with strain, and the unconscious release of the brakes on his overworked body cramped, terrifyingly, every muscle of every joint in order, from fingertip to root.
Pain—pain reaching every corner of body and mind—seized both body and soul and would not let go.

"It's burned clean through, I say! And then, uh-oh-oh?! Is this all right, I wonder?! Is this the state of a poor little fish washed up on the shore, I dare say?!"

"Silence, Rumela. However outlandishly adorable your appearance may be, I do not recall giving you leave to shriek in my ear. Besides, this is only the natural outcome, is it not."

"Natural?! Your Excellency, did you just say natural?! Did you know this would happen?!"

"My 'Sun Sword' burns what it wishes to burn, and cuts what it wishes to cut. That said, since this was not brought forth by the proper means, one cannot deny that some inconvenience should work against us, can one."

"...Did you happen to mention that beforehand, I wonder?"

"Need one say something so obvious? All of you are, every last one, older than even I, who am wise despite my youth. Or did the lot of you skive off on living your lives?"

Sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch. Swallowed in a muddy torrent of information caught by all five senses, forced into a churning of consciousness, such voices flew back and forth overhead.
That voice, too, was new information. Which is to say, one edge of the swelling torrent.

"Bad, bad, bad! If he dies like this, years of hard work go up in smoke, don't they?! After running all over creation, grinding our foreheads into the dirt over and over, we finally managed to pull it off, and... ugh, I won't be able to show my face to anyone!"

"This isn't the time to talk, my lady! In cases like this... yes, drastic measures! A little pain is fine if the result follows—elegant, even, in its way! Surely our lord would smile and say we did well!"

"That's it! Can you do it, Rumela-san?!"

"Absolutely impossible, I dare say! I am, blazing as I burn, a fire attribute through and through! All that flame can do is the destruction half of destruction-and-rebirth, I daresay! Ohohohoho!"

"Isn't this an absolute total waste of time right now, though?!"

Wah-wah, information stood out even amid the flood and torrent, uproarious, flying left and right.
While sensing that an exchange straddling him was resounding thunderously, Subaru, amid the confusion of body and mind's pain, slowly obtained a fresh piece of threatening information—a sensation of suffocation.

"—Oh"

His stomach wall convulsed, and the tube serving as the airway was on the verge of being blocked by internal contraction.
It was a tragedy brought on by unnatural peristalsis in the body, but what waited at the end of it was a comedy no one could laugh at. His body, already weakened to the extreme by exhaustion, was losing function due to respiratory failure.
And yet, amid the disturbance of countless pieces of information, no method to deal with it could be found.

"You lot, is it fine to keep making noise like that? This one is on the verge of dying, is he not. As he is a man I have no acquaintance with, his death would trouble me not at all, but the same is hardly true for you, is it not."

"No, that's really, truly true, but we've kind of thrown almost all our resources in already too, so we don't have room to spare! What about putting him back in that sphere from before, would that work, no, forget it, right, yeah!"

"Resolved that on your own at high speed, did you. An amusing man. —Hmm, indeed. Even with my radiance, there is nothing to be done for a half-dead clown, but... this one is different."

"What are you planning to do?" 

"Fool, why do you wear such a face of indifference, 'Cloud Dragon'? —Never mind that man, but there is something to be done for this dear girl here, is there not."

Unable to cope, the suffering piled up, and piled up, and piled up further.
Everything was painted over by that suffering, that pain, that convulsion, and the instant that should have been stretched into eternity was capped by a thoroughgoing end.
Was this—

"——"

—Was this the end, then?

"—Subaru!"

In that instant, another piece of information struck the consciousness that had been shutting into darkness. But this time, it was not something that only prolonged pain and suffering.
It was something that wiped away pain with pain, suffering with suffering, information with information.
And then—

"You mustn't die, I suppose. You mustn't die, I say...!"

And that information, that suffering, that pain, was something dear and familiar to him.

"—Ah"

His convulsing stomach wall, his half-closed throat, his tangled tongue, his flickering vision, his consciousness on the verge of being swallowed by the instant, the life about to scatter into the dark beyond—all halted.

Let go of nothing. Throw away nothing. Cling to it, to the world.
Only by doing that, only by being able to do that, could he—

"O, e, o"

"Subaru!"

The flickering, eyesore-red-and-black light vanished, and he saw tearful eyes lit from behind by backlight. Eyes with the distinctive pattern shaped like a butterfly, a beloved voice calling out his name, a great presence in a small body holding back his scattering existence with all its might.

"E-a, ko"

"—That's it, that's it, I suppose. Is it? All right, I suppose. It's all right. I'm right here, I suppose."

With a body that would not obey him anywhere, with a heart made fragile and numb and ragged, still, in order to prove himself, still, in order to convey that he remembered her, he spun the words.
She received them. Yes, like walking across a bridge of trust, eyes closed, trusting each step.

"Whatever's going on, Betty and you can pull through, Subaru."

Clutching that reassuring, heartening declaration close, his consciousness cut out.


—That was what happened on the first day of Natsuki Subaru's resumed journey.

When his eyelids faintly opened, the first thing that leapt into view was an unfamiliar ceiling.

"—Oah"

He tried to say just that as he saw it, but his gummed-up, parched mouth and his throat, wrecked to a ridiculous degree, would not produce a proper sound—all he managed was an ugly groan.
Like the death cry of a squashed frog.
But—

"—Awake, are we, I suppose. Quite the sleepyhead, and a bad child at that, I say."

That was more than sufficient to convey it to the one who had been waiting at his bedside for that awakening.
A small hand gently reached out and tickled his forehead along with his bangs, and Subaru, narrowing his eyes at the ticklishness, turned to look—finding a girl with softly downturned eyes: Beatrice.

An unchanged appearance, an unchanged gaze, her unchanged warmth—there, right there.
Without a doubt, without a single hair's difference, Beatrice, exactly as she was burned into his mind.

"E-a, ko"

"That's right, it's certainly Betty, I suppose. No need to worry, so don't do anything reckless, I say. Right now Subaru's mind and body's wiring is all a mess, and is unfree in the extreme, I suppose."

"Ha, au"

It wasn't that he'd been trying to do something so reckless as to be called reckless.
He'd only wanted to raise his body, sweep Beatrice up in his arms on a rush of overflowing emotion, and dash straight out of the room, then spin around and around together in some wide-open space.
But apparently even that was too much to ask for the current Subaru. —The mind and body's wiring, as Beatrice put it that way.

"—Oh"

In fact, it seemed to be true that Subaru's brain had forgotten how to move his body.
The edge of his unmoving eyeballs barely managed to reflect Beatrice, letting him catch her—but not even his eyeballs, let alone his fingers, would roll the way Subaru wished. Raising his body was out of the question; even managing to breathe at all felt like a miracle.
He supposed he should be grateful his heart hadn't forgotten how to beat.

"It's surprisingly no laughing matter, I suppose. While you were shut up in that thing, Betty was fine, but Subaru's vital functions had all but stopped, I suppose. To the point where your body forgot even the most basic things, I say."

"—Ah"

"That space... it was woven with a kind of dark magic Betty's never seen, an absolutely outrageous thing, I suppose. Without question, it had Mother's hand in it. Why that man had mastered a forbidden art of Mother's making, the mystery is endless, I suppose."

"—I"

"Betty has no intention of arguing about that with Subaru, I suppose. Whatever Subaru might say, Mother is Mother to Betty, an irreplaceable person, I suppose. Hmph, I say."

Slipping in were only Subaru's hoarse, faltering interjections.
And yet Beatrice, receiving them, made the conversation work with astounding comprehension, and on top of that, still touching Subaru's forehead, she puffed her cheeks in dissatisfaction and turned her face away with a "hmph."
That figure of hers, unable to lean fully into either affection or displeasure, was excessively endearing, but—

"...Is that really a conversation that's actually working? To me it looks like Beatrice-chan's just having her own little ups and downs on her own."

The voice that suddenly reached him belonged to neither Subaru nor Beatrice—a third party.
In his field of view, Beatrice went "hm" and turned her eyes toward Subaru's feet, so the speaker seemed to be standing there. Unfortunately, Subaru had no way to look that direction himself.
To the speaker outside his field of view, Beatrice, cheeks still puffed, said,

"That's a rude thing to say, I suppose. In the first place, the bond between Subaru and Betty is infinite... whatever Subaru wants to say, Betty can tell just by looking into his eyes, I suppose."

"I see, I see, I suppose that's what you'd call the bond between a spirit-user and their contracted spirit? I'm fairly close to some people connected to spirits, but that part's hard for an outsider to grasp intuitively."

"Others aside, Betty and Subaru's relationship is one of a kind, I suppose. Well, every contracted spirit probably thinks that of their own, so it's for your own good not to speak as though you understand, I suppose."

"Yeah, thanks for the advice. And, well, is it about time I got to say hello too?"

Saying that, it seemed the person beyond his feet was asking Beatrice's permission to greet him.
Consulted, Beatrice let out a small breath, and gazed at Subaru with her large eyes. To her checking whether it was fine to let him greet Subaru, Subaru nodded in spirit.

"—U"

"Can't be helped, so I'll let him gaze upon your mug. Be grateful, apparently, I suppose."

"Well, that's quite the honor! You really do have a smart mouth on you, just like I heard, jeez."

Saying that, footsteps thudded across the floor as the owner of the voice approached.
Somehow, the way of speaking felt familiar—but the voice itself was one he had no memory of. Subaru could do nothing but greet the approaching stranger, quite literally like a fish on the chopping block.
As Beatrice slowly drew back, in the space secured in his field of view, a face popped in, cutting into view.

It was the face of an "unfamiliar" mild-mannered man, dressed mostly in green with a large hat, hair a slightly unruly gray, and a face with an androgynous air about it—

"Hey there, nice to meet you, Natsuki Subaru-san. —My name is Nikola Suwen. I go by various titles, but merchant probably has the best ring to it."

The mild-mannered man with downturned eyes—Nikola Suwen—introduced himself as such.
Hearing the family name Suwen, so familiar to his ears, spoken by this man, Subaru's eyes went wide. But as if to fuel that shock and confusion further, he did not stop.

"I imagine you're surprised, this being so sudden, but while your body's barely able to move, I'll tell you something even more shocking, cruel as it is. —Since you were sealed by the hand of the 'traitor' Aldebaran, roughly four hundred years have passed until today."

"—. ——. ————ah?"

"Four hundred years. Do you understand? You—no, you and Beatrice-chan woke up four hundred years after the era you lived in. Also, let me tell you this too."

Comprehension simply could not catch up.
Swept along and swallowed not by the quantity of information but by its quality, Subaru sat before the mild-mannered man—the one who had caused such an outrageous torrent, who shared the same traits as a friend he knew well—as Nikola continued.
That was—

"—However you look at it, you've kept us waiting way too long, Natsuki-san."

Four hundred years overdue, delivered across the span of countless lives, the exasperated scolding of a friend.

"Truly, it was a long, long history of hardship. Quite literally the great undertaking handed down through generations of the Suwen family—the recovery of you both, sunk into the depths of the Grand Mogolade Geyser."

Saying so, the mild-mannered man—Nikola—twirled his removed hat in both hands, letting out a deep sigh as if recalling all the hardship up to now.
In the way he spoke of the burden he'd carried, in the air of "it couldn't be helped" about his manner, Subaru felt the common thread between Nikola and his friend—Otto Suwen—and his chest tightened.
They were alike. Alike to the point that trying to deny it over and over only made it more hollow.

But if he admitted that, he would have to accept it.
That the situation Natsuki Subaru now found himself in was not a dream or an illusion, but reality.

"Nikola, I understand you're excited, and I can't thank you enough. But Subaru's in no state to be doing more than just recovering, I suppose. Actually, just seeing your face and hearing your name was enough to make him faint. Recklessness is forbidden, I suppose."

"No, I get it, you know? I get it, but on my end there's been a whole lot of holding back too, or, I mean, no end to my patience... I got pulled back at the salvage site, at the first meeting too, so when am I going to actually get to feel that the Suwen family's four-hundred-year wish has come true?!"

"If it's a feeling you want, Betty's given you plenty already, I suppose. Or is being flung here and there through 'Door-Crossing' still not enough for you?"

"Would you please stop saying it like I asked for that to be done to me?!"

The sight of Nikola, eyes wide open, lamenting, was truly the spitting image of Otto.
But Nikola was slightly younger than Otto, and there was a slenderness to him too. His hair was longer than Otto's as well, tied up around the middle of his back, giving him a look more like a scholar than a merchant.
Differences in detail, and points in common—Nikola Suwen was, without question, exactly what he claimed to be—

"A quick one to catch on, aren't you, I suppose. Fitting, that you're Otto's descendant."

"I feel like I should honestly sympathize with whatever kind of relationship my ancestor had with you two. Well, I guess it's the kind of familiarity that comes from being close, isn't it."

"—I suppose. He was a good man, and a capable one, I daresay. To think he'd go so far as to have his descendants dig us out—it's almost overdoing it, I suppose."

That felt less like an episode symbolizing competence than one symbolizing tenacity.
Certainly, Otto had been active with an ability so indispensable to their group that it hardly seemed like something a chance addition should have provided—but what truly made Otto Otto was not his skill at domestic administration or the power of his "Divine Protection of Elocution," but his innate guts, quick wit, and sheer tenacity.

A persistence that spared no effort as long as it served the goal—that was Otto Suwen's greatest weapon, and in the end, he'd used it even on his own descendant Nikola and the others, and accomplished this.

Truly, an amazing man. —And yet, there was no longer any chance to praise him for it directly.
No more talking, no more laughing together—not anymore.

"—U"

"Subaru..."

Beatrice, noticing Subaru holding his breath as tears welled up mercilessly, blurring his vision, gently wiped away the droplets with a handkerchief.
While the sorrow was wiped away by that kindness of Beatrice's, Subaru sobbed.

He acknowledged it. He had no choice but to acknowledge it.
The cruel flow of time, and the outcome that was their own defeat.

"——"

Slowly gathering together his scattered consciousness, Natsuki Subaru's last memory before waking was a scene at Pleiades Watchtower, surrounded by the shelves of the Book of the Dead. —The moment when, betrayed by Al, who had worried over him, he and Beatrice together had been sealed by that forbidden art, by Al's own hand.

And since then, Subaru and Beatrice had remained sealed, spending four hundred years, and had awakened now, in this era. —No longer in this era, where no one from that time remained.

"—I"

He couldn't hold back the tears and sobs spilling out.
Purely, single-mindedly, he was so pathetic he wanted to die.

Wretched, miserable, unsightly, dumb enough to want to die. A single mistake could bring about something irreversible. He should have known that. Priscilla's death, if nothing else, should have taught Subaru that.

And yet, Natsuki Subaru had messed up.
Messed up, and left everything behind, cast aside every duty and mission, arriving at the present.

"—!"

He wanted to die. Just die.
That was not for reasons of misery or helplessness alone—it was a "death" worth striving for as a practical gain. If he used "Return by Death" now, he might be able to go back. Back to four hundred years ago.
However—

"——"

Subaru's body would not move. He couldn't even manage to die by his own hand.
Even setting that aside, Beatrice, who watched over him without leaving his side for a moment, could never allow Subaru's decision to wish for "death."

He couldn't die. He simply could not die.
Even "death" itself had, by now, slipped through Natsuki Subaru's fingers.

"Betty well understands the urge to cry, I suppose. Cry all you like, cry a great deal, Subaru. No matter how many tears you shed, Betty will wipe every one away, I suppose."

Beatrice's gentle hand wiped away the tears that kept overflowing from Subaru.
Unable even to grasp hope drowning in tears, and yet, simultaneously experiencing the sensation of being saved by Beatrice's consideration, Subaru's heart was thrown into chaos by the contrast between hope and despair.

If he alone had been cast out into this outrageous future, without question Natsuki Subaru's heart would have had no choice but to break and rot away.
But if Beatrice had not been here, Subaru might have chosen to end his own life. Of course, however that turned out, with his body unable to move as it was now, that was nothing more than a possibility he could not even choose.

"——"

Hope and despair, life and death, past and future violated Natsuki Subaru's soul.
There was nothing to be done. Nothing could be done. There was no way for anything to be done.
And yet, into that deadlocked despair, in one single point—

"—Ah"

In one single point, the soul being trampled harbored a suspicion that carried heat.
That was—

The qualification for becoming the "Witch of Melancholy" had stripped away so many things from ■■■. ■■■ couldn't even wish, on her own, to grasp at that possibility, to obtain it.
But—

"It's all right, ■■■. Subaru's a genius who goes around considering other people's feelings without being asked, imagining other people's hearts without being asked, and trying to make people happy without being asked, I suppose."

"That's how he wore Bea-ko down too. ■■■, you're next on the menu."

"—!"

Gently lifting the body he held, Subaru looked closely at her startled face. In those eyes, contrary to the emotion welling up, no tears showed.
Even the right to shed tears in front of someone was no longer in her hands. What remained to her—what possibility, what hope, what future was left?
At the very least, though, the qualification to look back at him with reddened cheeks and moist eyes—that, she had not given up.

"Doesn't make sense. All of you, none of it makes sense."

In a trembling voice, the "Witch of Melancholy," now caught by a mere human, let that spill out.
Any calamity, any pain, any hardship—the "Witch" who could turn it all into a mere instant through "Compression" trembled her lips, as though praying that this one moment, she never wanted to lose, never wanted to let go of.

"It doesn't make sense, and yet... and yet"

The "Witch of Melancholy," the "Witch," ■■■, smiled through moist eyes.
As if confirming, for herself, the emotion welling up inside her, the one she hadn't let go of.

"—I think... I love you. Isn't that weird."

"Nah? Not even a little."
"I suppose."

Had she been braced for rejection?
The love she'd spoken—received without a moment's hesitation—left ■■■ wide-eyed. On that face, utterly unlike a "Witch," looking like nothing more than an ordinary village girl, a smile broke through.

"Though I guess that's too pretty a face for a village girl."

"...You probably say that to everyone."

"Even if I did, only to a village girl too pretty to be a village girl. I don't think there's many people that applies to. As far as I know, there's exactly one."

"I see. ...Well, fine then."

Hearing Subaru's answer, ■■■ turned her face away in a sulky manner. Finding that endearing, Subaru let out a big breath, and,

"Hey, can I put you down? My back's kind of in trouble."

"Are you saying I'm heavy?"

"No, not that! ■■■'s not heavy at all, more like a puff of cotton! But I'm a sixty-year-old geezer! Not quite decrepit, but generously speaking, still pretty creaky!"

"Hmph... understood."

With her consent secured, he slowly set ■■■ down on the spot. Right then, Beatrice, rushing over, promptly settled herself into Subaru's arms instead.
Seeing that, ■■■'s eyes turned sharp once more,

"I'm not okay, but she is?"

"Just look at her, ■■■. Our Bea-ko's a spirit, genuine cotton fluff."

"Cotton fluff, I suppose."

"Perfectly in sync... never mind, forget it."

Turning away from Subaru and Beatrice, ■■■ began walking slowly, stroking the back of the small Scarlet Scorpion off to the side, muttering "traitor" at it quietly.
Then, even amid this exchange, she glanced over at the still-ongoing legendary-tier fierce battle,

"It's gotten pretty dangerous over there before you even noticed, but you want me to help, don't you?"

"Yeah. I want to keep Vague quiet around here for a good long while. And after that too, there's a lot I want your help with..."

"So you want to just conveniently use me? The type who doesn't feed the fish once you've caught it?"

"I'm the type who wants to say 'I love you' as many days as possible."

"Right. ...Guess I won't get my hopes up, then."

Waving her hand, her reddish-brown hair and large ribbon on her back swaying, ■■■, along with the Scarlet Scorpion, made to dive into the battlefield.
Watching that familiar-looking, memory-stirring ribbon, Subaru narrowed his eyes,

"—Pe■■!"

"——"

"There's another person I love, deep in this glacier here—can you help me dig her out later too?!"

"Drop dead, idiot."

However much of that was genuine, ■■■, saying that, stuck out her tongue and headed for the battlefield.
At that, Subaru scratched his head and let out a breath. Then, from within his arms, Beatrice poked his solar plexus with her fist,

"Even trying to relax someone, you're terrible at it, I suppose."

"Guess so. My prized banter's a little behind the times now, huh?"

"Can't say, I suppose. But..."

"—?"

"We got to talk with ■■■. Everything starts here, I suppose. We can't let it go to waste, I suppose."

At Beatrice's words, carrying the heat of hope, Subaru held his breath, and nodded.
Yes, that's right. Everything starts here, from here.

Subaru and the others had, at last, only just now reached the starting line.
Across four hundred years, and forty years spent getting there, finally, they'd reached the starting line of the present world.

"Betty and the others will join the fight too, I suppose. Subaru, are you ready?"

"Of course! Forty years' worth of matured teamwork, and the twelve original spells we've devised—time to let them roar!"

"Roar, I suppose!"

Raising a spirited shout, Subaru and Beatrice dashed headlong for the battlefield.
Amid roaring thunder wrapped in violet lightning, flames raging as though heralding the end of the world, a diamond-hard storm rampaging as though the very earth itself had gone wild, and the punishing rain of a "Dragon," freely dropping hundreds of tons of cloud—into that battle zone where the earth's most fearsome monster and the "Witch of Melancholy," leading her witchbeast, clashed—

there, a sixty-some-year-old geezer, holding a small, adorable spirit, plunged in.
That, too, was without doubt a challenge risking his life—and yet—

"Compared to that four-hundred-year emptiness, giving my life is nothing to be afraid of—!!"

He ran, and ran, and ran, and kept running.
Not in order to leave behind what he'd left behind, not to abandon what he'd let go of.

He'd been entrusted with it. Handed it. Been hoped for, prayed for, wished for.
Together with everyone who had truly been there, he ran through this irreplaceable now.

Even if, someday, it might not turn out to be the ideal future he'd wished for—

—Natsuki Subaru lives on, in this other world, with the comrades he loves.


△▼△▼△▼△


—God, Buddha, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that, for as long as I live, I will never forget having loved.


"Devoting an Otherworldly Life, from Zero" — Fin


So there you have it, this time Arc 9's IF! A branch off from the defeat route!
As for specifically what happened and how it led where it did, the branching factors are too strong to lay out the fine details, but broadly speaking, this is meant as one possible outcome of the choices made by those who were there in that scene.
As you can probably tell, this is a story for the sake of the "girl" who ended up vanishing at the end of Arc 9, and also an after-story for those who caused her to become that.
Timeline-wise, it deals with characters the main series probably won't get to handle, and it was quite interesting to write. It was something vaguely in my head, and this is what it turned into once I let it expand.

Anyway, sorry to keep you waiting, but thank you for another year! Thanks to you all, the Re:Zero anime's fourth season is starting this spring... well, actually, within a week, so please look forward to and support that as well!
