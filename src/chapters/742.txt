What Felt, twisting her cheek in a grin, saw was a small figure at the edge of her vision — Camberley.
Unlike Gaston, who'd been in the fight from the start, or Rachins, who joined partway through, Camberley entered the fray at just the critical moment, having waited for her chance. —And it was at that exact instant that the horrifically painful attack from before got unleashed. That's not just bad luck, that's something else.
But—

"—No luck, but the best."

Camberley, tears streaming from the corners of her eyes, held something in her hands, and Felt snatches it away — the "Star Staff" that could become their camp's trump card.

She'll fire it off. With everything Felt's got, gathering every ounce of strength in her body, throwing it all in without holding anything back.
The rest is—

"Where the hell do I aim it!"

△▼△▼△▼△

*— God, Buddha, Lord Od Lagna. I swear that for my whole life, I will never step on the shadow of someone walking beside me.*

△▼△▼△▼△

"—Aaaaagh! Strohonger!!"
"'Live, damn it—!!'"

A scream, a roar so intense it's no longer clear which is the Witchbeast's howl and which is human, thunders through the air, and the Black Serpent's serpentine body — like a thousand disasters bundled into one — is pushed back by a blast erupting from directly below.
Torrents of light and darkness rage across the battlefield, and a shockwave that seems to split the heavens sears Roy's entire body with shock.

What happened. How was it done. —All of that, he sets aside, unasked.

"Petra-chaaan!"

Who did it — that alone was certain, and Roy Alphard reached the peak of ecstasy.
It's amazing. It deserves applause. Along with the countless "memories" inside Roy, he has to send her a thunderous round of applause.

Just how much would one have to sacrifice of oneself to make the "Melancholy" Witch Factor do that.
Imagining it, speculating on it — unless one gave up absolutely everything, every scrap of an ordinary person's happiness, could this battlefield, this fierce a contest, this degree of outrageous defiance, even be possible?

—I want to be happy.

That should be humanity's absolute, unshakeable, indivisible wish.
It should be an absolute prayer that holds true even for Roy — no, even for Lye, even for Rui, even for the Sin Archbishops, even for villains, even for criminals.
It's what people live for. But to give that up, in order to reach this situation—

"——"

Roy blinks once, drying his eyes moist with elation, and looks at Felt snatching the white staff from her ally.
At this last stand, a member of the Dwarf Tribe, who seems like she'd contribute almost nothing to the fight, has gone to the trouble of bringing this along — it has to be a secret weapon held in reserve.
In other words—

"If that doesn't work, I might just cry!?"

Rainbow-colored butterflies dance through the air, spears of flame and ice rain down, blood scythes launch from all four directions, a compression begins that crushes not left and right but front and back, and a boyish soprano sings a cursed song.

"Protect Felt!!"

Wind cuts down every last butterfly; bullets of ice and flame shatter the spears of flame and ice; thick muscle takes the blood scythe head-on even as it's cut; an instant of double strikes pierces the earth surging from front and back; and the towering builds of an old man and a Pig Human hold the line against the cursed song that would collapse flesh and blood.

"Well done! But—"

The wave attack blocked by their ironclad coordination — but Roy doesn't buckle.
Up to now, he'd held back on using it — "Teleport" activates, and in an instant Roy's figure appears right in the middle of Felt's group. His target isn't Felt — but

"These two matter just as much, don't they?"

Among those who stood to intercept him, he aims at the backs of the two girls with no strength left even to stand.
The "Melancholic Witch" and the "Witchbeast Tamer" — both indispensable forces for keeping the fight against "Gluttony" and the "Black Serpent" in check on their side; no one can get a hand there in time against Roy standing behind the two of them.
Roy licks his lips, summons "Fist King" in his right hand and "Snow Vallum" in his left, gently—

—In that instant, Meili's hair writhes, and a flash of white light shoots through Roy's left eye.

"Gyaugh!?"

Pierced by a sensation of being seared with heat, Roy's vision in his left eye is lost. What is this — as Roy bulges the eye he has left, in his field of vision, reflected in the deft stroke that ruined his left eye, is a small scorpion lurking in Meili's hair.
An existence he doesn't know. A trump card, a hidden card not in "memory," viva total war.

"Even an ally this small—"

*Doing her best so earnestly,* he tried to continue, but couldn't.
Before he could, the ground he was standing on betrayed him, and without warning launched him into the sky.

"——"

Not magic. He felt no wavelength of Mana.
Then, the "Melancholic Witch" again? —No, even she doesn't have eyes on her back.
What occurred was an upheaval of the earth. Without warning, without any signal, that bounced him.

"—Damn, you're way too reliable, my comrade."

What he suddenly heard was a sharp voice with far too many things in "memory" that rang a bell.
A voice that, through the soles of his feet, resonates with the earth, becoming an ironclad wall unbeaten by anyone as long as his feet are on the ground — precisely because he's too dependable, a voice that should never have been heard.

Spinning through the air, Roy sees the ground reflected upside-down in his vision, and there he works to provoke the diseased Witchbeast writhing below into a frenzy.
Unlike before, distance now separates him and Roy. His blood armor is nearly stripped away, but if filth is scattered like rain, the prey's hands will be tied up dealing with it.

"Siiit!!"

A cry, almost tearful, disrupted the Black Serpent's movement for an instant.
But that's a span of time not even long enough to blink. Even with that instant stolen from him, Roy's plan wouldn't go astray — in that split second, the serpentine body of the Witchbeast, about to stretch toward the sky, bursts.
It bursts, spraying out filth, and onto that filthy ground, a single figure tumbles out.

"Bwahh, cough, gohh-gah..."

"No wayyy."

Choking, collapsed into the most repulsive pool of filth in this world, a red-haired man — struck by a shock that can't be explained by mere tenacity or grim survival — leaves Roy speechless.
Why is he alive. The question, along with a surge of appetite, makes Roy's stomach growl involuntarily.

"Yo, look this way."

With a light leap, the blond boy who'd caught up to Roy in midair gnashes his sharp fangs, and raises high, with exaggerated motion, a fist fitted with a silver gauntlet.
If that lands, Roy, in his half-dead state, wouldn't stand a chance.
Even knowing it's nothing more than a momentary stalling tactic, Roy, choosing to retreat, activates "Lunar Eclipse" once more to borrow the "Leaper's" extraordinary power, hoping to rely on it again—

"Bu—"

—His concentration is broken by the collision of a Zodda Bug diving in from his blind spot on the left.

"——"

Drawn by the irritating buzz of wings and the unpleasant sensation, his gaze is pulled, and far off, dozens of meters away, leaning against the trees, there's a pale-blue Earth Dragon, and a man — supposedly empty — resting his body against it.
The man points a finger this way, and noticing their eyes meet, smiles. As if he'd pulled it off.

Immediately after, the wind howls, and a fist wrapped in wind pressure is launched straight at Roy.
It seems oddly slow, as if there's time before it arrives — and then he realizes.

"This — is Petra-chan doing this?"

"Yep. Wanted to say it properly at the end. Serves you right."

The "Melancholic Witch" standing on the ground declares victory over Roy, within stretched-out time.
Against that, it seems there's nothing more Roy can do. And besides, the fist of an enraged tiger is closing the distance — down to five coins' worth.
In the time before that direct hit, what can he do. He thinks, and it strikes him.

"Amazing, truly amazing, Petra-chan. But you know, wasn't what you sacrificed to win a little too big?"

"Sour grapes? Nobody on our side got taken down. Pretty pathetic for a Sin Archbishop."

"No match for the 'Melancholic Witch,' that's for sure. But sacrifice isn't only about life. It's things like the future, hope, possibility — that kind of thing. From here on, we're genuinely worried Petra-chan's long, long life is going to turn out a lonely one."

Coins, four remaining.
At the very least, he'll dig up the price she surely wants hidden, and carve himself into her with that pain.
At that wicked feeding of Roy's, Petra blinks, her face going blank with surprise. Surely she wasn't paying the price without knowing what it was — that reaction is—

"—Could it be, you've got the wrong idea?"

"Wrong idea?"

"That's funny. I figured Otto-san would've said something like that. Like — this time the enemy is the whole world, or something."

"——"

Coins, three remaining.
He'd never said it directly. But that kind of resolve had been present in "memory."
Given what Aldebaran did, and the damage he caused, that would be a fair enough perception. But it should be no more, and no less, than that.

"Don't tell me..."

"That's the one."

Coins, two remaining.
In the midst of stretched-out sensation, catching his breath is a luxury he can't afford, but Roy holds his breath in his heart, and looks at her, smiling softly, "witch"-like.
The girl who shouldn't be compatible with a Witch Factor is, nonetheless, unmistakably the "Melancholic Witch."
Because—

"The 'Melancholy' Witch Factor — pay the price, and anyone can use it. And as long as the price is properly balanced, anyone can act as a stand-in for that payment. —That's why everyone in the royal capital is paying it, together."

"——"

"So, I'll say it again. —Serves you right."

Coins, one remaining.
Because Roy knows of no one who's abused the "Melancholy" Witch Factor to this extent.
If this isn't the "Melancholic Witch," then what is she.

"—Ahh, thanks for the feast."

Remaining, zero coins. —The blow of the "Gorgeous Tiger" detonates.

"——"

Mercilessly, it blows straight through Roy's face and knocks him down onto the ground where the Black Serpent writhes. His small frame plunges straight into the filth—

right before it does.

"—Blow it away!!"

In Felt's hands, roaring that cry, the glowing staff releases a white light that paints over the world.
Unerringly, becoming an inescapable blow of judgment, it swallowed up the villainous ones.

—And that was the closing scene of "Bad Eater's" eating activities.
