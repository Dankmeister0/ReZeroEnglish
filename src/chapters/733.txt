Arc 9 Chapter 37: "Mindset"

"—Subaru-sama and Beatrice-sama have fallen into the hands of a person calling himself Aldebaran. Regret. Countermeasures against that person are currently being advanced, centered on Emilia-sama. Report."

Having received that report from Clind, who had flown in using the Authority of 'Melancholy,' Roswaal found himself unsettled by the gap in difficulty between rationally accepting what had happened and accepting it emotionally.

The setting was the Barielle mansion — the estate the late Priscilla Barielle had inherited from her deceased husband, Leip Barielle, and which had now lost its master a second time. At present, Roswaal had parted from Emilia and the others to accompany Schult, Priscilla's attendant, on a visit to this place.

It was in one of the mansion's rooms that Clind had appeared and brought this report.

—To be honest, Roswaal had not welcomed Subaru's proposal to take the heartbroken Al to the Pleiades Watchtower. Not in the slightest.

Recovering Subaru after he'd been sent flying to the Vollachian Empire had already taken time enough as it was. — Rem had been swept along with him too, true, but Roswaal preferred not to deceive himself. Roswaal's objective had been bringing Subaru back; Rem's safety had been secondary.

Of course, Rem's return was cause for joy among everyone in the camp, but given the time it had cost, Roswaal could hardly afford to be optimistic. He needed to move at once to make up for the delays in the measures the Royal Selection demanded. — It was just as he'd reached that conclusion that Subaru had come to him about the trip to the Pleiades Watchtower.

What ought to take priority? Roswaal had tried to persuade Subaru from that angle, but couldn't change his mind, and in the end Subaru's proposal carried the day.

Even so, Subaru's decision wasn't entirely without merit.

"Please — please, I beg you to look after Al-sama..."

The one who bowed his slightly curly pink head as he said this was Schult, Priscilla Barielle's attendant — the loyal retainer she had so carefully protected.

A small child, yet clever, and desperately trying to be strong, Schult had been badly shaken by Priscilla's death, but he was holding himself up on his thin legs with everything he had, determined not to disgrace his late mistress's name. Schult was also close with Al, and had been worried the whole time about how deeply he'd been hurt.

Subaru's concern for Al proved tremendously useful for winning Schult's heart.

Leaving Schult with a favorable impression, putting him in the Emilia Camp's debt — that was a major advantage going forward in Royal Selection strategy, particularly regarding how to handle the now-vacant Barielle territory.

With the start of the Royal Selection, Emilia and Priscilla had become rivals, but in the course of that rivalry the two had come to recognize each other and had become friends deeply in tune with one another. Now Priscilla had died, and Emilia, carrying on her will, would support and guide the people of Barielle territory who had lived under her protection. — For that story to hold together, Schult, so deeply favored by Priscilla, was absolutely indispensable.

Roswaal had, after all, secured Emilia's own words on the matter — that she'd wanted to become real friends with Priscilla once the Royal Selection was over — so a little embellishment fell well within the range of ordinary business.

For that reason, Roswaal had reluctantly sent Subaru and the others off to the Pleiades Watchtower, and chosen instead to accompany Schult back to the Barielle domain. — Which had been a blunder.

"Subaru-sama and the others...? Then — what about Garf? What became of Garf? Garf went along to protect Subaru-sama and the others, didn't he!"

"Unfortunately, Garfiel was defeated by Aldebaran's scheme and suffered grave injuries. Unconscious. One would hope for some benefit from the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirit, but the injury occurred in the Augria Sand Dunes... land steeped in Miasma, to his misfortune, so the effect is weaker than hoped for. Faint."

"Garf...!"

Beside the pondering Roswaal, Frederica, who had come along to the Barielle domain, hung her head in frustration on hearing from Clind of Garfiel's condition.

What showed on her profile was not disappointment or discouragement at Garfiel's failure to fulfill his expected role, but chiefly relief that he was alive, tinged with concern as she imagined the pain he must be in.

Roswaal too felt relief that Garfiel had survived. But even so—

"...It doesn't sit right, no-o-o. To begin with, Al-dono's actual skill should fall well short of Garfiel's. Of course, Garfiel still hasn't finished forming on the mental side, so it's plausible enough that his mind got attacked and that opening got exploited, I suppo-o-ose."

"...If it's Garf, that's the most plausible story of all. That boy is too soft, after all."

"Kindness is nothing to be blamed for. Even if it's meant to discipline yourself, there's no need to deliberately choose such harsh words. Observation."

"Honestly, you — of all times to say that..."

At Clind's considerate words, Frederica ground her fangs together, her expression complicated.

The two of them had known each other since more than ten years ago, from back when Frederica had left the Sanctuary to come work for Roswaal, but a rift once opened had never fully closed.

At first, Frederica had looked up to Clind like an older brother, tagging along after him with "Big brother, big brother" at every opportunity, but from around the time Frederica began growing taller, that relationship had changed entirely.

As for what had triggered that, Roswaal, who knew the circumstances, was sympathetic to Clind's side of it, but Frederica, suddenly pushed away, had never accepted it. That was presumably why she'd gone on detesting him like a snake or scorpion ever since — though it was hard to say for certain.

In any case—

"Clind, given your attitude, I'd guess there's something going on that means we can't simply blame Garfiel for this... so what happene-e-ed?"

"—. The Dragonhusk of the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica has come to obey Aldebaran. In response, the 'Sword Saint' Reinhard van Astrea-sama has already set out to subdue him. Rushed."

"—! The 'Sword Saint'-sama himself? But then, in that case..."

"Don't tell me — has Al-dono gone and beaten even Reinhard-kun?"

"To be precise, 'contained' would be the more accurate word. Correction. Along with this, a great many problems have been cropping up in the royal capital as well. We would urgently like to borrow Master's strength there too. Recommendation."

At Clind's words, Roswaal narrowed his eyes and fell to thinking.

His feelings on the whole matched those of Frederica, who stood with her emerald eyes wide in blank astonishment, unable to take in the flood of information. The difference between them was that, while exposed to that same deluge of information — enough to leave anyone stunned senseless — Roswaal still had a choice before him.

Namely: whether this was, by now, the stage at which he ought to make good on his promise to Subaru.

"—————"

Before Subaru had formally received his knighthood, back when the crisis surrounding the Sanctuary had wound down, Roswaal had told Subaru his thinking and his stance.

If Natsuki Subaru swore to go on picking up everything, then should he ever drop any part of that everything, Roswaal would take merciless steps to make him start over. That would mean burning away whatever Subaru didn't want to lose, and abandoning the world in which the subjective Roswaal who was himself now, lived.

"Priscilla-sama was a difficult case to judge, though."

Whether to count Priscilla among the "everything" Subaru spoke of had been a vexing question.

But in the end, Roswaal had judged that Priscilla's death, as a political rival, would work as a tailwind for Emilia's ambition to win the Royal Selection, and so he judged her death fell outside the terms of their agreement. — What's more, from what he'd heard of the circumstances of her death, he'd also felt it shouldn't be undone.

Priscilla Barielle's death was the result of Priscilla Barielle's own choice.

So long as she wasn't a talent indispensable to Roswaal's own aims, Roswaal saw no reason to deny that her choice deserved respect.

—But Subaru's current situation, just hearing of it, sounded dire.

By Clind's account, there didn't seem to have been any notable casualties yet, but given the situation Al now found himself in, that was only a matter of time. If it was bound to happen sooner or later regardless, then given how thoroughly Subaru had already been outmaneuvered, this was surely the point at which he ought to be made to start over.

If a final push was needed to bring that about, Roswaal would deliver it.

And yet—

"—Beatrice."

Roswaal found a single thread of hope in Beatrice, said to have been captured together with Subaru.

Not because Beatrice was a Great Spirit who had mastered the Shadow attribute, nor because she was the supreme creation of the 'Witch of Greed' Echidna, the greatest mage the world had ever known, nor because, as Subaru's Contracted Spirit, she would surely try to protect him with strength of feeling and resolve.

It was a feeling deeply incomprehensible and hard to digest for Roswaal, but there was no basis to it at all.

Just that Roswaal found himself wanting to believe in Beatrice.

"—Margrave-sama, please, I beg that you go."

The one who spoke those words was neither Frederica nor Clind.

It was Schult, present in the mansion's parlor for the discussion of Barielle territory's future, who had cut in with those words.

At Clind's sudden appearance via instantaneous movement through 'Compression,' and at the report that followed, Schult had repeatedly let out startled little sounds — "Eh?" "Ahh" — but on the face of the small child who now spoke out so plainly to Roswaal, much of that former fragility had been stripped away — no, concealed.

That untouchable mask laid over it is what people call courage, or resolve.

"For what Al-sama has done, I will apologize in his place. And on top of that, I ask that you please not let Al-sama do anything more dangerous than this."

"Schult-sama..."

"I am all right. Everyone at the mansion is helping too, for Priscilla-sama's sake... so please, I beg of you!"

Schult bowed deeply, and Frederica, watching his trembling shoulders, turned to look at Roswaal. In her eyes was a prayer that he would grant Schult's wish, and a plea of her own that she wanted to be of help to him — but—

"If we're going to do our utmost to honor little Schult's wish, then I can't leave Frederica behind. All the more so if Al-dono has the 'Divine Dragon' with him now, don't you thi-i-ink?"

"Master, what do you mean by..."

"—As for that, I intend to handle it myself. I volunteer."

Frederica frowned, not understanding what connection she had to the 'Divine Dragon,' but before that question could be answered, Clind himself put forward that suggestion.

Frederica, not grasping the meaning, only grew more confused, but Roswaal was different. Clind's words carried more meaning than they let on at the surface.

Roswaal had thought that no matter how much he argued the point, Clind would never make that decision again. Under that gaze from Roswaal, Clind's lips eased into the faintest of smiles, and he said—

"A Soul shining too brilliantly for me to bear kicked me in the back and told me not to remain a bystander any longer. Envy," he said.

"—Seems that fellow can go back through time once it's passed. Doesn't look to be a long stretch of it, mind."

"—————"

The instant old man Rom said this, silence swept the room, heavy with all manner of unspoken thought.

Emilia's large, beautiful, jewel-like eyes went perfectly round, while Rem blinked her eyes — glittering like the clear surface of a lake — again and again.

Ram, meanwhile, wore an easily-read look of suspicion, and Meili tilted her head, fiddling with her braid.

"Hmm, I'm not really sure what you're going on about there."

—was the Emilia-esque remark she let slip.

Had Clind been present, there might have been at least one person able to take old man Rom's report a bit more calmly, but he, having just brought Rem and Ram here, had already been asked to go fetch Roswaal and the others, so his reaction went unseen.

Still, perhaps it was a small mercy that he didn't have to hear this news alongside Roswaal before he'd had time to prepare himself.

"Going back through time, that's..."

'—"Return by Death."'

Beside Petra, whose voice trembled faintly, 'Subaru' murmured this, arms crossed.

Even as Petra nodded at those words, she had no room in her, of all times, to feel a flutter of the heart at sharing this answer with the boy she loved.

The Authority — the extraordinary power Al wielded — that old man Rom, having once clashed with him, had worked out.

He'd driven off Garfiel and Ezzo at the Pleiades Watchtower, put up a good fight against Reinhard in the Augria Sand Dunes — even if he'd borrowed the power of the 'Divine Dragon' to do it — broken through five hundred men of the Felt Camp under old man Rom's command with only three people, and finally, from the royal capital, spirited away Roy Alphard of 'Gluttony,' evading a furiously angry, holding-nothing-back Emilia in the process.

Just how Al had managed to bring about that whole string of grave misdeeds and crimes — the dots were, at last, connecting.

It was possible. — If Al was, like Subaru, a wielder of 'Return by Death.'

'That guy Al, he's from another world too, same as me. So there was plenty of chance he'd awaken to a similar power. I mean, come on, that's basically a stock pattern in other-world stories, isn't it? And I never even considered it — man, I'm dense...!'

"Then Al-san really is the same as Subaru?"

'Yeah, chances are good. Actually, even if it's a similar leap-type ability, Al's version might be a more convenient type to use. It's practically a rule that these powers never turn out identical, and enjoying those differences is one of the real pleasures of an other-world story.'

"My own life's on the line here, so I don't care about "stock patterns" or "pleasures" or whatever. What matters is that we've figured out one of Al-san's secrets — and what we're going to do about it, right?"

'Right, yes, exactly, that's exactly right. ...Still, this untangles a lot. If that's true, then running from Reinhard or setting traps for him... could work? Could it, though? Is having "Return by Death" really enough to do something about Reinhard? Feels like you'd die about a hundred times trying.'

Having concluded that Al was, presumptively, a wielder of time-leaping, 'Subaru' held his head in his hands like that.

Honestly, Petra didn't think the 'Sword Saint' Reinhard van Astrea was quite as amazing as everyone around her made him out to be. Partly that was because she'd never actually met the man herself, but also — from what she'd heard, Reinhard was strong, so his doing work that matched that strength wasn't something to be surprised by.

If anything, she thought Subaru was far more impressive — the way he faced enemies beyond his own ability, wringing out every last drop of strength he had and more, all to produce results beyond what anyone expected of him.

So it didn't sit well with Petra, exactly, that this 'Subaru' rated himself lower than Reinhard — she wanted him to stand tall and know he was more amazing than that. And yet, having too many people wake up to Subaru's good qualities because of it would also be a problem.

"Well, between me and Emilia-neesama and Rem-neesama, plus Beatrice-chan and Patrasche-chan, I think we've got a perfect formation for now, though."

Petra prided herself that, even if someone who belatedly woke up to Subaru's charms tried to muscle in, an impregnable encirclement had already been completed that wouldn't be easy to breach.

If there was one worry at present, it was the chance that Subaru, being too kind, might sympathize too much with the other side — but even so, there was the trust that he'd always keep Emilia at the very top. — Though admittedly, that itself was quite the sticking point for Petra too.

In any case—

"If Rom-sama is in his right mind, and Rom-sama's eyes aren't just for show, then this is troublesome."

"There's plenty in that lass's manner of speaking that catches my ear, but I'm glad someone understands. That's the reason Aldebaran managed to break through even our encirclement. And it's likely the reason Emilia got outmaneuvered in the royal capital too — that Authority of his."

"Um, sorry. I still haven't quite gotten a clear picture of this..."

Ram and old man Rom, the especially sharp pair of the group, were about to charge ahead with the conversation, but Emilia called a halt right there. Beside her, Rem nodded prettily as well.

"Rem feels the same as Emilia-sama. Going back through time, is it? Onee-sama, whatever does that..."

"True, Rom-sama's explanation wasn't quite enough for Rem, freshly awakened and not yet back to full form. — Here, take a look at this."

"Is that... a holy gold coin?"

Ram stepped smoothly forward and showed Emilia and Rem the holy gold coin resting on her palm.

The coin bore an engraving of the very 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica in question, and after showing it plainly enough to Emilia and the others, Ram turned her hand over and let the coin drop.

Watching the arc it traced as it fell toward the floor, Petra understood Ram's intent. The holy gold coin, falling to the floor — she meant to use it to demonstrate the advantage of time-leaping—

"Watch out! Honestly, Ram, you can be so careless."

"Onee-sama can sometimes be unexpectedly scatterbrained. That's part of Onee-sama's charm too."

But before the coin could hit the floor, Emilia moved swiftly and caught it. Emilia handed the coin back to Ram, and Rem said something halfway between consolation and encouragement.

The expression on Ram's face as she accepted the coin back was rich with feeling, to say the least.

'Wait, wasn't Ram trying to show it falling, like — she didn't know it'd fall so it fell, but if you know beforehand that it'll fall, you don't have to drop it — that kind of thing?'

"Probably. But this works out well, so let's leave the explaining to Emilia-neesama and Rem-neesama to Ram-neesama. This isn't really the time for me to sit here feeling all satisfied just from talking with the two of them."

Sorry as it was to Ram, Petra left the lecture needed to bring Emilia and Rem up to speed to her, and turned her mind instead to the problem beyond it — specifically, what concrete steps to take about Al.

She wanted to know a little more about the details of Al's Authority. And if she could, by chance, disprove what 'Subaru' had just guessed—

"—Petra-chan, are you okay?"

"Meili-chan..."

While Ram and old man Rom spent their words explaining things to Emilia and Rem, it was Meili who suddenly called out to Petra.

Meili too should have been on the side with a poor grasp of time-leaping, but it seemed what she'd chosen to prioritize here wasn't understanding Al's Authority, but the color of Petra's face.

She gently reached out and touched Petra's forehead, murmuring "So wa-a-arm," and said,

"We've been so busy this whole time, from the tower all the way here, that I haven't said much about it, but — I know you read that book inside the tower, Petra-chan, you know?"

"Mm, that's right. — Thanks for keeping quiet about it with Emilia-neesama and the others."

"...I can believe that it's just because you don't want to worry your onee-sans, right?"

"Yeah, of course!"

Petra took hold of the hand Meili had pressed to her forehead and squeezed it tight between both her own.

Just as Meili had said, she probably did have a bit of a fever. But Petra figured this was less a matter of feeling unwell than something closer to a fever brought on by too much thinking.

There was already plenty to think about as it was, and now, on top of Petra's own thoughts, the little brain inside Petra's little head had also taken on the job of processing 'Subaru's' feelings and reasoning too.

Part of it, she supposed, was how deeply she cared, but even so — Petra was in this state just from reading one single Book of the Dead, Subaru's. Ezzo, who'd apparently read more than ten of the things, was simply off the charts, and Flugel, believed to be the architect of that tower, who'd built a grand library with a design that only someone as off the charts as that could handle properly, was even more out of his mind.

He was surely the type who assumed that whatever he himself could do, anyone could do — and, for better or worse, that was without a doubt the sort of thinking that rubbed Petra's nerves the wrong way.

Still, at the same time, Petra also thought this:

That without that grand library, there were plenty of things she might have gone the rest of her life never knowing.

And among the things she'd been able to learn, there had, for certain, been something Petra had wanted. As long as it kept that fire lit in her heart, she didn't want to stop moving.

Even if, once it was all over, this brain of hers ended up boiling over.

"And besides, I think—"

"...Think what?"

"If what old man Rom's saying is right, and Al-san's Authority is what I imagine it to be..."

'Petra?'

Meili and 'Subaru' both peered in at Petra as she pressed a finger to her thin lips and lowered her eyes like that. The two of them, unaware of each other's existence, wore such identically bewildered expressions that it was almost funny, and Petra, fighting back an inappropriately-timed laugh, declared—

It was—

"—I think I might've figured out Al-san's weakness."

"—So it's Reinhard's own bastard of a father who put his mother to sleep, huh."

Felt bitterly noted how out of character it was that she'd hesitated to say those words aloud.

Not because it had to do with Reinhard. Well — in a way, it was.

Ever since they'd first met, Felt's rhythm had been thrown off by Reinhard without letup, and there were many occasions where, faced with events he was tangled up in, she found it hard to reach the kind of judgments and decisions that felt like her own. Some of that was down to having had the outcomes of her own decisions blocked by his sheer force, again and again — and, to put it more plainly, some of it traced back to that very first time, around the business with the Loot House, when he'd seen her crying face.

But probably the biggest reason of all was that Reinhard had given up on so much.

That was what made Felt hesitate to accept Reinhard wholeheartedly. And, at the same time, it was the reason Reinhard never truly entrusted anything to Felt from the heart, either.

The slum philosophy Felt carried within her — "Live strong" — was a maxim worth its salt.

No matter where you were born, no matter who you were, she believed that spirit alone would go on living no matter what came. Measured against that philosophy, Reinhard was not living strong.

As lion, as dragon, as man — Reinhard was, in every case, only half-committed.

But if part of the reason for that lay in the fact Felt had just now realized—

"—Nah, that doesn't add up."

But Felt shot down the hypothesis she'd just come up with herself, immediately, on her own.

With a certain degree of conviction, Felt suspected Reinhard was connected somehow to his ever-sleeping mother — Louanna Astrea. But if Reinhard had actually known that fact, then his behavior up to now simply wouldn't add up.

Of course, it wasn't impossible that Reinhard had kept such a fact hidden from Felt, quietly concealing his own involvement in the reason his mother never woke.

"No way. That clumsy oaf, pull off something that slick? Not a chance."

Was there anyone else on earth who'd describe Reinhard in quite those terms besides Felt?

Those who pinned their ideals and dreams on the 'Sword Saint' would probably have complained at that, but within the Felt Camp, treating Reinhard like a hopeless klutz had, by this point, become fairly standard practice.

The Remendis sisters, Flam and Grassis, who'd known him a long time, made a habit — helped along by their easy familiarity — of flustering Reinhard with their cutely venomous tongues, and the three former stray-dog types with no use for reverence, Gaston, Rachins, and Camberley, never ran short of well-founded abuse to hurl his way either.

Ezzo, who had a lot to say about how the Astrea territory was run, had repeatedly held his head and muttered "How has this held together so far..." and old man Rom, who evidently held no great opinion of the 'Sword Saint,' had kept up his brusque attitude toward Reinhard from the very start without ever softening it.

And of course, the one in this world who had run Reinhard down, badmouthed him, and smeared mud on that title of "the ideal, greatest of knights" more than anyone else, was this very Felt.

Reinhard had taken in all manner of words and actions from the people of that camp.

And having taken them in, what did he do? — He laughed.

He laughed like he genuinely enjoyed it.

As if, just for that one moment, he hadn't given up on anything at all.

"Even if the bastard can hide his true feelings, there's no faking a laugh like that."

That was Felt's conclusion — a thought that could only come from someone who, unlike those who wanted to pile excessive expectations onto Reinhard, saw him as just another person, warts and all.

So the people besides Felt who'd describe Reinhard this way were the people of the camp, the knights who counted themselves his close friends, and—

"Thinking the bastard can't keep secrets — I'm starting to want to add "because you're his old man" to the list of reasons why."

Right in front of Felt, who scratched her head as she said this, Heinkel — who'd taken a punch to the face and dropped to one knee — clutched the sword tossed back to him, his face gone bloodless.

His grip on the sword was so tight his fingers and hand had gone white, trembling with a rattling shake.

That the emotion behind it was neither anger, nor fear, nor sorrow came through vividly to Felt from Heinkel's wavering blue eyes.

If that emotion had to be summed up in a single word, "despair" would fit best.

The despair of something that must never be known having been found out anyway — inescapable, inevitable. Etched deep into him, it bound Heinkel's whole body and drove him into a corner.

So pathetic was the sight that it nearly deceived Felt into thinking Heinkel's very life might be snuffed out by that despair.

But probably, Heinkel couldn't die that easily. If a despair thick enough to swallow him whole were going to take his life, it would have done so long, long ago.

"—Ah, gh, uh, no, uh..."

Heinkel's mouth opened and closed uselessly as he kept on making sounds that never became words.

Narrowing her red eyes at Heinkel's reaction, Felt, beneath a sky of stars peering down at them through gaps in the rock face, thought about the relationship between the man before her and Reinhard.

—Felt, who'd decided to take part in the Royal Selection and made Reinhard her knight.

To Felt, Reinhard was the strongest knight in the world, the man called 'Sword Saint,' and at the same time the very one who'd torn Felt and old man Rom apart, and beyond that, the hateful figure who'd tried to force education, dresses, and high-heeled shoes onto a Felt who wanted none of it.

With that as her starting assumption, Felt had never gone out of her way to pry every last detail out of Reinhard himself — but it reached her ears whether she liked it or not.

How Reinhard was treated within House Astrea, and how the world at large saw him.

The stories that came up most often were the various rumors surrounding the death of the previous 'Sword Saint,' Theresia van Astrea; the incident that had led to Reinhard, who had inherited the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint, being formally recognized as 'Sword Saint' — the affair when he was eight years old, in which he'd stopped an incursion into the Kingdom schemed up by a villainous mastermind from the Three Nations; and, on top of that, around the time he'd joined the Order of Knights, the ineradicable stain of having been, apparently, entirely under Heinkel Astrea's thumb.

None of it had ever been told to her in much detail from Reinhard's own mouth, and Felt had never pressed him with pointed questions about it either, but looking back now, nothing but a nagging sense of wrongness kept piling up.

Chief among it all was the claim that Reinhard had been entirely under Heinkel's thumb.

If that were true, then what on earth was all of Heinkel's desperate struggling about?

Felt hadn't looked into Reinhard, but in order to grasp the circumstances tied to headship authority within House Astrea, she had looked into Heinkel.

Heinkel's reputation was fairly poor — most of what she'd found painted him as unfit both as a knight and as a father — but every single time Heinkel had thrown House Astrea's name around to force something through, it had, without exception, been in pursuit of some means to wake the ever-sleeping Louanna.

She wouldn't call the man himself a picture of pious poverty, but he'd kept well clear of any noble-style extravagance, cutting corners in every part of his life and pouring it all into waking his wife — that was the impression the numbers gave of him.

But—

"If you'd really had Reinhard wrapped around your finger, you could've done just about anything, couldn't you."

In fame or in martial strength, there was no one in the Kingdom who surpassed Reinhard.

That power, Reinhard himself apparently never used for his own sake, seeming to take it as a point of honor to hold himself to that restraint instead. But the Heinkel of rumor would be a different story.

If Heinkel really was the man of rumor, he could have used Reinhard's fame, his martial strength, anything at all, and skipped entirely all the trouble of trudging to some site on his own two feet, negotiating face to face with whoever he needed to, and, when words fell short, forcing reason through at swordpoint.

Why go without doing that — keeping Reinhard out of it as much as possible, insisting on waking his ever-sleeping wife by his own hand alone?

If his wife truly mattered more than anything, then nothing placed on the other side of the Balance should ever tip it. — Unless something of equal weight had been set there too.

If that was so, then—

"The way you... the way you care about what matters to you — it's twisted way out of shape."

"—!"

In that instant, Heinkel, who had been shrinking into himself, clenched his back teeth, and light streaked through the air.

A beat late, Felt felt the swelling pressure of killing intent on the wind, and only from the cold of a blade's tip now resting against her throat did she finally understand: that flash of light had been Heinkel's sword-strike, the blade drawn and leveled in the blink of an eye.

"Don't you — go on and on — running your mouth about things that — don't concern you."

"Oi, I'll say this much..."

"Don't move!"

At Heinkel's furious shout, his eyes bloodshot and foam at the corners of his mouth, Felt bit the inside of her cheek and cursed her own blunder — she'd pushed him too far, cornered him too hard.

Unlike the plain, easily-read threat of a moment ago, when she'd been able to punch him back, the intensity Heinkel was giving off now carried an unmistakable edge of killing intent.

She understood the source of that killing intent wasn't hatred or fury but some kind of defensive instinct — but precisely because of that, she also thought she'd drawn out far too troublesome an emotion.

With Felt tensing her body before him like that, Heinkel, his blue eyes shaking violently, said,

"This is a family matter. I'll be damned if I let some outsider go tramping through it. Especially not a brat like you, learning anything more of it—"

"—Anything more? So there's still something else, huh."

"—! Shut up!"

Something caught on the edge of Heinkel's words, and Felt cut in without meaning to. The instant that seemed to carve another violent tremor into Heinkel's expression, he erupted.

And just like that, the blade tip, drawn back for an instant, now picked up speed, thrust forward a full step deeper — or so it seemed, for the space of an instant.

"Please, would you quit provoking the old man."

"Guh!?"

A figure came bounding in and, with the stone sword in hand — the Stone Dragon Blade — mercilessly clubbed the kneeling Heinkel across the side of the head, sending him sprawling to the ground.

Heinkel toppled sideways, eyes rolling white. Standing beside him, shrugging with a "good grief" air, was Aldebaran, who'd cut in at the last dangerous second.

At Aldebaran's appearance, Felt gave a small snort through her nose.

"You — you were watching, waiting till I was actually about to be in real trouble, weren't you."

"And you, Felt-chan, had a hunch I was keeping an eye out so you wouldn't accidentally get yourself killed, and went ahead and talked to the old man anyway. Not a cute way to play it."

"I hate being called cute more than anything, so."

Sticking out her tongue, Felt didn't so much as thank Aldebaran for the save.

She only made sure the fallen Heinkel wasn't dead, and let out a sigh. — Heinkel's last words: it seemed there was still something more to Reinhard after all.

The 'Sword Saint,' apparently the cause behind his own mother's endless sleep, and on top of that, something Heinkel was still hiding—

"At this point, just sitting back and not asking anything until you feel like talking is starting to look irresponsible of me."

"Well, that's a fine statement of intent. You do realize the situation you're in?"

"By your own account, you've got no intention of doing me harm, and once your goal's all wrapped up, you want to get the Royal Selection running again, don't you? I still think it'd be just fine if you lost, but either way, win or lose, what I do from here on is the same."

"Can't argue with that."

Felt snorted again at Aldebaran, who waved his right hand back and forth, then fitted the sheath to the sword Heinkel still gripped and wouldn't let go of, and sheathed it.

Just before it slid home, the gleam of that blade's edge, engraved with the name 'Astrea,' struck Felt's eye as needlessly, mercilessly cold — and it irritated the hell out of her.
