Arc 10 Chapter 29: "Graystar Mission"

—Everything was proceeding without a hitch.

Beneath a great chandelier suspended from the ceiling, blazing with brilliant light, the elegant strains of stringed instruments filled the hall, while guests dressed in vividly colored formal attire chatted amiably, cups in hand.
The soiree, held with the great hall of the manor thrown open by Viscount Disken Moreau, lord of the surrounding region, overflowed with the voices of a great many guests, presenting every appearance of a resounding success.

The guests were nobles on close terms with Viscount Moreau, merchants with substantial trade routes throughout the territory, and men of standing whose influence spanned several villages — a gathering whose pedigree left nothing to be desired.
When the viscount, as host, raised his cup and offered a toast to the peace and prosperity of the domain, voices rose in agreement, and the orchestra struck up a lively tune to reinforce the sense of unity.

Everyone voiced their approval of Disken Moreau's words, never doubting that he was on their side.
Small wonder — including the very invitation to this gathering, ample rewards had been prepared for the guests, letting them savor firsthand the sweetness of contributing to Moreau territory.
When people stand to gain enough, they naturally learn to overlook a little strangeness. These guests bore no malice, no guilty conscience. Unwitting collaborators — that was the ideal.

Viscount Disken Moreau had, of late, drastically changed the way he governed.

Until recently, Viscount Moreau had been conservative — to put it unkindly, cowardly, and afraid of change. But over the past several months, his attitude had departed sharply from that old stance of maintaining the status quo, and he had begun setting off one bold initiative after another, drawing in the influential figures around him.
Past his fiftieth year, enjoying wine and fine food while guarding a secure position — that had long been mocked as the whole of Viscount Moreau's philosophy of life. The change was so stark it seemed a lie, and it was warmly welcomed by all those to whom it brought profit.

Tonight's celebration, too, had been held under the pretext of gathering those who had profited from a recent major deal, to lavish them with gratitude for their cooperation.

Just as he was leaving middle age behind and approaching the threshold of old age, Disken Moreau's true talent had finally blossomed — or so he was regarded, here in this hall.

—For no one had any way of knowing that Disken Moreau had been replaced by another entirely, his position stolen from him, root and all.

"..."

Raising a glass of wine to his lips, the figure calling himself "Viscount Moreau" quietly surveyed the soiree.
He answered every word directed at him, met every proposal brought to him with a smile, and returned every flattering greeting with still greater praise for the one who'd offered it.
In doing so, he was carefully gauging just how far the "poison" had spread.

"Poison" — not in the sense of some physically lethal substance slipped into a drink.
Rather, one might say the "poison" was himself — the one calling himself Disken Moreau and usurping his station. Something that ate away slowly at its victims, that could reach even to take a life before anyone noticed. "Poison" administered to that end was said to now be spreading through every corner of the kingdom.
It was hearsay because he himself did not know the details of the plan to spread the "poison." But he could hardly be the only one, and if one watched the state of the kingdom closely, its effects could be quietly felt.

No doubt the moment when the "poison" would take full effect was drawing near.
For the sake of that coming moment, it was his duty to clear away every obstacle beforehand. Which was precisely why the veteran retainers and servants who, despite his form being an utterly exact match, had come to doubt the truth of what lay within it, all had to be made to disappear, together, at once.

Invitations had been sent to them as well.
Though not to the glittering soiree unfolding here — theirs specified, under the pretext of an explanation of circumstances and talks toward reconciliation, that they enter the annex through the rear service door.
All of them had already arrived and been led down to the mansion's cellar. And by the time the orchestra finished its final piece, they would quietly leave the manor — as corpses.

The bodies would be loaded onto a Dragon Carriage and made to look as though they'd been set upon by bandits along the road.
They had failed to mend their relationship with their lord, and left the soiree in a fit of indignation. Afterward, the people gathered here would hear "Viscount Moreau" pitifully lament that his talks with his retainers had fallen through, and accept it with words of comfort — only to learn of the tragedy that followed.

Once tonight was over, every discordant note would vanish from this domain. That was the poison's true purpose fulfilled.
So convinced, the figure calling himself "Viscount Moreau" returned a smile to the flattery of an approaching merchant.

"Ah, truly, what a splendid gathering this is. By the way, Viscount, have you had a chance to speak with that couple over there?"

At the end of the merchant's gaze, within a cluster of guests, stood a man and a woman.
A married couple, so it was said, though it was the wife who first caught the eye.
Her rich black hair was swept up in a glossy style, and both the makeup coloring her white skin and the evening gown she wore were arranged with tasteful elegance. The dark dress flared wide toward the hem, setting off her narrow waist and generous curves all the more.
With an open fan held to her lips, the woman traded pleasant chatter with an older noblewoman beside her. The way she moved her eyes, her every gesture, all measured to perfection — she was not merely beautiful, but seemed to know exactly how she appeared to others and how to comport herself to please them.

—Natsumi Schwartz.

If he recalled correctly, she was a young lady who had inherited a vast fortune from a father who died suddenly — or so the guest list recorded her. She was a guest who had joined at the last moment through a letter of introduction from another territory, one of the few new faces at a gathering meant to be filled entirely with allies.

Beside her stood her husband, a tall figure with deep green hair tied back in one bunch.
He wore formal dress that stopped just short of gaudy, an ornamented longsword at his hip. He appeared to hail from a martial family, his bearing and gait bearing the marks of long training, but what caught the eye most was his face — an eyepatch over his left eye that only deepened the impression of his already well-formed features. He struck one as a man in whom absence only sharpened the beauty that remained, and standing beside his wife, he lost nothing by the comparison — a couple well matched indeed.

"If you mean that pair, why, I spoke with them myself just a little while ago."

Noticing where the lord's gaze had fallen, a perfumed noblewoman cut into the conversation. With the curious smile of an inveterate gossip, she went on,

"Apparently they're touring various places searching for somewhere to invest the wife's fortune."

"It seems others have taken an interest as well. Naturally, I count myself among them, but it struck me as rather forward to approach them ahead of our host, the Viscount."

He answered the noblewoman's mischievous gaze and the merchant's self-satisfied remark with a courteous smile. But for a while after that, word of the Schwartz couple kept reaching his ears again and again.
By then, the name had firmly lodged itself in "Viscount Moreau's" mind.

"—I am Natsumi Schwartz. Please forgive the discourtesy of my late greeting."

Facing Natsumi Schwartz, who bowed with that graceful greeting, "Viscount Moreau" nodded magnanimously, hiding behind his smile the calculating eye of a predator sizing up its prey.

"My wife and I are both grateful to have been invited to such a splendid occasion tonight. This territory is remarkably lively — clear proof, surely, that the Viscount's rule has been widely embraced by his people."

Following his wife's greeting, the husband too bowed politely before praising "Viscount Moreau's" skill in governance.
Naturally, he had no shortage of replies prepared. Though the couple were new faces, they seemed to enjoy conversation a great deal, and he had gathered information one piece after another from the guests who had spoken with them.
But before "Viscount Moreau" could weigh which card to play, the husband's voice — "However," — cut him off, prefacing something evidently important.

"We stand in a position of managing funds entrusted to us by my wife's family. We cannot afford to make a rash judgment based on appearances alone."

"From what little has reached our ears, the Viscount is said to be advancing a number of new ventures throughout the territory. If it would not be too much trouble, might we hear more of the details?"

Matching her husband, who had lowered his tone just slightly, the wife broached the true matter at hand.
So, it seemed the order in which to proceed had been well discussed between husband and wife beforehand. If that were the case, dancing around the subject with roundabout talk would only waste both their time needlessly.
Better to move to a different venue and talk properly, and let the Schwartz couple, too, take on their share of the duty of spreading "Viscount Moreau's" "poison." His worries would vanish, and his hand would grow richer. What a fine day this was turning out to be.

"—Thank you for sparing us your time, Viscount."

Not even "Viscount Moreau's" own guards had been allowed into the parlor where the couple was received. Given that it had been the Schwartz couple's earnest request, it was a concession he judged necessary.
In any case, separated from the bustle of the hall by a single thick door, it was now just the three of them — "Viscount Moreau" and the couple.

"I've heard that over these past few months, this territory has seen tremendously vigorous change — the widening of roads, the building of new markets. 'Stagnation is the same as decline,' my father always taught me — that in the Schwartz family, change and growth are the true path to prosperity."

Wry amusement crossed "Viscount Moreau's" face at the wife's words, undisguised in their ambition — she plainly held to some bold family creed — as he poured wine into three glasses with his own hand and took his seat.
Without touching the wine, the husband picked up where his wife left off, saying, "Please, don't take offense—"

"—I, like my wife, sense a great deal of possibility in this Moreau domain. To trust in that possibility and move to act on it ourselves, there are a few things I would like to confirm."

Glancing at the wife as the husband moved to take the lead in this manner, she seemed content to leave the negotiation to him — her hand, holding her closed fan, rested calmly on her knee as she smiled, at ease.
Very well then — "Viscount Moreau" assured them that he would supply whatever was needed to earn the Schwartz couple's interest and understanding.

"The finances of the ventures matter a great deal, of course, and I would certainly like to examine the records. But more than that, what my wife and I place the greatest weight on is whether the party we do business with is worthy of trust. — I understand that of late, the Viscount has distanced himself from a great many of his long-serving retainers, in favor of bringing in new talent."

At the topic thus raised, irritation at his unwanted retainers welled up within "Viscount Moreau."
It was a worry that would be removed before the night was through, but that did nothing to dull his anger at the nuisance they posed — all the more so if they threatened to become an obstacle to bringing in promising new talent.

"Merit and ability shouldn't be spoken of in the same breath, I know, but casting off those who served long is no easy decision to make. Surely among them were people who stood at your side, Viscount, since your younger years."

Was what he wanted to know how he would be treated after the negotiations succeeded and they became business partners in earnest?
He seemed unable to help worrying over how "Viscount Moreau," who had ruthlessly cut off his old retainers for the sake of change, would go about treating whatever new party he took into his inner circle.
Needless worry. If a person proved useful, there was no reason to go out of his way to discard them. Though, granted, if they proved so useful that they might serve as "poison," he could not rule out the possibility of their being replaced — thoroughly, usably — with a piece all his own, just as "Viscount Moreau" himself had been.

In any case, if it would dispel the Schwartz couple's doubts, "Viscount Moreau" was willing to recount, with rising emotion, his memories of the retainers he'd had no choice but to tearfully let go, and all their accomplishments.
Not one of the memories was invented — all had truly happened. Take the story of the injury from a dragon fall: he could recite the exact place of the fall, the wounds sustained, even the faces and names of every servant involved.
Only by managing that much could he fulfill his duty as a true "poison," rather than some hollow placebo.

"—I see. That is more than enough to satisfy me."

To "Viscount Moreau," who had spoken through tears of his bonds with his retainers, the husband nodded as though moved. His amber eyes glanced briefly toward his wife seated beside him.
Was the division of roles such that the husband handled the negotiation while the wife held the final say? Even so, having heard the exchange thus far, there ought to be no option left but to simply nod in agreement—

"Might I confirm just one more thing?"

"Natsumi?"

Perhaps it hadn't been part of their arrangement, for the husband's brow creased slightly at his wife's words.
To the suspicious look from her husband's one uncovered eye, the wife responded only by resting her folded fan beneath her chin, her black eyes reflecting "Viscount Moreau" as she spoke.

"The Appa tree in the garden has borne fruit again this year. — Now, what might that mean, I wonder?"

It was a question of an entirely different flavor from everything exchanged so far — an angle utterly unlike the rest.
It had nothing to do with Viscount Disken Moreau's competence or the rights and wrongs of his relationships — it touched on some deeply personal memory of his, and it was strange indeed that she should be the one to voice it.
But in that moment, "Viscount Moreau," concealing how caught off guard he'd been by the question, carved a small wry smile onto his lips and shook his head.

What Natsumi Schwartz had spoken of was a memory involving a retainer who had served "Viscount Moreau" long and faithfully before retiring some years back — a trivial episode in which the real viscount had once been served Appa at that retainer's home, found it bitter, and thereafter promised sweeter Appa every year to come.
Four days earlier, a letter had arrived from that very retainer, containing similar wording. "Viscount Moreau" had, that same day, confirmed its meaning with the real viscount and filled in the gap in his memory.
It seemed that ever since, Appa had been sent every year without fail. And this year too, trusting that Viscount Moreau would receive it, the man would surely send his Appa along.

"Oh my... what a lovely little secret shared only between the two of you."

Hearing that, Natsumi, seemingly moved, pressed a hand to her eyes and wiped at their corners. Judging by her reaction, all her wariness of "Viscount Moreau" seemed to have melted away entirely.
Not that it mattered — even had there been another hundred, two hundred questions, there would have been no issue. Whatever answer the real viscount would have given, "Viscount Moreau" could deliver flawlessly—

"—A wind of lies is blowing."

When that dignified voice rang out, "Viscount Moreau" froze, thought halting for an instant.
Still frozen, the cup he had raised to wet his lips hovering at his mouth, "Viscount Moreau" watched within his field of view as the husband who had spoken raised a hand to his own face and removed his eyepatch.
The left eye now exposed was a dazzling gold — the Dragon's Eye.

"—!"

The instant he sensed danger, "Viscount Moreau" kicked up the table standing between himself and the couple.
With a clattering crash, the small table came up to block the space between the viscount and the couple — only to be cleaved diagonally in two by a blade drawn in silence, and it could do nothing to stop the long leg that lashed out from beyond it.
Taking the sole of a slender, extended kick full in the chest, breath knocked from him, "Viscount Moreau's" body was sent flying straight backward. But the fall and the roll that followed were exactly as planned.

Keeping his opponents in sight the whole while, he drew the wand he'd hidden behind the backrest of the settee he had been sitting on, and leveled the gem-set tip of the magic wand at the impertinent couple — no, at his great enemy.
"Viscount Moreau" understood full well that a being who bore the Dragon's Eye was an enemy to their "poison." And so, with no thought of holding back, a blade of wind began to swell—

"—E! M! T!" "—I suppose!"

The explosive gust that erupted should have surged toward the great enemy, ready to shred them where they stood.
But the blade of wind meant to fly from the wand suddenly lost all its potency, converted into something harmless, without meaning or shape, and scattered into nothing. And at the very moment this baffling phenomenon occurred, an even more baffling sight leapt into "Viscount Moreau's" eyes.

Behind the bearer of the Dragon's Eye, Natsumi Schwartz's billowing skirt flipped up wide, and from underneath it emerged a young girl in a dress.
Bewilderment seized his mind — had she been hidden under that skirt this entire time? — and that was what sealed his fate.

"I'm afraid you simply picked the wrong opponent."

With a flashing blade pressed to his neck, "Viscount Moreau" found his movement sealed — and yet, oddly, it was not the sword-wielding bearer of the Dragon's Eye but Natsumi Schwartz, standing beside her with the little girl in tow, who wore the look of triumph.

— Owing to Viscount Disken Moreau's sudden illness, the soiree came to an early close.

As the guests, leaving behind words of concern for the viscount's health, filed out one after another, and the orchestra hired for the occasion departed the manor as well, the clamor of the soiree at last faded away entirely.
Only then, surveying anew the people gathered in a room tucked away on the manor's first floor, did Subaru let out a small sigh of relief.

"Truly, I am so very relieved that none of you came to any harm..."

"—Oi, how long are you gonna keep playing the part!? You're way too into it — is that supposed to be some knightly skill or somethin', huh!?"

The one hurling abuse from the side at Subaru — still in full Natsumi Schwartz mode, dabbing a handkerchief to his eyes as he held back tears of emotion — was Rachins, dressed in a waiter's uniform.
Dressed in the sort of black-and-white outfit that called to mind a bartender or a garçon, and with his hair neatly styled, he looked every bit the promising young man. In fact, during the soiree they'd infiltrated, his performance seemed to have gone over rather well with the ladies.

"Even so, it's that look in your eyes, isn't it. I understand completely — people have said all sorts of things about my own eyes since I was little."

"Don't go lumping me in with you on your own say-so. We just happen to be crossing paths. I don't need some guy who's happy to crossdress in my crew."

"Are you quite sure you want to say that? You never know — there might just be a gentleman in your own camp who'd suit a dress surprisingly well."

"Like hell there is! Geez, first you, now 'Blue Serpent'..."

"—Hm? Were you just talking about me?"

Rachins's cheek stiffened mid-curse, lips twisted. At the far end of his sidelong, wall-eyed glance, the door to another room opened to reveal Ferris, dressed in the same waiter's uniform as him.
Having shed his jacket and rolled up his shirtsleeves, Ferris wiped the blood from his hands with a cloth as he glared at Rachins — and, for some reason, at Subaru too.

"Well done, 'Blue Serpent.' Are the injured all right?"

"...Well, just barely. The people gathered in the annex — the ones who were inconvenient to the swap and about to be executed all at once — thanks to Long-Tongue and the others stepping in, they're fine. Though the first one chosen was touch and go, y'know."

"You brought him back from that? Your magic's a real monster, as always."

"I'll take that as a compliment. And when Long-Tongue's about to keel over dying, this little monster will patch him right up too, don't you worry."

Against Rachins's sharp tongue, Ferris kept up his own edge of sarcasm without slackening.
Wryly amused at the two of them, forever one step short of getting along, Subaru watched relief spread among the anxious people nearby — Disken Moreau's former retainers, spared execution. Told that their gravely wounded comrade had been saved, they were likely only now beginning to truly feel that everyone was safe.

And then—

"Kuhahahaha, what a lukewarm bunch, all mood and no follow-through! If you're glad to be alive, whoop it up! If something's still bugging you, wail and rage about it! Nobody asked for half-hearted fence-sitting!"

A booming laugh threw cold water on the room just as it was settling into relief.
Stomping heavily across the floor, peering into the room without a shred of restraint, was a tall young man with dark skin and unruly wheat-colored hair, of a bold and valiant look. His face, which might have been rugged and handsome if not twisted into such a mocking sneer, was tipped back cheerfully to take a swig from the bottle of liquor in his hand.
He too was supposedly dressed in the same waiter's uniform as Ferris and the others, but strange as it was, his manner and speech made it look like an entirely different outfit altogether.
Then again, that impression could only stay that mild for those like Subaru who were used to his behavior — for people who had only just been saved from mortal danger moments ago, it was a great deal more jarring.

"Now really, 'Black Dog', what is that attitude? Speaking so thoughtlessly to people who were just in such terrible danger..."

"Ohh, what's this, a lecture? Buuut before you go on about my attitude, maybe take a look at yourself hiding the truth first, hm? And besides all that and this and everything else, I'm the guy who saved their lives, y'know. Nobody's got any business complaining at me, riiight?"

"That's a completely different matter..."

"If you get to split things apart however suits your own logic, then I'll fit things together however suits mine. There, go on and comfort them if you want — I'll be over here pouring my own drinks. Though if a beautiful duchess wanted to pour for me, I sure wouldn't say no. Kuhahahaha!"

When both sides could claim their own logic, there was little room to press the point against "Black Dog," who had, after all, delivered results.
In truth, without his cooperation, they could never have brought to zero the damage wrought here in Disken Moreau's mansion by what could only be called the vicious scheme of the "Replacement Puppets." — No, even now, perhaps it couldn't truly be called zero.

"Unfortunately, we found nothing resembling a trace anywhere in the mansion, I suppose. It seems we ought to conclude the real one is being held captive somewhere else entirely, I suppose."

Just as Subaru quietly clenched his fists, Beatrice returned bearing a report that confirmed exactly that fear.
Beside her, having accompanied her through the mansion in search of any magical traces or devices, stood the elegant figure who had joined the search for anything suspicious — Crusch.
Having played the husband's role opposite Natsumi Schwartz, she had already fixed her eyepatch back in place, and with a valiant beauty of her own, affirmed Beatrice's report with an "Indeed."

"I searched together with 'Librarian,' but nothing amiss caught my eye either. And the one masquerading as Disken Moreau still shows no sign of talking. Had any of his underlings survived, it might have been a different matter, but..."

"Kuhahahaha, well, ain't that just a shame and a pity."

Even under Crusch's sharp glare, "Black Dog" showed not a hint of remorse.
Even hidden behind mask and robe, he'd always given off the air of a man who took nothing seriously, but stripped of them, exposing a face that didn't quite match his personality, his eccentric impression only grew stronger.

In any case—

"—Then our lord is nowhere to be found..."

The one who let those hoarse words slip before the assembled faces was one of the former retainers, seated in a chair.
Fourteen of the viscount's vassals had been summoned to the annex and nearly executed. Every one of them was, in truth, a loyal retainer who had sensed something wrong with the fake Viscount Moreau, victim of the "Replacement Puppet" scheme.
Surely the others besides the one who had spoken shared the same worry for their lord.

"...'Long-Tongue'."

"Tch, sorry to say it, but we checked not just this mansion but the annex and the cellar too. Figured those two might turn up a hidden room or something, but — nothing. Came up dry."

"Then, our lord is..."

Their suspicions confirmed as fact, the real man whose seat had been stolen by the impostor was nowhere to be seen. Despair and resignation spread across the former retainers' faces at the realization.
But Subaru called out, "Wait—"

"It's too soon to give up. I confirmed it myself, with the impostor — the story about the Appa, between Viscount Moreau and a man who once worked at this mansion."

"Is that the story involving my predecessor as household steward? But that—"

"He's one of our collaborators. And the letter about that matter was written four days ago... What's more, the impostor answered it perfectly. Isn't that right, 'Emerald Beauty'?"

"Indeed. There was no falsehood in what he said. It wasn't his own memory, but there's no mistaking that the story itself was true. — Ah, I see. So that's why you pressed him with that question outside our plan."

"Yes. — Do you see? The impostor knew a memory he could only have learned by hearing it from the real Viscount Moreau. Which means—"

"—Our lord is alive?"

"—Yes, without a doubt."

Stepping up before the old retainer whose eyes had gone wide, Subaru took his wrinkled, dry hand in both of his and gave a deep nod. At once, tears welled in the old man's eyes, and the feeling spread swiftly to those around him.
With that as the turning point, the heavy air that had begun to smother the room shifted.
No one wept aloud, but one bowed, despairing face after another lifted, and in their eyes kindled a sense of purpose — the resolve to search for their lord, whose whereabouts remained unknown.

"Then we must find our lord, whatever it takes—"

"—Your feelings do you credit. But precisely because it's for your lord's sake, I cannot recommend such action."

"—!"

But it was Crusch, dressed as a man, who stopped the former retainers' resolve dead in its tracks.
Just as she had when subduing the impostor who'd posed as "Viscount Moreau" at the soiree, she rested a hand on the longsword at her hip and swept the former retainers with a stern, unwavering gaze.
Under that sharp look, their voices trembled with, "Why..."

"Right now, this domain stands without its lord. Among those the impostor has appointed, some will have followed him in ignorance, while others no doubt are in league with the enemy. Unless the chain of command is put in order, the administration kept from stalling, and the impact on the people held to a minimum, imagine how great the resulting chaos would be."

"That is..."

"If, when the real Disken Moreau returns, he finds his domain ravaged, his people suffering, and nothing left of what he swore to protect — that could not be called saving your lord in any true sense."

"..."

As Crusch went on, the confusion and resistance that had first shown on the former retainers' faces began to give way to understanding and acceptance, and they found themselves listening intently to her reasoning.
It was proof of the natural charisma she possessed, the gift to speak and to lead—

"From here on, what you must do is keep this domain looking exactly as it has until today. Take up the administration through collective counsel so no one suspects the impostor has been removed, and spread word that the Viscount has taken ill and is confined to bed. — Your task is to protect his seat, the place he will return to, for the sake of the lord who will one day come back."

Weighing the gravity of Crusch being the one to say such words, Subaru studied her profile.
There was no trace of personal sentiment on that profile as she faced the former retainers and spoke with such dignity. And yet she too was someone who had had her place stolen from her on unjust charges, and remained, even now, a hunted woman.

"There she goes again..."

The one who murmured that quietly from behind was Ferris, who of all people could not help feeling the most fierce, impulsive reaction to Crusch's present circumstances. But the words never reached Crusch, who was busy facing the loyal retainers as they slapped their own weak, faltering cheeks and nodded deeply.

"—Understood. Until our lord returns, we will protect this land and his seat both, without fail. So—"

"Yes. Leave it to us."

To the resolved retainer's words, Crusch too gave a deep nod in return.
And then—

"—Even should every drop of blood in this body be spilled to the last, I swear I will cut away the malignancy that has taken root in this kingdom, and put your worries to rest."

So she answered — a woman driven from her station, and yet, perhaps, with a pride befitting a candidate for the Royal Selection more than anyone else could claim.

"—Great work, everyone! No losses on our end — the operation's a success!"

"Well, with Betty and Subaru on the job, this much is only natural, I suppose. 'White Sheep' did rather good work too, I suppose. I shall praise her, I suppose."

"Oh — thank you very much. I mean, I'm honored, really!"

Bowing her head over and over, blushing bashfully at Beatrice's praise, was "White Sheep," who had played an especially large role in this operation.
Rather than her usual black suit, she had dressed in a pale blue evening gown to infiltrate the soiree, slipping in as a guest alongside the Schwartz couple played by Subaru and Crusch. Making the most of her easy rapport with people, she'd apparently guided the guests' conversation skillfully so that the impostor "Viscount Moreau" would take an interest in the Schwartzes.

"Sure, we did our best to pull our own weight without a hitch, but White Sheep's competence still caught me off guard. Really saved us."

"N-no, it was thanks to your effort, Subaru-san... I mean, Natsumi-san's — that's why we succeeded. I really didn't do anything that impressive."

"That's a lie if I ever heard one. Also, I already took off the wig and washed off the makeup, so it's Subaru now, not Natsumi. I don't want to go by Natsumi when I'm only half put-together."

"Huh, but you're still in the dress... ah, never mind. Yes."

Subaru tilted his head at "White Sheep," who had started to say something and then swallowed it back. But rather than press the matter, the exhaustion that came with the tension finally draining away hit him all at once.

*Honestly, that went almost too well...*

So thinking, Subaru, still in his dress, turned to look back the way of Viscount Moreau's mansion, where the former retainers had kept their heads bowed to see them off until the very last moment.
What had happened at that mansion tonight would be kept secret, and through the retainers' hard-fought efforts, the domain along with it would be kept intact until the day the real viscount returned.
Except—

"I'll tell you now, those folks have gotta know it too — that if the enemy catches wind of what happened, the real viscount might just get done away with."

"Rachins..."

"And don't go giving me some sweltering speech about how they entrusted us with it anyway. I can't just go picking up everybody else's baggage one after another. I've already got my hands full with your baggage as it is."

Subaru couldn't find a retort for Rachins as he clicked his tongue.
Whether Viscount Moreau's domain could truly be restored to what it once was hung on nothing but a slender thread of hope — there was nothing to do but cling to that faith and cross a rickety, precariously suspended bridge, trusting in a miracle to see it through.
And that bridge, that miracle they had to believe in, was not confined to Moreau's domain alone.

"It's already been two months..."

As if carving regret and impatience into himself, Subaru let his thoughts drift over the time that had passed. — That much time had elapsed since he and the others had been driven from the royal capital and begun moving together with the "Six Tongues."
In that time, moving from place to place across the kingdom, they had burrowed deep into the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, and the fight against the "Beloved Children," who were plotting nothing less than the overthrow of the nation, had gone on without pause.
What had startled him was that the "Beloved Children's" replacement of powerful figures wasn't confined to the upper echelons of the royal capital, but extended throughout the whole kingdom. — In these two months, Subaru and the others had resolved five cases like tonight's affair with Viscount Moreau. This had made six.

"The damage the 'Replacement Puppets' have done to the kingdom is serious. I get that there's meaning in crushing them one by one. I get it, but..."

"Subaru, impatience is forbidden, I suppose. Emilia and the others at the castle are surely safe, I suppose. Crusch has vouched for that much herself, I suppose."

"—To be precise, it runs through having one of our people left at the castle confirm it, and then 'Emerald Beauty' verifying with her Divine Protection that I'm not falsifying the report."

"..."

Beatrice laid her small hands over Subaru's clenched fist, drawing close to his heart. Just then, a figure emerging from the building cut into their exchange.
Standing at the entrance of the hunting lodge the "Six Tongues" had secured as a temporary rallying point, deep in the woods away from the Moreau estate, was none other than the organization's chief, Russell Fellow.

"Russell-san, did the chief himself really come out to greet us?"

"Yes. 'White Sheep,' whom I'd sent to meet you, never came back, so I wondered if something unforeseen had happened. And also — during the operation, I did ask you to go by 'Silver Fox,' didn't I, 'Ashen Star'?"

"Hm, we're still counting this as during the operation?"

"At the very least, until I can conclude that you've changed out of the dress, transferred custody of the captured fake Disken Moreau, and safely escaped this region."

At Russell's answer, cautious to the point of exhaustive caution, Subaru scratched his cheek with a finger.
Given everything he'd seen of the enemy's movements so far, an optimistic remark like "you're overthinking it" or "you don't have to go that far" was not something Subaru's mouth could form. The plain fact was that Russell's thoroughgoing policy was what kept the "Six Tongues" — and by extension, the kingdom — alive.

"Still, never mind my 'Ashen Star' or Bea-ko's 'Librarian' — isn't Rachins's 'Long-Tongue' a little too phoned-in? Though Crusch-san's 'Emerald Beauty' did go through with my suggestion."

"The requirement is that they be easy to remember while being hard to trace, that's all. Everyone, come inside. There are matters concerning our future policy I'd like to discuss. — Ah, and one more thing, 'Ashen Star.'"

"Hm?"

"Even though it turned out not to affect the operation in the end, inserting unnecessary exchanges like that carried an extremely high risk of arousing suspicion. Please stop doing that sort of thing without thinking it through. It's an act that endangers all of us — which is to say, the kingdom itself."

At the warning delivered in a quiet tone, eyes narrowed, Subaru let out a small groan.
Honestly, the point Russell raised had been a calculated risk, taken on purpose. It was Subaru himself who'd asked the former household steward turned informant to write that memory of the Appa into his letter in the first place.
All of it, every bit, was because he'd wanted certainty — that when the impostor was asked about information he shouldn't know, it would prove they were in contact with their source, meaning the real viscount, kept alive somewhere, and getting his answers directly from him.
And what that information would actually save wasn't anything so grand as the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, much as Russell placed weight on such things—

"It was something needed to save the retainers, the ones who would have bowed their heads in despair at losing their lord, right there in that room."

So spoke Crusch, present there as well, revealing from behind Subaru the true intent behind his actions.
If anyone had been dragged into the most dangerous position by Subaru's unilateral decision, it was none other than her — though of course, Beatrice, who'd held her breath hidden beneath the skirt, could say the same.

"'Emerald Beauty,' you mean to say you approve of what he did?"

"If one places weight on rationality, 'Ashen Star's' unilateral choice can hardly be called commendable. From the standpoint of a duchess as well, I would call it a mistaken judgment. — But—"

"But?"

"I am, at present, a woman hunted by her own kingdom, driven from her rank as duchess. And beyond that, going purely by my own personal opinion, I find myself rather fond of this man's softness. I will offer you my sympathy for the burden it places on you."

"...I'll take your opinion under advisement."

Rubbing at his eyes, Russell answered so, in the face of Crusch's brazen favoritism toward Subaru.

"Forgive me, but I'll be heading inside. Using this eye does take its toll, after all. I doubt I'll drop off to sleep, but I'd like to sit down."

With that, Crusch slipped past Russell and went into the hunting lodge. "Black Dog," who'd stayed aloof from the conversation the whole while, had already gone inside as well, and "White Sheep," after a hurried bow, dashed into the lodge too, probably to prepare tea for everyone.

"Heh, I get the urge to say all kinds of things about it. So go ahead, say whatever you want, I don't mind. Even a fool wouldn't go and do somethin' like that with zero chance of it working, probably."

Laughing merrily at Subaru getting scolded, Rachins slung his removed jacket over one shoulder and headed into the lodge. Russell didn't respond to those words — hard to tell whether they were meant as comfort or mockery — but instead looked past Subaru and the others to Ferris, standing behind them.

"'Blue Serpent,' you too, inside. I imagine you're tired."

"Not particularly? Fewer people, less severe injuries than the worst I've handled. If I got tired over something this small, I'd be useless back at the royal capital's infirmary."

Russell shrugged at Ferris, who waved a hand dismissively back at him, and returned to the lodge.
As for Subaru, well aware that he'd played his own part in adding to Russell's worries, he was honestly overflowing with the urge to apologize.

"It'd probably only rub him the wrong way even more, though."

"Can't be helped, I suppose. It's the burden of standing at the top, I suppose. It's actually unusual that Emilia and Roswaal are strangers to that kind of anguish, I suppose."

"Maybe so. Besides, if I came clean about it honestly, I feel like it'd only make Russell-san angrier."

Even knowing full well that Russell was already meeting him more than halfway, Subaru still couldn't stop himself from selfishly wanting even more.
Today's unilateral decision was the prime example of that, and though Rachins had said what he'd said—

"You didn't really have any chance of it working, did you."

"Ferris..."

"It's 'Blue Serpent,' you know. Wouldn't want 'Silver Fox' to hear that and get on my case — same goes for you, 'Ashen Star,' and you, little 'Librarian.'"

"..."

"You really are good at getting away with being spoiled, 'Ashen Star.' — Honestly, I'm almost jealous."

Struck by that sweet voice paired with an anything-but-sweet, razor-sharp look, Subaru fell silent.
But without allowing any further reply, Ferris too slipped past Subaru and went inside the hunting lodge, leaving only Subaru and Beatrice behind.

"Man, I'm really busting my ass out here, huh, Bea-ko."

"Betty's always watching over how hard Subaru works, closer than anyone, I suppose. ...Everyone's surely fine. They're a tough, unbreakable bunch no matter what, I suppose."

"—Yeah. This isn't the time for us to be crying uncle."

Though it came by way of Russell, they had at least managed to confirm the safety of Emilia and Ram, left behind at the castle. But the whereabouts of Rem and Otto and the others, who had apparently fled, remained unknown.
Still, he told himself their safety was implied, if indirectly — reasoning that if the enemy, who would exploit absolutely anything, had actually caught them, they'd surely have announced it far and wide. A grim sort of trust in just how opportunistic their foe was.

In any case—

"—Man, at this point it's not just Emiliase I'm running low on — I need my Remsitine, my Ramnoic acid, my Garfeine, my Ottogen, my Petramide, my Meilitol, my Frederimine too! Oh, and throw in some Roswanol while you're at it!"

"Can't be helped, I suppose. For now, you'll just have to make do with some Beatromin, I suppose."

"Whoa, absorbing Beatromin! Bea-ko inhalation, engage!"

Thinking of his companions, unseen for so long, and the feelings that kept building for them, Subaru scooped Beatrice up, hugged her light body close, and spun around and around.
As he spun, he prayed to the twinkling Stars overhead. — For everyone's safety, and for the day they would meet again.

"'Ashen Star'! 'Librarian'! I told you to come inside! I'd rather not have to repeat myself!"

So rang out, high into the night sky over the forest, Russell's scolding voice — one Subaru had heard so many times these past two months.
