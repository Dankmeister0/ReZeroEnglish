Arc 1 Chapter 12: "The Witch of Reunion"

"—Hey, kid, don't space out. Appa, you want one?"

The moment his consciousness surfaced, what lay before Subaru's eyes was a ripe, red fruit.
Looking at something that resembled an apple so closely, an idle, useless thought crossed his mind—wasn't that also called the fruit of knowledge? A forbidden fruit that, once eaten, gets you cast out of paradise.
If he bit into it now, would it save him from this incomprehensible situation?

"Oi, kid?"

The middle-aged man frowned and called out to Subaru, who wasn't reacting at all.
Subaru regarded him from the hazy edge of a still-unfocused consciousness, then jerked his head up as if startled, eyes sweeping his surroundings.

An early-afternoon street, in front of a street vendor's stall. The shopfront, arranged like a produce stand, was lined here and there with vegetables and fruits in every color. Each of them looked familiar and yet subtly wrong.
The street was choked with a suffocating crowd, hair colors that couldn't exist in his original world and races that were, quite literally, impossible, passing by as though it were the most natural thing in the world.

All of it was filled with a quiet clamor, and Subaru raked his hands through his hair.
His working left arm brushed his stomach, and he felt nothing but ordinary flesh there—no trace of spilled entrails, no mark of eyes torn open, nothing at all.

"I don't— I don't get it anymore..."

Muttering only that, Subaru was overwhelmed by rising nausea and dizziness, and his knees gave out beneath him.

"Here, water. Can you drink?"

He accepted the earthenware cup held out to him and sipped at the water poured into it in small, careful mouthfuls.
The water was ice-cold, spreading through his parched mouth from the tip of his tongue, and it brought some measure of relief to his darkly sunken spirits.

"Ah, that's good. Thanks. —Old man, you're seriously a good guy."

"Cut it out. Picture this—someone keels right over in front of my stall. Looks like they ate my goods and dropped dead, and that's no joke to laugh about. Just rest in the shade till you settle down."

Saying that without a trace of embarrassment, the scar-faced shopkeeper turned back toward his stall.
Watching his back from where he still sat on the ground, Subaru let out a deep breath, leaning against the shop wall at the edge of the street.
It was this by-now-familiar vegetable vendor who had caught Subaru as he grew dizzy and nearly collapsed.
Despite his rough appearance, the man had taken one look at Subaru and judged it heatstroke, then briskly prepared a wet towel and drinking water and carried him here to rest in the shade.
You really can't judge anyone by appearances. A muscle-bound, bald old man might turn out to be a kindly grandfather, just as a sultry, alluring woman might turn out to be a pleasure killer.

"My stomach and my eyes were supposed to be done for, though..."

He murmured that, touched his eyelids, and confirmed once more that he could clearly sense light there. The wound on his stomach was gone too, of course, and his left shoulder, which should have been shattered, was intact. Every last injury had been healed yet again, and he'd apparently been left, out of some sense of consistency, on a vegetable seller's doorstep.

"Still... that was... that was rough."

He lowered his hand from his eyelids and brought it up before his face.
Though it wasn't touching anything, his fingers trembled faintly. That trembling spread gradually up his arm, until at last it became a chill that seized his entire body and squeezed.
Just remembering it made his teeth chatter uncontrollably, and Subaru buried his head between his raised knees, his body shaking with a terror that pressed in on him, swallowing even the noise of the crowd.

It was the embodiment of despair—hideous, unknowable, utterly unreasonable.
He had never been exposed to killing intent like that, had never experienced violence that inflicted pain like that, had no memory of his body ever having been destroyed like that.

His companion's life and his own had been taken, and Subaru's heart had been so thoroughly shattered by that terror that all he could do now was curl up like this.
Even the thought of facing that abnormal existence again—he refused to even consider it.

"Don't think about it, don't think about it, this is stupid. There's nothing to be done about it, nothing I can do. Something like that—you die. There's nothing to do but die against something like that. I should just be glad I got my life back, right?"

Seeking agreement from no one in particular, Subaru desperately begged for absolution.
In his mind's eye, Rom the old man, brutally slaughtered, and Felt, cut down in her final moments, played back on a loop. Following them came the memory of pain and terror—being sliced, crushed, opened up, robbed of light.

Feeling nausea and murky negative emotion welling up, Subaru impulsively poured the water from the cup he was still holding over his own head.
It wasn't much, but it was enough to douse his whole head in cold water.
Wiping away the water dripping from his bangs, he slapped his own face several times, trying to shift his mood.

"Snap out of it, yeah, that's it, shake it off. Simple as that. Just forget about a place like that. There's way more stuff I should be doing, thinking about, right now. Like, for instance—another world. I need to figure out how to survive here. If I keep agonizing, trouble's gonna show up, and then—"

—And I'll get saved again, and want to repay that debt, and end up nearly dying for it again.

"That's not— that's not what I'm— I just said that!"

No matter how hard he tried not to think of her, the silver-haired girl kept shining in his memory.
To begin with, nearly everything that had happened to Subaru in this world had started because of her. So he couldn't talk about this world without her occupying his thoughts.
But whenever he thought of her, it circled back around, eventually, to that memory of agony and despair.

His thoughts kept spiraling downward, over and over.
The trembling wouldn't stop, and his gloomy thoughts, trapped in an endless loop, couldn't find even a glimmer of hope.
What he should do, how he could move forward instead of staying frozen in place—Subaru couldn't grasp even the thread of an answer.

"Hey, kid, how are you feeling now? Any better?"

Poking just his face out from the front of his stall, the scar-faced man asked, solicitous as ever.
For all his tough-guy act, there was a natural consideration in him that betrayed real kindness. Not wanting to keep imposing on that kindness, Subaru decided he ought to get moving and leave.

"Yeah, I think I've settled down some. Sorry for the trouble."

Somehow he managed to stand without looking too pathetic and returned the now-empty cup to the shopkeeper. The man took it back with a look like he wanted to say something more, but didn't press further.

"I'll repay you sometime, for sure."

"Don't need it. If you've got money someday, just come buy some of my Appa."

He gave a light poke to the shoulder of the still-solemn-faced Subaru, raised a hand, and went back into his shop.
Feeling a certain warmth toward that brisk, no-nonsense attitude, Subaru took a deep breath and stepped out into the street. The foot traffic had kept moving the whole time Subaru sat there sulking with his knees drawn up, and the bustle of the main street lined with vendor stalls showed no sign of dimming.

"...To the Loot House."

He should go, he thought, vaguely.
He thought it, but his feet wouldn't turn in that direction. Going that way meant facing that terror again.
If, by some cruel mistake, he ran into 'Elsa' again, that would be the moment Subaru threw away the life he'd only just managed to reclaim.
Summoned to another world, having accomplished nothing at all, only to rot away and end there.

"Ha. Well, that's the same as just running away right now, isn't it."

A self-mocking word and smile rose to his lips, and Subaru put his hands in his pockets and started to walk.
In the direction opposite the Loot House. That was the conclusion Subaru had reached.
Throw everything away, sink into a sea of resignation, forget the debt he owed and pretend not to see it.
That's how he'd lived before coming here too. He was, fundamentally, a conflict-avoider by nature. Debts, obligations, human connections—he'd always thought them a bother and lived accordingly.
So why, now, of all times—

"Eh...?"

Just as he raised his head and took that first step of retreat, a bewildered sound slipped from Subaru's mouth.
In his widening field of vision, the flow of passersby moved around Subaru, who had stopped in his tracks, giving him a wide berth. There was a tall figure with reptilian skin, a beast-person whose height barely reached Subaru's waist, a young pink-haired dancer, a swordsman with as many as six swords hanging from his belt, and—


—there, walking with a white robe draped over her shoulders, silver hair swaying, was the girl.


The girl glanced once at Subaru, who had stopped, and shifted her body slightly to avoid brushing against him as she passed by his side.
Her long silver hair, bound into a single tail, swayed, and a scent like flowers mixed on the wind teased his nostrils. Her amethyst eyes, sharp with strong will, were no longer looking at Subaru at all—they held only the keen focus of someone gazing straight down the path she meant to walk.

Her dignified bearing unchanged, her trembling beauty undimmed, the presence he had longed for was about to pass right in front of him.

"Wa—"

The voice caught in his throat, and he couldn't get the word out in time, chasing after the retreating back with a strangled sound.
The girl walked on smoothly, weaving through the crowd with ease. Chasing after the fleeing silver hair through a haze of bewilderment, confusion, and disorientation, Subaru called out in a voice on the verge of tears.

"Wait, please... wait... Please, wait..."

For an instant, when she glanced back at him, her eyes were as cold as if looking at a total stranger.
For her, it had only been a few hours—a chance encounter with someone she'd crossed paths with in passing. She might well see him as someone to resent, someone who hadn't even kept the most basic of warnings and had endangered himself as a result.
Even knowing she might think of him that way, Subaru still chased after her back.
He had no way of knowing what she really thought of him. So at the very least, he wanted her to tell him.
Better to be hurt by a painful truth than to be wounded by feelings he could only imagine.

"Please, wait. —Satella!"

What words did he even want to exchange with her, once he'd stopped her?
The instant a clear answer formed within himself, Subaru found he had shouted her name as though only just remembering it.

That cry cut cleanly through the street's noise and reached the retreating back.
The silver-haired girl came to an abrupt halt.
Subaru pushed through the crowd toward the girl who had stopped, and reached out to touch her slender shoulder.

"Please, don't ignore me. It really was my fault, disappearing like that. But I didn't understand what was happening either. I went looking for you at the Loot House afterward too, and I still couldn't find you—"

Satella, her shoulder touched, showed surprise on her face.
As she turned to face him, all that spilled from Subaru's mouth once he opened it were excuses. Rapid-fire, self-defensive words, as if he were afraid of being hurt.
Maybe it was because of the gaze she turned on him—one so utterly clear and piercing.

And yet, even under that gaze, Subaru felt something beyond his anxious urgency: relief.
At a glance, there didn't seem to be any obvious wounds on Satella's body. Like Subaru, had she also received treatment from someone after that incident at the Loot House? Or perhaps she'd healed herself. But more important than any of that was—

"Sorry, I'm just talking about myself. ...But I'm glad you're safe."

That the two of them had managed to meet again like this—that was, above all else, what made him happy.
And once he thought that, the next thing on his mind was the safety of her companion—Puck. There were far too many things Subaru needed to apologize to him for in particular.
He'd broken a promise between men. That was a sin that deserved reproach.

"You..."

As the rapid-fire Subaru fell silent, Satella's lips trembled in his place.
It had only been a few hours, and yet it felt as though he hadn't heard that silver-bell voice in a very long time.
Her figure was right in front of him, and he was even touching her like this, and yet an oddly misplaced sense of finally having caught up to her filled Subaru. But—

"Just what do you think you're doing—?"

What she turned on Subaru, who was busy accepting his own relief, was a face twisted with fury, eyes narrowed sharp.
Her pale cheeks flushed faintly, Satella twisted her body slightly and shook off Subaru's hand from her shoulder. She stepped back to put distance between them, and the eyes looking up at him now burned with open hostility.

Faced with a far harsher reaction than he'd expected, Subaru caught his breath and fell silent.
But thinking about it, that was only natural. From her perspective, what nerve did he have, showing his face like this.
Whatever abuse she hurled at him, he was ready to accept it without complaint.

That resolve of Subaru's was—

"I don't know who you are, but calling someone by the name of 'the Witch of Envy'—just what do you think you're doing!?"

—shattered to pieces by a burst of fury from a direction he never expected.

Struck by that unforeseen accusation, Subaru felt as if time itself had stopped.
All sound had drained from the crowded street. All he could hear was the pounding of his own heart and the breathing of the silver-haired girl, her shoulders squared with anger.
It felt as if every other sound had vanished—no, it wasn't just a feeling.

"What... is this?"

Muttering, Subaru turned his head stiffly and realized.
All around, on this main street thick with vendors and passersby, everyone was now staring fixedly at the two of them.
A deep unease had surfaced on every face, and everyone stood frozen, as if forbidden from so much as flinching.
As though this single conversation between Subaru and Satella now held total dominion over the entire place.

"I asked you what you think you're doing. Stop being silent."

But Satella wouldn't allow Subaru's bewildered hesitation.
She condemned him in a harsh tone, and he couldn't even manage a rebuttal to an accusation he had no memory of deserving.
Subaru and she were simply arguing over two entirely different points.

"I'll ask you once more. —Why do you call me by the name 'the Witch of Envy'?"

"No, I mean, because... you told me to call you that—"

"I don't know who told you to, but that's a tasteless joke to play, and a worse one to go along with. —The symbol of taboo itself, 'the Witch of Envy.' To choose a name people are too afraid to even speak aloud as someone's nickname—"

Her disgust plain on her face, Satella—the silver-haired girl—plunged Subaru into a sea of confusion.
Every single person in the crowd surrounding them nodded along with her words. That, in turn, only proved the correctness of what she was saying, which pushed Subaru's mind even further into bewilderment.

He had no idea what he was being accused of.
Subaru had simply called her by her name.
And yet she condemned him for it, and everyone around affirmed that she was right to do so.
Without even knowing what he'd done wrong, he was simply being scolded for being wrong. There was no way to reflect on it, no way to correct his attitude.

"—If you have nothing else, I'm leaving. I'm not exactly free either."

Cutting off Subaru, who could only hang his head, she said that and turned, her silver hair flaring as she strode briskly away. Subaru tried to call out to her retreating back, but the name froze in his throat before he could speak it.
Calling her by that name again would be a second offense. But then, what should he call her instead?

That hesitation dulled Subaru's judgment.
And so, he was left to watch, thumb in his mouth, as the events unfolding before him slipped past.

"—————!"

A small gasp came from a spot a head higher than Subaru's own height—from atop the canvas awning of a vendor's stall.
A leap. A small body, pulled by gravity, dropped lightly, and the instant it landed, it caught the wind and accelerated.
The gust wore threadbare clothes, golden hair streaming behind it. It slipped through the crowd with an almost supernatural fluidity of movement, and a hand shot out, invading the inside of the robe embroidered with a hawk.

The contact lasted only an instant, but for the wind, that two seconds of encounter had been more than enough.
The wind sent the robe fluttering and, as the girl twisted her body, sprang back and away like a rebound.

"No way—!"

The silver-haired girl let out a cry of shock and thrust a hand inside her own robe.
Not finding what she was looking for there, her widened eyes chased after the rapidly retreating shape of the wind.
Seeing the dragon-shaped emblem clutched in that gust's hand, and its retreating figure, Subaru shouted on reflex.

"Felt!?"

The wind seemed to falter for an instant at the call. But its speed didn't slacken, and in one motion it dove from the main street into a narrow alley.
A dazzling display of speed. And that brief, split-second encounter with the wind that had pulled it off. He'd only glimpsed it for an instant, but that figure was, in all likelihood—

"She got me. So that's what the delay was for... You're in on it too!?"

Unable to keep up with the whirlwind of events, Satella growled resentfully at the stock-still Subaru.
She raised a palm toward him for a moment, but seemed to reconsider and instead took off running, hurling herself into the alley where the wind had vanished.

"Hey, wait! It's a misunderstanding! I—!"

Trying to clear up that entirely wrong-headed misunderstanding, Subaru too chased the two shadows into the alley.
As he ran, his mind filled with bewildered questions.
There was simply too much information being thrown at him all at once, and his flustered head couldn't process it. Even setting that aside, he'd already nearly died twice today and was thoroughly rattled.

"Somebody be nicer to me for once! What's the point of being summoned to another world if it's like this!?"

Cursing the unfairness of it all, he staggered and sprinted down the dim alley.
He had no confidence in his stamina. But over a short distance, his speed was a match for either of them. He'd catch up to that back soon enough and clear up this confusion.
That was his plan as he ran, but—

"Damn it... a wall!?"

Right in front of Subaru, who spat the curse out, stood a dead-end alley, blocked off entirely.
Neither of the two he'd been chasing was anywhere in sight. If his memory served, Felt was nimble enough to scale a wall with ease. Satella too, if she used magic, could probably clear a wall or two without much trouble.

"I could climb it too, but... I doubt I'd catch up even then."

More than that, if the distance between them opened up, Subaru wouldn't be able to keep running for long.
He could build muscular strength cooped up indoors, but aerobic endurance wasn't something you could train inside. His lack of stamina was a serious weakness of Subaru's, and it held him back from that decision here too.

"If this is a dead end, then... the Loot House? If Satella and Felt are both alive, then... maybe Rom, too—"

He decided he should get ahead of them and hurry to rejoin Rom.
For now, his priority was to get out of this dead end and head for the slums. With that, Subaru turned around, and—

"...You've gotta be kidding me."

There, in the direction he turned, he noticed figures blocking the alley's entrance.
Three of them, shabbily dressed, their coarse, violent nature written plainly on their faces. Obstacles standing in Subaru's way, so tiresomely familiar in shape that it barely bore describing.

A trio of thugs who hunted this back alley as their territory—and, on this day, Subaru's third encounter of its kind.
