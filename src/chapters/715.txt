Arc 9 Chapter 20: "How to Spend a Life"

Light tears through the pitch-black darkness, and a thread of hope sets the heart boiling.
The hopeless end pressing in right before your eyes feels like something that could be pushed away, driven back, escaped, if only you forced it — and expectation makes your heart surge.

But no. You aren't that strong.
You'd thought you knew it. That you weren't strong. — Truly, you'd only thought so.

Knowing nothing of the truth, like a nestling merely begging for food, you pecked away at each second you were given, never knowing what had been spent to buy that second.

"Emilia...?"

Cold water is dashed over your boiling heart, and your pulse leaps hard.
From beyond a veil black as darkness itself, a face you must never look upon peers out.

Long eyelashes, beautiful violet-blue eyes, finely-shaped features, thin cherry-blossom lips — the beloved you.
Beyond the veil, the face that should be most hateful is the same as the one most beloved.

"—!"

In that instant of hesitation, freedom is stolen away, and even the soul is caged by an eternal obsession.
The means to refuse it lies in your hand as a scrap of white cloth wrapped in light.

Stop, you screamed, more fiercely than you ever had before.
Make it stop, you clung to that plea more desperately than you ever had before.
Please stop it for her sake, you wished more fervently than you ever had before.

But none of it was in time, none of it was enough, none of it —

"I will — without fail —"

"— save you. I swear it."

You, who held that resolve and vow in your heart — Natsuki Subaru — 'I' killed you.

— From the impact, Aldebaran's whole body crumples in midair.

A massive fist, easily the size of his own head, struck the body that had been relying on the steel thread wound around the great tree for support.
The boulder-like fist connected to an arm as thick as a log to match, and that arm extended from a torso clad in even thicker, heavier armor of muscle. That torso was wrapped in a black suit ill-suited to a battlefield, worn by a giant, pig-faced man —

"Don't go squealing like a pig."

The pig-faced man snorted through his pig snout, delivering the dull joke in a low, growling voice.
For all his nearly two-meter height and a weight surely no less than two hundred kilograms, Pig-face closed on the midair Aldebaran with a cannonball-like leap and delivered a blow.
It was a total ambush, and surviving it without dying instantly was nothing short of a miracle — no, not a miracle.

"...At that rate, is your arm headed for tonight's dinner table?"

Beside Aldebaran, who had just taken that ferocious blow, Yae murmured with an expressionless face.
Her narrowed eyes were fixed on Pig-face's outstretched arm — on the fist that had struck Aldebaran, coming apart from the fingertips inward in neat little cubes.

Yae's steel threads, strung through the air, had caught Pig-face's punch head-on — and the sheer force of his massive body had driven his own arm into the unyielding threads, shredding it to pieces.
As a result, Aldebaran escaped instant death, and Pig-face lost an arm.
But —

"Indeed. I'll feed it to my subordinates as a reward. Though a lopsided diet makes for fatty meat."

"Kyaa~, I just love it when Demi-Humans make racist jokes! By the way, I'd recommend stewing meat that's high in fat."

"Then let's stew it in blood wrung out of the likes of you."

Both Pig-face, who'd lost an arm, and Yae, whose trump card had just been muscled through, kept up an air of composure on the surface — even as their eyes held murderous intent pushed to its very limit — and exchanged these words with impeccable manners.
It was all well and good for these two, who'd slipped free of any ordinary yoke, to keep up their one-rank-higher conversation, but Aldebaran, one rank below, was already at his limit.

His body, which had been kept from falling only by being pressed against the great tree, slid and tilted at an angle, unable to muster the strength to hold itself up any longer, and was swallowed into free fall.
He'd escaped instant death, but unfortunately his head had split open, and inside his helmet things that really shouldn't be spilling out were probably sloshing around.
Naturally, he couldn't keep going from here in that state, so —

"Y'ae."

— he called for Yae with lips that couldn't quite form the word properly.
The next instant, the steel thread wound around the falling Aldebaran's neck snapped taut — his vision jolted wildly, and his neck and torso parted ways in tears.
It was a system meant to leave no possible gap — a safety valve so that even if Aldebaran failed to kill himself, Yae could still trigger his Authority.

"—"

Even as he confirmed the effect taking hold, watching the world spin and tumble before his eyes, Aldebaran steeled himself: *Here we go.*
And then —

"N'xt."

"—Al Goa!!"

A chant that sounded like a furious roar, followed by what looked like a second sun swelling up in red — or rather, a papier-mâché fireball made to look like one; the thinly spread flame was as flimsy as a goldfish-scooping net.
Look closely and the control was sloppy — leave it alone for even three seconds and it looked ready to scatter into nothing from the edges inward, a shoddy piece of fakery. And yet, mixed into the whirlwind of a hastily assembled battlefield, even that functioned as a perfectly serviceable ploy.

"Pops! That's a fake—"

"Uwaaaaaaargh!!"

As a result — and partly because he'd only just returned — Aldebaran's warning came too late.
Heinkel, in a panic, yanked frantically on the steel thread, and the fallen-tree cannons that had already been readied for launch fired off one after another, hurtling straight into the fake Al Goa.
Since he hadn't been able to stop it, the outcome was the same as before. — Aldebaran clicked his tongue inwardly. Not at Heinkel, who wouldn't listen, nor at Rachins, who covered his unpolished skill with sheer nerve, but at himself, for botching it.

Once the unimpressive explosion went off, despite its appearance, the other two — who unlike Aldebaran hadn't already had the answer sheet — finally caught on to the enemy's decoy.
Regret came too late, and unable to halt the charging enemy's momentum, Aldebaran borrowed Yae's strength to block the charge with the same cage of steel thread as before.
This time, though, so as not to repeat what happened before, instead of fleeing up into a tree he deliberately leapt backward, standing beside Heinkel to change the situation.
This time, then—

"—What's this, slow as a pig?"

The blow he'd braced for arrived straight from above, right along with that cruel black humor. Struck by a savage blow, Aldebaran was driven headfirst into the forest floor.
He'd changed position. He'd been on guard, too. And still, the attack came smothering down from above regardless.

"Wha— From where—? Guh!"

"Don't squeal like a pig. Or I'll line you up at the butcher's."

Coming in with the force of a missile, his whole body wounded by steel threads, the blood-drenched Pig-face swung his arm and slammed the helpless Heinkel into the great tree behind him.
As for Aldebaran, buried in the ground, his spine seemed to be done for — none of his fighting spirit was reaching his limbs at all. Yae, who'd leapt down, seemed to have begun clashing with Pig-face, but he couldn't even raise his head to watch.

Still, a doubt worth verifying had arisen. — Whether in midair or on the ground, Pig-face had somehow gone straight for Aldebaran with the first strike, every single time.
With just that piece of information—

"—Next."

Before Yae, up in the air, could deliver the final blow, Aldebaran swallowed the poison himself.
A ferocious, overwhelming, lethal heat burned through Aldebaran from throat to innards—

"—Al Goa!!"

"Uwaaaaaaargh!!"

Out of the corner of his eye, Aldebaran watched the meaningless resistance shaped like a great tree go flying, spooked by the fake sun, while he swiftly scanned his surroundings, searching for the next place to fall back to — somewhere that was neither midair nor the ground.

Verification was necessary. — So as not to let go of the meager best he had.

—Seventy-three times.

The matrix that restarted from the moment the fake Al Goa rose into the sky — verifying it from every possible angle had revealed one thing: no matter where Aldebaran fled or hid, Pig-face's mighty arm came, every single time, without fail, to grind him to paste.
In other words—

"My whereabouts are leaking straight through to him."

Whether beside Yae or beside Heinkel, on the ground or in the air or in the shade of a tree or putting distance between himself and the battlefield, Aldebaran could not escape Pig-face's fist.
A punch thrown with the resolve to lose an arm for it was hard to fully block even with Yae's Steel Thread Technique. — Of course, merely weathering the first strike was possible with a combination of Yae's steel thread and Aldebaran's magic.
But even if they weathered that one blow, he still had one more arm, and for that matter two legs as well. And Pig-face had the sheer will to bring his target down no matter how many limbs he lost doing it.

Neither pain nor loss gave that pig-faced man any reason to stop.
And yet, whenever he tried to get Pig-face to talk, to pry out even a scrap of information about who he was—

"—Don't squeal like a piglet."

That one line shattered the attempt; he wouldn't listen at all.
In Aldebaran's experience, an opponent who had fully decided on what he was going to do and hardened his resolve to never budge from it was a troublesome one.
Against this sort, all of Aldebaran's trial and error lost its meaning. Because someone who's decided to kill you no matter the cost will kill you no matter the cost.
Which meant that changing the situation drastically required some trigger, some drastic lever.

"First — how are they pinning down my location?"

Some hand had been played to relentlessly track Aldebaran down and finish him off for certain.
To begin with, it was unnatural that, despite even using the Divine Dragon as a diversion, Aldebaran and the others were being hounded through the forest like this.
If he didn't expose, here and now, the method behind this completed encirclement, then even if he dodged Pig-face and drove off Felt and the others, a second and third arrow would follow, and the seven days would be wasted.
To keep that from happening—

"—What's this, slow as a pig?"

The all-too-familiar dull joke from Pig-face arrived along with the punch, and this time it was Aldebaran's own cervical spine that snapped, after he'd tried to use Rachins as a meat shield.
As an aside, Rachins, caught in the collateral, had also had a thoroughly miserable time of it — that much grazed the corner of his eye an instant before his consciousness cut out—

"—Al Goa!!"

"Uwaaaaaaargh!!"

A fake great spell rose into the sky, and the panicking Heinkel jumped the gun.
Keeping the now-familiar flash of the fireball's explosion at the edge of his sight, Aldebaran called over Yae — who wore a face of exasperation at Heinkel's runaway antics — and had her string up a separate thread, apart from the cage meant to lure in Rachins and the others.
This new launching platform of steel thread, the same one that had been flinging fallen trees this whole time — what it would launch next was—

"Pull!!"

Heinkel, eyes rolling, thought stalled out entirely, pulled the thread just as told. Propelled by that extraordinary strength of his, it was Aldebaran and Yae who traced a parabola up into the sky.
Savoring the fierce sensation of being torn free from wind and gravity alike, Aldebaran, holding Yae with one arm, sailed clear over the tops of the forest's trees, breaking away from the battlefield Rachins and his men were converging on.

Left behind alone, Heinkel would surely get roughed up by the avalanche of Rachins's men pouring in, but this round was a sacrifice — spent to confirm what needed confirming.
For the duration of this verification, Aldebaran would close his eyes to Heinkel's noble sacrifice.

"Please don't bite your tongue now, okay~!"

With a sidelong glance at the Heinkel he'd left behind, guilt and all, Yae, in his arms, swung her arms just so.
Around the base of the five slender fingers of her already-slender hands were plain, unadorned rings, and from them extended threads of steel so fine they were nearly invisible even on close inspection.
The Steel Thread Technique — a skill among the shinobi renowned for their outstanding mastery, one that, even among them, only she had properly managed to learn — she wielded to its fullest, casting threads onto the trees they flew toward one after another, gaining distance rapidly Tarzan-rope-style, carried by the momentum of their parabolic arc.
However—

"—!"

The path that Aldebaran and Yae, swinging with their whole bodies, cut through was the escape route the smoke had been herding them down — that is, the area still relentlessly choked in white smoke.
By forcing their way through with steel thread, Aldebaran and the others reversed course along the white-smoke escape route they'd been driven down, flying now toward the final confirmation at the end of all their accumulated verification.
Still, though they could cover speed and distance beyond any comparison to running along the ground, their only chance to breathe came at the peak height of each swing — the instant they broke clear of the smoke above the treetops.
And even that grew harder the further they went, as the smoke thickened and rose ever higher, an unrelenting agony beginning to invade their lungs, their eyes, their mucous membranes.

Keep flying around recklessly like this, and while Yae, being superhuman, would be fine, Aldebaran — with nothing more than middling, average specs — would be done in by the smoke in short order.
So he had to pick their destination with care—

"—Northwest."

He put his mouth close to Yae's ear as she extended her steel thread and swung, and gave that order.
The target was northwest, no mistake. — Because he'd already confirmed every other direction by now.
That the answer wouldn't come up right until the very last try showed just how thoroughly luck had abandoned him, through and through. Not that he'd ever once, since he was born, thought himself blessed with luck.

"...No, wait. There was one time."

— Meeting her, at least, had unquestionably been Aldebaran's good fortune. Even now, having ultimately lost her, he never once thought that meeting her had been a mistake.

"—!"

He clenched his teeth hard, holding back the surge of raw feeling welling up in his chest, and in that instant the situation changed.
The moment their swing broke through the white smoke and they burst out above the treetops, thrown stones came hurtling in from beyond the smoke at ferocious speed, aimed straight at Aldebaran and Yae in midair.
Mere thrown stones, yes, but with a force nothing to scoff at, they closed in like hail, and Aldebaran hastily threw up a wall of earth, forcing his way through the first wave with it.
But the stones would keep coming after that. So before they did — separation.

"—Al-sama!!"

Yae's high voice echoed inside his helmet, and Aldebaran, feeling a numbness ring through his eardrums, lost his connection with her in midair and was flung high up into the sky.
At the very peak of their swing's arc, she had hurled him even further onward.

"Ohhhhhhh—!!"

Roaring, he covered the front of his hurtling body with a wall of earth and dove headfirst into the welcome of thrown stones.
Unstable, and unable to be built thick enough given the weight involved, the earthen wall was flimsy, protecting him from the stones raining down about as well as if he'd merely pulled a blanket over himself.
But even so, Aldebaran punched through the white smoke and made it out of the forest once again.

Out into that same plain where he'd received the very first declaration of war, and been neatly caught by Grassis's strike as vengeance for her sister.
And there, just as Aldebaran had hoped—

"—So you came alone to seize the flagbearer. That's the sort of poor plan better counted from the bottom of the list."

Below him, a bald-headed old Giant looked up at Aldebaran as he burst from the white smoke and said so. — On that thick, long Giant's arm was fitted a gauntlet, worn by the man himself, set with multiple Dialogue Mirrors.
Seeing that, it clicked. — Every trap had been the old Giant's design.

"You're the brains behind this...!"

The gap between his appearance and the role was too large; Aldebaran hadn't even imagined it.
It would've made more sense, casting-wise, to be told that Rachins, with his thug-like look, was the strategic mastermind. Normally, Giants would be front-line fighters, making the most of their physicality. For them to be the ones handling the brainwork instead — same as with Ezzo — a Dwarf and a Giant each cast against type like this was almost too on-the-nose.

Filling his head to overflowing with that useless griping, Aldebaran realized something.

"Felt, the kid—"

—was nowhere to be seen anywhere on the plain. — This, too, was a blow to Aldebaran.
Naturally, wiping out five hundred enemies to end this fight wasn't a realistic option. So, as an achievable goal, securing Felt's person had always been in the back of Aldebaran's mind. And that option had been neatly sealed off by the other side.
Of course — his own most obvious win condition was, from their side, the most obvious loss condition. Infuriating as it was, pulling Felt back was the reasonable move.

"Damn it."

Aldebaran's victory wasn't shaken. It was already settled.
So all that was left was finding the way to win — and yet.

"Hey, don't you go looking away from me!!"

"—!"

In the instant he spent his mind updating his win conditions, an impact from directly above tore through that same head.
The voice he heard was deep, but the words weren't the usual dull Pig-face joke. Looking up, he saw it was the big-built thug who'd leapt up beside Aldebaran, higher than the white smoke, and driven a hammer-blow down with clasped hands — a strike from Gaston.

"...You're kidding me."

Aldebaran let out a groan of pain at an attack that had unmistakably come from someone who'd mastered the Flow Method.
The Flow Method wasn't something you picked up with ordinary training — it was the gateway that decided whether you could become a first-rate warrior. With this added threat now confirmed, Aldebaran revised his estimate of the enemy's strength, which he'd underestimated.
Even as he revised it, that single blow sent him crashing down to the ground.

"Guh— Guaaahh...!"

He failed to fall properly in time, and his right arm, thrust out at a bad angle, snapped at the elbow. Writhing in the pointless agony of broken bone tearing through flesh and skin, Aldebaran ground his helmet against the dirt.
It hurt, it hurt, it hurt it hurt it hurt it hurt it hurt, it hurt, it hurt — but he'd seen what he needed to see.

"From here on... it's a battle of wits... between me and you..."

Grinding his clenched teeth hard enough to crack them, faced with the mounting suffering of the drifting smoke invading his lungs, he opened the poison packet with his tongue, a motion he knew all too well.
The smoke was so thick that he coughed, nearly spitting the poison right back out. He doubted the old Giant had planned even that far, but the problem was that his bad rolls kept coming up far too often.
He had to cut off this run of bad luck. And beyond that, search — search for the path to victory that had to exist somewhere, search, search, search search search search, search search saahch saarch saaach saach sa—ah.

"—No need to add any more pyro trees. Any further and there's a risk of the enemy turning it to his advantage. Better that our scheme falls a touch short than overreach."

Giving the order to wind down the smoke-driven pursuit operation, Valga Cromwell — no, the former grand strategist of the Demi-Human Alliance now known as Rom Gramps — turned his aged mind over with everything he had.

The encirclement of the man known as "Helmet Bastard" — the knight of the late Priscilla Barielle — was complete; all that remained was to slowly reel him in. But carelessness was forbidden.
This opponent had used the Divine Dragon Volcanica and the Witch of Envy to devise a method for sealing away the Sword Saint, Reinhard van Astrea. Rom couldn't help but marvel at such uncannily brilliant scheming.

Still, the existence of such a dangerous, cunning "enemy" was exactly what Rom Gramps had wanted.
Not for some ridiculous reason like — the Giant Tribe, most of whom were fiercely battle-loving and rough by nature, having instincts that craved a blood-stirring fight.
What he wanted was a peril grave enough to shake the kingdom, and the honor of eliminating it.

— For Felt to win through the Royal Selection, he wanted fame that didn't rest on the Sword Saint's presence.

The events at the floodgate city of Priestella, where the Royal Selection candidates drove back a great disaster caused by the Witch Cult, were still fresh in memory, but that was an achievement that raised all five candidates' standing uniformly.
Their relative standing within the Royal Selection hadn't changed. So Felt needed an achievement of her own alone.
Helmet Bastard's actions, which threatened to shake not just the Royal Selection but the whole world, were more than sufficient for that.

"Of course, Felt herself surely wasn't thinking of any of this."

Felt had stood to face Helmet Bastard purely because, faced with the peril bearing down on her, she happened to have the power to resist it, and for the sake of her First Knight, who bore the duty of being the first to charge into a situation like this — not out of any such calculating, long-term perspective.
So it was fine that the only ones scheming to make use of this situation for later gain were himself — sly despite his great size — and the thoroughly corrupt adults who ruled the underworld.
Using the rough men of the three great organizations famed in the "City of Earth Dragons," Flanders — the Balance, the Flower Prison Garden, and the Black Silver Coin — he would win the achievement needed to place the crown on Felt's head.

— At first, Rom Gramps had been firmly, resolutely opposed to Felt taking part in the Royal Selection at all, to her getting involved with the core of something as vast as the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica.

That was only natural, coming from Rom Gramps, who knew of Felt's birth and the karmic burden of the blood flowing through her.
And yet, even so, Rom Gramps had kept staying in the royal capital together with Felt. If he'd wanted to, he could have taken Felt, before she was even old enough to remember, and lived out in some remote countryside far from the capital instead.
Yet Rom Gramps hadn't done that. Probably, he thought, because somewhere in his heart he'd had a premonition — that an unavoidable, great surge would someday catch up to them.

When that time came, if he could be at Felt's side, he'd make full use of this needlessly huge body of his, playing whatever role was needed — a shield, a suit of armor, anything.
But there was every chance he wouldn't be able to. He, too, had grown old. He felt no imminent decline for now, but from here on, Giant or not, it wouldn't be strange for his own end to come at any time.
That was precisely why he needed it — the certainty that Felt, his beloved granddaughter, would be protected for all eternity.

If that promissory note was the summit of this kingdom, then he would place the crown upon Felt's head.
To that end, he wouldn't hesitate to wield again the command he'd sworn never to wield twice.

"Compared to the old days, the occasions have grown far fewer... but even now, the nightmares won't let go of me."

Nearly half a century had passed by now, and the Demi-Human War was a memory grown distant.
Even so, through all that long, long span of time, the embers of regret had gone on simmering away inside Rom Gramps, kept over a low flame. Now, with the chance to finally see the light of day, the lid comes off that pot.
The foolish Valga Cromwell of back then hadn't been enough. — Making up for that foolishness with time, even as his slow-wittedness remained just as unchanged, Rom Gramps turns his mind to strategy.
In other words—

"From here on, then, it's a battle of wits — between me and Helmet Bastard."

"—Threads? These?"

The moment he leapt into the forest, Rachins had his whole body caught and bound in ultra-fine steel threads, and Aldebaran, standing within arm's reach, listened to him mutter that with frustration.
Aldebaran had deliberately exposed himself, luring the enemy to swarm toward him and springing the trap that way.

Yae's Steel Thread Technique had bound fifty-two enemies.
They were enemies he'd dealt with long enough to have counted the exact number by now, though of course Yae wasn't pinning them down through sheer arm strength.

"I've got these skinny little arms, you can see that, right~? I'm using the trees around us, obstacles, even their own bodies against each other as anchor points, and suspending them while their feet stay right on the ground."

Yae had explained the trick to him, but even hearing the gist of it, Aldebaran hadn't understood a thing.
Regardless, all he needed to know was this: caught in layer upon layer of tangled steel thread, Rachins and the others had almost no way of escaping without Yae willing it, and that was good enough.
With that settled, Aldebaran, glared at by Rachins's four-white eyes as the man sat immobilized right in front of his target, grabbed at his clothes without a word and rifled through his coat.

"Wha— What the hell, you bastard!? I don't swing that way...!"

"Wow~, Al-sama, so bold! No wonder you never took me up on it no matter how much I tried to tempt you. It all makes sense now, and at the same time, my pride as a woman is bouncing right back..."

"Quit screwing around! It's a mirror! Look for a mirror! Someone's carrying one... Pops has one too!"

Pinning down the struggling, escape-seeking Rachins, Aldebaran shouted that at Yae and Heinkel, then went on searching through the belongings of his immobilized opponent, trying to root out the Dialogue Mirror.

— The speed and accuracy of communication had changed the very shape of warfare.
It was the sort of line worn smooth by every piece of fiction across every age and place, but struck now by a concept he'd only ever taken in vaguely, as words on a page, Aldebaran finally felt it for himself.

The old Giant playing the brains had prepared a gauntlet set with numerous Dialogue Mirrors.
With that, the old Giant became the command center of his side, exchanging information in real time and moving five hundred men exactly as he wished. If Aldebaran's location and the number of his allies kept getting shared like that continuously, they'd never be given a moment to breathe again.
So the Dialogue Mirrors held by each unit had to be smashed, without exception.
To that end—

"—Found it!"
"—Found it!"
"—Found it!"

Aldebaran, finding at his fingertips the sensation he'd been searching for, pulled it out and raised it high in triumph — but that joy was instantly painted over by two voices rising almost simultaneously.
"Ah?" Aldebaran turned around, and found that Yae and Heinkel — who, apart from him searching Rachins, had been frisking the other rough men — were also wide-eyed.
— Both of them held a small mirror in hand.

"A Dialogue Mirror is a Meteor that can be duplicated, but even so, the cost is absurdly high."

That Rachins, who was likely this unit's commander, would carry one made sense.
That an aide would carry one too, so the commander wouldn't have to handle communications alone, made sense as well. But for even others besides them to be given one made no sense at all.
No matter how you looked at it, it should have been impossible to equip the bulk of one's forces with Dialogue Mirrors.
Which meant this was—

"—A decoy? These?"

"Idiot."

Rachins's jeer, tongue stuck out, overlapped with Aldebaran's stunned mutter.
Catching sight of the colored ring swaying on the tip of that long tongue, Aldebaran reflexively worked his tongue to break open the poison packet tucked behind his back molar. — That very instant.

"—What's this, slow as a pig?"

The joke he'd heard so many times his ears had gone numb to it arrived along with the punch, and Aldebaran couldn't tell which had severed his lifeline first — the impact, or the ferocious poison.
Still, what crossed his mind wasn't some curse aimed at Pig-face—

"Damn geezer."

It was less an insult than it was, despite everything, praise — for an enemy who kept pulling out moves that shouldn't have been possible, let alone merely tough.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△

"—Threads? These?"

The Goa disguised as Al Goa, the ruse it pulled off successfully, let Rachins charge into the thick of the enemy having minimized the damage from the fallen-tree cannons — but he couldn't hide his shock upon realizing that whatever had sealed his movement was thread so fine he could barely even see it.

If it had just been him alone caught, he could've forced himself to accept it, somehow.
But it wasn't only Rachins whose movement had been sealed — it was the fifty rough men who'd charged into the forest alongside him, all of them together.
He tried to picture in his head just what sort of geometric cage of thread would have to be built throughout the entire forest to pull this off, and gave up. It was that far beyond the ordinary.
But what shocked Rachins even further than the woman who'd bound them all in thread was—

"—Sorry, but I'm gonna break this."

—saying that, Helmet Bastard's action: rifling through the coat of one of the rough men, the one chosen to carry the mirror, pulling out the Dialogue Mirror, dropping it at his feet, and simply crushing it underfoot.

"—Tch, don't screw with me."

Fury, wrung out despite himself, spilled from his mouth, and the thread cinching Rachins bit in a notch deeper. It seemed less a result of him struggling than a warning from the red-haired woman handling the thread, aimed at his cursing.
But in this moment, his anger outweighed even the thread cutting shallow into his flesh.

"What the hell is going on! How the hell did you get it in one shot..."

"Wasn't one shot. Wasn't ten or twenty either. My luck's just that damn bad."

"Hah?"

Rachins ground his teeth hard at Helmet Bastard, who clearly had no intention of giving a straight answer.
By some method or other, Helmet Bastard had seen through the fact that they were using Dialogue Mirrors to coordinate around Cromwell — Rom Gramps. And how thoughtful of him — even if he'd figured it out, ignoring the decoy mirrors meant to buy time and going straight for the real one, without fail, was quite the piece of work.

Even though he'd only just now reined himself in, it reared its head again.
An inferiority complex, welling up inexhaustibly, toward those beyond his own reach.

"Hey, what are we gonna do with these guys. ...We killing all of them?"

Setting Rachins's inner thoughts aside, the one who cut in with that was a middle-aged redhead — Heinkel.
The man he'd heard was Reinhard's father. Sure enough, the red hair, blue eyes, and needlessly splendid knight's sword all carried plenty of echoes of House Astrea.
Given that he'd sided with the rival candidate Priscilla instead of Felt, whom his own son backed, Rachins had already figured him for a fool who couldn't even choose his own footing straight — but this was worse than he'd thought.

"—What an idiot."

Rachins hated Reinhard.
Reinhard, for his part, probably thought nothing of Rachins at all, but in being born to a family with some measure of standing and having a rift with his father, the two of them were the same breed. Of course, there was a difference in the weight of what each of them had been born carrying, and Rachins understood perfectly well that Reinhard would never even consider abandoning what Rachins himself had thrown away out of pure selfishness.

Born, nominally, into a noble family, having run off on the grounds that he couldn't get along with his own family, and now, for whatever reason, working for Felt — he himself was a half-measure through and through.
But even if the trouble Rachins and Reinhard each carried was the same, on one decisive point it differed.
Rachins's father was someone he could respect. He hated the man, but he could respect him.

"But you — you can't, can you."

Reinhard, clashing with a father who didn't deserve any respect and letting it wound his heart over it — that made him an idiot.
So Rachins would just go ahead and remove the reason for Reinhard's misery himself.

"...Goa."

Even bound as he was, he could still chant.
With his whole body caught and creaking in thread, Rachins pulsed the Gate inside him anyway and floated a ball of fire right in front of his own face, drawing Helmet Bastard and the others' guard up.
But what rose up was only a small lump of flame, no bigger than the tip of a thumb.

"Ah, trying to rattle me. Like I'd fall for a cheap trick like that twice!"

As payback for having been fooled earlier by the fake Al Goa's appearance, Heinkel, his face red with anger, swung his knight's sword up and slashed through the fireball.
For a moment, that casual sword-flash looked strangely refined, refined enough to catch the eye — but never mind that.
More than any such musing right now—

"Idiot."

—sticking out his tongue and jeering with all the mockery he could muster took priority.
— He'd stretched Goa thin to make it look like Al Goa. So by that same logic, he could make Al Goa look like nothing more than a tiny little fireball too.

*"Rachins, my boy, you have no exceptional talent, but you're steady. Steady steps will surely be the strength that carries you to wherever it is you want to end up."*

He remembered Ezzo, who'd first taught him the basics of magic, lecturing him pompously with that speech despite his short stature, and Rachins, in a rare moment, silently apologized to him.
Ignoring steadiness entirely, abusing magic by skipping straight past the proper stages — it was the exact thing that would make Ezzo angriest.
But since there'd be no more chances for that lecture to come anyway, this was something like his own way of running from the argument—

"—"

Struck by the sword's edge, the fireball came undone in midair, releasing the vast firepower compressed and stored within it — a hellish flame, meant to burn away the entire space in one stroke, spread out in an explosive burst.
An instant before he himself was swallowed by that flame, Rachins found it hilarious that the lament he'd been indulging in just moments before — *where did my life go so wrong* — didn't come welling up again.

Just like that, Rachins's body, even with a smile on his face, was swallowed by the flame—

"That's far enough."

The life-or-death flame that should have gone off was crushed in the hand of Helmet Bastard, who cut in as if he'd known it was coming all along, and never got to unleash its firepower.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△

—Two hundred sixty-four times.

"—"

Slumped limp in the steel thread binding him, head bowed, Rachins looked as though he'd given up.
But nothing could be further from the truth. Far from giving up, Rachins hadn't the least intention of abandoning, even in this situation, the goal of taking his opponent down.
So—

"That's far enough."

The moment the words left his mouth, he remembered how irritating they sounded, and clicked his tongue inwardly.
Setting that bitter thought aside, Aldebaran shoved Heinkel out of the way and stopped the activation of the Goa he'd carelessly slashed open — no, this time, the great spell it had been disguised as.
He unraveled the structure of the massive firepower rising into the air, meant to burn away the whole area, and scattered it while it was still nothing more than a spark, dispersing it as mana that had lost all direction.

If he failed to stop this, the whole area would be burned away by that towering flame, and Aldebaran and the others too would suffer devastating harm, unable to keep fighting.
Sorry as he was to Rachins's resolve, in trying to pull off a desperate, life-or-death self-destructing attack, throwing away one's life to accomplish something was, in front of Aldebaran, the single most meaningless act there was.

No matter what someone staked their life on, Aldebaran would render every last bit of it null and void.

That's why it was pointless. Risking your life meant nothing at all.
Pointless. Pointless, pointless pointless pointless pointless pointless pointless pointless, pointless.
And yet, pointless as it was—

"Goa..."

"Stop it!!"

Aldebaran roared at Rachins, who kept chanting even after his first spark had been snuffed out, and drove a raised fist into the body of the man who couldn't even move.
He sent a fist across his cheek, drove a kick into his gut, and slugged him in the head again. Unable even to brace himself to endure it, Rachins took it all like a punching bag, plain and simple.
Even granting that Aldebaran was, at best, a second- or third-rate warrior — one who could bring down even a Garfiel if he read the key moment right — even so—

"Ghh, ah..."

—Rachins refused to stop chanting.

"Why!?"

He crushed the spark trying to take shape yet again, and drove the very fist that did it straight into Rachins.
But even taking blow after blow in quick succession, the signs of magic trying anew to reach out and interfere with the world around Rachins never stopped rising.
Which was, in other words, proof that even with his last resort seen through, Rachins wasn't giving up.

"—Al-sama, this has no end to it."

That voice was gently cast toward the ears of a breathless Aldebaran.
Next, the head of Rachins, hanging suspended before him, was lifted, steel thread coiling tight around his neck, tightening around his slender throat — no, beginning to try to sever it entirely.

"His eyes are telling us — if you won't kill him, he'll pull the same move again as many times as it takes. Really quite admirable. Isn't this every man's proudest wish? In that case..."

"Shut up!"

"—"

Yae moved the fingers of her raised right hand, drawing up a red line of blood on Rachins's throat. Aldebaran silenced her and glared straight at Rachins from the front.
Rachins, his chin forced up, looked down at Aldebaran from that angle, as if looking down on him.
And then—

"I—diot..."

Covering his right hand in a rock protector, Aldebaran's blow struck Rachins down.
With a heaviness different in kind from anything before, the blow split Rachins's forehead open, blood spraying out. It was the sort of blow that wouldn't have shied from beating him to death outright, but—

"...Well done~"

Clapping her hands, Yae offered her praise, looking at the now-limp Rachins.
Yae was far more attuned than Aldebaran to the line between someone else living and dying. By her judgment, it seemed Rachins hadn't lost his life — knocked out was as far as it went.

"—"

Neither that praise nor the fact that Rachins had gone down unconscious offered Aldebaran any comfort.
He simply had to remember, more than he'd ever thought necessary, not to underestimate this number — five hundred. That this wasn't just a number, but five hundred men whose resolve had been pushed to its very limit, and he had to take it as exactly that.

"H-Hey, Aldebaran..."

"Sorry, but keep quiet for a bit."

Heinkel called out to Aldebaran, who'd just finished beating Rachins down. But the mental toll — worse than he'd expected, worse than deploying his Territory even — left him unable to answer properly.
In any case, the Dialogue Mirrors had been smashed, and the threat of the first wave had been driven off.
All that was left—

"—What's this, slow as a pig?"

"—"

The arrival of that racist joke, thoroughly familiar to his ears by now — except this time it took a different shape. This time, it didn't come with a punch attached.
But whether that meant it was fine to treat this as a difficulty already cleared, he couldn't say.
Because—

"Stopping your feet needlessly like this — have you lot got pig feed stuffed inside your heads instead of brains?"

—because the pig-faced man declaring that in a weighty voice was not alone; leading a unit just as Rachins had, rather than charging out ahead of the others, he stood before Aldebaran and the rest as the second wave.

"—Territory deployed. Redefine the matrix."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△

*"Stopping your feet needlessly like this — have you lot got pig feed stuffed inside your heads instead of brains?"*

The voice of the "Pig King," Doltero Amule, came through the Dialogue Mirror set in his gauntlet, and Rom Gramps grasped that Helmet Bastard had made contact with the second wave.
It was fortunate that the first contact had been Rachins and his men, and that the ones positioned nearest to them had been Doltero and his group — among the most powerful warriors of all those gathered.

"—"

Still, the fate of Rachins, who'd clashed with Helmet Bastard before Doltero arrived, and of the group he'd led, remained unknown. Naturally, that went for Rachins himself as their commander too.
If anything, given the enemy's cunning, it would be more fitting to assume Rachins was dead.

"—No good."

Once a fight had broken out, there was no way for things to be settled without harm.
Naturally, casualties would come not just among the underworld's rough men, but within his own camp as well. — To lean on the single handful of hope in Flam's report to Grassis would be foolish.
So, with a single blink, Rom Gramps sealed a lid over his own feelings and focused on the situation right in front of him.

If it's Doltero, he can finish off Helmet Bastard and his men in one breath. — He couldn't afford to think that optimistically. And that wasn't only because of Helmet Bastard's track record of having, in a sense, played the Sword Saint, the Divine Dragon, and the Witch of Envy all at once.

"...That man smashed, without a moment's hesitation, the Dialogue Mirror he'd given to the one carrying it."

Given that Rachins and his men's movements could no longer be tracked because of it, there was no doubt the enemy had played the best possible move as a means of cutting off their information.
But the way that best move had been played was, no matter how you looked at it, unnatural.

Even granting that he might have guessed, from their coordination and preparations, that they'd readied Dialogue Mirrors in advance — his actions beyond that point—

"—should have been impossible, unless he could see straight into the contents of our heads, or something else entirely."

Either way, it was the realm of nonsense — the sort of absurd talk that deserved to be scoffed at as impossible.
Even so, having lived over a century, Rom Gramps knew that powers beyond his own common sense and imagination existed in this world, and that those who wielded them could climb onto the very same board he stood on.
And against such things, the fate awaiting anyone who simply shut down their thinking with "impossible" was always the same.

And there was no way he could ever let someone he loved walk that same miserable path.

"—There's something I want to confirm. All units, I'm changing the orders."

Speaking all at once into his open Dialogue Mirrors, Rom Gramps narrowed his eyes and turned his mind to strategy.
Helmet Bastard, too, surely understood.

— That this battle, for both sides alike, was a race against time.
