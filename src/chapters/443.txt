　――My neck was being strangled, tighter and tighter.

　Straddled, my slender body weighed down, my movements sealed away.
　Both my legs and shoulders were pinned down by thin knees, stripping away the freedom of my struggling body. Before my eyes, on the white arms strangling my throat, were numerous red abrasions. I held the out-of-place sentiment that they looked like blooming flowers.

　――Tighter and tighter and tighter, my throat was choked.

"――――"

　Right in front of my eyes were eyes brimming with fury.
　Within those large, round eyes, endless anger and a hollow sense of despair spread deep, deep within.
t I thought blankly that I might be sucked into the depths of those empty eyes.

"A-Ah... ugh..."

　――Flailing, flailing, flailing, my legs kicked out unsightly.

t It wasn't that I was struggling to escape.
t I had no intention of escaping long ago. Even so, the flailing of my legs was not an expression of a desire to live, but merely my flesh crying out in pure agony.

t My oxygen-starved head, my spirit lacking the willpower to live, and my body attempting to struggle in spite of them.
t Everything was detached, and the ugly nature of my unbalanced existence felt hideous to me.

t I couldn't die quietly. I wanted to die quietly.
t If only I could die peacefully, quietly, as if falling asleep, that would have been best.
t But such a wish would not come true. Far from coming true, my final moments were the exact opposite.

"B-Bu... buh..."

t Frothing at the corners of my mouth, my eyes wide open as if they might pop out, twisting a body that had shriveled into a wooden stick in just a few days, letting out beastly groans.

t Should I call it a fitting end?
t Should I call it a fitting conclusion?

t Just what on earth happened for me to end up reaching a place like this?

"――What's so funny?"

t Suddenly, a voice rained down.
t Distinct from the beastly groans, it was a cold, yet clearly ringing, clear voice.

t While strangling my throat, the owner of those wrath-dyed eyes spun words through her thin lips.

"――――"

t Even if asked what was so funny, no words could be found to answer.
t In the first place, nothing was funny. And yet, what was I being asked?

t The question was unreasonable. Riddled, unkind, and absurd.
t Even if forced upon me, there was no way to answer. Even so, the silence was uncomfortable.

t Unreasonable, absurd, tossed around by providence and left behind—just how many times made this now?

"――What's so funny?"

t Nothing was funny.

"Fu... he... hehe..."

t Then, was the person asking the question mistaken?
t Or was I enjoying this moment without realizing it myself?

t Straddled by a woman, having my neck strangled—was I enjoying this situation?
t If so, how awful, a rational impression spilled forth.

"――What's so funny?"

t Nothing was funny. Nothing at all, yet the question was asked repeatedly.
t It wasn't even "asked." At this distance, it wasn't even cast.
t At a distance where we touched, where our breath reached, looking up at the beautiful face of the woman, my ears were painted over with her voice. Smearing over me.

t Not even words of rebuke, not even insults to demean, her voice existed within pure hatred—

"What's so funny—"

t —she asked.

t Repeatedly, the question that was supposed to demand an answer suddenly vanished like haze in mid-sentence.

"――Guh."

t Swaying, the woman's face before my eyes wobbled to the left.
t Just like that, her tilting body could not recover. Maintaining her collapsed posture, the woman's body tumbled onto the white snow. Naturally, the arms around my neck slipped away, and the path to asphyxiation was cut short midway.

"――Ze... ah."

t From my coughing throat, the bitter taste of blood surged up.
t Expanding my shriveled lungs, deflating them again, the missing oxygen was sent into my body one after another. That, too, was a reflexive survival instinct. Refusing to breathe and dying is impossible for a normal human being.
t Whether I myself was normal or not was something I didn't even want to discuss in a place like this.

"――――"

t Until a moment ago, the mindset that had calmly accepted death vanished, and now the attachment to life called oxygen could not be released. Desperately, earnestly, greedily devouring it to an unsightly degree.

t And so, after filling my lungs with cold air time and time again, I noticed.

"――――"

t As white snow silently piled up, the woman lay sideways on the light snow.
t Her pale face and lips sublimated the woman's unreasonable beauty into something even more breathtaking. Her raspy breath clouded white, and the signs of fading life burned with unnatural radiance into my eyes.

t Looking closely, her attire was terribly unfitting for a snowy landscape.
t A uniform exposing her shoulders and thighs, the thickness of the cloth entirely insufficient against the cold air. Her neck and ears, parts easily chilled, were exposed to the wind, causing a pain that felt as if it were cutting into the onlooker.

t Just as she was—though that condition applied not only to the woman, but to me as well.

"――――"

t ――Clack-clack, clack-clack, clack-clack, my unaligned teeth chattered.

t Whether the cause was the cold or the gloom lingering in my chest was unclear.
t Only, at this moment, I couldn't take my eyes off the woman in front of me rather than the abnormalities of my own body.

"――Rem."

t Fallen on the snow, half her face buried in it, the woman was still beautiful.
t Unending hatred and crimson rage lit a fire in that delicate body, making it seem as though that alone kept her alive. The woman was covered in so many wounds that it was a miracle she was even living.

"――――"

t In the middle of the white, snowy landscape, surrounding myself and the woman, lay countless corpses.
t Despicable beasts that had gathered to devour life, feast, and violate souls had all laid out as corpses before the woman's wind. Thus, there were only two living beings in this place. Only myself and the woman.

"――――"

t Standing up slowly beside the woman who faintly muttered something.
t My numb fingers on both hands had turned a reddish-black color. The drop in body temperature was severe, and my cold fingers had no sensation. Only a faint itch served as the sole proof that my fingers were attached to my body.

t Trembling with those unreliable fingers, I somehow managed to lift a stone about the size of a human head.

t An ordinary stone with no special features or history, simply one that had fallen there.

t For a moment, the stone in my hands felt like the face of a woman closely resembling the fallen one.
t Perhaps she had smiled once. But the facial expression burned into this heart at the very end was nothing but a fiercely demon-like visage, hurling murderous intent and hostility.

t As if brushing that away, I raised the stone held in both hands.

"――I will definitely kill you."


t ――The sound of the stone smashing against something hard echoed dully, dully through the snowy forest.


t Echoed.

"――――"

"――――"

"Roswaal was a miscalculation on my part. He's just a pitiful human."

"――Insulting the master of this house, Lord Roswaal, is something I, as a maid, shall not allow!"

"You're a pitiful child too. You were the only one desperately trying to protect a place that's already finished."

"Please refrain from using the past tense. It is not finished yet, I assure you!"

t She barked back. Against a Great Spirit she had no chance of defeating.

"Emilia-sama will grieve!"

"Unfortunately, I'm overly soft on Lia. After all, I'm a parent cat who can't let go of his child."

t Whether that turned into regret, grief, or whatever emotion, there was no time for it to change.
t Merely a white wind blew, and with just that, the woman named Frederica froze.

t That was the beginning of the collapse of the Roswaal Manor.
