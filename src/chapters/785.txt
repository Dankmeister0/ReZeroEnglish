Arc 10, Part 26: Congratulations


　――From the very beginning when he was appointed as Philoore-jou’s knight, Heinkel had felt a sense of crisis regarding her reckless, headlong personality, but that perception was not entirely correct.

　Philoore’s precocious recklessness was far beyond anything Heinkel could have imagined.

“――So, in other words, even though the medicine is in the warehouse, it can’t be delivered?”

“Yes. The congestion of dragon carriages entering and leaving the royal capital is terrible, and the church’s carts are also completely stranded. At this rate...”

“The medicine will spoil, and there are many elderly people at the healing clinics whose lives would be in danger if they don't take their medicine daily... In that case, how about we do our best to carry it out with our own hands?”

“Eh?! W-With our own hands?!”

“Yes! If the dragon carriages can’t move, then we’ll carry just the cargo out by hand... Don’t you think that might actually work?!”

At the entrance of the "Divine Dragon Church," a young man clutching a manifest of medicine recoiled sharply at the sheer force of Philoore leaning forward, clenching her fists.
Perhaps mistaking the young man’s reaction for agreement, Philoore looked ready to dash out to the commercial district at any moment. Heinkel hurriedly held her back, saying, “Wait, wait!”

“Philoore-jou, doing it that way won't solve the problem even if the sun sets and rises again tomorrow. You don’t actually intend to carry all that cargo by yourself, do you?”

“What are you saying? Of course not! Heinkel-sama is my knight, so we are in the same boat, in the same boat! Not one, but both of us will go!”

“Like hell we are! Even if we resort to brute force, there’s no way we’d let someone of your standing, Philoore-jou, go. If anyone is going, I’ll go alone.”

“Please don’t be so distant. I am surprisingly strong, you know. Back at the monastery, I often rearranged the rooms on behalf of the old women with bad backs!”

“That’s! Not! What! I’m! Talking! About!”

Philoore, who was running on sheer momentum, blinked her round red eyes at Heinkel’s roaring voice.
As the girl blinked her sparkling, gem-like eyes, Heinkel ran his hand through his red hair and turned a grumpy voice toward the delivery youth. “Hey.”

“What you need is a means of transporting medicine to the healing clinic in the East District, right? There should be healing clinics in the West and South Districts as well. What about those?”

“The West and South? There are still passable roads over there, so there’s no danger of the medicine being delayed...”

“Then it’s simple. Instead of moving the medicine, just move the patients.”

Saying so, Heinkel snatched the medicine manifest from the youth’s hand.
The medicinal items listed there were all familiar to him, and he could roughly imagine the condition of the patients who needed them. ――Ever since his wife fell into her deep sleep, he had researched treatments for every kind of illness one by one. Utilizing his privileged status as a resident of the capital, Heinkel had more knowledge of medicine than your average healer.

“However, I haven’t seen the patients directly. The final decision rests with the healers, but this is much more realistic than trying to figure out a way to get a whole dragon carriage through. If we can secure the path to the East District during this temporary treatment, things should go smoothly from next time on. ――So, for now, what do you think, Philoore-jou?”

“Eh? Yes! You’re right! Ah, but what about those who can’t be moved from the clinic...”

“The clinic shouldn’t be entirely filled with critically ill patients. In that case, having just one wooden box packed with high-priority medicine should suffice for the time being.”

“I see! Then I will take that wooden box――”

“Hey.”

“――I mean, I’ll leave it to you! Carrying cargo is your job, after all!”

Glared at by Heinkel, her enthusiasm dampened, Philoore turned a smile to the young man. Having the manifest thrust back to him by Heinkel, he nodded at Philoore’s words.

“Understood, we will do just that. At the clinic...”

“Don’t worry. The healing clinic and the Divine Dragon Church are closely linked! I’ll send a messenger right away so there’s no misunderstanding. You do your duty.”

“――Yes! Thank you very much, both of you!”

With a look of relief, the youth bowed with a sense of duty and rushed out of the church. Watching his retreating figure, Heinkel’s shoulders slumped with exhaustion.
Philoore, who continued to consult without catching her breath from start to finish—it was fine for her to be thanked, but for Heinkel, who was just standing beside her, to be thanked as well made him feel awkward.
After all, he was a man who, in truth, had no right to be standing here in the first place.

“Good work, Heinkel-sama! That consultation was a brilliant showcase of our perfect coordination. Since I sent the messenger, he should be able to perform his duties without worry as well.”

Utterly oblivious to Heinkel’s inner thoughts, Philoore, who had left the errand to a church believer, returned and lightly raised her hand toward him. She didn’t lower it. When he stared at her for a while, she still kept it raised, waving it left and right in front of Heinkel.

“……What is it?”

“It’s not ‘what is it,’ it’s a ritual to celebrate our mutual hard work. When I hold my hand up like this, Heinkel-sama, you also match your hand to mine. By doing so, we share the feeling of ‘we did a good job guiding them.’ It’s a promise of the Divine Dragon Church!”

“I never saw anything like that in the church back when I used to go.”

“It might be a recent promise! Also, it might have a little bit of my personal touch added to it!”

Considering the nature of a church that had worshiped the "Divine Dragon" unchanged for four hundred years, the idea of Philoore’s influence being the cause was far more convincing than the idea of rituals coming and going out of style.
Unfortunately, since Philoore didn’t look like she would back down until this ritual was completed, Heinkel let out another sigh and slapped his palm against hers.

“Philoore-jou, about earlier...”

“Ah, that was a wonderful stroke of quick wit! Don’t worry. I made sure to tell the messenger to inform the clinic staff that this was Heinkel-sama’s idea.”

“――. Exercise self-restraint. In two senses.”

“Self-restraint?! My least favorite word...!”

Her beaming smile turned into a betrayed expression, and a pale Philoore tottered over to lean against a chapel pew. Watching her, Heinkel let out a deep breath through his nose.
He wished she wouldn't increase the number of things he wanted to say to her in such a short span of time.

“First of all, stop sticking your head in and poking around the ashes of a fireplace. You have no idea what’s hidden in the ashes, and most of the time you’ll just get burned. If you absolutely have to search through the ashes, use someone else. Starting with me.”

“Is that... because Heinkel-sama is an immortal knight who won’t lose to the flames?”

“No. It’s because if someone’s going to get hurt, it should be someone who doesn't matter if they get hurt.”

He deliberately tried to correct Philoore’s thinking with clear, harsh words.
Philoore’s methods were perilous. However, her natural ability to draw people’s attention was an indispensable quality for a ruler. Since she had joined the Royal Selection late, she had to avoid situations where she might get burned—literally and figuratively—and play her hand as effectively as possible.
Therefore――

“Don’t go out of your way to bring up my name every single time. To put it bluntly, it will only get in the way of your Royal Selection campaign, Philoore-jou. No joke.”

“That’s not...”

“No, because it was my idea, the clinic thing might actually become harder to pass. That’s exactly the kind of position I’m in right now. Sorry, but I am well aware of it.”

“――!”

Philoore looked frustrated at that response, but since he was the one causing her to make such a face, Heinkel had no words of comfort or encouragement to offer.
To be honest, he still regretted his choice. ――As expected, he should not have accepted Philoore’s request, which could only be described as a momentary lapse of judgment.
Watching the attitude of the girl who had become his master, earnestly facing the Royal Selection despite her clumsiness, he found himself—nothing but a hindrance to her ambitions—all the more cursed.
Even more so if that piled-up bad reputation was something he had earned of his own accord.

“In the end, no matter what I do, nothing goes right...”

Even though he intended to act in search of the best possible outcome at any given time, his past actions would inevitably come back to slowly strangle him later. It was like an unavoidable trap he had left behind, repeatedly catching up to Heinkel’s life a lap late. He had tripped over the holes dug by his past self more times than he could count.
That was precisely why, whenever there was some sort of achievement to be made, not mixing in an impurity like Heinkel was the best possible outcome for Philoore――

“――Agh, this is so unlike me!”

Just as Heinkel was narrowing his eyes in thought, Philoore suddenly shouted and pinched the back of her pale hand hard with her fingers. Heinkel’s eyes widened as she shrieked, “Ouch, ouch, ouch!”

“W-What are you doing?”

“A lesson for my own helplessness! If I could, I’d slap my own cheek, but if I did that, I’d have to meet people with a bright red face, wouldn’t I? So, from long ago, this is what I do when I want to discipline myself. If only my hand is red, I can make the excuse that I slammed the table in anger!”

“Is that even an excuse? The part about anger is completely exposed...”

“It’s fine! Because the part about anger is the truth! And no matter what anyone says, I will not back down on this. Even going forward, I will not hide your achievements, Heinkel-sama!”

With a finger pointed squarely at his face, Heinkel instinctively recoiled. Philoore closed the distance by that same amount and stepped forward. Heinkel backed away. She stepped forward. He backed away. She stepped forward. Cornered against the wall, her finger was pointed right between his brows.
And with Heinkel pinned against the wall, Philoore said,

“I will swallow the fact that the reason Heinkel-sama gets bad-mouthed left and right is due to your past behavior. Even though it makes me go ‘grr,’ I will swallow it. But! I will not allow your past deeds to dull the value of your actions today or tomorrow!”

“Philoore-jou...”

“If there is a life saved by your words, then that might be a life that could never have been saved without them. If I become King, and you are my number-one knight, the opportunities to witness such scenes will increase. So, in preparation for that day to come, Heinkel-sama’s honor must be restored starting now!”

“――”

“Therefore, while I will listen to Heinkel-sama’s advice as much as possible, I will not comply with opinions like ‘belittle Heinkel-sama,’ ‘keep him hidden in a box,’ or ‘let’s just throw away that drunkard knight.’ I declare this right now!”

Slowly lowering her finger, Philoore touched the uniform Heinkel wore—the official church knight uniform he had been dressed in since he was the knight of the Divine Dragon Church’s Priestess.
The blue-based uniform was holy and solemn, roughly on par with or even more awe-inspiring to wear than the Imperial Guard’s uniform, making it clear to Heinkel that it was a set of clothes he couldn't pull off.
However――

“But eventually, everyone who looks at this outfit will think of Heinkel-sama. When that time comes, you can even use the church’s authority to act as high and mighty as you please!”

Winking with one eye, Philoore put on a clumsy display of charm.
At her words, Heinkel let out a sigh. Her attitude of going all-out at full throttle chose no targets; naturally, it was the same for Heinkel.

“...’When Philoore-jou becomes King,’ huh. In that case, is helping people at the church just out of calculation?”

“Please don’t say such outrageous things! You see, this is a tremendously good deal. If I consult with those who come to the church, the consultee is helped, I gain a reputation for doing good, and we get to do good deeds and feel great... right?”

“Don’t go including me in that as if it’s the natural order of things. I’m...”

“But being thanked by people feels good, doesn't it? It does for me. That’s why, even though the title of ‘Priestess’ is heavy, I like the church.”

To Philoore, who puffed out her chest with a nod, Heinkel could say nothing back.
Perhaps because she had said her piece, or perhaps out of consideration for Heinkel, Philoore did not press Heinkel, who couldn't find an answer, to reply.
And the chance to voice that answer was postponed by the sound of the church doors opening once more.

“――Excuse me. There’s a clash between residents happening very close to the church.”

“Oh dear, how terrible! We must rush over immediately! Heinkel-sama, we are going too!”

“――. Don’t run. We can’t afford to increase the number of people who fall and need to be looked after by our scarce medical supplies.”

“Then, a brisk walk!”

As she spoke, Philoore took three giant strides toward the messenger, and then immediately started running as if she had forgotten her reason for a brisk walk. Heinkel scratched his head again.
Then, letting out a click of his tongue, he hurriedly chased after the back of his reckless master.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


“――You do your job much more properly than I thought.”

During the hour when the orange setting sun filtered through the large high window, such a voice called out to Heinkel’s back, who was staring down documents in a room of the church.
Turning around, the one looking at him from the entrance of the room was a slender, handsome man—Tiga Laureon, a priest of the "Divine Dragon Church" who served as Philoore’s bodyguard.
Having apparently accompanied Philoore as her attendant even before her entry into the Royal Selection, he was introduced to Heinkel right after Heinkel was appointed as her knight, and they had met frequently since.
Naturally, Tiga’s first impression of Heinkel was the absolute worst—no, it wasn't just Tiga; it could be said that everyone around Philoore felt the same way. In fact, the only one who welcomed Heinkel’s appointment as her knight was Philoore herself.

“Well, that’s only natural. Anyone would, and I would too.”

Looking at it objectively, Heinkel’s current position was beyond inappropriate.
With Philoore already being so precarious and causing endless anxiety to those around her, having Heinkel, who had no shortage of bad reputation, stand right beside her was something that anyone with a shred of decent judgment would find utterly unacceptable.
Even though there was Philoore’s own "Command of the Dragon," it would be harder to find someone who agreed with it than someone who didn't.

“――? What’s with the sudden distant look? Did my sarcasm not get through?”

“No, it got through loud and clear, and I sympathize with you guys. It must be hard enough taking care of Philoore-jou, but then having someone like me brought in on top of that.”

“If you understand that, then I would have preferred it if you had quietly declined in the first place.”

“I have no excuse. That probably would have been the correct, adult answer, but...”

Cutting himself off there, Heinkel hesitated whether to continue. However, prompted by Tiga’s gaze to finish what he was saying, he had no choice but to open his mouth.
The reason he couldn’t refuse the hand Philoore reached out to him was clear.

“In short, it’s a disgraceful attachment, far from any mature judgment.”

If an adult was defined as someone who could draw a line, accept things, and give up, then Heinkel was a terribly pathetic creature who could never truly become an adult.
Yet, on the other hand, the adult Heinkel admired most in this world was a man who never let go of his sword until the very end, cutting down even the impossible, and never giving up on the things he loved.

Admiring something that he could never reach, something he couldn't even lay a finger on.
That might have been the beginning of Heinkel’s curse. ――Though telling Tiga such things would surely be of no interest to him.

“――. I had your circumstances looked into. Don’t take it personally. I don’t want to keep a dangerous person near Philoore. Besides, it was already a famous story in some circles.”

However, Heinkel raised his eyebrows at the tone of Tiga’s following words.
Having lived for a long time by constantly reading people’s expressions, he was sensitive to the nuances of negative emotions, such as contempt, mockery, sympathy, or pity. That was exactly why he felt like tilting his head in confusion.
Because Tiga’s voice undeniably held a tone of concern for Heinkel.

“You’ve been glaring with a sour face for a while now. Is that Philoore’s schedule? Isn’t it incredibly difficult to get that girl to stick to a schedule?”

Ignoring Heinkel’s unspoken query, Tiga peered at his hands from behind, making an observation that showed high understanding of Philoore.
In fact, tomorrow's schedule—making the rounds as a Royal Selection candidate, and performing her duties as the "Priestess" of the church—meant Philoore’s days were dizzyingly busy. Even outside of what was written on the schedule, she attended services at sunrise, and after finishing her prayers, she voluntarily listened to the consultations of the people visiting the church.

“On top of that, she has a personality that absolutely cannot overlook a problem she finds. If she runs into a lost child or an elderly person struggling with cargo on the roadside, the rest of the schedule for the day is finished.”

“Yeah, I get it. Once before, Philoore vanished, and it turned out she had accompanied an elderly person to carry their luggage all the way to a large town three stops away, and came back after being treated to tea at their house.”

“...As long as her title is 'Priestess,' that might be fine. Well, even with the title of 'Priestess,' doing that is precarious enough.”

Now that she was a Royal Selection candidate, she couldn't keep going on as before.
Irreplaceable roles attract irreplaceable work. That was expected of Philoore as well, and there was no need to say which was of higher importance.
And yet――

“Philoore-jou doesn’t want to neglect her duties as a member of the church up until now, and on top of that, she intends to see her run as a Royal Selection candidate through... That’s asking too much of herself.”

Dragged along by her enthusiasm, Heinkel continued to run himself ragged.
In place of Philoore, who stuck her nose into everything, he figured out who to hand over newly raised problems to solve them, rescheduled delayed plans, apologized to interviewees, and proactively prepared the documents and proposals that would be needed next, fully occupied with work that didn't have a shred of the typical first knight about it.
In fact, ever since he became Philoore’s knight, the opportunities to draw his sword had been zero.

“Even so, during Philoore’s morning service every day, you don't seem to miss your training. I had assumed on my own that you were bad with mornings.”

“...How could someone bad at waking up early serve as a knight?”

“I see, it seems increasingly unsuited for me. I’m glad such a heavy role as a knight didn't come my way. In any case, there’s no denying my lack of skill.”

Adjusting the position of the hat he wore, Heinkel glanced sideways at Tiga, who was casually analyzing himself.

“Lack of skill, huh.”

Honestly, Heinkel found it hard to agree with Tiga’s claim.
Having been appointed as Philoore’s bodyguard, Tiga, who carried a scimitar, possessed skill that was considerable even in Heinkel’s eyes. His limberly trained physique, his bearing which suggested rich combat experience, and above all, his composure that remained unfazed by events, were outstanding.
He was neither a swordsman nor a knight, but he was a warrior. ――That was Heinkel’s assessment.
Nonetheless――

“You, how is your wound?”

“Hmm, I still can't say I'm in top shape. It's gotten much better, but it was quite a deep wound to begin with... As expected, the alias 'Valkyrie' is not just for show.”

“Duchess Karsten, who killed Lord McMahon and fled...”

Heinkel knit his brows at Tiga’s reply as he stroked his flank through his clothes.
The name he uttered belonged to the very culprit who had inflicted a deep wound in Tiga’s flank, and who was seen as the one who had sparked the current chaos swirling in the capital.

――Initially, Crusch Karsten, who was considered the clear favorite in the Royal Selection and known by her alias 'Valkyrie'. Now, she was wanted as the actual perpetrator who assassinated Miklotov McMahon, a central figure of the Council of Sages, and her whereabouts were being pursued throughout the kingdom.

While the incident itself was quite a shock, what forced them to tilt their heads in puzzlement was that Natsuki Subaru, who was supposed to be Emilia’s knight, was also wanted as Crusch’s accomplice.
Furthermore, not only was he suspected of being an accomplice, but he was also under the absurd suspicion of being the Archbishop of 'Sloth' himself.
Accordingly, Emilia, who was suspected of treason against the royal castle, and Felt, who could not dispel the suspicion of identity fraud, were detained in the castle, while the associates of both camps fled the capital――at which point, a large mabeast was released into the city, and the "Sword Saint" was deployed to resolve the situation, so the story went.

That was what had already transpired by the time Heinkel was brought back to the royal capital, and it remained the factor spreading a nightmare-like chaos not just through the capital, but across the entire kingdom.

“By now, the bleakness of the capital must be at a level that will go down in the kingdom’s history. On the streets, knights and guards create a grim atmosphere, and the townspeople have nothing bright to talk about, just keeping their heads down.”

“People who have choices can leave the capital. But the overwhelming majority of people don't have that option. Honestly, the church is busier now than it has been in years.”

“And in return, Philoore-jou’s support will solidify, huh?”

“I don't like that way of thinking. Not me, and certainly not Philoore.”

Tiga’s gaze grew sharp at Heinkel’s slight irony.
As if seriously offended rather than taking it as a joke, Heinkel raised both hands in a gesture of surrender.

Howbeit, regardless of Tiga’s feelings, such a trend did exist.
The more the kingdom’s chaos spread, the more people’s trust in the royal castle eroded, leading them instead to turn their hearts toward the church that revered the "Divine Dragon," which had continuously protected the nation with a proven track record.
This effect was undoubtedly serving as a tailwind for Philoore, who had made a late start.
And that was surely something to be thrilled about, even for Heinkel, who was Philoore’s knight――

“Father...”

As Heinkel muttered, the existence of his missing biological father, Wilhelm, crossed his mind.
Crusch, who was wanted as Miklotov’s assassin, and Wilhelm, who served her, was also one of the associates who had vanished from the capital. He was presumably joining his master and assisting her escape, but it went without saying that they were at an overwhelming disadvantage.
Of course, it was not as if Heinkel lacked the daydream-like expectation that the legendary 'Sword Devil' might be able to carve through any predicament with his blade.

“You're in a tough position too, Heinkel-san.”

Tiga shrugged his shoulders, as if reading Heinkel’s inner thoughts from his mutterings and expression.
A tough position—that point was likely correct. But if they were going to talk about that, the Astrea family had been completely fractured ever since Heinkel was in Priscilla’s camp.

“...My struggles are nothing to write home about. The only problem is that this might cause trouble for Philoore-jou. My father and my son... they're all grown men. It's only natural they wipe their own asses.”

“――. At least, your father wasn't present at the scene where I clashed swords with Duchess Karsten. I hope your father is just being falsely accused.”

Tiga patted Heinkel’s shoulder as if to encourage him, and Heinkel, unsure how to reply, ended up giving a flat “Yeah” as a mere half-hearted response.
The footnote that Wilhelm was not at the scene was something Heinkel already knew without being told. If Wilhelm had been there and Tiga had been in a position to clash swords with him, Tiga would not be of this world right now.
Furthermore, there was one more reason why his reply had been vague. And that was something he could absolutely never reveal to Tiga.

――Because that was the unerasable distrust toward the "Divine Dragon Church" that was sprouting within Heinkel.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


――In the current Kingdom of Lugnica, the role played by the "Divine Dragon Church" is massive.

Originally, the Kingdom of Lugnica had been promised peace and the protection of the nation under the aegis of the "Divine Dragon" Volcanica, continuing its prosperity to this day.
For that very reason, many of the kingdom’s citizens naturally held a sense of awe toward the "Divine Dragon," which inevitably linked to an unconditional trust in the "Divine Dragon Church."
Heinkel was no exception, which was why, when his wife fell into her unawakening sleep, he had frequented the church, believing in a place to pray and in miracles.

Unfortunately, Heinkel’s prayers had no effect, and furthermore, he had committed a great sin for which he could not show his face to either the "Divine Dragon" or its believers. Since then, he had kept his distance from the church, leaving him with no choice but to mock his own inappropriate position once again.

Once the time spent lamenting his own foolishness was over, Heinkel had to face it. The distrust toward the "Divine Dragon Church" that had sprouted within him—regarding Philoore’s existence. To be more precise, regarding the church’s intentions behind having Philoore participate in the Royal Selection.

Having been appointed as her knight and observing Philoore closely, what he was repeatedly forced to realize was her terrifyingly straightforward goodness, devoid of any two-faced nature.

Philoore, who continued to go all-out like that in front of anyone and everyone, possessed—aside from her precarious judgment in appointing Heinkel as her knight—the executive ability to make decisions on her own responsibility rather than flustering when forced to make a choice, and to take action to make that choice a reality.
For that sake, she talked much, laughed much, cried much, and moved much. ――The talent that could be called virtue, which moved her surroundings by voicing her desires, was truly the bloodline of the Lugnica Royal Family.
Philoore herself said she did not hold the answer to whether she was the princess who had vanished fifteen years ago, nor did she seek it――

“――There cannot be two princesses.”

That was unmistakable, as Heinkel was well-acquainted with the King’s younger brother, Fold Lugnica—the father of the vanished princess and the biological younger brother of the late King Randhal Lugnica.
Fold’s daughter was neither a twin nor a triplet; she was unmistakably a single child.
And that single child had vanished from the royal castle on that night fifteen years ago.
The princess who had vanished――it could only be either Philoore or Felt, both of whom had stepped onto the stage together as Royal Selection candidates.

Therefore, someone was plotting. ――To raise one of the two girls, Philoore or Felt, as the surviving royalty, in order to use them at will.
Thinking of it that way, including the circumstances under which Philoore joined the Royal Selection, he had no choice but to harbor a strong distrust toward the "Divine Dragon Church."
However――

“...What is it that I want to do?”

Thinking this far, Heinkel’s thoughts ground to a halt as if terrified.
It was a highly foolish act, equivalent to doing nothing while knowing one was surrounded by a dense, blinding fog with sunset fast approaching.

Yet, within that dense fog lay the light Heinkel had yearned for over many years. And if he took a wrong step, that light would be lost forever.
Therefore, he could not move. He could not take action. Fearing a definitive mistake.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


――In the end, without being able to find an answer, only time went by.

The master-servant relationship between Heinkel and Philoore was forged, and days turned into weeks, until almost a month had passed.
During this time, the Philoore camp smoothed out the flow of supplies that had stagnated due to the capital’s turmoil, responded to consultations sent from churches and healing clinics in various regions, and steadily grew her support as a Royal Selection candidate. As for Heinkel, still unable to erase his distrust of the church yet unable to bring himself to obstruct Philoore, he ended up spending his days escaping from approaching anxiety by desperately focusing on clearing the issues right in front of him.

However, while those Royal Selection activities went on, the two matters that Philoore herself desired most strongly made absolutely no progress.



“...Did they send them back again?”

In a room of the church, looking down at a mountain of documents piled high on the desk, Heinkel knit his brows in disgust as he confirmed the presence of a familiar envelope there.
On the other side of the desk across the mountain of documents, a woman working while stifling a yawn—Sakura Element—contrary to her unmotivated attitude, was efficiently categorizing the documents by sender, distributing them so the letters would be easy to organize and check.
With her sleepy eyes and lazy atmosphere, she glanced at Heinkel.

“Yeees, it seems that way~. It helps that their replies are as quick as ever, but as for the contents... ah, it looks like it’s a postponement again~.”

“A postponement, huh. How many times has it been now?”

“Let’s see~, forty-seven letters to the royal castle, big and small. Thirty-two to the church, and since there are additional materials and attached testimonies, and Philoore-chan is quite a diligent writer, it seems to have easily surpassed a hundred letters already~.”

Categorizing letters with one hand while skillfully checking the record book at the edge of the desk with the other, Sakura replied, and Heinkel let out a weary sigh at her answer.

An obsession exceeding a hundred letters; the documents Philoore sent almost daily to the royal castle and the church—their contents were, in short, requests for hearings and visitation with Emilia and Felt, who were detained in the castle. And, requests for permission to administer treatment using the sacrament possessed by Philoore to the victims of the Witch Cult left behind in the Water Gate City of Pristella.
Philoore was tenaciously struggling to go through formal procedures for both, but――

“Why won't those guys nod their heads? Setting aside visits with Emilia-sama and the other, surely Pristella is worth a try. Or is there some defect in the paperwork?”

“Oh my~, are you doubting my work~? If that were the case, having Philoore-chan waste her time on a hundred letters would make me quite a troublemaker, wouldn't it~?”

“I was just asking. I'm not doubting you. If you were that incompetent, you wouldn't have been given such a grand title as 'Secretary to the Priestess' in the first place.”

“Is that so~? I think nepotism is highly possible too~. Don’t you think so, Heinkel-chan of the Royal Guard?”

“――. Don’t call an old man ‘chan’, Sakura-chan.”

“I’ll put that request on hold, Heinkel-chan~.”

Giving a reply she had no intention of keeping, Sakura handed Heinkel the letter returned from the royal castle. Flipping it open, the contents were something he could have written with his eyes closed.

Currently, the request is under careful deliberation by the Council of Sages, and we ask for your patience for a while longer before a conclusion is reached. ――Whether it was the treatment of the two detainees or the use of the sacrament on Pristella, the answer was the same, with only the words slightly rearranged.

“They don’t refuse, but they don’t permit either. How long do they intend to keep this up?”

“Who knows~? It’s decided by the high and mighty at the castle~. The situation being what it is, isn’t everyone just being cautious~?”

“They’re piling caution upon caution. Even Philoore-jou is at her limit, and lately she’s constantly complaining that her heart is ‘grated raw’.”

“That’s such a Philoore-chan way of putting it~.”

Sakura giggled with her hand over her mouth, but unfortunately, Heinkel was not in the mood to agree and laugh along. Rather, watching Philoore, whose forced cheerfulness grew day by day as she became visibly painful to watch, made his own spirits sink.

“If the castle is a no-go, what about the church? Philoore-jou is the ‘Priestess’, isn’t she? Can’t she even decide for herself whom to use her own sacrament on?”

“She can’t~. It’s not something that cheap, after all~.”

Being flatly told so, Heinkel was slightly taken aback.
Sakura tapped the letters on the desk, divided into three piles, in order with her finger, saying, “Listen closely~,”

“In the first place, ‘Priestess’ is not an official rank in the church~. It’s a special title awarded to believers who have received an exceptionally strong grace of the ‘Divine Dragon’. While people are grateful to her and she gets to sit in front of the high-ranking officials at ceremonies~, that doesn’t mean she has a seat on the Sacred Council where the church elites gather, or that she has a strong voice there...~”

“Despite that, she is treated as the face of the church on the surface.”

“Serving as a symbol that visibly supports the people’s faith is also an important job for the ‘Priestess’~. That said, even if you tried to assign only the role of a quiet symbol to Philoore-chan, it seems like it would be harder than teaching the ‘Dragon’s’ tail how to sit~.”

He had nothing but agreement for that, but he disliked the explanatory part before the conclusion.
Because it seemed to imply that Philoore, who was currently saddled with the role of a Royal Selection candidate, was expected—even without that—to fulfill her position as a symbol of the "Divine Dragon Church" in her capacity as "Priestess"—to put it badly, as a mere figurehead.

“I actually think Philoore-chan is quite suited to be King~. Your attitude too, Heinkel-chan, is probably the result of being charmed by Philoore-chan’s royal caliber, isn’t it~?”

Heinkel was bad at dealing with Sakura, who laughed while seeing through his silent inner thoughts.
Her competence, which did not match her appearance and attitude, could be sensed from every word she spoke. While he thought Tiga was top-tier as a church guard, in Sakura’s case, rather than just being top-tier among the civil servants dispatched by the church, she made him wonder if she was part of the elite few.
In fact, while it was Philoore’s diligence that wrote nearly a hundred letters, it was Sakura who gathered all the necessary documents and handled the procedures for them, effectively stretching her hands into almost every department associated with the "Divine Dragon Church."

“With just you alone, the church seems like it would run mostly fine.”

“Oh no, you overpraise me~. I could never put on a face that looks like the hope of so many people like Philoore-chan can~. Besides, an organization where just one person’s mistake makes the whole thing fail is too precarious to run~.”

“...I see, how modest of you.”

Sakura’s reply as she waved her hand was, in a way, provocative toward the monarchy.
In the Kingdom of Lugnica, the Council of Sages existed to compensate for that, but under the current circumstances where even the Council of Sages’ movements seemed sluggish, even as a mere remnant of a kingdom knight, he had no words to reply with.

“There’s talk that they’re in a flurry after Lord McMahon passed away, but...”

It wasn’t as if he had observed the discussions of the Council of Sages from up close, but Miklotov was indeed their central figure and coordinator. However, that did not mean the other members of the council lacked voice or decision-making power; led by the conspicuously fiery-debating Lord Bordeaux Zellgef, lively opinions should have been flying back and forth.
In reality, the issues faced by the capital and the castle were not just one or two, and responses to them were being carried out as swiftly as before, even with Miklotov’s absence.

In short, what was being kept in limbo were the two matters Philoore wanted to actively involve herself in—though even Heinkel did not understand what that signified.

“Is the church’s refusal to grant Philoore-jou permission to use her sacrament... related to Duchess Karsten being wanted?”

“That can’t be denied~. In fact, Philoore-chan’s use of her sacrament without going through the Sacred Council was viewed as a problem, and apparently, revoking her certification as the ‘Priestess’ was even discussed~.”

“Can a ‘Priestess’ status actually be revoked?!”

“Under the rules. It should never have been applied, though~. She escaped it this time too, but once it’s been discussed, things won’t be entirely safe~.”

Sakura said this casually, but Heinkel could only be surprised anew.
Philoore’s independent action, which had greatly shaken the royal castle, was apparently not only a major event for the "Divine Dragon Church," but had even put Philoore’s own position in jeopardy.
And the situation where Crusch, who had gone to such lengths to receive treatment from the sacrament, was now wanted as the perpetrator of Miklotov’s assassination, was nothing but a headwind for Philoore.

“It takes a toll when everything you do backfires...”

“You’re quite passionate about this~. Ah, but that’s only natural~. For Heinkel-chan too, the permission to use Philoore-chan’s sacrament is a matter of life and death, after all~.”

“――”

“Or is it the result of you starting to fit into the role of Philoore-chan’s knight?”

Heinkel snorted loudly at her teasing gaze and voice.
Unfortunately, he felt no obligation to answer Sakura’s sadistic sarcasm. Of course, it was a fact that he wanted to confirm as soon as possible, even by a single second, whether he could save his wife—Louanna—with the power of Philoore’s sacrament.
But――

“The people waiting in Pristella aren’t a case where we don’t know if it’ll work; its effectiveness is already a proven matter. If whether they can be saved or not changes based solely on having permission or not, it’s only natural to want it to be approved quickly.”

“Prioritizing others over yourself? Was Heinkel-chan such a good person?”

“I’m not talking about that. I’m saying that while waiting for salvation is one thing, being kept waiting is another.”

“――I suppose so~.”

To Heinkel’s honest feelings spat out in a tit-for-tat exchange, however, the teasing tone had vanished from Sakura’s voice as she responded.
Even when he looked at her in suspicion, he couldn’t detect any change in her expression or the color of her eyes that had made him think so; it seemed it had just been his imagination.
In any case――

“――Let’s just quietly perform our duties~. If we do that, surely at least one good thing will happen~.”

Heinkel felt that this murmur was far too modest for a member of the "Divine Dragon Church," and at the same time, terribly earnest.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


“――I’ve decided! If writing a hundred letters didn’t work, then I will go hand-deliver the hundred and first! If I plead with them looking right at their face, they will surely find it hard to refuse!”

At the words of Philoore, whose face looked as if she had received a divine revelation from the "Dragon" itself, her knight, bodyguard, and secretary were each forced to accompany her to the royal castle.
Philoore’s attitude of determination that *this time* she would succeed was something to be respected, but――

“――I cannot accept this!!”

The moment they stepped out of the drawing room and began walking down the chalk-white corridor, Philoore raised her voice as if she had reached the limit of her patience.
At her furious voice echoing down the castle corridor, passing civil servants and servants looked over in surprise. Mindful of them, Tiga placed his index finger over his lips.

“Philoore, raising your voice in a place like this is too conspicuous. If that reaches the ears of the higher-ups, next time you’ll be turned away at the entrance hall instead of the drawing room.”

“But how can they treat us like this?! I told them that if they let us know what’s missing, we’ll prepare it on our end, yet it’s always ‘under consideration’, ‘postponed’, or ‘completely in the dark’...! What on earth are we supposed to do, and how?!”

“There’s probably nothing to add or correct in particular~.”

“Then they should just let it through!”

“――They have no intention of listening from the very start.”

Philoore, appealing in her anger, turned around at Heinkel’s words. Her red eyes were moist with tears, but Heinkel had no handkerchief to wipe them with.
In place of a handkerchief, he gave her the answer she wanted without mincing words.

“The reason you’re being blocked is neither a defect in the paperwork nor a lack of conditions. What those guys want is for you to give up and stop bringing this topic up, Philoore-jou.”

“That’s, that’s just wrong! Who would think of such a thing?”

“Yeah, I think that’s the real problem too.”

Though Philoore looked as if she were throwing a tantrum, she had done everything she could before resorting to one, yet she was not allowed to do anything beyond throwing a tantrum.
In truth, the question Philoore currently harbored was one Heinkel could not answer either, and the mystery over the past month and a half had only grown.

“...Just when I thought everything was being set up all too conveniently.”

Honestly, Heinkel had felt that except for Philoore’s late entry into the Royal Selection, everything else had been thoroughly set up by the "Divine Dragon Church."
However, once the lid was opened, while her support in the chaotic capital was steadily rising due to her own energy and goodness, the visit to the Water Gate City that should have solidified her foundation—was blocked by none other than the church’s upper echelon, the Sacred Council, who refused to grant permission.

If they wanted to make Philoore King, this was undoubtedly a move that had to be played.
Despite this, the "Divine Dragon Church" consistently took an attitude of blocking this path rather than backing it, and the actions of the Council of Sages were not siding with Philoore either.
At this rate, Heinkel’s distrust toward the church was starting to accumulate in a different form than it had initially.
However, even if the way his distrust grew changed, some things remained the same. ――The fact that Philoore was an immature child whom adults had to stand by.

“Philoore, I understand how you feel, but let’s head back for now.”

Prompted by Tiga’s gentle voice, the frustrated Philoore let her hand be pulled as they started walking.
On the way to the castle entrance, the castle affiliates passing by stopped and bowed politely upon seeing Philoore.
As a Royal Selection candidate and as the "Priestess," there was no lie in the respect directed toward her.
Yet, it was ironic that not a single one of her wishes was granted.

And so, dragging a heavy silence, they turned the corner of the corridor――

“――”

Involuntarily catching sight of the person walking from straight ahead, Heinkel gasped.

“――Reinhard.”

Before he could think, the breath he had swallowed exhaled that name into sound.
Dressed in a white knight’s uniform, the red-haired youth with the "Dragon Sword" at his waist—Reinhard van Astrea—also looked straight at the person who had called his name.
Naturally, their respective blue eyes, which indicated their blood relation, locked gazes.

“It has been a long time, Father.”

“……Yeah.”

He cursed himself for such a pathetic reply.
It was a complete surprise attack. Caught up in his thoughts, it was such an unexpected encounter that he couldn’t instantly bring up the insults and curses he usually would have prepared.
In the end, unable to find any words other than that stupid response, silence fell between father and son.

“――”

Behind Reinhard, two people clad in the white uniforms of the knights accompanied him. At first glance, they could be seen as subordinates accompanying the "Sword Saint" on a mission.
However, the gazes of the two were not on their surroundings, but constantly focused on Reinhard’s movements.
Above all, Heinkel did not recognize the faces of those knights. ――At least, they were knights of unknown origin, unfamiliar to Heinkel.
Why were these knights accompanying Reinhard――

“――! Reinhard! This is perfect! I wanted to talk to you too!”

Treading right through the murky air between father and son, Philoore ran up to Reinhard. The knights behind him instinctively braced themselves, but Reinhard raised a hand to stop them.

“Philoore-sama. You were at the castle.”

“Yes. I was talking about Emilia, Felt, and Pristella. But right now, instead of my matters, it’s about you.”

“Me?”

“Yes. How is Felt? You meet with her often, don’t you?”

“――”

Having the distance closed without any holding back, Reinhard quietly caught his breath.
Eventually, Reinhard slowly shook his head. “No.”

“Pardon? Does that mean... you haven’t met with her today yet?”

“That is not the case. I mean since Felt-sama entered the castle.”

“Felt is in the castle... and not once?! Even though more than a month has already passed?!”

Philoore’s voice jumped, and the knights behind him tensed their shoulders in unison. Yet, Reinhard quietly nodded at Philoore’s reaction.
However, it was no wonder Philoore was surprised. This was on a completely different level from Philoore not being granted permission to meet with Emilia and the others.

“You are Felt’s knight, aren’t you? For you not to be able to meet Felt, that is incredibly strange. Don’t you think so?”

“Visitation is forbidden, so it cannot be helped.”

“B-But you want to meet her, don’t you? You must want to talk.”

“I am the sword of the kingdom. Since an order was handed down from the castle, I have a duty to obey it.”

“You are not just the kingdom’s sword, but Felt’s sword as well!”

Philoore’s words, spoken with wide eyes as if she had been told something unbelievable, were so straightforward that one could almost see them piercing Reinhard’s chest.
However, Heinkel knew all too well how Reinhard would respond to Philoore’s unconscious denunciation.
That was――

“――I am the sword of the kingdom, Philoore-sama.”

The conversation went in a circle, returning to the initial answer.
It wasn’t that Philoore’s words were lacking. Nor was it that Reinhard didn’t understand what she was saying.
No matter what was said to him, no matter what he felt, Reinhard would not change his answer.
Perhaps it was more accurate to say he *could not* change it.

“Do you think Felt deceived the kingdom?”

“No.”

That, too, was an immediate reply without hesitation.
Before Philoore, her eyes trembling, could ask “Then why?”, Reinhard continued.

“Me believing in Felt-sama and being able to prove her innocence are two different things. If I disobey orders, it will only deepen the suspicions against Felt-sama and make her position even worse.”

“So, you have no choice but to listen?”

“――”

To that question, Reinhard chose neither affirmation nor denial.
However, choosing neither was, in itself, a declaration of his stance.

“――”

Heinkel looked at the two knights standing behind Reinhard.
It was obvious they were not Reinhard’s allies. If so, their role would be to monitor Reinhard, but what a meaningless measure.
If Reinhard wished, he could knock them down in an instant, or vanish before they could even notice, reaching Felt without being stopped by anyone in the castle.

But Reinhard would not do that. He could not.
As long as he was the "Sword Saint," that was――

“In that case, at least tell Felt.”

To the silent Reinhard, Philoore clenched her fists and spoke.

“I believe in Felt. I believe in Emilia as well. Please tell her that there are people outside the castle who do not think the two of them betrayed the kingdom.”

“……Thank you very much.”

“A letter is fine too. I will write it.”

“But I cannot make that promise. I am also forbidden from delivering words or things to Felt-sama.”

“How can that be...!”

“I am sorry. I have my next mission.”

At his utterly painful reply, Philoore’s delicate face contorted.
Yet, Reinhard did not yield to Philoore’s reaction; leaving behind a bow, he attempted to pass by their side.
Just before his shoulder brushed Heinkel’s, his steps suddenly stopped.

“Father, I heard that you became Philoore-sama’s knight.”

“……Yeah, apparently. Got a complaint?”

“――Congratulations.”

Being told that before he could spit out a curse, Heinkel was lost for words.
At a distance close enough for their shoulders to touch, Reinhard’s gaze turned directly toward Heinkel. ――Faced with his son, whose height had fully caught up to his own before he knew it, Heinkel couldn’t bear it and averted his eyes.

“……Like I’d know if it’s something to congratulate.”

“Even so, I am glad that you were chosen, Father.”

“――”

Heinkel could say nothing back to those words.
It was neither sarcasm nor irony. Reinhard was sincerely congratulating his fallen father on being chosen as a knight by someone.

Did he not remember what kind of verbal abuse had been hurled at him in Pristella?
Had he forgotten that in that same city, Heinkel had pointed his sword at his own master?
Or were even those things mere trivialities that left not a single scratch on Reinhard’s heart?

With a small bow, accompanied by the two knights, Reinhard walked down the chalk-white corridor.
Philoore continued to watch his back with frustration. Unlike Philoore, Heinkel could not even bring himself to glare at the retreating figure of his son.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


“――Philoore, as expected, those remarks to Reinhard were careless.”

As soon as they returned to the church of the "Divine Dragon Church," Tiga closed the door to the drawing room and turned to Philoore with a stern face.
Though he had remained silent inside the dragon carriage on the way back from the castle, in his mind he must have been thinking deeply about how to reprimand her.

“You might have thought you were choosing your words, but there were castle knights and other affiliates present. If word reaches the Council of Sages, they will think even Philoore is rebellious toward the castle.”

“Let them think that. I really *am* rebellious.”

“That’s not what I’m talking about. I’m saying that if even you are banned from visits or entering the castle, you truly won’t be able to do anything.”

“I know that.”

“You don’t know, which is why you did such a――”

“I know!”

Philoore strongly cut off Tiga’s words.
Tiga gasped at her intensity, and Sakura also lowered her eyes. In response to their reactions, Philoore muttered in a hoarse voice, “I’m sorry,”

“I know... I’m sorry. But because I know, it makes me all the more angry. If we don’t obey what the castle decided, they won’t treat me as a Royal Selection candidate anymore. If they don’t treat me as a candidate, I can’t help Emilia and the others. That’s why I have to wait as I’m told.”

“Philoore...”

“I’m sorry. I fully understand that you two are speaking for my sake.”

“――Yeah.”

“Let me talk with Heinkel-sama alone for just a bit.”

At her request, Tiga nodded despite his hesitation.
Sakura left the room first, and Tiga followed behind her. The door closed, leaving only Philoore and Heinkel in the room.

“――”

Lined up on the desk was a month’s worth of replies received from the castle.
Philoore looked at each and every one of them, and eventually let out a quiet sigh.

“Heinkel-sama.”

“What is it?”

Even Philoore must be considerably worn down.
Her desperate appeals went unheard, and she couldn’t say her voice had reached Reinhard either. Even for her, who tackled everything with headlong momentum, it was only natural to be exhausted by this lack of response.

Heinkel also had a huge mental drain from the unexpected encounter with Reinhard and the unpredicted congratulations.
Even so, with at least some pride, he braced himself to receive Philoore’s words.

And, leaning her back against the closed door, Philoore told Heinkel a story she couldn’t let—didn’t want to let—the departed Tiga and Sakura hear.
That was――

“――I wonder, how does one successfully break out of the castle?”
