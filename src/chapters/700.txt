Arc 9 Chapter 6: "Setting Out"

—With those blue eyes, it had watched over countless choices.

A self born from nothing, from an empty space, was drawn toward the great light that had been there beside it all along, and as it touched that warmth, it slowly began to shape the outline of a vessel.
At first it was harshly kept at a distance, but the kindness inherent to that light would not allow itself to remain so through and through, and it went on letting the self stay close, again and again, without truly turning it away.
Before long, that overlooking became more than mere overlooking — a hand was actively extended, and at last it was acknowledged as a new being and given a name.

Rejection and compromise, conflict and acceptance, trust and faith, and forgiveness and absolution—.

He had always chosen.
Countless choices, innumerable choices, inexhaustible choices, chosen again and again by his own will and his own steps. It had watched them — sometimes up close, sometimes from afar, sometimes across distances that transcended the physical or the spiritual.
Not all of those choices of his ever led him to the place he wished to reach.

And still, he never stopped choosing, using every bit of strength he had.
With nothing else to rely on, it had kept watching that figure as its model. Even at the end of a painful prayer, even when a conclusion that made one want to weep waited at the far side, he never once stopped walking.
Was it really so strange, then, to have come to yearn for that back, that way of being?

—It, too, would have to choose.

As one fortunate enough to have come to know his rejection and his compromise, his conflict and his acceptance, his trust and his faith, his forgiveness and his absolution, it wanted to follow in the way he walked.

It seems this self has committed no small number of faults and mistakes.
So that the scars carved into it, which will never fade, are neither sealed away nor covered over, but never forgotten, never looked away from.
Not caring whether it is right, but believing in wanting to be right — just as he chooses his own path.

So that his — Natsuki Subaru's — choosing it will never become a mistake.


"—I never imagined you would be the one to come seeking words with Us."

Beyond the door that felt heavy not from any physical weight but from the weight of one's own resolve, a black-haired man sat at a fine desk, looking over a great stack of papers, and it was he who spoke those words.
It knew that with those sharp black eyes, he too had stared down countless choices, had chosen the one thing among all that could only be chosen once, and now sat in that seat because of it.

All of it was the result of the man's choices — and of his choices as well.
And then—,

"As you can see, We are busy. It is only because that woman — Medium O'Connell — put in a word for you that We have made time at all, but We cannot spare much. If there is something you desire, We shall provide it, provided it is fitting to your service. Although—"

There, the man paused, lifting his gaze from the stack of papers to look this way. Deep within those dark black eyes turned a very clever king, weighing all manner of intentions.
He kept his eyes fixed on the one standing before the door and opened his thin lips.

"We have no words to offer for that man's current state. At the very least, We cannot go to him Ourself, nor should We. —That is, if that was what you had hoped for."

Asked that, it shook its head hard, side to side.
There was something it wished to ask of the man before it, but it did not concern him — Subaru — directly.
There was probably nothing it could do for him now, just as it was for all the many others around him besides itself. So the reason it had come here was something else.

Surely, in the end, he would choose to stand again.
It had come here to prepare for that moment, for when he stood up again.
So that when he chose to stand, it too would be a self that had properly chosen—,

"Then what is your purpose here?"

Gazed at by the man's black eyes, it looked back with its blue ones, and let a beat pass.
Probably, in this vast, vast country called the Empire, the eyes that had watched over his choices the most were its own blue eyes, the pale blue eyes of that gentle woman, and the black eyes of the man before it.
Precisely because of that, it opened its mouth to the one who knew the weight of choosing.
And then—,

"—Uh!"

—so the single life born from an empty self stepped forward to choose.

—On the day Subaru and the rest of the Emilia Camp were to depart the Vollachia Empire, rain fell from the morning on — a rarity for a Vollachia that had seen nothing but absurdly clear skies on end.

"Seriously, I really hate the Vollachia Empire..."

Subaru twisted his lips and grumbled that, unable to get along with the Empire's climate from start to finish, it couldn't even manage to send them off on a pleasant note.
Though he hated the Vollachia Empire, the encounters he'd had in this country hadn't been bad at all.
If anything, that might be the single worst thing about the time he'd spent in this country—.


"Boss, you really have done so much for us! Do take care in the Kingdom too!"

"What are you talking about, Flop-san. I'm the one who's been in your debt this whole time. And I haven't managed to pay back a single bit of it..."

"Hahaha, don't be silly. Do you have any idea how much luck you shared with me and my little sister through everything you did? Both Medium and I even found ourselves partners! It's cause for celebration all around!"

So he said, thumping his own thin chest with vigor.
While Subaru had been down and out, this man had apparently shown what he was made of — succeeding, of all things, in proposing to Taritta — which left Subaru equal parts astonished and impressed.
Taritta had apparently always had a thing for looks, just like her older sister Mizelda, but had also been drawn to Flop's character, so Subaru was glad to hear things had worked out well.
Subaru felt nothing but gratitude toward both Flop and Taritta.

"Boss, allow me to express my gratitude."

"—————"

And with that, Flop embraced him straight-on, and Subaru's breath caught. To Subaru, who stiffened despite himself, Flop spoke in his usual — no, even more than usual — sentimental voice.

"That day, when you appeared before Medium and me carrying your wife seated in a chair, I thought I'd had a tremendously fateful encounter. And that wasn't a mistake at all. From beneath a distant sky, I'll be wishing for your happiness, and the happiness of everyone dear to you."

"Ah..."

"If you ever come back to the Empire, please get in touch anytime. And if I ever find myself in the Kingdom, I'll be sure to rely on you! I do hope you'll lend me your strength!"

He pulled back, laughing brightly, thoroughly, like the midday sun — Flop O'Connell.
From their very first meeting to this moment of parting, he had never once lost his cheerfulness, and the presence of a man like that shook Subaru's heart to its core.

The first person he'd met in the Vollachia Empire had been the emperor with his face hidden, and after that, the People of Shudrak, who had fought alongside him the whole way through. —But surely, probably, the encounter that had blessed Natsuki Subaru the most in the Vollachia Empire had been the O'Connell siblings, Flop and Medium.
Feeling that anew, strongly, strongly—,

"—Yeah, thank YOU, Flop-san! I love you, man!"

Subaru laughed back at him, putting into those words the greatest gratitude he'd felt in all of the Vollachia Empire.

"...I don't think you should go around saying 'I love you' so lightly to just anyone. It makes everything you say feel cheap."

"No, I don't say it to just anyone. Only to people I actually love."

"Is that so? Including Emilia-san and Beatrice-chan?"

"I tell Bea-ko morning, noon, and night, but it's a little hard to say to Emilia-tan... Oh, but I think I might've said it to sis before."

"Huh?"

"Sorry, it was just the momentum of the moment, but important is important, okay."

Glared at sharply by those pale blue eyes, Subaru shrank back meekly, apologizing over and over.
He'd been putting on a moving farewell with Flop just moments before — the contrast was too stark. Still, the eyes that had welled up at parting with a friend had managed, somehow, not to spill any tears.

"A man can't just cry easily... A man only gets three times in his life he's allowed to cry in public."

"Is that so."

"Yeah, that's right. ...Huh? Don't you usually ask what the three times are?"

"That face you made, so obviously wanting me to ask — it was unpleasant, so..."

"Isn't 'unpleasant' a bit much!?"

Subaru raised his voice at such an awful thing to be told, and hearing it, Rem sighed. At this natural back-and-forth with Rem, Subaru too let out a long breath.
These past few days, largely due to a problem of Subaru's own making, things had been terribly awkward with Rem, of course, and with the others too. Probably, likely, considerably, outrageously worrying them all — so from here on, he intended to mend his attitude and make up for it through his actions.

"What about you, Rem? Did you get to talk properly with Katya-san?"

"Whether it was proper or not, I'm not sure... but yes. She said she'd send me letters. I'd like to send some too, but I don't know what to write."

"It's letters between friends, right? Something like updates on how you're doing, or 'are you doing okay?' — that kind of direction, I'd think. Though I've never actually exchanged letters with a friend myself, so I can't really say."

"Is it really all right for it to be such aimless, rambling things?"

"I think it's fine. Aimless, rambling things and all."

Answering the troubled-looking Rem, Subaru presumptuously tried to imagine what was in Katya's heart.
As Todd's fiancée, and as someone who'd witnessed his final moments, Subaru hadn't really managed to sit down and talk properly with Katya. Still, toward Katya, who had stayed with Rem after she was taken and had become her friend, Subaru felt nothing but gratitude and goodwill.
He didn't think what someone like Katya would want out of exchanging letters with Rem was some dramatic tale being written out.

"What matters is proof — that you're still connected, that you're thinking of each other, that kind of thing. Even if you're not within arm's reach, I think it's important that it comes across that you've still got a place in the corner of each other's minds."

"—————"

"D-Did that not make sense, or come off creepy or something?"

"......No. For once, I was simply convinced, that's all."

To Subaru's timid question, Rem shook her head slowly. Then she loosened her lips and continued, "Yes, I suppose so,"

"Just as you say, I don't think it's something to be anxious about. If I put myself in the reverse position, what I'd want from Katya-san's letters wouldn't be anything special either — just that a letter from Katya-san arrives at all."

"Yeah."

At Rem's nodded reply, Subaru grinned and gave her a thumbs up. The gesture itself was ignored, but Rem, without any sign of dislike, walked along beside him.
Considering how their new relationship in the Empire had begun, even this was a miraculous, dramatic change.

"—————"

And so, having finished their respective farewells, Subaru and Rem walked side by side toward their next destination — there too waited someone with whom the two of them needed to exchange parting words.
The one the two of them, Subaru and Rem, needed to speak with together was—,

"—Spica."

"Uau! Eu!"

Turning at the call, Spica's face lit up in an instant. Swinging the golden hair tied back at the nape of her neck like a tail, she came dashing furiously toward Subaru and the others.
Subaru spread both arms wide, bracing himself to catch her—,

"Whoa."

"...Wait, that's how it's gonna be!?"

Subaru swung and missed, and beside him, Rem caught Spica as she leapt into her arms.
With an "Euu—," Spica rubbed her cheek and head against her insistently, and though Rem was startled at first, she soon broke into a loving smile, stroking Spica's head in answer to the affection.
At the sight of the two of them, Subaru sighed "geez" and folded his now-purposeless hands behind his head.

"You're still glued to Rem as ever. If I tried the same thing, I bet Rem would give me the coldest look imaginable."

"What? What's with suddenly stating the obvious?"

"See, this! Spica, help me out here!"

"Uu, u! Au, aau!"

"See, even Spica's on my—...huh? Is she mad at me? Don't tell me Spica's on Rem's side too? I've got no allies at all!"

Still clinging to Rem's waist, Spica fixed Subaru with a sharp, resolute look. His shoulders slumped at that gaze, and Rem said, exasperated, "What are you even doing."
At the exchange between Rem and Spica, Subaru felt oddly, strangely at ease, and scratched his head.

"—————"

Looking back, these outrageous days of hardship in the Vollachia Empire had begun with the three of them present here — Subaru, Rem, and Spica.
They had walked this far on a power balance almost like rock-paper-scissors — Subaru weak against Rem, Rem weak against Spica, Spica weak against Subaru.
That triangular relationship had, at some point, changed shape — and now, on this day, it would change once again.
After all—,

"You don't want to be apart from Rem, right? Wouldn't it be better to give up on staying in the Empire and come back to the Kingdom with us instead?"

"U!"

"Oh, so you're coming around! Yeah, that's right! The Empire's the worst, isn't it!"

"She's shaking her head no. Please stop twisting Spica-chan's intentions."

Glared at by a flat-eyed Rem, Subaru went stiff-cheeked with a "ugh" and fell silent. Leaning her back against Rem's chest, Spica nodded over and over with a serious face.
Even from that expression alone, it came through clearly that she had no intention of bending her will.
For Subaru, it was a decision terribly, terribly hard to nod along to.

—Spica, remaining behind in the Vollachia Empire.

Parting from Subaru and the others as they returned to the Kingdom, she would stay in the Empire and take up a certain duty.
That was the course Spica had settled on for her own future, decided through discussions with those around her while Subaru had been absorbed in his 'Sparka' with the Pleiades Battalion.

"Staying in Vollachia, and sending every last Undead brought back to life by the Great Disaster back to Od Lagna with 'Starfeast'... I do want to support that idea itself, but—"

Many Undead had been revived by the 'Witch' Sphinx and used as the vanguard of the Great Disaster.
Even now that the Great Disaster had been resolved, those who hadn't been destroyed had fled and remained scattered throughout the Empire. Spica had determined that it was her duty to consume every last one of those Undead, without exception.
But—,

"Unlike before, the Undead can't infinitely revive anymore. Even without 'Starfeast,' they should be able to be dealt with just by defeating them. Spica doesn't have to be the one to do it, you know?"

"Aa, au. U! Uu, u!"

"If it's like that, the souls used in reviving the dead won't properly return to where they belong? That's... something you probably understand instinctively better than I do, but — does it have to be you?"

"U!"

At Subaru's persuading words, Spica nodded with a face that said her resolve was firm.
They'd already gone through this exchange nearly ten times, Subaru struggling to change Spica's mind by every approach he could think of, but up to this day, he hadn't gotten the outcome he'd hoped for.

What was infuriating was that Abel, having been consulted on this resolve and decision of Spica's, had taken advantage of Subaru being unable to pull away from his 'Sparka' and had thoroughly talked her into it.
Of course, even if that tunnel-visioned Subaru of that time had been consulted, he doubted he could have had a positive, constructive discussion for Spica's sake.
He doubted it, but—,

"It's that way he has of getting everything to go exactly how he wants, all under the surface where nobody sees... that's what I don't like."

"I don't think it's fair to talk like it's all Abel-san's fault. This is Spica-chan's own idea, and besides, she went to consult Medium-san first."

"I know that! I know, but — this uneasy feeling, I can't help it, all right...!"

To Subaru, who could only argue from pure emotion, Rem shook her head slowly.
Though even as she did, Rem's expression couldn't quite hide a sense of loneliness — she too was one who, just like Subaru, had earnestly discussed Spica's future with her directly.
It might be an odd thing to say, but for Rem, whose memories hadn't returned, Spica's influence was a large part of what shaped who she was now. It should be far harder for her to be parted from Spica than for him.
And yet—,

"When you and I move to realize our own thoughts and wishes, is it not allowed for Spica-chan? ...This child has spent so long caring so much for us."

"Rem..."

Gently stroking Spica's head, Rem honored the girl's momentous decision.
Ahead of the less-than-graceful Subaru, she had firmly resolved to see Spica off. That stance of hers was admirable, and of course, Subaru understood what she was saying.

"Even so, hard is hard..."

Even if he was told he lacked grace, that he couldn't accept things gracefully, that he was overprotective, overbearing, and creepy about it — it was hard.
Spica's decision was, of course, admirable, but to be honest, while Subaru wanted the souls of the dead used by the Great Disaster to find peaceful rest, his feeling that he didn't want Spica doing anything dangerous outweighed even that.
In his current heartbroken state, Subaru's true feelings were such that he wanted to put all the people important to him into a box and carry it around with him, never letting it leave his side.

"But neither Spica nor Rem will get into my box..."

"...How did we get from that conversation to talk of a box?"

"The box doesn't matter, forget the box! What's important right now isn't the box, it's Spica. Right?"

"The box is something you— Abel-san takes this matter seriously too, you know. That's why he's arranging for the two strongest people in this country to accompany Spica-chan, isn't he."

"Cecil's nothing but strong! He'll be bad for Spica's upbringing!"

Subaru loudly insisted that Rem's counterargument wasn't really a counterargument at all.
For Spica, who had decided on a journey devouring the Undead, the ones Abel had proposed as companions were none other than Cecilus Segmunt and Arakiya.
Certainly, any worry about Spica in situations requiring combat strength would vanish.

"But that's all there is to it. If anything, the downsides outside of raw strength stand out way too much, and I strongly suspect that jackass Abel just dumped a headache onto Spica."

"You're being far too suspicious. Arakiya-san — the Spirit that woman incorporated was the source of the power that revived the dead. So she has the ability to sense the whereabouts of Undead who've fled, as Abel-san himself explained to us..."

"I've got trauma from Arakiya too! I've been done in by water more times than I can count! Cecil, who seems like he gets what you're saying but doesn't, and Arakiya, who seems like she won't get it and doesn't... the anxiety's coming right back up! Spica! I can't just let you become a beast-tamer—"

—was what Subaru meant to say, but the words were cut off partway through.
That was because Spica, leaving Rem's side, had leapt at Subaru's chest. Catching her light body, Subaru caught his breath, and Spica looked up at him from below.
And then—,

"Uau."

At the sight of Spica's face, smiling as she said just that one word, Subaru found he couldn't say anything more.

"...That damn Abel, going and settling things with Spica while I was busy — he's a truly cowardly, filthy bastard."

"That's too much. Let's stop at calling him underhanded, cunning, and a bit lacking in character."

"Is that actually any different from what I just said?"

"...I don't want to be separated from Spica-chan either, you know."

To Subaru, who had voiced his resentment in a weak, rasping voice, Rem answered while hiding the tremor in her own.
Feeling Rem's loneliness come through in that, Subaru swallowed his own weakness. Then, pulling Spica in his arms in tight,

"Listen, don't go relying on Abel or Cecil, all right? If you run into trouble, turn to Medium-san and Flop-san. And get their help writing your letters too."

"Uu."

"Having a purpose and a goal is important, but don't push yourself too hard. No need to rush — go at your own pace, that's fine. Rushing and overdoing it is the most dangerous thing there is. That's my golden rule, so remember it."

"Aa, u!"

"There's no clear-cut deadline, no fixed date set for this. So neither you nor I can say 'about this long,' but even so... I'll be waiting, okay."

"Uu-u, eau, aua, aau."

"...You decided for yourself, properly, how to use your own power. That's amazing, Spica. —I need to take a page from your book too."

He truly meant that, from the bottom of his heart.
Spica had decided, of her own will, to use that dreadful Authority of 'Gluttony' rightly. That through the way she used that power, she would become a help in removing the sorrows of a deeply wounded Empire.
That, precisely, was Spica's means of breaking away from her own detestable past self, from Rui Arneb.

"—Uau!"

Whether Subaru's heartfelt admiration had gotten through to her, he didn't know.
But Spica spun around and slipped out of Subaru's arms, taking his hand instead and starting to pull him along. Dragged by the momentum, Subaru was forced to walk along with her.
And so, while pulling Subaru along, Spica held out her free hand to Rem,

"Eu!"

"...Yes, Spica-chan."

Called to with a smile, Rem took the hand Spica held out.
Just like that, with Spica in the middle holding hands with both Subaru and Rem, the three of them began walking side by side. —Even though the three of them had been together all this while, they had never once walked side by side like this.
Honestly, he hadn't even thought it was something they could do. And yet, on their last day in the Empire, here they were, doing just that.

"U!"

Putting her weight on Subaru's and Rem's hands, Spica swung her body cheerfully back and forth. Over the top of her head, Subaru's and Rem's eyes met.
And then, without either of them quite meaning to, they both burst out laughing.

"Pfft."
"Fufu."
"Aau, uau!"

With Spica joining in too, the three of them walked on toward the meeting place, laughing.
That was the moment the three of them closed out, together, the story of the Vollachia Empire that the three of them had begun together.

"—That girl in question can now set aside that troublesome title of hers, 'Sin Archbishop.' You, too, should find a fitting weight lifted from your shoulders."

"You, seriously..."

To Subaru, who was stroking the neck of Patrasche — who had come all the way out to the sticks of the Empire, hauling luggage far too little for the sheer number of events that had happened — His Excellency the Emperor of Vollachia declared that with a thoroughly high-and-mighty expression.
That he'd gone out of his way to come see them off, and still never failed to be sarcastic about it — Subaru had to admire the sheer nerve of it.

"I'm seriously begging you, don't go and pull some rebellion-followed-by-beheading regime change thing again. I'm sick and tired of running myself ragged to save you."

"Fool. Your hand is no longer needed. Be swift, and return to your Kingdom."

"You bastard."

"You have your own duties to fulfill. See to it that you never mistake them, not for a moment."

"—————"

It sounded like the sort of sarcasm Abel uttered as naturally as breathing, and yet it carried, ever so faintly, a nuance beyond that alone.
It made him a little anxious, wondering whether an emperor who required people to pick up on such needlessly hard-to-read subtleties could really manage well in the Empire going forward—

"Well, I guess he'll be fine as long as Medium-san and the others are around."

Having heard the Stargazers' prophecy of a coming Great Disaster and an emperor who would be lost, Abel had imposed upon himself days with no private self at all, spending them so until now.
That self-imposed restraint had now been lifted, and from here on, Abel would go forward as Vincent Vollachia, for the first time as an emperor bound by nothing beyond preparing for the Great Disaster.
He might be at a loss at first, but surely, he too would come, at least somewhat, to look after those around him.
As proof of that—,

"In this recent crisis, that girl bore a great role. What's more, she has pledged her efforts toward removing the Empire's future sorrows as well. —We shall see that she is given renown to match her ill repute."

"—————"

That was Abel's true intention behind pushing Spica, who had decided to remain in the Empire, toward that decision.
The story in the Empire had begun with the three of them — Subaru, Rem, and Spica. —And this was, in his own way, how Abel, one of the first to intervene in that story, chose to repay Subaru.

No matter how many words were spent, the fact that Spica's past self had been the Sin Archbishop of 'Gluttony' would not change, and there would always be many who condemned and rejected her for it.
Those who held such negative feelings could not be denied that right, nor should they be.
And still, if one wished to affirm the life, the very existence, of a single girl named Spica, and wanted to give that wish form, there was nothing to do but build it up, piece by piece.

—A renown greater than her infamy, one that would allow Spica to live as Spica.

"Remember this, Natsuki Subaru. The Sword-Wolves of the Sacred Vollachia Empire never forget a wound dealt them. It is the same whether that wound comes from hatred or from friendship."

"Abel..."

"If anything should arise, speak of it. You, and those who walk the same path as you, are all counted equally among the Sword-Wolves."

With that single line, Subaru felt that he'd been shown proof of the inner heart of Abel — Vincent Vollachia, a man never permitted to bow his head to another or curry favor with anyone.
To Subaru, tasting a tremor from the very root of his heart at that feeling, Abel nodded, and said,

"Well done. —On the road, and every day from now on, walk with your feet planted firmly on the ground."

"...You too. I'm not lending you a hand anymore if you fall."

"Fool."

So he said, cursing at Subaru, and yet at the corner of his mouth there was, without question, a smile that was something other than mockery.
Having witnessed that—,

"—————"

Turning his back, Subaru set off walking toward the dragon carriage.
As Subaru walked with the Emperor's gaze on his back, Emilia and the others waited by the dragon carriage. Along the way, he took in the faces that had come to see them off, and at the sight of the Pleiades Battalion, of course present among them, Subaru caught a small breath.

"—————"

The 'Sparka' that had begun at Subaru's own pleading, the reckoning owed to nine hundred and thirty-one people, with six hundred seventy-three strikes still remaining — everyone had refused to listen when Subaru insisted it couldn't end like this.
And now, with all of them gathered, when their eyes met Subaru's, who had failed to make good on his word—,

"—Ah."

—one after another, the assembled members of the Pleiades Battalion raised their hands, their rough, work-worn hands lining the path Subaru would walk like a flower-strewn aisle.
Understanding what they intended, Subaru lifted his face once. He endured, and endured, and endured, and endured, so that what was welling up wouldn't spill from his eyes, and then—,

"I love you all!"

Voicing once more the very words that had gotten him scolded by Rem, Subaru ran.
To the many hands reaching out from left and right, Subaru went and met them with his own. A sharp smack rang out, hand meeting hand, chaining without pause, one after another, like the sound of falling rain.
Beyond just clapping palms, there were hands that struck at Subaru's whole body too. Buffeted by them, buffeted and buffeted again, probably forgetting entirely that six hundred seventy-three strikes still remained, those who knew Natsuki Schwartz lent their strength to Subaru's 'Sparka.'

"This was quite fun in its own right, even for a professional."

His back was struck by four thick, enormous hands.

"Thanks to you, a plain old miller's son got to dream a dream!"

He clapped hands with a man far more solid and lithe than his looks would suggest.

"Dammit, dammit! Thanks, Schwartz!"

As always with tears in his eyes, his tongue trembling as he shouted, Subaru bumped fists with the Lizard Human's tail.

"Don't forget... we're your... comrades...!"

As they passed each other, a fairly hard knuckle punch landed on the side of his face, and Subaru, holding his jaw, nodded, "Got it."
And then—,

"—Schwartz-sama."

Passing through the crush of being roughed up by everyone, he came at last before the girl in a kimono who had been waiting for him. She waited alongside a woman likewise dressed in a kimono, Yorna.
Stopping before the two of them, Subaru first met Yorna's eyes.

"Yorna-san, thank you for everything. And also..."

"No need for apologies, dearie. What's between me and that child is a matter between me and that child."

"—————"

"Do come show your face again anytime, you hear. You'll always be more than welcome, dearie."

To the mistress of the demon city, her lips loosening into a gentle smile, Subaru gave a deep nod. "Yeah."
Then, once more, he turned to face the girl standing beside Yorna, Tanza—,

"You called me Schwartz-sama again."

"......That's simply the way of addressing you that comes most naturally to my tongue."

"And it wouldn't sit right with me either, if you called me anything else."

So saying, with a soft, loosened smile, Subaru crouched down where he stood.
They had once been at the same eye level, but now he had to bend his knees like this for their heights to match. Even now, back to that difference in height, he wanted to speak with her at the same eye level.
Because Tanza had been, without question, an unshakable, indispensable benefactor to Subaru in the Empire.

"—Next time, I surely won't lose."

"—? What do you mean?"

"It's the spell you cast on me. Because of it, I didn't have to give up."

Blinking her dark, wide eyes, Tanza searched her own memory for those same words. But no matter how hard she tried, they wouldn't surface.
That was because that great debt of gratitude Subaru could never fully repay existed now only within Subaru himself.
That was fine. Whether or not it had actually happened in this world, that was fine.
Because within Subaru, an undimming gratitude and joy existed, without question—.

"I love you, Tanza."

"...Carrying on like that, you're sure to regret it eventually, Schwartz-sama."

"Rem said something similar to me. If we had more time, I bet you two would get along great... though in exchange, I might just die from all the cold stares!?"

"Would you please not get all worked up on your own and then die on your own too. ...Besides, I think acting as though this were some final farewell in this life will come back to strangle you later, Schwartz-sama."

"Hm? What do you mean by that?"

"You'll find out eventually."

Tanza gave no answer to Subaru, who tilted his head.
She only lightly clapped her hand against the one Subaru held out, joining in on the 'Sparka' just as the Battalion's members had, and then took that hand gently in both of hers,

"I had the pleasure of serving you for far longer than I ever expected. Please, do keep well in mind and spirit."

"That's a pretty long way to say goodbye — there's a shorter way to put it, you know."

"I won't say it. Please, go on home."

Released by an unaffectionate Tanza, Subaru gave a wry smile and used that same hand to stroke her head.
That she hadn't shaken it off, and the little mystery her attitude had left behind — Subaru received both as gifts from her.
And then—,

"U!"

Turning around, he found Spica waiting there beside Rem, in front of the dragon carriage.
He'd already said more than enough clingy, reluctant things. And still, he thought to convey once more his wish to just take her along with them — but Spica's embrace cut all of it short.
Holding tight to Spica as she leapt at him, stroking her head, Subaru lifted his face,

"I'm counting on you for real, when it comes to Spica, Cecil."

"All right, Boss!"

At that reply from a guardian who was reliable and yet endlessly worrying at the same time, Subaru gave him one last high-five.
With that done, he turned around.

Taking in every face gathered there, Subaru drew a breath.
And then—,

"See you all again! —VICTORY!!"

"—VICTORY!!"

With both arms thrust up toward the sky, at Subaru's shout, the voices of the Pleiades Battalion rang out in answer.
At that force, those unfamiliar with the Battalion's little tradition were thoroughly startled, while the Battalion's own members, in contrast, laughed uproariously and wept, and a ridiculous, raucous scene spread out.

"—A thoroughly insolent, colossal fool, right to the very end."

Such was the ridiculous, raucous scene of farewell — enough to draw even that comment from the Emperor of Vollachia himself.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


"...I wonder if Spica will be all right. Can she really manage well without us?"

"Honestly, you've been fretting on and on and on about it, I dare say! There's no need to worry, I suppose! That girl has her feet planted on reality far more firmly than you do, Subaru, wouldn't you say!"

Inside the dragon carriage on the road home, Beatrice, sitting on the lap of Subaru — who kept propping his cheek on his hand and staring out the window — scolded him by pulling on his cheek.
Twisting his lips at Beatrice's scolding, Subaru still couldn't argue back.

In truth, it was so. Just as Beatrice said, Spica was the one thinking more realistically.
Taking her past as a Sin Archbishop seriously, Spica had begun to act to drive away that ill name — even if it was for the sin of a "self" she had no memory of.

"Given how thoroughly cold you were to Spica-chan, that child had plenty of chances to learn just what position she'd been placed in."

"Oh my, is that so. Circumstances being what they were, that's just like you, Barusu. Even toward a girl who's so attached to you, your narrow-mindedness remains alive and well."

"Uguuh."

From across the way, the demon sisters, sitting side by side with their hands stacked together, launched demonic verbal barbs at him.
That too was a pointed remark about Subaru's own past conduct that he couldn't argue against. It was heartwarming, how in sync the sisters were — but the sharpness of it left little room to just feel warmed.

"Personally, I find myself relieved by that child's judgment. To begin with, if we'd brought her back with us, she was the sort of child who'd have needed risk management on the level of locking her in a box..."

"Is it just me, or does your idea of a box sound like it'd have a rather different purpose and comfort level than mine?"

"Who's to say. In any case, on this matter, we're accomplices with both the Empire and the Anastasia Camp alike... As a landing point, I'd call it quite a good one."

"Dunno 'bout anythin' too difficult, but once all the zombies're cleaned up, she'll come on back from the Empire free an' clear, righ'? Then she oughta just hurry up an' get it done, tha's all."

Against Otto, who spoke from a harsh, realistic point of view, Garfiel's attitude — simply, honestly looking forward to Spica rejoining them — was something Subaru was grateful for.
Garfiel too surely had some reluctance about a Sin Archbishop, but—,

"Even I've gone toe-to-toe with the boss an' the others an' damn near killed 'em before. A second dip at the sauce, like tha' 'Vinueur double-dip' thing — ain't a bad chance to have, is it."

"You've got a big heart, huh. Compared to that..."

"I'll have you know, it's not that Natsuki-san's and Garfiel's hearts are big — it'd be more accurate to say they've got irresponsible holes blown clean through them!"

To Otto, raising his voice to shout that, Subaru and Garfiel bumped fists with each other.
It had been a while since he'd heard Otto's voice with this much energy in it. At long last freed from the heavy burden of having to save the Empire, Subaru too was finding his old rhythm returning.
Though that didn't mean his worry for his companions, or his concern for a Spica now parted from him, had grown any smaller—,

"—We're all here together, you know."

Emilia, seated beside him, spoke those words to Subaru's profile as he narrowed his eyes.
At the gentle feel of his hand being squeezed, he turned, and found her soft violet eyes gazing at him with such tenderness that his chest tightened.

No one asked Subaru.
Whether he was all right now, whether he was feeling down — those obvious sorts of questions.
He was down, and he was hurt, and whether he was really all right or not, he didn't even know himself. It was because they understood how he felt without even having to ask.

"Thank you, Emilia. —For coming to save me."

"Mm."

"Thank you, everyone. —For coming to save us."

To Emilia, to everyone, who had crossed the border into the Vollachia Empire — that land of carnage — to come save Subaru and the others, Subaru voiced that gratitude.
To Subaru's thanks, each and every one of them answered in their own way—,

"—————"

There were things that had slipped through his fingers.
Things that had slid through the gaps between his fingers and gone off to some far place he could never reach again.
It had been a way of living so very like her. Not a single thing had gone the way Subaru or anyone else wanted. To the very last, no matter how far it went, truly, not once.
Perhaps that was why, even though there was so much, so much regret—,

"—Right now, all I can remember is your smiling face."

He mourned her, the one who had burned only that fearless, arrogant image of herself into Subaru's memory.
That was Natsuki Subaru's mourning — a mourning tangled through with heartfelt gratitude and thanks toward her, who had spent her very last moments in conversation with him.
And then—,

"—————"

Blessed with companions who stayed close beside him, Subaru's thoughts still turned toward the other dragon carriage heading for the Kingdom — toward the camp of the late Priscilla, toward those left behind.
As for what would become of Priscilla's Barielle domain going forward, Roswaal, riding along with them, was likely already lending his counsel — but Subaru's mind was on a different matter.
That was—,

"—Al."

Al, who had been present at Priscilla's final moment, who had held her tight in his arms right up to the very end.
While Subaru had thrown himself into his 'Sparka,' while Abel had buried himself in work so as not to forget his sister's dying wish, while so many others struggled to fill the hole left by her absence, he had shut himself alone in a room.
Al, who had seemed so overwhelmed by that sense of loss that one might have thought he would simply vanish and disappear altogether, was now on the road back to the Kingdom.
And the purpose behind Al's return to the Kingdom was—,

'—Brother, I got a favor to ask. Caught wind of somethin'. Talk of the Sage's tower. There's a book inside that tower, one that lets you read the records of the dead — I wanna read it.'

"...Priscilla's Book of the Dead."

Whether getting his hands on it would trample on the wishes of a Priscilla who had vanished so nobly — Subaru had his own hesitations about that too.
And yet, precisely because he had been there in that place, because he knew of the parting between Al and Priscilla and of Al's despair afterward, Subaru wanted to be of help.
So—,

"Can I come borrow your strength again, Shaula?"

Making a pilgrimage once more to that tower at the far end of the sea of sand — that was the answer Subaru could give for Al's sake, one that had nothing to do with 'Return by Death.'
