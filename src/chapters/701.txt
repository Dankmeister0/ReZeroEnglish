Arc 9 Interlude: "Katya Aurélie"

——In the imperial capital of Lupugana, the 'Witch' Sphinx was slain, and the losing move of the 'Great Disaster' was decided.

The large-scale exercise of the Forbidden Arts that the 'Witch' had orchestrated was interrupted, and the dead who had been revived into the present world by the distorted ritual formula were, one and all, forced to remain on the earth as finite lives once more. Those who had been revived against their will, made to serve the 'Witch's' designs, seized the chance and returned to the place their souls belonged by turning to dust of their own accord — but naturally, there were many who did not.

Even as Undead who had lost their lives, they were, every one, imperial subjects who knew the Bloodlust Creed. Even revived by a power beyond reason, if they could still swing a sword, many of them meant to press on regardless of whether they were alive or dead, and that fighting spirit was present even among the host surrounding the 'Fortress City.'

And yet, in truth, the battle encircling the 'Fortress City' of Garkla was settled almost the instant the losing move of the 'Great Disaster' was decided, and the Undead who had failed to die scattered and fled in all directions. Why did the army of the Undead abandon the fight and choose to flee? — It was the collapse of the battle line brought on by the absence of its commander, and the abandonment of combat that followed from it. Even imperial subjects who feared no death, even an army of the Undead, would not accept squandering lives for nothing in a battle already lost.

And as for how such a thing as the absence of a commander came to pass——



"Such, such an absurd thing could never happen...!"

Clawing at his own green hair, Palladio spat the words out of the corner of his mouth as he watched his encampment being smashed to pieces.

This should not be happening. It should not be happening, and yet it was. All of it was caused by the unreason that had worked against every single thing surrounding Palladio.

Given the role of commander over the great host of Undead sent against the Fortress City, Palladio had meant to fulfill the duty owed by a Vollachian imperial scion worthy of reverence. But every stratagem Palladio devised was thwarted by the city's common rabble one after another, compounded by the bungling of Undead who would not properly obey orders, until he had been driven into dire straits. Of course, he had not the slightest intention of giving up over that. But the debts of unreason, piled one atop another, had erupted as distortion, and at last Palladio had been reduced to the ignominious fate of fleeing.

——No, this was not flight.

This was an honorable strategic withdrawal, a stone laid for eventual victory. It had to be so — otherwise the accounts would not balance, that he, a Vollachian imperial scion carrying the blood of the superior Evil Eye Tribe, should meet with such a fate.

"What is this 'Witch of Greed'...! Is this the end result of the grandiose words that got my hopes up...!"

What crossed Palladio's mind was the accursed presence that had been the first to collapse amid the piled-up debts. Rage at the white-haired 'Witch' who had joined his command, who had once very nearly overturned the tide of battle, only to irresponsibly and suddenly abandon that role and vanish.

Up to the point of using magic to rain down the Stars and deliver the blow that annihilated the Fortress City, all had gone well. But even that had been cancelled out by interference from the capital, and to make matters worse, immediately after, a fierce counterattack had begun from those whose eyes now held flame. As far as Palladio's Evil Eye could see, every last living soul who had shut themselves up in the Fortress City — even the helpless refugees — had taken up flame-bearing eyes and weapons, and were pushing the Undead back.

It was immediately after that. That the 'Witch of Greed,' without even attempting to bring the situation under control, had turned to dust and vanished.

"'——Ah, I see. So this is what it means, to be Sphinx.'"

That the 'Witch's' profile, which had vanished after leaving those words behind without so much as a glance in his direction, should be so infuriating. Why had the 'Witch' ended things with such a satisfied expression? He understood nothing of it. And understanding nothing, there was nothing in it for Palladio but ruin.

"Damn it, damn it, damn it damn it damn it damn it damn it...!"

Spitting out curses as if coughing up blood, Palladio fled the plain and ran into the forest. Around the time the 'Witch of Greed' had vanished, Palladio had come to understand the strange change in the Undead — that the ones who should have revived over and over were no longer reviving, that the undying host had ceased to function.

There is a flow to battle. That is true of any battle. All the more so when it is a great war bound up with the future of the Empire.

And so, sensing the change in that flow, Palladio had at once abandoned his encampment and, taking only a small retinue with him, withdrawn from the battlefield to prepare for a comeback — the best possible judgment. Which was precisely why this was not flight, but a stone laid for eventual victory.

"For today, let them think they have won. Vincent, Priscilla...!"

But that was, in the end, nothing more than a temporary, conceded victory. Once Palladio had escaped, recovered, and seized with his next, irreversible move an absolute victory owing nothing to any 'Witch's' hand, it would all be nothing but a fleeting moment. Next time — this time for certain — he would place the Vollachian Empire under the rule of its rightful emperor.

Palladio had his Evil Eye for that purpose. He who had been granted this eye of heaven was surely destined for the throne of emperor——

"————"

With that conviction in his breast, Palladio moved to use the power of his Evil Eye to determine the road he should take, and noticed — that, somewhere along the way, not one of the retinue he should have had with him was anywhere to be seen.

"What? Oi, oi!? Where did you go!? You lot, leaving me behind——"

His anger at where they had vanished to was almost instantly overwritten by a sensation like being doused in cold water. For a moment he suspected his retinue had committed the insolence of abandoning him and fleeing — but if so, there was no explaining why his Evil Eye could not catch sight of their fleeing backs. If that was so, then the reason for their disappearance was simple and clear — they were dead.

"——!"

At the sensation of his bloodless Undead body bristling with gooseflesh, Palladio reflexively drew the 'Sunlight Sword' — the destiny in the shape of a blade that served as proof of Vollachian imperial blood and the qualification to become emperor——.

"——ah"

——The arm gripping it was sheared off at the shoulder, and Palladio collapsed onto the ground.

"Wha, wha, what..."

He opened all three eyes, brow included, wide at what had happened. Immediately after, a shock ran through his remaining arm as well, and in an instant both hands capable of gripping a sword were gone. That, Palladio understood, was the result brought about by a surprise attack with an axe. But that was as far as his understanding went — that much, and no more.

"Why..."

Why had the Evil Eye that made Palladio special, the eye of heaven, failed to catch that surprise attack? He did not know, did not know, did not know. — In that instant, unbidden, a certain possibility crossed his mind.

A tale Palladio had once heard. Of a foolish race that had committed an unpardonable sin, drawn the wrath of the greatest and most feared emperor in Vollachian history, and been branded into the Bloodlust Creed as a presence to be spat upon, its very existence stricken from recognition. Those regarded, in the Empire, as criminals deserving death simply for living — the dreaded beastfolk——

"——wai"

"Not waiting."

That cold, merciless blow drove Palladio Manesque from the stage forever.

——That was the decisive stroke, hidden behind the scenes of history, that lost the commander of the Undead host surrounding the Fortress City and brought about the collapse of the battle line in the utter defeat of the 'Great Disaster.'

The wheels of her wheelchair creaking, Katya sat at a loss before the sheer size of the room she'd been given.

"Just because my brother became a special 'General' doesn't mean I, as his relation, need special treatment too... this is more than I can handle..."

Looking around the spacious room, Katya's shoulders slumped as she thought of what lay ahead. The capital where she'd originally lived was apparently going to take quite a while to restore, given the effects of the so-called 'Great Disaster,' and for the time being the capital's refugees were being asked to disperse and settle in the Fortress City and the surrounding towns and villages.

Amid all that, Katya — whose brother Jamal had, through various twists of circumstance, secured a position at Emperor Vincent's side — was enjoying treatment far better than most.

Still, Katya wasn't the type who wanted a life of luxury, and even if she were showered with all manner of fine things, she was, in the end, just a girl with bad legs and a bad attitude, without much confidence she could actually enjoy any of it. So she had turned down nearly every privilege offered to her.

"And all those people fussing around trying to get close to me are annoying, too..."

House Aurélie was, technically, a minor branch of imperial nobility, and Katya herself did hold the position of a noble's daughter. There were servants in the household, and she felt no resistance to being looked after by them — but unlike before, when they'd been nothing but a declining noble house, Jamal's current standing was something else entirely. Perhaps because of that, no small number of people tried to curry favor with Katya for Jamal's sake.

It was precisely because she'd never cared a whit about such tedious premises and outside factors that her distance with Rem had, for Katya, truly been a comfortable thing.

"...Well, no use talking about a girl who's already gone."

For Katya, who knew herself to be difficult and troublesome by nature, Rem — surprisingly blunt-mouthed, willing to say things bluntly even when Katya didn't want to hear them — had been a good match. Put plainly, she had been Katya's first friend.

And now that Rem, too, had gone back to the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, together with the man and the older sister who had come to fetch her. As the day of parting drew near, unable to bear watching Rem's lonely face, Katya had ended up promising to exchange letters — but she had absolutely no idea what she was supposed to write in a letter.

"E-even without that, my days are already nothing but the same thing over and over..."

It was only these past several days that had been turbulent; ordinarily Katya's days were flat and dull, nothing special ever happening, just time slowly dying away. Even trying to write Rem a letter under those circumstances, she felt she'd have nothing worth writing. And while she failed to write anything worthwhile, Rem would grow tired of her, the letters would gradually thin out, and eventually stop coming altogether.

Such a pessimistic future rose up vividly before Katya's eyes.

"I hate this... I really, really want to die..."

The moment she was alone, a wave of loneliness came crashing down on her, and Katya's mood kept sinking. By nature, Katya was pessimistic about everything, and prone to strongly self-punishing thoughts. It was only ever whoever was beside her that had held those thoughts at bay. Until yesterday it had been Rem, and before that——

"...Idiot. Idiot, idiot, idiot, you're really such an idiot."

Gripping the wheels of her wheelchair tight, Katya muttered hatefully, without any force behind it. Someone who had barged uninvited into Katya's world with his shoes still on, who had pulled her close by force even after she'd told him not to come near her, who had whispered all manner of sweet nothings she hadn't wanted to hear — and then gone and vanished on his own.

Remembering that enormous idiot made everything terribly, terribly painful.

"Ah, no, no, I don't want to cry..."

Little by little, a hot pressure was welling up behind her eyes, and Katya struggled against it. Rather than feel this miserable, she should have just died shielding Rem in the midst of that 'Great Disaster' chaos. If she had, Rem probably would have called her an idiot, but it would have stayed lodged in her heart like a thorn, deep and lasting, and she wouldn't have been forgotten. She could have avoided writing pathetic letters and being disliked even more.

Dead people are unfair. Once you're dead, no one can speak ill of you anymore. So, to keep the dead from getting away with that unfairness, Katya made a point of speaking ill of the dead especially.

"Idiot, idiot idiot, idiot idiot idiot, idiooot...!"

That attempt, whether at venting frustration or at spite, only backfired on her. To keep speaking ill of someone who has died means, for as long as you keep speaking ill of them, that you can't stop thinking about them. Because of that, not only did the tears well up, but her nose started to sting too.

If she looked in a mirror now, she was sure she'd see an unbelievably ugly, ill-tempered woman sitting in a wheelchair.

"I hate this... I don't want to see anyone..."

Muttering that, Katya turned her wheelchair around, aiming toward the bed. It was bad manners, but she thought she might just collapse onto the bed without undoing her braided hair or changing her clothes, and lie there until she starved to death. Of course, she couldn't actually starve herself to death. She'd surely get hungry, give up on starving, and end up eating something while sniffling back her runny nose anyway. Even so, feeling like this, wanting to cry her eyes out thinking of someone——

"——? Who is it?"

Just as she was about to wheel herself toward the bed, someone knocked on the door from outside. Bad timing, she thought, but she hesitated to just ignore it and go to bed anyway. Right now, communications were disrupted here and there, problems were cropping up everywhere, and everyone was having a hard time of it. If Katya threw some pointless tantrum here, it might make someone, somewhere, miss something important.

Her fundamentally earnest side winning out, Katya headed for the door. For a moment she hesitated over what to do about surely looking a wreck, but it really was only a moment. Not caring what anyone thought of her, Katya reached for the door.

And then——

"Yes, who is it...?"

Saying that, Katya slowly opened the door——

"——ah"

A hoarse breath leaked, weakly, from that slender throat.

"——Good work out there, Sub-General Jamal."

"Yeah, you too."

Snapping off a crisp salute and returning the salute of the imperial soldier who stepped aside to let him pass, Jamal twisted the corner of his mouth into a grin.

The title he'd been addressed by, 'Sub-General,' was the special rank of 'General' prepared for Jamal in recognition of his combat service at Emperor Vincent's side during this great war. His role was the honorable position of serving as Vincent's personal escort — a post Jamal, who had originally aimed to become a 'General' for the sake of House Aurélie's advancement, had very much wanted.

With this, he could show up all the people who'd laughed at him for overreaching, and more than anything, he could give Katya — who couldn't live without the support of people around her — a good life. He knew perfectly well that Katya, who was negative about everything and never recognized her own worth, would hate this sort of consideration from him. But even so, she was his one and only precious little sister; the two of them were all the family they had. He knew that no matter how much she complained, Katya could never fully refuse him.

"Damn it, this kind of meddling from her big brother really shouldn't even be necessary, huh."

Muttering his irritation at having nowhere else to direct it, Jamal's feet carried him toward Katya. Ever since Jamal's promotion to Sub-General, his relation Katya's treatment had shifted to something quite different from that of an ordinary imperial citizen. For now it was only temporary lodging until the capital's reconstruction progressed, but after all the hardship she'd been through, he wanted to give Katya a life free of want.

The daughter of his friend, who had been keeping Katya company for a while now, had gone back to the Kingdom too. It was about time, he figured, that Katya was allowed to properly grieve.

"Once that's done, it's the good stuff — booze and meat."

Saying that, Jamal jostled the wine bottle he'd requisitioned from the storehouse under his authority as 'General,' along with a bag of fine meat, turning over in his mind how best to comfort Katya. Grilling up a thick, extravagant cut of meat, tearing into it while knocking back some good wine — that was the Jamal way of dealing with grief.

"Katya, you in there? It's me."

Pounding on the door, Jamal called out to Katya, who should have been in the room. Katya, for whom even going out alone was a struggle, generally spent her time shut up in her room when nothing else was going on. So she should be doing just that now too. But there was no answer from inside the room, and Jamal's brow furrowed suspiciously.

"You asleep? Oi, I'm coming in whether you like it or not."

Since it wasn't unusual for her to sulk and keep on sleeping like the dead, Jamal thought nothing of it and opened the door, stepping into Katya's room. In a room that still bore no trace of its occupant, having only just been moved into, Jamal glanced around and found it strange that there was no sign of anyone in the bedroom.

"Katya?"

Sure enough, when he looked into the bedroom, there was no one there. What's more, there was no sign of Katya anywhere in the room at all. It wasn't very common, but Jamal was just starting to think maybe he'd crossed paths with her while she was out, when——

——he noticed the letter placed on the desk in the bedroom.

"————"

At first outside his notice, that letter suddenly seized Jamal's attention with intensity. There was nothing strange about the letter itself. What stood out was what had been placed alongside it. Lying there was a bandana — one Jamal remembered vividly, one a certain man had always worn wrapped around his head——.

"——!"

Reaching for the letter impatiently, he tore the seal open carelessly and ran his eyes over the contents. What was there was, unmistakably, Katya's thin, lifeless-looking handwriting, accompanied by a different, forceful hand — a marriage registration form.

"——That son of a bitch."

The instant he grasped the intent behind that marriage registration, that curse slipped from Jamal's mouth. Contrary to the words themselves, it was filled with undisguised relief and joy — the complicated feelings of an older brother whose precious little sister had been carried off by a hateful friend of his.

"Ha, hahaha, ha—hahahahaha!!"

And then, pressing a palm to his face, Jamal burst into great peals of laughter before the letter. He laughed, and laughed, and laughed so much that tears streamed down from the eye covered by his eyepatch.

That it was one of the precious few tears of blessing shed in an Empire where so many tears had been shed for partings — that would never be recorded anywhere in the history books of the Empire.

But it was, nonetheless, a fact that on this day, in this place, a marriage was made between a vanished noble's daughter and the scoundrel who had carried her off — and that a single 'General' blessed it.


——From this point on, Katya Aurélie's whereabouts would become entirely unknown. But that this was nothing to grieve over would, in time, be proven beyond doubt by the fact that letters kept arriving, without fail, addressed to her one and only friend — and that not a trace of sorrow or unhappiness ever colored the words written in them.
