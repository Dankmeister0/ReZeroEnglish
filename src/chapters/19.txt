Arc 1 Chapter 18: "The Battle for the Loot House"

With an involuntary "ugh," Subaru realized his slip had been seen, and with it, his own blunder.
This was exactly the moment he should have deployed some glib comeback like before, but going out of his way to show weakness like that was the fruit of his poor communication skills.

In negotiations, nothing costs you your edge faster than letting your weak point be seen.
As a result of the silence Subaru chose, Felt's mouth eased ever so slightly at the corner.

"Even the big sister who hired me didn't want to talk about it, and you're the same, aren't you, mister?"

"...To begin with, theft itself is a shady business, so I'd think everyone involved has something shady behind it, don't you think—"

"But you especially got somethin' shady goin' on. Well, think about it calmly — you're tryin' to snatch away a job somebody else already hired me to steal."

With the cruelty of a cat toying with its prey, Felt pressed her attack on Subaru's shortcomings.
Keeping her sadistic smile in place, she cast off the silent Subaru and pushed further.

"So what is this badge, huh? Turns out it's worth more than it looks. That's why you want it so bad, right? Which means it's worth more money than the Magic Vessel."

"Wait, Felt. That thought of yours is seriously dangerous. Going by the flow of the conversation, I've got a pretty good guess where you're headed with this — that's my gamer brain talking, I know — but seriously, don't."

Seeing Felt's miser-gauge climbing, Subaru broke into a cold sweat but tried to stop her. Left unchecked, her asking price would climb without limit.
And if that happened, the negotiation would drag on, and waiting at the end of it was nothing but a BAD END.

"Twenty gold royals or more — settle for that price! Don't be greedy for more than that! El— the one who hired you, even she's only got twenty gold royals to spare! She won't put out any more than that!"

"How the hell would you know that?"

"Ah—"

"Now you've gone and given yourself away. So you're involved after all."

If only he could insist he simply knew because of his "Return by Death," how much easier this would be.
But even if he actually gave that explanation, there was nothing in it that would make her believe him.

To Felt, whose suspicion was only deepening, Subaru's words probably weren't worth trusting anymore, not without some counter-evidence.
At this point, the only option left was to just take the badge from her by force, but—

"There's the little problem of the muscle-bound old man standing in my way..."

"Gettin' pushed around good, aren't ya, sonny. Pretty pathetic, losin' to a little girl younger than you."

"That's on account of your training, isn't it? She's too strong, it's almost enough to make me cry."

If it came down to violence, the outcome was a foregone conclusion — Old Man Rom would flatten him.
And even if he somehow managed to snatch the badge, he couldn't imagine beating Felt in a footrace. Once it turned into a long-distance run, Subaru's combat rating would rank near the bottom of the class.
Even with several top-three rankings in other stats, it was the sort of level where dead-last in the long-distance run would routinely knock an A grade down a notch.

"Felt, please..."

"Beggin' won't help. I'll admit you as a negotiating partner. But it ain't fair if I don't hear the other side's story too, right? Talk about what this thing's really worth, and put up a fair price, then maybe we'll talk."

There was not a shred of mercy or leniency in the eyes pressing him for answers.
The girl's twin eyes seemed desperate to wring the truth out of Subaru's attitude. But the fact was, even watching Subaru closely wouldn't reveal the badge's true worth.
Subaru's reason for wanting the badge was nothing like Elsa's — it was simply a matter of a debt of gratitude.
Beyond that, all he knew about the badge was its appearance — a badge set with a jewel — no more than what Felt already knew.

And so, Subaru had no answer to give that would satisfy what Felt was asking for.

"The reason I want it is... because I want to return it to its original owner."

"—Huh?"

So it slipped out — the truth, blurted plainly. That was the full extent of the sincerity Subaru had to offer.

"I want to return it to its owner. That's why I want the badge. That's all there is to it."

To Felt, whose eyes had gone wide, Subaru lifted his face and repeated the same words.
Her crimson eyes bore into him, brimming with hostile intimidation, but all Subaru could do was take it and hold his silence.
With no room left for joking around, he simply knelt, sincerely, and bowed his head.

"...Felt. Somehow, this little runt doesn't look like he's lyin'."

"Don't you go gettin' soft on me too, Old Man Rom. It's gotta be a joke, right? Return it to the owner? You mean buy it back off the person you stole it from after payin' a fortune for it? Ridiculous. All you'd have to do is bring one guard along and haul me off — that'd settle it easy enough."

That was an option he couldn't take.
The fake Satella did not sanction the guard getting involved in this matter.
That was precisely why she had turned down even Reinhard's goodwill offer of help.

If it would work against her interests, then that was something Subaru could not do.
That alone was the sincerity Subaru had to give — his answer to the debt of having his life saved.

"If you're gonna lie, lie better than that. Actin' all serious won't fool me. If it did... yeah. I ain't gettin' fooled."

"Felt..."

As if shaking something off, Felt forced out a strained, hoarse voice.
Old Man Rom, looking concerned, seemed to know what was in her heart — his expression was pained.

Still, it was clear that her stubborn attitude firmly forbade any change of heart in Felt.
Which meant the negotiation had ended in failure.

"—Who's there?"

It was right then that Old Man Rom's expression changed, his glare snapping to the entrance of the Loot House.

Kneeling with his head bowed, Subaru was still dazed from the shock of the failed negotiation.
Because of that, it took him one beat too long for his thoughts to catch up to the answer.

A sharp knock sounded several times in succession, and Old Man Rom turned at the silence that followed.
It was Felt who nodded, receiving that glance. She pointed at herself.

"Might be my customer. Feels a bit early, though."

Saying so, she headed toward the door, her face still simmering with unspent anger, and reached for the latch.
Watching her back, Subaru suddenly became aware of a surge of anxiety welling up rapidly within him.

Loot House. Knock. Felt's customer — those pieces lined up, and there was only one answer they could point to.

"—Don't open it! You'll be killed!!"

It was earlier than scheduled. The sunlight slanting faintly through the window was still fairly high, just beginning to take on an orange tint, but its brightness was still too strong to call it sunset.
In both the first loop and the second, despair had come after sundown.

It wasn't that he'd been careless, assuming there was still time left.
It wasn't that at all — but this was still far too soon.

—And not a single condition for changing the world had been met yet.

But Subaru's desperate, anguished cry was in vain; his call to stop her couldn't make Felt hesitate in opening the door.
The door swung open, and the sunset-colored light dimly, faintly swept away the dimness of the Loot House.

And then—

"—I'm not gonna do something as scary as *kill* you, just like that."

Lips pursed in a sullen scowl, the silver-haired girl stepped into the storehouse.

"Good, you're here. ...This time, I won't let you get away."

At the sight of the silver-haired girl who had stepped inside — the fake Satella — Felt fell back wordlessly.
As she retreated, her expression was one of frustration, her lips twisting with irritation.

"You really are one persistent woman, you know that?"

"'A thief has some nerve,' as the saying goes. If you behave yourself, you won't have to suffer for it."

Against Felt, who looked ready to grind her teeth, the fake Satella's voice carried a bitterly cold temperature.
Indeed, Subaru could feel the temperature of the room beginning to plummet rapidly, and he couldn't suppress a shiver at the shifting atmosphere inside.

—Why was the fake Satella here!?

It was still only just heading into evening — by Subaru's own experience, this should have been around the time he hadn't even reached the entrance to the slums yet.
From there, it took nearly two more hours of asking around before he'd finally reached the Loot House, in the first loop.

"So that's how it is, huh. Meaning, without me around, she gets here this much faster."

If not for the whole incident of Subaru being treated back in the alley, she would have made her own way here — and at this speed, no less.
His own uselessness had just been proven across time itself, leaving him with an indescribable feeling.

Leaving Subaru behind to wallow in that emptiness, the situation kept moving forward by the second.
Felt, retreating, had already moved from the center of the room toward the back, while the fake Satella smoothly blocked the path to the door, turning her palm toward them.
A faint cracking sound rippled through the air — the result of Magic unfolding from the point of her palm. Her specialty apparently lay in ice, as icicles began forming in midair, and the room temperature dropped in step.

"I only want one thing from you. Give the badge back. That thing matters to me."

There were six icicles floating in the air. Their tips were blunted round rather than sharpened — their power seemed built more around weight than sharpness. Still, there was no doubt that a direct hit would deliver an impact far beyond that of a mere thrown stone.
Naturally breaking into a cold sweat at realizing he himself was among the possible targets, Subaru could do nothing but watch their exchange in silence, careful not to make a wrong move.

"...Old Man Rom."

"Can't move. You sure brought a nasty piece of trouble along with a nasty customer, Felt."

To her quiet call, Old Man Rom answered with a shake of his head, even as his massive frame tensed.
In his hand, a familiar club had somehow appeared, but the burly arm gripping it seemed oddly unsteady. As if hesitating, as if at a loss, he kept opening and closing his grip.

"You gonna admit defeat before the fight's even started?"

"If it were just some ordinary mage, I wouldn't back down either... but this one's bad news."

Old Man Rom chided the provocative Felt.
He narrowed his gray eyes slightly, watching the fake Satella as she deployed her icicles.
In the eyes looking down on her — what showed there was sharp wariness, and something beyond it: awe.

"Little miss... you're an Elf, aren't you."

At Old Man Rom's question, his lips trembling as he asked it, Subaru couldn't help but look up and react.
Elves — one of those races indispensable to this sort of fantasy setting. Long-eared, long-lived, and beautiful.

At Old Man Rom's question, the fake Satella closed her eyes for a moment, then let out a small breath.

"Not quite right. —I'm only half an Elf."

Something in the way she confessed it, like admitting to a wound, made Subaru frown with a pang of sympathy.
But it was the other two who reacted most strongly to her admission.
Felt's reaction in particular was pronounced — she flinched back sharply.

"A Half-Elf... and silver hair, too!? No way..."

"It's just a resemblance to someone else! ...Even I find it a nuisance."

Subaru couldn't follow the exchange, but he could sense, at least a little, how unwelcome this exchange was to the fake Satella.
Despite receiving a denial whose context she didn't understand, Felt's expression didn't soften.

If anything, she turned to look at the still-silent Subaru, her crimson eyes narrowing with sharp hostility as she glared at him.
Subaru's face stiffened in bewilderment, and Felt gave a self-mocking laugh.

"Mister. So you set me up good, huh?"

"What?"

"Sayin' you wanted to give it back to the owner — I thought somethin' was fishy about that. And you not lettin' the alley crew cut in on the job — that was part of the plan too, wasn't it? You two were in on it together, huh."

In her accusation, laced with something like a grudge, Subaru realized a misunderstanding had taken root.
But at the same time, part of him was also piecing together how the fake Satella could have arrived here so quickly.
After all, under normal circumstances, there was no way the fake Satella could have reached this place at this hour. Normally, the slum crew hired by Felt would have gotten in her way, delaying her arrival.
This time, though, that interference hadn't happened, thanks to Subaru's own interference earlier — which was why the fake Satella was showing up here at this timing.

In terms of cause and effect, it was largely Subaru's influence at work, so Felt's anger wasn't entirely off the mark. In fact, the situation was tilting in the direction Subaru wanted.
If Felt and Old Man Rom couldn't land a decisive blow as things stood, the badge would have no choice but to return to the fake Satella's hands through force of arms.
If he were honest, he'd rather be the one to recover the badge himself and hand it to her directly, but as long as it ended up back in her hands, he couldn't afford to be picky.
If this flow carried through to its conclusion, it could be called the next best thing. However—

"...? What's going on here? Aren't you two supposed to be working together?"

Seeing the falling-out between Felt and Subaru, the fake Satella couldn't help but react to it.
To the bewildered fake Satella, Felt let out a scoffing "Hah" through her nose.

"Don't put on some little act. I'm the one cornered here. Go ahead and take the badge back nice and proudly, laugh at how dumb I was for it."

"That's way too pathetic, come on. It's not that bad a spot you're in."

"You get me into this and then say that? Ugh, damn it. I got played!"

Felt raked her fingers through her golden hair, groaning in frustration.
Watching her utterly un-maidenly display, the fake Satella's frown deepened further.
Watching their exchange, subtly talking past each other, Subaru clapped his hands and stepped in between them.

"Come on, come on, this is just gettin' complicated, let's just leave it, yeah? Felt, just give the badge back. And Sate— you, get out of here quick. So you don't get robbed again."

"I don't get why you're being so considerate about this, and it's bugging me."

"I'm just as unconvinced as you are. Mister, what the hell are you, anyway?"

His attempt to smooth things over and paper the whole thing over had failed spectacularly.
Instead, it had drawn the full attention of both women embroiled in the matter, and he found himself on the receiving end of concentrated fire.
Struggling to explain, he turned to Old Man Rom for help, but the old man just folded his thick arms.

"This ain't Magic we're dealin' with — it's Spiritual Arts. Not somethin' I can jump into lightly."

With that, offering a reason quite different from what Subaru wanted, he refused to send reinforcements.
The casual appearance of a new term nagged at him a little, but the sharpness of the two women's glares was more than he could bear.
Somehow, some way, he needed an escape route — and it was just as he glanced toward the door that—

—A black shadow slid silently, creeping up behind the silver-haired girl.

"—Puck! Block it!!"

A beguiling smile slipped through the shadow of the room, and a flash of silver came whipping toward the white nape of her neck like a writhing thing.
An instant later — before Subaru's widened eyes, the girl's head would have been sent flying — that would have been the future that could have unfolded, had things gone differently.

—A sharp, clean sound.
Not the sound of steel cutting bone, but the ring of steel striking glass, shivering against his eardrums.

Behind the fake Satella's slightly lowered head, a Magic Circle bloomed, glowing faintly blue.
It caught the descending blade head-on, completely shielding that white nape from the murderous edge.

"Puck...!"

"That was close, wasn't it. Truly."

The fake Satella lunged forward and spun around.
Trailing a beat behind her motion, her long silver hair swayed, and from the edge of her shoulder peeked a small gray-furred creature.
Wiggling his pink nose smugly, Puck glanced at Subaru with his black eyes.

"That was quite the razor's-edge timing, wasn't it. You saved us."

"You're the one who saved us. Thanks a bunch."

In answer to his thumbs-up-like gesture, Subaru offered a feeble thumbs-up of his own in return.
The fake Satella's appearance before sundown — that meant, in other words, that this was during the working hours of her reassuring backup.
Even though it had come out of his mouth on reflex, her safety had been protected by a performance beyond what he'd expected.

And the attacker, whose ambush had been so neatly foiled—

"—A Spirit. A Spirit, huh. Fufufu, how lovely. I've never killed a Spirit before."

Holding her kukri knife up before her face, wearing an expression of rapture, was that all-too-familiar murderer — Elsa.
Subaru and the fake Satella both tensed warily at her sudden appearance. But it was neither of them who moved to act against her.

"Oi, what the hell's goin' on!"

Shouting, it was Felt who stepped forward, raising her voice in anger.
She jabbed a finger at Elsa, pulling the badge she carried out from inside her clothes.

"Buyin' the badge off me was s'posed to be your job. If you're gonna turn this place into a bloodbath, that ain't what we agreed on!"

"Buying a stolen badge — that was my job, yes. But with the owner himself brought right to me, there's no room left for any business talk. So I've decided to change my plans."

Felt, her face flushed red with anger, couldn't help but step back under the gaze of those eyes drenched in murderous intent. Elsa looked down on Felt's fear with something like affection.

"Everyone connected to this here dies. I'll retrieve the badge after that."

Still wearing her motherly smile, she declared it with cold cruelty, tilting her head.

"—You failed to see your job through. You can hardly complain about being cut loose."

"—————!"

The way Felt's face twisted wasn't from fear — it looked like some other emotion entirely.
What chord that had struck within her, Subaru couldn't say. He didn't know — but,

"You bitch, don't screw with me—!!"

It was enough to make Subaru shout in fury, forgetting entirely the difference in strength between them.

Elsa looked at Subaru, startled. So did Felt, Old Man Rom, and the fake Satella, no exceptions. But the most surprised of all was none other than Subaru himself.
Even he didn't know the reason he was this enraged.
Not knowing, he let the emotion welling up carry him, and decided to just let it all out.

"Don't you go tormentin' some little kid for fun, you sadistic gut-lovin' woman!! And another thing, you always show up so damn sudden — were you just waitin' outside for your timing!? Gettin' my hopes up thinkin' maybe things'll go fine and then — you're seriously terrifying, I seriously never want to see you again! You got any idea how much pain, how close to tears you've put me through!? If I got a coin every time you stabbed me full of holes, I'd be a billionaire by now! Okay, that one's an exaggeration!"

"...What on earth are you saying."

"My tension and my anger gauge are both maxed out, I don't even know what I'm sayin' anymore myself! And that's the weather report for today, folks, stay tuned, we'll be right back!"

At Subaru's incomprehensible outburst, Elsa let out a small sigh, unusually exasperated.
Subtly stung by her reaction even so, Subaru pressed on with the momentum of his own spit flying.

"Time's up on the stalling — get her, Puck!!"

"What a magnificently pathetic display that was. —Allow me to answer your expectations."

To Subaru stamping his foot against the ground, an airy voice responded, and Elsa lifted her face.
All around her, surrounding her from every direction as she stood motionless, were icicles with sharpened tips — more than twenty of them.

"Haven't even introduced myself yet, have I, miss. My name's Puck. —Try to remember it, at least, on your way out."

An instant later, a barrage of icicles came crashing down on Elsa's entire body from every direction at once.
