Arc 9 Chapter 53: "Aldebaran II"

"—Sorry for the rerun, but here's the trump card, take two."

The moment he spat that out, a boulder of monstrous mass came plunging straight down from directly overhead.
At Aldebaran's scheme—one he was fully prepared to be cursed at as an unoriginal rehash—Emilia's expression changed as she flew in, dodging the giant stone arms reaching out from the canyon walls.

"—————"

First her eyes widened at the sight of the massive boulder, then regret rose to her cheeks and the corners of her eyes—the memory of letting Aldebaran slip away so neatly back in the royal capital. Refusing to let that sense of defeat get the better of her, she squeezed her eyes shut, and when she opened them again to find the boulder still filling her view, a flicker of weakness crossed her mind—*can I really do this...?*—before she steeled herself with an "I have no choice but to do it!" and fixed her gaze dead ahead, her eyes now sharper and stronger than before her moment of doubt. All of this—an entire hundred-faced performance—had taken barely a second. Her resilience really was something else.

"But spirit alone won't cut it."

The great boulder kept falling, scraping and gouging the canyon walls as it went.
Having found her resolve again, Emilia's first move was to conjure a great lid of ice across the boulder's falling path, hoping to catch its weight. But Aldebaran interfered by firing a magically conjured stone shell from his rock cannon, blasting straight through the canyon wall itself.
With the wall shattered, the ice lid could no longer seal the canyon's width perfectly, and it simply fell alongside the boulder—just another projectile joining the assault.

"In that case—!"

The instant she saw her ice lid fail to catch the boulder, Emilia moved straight to her next plan.
She summoned back the ice wings she'd once shed, her angelic form reborn, and folded her long legs beneath her. Then, bringing the soles of her feet flush against the kicking legs of the ice soldiers positioned directly behind her—borrowing leg strength the original Natsuki Subaru could never hope to produce—she shot forward in a single burst.

"—————"

Caught off guard by Emilia's sudden ferocious acceleration, the hundreds of arms extending from the canyon walls all swung their fingertips through empty air, letting the angel's escape—no, her charge straight at Aldebaran—go unhindered.
Having split his attention and effort toward intercepting her, Aldebaran's own speed and momentum as he fled had slackened, and the distance between him and the rapidly advancing Emilia shrank fast.
With the advantage that Yae—left behind to buy them time—had built up now thoroughly squandered, he could practically hear her sniping, "Honestly, Al-sama, there's just no point in supporting you, is there~"

"Yeah, well, if I get caught here, maybe you'd have a point!"

He snapped back at the auditory hallucination of Yae, then met Emilia with a barrage of thrown stones. But Emilia twisted her body through the air with an almost divine grace, dodging this second rehash that followed the falling boulder.

"You've gotta be kidding me!?"

Aldebaran's eyes went wide at the fact that she'd pulled that off with wings that should have had no freedom of movement, being nothing but constructs.
Naturally, the angelic wings made of ice weren't flapping. The truth behind Emilia's three-dimensional flight path was a set of smaller ice wings added inside the larger ones—by layering multiple ice wings together, she'd created variation in how she cut through the wind, adding the ability to carve edges by tilting her body.
And no doubt she'd arrived at that aerodynamic solution on pure natural instinct.

"I'm catching up—!"

Even rolling sideways to evade, Emilia barely lost any speed.
The distance between them, as she shouted that, kept vanishing. At this rate, she'd escape the boulder's blast radius with time to spare. Even his intended trump card had been dodged with ease—Aldebaran was, in every sense, on the razor's edge. That should have been the shared conclusion of the two people involved, and no one else, in that instant.

"—!"

Emilia's violet eyes went wide, her large eyes flooding with shock.
In her field of view, Aldebaran wasn't doing anything special. He kept up his volley of stones that never landed, but there was no new spell, no superhuman maneuver to dodge her.
The same held true against Emilia's flight, its momentum now boosted by the strength of her ice soldiers—at this rate, she'd catch up to him, and he'd have no way to dodge her frost-tipped fingers.
Which meant Emilia would satisfy her victory condition right here. And yet there was no joy, no sense of triumph in her eyes.
Because—

"—I told you, didn't I. This is a rerun."

In exchange for stopping the massive damage the falling boulder would cause, Aldebaran had been allowed to win and flee from his fight with Emilia back in the royal capital.
So, even if it meant being called unoriginal, he'd execute the same plan that had worked once before. Except this time, there wasn't a single innocent bystander here to get caught up in it. —No innocent bystander, that is.

"—Al!!"

Emilia's hand, as she screamed, stretched desperately toward Aldebaran—toward the one and only person, besides herself, who'd be caught in the falling rock's force.

"—It's decided! Well done, surviving the 'Sparka'! From here on, you too are one of the sword slaves of this Sword Slave Isle! Welcome, you good-for-nothing!"

The rough welcome and praise were indistinguishable from abuse.
Listening to the crude, boorish cheers that swallowed the arena in the wake of that welcome, Aldebaran sank to his knees, leaning against the great corpse beside him.

What he slumped limply against was the corpse of the Witchbeast Giltirau, dead from a sword driven deep into its thick neck. Giltirau had lost its right foreleg and hind leg, and its face, in death, showed more confusion and bewilderment than pain.
At that pitiful dying face, Aldebaran—his shoulders heaving with ragged breath—felt a pang of grief. Though he immediately found himself sickened at how deceitful that sympathy was.

"Well, you actually survived. I'm surprised."

The one who greeted the battered Aldebaran with those words was a shabbily dressed man—one of the sword slaves of Sword Slave Isle, and the person who had found Aldebaran when he first woke here.
The complicated look on the man's face was likely because he found it deeply baffling that Aldebaran had survived the 'Sparka,' the trial by which newcomers proved themselves. Unluckily for him, having been assigned as Aldebaran's minder for being the one who first found him, some part of him might have preferred that Aldebaran had simply died in the Sparka, sparing him a longer round of trouble.

Either way, Aldebaran's survival had startled the man—but the man wasn't the only one startled.
Aldebaran himself couldn't hide his own shock at having survived.

—Having lost a battle he was never meant to lose, Aldebaran had been left stranded in an unknown world.

An unfamiliar setting, unfamiliar people, rules laid out with barely any explanation—for Aldebaran, it truly was uncharted territory, as if he'd been thrown into an entirely new world.
The only certainty was that he'd surely lost his reason for existing—and that he'd likely lost even the one person who would have grieved that loss with him.

And so—

"And here you were saying you wanted to die. Guess it was all talk after all."

The man's words, equal parts spite and sarcasm, sliced Aldebaran's heart to ribbons.
When Aldebaran realized he had survived his own first defeat and the ending that should have come with it, an endless sense of loss and self-recrimination had made him want, from the very bottom of his heart, to die. For an Aldebaran in that state, the 'Sparka'—the trial by which Sword Slave Isle's newcomers were tested—was almost too convenient.
The blunt, offhand explanation of it had been unkind to the point of uselessness, but in essence, it was a forced, life-or-death test of skill—one where anyone who couldn't become a sword slave simply died.

For an Aldebaran desperate to die, it was almost too perfect an opportunity.
In fact, when Aldebaran had been led into the arena together with four other newcomers in the same situation, and made to face down Giltirau, he'd fully intended to let himself be killed without resistance.

But, and yet, Aldebaran had survived.
While the other men, one after another, challenged the beast, fled, or begged for their lives before being killed regardless, Aldebaran—left as the last one standing—had taken the Witchbeast's claws, been stomped on and had his ribs broken, and should have met his end coughing up bloody foam.

And yet—

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

The instant those words crossed his mind, Aldebaran had already deployed his 'Domain.'
Compared to an opponent he'd challenged hundreds of millions of times and still never beaten, a 'Sparka' he could kill his way through in three hundred and four attempts was no great pain for Aldebaran.
The real agony lay afterward, in the endless self-interrogation that followed his survival.

—Why? How? What was the meaning of it, that he hadn't died?

"Hm? You want to know about the outside world? Well, I've been here a long time myself, so..."

Recognized as a sword slave, Aldebaran had earned the right to live on this island.
Within the closed-off island, he learned that this place was part of the Vollachian Empire, that the captive sword slaves were forced into spectacle killings for entertainment, and that whatever order the island once had was long dead. But there was still far more he wanted to know.
As a result, it was his assigned minder among the sword slaves who bore the brunt of Aldebaran's relentless questioning.

"You don't want to know current events, you want history...? Not that I know all that much myself. Well, I do know a bit about the 'Witches'... at least the 'Witch of Envy.'"

Aldebaran was first surprised the sword slave knew anything at all about the 'Witches'—he'd only asked on the off chance. But the greater shock came from the way the legend of the 'Witches,' told as though even speaking of it were terrifying, had come to refer solely to the 'Witch of Envy.'

The Witch of Envy, who had nearly destroyed the world and been sealed away by the hands of the Three Great Heroes.
No legend of any other 'Witch' existed in this world—no record of the Sin Witches anywhere. And on top of that, hearing that even this had happened nearly four hundred years ago, Aldebaran fell into despair.
Four hundred long years had erased every trace of the Sin Witches from the world—had it truly buried even the memory of the Witch who'd been ready to cast everything aside to keep her promise?

But crueler still—

"—The 'Witch of Greed'? What's that. Isn't that just something you made up?"

To the man's suspicion, Aldebaran had no words to answer with.
It couldn't have been a delusion. The days he'd spent with the 'Witch' were unmistakably real within him—her teachings, the touch of her fingertips, the words she'd spoken to him, he remembered all of it.
In this world, there had truly existed another Witch, one who had tried to stand against the Witch harboring that fearsome power. —Aldebaran had been her pupil, and her trump card.

"Damn it! What's your problem?! I was just answering what you asked, and you go and take a swing at me out of nowhere!"

The man spat those words out after being struck by an Aldebaran enraged at having the 'Witch's' existence denied.
That was how the falling-out with his first benefactor in this environment—no, this new world he'd been thrown into—had gone. Aldebaran broke with the other sword slaves in similarly abrupt ways, one after another, and grew isolated.

—He heard more than once that he was being whispered about as a madman bewitched by the 'Witch.' It seemed his former benefactor had been the one spreading it around most eagerly, but in a sense, it wasn't a wrong assessment. He had no intention of correcting it.

What did irritate him, though, was being lumped in with those incomprehensible people calling themselves the Witch Cult.
From what he'd heard, that lot went around preaching the 'Witch's' resurrection while committing what amounted to terrorism all over the place. He raged again at being compared to the likes of them, and his reputation worsened once more.

"...A witch-crazed lunatic like you won't die a decent death."

Those were the final, bitter words of that first benefactor, cut down and killed by Aldebaran's crude sword in a death match arranged by the island's governor at the time.
Wiping off the benefactor's blood that had splashed onto him, Aldebaran nodded deeply at that final "you won't die a decent death"—no doubt about that, he thought.

His 'Domain' remained intact, and Aldebaran continued to survive every death match on Sword Slave Isle.
Every single person who called Aldebaran witch-crazed was, sooner or later, matched against him in a death match, and every one of them fell before his crude blade.

It wasn't meant as spite. Nor did he wish to clear the 'Witch's' name of any dishonor. To begin with, far from being dishonored, having their very existence erased was the fate that had befallen the Witches.
So Aldebaran didn't seek his reason for living on from the 'Witch.' —He simply wanted to know for what purpose he had been allowed to live.

And to find that out, he couldn't afford to die.
That was why he'd survived the first 'Sparka,' and why he kept surviving every death match after.
So he used his 'Domain,' swallowing hundreds of deaths each time he crossed blades with someone, trampling the lives of every sword slave underfoot. Live. Live. Keep on living.

And then—

"—Well survived. I take my hat off to luck like yours, capable of besting even those above your rank."

So spoke the governor, offering praise that was equal parts exasperation and admiration, for Aldebaran's victory—having seized even the slimmest chance to kill an opponent far above him.
Tossing aside his broken crude sword, Aldebaran turned his back on an arena thin on cheers.

—Ten years had passed in a flash since he first woke on Sword Slave Isle.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

Ten years was a long time, and it wore away mercilessly at Aldebaran's humanity.
The face, the voice, the warmth he should have been able to recall vividly with closed eyes seemed to be painted over, again and again, by the dying faces, death throes, and cold spattered blood of everyone he'd killed with his own hands.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

It was true. Those words were true. Indeed, no one could kill Aldebaran.
Over these ten years, called upon again and again for Sword Slave Isle's 'Sparka,' he had crossed countless brinks of death in the arena, cutting down masters and inhuman things he had no business ever defeating.
With his giant-killing streak piling up, more than once or twice reckless death matches had been arranged specifically to get him killed, and each time it was some promising sword slave who ended up dead instead—so that Aldebaran's treatment had begun to take on a kind of untouchable status.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

After ten years, the faces on Sword Slave Isle were a different set entirely from those at the start.
Aldebaran had long since become one of the oldest hands there, and not a single person remained who knew the old rumor of the madman bewitched by the 'Witch.' Aldebaran himself, once he understood that the subject of the 'Witch' was taboo, and that almost no one knew anything beyond the surface of the legend, had entirely stopped bringing it up.
But that change hadn't come from disappointment or resignation at never getting the answer he wanted—it was something far more serious than that.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

Ten years, stranded and forced into killing after killing on Sword Slave Isle, in a space that repeated nothing but the exchange of lives—what came to dominate Aldebaran there was a vast despair.
A despair that questioned whether the 'Witch' had truly existed at all—that doubted her very existence.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

When he closed his eyes, he could indeed hear that voice. He remembered being told those words.
But, still, had it really, truly happened?

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

He couldn't recall the 'Witch's' face.
He couldn't recall the 'Witch's' voice.
He couldn't recall the 'Witch's' warmth.

Ten years—like a videotape he'd played back over and over in every spare moment, the memory had worn thin, until even splicing the torn tape back together couldn't make it play, and finally the tape itself crumbled to dust.
And so the memory itself scattered to pieces, and once the dust had finished spilling out from between his fingers, Aldebaran no longer knew.

—Had the 'Witch' truly existed?
—Were Aldebaran's memories events that had truly happened?

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

Every so often, an overwhelming urge to destroy his own existence would seize him.
At those times, Aldebaran would deliberately release his 'Domain' and step into a death match, letting an opponent attacking him with everything they had tear him to shreds, trying to peer over the edge of death. —No, not merely peer over it; there were times he even thought he might as well throw himself right off that cliff.

But it was always precisely at those times that his 'Domain' saved him, in some unexpected way.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

As if to prove that phrase spoken by a 'Witch' whose very existence was now in doubt, there were more than a few times an opponent, one step away from finally killing Aldebaran, would suffer a mental breakdown right before his eyes.
Each time he watched an opponent's eyes roll back, losing both sanity and the will to fight, Aldebaran felt that some unseen thing was trying to keep him alive, and with an unbearable feeling in his chest, he would cut off their head.

Even after ten years passed, the meaning behind why he'd been kept alive—the thing Aldebaran sought—remained unknown.
If anything, it was even doubtful whether anything had ever truly tried to keep him alive at all.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

It was simply terrifying.
That everything that had shaped him was a sham—that not one bit of the care the 'Witch' had lavished on him, not one word she'd spoken, had ever truly existed—that Aldebaran really was nothing but a madman bewitched by the 'Witch.' Eaten away at by that endless self-questioning, sleepless nights stretched on for years.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

Words that should have been, for Aldebaran, something to cling to, the very foundation of who he was—now, they had become far too terrifying.
To keep suspecting that all of it was a product of delusion—that the mission Aldebaran had carried, the meeting and the parting that should have happened, none of it had ever existed at all.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

An endless spiral of self-interrogation.
A sensation like sinking, not just to the waist but up to the chest, into a swamp filled with unanswerable, negative despair, unable to move.
Time like that, where only the manner of his death remained undecided, seemed set to go on endlessly into the future. —But Aldebaran's days of stagnation would, one day, abruptly meet their turning point.
That turning point was—

*"—Hear me, you rabble scattered across Sword Slave Isle."*

—A voice carrying a fierce heat like flame, a young girl's voice, echoing across the entire island through the speaking tubes.

—A mass uprising of the sword slaves, and an assassination plot against the new emperor using the response to it as a diversion.

That, apparently, was the true nature of the turning point that had befallen Aldebaran.
That said, there was no way detailed information would trickle down to a mere sword slave of Sword Slave Isle, and Aldebaran had no particular desire to learn more than that. For the time being, he simply assumed, from the lack of any change in how the island was run, that the coup hadn't succeeded.
At the very least, no change of emperor at this point in time was a thing Aldebaran had any knowledge of.
If there was just one thing, amid all this, that made Aldebaran feel a great change within himself, it was obvious—he'd saved a silver-haired girl who'd fallen into danger.

Of course, that had nothing to do with Aldebaran's true purpose.
The silver-haired girl was just a Dog Human girl he'd happened across, with no connection whatsoever to the silver-haired person Aldebaran was tied to. And yet, being able to save that girl, together with the voice of that flame-like girl who, in the middle of the chaos, had shown an attitude of walking her own path answering to no one—it was enough to make Aldebaran finally resolve himself.

—To go beyond the closed world, in order to confirm whether the 'Witch' had truly existed.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

Killed over and over by an aquatic Witchbeast released into the lake, Aldebaran finally broke free of the island that had held him captive for over ten years, and stepped onto land not surrounded by water. —Strictly speaking, since the entire world was encircled by the Great Waterfall, you could argue no land anywhere was truly free of water, but he could still muster the false cheer to think up a joke like that.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

It was, for Aldebaran, a curse, and a comfort, and a shackle, and an absolution.
To finally make out the color, the heat, the uncertain certainty at the source of that vague, directionless, and utterly enormous emotion, Aldebaran decided to trace the 'Witch's' footsteps.

There wasn't much time to spend on it. In the more than ten years he'd spent on that closed-off island, the world Aldebaran now walked had, without his intending it, drawn closer to the world he remembered.
All that remained was to confirm whether the thing inside Aldebaran was real. And what he needed to confirm that was proof of the 'Witch'—

"—Why is it, I wonder, that your existence isn't recorded in the 'Gospel'?"

So said a Witch Cult Sin Archbishop, tilting their head at an unnecessary angle, and though Aldebaran had a rough idea of the reason himself, he fiddled with the fittings of his helmet and left his answer vague.
The jet-black iron helmet he'd begun wearing back then was a parting gift he'd carried out of Sword Slave Isle, and he prized it both for its sturdiness and for being a full-face type that could hide his features.
That someone with such a conspicuous appearance wasn't recorded at all in the precious 'Gospel' apparently didn't sit well with this Sin Archbishop, who was needlessly noisy in both voice and movement.

"—You're 'Sloth,' aren't you?"

Honestly, the fight against this Sin Archbishop ranked among the toughest struggles of Aldebaran's life.
To begin with, he rarely won any fight without dying at least once, but even so, this was a formidable opponent, one where finding even a sliver of a chance to win was a struggle. In the end, he'd managed to satisfy his victory condition, but he hadn't actually won.
Compared even to the Sin Witches—whose existence was, like the 'Witch's,' now doubtful to him—this opponent, he thought, was in no way less vicious.

In any case—

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

He'd finally lived up to being told that, Aldebaran thought, having obtained the information he wanted. —To confirm the 'Witch's' existence, the group most likely to hold the information he needed was the Witch Cult, that heretical group he'd heard mentioned time and again even on Sword Slave Isle.
They were apparently a group whose hiding places were utterly unknown under normal circumstances, but that only applied if you were trying to hunt them down—luring them out wasn't especially hard. —In fact, just spreading a rumor about the 'Witch of Envy' whom the Witch Cult worshipped was enough to bring some of them running in dashing fashion.
Of course, if you couldn't handle whatever came dashing in, you'd simply have your life taken from you in the most pathetic way, dying the most foolish death in the world.

"Stop it, I say! It's blasphemous! Satella! Anyone other than Satella! Any 'Witch' other than the 'Witch of Envy'—the very idea is the height of blasphemy! The only one qualified to be reborn into this world is her alone, the one who bestows her favor upon me!!"

How much to believe in the words of a Sin Archbishop who had piled up such an enormous number of unhinged behaviors and deranged remarks, Aldebaran couldn't say.
But that madman's fervent confession, meaningless as it might be to anyone else, meant something to Aldebaran.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

Patiently, he redid his encounters with the Sin Archbishop over and over, drawing out words, exchanging what needed exchanging, provoking outbursts and offering up his own life, and starting over again from the beginning—repeating that endlessly, Aldebaran obtained the information he wanted from the Sin Archbishop.
And then, he simply passed right by the Sin Archbishop as they came dashing in, making it so their meeting had never happened at all.

"Ahh! Aahh! Ahhh! Sloth, Sloth, Sloth, Sloth, Slooooth—aaah!!"

He could easily picture the Sin Archbishop, having let him slip away, screaming as though coughing up blood over the vanished Aldebaran who had escaped the Witch Cult's encirclement—but it no longer concerned him.
The one wish inside Aldebaran at this point was singular—confirmation of the 'Witch's' existence.

He knew how each of the Sin Witches had met their end.
The Witch of Wrath was caught in a trap and died raving mad; the Witch of Lust was consumed in a great fire; the Witch of Gluttony withered away to death in a sea of sand; the Witch of Pride was drowned beneath a great flood; and the Witch of Sloth slew a dragon and fell into the Great Waterfall.
And the Witch of Envy was sealed away at the ends of the earth. As for the last 'Witch'—

"—I see. Being able to enter this place from somewhere other than the Tomb is a structural flaw. Much like the mechanism of the 'Sanctuary,' it's rather sloppy work. Though I suppose some of it can't be helped."

A grassland stretching so vast it had no visible end, beneath an unreal, boundless blue sky. A white table and parasol, set atop a small rise where a cool breeze blew through.
It was, without any doubt, the scene from Aldebaran's own fading, nearly vanished memory.
At the center of it was a beautiful presence expressible in just two colors, white and black—

"—Why is it, I wonder, that love diminishes."

After all manner of twists and turns, he had finally made his way back to the castle of his dreams.
Doubting his own sanity, Aldebaran had even come close to believing this might all be an illusion.

That reunion with her pupil—more than a decade in the making for him, four hundred years in the making for her—was celebrated by the 'Witch' who had, after all, truly existed. No—by the Witch of Greed, wearing a smile with no warmth of blood in it.

"—————"

For Aldebaran, the life he'd lived since losing an arm now outstripped the life before it in length.
So he rarely felt having only his right arm as any kind of handicap in daily life—though even so, on very rare occasions, he did feel the inconvenience of it.
For instance, at times like now, when he had to carry someone unconscious in his arms.

"Sorry about the lumpy ride."

In the arms of Aldebaran, who muttered that—arms that included a magically crafted stone prosthetic—was a limp, unconscious Emilia.
The aerial chase between Aldebaran and Emilia, which had begun across that vast canyon, ended with Emilia falling, brought down by a foul move Aldebaran had sprung on her.

Nothing complicated about it. His trump card was the same one he'd used to slip out of Emilia's grasp back in the royal capital.
The kind-hearted Emilia couldn't stand by and let someone be lost in the middle of a fight. —She, too, was among those left with a deep wound in her heart, having watched Priscilla die in the Empire's 'Great Disaster.'
So, to shield Aldebaran, who had planted himself right in the falling boulder's direct path, she hadn't hesitated to throw her own body in the way.

"Even if there was no other surefire way to land a hit, this was a garbage plan, even by my own standards."

Of course, that conclusion had come only after Aldebaran had trial-and-errored his way through other tactics first.
Adjusting where the boulder would land, shattering rock into a landslide, choosing the trajectory of his stone barrages, mixing in other spells—he'd tried it all, but apart from the trick of making himself the hostage, Emilia had brilliantly broken through every single one of them on nothing but in-the-moment inspiration and gut instinct.
Every time foolish Aldebaran had gone with a different tactic, he'd failed to escape the fate of being touched by Emilia's hand and frozen solid, so it could be said he'd had no choice but to fall back on this desperate measure.

"Not that it makes the guilt go away, or gets me forgiven, or anything."

The excuse, spoken to no one in particular, was clumsy enough that even Emilia probably wouldn't forgive it.
Having thrown herself in front of Aldebaran and taken a defenseless graze from the boulder's mass, Emilia had been struck hard across her whole body and, understandably, lost consciousness. At a glance, there didn't appear to be any injury that would leave lasting damage—it should amount to little more than a fairly strong concussion. —He'd carefully calibrated things to make sure of that.

"That was quite the detour I got forced into. ...Guess waiting around for Yae isn't the smart play, huh."

Once the fall from high up and the whole running battle with Emilia had finally ended, he at last had the breathing room to take stock of his surroundings. What he found was that this was likely somewhere within the Agzadd Canyon, the largest canyon in the world, and that he'd been put at a substantial difference in elevation from his destination, the Grand Mogolade Geyser.
He'd already crossed the border of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica and made it into the Kararagi City-States, but climbing back up the several thousand meters he'd fallen and returning to his original route would take a considerable amount of time.

And besides—

"Never mind Olta—can Roy and the old man actually make the rendezvous...?"

Among the accomplices scattered by all this, he had no worries about 'Aldebaran's' fighting strength, but Roy Alphard and Heinkel were another matter entirely—both their competence and their character were deeply worrying. Of course, Roy's actions were bound by a curse seal, so as long as he valued his life, he shouldn't break the rules.

"Either way, the safest bet is just to hope everyone converges at the rendezvous point."

Aldebaran's top priority was reaching the Grand Mogolade Geyser and accomplishing his goal.
That goal required borrowing 'Aldebaran's' strength, though at worst, there were ways to manage even without 'Aldebaran.' On top of that, the power of Roy's Gluttony Authority was indispensable for cleaning up after the disaster he'd exploited for this plan.
So, even if it had to be after the goal was achieved, he needed to somehow regroup with them and get them to follow instructions.

"Meant to prevent secondary damage, and instead I go and cause tertiary and quaternary damage—what a joke."

Muttering that, Aldebaran tilted the stone wings on his back, and while maintaining his speed, slowly and somehow managed a clean landing at the bottom of the canyon, on the bank of the great river flowing through it there. —That was a lie. He'd blown himself up six times while carrying Emilia, and only on the seventh attempt did he finally manage to land.

"If I can't even stand up after this, it'd be a joke."

At one point he'd taken damage not just to both legs but up to his hips, and had nearly ended up fully paralyzed, unable to even drink his poison. He'd managed to roll into the river's surface and drown himself, barely, but he had no wish to repeat the same mistake.

In any case—

"Sorry about this, Emilia... kiddo."

So that she wouldn't drown if the great river happened to swell and rise for some reason, Aldebaran laid Emilia not on the canyon floor but in a hollow in the rock wall, set slightly higher up.
For his last words before leaving her behind like this, he hesitated a little over what to call her. And after that hesitation, in the end, he addressed her as always—as Aldebaran. —As for any other way of naming the true relationship between himself and her, he lacked the courage to use it now, in this relationship that wasn't the true one.

"—!"

The instant he cursed his own cowardice, a blinding headache shot through him, and Aldebaran gritted his teeth.
The moment his guard relaxed after clearing this hard stretch, all the accumulated debt tried to burst out at once. Somehow holding it in check, he took ten seconds to breathe deep, then gulped down poison—repeating that, over and over, to steady his mind.
But while this method let his heart catch its breath, it couldn't do the same for his body—especially not his brain.

In real time, he'd already had his brain running non-stop for close to a hundred hours.
Along the way, he'd been getting by, faking it again and again with 'Aldebaran's' healing magic, but unless he shut his mind and body down completely at some point, this overheating wasn't going to ease up.
Even so, he told himself: just one more push.

*"—No one can beat the you that I made."*

"Yeah, that's right, Sensei. —No one can beat me. That's right. Damn right."

When he closed his eyes, those words came back to life in Aldebaran's mind.
Leaning on them for support, Aldebaran—

*"—See, there it is again."*

"—————"

*"Once again, my—"*

"—————"

*"My—"*

A voice came, broken and indistinct, never quite clear.
Because he knew whose it was, when those words had been spoken, and just how much they would sear his soul, Aldebaran shut the ears of his heart to it.

He wouldn't stop. That was what he'd decided. He had decided it.
That he mustn't stop, after all—that was what he'd decided.
Because he had decided—


△▼△▼△▼△


—It happened just after Aldebaran, having shut the ears of his heart, finally hauled himself up out of the canyon and resumed his rush toward the Grand Mogolade Geyser.

"Wha—"

was the rasping breath that escaped Aldebaran's mouth.
It was no wonder such a sound had slipped out unbidden. After all, this was an accident that had come flying in from outside his awareness, an unforeseen event that some deep corner of his mind had dismissed as impossible. —A massive icicle had come plunging down and buried itself in the ground, a mere two meters ahead on Aldebaran's own path.

"—————"

Swallowing hard, Aldebaran turned back and understood: far below, at the bottom of the canyon, Emilia—the one he'd left behind there—had dropped this colossal spike of ice.
But given the damage she'd taken, it should have been hours before Emilia's consciousness returned—meaning she'd landed this parting shot without ever regaining consciousness at all.

"What monstrous tenacity..."

Still, considering that if Aldebaran had climbed up out of the ravine just five seconds sooner, he'd have ended up crushed beneath that very icicle, it could be said he'd dodged tragedy in a double sense.
Of course, even had it struck him directly, Aldebaran could simply have used his Authority to redo it and pull off a result where it never hit him at all—but for Emilia's sake, and for the sake of Aldebaran's own peace of mind, it was best if the chance to be finished off by her hand never came up at all.

Even so—

"It's a thing that happens now and then mid-battle, but damn, that's scary."

Even in Sword Slave Isle's 'Sparka,' there were times during a life-or-death fight when an opponent, dealt a fatal wound, would wring out the last of their life force for a final blow that could easily have taken him down with them. In Aldebaran's own experience, there had even been one who came at him to kill even after having their own head severed.
An action someone had resolved to carry out—even once their consciousness or life itself was gone—would have some residue of that intent left in their limbs, and that residue would still try to see it through.
Emilia's parting shot just now, too, was the shape given to a resolve to stop Aldebaran no matter what—

"—Wait."

Just as he meant to praise that force of will, Aldebaran caught his breath.
Right before his eyes, the icicle that had grazed past Aldebaran and driven itself into the ground—it was a huge, huge spear of ice, the kind a mythical Giant might swing around. But as something meant purely to finish off Aldebaran, it was, no matter how you looked at it, absurdly over the top.
Something this enormous—it was almost like—

"—An anchor."

That was the word that slipped out of him, right after he murmured it. —Just as Aldebaran, using the ice spear Emilia had launched as a marker, moved to head for the Grand Mogolade Geyser, a new assassin was sent in against him.

It was—

"—Yo, helmet bastard. Look at you, all busted up after just a little while apart."

"...Pot calling the kettle black, and your mouth's gotten pretty foul too, Filore-chan."

Two fighters, each having cleared the first phase of the battlefield and moved up to the next.
Filore Lugunica—no, standing in his way just as she had when the mixed army led by Felt had thrown their 'Memory' flashbang grenades at him.
