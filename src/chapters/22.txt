Arc 1 Chapter 21: "The Sword Saint's Might"
<side: F>

Riding the wind, her body tore through the battlefield, and the instant she passed through the entrance, Felt felt herself released from despair.
Behind her, in a world she had no leisure to look back on, the battle still raged.

A dry sound rang out as the air itself froze solid, then came the scatter of a mass shattering against steel, a blunt weapon striking the air apart, and now and then the graceless cries of "hyoeh!" or "fwaa!" as someone dodged.

The battle was still going on.
Even though Felt had run away, leaving everything behind.

"What the hell am I thinking? Lucky to get away at all, ain't I? Right?"

If she'd stayed there, it was plain she would have lost her life.
Against a woman who'd struck down even old man Rom with a single blow, there wasn't the faintest chance Felt could have paid her back. The same went for the silver-haired half-elf — without her spirit's support, there was no way she could have stood against that woman.
And Subaru was even more hopelessly outmatched, in a much more obvious way.
He was plainly an amateur, without a shred of battle-hardened composure about him. His fingers were clean, like he'd never once gripped a weapon, and his fine black hair and unmarked skin surely meant he'd never been hurt in his life.

In other words, he was sheltered. The sort of person who had no business even thinking about stepping into a fight.
Bringing that ridiculously expensive magic Vessel along fit right into that picture, when she thought about it that way.
She should just think, *serves him right.* Some naive rich kid, moved by a sliver of chivalry, doing something wildly beyond his station — she should sneer at his recklessness and leave it at that.

"Yeah, that's right, run. I can't stay in this town anymore. There's a monster like that around. Before she notices me and I can't get away — quick, hurry—"

Even within the slums, she should pick a route that only the people who called this place home would know. Thin, cramped, practically an animal trail — but perfect as an escape route.
There was no one who could catch someone as small and quick as her, not here. If she put her own life first, that was the wisest course.
Even Subaru — who might already be dead by now — had played the hero to let her escape, so it was only right that she actually make it. That's how it should be.
That's how it should be, and yet—

"—Somebody! Isn't there somebody?!"

Even as her mind screamed at her to take the narrow paths, Felt's feet carried her down the main road of the slums that connected to the main street. Her face was strained, her breath ragged, her eyes unable to settle on anything.
She glared at a ramshackle building, scrambled up a wall, stood atop a fence, and spat.

*Somebody* wasn't enough.
It had to be somebody with the strength to stand against that murderer — against Elsa.
If anybody would do, there had been people along the road she'd already run. But Felt was hardly a beloved figure in this quarter.
And someone willing to take on a girl dragging this much trouble behind her — in this place, there was no one but old man Rom.

And that very old man Rom would be killed if she just left him there.
And Subaru, who'd tried to get her to safety, was right now in danger of his life too.
It was a strange feeling. It made no sense, and Felt rubbed furiously at eyes that threatened to well up with tears.
Old man Rom, sure, but why should it matter if some boy she'd only just met died? Dressed like he'd grown up soft, saying stupid things left and right, thoughtless to the core.
But he'd gotten angry in her place, when Elsa's words had nearly brought Felt to tears. And even now, he was making himself a sacrificial stone just to keep her alive.

It was a feeling she couldn't make sense of. And yet, because of it, Felt's heart demanded that she run.
Something about that boy's actions had struck a chord in her. Because of that — because that spark refused to stop craving heat — Felt ran, carrying a fury that wanted to scream itself raw.

And then, after tearing through street after street, she—

"—Please, help me."

"Got it. I'll help."

—met a young man like red flame, and her fate was shaken.

"...Reinhard?"

"That's right, Subaru. Been a little while. Sorry for the delay."

To Subaru, still slumped where he'd fallen, the tall figure — Reinhard — turned only his head back, offering an apologetic, faint smile.
Despite having crashed through the roof and fallen a good five meters, the ease with which he stepped forward showed no trace of the impact. Even the single motion of brushing off dust carried something refined about it, and Subaru, in that instant, touched a piece of his true nature — something altogether different from the bumbling exchange they'd had back in the alley.

Reinhard's gaze swept forward without a flicker of carelessness, settling on the beautiful figure in black hurling hostility his way. For a moment, those blue eyes narrowed, as if recalling something.

"Black hair, black attire. And a curved blade unique to the northern lands, bent like the character for 'く.' With features that distinctive, there's no mistaking you. You're the 'Bowel Hunter.'"

"What kind of insanely violent nickname is that..."

"It comes from the distinctive way she kills. She's known as a dangerous figure — her name's come up even in the royal capital. Though word has it she's just a mercenary."

Answering Subaru's muttering dutifully, Reinhard fixed his clear blue eyes on Elsa. Under that gaze, Elsa shifted slightly.

"Reinhard — ah, yes. The knight among knights. Of the 'Sword Saint' bloodline. How wonderful. To think I'd have the good fortune of facing such delightful opponents, one after another. I really must thank my employer."

"There's a great deal I'd like to ask you. I'd recommend surrendering."

"Would a starving carnivore really hold back, with the finest steak, dripping blood, laid out right before it?"

Licking her thin lips seductively beneath their red, Elsa gazed at Reinhard with an expression of rapture.
Receiving that look, he scratched his cheek with a troubled gesture and murmured, "I suppose that answers that."

"Can't be helped, then. Subaru, keep your distance for a moment. If you can, get that old man somewhere safe. After that, I'd be grateful if you stayed close to that lady."

"Got it. ...That woman's a monster, so don't let your guard down, alright?"

"Fortunately, monster hunting happens to be my specialty."

Leaving those reassuring words behind, Reinhard stepped forward without a trace of tension.
He didn't so much as touch the sword at his hip — he advanced completely unarmed.
Subaru couldn't help but catch his breath at that overwhelming composure, but what left him truly stunned came the moment battle broke out right after.

"—Hah."

With a sharp exhale, Elsa's hand flashed the kukri knife she held in a single slash aimed at his throat.
The streak of silver, freed from whatever restraint she'd shown against Subaru, cut clean through the very air as it lunged for Reinhard's slender neck. Reinhard, in turn, was utterly defenseless — he made no move to draw his sword in defense, didn't even attempt to dodge.
The blade swept clean through, and Subaru saw the illusion of Reinhard's head flying off. Blood spraying, and — like something out of a manga — his head dropping a beat later.
That was the vision he saw. But—

"I'd rather not get too rough with a lady, if it can be helped..."

Lowering his voice, if only slightly, Reinhard offered that courteous preface. And then—

"Excuse me."

A kick, delivered with such force the floor burst apart beneath his footing and a shockwave rippled outward, sent Elsa flying.

The aftershock of the blast even reached Subaru, and he was left speechless as the wind buffeted him.
What had looked like nothing more than an ordinary front kick had, from its force alone, generated a gust powerful enough to shake the entire building. Elsa's body, catching the blow head-on, flew like a scattered leaf; she used the wall as a foothold to kill her momentum, and even as she rolled to a landing on the ground, astonishment was plastered across her face.

"No, no, no, seriously?! What was that?!"

Subaru had used the word "off the charts" plenty of times before, but only now, here, did he realize how completely wrong that definition had been.
The phrase "off the charts" existed precisely for this handsome man standing before him.
Compared to him, running like the wind, swinging a club like a twig, combat instincts beyond the ordinary, an overwhelming abundance of magical power — all of it paled into insignificance.

"Just as the rumors say... no, you're even more than the rumors, aren't you."

"Whether I can live up to your expectations, I couldn't say."

"Won't you draw that sword at your hip? I'd love a taste of that legendary edge."

Recklessly, Elsa pointed at Reinhard's sword, wishing to face him in earnest.
But Reinhard shook his head at her request.

"This sword can't be drawn except when it must be drawn. If the blade hasn't left its sheath, that means now isn't that time."

"How cheaply I've been valued."

"Personally, it's a judgment that leaves me rather inconvenienced. Which is why—"

Casting his gaze briefly aside, Reinhard swept his eyes across the Loot House's interior. Spotting a beaten-up two-handed sword among the stolen goods leaning against the wall, now fallen to the floor, he unearthed it.
Kicking the hilt up with his foot, he caught the spinning blade with ease, gave it a light test swing, and—

"I'll face you with this instead. Any objections?"

"—None at all. Ah, wonderful. How wonderful. Do let me enjoy myself, won't you!"

Against Reinhard, now armed, it was Elsa who struck first, twisting her body sideways.
An instant later, throwing knives launched from her spinning form — four of them at once, no less. Even when she'd targeted Felt, she'd been holding back.
The blades, thrown with staggered heights, were slower than the fake Satella's ice shards, but the difference in their trajectories made evasion difficult. In other words, the only way to stop the knives was to deflect them —

"Naturally, I intend to make use of the opening that creates."

Like a spring-loaded doll, Elsa's body leapt high. Flipping upside down as if using the ceiling as a foothold, she pushed off again with her knees, hurling herself straight at Reinhard.

Even deflecting the knives, he'd still have to meet the follow-up Elsa in a two-stage assault.
Against the combined attack — four knives and one strike, each with its own timing — Reinhard raised his sword before him.

"Forgive me, but — ranged weapons can't reach me."

The instant he murmured that, Subaru's eyes bore witness to the laws of physics themselves going askew.
The throwing knives, which had been on a direct line for Reinhard's body, suddenly changed course, veering off him entirely to strike a bare, empty wall.
It didn't look like he'd used some special magic, and it certainly wasn't a case of him swinging his sword too fast to see. Just like the moment before, when he'd taken Elsa's first strike completely undefended, it was an inexplicable sight, as if Subaru had just witnessed something fundamentally unreasonable.

"The Divine Protection of Arrow Avoidance—!"

"Something I was given from birth... I'd ask you not to think it unfair."

The knives failed even to serve as a feint, and the clash that followed became a head-on duel.
Elsa put the speed of her leap behind her slash; Reinhard, meeting her, held his sword poised to swing up from below.
Despite it being a clash of mere instants, Subaru found he could take in the scene as if in slow motion.

Perhaps it was because every part of Reinhard's movement was so refined, glimpsing in that single motion a technique so masterful it bordered on the divine.
In his hands, even a shoddy blade stolen from a backwater warehouse seemed to shine like a legendary sword out of some old legend.

It was truly swordsmanship that wrung out every last drop of the blade's potential.
The edge struck exactly where it was aimed, at the base of the kukri knife — and despite the collision of steel on steel, that impossible cutting power stripped the blade clean out of Elsa's grip as if licking it away.

Bracing a hand against the ground to spin her body around, Elsa had no words for the fate of the weapon in her hand.
Only the hilt remained of the knife, its blade sheared clean off — and that severed blade—

"With your weapon gone, I'd recommend surrendering."

—was caught, one-handed, by Reinhard as he swept his blade down and turned back, without even a glance.
With a twist of his wrist he flung the captured blade, and without so much as watching it strike the wall with a sharp clang, he let it go. Subaru even heard the sound of Elsa's throat swallowing hard.

"That's... not normal. Not even in me to make a joke out of it."

Squeezing out that comment, Subaru hastily put some distance between himself and the battlefield.
He made his way to the fallen old man Rom, dragging the man's bulky frame toward the safety of a wall.

"Old man Rom, old man Rom. Hey, Baldy, c'mon, you alive?"

"Ugh... who're you calling... bald..."

"Who else would I be talking to, obviously you. My one life goal is to never become bald or fat. You're my cautionary tale number one."

Tapping the man's forehead, weak as his response was, Subaru let out a sigh of relief.
Aside from the serious damage to his head, the old man would probably be fine. His memory might end up a little worse for wear, but as long as he was alive, that was a minor problem.

"Is he going to be okay?"

The fake Satella, having hurried over to Subaru now that the weight was off his shoulders, spoke up.
Letting her long silver hair fall down her back, she checked the extent of old man Rom's injuries where he lay unconscious, murmured "This needs treating," and began wrapping her palms in a faint blue glow.

"Hey now, just so you know — this old geezer's part of the crew that stole your emblem."

"That's exactly why. Heal him properly, then use that debt of gratitude to pry information out of him. Someone who owes you their life surely won't lie to you. This is for my own sake too, you know."

Did she really have to justify every single thing she did like that, just to convince herself it was alright?
Wryly amused by her roundabout way of putting things, Subaru turned his eyes back to the battle.

He couldn't make out Elsa's expression where she knelt, but had Reinhard judged that he'd stripped her of the will to fight? Sword lowered at his side, he walked toward her without a shred of caution.
It was surely a move born of confidence in the gap between their skill, but overconfidence always bred the worst outcomes. Some warning bell rang in the back of Subaru's mind, and just before Elsa's body sprang into motion—

"There's a second one, Reinhard!"

The second kukri knife, drawn from her hip, grazed the tips of Reinhard's red bangs as he leaned back to avoid it.
Her ambush dodged, Elsa turned her black eyes toward Subaru.

"How did you know?"

"Had personal experience with it!"

Raising his middle finger in a boast that wasn't much of a boast, Subaru got the words shrugged off as nonsense by Elsa, who—

"But my fangs aren't limited to two. ...Would you care to accompany me for another round?"

"If cutting down every weapon you have would satisfy you, I'd be glad to."

"When the fangs are gone, there are claws. When the claws are gone, there are teeth. When the teeth are gone, there are bones. And when the bones are gone, there is life itself. — That's what it means to be a battle maniac."

"In that case, allow me to break that particular sign of yours."

Drawing a third knife from her hip, Elsa took up a dual-wielding stance and leapt once more.
Blade met blade, sparks scattering from the collision of steel on steel. From the point where one blade was parried, her other hand loosed a strike from a different angle.
The whipping blade aimed for Reinhard's throat, but it was kicked away by a long leg snapping up before it could land. Elsa fell back with the force of the deflection — and used even that momentum, stretching her leg out to the wall, using the rebound to reach the ceiling. Then, kicking off the ceiling, on to yet another wall.

Just as Subaru had cursed her as a spider-woman, she used movement that defied the room's very gravity to keep Reinhard from ever concentrating his attacks on a single point.
Reinhard endured her hit-and-run tactics, striking from every direction, without ever moving a single step from where he stood, devoting himself entirely to interception. But there was no end to it.

"Don't tell me even Reinhard can't land a finishing blow..."

The sheer inhuman degree of Elsa's skill was now on a completely different level from when she'd fought with a single knife.
It was like a boss's second form. A change on the level of an entirely different species.
And Reinhard, meeting her blow for blow, was operating on that same inhuman plane too, thanks to the strength of that Divine Protection he'd displayed (though Subaru still wasn't entirely sure what it even was) and his sheer combat prowess.
This had to be nothing less than a clash between two beings from some higher realm. Even so, Subaru held onto the somewhat optimistic thought that, in terms of raw ability, surely Reinhard had the edge.

"...He's holding back, out of consideration for us."

As if in answer to Subaru's unspoken question, the fake Satella, still tending to old man Rom, murmured.
Glancing sidelong at Subaru as he said "Huh?", she bit her lip, looking uncomfortable.

"Because I'm using Spiritual Arts, he can't go all out. At least, not until this man's treatment is finished..."

"What's the connection there?"

"If Reinhard truly meant to fight seriously, the mana in the air would turn away from me entirely. — The treatment's almost done. When I give the signal, call out to him."

"Ah — okay."

The explanation hadn't quite made sense to him, but, asked as he was, Subaru agreed despite his confusion.
The blue glow continued to heal the bruising floating over old man Rom's head and the small wound that had been bleeding. Bit by bit, the traces of blood and the wound itself faded away, and before Subaru's admiring eyes, the fake Satella let out a deep breath.
After wiping the wound clean with a cloth, there was no trace anywhere of the blow that had knocked old man Rom unconscious.
Subaru, letting out an impressed "Whoa" at the sight, felt the girl tap his shoulder.

"Please."

"Leave it to me. — Reinhard! I don't really get it, but go all out!"

To Reinhard, who had been purely on the defensive until now, Subaru hurled the news that the treatment was complete.
Reinhard glanced back with just his eyes. Seeing Subaru, meeting his gaze, he gave a faint dip of his chin in acknowledgment, and—

"—What will you show me?"

"The sword strike of House Astrea—"

To Elsa's question as she leapt, Reinhard answered, short and solemn.


—In the next instant, Subaru felt the very space within the Loot House seem to warp and twist.
