Arc 10 Chapter 12: "Groan"

---

—That day, the sky had been clear since morning, as if blessing the buoyant future yet to come.

"Is that overthinking it a bit? Getting too carried away?"

"Betty doesn't think so, I suppose. In fact, if Filore's Sacred Rite works as well as everyone hopes, then Subaru's high spirits are just a bit of early celebrating, I suppose."

"Once you call it 'early celebrating' on my own say-so, I really do start to feel like I'm getting too carried away."

At Beatrice's follow-up, walking hand in hand beside him, Subaru scratched his cheek with his free hand.

Given the circumstances, he wanted to defend himself—no wonder he was in high spirits—but considering how little he'd actually contributed to it, he couldn't deny he was getting a bit too worked up.

In truth, Subaru's immediate plans—heading to the floodgate city of Priestella, where the Divine Dragon Church's Sacred Rite would be used to save the Witch Cult's victims—wouldn't actually put him to any use in that scene.

He simply wanted to be there: as the knight of Emilia, who would be one of those credited for the achievement, and above all as one who knew firsthand the fierce battle that city had endured, to witness the moment the ghastly scars that fight had left behind finally began to heal.

"So I asked Reinhard, who's been working himself ragged lately, and got myself added to the expedition group he's taking to Priestella."

"Naturally, Betty's coming along too, I suppose. Along with Emilia, who's going to thaw out the frozen townsfolk, and Garfiel and Otto, who both still have unfinished business in that city, I suppose."

"Garfiel's got that family he's close with—their father was one of Lust's victims, from what I hear. And Otto's... picking up the book he left with the restorer, huh."

"So it seems, I suppose. ...Honestly, what a shrewd man, I suppose."

Puffing out her cheeks in irritation as she muttered that, Beatrice's complicated feelings were not lost on Subaru.

The book Otto had left with the restorer in Priestella was the Book of Wisdom said to have been left behind by the Witch of Greed, Echidna—though it was the copy Roswaal had inherited. Otto's obsession with bringing it back, reduced almost entirely to ash, driven purely by his desire to confirm its contents, was remarkable enough on its own—but harder still to credit was the restorer's skill, who had apparently boasted they could fix it.

Still, for Beatrice—who had let the Book of Wisdom entrusted to her burn to nothing—it was bound to stir up some hard-to-name feelings.

"There's no need to worry, Subaru, I suppose."

"Beatrice..."

"As for Betty's guide to the future, Subaru's right here now, I suppose. Otto's penny-pinching nature, trying to recycle even the ashes, is honestly a little much, I suppose. But if it means putting one over on Roswaal, it doesn't feel like such a bad thing, I suppose."

"...Yeah."

Ignoring his worry entirely, Beatrice puffed out her chest with such conviction that Subaru found her endearing, and he stroked his adorable Contracted Spirit's head to his heart's content.

As mentioned, traveling to Priestella via the Reinhard Express meant, oddly enough, the same members as the previous trip there. Ideally, Subaru would have liked to move with his whole camp, but he couldn't ask that of Ram, currently laid up from overwork.

Joining them from other camps were Reinhard, tasked with driving the Dragon Carriage, and Felt, who apparently served as his supervisor—and then there was Filore, the star and key figure of this expedition, along with her chaperone, Sakura.

"Honestly, with things already backed up the way they are, throwing Felt and Filore together in one Dragon Carriage all of a sudden feels a little like a 'do not mix' warning label to me."

"But from what I've heard, the two of them have already met, I suppose."

"Yeah, the day Filore first showed up at the castle and treated Crusch. The two of them apparently ran into each other that very day... and it sounds like it was Felt who had Filore hold the emblem to check whether she qualified as a Royal Selection candidate."

"There's fearless, and then there's whatever that girl is, I suppose. Normally you'd hesitate, worrying it might put your own position in jeopardy, I suppose."

"Guess she never counted on her birth to begin with. That kind of decisiveness—it makes me think she's kind of cool, honestly."

There was a charisma to the cleanness of Felt's way of living that drew many people in.

In truth, those who wished to see Felt seated on the throne followed her because they were captivated by her words, her actions, her very way of being—finding their ideals in that small back of hers.

If that was the pure, unadulterated result born from the shine of Felt's soul, then the current state of things was practically the same as tainting it with needless complications.

"Felt and Filore—one of them's the real princess, and the other's a fake, huh."

"It's a strange story to begin with, I suppose. Whichever of the two is real, if someone meant to make use of that possibility, they should be pushing it far more aggressively, or it's just a wasted asset, I suppose. Getting a great many people to believe it, and a great many people to doubt it—that ought to be the best use for it, I suppose."

The more he thought about it, the more Subaru felt there was something forced about how the surviving-princess theory was being used.

Just as Beatrice said, far from making the most of what should have been a powerful advantage, it could even be said that Felt and Filore had instead been saddled with the negative image that, if one of them was genuine, the other was certainly a fake.

And if that was the case, what bothered him most, first and foremost, was—

"—In the end, it's what the Church was thinking, keeping Filore's existence hidden."

According to Filore herself, fifteen years ago she'd been abandoned in front of the Church and taken in, growing up as an orphan in the Divine Dragon Church's orphanage. Raised there as quite the sheltered young lady, she'd emerged as a monster of proactiveness with almost no knowledge of the outside world.

Setting aside the fact that this proactiveness had produced today's chaos, the Divine Dragon Church surely knew her physical features, her age, and even her name.

For none of that to have been connected to the Lugunican royal family was, absolutely and without question, impossible.

Furthermore, if the Sacred Rite Filore wielded had power enough to counter the Authorities of the Sin Archbishops of Lust and Gluttony, then surely it would have had some effect on the illness that struck the royal family as well.

If so, then why had the Lugunican royal family been left to die by the Divine Dragon Church?

"If that mystery at least doesn't come to light, nobody's going to be satisfied."

Just now, with news of the Royal Selection spreading across the entire kingdom, people throughout the land were watching to see who would take the throne of the age. For better or worse, they wouldn't overlook—and wouldn't forget—any conspicuous act by a Royal Selection candidate.

That's why he wanted, no matter what, to know what the Divine Dragon Church was thinking.

To that end—

"—Miklotov's invitation was a real stroke of luck."

"It was, I suppose."

Beside Beatrice, who nodded with few words, Subaru recalled the other day's events—accompanying Filore as she accepted the request to join the Royal Selection, the time spent in the castle's meeting room with Emilia, exchanging words with Miklotov.

That scene had ended in an uproar and a rushed, scrambling close after Filore's bombshell statement about choosing a knight burst out—but Subaru had gained something from it too.

That was the promise that Miklotov—an important figure in the Council of Wise Men, and likely one of the foremost scholars in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica—would set aside time to speak with him.

Amid the noisy uproar of Filore's theatrics continuing on, Subaru had figured it couldn't hurt to ask, and proposed wanting to talk—and Miklotov had graciously agreed.

"Hmm. In that case, let me clear some time for Natsuki-dono. As it happens, I too had a private matter I wished to discuss with Natsuki-dono."

"That would be incredibly appreciated, but... you want to talk to me?"

"Yes. There's something I'd like to confirm, something I'd like to tell you, and something I'd like your advice on... I'm sorry, but I'd be grateful if you'd bear with an old man's long-winded talk."

So saying, Miklotov flashed a kindly, grandfatherly smile, told Subaru where his residence was, and arranged the time for their private appointment.

That appointed day was today—the very day they were scheduled to depart for Priestella after the Hour of Fire.

Before that momentous departure, true to his promise with Miklotov, Subaru was heading alone—no, having given advance notice, bringing only Beatrice, his other half in body and soul—toward the promised address.

"What Miklotov wants to talk about... setting aside the wanting-to-hear and wanting-to-tell parts, that 'something to ask advice on' bit is kind of scary. But I've got a ton of questions of my own too. Ideally, I'd love it if he showed me an actual Dragon History Stone somewhere."

"From what I hear, the Dragon History Stone is kept under tight guard, same as the Dragon's Blood and the like, I suppose. But if Betty got a look at it, she should be able to form a hypothesis about what its workings resemble, I suppose."

"It's not that I think the Dragon History Stone itself is suspicious exactly, but the fact that they let the entire royal family die out and then only bothered filling in the details for the Royal Selection that followed—that bugs me."

He couldn't help suspecting some kind of malice or design lurking behind it. He didn't want to think that even the deaths of the royal family had been orchestrated by something.

"――――"

He had seen with his own eyes, in the mausoleum, the stone coffins of the Lugunican royals who had actually lost their lives. Thinking of how the beloved royal family—especially the death of the Fourth Prince—had affected people he knew, like Crusch and Ferris, if someone's will had been involved in that, Subaru could never forgive whoever it was.

Just as he thought this, and clenched the hand opposite the one holding Beatrice's—

"—Now that's some seriously dangerous stuff you're thinking about there, my friend."

Startled by the light, breezy voice suddenly calling out from the side, Subaru turned with a jolt. There, at the apex of a T-shaped intersection, he spotted a stylish young man waving at him.

At that easygoing greeting, Subaru's face broke into a grin.

"'My friend'? Getting awfully familiar all of a sudden, huh..."

"Whoa, whoa, whoa, you're the one who called me that first! Don't kick the ladder out from under me!"

"I'm kidding. Good to see you again, Teaga."

His eyes flying wide for a moment, the young man—Teaga—narrowed them again into a glare at Subaru's shamelessly outstretched hand, then, after shrugging, shook it.

With the handshake safely exchanged, their friendship was duly reaffirmed, but—

"Lady Beatrice, you look well today. It's an honor to see you again."

"Betty supposes there's no harm done, I suppose. ...But why are you here?"

"That's exactly the line I wanted to hand right back to you. I wasn't exactly sneaking around, but this was supposed to be a secret summons, if I'm not mistaken."

"Secret summons... don't tell me you got called by Miklotov too?"

"—So that means you did too, huh?"

Subaru and Teaga, eyes wide, pointed at each other, and Beatrice, standing between them, stood on tiptoe to push both their pointing arms down.

Maybe helped along by that adorable gesture, Teaga let out a soft breath—"Ha"—and said,

"Geez, for a second I panicked, thinking I'd been tailed. Then again, I figured no one would tail someone while dragging a kid along... nah, never mind, I'll stop there."

"What, don't stop halfway. I won't get mad, so just say it."

"Yeah? Alright then, I'll say it—famous as the 'Little Girl Wrangler' that you are, Natsuki Subaru, tailing someone with a kid in tow might be a piece of cake for you..."

"You bastard, who're you calling a 'Little Girl Wrangler,' huh!"

"You said you wouldn't get mad—that's the only reason I said it!"

At that dishonorable nickname resurfacing after so long, an indignant Subaru lunged to grab him. But Teaga deftly dodged every one of Subaru's hands and darted behind Beatrice in a flash.

At that impressively shameless bit of scrambling, Beatrice shrugged at Subaru and said,

"This isn't getting us anywhere, so let's call it settled here, I suppose. Subaru, you be the bigger person, I suppose."

"Tch, fine. Since I'm the adult here, I'm not gonna keep grumbling at you for pulling some childish stunt. Because I'm the adult, see."

"I don't think adults go around flaunting how adult they are quite that much, honestly."

Straightening up from his crouch with a light stretch, Teaga adjusted his hat and jerked his chin toward their shared destination at the intersection where they'd stopped.

"Our destination's the same. Probably the appointed time is too. Let's go together."

"I don't mind, but if that's the case, he didn't need to make a weird point of specifying just me. He could've let Emilia-tan or Rem come along too."

"Setting Rem-san aside, isn't Lady Emilia your liege? You'd drag her along on your own personal errand?"

"It's not dragging her along—I just don't want to be apart from her, even for a moment, if I can help it."

"That's—"

Banter for banter. Subaru had lobbed that out meaning to trade quips, but Teaga, as if suddenly at a loss for words, shut his mouth and let his eyes wander for a while.

At that reaction, Subaru exchanged a look with Beatrice and tilted his head.

"What's wrong? Did my Big Love for Emilia-tan and everyone scare you?"

"It's not that 'Big Love' of yours that scares me. It's just that your heavy dose of affection struck some chord in my heart that even I don't quite understand... I guess you could say."

"—I'm flattered, really, but I've already got a whole lot of people set in my heart, so..."

"That's the line of the worst kind of guy, dressed up to look devoted!?"

Feeling his rhythm return, Subaru laughed at Teaga's sharp, satisfying retort. He'd thought from the start that they were somehow on the same wavelength, and it seemed "my friend" wasn't just for show.

Teaga, too, caught up in Subaru's laughter, forgot the odd feeling from a moment before and smiled along.

"That's enough excitement out of you two, I suppose. It's come into view, I suppose."

At some point Beatrice had gone on a little ahead of them, and now stood at the corner of the street, waving them over. With long strides and quick steps, Subaru and Teaga hurried—just short of breaking into a run—and fell in beside Beatrice at almost the same moment.

And there, at the edge of the noble district, they found a large mansion matching both the specified address and the roof color they'd been told about, and guessed it must be Miklotov's residence.

"So what's your business with Miklotov, Teaga?"

"Consulting about something I'm looking for, I guess. What about you two?"

"Me and Beako are chasing after some historical romance—wanted to ask about various dark corners of the kingdom's history, and hoped he might let us see a Dragon History Stone, just once."

"I see. So that's how it connects to that dangerous talk from earlier. Just some friendly advice, but you probably shouldn't go around saying stuff like that about the Dragon History Stone out on the street—"

He'd probably meant to continue with something like "it's fine," but Teaga's words cut off there.

Wondering what was wrong, Subaru and Beatrice turned to look, and found Teaga had stopped in his tracks, partway toward the gate of Miklotov's mansion, glaring at the stone wall surrounding it.

And on his handsome, finely-featured profile, a clear wariness settled as he murmured.

It was—

"—This is bad. I smell blood."

Beatrice in his left hand, Guiltywhip in his right—with both of these trusted companions settled firmly into his grip, Subaru held his breath and advanced through the mansion, thick with the smell of blood.

"—Subaru, don't fall behind. We cover each other's blind spots."

The one calling out in that hushed voice, watching the way ahead of them, was Teaga.

Crouched low, moving without a sound, Teaga held in his hand a blade drawn silently and smoothly—a deep, sharply curved silver crescent moon, a shamshir.

From the air about him, it was clear he possessed the skill to wield that curved blade freely, and Subaru, out of place as the thought was, found himself thinking that Filore had no eye for picking knights.

"――――"

Through their joined hands, Beatrice's tension reached Subaru, and Subaru's tension reached Beatrice in turn.

Fortunately, thanks to Beatrice being far calmer than he was, they managed to avoid falling into the negative spiral of their mutual tension feeding on itself—a self-inflicted Authority of Wrath, in effect.

—Miklotov's mansion had become a scene of carnage so horrific it made him want to look away.

Entering from the rear of the mansion, they had already found more than ten people connected to the household, but every one of them was already a mutilated corpse, cut down in a single stroke.

Should he call the single blow that spared them suffering an act of mercy? Or should he call the overwhelming force that gave them no chance to fight back merciless? He couldn't think of any final words to dress up their deaths.

He could only feel, up close, the raw, still-fresh breath of death, and his heart pounded as if it might burst.

"――――"

The situation had already exceeded Subaru's capacity to handle. Judging as much, he was certain the better call would be to leave the mansion at once and go seek help and reinforcements.

But at the same time, there was, without question, a chance that by pressing forward now, they might save a life.

Because—

"Every one of them was just killed."

"...Betty agrees, I suppose. If only there were even one person still breathing, I suppose..."

They found another one—a slash mark carved into the corridor wall, and beneath it, a fallen body. A single sweeping blow had cut diagonally through the person and into the wall behind them.

The blood of the body, cut down diagonally across the shoulder in a moment of helpless regret, hadn't dried yet, and its warm, raw stench carried the peculiar odor of death mixed with lingering vitality—the unmistakable smell unique to something that had been alive only moments before.

"Pisses me off that my experience in the Empire is actually coming in handy at a time like this..."

The most horrific, hellish sight in all of Natsuki Subaru's life—troublingly enough—was the carnage of the Sword Slave Island, where so many people he'd come to know had built a mountain of corpses. The disaster brought by the hateful Todd Fang had burned Subaru's world to ash with countless deaths.

He'd seen so much death there. So he could tell. How long ago a living person had become a corpse, and how much they'd suffered before their breath finally stopped.

"They weren't made to suffer."

Inside the mansion, the bodies they'd found now numbered thirteen, and not one of them had been made to suffer. But whether that was the result of mercy, or simply the result of sheer technical skill, there was no way to tell them apart.

The fact that they hadn't died in agony was probably a comfort only to the hearts of those who'd confirmed it themselves. Arrogant—Ferris's old insult crossed his mind even at a time like this.

Why was that? Perhaps it was because he thought that Ferris, who had said he hated death more than anything else in this world, might be able to understand him now, feeling this helplessness in the face of tragedy.

"Damn it."

Having witnessed more deaths than he could count, and still, even now, unable to grow used to the nausea and sense of loss that came with them, Subaru closed the eyes of the dead and pressed onward.

He wasn't backing down, because there was still the possibility that someone had survived this tragedy, one that had only just begun.

Because if he backed away, he didn't want to be the one to snuff out that possibility of life.

And besides—

"—Miklotov is still someone this country desperately needs."

It wasn't as though Subaru had exchanged all that many words with Miklotov.

Nor did he have particularly fond memories of the Council of Wise Men, those who stood at the pinnacle of the kingdom's scholars.

But within the handful of points of contact, and the memories smeared over with negative feelings he'd rather not recall, there was certainly something Subaru remembered.

"He has, at least, shown that he's not the sort of Half-Elf the world fears. —You have a fine attendant."

Those were the words spoken to him at the moment when, through his own selfish recklessness, he'd made a spectacle of himself in front of everyone, when not a single ally remained, and he was driven from the throne room.

He hadn't remembered it, not until this very moment. —But there had been someone who, on that day, in that place, had looked at what Subaru did with different eyes than everyone else.

He hadn't noticed at the time. But so what. Whatever the case, Subaru was grateful.

This feeling of having been saved, worth more than gold—Subaru would repay it to Miklotov.

"—Subaru, Lady Beatrice."

Having steeled himself with firm resolve as the driving force behind his refusal to run, it was Teaga—now arrived before the great door—who called out to Subaru and the Beatrice at his side.

They couldn't say they'd checked every corner of the mansion, but they'd finished confirming the larger rooms. Given the mansion's layout, the room that would serve as a refuge in an emergency was precisely the one now before them.

Therefore, the likelihood of the master of the house being here was highest in this very room—

"—!"

Not with a shout, but right after a nod, they burst through the opened door with Teaga in the lead.

The moment they entered, the first thing that leapt into view was, in the plain room, a very large national flag of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica hung to cover the white wall—depicting two dragons facing each other around a single sword—which had also been cut diagonally, one half of it fallen to the floor.

—And then, with the flag hanging down and wrapped around his body, a single figure who had apparently taken the same blow could be seen collapsed against the wall, sunk in a pool of blood.

"—Ah."

A groan escaped from the back of his throat, unbidden.

The figure slumped against the wall had his bowed head obscured, not clearly visible. But it was unmistakably clear who it was. Even the distinctively long white beard—impossible to mistake for anyone else's—was soaked through with the enormous quantity of blood that had poured out, dyed a deep crimson.

"――――"

It was, now, a figure that would never move again—Miklotov McMahon. The end of the great sage who had long supported the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica had come far too abruptly, far too mercilessly.

But, even so, neither his death nor his final moment was the reason for Subaru's groan.

The reason Subaru had groaned wasn't Miklotov, the victim. —It was the perpetrator.

At the center of the room, facing the diagonally slashed flag and Miklotov's lifeless body wrapped in it, stood the figure of the perpetrator, back turned toward them.

Long, beautifully lustrous green hair swayed against a slender, willowy back. The rich, feminine curves of her body couldn't be fully hidden by her dark navy attire, painting the scene of carnage with a decadent, aesthetic beauty.

And on the long, sharp straight sword gripped in her hand, there wasn't a single drop of blood. —Of course there wasn't. Because her sword strikes cut down her opponents not with steel, but with wind.

"――――"

To Subaru and the others, frozen in stunned silence where they stood, she slowly turned around. One eye covered by an eyepatch, her remaining amber eye reflected them—a beauty in men's clothing—


"—Ah, Natsuki Subaru, is it. —I wonder, are you truly the 'real one' I know?"


—Amid the carnage where blood and death ran rampant, in answer to Crusch Karsten's question, all that escaped from Natsuki Subaru's throat was a pitiful groan.
