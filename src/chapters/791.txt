Arc 10 Chapter 31: "I'm Home, Welcome Back"

—Come to think of it, Flugel too was a man of many mysteries.

The first time Subaru heard the name was, fittingly, when he'd learned about "Flugel's Tree." The story went that aside from planting that one great tree, the man had left behind no particular achievements to speak of — a great figure, supposedly, but Subaru's impression of him amounted to little more than "well, leaving your name in history is impressive enough on its own."

Afterward, circumstances had led them to fell that very tree in order to defeat the White Whale, so Subaru felt a certain guilt about it — but at the same time, he supposed Flugel counted as a benefactor too, deserving thanks for having planted the tree in the first place.

But the next time he heard Flugel's name, things had suddenly taken on a much more suspicious odor — when the Star Watcher of the Pleiades Watchtower, Shaula, had told him that Flugel was her teacher.

Shaula had originally been one of the Three Great Heroes of Lugunica, celebrated as the "Sage" — only for Subaru to learn that her achievements had actually been foisted on her by Flugel. On top of that, Shaula, for whatever reason, kept insisting that Subaru himself was that very teacher of hers. In the end, the truth of the matter had never come to light, and Subaru's impression of Flugel remained a complicated one.

He could call the man a benefactor who'd given them that final push in the fight against the White Whale, or he could call him an outrageously cold-hearted so-and-so who'd shut Shaula up in that watchtower for who-knew-how-long. If he were to say something utterly self-serving, he'd like to insist that if Flugel was going to be like that, the title of Shaula's teacher ought to belong to him instead.

"Man, even I think that's petty of me. ...Still, once you've heard the name Kugelschreiber Castle, and then you hear Flugel on top of it, it starts to feel like a real nod of respect to German..."

If Kugelschreiber was German for "ballpoint pen," then Flugel ought to likewise be German for "wing." There'd been a stretch of time when Subaru, in his fervent quest for words that sounded cool, had washed up on the shores of German. A rite of passage for any teenager, you might say.

"Staubsauger — vacuum cleaner — Zitronensaft — lemon juice — stuff like that. And get this: Schwarz, as in Natsumi Schwarz, also means 'black' in German."

"You can say 'get this' all you like, Betty hasn't the faintest idea what that means, I suppose. ...So, does that mean Betty's family home and Subaru's hometown use the same words?"

"Guess so. Well, I've got a hunch that Hoshin of the Wilderness came from the same hometown as me, so it's not that strange for words like that to be scattered around."

"Even Betty doesn't know the circumstances behind the castle's name, I suppose. But if it's Mother we're talking about, it wouldn't be strange for her to have known just about anyone, I think."

At Beatrice's words — she being carried in his arms — Subaru shifted his hold on her and, with a troubled look, breathed a heavy sigh. "Yeah, guess so."

Now that he thought about it, back at the tea party in the Sanctuary, Echidna — the young Dona, that is — had said something about all sorts of people from all over the world gathering because they wanted knowledge from her, wanted to meet her. At the time, Subaru hadn't had anything in particular he wanted to ask Echidna, so he'd let it slide, but now he was starting to feel like he'd missed out on something.

"Well, it's iffy how much you can trust anything young Dona says, but she seemed to have a certain pride in showing off her knowledge, in her own way. Maybe I should've asked her about the truth of the world or something."

"What would Subaru even do with the truth of the world, I wonder. To begin with, Betty doesn't think the world's been lying about anything, I suppose."

"Yeah, well, fair enough."

He'd only said it on a whim, but truth be told, even if he learned the truth of the world, he'd probably have no idea what to do with it.

What he wanted right now was word of his friends' safety and a way to contact them, plus a means of saving the kingdom, plus some technique for keeping the enemy at bay for good — that was about the extent of it. The truth of the world really didn't have much of a role to play in any of that.

In any case—

"—Who'd have thought a trip to Flugel's Tree would get approved this easily."

Murmuring that, Subaru looked out at what could be seen standing alone in the distance — the very Flugel's Tree they'd been discussing. Or rather, its enormous, enormous stump.

The trunk that had been felled to defeat the White Whale had apparently been hauled away afterward and, since it was there anyway, put to use as sacred, miraculous lumber for the kingdom's benefit. It had probably gone into buildings made from Flugel's Tree now doing good service in various places.

The stump that remained had become a valuable tourist attraction and seemed to draw a fair crowd, so at present, Subaru and the others found themselves waiting for the area to be cleared of people.

"Up till now it was just a big tree nobody so much as glanced at, but really, everyone's so fickle, aren't they~"

The one who called out to them like that was Ferris, equally at loose ends.

Leaning his back against the Dragon Carriage, he'd come to stand beside Subaru and the others as they gazed at the remains of the great tree, narrowing his yellow eyes as he grumbled his take on the whole situation around it. Subaru agreed with him on that score — evening had already passed and night was creeping in from beyond the sky, yet the flow of people never let up.

The stump business, you might say, was an unexpected byproduct of the Subjugation of the White Whale.

"Still, I'm surprised — who'd have thought I'd end up coming to Flugel's Tree with Subaru-kun again."

"Yeah, I never figured this place would get another turn in the spotlight either. If I'd known way back then it was some spot treated as a landmark for the Witches, I'd have proposed a different plan even for the White Whale."

"Oh? Like what, a different plan?"

"Something like, squeezing out enough firepower to rival cutting down the great tree, purely from everyone's wisdom and courage."

"Ahaha, amaaazing. Subaru-kun really is a geniu~s."

Said in a tone that clearly meant not one word of it, Subaru gave a wry smile and eyed his companion sidelong.

He'd already spent two whole months traveling with Ferris, and yet he still hadn't gotten used to the sight of him dressed as a man — even though, purely in terms of hours, he'd spent far longer with him like this than he ever had with him dressed as a woman.

He didn't think it was simply because the dress had suited him that well. — As for why the awkward little knot of feeling lingered on, even Subaru couldn't quite put it into words.

"Ferris, are you really coming along too, I wonder?"

"Hmm~, does Beatrice-chan not want me to?"

"It's not a matter of wanting or not, I suppose. Your power is precious, is it not? If something were to happen to you..."

"Aw, you're worried about me? How sweet~. But there's no need to worry."

Ferris gave a little poke to the nose of Beatrice, cradled in Subaru's arms. When she blinked in surprise, Ferris let the corners of his eyes soften just slightly, and said,

"It's exactly when you're headed somewhere you don't know what'll happen that it's better to have me around. If it's me, even if your head goes flying, I can save you within a second."

"...Betty's a Spirit, so ordinary healing magic doesn't work well on her, I suppose."

"Yeah, I know. That's why I made sure to properly update it, over these past two months."

"—Seriously?"

"Seriously, seriously. — In front of me, not even someone who wants to die gets to die, okay~?"

Whatever it was about that answer, Beatrice, upon hearing it, gave a small swallow, as if her throat had suddenly gone dry. To Subaru, though, it was genuinely reassuring.

In truth, ever since the fighting against the Beloved Children had begun, there'd been a constant, crackling tension in the air, as if a blade were watching them every hour of the day.

"Oh, and there's something I've been meaning to say for a while now..."

"Hm? What, all serious like that?"

"—"

"Ferris?"

Subaru tilted his head at Ferris, who'd brought the subject up himself only to fall silent. For a while after that, Ferris kept opening his lips and closing them again, until finally—

"—You don't need to keep worrying about the whole me-and-Crusch-sama thing."

"Ugh, you noticed, huh."

"'Course I noticed, it was obvious. ...I do feel bad for making you tiptoe around it, though."

"But?"

"I mean, if some rando started running their mouth about you and Emilia-sama, Subaru-kun would flip out too, right? Figured it's the same kind of thing."

At that comparison, Subaru felt like Ferris wasn't exactly "some rando" to him — but that wasn't the point being made here, so he swallowed the objection.

Even so, if there was something he could say, he wanted to say it—

"...Looks like there's movement at the stump. I'll go get ready."

But mercilessly, right as he was about to say what he wanted to say, Ferris noticed movement over by the stump and turned his back. He hurried off toward the Dragon Carriage without another word, and the reason soon became clear — Crusch was coming this way from the stump.

"Geez, that guy..."

"—? Preparations on our end are complete. Has something happened, 'Ashen Star'?"

"Nah, it's nothing, Crusch-san... whoops, my bad. 'Emerald Elegance,' 'Emerald Elegance.'"

He hastily clapped a hand over his mouth in contrition — the codename kept slipping out whenever he let his guard down. At that, Crusch let her lips ease into a small smile and went on, "It's fine."

"So long as there are no ears about, I'll answer to either. I've rather taken a liking to the sound of 'Emerald Elegance,' the name you gave me."

"Well, if the person herself likes it, then good, it wasn't overstepping. If I'd gotten stuck with something like 'Long Tongue,' basically an insult, now that'd clash with how I see you."

"Clash with how you see me..."

"It's some mysterious fixation of Subaru's, I suppose. It's a sickness he gets sometimes, so you needn't mind it. More to the point, Betty gathers the crowd-clearing this time was 'White Sheep''s doing again, was it not?"

"Ah, indeed. As ever, 'White Sheep''s negotiating is a marvel. I wouldn't call it cunning wordplay, exactly — there's no such craftiness to her — but somehow one naturally finds oneself wanting to listen to her."

"That's a kind of moral authority only a fragile-looking beauty is allowed to have, I guess."

Come to think of it, there were plenty of good-looking people around Subaru already, but with Emilia chief among them, hardly any of them really fit the description of "fragile-looking."

"Feels like everyone around me is more the vigorous, full-of-life type of beautiful. Wait, does that make me the most mopey, fragile-looking one...?"

"Hm, I don't understand the source of your anxiety, but should anything happen, feel free to rely on me at any time. Within the reach of my power, I'll swing this sword for you without reservation."

"Right after saying that, a powerful beauty...! If I were the heroine here, that'd be dangerous... ow, ow."

"Honestly, there you go joking around again, I suppose."

Just as his heart was racing at how reliable Crusch was, Beatrice pinched his cheek in punishment. Smiling faintly at the sight of them, Crusch then turned back to face the stump.

And then—

"—To think I'd visit Flugel's Tree with you again."

"Ah—"
"Ah—"

"—? What is it? Did I say something odd?"

Crusch turned an uneasy look on Subaru and Beatrice, who'd voiced their surprise in unison. At that reaction, the two of them shook their heads together, saying "no, no,"

"It's not that, it's just, only a moment ago..."

"That Ferris fellow just finished saying the exact same thing as you, I suppose."

"He said the same thing as I... I see. I suppose he would."

Her eyebrows rose for only an instant before Crusch nodded, seeming to accept it readily.

Naturally enough, if Subaru, Crusch, and Ferris all came to this place, what they'd all have in common was the memory of the fight against the White Whale. — Wilhelm, the central figure of that battle, was also among those who'd vanished from the royal capital, his whereabouts unknown.

"Wilhelm-san's in the same boat as our own missing people, from what I hear. Worried as I am about him, though..."

"No, if it's Wilhelm, he'll see his way through a bit of hardship. If I'm to worry over anyone, it's my father who troubles me far more. Being the anxious sort that he is, I imagine we're causing him no small amount of distress."

"—"

"But my father, too, was a duke in his own right before he passed the headship to me. In this crisis facing the kingdom, I'll have him stand firm with all he has, body and spirit. What we can do is bring this whole affair to an end as swiftly as possible."

"Betty agrees, I suppose. That's what today's plan is for, is it not?"

Crusch, who trusted in the inner strength of her scattered vassals and family, gave a deep nod at Beatrice's words. Subaru, too, felt himself brace anew and nodded back.

"Ah, no need to worry. This place is a lucky spot for us, after all. Any plan we've ever run at Flugel's Tree, we've never once failed."

"That's... heh, only natural, I suppose."

At Subaru's shrugged-off quip, Crusch smiled, the tension easing from her shoulders. Winking at that smile, Subaru swore to himself that this offhand remark of his wouldn't stay just an offhand remark.

Worrying over the safety of their friends was something Subaru and the others shared too, and if the way to resolve that was to save the kingdom, then they'd move to put it in checkmate as soon as humanly possible.

To that end—

"—Here we go: a visit to Bea-ko's family home!"

"Put that way, it makes Betty feel a strange, indescribable itch, I suppose!"

"—For our part, confirming the truth of the information we've obtained comes first. Making contact with Roswaal can wait. However..."

"However?"

"If, right when we finally need to rely on his knowledge, some alternative means turns up instead, it does raise the question of whether maintaining a friendly relationship with Roswaal even has any point."

"Well, you know, they say true friendship doesn't ask for anything in return..."

At Subaru's utterly unconvincing attempt to smooth things over, Russell's face soured openly.

Subaru wanted to believe that human relationships weren't built solely on a tally of costs and benefits, but measuring the values of the head of the Six Tongues by ordinary common sense was no easy task either.

"Especially when the other party's Roswaal, of all people... I have no idea by what standard he even looks at people, so I figure he's got no room to complain about how others see him."

Subaru did think that, for all that, there was a surprisingly human side to Roswaal that was part of his charm — the trouble was the way he'd stride right over that human side with an unshakeable sense of purpose, brazen as anything.

With the whole matter of Roswaal and Russell's friendship tucked to the side of his mind, Subaru went on,

"But is this really okay? We've been holding back on anything that'd draw attention, so we don't get spotted by the enemy, and now we go and clear out a whole tourist spot."

"Of course, it's far from an ideal situation. No matter how much effort we put in, rumors seep out like raindrops. So I'd say we're looking at a single night's gambit."

"One night, huh."

Looking up at the night sky, Subaru clenched his fist at how precarious that bounded stretch of time felt.

Right now, Beatrice was making her way around the stump of the great tree, searching there for the Port she sought — something like a hole in space, connected to the castle by the effect of "Door Crossing." But according to her, several hundred years had passed since she'd last left the castle, and the surroundings of the Port must have changed drastically in that time.

"Even if Bea-ko's the only one who can find the key, it's frustrating having no choice but to leave it entirely to her. Please, don't rush yourself. But, you know, as calmly and quickly as you can..."

"Oi! What're you standing around for! She found it!"

"Seriously!?"

Right as he'd been holding his breath in something like a prayer, Rachins let out a cheer of triumph. Subaru exchanged a glance with Russell and rushed over to where Beatrice had been doing battle with the stump.

The stump, which had looked like a small hill even from a distance, had its outrageous size stand out all the more the closer they got — enough that it made complete sense why the place had become a tourist attraction.

"And with tiny Bea-ko standing next to a stump like that, it looks like a scene straight out of a fairy tale."

"Aren't you full of it, I suppose. Well, it's true that Betty is a mischievous little imp, like that Tinker Bell from the fairy tale Subaru told her about."

"A little imp? I've never once thought of you as anything but a clever, adorable, super-competent Great Spirit. So? So? So?"

"Honestly, aren't you getting a bit too worked up, I suppose! Now, come here, all of you."

Beatrice must have been relieved to have found what she was looking for, yet she didn't let it show, wearing instead an adorably smug look as she beckoned them over — and everyone jogged over to gather around her.

"As expected." "Amazing!" "So it wasn't all just talk and attitude, huh." "...That's amazing~." Praised from all sides like that, Beatrice, thoroughly pleased with herself, gave an "ahem" and cleared her throat.

"Honestly, there was a chance the Port had been set with the great tree as its base point, so Betty figured it was fifty-fifty whether this would even work, I suppose. But it's here safe and sound. Just sleeping, is all."

"Sleeping, is it. In that case, it can be woken, then?"

"Naturally, I suppose. — Betty is Mother's finest masterpiece, after all."

Grinning, Beatrice reached her small hand out toward Subaru.

Taking it as only natural, Subaru took her hand, swept her light little body up into his arms, and stood together with her right where she'd been standing.

And then—

"Subaru, Betty's going to borrow your strength to do the 'Door Crossing,' I suppose."

"Don't be silly. Ever since I brought you along, I'm the one who's been leaning on your strength the whole time!"

"—Here we go, I suppose."

From their joined hands and the bodies pressed together, Subaru's Mana flowed into Beatrice. — Whatever this was, it seemed to be quite a stubborn sleepyhead, and he could feel his stored Mana being consumed all at once.

He hoped it would wake up before Subaru himself wilted and ran dry, but—

"...There's nothing I can do about Mana depletion, so please don't go and die on me, okay?"

"Don't say something so ominous...!"

He shot that back at Ferris, who'd muttered it under his breath, apparently uneasy at how visibly Subaru's Mana was dwindling. — It was right after that.

Suddenly, the stump gave a pulse. — No, it was the space just in front of it that reacted.

"—"

There hadn't actually been any sound.

But everyone present felt it — a great wave of Mana, probably sensed through their Gates.

Before long, a single thread of pale purple light streaked through the space that had reacted, slowly tracing a line through the air, and a drawing of light began to take shape there.

"This is..."

"—I see. A door, is it."

Crusch spoke the conclusion beyond her astonishment, and just as she said, the purple light that had begun from Beatrice's raised hand traced through the air and formed a single door of light there — a Port.

It was, beyond any doubt, a perfect entrance to a hidden dungeon.

"How's its condition?"

"It's stable, I suppose. It just gave me trouble waking it up — once it's awake, it should hold for a while. Even with Betty's hand off it... see, just like this, I suppose."

Matching Beatrice as she drew her hand back, Subaru too took a step backward. Even so, the Port that had appeared before them showed no change, nor did it vanish out from under their feet.

Seeing that, Russell gave a nod and said, "Good," then—

"—'Black Dog.'"

"Yeah, yeah, sure, I get it. Best to leave the dangerous, risky stuff to the guy whose heart won't break even if he keels over, right!"

"It's simply a matter of putting the right man in the right place. You're a piece we'd feel the loss of too, make no mistake."

"Kuhaha! So glad and so sad, it just brings tearsss to my eyes!"

Waving a hand airily and grumbling snide remarks, "Black Dog" stepped forward. He reached his hand toward the Port, moving to fling it open without a moment's hesitation.

To his back, "White Sheep" called out in a voice pitched high with nerves, "'Black Dog'—"

"P-Please be careful."

"Sheesh, everybody's the same... This is that little squirt's first homecoming in what, years, decades, centuries? Don't go bad-mouthing her family home so much!"

A retort was a retort, but unlike the one directed at Russell, the words aimed at "White Sheep" seemed to carry, somehow, a note of consideration for her. Subaru didn't quite understand the relationship between the two of them either, but for all their bickering, it was strange how much they seemed to trust each other.

In any case—

"—"

With everyone watching, "Black Dog" touched the Port — and with a slick, sliding motion, his figure was swallowed into the space beyond. There truly was an entrance, hanging in midair, to some unseen space.

Black Dog's body hadn't punched through to the other side of the Port so much as arrived somewhere connected to it, across the divide of space. And then, before Subaru and the others as they waited with bated breath, some time passed — and slowly, only his hand returned to this side, thumb thrust firmly upward.

"Is that..."

"Ah, looks like the 'all good' sign. It's a gesture from back home, actually."

"You have been getting along with 'Black Dog' lately, 'Ashen Star.'"

"Getting along... I guess so? Well, figuring out he's the kind of guy who won't bite is progress, at least."

He hadn't meant it that way, but sending a hand sign like that made Subaru realize, belatedly, that he'd been treating "Black Dog" like an actual dog, true to his codename. Though a big dog that wild, that wouldn't warm up to just anyone, would absolutely be too dangerous to ever let near Beatrice.

In any case—

"If there's no problem, then very well. — 'White Sheep,' you're next."

"Y-Yes! Um... here I go!"

Prompted by Russell, "White Sheep" hesitated a moment before timidly taking hold of "Black Dog"'s hand, still left hanging out on this side. The instant she did, she was yanked forward with a jerk, and with a shriek of "Uhyaaa," her figure too vanished beyond the Port.

"Tch, why the hell do I gotta go to some castle tied up with a Witch, of all things..."

"Don't grumble, 'Long Tongue.' Your quick wits are something to be relied on. Felt found herself a fine retainer."

"Shut up! I ain't no 'retainer' or whatever! I'm just her tagalong, that's all, a tagalong!"

With that, Rachins — who'd apparently ended up going along with the Royal Selection through sheer circumstance — and Crusch, who seemed utterly fearless, stepped into the Port. Just before following them in, Ferris glanced back this way for a moment, perhaps worried about how drained Subaru had gotten a little while ago.

"I'm fine, right as rain. If anything, you look light enough, I could scoop you up too."

"No thaaanks! ...But tell me right away if anything happens, okay?"

With just that said, Ferris too went through to the other side of the Port. In Subaru's arms, watching him off, Beatrice shifted a little and said,

"Told you so, didn't he, I suppose."

"Yeah, he told me alright. — So, Russell-san, are you coming too?"

Watching off one companion after another as they passed through the Port, Subaru turned to Russell, the last one remaining.

Russell was the head of the Six Tongues, a position with no ready substitute. Subaru had asked assuming there was no way the man could simply head straight into the field himself — but unexpectedly, Russell shook his head.

"If it were up to me, I'd prefer to stick strictly to giving orders, but when it comes to investigating a Witch's research, there'd be no sense in not putting this eye's value to use."

"Ah, the 'Divine Protection of Appraisal,' huh."

"Yes. Where a Witch's research is concerned, one must be prepared for things beyond an ordinary person's comprehension. But even for things I cannot understand, this eye can still judge whether they hold value, and how much."

"I see... Now that you mention it, that really is an indispensable skill for a dungeon crawl."

In hack-and-slash games where you delved into dungeons, it was common enough to bring back unidentified items with no way of telling whether they were valuable until you checked them out back on the surface — but Russell's Divine Protection could judge an item's worth on the spot.

Being spared from grabbing things at random and wearing yourself out for nothing was an enormous relief.

"So I'll leave securing this area to the rest of the personnel and accompany you."

With that, Russell snapped his fingers and sent an intricate hand-signal toward the Dragon Carriage.

It seemed to be some code unique to the Six Tongues, and since Subaru kept catching glimpses of it being used here and there, he'd been trying to pick it up whenever he could, but it wasn't going especially well.

"It would be a problem if it were easy to learn, so that's exactly as it should be. Though someone like 'Black Dog,' who has no intention whatsoever of learning it, is also a problem."

"'Black Dog' seemed to take a liking to Subaru's hand sign just fine, so maybe it's a problem with how it's taught, I suppose. Might be a problem with the teacher, is it not."

"Sheesh, indeed. — Well then, I'll go on ahead."

As if plugging his ears against an inconvenient topic, Russell went through the Port first, crossing over to the other side.

That left only Subaru and Beatrice in front of the stump. They could have simply followed sharply on the heels of everyone who'd gone ahead, but—

"—Four hundred years is asking too much, but how about I wait three hundred or so, until you've got your feelings sorted out?"

"...Honestly, you worry too much, I suppose. I'm only the tiniest bit nervous."

Held in his arms, Beatrice let the slight stiffness ease out of her body and gave a soft, wobbly little smile. Then she stared at the front door of the family home she hadn't returned to in four hundred years, and—

"—Now, I suppose!"
"All right, here we go!"

Building up a pointless amount of momentum, Subaru, holding Beatrice, hopped straight into the Port.

In that instant, there was the faint sensation of passing through a thin curtain of light, and the grassy air of the Lifaus Plains fell away behind them — replaced instead by a cold, dry wind that greeted the two of them.

"—"

Beneath the soles of his feet as he landed, Subaru felt the texture of a stone floor.

Right behind them, the Port connecting to the site of the great tree stood open just as it had on the entrance side, and the six who'd gone in ahead of them stood a short distance away, looking around at their surroundings.

A castle. — This place was, unmistakably, a castle.

The dimly lit interior was illuminated by moonlight slipping in from here and there, the outlines of walls and ceiling rising faintly into view. The air seemed to carry a peculiar weight to it, though whether that was because this was a Witch's home, or the weight of four hundred years of silence, he couldn't say.

"So this is..."

"Kugelschreiber Castle, I suppose."

Murmuring nervously, Subaru gently set Beatrice — who tapped his shoulder — down onto the floor.

On her own two feet, Beatrice walked across the castle floor. The sound of her footsteps echoed through the interior, and the walls and pillars buried in the darkness, the long corridors stretching off to either side, carried the reverberation far into the distance.

As if welcoming the return of a resident, home for the first time in four hundred years.

"—"

Eyes closed, Beatrice took in the air of the castle with the whole of her small body.

Grateful to the others for holding their tongues, out of consideration for Beatrice's feelings, Subaru — standing in for the silent castle — set a hand lightly atop Beatrice's head.

And then—

"Welcome back, Bea-ko."

"—. I'm home, I suppose."

As if in answer to that greeting, the castle's walls suddenly began to give off a faint glow.

Subaru raised a hand to still the others, who instinctively caught their breath, and looked around — the faint light struck him as familiar, and he remembered the burial ground in the Sanctuary glowing the same way inside.

Sure enough, seeing as it called itself the castle of the "Witch of Greed," there seemed to be an unusually large number of things it had in common with that place.

"Still... this place is really run down."

The darkness was pushed back by the light, and the castle's interior was lit up more clearly than the moonlight had managed. — What became visible was, to put it kindly, in no state to receive any guests.

After all, the walls were caved in, as if crushed by some enormous palm, and some of the thick pillars had been forcibly bent partway through. The ceiling had been gouged out in a great swath, and the shattered stone had been compressed together and, inexplicably, rolled up into a ball and left lying about. Of the corridors leading deeper into the castle, one had been crushed halfway through, and the doors, the windows, even the floor, had all failed to hold their proper shape.

"This isn't the kind of run-down you get from just being left alone a long time. Unless whoever lived here was really, seriously bad at tidying up... don't tell me this is the reason Bea-ko left the castle with your mother?"

"That's about right, I suppose. It's true Mother wasn't especially good at tidying up, but this is a different matter entirely, I suppose. — This is the work of the 'Melancholy Warlock,' is it not."

"The... 'Melancholy Warlock'...?"

It was an epithet Subaru had never heard, and the combination of words had an unpleasant ring to it.

At Subaru's furrowed brow, Beatrice gave a wry smile, prefacing it with, "That's exactly the right reaction, I suppose," before going on,

"The 'Melancholy Warlock' Hector... a man who was a longtime enemy of Betty's, and Mother's, and, incidentally, Roswaal's too, I suppose. He was obsessed with Mother, and stalked her endlessly, I suppose."

"Stalked her... A stalker after a Witch, huh. That's a pretty bold guy. So he must've fallen head over heels for your mother?"

"In the sense of a warped kind of love, I suppose so. But it wasn't that sort of falling in love, I suppose. Because Hector was Mother's father, was he not."

"—"

Hearing that, Subaru caught his breath at the unexpected shock. His hand flew to his chest on reflex, his fingers brushing the black orb hanging from his neck, and he found himself clutching it, for no particular reason.

—The "Melancholy Warlock" Hector, father of the "Witch of Greed" Echidna.

"The more I learn, the more this Hector guy sounds like a real piece of work. ...Ah, but wait, when you say Echidna, you mean Bea-ko's Echidna, so that's fine...? No, damn it, this is getting confusing. Dammit, Echidna, of all people...!"

"No need to get all worked up about it, I suppose. Don't worry, Hector... Mother's father isn't around anywhere anymore, I suppose. And not just her father — her mother too, I suppose."

"The way you say that, sounds like there's a story behind her mother too..."

"A Witch, I suppose. — The 'Witch of Vanity,' Pandora, that was Mother's mother, I suppose."

"—"

Saying it as though it were nothing at all, Beatrice set off walking with quick little steps.

Honestly, Subaru was quite thrown by this brand-new piece of information. — That Echidna was the child of a Witch and a Warlock made a certain kind of sense, now that he thought about it.

Come to think of it, Echidna had seemed to hold a particular fixation on the concept of "Witches" during that tea party. Whether that had anything to do with her own origins like this, he couldn't say for certain, but—

"...Guess I really should've talked with you a little more, after all."

Regretting a chance that would never come again, Subaru murmured that to himself.

Leaving Subaru to his thoughts, Beatrice walked on ahead to where an especially large hole had opened in the floor, into which "White Sheep" was timidly peering.

"'White Sheep,' it'd be no laughing matter if you carelessly peeked and fell in, so do be careful, I suppose."

Coming up alongside "White Sheep," Beatrice kicked a piece of stone at her feet down into the hole. — But no sound came of the stone hitting bottom.

At that fact, not only "White Sheep" but everyone else widened their eyes as well, and Beatrice, planting her hands on her hips, turned back to face them all.

"This is Kugelschreiber Castle... a Witch's home, set afloat in the sky of another dimension, I suppose. If you fall, there's absolutely no coming back, so all of you had best watch your step, I suppose."

And so she gave her lecture on how to walk through her family home, for the first time in four hundred years.
