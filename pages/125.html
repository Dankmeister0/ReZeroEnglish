Arc 3 Chapter 48: "I Won't Let It Become Words"

With a creaking groan, the Dragon Carriage kept moving on.

Slumped against the driver's seat, gripping the reins in a hollow pretense of driving, Subaru was exhausted, his consciousness dim and hazy. There was fatigue, there was the toll of his wounds, and above all, there was the enormous mental attrition.

His broken bones and the gash on his forehead had received no proper treatment, and his left shoulder, still dislocated, throbbed with intermittent pain. Inside his mouth, the feel of his cracked teeth was the peak of discomfort, and his clothes — filthy with blood, mud, and urine — pressed the sensation of cold directly onto his bare skin.

—Why had he survived, of all things?

With his consciousness hovering at the edge of awareness, not quite falling into a dream, Subaru thought. Protected by Rem, having lost her, abandoned by Otto, having begged shamelessly for his life, cast aside even by the White Whale, he had crossed the mist-shrouded highway unguarded, and made it through. Beyond it he'd come upon a Dragon Carriage, and now, together with an Earth Dragon that had lost its master, he pressed onward down the road.

Where could this road possibly be leading? Even if it led somewhere, once he arrived, what could he possibly do?

He'd believed, all this time, that he was moving only on the feeling of wanting to protect, wanting to save, wanting to help. But in truth he had only been dressing up in pretty words the things he didn't want to look at. He had realized that he was a lump of flesh made from nothing but self-love, a self that cherished his own life above all else, without limit.

When he'd left Rem behind in front of the White Whale, when he'd ordered Otto to turn back — as Otto argued back, hadn't Subaru, pretending to be broken by the argument, actually felt relief? Against an opponent even something like a Sword Saint couldn't defeat, going back would only mean dying uselessly. That wasn't what Rem wanted either. —So there was no need for him to go back. No need to die.

He wanted to believe he hadn't thought that way. But as a matter of fact, Subaru had begged for his life in front of the White Whale, the very thing he ought to have hated. He had wept and screamed that he didn't want to die, wet himself, crawled and scrabbled about, fled in a panic. In that moment, Rem's safety had not crossed his mind even once. All he had been thinking about, single-mindedly, was how to keep his own ridiculous, pitiful life going.

And as a result, even if he managed to hold on to his life, there was nothing he could do with this life he'd salvaged. He was just breathing, wastefully consuming oxygen. To throw away her life to protect a man like this — what a foolish thing for Rem to have done.

"Even so... the biggest fool is..."

She wasn't anywhere anymore. The merchants who had been traveling with him had also vanished, and Subaru was alone.

The Earth Dragon, without waiting for instructions from its passenger Subaru, went down the road as though it had grasped his intent on its own. The mere fact that there was a road, reasonably paved, was proof that this was not an endless journey to nowhere, but a path meant to arrive somewhere.

Anywhere would do. Take me anywhere at all.

Feeling reckless, Subaru finally let go of the reins and lay down across the driver's seat. Just above his prone line of sight, a cross-shaped dagger was lodged, one that hadn't been removed — a remnant of the attack by Witch Cult believers that Otto must have run into after passing through the fog.

If only, Subaru thought, he too might follow the same fate as Otto and be attacked by the Witch Cult, just like this. Or, if he actually found himself face to face with that scene, would he, too, end up begging for his life the same way he had with the White Whale? Even in front of that man — Petelgeuse.

"Pe-tel...geuse."

Murmuring the name under his breath, Subaru came to realize the hollow emptiness of his own heart. Even calling the name of that madman — who had butchered Rem, mocked Subaru, and was the root of every single evil — no ripple rose in his heart at all. Just a few hours ago, nothing but his endless rage at that man had given Subaru any vitality from the depths of his soul. Hatred had driven his feet forward, fury had lifted his face upward, murderous intent had given Subaru the will to live. —That filthy vitality was now nowhere to be found.

Having lost even his murderous intent, even his hatred, what was he even doing?

"What in the world am I even..."

The wheels of the Dragon Carriage creaked, an awfully shrill sound scraping at his eardrums. Wincing at a sound that felt almost painful, Subaru quietly pulled himself upright. Looking around, he found that at some point the Earth Dragon had stopped walking. Naturally, the Dragon Carriage, which had been following the Earth Dragon's movement, had also stopped advancing, and the now-still scenery was—

"A forest...?"

Pushing through a forest path surrounded by trees, the Dragon Carriage had been moving over bare dirt ground. Some time must have passed since sunrise, because when he glanced up, he could see the sunlight lightly scorching his body. When he became conscious of it, the heat gradually seeped into his body, and—

"—Huh, is that Subaru?"

A vague, high-pitched voice suddenly spoke, and Subaru was startled that it had called his name. Leaning forward, he looked down from the driver's seat at the ground where the voice had come from, and confirmed that several small figures stood in a row there.

"It really is Subaru!" "What happened, Subaru?" "Subaru's all dirty," "Subaru stinks."

They too recognized Subaru, and began pointing at his pitiful state, laughing amongst themselves. But it wasn't the unpleasant kind of laughter meant to mock Subaru — rather, it was the kind only permitted between people who were close,

"You, guys..."

Pointed at by the familiar village children, Subaru's throat trembled. Faces he knew. Faces he'd seen several times these past few days. Faces he had only ever seen twisted in agony and grief, faces that had already stopped moving.

They were the smiling faces of the children who lived in the village near Roswaal Manor.

Lifting his face in a daze, Subaru turned his gaze ahead down the forest path, toward the place beyond the hill. Catching sight of white smoke rising — proof of life — Subaru understood.

—He had finally arrived at the place he had wished for so much, prayed for so much.

Having lost everything, having despaired of everything, having given up thinking there was nothing more he could do — it was exactly then that Subaru had made it in time.

"—Subaru?" "Huh, what's wrong?" "H-hey, that's dangerous!"

The children's voices grew higher, and he could tell they were worried about him. He understood, but his head had already grown too heavy, and he could no longer support his own body.

Like something stretched taut suddenly snapping with a sound, Subaru's consciousness, too, quietly and deeply sank down, as though being torn away from every last anguish.

"Ah, falling—"

—falling, down.

—When Subaru woke up, the first thing he saw was a familiar white ceiling.

The simple ceiling, fitted with nothing but a Crystal Lamp, was a rare thing in this mansion full of over-decorated rooms, and it made him keenly aware of the small-minded commoner sensibility that had led him to choose it for his own room. Beneath his head was a pillow whose hardness and height never did quite suit him no matter how long he used it, and a sheet was pulled loosely up to his shoulders — he understood at once that he was lying in a bed.

No matter how terrible his condition, it was Subaru's constitution to snap fully awake the instant he regained consciousness. He slowly looked around the room, confirming it was the room he'd spent so many nights and days in. And partway through that,

"—Ah."

There was a girl sitting in a chair beside the bed, quietly looking down at a book.

A girl clad in an apron dress done in black, her hair falling to her shoulders adorned with a vivid white frill. Her cute, well-formed profile had earnestly wiped away all emotion, and her somewhat rigid beauty proclaimed her nobility both inside and out.

The instant he noticed her, Subaru shot upright as if bouncing, and grabbed the hand of the girl who hadn't noticed his waking, startling her. At Subaru's sudden action, the girl's eyes went wide, staring fixedly at Subaru's face. Her expression, her attitude, her gestures, her every reaction — all of it was exactly what he'd longed for, and Subaru felt relief,

"—What do you think you're doing, touching me so casually, Barusu?"

Cold and curt — at the sensation of the hand shaking free of his, at that tone of voice, his fantasy shattered. The reunion with the girl he'd thought he'd lost was driven home as fiction the very instant he noticed the hair of the girl in front of him was pink. The twin elder sister of the girl Subaru had wanted to see, identical to her in every way except the color of her hair.

"I understand you're happy to see Ram again after several days, but pouncing on instinct like that is more animal than manly. Beast."

Casting a scornful look his way, Ram moved her chair back away from the bed. In the chill of her gaze and her words, he could feel a clear difference from the younger sister who looked so alike in appearance alone. Rather than the reunion he'd longed for, Subaru was made to realize his own arrogance anew. Hadn't it been himself who had thrown away even the right to wish for such a thing?

Falling silent, biting his lip, and hanging his head, Subaru drew a puzzled frown from Ram. To her, what had just happened was no more than a level of banter equal to a mere waking greeting. It was the kind of opening exchange that, if this were the usual Subaru, would have been met with a loud, exaggerated reaction. And yet Subaru, far from playing along, had gone silent with a grave expression.

"Hmph. —I'd rather you not make Ram do something so out of character."

As she said this, Ram leaned in close, her outstretched hand pressing down on his head from above. Then, sliding her fingers into Subaru's short black hair, she began slowly stroking it.

Quiet, tracing a gentle rhythm, easing Subaru's heartbeat, it was full of mercy and tenderness, and it gave Subaru — who knew the girl called Ram — an unexpected jolt.

"You've got the face of someone thinking something rude, Barusu. Surprised?"

"I am surprised. ...You're the type who'd kick a man while he's down, aren't you."

"There's no maid better at reading the mood than Ram, you know. Tormenting Barusu right now would be far too vicious. I'll set this aside for later, and unload it all at once next time."

"Take that back, you really are the worst."

Responding to Ram, who was watching for her next chance to tease him like a predator eyeing prey, Subaru nevertheless felt through the touch of her fingertips that the tenderness had not vanished. Their mannerisms, their way of speaking — everything about them was different, and yet, after all, they were sisters. That their care shared the same quality came through so clearly that it tightened Subaru's chest.

There was something he had to convey, and it wasn't a matter that could be avoided. No — it was a matter that must not be avoided. At the very least, that much he had to be able to do.

"Ah..."

Uncertain how to say it, uncertain where to even begin, while he hesitated, her fingers left his head, and the reluctance escaped his mouth as a small sound. He hurriedly clapped a hand over his mouth to stifle it, but Ram's eyes caught it and lit with mischief far faster. Watching Subaru's embarrassed expression while that light still lingered in her eyes, she said,

"Wanted more?"

"I'm not a kid. Don't need it..."

Snapping back with a spiteful remark, Ram let out a "hah" through her nose, a wry laugh he hadn't heard in what felt like nearly two weeks.

"Making a face like you're about to cry, like a child, and still talking big. Even the way you're being stubborn is pure toddler."

Leaving Subaru wallowing in sentiment aside, Ram shrugged and spoke down to him from on high. And after watching Subaru's mouth run on for a while,

"Well then, Barusu."

"—Yeah."

Moving her chair to sit facing him, Ram gazed at him from the front. It was a look as if trying to see straight through to his very heart from the depths of his eyes. Naturally, Subaru shifted uncomfortably under it, and Ram, after a beat,

"—Let's have you tell me what happened."

"It was a terrible sight. Just when I thought some unfamiliar Dragon Carriage had come rolling into the village, there was Barusu, half-dead and filthy. When the villagers came to the manor to fetch Ram, at first I thought it was some kind of joke."

Flatly, Ram recounted the course of events from when Subaru had lost consciousness up to being carried into this mansion. She pointed at Subaru, who listened in silence,

"A dislocated shoulder and a cut on your forehead. Your broken ribs have been set, but push yourself and they'll come apart again. Your clothes, filthy with blood and mud, I disposed of. —As for having wet yourself, I'll keep quiet about that in front of Emilia-sama."

"...Ah, well, that's a relief."

Ram, hearing Subaru's hoarse reply, shrugged with a bored look on her face. She had probably meant to use this information as leverage to pile unreasonable demands on Subaru going forward, but with Subaru in this state, that plan wasn't going to work.

Setting aside whatever she'd intended, Subaru confirmed that his injuries had healed just as Ram had pointed out. His left shoulder moved fine, if with a faint ache, and neither his forehead nor his chest retained any sense of discomfort. Thank goodness for healing magic. The problem was,

"The one who healed my wounds... was..."

"Emilia-sama."

Ram said it plainly, exactly the content Subaru had been dreading. Placing a hand on her hip, she prefaced with "It couldn't be helped," then turned a look tinged with worry toward Subaru, who hung his head,

"At first I asked Beatrice-sama, but she refused. She's a difficult person, so I knew that was a risk."

"So... did Emilia say anything?"

"That's something you should discuss with her yourself."

Reaching a hand to his once-dislocated shoulder, Subaru asked timidly, and Ram answered coldly. At the chill of her gaze, Subaru's shoulders trembled, and seeing such a pathetic reaction from him, Ram let out a sigh,

"Ram doesn't know what happened between Emilia-sama and Barusu in the royal capital. I can guess that something happened, but I haven't heard the details. Nor am I interested. Judging by your reaction just now, you've probably done something worthless, as usual."

"That's harsh."

"I'd say it's a fitting assessment. At least, for someone who lacks the courage to bring up a hard-to-broach topic, and instead latches onto a different subject as a welcome excuse to put it off."

Seeing through his psychology completely, Ram gave a small snort. Struck precisely where it hurt, without mercy, Subaru couldn't even manage a retort.

He knew the words she was waiting for. After all, Subaru had come back, and yet that presence wasn't beside him. It was, by rights, a topic that should have come up before anything else. That she hadn't brought it up herself, and was instead waiting for Subaru to speak of his own accord — was that her kindness, or her severity? —Probably, it was a kind severity, or a severe kindness.

He couldn't keep leaning on that forever. Even Subaru had that much sense. Above all, there was so much he had to say. There was. So Subaru drew a breath, let his gaze wander, and forcing down every part of his body that was cowardly trying to buy time, at last, in a rasping breath, he said,

"—Rem died."

The instant he said it, something slipped free from inside Subaru's chest. It was something that had been lodged deep in his chest all this time, a heavy lump, and the moment he spoke those words, it crumbled and lost its shape, falling into the pit of his stomach, and began asserting its presence.

What that lump had actually been — he only understood once he felt the hot trickle running down his cheek.

It was grief, it was regret, it was a plea to a life now lost. Only now, at last, did he acknowledge what until this very moment he had only admitted with lip service; only now did he finally swallow what he could not bear to accept, understanding it as it dissolved into his heart and body.

—It was I who let Rem be lost.

Tears spilled, streaming without end. And then he realized. Finally, he realized. Subaru had let Rem die, over and over. Counting the loop at the mansion before this, this made the fourth time Subaru had let her die, the fourth time he had truly grasped her death as reality. Four times he had let her die before he finally understood. This was the first time Subaru had shed tears for her death, for her sake.

Shedding tears for her sake did not mean Subaru's conduct was forgiven. He had squandered, to no purpose, the time she had made by staking her life; he had groveled and begged for his life before the very one who had killed her, and had fled all this way, even averting his eyes from that fact. Because he could now see his own ugliness, because he could no longer avert his eyes and get away with it — at the very least, for the sake of the one who had devoted herself to a man like this—

"I... couldn't do anything. On the road, there was fog... the White Whale appeared. And Rem tried to get me away... but all the other Dragon Carriages got scattered too, and I was the only one left behind in the fog... and in the end..."

He couldn't gather what he wanted to say. His words, choked with sobs, slurred and stumbled, and the sequence of it all didn't fit together properly. Excuse-like content kept spilling out without end, and it felt as though it were defiling Rem's final moments, so, unable to bear it, Subaru shut his mouth, giving up on speaking under the momentum. He had admitted his guilt. Now he would accept his punishment. For that, he needed to give a proper explanation—

"Who is this 'Rem'?"

————. ——————————. —————————————————.

He didn't understand what had just been said to him.

"Ah, eh, wha... huh?"

Unable to swallow the meaning of Ram's words, Subaru asked back with a blank, foolish voice. What did she mean, "who is Rem"?

But Ram, looking dubiously at Subaru's face, as though it were Subaru who was saying something strange, asked again,

"Who is 'Rem,' Barusu?"

Without so much as a flicker of her brow at her twin sister's name, she asked who this person even was.

"Wh-who, what do you... don't say something stupid...! It's — it's your, your sister's name! Rem! Rem, right? It's Rem. It's Rem."

"Ram's sister..."

Pressing a finger to her lips, Ram closed her eyes as if thinking hard. Just the gesture of her trying to remember was already unbearably maddening for Subaru now. So maddening that he wanted to shout at her, asking what she thought she was doing, and shove his own sister straight into the fog-choked highway this instant. And then, after a moment's silence,

"Ram's sister, Rem. Ah..."

"You remembered!?"

Leaping at the exclamation, Subaru's eyes opened as though he'd seen a ray of light. But Ram gave him a single nod, and then, forming an utterly cold, dismissive look,

"There's no way I could remember a fact that never existed. Ram has always been alone. There's no such thing as a sister."

She declared it flatly, with no hint of suggestive ambiguity whatsoever. At her manner, Subaru shook his head, muttering "That's crazy," and,

"What are you... talking about..."

"—Ram doesn't have a sister."

Her tone wasn't the kind that suggested she was hiding something, or forcing her emotions to freeze over — it was far from any such thing; it was an utterly cold, matter-of-fact statement.

—Inside her, the existence of a twin younger sister truly had vanished, root and all.

"If Rem doesn't exist, then what happened with the commotion in the Witchbeast forest!? Me, and Rem, and you — we took down the Juggernaut..."

"You really are out of your mind, Barusu. It galls me to say it, but exterminating the pack of Juggernauts was half Barusu's contribution, the rest all Ram's effort and Roswaal-sama's power. ...There's no room in there for some estranged sister named 'Rem' to slip in."

Ram stubbornly refused to acknowledge her sister's existence, no matter how Subaru protested. Even regarding the outcome of those repeated days that had happened at the mansion, it seemed she had arrived at a conclusion different from Subaru's own. Subaru, running around fit to vomit blood, advancing with Rem, protected by Rem, going with Ram to the forest to save her when she'd rushed ahead recklessly, burning his soul there at the risk of his life — those events that surely had happened were, within her, painted over in a false shape.

It made no sense. He couldn't understand why she would answer this way at all.

"It's not even a decent joke... even as a nightmare, it's too poorly made..."

"Ram is always serious. If anyone's dreaming, it's you, Barusu."

"Don't screw around with me—!"

Enraged by Ram's utterly unyielding attitude, Subaru flung off the sheet and stood up from the bed. His legs, not yet fully recovered in strength, wobbled beneath him, but the surging fury he couldn't contain lifted his face upward, sparing him the humiliating fate of collapsing.

"Barusu, you shouldn't stand up yet—"

"Shut up! Just — just shut up and watch!"

Ram, seeing Subaru wobble, reached out a hand toward him. Subaru violently shook off that outstretched hand, and stepped forward one careful step at a time, and once he was sure he wouldn't fall, he burst out of the room.

The room Subaru had been laid in was on the second floor, the floor used by the servants — his own personal room. Though many rooms were prepared for servants, in practice only three people actually filled that role in this mansion, so most stood empty, unused. Knowing that every other room on this floor was one of those vacant rooms, Subaru passed by their doors without so much as a glance, as though there were nothing there.

His destination was the stairwell hall, and he hurried, aiming for the upper floor. Along the way,

"You haven't recovered your strength, and if you keep pushing yourself, you'll collapse and be a bother to Ram."

Ram, following behind him, tossed out those words, but Subaru, walking with his shoulders squared in anger, wasn't listening. Taking considerably longer than usual to climb the stairs, Subaru cut straight through the third floor corridor and headed for the room he had visited so many times.

Third floor of the servants' wing, the third room from the west staircase — there, surely, the answer that would prove the feeling Subaru carried was neither a nightmare nor a delusion still remained.

Standing before the room, he gripped the doorknob and shoved it open in one go. No hesitation needed. If he spent time here, he would only be giving his own heart the chance to grow afraid. —There was no need for time to agonize or waver. Without so much as a knock, he pushed into the room, where a simple yet modestly decorated, girlish room ought to have been—

"...No way."

There was nothing.

All that was in the room he'd entered was a bed made up for form's sake, just like the other rooms, and a small desk set in the back of the room. Her room had certainly been simple, but it hadn't been simple in the sense of being like this, a mass of pure characterlessness.

"There's no way..."

Looking around the room, unable to believe it, Subaru burst back out. Ignoring Ram, who stood silently beside the door watching him, he stuck his head into the hallway and counted. Third from the stairwell hall — there was no way he'd miscounted, and to begin with, it was a place he'd visited so many times he didn't even need to count to get there. He could arrive there with his eyes closed. —And yet, why?

"Are you satisfied now, Barusu?"

She must have understood what Subaru had come to confirm. Ram spoke quietly to his profile as he stood there, stunned, unable to find what he sought. Her expression carried a trace of sorrow, and he could tell, more than was usual for her, that she was worried about him. Precisely because he could feel that so keenly, Subaru could not help but understand.

"Rem is... not here..."

"—There was no such person in this mansion."

Shaking her head, Ram looked straight at Subaru, whose eyes had clouded over, and drew a breath before saying, "And,"

"Ram... doesn't have... a sister."

This time, as if delivering a finishing blow, she said it again.

—Subaru had disciplined himself to bear the responsibility he owed for Rem's death.

Rem had thrown away her life for Subaru's sake. That responsibility was his to bear. And this thing too heavy to carry, this thing he would once have thrown aside and fled from, he had finally caught hold of, and was about to take his first step forward—

"I... can't even..."

—Was he saying he didn't even have the right to beg forgiveness?

Everything was pushing Subaru away. Everyone was leaving him behind. The action he'd taken for Emilia's sake had not been accepted by her, and he still hadn't managed to leap across the rift it had created. Rem, who threw everything away for Subaru's sake, met a violent end losing her life each time it repeated. And when he tried to take responsibility for that life, the world stole even that duty away from him. Time, the world, the Witch Cult, the White Whale — every obstacle stood in the way of what Subaru wished for.

Why was it like this? What had he done wrong? Why was the world so cold to him, why did every feeling betray him like this?

That was— That was—

"Barusu, go back to your room."

To Subaru, standing there dazed, thought frozen, simply standing stock-still, Ram spoke. Drawing close, she pushed at Subaru's back, toward the stairs,

"You're tired, so all sorts of confusing things must be coming up. Go back to your room, get in bed, and keep dreaming. Ram has been called for — there's something I have to do. I can't keep tending only to Barusu."

Even faced with Subaru, swallowed up by shock like this, Ram's judgment was harsh toward him. She offered no comforting words about staying by his side — she went off to fulfill her own duty. Giving his back one firm push, Ram turned toward the stairs opposite the ones she'd pushed him toward. Before moving away from there, she turned back once, giving her final instruction: "Go back to your room, and sleep." This time she truly left, and Subaru was left behind in the corridor.

Shuffling along on unsteady feet, he walked in the direction of his own room, just as Ram had told him. Perhaps, as she said, if he lay down on the bed and let his consciousness sink, he could escape this loneliness in which everything now felt utterly despised. It was a bad dream, surely. Getting into bed to have a dream within a dream was a strange notion, but as a way to counter a dream by seeking another dream, it didn't seem so bad.

Above all, if he could just shift his consciousness to sleep, couldn't he at least part ways with this noise churning over and over inside his skull? He could just run away. He'd run, and run, and run and run and run, all the way to here. In the same way, as before, if he just kept fleeing, kept running, kept scrambling away—

"Run... and then what?"

Muttering that, Subaru's feet stopped just short of descending the stairs. Pulling back the foot that had been about to step down toward the lower floor, wanting to flee, he lifted his chin slightly and turned his gaze in the exact opposite direction from going down.

Running away wouldn't fix anything. Once again, Subaru had been about to betray Rem. For what reason had she protected Subaru, let him flee from the White Whale? So that Subaru could accomplish his purpose. Subaru's purpose was to keep the people living in this mansion far from the clutches of the Witch Cult. If he abandoned even that purpose now, throwing his consciousness into escape—

"That would be more cowardly than begging for forgiveness..."

Turning, he set his back to the stairs he'd been about to descend. This time, there was no hesitation in the steps he took forward. Subaru passed by the room of the girl whose very traces of existence had vanished, and headed for the stairs on the same side Ram had disappeared toward. Only from there could he reach the upper floor of the wing where the reason Subaru had come back was waiting.

He climbed the stairs he reached slowly, step by careful step, upward. Climbing the spiral staircase, at the top was a door leading to a single room. As his hand touched the doorknob, Subaru noticed his heartbeat was strangely quiet.

Just now, when he'd stepped into the room of the girl who was lost, his heart had been pounding so hard — now it beat with a terribly quiet rhythm. He couldn't tell whether that was because he'd calmed down, or because his heartbeat had gone past pounding and sunk down heavy instead. But even so,

"Lend me your courage, Rem—"

The instant he spoke that name, a faint strength returned to Subaru's hand. It turned the heavy doorknob that hadn't budged, and pushed open, gently, the door that had seemed so firmly shut. And then, beyond the opened door, the girl who had been facing her desk turned her head to look back at him,

"—Subaru?"

Hearing that silver-bell voice call his name, Subaru closed his eyes. He tasted a feeling that was terribly hard to put into words, one that ran through his chest, through his entire body, and finally, he remembered. —That he had come back in order to hear her voice.

Her silver hair swaying, her skin translucent white, her eyes a deep violet-blue. That fleeting, beautiful girl, a tinge of sorrow coloring her face, rose to her feet — Emilia — and said,

"...Why did you come back?"

—Her voice sounded unbearably weak, and Subaru's attention was seized more by the tone of it than by the content of her words.

Her lips trembling, her eyes holding a light devoid of strength — Emilia. In terms of felt time it had been two weeks, in terms of real-world time roughly a week — that was all that should have separated them, and yet the figure of her looking at Subaru seemed to have grown just a little smaller. She looked thinner than when they'd last parted. Both her voice and her eyes carried a deep tinge of fatigue, enough to make him wonder if she'd been sleeping at all.

Emilia, cornering herself this way, and above all, seemingly cornered by outside interference too. Subaru had an idea of the reason. Precisely because he did, he had come back here to her like this. So,

"Let's go, you shouldn't be here."

Pushing forward, Subaru ignored Emilia's question and held out his hand. At his forceful manner, Emilia's eyes widened slightly, and she drew back just a little, putting distance between them. The gap she'd closed opened again, and Subaru, frowning, watched Emilia shake her head,

"Go where... No, for what?"

"Anywhere that isn't here is fine. If you're asking what for — for your sake, I..."

"That again?"

Emilia said it in a voice tinged with disappointment at Subaru's answer. Her violet-blue eyes glistened faintly, and she glared up at Subaru, who had fallen silent,

"Coming back so suddenly, covered in wounds, worrying everyone. —Weren't you supposed to be getting treatment from Ferris in the royal capital? Why are you here now?"

"A lot happened! I want to explain everything, believe me, but there's no time to spare for that. Please, just listen to me. Let's go, together, away from this mansion—"

"I told you I can't, didn't I? I said I can't trust Subaru like this. ...I said it."

Stubbornly shaking her head, Emilia refused him in a trembling voice. It was a continuation of the exchange they'd had in the antechamber at the royal castle, and above all, a repetition with no progress whatsoever. Subaru's desperate feelings didn't reach her, and Subaru couldn't understand why Emilia wouldn't understand his feelings. Even so,

"If you don't want to, I'll take you by force if I have to. Give it a few days, and you'll see I'm right whether you like it or not. So..."

"Wait, wait, Subaru. What's happened to you? You weren't... you weren't like this. I... Subaru..."

"Just shut up and listen to what I'm telling you!!"

The instant he shouted, Emilia's shoulders gave a small shudder. Her eyes widened in disbelief, and before her, Subaru, still riding the momentum of his shout, breathed hard and glared fiercely at Emilia.

"You can't stay here. You'll regret it. You definitely will. It won't do anyone any good. No one will be saved. I don't want to suffer anymore, I don't want to cry anymore!"

"What are you... talking about? Subaru, I don't understand."

"Shut up! All of you... you just need to do what I tell you! That's how it'll work out! That's how it is! Why won't anyone understand me...!"

Clawing at his hair, Subaru spat out curses, not really at the Emilia in front of him, but venting his resentment at every unreasonable thing he'd ever collided with. Throwing a tantrum, raising his voice, whatever Subaru said must have made no sense at all to Emilia. But for Subaru, this was the only place he had to let it out. Every unreasonable thing Subaru had encountered — only in front of her, the starting point of it all, could this be let out.

Watching Subaru appeal to her in a tearful voice, Emilia lowered her eyes slightly. Then, murmuring a small "I'm sorry,"

"I don't understand what Subaru's saying. I can't understand it for you. I want to understand, but I don't even have the time to do that for you. ...There are so many things pressing on me that I have to do. So..."

"It won't work out."

Cutting her off, Subaru trampled Emilia's feelings with a short retort. Emilia caught her breath faintly, blinking again and again as though unable to accept Subaru's words, filled with nothing but malice. Seeing her like that, Subaru felt as though he were looking at a mirror image of himself. A sympathy born of having every single thing he'd tried to begin have its hope crushed. It brought Subaru a terribly cruel, sweet sensation, and,

"It won't work out. You're no good. You'll fail. There's no way you can do it. Completely hopeless. All talk. You can't save anyone. You won't be saved. Pile up reckless act after reckless act, and you'll see the same number of corpses pile up in turn. —That's your future."

It was a pitch-black, hideous, base, contemptible — pleasure.

Every time a word he spoke, each and every one, made her eardrums tremble, he could feel it — pain in her expression, cracks in her heart, a blade slipping into her feelings. In that instant, all of her was directed at him. Only for that fleeting moment, there was a dark joy in the fact that all of Emilia was being offered up to him.

Her resolve mocked, her determination laughed off, her actions cruelly cut down, her past scorned as meaningless, her future declared pitch-black. Watching Emilia, tossed about by each of these in turn, Subaru—

"Why?"

Emilia murmured, quietly.

Emilia, her face stiffening in anguish at Subaru's heartless words, at the pain of being told her future held nothing but darkness. And yet even then, her violet-blue eyes never once clouded over. Drawn in by that eerie glow, seeing the world reflected there — namely, Subaru himself, as she gazed at him—

"Why is Subaru crying, looking so pained?"

—He realized that even as he wept, he had been wearing a twisted smile.

"————"

He felt everything come rebounding back onto himself. Looking back at the tears he'd shed, at every word he'd used to trample her feelings — of course, there was nothing to it: every one of those words came bearing down on Subaru himself.

His resolve, his determination, his actions, his past, his future — Subaru too had had all of it denied, the same as her.

There was a part of him that felt nothing he did would matter. There was a part of him driven by a sense of obligation that he had to do something. No one could say anymore what it was even for. He didn't know, but if there was one thing he did know, it was,

"I... for me, for Rem, who brought me... no, who carried me all the way here — for Rem's sake too, there are things I have to do..."

"Rem?"

Subaru's halting words. Struggling to form into anything resembling sense, and hearing them, Emilia tilted her head at the word that had suddenly come up. The way that word sounded coming from her mouth was, unmistakably, the way someone speaks a word whose meaning they clearly don't know, and,

"—You too?"

"Huh?"

"Are you, too, going to forget Rem—"

Forgotten even by her own twin sister, every trace that should have existed of her vanished, even the very person for whose sake he had staked his life to come back didn't remember that girl. Then where did her days, her time, her feelings, her way of living, her wishes, disappear to?

That smile, that anger, those tears, that contact, everything that made up that girl — how could any of it just cease to be?

"—Fine, I'll tell you everything."

What came out of his mouth was something that sounded like he'd let go of something.

Emilia's face showed surprise at Subaru's words. Looking up at her face, Subaru reconfirmed the source of the feeling driving him this far. If her, her feelings, were just going to vanish into the distance as things stood,

"Better to lay bare everything, and vomit blood doing it."

He decided. To reveal everything, to speak the truth, to expose the inside of his own heart.

Subaru's eyes changed, and sensing it, Emilia caught her breath. Before her, Subaru placed a hand on his own chest. His heartbeat was fast. It was fear, born of already understanding what would happen as a result of what he was about to do.

It would be pain. Pain enough, even, to drive him mad. His heart would be toyed with as if crushed in a grip, and he'd be given, endlessly, with no telling when it would end, a suffering that wouldn't even let him cry out.

But he thought. He thought, even so. Who cares. What does it matter. What is pain, compared to this suffering?

Not being believed, not being understood. Above all, compared to enduring a suffering like this, where no one seems to remember Rem even existed — pain, however great, was nothing.

—Let it come if it's going to come. I'll give up my heart, if that's what it takes.

"Emilia. I've seen the future. I know things. And the reason is, I'm... I'm a 'Return by D—'"

The instant he spoke, resolved to lay everything bare, the stagnation came after all, just as he'd expected.

Exactly as he'd anticipated, the movement of the world gradually slowed, and at last came to a stop. At once, the scenery began to lose its color, and every sound that had existed until then vanished. The sound of the wind, the sound of breathing, the beat of his heart, receding further and further, never to return. His consciousness left every one of his five senses behind, and Subaru was cut off, isolated from the world.

—And then, so as not to let the isolated Subaru be alone, a palm of mercy he hadn't even asked for slowly began to show itself.

The black mist that had arisen quietly, yet with a gliding speed, formed the shape of an arm. Until now, it had only ever clearly formed the shape of a right arm. But now, chasing after it, a left arm too took shape clearly before Subaru, all the way up to the shoulder.

Both hands closed in on Subaru; the left hand, as ever, as though caressing him fondly. The remaining right hand slid, as if gliding, into his chest, passing through his ribs, arriving at his heart.

Softly, gently, the sensation of his heart being toyed with in that palm sent a chill of horror through his entire body. Unlike before, when it had dealt sharp, sudden agony in one stroke, the black mist, which surely held Subaru's very life in its grip, handled it viciously now, as though trying to break Subaru's resolve through the extremity of terror instead.

The pain that should have come did not arrive, and a quiet fear began to sprout in Subaru's heart. Unlike its behavior up until now, this movement had begun to take on a kind of cunning, as if toying with Subaru. That would surely gouge into Subaru's heart more than ever before, and bring pain in a form different from before as well. At this way of inflicting suffering, so different from what he'd imagined, Subaru nearly let out a scream from a throat that wouldn't move. But his unmoving jaw refused to let it. No matter how much pain, how much fear, how much of the unknown tormented him, he would absolutely not look down.

If he didn't, nothing would be rewarded. If he didn't, nothing would be forgiven. In a world where no one remembered her existence, if Subaru, who bore the responsibility for her death, were to beg forgiveness, it could only be from his own soul.

Let the pain, the suffering, come at him however it liked. In exchange, Subaru would spill out everything he had experienced, and reclaim everything he was meant to gain. With that, this bargaining with the black mist would be over.

To call it forced bravado would be too bleak; to call it a decision would mean losing far too much. But it was, more than ever before, an unshakable resolve for Subaru, one that had never once solidified until now.

—This resolve, this determination, would not be so easily broken.

Glaring at the black mist toying with his heart, Subaru held his breath and braced for the approaching moment. But the mist showed no sign whatsoever of unleashing its malice on him. Precisely because it could act anytime, it could also choose not to act, indefinitely. In a world where time had stagnated, if challenged to a war of attrition, there was nothing to do but fight until the spirit itself wore down. Even in a state now flush with battle-fervor, unthinkable of breaking, eventually it would collapse, swallowed by resignation, and come to wish for an end. —Such thinking was far too naive.

However many hours, however many days, he would endure the hardship. He hadn't died so many times for nothing. If it wasn't going to kill him, then he would endure the pain, however much of it there was. Such was Subaru's resolve—

"—Huh?"

Abruptly, color began to bleed back into the stagnant world. The pain that should have come ended with nothing more than a gesture, vanishing off to who knows where. Left behind, Subaru found sound, color, and time steadily returned to him.

Breathing, heartbeat, the sound of the world starting to move again surrounded Subaru, and 'normalcy' began to swagger back in, as if mocking his stunned bewilderment.

Unable to grasp the black mist's true intent, Subaru blinked over and over. But even doubting whether this was some mistake, even wary that after another blink that stagnant world might be waiting for him again, the color of the world did not change.

Had the black mist, faced with Subaru's unbroken resolve, realized the futility of its own actions? There's no way that could be, he nearly laughed off — and yet, the possibility that his own resolve had summoned an unexpectedly good result kept him from doing so.

Even now, the sensation of the black mist's right hand gently gripping his heart remained in his chest. If that had tightened its grip, by now he'd already be—

"————"

Thinking that far, Subaru suddenly felt a question rise. He clearly remembered the right hand gripping his heart, scheming to break his spirit. But during that time, where had the left hand — the one that had first touched his cheek, the one that had slipped from his awareness — gone—

"—Hah."

Before an answer to that question could come, Emilia, in front of him, murmured something.

Pulled back to reality by her voice, Subaru remembered the continuation of the words he had to speak. Though the unreal stillness, like something out of a dream, had stirred unease in him, considering the effect it might have had on what Subaru had been trying to do, it was nothing but a turn for the better.

The restriction binding this stretch of time — the Curse, one might call it, that forbade explaining the situation — was finally over. To lay everything bare, to share the future he had gained, to take action toward the world everyone wished for, the world that wished for them — this time, it would finally bear fruit—

"Ah."

Suddenly, Emilia's body leaned forward, toward Subaru. He reached out reflexively to catch her. At the warm, soft sensation against his palms, Subaru felt a slight jolt of surprise—

Splat.

"—Huh?"

Splat, splat, splat.

"—Emilia?"

Splat splat splat, splat.

Held in his arms, from Emilia's mouth, blood — in massive amounts — overflowing.

—While the right hand touched Subaru's heart, where had the left hand vanished to?

Her face resting against Subaru's shoulder, Emilia's blood would not stop. The overflowing, tremendous amount of fresh blood dyed Subaru's body a deep red, and made Emilia's body grow light.

"Stop... huh? Wa-, huh?"

Trying to stop the blood pouring out, he tilted her face upward, and her limp, lolling neck, her drooping shoulders, her eyes that had lost their light, told him everything.

—Right now, before Subaru's eyes, Emilia, her life—

"Ohhhhhhhh, ohhhhhh, aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaah——!!!!"

A scream echoed. If screaming, screaming until his throat tore, could let him forget everything, then someone, please, slit his throat right now, rip it open, tear it away.

In his arms, Emilia's body, her strength-less body, grew lighter and lighter. The blood pouring out would not stop. Subaru's body turned red, redder and redder and redder.

—When the right hand touched Subaru's heart, the left hand had been on Emilia's—

His resolve, his determination, his actions, his past, his future, trampled beneath mockery, one after another.

The unshakable resolve, the determination he had only just steeled against being shattered, was smashed to pieces, driving Natsuki Subaru down into the depths of despair.

—The scream stretched on, high and long, and would not fade.

Subaru had finally, finally.

—Killed Emilia.
