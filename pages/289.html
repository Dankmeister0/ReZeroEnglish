Arc 4 Chapter 120: "Permafrost of the Great Elior Forest"

—By now, the place had changed shape so completely it seemed to have forgotten what its original terrain had ever looked like.

Traces of destruction as though some enraged great serpent had thrashed about in a fury. Every tree that had stood in ranks there was cut down and flattened, and more than a few had been torn out by the roots to go tumbling violently through the air. The earth—cracked, gouged, and caved in—was riddled with bottomless holes, trampled and ravaged to the point that it looked ready to plunge headlong into hell at any moment.

All of that destruction was the work of a single figure standing at the center of the site of that overwhelming violence. A man drenched in blood from head to toe, breath ragged, forcing his failing legs to keep him upright. A blasphemer who carried within himself a "Sin" too large for his frame, gaining power at the cost of a life being whittled away. Jous—Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti.

"————"

His breathing was ragged, his face drained of blood well past pale into ashen. And yet, even in that state, Jous had regained some measure of composure compared to how he'd first been. The "Something" that had been rampaging inside his body seemed, for the time being, to have accepted this uncomfortable place as a temporary lodging.

After it had done as it pleased with the inside of his skin and bones to its heart's content, control of the flesh had at last been handed back fully to Jous, and the power that served as his rent had only grown more precise and more powerful.

The Authority he wielded, that overwhelming force of destruction. The power of that "Unseen Hand," invisible to any eye, was immense—it could reach places no hand could reach, touch things that could never be touched, and strike blows against foes it had no business striking. Within the Witch Cult, Jous's power as figurehead of the Moderate Faction fell far short of the war-hardened Radical Faction. Let alone against an opponent like Regulus Corneas, the Sin Archbishop of Greed, whose power was unmatched even among the Witch Cult's foremost fighters.

That Jous had managed, however tenuously, to stand against him and not been turned to mincemeat in an instant was, without question, owed entirely to the Witch Factor he had taken into himself.

Still, the resistance Jous had mounted—quite literally with everything he had—

"How's this... for an answer, Death...!?"

Glaring straight ahead with bloodshot eyes, Jous forced the words out as he raised a trembling arm. A merciless, unrelenting, concentrated storm of violence unleashed by the Unseen Hand. The enemy who had endured that assault without end now stood wreathed in billowing clouds of dust—

"Ahh, that it? We done?"

Regulus poked his face out from the clearing smoke, bored, digging a finger into his ear. Scratching roughly at his ear, he seemed entirely unaffected, as if cut off from the surrounding carnage—as though his figure alone had been pasted onto the scene of destruction as an afterthought.

"After all... that... !"

"Come on now, when are you gonna figure it out already? That we're just not the same. You and me, we're not even playing the same game. It's not even about compatibility or how strong you get—none of that's even the issue. Nobody, and I mean nobody, can beat me, can't even scratch me. Doesn't matter if you swallow a Witch Factor, doesn't matter if you drag along a Dragon or a Sword Saint, it's pointless."

"...And yet, for all that... you've been quite... generous with your time, keeping me company... while I stall, Death."

"That's 'cause I've got no reason to rush after you. Can't you tell just by looking? I'm just tagging along here. Otherwise, why would I bother coming all the way out to a place like this? I'd be back at the mansion, surrounded by my wives, enjoying my own tiny little peace. But, well, I'm starting to get bored of this."

Answering Jous's words flatly, Regulus took a slow step forward. Walking without a hint of unease across the transformed terrain of the forest, he lowered himself until his eyes met Jous's at the same height, then gave a light wave of his raised hand.

The gesture was like swatting away an insect, and Jous braced himself, unsure what was about to happen. Calling out to the thing within himself, he offered blood and flesh to that black, writhing thing to gain power. Just as he drew breath to hurl the surging impulse at Regulus—

Both of Jous's arms tore free from his shoulders and burst apart.

"Wh—ah!?"

"What a boring reaction. If you're going to bother me this much, the least you could do is thrash around in a way that's actually fun to watch—that'd only be polite, wouldn't it? Though I guess expecting that much was a waste of time."

"Aaaghhhhh!!"

The severed arms went flying, scattering blood as they rolled across the ground, and Jous—now missing both arms from the shoulders—rolled his eyes back and screamed. The stumps at both shoulders were ugly, ragged wounds, as if torn away by the fangs of some beast. The right arm was gone from the shoulder; the left, from about the middle of the upper arm.

Jous arched backward, his body convulsing violently under the sheer agony. He frothed blood, his teeth cracking one after another from clenching against the pain. His legs, already on the verge of giving out, buckled at the knees, and he struck the ground forehead-first, his face plunged into despair.

"At the end of the day, all that resolve of yours, that determination, all of it—that's all it ever was. It's the same for everyone, nothing to feel bad about. Not a single person alive can carry more than what fits in their own two hands. You settle for your own tiny little world, get satisfied with it, live looking only at what's inside it. Know your place. And especially now that you've lost the very arms you'd use to carry anything... well, isn't that just self-evident?"

"Aaah! Aaaahh...!"

"Honestly, you know, this really bugs me. Watching me toy with you like this, maybe you think I get off on tormenting people—that I've got some kind of sadistic streak. But that's a huge misunderstanding, and a real insult to who I am as a person. I'm not doing this because I want to. There's nothing left in my life that I do because I want to. I'm fulfilled—in both the good sense and the bad, I don't want to be influenced by anyone. I have no desires. I'm satisfied. You have no right to resent me. All that happened is you stood in front of me while I was just walking along."

The force of the gushing blood weakened, and Jous's screams grew smaller and thinner as well. His ragged, short breaths repeated with a rasping sound, and his body, hunched over and frothing blood, convulsed like an insect on the verge of death.

There was no malice, no hostility whatsoever in the words Regulus spat out. That was simply because there was no reason to attach any emotion to stating what was, to him, plain and simple fact. Regulus genuinely believed it, without any need to hide anything. Jous's desperate, life-or-death effort hadn't affected Regulus Corneas even so much as a breeze ruffling his bangs.

"Honestly, this is kind of a letdown. Summoning me, of all things—I figured there'd be something to it, but... well, nothing's ever not been a letdown for me, but if you're gonna go through the trouble of calling me out, I'd at least like to see something worth the walk."

"My apologies, Bishop Regulus. For all your trouble in coming, that I could not live up to your expectations."

To Regulus, standing over the nearly ruined Jous, Pandora—who had merely watched the fight unfold until now—offered those words. She too stood alone, unmoved from her original spot amid the forest that Jous's Unseen Hand had utterly ravaged. Like Regulus, not the slightest change had come over her appearance. The white cloth wrapping her slender, small frame remained pristine, without a speck of dirt, and her naturally beautiful face bore not a single wound.

"It's not your fault, Pandora-sama. It's just that the forest folk, and these useless Moderates, are all pathetic, every single one of them. Worthless trash without an ounce of ambition. Unlike me—someone so far above the rest that I don't even need to improve any further—they're mediocrities who'd rather give up struggling to live than push forward. Refusing even to fill their own Vessel—coming from me, 'Greed,' that's a shallowness of desire I can't even fathom."

"Not everyone can think the way you do, nor reach the heights you have. You are more special than anyone, and you are content with yourself. You, in your completeness, are wonderful. And they, in their incompleteness, are wonderful too."

"I'm not much for splitting hairs like that. I don't mind hearing praise from you, Pandora-sama, but it's not like I'm looking for compliments either. In the first place, there was no need to bring me or the Black Serpent along at all. You alone, Pandora-sama, could've handled subjugating a forest like this."

Even now, somewhere in this forest, a Witchbeast was spreading a deadly plague. Regulus, his face twisted with disgust at the existence of that hideous malice, had no awareness that, seen through other eyes, he himself was a distortion deserving the very same treatment. At Regulus's words, Pandora tipped her chin down with an "Mm."

"If all we needed was to strip away their resistance, then yes, I alone could have managed it. But that would have missed the point entirely. We never came here to do harm to the people of this forest."

"You bring along that indiscriminate Black Serpent and then say something like that? I'm sure it's true you never meant to cause harm... but you've resigned yourself to accepting the casualties as unavoidable, haven't you?"

"For a noble purpose, it is inevitable that lives will be sacrificed. But even so, never to forget the spirit to defy so cruel a fate—that beauty of heart is not something to be denied."

"You're dodging the point, but basically what you're saying is, you kill people for the sake of your goals. Ha ha ha. I think it's better when things are that simple—easier to understand. Much better than making me twist my brain around thinking about it, wasting my day."

"That resignation of yours—I find it quite admirable, you know."

At the smile Pandora turned on him, sweet enough to melt the heart of whoever it was aimed at, Regulus merely shrugged. Then he turned his gaze back to the collapsed Jous and started walking, meaning to finish off the man who, left as he was, would surely not escape death.

"Well, I guess it's not like you'd actually die even if that body did, but it'd be easier to manage things if I just yanked the contents out and pinned it down by the scruff of the neck. Kind of a weird thing to say about something with no neck and no head, though..."

Raising his foot to bring it down and crush Jous's head, Regulus moved to do just that—but a voice cut in just before he could.

"Al Hyuma!!"

Following the incantation, the world accepted mana's transmutation, and matter took shape. With the crack of bursting air, a massive block of ice manifested, vast enough to blot out the sky above. Across the whole stretch of sky, now easy to see with the trees knocked flat, a pale, ice-white landmass had formed.

"Ohh... honestly, every single one of you..."

Looking up at the sky, Regulus clicked his tongue at the sight of the icy continent floating directly above his head. An instant later, the enormous mass of ice came crashing down from directly overhead—

"————"

A violent tremor and an inescapable shockwave smashed Regulus's body flat.

At the blast and the earthquake—who could say how many times over by now—the ruined remains of the forest, which could only be called a disaster site, sank further into collapse. Shattered shards of ice scattered everywhere; trees and rocks crushed from directly above; ground buried under overwhelming mass—how many times had the scenery changed on this single day alone?

White fragments of ice danced through the air in a world of dancing light. Through it all, a silver-haired woman dragged a man who had fallen, limp and powerless.

"Jous! Jous, hang on! Like this... oh, what am I supposed to do...!"

"Fo... Fortuna... -sama... is that you... Death...?"

Responding to the voice calling out to him, a faint light returned to the eyes of Jous, hanging on by a thread. The danger to his life was still just as grave, but to Jous, barely clinging to consciousness, the returned Fortuna nodded again and again.

"Yes, yes, it's me. Jous, to see you like this..."

"It's all right, Death... this body of flesh will wither away eventually... I believe... the Fingers who trusted me, who entrusted this to me, surely understand too... more than that, Lady Emilia..."

"I left her with a child I trust, and had her flee the forest. I'm sure... she'll be fine."

"Is that... so... Death... that's... good to hear... Death..."

"—There's nothing good about it, damn it!?"

Regulus's furious voice overlapped with Jous's words as his blood-soaked face relaxed in relief. The ground, buried in ice, exploded apart, and Regulus—having taken the direct hit of that massive projectile—wore an expression of pure rage. He swept back his bangs, his eyes now lit with unmistakable hostility.

"So you come back, and the first thing you do is—who the hell do you think you are, huh? I was just about to stomp him flat! What right, whose permission, do you have to interfere with my—my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my my's—!! Business!! You think you can just—!!"

Screaming as if throwing a tantrum, Regulus crouched and drove both arms into the ground. As he raised them back up, dirt kicked up into the air, and soft soil scattered toward Fortuna and Jous. Though the amount of scattered dirt was nothing much. Like a child flinging sand around in a sandbox—a crude, childish manifestation of anger.

Fortuna looked up at the dirt Regulus was flinging around and, ignoring its effects, immediately began gathering her magical power to counter. But—

"You mustn't!! You have to dodge that dirt—all of it...!"

"Eh...?"

Cutting off Fortuna's incantation, Jous dove headfirst into her, knocking her down. Then, with the last of his strength, he used the "Unseen Hand" to hurl the two of them, sprawled defenseless on the ground, backward.

Rather than intercepting the dirt, rather than defending against it, he chose to send them tumbling across the ground with no chance to break their fall. At that decision of Jous's, Fortuna, for a moment, opened her mouth to ask what he thought he was doing—and then she saw it.

The instant the fragments of dirt and stone Regulus had thrown struck the ground, a sound like heavy rain hammering a rooftop rang out, and countless tiny holes opened up in the earth. Each hole was no bigger than a grain of sand—but their density and penetrating force were the problem.

Most of the mysterious attack had ended up merely gouging the ground, but one fallen tree trunk, barely still holding its shape, had brushed against the edge of that fury. As a result, a trunk about as thick as Fortuna's arms could encircle had been riddled with countless tiny holes and, quite literally, blown to smithereens.

It wasn't hard to imagine that a direct hit would have turned a human body into a haze of blood in an instant. And what was most terrifying of all was—

"What are you two dodging for!? You should've just taken it straight on and turned into chunks of meat, food for the bugs. That trash Petelgeuse and that woman too. Here I was thinking I might even add her as my seventy-ninth wife, and you go and pull something this stupid...!"

Once again, Regulus drove his arms into the ground the same way. What was most horrifying of all was that the act which brought about that much destruction cost him no more effort than digging up dirt and throwing it—child's play, nothing more.

Enraged, wielding that force of destruction, Regulus's appearance had not changed one bit even after taking the full brunt of Fortuna's all-out ambush. Abnormal—there was no other word for it. In terms of pure offense and defense, Regulus Corneas wielded a power on a wholly different level. And despite possessing that much power, that power was sealed inside a body housing a selfish, childish spirit. A dangerous existence—like handing the power of a Dragon to an ill-disciplined child who might turn its point on anyone depending on its mood. That was how Fortuna judged the monster before her.

"If you don't like being chunks of meat, I'll just tear off your arms and legs and put you on display! I'll make you regret ever making a fool of me—of 'Greed'!"

"Please wait, Bishop Regulus."

As Regulus was about to unleash another hail of dirt shrapnel on Fortuna and Jous, Pandora's voice called out to stop him from behind. Still with his arms driven into the ground, Regulus turned only his head to look back at her. Fury remained thick on his face, undimmed even toward Pandora, whom he treated as a superior—he showed no sign of standing down.

"...What is it, Pandora-sama. Right now I'm shaking with fury, right down to the bottom of my soul, over having my rights violated. What business could you possibly have with me in that state? What do you think you're doing, stopping me? Choose your words carefully, and answer me right now..."

"Calm your anger, Bishop Regulus. I will not permit you to kill those two here. Looking at those two—does it move nothing in you at all?"

"Does it look to you like what I'm doing right now is 'nothing'? —If I go easy on you just because you're a woman, don't you dare get carried away!"

At Pandora's words, so out of step with Regulus's jealousy, Jous's condition, and Fortuna's resolve, Regulus's fury turned upon her instead. As though forgetting entirely that they were supposed to be comrades, Regulus swung the arm buried in the ground up toward Pandora. A shrapnel-blast of dirt launched into the air, shattering the trees in its path as it bore down on Pandora, who stood there calmly, unflinching. On direct impact, the girl's body burst into countless fragments of blood and flesh.

"...No way."

Fortuna murmured, stunned, at the sight of Pandora—struck defenselessly by the dirt shrapnel and blown to pieces. She had just witnessed her hated enemy die a gruesome death at the hands of infighting among allies. She had been certain Pandora had some trump card that could turn aside even an attack from Regulus, but instead Pandora had become bright red scraps of flesh, scattered across the forest floor, fertilizer for the ravaged earth.

"This is what happens when you talk back to me like an idiot. Why can't any of you just show a normal amount of consideration? Don't get in my way. Don't block my path. Don't put your mouth in my business. Don't oppose what I do. Am I really asking so much? Hey, what do you two think about that?"

Having killed Pandora, Regulus turned back to the pair, his eyes holding a dim, dark light. This was not a moment to simply be glad that one hostile opponent had been reduced from the equation. Even if the enemy had gone from two down to one, if that remaining one was an absolute powerhouse, the balance of the fight hadn't changed at all.

In her ambush, Fortuna had poured the greatest force she could possibly muster into striking Regulus. And having taken it head-on, Regulus hadn't so much as gotten a wrinkle in his clothes, let alone a wound. It was maddening, but Fortuna alone had no way of defeating Regulus. And Jous, too, had been driven into a position dire enough that his body had been destroyed to this extent. Even forcing the near-dead man to his feet again would only turn the fight one-sided.

All that was left was to draw Regulus's fury onto themselves and buy time for their beloved daughter to flee.

"Leave this to me... Fortuna-sama..."

"Jous, you—"

"No matter... how much blood I lose... until every part of this body has died, I can still... move, Death. I'll... buy you time, so... please, Fortuna-sama, run..."

"Don't say such a foolish thing."

Fortuna loosened into a smile at Jous, who was trying to sit up within her arms. Even at a time like this, she was a little surprised—and a little proud—that she could still manage a smile.

"Leave you here and run away myself? If that were the kind of person I was, I never would have come back at all. I came back even after parting ways with Emilia, and now you want me to run and leave you behind—what am I supposed to do with that?"

"But, Death... then why did you come back... I..."

"So that you wouldn't die. And if you were going to die anyway, so that I could be by your side."

Pierced by the gaze of Fortuna's violet-indigo eyes, Jous forced open his blood-clouded eyes. Pulling close the man who, having lost both arms, had become so terribly light, Fortuna spoke to him from a distance close enough to share breath.

"In a world without you, in a forest you never come back to—what would you have me wait for? I am weak. I cannot live through the long stretch of time in which you no longer exist."

"You, weak... you could never be—"

"I am weak. I was only ever putting on a brave face in front of you and Emilia."

Speaking as though something in her had finally come loose, Fortuna raised Jous's body up. Trembling, Jous rose to his feet using Fortuna's body for support, and she leaned close, holding him steady in turn. At the sight of the man and woman standing there as though embracing each other, Regulus's face twisted with pure, undisguised displeasure.

"You two go on and on ignoring my question, and now you're getting all sentimental? What exactly is going on here. What does it even mean. I've shown you the difference in our power, made it clear as day to you, and yet you keep repeating exactly the thing I told you not to do. What is going on inside your heads?"

"What a whiny, tiresome man. You've made your point clear enough with your attitude alone, haven't you? You can go on lecturing all you like, but there's only one answer we have for you."

"Yes... Death..."

Fortuna and Jous exchanged a glance, and together they spoke to the raging Regulus.

"—Who cares, idiot."

Their voices overlapped, and Fortuna added a raised middle finger for good measure. Having declared their united defiance, Fortuna and Jous each drew together their inner power. And Regulus, having been thus told off, flushed red with fury.

"...! Fine, then! I'll turn the two of you into a shapeless lump of blood and shove you both into the Black Serpent's filthy mouth to—"

"I told you to wait, Bishop Regulus."

The third interruption, all told, of Regulus Corneas's will. Pandora's arm, descending from above, pressed down on Regulus's head, and his body sank into the ground without resistance. Buried instantly from the tips of his toes up to his chin, Regulus looked straight up from below at Pandora, who landed beside him.

"Again, and again, and again...!"

"If it becomes necessary to break your will, then I will do so. At this point, the objective for which we brought you here has essentially been accomplished. You may return home now, if you please."

"You bring me all this way and now that you're satisfied, you tell me to just go home? You think anyone's going to be satisfied with that kind of talk? Until this irritation of mine settles, until I'm back to being myself, I absolutely will not—"

"Is that so. Then, on my end—'Bishop Regulus could not possibly be here. He is at his own mansion, spending his time surrounded by his wives.'"

"Wai—"

The next instant, Regulus, who had been about to shout something, vanished without a trace. It wasn't that he had simply sunk fully into the buried earth. He had truly vanished from the spot, without a trace. Where he had been, there remained no sign that his body had ever been embedded there at all. As though affirming Pandora's own words that he "could not possibly be here."

"Now that the noisy one has left, we can talk at leisure."

"...Before that, may I ask just one thing? Why are you here? I saw it with my own eyes—I was certain I watched you die."

Pandora stood there as though it were the most natural thing in the world. The woman with the gentle smile should have been scattered into pieces of flesh, that very smile with it. Turning her gaze toward the spot where the remains should have been strewn, Fortuna caught her breath faintly. At the point where the dark red fragments of flesh should have been scattered, there was no trace of it whatsoever. Just like the vanished Regulus, her corpse too had disappeared.

To Fortuna, at a loss for words, Pandora tilted her head.

"Might it be... that you simply 'misremember' what you saw?"

"—!"

Fortuna went cold at Pandora's manner of speaking. It shouldn't have been possible, and yet the world was reshaping itself to prove Pandora's words true. An abnormal situation in which the scene she was certain she had witnessed was being denied and overwritten with an unfamiliar one.

The corpse had vanished, Pandora had revived. Regulus had vanished, and every trace of his having been there vanished with him. The instant she realized this, Fortuna glanced beside her and very nearly screamed at an even more astonishing sight.

Jous, standing beside her—the arms that should have been torn off and blown away were back where they belonged.

"With Bishop Regulus not here, the results of Bishop Regulus's actions cease to exist as well. It's really quite simple. Though, healing Bishop Petelgeuse's wounds was my own act of goodwill."

Pandora explained calmly to Fortuna and Jous, their throats seizing. Jous rotated his restored arms as if to confirm they were real, and Fortuna, trembling, turned her eyes to him.

"Ju-Jous, that arm..."

"It moves, safely, it seems, Death. My body, too... aside from what's inside it, is unharmed."

"I haven't rewritten as far as your having taken in the Factor. I wish to commend you for that act of yours, and for the act of the woman who came back seeking you. Consider this my sincerity, offered as proof of that."

Pandora continued speaking in a tone completely unchanged, despite the plainly abnormal circumstances. To Fortuna, Pandora's very existence was an object of hatred. That hadn't changed, and she was still someone whose presence alone made it impossible to restrain her anger the instant she laid eyes on her. And yet, she had never imagined the woman would prove to be this unfathomable an opponent.

She couldn't even guess at what had been done. She couldn't understand what was happening. Everything that had occurred in this forest today surpassed Fortuna's imagination entirely. The one thing she did understand was that, as a result of all this incomprehensible madness, everything was on the verge of coming to an end.

"Fortuna-sama, please, stay with me!"

At the relentless barrage of shocks, Fortuna's thoughts, which had been on the verge of stopping, were jolted back by a sharp cry. Blinking at the sting of a slap on her cheek, she found Jous right in front of her, looking at her. He gripped both her shoulders.

"You must have questions. You must be bewildered as well. But, Death, that will have to wait for now. What matters is protecting the forest, protecting Lady Emilia! And... that can be achieved by defeating that woman, Death!"

"—Jous."

At his cry, Fortuna's eyes regained their focus, and she glared at Pandora.

Yes—he was right. She was, without question, an unfathomable opponent, and there was a real terror in not knowing what she might do. And yet, to push her own agenda through, Pandora had made the fighting force that was Regulus vanish from this place, and restored the arm Jous had lost. It was a foolish act—cutting down her own side's strength while restoring her opponent's. Perhaps she wasn't even aware she'd cornered herself by doing so.

"Jous is right. What happened doesn't matter right now. Right now!"

"We join our strength and bring her down, Death! If we can only drive her back, the rest of the Witch Cult in this forest should retreat as well. —We can save Lady Emilia, Death!"

At Jous's words, the image of her beloved daughter flitted through Fortuna's mind. She had steeled herself, believing this might be their final parting in this life. In fact, up until just now, she had intended to die fighting with that very resolve. But now, at last, a new hope had come into view.

Save Emilia. By no one's hand but her own and Jous's.

"—Frozen white, the frost hand that seals even the wavering of time itself."

The magical power that had been meant to strike Regulus still swirled within Fortuna's body, seeking a place to explode. Given form, given a target, through the incantation, mana intervened in the world.

With the sound of the world itself cracking, what came into being was a single pillar of ice, its point ground to a sharp edge—a spear of ice, the kind it would take several giants working together to lift. Its tip was leveled at Pandora. If the launched ice-spear struck true, the girl's body would be scattered to pieces, all of it frozen solid, leaving no room even for revival.

Beside Fortuna, pressure surged from Jous as well, his arms wrapped around his own shoulders. Beneath his tattered priestly robes, power raged out of control, and blood flowed anew from every wound save the restored arms. Even reduced to so pitiful a sight, the man burned his very soul for the sake of what he believed in.

And before the manifestation of the two of them's power, Pandora, without even bothering to brace herself, smiled.

"Come, then. —Let me embrace it, let me savor it, all the way to the very end of that resolve of yours."

To tear that smile apart, the power of the two of them shook the world. And then—.

Waking in a hollow of the ground, Emilia shook her head, and as she looked around, glancing this way and that to figure out where she was, the memories came flooding back.

"That's right... I..."

Herself, covered in mud, and a landscape she didn't recognize. Scraped knees, and legs aching from running too much. All of it pressed down on Emilia now that her consciousness had returned, a suffocating urgency squeezing her chest, forcing her to realize that the memories flooding back were neither lies nor a dream.

"Mother... Jous... Archi..."

The precious people who had done everything they could to get her away to safety. Their faces came back to her one by one, and Emilia remembered what it was she had to do. Run, they'd told her—everyone who had tried to protect her had said it. Run straight ahead, don't look back, get out of this forest.

And yet, Emilia was sure she'd also been thinking this— that there must be something she could do too, for everyone's sake.

"Th-that's it... the Seal... the Seal!"

The word "seal" dragged out of Emilia's memory, just before it would have slipped away into the hollow. The words Fortuna had exchanged with Archi, her expression stern. The talk of the frightening people who had come to the forest, aiming for the forest's Seal.

The forest's Seal was a strange door, deep, deep in the depths beyond the depths of the settlement where Emilia and the others lived. A door like iron, standing alone in the middle of the forest, connected to nowhere at all. Emilia knew where that place was—the one the adults called "the Seal."

"I have to go."

Not that there was much a small child like Emilia could actually do, even if she raced there. Emilia didn't know how to open the door, and honestly didn't even really understand what the word "seal" meant in the first place. All she knew was that something incredibly important was there, and that she was the one who knew where it was—and for Emilia right now, that alone was enough.

It wasn't what she could do that drove the small girl's body forward. Surely, it was the hope that something would change if only she got there—that was what pushed the girl onward.

"To where the Seal is... but, which way...?"

She had cried herself hoarse parting from Jous, cried and clung to Fortuna as she was carried, been carried by Archi as he tore through the forest, and then run alone, straight ahead, through unfamiliar ground. This place was no longer, even though it was the same forest Emilia lived in, the forest Emilia knew. The area she could run around like her own garden extended, at most, only to the settlement's immediate surroundings. Never mind the location of the Seal—right now, Emilia had no idea where to find even her separated mother or Jous.

"Uh, hh...!"

At her own helplessness and powerlessness, a sob welled up in young Emilia's throat. She had found the thing she needed to do, but she had no power to do it. Her mother, the one she'd turn to and cling to whenever she was in trouble, was not here. It was for that very mother's sake, no one else's, that she had to act on her own now.

"—Hm?"

Emilia's single-minded, desperate feeling moved a supernatural presence watching over her. Wiping away the overflowing tears with her hand, Emilia blinked as a faint light suddenly passed in front of her face. Looking up, she saw several points of light crossing her field of view.

"Fairies...?"

The beings Emilia called fairies, and Fortuna and Jous called Spirits—beings beyond the ordinary. The minor spirits, who supposedly held neither words nor will, answered the desperate wish of a small girl.

Before the frozen, astonished Emilia, the minor spirits began circling as if dancing, then repeatedly moved back and forth as though indicating a direction. Seeing this, Emilia's voice trembled as she grasped their intent.

"Are you... telling me something?"

There was no answer. But the minor spirits swayed their light up and down, as though affirming the question.

"If I go that way, will the Seal be there? Can I... save Mother and everyone...?"

The minor spirits blinked their light with vigor. Watching that pale, bluish light before her, Emilia wiped her tears with her sleeve and shook her head.

There was no time to keep crying and sniffling here forever. She'd been helped by her mother, by Jous, by so many people, and had cried and cried—and now even the fairies were cheering her on. She couldn't forgive herself for staying huddled here any longer.

"Okay... okay, okay."

The minor spirits swayed as if checking on her state. Nodding back at them, Emilia shook her small body and started running. Following the minor spirits' lead, she kicked desperately across the ravaged ground.

She climbed out of the hollow, scrambled up the slope, made her small body even smaller to slip through narrow gaps between trees. Even where the minor spirits could pass through freely, there were many paths Emilia could not take. She stumbled, had her cheek scratched by branches, fell and bit into dirt, spat it out, and got back up.

Her breath grew ragged, and from the pain and the fear, tears welled up again. She sniffled, wiped her tears with a mud-caked sleeve, slapped at her scraped knee, and ran.

Enduring the pain, enduring the exhaustion, running with all her might, memories rose up in Emilia's mind.

For as long as she could remember, Emilia had spent her time in this forest, in this settlement. Fortuna was a strict mother, one who never spoiled Emilia. I am not your real mother. Your true parents exist, properly, somewhere. That refrain, repeated like a habit, Emilia had believed while also not believing. That her true parents existed—that made her happy. But Fortuna, too, was a true mother. To Emilia, that was an unshakable truth. And today's events had made her understand that, truly, for the first time.

She remembered the days she'd been scolded. She remembered the night Fortuna had held her and slept beside her as she cried and apologized. She knew that Fortuna had kept stroking her head in bed, the whole time until she woke, so that Emilia wouldn't feel lonely upon waking. Emilia knew better than anyone that she had been loved by her mother.

Everyone in the settlement had been kind to Emilia. There had always been a lingering sense of isolation, of being kept at a slight distance, as if no one quite knew how to treat her. And yet, none of them had ever spoken words that would hurt Emilia, and they had all always been kind to Fortuna, who protected her. Even that princess's room—Emilia knew that everyone had gone to great lengths to make it comfortable for her. So that Emilia, cooped up inside, wouldn't be lonely, they'd prepared toys for her, made her countless hand-sewn dolls. The dolls, increasing day by day, had long since grown to more than could be counted even using all the fingers and toes on Emilia's hands and feet. The number of those dolls, each thread spun into them, was proof of the consideration they had shown Emilia.

At first, Emilia had absolutely despised Jous. Because it was always when Jous and the others came that she was kept away from everyone and shut up in the princess's room. The adults were surely up to something fun without her, hiding it from her. The first time she'd snuck out of the princess's room and witnessed Jous and Fortuna together, seeing Fortuna laughing, Emilia had felt a jealousy toward Jous. She'd thought she would never forgive him. But he, upon seeing Emilia, whom he'd met by chance, had wept. He cried and cried, cried tears of joy and clung to her, and Emilia forgave him. Because those tears had been warm. Remembering the comfort of being held by Fortuna, Emilia had stroked Jous's head. She stayed by his side so that he, weeping tears of joy, wouldn't feel lonely once he stopped crying. Well, I suppose it can't be helped, she'd thought. It really couldn't be helped, she'd thought.

"I... want to be with everyone again... !"

She wanted to sleep in the same bed with Fortuna again. This time, she wanted to invite everyone into the princess's room. And Jous, who had so cheekily tried to protect her—this time, she'd stomp on his foot for real.

So, she wanted to see everyone again.

"I'll be a good girl..."

And then, running through a vision blurred with tears, ducking under branch after branch and passing through—Emilia found the Seal door she had been searching for, and—

"Welcome. Come on in."

A girl with platinum-blonde hair stood before the door, arms spread wide as if to welcome Emilia.

"I'm so glad. That you came to me. It was fortunate I managed to find the Seal itself, but I couldn't figure out where the key was. Still, I'm relieved it was found safely."

"Why... are you here...?"

At the strange, overwhelming pressure of the girl greeting her so amiably—Pandora—Emilia's throat trembled as she forced out the question. Pandora, in response, gave a small clap.

"Fufu, you're surprised, aren't you? It's a simple thing, really. This Seal is something very important to me. That's why I've been searching, all this time, for where it might be. That's the reason I came to this forest today, too. So, my being here is simply inevitable."

"…………"

Pandora's answer wasn't the answer Emilia had wanted. What Emilia wanted to know was how Pandora had come to be here at all. The last time Emilia had seen her, she'd been together with Regulus, and Jous had been blocking their path. If she was here now, that meant Jous's desperate, body-and-soul sacrifice had—

"Why... are you here...?"

So, not wanting to understand what that meant, Emilia repeated the same question. Perhaps noticing that Emilia's heart looked ready to shatter, Pandora widened her eyes, then placed a hand on her chest as if reflecting on what she'd said.

"I'm sorry. I gave you a strange answer, didn't I. What you wanted to ask about wasn't me, but Bishop Petelgeuse, and your mother."

"...!"

Pandora's belated understanding arrived, nonetheless, at the correct conclusion. If only she had kept misunderstanding, at least Emilia could have avoided knowing the answer to her own question. Even as she herself was starting to lose track of what she was even asking for.

Faced with Emilia's inner turmoil, Pandora smiled gently. That smile held no malice, no venom whatsoever—only a consideration overflowing with the wish to genuinely ease Emilia's worry.

"Please, don't worry. Both Bishop Petelgeuse and your mother, whom you're so concerned about, are safe and unharmed."

"Eh...?"

"There's no need to look so anxious—you could have simply asked from the start. Neither my followers nor I came to this forest to do any of you harm. As I said before, we came here for business regarding this Seal. So we're not so foolish as to create needless sacrifices."

Pandora's words, spoken gently as though comforting her, settled down into Emilia's overwhelmed heart. If her words were to be believed, then Fortuna and Jous were safe. Perhaps no one in the forest had suffered the terrible things Emilia had imagined. What's more, she had just said she had business with this Seal. Meaning, once that business was finished—

"Once you're done with the Seal, will you go home?"

"…………"

"Once your business with the Seal is finished, will you leave the forest? Will you go home without doing anything terrible to everyone...?"

"—Yes, of course. Needless sacrifice is not what I wish for either."

At Emilia's clumsy plea, Pandora nodded deeply, as if making a promise. Then Pandora pointed at the sealed door and tilted her head at Emilia, who looked on the verge of tears.

"So, please, hand over the key. Once this sealed door is opened, we will withdraw from this forest at once."

"Key...?"

"That's right. A key. This seal, being shaped like a door, cannot be opened without one. And that key should be in your possession."

"I don't know anything about that..."

Emilia shook her head at Pandora's assertion. In truth, it was a claim she had no memory of. Emilia had no recollection of anyone giving her anything resembling a key, and to begin with, this very Seal had been kept a secret from her within the village. There was no way Emilia, who had been kept in the dark about the Seal, could be holding its key. It was an obvious conclusion, needing no further thought.

Having no idea what she was talking about, Emilia shook her head side to side. At Emilia's answer, Pandora, too, slowly shook her head.

"There's no need to hide it, you know."

"Th-that's not it...! Really, I really don't know! I don't have a key, no one's ever given me one! I can't even open this Seal!"

"I see. —Then, I suppose we'll have to dig up the entire forest to find the key."

At Emilia's answer, Pandora's face took on a deeply pained expression, and she lowered her eyes. That attitude and manner of speaking, while sympathetic toward Emilia, carried within it a firm resolve that whatever she said, she would absolutely carry it out, and Emilia shuddered.

If the sealed door wasn't opened here and now, this girl would dig up the entire forest. "Dig up" was far too gentle a euphemism. Pandora would "dig up," root and all, the forest itself, and everyone living in it—her mother, everyone in the settlement, Jous and the others. Even young Emilia could clearly tell that Pandora possessed the power to do exactly that.

This was an abnormal existence. So much so that even Fortuna, who to Emilia was the very symbol of strength, could surely not stand against her.

"I—I'll open it! I'll open it!"

So Emilia raised her voice, to keep Pandora from acting on her threat. Hearing Emilia's answer, Pandora's face brightened at once.

"Really? I'm so glad. So the key really was with you after all. I thought as much. After all, you're clearly the Witch's daughter, aren't you."

"The... Witch's...?"

"Yes, that's right. Now then, please, the Seal. Once the inside of the door has been examined, we'll withdraw at once."

Yielding the way to Emilia, Pandora waited for her to act, her face full of joy. Even with her heart shaken by the word Pandora had spoken, Emilia—unable to back down now—stepped forward. The sealed door was so tall that small Emilia, looking up and up, still couldn't see the top. Before a door massive enough to have been built for a giant even greater than a Giant to pass through, that this tiny girl should have to open it seemed like something out of a fairy tale.

"…………"

She stood before the door. Having done so, Emilia still had no idea whatsoever how to open it. When she had confirmed the location of the Seal before, Emilia had already tried every approach she could think of on this door. Pushing, pulling, trying to climb it—she'd already done all of that, long ago. The ancient door hadn't budged even slightly for tiny Emilia's small body; far from opening, it hadn't even so much as creaked, remaining as serenely composed as ever.

Today was no different. Reaching out and touching it produced not the faintest sign of movement.

"Hah... haah... hah... ah."

Her heartbeat quickened abnormally, the sound of blood rushing through her head ringing on and on. Her chest burned hot, her pounding heart feeling as if it might leap right out of her mouth. And yet her hands and feet had gone cold, the tips of her limbs heavy as if stuffed with lead.

She had to move, but she couldn't. If she couldn't open this, everyone would be in terrible danger.

She knew that, and yet she could do nothing. Her mind went white with fear and despair, and the very existence that was Emilia was painted over, erased.

"—Think of yourself as the key."

That voice slipped very smoothly into the ear of Emilia, who was desperate for something to cling to.

—I am the key.

Just as the voice commanded, Emilia's heart traced out that single thought. The instant it did, Emilia felt a weight in the palm resting against the door. She looked at her hand. There, she saw a large, old silver key, gripped firmly.

"A key..."

"Has it become visible to you? If so, then indeed, you are the key."

Catching Emilia's murmur, Pandora pointed it out with delight. And yet there was something faintly unnatural about her words. It was as if Pandora herself could not see the key now in Emilia's hand.

"You... can't see this...?"

"—. No, I cannot see it. That key can only be entrusted to the hand of one who is qualified. There are, I imagine, only two people in this world who could open that lock."

Pandora seemed to envy it. Her gaze was certainly directed at Emilia's hand, and yet she couldn't see the key. Emilia had no idea what it meant that she couldn't see this key, one heavy enough to feel its weight, but still holding it in hand, she turned back toward the door.

The manifested key—but there was no sign of anything resembling a keyhole on the door. The door didn't even have a handle. Even calling it a "large" key, it was far, far smaller compared to this enormous door. Could a door really be opened with such a paltry-looking key?

"—Ah."

And yet, Emilia instinctively understood how to use the key. There was no need to look for a keyhole. This door itself was, in essence, the keyhole.

This door was not the thing that carried out the sealing. It was merely a lid placed over the seal. This door itself was not what sealed anything. The seal—something far more formless—was being maintained on the inside of this door.

"Now, please, open it."

At Pandora's demand, Emilia caught her breath and stepped forward. Pressing the key in her hand against the door, all she needed was to twist it with the intention of "opening" it, and the door would open. Just by doing that, this door would be relieved of its long, long duty as a seal.

—If I open this door, everyone will be saved.

"...Is something wrong?"

But just as she was about to press the key against the door, Emilia's outstretched hand stopped. Seeing her trembling fingertips freeze, Pandora's brow creased faintly.

Not answering that question, Emilia stared at the key in her hand. If she simply pressed the key to the door now, the seal would open. But—

'Emilia. —Our promise.'

What echoed in Emilia's mind was the word her mother had whispered to her at their parting.

That had been the phrase spoken when they'd made a promise unrelated to this seal. But Emilia remembered. She remembered promising her mother that she would keep her word.

She didn't know anything about the Seal. She wasn't supposed to know. Emilia didn't know this place, and she must not interfere with it.

She had promised Fortuna. And keeping that promise had to come before anything else. Because breaking it would mean betraying the trust placed in her—and that was something she must never do. If she became a bad girl, no one would ever forgive her. She wouldn't be able to forgive herself either.

So, opening the seal was something she must not do.

"I... I can't open it..."

"—Why not?"

"There's a promise... I made a promise. I don't know anything about the Seal. And I can't open it. That's not allowed."

"I see. A promise is an important thing. I think the feeling that makes you want to keep it is a wonderful, important thing. But... even so, it depends on the circumstance."

To Emilia, shaking her head over and over, Pandora met her gaze as if reasoning gently with her. She reached out and touched the silver hair of Emilia, who clutched the key tightly with both hands.

"That promise—was it made with your mother? Your mother is a very fine person. She taught you something correct, something precious. That aspiration is something worth protecting, dearly."

"Th-then..."

"But sometimes, there comes a moment when one must make a decision even if it means breaking a promise. Perhaps it's cruel to demand such a decision of one still so young as you. But fate, in forcing a decision upon us, does not consider the circumstances of the one being tossed about by it. Fate loves those who struggle against it upon its own churning waves, and grants hope according to the result. Which hope is it that you seek?"

"Which... hope..."

At Emilia's faint, cracked voice, Pandora nodded with a "Yes," her smile that of a loving mother. She gently held both her hands out before Emilia's eyes.

"One is the hope of keeping the promise made with your mother, of not opening the seal, of facing us as we are, and of overcoming this hardship all the same."

Raising her right hand, Pandora made a gesture as if holding an invisible hope in it.

"And the other is the hope of breaking the promise made with your mother, opening the seal, granting our wish, and thereby bringing this situation to a close without any further harm."

Raising her left hand, Pandora presented, in the same way, another invisible hope to Emilia.

"————"

Looking at the two hands held out before her, Emilia froze, unable to speak. Even her breathing wouldn't come—her lungs felt as if they had frozen solid. If she carelessly said anything, wouldn't Pandora simply withdraw her hands the instant she did? Of the two hopes offered to her, might not both be snatched away from before her eyes, before she could reach out and touch either one?

—That kind of fear seized the heart of the small girl and would not let go.

"Which hope will you choose? —I leave that to you."

The hope on the right. The hope on the left. The hope chosen by breaking the promise. The hope chosen by keeping it.

Pandora's voice, sweet enough to melt, beckoned to her. Fortuna's voice, gentle and admonishing, called to her.

She could no longer hear the pounding of her own heart, so loud until just moments ago. Sound vanished from the world, and even color drained away, leaving Emilia stranded within it.

She thought. She agonized. Her mind burned, her brain on the verge of boiling over. Every function of her body concentrated in her head, giving her the illusion that everything below her neck had died. As proof, she couldn't hear her own heartbeat, and her limbs, cut off from her own will, wouldn't so much as twitch.

I can't choose, I can't choose, I can't choose I can't choose I can't choose I can't choose I can't choose I can't choose.

Which one would save everyone? What would help everyone? What could she do to be of use to them? What should she do—someone, please, tell her.

"—Ah."

"I see. So that is your decision."

As her thoughts turned incandescent, her very vision clouding to white, Emilia let out a faint sound. And seeing the answer chosen, Pandora lowered her eyes, framed by long lashes.

—Emilia's hand had touched Pandora's right hand.

The path of not breaking the promise, not opening the seal, and praying for everyone's salvation.

"I promised Mother... I'm keeping the promise... keeping it, so... Mother..."

"To the very end, you trust the words of your mother, who is your compass. That answer too, arrived at through your struggle, is the result your life has led to—let us respect it."

At Emilia, tears spilling one after another, Pandora nodded, her expression full of understanding. She then released Emilia's hand from her own right hand and gazed with a tender eye upon the girl who crumpled to the ground.

Had she wished it, Pandora could have simply forced Emilia's hand, key and all, against the door. Whether that forced act would have allowed Emilia to hold the intention of "opening the door" was another matter entirely, but it can't be denied there was a real chance it would have become the decisive push for Emilia, who had, after all, been seeking someone to push her toward a conclusion. And yet, even knowing this, Pandora had not done so. That much, at least, was something one could genuinely believe about this girl in whom everything else was abnormal. However—

"So then—"

"...Eh?"

"Please, respect my decision as well—to take the measures necessary to open the seal."

At Pandora's words, Emilia lifted a blank face. Pandora before her was not looking at Emilia. Her gaze was fixed behind them. When Emilia, drawn along, turned to look as well, there was a shadow bursting forth, pushing aside the underbrush. It was a woman with short silver hair, and—

"Pandora!!"

Drenched head to toe in blood, it was Fortuna, leaping into view.

"Leave to me—!!"

Voicing a cry with a faintly mad ring to it, a man came into view as though leaping over the trees. A bounding leap along the top of a tall, great tree—arriving on the scene with the force of someone having been thrown—was Jous, in his blood-soaked priestly robes.

"Jous!"

"Fortuna-samaaa!"

Fortuna and Jous called out each other's names, and with that alone, their coordination was complete. With Pandora caught between them at the center of the forest clearing, Fortuna and the others launched their assault from front and back.

Fortuna's left hand gripped tight the right hand of Emilia, which had been trembling helplessly. Emilia looked up at her mother's profile. —Facing straight ahead, piercing the enemy with her gaze, that profile was beautiful enough to make one tremble.

"Al Hyuma!!"

"Unseen Hand!!"

The most powerful spell Fortuna could weave, and the outlawed power of the Witch Factor that Jous drew fully out, at the very last moment. A tremendous force of destruction surged straight forward, and then.

"—Mother?"

Pierced through the chest by the "Unseen Hand," Emilia was drenched, head to toe, in her mother's spurting blood.

The strength drained from the joined hand, and Fortuna's body collapsed to the ground right before Emilia's eyes.

"There—that's it, Death!"

Landing roughly on the ground, Jous cried out and swung his tattered arm hard to the side. As though pulled along by that motion, Fortuna's body, too, sailed through the air on the same trajectory. Limp as a doll, tossed aside to tumble across the ground, Fortuna's convulsing body gushed blood like a geyser, and in an instant, the meadow was dyed crimson.

"There was resistance in that hit, Death... if that was enough, this time for sure..."

Panting hard, Jous dropped to one knee. His eyes still fixed warily on the fallen Fortuna—but Emilia was not looking at him.

"————"

Only, on stumbling feet, she walked and drew close to Fortuna, lying face down. Her mother's body had holes torn through both chest and back, damaged badly enough that the inside could be glimpsed through the ruptured flesh. The force of the bleeding rapidly weakening, Emilia sank down into the pool of blood.

She cradled her mother's pale head, managing to lift it onto her own lap. Fortuna's beautiful silver hair, too, was mottled and soaked with red, and Emilia desperately tried with her fingers to wipe it clean. But Emilia's own fingers were already stained with blood, and the more she touched it, the more Fortuna's hair only grew more soaked with blood.

"Fortuna-sama! Please, keep your guard up, keep watch! I will go confirm—"

"Jous...?"

"————"

Exhaling sharply, Jous forced his heavy body up and turned his palm toward Fortuna. Hearing that voice, Emilia slowly lifted her face and called his name. Hearing his name spoken, Jous, for a moment, looked as though gazing at something far away, then blinked and—

"Lady Emilia?"

He murmured, seeming, for the first time, to notice the small girl sitting in the pool of blood. Then his gaze traveled down, catching sight of the person whose head rested on Emilia's lap, whose body lay flung out limp. His eyes went wide.

"...That's... impossible."

With the look of a man who had just seen something unbelievable, Jous turned to the side. Between himself, having dragged his body over, and the fallen Fortuna and Emilia, stood the platinum-haired girl.

Pandora smiled at Jous as he looked at her.

"It's all right. You simply 'misremembered.'"

"Ah, aaaaah...aaaaaaaaaaaahhh!?"

Bringing both hands to his own face, Jous, without mercy, dug his nails in, carving lines of crimson. A dull sound accompanied the force enough to tear nails away, and the blood shed from his gouged cheek flesh dyed his whole face bright red.

"Impossible impossible impossible impossible impossible!? I—what am I, what am I doing, Death? What have I done, Death? Why, why why why why why why why!? Then what was it all for, this... aaaah! Aaaahh!? Aaaaahhh!!"

Jous, who had taken the Witch Factor into his body and suppressed a Noh far too great for himself through sheer force of will—the most crucial part supporting that iron will snapped, just like that. With a sound, everything inside Jous began to crumble apart.

Having destroyed, with his own hands and with the very power he had gained by risking his life, the very thing he had risked his life to protect— Jous's heart suffered a wound beyond repair, and he screamed, his sanity lost.

"What was I—for the sake of what!?"

"All of it, for love."

Rolling his eyes back, foaming at the mouth, staring up at the heavens, Jous howled. To the cry of that soul, Pandora answered in a quiet voice.

"You offered up even your very soul, to save the one you loved. That is no small feat. All those long, long days you spent supporting the Witch Cult—all of it, for that love. Everything you have done is the fruit of love. What a splendid, guiding light of love."

"Love... love... AI... ai... love...!"

Repeating the whispered words like a delirious mutter, Jous's heart shattered into countless pieces. And with that, the color drained utterly from his eyes, and Jous, reduced to a blank, hollow shell, could no longer move.

Muttering, over and over, words no one could hear—a living corpse. Watching Jous, his heart broken in such a way, Pandora let out a satisfied breath.

"Em... ilia..."

And, at the very same moment that the existence called Jous shattered into fragments, one more flame of life was also on the verge of guttering out.

"Mother."

Called by name in a faint, fading voice, Emilia called back, dazed. Pulling her mother closer with trembling arms, she found that body had become sorrowfully light. At some point, all that blood that had been gushing out had completely stopped.

If that were so, then was her mother's wound all right now? Emilia's youthfulness was not enough to shield her heart from the truth—not to the point of letting her believe such a thing. Fortuna's face, no longer having even the strength to move, wore, to anyone's eyes, the color of a dead person.

"...Big... brother, I'm sorry..."

"Mother."

"I... didn't keep... what I was told... none of it..."

The words came out with the tone of a child apologizing, spoken in regret. Fortuna, her body no longer even bleeding, let only tears spill endlessly from her eyes. Feeling the hot droplets against her fingers, Emilia tried desperately to gather them up. Because to Emilia, it felt as though those tears were all that remained of her mother's life force.

"Sis... ter... you're angry... aren't you... you won't... forgive me... will you...?"

Listening to her mother's delirious murmuring, Emilia finally understood. Fortuna's open, violet-indigo eyes no longer reflected any light at all.

They had become organs that did nothing but shed tears; Fortuna's sight had long since been lost. She couldn't even see Emilia's face. She wasn't even aware that Emilia was right there beside her.

No matter how much she touched her, held her, it wouldn't reach. Before Fortuna, weeping like a child, seeking forgiveness, Emilia—

"—I forgive you, Mother."

"…………"

"Mother is my... Mother, and you always cherished me so much... you loved me as much as any real father or mother could, maybe more..."

"…………"

"So there's nothing to apologize for. Nothing at all. Emilia will always... always love Fortuna-mother. I love you. I love you, I love you... I love you..."

The dam broke. Her voice lost all composure, and tears she could no longer hold back fell one after another onto Fortuna's face.

If tears were life force, then this final miracle was the strength given by Emilia's tears.

"...Mother?"

"Lia."

A hand, slowly reaching out, touched Emilia's cheek. A hand that shouldn't have been able to move stroked Emilia's cheek, her ear, tousled her hair. As if touching something beloved, as if it might break, as if loving it.

"Such a crybaby."

"…………"

"I love you... so, so much..."

The strength went out of her. Her arm fell, making a soft sound as it dropped.

As her cheek was stroked, Emilia felt Fortuna's body grow lighter. Her whole body had gone limp, and though the weight on her lap should have increased, within Emilia's arms, Fortuna's body had, without doubt, grown lighter.

From her mother's body, the most important thing, the thing that must never slip away, had slipped away. That much, Emilia understood clearly, too.

"————"

Her mother, Fortuna, was gone. Jous, Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti, had had his heart broken. And Emilia—

"Now then, are you ready to choose the hope of releasing the seal?"

"————"

Approaching, Pandora spoke to Emilia, who held Fortuna's remains. Watching the seated girl, Pandora waited quietly, her expression gentle, for an answer.

At that manner, Emilia understood.

"Will you... open the Seal?"

"Yes. The mother with whom you made that promise has, regrettably, passed away. There is now no shackle of promise left to bind you. What do you say?"

At Pandora's words, delivering that outrageous, unreasonable logic as though it were simply obvious, Emilia understood. She understood exactly what this demon in human form standing before her had been thinking, to do all of this.

This demon had done all of this in order to make Emilia break her promise. Simply to make Emilia lose sight of the meaning of that promise—for that alone, Pandora had let Fortuna die, shattered Jous's heart, and utterly trampled this forest into ruin.

"Ah, that's right, I nearly forgot."

"…………"

"You no longer need these two, after all, do you."

Beside the unresponsive Emilia's face, Pandora gently extended her hand. At that, a faint light welled up, surrounding Emilia's body, and it chose to make Pandora's arm its resting place, taking her body as its perch.

The minor spirits. The fairies who had guided Emilia, showing her the way to this sealed door. That they should now be here, at Pandora's side—why?

"I didn't think you would come here on your own, so I had them help me. We can't communicate with words, but they've been remarkably reliable."

Smiling, Pandora offered her thanks to the minor spirits, and in response, the minor spirits danced in the air.

Where had it all even begun? Emilia could no longer tell.

"…………"

Swaying her head unsteadily, Emilia looked up at the sealed door. The door, calm and serene, seemed to her to be gazing back at Emilia, awaiting the moment it would finally be opened. In her hand, she felt the heavy weight of the key. The key she'd thought she must have dropped somewhere was, once again, in Emilia's hand.

"So, you still have the key. Then, you understand, don't you?"

Before Pandora, nodding at her, Emilia slowly rose to her feet. She lowered her mother's head from her lap and laid it gently down on the grass. Fingering the strands of hair over her forehead, she carefully arranged her beloved mother's beautiful face.

And then—

"Die."

—A blade of ferocious cold wind howled forth, and Pandora's body was sliced apart, scattering into pieces.

The spurting blood froze instantly, and blossoms of red ice bloomed in wild profusion. With a single ice pillar standing at its center and scattered blood forming petals around it, it was a work of art wrought from death and ice.

"How violent of you. Whatever could have caused such a sudden—"

"Die."

Falling spikes of ice pierced Pandora's arms and legs, an ice lance thrust up from the ground skewered her from hip to head, and the frozen body, wrung out top and bottom, shattered into pieces with a high, ringing sound.

"Please, calm down. If we talk, surely we can understand one another."

"Die."

Blocks of ice closed in from both sides, crushing Pandora's body between them into a haze of blood.

"Let's stop this. You are a girl with a kind heart, someone who could never bring herself to hurt another person. Didn't your mother teach you to be that way?"

"Die."

A spinning blade of ice cut Pandora apart from the feet up, rendering her into a bright red sherbet.

"If your mother saw you like this, she would be so sad. Neither your true father and mother, nor Bishop Petelgeuse, who staked his life—none of them wish for this."

"Diiiie!"

White mist enveloped Pandora's body, transforming it into an ice statue. An instant later, a massive ice sword crashed down, more crushing than cutting, returning Pandora's icy form to the earth.

And yet, despite the storm of destruction and murderous intent unleashed with such fury—

"How troubling. It seems, if anything, this has had the opposite effect."

"Die, die, die, die...!!"

Wailing and swinging her arms, Emilia unleashed one icy blow after another upon Pandora. And yet Pandora took every single one of them, and though she displayed a gruesome death each time, the moment one blinked, she was back, whole and unharmed, chattering away again.

"Hff! Hff! Hff!"

Gradually, it was Emilia—wielding magic far beyond her means—who was approaching her limit. From the repeated casting of magic too great for her small frame, half her flushed face had begun to freeze over. The vast amount of mana taken into her young body had run out of control, and she could no longer release it outward fast enough.

"That the manifestation of power beyond one's own vessel should not even spare her own body from harm—truly, this is a matter of bloodline. The blood of the Witch cannot escape that causality. —Or perhaps, this forest was necessary precisely so that she would not become aware of this power."

Pandora mused aloud. Emilia shook her head, as if denying a sound that couldn't reach her ears. Her right leg had frozen completely solid, making it difficult even to stand. Dropping to one knee, her eyes, filled with murderous intent, pierced Pandora.

Seeing that sharp, ominous light in her eyes, Pandora shook her head.

"I'm sorry to disappoint you before your cherished wish is fulfilled, but I think we'll withdraw for today. It seems I won't be hearing you willingly agree to open the door, not any further than this."

"Die, die, die, die, die..."

"For today, let us be satisfied with the existence of you, of this bloodline, and the birth of a new Sin Archbishop. All that remains is to take the time, eventually, to see the wish fulfilled."

A selfish conclusion, entirely self-centered, leaving everyone else behind. As Pandora, having decided to cut her losses on the situation, gazed about, a flicker of white crystal suddenly caught the corner of her eye.

Snow.

Emilia's boundless magical power, out of control, had warped the weather to its very limit, and snow began to fall. At first, only scattered flakes—but soon the snow grew stronger and heavier, and before long it carried winds fierce enough to be called a blizzard.

"Even if we are to speak eventually, it seems we won't even be able to face each other properly until you've spent it all."

Looking up at the sky, Pandora exhaled a white breath and walked toward Emilia. Even watching her hated enemy draw near, Emilia found she could not move. The freezing of her body had already spread past her waist, and she could no longer even lift her arms.

"Having invited this runaway surge of power, you will now fall into a long sleep. Whether the mana of this forest, now turned to permafrost, runs dry, or whether some being with power to rival your own comes to cancel it out—either way, you'll be spending no short amount of time beneath the ice."

"Die, die...!"

"I'm afraid you won't die, though. Both you and I will remain unharmed until the day the ice melts and we meet again. And when that day comes, if things remain as they are now, we won't be able to have a proper conversation, will we. So."

Pandora's white fingertip touched, cold, against the forehead of the cursing Emilia. To Emilia, her violet-indigo eyes burning with hatred, Pandora smiled with an expression free of any malice.

"Within you—let all memory up to this day be complete without my having existed in it."

"—Ah."

"Please, fill in the rest as you see fit. Let's see. You worked so hard to keep your promise. I would be pleased if you kept that fact firmly engraved in your heart, and remained just as you are now."

Emilia's face, already half-frozen up to her chest, arched backward, her unfocused eyes wandering. Her eyes rolled, drool trickling from the corner of her mouth, and inside Emilia's mind, everything was stirred and churned.

With a sound, it all crumbled down. Indiscriminately, carelessly, the wallpaper of memory was replaced. The words that had been exchanged faded far away, and abuse she had never actually been given rose up to condemn Emilia.

The important thing, the thing that would not vanish, was the promise. That she had kept the promise—that alone, she would absolutely never forget. That a promise is meant to be kept—that too, she would not forget.

She kept her promise. The promise was kept. So, having kept the promise, she—she had no reason to be blamed by anyone.

"What resolution your heart reaches, and with what kind of smile you will greet me the next time we meet—I look forward to that wonderful reunion."

As the raging blizzard howled, Pandora pressed down her disheveled, wind-tossed hair and began to walk away. Jous, still kneeling in a daze, was already half-buried in the piling snow. As Pandora whispered something to him, Jous rose to his feet, his face devoid of any strength.

The two of them, Pandora and Jous, walked side by side away through the snow-covered forest.

Emilia only watched the two of them go. The freezing of her body had already reached her face; her consciousness remained only in her eyes.

Suddenly, lowering her gaze, Emilia noticed something.

On the ground before her, there was a place where the snow had piled up unnaturally. As though, within that white, snow-covered scene, someone were embracing another.

"————"

Her mouth would not move. She could no longer even close her eyelids. Her body froze, and her heart froze along with it. And then, Emilia's consciousness—

"—...other."

—would go on to spend a hundred years, endlessly, within ice that would not melt.

Until the Spirit who had searched for her, who had been born into this world for her sake alone, would find her.

—And so, Emilia simply kept on freezing.

Having watched it all unfold, standing before her own frozen self, Emilia stood motionless.

"————"

She had remembered everything—all of what had happened. Slowly, tracing the memories that had begun to awaken, the scene of the past had unfolded before her.

All of it, stripped now of its thin, false skin of unreality, came pouring out. The child Emilia had watched it all. Fortuna dying in her arms. Jous's mind shattering into madness. And with her own eyes, she had seen exactly who was the root of all that evil.

Had she forgotten because of her own weakness—the weakness of wishing to forget?

"If you're blaming yourself over the falsification of your memory, that would be a mistake."

"…………"

At that, Echidna—the girl standing beside Emilia—spoke up. Just as Emilia had relived the memory, Echidna too was someone who had watched that same scene unfold, from beginning to end. She turned to Emilia's profile, gazing out at the snowy landscape, and said,

"What the two of you were up against was Pandora, the 'Witch of Vainglory.' She wields shallow, self-serving logic to 'rewrite' events however she pleases. That her influence weakened somewhat was likely due to the passage of time, and to your own power."

"My... power..."

"As you can see, your power, uncontrolled, is immense. In terms of raw combat strength alone, you surpassed Pandora even back then, as a child. But a battle isn't so shallow that superior strength alone decides it. Especially not against Pandora, a Witch uniquely gifted at survival."

However vast Echidna's knowledge might be, she seemed to know about Pandora as well. Though, as she spoke with Emilia, her profile remained as harsh as ever, and it seemed unlikely she'd give an honest answer even if asked directly.

"...You're not hurling insults at me like you were until a moment ago?"

"That's exactly the kind of thing I find distasteful about myself. Even I have the decency to show some consideration for someone who's just relived the memory of losing her mother. Even if that someone happens to be a filthy little strumpet of a person."

"Thank you."

At Emilia's thanks, Echidna let out a breath and said nothing more by way of comfort. Catching herself on the verge of a faint smile at that attitude, Emilia realized just how weak she was, trying to divert her attention, even a little, from the devastating memory before her.

The revived memories were nothing but things that overturned Emilia's worldview from its very foundation. In the truest sense, Emilia's life had been turned upside down right from its beginning.

Because Emilia had thrown herself into the Royal Selection with one single wish—to save everyone in the forest.

"In this frozen forest... who's still alive, I wonder..."

Fortuna was dead, and Emilia had witnessed Archi's death as well. The attack of the Witchbeast, the "Black Serpent," was information Emilia hadn't had in her memory. She knew the threat that monster posed, and its malice-laden nature.

The plague-bearing Witchbeast, the Black Serpent, could infect a living creature with a hundred diseases with a single touch. And any land the Black Serpent crawled across would be cursed, transformed into dead ground where nothing but Witchbeasts could dwell.

—How many survivors had there been in the settlement, before it was buried beneath the snow? And could those survivors, beneath the ice, have escaped being ravaged by the Black Serpent's plague?

To Emilia, the very reason she had for fighting felt, in effect, as good as lost.

No wonder, then, that she had sealed away this memory. Even without Pandora's interference, wouldn't she have wanted to forget a memory like this regardless?

That was how utterly devoid of salvation this memory was.

"...Even if I just keep standing here, the 'Trial' won't end."

In the stillness of the frozen world, Echidna, gazing out at the snowy scene, murmured.

"The past has run its course without a hitch. We've confirmed the greatest regret held by you, the challenger of the 'Trial.' All that remains now is for you to give your answer."

"An answer... to the 'Trial'?"

"The first 'Trial' is completed by drawing a line under the symbol of one's own regret. Whether you affirm your own past actions, or deny them outright. If you refuse, unable to accept it, that too counts as a conclusion—of the unaccomplished sort."

At Echidna's words, Emilia let out a deep breath. Over and over, again and again, she had thought about what she needed in order to overcome the 'Trial.'

Faced with the scene of that falsified memory, she had asked herself why she could not get past it. Having lost Puck, and having taken on herself the part she had leaned on him for, Emilia had, for the first time, been able to open the lid on the memory sealed within her.

Now, at last, Emilia had finally arrived at the starting line of the 'Trial.' And yet, even though her feet had only just now reached that starting line, her heart had lost sight of where the starting line even was to begin with.

She had left the forest to save everyone in the village, to save her mother. And yet, that whole decision had turned out to be nothing more than a pipe dream, worse even than mere idealism.

Her mother was dead, and the fate of everyone in the village remained unknown. Having lost even the reason to keep walking forward, what did Emilia have left?

"—That, I've already been told."

Just as Emilia's heart began to waver over the answer, a hand reaching out from the light held her fast. A strong arm, pulling forward the bewildered Emilia, unsure where to go.

Don't give up. Look ahead, lift your face, look at me.

Again and again, over and over, countless times, he had said that to her.

Even knowing how weak Emilia was, he'd shouted at her not to stay weak. When Emilia shook her head and said it was hopeless, he'd hauled her up, telling her there was no way it was hopeless.

To Emilia, who wanted to give up, saying someone like me couldn't do it, he had declared, without any basis at all, that she was the best.

The pain of clashing teeth, the heat of overlapping lips, lit a flame in Emilia's heart.

"Mother loved me."

"————"

"I wanted to save Fortuna-mother. I wanted her to hold me again, to sleep in the same bed with her again. I wanted to tell her 'I love you' over and over, as many times as I could."

"Then, do you regret it?"

Echidna's question, with no stated subject, was asking about the moment Emilia had been forced to choose between the two hopes. At that time, had she taken Pandora's hand and broken the promise, would Pandora and the others have withdrawn from the forest, and would Fortuna and Jous never have been taken from her?

If she were to look back on the past with "ifs" and "what-ifs" and "if only I hads," then perhaps that would be so.

"I don't regret it."

"…………"

"I don't regret keeping the promise, not giving up that ground. If there's anything I regret, it's that I didn't have enough power back then, that I couldn't manage to be clever enough. I will never, ever let myself regret not breaking Mother's word, not giving in to what Pandora wanted."

Because Fortuna had said so, right until the very end, hadn't she— that she was proud of Emilia for choosing to keep the promise, that Emilia was her treasure.

Those words, those alone, remained a treasure within Emilia forever.

"But hasn't the meaning behind fighting been lost to you?"

"That's not true. Mother... I couldn't save her. But the rest of the village, I still don't know. Everyone might still be waiting for help, under the snow, even now. I'm the only one who can save them."

"That is land tainted by the Black Serpent's contamination. Even supposing villagers remained within the permafrost, it seems unlikely their bodies could have endured, plagued by that sickness, and survived."

"That's just guessing. A bad guess. Everyone's under the snow, waiting for help. I'll pull them all out quickly, and I'll get scolded by everyone. And then, they'll smile and say how glad they are to be alive."

"That's a ridiculous fantasy."

"No, it's a happy vision of the future!"

Against Echidna trying to cut her down, Emilia pushed back and stepped forward. Facing the Witch head-on, Emilia gestured at the snowy landscape with her hand and said,

"I won't let you deny something no one has even seen yet! I won't accept that what Mother left behind ends in something so sad! I'll be the one to carry out Mother's ideal!"

"Ideal? What is it your mother wished for, exactly?"

"Mother used to say it. That someday, everyone would leave the forest, and live normal lives. Just like Jous and everyone in the village managed to get along, just like Subaru told me he liked me—surely, a world will come where Mother and Jous could have walked side by side!"

"And in that world, do you imagine there being frozen villagers? Villagers you yourself sealed away in permafrost?"

"I'll apologize, so, so much. Over and over, as many times as it takes, until I'm forgiven! And once I'm forgiven, I'll introduce everyone to the world. I'll tell them, there's no more need to hide anymore. This—this is the world Fortuna-mother talked about!"

"————"

Drawing a breath, Emilia cried out. Before she knew it, she and Echidna were no longer standing amid the snowy landscape, but in a world of pure white light.

Neither the cold biting at her skin nor the vanishing of the landscape dominated by countless regrets did Emilia notice, as she raised her voice.

"I'll sing this dream until my voice goes hoarse, loud enough for Mother, up there in the sky, to hear!"

"————"

"In the world Mother loved, I'm happy—I'll say it, loud enough for her to hear—!"

In that instant, with a sound, the world cracked apart.

Seeing the fissures race across the white space, Emilia finally noticed the scenery changing around her. Before Emilia's wide, astonished eyes, Echidna brought her hands together. Applause.

"I see. Now I understand. I thought I already knew, but you exceed even my imagination. Overbearing, arrogant, self-righteous, selfish, a hard sell of hypocrisy."

"That's right. Something wrong with that?"

"Not particularly, no. Though—in that respect, you really are just like your mother."

At Echidna, furrowing her fine brow, Emilia voiced her question. What she'd just said certainly implied—

"You... you know my mother? Not Fortuna-mother—the other mother?"

"I know her. It's not unrelated to why I become so emotional over you, in fact. Something like envy, that it should only ever be you."

Echidna's shrugging figure began to blur before Emilia's eyes. At the same time, Emilia's own consciousness began to take on a vague heaviness, and a floating sensation, like waking from a dream, began to wrap around her entire body.

"This is the end. However self-righteous the conclusion, a settling of the past is a settling of the past. Use your mother's sacrifice as an excuse, if you like, and dance about as clumsily as you please."

"Say whatever you want. I'm getting used to your insults, you know."

Placing her hands on her hips, Emilia showed a bit of composure toward Echidna, who never forgot her spiteful remarks even to the very end. Echidna, at that attitude of Emilia's, averted her eyes and—

"As for the remaining two 'Trials'... regrettably, I doubt they'll pose much of an obstacle."

"Is that so?"

"Resignation and acceptance are always the mortal enemy of introspection. The nature of the 'Trials' that probe your inner self is a poor match indeed for you as you are now. In a sense, it's the fruit of abandoning thought altogether."

"Being told I look like I'm not even thinking is really uncalled for."

Emilia showed her displeasure at Echidna's lecture. In any case, this exchange seemed to be drawing to its close.

Echidna's figure was growing nearly invisible now, and Emilia gave her hazy head a light shake. But she could no longer hold onto consciousness.

"—I dislike you, you know."

"But I don't dislike you all that much, myself."

Though she couldn't see it, Emilia felt she knew, somehow, the expression on Echidna's face at that answer.

Her consciousness rose to the surface.

When consciousness returned, Emilia gave a small groan at the hard sensation against her back.

Behind her, directly at her back, was a wall. It seemed she had slumped down and lost consciousness leaning back against it. Reaching out to touch the wall, she felt the traces of something roughly carved into it. Right there, carved into it, were the words "I love you," and at the perfect timing of it, she couldn't help but laugh.

Right now, more than anyone else, it was his words that Emilia wanted to be affirmed by.

"—I'm sorry, Mother."

Her smiling mouth twisted, and Emilia let out a stifled voice. The words of apology echoed in the dim little chamber, and the sound of her sniffling repeated again and again.

The tears flowed out, one after another, unbearable, impossible to hold back. Holding onto her pride, putting up a brave front, determined never to let the Witch see her cry—here, in this tomb where no one could possibly see, Emilia pressed her face against the wall and let it all show, without restraint.

"Mother... Mother..."

Tears flowing out. Tears that, by rights, should have been shed long, long before. A hundred years before.

Because she had forgotten, she had been unable even to properly mourn her mother's death, until now—and so, in this small chamber where no one would ever know, Emilia went on mourning her.

So that when she stepped back outside, no one would know she had been crying like this. So that she wouldn't have to show her weakness to the one who told her he loved her even weak as she was.

She cried, and cried, and cried, and cried. Mourning the memory of her mother, her mother's love, everything she had been given.

Emilia went on and on, pressing her face into "love," and continued to weep.
