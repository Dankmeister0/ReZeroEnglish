Arc 8 Chapter 70: "The Final Gambit"

A blast of wind spread out as if blowing apart the thick, swirling clouds from within, dyeing the imperial capital's sky red ahead of the approaching dusk.

The light of the explosion scorched the eyes of those who looked skyward, but compared to the catastrophe that could have blown away the imperial capital itself, the damage could be called minuscule.
That was proof of the cooperation among the members of the "Save the Vollachian Empire from Annihilation Squad" gathered in the imperial capital, who had continued to dodge the many layers of ruinous traps laid by the Great Disaster.

Not every single one of them grasped the full picture of the traps the Great Disaster had set.
Each had simply exercised their potential to the fullest and removed the causes of ruin within their own reach. The relay of lives for that purpose had by now surged past the boundary between the living and the dead, and the power map of the imperial capital Lupugana was at the height of chaos.

Even so, there remained one fact that stood unshakably firm: through and through, Sphinx, who led the Great Disaster, was the enemy of every living thing in the empire, no matter what.
And the many stepping-stones toward ruin that the "Witch" had laid were about to enter their final stage—

"—Whoaaaa!?"

With a thunderous roar, an explosion blew apart the clouds in the distant sky, and Subaru, his eyes caught by the light, felt his throat shudder violently at the sudden, relentless upheaval of the situation.
A scream that could have been either a shriek or a cheer was his honest reaction to the events unfolding before him.

Together with Beatrice, he had just snuffed out the flames of destruction unleashed from the Crystal Palace, then been saved by Roswaal from the ferocious onslaught of the "three-headed" Blight Dragon that had appeared there, and just as he thought a dizzying aerial battle was about to begin, a massive explosion had erupted in the distant sky instead.
It was more than enough chaos to leave anyone bewildered—but the dead dragon paid the light in the sky no mind at all, and stretched each of its heads toward the pitiful prey whose concentration had broken.

"—————!"

The Blight Dragon had three heads, and the prey—Subaru, Beatrice, and Roswaal—numbered exactly three. A single head's great maw would have been more than enough for each target, yet all three of the dragon's heads surged forward as if in competition.
Those sharp fangs of the undead dragon were on the verge of tearing the lives from Subaru and the others—

"One stroke, cleaved in two—!"
"Not gonna let you have that."

From the left and right, two shadows shot upward diagonally from beneath the dragon, each slamming a blow home from its own direction, and the left and right of the three heads were struck down, gouged away, sent flying.
The ones who did it were a Wolf Human of jet-black fur, and a blue bolt of lightning that had shot up thunderously from the ground below—

"Halibel-san, and big Cecilus!?"

"Hahahahaha! Fancy meeting you here, Boss. Since this is the final stage I figured you'd be around, but that display just now was a sight to behold! Couldn't let myself fall behind, so I rushed up into the sky in a hurry, but dragon-slaying alone leaves me a bit unsatisfied, don't you think?"

In Subaru's wide-eyed field of view, the one who'd slashed his sword in a single flash while airborne from his jump was Cecilus—and moreover, this was the first time Subaru had ever laid eyes on him in his adult state. Combining the fact that his "Infantilization," which had remained only suspected until now, had indeed been real, with the fact that it had already been undone before Subaru caught up—

"Did you meet Olbart-san and get him to undo it?"

"Nope, undid it myself! That's kind of my specialty, so let's set that aside—quite the delightful pairing here, isn't it? That's Halibel-san over there, right?"

Answering breezily, Cecilus jutted his chin toward where Halibel stood.
He was the one who had lent his strength to get Subaru, Beatrice, and the Magic Crystal Cannon there in time, and he had immediately caught up into the sky himself as well, joining in to stop the Blight Dragon's attack.
At Cecilus's glance, Halibel gave a wry "well now" and said,

"From where I'm standin', I'm just fillin' in for the work that's rightfully yours."

"Ho-ho, my stand-in—now that's a bold claim! Though of course, only a performer of equal caliber could pull that off, so I'd say you're doing pretty well for yourself, Halibel-san. —Whoa, hold on."

On the surface it was an amiable exchange, but this was mid-air, in the middle of a death match.
Its two heads blown away, the Blight Dragon, with only its middle head remaining, beat its wings and put distance between itself and the two transcendent beings who had suddenly cut in, trying to flee.
It was likely an instinctive judgment, but the right one. Indeed, even though Cecilus and Halibel could leap up into the air, that didn't mean they could fly freely.
But—

"—That's a matter I can cover-r-r."

Roswaal, with Subaru and Beatrice tucked under each arm, had no sooner spoken than the dust in the air instantly gathered, and an improvised foothold formed on the spot.
That foothold gave Cecilus and Halibel the purchase they needed to gather themselves for another leap.

"Even with just one head, a dragon's a dragon."
"Interrupting our big showdown, how rude. Let's drop it quick and clean!"

How many people could say such a thing so lightly about facing down the mightiest of all creatures on the ground, the "Dragon"—undeniably one of that very species, a Blight Dragon at that?
And the two of them immediately proved with their own skill that it wasn't a joke or an empty boast.

—Streaks of blue and black light shot across the sky, instantly catching up to the Blight Dragon as it tried to create an advantageous distance.

In an instant, the Wolf Human split into three bodies in midair and struck its "death points," and the Blight Dragon's wings were both torn off. But no warrior would let a dragon suffer the indignity of a pathetic crash to its death.
A flash of lightning—the sword that made dreams a reality split the Blight Dragon's black scales vertically, and its life was cleaved in two.

It was the most anticlimactic, legendary dragon-slaying the world had ever seen.

"...What outrageous people, I suppose."

At Beatrice's murmur, having witnessed the same thing, Subaru agreed from the bottom of his heart.
The pairing of the two strongest on the ground, something he'd known of only as information, gave off a sense of invincibility that surpassed his simple imaginings. In truth, it was the same feeling as having Reinhard on your side—he couldn't imagine losing.
And now that Cecilus had grown up, combined with Halibel, that feeling only grew stronger—

"—Not yet, brother!!"

Into Subaru's inner slackening burst a voice like a slap across the face.
Looking down, he saw it came from Al, a man in a familiar helmet standing amid what had once been the Second Apex—an area now reduced beyond recognition, the ground turned into something like bubbling, boiling magma.
Al, one of those who had gone missing in the imperial capital, raised his voice from the ground in a desperate shout.
It was—

"The turning of the stars ain't changed yet! The 'Witch' is still cookin' somethin' up!!"

Amid the rapid-fire changes in the situation, Vincent felt his thoughts heating up, accelerating.

However much he was lauded as the "Sage Emperor," Vincent had never once thought himself especially wiser than others. If there was one thing he consciously prioritized over others, it was nothing more than the depth and breadth of the thinking he brought to bear, and the difference in time spent arriving at an answer.
And that difference in time spent came down to something as trivial as whether one could act even while everyone else had their eyes stolen by the glow overhead—how one used those small slivers of time.

"—————"

Ignited by the "Witch's" scheme and brought to the verge of collapse, the Crystal Palace's magic crystal—Moguro Hagane's true body—had been carried into the sky by the undead Balleroy Temeglyph, who should have been dead.
As a result, it had averted a chain explosion at the Crystal Palace, saving the imperial capital, and by extension the empire, from annihilation.
Though knowing Balleroy, what he'd truly wanted to protect from the bottom of his heart was probably neither the empire nor the imperial capital, but someone closer to him, someone he held dear.
Either way, the fruit of the dead Balleroy's self-sacrifice was the safety of the imperial capital, and the sight of the evening sky with its swirling clouds blown away—

"But is this truly the end of it?"

Right beside him, tears traced down the cheeks of Medium, who cradled the unconscious Madelyn. Taking that in at the edge of his vision, Vincent set his thoughts running with ruthless clarity.
Medium had stopped Vincent when, shielding himself behind his position as the one who would become Empress, he'd tried to throw his life away. A strong, composed emperor would have offered her words of comfort, a gesture of closeness.
But Vincent was neither. —And so, there was nothing he could do for Medium. Precisely because he could do nothing for her, his mind worked all the colder.

"The Great Disaster has never once rested its hand, continuing to strike blow after blow toward ruin. On top of that, every single scheme has come in a two-layered fail-safe, leaving no opening."

The "Sacrament of the Immortal King," which had raised the dead, drew its power from the "stone mass" Muspel—meaning that if the undead were continually defeated, the "stone mass's" power would eventually run dry, inviting the collapse of the empire's very earth.
The earlier bombardment by the Magic Crystal Cannon—its target was unknown from Vincent's position. But after the bombardment, a parting gift had been left behind: the heightened magic crystal ignited, turned into a bomb meant to blow away the imperial capital.
To think that simply repelling the immediate threats meant they had weathered it all was shallow thinking.
If the Great Disaster had yet another move prepared—

"—————"

A certain possibility suddenly crossed Vincent's mind.
It was less a product of deep, far-reaching calculation than something closer to a baseless accusation, or paranoid delusion. But having thought of it, he had to verify the possibility. Vincent understood well that anything he could think of, anyone else could think of too, given enough time.
So this was no different.

"Olbart Dunkelkenn! Did you search the castle thoroughly, every corner?"

"Oi oi, Your Excellency, I'm short both my hands here, y'know? Could stand to spare a lil' more pity for this old man's sorry care-needin' life ahead o' him..."

"—Did you see Priscilla Barielle within the castle? Living or dead, it makes no difference."

"—————"

"Answer! There is no time to waste!"

"...The girl with the red eyes and the flashy dress, that the one? Never saw her. Though I did go 'round killin' the troublesome sorcerers and the folks whose souls got meddled with."

At the edge of Vincent's sharp tone, Olbart raised one white eyebrow and answered so.
Originally, the task entrusted to Olbart in infiltrating the Crystal Palace had been a thorough search of the castle—the broad purpose being the destruction of the "Sacrament of the Immortal King's" ritual formula.
Given the disruption, it seemed even that purpose hadn't been satisfactorily accomplished either—

"Not just empty-handed, lost my hands too and nothin' to show for it. Old man's gettin' fired from bein' a 'General,' is he?"

"Your standing can wait. But if she is not to be found even by your eyes—"

"Abel-chin?"

Her face pale, drained of color, and yet still managing to play the fool as usual—the bizarre old man. At the exchange between Olbart and Vincent, Medium, lowering her gaze from the sky, voiced a question.
Still trailing the lingering weight of Balleroy's final moments, Medium asked with her blue eyes fixed on him, and Vincent, bitterly, pressed his foot down on the debris-strewn floor.
And then—

"—Priscilla is not confined within the Crystal Palace. I cannot deduce the reason even that far... but the enemy's aim—or at least part of it—lies in making her bear witness to the empire's ruin."

—Aldebaran called out to Natsuki Subaru about the turning of the stars, and Vincent Vollachia, though he couldn't grasp the true intent, saw through the aim of the "Witch" at the heart of the Great Disaster.

Having witnessed with her own eyes the chain of events leading up to that point, the captive Priscilla Barielle too arrived at an accurate inference about the situation she'd been placed in.

"It was strange to begin with. If your aim lies with me, then—setting aside Arakiya's death—the empire's collapse ought to be inconvenient for you."

Displayed across several mirrors of water were not only the battle in the imperial capital, but also the struggle unfolding in the "Fortress City," and beyond that, people across the empire falling victim to the undead.
It was, indeed, proof that the threat of the Great Disaster was spreading like fire across the entire empire.
To show all this to Priscilla, to dye her heart in despair—whether or not that could actually be achieved, such an aim was easy enough to understand.
And yet—

"The empire's ruin brought about by the 'stone mass's' death cannot serve your purpose. Ultimately, taking my life here is not what you desire. Therefore—"

"Therefore?"

"—It is not the Crystal Palace's underground cell that binds me."

That Sphinx continued devising schemes indifferent to the empire's ruin, again and again, could only be because she was certain that ruin would never reach Priscilla.
To destroy the empire while keeping Priscilla alive—the method that satisfied that condition was simple and clear.

"—————"

Having arrived at that certainty, Priscilla looked around once more, and found no discrepancy between the underground cell before her eyes and her memory of it.
In her days as Priska, she had set foot in the castle's underground cell only once, out of curiosity—but the cell that matched Priscilla's memory down to the smallest detail was, in all likelihood, something that had shifted the entire space wholesale.
The space of the underground cell itself had been relocated to somewhere unrelated to the fall of the Vollachian Empire—a sort of pocket dimension—placing Priscilla there as an observer of the imperial capital turned battlefield.
As proof of this, at some point the tremors of the fierce battle raging in the imperial capital had stopped reaching the cell.

"But of all things, to place me in the position of observer. Whatever the other Vollachian royals may deserve, you at least understand how to show due disrespect to me."

"I forbid careless interference. For you, it is an effective measure. Necessary—countermeasure."

"Careless and measure are both words I dislike, but in deference to your spirit of defiance, I shall overlook it."

Responding so to Sphinx's reply, Priscilla fell into thought.
As for the "Witch's" intent, rather than worrying about Priscilla taking some action herself, the aim was likely to nip in the bud the possibility of anyone coming to rescue her.
That much was correct in itself. —Confinement in a pocket dimension whose entrance was unknown. With this, one could say the chances of Priscilla being rescued from outside had all but vanished.
However—

"Would you not say the same constraint applies to you as well?"

"...What do you mean by that?"

"A simple matter. —Why do you not replicate yourself without limit, and with your countless selves bring down countless stars, scorching and utterly destroying the empire's earth?"

"—————"

"If you are certain you will not catch me in the falling heavens, that would be the swiftest way to accomplish your goal. The reason you do not do so is plain as day... I can only conclude you would if you could, but cannot."

To the silent Sphinx, Priscilla laid out her own theory at length.
Once, burned by the flames of the "Sword of the Sun," Sphinx had transformed the very nature of her soul, remaking her soul's vessel into the "Witch of Greed" in order to fulfill whatever purpose she had been created for. At that point, one of the two goals behind Sphinx's instigation of the Great Disaster had already been achieved.
All that remained was to destroy the Vollachian Empire in order to plunge Priscilla into despair. For that, all she need do was create Sphinxes without limit and rain down a meteor shower upon the land.
And yet Sphinx did not do this.

"Galling as it is, seeing you multiply your numbers in imitation of Lamia gave me the intuition. However much you increase the number of vessels themselves, the soul underlying them all remains shared. —Which is to say, the wellspring of mana held by the soul that is 'Sphinx' must be singular."

That was the existential flaw borne by Sphinx, reborn as the "Witch of Greed."
Through the abuse of the "Sacrament of the Immortal King's" ritual formula, she existed in a state where she could revive endlessly and create any number of copies of herself—but so long as the underlying soul remained the same, she could never possess more mana than that soul held.
Sphinx had already waged fierce battle numerous times against those seeking to retake the imperial capital, and had, moreover, invoked the great magic of raining down stars again and again. However excellent a sorceress the "Witch of Greed" might be, there was naturally a limit to the power she could hold.

"For that reason, you must place a limit on the number of copies you make of yourself. And beyond that, the fact that you keep one of them pinned down here with me is surely not merely to serve as my conversation partner. It must be because, without you here in person, this space itself cannot be maintained."

Ever since declaring herself the mastermind behind the enemy and appearing before Priscilla, Sphinx had not once left Priscilla alone in the underground cell. That could be read as a way of not missing any crack forming in Priscilla's expression or heart amid the ever-shifting circumstances—but a more urgent reason could also be considered.
Put simply: the answer was that she did it because it was necessary.

"—————"

Against that observation of Priscilla's, Sphinx maintained her silence.
There was a line of thinking that said silence, for better or worse, kept information from passing to the other party—but to Priscilla's mind, that was second-rate theatrics. Surely Sphinx understood as much herself.
That at times, silence can back up a suspicion more eloquently than words ever could.

—If Priscilla's inference was correct, then Sphinx now stood in a state where she had exhausted nearly the entirety of the power her soul could hold.

If that were not the case, then even accounting for the limits placed upon her, she ought to have driven the identical copies of herself scattered throughout the imperial capital and across the empire into a relentless offensive, giving no one a moment's respite.
Strategically speaking, there was no reason not to do so.
In other words, the scheming of Sphinx, who led the Great Disaster, had at last reached its limit—

"—That is not so, is it?"

That very inference of her own, Priscilla denied, closing one eye as she spoke.
The emotion bound up in that denial, had there been another listener present in that place, might have made them doubt their own ears. For that question of Priscilla's was not born of the sense of superiority in wanting to see through her opponent's intentions, nor of suspicion rooted in wariness.
What lay there instead was a kind of anticipation.

The assumption that her opponent would clear even this dead end, this state of impasse, that she had fallen into.
And in response to that question of Priscilla's, Sphinx's eyes widened, faintly. Widened—and then the "Witch's" lips twisted into the shape of a smile.

—That was the moment the curtain rose on the final, great confrontation between Priscilla and Sphinx.

The Sphinxes revived from the same soul each existed independently; their consciousnesses were not shared.
For this reason, among the seven manifested bodies of Sphinx, the "Witch of Greed," only the one body facing Priscilla knew that Priscilla had read through their intentions.

However, this did not affect the intentions of the seven Sphinxes as a whole.
For from the very start, Priscilla had devised her scheme premised on the assumption that Priscilla would see through their intentions and call them out on it.

Only the one body facing Priscilla had gone through the answer-checking, but nearly all of the inferences of the Priscilla bound to her proved correct, and her extraordinary insight left Sphinx with nothing but admiration.

Just as she said, the multiple Sphinxes shared mana through their underlying soul, and the more their number increased, the more the amount of mana a single Sphinx could wield dwindled.
Through the ceaseless battles of these past several days, the reality was that barely enough mana remained to invoke great magic like Al Shario, the star-dropping spell.

Of course, great magic was not the only showcase of a sorceress's skill.
Sphinx, having inherited the knowledge of the "Witch of Greed," had any number of options available to her in the form of minor techniques whose results outstripped the mana spent on them.
But it was also true that transcendent, extraordinary beings stood ready, beyond the reach of being toyed with by such minor tricks.

In particular, the presence of the Wolf Human who brought the curse of death, and the swordsman who cut down stars, could not be ignored.

For that reason, the seven Sphinxes—excluding the one participating in the great battle at the "Fortress City" Garkla, and the one keeping watch over Priscilla—the remaining five Sphinxes reached for their final gambit.

The direct assassination attempt on Arakiya, unified with the "stone mass," had failed.
The attack meant to annihilate that same Arakiya, the massive bombardment by the Magic Crystal Cannon, had also failed.
Igniting the magic crystal immediately after the Magic Crystal Cannon's discharge, seeking the annihilation of the imperial capital through runaway overload and critical breach, had failed as well.

Using all of those failed schemes as stepping-stones, the Sphinxes scattered across five locations in the imperial capital of Lupugana activated the magic circles they had laid—now interfering with the true objective, the magic crystal stone of the Crystal Palace.

"—Multi-layer magic circle activation. Executing the final gambit. Necessary—important."

This was a plan fundamentally different from the scheme that had sought to use the magic crystal stone of the Crystal Palace as a catalyst to blow away the imperial capital through the magic crystal's runaway state. Runaway as it may have been, control over the "Meteor" that was the Crystal Palace had still belonged to the magic crystal, Moguro Hagane.
But now that magic crystal was gone, and the Crystal Palace, having narrowly escaped the crisis of explosion, was presently in a defenseless state.

With Moguro Hagane's existence extinguished, the seat of control over the magic crystal stones making up the Crystal Palace now stood vacant.
What remained there was a colorless mass of mana, accumulated over long ages into an enormous reservoir of power—and to a "Witch" on the very verge of running her mana dry, it was an irresistibly tempting offering.

Positioned at the points of an inverted five-pointed star that did not overlap the five peaks of the star-shaped fortress symbolizing the imperial capital of Lupugana, the five Sphinxes deployed an enormous magic circle.
It was meant to interfere with the magic crystal stone of the Crystal Palace, now stripped of the control once held by Moguro Hagane, and to wholesale plunder its vast store of mana.

And this scheme of the "Witch," not directly tied to the fall of the Vollachian Empire, would not break the resolve of Natsuki Subaru, who had shattered so many plots up to this point, nor that of Vincent Vollachia.
The same was true of the "Blue Lightning" Cecilus Segmunt, the "Adorer" Halibel, the "Spirit Eater" Arakiya, the "Vile Old Man" Olbart Dunkelkenn, the "Kaleidoscope" Yorna Mishigure, the "Bramble Emperor" Eugard Vollachia, Roswaal L Mathers, Beatrice, Garfiel Tinzel, Medium O'Connell, Tanza, and Heinkel alike.

Only, one alone—

"...The magic circle—is incomplete?"

Among the five Sphinxes who were meant to activate the magic circle, the northernmost one alone did not join in its activation, and the "Witches" felt an unease at the imperfect invocation of the ritual formula.

That this was, ironically, caused by a collision between the "Witch of Greed" and the "Witch of Envy"—between two who each inherited the mantle of "Witch"—was something no one, at this point, had yet come to realize.
