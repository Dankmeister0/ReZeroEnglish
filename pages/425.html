Arc 6 Chapter 23: "A Review of the Third Layer, Taygeta's Library"

The white space around him vanished, and what appeared before his eyes was a stone-built room and countless bookshelves. Confirming that the monolith he'd been touching had disappeared from existence, Subaru judged that the conclusion he'd reasoned his way to had probably been the right answer.

He judged that it was — but—

"We did it! Subaru, that's amaz—"

"Whoever came up with this is a rotten piece of work!!"

"Wha— that's your first reaction!?"

Having watched the third layer, "Taygeta," unlock, Emilia let out a cry of delight, only for her eyes to go wide. Subaru's curse echoed through the tower, his face screwed up in a magnificent scowl. Turning back to the startled Emilia and the others watching, Subaru said "Ah, sorry," and continued,

"Solving it the way I figured is a credit to me, sure — but the fact that I'm the one who solved it is actually a huge problem. No, seriously, this isn't fair."

"Is that so? I thought it was solved because Subaru is so knowledgeable... that's what I figured, anyway."

"It's less that I solved it because I'm knowledgeable, and more that nobody but someone like me could've solved it at all — that's the real problem."

Subaru scratched his head, but Emilia tilted her head, clearly not following. He wondered how to explain it, but even a detailed explanation would make for a somewhat troublesome solution to describe.

The "Trial" that existed on the third layer, "Taygeta," had — as Subaru narrated while solving it — been modeled on the legend of the constellation Orion. That fact alone was enough to make him think, "whoever came up with this must be a stargazer, or some kind of romantic," setting aside his own hypocrisy in saying so. But the real problem was that knowledge of "Orion" had no way of existing in this world.

Orion, and of course the fact that Shaula was the name of a star, right down to the arrangement of the constellations — all of it was knowledge and astronomy from the original world Subaru had come from. If there were some outlandish twist where this world turned out to be the far future of Subaru's original world, or some lost civilization from its past, the possibility would remain open. But it had already been confirmed that the way the night sky looked in this world was nothing at all like the heavens Subaru knew.

"Well, if so much time had passed that the arrangement of the stars looked completely different... that'd be a lost cause. But then Orion wouldn't even survive as a story to begin with."

If enough years had passed for Orion to vanish from the night sky, then the legend of Orion would have disappeared long before that. Considering these circumstances, there was only one answer to arrive at. Whoever devised this puzzle was someone who knew the same night sky as Subaru.

Put more harshly, they were someone so broken in character that they'd call a puzzle nobody could solve — except someone versed in another world's night sky — a "Trial." And given the flow of the conversation so far, there was no doubt that the one who'd composed the question was the "Sage," Flugel.

"Your master, huh. Sounds like a pretty rotten piece of work himself."

"H-hey now, what are you sayin', boya! It ain't like Master to badmouth himself! Not that I'm denyin' he's got a rotten streak, but the fact that it can be solved means he's got heart, no doubt about it! If it was Reid, there'd be no way — he'd probably leave behind a clone of himself and say you can't pass unless you beat it or somethin'."

"That's terrifying in its own way, but which possibility's actually better..."

Either way, it seemed the heroes who'd once driven back the "Witch of Envy" all had something wrong with their characters. Given this bunch, maybe just being tested on knowledge of another world was the lesser evil.

"So, but—"

Subaru sighed at Shaula's non-explanation of an explanation, and into that gap stepped Anastasia, who'd been surveying their surroundings. She kept fidgeting with her muffler as she ran her eyes over each of the bookshelves crammed full of books, and said,

"Natsuki-kun's the hero of the hour for gettin' us past the 'Trial'—that's all well and good, but what's this library actually for, I wonder? What kind of books are in here, I'm curious."

"By Lady Shaula's account, it was described as a treasure trove of knowledge — one where you could learn anything you wished to know."

Nodding at Anastasia's question, Julius glanced briefly toward Shaula. But Shaula had a face that had forgotten anything she'd ever said in the past, and was busy playfully poking at Meili, who was letting her touch her bare skin without any reservation. Expectations had been low from the start, but it now seemed all but certain that getting a proper explanation of the "Taygeta" library out of Shaula was hopeless.

"Goin' by how that one's actin', it's the first time 'Taygeta' has ever been opened at all, I suppose. We'll just have to look around and check for ourselves."

"Yeah. ...Say, aren't you a little pumped up right now?"

"There's no such... there might be such a thing, I suppose."

Coming right up beside Subaru, Beatrice pinched at the hem of his clothes, speaking a bit faster than usual. Her eyes glinted faintly, her gaze sweeping curiously over the library — and realizing the reason for it, Subaru found himself smiling fondly despite the situation.

"I figured you only had bad memories of the Forbidden Archive."

"...It's true they're not all good memories, I suppose. But no matter what, that place is where Betty spent four hundred years. And besides—"

"Besides?"

"It's the place where Subaru wooed Betty by saying 'choose me,' I suppose. No matter how hard you try to forget it, it's not a place you can forget."

"————"

At the unexpected words, Subaru's eyes went wide, and Beatrice turned her face away from him. But the ear on that averted face had gone bright red, plainly betraying her embarrassment.

"You said that yourself and got embarrassed by your own words — what are you even trying to do?"

"It's proof that Betty's memories of the Forbidden Archive are properly closed out with a memory of Subaru, I suppose. ...That's all it is, nothing more."

"You're adorable, you know that?"

"Myaa, I suppose!"

Overcome with affection, Subaru ruffled Beatrice's hair until it was a complete mess. Partway through, Beatrice let out a cat-like yelp and scrambled away, and Subaru watched her go with satisfaction before turning back to face Anastasia and the others, who wore exasperated expressions.

"Anyway, that's enough chit-chat — let's go check out the library."

"Well, thanks for the show. It's sweet to watch, but it sure looks like a parent and child..."

"At least say siblings."

Sticking his tongue out at Anastasia's remark, Subaru stretched and looked around once more. They were standing right in the middle of a cylindrical, stone-built room. The structure itself had returned to being a continuation of the original tower, revealing that the seemingly boundless expansion of space had been an illusion. The sixth and fifth layers had been vast open spaces with no notable features besides the spiral staircase. The fourth layer, by contrast, had been divided into a number of rooms — a multi-purpose den, in Shaula's own words.

The third layer, meanwhile, was a space of the same size, but packed edge to edge with bookshelves, the tall shelves crammed full of countless books. The room had several circular tiers, with the center where they now stood being the lowest point, rising in height as it moved outward. The sheer volume of the collection was dizzying — Beatrice's Forbidden Archive had been considerable too, but in terms of sheer number of books alone, this place overwhelmingly outdid it.

"Man, I could really use a search engine to find the book I'm after."

"Inside the Forbidden Archive, Betty knew perfectly well where every single book was, I suppose."

"That's amazing. Are you a genius or something?"

Marveling at Beatrice's quiet boast, Subaru approached the nearest bookshelf. Looking around, Emilia and the others had each drifted toward their own shelves, but none of them seemed to have the nerve to actually pick up a book.

"You're the one who solved it, right, Subaru? So I was wondering if it's okay for anyone besides Subaru to touch them."

"Ahh, good point, huh. But if it's set up so only whoever answers correctly can read them, wouldn't it be weird that Emilia-tan and the others got let into the library just for watching me solve it?"

"Oh, I see. So the fact that we got let in here at all should be taken as permission already granted."

"Yeah, I think so — wait, Emilia-tan!?"

At Subaru's guess, the wary Emilia nodded, satisfied by the reasoning. Then, without any caution at all, she pulled a single book from the shelf right in front of her. And right in front of the very Subaru who'd just been the one to reassure her — and was now startled — she flipped through it, scanning the contents.

"Hmm, seems like a normal book... I guess? Subaru, what's wrong?"

"No, it's fine, I'm just impressed all over again by Emilia-tan's ridiculous guts. I mean, I'm the one who said it should be fine, but seriously, me?"

"—? You said it was fine, so it should be, right? Huh, did I say something weird?"

Faced with Emilia's face, which genuinely didn't understand what the fuss was about, Subaru found himself at a loss for words. Covering his face with his palm against an emotion too hard to put into words, he muttered, "Uwaah."

"Man, that look of pure trust hurts a little."

"That's what you've built up, that's all it is. And in fact, you're the one who solved the riddle of 'Taygeta' that no one else could crack. That achievement can no longer be denied."

"This kind of thing's basically a fluke. It just happened to be me."

At Subaru's confusion, Julius shrugged, but Subaru looked away from the knight's words. Emilia's trust, Beatrice's affection, Julius's sincerity — surely all of it was exactly what Subaru had wanted, and yet he couldn't shake the discomfort of being given it. He kept doubting, endlessly, whether he was really worth that much.

"Just like Emilia-san said, it's a normal book, huh. Doesn't seem like there's any scary trick where your body bursts into flames the moment you touch it or anythin'."

"The material of the book is... slightly opaque. Can't tell the age either. But as for the contents...?"

With Emilia's taste-testing bravado paving the way, the others reached for books one after another. That said, the world wasn't so simple that one or two books out of such a vast collection would reveal everything. Anastasia and Julius examined the contents and the binding, tilting their heads together in puzzlement.

"Bea-ko, how's it look?"

"From what I can see, the books all follow the same format, I suppose. But the titles are all different. This one is 'Noah Libertas.' This one is 'Libre Fermi.' ...The way they're arranged looks completely haphazard too, I suppose."

Perhaps her librarian's blood was stirring, but Beatrice looked displeased at the careless arrangement of the books. Subaru didn't remember her doing much tidying up in the Forbidden Archive, but he supposed things must have been categorized somehow. Setting aside Beatrice's indignation, Subaru noticed something while looking at the spines of the books.

"These book titles... could they all be people's names, by any chance?"

"Hm... yeah, looks like it. This one's 'Palma Eure,' this one's 'Coyote.'"

"I see nothing but unfamiliar names. I can't claim to be especially well-read, but as far as I know, there isn't a single name here I recognize. Though of course, that may change with a proper search..."

"If you don't know 'em, then probably nobody here does."

Whether it was genuine or false modesty was unclear, but Julius had been showing signs lately of a bit of a history-buff streak. If it wasn't in his knowledge, it was probably a fact that the personal-name titles were more or less random. Subaru also picked up a random book to look inside, but the characters lined up inside were, as usual, this world's peculiar script — "I-script," "Ro-script," "Ha-script," and so on.

When it came to Gospels, they were written in geometric characters unreadable to anyone but their rightful owner, but these books had no such trick played on them. Whether because the print was too fine or the text itself simply too dull, no matter how much he read, nothing seemed to sink into his head — but that was a common problem with books that failed to hold one's interest.

"Just to be sure, let me check with you too, Anastasia-san... any names you recognize?"

"—Nnn, nope."

Just to be thorough, Subaru confirmed with Anastasia alone. Of course, it wasn't really Anastasia herself he was checking with, so much as Eridna, who held control over that body. There was a good chance Eridna possessed more knowledge than Julius. There was no reason for Eridna to lie here, except if that spirit harbored hostile intent from the very start. Taking that report at face value for now, Subaru fell into despair over what to do next.

"Guess it's too early to fall into despair. The best place to hide a tree is in a forest... but man, it'd suck if there's some book packed with critically important information buried somewhere in these shelves."

"Don't give up partway through. This is way more hopeful than a riddle you can't solve at all. We've gotta try hard!"

Faced with the sheer volume of books, Subaru's spirit was already threatening to break under the weight of anxiety about what lay ahead. Emilia raised a small fist toward him, calling out as if to fire him up. Mimicking Emilia's fighting pose, Subaru turned back to face the shelves. The rows of personal-name titles were all things he had no memory of. If only he could stumble across a name he actually recognized, that would at least give him something to confirm — and with that thought, he ran his finger along the spines, tracing the titles.

"...?"

Partway through tracing, a title suddenly caught his eye, and Subaru's finger stopped. Hooking his finger on the spine of that book, he tilted it and pulled it free from the tightly packed shelf. The title on the book was a name he knew. Picking it up almost absentmindedly, Subaru opened the book. And he ran his eyes over the contents of the book bearing an acquaintance's name — and immediately after, it hit him.

—His consciousness went dark.

—A woman. There was a woman.

A woman — though it felt wrong to call her that, she was still so very young. A thin body in shabby clothes, sun-browned dark skin, and green hair.

A woman of an age one might call a young girl — and yet her heart was dominated by an endless worry. It was a proposition with no answer, one she'd been born with.

"————"

Endlessly troubling her mind, an inexhaustible, supreme proposition. It concerned a principle that existed in the world — its white and its black — that is to say, good and evil.

Righteous deeds, wrongful acts. However countless the choices that existed in the world, every act was ultimately judged as one or the other of two poles.

For the woman, still a young girl, there was a reason she kept agonizing over that principle. A necessity. It was the woman's father who had split her world into white and black, good and evil, good causes and evil causes.

"————"

The woman's father made his living by beheading criminals, meting out punishment fitting for their crimes. To give a sinner who had committed a sin the punishment fitting that sin, the ending fitting their life — that was her father's trade.

"—Executioner."

That was what he was called, and from her earliest days, the woman had witnessed his deeds, the way of the execution ground. Horrifying, cruel acts; the death throes of the condemned as their lives ended; an execution ground ruled by blood and death.

—And it was none other than the woman's father's own will that kept showing her "death" there, again and again.

Punishment given for crime committed, evil answered with evil in kind. Her father tried to convey to her the way of good and evil that existed in the world, the way he himself believed in as an executioner.

Her father's intent was noble, without question a lofty ideal. But given the woman's youth, it was self-indulgent — too soon to demand an ideal of her.

The woman watched death after death, breathed in the scent of blood, burned into her mind the sight of criminals being punished. As a result, before she ever learned the preciousness of life, the principle of human mortality, the woman learned punishment fitting for crime.

Good deeds bore good causes, evil deeds called forth evil causes, and a criminal's soul grew tainted in a manner fitting their punishment. Understanding her father's teaching this way, the woman came to crave a way of determining "punishment fitting for crime." She sought something that could serve as a guide toward that end — a scale of good that could weigh evil deeds and name them evil.

"————"

But the scale the woman sought did not exist within the range she searched. There was no simple answer to the good and evil of a matter; right and wrong, crime and punishment, were swayed by countless factors.

"————"

But the woman, still young and unacquainted with compromise or resignation, did not stop. She had to obtain an answer. She had to house within her heart a scale fitting for good and evil. She had to offer up an answer to the question that would not fade from within her chest.

"————"

Days of anguish continued — and then, suddenly, an answer was granted to her as if by heaven's grace.

She broke her father's wine cup, and the woman was overcome with terror at the crime she had committed. Even prepared, perhaps, to have her own head taken, the woman confessed her crime to her father.

"—Confessing your own mistake, and apologizing for it, was the right thing to do."

The woman's father forgave the transgression, and even smiling, said this to her. At her father's smile, and the feel of his palm stroking her head, the young woman understood.

—The scale that measures a crime committed lies nowhere else but within the sinner's own heart.

Even if no one else is watching, a sinner's own heart knows their sin. Good and evil — she didn't know. It was difficult. Right and wrong — there was no certain guide. It couldn't be found.

But the awareness of sin exists within oneself. There is no standard for what punishment fits a crime. But the awareness of what crime fits a punishment exists within oneself.

The woman understood, was satisfied, had finally obtained her scale. Without ever knowing the preciousness of life, the principle of human mortality, the young woman had uncovered the crime fitting a punishment.

"————"

Following in her executioner father's footsteps, in order to hand down punishment fitting for crime, the woman set out to walk beneath the sun. To lay bare the hearts of criminals deserving of punishment.

"————"

That was the culmination of the woman's life — dividing good from evil, right from wrong, truth from falsehood, into two. To the young woman's questioning, some laughed, some grew troubled, some were bewildered. But the result of answering the woman's question was, without exception, the same for everyone.

—The crime fitting a punishment lies within one's own heart.

She looks around. No one is left. Here, there is nothing left but sinners who have received their punishment. Stepping over the shattered fragments of people scattered to pieces, and finally over the fragments of her own father, the woman sets out walking, seeking the crime fitting the punishment, to fulfill the long-cherished wish given to her.

—The "Witch of Pride" interrogated crime, meted out punishment, and continued judging sinners without end.

Having witnessed the beginning of a "Witch" he knew, Subaru's consciousness returned along with pain.

"Guh—AGH!!"

With a ripping, tearing sound, his consciousness was torn free from the book. Gripped by a sensation like blood dried and stuck fast, he yanked forcibly, not caring that the outer layer was peeling away with it.

The pain wasn't in his head or his body — it was in his soul. His soul had been pulled into the book, and tearing it free from there was what caused the pain.

"Subaru!"

"—Hah!"

A voice pierced in from the side, and at the same moment a sharp blow struck his arm. Stepping in, Emilia had struck Subaru's wrist with a knife-hand strike. The impact jolted Subaru's hand, and the book he'd been gripping fell from it. The book landed open, face-down, on the floor, and Subaru staggered, leaning against the bookshelf.

"O-oh?"

"A-are you okay? You looked really, really rough just now..."

"Somehow, I didn't get dragged all the way in... I think? No, I'm not sure."

Nodding at Emilia, who supported him with anxious eyes, Subaru caught his breath. Even though he hadn't been running, his heart pounded, and his breathing refused to settle, staying ragged. Placing a hand over his chest, where his heart kept hammering wildly, Subaru breathed deeply, again and again. His eyes wandered here and there before finally settling, and calming, on Emilia.

"You okay?"

"Seeing Emilia-tan's face calms me down. Lend me a hand a bit longer."

"That's fine, but — what happened?"

Taking Subaru's clingy remark at face value, Emilia asked while still supporting his shoulder. At her words, Beatrice moved, reaching a hand toward the book that had fallen to the floor.

"Just now, touching this book made your face go strange, I suppo—"

"Wait, Beatrice! Don't touch it!"

"—?"

He tried to stop Beatrice as she reached to pick up the book, but the girl picked it up and hugged it to her chest before he could. Beatrice, who hadn't looked at the contents, read the title aloud with a puzzled look at Subaru's alarm.

"—Typhon. Is that a name Subaru knows?"

"Ah, yeah... what about you?"

At Beatrice's question, Subaru almost shot back with "don't you know it either?" But whether her answer would be yes or no, he felt he'd be at a loss for what to say either way, and he furrowed his brow over how to respond. In that pause, Beatrice opened the book and checked the contents herself.

"Idiot—!"

"Calling me an idiot is rude, I suppose. Nothing about it seems any different from the other books, I suppose."

The same shock Subaru had experienced should have struck Beatrice too — or so he expected, but the girl showed no reaction at all to the book's contents. Treating it the same as any other book, she thrust it toward Subaru with a displeased expression.

"But it seems Subaru couldn't see it as just another book... that's how it looks, I suppose."

"...Yeah. But why just me?"

"Could it be the same as the room's puzzle — something only Subaru can understand? Or maybe, after all, only the person who solved the puzzle — only Subaru — gets an effect from it..."

"If that's true, this thing's character just keeps getting worse..."

At Emilia's thoughtful remark, Subaru shook his head with a bad feeling creeping over him. In any case, faced with the book thrust at him, he couldn't summon the will to look through it again.

—What crossed his mind was the memory of that "woman," experienced with an unpleasantly vivid sensation.

There had been smell, taste in the air, the feel of the earth beneath bare feet, the weight of a shattered life. To have relived "someone's" memory that densely, and still make it back — that was a miracle.

Or perhaps, left as it was, he would have been swallowed whole by another person's life. That kind of outlandish fear and revulsion had, without question, existed within that experience.

"Subaru, where'd you meet this Typhon person?"

"Hard to explain... no wait, is it not hard for you, Emilia-tan? Since you don't know her, I guess you never met her — she was at the Sanctuary of the Dead."

"The Sanctuary of the Dead—"

At that phrase, Emilia and Beatrice both stopped moving at the same time. The "Sanctuary of the Dead" was a place with connections not only to Subaru, but to Emilia and Beatrice as well. Given the "Witch's Tea Party" that had unfolded in that place, it wouldn't be strange at all if the two of them knew Typhon.

Though, whether Echidna had honestly invited Emilia to it was unclear, and there was a good chance that the Echidna known to Beatrice differed from the one Subaru knew.

"Typhon was one of the past 'Witches.' The 'Witch of Pride' — she looked like a dark-skinned little girl about Bea-ko's size. But she was more like innocent cruelty given physical form."

At Subaru's explanation, Emilia and Beatrice shook their heads, having no idea who that was. It seemed Echidna's witch exhibition had been a special measure taken only for Subaru's benefit. Even if it had all been to serve her own ends, she'd certainly gone to some trouble over it.

"Innocent cruelty... huh."

Saying it aloud, Subaru recalled Typhon, whom he'd only interacted with for a brief time. In truth, even though it had happened within a mental world, having his limbs shattered by her wasn't something he could easily forget. Even though they'd healed right after, the shock of losing all four limbs wasn't the sort of thing that simply faded. Still, as for what reason lay at the root of the odd abnormality he'd glimpsed in her, he felt he'd gotten a sense of the surface of it through this "reading" just now. Of course, whether that led immediately to real understanding was an entirely separate matter.

"Anyway, just now, I read that book and relived that Typhon kid's... memories? Life? Roots? Something like that, anyway. It wasn't a pleasant experience."

"I can tell that much just from looking at Subaru's reaction... reliving someone else's memories. That sounds more and more like the 'Trial' at the Sanctuary."

"In that case, it was a head-on fight against your own memories. Well, it was a total breeze, though."

"R-right. It was a breeze."

Pretending there had never been snot-streaked wailing, never been repeated failures that nearly broke her spirit, Subaru and Emilia nodded at each other. Casting a jaded look at the two of them, Beatrice brushed the dust off the book.

"A book that lets you relive someone else's memories... in other words, a means of tracing the past, I suppose. If that's the case, then the idea of this being a library where you can learn whatever you want to know..."

"Bea-ko, did you think of somethi—"

Beatrice muttered to herself, lost in thought over what had happened and the contents of the book. But before Subaru could finish his question to her profile, a voice cut in once again.

"—!"

The voice came from where Julius and the others had been examining a different set of shelves. Turning to look at the sound, which had come out like a groan of pain, he saw Julius on one knee, a book in hand. Anastasia, at his side, shook the knight's shoulder with a startled expression and pulled the book away from him.

"Julius? Julius, get a hold of yourself! You can hear my voice, right?"

"...Anastasia-sama."

"That's right, that's good. Slowly, take a deep breath. ...Mm, you're alright, yeah?"

In exactly the same manner as Subaru just before, Julius's consciousness returned to reality. Anastasia's expression showed relief at the sight of Julius, whose exhausted state somehow still looked like a painting. Rushing over to the two of them, Subaru called out, "You alright?"

"Brain fever from reading too much difficult stuff? I feel you."

"Indeed, of late I've grown distant from a lifestyle of poring over books. As a knight who must be equally versed in both the martial and the literary arts, this is a stance I ought to be ashamed of. I suppose I should take a lesson from the depth of your insight, having so easily solved the riddle."

"Man, the way you just rattle that off, smoothly as ever..."

If what he'd experienced was the same as Subaru's, the toll on his soul should have been considerable. And yet, being able to gracefully put up a brave front immediately after was almost infuriating. Ignoring Subaru's private feelings, Emilia delivered a knife-hand chop to the back of Julius's black-haired head.

"Ow."

"Don't be sarcastic like it's some conditioned reflex. Julius, are you really okay?"

"My apologies for causing you worry. I'm almost ashamed of how exaggerated my reaction was. ...That said, it was an experience hard on the heart, I'll admit."

Hiding his mental strain, Julius responded to Emilia with elegant composure. Still, the shock he couldn't quite hide was proven by the thin sheen of sweat on his brow. When Anastasia, standing on tiptoe, pressed the handkerchief in her hand to his forehead, Julius bowed his head as if humbled.

"Puttin' up a brave face is a boy's instinct, so it can't be helped, but when it's hard, say it's hard, y'know? If you push yourself and somethin' goes wrong, it's the people around you who get troubled."

"Yes. Thank you for your concern."

"Mm-hm, Anastasia-san's right about that. Right, Subaru?"

"Not sure why you needed to make sure I agreed, but yeah, that's right!"

With that exchange finished between the two camps' lord and vassal, attention turned to the book in Anastasia's arms. Julius had read its contents and presumably experienced the same thing Subaru had. Looking at the spine, the title written there was—

"—Balleroy Temeglyph. Do you know him?"

"Never heard of him. Pretty sure, anyway."

Glanced at sidelong by Emilia as she read the name aloud, Subaru nodded back with confidence. Say what you will, he had a fair amount of confidence in his memory. When it came to acquaintances in this world, he had it down cold, from Arlam Village all the way to the old man running the fruit shop in the royal capital. And on that mental roster, there was no name like "Balleroy." But at that name, it was Anastasia whose head tilted, her face showing she had some clue.

"That name, I feel like I've heard it. If I recall... yeah, that's right. Wasn't there a general in the Vollachian Empire with a name like that?"

"—To be precise, a former general."

Julius added the correction to Anastasia, who had spoken from a half-remembered recollection. Just from hearing that exchange, it was clear this was someone connected to Julius. Still, Subaru frowned at how distant that title felt.

"Vollachia's that country to the south, right? You're acquainted with a general from over there?"

"That's the second time I've had to correct you — former general. Is it really so surprising? I am, after all, a member of the Royal Guard. The Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica and the Vollachian Empire are neighboring nations, so it's not so strange that I'd know a name one-sidedly."

"I see, a one-sidedly known acquaintance... huh."

Taking in Julius's explanation, Subaru nodded along. Then, exhaling lightly, he suddenly reached out and snatched the "Balleroy" book from Anastasia's hands.

"Natsuki-kun?"

"Sorry to startle you. But there's something I want to check."

Anastasia, whose book had been taken from her, blinked in surprise, while Subaru traced the cover of the recovered book. Then he flipped it open and ran his eyes over the contents. For a moment, Subaru braced himself, wondering if that "relived experience" might come for him too. But he'd also had a hunch that it wouldn't — and it turned out that hunch was the right one.

"Guess this became a name I know one-sidedly too, so I tried reading it, but nothing's happening."

"...Subaru."

"Right now, what matters between us is trust, right? Whether that exists between you and me... I mean, was I the only one who thought it did?"

"—That's an unfair way to put it."

To Subaru, glaring at him, Julius answered with his eyes lowered. Touching his own forelock, he continued,

"There is no one now in whom I ought to place more trust than the people here with you. I daresay I've received emotional support from you and Anastasia-sama that I couldn't even find in Reinhard."

"...The way you say that, it's kind of creepy, somehow."

"Even I felt my tongue itch just saying it."

Scratching the tip of his nose, Subaru watched as Julius, still pinching his forelock, closed his eyes. Then he sighed and immediately bowed his head to Anastasia and Emilia.

"My apologies for my discourtesy, Anastasia-sama, Emilia-sama. Just now, I let a trivial personal matter creep into my answer. In a setting where the contents of the book ought to be shared, that is unforgivable."

"Whether to forgive that or not is a matter of Ram's — I mean, of Ilya's and my own capacity, isn't it. What do you think?"

"What I wanted to say, it seems Subaru and Anastasia-san already said for me. So right now, I'm thinking, 'so what.' Period."

With Emilia and Anastasia accepting his apology so readily, Julius bowed once more, deeply, at the waist. His inner feelings were plain as day to Subaru. Someone who had steeled themselves to apologize, believing they'd done wrong, was weak against being forgiven. Since Subaru knew that feeling all too well himself, it wasn't exactly someone else's problem to him.

"Balleroy Temeglyph. A former general of the Vollachian Empire, but... he is a man who has already passed away. And the one who took his life was none other than myself."

"Got a general from another country killed. That's quite the surprise, huh."

"Anastasia-sama... no, right now, that would be something you've forgotten, wouldn't it."

"————"

At Julius's confession, spoken now with resignation, Anastasia narrowed her eyes. Given Julius's current attitude, this was likely information that had been shared between the Julius from before his memories vanished and the Anastasia from before she was taken over by Eridna. That said, Anastasia — who, by the circumstances, should have been hearing this for the "first time" again — showed little surprise, while Subaru and Emilia's shock was considerable.

"Um, if the books I studied are right, I heard that Lugunica and Vollachia are on really, really bad terms..."

"Getting a general like that from an empire killed — doesn't that turn into a war or something?"

At the two of them's honest, naive questions, Julius nodded with a slightly relieved expression.

"It's the result of a complicated tangle of circumstances. It's a story not entirely unconnected to Reinhard and Ferris either, but to put it simply, the former general was plotting a coup within the Empire. The reason I came to cross paths with him was that I happened to be staying in the Empire at just that time."

"Those two as well, huh. Wasn't Reinhard banned from export or somethin'?"

"A special exception was granted. The Emperor of the Empire wished to meet him personally, that was the circumstance. ...Even from you, I don't think 'export' is quite the fitting word for Reinhard."

"The right word just didn't come to me in the moment. What should I have said? Smuggling?"

In the sense of "something that shouldn't be brought in," it wasn't exactly the wrong expression. In truth, having once again felt firsthand in Priestella just how far outside the norm Reinhard was, the nightmare of having Reinhard as another nation's main military force wasn't hard to imagine at all. It was quite understandable that an international treaty would include a clause about him not approaching the border.

"In any case, that former general's name was Balleroy Temeglyph. I'm sorry. As a rule, I was forbidden from disclosing the circumstances, and it's also a bitter memory for me personally."

"So it's a situation you can't openly talk about. Got it. I'll keep my mouth zipped."

"Mm, understood. I'll keep my mouth zipped too?"

With Julius's circumstances now clear, Subaru and Emilia agreed to keep the secret between them. And so, with the identity of the person in the book Julius had "relived" now revealed—

"I think I've got it figured out, I suppose. In other words, the books in here are books that let the reader relive the past of someone they 'know,' I suppose."

"I got a 'Witch,' and Julius got a former general, huh. That fits, I guess."

"Hold on, I feel like I just heard somethin' I can't let slide — Natsuki-kun, you're acquainted with a Witch too? Ugh, that circle of friends. That's basically the Witch Cult right there."

"Even I find myself super scary, but I'm nowhere near as intense a character as that lot, so don't worry. Being lacking in personality's actually my worry lately."

Subaru shrugged at Anastasia's remark. At that, Emilia, Beatrice, and even Julius all made faces like they'd bitten into something sour. Frowning at that undeserved reaction, Subaru watched as Anastasia sighed and said,

"I've more or less got a handle on what the library's books mean now. I do, but — mind if I say something scary?"

"Not really something I wanna hear, but what?"

"Every single book in this library has a person's name written on it, right?"

Stating the obvious, Anastasia sought Subaru's agreement. Sensing that whatever came next after answering that question would be the scary part, Subaru nodded anyway. And then Anastasia pointed at the "Balleroy" book, and at the "Typhon" book Beatrice held, and said,

"An imperial general, and Natsuki-kun's friend the 'Witch.'"

"Not friends, though."

"If there's even a book for your 'Buddy' the Witch, that means there's books for people who've already passed away too."

"————"

Calling Typhon's state that of "the deceased" still felt somewhat off, but now that the tea party's space had vanished from the Sanctuary, it was probably right to consider them completely dead. As for Echidna, there were too many suspicious points, including Eridna, for Subaru to feel at ease about her status.

Setting aside that nagging discomfort in the back of Subaru's mind, Anastasia spread both her arms right where she stood, turning in a slow circle to indicate the entire library, and said,

"The books in here — aren't they the names of every person in the world, from the past all the way to now? If that's the case... how long do you think it'd take to find the book of the one particular person you're after?"

—Correction. This library's creator wasn't merely a rotten piece of work. —His character was the absolute worst.
