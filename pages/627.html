Arc 8 Chapter 11: "Shut Up"

"Are you going to see that person, Abel-san?"

Stopping at the call from off to the side, Vincent turned around.
By coupling the passenger cars together and treating them as one long dragon carriage, the Coupled Dragon Carriages let the "Divine Protection of Wind Evasion" of multiple Earth Dragons apply at once — and at the coupling Vincent had just crossed, he narrowed one eye at the sight of a familiar blue-haired girl standing there.
Come to think of it, it had been a while since he'd last exchanged words with this girl.

"Have the uninvited guests from the Kingdom accomplished their purpose?"

"Those people's purpose isn't me, it's—"

"Enough. Do not waste my time on nonsense you don't even believe yourself. Time is precious. Especially in the current circumstances. You understand that much."

"――――"

At Vincent's sharp voice, the girl lowered her eyes, downcast.
But she soon raised her face again, reading Vincent's meaning that time was finite, and

"Are you going to see that person, Abel-san?"

—she flung the same question at him once more.

"Not Abel. I am Vincent Vollachia."

To that question, Vincent shot back without so much as a change of expression.
Abel was merely an assumed name, one he'd taken up for convenience while on the run. Borrowed from Abelux, the family name he'd held before ascending to the throne — he had no particular attachment to it.
Only, the displeasure of being called that was, right now, quite strong.

"――. There has been a report that it has awoken. I must confront it."

Pushing that displeasure aside, Vincent answered the girl's question.
Whatever the girl might think, it would not change Vincent's course. To begin with, he hadn't credited this girl with having much will or thought of her own to begin with.

Protected, carried along by the current, tormented by doubt, and arriving after a long while at the obvious conclusion.
Like so many others, she too was merely one more of the ordinary.
And so Vincent moved to cut the exchange short.
But—

"That person isn't your enemy, Abel-san."

"—What?"

Vincent's brow furrowed — both because he'd expected the girl to remain silent and downcast, and because of how unexpected her answer was.
The girl who met his gaze had genuine will in her pale blue eyes.
A light that hadn't dwelt there when they parted ways, timid yet resolute, seemed to pierce through him as if trying to pin him down.

"――. I am Vincent Vollachia. There will not be a third time."

"I'm sorry. But I don't have my memories, so I don't know much about His Excellency Emperor Vincent. Which means there's nothing I can say to His Excellency."

"――――"

"That person isn't your enemy, Abel-san."

Without once looking away, the girl repeated the emphasis.
Vincent understood her through the Authority he held — she was the rein by which Natsuki Subaru could be controlled, and also a precious wielder of healing magic.
If she were speaking boldly to Vincent with full awareness of that position, it would take no small measure of nerve, but that didn't seem to be the case.
And then—

"You. What was your name again?"

"—Rem. At least, that much I can answer clearly, for now."

"A girl with no memory states it so plainly?"

"It's strange, isn't it. Even when your head and your heart don't remember, sometimes your surroundings tell you. Thanks to someone who kept saying, all this time, that I'm Rem."

Whether that was gratitude or exasperation, even the girl — Rem herself — didn't seem to know.
Only, it was clear that it wasn't tied to any negative feeling of loathing or disgust, and Vincent judged further conversation unnecessary.
He turned his back and started walking. Partway through, he said,

"Rem. Do not waste my time again. The next disrespect will cost you your head."

Leaving only that, he ended the exchange with Rem.
And then—

How far do you intend to spin your eloquence, Natsuki Subaru? — you Stargazer of the Kingdom of Dragons.

△▼△▼△▼△

Feeling the hostility directed at him pressing in, Subaru faced the man down.

Thinking back on it, his relationship with this man could be summed up in a single word: incomprehensible.
Their first meeting had been with a suspicious bandaged man camping out in the woods. Back then, Subaru had been too desperate searching for Rem and Rui, who'd gotten separated, to press deeper into it — but the man had been far more than merely suspicious.
Their next meeting was inside a cage in the Shudrak settlement, and there was no accounting for how he could act so high-and-mighty while a prisoner. If he'd had some means of escape, fine, but after that he'd been made to go through the "Lifeblood Ritual," so he must have had no plan at all.

After that, he'd lent his strength to rescuing Rem, only to part ways once — while all along knowing full well that Subaru and the others would have no choice but to come back, and displaying real malice in withholding that fact from them.
And yet, in the assault on the fortress city of Guaral, he'd cooperated fully with Subaru's plan, and together they'd stormed the enemy headquarters disguised as the dancers Natsumi Schvarz, Bianca, and Flora.

He'd let it be suspected there was something more between him and Priscilla, had been courted even by that Yorna, and in the Cursed Capital had declared without hesitation that Rui couldn't be allowed to live — an emperor deposed from his throne, and yet, after escaping the Sword-Slave Isle Prison, when Subaru had heard of the state of affairs sweeping the whole Empire, he hadn't been able to doubt for a second that it was this man's doing.

The famed sage-emperor who, having abandoned the imperial capital of Lupugana and overseen the mass evacuation and retreat now underway, had taken command once more, wielding authority as the apex of the Empire's people—

"An honor to have Vincent Vollachia-san himself come visit the sick. Since you're here, want to peel me an Appa?"

"Do not jest. To begin with, I do not peel fruit. And more to the point — where is there an Appa here?"

"Where, he says..."

Stepping into the sickroom and approaching, the man's question made Subaru's gaze slide sideways.
Whether it was some rule of these situations or not, bringing fruit to visit the injured or sick was apparently standard even in another world. Hence the basket of Appa sitting beside the bed.
Surely he could see it too, of course.

"That fruit basket right there. All red and looks delicious, doesn't it."

"Fool, are you trying to deceive me? An Appa is a white fruit."

"Well, sure, the inside's white once you peel it, but... wait, hang on, I feel like I've had this exact same exchange somewhere way back in my memory."

The memory was pretty hazy, but he was fairly sure he'd had this same conversation a long time ago.
He couldn't recall the details of who it had been with, but he remembered thinking the same thing he was thinking now — that the upper class really did take things to this extreme.
Anyway—

"So there are things even you don't know. That's a surprise."

As an opening line to their exchange, it lacked any real punch, but it didn't feel like a bad start either, so Subaru said it anyway.
At Subaru's words, the man — Abel — reached for the fruit basket and split an Appa in half with the knife lying there. Naturally, the white cross-section inside was laid bare.

"――――"

Having seen the immovable evidence, had he decided to admit the truth of the earlier exchange? Abel set down the knife, left the split fruit sitting in the basket, and turned to face him.
Still swimming in those black eyes was hostility, unmistakable for anything else.

"Don't tell me you're mad because the Appa's color made you look foolish. ...Earlier, that weird way you called me."

"The Stargazer of the Kingdom of Dragons."

"That's it."

Made freshly aware of being called that, Subaru frowned, unable to think of any reason for it.
It wasn't an unfamiliar term. He was fairly sure he'd heard that a fortune-teller stuck close by the Emperor's side in this Empire went by that name.
Something about being a being that showed the future, like the Kingdom of Lugunica's prophetic tablet, the "Dragon History Stone."

"No, it was a worse way of putting it... something like peeking in on tomorrow, I think it was."

"That perception is not mistaken. That is how I regard the thing in question."

"So basically you just called me a Peeping Tom, you know that?"

If it were a different age, wouldn't that be grounds for a perfectly ordinary cut-down-without-question insult? In modern terms Subaru knew, he'd be ready to take this to civil court for defamation.
But at Subaru's retort, Abel's expression didn't waver in the slightest. His black eyes — the same color as Subaru's own, but of a different depth — told him plainly that he wasn't in the mood to play along.
Of course, Subaru had no intention of playing around either, not in the slightest—

"How much do you grasp?"

"Grasp how much, like — the situation? If that's it, I've barely been told any details. Emilia and the others were worried about me since I just woke up... I do know we're retreating."

"――――"

"The civil war's on hold for now, and everyone's in the middle of fleeing the capital... there should've been chaos, real danger, but it doesn't seem like there's been any uproar."

The dragon carriage kept moving; outside the window, the night scenery drifted by slowly, and even accounting for the "Divine Protection of Wind Evasion" shielding them from shaking and wind noise, it was quiet.
Some of that was probably the high soundproofing of the carriage itself, but the fact that the dragon carriage hadn't stopped even once was also proof that no major trouble had occurred.
Despite the fact that a considerable number of people — on the scale of a hundred thousand — must be on the move.

"I figure the 'Nine Divine Generals' or whatever give you some pull, but that alone wouldn't make it go this smoothly. You really were something, huh."

"――――"

"Honestly, when I first heard you'd been betrayed by your right hand and your strategist and thrown off the throne, I seriously wondered if it'd be okay for you to go back to being Emperor. But — you were right to."

Leadership, charisma, the so-called qualities of someone fit to stand above others, that was probably it.
Something that couldn't be accounted for by sheer competence alone — the kind of thing sometimes earned through mud and sweat, but which no small number of people are simply born with.
The kind of talk you hear about royalty of various nations, or the heads of various organizations.
In that sense, Abel — who had secured his own footing in this crisis — clearly did have the potential to have people follow him, to look up to him.

"It bugs me that I leaned more toward believing than doubting, but you being Emperor was the right call..."

"—Was that, too, exactly as you intended?"

"Huh?"

Quiet, but with unmistakable feeling packed into the voice. It seemed so wildly out of place that Subaru's mind refused to process it.
As a result, Subaru failed to dodge the hand reaching toward him, and was pushed down onto the bed by the forehead. And before he could protest, wondering what on earth — he felt something sharp press against his throat.

He realized almost instantly that it was the tip of the fruit knife Abel had used to cut the Appa a moment ago.
Even realizing that, he couldn't grasp the intent. Couldn't grasp the meaning behind Abel gripping that knife and turning hostility — no, hatred, the same hatred that had been packed into his voice — on Subaru like this.

"――――"

At point-blank range, close enough to feel each other's breath, Subaru and Abel glared at one another.
If Subaru carelessly raised his voice and called for help here, Abel would slit his throat with the knife without hesitation.
But the very fact that he wasn't doing it right now meant Abel had some reason not to.

"What the hell... do you think you're doing..."

"It is you who should be asked what you're thinking. Just how much of this course of events did you see coming? Has everything up to this point unfolded exactly according to the picture you painted?"

"So— ! I keep telling you, I have no idea what you're talking about. What would I even be scheming—"

"—Why did you leave me behind, and not Chisha?"

A voice that cut far more sharply than the blade at his throat. It struck Subaru silent, and made the hand gripping the knife tremble violently.
Gritting his teeth, Abel glared at Subaru from point-blank range and spat out those words.
Words that Subaru couldn't immediately make sense of.

"Abel..."

"Vincent Vollachia."

"――――"

"I am no Abel. I am none other than Vincent Vollachia, the seventy-seventh Emperor of the Vollachia Empire. — That, surely, is the outcome you wished for."

Wringing the words out, Abel — no, Vincent — named himself with bitter resentment.
It was an attitude as if he were cursing the very act of naming himself so, and not understanding the meaning of it, Subaru's brow furrowed. Because, of course it would.

"Weren't you fighting to take back that name and that crown...?"

"Wrong. I strove to fulfill the duties of Emperor. The Covenant with the 'People of Shudrak,' the fall of the fortress city, the negotiations with Yorna Mishigure, the incitement of the civil war — all of it was for that purpose."

"The duties... of Emperor?"

"Upon the throne, Vincent Vollachia falls. From there begins the 'Great Disaster' that will seek to destroy the Empire. To leave behind the means to resist it — that is my duty."

"Ha..."

At Vincent speaking, for the first time, the details of his own scheme, a hoarse breath escaped Subaru.
The unfamiliar term "Great Disaster" was part of it, but what shocked Subaru even more was the very manner of Vincent's telling.

He had said that Vincent Vollachia would fall, and that the "Great Disaster" would begin.
And he had said he would leave behind the means to resist that "Great Disaster."

Not to search for a way — to leave one behind.
The way he'd put it, it was as though—

"That's — that's the way you'd talk if you already knew you were going to die..."

Subaru murmured it while keenly feeling the cold tip of death held to his throat.
Perhaps in front of Subaru, who had death so near at hand, Vincent's black eyes wavered eerily. Through that pitch darkness, Subaru intuited the true nature of the certain vagueness lying beyond.
That it was resignation, dressed up in the trappings of a will to resist.

"That's right. I made my own death part of the plan. So that even should I fall, the Vollachia Empire must never be allowed to perish — I left that scheme behind."

And with that intuition confirmed by none other than Vincent himself, in the next instant Subaru's thoughts flooded red, his emotions erupting.

"You bastard, don't you dare joke around with me!"

Baring his teeth, eyes blazing with fury, Subaru glared at the Vincent before him — no, the spineless coward — and shouted. A sharp pain shot through his neck, but that was the last of his concerns, and the last of the last.
Right now, beating some sense into this smug-faced coward mattered so much more, so much sooner.

"You dragged around that many people, made them cross that many dangerous bridges, dragged them through that many unnecessary detours — and in the end, you were planning to just die?! Don't screw around with me!"

"I am not joking in the slightest. Every step of the path you have described was a path taken because it was necessary. Even if its conclusion was my death, it cannot be weighed against a matter on which the survival of the Empire itself hinges."

"That's not it! What I'm asking is why the hell you're the first one to give up on your own life! A huge disaster? I don't care! You should just live and meet it head-on!"

"Do not chirp nonsense, you who understand nothing. So long as I live, the 'Great Disaster' cannot come. It is a plan premised on that fact. That this could be overturned—"

"Who says it can't be overturned?! You're smart, aren't you — so trick this 'Great Disaster' or whatever, lure it out somehow, and just—"

"—It was you 'Stargazers' who told me it cannot be overturned!"

To Subaru's emotional tirade, Vincent had been answering with his emotions suppressed. But now, right here, that emotion exploded in front of Subaru.
Just how unforeseen that eruption of feeling was for Vincent himself — the surge of emotion was such that if he hadn't grabbed the blade of the knife pointed at Subaru with his own opposite hand, there was no telling where its tip might have plunged.

Drip by drip, blood dripped from Vincent's left hand where he'd gripped the blade's edge, staining the white sheets in scattered spots.
But neither Subaru nor Vincent had their attention on the dripping blood — their focus was on each other's eyes, and on the very being that lay beyond them.

Once again, Vincent had called Subaru a "Stargazer."
That was precisely the reason Vincent Vollachia hated Natsuki Subaru, harbored hostility toward him, and had even turned a knife on him like this.
And precisely because he could understand that much, the question refused to resolve.

"The 'Stargazers' told you..."

"――. The 'Stargazers,' who hear the voice of the Observers from beyond the heavens, outside the frame of this world. They tell of events that may occur in the future, granting a chance to minimize the damage. But that is a matter of degree of damage. It is not a matter of preventing it before it happens."

Bitterly, in a voice with as much emotion suppressed as he could manage, Vincent began to speak.
What he described was something Subaru himself had some inkling of. If the "Stargazers" truly foresaw the future, then the fact that it couldn't easily be changed was something he knew from his own experience.
Call it the force of fate, or the natural flow of time — it seemed to want to trace out its fixed shape as closely as possible, and even attempts to avert an impending tragedy would, in the end, be brought to their knees.

The "Stargazers'" foresight was, it seemed, something close to that same rule of experience Subaru knew.
For instance, even Subaru himself — when he Returned by Death from a natural disaster, he could urge everyone to evacuate in preparation for the coming natural disaster, but he couldn't stop the natural disaster itself.
Whether all the destruction of houses and buildings, or the loss of human life, could be entirely prevented — that he couldn't know without trying, but that it would be difficult was beyond doubt.

"Many times now, the 'Stargazers' have foretold the Empire's coming crises ahead of time. As much as possible, I dealt with those crises, but there are those who cannot be saved. Hence, a countermeasure is necessary."

"A countermeasure..."

"A measure for how to minimize the damage, to bring what is lost as close to zero as possible."

—A disaster awaiting in the future; what could be done so that as many as possible could overcome it. What could be done so that as many as possible would not be left behind by it.

If Vincent Vollachia had continued to rack his brains in pursuit of making that a reality—

"It was you who whispered of my death, 'Stargazer.' From your own mouth, how can you now tell me to overturn my own death? Are you mocking me... mocking me!"

"Wa— wait, wait a second! I keep telling you, I'm not any 'Stargazer'—"

"—You see what has not yet come to pass. Stop your scheming, Natsuki Subaru."

At that one line, Subaru felt as though his own heart had been stopped.
In the process of pouring out what was in his chest, the Emperor's mask had come off, and what showed from behind it was not Vincent Vollachia but the face of the flawed man who called himself Abel.
And at the words that man, looking straight at him, spoke, Subaru understood.

"—Ah."

He understood why Vincent — no, Abel — had called Subaru a "Stargazer."
Abel had seen through Subaru's "Return by Death."

More precisely — not that he'd seen through "Return by Death" itself, but he'd seen through the fact that Subaru, by losing his life, went back in time and brought back information from the future — that he had some means of knowing what lay ahead.
And he had understood that to be the same thing as the "Stargazers'" foresight that he himself knew of.

"――――"

Thinking about it, it was a mix-up that could naturally occur.
Of course it would be. Subaru himself had found, in Abel's explanation of the "Stargazers," a point in common with his own "Return by Death," and had been chasing after understanding, after being convinced.
Once he knew a real example of a "Stargazer," and Subaru produced results beyond what his own ability should allow, it was no wonder at all that a connection — no, an identity — would be found there.

Abel's insight was accurate. His powers of discernment scrutinized things correctly.
With his discerning eye, it would have quickly become clear that Subaru hadn't survived all the situations he'd faced in the Empire through mere inexperienced skill and simple good luck.
That's why. That was exactly why.

"Is that why you always made sure to actually listen to my opinions?"

If Subaru's suggestions were interpreted as proposals resulting from seeing the future and choosing the best move, then no matter how ridiculous they might sound, they couldn't simply be dismissed.
Not letting the Shudrak settlement be burned, cross-dressing to conquer the fortress city, the journey to the Cursed Capital of Chaosflame, the negotiations with Yorna — all of it, that was why.
Abel had always earnestly weighed Subaru's proposals, seeking out the possibility of realizing them.

In that way, Abel had drawn even Subaru, likely a "Stargazer," into the board on which the fate of the Empire was contested, trying to turn him into a piece — one more means of resistance for after he himself was gone.
And yet—

"...You left me behind, not Chisha."

"――――"

"Natsuki Subaru, you are a mediocre human being."

It was a voice emptied of emotion, in a different way from just before.
It wasn't that he was trying to suppress his feelings. If he were suppressing them, you'd hear the living emotion groaning, trying to call out to the outside.
But nothing could be heard. It wasn't suppressed — the emotion had simply died.
In that voice with dead emotion, Abel flung colorless words at Subaru.

"A naive, immature, young human, one who has left much bitterness unresolved. Not remarkably good, nor remarkably wicked. Left with nothing, you would have done nothing, and died an ordinary man."

The dispassionate assessment of Subaru's character left absolutely no room for rebuttal.
Just as Abel's on-target assessment had it, Subaru could never become a special human being. Even if his body and heart, steeped in immaturity, could return in spirit to the days when he'd once believed himself a prodigy, he was a half-formed person who also knew the shape of his future self yet to come.
But—

"A mediocre, ordinary man was given a chance beyond what he deserved. You made full use of it and have survived to this very day. Neither leaning strongly toward good nor wicked, remaining mediocre, remaining an ordinary man."

"Abel..."

"You are a mediocre human being. ...Then why?"

Gritting his teeth hard, Abel stared at Subaru.
Casting off all nobility, all composure, all confidence, showing the bare face beneath the stripped-away mask of the Emperor, his voice trembled.
And with that trembling voice, that shaking voice, he cried out.

"Why did you leave me behind, Natsuki Subaru!"

"――――"

"You have no ties, no obligation whatsoever to this Empire. You should have been satisfied just protecting the handful of people you've come to know up until today, and nothing more. Why did you save me?! Why would you even think to save me, someone who doesn't mesh with you at all...!"

"――――"

"Why...!"

The hoarseness of his voice, born of overwhelming passion, spoke to the sheer size of the feeling behind Abel's "why."
Gripped harder and harder, the knife's blade bit deeper into his hand, and the blood flowing ever more freely was so painful to see that Subaru instinctively tried to make him drop the knife, to stop the bleeding.
But—

"Don't touch me!"
"Ngh!"

His hand roughly batted away, Subaru winced at a sudden pain in the hand he'd reached out.
Looking down, he saw that the knife in Abel's swung hand had cut his own hand, and blood now dripped from it too.
Between the two of them, facing each other, the sheets were growing filthier with blood left to drip freely.
And then, ignoring the flowing blood, ignoring the sheets and floor being stained,

"...Even that, reaching your hand out just now — that's it. That is the thing about you that I find most inexplicable, most incompatible with myself."

"――――"

"Bound by whatever fleeting feeling strikes you in the moment, you trample even your own prior convictions and beliefs underfoot. You do not keep to what you've decided; you readily overturn even what you've resolved should be so. You cannot even sort out what should be saved from what should not be, and thoughtlessly extend your hand regardless."

"—Ah."

At the sight of blood dripping and Abel's glaring eyes, at his words, Subaru caught his breath.
Even the pain of his bleeding wound grew distant, and what froze Subaru's thoughts was that Abel's words just now meant exactly the same thing as what had tormented him most recently.

"—But you still think you get to choose whether or not to save Katya, don't you?"

"――――"

Those were words hurled at him before he regained consciousness, before he'd let go of it.
The blade of words directed at him by Todd Fang, who had judged him utterly incompatible with Natsuki Subaru, a monster Natsuki Subaru could never understand, and had forced their parting of ways.
He too had never called Subaru "Return by Death" or "Stargazer," but perhaps it was an eye honed from living as a Werewolf — he had seen through Subaru's peculiarity and defined it as such.

"You're all mismatched! You go around with eyes like it doesn't matter when you die, weighing other people's lives on your own selfish scale — and then when it comes down to it, you resist for dear life. It's disgusting!"

"—!"

Todd's cold gaze, his heatless voice, overlapped now with Abel's before him.
There was heat of anger in Abel's gaze, in his voice, and yet still the two overlapped. Surely the reason was that both were attempts to sever ties with Subaru.

A preface to rejecting Natsuki Subaru, whose way of being was incompatible with theirs, and removing him from the world.

"What's worse is you don't even realize it. You're picking and choosing who lives and dies. Deciding freely who to save and who to let die. You dote on whoever wags their tail and shows you their belly, and you don't spare a thought for anyone who doesn't. Me, I don't hesitate to wag my tail or show my belly to anyone..."

"There's no way I could stand being around someone who decides who lives and dies on a whim like that."

He said it was Subaru's way of thinking, Subaru's attitude, that had driven Todd to such a monstrous act.
Of course, Subaru didn't swallow that whole and think himself entirely in the wrong. He'd done what he believed was right, in his own way. To simply reverse course on that now would leave him unable to face the comrades who had trusted and followed him up until today.
And yet, at the same time, there was an undeniable truth in it too.
—That he had been told he chose who to let live and who to let die.

Through the Authority of "Return by Death," Subaru had bent the fates of the people important to him.
People who otherwise had no path but to lose their lives, to hurt one another, to fight over what mattered to them, to be forever kept from any chance at understanding one another — he had twisted their fated course, and lived on.

Through Subaru's decisions and actions, the number of people who died or fell into misfortune around Subaru had surely, definitely decreased.
And yet, Natsuki Subaru had not been able to save every single person either.

Whether he called it not saving, or being unable to save — whether the framing was forward-looking or backward-looking — the fact remained, immovable, that he had decided to move forward that way and not turned back time.

He had not annihilated every single "enemy."
But neither had he ever saved every single "enemy." Which to save and which not to — just how had Subaru sorted that out?
That it might be nothing more than Subaru's own personal likes and dislikes — that was what Todd had feared.

"No, that's not it, Natsuki Subaru. What you have isn't even a matter of liking or disliking other people."

"...Eh?"

The rift with Todd, left unfilled — as Subaru became freshly aware of it again, Abel's words, shaking his head, stopped him.
Holding his breath, Subaru lifted his face, and Abel told him:

"If you decided who to save and who not to based on subjective likes and dislikes of a person's character, I could still understand that. But you save even people you despise. Just as I am to you."

"That's not..."

He tried to say it flatly wasn't true, but the words wouldn't come.
Whether Subaru disliked Abel enough to abandon him at the crucial moment was something he'd have to search himself deeply to know, but the fact remained: he had reached out a hand to someone he despised.

It hadn't reached. And yet Subaru had still tried to reach out to Todd.
He hadn't had to let go. Struggling with it the whole way, Subaru had stayed alongside Rui right up to today.

If it were said that he'd reached out even to someone he despised — well, that was exactly what he'd done.

"—Take your kin from the Kingdom, and leave Vollachia."

Struck dumb, reflecting on his own actions, Subaru was hit by the sudden words.
Slowly raising his eyes, he saw Abel, the knife he'd been holding now returned to the fruit basket, the color of fury gone from his expression, weaving his words in the same manner as before.
He went on, toward Subaru who was startled both by the change in demeanor and the content of what had been said.

"From here on, the Empire plunges into war against the 'Great Disaster.' The essentials differ from the board I had meant to set — but there are ways to manage. You are not needed there."

"Wha—"

"That was a pointless question to ask. Whatever your intentions may have been, what has already come to pass must be regarded as having come to pass. To question the rights and wrongs of the reason is the height of futility."

Those were words directed less at Subaru than at himself.
Here, the emotional exchange with Subaru, the exchange stripped of emotion, the whole exchange taken together — Abel was moving to unilaterally bring it all to an end.

It was beyond simply out of character.
The name "Chisha," which had come up both in the moment of emotion and in the moment emotion died, was one Subaru too had some memory of.
Judging from the way Abel spoke of him, Chisha — who was thought to have lost his life during that Battle for the Imperial Capital — must have been the one Abel's true feelings were really asking about: why hadn't he saved him.

Sealing away that true feeling, Abel meant to move forward as though it had never happened.

"Make preparations to return home. I will see to all the arrangements necessary to cross the border. Everything after that is entirely the Empire's problem — mine."

Yes, the fact that he had corrected how he referred to himself was the mark of a resolve: to seal away his conduct as Abel and proceed as Emperor Vincent Vollachia.
And just how much that meant to him, just how heavy a burden had been entrusted to him for him to make that decision — that, Subaru could not understand.

Natsuki Subaru could not understand Vincent Vollachia.
Vincent Vollachia could not understand Natsuki Subaru either.

And so, this fruitless moment would end with nothing but blood shed on both sides, and—

"You're a monster. Way more of one than that zombie."

Judged unable to be understood, judged unable to be bridged, judged that parting ways was unavoidable — all decided arbitrarily, all concluded arbitrarily, all ended arbitrarily.
Hadn't he regretted, in just that way, having let it end? You colossal idiot.

"Hey. Abel."

Turning his back, showing by his very bearing that the conversation was over, the Emperor moved toward the door. At the call directed toward that departing, slender back, the Emperor let out a quiet breath and stopped.
As he was, the Emperor turned back with heatless eyes and expression, and—

"Understand this. I am Vincent Vollachia, and a man called Abel is nothing—"

"—Shut up. Grit your teeth."

Using the bounce of the bed's springs, Subaru sprang up and lunged at that composed face.

Into that face — that arrogant face that decided to end everything on its own and kept spouting whatever it wanted about getting out already, that hateful face carrying every one of its true feelings buried inside, that face he'd wanted, more than anything, wanted for so, so long, to just be furious at and punch —
Subaru's small, blood-smeared fist crashed home.
