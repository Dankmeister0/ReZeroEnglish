Arc 7 Chapter 45: "Knock Knock"

—A tiger at the front gate, a wolf at the back.

Strictly speaking, the situation was different, but that was the phrase that flitted through Subaru's mind. Though the fact that it took him a moment to even dredge up that saying was itself a vivid symptom of just how troublesome Subaru's current situation was.

"Nearly a hundred enemies!?"

Al's voice cracked at Taritta's astonishing report, delivered after she'd peered outside from the inn's back door.

At his reaction, Taritta gave a quiet "Yes" of a nod, then,

"We're surrounded. Though it feels less like hostility and more like… wariness, I'd say."

"One or two would be one thing, but pushing a hundred, we can't afford to overlook it."

Abel said this to Taritta as she shut the back door and lowered her voice. At his words, Taritta drew in her chin, and beside her, Abel's gaze turned to Subaru. Behind the oni mask, his black eyes narrowed, and Subaru's heart quailed under that sharp stare.

And then—

"—You, for your part, are carrying a problem beyond simply being surrounded, are you not?"

"—Ah."

"Your limbs shrank against reason. Nothing that happens now would be strange."

With a faint sigh mixed in, Abel pointed out Subaru's altered state, having sensed it. At his words, Subaru felt his cheeks heat with shame—both at having his trouble seen through so neatly by Abel, and at his own awareness that he was the one holding everyone back.

Part of him even wanted to just bluff it out, insist nothing was wrong.

But—

"Subaru-chin, you okay?"

Medium's voice, which had comforted a weakened Subaru just moments ago, stopped him from doing that.

Digging in his heels here would solve nothing. Worse, if he did dig in, the ones who'd suffer for it wouldn't be Subaru alone—it would be his companions too, Medium included.

So—

"Actually, um… I feel like my memory's gone kind of strange. Things I should remember, I can't quite pull up right, and it might not be good."

"…So that's what that weird look on your face was."

Swallowing his pride and confessing this, Subaru left Al momentarily speechless. Al, too, must have sensed something off in Subaru's earlier reaction. Now that it had a concrete shape, unease rose in him as well.

What had happened to Subaru wasn't something Al could treat as someone else's problem.

"—. Specifically?"

"…An important family member's name wouldn't come out. No, wait! I mean, it came out, it just took a while."

"Brother, this ain't the time to act tough…"

"I'm not acting tough…"

He almost reflexively said not, then hesitated—wasn't that itself a kind of bluff?

He didn't want to be told, flatly, that he'd forgotten an important name. Subaru remembered Beatrice, and Emilia, and Rem, properly. He remembered the companions left behind at the mansion too, properly—he had to.

"So I'm not acting tough at—"

"Tell me exactly."

"Huh?"

"Did you forget? Or did it simply take time to voice? Which is it?"

Asked this, Subaru found himself faltering. Abel had closed the distance by a step, and his question carried a force, an edge that wouldn't tolerate a lie.

A numbing sensation spread through his brain, and he couldn't tear his attention from the man before him. To Subaru's silence, Abel pressed again: "Which is it."

"Having lost sight of your surroundings, have you now lost sight of even the inside of your own head?"

"—! No, that's wrong! I didn't forget, it's just hard to recall! That's right, it just doesn't come out smoothly, it's not that I forgot!"

"…Takes time to draw out, is it."

Snapping back at the merciless interrogation, Subaru got his answer received quietly by Abel. Even Subaru himself didn't know what distinction he was making with those words—it was an excuse he'd blurted without understanding it himself. So it surprised him that Abel didn't dismiss it out of hand.

Indeed, Al, irritated by Abel's reaction, snapped, "Oi,"

"How's that different? Is that something worth splitting hairs over right now?"

"Vastly different. Between forgetting and mere difficulty in retrieval lies a gulf as wide as heaven and earth."

"Ah?"

Al growled sullenly in his throat, but Abel offered nothing further. Ignoring the bewildered Subaru and Al, he turned back to Taritta, who was straining her ears toward the outside.

"How do things stand outside? Have they not made their move yet?"

"For now, they're just holding their breath. Though the encirclement should already be complete."

"Then we do not yet satisfy their conditions."

"Conditions, you say…?"

Without responding to Taritta's puzzled tilt of the head, Abel stroked his chin with a finger, thinking, then turned back to Subaru once more.

Unable to keep pace with the speed of his thoughts, Subaru flinched back as Abel turned toward him. Abel closed that distance, bringing his face close to Subaru's, and asked—

"—What would make the ones outside attack?"

"Eh…"

"Answer. Under what conditions would those outside move against us?"

"T-that's… you're asking me, but…"

Pressed with the question again from behind the oni mask, Subaru held his breath and stiffened. He didn't know, even asked like that. After all, even recalling what came before the Return by Death, it had been a death far too sudden to comprehend at all.

If only he had more information—then perhaps he really could offer some proper countermeasure—

"Answer."

But trampling over Subaru's hesitation and confusion, Abel hurled the question again. When Subaru failed to answer, the oni mask, still wearing its wrathful expression, gripped his small shoulder.

"Answer! Natsuki Subaru!"

"I-I don't know! Th-the moment we step outside, they attack! That's all!"

Answering as if under threat, Subaru's eyes went wide and he pressed a hand to his chest. Once again, he'd impulsively revealed information gained from a Return by Death. He braced in fear for the possibility of another penalty descending for breaking the taboo again. But nothing happened.

The surrounding time didn't stop, nor did some malicious hand come to make Subaru pay for his foolishness—neither materialized.

Instead—

"Uuu—!"

Growling, Rui wedged herself between Subaru and Abel, shielding Subaru from behind while glaring straight at Abel for his forceful stance.

Abel shook off the hand gripping his shoulder and turned an unpleasant look on Rui. But Rui immediately gained a reliable ally—Medium, who came up beside her.

"Abel-chin! Don't bully Subaru-chin! I'll tell on you to bro!"

"…What of it, should you tell him. I merely made him speak what was needed. —So stepping outside the inn is the trigger, then."

Utterly unrepentant, Abel shrugged at Medium's accusing glare. At his thoughtful demeanor even so, Medium put her hands on her hips with a "Honestly!" and turned to Subaru instead of Abel.

"Subaru-chin, you okay? Abel-chin was scary, huh~"

"I—it's not that I was scared. Just a little surprised, is all… yeah."

"Really? Well, if you say so. Good boy, Subaru-chin, such a strong kid."

As she spoke, Medium's palm gently stroked Subaru's black hair. The gentleness of her touch, and being praised as a "strong kid," brought fresh embarrassment—though there was no denying his pounding heart was slowly settling.

Something was really, truly off with him.

The relief he felt at Medium's comfort was one thing, but the bigger problem was the wordless resistance—something like fear—he felt toward Abel. Unable to push back against Abel's force of will, was he not simply being pressed down, again and again?

As if—

"As if it's an adult and a child. And not just in looks, either."

"Ngh…"

Al's voice came at Subaru's downcast profile as he pressed a hand to his chest. Still dragging the residue of his exchange with Abel, Al's tone was stiff, and Subaru's cheeks tightened as if he were being blamed.

An adult and a child—hearing it put that way, something in Subaru resolved into clarity.

Yes, as Al said, that was the fitting way to describe it. In fact, the current exchange between Subaru and Abel bore a resemblance to the power dynamic between a child and an adult.

Not just in appearance—it felt as though even the substance of him was being dragged along in that direction.

"—Listen. I have a guess as to who has laid this siege."

"I-Is that true…!?"

While Subaru tasted an unfamiliar chill, it was Abel who spoke, low and flat. At his words, Taritta's eyes widened in surprise. She wasn't the only one—everyone besides Abel, caught in the same situation, reacted the same way. More precisely, everyone except Rui, who hadn't been keeping up with the conversation.

In any case, Abel drew everyone's attention and nodded, "Indeed."

"Olbart's stated conditions, and the hundred-strong enemy force Taritta sensed… eliminate the impossible possibilities, and the remainder narrows itself naturally."

"So, what's clever Abel-chan's theory? Who's after us?"

"—Chaosflame."

"Chaosflame is… this city?"

At Al and Medium's questions, Abel nodded gravely. Then, surveying the still-uncomprehending faces of Subaru and the others, he continued.

"Chaosflame—that is, the residents of the demon capital. Those surrounding the inn from outside, watching for their moment to strike—there is little doubt they are this city's own inhabitants."

—The enemy: the residents of Chaosflame. Abel had declared it flatly.

"————"

At Abel's assertion, Subaru gaped, opening and closing his mouth, at a loss for words. —No, not just Subaru. Al and the others each wore expressions of shock as well.

Naturally so. Being told out of nowhere that the city's own people had turned hostile—

"That's not a 'so that's how it is,' though. Why would you even think that?"

"It is the most natural line of reasoning. To begin with, few factions could muster a force of a hundred to surround this inn. That alone narrows it to two choices—the emperor's retinue, or the demon capital's forces. But Chisha is impersonating me. What I would not do, he cannot easily do either."

"So it doesn't blow his cover as you? What wouldn't you do?"

"Recklessly leading his own troops into the demon capital."

Subaru nearly nodded along at the decisive tone, but he had no way to judge whether this was in fact settled information. A hundred soldiers accompanying the emperor—was that a lot, or a little?

It didn't strike him as particularly strange, honestly.

"I dunno much about it, but how many soldiers does Abel-chin's castle have?"

"Are you asking the troop count of the imperial capital? Then—"

At Medium's raised-hand question, Abel glanced briefly at Subaru. Failing to grasp the intent behind that look, Subaru watched as Abel touched a finger to the oni mask's brow, and after a moment's thought,

"No fewer than thirty thousand. But this is not a matter of total military strength. Whether the emperor would bring them along—that is the crux of the matter here. And—"

"If it were the real emperor, he wouldN't do such a thinG."

"Just so."

Abel nodded, denying that the false emperor could have made such a careless decision.

Hearing this, Subaru felt there was still a chance the impostor had simply slipped up, but sensed that saying so wouldn't be well received, so he held his tongue.

In any case—

"The emperor has declared no move be made against us, and Olbart showed a posture of compliance. That being so, dispatching troops against us defies all reason."

"…Isn't there some theory like 'I didn't personally lay a hand on them'?"

"Then let me ask—do you think I would permit such logic?"

"I do noT."

Beside Taritta's answer, Al likewise shook his masked head side to side.

Since the false emperor was impersonating Abel, his standard of judgment ought to be identical to Abel's while playing emperor. In other words, whatever Abel wouldn't permit, the false emperor couldn't permit either.

Which meant the false emperor had to keep up an awfully narrow-minded front as well.

"Even should he twist some petty logic, Kafma Irulux would not allow it. That one lacks flexibility. Precisely why he is a valuable asset—Second General Kafma would not overlook a bent interpretation."

"Kafma… the stiff-looking guy from yesterday?"

"He is obedient to the emperor, but where reason is not served, he will voice his objection without hesitation, even to a General. Not even Olbart's cunning could bend Kafma's convictions."

"That guy's got that much clout?"

"He was once approached about promotion to the Nine Divine Generals. Various circumstances saw it postponed."

At Abel's answer, Al let out a disgusted "ugh." If that were true, then yesterday's Crimson Lazuli Castle had held two people of Nine Divine Generals caliber—no, with the false emperor counted as one himself, three.

That was, admittedly, perhaps a more reliable set of hands to have on-site than some middling army.

"But if the false emperor isn't the enemy, then…"

Naturally, the conjecture Abel had voiced began to take on a ring of reality. Namely, that those waiting outside were residents of the demon capital Chaosflame, and the one who could move them—

"You mean Yorna-chan is trying to have us attacked?"

"…Hard to think of anyone else, isn't it? To begin with, there's nobody else who knows about us and has reason enough to want us targeted."

"—. What does Abel think?"

The demon capital Chaosflame, and a person able to move its residents as pawns—naturally, the first name that came to mind under those conditions was the demon capital's ruler herself, Yorna Mishigure.

But could she—whose mind was even less readable than Olbart's, no, more so—really be the mastermind who'd arranged this whole situation, orchestrating everything from the shadows?

At Subaru's question, tinged with that suspicion, Abel tapped a finger against the oni mask's brow and answered shortly,

"It does not sit right."

"Doesn't sit right, meaning, um, you're not convinced? Why?"

"To assume every question yields an answer would be arrogance. —The letter."

"The letter?"

Adding an unnecessary remark, Abel answered Subaru's question that way.

The letter referred to the missive delivered to Yorna yesterday. Delivering that letter had been yesterday's objective, and it was presumably the reason for today's summons. Though Abel hadn't told them exactly what was written in it.

"In the letter, I wrote that whatever she desires shall be her reward."

"That older sister's desire… meaning, the throne of empress?"

"A bride?"

At Abel's mention of the letter's contents, Al and Medium spoke up one after another. From yesterday's conversation, what Yorna wanted was "the emperor"—not Abel specifically, but the position of emperor, or so it had seemed.

So if he'd written that he'd give her what she wanted, that must be what it meant.

"Is that really something you should say in a letter?"

"Do not shrink this matter down to your own scale. Regardless, she stands to gain what she desires. That is precisely why she summoned us to the castle. Dispatching troops against us makes no sense in light of that."

"Aah, is there a chance that she's so mad she wants to kill you, like, mad enough that becoming Abel-chan's bride sounds worse than death…"

Or perhaps something rude enough was written in the letter to make her murderous.

Given how he behaved even in direct conversation, Subaru thought it highly likely Abel treated even a prospective bride with the same arrogance.

But Abel scoffed at their reasoning,

"I would do what is necessary. Emotion is secondary. I am not Priscilla."

"I thought that older sister had something in common with the princess, troublesome-wise!"

That, Subaru agreed with too. Priscilla and Yorna—both were frightening.

Still, spoken with such conviction, Abel's reasoning began to seem to have a point. —And, thinking back on Yorna from yesterday, he had to admit—

"I too am mistress of this demon capital—I'll speak no falsehood before my own retainers."

Yes, that had been Yorna's smilingly-spoken promise—more precisely, a pledge that she wouldn't throw away the letter Subaru and the others had delivered without reading it.

But afterward, when Subaru and the others had fallen into the stable, Yorna's attendant Tanza had told them, "Yorna has acknowledged you."

At the very least, Subaru hadn't taken her for the sort of woman who'd casually go back on her own word.

Since he hadn't seen her that way, he wanted to believe Abel's reasoning too.

"In any case, those outsidE will lose patience soon enougH. What shall we dO?"

Keeping watch on the outside, Taritta pressed for a decision, saying they were nearing their limit.

The condition Abel had named for the attack—the reason, so to speak, for the enemy to strike—was, for now, believed to be simply stepping outside. But given that the other side was preparing to attack, there was no telling when they might force their way in regardless.

On top of that, Subaru and the others were in the middle of their game of hide-and-seek.

"Even knowing the people outside will come rushing at us, we can't just not go out, right?"

"Well, if that old man's outfoxed us and gone into hiding at the inn again for round two, that'd change things… want me to check the rooms once more, just in case?"

"Had we no lead on the 'Abyss with a fine view,' I might have considered it."

Rejected by Abel, Al tilted his head, looking disappointed. In truth, Subaru too had wondered whether Olbart might be hiding in the first room again. But no matter how he thought it over, it wouldn't connect to "Abyss with a fine view," so he'd said nothing.

Even as they talked, their course of action solidified. Naturally, there was no option but to strike out. The question was what plan to employ to make that option succeed.

"—Taritta, draw their eyes."

"Abel!?"

In that instant, what Abel proposed was about the cruelest instruction imaginable. Taritta's cheeks stiffened at the words, and Subaru's voice cracked as he denounced Abel. But Abel ignored Subaru's protest, keeping his gaze on Taritta, and pressed on.

"Rampage greatly, draw the attention of those outside. In that interval, we make our escape."

"Abel-chan, isn't there some better plan we could actually understand…"

"There is none. Given our current hand, this is the best strategy available. Had you all not shrunk, other means might have been explored."

"That's one word too many!" "Auu—!"

Abel cut down Al, who was worried for Taritta's safety, with that remark. At his coldness, Subaru snapped at him, and Rui likewise raised a voice of righteous indignation. But to that reaction from Subaru and the others—

"No, understooD. I'll draw the attention of those outsidE."

"Taritta-san! Even so, that's…"

Shaking her head, Taritta moved to accept Abel's reckless order.

Subaru wanted, somehow, to secure her safety with a better plan. But all that came to his mind now were two kinds of thoughts—wariness and anxiety about the terribly dangerous enemy outside.

"Taritta, hand over the pack. It will be needed further ahead."

"Here you gO. Once I've drawn their attention sufficientlY, please slip out through the openinG. I doubt there'll be time to give a signaL…"

"Medium shall judge that."

"Eh, me?"

Abel took the pack Taritta had been carrying, slinging the bag over onto the thinner one. As he continued laying out the plan, Medium, named directly, widened her eyes.

Turning to her, Abel nodded, "Indeed."

"Shrunk though you may be, your eye for timing remains. Among us, you are best suited."

"Hmm~, got it. I'll watch real careful too! So Taritta-chan, you be careful too, okay?"

"YeS."

Leaving Subaru behind, the discussion was proceeding at a rapid clip. Abel aside, Taritta and Medium were far too composed—Taritta especially, given she'd just been entrusted with the most dangerous role.

"WelL, I'll be off theN."

Gripping her bow, Taritta edged forward and put a hand to the back door. Unable to hold back, Subaru called out to her back, "Taritta-san!"

"Um, p-please don't die…!"

What an obvious thing to blurt out, not even worth calling encouragement, he despised himself even as he said it. If he couldn't offer a useful opinion or a surefire strategy, he should at least say something to raise Taritta's spirits.

And yet all that came out was a trembling plea. —But Taritta's eyes softened slightly at the corners,

"YeS, let's meet again latER."

With a faint smile, Taritta's body burst outward as if flung.

Just before, as Taritta headed into enemy territory, Abel threw one last remark at her back.

It was—

"Taritta, there is no need to hold back. Fight in earnest. —No one in this city, whoever they may be, can be killed so easily."

—an eerie warning that could be called neither encouragement nor a guaranteed strategy.

—The instant she stepped outside, hostility swelled up with ferocious force.

Tasting it against her dark skin, Taritta swept her narrowed eyes across the full breadth to either side in a single sweep.

Deep in the jungle, for the People of Shudrak who lived as hunters, instantly grasping terrain and circumstance was a bare minimum required skill, and Taritta was no exception to that.

—No, rather, Taritta was the one entrusted with the chieftainship by her sister Mizelda.

Therefore, Taritta needed to excel at these skills above even her fellow Shudrak. And in fact, she did.

"—Ah."

A faint catch of breath in her throat, Taritta gauged the number of hostilities aimed at her.

Around her, those harboring battle intent numbered roughly a hundred, but of those, fewer than twenty had caught sight of Taritta as she'd leapt from the back door.

On top of that, the number capable of moving quickly would be fewer still.

For now, starting with the ones who'd turned to face her—

"—No."

The warning Abel had given just before she'd leapt out—fight in earnest—flitted through her mind.

Abel was arrogant, and a handsome man. He fit the conditions of the sort of man Mizelda favored, and Taritta wasn't particularly good with that type. Weak of will herself, she was easily swept along, and when facing someone of her sister's or Abel's disposition, she found herself unable to voice a single opinion of her own.

So Taritta preferred someone who would listen. Especially since she herself took time to gather her thoughts, being rushed often left her head spinning.

From that standpoint, Flop, whom she'd left behind in Guaral, was so much more—

"—! What am I thinking about right nOw."

Her face reddening with a flash of embarrassment, Taritta drew her bowstring taut as if venting her frustration.

Then, in an instant, arrows nocked to the bow were loosed—three at once. Taritta's archery, showing its true form, sent three arrows each toward a different target.

One flew straight; two curved through the air, cutting the wind of the alley outside the inn, and struck their prey.

The three men who had turned toward the leaping Taritta and started forward had their necks and chests pierced respectively, stripping them of combat ability in an instant.

"Hunting iS fine."

Not having to speak with anyone—that suited Taritta.

Prey didn't want Taritta to speak. Nor did Taritta wish to share feelings with her prey. The only exchange resembling dialogue was the meeting of loosed arrow and target.

And even that—the outcome of life and death—needed no discussion to reach.

"—Ohh!"

With a roar, multiple shadows leapt into the alley to replace the pierced men.

The first to enter Taritta's field of view was a bull-type beastman of towering build. The man, with short, thick horns growing from the crown of his head, hurled himself forward, a furious charge leveraging his massive frame.

A direct hit from the front would shatter every bone in Taritta's slender body.

That said, there was no room to flee behind or to the side. Leaping upward risked having her leg caught.

Having assessed that much, Taritta lowered her body and ran forward.

"Wha—!?"

Whether the move was unexpected, the bull-man's eyes went wide, his throat catching. Taritta extended her leg toward that bull-man's face, crushing his snout and using it as a foothold.

Bending her knee to absorb the impact softly, Taritta's body leapt lightly into the air off the face of the nosebleeding bull-man. Then, rotating halfway, she took an inverted posture in midair.

"————"

Spinning round and round, she secured a full three-hundred-sixty-degree field of view, including what had been behind her moments before, catching all at once the shadows lurking in building alcoves, atop roofs, on the scaffolding strung throughout the city—everyone poised to strike from such positions.

"Not enough arrowS."

Even as she said it, she pinched a bundle of arrows from her quiver, drawing them out and nocking them to her bow at a dizzying speed, unleashing volleys of three in a raging torrent, whittling down the enemy.

The number of enemies she'd spotted didn't match her stock of arrows. Left with no choice, she'd have to whittle down the highest-priority threats first, and from long experience, Taritta targeted those with the greatest ability.

The faint sense of wrongness she felt at the sight of those in view, she ignored for this moment—

"Guaaaaah—!?"

A raging storm of arrows tore through the air, and those struck were flung back by the impact.

Following Abel's warning, she aimed without mercy at vital points—chest, throat for everyone, eyes and mouth wherever she could manage—dealing fatal blows, striking down roughly thirty enemies.

However, her arrows had already run dry in this first clash.

All that remained was to recover as many spent arrows as possible while continuing to play decoy—

"…This iS a bit unexpecteD."

Taritta's feet stopped as she moved to retrieve arrows from those she'd pierced and felled.

Taritta too, as a Shudrak warrior, had pierced countless beasts over the years. Though the shape might differ somewhat, the feel of striking a living thing's vital point was something she could recognize the instant the bow snapped.

And based on that experience, she'd been certain she had taken the lives of those she'd struck.

"Guh, guh…"

And yet, of those groaning and rising back up, not a single one had lost their life.

Even setting aside death, they ought to have been at death's door. But those standing back up were far from near death—their eyes, glaring at Taritta, hadn't even lost their fighting spirit.

And in those eyes fixed on Taritta, a change was occurring.

"What iS that, exactlY?"

Frowning, Taritta questioned the risen bull-man. He gave no answer, but the change that had come over his body was too bizarre, too eye-catching to ignore.

—His right eye was covered in blazing red flame.

"————"

The bull-man wasn't the only one with a burning eye.

All those Taritta had shot down and felled, whether it was the right eye or the left that differed man to man, each now bore a red flame in one eye, fixed on Taritta.

Nor were those whose eyes now burned limited to the ones Taritta had pierced.

Those who'd arrived late to surround the newly-emerged Taritta bore the same red flame in their eyes as well—flickering, scattering sparks as it burned.

And astonishingly, that same flame was searing shut and healing the wounds the bull-men had suffered.

The bull-man's crushed snout, the arrow wounds of the pierced—all of it, healing completely.

"…Aren't you being a touch stingy with words, AbeL?"

That sight, overlapping with Abel's earlier warning, drew that comment out of Taritta along with a breath.

She couldn't be certain, but Abel had likely anticipated this turn of events, at least in part. If so, she wished he'd been kind enough to spell it out more clearly.

Even as she thought that, she caught herself wishing for something convenient from someone she'd just decided she wasn't suited to dealing with, the sort who pushed conversations along too fast.

But still—

"My role is decoY, not to wipe out the enemY."

So, in terms of focusing on her role, this could be called a success.

As for the rest—

"…They snap the arrows, of coursE."

Those she'd pierced were snapping, one after another, the arrows that had fallen from their bodies. Her hope of recovering her spent arrows dashed, Taritta found her prized archery sealed off.

Still, that didn't mean she was out of options—that too would be a mistake.

"Hunting also makes use of daggers, and thrown stoneS."

Saying so, Taritta lowered her stance, readying the dagger concealed within her formal wear alongside the now-arrowless bow.

Still, Taritta's fight to fulfill her role was only just beginning.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—! Taritta-san, incredible…!"

At the sight of these people, their wounds ablaze and their eyes burning, Subaru's voice trembled as he ran.

Taritta's valiant fight, the one they'd sent her out to wage, showed skill far beyond anything Subaru had imagined. Honestly, he'd underestimated just how capable the shy, quiet Taritta was among the Shudrak.

He'd even seen her, not long before, brought low by helplessness against Olbart.

Of course, given that Mizelda had named her the next chieftain, he'd figured she couldn't be too far off from Kuna or Holly in raw ability. But watching that precise aim, that rapid-fire barrage, that spectacular one-woman stand against overwhelming numbers, he began to wonder if she might rival Mizelda, or even surpass her in skill.

"Even so…!"

Even Taritta's evident skill couldn't erase the strangeness of those assassins. To begin with, their very appearance was bizarre. None of them wore armor, none carried anything resembling a weapon. They weren't armed at all.

Their look was, to all appearances, identical to the many people Subaru and the others had passed in Chaosflame since yesterday—nothing more than ordinary citizens of this demon capital, distinguished only by their eye-catching appearance.

And yet these very citizens, having surrounded Subaru's group, now rose back to their feet with utter composure even after taking fatal wounds from Taritta's masterful arrows.

From a distance, the assassins appeared to include women and children among their number—not a gathering of hardened warriors. Surely even Taritta must be shaken by that.

She'd magnificently drawn their attention, and on Medium's signal Subaru's group had burst out into the city. But was it really right to flee like this? Shouldn't they be supporting Taritta instead?

Unable to tell what was right, the options kept spinning round and round in Subaru's head.

Beside him, Abel, having resettled the bag on his shoulder, watched the same scene out of the corner of his eye—

"So then, they've laced 'Soul Marriage Technique' throughout the entire city after all."

"Knock knock…? Hey now, Abel-chan, what's that supposed to mean?"

"The trick behind those who face Taritta and will not fall."

As Al ran along clumsily, Abel gave an answer to his question that wasn't quite an answer.

The content didn't quite settle into Subaru's mind, but one thing was clear—Abel had some idea of the connection between those burning eyes and the assassins.

"Abel! Don't keep secrets! Tell us everything!"

"—. It is one of the lost secret arts spoken of in ancient texts. Called the 'Soul Marriage Technique,' it involves dividing a portion of one's own soul and granting it to another, thereby adding to that other's worth."

"I don't get it! What's that mean!"

"Put plainly, souls bound by the 'Soul Marriage Technique' share a portion of their power between them. And in this city, there is but one practitioner of the 'Soul Marriage Technique'—"

Pressed from both sides by Subaru and Al, Abel offered as digestible an answer as he could manage. Even so, the content remained difficult for Subaru to fully grasp, but somehow, he managed to catch at least the gist of it.

In other words—
