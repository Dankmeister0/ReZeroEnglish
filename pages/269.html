Re:Zero EX: "The Dream of the Butterfly"

Site of the April Fools' Special Extra Chapter.

Do you happen to know what "The Dream of the Butterfly" means?

One day, a man dreamed that he had become a butterfly, fluttering gently through the sky. In and of itself, that has no deep meaning, but when he awoke from that dream, a thought occurred to him:

"Well now, did I just awaken from dreaming I was a butterfly? Or is the me standing here right now merely a dream being dreamed by the butterfly?"

Don't you think that's an intriguing way of looking at things? This isn't a thought limited only to butterflies. What matters is: what proof is there that allows us to definitively define "the self that exists here and now"?

When you behold a scene that would be a "dream" to someone, can you really say that the self existing there isn't truly yourself? When you see a place that might well be the ideal world—could you truly give it up, resigning yourself to the idea that it was only a dream?

Or perhaps, when someone's logic swings so completely toward the dream, one might describe it as... having lost sight of the boundary between dream and reality. No, no, beating around the bush is a bad habit of mine. Let's summarize what I wish to say a bit more clearly and concisely this time.

In short, though it was only for a fleeting moment, there was indeed a dreamlike scene that existed right here. It was a world apart from the natural flow of time, and whether things truly transpired that way is uncertain... but whether it was a world where you became a butterfly and danced in the air, or a reality that the dancing you desperately wishes to believe was "just a dream"—in the end, deciding that comes down not to those around you, but to your own perception.

—The world he sees presents him with a different facet time and time again. To select a single world from among them and walk forward in the reality meant for him is the duty thrust upon him. So then, what becomes of the worlds that were not grasped?

Troubling my mind over answerless questions is a pleasure of mine, as well. In time, will he affirm or deny the dream he saw? Or how will he judge whether the world he walks is dream or reality?

And that I cannot witness it at his side is terribly regrettable. Goodness, I made a blunder quite uncharacteristic of myself.
