Arc 9 Chapter 2: "The Villain Doesn't Put on Airs"

"Kakakakka! As if someone like me was gonna die in some safe zone 'stead of the battlefield. Never heard of a shinobi dyin' of old age—too damn ironic for words, ain't it."

So saying, the eccentric old man laughed uproariously, waving the empty sleeves where his hands used to be. Listening to that booming laugh, Garfiel felt conflicted—healing magic could only do so much.

—After the battle against the "Great Disaster," Garfiel had joined the healers' corps and was tending to the wounded.

Ever since arriving in the Empire, Garfiel had fought one desperate, life-or-death battle after another with barely a moment's rest. Naturally, his companions had all told him, one after another, that resting came first. But he couldn't bring himself to sit still. Fortunately, Garfiel's body was tough, and thanks to his Divine Protection of the Spirits of the Earth, simply standing on the ground was the best rest he could get. Of everyone in the Emilia Camp, Garfiel was the furthest from working himself to death. Needless to say, the one closest to it was Otto. In any case—

If you've decided to do it once, there's no point arguing, is there? Then you might as well sell the Empire every last favor you can. Strike while the gratitude's hot.

Yes—while everyone else had opposed Garfiel's decision, there had been one person who had clearly, plainly taken his side, and that had made him happy. Even her cynical way of putting it—he liked that about her, that it was so her. And so, even while his companions looked at him half-exasperated, Garfiel had volunteered for this duty. In the end, no one had tried to stop him—probably because everyone understood, deep down.

"If the big man who's got it worst of all is doin' that much, we sure as hell ain't got time to be standin' around."

Grumbling that, Garfiel clicked his sharp canines together. The one they'd lost had been strong—truly strong. He couldn't say he'd fought the same enemy side by side with her, but the dazzling blaze that had risen again and again over the imperial capital was burned into his eyes all the same. It wasn't a loss he could just call "Emilia's rival is gone" and leave it at that.

That was exactly why he was here. To keep even one more person from being lost. With these same hands that had to smash the Undead without mercy, that sometimes had to force aside enemies standing in his comrades' way—with these hands, he would pick lives back up. It was a contradiction. But that was fine. He wasn't going to overthink it. He'd just follow his heart.

Follow his heart, beat back enemies with his own strength, heal his allies, save them. If staying awake meant saving even one more person, then he'd keep doing it.

"...Damn, you young'uns really do leave us old folk behind in a hurry. That's the kinda thing that makes you so infuriatin'."

"Huh? That sarcasm, old man? Just 'cause your arms can't be healed by me—"

"Kakakakka! Ain't no sarcasm in it. Let me tell you somethin'—decades of sayin' 'I envy you, I'm jealous of you' is what's kept me strong all this time. When I say I'm envious, that ain't no lie. Ahh, envious, jealous."

Wagging his long white eyebrows up and down, the armless Olbart deftly scratched his own neck with his foot. The way he carried on as though not having hands were no inconvenience at all—Garfiel couldn't tell if it was consideration for him, given that healing magic couldn't help the man, or if he was just being teased. Either way, setting the missing arms aside, the old man had been at death's door not long ago, and yet he was already moving around this much—a monster in his own right. Though, if it came to talking about someone who'd nearly died and survived—

"—Shut your damn mouth already, you crusty old geezer! Someone's dead damn tired workin' their ass off for you, and you're givin' 'em a hard time!"

"Ohh, scary, scary. That ain't the vigor of a half-dead man."

At the furious shout from the figure sprawled on the bed in the back, Olbart chuckled low in his throat. Garfiel, faced with that fierce outburst, scratched his head and said, "Now hold on—"

"I appreciate you backin' me up, but you oughta be worryin' about your own damn body first. You're one step from really dyin'—lookin' like a walkin' deathbed offering, that's what you look like."

"Ahh?! I'm sayin' I'm damn grateful thanks to you! You gonna go and say you can't take my damn gratitude or somethin'—guaaaargh!"

"Shut up and lie down!"

Garfiel pressed a hand to the short man's head, and the man—who let out a scream through his whole body—toppled backward. It was exactly the kind of severe injury that would warrant such a reaction, damage that outpaced even continuous full-power healing magic. In fact, even now, he had to be in constant, unrelenting agony running through his whole body.

"Not that I'm one to talk, but you sure manage to stay awake and yappin'."

"Damn hurtin' or damn achin' ain't gonna stop this Groovy Gumlet from doin' what he's gotta do. If it takes poison or medicine or whatever damn trick to fool my own body into it, then—gaaaaahhh...!"

"Now, now. And look what happened when you ignored every signal your body was sendin' you—that's the state you're in now. 'Bout on par with me and my missin' hands, deathbed-wise. Why not retire?"

"Like hell I will, you damn old fool!"

Baring his fangs and snarling at the teasing Olbart was Groovy Gumlet—one of the Nine Divine Generals, and quite possibly the person in the Empire who had come closest to death and lived to tell of it. In the desperate battle against the "Great Disaster," the role he'd played had been enormous. After all, he had crossed blades with the very protagonist of "Iris and the King of Thorns" itself—the "Thornthrone Emperor" Eugard Vollachia—and, working together with Halibel, had been the one who turned back that curse and won the day.

"If you hadn't done that, everyone woulda been stuck helpless in them damn thorns, that's the story I heard."

"Just squeezed out what damn little I could. After seein' off the 'Thornthrone Emperor' and the 'Worshipper,' all I did was lie around till it was time to hand the damn 'Sinsword' over to that damn Cecilus."

Groovy grumbled it out sullenly, but the work he'd done before ending up flat on his back had been enormous. The story of how he'd taken on Eugard's curse into his own body, letting the "Sinsword" cut him down along with it, made no sense no matter how many times Garfiel heard it. But according to Groovy and the others, Garfiel taking on the "Cloud Dragon" made just as little sense—so it was mutual.

The accumulation of each person's best efforts and small miracles—that was the reality that had unfolded that day in the imperial capital, no, across the whole of the Empire. Garfiel, who had fought the "Cloud Dragon"; Groovy, who had saved the "Thornthrone Emperor" from the thorns; Olbart, who had stopped the Witch's scheme at the cost of both his arms—every one of them had given everything they had. There was no need to praise those achievements to excess, nor any need to deny the facts.

"Well, for an old geezer with not much time left, watchin' decent young folk drop one after another sure does take a toll, so I'm plenty glad you lot survived."

"...Me, sure, but whether that one over there counts as havin' survived, that's a damn shaky claim."

"That one bein'..."

At the mention of whoever it was they were talking about, Garfiel's gaze drifted to the edge of the infirmary. There, he found a strikingly distinctive figure standing stock-still, impossible to miss. Even now, with people bustling busily back and forth tending to the wounded, the figure stood pressed into the corner of the infirmary as though trying to blend into the wall so as not to get in the way—a being with a body made of steel. The moment it noticed Garfiel's gaze, the green gem set in what looked to be its head lit up, and it said—

"I. Detect gaze. Predict: business, order, or instruction."

Speaking so, clunking heavily toward Garfiel and the others, was the one called the "Steelfolk"—another of the Nine Divine Generals, Moguro Hagane. Though, to be precise, this wasn't actually a member of the Steelfolk race—the truth of the matter, apparently, was that its true identity was the Meteor that served as the core of the Crystal Palace. Now that the all-important Crystal Palace and the Magic Crystal Cannon had both been destroyed, that title carried no meaning anymore, but—

"Ah, my bad. My eyes just happened to land your way, ain't got no business with you."

"Acknowledged. The other two?"

"Ain't nothin'. Just stand around there, don't be a damn nuisance, Moguro."

"Moguro. That is me. Acknowledged. Instruction: will wait again."

Beside the awkward-faced Garfiel, Groovy jerked his chin and issued the order, and Moguro obediently complied, plopping down flat beside the bed. Sitting there like that, it looked rather like an oversized decoration.

"Sweet as a well-trained Earth Dragon or Thunder Wolf, ain't it. Though I'll say, even the Crystal Palace's got itself some memory loss, seems."

"Crystal Palace and the Magic Crystal Cannon both got damn blown to bits. It's a damn miracle Moguro's core even survived. And it just so happened to drop near where I was sleepin'—that's a bit too damn convenient too."

"Came to see the fella it got along with best, maybe?"

Chuckling and cracking wise, Olbart got a displeased snort out of Groovy in response. As their conversation made clear, Moguro Hagane remembered nothing at all of anything before the "Great Disaster." According to Subaru, it was something like a "reset state"—everything the Meteor had built up as Moguro Hagane until now had apparently vanished entirely.

Vast power that could have leveled the whole imperial capital, forcibly suppressed and carried up into the sky, then made to explode so as not to drag the surrounding area down with it—given that, perhaps the price paid should be called light.

"How's the hell am I supposed to say somethin' like that, with what happened to Rem..."

Just how heavy a thing it was for a person's "memories" to disappear—Garfiel understood that all too keenly, from someone close to him. It wasn't only the person who lost the "memories" who suffered—those around them, the ones who'd been lost to, were shaken deeply too. So he understood the feeling—though he wasn't about to say something so glib as that.

"Might be, this old geezer here ain't wrong, though."

"Ahh? Even you gonna crack damn jokes and mess with me now?"

"Nah. Sure, might be he don't remember a damn thing. But look—this one here ain't tryin' to leave your side, is he."

So saying, Garfiel narrowed his eyes at Moguro sitting beside the bed. Honestly, having that huge frame parked in the infirmary was a nuisance, but the way it made itself as compact as possible, trying not to bother anyone more than it had to, earned some goodwill. In the first place, the reason Moguro was staying here at all, going to such lengths, was surely because it didn't want to leave Groovy's side.

Maybe that was something like imprinting—the first person it had recognized after losing its "memories." Still, when Ram and Rem had finally been reunited here in the Empire, Rem had said she'd felt a strange sensation, an intuition that told her Ram was her older sister. Maybe some kind of "something" like that remained inside Moguro too, Garfiel thought.

"Well, the big man did say Rem went and broke his fingers right off the bat, so..."

There were cases where that kind of "something" hadn't remained, so it wasn't something you could generalize about. But if this infirmary was a place for healing someone, and Moguro—alone, in a place where no one knew it—was here trying to heal its own heart, then Garfiel had no reason to chase it out.

"If anything, it's the geezer here who can't be helped and won't shut up that's more of a damn nuisance."

"Kakakakka! Look at this whelp, talkin' back! Well, I get where you're comin' from, though."

Olbart laughed, flapping both his empty sleeves, at Garfiel's shrugging response. Off to the side of their exchange, Groovy rubbed his own black nose with a finger and looked over at Moguro. The comrade-in-arms who didn't so much as twitch, simply staying close by his side.

"Don't go gettin' all damn sentimental on me, damn it."

"Detected in statement: sound of water. Possible emergency. Please instruct."

"Shut the damn hell up!"

"Acknowledged."

Hearing that utterly pointless exchange, Garfiel couldn't help but click his fangs and laugh. He laughed, then hesitated a moment over whether it was all right to laugh—then, following his heart, laughed properly, all the way.

"—So then, Lady Anastasia, you'll be returning to Lugunica by way of Kararagi."

"Mm, that's the plan. I came into the Empire in the first place under the pretense of bein' a special envoy from Kararagi. Sure, everyone knows it's all just formality, but formality or not, gotta see it through properly."

Otto received Anastasia's languid smile and her open admission of the state of affairs with an outward calm—"I see," nothing more, as though it were of no particular consequence. But inwardly, he couldn't remain as untroubled as his expression suggested. Whatever the circumstances, Anastasia had fulfilled the duty she'd promised the Kararagi City-States. This would raise her standing with the Kararagi Council that had sent her out, and Anastasia would once again catch a solid tailwind in the Royal Selection.

Of course, Emilia too—faced with the Vollachia Empire's crisis of survival, personally asked for aid by Emperor Vincent himself—had contributed greatly to the fight against the "Great Disaster." Where they'd entered the Empire essentially as illegal border-crossers, they could now say they'd return home with enough of a trophy in hand to make for a proper, triumphant homecoming. And yet—even so, it wasn't good that not just the Emilia Camp, but the Anastasia Camp too, had a hand in the fight against the Witch Cult in Priestella, the conquest of the Pleiades Watchtower, and now the rescue of the Vollachia Empire as well. What they needed was renown belonging to Emilia alone, not standing shoulder to shoulder with Anastasia's.

Of course, if they hadn't had a hand in this fight against the "Great Disaster" that had struck the Empire this time, their camp's standing would have fallen far further behind than merely that.

"You've got an awful lot to think about, don't you, Otto-kun? Rough."

Into that momentary silence, Anastasia's pale-blue eyes bit through. It was almost certainly a fishing question, and yet the way she asked it made it feel as though she held some certainty already—probably a product of the shrewdness Anastasia had honed through sheer experience. Against that probing of hers, Otto returned a smile and said, "No, not really,"

"It's true my mind is heavy, but if we're talking about who has the most to think over, I'm nothing compared to His Imperial Majesty or Chancellor Berstetz, who have to rebuild the Empire from here."

So saying, Otto turned the topic away from himself, toward the other person present—the Imperial Chancellor, Berstetz. In the meeting room where Anastasia, Berstetz, and Otto sat face to face, discussion had turned, now that the battle against the "Great Disaster" was over, to the future policies of those who'd cooperated with Vollachia as it fought its way toward reconstruction and recovery. Amid that, Berstetz, prompted by Otto, kept the color hidden in the depths of his thread-thin eyes and said,

"As you say, the challenges are piled high. In truth, however, this humble servant does not consider them a burden."

"Heh, is that so? Awfully reliable thing to say."

"To begin with, this humble servant is a scheming vassal who colluded with the 'White Spider' Chisha Gold to endanger His Imperial Majesty's very position. Given the circumstances, my head was not taken during the battle against the 'Great Disaster'—but by rights, this is a body that should not escape execution once the war is over."

"...And yet, here you are, speaking with us. There will be no such punishment, will there?"

"Indeed. His Imperial Majesty has decided as much. Naturally, should there be those who feel they cannot forgive this humble servant, I thought it only fitting to accept their blade should it come to that, but..."

Berstetz trailed off there, and Otto could guess well enough what he was thinking in that silence. It was, most likely, the greatest reason Berstetz's head still remained attached to his shoulders—the declaration made, after the war, by the strongest man in the Empire, the "Blue Lightning."

"Just so no bit players get any bright ideas, let me declare this now—not killing Berstetz-san was His Excellency's own decision, so all of you had better follow it too, all right? And if anyone tries to go against that, well, I suppose I'll have to show off my skills as His Excellency's blade again, it's been a while. Also, I dunno, somehow I get the feeling that even His Excellency not killing Berstetz-san was part of Chisha's scheme. I mean, seriously, isn't it gonna be rough going forward without Berstetz-san around? Chisha's not around anymore either, after all."

It had been far too long-winded, said all in one breath, to really be called a "declaration," but that it had been the deciding factor in making those who knew of Berstetz's misdeeds hold back was undeniable. Berstetz, apparently thinking the same thing, twisted his mouth slightly and said,

"To think this humble servant would be shielded by First General Cecilus Segmunt of all people. Were it not for the 'Sunblade,' I had thought that man's blade would surely have taken this head."

"That's not what happened, though. It's the Empire's internal affair, so an outsider like me can't say much either way, but I do think it was a wise decision. In times of crisis, there's no replacing a skilled military officer—and there's no replacing a skilled civil officer in the times before and after one, either."

"Agreed. If even Berstetz-san got taken outta the picture and I was the only one left who could do the thinkin', even His Majesty, with his cute wife cheerin' him on and all, might just about drop dead, don't you think?"

"Indeed. For my part, too, I am glad that His Imperial Majesty has at long last taken an Empress. Absent some cause for celebration, His Imperial Majesty has had far too much sorrow piled upon him of late."

"—————"

Berstetz shook his head slowly, murmuring that, and silence fell over the meeting room. Within that silence, Otto's awareness drifted, for a moment, toward Anastasia. She, too, was tasting that same silence—though surely its flavor was different for her than it was for him. The reason for this silence needed no explaining—the calamities said to have piled up on Vincent: the death of his most trusted retainer, and of the younger sister who shared his blood. The latter's impact was not something Otto, nor Anastasia, could dismiss lightly.

"Honestly. Such a selfish princess she was."

The sound Anastasia's lips murmured, softly, made Otto close his eyes. The faint weight carried in those words felt like a small, rare crack in Anastasia—someone he'd thought possessed a boldness unshaken by any turn of events. It was the very weakness Otto had always, quietly, wished to see in Anastasia, in order to surpass her—and now that long-awaited weakness, he let go of, without the slightest inclination to use it. If he were asked why—

"Because it isn't reproducible."

The opening that had appeared here wasn't some convenient tool he could use again next time. Recreating the same opportunity would be difficult, and even if he could, he shouldn't. After all, a hole far bigger and deeper than the one that had opened in Anastasia had opened among his own people. Honestly speaking, that, too, wasn't something Otto found desirable. Over these past few months, the Emilia Camp and the Anastasia Camp had drawn too close together.

"You don't have to try so hard to consciously play the villain, you know."

"—. Was that... perhaps directed at me?"

Otto reacted to a voice that came, suddenly, from a third party—neither Anastasia nor Berstetz. His gaze turned toward where the voice had come from: Anastasia's collar, the fox stole she'd taken the form of—the Spirit Echidna, watching him with black eyes. At Otto's words, Echidna narrowed her eyes slightly and said,

"That you took it as directed at yourself—isn't that proof enough of what's in your heart?"

"Words spoken while looking straight at me aren't exactly convincing."

"Stubborn, aren't you. A fine match for Ana."

Echidna spoke in a sulky tone, and her fox head was pressed down with a "now, now" by a slender finger. Anastasia. She looked down at her own stole, tangled up with Otto, and said,

"Don't go pesterin' Otto-kun like that. The second you're free to talk to anyone you like, you go and turn into such a chatterbox."

"Mm, don't make it sound as though I'm simply speaking out of my own impulses. Though, I'll admit, given my current position, the previous circumstances were rather stifling."

"Honestly. Sorry 'bout that, Otto-kun, my kid buttin' in."

Apologized to so by Anastasia, Otto merely waved a hand and said, "Not at all." That single remark of Echidna's—that had surely been meant to assert her own presence, not truly out of concern for Otto. Naturally, Echidna, who stayed closest to Anastasia, must have noticed the same crack in Anastasia that Otto had. That remark had been a check, meant to keep him from probing it further. As long as Otto's inner resolve—that he had no intention of exploiting that opening—remained invisible to her, such a check had been necessary. And yet—

"I don't think of myself as a villain. ...If it's a role, then I'm only playing it."

In that sense, Echidna's remark had missed the mark entirely. Even the news of Priscilla's death—which had shaken Anastasia herself, and brought a disturbance to the camp greater even than that—Otto found, to a degree that even he found coldhearted of himself, that it hadn't shaken his heart. He'd had his share of conversations with Priscilla, and he'd acknowledged that she was, without question, a formidable figure among the royal candidates. And still—

"In the midst of the Royal Selection, it's entirely plausible for a candidate to be lost."

Granted, joining a fight for the survival of a neighboring nation and losing her life there was a case beyond anyone's expectations. But even setting that aside, when it came to political struggles tied to matters of national importance, there had always been a real possibility that an assassin might be sent after a royal candidate's life, and that she might fall to that murderous blade. In the Emilia Camp's case, the twist had been that the assassin sent turned out to be, of all people, one of their own—but setting aside such exceptions, the general point still held.

Otto didn't think the Council of Wise Men, currently overseeing Lugunica's governance, had failed to consider such a situation at all. Should a royal candidate be lost, would someone new be brought in to fill that seat, or would the Royal Selection continue on with one of its five seats left vacant? The latter seemed the more likely outcome—and, if Otto were honest, the one he'd prefer. Given that this was a matter of grave importance for the kingdom as well—

"Within the next few days, we're set to return to the kingdom. I imagine, from the Empire's side too, you'd rather avoid having outsiders learn any more of your internal affairs after the 'Great Disaster' than necessary."

"Same for us. Wouldn't make much sense to march in step with each other, so we'll set out for Kararagi as soon as we're ready—maybe even tomorrow. Otto-kun, the report to the castle..."

"Please leave that to us. It's not the kind of matter that can be handled through letters."

Anastasia dipped her chin, entrusting the duty of reporting to the royal castle over to their side. Frankly, it was a heavy duty to bear, but at the same time, a weighty responsibility. Of course, Otto would accompany her for this, but it would be an exceedingly painful role for Emilia to play. At times like this, it ought to be the First Knight's duty to support Emilia above all else.

"...The way Natsuki-san is right now, it might have the opposite effect on Lady Emilia."

"...From how you're sayin' that, sounds like Natsuki-kun's condition hasn't changed any."

"No. Sir Julius has come by to check on him several times, but it's difficult. This might sound strange to say, but I always had the impression Lady Priscilla did nothing but drag him around..."

"For Natsuki-kun, that part of it don't matter none. —It's dangerous."

Speaking in a quiet tone, Anastasia's fingertip tickled at Echidna's neck. Trembling at the touch, Echidna's black eyes, too, seemed to be worrying over Subaru. In that, there was a plain, unadulterated warmth, from both Anastasia and Echidna, that Otto could feel.

"I understand the opinions of both the kingdom's and the city-states' envoys. His Imperial Majesty has granted this humble servant the authority to see that due courtesy is paid to both nations. Should anything arise—"

"Wouldn't say lightly, no. Bein' handed a blank promissory note is exactly the thing that makes a person most nervous."

Berstetz, keeping his emotions hidden behind those thread-thin eyes, drew a wry laugh from Anastasia. Otto shared her sentiment, but he also thought that whatever words could be drawn out here should be put to full use. This was his post, after all—the one who took on the role of "necessary evil," if not quite "villain," for the Emilia Camp.

Being who he was, even had Priscilla not died, Otto had considered—and could have chosen—the approach of having her step down as a royal candidate on the grounds that she was of imperial Vollachian blood, unfit for the position. Yes. He could have chosen that. And so—

"—Even if you hadn't died, you never would have become our enemy. With Natsuki-san and Lady Emilia both hurting as they are, your death is... a loss to me too."

As someone from a different camp, with no particular reason to mourn or grieve her death—that was the least of the condolences Otto Suwen could offer the woman who had been lost.
