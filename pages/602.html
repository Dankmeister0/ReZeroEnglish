Arc 7 Chapter 97: "That Which Comes from Beyond the West"

The battle raging across the imperial capital of Lupugana had reached its climax in every corner of the field.

Cloud-wreathed dragons descended upon a sky shedding white snow, and blazing red flames scorched the heavens like roiling storm clouds. Stone golems and soldiers with the faces of men resigned to death collided with one another, and before a crumbled rampart, a beast's mighty arm clashed with cunning malice, the tide of battle twisting back and forth with no clear victor.

But in this instant, this single fleeting moment, the greatest point of focus on this battlefield was the white light unleashed from the innermost depths of the capital — from the castle called the Crystal Palace, the most beautiful castle in the world.

Magic crystal — a purer crystallization of mana even among magic stones — had been worked into the entirety of the castle's structure, and it now amplified the mana stored within to sweep away its target: the strategic weapon known as the Magic Crystal Cannon.
It was, in truth, the Crystal Palace's trump card, the single greatest firepower possessed by the Vollachian Empire. Even on this battlefield, it was an ultimate blow capable of deciding the outcome of the war with a single shot.

To begin with, those who knew of the Magic Crystal Cannon's existence were few.
Naturally, Abel, who had once sat upon the imperial throne, knew of it, as did Zikr Osman, who had been briefed on the outline of the operation. Priscilla Barielle, who would hardly be surprised to have learned of it beforehand, and only a precious few among the rebel army's own ranks knew as well.
On the capital's side, the numbers were likely much the same. The false emperor leading the rebellion, Vincent Vollachia, and the chancellor who stood at his side, Berstetz Fondalfon. And then there was Moguro Hagane, who was the Crystal Palace itself — not even the Nine Divine Generals knew. It was a hidden card, through and through.
And then—

"—The Magic Crystal Cannon has strength enough for three shots at most, but on this battlefield, only one can be fired."

That was Abel's read on the situation, and by extension, an understanding he shared with Vincent as well.
Many factors were entangled in it, but in every sense, the Magic Crystal Cannon was the empire's ultimate trump card for national defense. To recklessly empty its remaining shots, with an eye toward what would come afterward, was simply not something one could allow oneself to do.
Of course, that would be a different matter entirely if Vincent intended to disregard everything that came afterward and burn through every last resource on this battlefield alone.

"You are not the sort capable of such self-destructive recklessness."

What to call that certainty, Abel deliberately chose not to consider.
But a conviction was a conviction, and rather than cast aside the one that had taken root in him, Abel had laid out the battlefield accordingly. And he had chosen to reliably provoke the one and only shot of the Magic Crystal Cannon into firing at nothing.
That scheme had been drawn out by Zikr, a precious asset of fighting strength, and the high-morale soldiers under his banner, along with the decoy formed by uniting the various tribes.

"—Our elite troops serve as the decoy. The enemy cannot afford to ignore them."

And so the Magic Crystal Cannon was turned toward the Third Apex, the battlefield judged to be at a disadvantage, defended by Moguro.
The Magic Crystal Cannon's flaw — the risk of devastating damage even to allies — need not be considered on Moguro's battlefield. The stone golems scattered across that field, whatever their numbers, were nothing more than fragments of Moguro himself, and even the massive Moguro fused with the rampart was not his true body.
Thus the two sides' schemes overlapped, and the unleashed Magic Crystal Cannon annihilated a contingent of the rebel army — but in doing so, it should also have canceled out the cause of the rebel army's helpless defeat there. — That was how it should have gone.

"——"

In that instant, the majority of the battlefield bore witness to a black light swallowing the white light that had been unleashed.
What had brought this about, where the Magic Crystal Cannon's overwhelming blow had vanished without inflicting any damage at all, whether the one responsible belonged to friend or foe — none of it was clear.
Countless thoughts flew through the blank space that had opened up, and everyone froze, struck dumb with astonishment.

An event outside all expectation — and Abel, at the rebel army's command post, was no exception.
He too had his eyes stolen by this unforeseen phenomenon, his thoughts creating a brief blank space of their own. — The only difference in him was that his recovery from it came faster than anyone else's.
Who had done it, how, to where — none of that was clear, but—

"—Launch it, as planned!"

If they could proceed from a better position than expected, so much the better.
In most cases, what a battlefield demands is not the correct judgment but the fast one. And then, updating the events that arise from that judgment, turning it retroactively into the correct one.

Cutting his gaze away from the battlefield the white light should have swept clean, Abel gave that order. At the sound of his voice, the frozen soldiers of the command post started and dropped their eyes.
To the several gazes unable to hide their unease, Abel responded with black eyes seen through the mask of a demon.

"Launch it now! Give the order! If you delay, it will bring about Zikr Osman's death!"

"—!"

"Move, quickly!"

"Yes, sir—!!"

The soldiers of the command post around him were, without exception, subordinates personally chosen by Zikr.
From the start, this operation had factored in Zikr's life as part of the cost. The hollowness of invoking Zikr's life as leverage — the soldiers swallowed it down and moved at once regardless.
This was not so much Abel's decisiveness at work here as it was the trust Zikr had built up over time doing its work.

"The suicide squad could not fulfill its role. That said, do not ease your grip, Zikr Osman. If you have survived, then there is still a role for you left to fulfill."

Far away, at the Third Apex of the ramparts protecting the capital, a clash that should never have happened by rights was taking place — a battle led by Zikr, with the People of Shudrak and the various tribes at his back.
Whether they could break through Moguro and press into the capital had become, in truth, a pure contest of raw strength.
Watching that unexpected fight from afar, Abel touched the demon mask on his face and murmured.

"—By necessity, my own role to play draws near as well."

Behind Abel, who let those words slip, the soldier he had ordered launched a magic stone shell into the sky.
It signaled the progress of the situation — a reply to the capital's side, which had played its trump card of the Magic Crystal Cannon: proof that the rebel army, too, had played its own trump card.

Taking that light into his eyes, Abel turned his gaze beyond the Third Apex, and—

"—You who seek to move the board from the west... who are you?"

"—Surely not."

Gazing out at the distant battlefield from the uppermost level of the Crystal Palace, Berstetz Fondalfon let his aged throat tremble, ever so slightly.
The imperial chancellor, who rarely let emotion show, betrayed no small amount of bewilderment and unease — enough to bring surprise even to the guards stationed at Berstetz's side.

"——"

Feeling the soldiers' eyes upon his profile, Berstetz immediately tightened his expression.
To show weakness invited contempt in the empire. Even if those around him did not think so, the moment he himself believed it, he could no longer stand tall. That was the Vollachian way.
And it was precisely the way of Vollachia that Berstetz himself upheld as how one ought to be.

"Of course, given that they dared to challenge the capital, I had assumed they would have some countermeasure prepared... but even so."

Steadying his resolve once more, Berstetz took the event that had occurred head-on.
What spilled from his lips was his analysis of the fact he had just witnessed — that the very blow of the Crystal Palace's trump card, the Magic Crystal Cannon, had been made to strike nothing at all.

The existence of the Magic Crystal Cannon was treated as a closely guarded secret, known to only a select few even within the empire.
But with the true emperor, Vincent Vollachia, present on the enemy's side, the secrecy of the cannon's existence was already meaningless. Even so, its unprecedented power and its very worth lay in the fact that, even knowing of it, there was fundamentally no way to defend against it.

For that reason, Berstetz had surmised that Vincent's likely course of action would be either to avoid a decisive battle at the capital altogether, or to refrain from deploying his own forces into any situation where the Magic Crystal Cannon might be used.
And yet Vincent had thrown himself into this very battlefield, had gone so far as to seize command of the rebels who had rushed ahead of themselves partway through, and had begun to manipulate the course of the battle. Presumably, that had been a desperate measure meant to deliberately push expendable forces into the cannon's line of fire.

If so, then all the better — seeking maximum efficiency, Berstetz had aimed the Magic Crystal Cannon at the Third Apex.
The battlefield where the rebels' forces were most concentrated and where allied casualties would be least — striking there, too, was surely part of Vincent's scheme, but that did not change the fact that it would cause the man difficulty.
Devour what has been offered up, and use the power of the Nine Divine Generals to shatter the enemy's scheme along with them. — So he had reasoned, and had, as a formality, sought permission from the other Vincent upon the throne as well.
And then—

"If each side's stratagems cancel each other out, all that remains is the pure difference in fighting strength. — Do it."

Having reached agreement with Vincent, Berstetz ignited the Magic Crystal Cannon.
True to its aim, the unleashed, overwhelming destructive force should have flown straight into the battlefield, vaporizing the many brave soldiers in its path and sending them beyond.
And so he had believed that what would decide victory or defeat was the action taken after the Magic Crystal Cannon swept the rebels away and the tide of battle was reset — but—

"You would render the Magic Crystal Cannon's damage nil?"

Given the man's proven feat of reviving the Magic Crystal Cannon sealed within the Crystal Palace, it had never occurred to Berstetz to consider that Vincent might also possess some means of offsetting it entirely.
The skill of keeping a hidden card concealed right up to the very last moment — this was exactly Vincent's method. Even as he regretted being so thoroughly ensnared by it, something welled up within Berstetz's chest all the same.
Above all else, what he felt was this: how regrettable.

"Your Excellency, I mourn this from the bottom of my heart, truly."

The Vollachian way, where the strong are revered — and Vincent, who while standing at its very pinnacle governed the empire through wit rather than violence, was a man whose bearing was a combination of tedium and terror in equal measure.
Political skill that prevented any great war from breaking out — Berstetz found himself both admiring it and, at the same time, disdaining it. And yet he held no dissatisfaction in serving Vincent.
So long as the man merely fulfilled his duties as emperor and intended to preserve the glory of the Vollachian Empire.

"What do you intend to do, should you return, Your Excellency..."

Reclaim the throne, execute every last soul who had joined the treason, and then — what of the empire thereafter? Maintain the empire's ways unchanged from before, and abandon the duties of emperor once again?
If that were to be accepted, if things were only to be allowed to end that way, then even an excellent emperor had no business sitting upon the throne.

"—Prepare the next Magic Crystal Cannon."

The number of shots the Magic Crystal Cannon could fire was fixed, and using it up entirely here was not something to be permitted.
Even so, if the situation demanded a change, Berstetz would exercise the entirety of his authority to bring it about. That said, if the enemy held two trump cards capable of nullifying the Magic Crystal Cannon, the same mistake could not be repeated.
Until he could discern that, he could not move to strike.

"—! Chancellor Berstetz! Look there!"

Suddenly, one of the soldiers standing guard at Berstetz's side raised his voice.
Following the soldier's gaze, far beyond the battlefield — likely near the point where the rebels' main encampment had been set up — he saw the light of a magic stone shell fired into the sky.

Some sort of signal.
Against a sky stained in the two colors of white and red, it was a terribly faint thing, but to those who had been waiting for that signal's arrival, it carried more than enough meaning.

"A signal..."

Behind his thread-thin eyes, Berstetz weighed the other side's intentions.
That the situation ought to be moved after the cannon's shot had been fired — that much both sides shared in common. If so, then was that signal an instruction to the soldiers on the front line? But if it was meant to notify the soldiers fighting on the front, that signal was, if anything, far too faint for the purpose.
Therefore, the one meant to receive it was not the soldiers on the front line, but—

"—Reserve forces."

But at the same time, Berstetz considered further.
With the majority of rebels gathered from various regions already spent, rampaging across the battlefield and being crushed beneath the power of the Nine Divine Generals, what manner of force could possibly still function as a reserve?

Reserve forces were not simply pieces held back out of stinginess.
They ought to be a true trump card in the proper sense, deployed to decisively alter the course of the battle. Naturally, there was no point unless it consisted of forces with commensurate decisive power.
Just what sort of fighting strength might be conceivable? — Thinking so, Berstetz widened his thread-thin eyes and turned them toward the far western horizon.

"Surely not."

Reflected slowly into Berstetz's field of view, murmured so faintly, was a shadow coming from the western sky — spread wide across the horizon, advancing with unhurried majesty: a swarm of Flying Dragons, sovereigns of the sky.
Flying Dragons, which lived in great numbers throughout the Vollachian Empire, and yet required techniques forbidden outside a select few and a rider's own talent to command — the great noble house that had gathered the greatest number of those wings beneath its banner, possessing among the empire's finest striking power.

The upper-rank countess who had refused to march in this decisive battle for the capital, choosing instead to remain and hold her own domain, the one who commanded the empire's mightiest Flying Dragon Squadron—

"—Upper-rank Countess Serena Dracroy, is it."

Discovering, amid the Flying Dragon Squadron pressing in from the far sky, a Dragon Ship trailing sails as fiery red as flame, Berstetz grasped the threat posed by this reserve force the rebel army had prepared.
In terms of commanding Flying Dragons, their own camp had one of the Nine Divine Generals as well: Madelyn Eschart. Before her, a Dragonkin, every Flying Dragon was hers to command at will — save, that is, for those bound by the technique of the Flying Dragon Rider, tied by bond to their own individual riders.

"Even so, it would only balance the contest for air superiority. No — if she has called forth the 'Cloud Dragon,' then even that premise changes."

Even amid the shock of the Magic Crystal Cannon being nullified, Berstetz had not neglected his grasp of the wider battlefield.
Among the fierce battles being waged across the five apexes, there were several fields so unnatural they altered the very color of heaven and earth, and he had caught, in one of them, a great Dragon descending.

Madelyn, being of Dragonkin blood, was capable of summoning the 'Dragon' that was her own kin.
That she had summoned the 'Cloud Dragon' Mezoreia — a being she had never once before been willing to call down to the earth — would surely bring about a great shift in the tide of battle.
Of course, the possibility that Madelyn had been driven to such straits that she had no choice but to summon Mezoreia lay just as close at hand, so this was not a situation that allowed for hasty conclusions.

"In any case, so long as it comes down to a contest for air superiority between Flying Dragons—"

In both quantity and quality, Berstetz judged their own side to hold the advantage.
Upper-rank Countess Serena Dracroy, who had shrewdly thrown her lot in with the rebels — there was something in her judgment and resolve that stirred the blood of a man of Vollachia — but in order to render that decision a mistake—

"—?"

Thinking so, as he glared toward the west, Berstetz suddenly noticed something.
His eyes had been drawn entirely to the Flying Dragon Squadron slowly approaching from the far sky, but that was not the only change to have occurred in the west.

—On the western hills, upon closer look, there stood the figure of a new contingent, freshly deployed.

For a moment he thought it might be Countess Dracroy's forces, but Berstetz quickly reconsidered — it did not appear to be so.
The reason was simple: the banner that contingent flew.

It bore neither the scarred Flying Dragon of House Dracroy's crest, nor the national emblem of the wolf pierced by the Vollachian Empire's sword. An entirely different banner was raised there.
Berstetz puzzled briefly over what was depicted upon it, and then said—

"—Is that... a star?"

"Hey, brother, what are we gonna do!? We're totally missing our chance here!?"
"Quit fussing, lizard boy... Schwartz is busy right now...!"
"Busy, no argument there, but Hiain's got a point too. What are we going to do. Are we joining the rebel line as it stands?"
"Whoa, do you see that, Boss? Left and right, everything's a total mess, exactly like some grand decisive battle for the fate of the world! My heart won't stop pounding wondering which side to throw myself into!"

"Argh, quiet, all of you!! I'm having a really touching moment right now, okay!?"

Natsuki Subaru shouted down the chorus of familiar voices come bursting in all at once, and felt a fresh pang of crisis at how much more naturally "Natsuki Schwartz" had started to sit with him lately.
Still, Natsuki Schwartz was, in the end, nothing more than a false name. His true name was "Natsuki Subaru" — hearing himself called by it made him feel that all the more strongly.
Even so—

"That's you calling my name, right? I'm safe assuming that much?"

"Uawoo! Uawoo, uawoo, uawoo—!"

"Okay, got it! Got it, so don't wipe your snot on me! Gross!"

The blond-haired girl clinging to him — Rui — pressed her face insistently against his, and Subaru answered while taking the full brunt of her tears and snot.
But the reason he couldn't just brush her off in one motion was that what Rui had done for him carried too much weight, combined with the mountain of guilt he owed her.
He had, after all, done more than enough to Rui that he could never fully make up for.
He could tell himself it had been unavoidable, sure.

"If I did that, I wouldn't have any right to face you. So I'll do this properly."

"Uu..."

Clinging to Subaru's back, pressing her forehead against him, Rui gave a low moan.
Whether it was her own way of forgiving him, or whether she was simply glad they'd been reunited, Subaru couldn't tell — but sensing nothing negative in it, he let out a breath.
There was more than just an apology Subaru owed Rui — there was plenty of gratitude to give as well.
And the greatest of it—

"—Beatrice."

Calling the name of the girl he held tightly in his arms, Subaru stroked her voluminous hair. Her extravagant curls and her elegant dress made her a girl utterly out of place on a battlefield — and without a doubt, she was here on this battlefield, from every possible angle, for Subaru's sake.
The reckless act that had nearly caused her to vanish, too, had surely been for the same reason.
So—

"—Subaru."

Hearing those lips call his name, Subaru savored a wave of relief.
Yes, his name really was "Natsuki Subaru" — reaffirming that once more—

"—Schwartz-sama, might I have a moment?"

"Uawoo!"

Right at his ear, an unexpected whisper broke in on Subaru as he savored his relief, and his shoulders jumped. Turning around on reflex, he found the familiar, adorable face there — Tanza.
The kimono-clad Deer Human girl, wearing her usual expression that made her feelings hard to read, sat plopped down on the ground, and looked over at Subaru — hugging Beatrice, with Rui clinging to his back —

"My apologies for interrupting your moment of joy. But I believe what Hiain-sama and the others are saying has merit as well. There may not be much time to spare for taking things leisurely."

"Leisurely, that's kind of a sharp way to put it..."

"There may not be much time to spare for taking things leisurely."

"Sorry, sorry, my bad! ...I get how you feel too, Tanza. She's probably somewhere on this battlefield too, right — Yorna-san."

Pressed by the cold, hard edge in her voice, Subaru, acknowledging his own fault, showed his understanding. At that, Tanza's round brows drooped just slightly, and she murmured a small "Yes."
Though Tanza had come to accompany Subaru through the course of events, she had originally been a girl in service to Yorna Mishigure, the ruler of the demon capital of Chaosflame.

Suppressing her desire to reunite with Yorna as quickly as possible, Tanza had nonetheless stayed with Subaru through his journey, and now, at last, that reunion was within sight.
With her heart racing in anticipation like that, it was understandable she'd want to speak up if Subaru went off and savored his own reunion first.
But—

"...Subaru, what is this insolent deer girl, I wonder?"

In Subaru's arms, Beatrice twisted around to look up at Tanza, and made that remark in a somewhat displeased tone. Immediately, Tanza's gaze turned toward Beatrice as well.

"I am called Tanza. Through the course of circumstances, I have been serving as Schwartz-sama's attendant."

"Hmm, I see, I suppose. Well, good work up till now, I suppose. From here on, Betty and the others will properly take Subaru back, so you're relieved of your duties, I suppose."

"Take him back, you say? In that state? Without Schwartz-sama to carry you, it seems you're barely able to move on your own, however..."

"Carrying Betty is Subaru's reason for living, so this is just fine, I suppose."

"Wait wait wait wait, why are you two fighting!? Little girls, get along, would you!?"

Sandwiched between the two, who had somehow burst into a hostile mood, Subaru's voice cracked despite himself.
It wasn't like the usual Tanza, who was calm and, in a good way, not particularly cute or coy. He knew better than to call a young girl childish, but still.

"Both of you, Tanza and Beatrice, stop fighting. We're allies here. Allies. Right?"

"It was Beatrice-sama who provoked me first. Yorna-sama also instructed that anyone who carelessly looks down on or demeans us should receive their due punishment."

"They actually told you something as Code of Hammurabi as that? I had no idea... well then, maybe you should be the one to apologize... Beatrice?"

Trying to mediate, Subaru instead found himself pinned by a hard stare from Beatrice's round eyes. Drawn in by the distinctive pattern floating within them, Subaru tilted his head and asked, "What's wrong?"
At that, Beatrice's lips trembled, quivering with indignation.

"Just now, you called that girl's name before you called Betty's, I suppose. What exactly did you mean by that, I wonder?"

"Huh? Was that a thing?"

"It is, I suppose! I am utterly furious, I suppose! Depending on how things go, I may not forgive you, I suppose!"

Her eyes blazing with anger, Beatrice grabbed his collar and shook his head back and forth. Confronted with this unexpected personal rule of hers, Subaru's head spun right along with it.
And then, growing exasperated with an exchange that wasn't going anywhere—

"—Schwartz, even my patience as a professional is not infinite. You are aware of this, surely."

"Myaa!" "Whoa!"

A deep, heavy voice descended from above, and Beatrice let out a shriek even before Subaru could. Beatrice's eyes went wide, and following her gaze, Subaru understood the reason for her surprise.
Standing there, arms — all four of them extending from a black cloak — crossed, looking down at them with a stern face, was Gustav Morello.

The warden of the Isle of Swordsmen's Exile, a man who by all rights ought to have been holding Subaru and the others captive, was now, in this moment, openly walking outside the island alongside them as an ally — no, a comrade.
And it was not only him. Everyone gathered here, the many who had come with Subaru — not just from the Isle of Swordsmen's Exile, but also those companions who had joined along the way to this land — all of them were comrades.

"S-Subaru, this man is..."

"That's Gustav-san. Looks scary, but he's serious and dependable at heart. Gustav-san, the one on my back is Rui, and this is Beatrice. And this girl is my..."

"...My?"

"Um... that's, well, you know, that thing."

Stroking the back of the shrinking Beatrice, Subaru found himself at a loss for words while explaining to Gustav. Gustav and Tanza, and Beatrice too, all raised their brows at that.
Feeling the weight of their several gazes, Subaru scratched his head awkwardly.

"...Let's just say the details will come out eventually."

"Eventually."

"Hold on a moment, I suppose! Subaru, don't tell me it's not just your appearance that's shrunk, but the contents of your head too... mmf mmf!"

To Subaru's overly vague excuse, Gustav accepted it earnestly and at face value. But Beatrice looked about to blurt out something uncomfortably close to the truth, so Subaru clapped a hand over her mouth.
Then, to the flailing, struggling Beatrice, he whispered softly.

"If people find out I've shrunk, it'll get complicated. Let's keep it secret for now."

"...You do properly remember Betty, I suppose, right?"

"That I love you, and that I have to do this — yeah, I remember."

To Beatrice's suspicious eyes, Subaru answered that much, then took her hand and squeezed it tight. Beatrice's small hand, and Subaru's own small hand, fingers intertwining together.
The moment he did, Subaru was struck, for an instant, by a dizzying sense of depletion. But it didn't last — it was only that first beat that had been larger than the rest.

"I can't live without you. Right?"

"...Somehow, I feel like I'm being cleverly deceived, I suppose. And also—"

"And also?"

"—. This, Subaru, this is strange, I suppose."

Hand clasped in hand, facing each other directly, warmth flowed from within Subaru into Beatrice. He could sense that it wasn't a bad thing — if anything, it felt like something that led to reassurance.
There were parts of his memory concerning Beatrice that were a bit, subtly, hazy, but he held the optimistic belief that it was only the details that were blurred, and that things would be fine once his body returned to normal.
In any case, while Subaru took things in that positive light beside her, Beatrice furrowed her shapely brows, seemingly lost in some thought.

"You look cute even when you're thinking hard, but what's strange?"

"...While Betty and Subaru were apart, Subaru had no way to release his mana, I suppose. So it should have just kept building up, but, this amount—"

"—?"

Her long eyelashes trembling, Beatrice tightened her grip on his fingers as if to confirm something. Before Subaru, tilting his head, her lips let slip a voiceless murmur.
It had, in fact, whispered this: — too much.

But before he could confirm her true meaning—

"—Ah, brother, this is bad! Cecilus went and rushed ahead on his own!!"

"Whaaat!?"

Hiain's voice rang out like a scream, and Subaru spun around in a hurry. In the direction of his pointing finger lay the battlefield, still locked in fierce clashing, and toward it a trail of dust surged furiously.
Proof that the impatient troublemaker had bolted down the hillside, unable to hold himself back any longer.

"What do we do, should I go bring him back...?"

"No, I appreciate the thought, but even Weitz couldn't manage it. I mean, nobody, anywhere, can stop Ceci once he's going. ...The banner."

"Understood, Schwartz."

Even as he found the receding dust cloud irritating, Subaru took the runaway charge in stride, knowing full well from the journey so far that no one could keep a tight grip on those reins.
Rallying himself, Subaru gave the next order, and it was Idra who moved, already understanding what was needed. Together with Weitz, the two of them slowly raised the banner where they stood.
And at that motion, the many comrades gathered around followed suit.
One after another, banners were raised, and the unity of the contingent gathered on this hillside would surely have been plain to see from all around.

"Rui, get down off my back."

"Ah, uh!"

"I'm not just gonna disappear out of nowhere anymore, so you're fine there. I made peace with the reason I got flung away."

Whether it was traumatic for her that Subaru had vanished before her eyes, Rui, reluctant to be parted from him, was persuaded by these words. Still doubtful, Rui nonetheless obediently pulled away from Subaru's back.
Even so, the fact that she still refused to move from his side suggested her trust in him wasn't exactly solid yet.

"—. Betty has no intention of moving away, I suppose."

"Yeah, you stay with me. —Tanza, you ready?"

"Yes. —The Pleiades Battalion can move out at any time."

Behind Tanza, bowing deeply, the assembled comrades stood raising their banners, raising their weapons, raising their fighting spirit — witnessing that sight, Subaru allowed himself a small, satisfied grin.
Morale was high, motivation was full, and with the objective right in front of them, all that remained was to charge in.

"Ceci probably already gets my whole plan without me having to spell it out every time, so that's just his usual charm, I guess... alright, let's do this, everyone!"

"'Ohhhhhh—!!'"

"—!"

A tremendous response thundered back at Subaru's call, and Beatrice caught her breath.
It was the kind of thing bound to startle anyone hearing it for the first time. But to Subaru by now, it was a sound his ears had grown used to — and a deeply important ritual before battle.

"Uawoo?"
"Subaru, what are you planning to do, I wonder?"

Rui and Beatrice, newly reunited with him and unaware of the details, looked up at Subaru.
Meeting their gazes, Subaru closed one eye, and taking the surge welling up from deep within his body and channeling it firmly down into his slender legs, he took one step forward.
And then, he declared it plainly.

"Obviously, right? — I'm gonna wreck the pointless war my damn old man started."
