Arc 8 Chapter 60: "Witch"

In the Crystal Palace of the imperial capital, Lupugana — in what had once been the audience chamber — the shinobi who had slipped inside, Olbart Dunkelkenn, gave his right arm, gone from the elbow down, a shake and knit his white brows.

"Finally quit moving, has it. Sheesh, you're putting this old man through way too much depressing business."

Before the eyes of Olbart, who shrugged his shoulders slowly and heaved a sigh, lay the remains of the very thing that had left even this uncanny old shinobi thoroughly sick of it — a form grotesquely remade, its shape twisted beyond recognition, a monstrous thing so altered that calling it merely an Undead would be too generous.

Olbart regarded what he had done to that strange, misshapen thing as a mercy killing.
A slain Undead is revived retaining its memories. What's more, reviving an Undead consumes the Mana of the "Stone Mass" that underpins the empire's very earth. — Hence, killing an Undead was an order reserved only for the barest necessity.

"And even so, better to kill it. The fact that it could make even me think that — that's way too messed up, isn't it."

Respect for human life, mercy for the weak — the kind of slack only someone with room to spare in their heart could afford — Olbart, with little time left to him and less room to spare, had long since thrown all of that away.
And yet even to Olbart, this audience chamber — no, this laboratory for experimenting on Souls — had gone too far.

"This whole setup reminds me of the bunch who made Arakiya. Not doing it for fun, not doing it for revenge, and yet they go and pull this kind of shit anyway — that's what makes 'em impossible to deal with. Though I did deal with 'em."

As he grumbled that, a picture rose in Olbart's mind of the singularly depraved group he had disposed of in the past.
The perpetrators who had produced a vast number of victims in order to create Arakiya — the finished form of a "Spirit Eater" — had spoken of their motives as a sense of duty, or for the empire's future.

The carnage of this audience chamber — tampering with the Souls of Undead revived in Vessels made of clay and earth, things with Souls mingled together, things transformed beyond recognition. Savagery carried out not to win this war, but for some purpose that disregarded the victory or defeat right in front of it. In the process that had produced these grotesque, alien things, he sensed the same kind of inquiring drive, detached from all feeling and fixated only on results, that those depraved men had had.
And Olbart could guess at the purpose of that inquiry, because he himself had once brushed up against a corner of it.

"—They're trying to tamper with the Soul itself, aren't they."

Olbart could use a Shinobi Technique to round off and shrink another person's Soul — their Od. That technique, which Chisha had even stolen from him, only ever grazed the surface, though.
But this seeker of knowledge wanted results beyond that.
What exactly that meant, Olbart — not knowing who this seeker was — had no way of telling. But—

"Got a rea-al bad feeling about this, Your Excellency. ...Yeah, guess I really don't have room to double-cross you after all, huh?"

—The Demi-Human Alliance's defeat in the "Demi-Human War," and Sphinx's own personal defeat.

Regarding that defeat, Sphinx had little to say.
She had lived more than three hundred and fifty years, but for Sphinx — who had spent nearly all of that time hiding and surviving — she was no match for an opponent who had trained with the resolve to strike her down.
If one were to describe what happened, that alone was the whole of it.

To be honest, even as she faced death at the hands of the "Witch of Greed's" disciple, Sphinx's mind held no great feeling about it either.
To begin with, she had next to no attachment to life. She had spent long years prioritizing mere survival only because doing so seemed the rational course toward fulfilling her purpose of creation.
And now, faced with vanishing without having fulfilled that purpose, all that remained to the very end was an instinctive aversion — a reluctance to let go of that purpose.

That was why, fleeing into the depths beneath Royal Lugunica Castle, the site of the decisive battle, she had once mustered the will to shake off the "Witch of Greed's" disciple — simply a result of following that instinct.
And yet that flight, undertaken without any attachment to life, was what changed Sphinx's fate.

"—You have your uses. You'll serve me well, in fulfilling my desire."

In that man's clouded eyes, she could see the flicker of an intensely burning flame of ambition.

△▼△▼△▼△

—The flame of the "Sunlight Sword" Vollachia burns to ash whatever it has been decided will be destroyed.

The scheme devised by Vincent Vollachia and Natsuki Subaru had convinced Sphinx that the Emperor had, for some reason, parted with the crimson treasured sword.
As a result, after a fierce, life-or-death exchange, the flame burned Sphinx, who had lost the battle of deception.

The blade of the "Sunlight Sword," having reached the "Witch" Sphinx, sent its crimson flame through to the Soul of the one who bore the "Great Disaster," revived as an Undead — and all at once, it set ablaze the multiple Sphinxes confronting Vincent and the others, the Sphinx waiting in the Crystal Palace, the Sphinxes observing each battlefield across the imperial capital, and the countless Sphinxes who had been about to begin their assault across the whole of the empire.

"—Countermeasure required."

Burning in the blazing red flame, the cut-down Sphinx murmured that. But even taking advantage of an Undead's near-absence of pain, she could not keep her burning self intact for long.
Then should she abandon the multiple bodies already aflame, and carry out the plan with a new Sphinx who had learned from this "death" as nourishment? — No. Impossible.

The flame of the "Sunlight Sword" was burning Sphinx's Soul itself.
No matter how she tried to produce a new Undead Sphinx, so long as the Soul at its very root was burning, the clay-and-earth body could not avoid being made while already on fire.

"————"

There was no countermeasure. With that sense of deadlock, this Sphinx's body collapsed.
Burned to death, and even carrying what she learned from this "death" onward, the reconstructed Sphinx's body would only burst into flame again, ending each time with the same conclusion: there was no countermeasure.

The end. This is the end. The journey of inquiry of Sphinx, the failed creation of the "Witch of Greed" who had wandered the world across a long span of time, closes its curtain here.

She had done everything she could, laid every trap in full — but it hadn't been enough.
It was the same defeat she had once tasted during the "Demi-Human War." That time too, Sphinx had done everything she could and still fallen short, with nothing left but to die in defeat.
That it hadn't ended that way then was not due to any action of Sphinx's own, but interference from the outside.
And this time, she could not hope for the same.
Because the one whose ambition had saved Sphinx back then — he was no longer here.
Leip Barielle was dead. So, now—

"—Reconsideration required."

Suddenly, Sphinx — who should have simply burned and met her end — moved.
With the flame of the "Sunlight Sword" still lit across her entire body, Sphinx pressed a finger beneath her own jaw and blew it apart, taking the core Insect lodged in her head along with it, bringing about her own "death."
And then, in a chain reaction, every Sphinx on the battlefield, in the capital, across the whole empire, did the same.

""—Reconsideration required.""

She hadn't fallen into self-destruction. Nor was she in the grip of a death wish.
It was simply that, by dying, Sphinx could integrate her memories into her Soul. By having countless Sphinxes die all at once and integrating their countless memories, she could build up the collective intelligence that was herself.
Of course, the Soul into which those memories gathered was itself burning, and it was only a matter of time before it burned away entirely.
But until it did, Sphinx kept creating more of herself, born only to burn, piling up countless rounds of deliberation and discussion over the situation and its countermeasures.

Once, "death" had stirred no feeling in her at all. But now, it was different.

"""—Resistance required."""

Resisting, resisting, resisting all the while, Sphinx gathered up every possibility and weighed it.
The resolution at each apex battle, the Soul experiments in the Crystal Palace, the history of the Vollachia imperial family, the "Spirit Eater," the foreign matter from the Kingdom, the "Stargazer," heaven's mandate, the "Great Disaster" — every possibility, every fragment of all creation.
—And then,

—Priscilla, chained in her cell, had witnessed the entire sequence of events unfolding before her with Sphinx, taking it all in clearly with her crimson eyes.

That the "Witch," whose entire body had suddenly burst into red flame, was burning with the crimson fire brought by the "Sunlight Sword" passed down in the Vollachia Empire — that much Priscilla understood at a glance.
As did the fact that it was Vincent Vollachia who had delivered that crimson flame to the "Witch."

"So he's finally flipped his hidden card. Truly, an utterly unreadable brother, through and through."

That Vincent hadn't drawn the "Sunlight Sword," keeping it in reserve as a trump card, was self-evident to Priscilla, herself a member of the Vollachia imperial family. Of course, given the troublesome nature the "Sunlight Sword" possessed, it also stood to reason that Vincent couldn't have relied on it carelessly.
In any case, having lost the battle of deception, the "Witch" should have been destroyed by the flame.

"What did you do?"

"—Reconsideration... required."

The flame of the "Sunlight Sword" burns what it wishes to burn; the blade of the "Sunlight Sword" cuts what it wishes to cut.
That principle cannot be bent. And yet, before Priscilla's eyes, the "Witch," who should have been consumed by the blazing red flame, slowly stepped free of that crimson fire.

The flame had stopped burning the "Witch."

The flame of the "Sunlight Sword" burns what it wishes to burn; the blade of the "Sunlight Sword" cuts what it wishes to cut.
Therefore, the fate of the "Witch," burned and cut by Vincent's "Sunlight Sword," cannot be changed.
However—

"Just what are you?"

If what it had decided to burn, what it had reached out to cut, had become something else — that's a different matter.
Vincent had surely killed the "Witch" — Sphinx — with the "Sunlight Sword."
But the figure of the "Witch" who had stepped out of the flame was not the "Witch" Priscilla knew.

The cracked, pale skin, the eyes of black sunk with gold, the childlike, girlish appearance — all of it had been shed, and the being now stood before Priscilla.
Long white hair reaching down her back, and black eyes holding a clear, intellectual curiosity. —White and black: if one were to try to depict this being, those two colors alone would suffice to capture her entirely.

"Just what are you?"

"—The 'Witch of Greed.'"

To Priscilla's repeated question, there came a quiet answer, full of conviction.
It was a being who, at the end of a long span of time, had fulfilled her purpose of creation and remade the very nature of her Soul — the incarnation of the "Witch of Greed," having escaped the fate of being burned by flame, declared:

"My purpose of creation has been fulfilled. —I shall go now, to fulfill my purpose for living."
