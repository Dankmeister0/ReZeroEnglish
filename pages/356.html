Arc 5 Chapter 40: "Encroachment of Wrath"

The shelters in the city of Priestella numbered twenty-five per district, one hundred in total.

Following the suggestion of Al, who'd come along with them, Subaru and the others decided to make their rounds starting with District One, where the person they were searching for was most likely to be, and had been asked to move cautiously in order to avoid combat wherever possible.

"Still, feels like overkill to me. We've got that big brother of ours with us. Ain't like we got any reason to be this jumpy, is there, brother?"

"Better safe than sorry. Our forces are limited, and the enemy's strong. We can't afford to let our guard down for even a second. And there's the enemy, and then there's people who aren't the enemy — I don't want to provoke either."

Anastasia's opinion at the city hall — that public unrest might spiral into rioting — was something Subaru didn't want to come true, but he couldn't dismiss the possibility either.

Subaru's policy of touring the shelters, gathering fighting strength while checking on conditions, was itself a desperate measure meant to counter that very unease.

"Yeah, yeah, sure. ...Well, can't complain about being asked to take the safe road. If it comes down to a real fight, I'm counting on that big brother of ours anyway."

"...You keep saying stuff like that all the time, but can you seriously not fight? You made it to city hall just fine, didn't you?"

"If we're talking in simple binary terms — can fight, can't fight — then I'm on the can-fight side. But that's only within the bounds of what counts as human. Try to throw down with the guys who've stepped outside that domain, and I'll end up an extra who dies off-screen in an instant. No way, no how. Way too reckless."

Al rattled the joints of his helmet and gestured with his one arm to declare his own helplessness.

Subaru felt a flicker of dissatisfaction at that attitude, but at the same time, Al's detached resignation was something Subaru shared too. No matter how much effort you poured in, there were realms you simply couldn't reach.

Maybe it came down to a fundamental difference in how their bodies were built, in terms of surviving in this world. No matter how much Subaru trained his body, he couldn't picture himself ever reaching Julius or Garfiel's level — let alone Reinhard's.

"But that's not a reason not to fight."

"You think? Not being able to win seems like plenty reason not to fight, if you ask me. Oh, and I'm not talking about leaving something behind for posterity or whatever. That's not really my character."

"—"

"Don't get mad, brother. What you're trying to do is admirable. I just can't do it. That's all there is to it. Whoa, there."

Since each of their positions was firmly set, this conversation, too, ran along parallel lines with no meeting point.

When Subaru fell silent, Al apologized for having soured his mood, then noticed something and looked up. His gaze landed on Garfiel, who'd gone ahead to secure the road up front.

Garfiel, having returned on all fours across the cobblestones in near-total silence, wrinkled his nose and said,

"For now, the path's clear up to the next three streets. It's creepy how quiet it is. Almost makes me more suspicious that it's this safe."

Garfiel snorted as if he smelled something foul.

They wanted to avoid a fight. But not encountering the disaster that should have been unfolding stoked its own kind of unease. It was a torn feeling, but the fact remained that time wouldn't wait for hesitation.

"No use fretting over it. The shelter we're after is right there. Let's just hurry up and get there for now. The road's clear, right?"

"Ain't caught nothin' on my nose or eyes. Tch, fine."

Garfiel scratched at his short blond hair and kicked irritably at the ground.

When Subaru made to nod, Al shrugged wordlessly to show he had no objection.

About fifteen minutes had passed since leaving city hall, but contrary to the caution they'd exercised, the road had been peaceful. Given that they'd braced for the worst-case scenario — the Witch Cult having packed the streets solid after seizing the city — it wasn't an exaggeration to say they'd been let down.

"Way I figure it, maybe the Witch Cult didn't actually bring that many hands for this raid?"

Al said this while running, glancing around restlessly. Subaru flicked his eyes over and asked, "Why do you think that?"

"Simple, really — they don't have enough hands to watch everything. You feel it too, don't you, brother? The Witch Cult's been sharp about seizing the city's vital points, but they're sloppy everywhere else. Letting the resistance run around free, for one. If they were serious about getting their demands met, they wouldn't leave things like that."

"The standard play would be to lock down every district to make sure the demands go through. So why do you figure they're not doing that? They got some other aim?"

"Beats me, that's past what I know. But well, if I had to guess—"

Al tried to toss out his line of thinking and leave it there, but when Subaru gave him a hard look, he presumably grimaced inside his helmet, mulled it over for a moment, and then said,

"—Maybe they don't actually care whether the demands go through or not?"

"Huh?"

"If they seriously wanted the demands met, they'd nail down every detail more tightly. But it's full of holes you could feel a draft through — you and I agree on that much, don't we? So... they're probably not serious about getting the demands through. Fine if it goes through. Fine if it doesn't. Something like that."

"What the hell... that'd make it nothing but harassment, wouldn't it...!"

"Ain't it, though? You think they're not the type?"

Al answered in a flat, distant voice to Subaru, who'd been struck speechless. At that final question, Subaru caught his breath, unable to form words of denial.

—Mere harassment.

Given how vile the Witch Cult was, that possibility wasn't something he could laugh off. This raid seemed, at every turn, to have taken the initiative and played the optimal hand — and yet there were plenty of things that didn't add up.

During the clash at city hall, there had been signs that Capella and Alphard had lain in wait for Subaru's group. And yet they'd lacked Sirius and Regulus, avoiding a full commitment of their forces. The fact that they hadn't killed a single one of Subaru's party was the same kind of thing.

And now, this very shortfall in their handling of the city's occupation — the problem currently at hand — was proof that they weren't putting their best effort toward whatever their actual goal was.

"Boss! Don't go lendin' your ear to what this bastard's sayin'! You keep feedin' our boss garbage and I'll flatten you, got it?!"

Garfiel's rebuke cut in from the side, aimed at Subaru, who'd been on the verge of sinking into his thoughts. Garfiel's ferocious gaze then swung toward Al, running alongside Subaru.

"Yappin' on and on about a buncha nonsense. A guy with no guts to fight oughta shut his damn mouth! Whatever they're plottin', we smash 'em, beat 'em down, and crush 'em! That's all anybody needs to know!"

"That's about as extreme and unreasonable as it gets, ain't it? Sure, it's a fact I ain't got the stomach to go head-on with those guys. But sayin' it don't matter what they're thinkin' — that's takin' it too far. Or what, is there somethin' inconvenient about knowin' what they're thinkin'?"

"—You bastard."

There was a sharp clack of fangs, and Garfiel dug his heel into the ground, scraping to a halt. At the same moment, Al stopped too, and the two glared at each other, the air between them turning ugly.

Subaru instinctively wedged himself between them, pressing a hand to each of their chests.

"Wait! What's with you two? Now's not the time to be fighting each other!"

"Ain't your comrade, Boss. This one's, I dunno, bad news. Better we cut him loose right here."

"Actively fightin' is a hard no from me, but I ain't so devoted to pacifism that I'll just stand here and take it from a guy who's itchin' for a scrap either."

Garfiel cracked his knuckles; Al rolled his neck.

Watching the two of them turn oddly combative, Subaru felt anger welling up in himself as well.

Something prickly stabbed through his body, centered on his stomach, and on that impulse he felt the urge to punch both of them, to beat them down—

"...This is wrong."

He clutched his head as his thoughts overheated, nearly rushing to a brutal conclusion.

This line of thinking had leapt far too fast, no matter how you sliced it. To feel this much hostility and irritation toward his own comrades, of all people — something was off.

As if he'd been infected by the collision unfolding right in front of him between the two of them—

"No way..."

The moment he framed it as emotions and thoughts not under his own control, Subaru felt a chill race up his spine. Because that exact same sick feeling was one he'd already experienced more than once before.

"Is it Sirius's... influence reaching us?!"

Slapping his own cheek to jolt his mind awake, Subaru scanned the area.

Within his sweeping field of view, there was no sign of the monster. No grating voice reached his ears either. And yet a bleak, oppressive sensation was pressing down heavily on his entire body.

"Hey, Garfiel, Al, stop it! Breathe. Calm down. You can feel it too, right — that this turned into a shouting match way too fast? It's probably 'Wrath's' influence. Our emotions are out of our control."

"Ahh? What're you sayin', Boss. Ain't no reason for this bastard to piss me off... wait a—"

"—"

Trying to bite back at Subaru's claim, Garfiel instead pressed a palm to his own face. He shook his head slowly, blinked several times, and said,

"...You serious? From just lettin' my guard down that little bit?"

"That's the power of that 'Wrath' bastard. Let me check — there's no enemy presence around, right?"

"No smell, no presence... that much I'm sure of. But—"

Because he trusted his own senses, Garfiel's shock was plain and unambiguous.

Which meant Sirius's Authority reached far beyond what they'd imagined. If the monster was indeed stationed at the control tower, its Authority's range covered nearly the entire city. Granted, there was surely a difference in intensity compared to what they'd felt in the plaza, but even so—

"I see. So this is what it feels like to have your emotions toyed with by someone else. Sure ain't somethin' I wanna taste twice. I get why they call it the worst."

Al, having shaken off the same momentary flood of emotion as Garfiel, muttered as much. He looked toward Subaru and jerked his chin.

"But hey, brother. This might be bad news if we don't hurry, don't you think?"

"What? What are you talking about?"

"We got to notice somethin' was wrong 'cause you caught it, and 'cause we were listenin' in on it. But folks who ain't involved won't have that. If this feeling's spreadin' across the whole city... regular people who don't know a thing about it won't be able to keep their sanity."

"—!"

At Al's point, the worst-case scenario flashed through Subaru's mind, and he exchanged a look with Garfiel. Garfiel, having arrived at the same thought, nodded back, and the two of them immediately spun around and broke into a full sprint. Their destination: the nearest shelter.

"Hey, wait up!"

Lagging behind the two, Al scrambled after them in a fluster.

Chafing at his own speed, Subaru kicked off the ground and paid no mind to his surroundings. Garfiel's stride was sharper and longer than Subaru's; in no time he'd left Subaru behind, and after turning a corner three streets ahead, he vanished from sight.

According to the map, the corner Garfiel had disappeared around should lead to one of the shelters.

"What about watching the road?!"

"Garfiel ain't dove in and started a ruckus. Means nobody's there!"

Answering the call from behind him roughly, Subaru and the others reached the corner a little behind Garfiel. Rounding the bend without losing momentum, they saw a small stone building.

Rushing through the roughly flung-open door, they confirmed a stairway leading underground. Impatiently, they raced down the stairs, and at last the dim view opened up before them—

"You've got to be kidding me..."

—A hellscape of pooled blood and groans of agony, a scene of utter carnage, spread out before them.

"They really went at it, killed each other real good... only two out of ten are still breathing. And even they need absolute bed rest. Worst kinda situation."

Surveying the wreckage anew, Al let slip a mutter with his emotions pressed flat.

Sitting down beside him, Subaru didn't have the energy left to answer.

Inside the shelter they'd rushed into, the gathered refugees had turned on each other in mutual slaughter.

Probably, the first spark had been something trivial.

Crammed into a cramped space, spending hours in fear of the Witch Cult's demands.

As that time wore on, a quiet, ugly thought had crept into their hearts. Wouldn't things stay bad if they just sat there doing nothing? Shouldn't they take some kind of action?

The thought that surfaced called for action, and action required contact with others. But contact of that sort didn't always lead to good outcomes.

The same thing that had almost happened between Garfiel and Al on the way here had happened here too.

Except there had been no Subaru here to stop it. The arguing accelerated, their emotions collided, spread to those around them, and came back amplified by that same measure.

The more they stoked each other, the more unbearable the hatred and irritation toward one another became — and the result was tragedy.

"Knocked out the survivors, treated their wounds. Buryin' the dead's gonna have to wait. Boss, you send word?"

"Yeah. The Iron Fang should be heading this way. I think we can leave transporting the survivors to them. ...The problem's from here on out."

Subaru had underestimated the damage that amplification and transmission of emotion could cause.

Within the closed space of a shelter, there was no telling how wide the swings of emotion could get among people trapped together. Positive emotions were, of course, too much to hope for — but even negative ones could lead to wildly different outcomes.

If it started with sorrow or grief — something that didn't lead to active behavior — that would be fine.

But if the emotion that first spread was something like anger, it was clear the result would end up just like this shelter.

"They were told, thoroughly, to take refuge in the shelters... that might've backfired on us."

If you assumed the Authority of "Wrath" was something like the sharing and amplification of emotion, then its effect would grow stronger the more people were gathered within its range.

Put simply, the people around you became mirrors, and the light you cast out came back to you multiplied by the number of mirrors. Replace "light" with "emotion," and the amplification went without saying.

Even contact with another person could invite a terrifying outcome.

And in a situation crawling with this kind of anxiety and fear, this was an Authority that forced people to be alone.

"Makes me sick..."

"Boss, what're we gonna do. I still think goin' round to the shelters is the right call, but like this—"

Having finished the treatment, Garfiel, sweat beading on his brow, wore an uneasy expression. Subaru understood his unease and struggled to find the right words to respond.

His thoughts wouldn't settle. He still believed the plan itself — touring the shelters, rallying cooperation for the fight, and urging people not to give in to despair — wasn't wrong.

But the urgency of the crisis wouldn't allow Subaru the leisure of taking his time touring the city.

The very people who could be counted as fighting strength were the ones whose fighting spirit might instead invite tragedy. With the shelters currently functioning as test tubes for emotion, Subaru even found himself thinking that persuading people to leave the shelters might actually raise each person's chance of survival.

"But if we do that, and we can't take back the control tower, everyone dies."

These were shelters that existed within the city of Priestella.

They were presumably designed to withstand the great flood that would come if the floodgates were released. If, worst came to worst, Subaru's group failed and the floodgates opened, the people who'd left the shelters for fear of turning on each other wouldn't survive.

Whether inside or outside, there was no perfectly safe option.

"Boss..."

"—"

Garfiel's voice pierced into Subaru's profile as he struggled to decide.

It was a face and a voice seeking an answer, a salvation, from Subaru. The look of someone groping in the darkness for a reliable light, searching for a guidepost.

What could he possibly say to that face?

He was groping blindly too, just the same. In the dark, unable to find the answer — Subaru was no different.

But what point was there in flinging out such weakness? Who could be saved by a tantrum, by taking it out on someone else? If he had time to voice weakness, he should use it now to say even one meaningful thing.

Something, anything, that could help someone.

"Boss, Boss, huh... You really lean on people like some kinda convenient magic spell, don't you, kid. So damn admirable it's almost enough to make a guy cry."

But it was the one-armed man who broke Subaru's silence — a silence with no decent thoughts coming to mind. Leaning against the wall, Al had been surveying the carnage, and now his gaze turned to Garfiel.

At that dry remark, Garfiel was momentarily taken aback, but—

"Ahh? You bastard, what's that supposed to—"

"You need it spelled out? Means exactly what it sounds like. Easy, ain't it, shuttin' off your brain and tossin' the whole thing at someone else, leavin' the decidin' to him. 'Boss' — is that some magic word? The name of a superman who'll somehow pull through no matter the situation?"

Al cut him off, his tone mocking Garfiel.

"You been leanin' on him like he's somethin' special this whole time, but does the guy standin' here really look like that big a deal to you? In a fistfight, you'd beat him. Match wits, and plenty of folks'd do better. Even luck — you really gonna tell me you buy that his is any good, in a situation like this?"

"Shut the hell up! Don't you go talkin' about the Boss like you know somethin'! What do you know about this man?! What do you know about how amazin' he is?!"

"Amazin', amazin' — what are you, a kid? What's so amazin' about him? If he really were that great, he'd have sorted this whole mess out already. Or what, he's already figured out how to fix it and he's just draggin' his feet for dramatic effect?"

Against Garfiel's shouting, Al didn't drop his sarcastic stance for a moment. Bending at the waist, peering into Subaru's face, he left Subaru with nothing to say back.

Straightening up at that reaction, Al added, "See, told you," and continued,

"If shoulderin' everything and somehow pullin' through works out, that's mighty impressive. Protagonist material, that. But most folks — the vast majority of ordinary people — can't carry that kinda role. Don't have the strength for it. Me, sure, but the brother here's no different. So why's he gotta be saddled with carryin' so much? Don't go expectin' that outta him. It's pitiful, ain't it?"

"—"

Subaru couldn't quite grasp what Al was trying to say.

Was it possible he, too, was being swallowed by "Wrath's" Authority once again? If so, what emotion was currently dominating Al's heart?

Anger, or sorrow — which emotion was it? He seemed angry, seemed to be grieving, seemed to be laughing, all at once — it was hard even to pin down which emotion had sparked this.

"Hey, brother. What is it you've been agonizin' over this whole time?"

"...What am I— well, that's—"

A way to save this city. A way for the people who'd fled into the shelters to come through unharmed.

A way to rescue Emilia. A way to heal Crusch. A way to get Rem back. A way to drive out the Witch Cult. The optimal path — the one where everyone was saved.

"Can't even answer right off for your own princess, huh?"

"—"

At the disappointment in Al's voice, Subaru slowly lifted his face.

Al was staring straight down at him. With the helmet on, there was no way to read his expression. And yet, for some reason, Subaru felt his chest tighten.

"Me, I act for my princess... for Priscilla. So as for everybody else, honestly, I got no interest. Even movin' around with you all like this — it's calculated self-interest, raisin' my own odds of survivin' and my odds of runnin' into the princess."

"Al..."

"So I don't get how you feel, brother. This matters, that matters too... do that, and you lose sight of what actually matters most. Wantin' to fix everythin' — that's just the standard line of a guy who can't throw himself all the way into the thing that actually matters."

Clicking his tongue, Al was suppressing some emotion.

Faced with his fierce intensity, even Garfiel couldn't cut in. And Subaru, on the receiving end of that emotion directly, couldn't find the right words to answer him either.

"The thing that matters most to you, brother, ain't it that silver-haired girl? If all you cared about was savin' her, you could just act instead of moanin' and second-guessin' the whole time. That'd be easy, wouldn't it."

"...Don't talk like it's that simple. I will absolutely kill the bastard who took Emilia, but I still don't have any kind of plan for how to do that. It's not easy."

"But it's a hell of a lot lighter a load than tryin' to save everybody. Cut the weight, travel light, and the range of what you can reach and what falls within your grasp changes. Ain't that right?"

Subaru's feeble protest was brushed aside by Al's biting argument.

"You think you're some saint, or a hero? If that's it, it don't suit you one bit."

Shrugging, Al dismissed Subaru's confusion and hesitation as flatly absurd. Listening to his opinion, Subaru couldn't help but feel bewildered.

Back at city hall, when Subaru had argued with Anastasia and made a stand out of sheer stubbornness, it had been none other than Al who'd taken his side.

It was Al who'd voiced agreement with what amounted to self-satisfying, futile resistance.

So why, now, was he flipping his position?

"What you're saying now is completely different from before. Are you on my side or not — which is it?"

"No, no, not at all different. I ain't sayin' it's wrong to see your self-satisfaction through. I'm sayin' the limits of that self-satisfaction are startin' to show, ain't they. Your idea of savin' everybody, every single person — that got shot down lookin' at this shelter's wreckage, didn't it? So ain't it fine, now? Just go, honestly, and protect only what matters, and skip town."

"Skip town... you mean run away? In a situation like this?"

"Somethin' wrong with that? Facin' somethin' you can't handle, what's so strange about choosin' to run? Me, I'm gonna collect my princess and make a run for it outta this city. Ain't got any duty to risk my life for the people here, no reason to do that either."

Slipping a finger under the collar of his helmet, scratching his neck, Al glared at Subaru, who'd been struck speechless.

"You oughta do the same, brother. Just save the girl — Emilia — and run. Those Witch Cult bastards are pests that keep crawlin' back no matter how many times you crush 'em. Like a random mugger on the street. Gettin' involved just costs you."

Al's reasoning was, in its own way, a valid answer.

That the Witch Cult were pests — on that, Subaru agreed completely. That getting entangled with them brought no benefit at all was, likewise, an undeniable fact.

But they were the ones who'd come at Subaru's group first. To brush off the sparks raining down on him, Subaru had to act.

From Al's point of view, he probably couldn't understand the reason Subaru felt compelled to act at all.

Of course, that was setting aside the pressing fact that Emilia was currently a captive. But even if, hypothetically, Emilia had nothing to do with any of this, Subaru still wouldn't choose to run.

Why? Just why was that—

"Don't have to be you, brother. Why's it gotta be you who does it?"

"If a kid darts out into a crosswalk against a red light, you grab their hand and pull them back onto the sidewalk before you even think about the reason why. ...I guess it's something like that."

"—"

Subaru could tell that Al had caught his breath at that answer.

He himself wasn't entirely sure whether that answer he'd just given was really the right one. But it was also true that when he'd stopped to think it over, the words had come out smoothly, and it was also true that his chest had felt lighter for it.

"I'm not thinking anything complicated. I'm here, so I do what I can. I already know there's a lot I won't be able to reach — I can see that just looking at the people here. But still."

To just let everything he couldn't reach stay unreached — that felt like cowardice to him.

That, surely, was something Subaru mustn't do. That's what he believed.

"Bo—"

At Subaru's calm-faced answer, Garfiel started to call out to him.

But Garfiel hesitated to say the word "Boss." Having just been called out on it by Al a moment ago, he must have felt some resistance to saying it again.

Garfiel's consideration in that moment of hesitation made Subaru just a little bit glad.

And at the same time, he'd realized something. An idea had come to him.

A clever move that might just let him turn this Authority of "Wrath" against itself.

"Garfiel, don't hold back. Keep calling me what you always have."

"—"

"Felt embarrassing at first, but it fits now. Can't promise I'll live up to what you expect of me, but I'll do everything I can, as far as I'm able."

Subaru had no way of knowing exactly how he looked in Garfiel's expectant gaze.

But the fact that Garfiel could see hope in Subaru even in a moment he'd thought was a dead end — that was the result of Subaru's own actions. Just as it had been, once, when he'd placed a curse on a single girl.

So Subaru had to take responsibility for his own actions.

"...Y-yeah, Boss. Got it. I'll lend you all the strength you need, however much. So — you don't gotta say weak stuff like that no more."

"Alright. Then, help me out. Once we hand the injured here over to the Iron Fang, we need to head straight back to city hall. Anastasia's probably going to object."

Brushing off his backside and standing, Subaru clapped the shoulder of the nodding Garfiel.

Garfiel snorted and bumped fists with him; after seeing this through, Subaru turned around and tilted his head toward the silent Al.

"My mind's made up. Might not be the direction you wanted, though."

"...Do what you want, brother. At least until we find the princess, or until stickin' with you all stops meanin' anything, I'll keep tagging along."

Despite having his proposal flatly rejected, Al answered without showing any sign of displeasure.

Feeling something not quite sitting right about that attitude, Subaru nonetheless turned toward the shelter's exit, preparing so they could move the moment reinforcements arrived.

Garfiel followed at his back, and Al trailed a little behind.

Watching the backs of the two ahead of him, Al reached his one right arm around behind his head to prop it up, let out a long breath, and murmured,

"Livin' up to everybody's expectations like that... how's that gonna turn out. Guess you won't know till it hurts."
