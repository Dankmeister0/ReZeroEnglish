Arc 4 Chapter 44: "Taboo"

—The body takes form.

The flesh that had been torn away in mouthfuls, the skin that had been ripped off, the bones that had been ground to pieces, the nerves chewed to nothing, the blood that had been lapped and sucked dry, the soul trampled by a monstrous, ravenous appetite—all of it returns to its original form.

Sensation flooded back into his fingertips, and from that point Subaru's whole body jerked as if in convulsions.

Writhing on the dusty, cold, hard ground, groans slipping from his throat, white foam spilled from the corner of Subaru's mouth.

No pain. No sense of loss. His limbs were attached to his torso as they should be, and neither the head that perceived this nor its contents had suffered any physical damage. His thoughts were still hazy, as if just waking, but that too was an uncertainty promised to clear within mere seconds.

And yet Subaru's body, his consciousness, refused to accept its return to the reality he'd come back to. That was how horrific the events before his return had been.

Who else had ever experienced being invaded through the mouth, their entire intestines devoured? The skin flayed from his whole body as if in some contest, and then the sensation of rough tongues scraping away at the exposed, blackish-red muscle and pink fat beneath, lapping it clean.

His brain had refused to register the pain, so that even though it was his own body, he could perceive the reality of "being eaten" objectively, as though it belonged to someone else—a waking nightmare.

"Guh... guh... guh..."

Nausea surged up, but all that came from his empty stomach was a yellowish trickle of gastric fluid. A sour taste mixed into the foam spilling from his mouth, and Subaru, still lying on his side, went on convulsing.

Like someone in the throes of a seizure, or a fish flopping on dry land—it wasn't Subaru's will rejecting reality, but his soul making that choice.

Who could possibly accept having their very existence devoured? Who could blame Subaru's breakdown, returning as he had while still fully grasping the fact that he had been eaten?

By what twist of fate, whose scheme, had he been made to suffer such a thing?

"—————"

His consciousness flickered on and off.

Whether his eyes were open or closed, he couldn't say; control of his own body would not return to him.

His soul was refusing to go on living in reality. Far from letting his consciousness choose, it wouldn't even allow Subaru to stand before the choice at all.

His body simply kept forcing him to tremble before the "fact of what he had lost," drowning him again and again in despair.

—Why.

If there was one certain word left in Subaru's mind, it was only this.

—Why.

What had happened? What had occurred? Why had it come to that? Why had there been no way to avoid it? What was he now? What must he become now? What should he do from here?

—Why, why, why, why, why.

Before a proposition with no answer—one whose very question was not even clear—his soul screamed on and on.

—Why?! Why?! Why?!

And there it was: the unsightly, stubbornly unyielding figure that kept hurling out an unanswerable question, over and over.

Drowning in reality, tormented by nightmare, having lost sight of any path forward, still asking "why."

That, precisely, was—

'Once again, you have earned the qualification.'

To Subaru, trembling in small shudders, it was a voice that seemed to whisper right beside his ear.

A high, buoyant voice. One Subaru could hear even now, though he couldn't yet chew through its meaning. And yet it was a voice that resounded terribly, deep within him even now—

'I invite you—to the witches' tea party.'

In the next instant, Subaru's consciousness, only just returned, lost its grip on reality once more.

Atop a low hill thick with lush green grass, a refreshing wind blew, one that called summer to mind.

The cool breeze tousled Subaru's bangs and set the tall green grass swaying wildly, then swept on past the hill, across the meadow, and away toward the far blue sky where white clouds danced.

Lightly touching his fingertips to the bangs tickled by that unseen wind, Subaru narrowed his eyes against the dazzling sunlight before slowly lowering his gaze to look ahead.

He found himself seated, without knowing when, in a rocking chair much like a recliner. Across a small white table sat someone in an identical chair, legs crossed—a girl whose hair and every inch of exposed skin were colored pure white, clad in mourning black that overturned that very impression—no, perhaps "girl" wasn't the fitting word for her.

"After all, she's a witch who's been lazing about for over four hundred years..."

"To say that of a girl—my, what a way to talk. In my case, I died at nineteen, so appearance-wise I ought to be a young girl well-suited to you, don't you think?"

"'Died at nineteen' carries some quiet weight, you know. And don't go pulling some nonsensical logic to plant yourself beside me. 'Well-suited to me'—I'd laugh that off so hard my navel would boil the tea."

"Oh my, oh my... Have I been turned down, I wonder?"

"That's not it. I'm saying don't sell yourself short. I'm well aware I'm the lowest of the low. 'Well-suited,' 'a good match'—that's not a rating you get handed down to you, it's something I have to climb up and earn. And right now, I haven't got a single thing in hand that'd make me worthy of it."

Opening and closing his fists on his knees, Subaru muttered this while looking up at the sky with a bitter expression.

Hearing this, the witch—Echidna—propped her chin on her hand against the white table, looking Subaru up and down slowly as if studying him.

"You seem to think rather little of yourself."

"When everyone around you is bigger, you naturally get in the habit of talking with your head tilted up. I'd like to think I've at least graduated from the pathetic state of not even noticing I'm looking up, though."

Looking back on who he'd been until now, Subaru clenched the fist he'd been holding open.

He made his knuckles crack with the strength of his grip, drew one deep breath, and then—

"So... what wind blew this invitation to tea my way?"

"Nothing complicated. I am the Witch of Greed, and specifically the embodiment of a hunger for knowledge. A heart that seeks, that craves, is a pleasing thing to me—and if that craving takes the form of a desire to know, a lament that asks 'why,' then so much the better."

As she spoke, she lifted the white cup from the table to her lips.

Her throat working as she swallowed the contents, she smiled faintly and said—

"If I had to name a cause, I'd want you to understand it lies in your own act of wishing for this in a place where the path to my castle runs easy."

"Don't make it so convoluted. ...Well, never mind, for now I won't complain about being invited to tea. There's something else I want to ask, more than that."

Waving off Echidna's pompous phrasing with a flick of his hand, Subaru leaned forward. Glaring so as not to look away from the white beauty before him, he said—

"What happened to me?"

"Don't you already understand that yourself?"

"Understanding and accepting are two different things. I think I get, objectively, what state I was in—but that understanding doesn't connect to my current state."

"Meaning?"

"A minute ago I'd lost my mind, drowning on dry land, foaming at the mouth—and here I am in this place, properly composed, with my head clear enough to at least hold a conversation. It's more natural to assume you've had a hand in that somehow."

Even as he traded these glib words, Subaru's mind was spinning at full speed to grasp the situation.

The fact that he'd been drawn into this dream world Echidna called a tea party, and that at the very least enough composure had arisen within him to turn his mind toward such things—

—it was precisely because he now had the composure to think things through calmly that he understood how dangerous his body's condition had been in the real world, before he'd slipped into this dream. He wasn't free of unease at the thought of his mind being here alone, having left behind a body caught in some kind of shock symptoms, but—

"Last time I came to your tea party, I woke up in a bed afterward, so I don't know—what about elapsed time? No, more to the point, what about the outside world..."

Thinking that far, Subaru realized this was no time to be sitting here calmly drinking tea.

—Subaru, who had merely been convulsing in shock, had never confirmed the time at which he'd returned via Return by Death.

Worse than that, he hadn't even confirmed the location where he'd been foaming at the mouth.

Burning with a regret that had come far too late, Subaru shot to his feet as though kicking the chair away.

"Echidna! Get me out of here, right now!"

"Leaving a witches' tea party without so much as touching your tea? How presumptuous. You might want to take a bit more time to consider just what sort of being I am, sitting here before you—"

"I don't have the luxury of humoring your little speeches! Get me outside right now! Even as we speak, out there—"

"You failed once already, going in with nothing in hand—and now you'd walk out of here empty-handed again... Meaning to repeat the same pain, the same loss?"

Unable to rein in his impatience, Subaru had been raising his voice at Echidna's leisurely manner. As if dousing that mind of his in cold water, Echidna posed the question in a voice frozen of all emotion.

"...Ah."

"I applaud the act of challenging something in order to obtain a result. Whether the result is the one you wanted or not, I find beauty in the process of trial and error undertaken to produce it. Never losing that spirit of challenge feels, to me, precious and noble. But—"

Raising a finger toward the tongue-tied Subaru, Echidna continued haltingly, her eyes narrowing.

"—to pay no heed to the previous result, and walk the same path to reach the same conclusion... That is an act that desecrates the accumulation of knowledge, and I'd rather part ways, with contempt, from anyone who does it."

"You... are—"

"By the way, to answer your question... out there, the time right now is just after you overcame the first Trial in the tomb. Fortunately, time flows differently here than outside. Just having tea with me here won't leave you all that far behind, time-wise."

Echidna knocked down, one by one, both what Subaru had wanted to know and what worried him.

If her words were to be believed, the time outside was just after the first Trial—meaning the restart point of his Return by Death had not changed.

Subaru's body, left behind for this tea party, was presumably still lying discarded within the tomb's Trial, beside Emilia even now as she fought on against it.

He wasn't about to swallow her words whole, but hearing that fact from a third party had, to no small degree, poured a measure of calm water over his panic.

And once relief had opened up a little room in his heart, the next question to surface was—

"Echidna... how much do you know?"

"Of what I know, I know as much as I know. Of what I wish to know—well, I'd like to know everything in this world."

"Don't dodge, this is important. For instance... right, you just said this is happening right after the first Trial."

The first Trial—even that phrase felt terribly distant to him now.

After Subaru finished bidding farewell to his parents in that dream-world, Echidna had been the one to greet him in the empty schoolhouse. After a brief exchange of questions with her, Subaru had returned to reality.

After that, before things settled into his present circumstances, more events had befallen him than words could ever fully recount—but.

"Setting all that aside, then, you and I meeting again would mean it's right after we parted..."

"That's right. Certainly, in real time, and even in the skewed sense of felt time, the interval between our parting and now is short. It works out to us seeing each other again after a mere few minutes."

"Just how much did you miss me?" Echidna laughed, half-joking. Subaru let the quip roll off him with a blank expression, and she shrugged, looking disappointed.

"You really don't give me the reactions I'm after, do you. Not having things go the way I want is frustrating, and yet pleasing too—it's a complicated feeling."

"I'll indulge your complicated maiden's heart at leisure, sometime when there's time to spare. More importantly..."

"If you had some feeling of celebrating our quick reunion, I think my tongue would loosen up more. Well, I suppose it can't be helped that you're so impatient. After all—"

There, Echidna paused.

In that brief gap for breath, she cast a sidelong glance at Subaru with her black eyes. Subaru, as he was now, couldn't read what emotion had flickered for an instant through those eyes.

Only, as if welcoming Subaru's bewilderment, Echidna smiled a bewitching smile, and—

"Unlike me, for whom our parting was a mere few minutes—for you, this reunion comes after a gap of hours, no, perhaps even days. That's how it will be."

Replaying Echidna's words in his head, Subaru chewed through them and understood that their meaning could be taken in only one way.

Given her tone just now, and that meaningful look in her eyes and smile, there was no way his understanding could be mistaken.

She—the witch—knew. She knew of the residue of futures Subaru's soul had piled up and failed to grasp, futures that by rights should no longer remain anywhere in this world.

"How...?!"

"Let me answer that question this way: because this is my castle, and because I am the Witch of Greed. I don't believe I've shown you yet, have I?"

Before Subaru, still narrowing his question down, Echidna tilted her head and held out her right hand.

Light descended onto her palm, and that light gradually took shape—giving rise to a single book bound in pure white.

A book with no title or anything else written on it, about the size of a dictionary. Setting aside the fact that its color was a white so pure it hurt to look at, its shape alone stirred nothing but unpleasant memories in Subaru.

"Don't tell me that's... the Gospel."

"My, without even needing this book, I can tell exactly what you're thinking from your face. A face that seems to want to say 'you too?' I could guess a fair bit about what you've been through out there, just from that alone..."

Reading Subaru's inner thoughts from his expression, Echidna gently opened the white book in her hands. She ran her eyes over its contents, murmuring "Mm-hm, mm-hm" as she traced the letters and nodded several times.

"I see, I've grasped the general shape of it now. There are parts I couldn't follow entirely, but I'll just fill those in by reading you yourself. ...Mm, this really is lovely. This sensation of filling in missing pieces, this indescribable anticipation of drawing near an answer. It's precisely this that makes life worth living, after all...!"

"...You're already dead, you know."

Overwhelmed by the heat in Echidna's words, Subaru barely managed that retort. Hearing it, Echidna twirled the end of her white hair around a finger with a sulking expression.

"Having cold water thrown on me like that saps my enthusiasm, even if it's true. Anyway, let me ease that anxious worry of yours. ...This book is different from that 'Gospel' thing in your memory. What's written in it isn't the future, or an optimal solution. Just facts."

"Facts...?"

"If the Gospel belongs to the category of prophecy books, then what I hold here should be called something like a history book, perhaps. It never settles into a fixed form as a book—forever hazy, ambiguous, vague and boundless, no book at all and yet every book, holding the possibility of any book, capable of becoming any book at all... The people of the world four hundred years ago called this thing, which fills its blank pages with nothing but facts, the Book of Wisdom. But I call it something else."

"—————"

"—The World's Memory, I call it."

A grandiose name—but it carried power enough to live up to it.

If her words were true, then that book could read anything, about anyone or anything at all, so long as it was history that had already occurred in the past. For gathering information, it was, indeed, an absurdly overpowered ability.

"Though, I'm not overly fond of relying on this, myself. After all, whatever content I lay eyes on gets burned directly into memory, just like that. A magic tome that skips the process of 'coming to know' and rewrites it straight into 'having already known'—for someone like me who loves to want to know things, the value of a book that skips the process of knowing is a difficult thing to judge."

"If you don't want it, you could just hand it over to me. I've got plenty of uses for it. In fact, with that, I might even find a thread toward solving my problems—"

"If you don't want to end up a vegetable, you'd best give that up."

With that, Echidna cut Subaru's opportunistic suggestion off flat, and held the white-bound World's Memory out in front of his face.

"Harmless as it may look, it's still a witch's tool. An ordinary person's brain would burn out from the sheer volume of information seared into it. Better not to read it, if you want to stay safe."

"Then don't go waving it in my face like that, it's terrifying!"

Learning it was the sort of breakthrough that lay beyond his reach, his spirits sank at a steep angle.

Pushing the magic tome back firmly with his palm, Subaru watched Echidna wave her hand again to turn it back into particles of light. Setting aside, for later, the point that it was convenient for storage since it took up no shelf space, Subaru found himself convinced of the sheer scale of her knowledge.

With that magic tome, sure enough, one really could reduce "things unknown" to an absolute minimum. That she chose not to indulge in it might simply be a way of thinking peculiar to witches.

"But if you already understand that much, this'll go quick. Hey, Echidna. If you already know about my situation... that is, my Death—"

Subaru's throat abruptly froze as he tried to say the words "Return by Death."

But this wasn't caused by the unconditional penalty that came whenever he tried to speak the forbidden words.

Before Subaru as he froze, Echidna simply waited in silence, her white hair swaying in the wind, as though waiting for his words.

The more he sensed something almost like consideration in her, something so unlike a witch, the faster his pulse raced, and in turn the heavier and slower his tongue became.

What had frozen Subaru's voice was a single primal emotion—namely, fear.

"Haah... haah...!"

Until now, Subaru had had numerous chances to speak those forbidden words.

The Authority of Return by Death, dwelling within Subaru.

This thing, whose very right to be called an Authority was hard to judge, forcibly obstructed any attempt to convey its circumstances to another person. And it did so with an unreasonableness and coercive force impossible to resist or endure—by directly assaulting the heart itself.

And that venomous fang had once struck not only Subaru's own body, but Emilia's as well, when he had tried to confess everything to her. Subaru could never forget the sense of loss and the anguished grief of that moment.

There had been few times he'd wished so badly to simply die, to vanish.

The fact that, through his own foolishness, he had piled up regret and bitterness too many times to claim it had never happened even once—that was pathetic, infuriating—and yet, still, Subaru's cowed heart feared taking that step forward.

It wasn't the heart being tormented that frightened him. Of course pain was frightening. But if it were a sacrifice necessary to draw in the future he wanted, he would endure it however much it took, even if it tore his throat raw.

What Subaru feared was that by speaking that information here, in this place, those black fingers might reach for someone else besides himself.

No, Subaru shook his head.

During the fight with the White Whale, during the final reckoning with Petelgeuse—each time, Subaru had spoken those forbidden words in situations where others were present.

Whenever he was dragged into that unreasonable punishment, the words he'd tried to confess would cut off, and their continuation would be flung away beyond a world where time had stopped. Because of that, he had never once been questioned about it.

He had never made anyone who sought the rest of those vanished words a victim of that black demonic hand.

—Except for Emilia.

"—————"

The memory returned to him: the silver-haired girl growing light in his arms.

If he were to taste that sense of loss again, this time it would be unbearable.

He thought, even now, that it was a wonder he hadn't gone mad, back then. Having killed Emilia, having walked around still holding her corpse—it was a wonder he hadn't broken entirely.

That was how sinful it was. That was how terrifying it was.

And so fear bound Subaru fast, making him hesitate to speak the words here.

Standing before him now was the witch Echidna. Frankly speaking, his relationship with her was shallow enough that even comparing it to Emilia felt presumptuous.

He was even making the coldly calculating prediction that, even if her heart were crushed, he wouldn't taste anything like the despair or loss of that moment.

And yet he still couldn't move—both because Subaru was soft-hearted, and because the conditions were too different from anything before, leaving him with a strong unease at not being able to predict the outcome.

Whenever someone ignorant of Return by Death was involved, the forbidden words tormented Subaru's own heart.

When he'd tried to confess Return by Death to someone precious to him, the forbidden words had crushed that precious person's heart instead.

Then, if he tried to confess it to a being who had already seen through Subaru's Return by Death from some other direction entirely, what would the result be?

Would only Subaru suffer for it, or would that demonic hand reach the being before him as well—

"Why don't you try it and see."

"—————?!"

"Acting in order to obtain the result you want is a precious thing. That belief hasn't changed. And I think it's precisely those who take such action who have value in living."

Not knowing—or rather, no, that wasn't it—that the harm from Subaru's hesitation might turn upon herself.

The witch had, in all likelihood, already seen through exactly what Subaru was worried about.

And the witch herself knew, too, that she couldn't foresee the result. Even so, that she could tell him to go ahead and do it anyway was for no reason other than that she believed, to the very core of her being, in the conviction she had just voiced.

"There might not even be time left to regret it, you know...?"

"Should it come to that, I suppose I'll look forward to you breaking down in tears before my corpse."

To Subaru's stance of refusing the choice right up to the very edge, Echidna responded with an attitude of unwavering ease.

Even that attitude, perhaps, was a consideration meant to keep any unnecessary circumstance from intruding on Subaru's decision.

It wasn't so much consideration for Subaru, he thought, as her own sincerity toward the result that decision would produce.

He wasn't being expected to do it, nor was it being wished of him.

It was simply the sight of her, wanting nothing but possibility—the possibility that was an answer—that pushed Subaru's back forward.

A way of living that held no doubt whatsoever about the reason and meaning of one's own existence.

He knew he couldn't live that way himself, and yet he felt, somehow, saved by that strength.

"Echidna. I have the power of Return by Death—"

And so, he spoke the forbidden words—
