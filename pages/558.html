Arc 7 Chapter 59: "Taritta Shudrak"

"—Hey, Taritta. I was chosen as a 'Stargazer.' I've been given a heavenly mandate."

The one who confided this to Taritta was Mariuli, a fellow member of the People of Shudrak.

All of the People of Shudrak are born, raised, and end their lives in the Buddheim Jungle. For that reason, everyone in the settlement shares a bond like family, and Mariuli was no exception. That said, Mariuli held an especially close place in Taritta's heart. The reason was that the two of them had been born on the same day, in the same place, raising their first cries together—soul sisters.

—Among the Shudrak, children born on the same day are believed to share a bond of souls.

That bond is considered stronger than that of blood sisters, and the two are treated as veritable halves of a single whole. It is an ancient Shudrak custom, and soul sisters are bound by deep, deep ties. Kuna and Holly, too, are soul sisters born on the same day. Considering how close those two are, one can understand the existence of a bond that transcends blood. A beautiful Shudrak woman with the tips of her black hair dyed pink—that was Mariuli, Taritta's soul sister.

She was gentle and calm, and got along well with Taritta, who was withdrawn and shy around others. Taritta, too, often brought her worries about her own blood sister to her soul sister. She confided her anxieties about lacking the confidence to one day support her sister, who would eventually inherit the chieftaincy. Whatever the conflict, however unseemly the anxiety, Taritta could naturally open up about it with Mariuli.

So when Mariuli said she had a secret she wanted to confide, Taritta felt she had to live up to that trust—she was happy to be relied upon. And then Mariuli said it.

—That she had been chosen as a "Stargazer," and given a heavenly mandate to fulfill.

"Fulfilling the mandate is a Stargazer's important duty. It's such, such an honor, Taritta."

"I don't... really understand. What would the heavens have to say to Mariuli?"

"Not the heavens, Taritta. The stars tell me. The stars give me my duty. Such a very important duty... I really wasn't going to tell anyone. But..."

"—————"

"You're my soul sister."

Turning back with that smile, Mariuli's trust left Taritta unable to say anything. It was simply a story Mariuli, her soul sister, had trusted her enough to confide. The thought of telling anyone else never even occurred to Taritta.

Besides, something like that—a mere delusion—would surely be forgotten soon enough, a passing moment of confusion. It wasn't something to be taken seriously, just a fleeting lapse. By believing that, Taritta let herself look away from Mariuli's trouble. But the change crept in gradually, and yet unmistakably, taking on the shape of something abnormal.

One day, it happened. When Taritta and Mizelda returned from the hunt, Mariuli, who was looking after the settlement's children, was singing a song neither of them knew. —That was the strange part.

Within the settlement, the People of Shudrak are divided into two roles. One is the role of hunter, leaving the settlement to make a living by taking down prey. Mizelda, Taritta, and most Shudrak belong to this side. The other is the role of guardian, remaining in the settlement, raising children, and working to protect the village. Mariuli belonged to the guardian side.

Her skill at hunting never improved at all—a disastrous shot who'd miss with all hundred arrows if she drew a bow. But she was skilled at cooking and sewing, and good at telling children songs and old tales. So the mere fact that Mariuli was singing wasn't strange in itself. Mariuli was quite accomplished at singing, so much so that at festivals everyone would beg her to sing more. Taritta, meanwhile, was hopeless at most guardian duties, singing included. Truly, the two of them together made one whole person—without each other, neither could fully carry out her share of the work. And yet—

"—Never heard that song of Mariuli's before."

"S-sister..."

"But it's a good song. Pleasant to the ear."

Mizelda, who had also stopped at the sound of the same song, paid no mind whatsoever to how strange it was. Kuna, Holly, and the others were the same. Everyone noticed that Mariuli was singing a song they'd never heard, but no one gave a thought to what it might mean. Only Taritta feared the song's origin, and grew anxious over it.

This was the same Mariuli who had spoken of a heavenly mandate, of the stars teaching her a duty she must perform. Surely, Taritta feared, she would go on to claim she'd learned even that song from the stars—

"That's not it, Taritta. You misunderstand. I didn't learn it from the stars—I just sang a song I already knew."

"A song you... already knew?"

"That's right, a song I already knew... not me, but another 'Stargazer'..."

"—! I don't understand what you mean!"

It wasn't the answer Taritta had feared. But in a certain sense, the answer Mariuli gave was worse than what she'd feared. The mention of "another Stargazer" was proof that she hadn't forgotten the fantasy she'd spoken of. Proof that the daydream was still going on. —No, proof that it was getting worse.

But seeing Taritta unable to understand talk of the "Stargazer," Mariuli's face fell with loneliness. It felt as though she were betraying that trust, and Taritta writhed in the agony of incomprehension.

—After that, too, Mariuli's honeymoon with the "Stargazer" continued.

Displaying knowledge she shouldn't have, singing songs she shouldn't know, telling tales she shouldn't know, spending her days fulfilling some unknown heavenly mandate. It was a Mariuli that Taritta didn't know.

They had always talked everything over, no matter what. There had never been a decision in Taritta's life for which she hadn't sought Mariuli's opinion. It was the same for Mariuli—even her daughter's name had been decided that way.

And yet Mariuli's attention was always on the sky now. As if prioritizing something above even the People of Shudrak, above even Taritta and her own daughter, her life was gradually, gradually being seized by the stars, bound by the mandate.

Someone—someone who was not the stars—must be putting ideas in her head. Suspecting as much, Taritta had once quietly investigated her surroundings. But as a guardian who never left the settlement, Mariuli had no opportunity to gain outside knowledge. It truly was as though the whispers of the stars themselves were feeding her all manner of things.

"Is it you who is leading Mariuli astray?"

Looking up at the night sky, she asked the stars visible through the gaps in the trees. But the stars that spoke to Mariuli said nothing to Taritta, soul-bound to her though she was. Not the unknown knowledge, not the unheard songs, not the mandate she had to fulfill—none of it.

"—Give her back. Give me back my soul sister."

Drawing the bowstring taut, Taritta aimed at one particularly bright star in the night sky. When she released the drawn string, the sharp arrow shot howling into the night sky—and, of course, fell back down uselessly without reaching anywhere at all. The stars would not even acknowledge Taritta's will to rebel.

The stars said nothing. They granted no mandate. Then what, exactly, had changed Mariuli? That, she didn't know.

Nor did she know who she ought to voice this lament to. All the worries and troubles she couldn't speak of, she had always had Mariuli listen to. So when the worry and trouble was caused by Mariuli herself, who was there left to listen?

Surely her sister wouldn't understand. Her sister was strong, composed. There was no way she'd fret over something, hesitating and faltering. And beyond simply not being understood, there was another fear in confiding to her sister.

What Mariuli was now ran contrary to the teachings and the very way of life of the People of Shudrak. Would Mizelda, now chieftain, allow it? It was even possible that Mariuli could be banished from the forest for having arrived at such dangerous thinking. There had been those exiled from the tribe in the past, and such people could never return to the forest again.

Taritta didn't want to lose the chance to see Mariuli ever again. Even if the Mariuli of now had changed from the one whose soul was bound to Taritta's.

And so Taritta kept the anguish she carried locked away deep in her own heart. And then—

"—What should I do, Taritta? I'm a 'Stargazer,' and yet..."

—And then, just as in the nightmare she saw every night, that day's events truly began.

Sensing the shift in the tide of battle, everyone on the battlefield began responding in their own way. By this point, with everyone's nerves honed razor-sharp, no one was falling behind.

The Great Disaster rampages, its raging shadow swallowing and scouring everything around it, the annihilation spreading wider. Yet against that calamity, not a single one of those who had resolved to resist it flinched. That was the hard-won reward of those who had cleared the first wall.

There are conditions for challenging the Great Disaster. One must be a warrior. —This means one who possesses the power to fight. But at the same time, it also means one who possesses the heart to resist. Lacking either power or heart, one cannot stand on the battlefield as a warrior. Conversely, so long as one could prove possession of both, there was no other wall barring the way to challenging the Great Disaster.

"Uaaugh!"

Rui, letting out a wordless cry, gritted her teeth as she kept dodging the shadow's attacks. She had no connection whatsoever to Chaosflame; whether this city lived or died meant nothing to her one way or the other. Even if the city vanished entirely, it wouldn't cost her a thing. That someone so young, so frail-looking, clung so desperately to the fight against the Great Disaster was for a reason other than that. If that reason was what gave Rui her power and her resolve, no one had the right to stop her.

And this wasn't true of Rui alone. Medium, swinging her cutlass with her small frame; Al, holding a dao to his own throat; Kafma, mobilizing the full power of the Insects within his body; the citizens of the demon city with flames lit in one eye—all of them, no less than Rui, were people who had resolved not to retreat before the Great Disaster.

Each had different reasons, but each had a reason to challenge the Great Disaster that sought to destroy the demon city. And for those who had cleared that first wall, the Great Disaster's behavior was simplistic—not, despite its danger, something on a level where dealing with it left them at a complete loss.

—The Great Disaster is not a living thing. This was the single greatest factor.

The Great Disaster was, by now, a calamity of that particular kind—it could be called the phenomenon of ruin itself. The shadow that scoured away anything it touched, the shapeless, bulging, writhing main body that gave no confirmation an attack had even landed—but that was all it was.

"It's got no brain to adapt to our attacks or change up its approach."

Racing across the battlefield over the rubble, calling out instructions as he went, Al glared at the Great Disaster. There was no way to erase the fear leaking from the very core of his body, but false bravado and a grasp of the situation could buy his mind a little room to breathe. That room gave Al space to think.

Put plainly, the Great Disaster's behavior was one-pattern. It swept away or absorbed anything that came near, and, if unimpeded, writhed to swallow its surroundings in order to expand the damage further. But the defensive forces, Al included, weren't letting the Great Disaster expand any further than that. Landing blows, or becoming targets of the shadow's onslaught to scatter its aim, feeding it bait to buy time while it chewed—over and over, that was the pattern.

If the Great Disaster were a living thing, it would have learned by now, adapted, and changed the tide of battle. Or, growing emotional, its behavior pattern would have shifted, likewise drastically changing the battle. But because neither happened, this force could just barely hold it back. However—

"—Everyone, prepare to fall back, if you please."

With a single step, the ground shattered, and the surging shockwave split the Great Disaster's massive body in two. Of course, the Great Disaster writhed and immediately reformed into one, but the ability to deal visible-level damage was the absolute dominion of this demon city's mistress.

Having pulled that off, Yorna Mishigure rang out sharp footsteps on her thick-soled geta, tearing up the very earth to completely block a wave of shadow that surged like a tsunami.

"Yorna-chan!"

"Fall back, if you please. It vexes me sorely, but let us do as that man says."

"That man—you mean Abel-chin?"

Words were exchanged amid the raging storm, and Yorna dipped her slender chin at Medium's question. Her narrow eyes looked toward the demon-masked, handsome man standing on the high ground overlooking the battlefield. Though he had no ability to join the fight, that he remained there rather than fleeing was some kind of stubbornness on his part. In any case, through whatever calculations turned within that mind of his, the strategy he'd devised had been conveyed to Yorna, and she had chosen to carry it out. Then—

"—We shall swallow this city whole, and I myself shall make it burst from within. There is no other way to nip in the bud the seed of this calamity's rampage."

"Can it be done?"

"If one city was not enough with a castle thrown at it, then all that's left is to try throwing a whole town at it as well. Though, mind you, should even that not suffice, there'll be nothing for it but to make me an empress and throw a whole nation at it."

"Such a thing—as a retainer, I must firmly counsel against it!"

Hearing Yorna's plan, Kafma, his face reddening at that unnecessary remark, bellowed. But that earnest Insect Cage Tribe warrior quickly shifted his expression and nodded, "Understood." That nod was likely born of understanding that Yorna's decision to abandon the city was, for her, a choice that came with the pain of cutting into her own flesh. Hearing Kafma's brief reply, Yorna lowered her eyes faintly for a moment, then quickly smiled a bewitching smile.

"It is nothing much. So long as I and those who love me remain, a city such as this shall take shape again easily enough. —Peace of mind comes with me."

Placing a hand to her chest, Yorna spoke thus, and no one voiced any objection. In truth, no one knew just how much power Yorna's trump card would unleash, but it was equally clear that, at this point, she had no other decisive means against the Great Disaster. And so—

"—Everyone fights while we fall back! On the double!"

That single declaration from Medium was the signal to carry out the final strategy of the battle for the demon city.

—The nightmare she saw every night began on a chilly night when the moon hung white and frozen.

Mariuli, calling herself a "Stargazer," began showing off knowledge she shouldn't have. None of her kin paid any mind to the source of that knowledge, but Taritta's fear grew day by day. Yet she could confide her burden of anguish to no one. Mariuli had told no one but Taritta that she had become a "Stargazer." It wasn't calculation—simply a matter of the bond between them.

There are vows and secrets exchanged only between soul sisters. Even Kuna and Holly, so strongly bound to their Shudrak identity, surely had secrets between themselves that they hadn't told the rest of their kin.

For Mariuli, that was the role of "Stargazer" she had confided to Taritta. And the thought of leaking elsewhere a secret her soul sister had trusted her enough to share was inconceivable. So Taritta's anguish went on, shared with no one, tormenting her chest endlessly. But those days came to an abrupt, unexpected end. It happened like this—

"—What should I do, Taritta? I'm a 'Stargazer,' and yet..."

At Mariuli's stunned, murmured words, Taritta's eyes went wide and she froze, unable to move. Because what spilled from her mouth was not only that feeble lament, but also bright red blood, soaking her chest through.

She had never been particularly sturdy to begin with. The reason she worked as a guardian rather than a hunter wasn't only her unpolished hunting skill, but also that frailty of body. As a child she had often taken to bed. That, too, had grown less frequent as she became an adult, and no doubt everyone had let their guard down, assuming she was surely fine now. And yet the sickness had been slowly, irreversibly eating away at her depths all along.

Her face drained of color, Mariuli coughed up blood again and again. Her body grew thinner day by day, and it was plain to anyone's eyes that the flame of her life was flickering out. And so—

"The rest depends on Mariuli's will to live, Taritta."

"S-sister..."

"Stay with Mariuli. Until the end. That is a soul sister's duty."

Having made her drink medicinal broth, Mizelda's parting words weighed down on Taritta. A soul sister's duty—it was almost like a heavenly mandate. A mandate she must fulfill, spoken by a sister who was practically heaven itself to her.

The other Shudrak, too—Kuna, Holly, and the others who had known her long—each exchanged words with Mariuli, left Taritta with a word, and departed. Everyone understood that this was the last chance to speak with Mariuli. And that the one to see Mariuli's final moments through to the end could only be Taritta—the soul sister born on the same day, but fated to die on a different one.

"Mā..."

Mariuli's young daughter, not yet understanding the situation, gripped her mother's hand and gently nuzzled her cheek against it. Prompted by those around her, she said her farewell without fully comprehending it, and mother and child finished their last parting—the parting of an innocent daughter who never doubted she'd see her mother again tomorrow, and a mother on her deathbed.

"Sleep well."

Bidding farewell to the child who waved at her, and finishing her farewells with her kin who each in turn spoke to her, Mariuli faced her own death with dignity. That strength of core was surely the very Mariuli that Taritta had believed in and loved. And yet—

"What should I do, Taritta? I still haven't fulfilled my mandate..."

"Mariuli..."

"To leave it undone, like this... what was it all for..."

The instant she and Taritta were alone, whatever had been holding taut inside her snapped with an audible crack. Her face paling beyond even the loss of blood, what Mariuli fretted over was neither the "death" bearing down on her, nor her own child's future, but that incomprehensible thing—the whispering of the stars.

Tears welling in her eyes, Mariuli despaired, asking what it had all been for. At those tears, at that expression, it was Taritta who fell into despair first, even before her.

"You're still—still saying such things...!"

"Taritta..."

"There is no such thing as a mandate. There isn't! You are Mariuli of the Shudrak! Nothing more, nothing less—what else could you possibly want!"

To live as Shudrak, and die as Shudrak. That alone should be the most important thing, the thing that should take precedence above all else. What Mariuli, one of the Shudrak, needed in order to die as Shudrak—

"—Meaning. For having been born."

"...M-meaning?"

"That's what I want."

Even now, at this juncture, Mariuli's words—finding value in the mandate—pierced straight through Taritta. Born Shudrak, living as Shudrak, dying as Shudrak. Asked what more she could want beyond that, she answered that she wanted meaning for having been born.

—She had a daughter. She had Taritta. She had the Shudrak. And still she said she wanted meaning.

In that instant, she—Mariuli—had cast aside the blood flowing through her as meaningless. She had betrayed that blood, and declared that value lay instead in the whispering of the stars—a mere delusion. To Taritta, that was an unforgivable betrayal. And so—

"—Stop. Please, just stop."

"—————"

"I am your soul sister... and in front of me, you speak of meaning for having been born—"

Unable to bear hearing any more, Taritta declared her will to Mariuli with cold steel. The drawn dagger's tip was gently pressed to Mariuli's pale throat. She prayed that the blade's cold sharpness would bring Mariuli back to her senses, make her take back her words.

Of course, such a threat might not work on Mariuli, with death so near. But Taritta could think of no other method. So this was all that was left to her. Yet that was the greatest mistake of Taritta's life.

"That's right, Taritta. —I had you."

"Eh?"

Her face drained of color, Mariuli's eyes focused on Taritta. The color of those eyes looked different from any Mariuli she knew, and her body stiffened. That instant proved fatal. —For Mariuli.

"Mariuli...!?"

Mariuli's thin hand closed tightly over Taritta's, pressing the blade held against her downward. Taritta tried instinctively to resist, but a moment's confusion and a strength unbelievable for Mariuli's body stopped her.

It was a blade meant to counter the thick hide of beasts. Sharpened keen, it sliced through Mariuli's body like the surface of water, drawing blood. There came an awful sound and sensation, and Taritta understood: the dagger's tip had fatally gouged into Mariuli's chest.

"Right away...! I need to treat it right away...!"

"No, Taritta! You have a role to play...!"

"Don't say such foolish things! Now isn't the time for—"

"Listen!"

"—!"

It was a scream as if coughing up blood—no, one literally screamed while coughing up blood. Her chest gouged by the dagger, the flame of her life weakening moment by moment, and yet Mariuli's arms would not let Taritta go. Blood foaming at the corners of her mouth, her eyes fixed on Taritta. Clinging to a life on the verge of being lost, digging her nails into it by force—

"Beyond a thousand nights... I... you will meet a traveler..."

"—————"

"The traveler is an ally of the 'Great Disaster' that will destroy this land... so you must kill them..."

"Mariuli..."

"Kill... them..."

"Mariuli? ...Mariuli!"

The pain-filled voice, spun out along with the blood, drained the strength from Taritta's arms. At some point, Mariuli's unbelievable strength had vanished. That was the moment she should have stood and called for someone. —No, even calling would have been too late. Everything was too late. Mariuli's soul had already had Death's fingers hooked into it, on the verge of being carried away. The only thing delaying it even a second was an obsessive, delusion-like conviction.

"Black hair and black eyes, the traveler... kill them."

"—————"

"Stop the 'Great Disaster,' Taritta... that is my role... I can no longer... fulfill it, so... please, Taritta... my soul... sister..."

"Y-you're doing this here, now...!"

For the first time, Taritta hated Mariuli, who, bleeding, spent her last strength on this plea. All this time, Taritta had desperately tried to hold her back, invoking the bond of their souls. She had ignored all of it—and now, at the very last, to bring it up herself. It was cowardly beyond words, and the words struck straight at Taritta's heart.

Hearing the tears in Taritta's voice, Mariuli's eyes wavered faintly. Then the strength drained from her body, and slowly, her head slumped down, lifeless.

"—U, ah, ta"

"Mariuli?"

"—————"

"Mariuli!!"

At the last, a faint, ragged breath escaped, and that was the end. Limp, the dagger still driven into her chest, Mariuli's body had stopped beating. Taritta stood stunned for a few seconds, then rushed out of the hut to call for her sisters. But—

"—Ah"

Having flung open the hut's door and dashed to go outside, Taritta's feet stopped. There, a small shadow that should not have been there sat huddled.

Whether out of worry for her sick mother, or some ill premonition felt even in her youth— In any case, slipping past the eyes of the adults, the child had displayed, on a night when it absolutely should never have been displayed, the very talent that could one day make her a superb hunter.

Drenched in her mother's blood, tears streaming down in torrents—Utakata was staring at Taritta.

The Great Disaster's shadow raged, and violent tremors cracked the demon city from within to without.

Yorna's decision spread among the demon city's residents, each abandoning the land that had become their home. It had been an agonizing decision. To them, this was the farthest edge of the earth that had taken in people with nowhere else to go, a place that had given them the freedom of no longer having to hide. With all of that about to be lost, how could they not grieve? And yet—

"So long as you live, I shall save you yet, myself. Hurry, so as not to fall behind, that you may lay eyes upon a new demon city."

Because the demon city's mistress, the last bastion for those with nowhere else to belong, declared as much, the people believed in rebuilding and renewal, and ran, leaving behind the homes and hometown they had grown used to. Destruction swallowed the demon city, smashing it apart, and the scale of the Great Disaster wreaking it expanded. The shadow it wielded grew thicker, and the writhing jet-black mass swelled visibly, doubling and doubling again.

The expansion in scale and increase in shadow directly meant an increase in the Great Disaster's danger. The range it swept away widened all at once, and even as she glanced sidelong at the earth and cityscape being gouged away, Yorna marveled at the achievements of Al, the man responsible for keeping human casualties to a minimum.

"Medium, girl! Fall back! That's enough! Any more and you can't outrun it as you are now! It's time!"

"Uu~! I'm frustrated! But sorry!"

Trusting Al's shouted judgment, Medium, cutlass slung over her shoulder, fled at full speed. A barrage of shadow shot after her back, but thorns bursting out from the side formed a wall to block it, and the girl who had fulfilled the dangerous role of decoy with her small body made good her escape.

"Antenna boy, you saved me! But you should get going too, soon..."

"Soon what? Surely you don't mean to tell me to retreat? If so, I, Kafma Irulux, absolutely cannot heed such a suggestion!"

"Nah, figured you'd say something like that, but I meant it for real."

"What was that?!"

"Nothing."

Shrugging, Al slowly shook his head at Kafma's roar. Honestly, without Kafma's support, the fight against the Great Disaster would have been far more precarious. He was grateful for the help, but the man's intensity wore on him. Anyway—

"—Fox lady!"

"I dislike that manner of address, but I understand well enough."

At Al's voice calling to her, Yorna dipped her chin in acknowledgment. Passing from the city center out to the outer gardens, the fighting retreat from the Great Disaster was finally reaching its climax. Yorna's decision to abandon the demon city had spread to all, and a considerable portion of the residents' evacuation was complete. All that remained, as decided, was to make the Great Disaster that had swallowed the demon city pay the price for what it had done. —Though she hoped, by some stroke of luck, that it might not prove necessary after all.

"That would be lingering attachment, I suppose."

No convenient miracle occurred—the Great Disaster suddenly reaching its limit and crumbling away from the edges, or a weak point suddenly exposed that they need only strike. Fighting it had made this much clear. —That Great Disaster was a calamity that would go on rampaging inexhaustibly.

If it wasn't stopped, the damage wouldn't be confined to the demon city—far, far more would be swallowed up in ruin. To prevent that, the precious demon city had to be sacrificed.

"—————"

Closing her eyes, Yorna spared a single moment's thought for hesitation and regret. Yorna's Soul Marriage Technique was a singular thing, even within the history of that art. Ordinarily, the total quantity of soul a single individual could hold, however much individual variance there might be, didn't differ by any great margin. But due to certain circumstances, Yorna possessed a soul thousands of times greater than others'.

It wasn't a power she had wished for. If anything, it was something she'd rather have been rid of. That it had proven useful had led to her present position, but she had never imagined an occasion would arise where she'd have to put it to use like this. —No, it would be more accurate to say she had avoided even thinking of it.

And so, when the moment of decision came, it had taken her time to decide. That—

"—That man..."

Had seen through it, Yorna realized, glancing back at the distant figure of the demon-masked man. Standing in the outer garden of a demon city now beyond saving, the man stood with arms crossed, not so much as swaying. Was that emotionless gaze one that saw the sacrifice needed to stop the Great Disaster as only natural? Even so, that he remained there without moving, intending to see the outcome through to the end, was perhaps, in his own way, a matter of honor for having pressed Yorna toward her decision.

"—! That fool!"

At that instant, as Yorna resolved to strike with everything she had against the Great Disaster, a curse struck her eardrum. Looking over, she saw it was Al, gazing at the Great Disaster, who had shouted the oath, and following the line of sight behind his mask, Yorna too understood what he was cursing at.

Around the Great Disaster, kicking off rubble, leaping and bounding to keep harrying it—a small figure. Rui. Her golden hair flying, her white clothing soiled with dust, the girl kept up her assault on the Great Disaster, her shoulders heaving greatly, her whole body drenched in sweat. Rui's purpose was singular: the black-haired boy who had been at the very center of the Great Disaster's beginning—

"That child..."

Unable to find a means to save him, Yorna had, in bitter anguish, already made that decision. It was perhaps a pain to her equal to, or even greater than, abandoning the demon city. When this strategy succeeded, what would become of what the subdued Great Disaster had taken in? If anything did come back, that black-haired boy might be among it. But Yorna understood well enough that the possibility was smaller than the eye of a needle. And so she understood, too, that Rui's desperate defiance would end in vain.

"We must bring that girl back!"

"Screw that! Leave the brat alone! Besides, that girl's..."

"What of the girl!?"

"She's...!"

At the sight of Rui leaping about, Kafma, having raised his voice, glared at Al. Under that sharp gaze, Al, who seemed to have some feelings on the matter regarding Rui, found his words catching in his throat. Yorna wanted to save Rui. —If Rui's wish couldn't be granted, then at the very least, Yorna wanted to save her life.

Perhaps there were times when dying together was the happier outcome. Rui might wish for that even with the boy who had been swallowed into that darkness.

"Damn it, damn it damn it damn it, dammit! Why the hell do I have to worry about her of all people! I'll hold this against you, partner!"

Shouting that roughly, Al took off running with a force that matched his voice. He headed toward Rui, weaving nimbly through the Great Disaster's onslaught. Rui was too quick for that pitch-black menace to fix its aim on, but—

"I can see you!"

"Uh!?"

Dodging the falling rain of rubble and the shadow's aftershocks, Al circled around with an accuracy that seemed to know exactly how Rui would move, and swept the girl up sideways into his arms. Even against the storm of shadow unleashed at the two now grappled together, Al dodged with unbelievably razor-thin margins, escaping just barely out of range.

"Uu! Uaugh! Aaau!"

"—Stay down!"

"Guh"

All the while, Rui struggled in Al's arms, desperately trying to reach out toward the Great Disaster. But Al drove a merciless blow with the hilt of his dao, and Rui's head slumped limp. Carrying the small girl's body just as she was, Al turned his back on the Great Disaster and ran.

"Antenna!"

"It's Kafma Irulux!"

Responding to the desperate cry, Kafma's thorns opened an escape route for the fleeing Al. The Great Disaster chased and chased and pressed after Al as he dashed into that path of thorns. But even as the thorns carved a path for Al, they also formed a wall to block the Great Disaster. Under that furious covering fire, at once offense and defense, Al, carrying Rui, cut through the battlefield, and then—

"—Do it, Yorna Mishigure!!"

The final move—a voice from behind urging the decisive act—rang out, and Yorna thrust both hands forward. The gesture itself held no meaning. It was the same as when the castle had been swallowed. It was simply an act to make known just how great a sacrifice Yorna was paying to accomplish this.

"I do not love my lord."

With those words, Yorna's soul detonated against the Great Disaster that had swallowed most of the demon city Chaosflame. Its force was truly as if the city itself had been hurled—

The blast produced something like an explosive gale, a tremendous wind blowing the demon city apart. Battered by the shockwave, everything that made up the demon city broke, shattered, and was blown away. Amid it, the Great Disaster, at the very center of the impact, was swallowed and torn apart by the destruction.

And so, the blow unleashed at the cost of the city itself struck to erase the Great Disaster—

"—Not enough."

Amid the still-raging gale and drifting smoke, a man's quiet voice rang out. That it was the voice of the one man who, even in a situation where everyone else could not help but bow their heads, still had not looked down—Yorna understood this, and, her whole body slumping with exhaustion, held her breath.

Even having taken that full-powered blow, still, beyond the smoke, there lingered the presence of writhing darkness. It had landed. But it hadn't been enough—that was the conclusion, grinding at the back of her teeth.

"—Ah"

Before the resolve to move, compounding the disappointment, could rise up, a sound escaped Yorna. The reason was simple. —Something had moved faster than she had. But it wasn't the dying embers of a Great Disaster that hadn't been fully destroyed. It was—

"—Tanza?"

The figure of a small Deer Human girl, leaping out from beneath the blown-apart inn.

"Uu, I saw. Mā, sh— stabbed herself with Ta's thing."

The decisive testimony was that of the young child who had witnessed it all—Mariuli's daughter, Utakata. Taritta's dagger had been found lodged in Mariuli's body, and Taritta had made no move to wipe away the blood she was drenched in. To any eye, it looked like a horrific scene in which Taritta had killed Mariuli. But none other than Mariuli's own daughter overturned that, denying Taritta's defilement.

—Killing one's own kin defiles the soul, forbidding it from returning to the same earth as the ancestral spirits.

That is the thing the Shudrak most abhor—the "Shudrak's Defilement." That suspicion was denied by Utakata's testimony. Moreover—

"The angle the blade went in. And besides, if Taritta had meant to strike, she would have aimed for a place that wouldn't cause suffering. That was not my sister's doing."

With just a glance at the angle the blade had entered, and the position of the wound to begin with, Mizelda pronounced the truth: that Mariuli had taken her own life. The other Shudrak, the sharp-eyed among them, noticed the same thing, and even those who hadn't were convinced upon hearing Mizelda's judgment.

It wasn't that Mizelda, as chieftain, had judged this way to protect her own blood sister Taritta. If anything, it was the opposite. —Mizelda, of all people, would never try to protect Taritta. It wasn't that she lacked sisterly affection, but that Mizelda was Shudrak among Shudrak. It was precisely that depth of Shudrak blood that formed the basis of Mizelda's judgment.

Taritta was found blameless, and it was concluded that Mariuli had taken the dagger from her and ended her own life. Perhaps to escape the suffering of her illness, or perhaps to entrust her final moment to her own hand rather than to the sickness. If the latter, all the better—that became the Shudrak's view of it. But—

"I... I let Mariuli die..."

None other than Taritta herself could not think of it that way. Taritta had drawn the dagger in order to keep herself from hearing words she didn't want to hear, to prevent any further curse from being uttered, to silence Mariuli's mouth. Had it not been for Taritta's rash act, Mariuli would never have died like that. More than anything—

"—The mandate."

At death's threshold, Mariuli had told Taritta of the heavenly mandate laid upon her. Until then, though she had spoken of a mandate having come down to her, Mariuli had never once told its content. It was only on her deathbed that she finally told Taritta.

"—The 'Great Disaster.'"

Mariuli had spoken of a mandate to avert ruin, and that the ruin itself was the "Great Disaster." A countermeasure to prevent it, and carrying that out, was Mariuli's mandate.

"—A traveler, black-haired, black-eyed."

A traveler said to appear a thousand nights hence—could such a thing really exist? And how could that traveler's death possibly ward off a ruin so great it was called the "Great Disaster"?

And yet, to the very end, Mariuli had believed in the mandate, in her duty as a "Stargazer." So much so that, unable to fulfill it, she had felt her own life had been meaningless.

"Meaningless..."

Taritta could declare, plainly, that this could not be. How many times, how much, had Mariuli's words, her kindness, saved her? Because she had been there, Taritta had never been crushed by feelings of inferiority toward her Shudrak-like sister. Without her support, Taritta would not have made it to today.

That, too, was Mariuli's achievement. She would let no one say it had no meaning. And more than anything, Mariuli had a child born of her own body's pain—Utakata was here.

"I won't let anyone say it was all meaningless..."

Gritting her teeth hard, she looked up at the night sky. The star-filled sky remained unchanged, telling Taritta nothing. Of course. The stars do not speak of anything. Mariuli's heart had been captured by a mere delusion. And yet, if she had sought salvation in that delusion, had sworn an oath to it—

"May the stars all just shatter and scatter to nothing...!"

Directing her hatred at the night sky like that, Taritta simultaneously wished for something else too. That this forest bring forth the very traveler that had been whispered to Mariuli, just as foretold.

Mariuli's soul, ruled by despair and lingering attachment, would wander on without ever reaching the ancestral spirits. Unless that despair and attachment were severed, her soul sister could not be saved.

What happened in that final moment was a secret even young Utakata had no way of knowing. A secret belonging only to soul sisters, one not even blood-bound family could intrude upon.

"Surely, with my own arrow—"

She would shoot the traveler dead, and sever Mariuli's lingering attachment herself. For that, she would wait for the traveler's coming, wait, and wait, and wait and wait and keep waiting, and then—

—On that day, at the sight of the long-awaited traveler, Taritta drew her bow.

"—Rem!!"

The powerful bow loosed its shot and missed its mark; the black-haired, black-eyed traveler had leapt to shield the girl beside him. Aiming for his heart, gritting her back teeth, Taritta's own heart pounded fiercely.

A thousand nights had passed, and the black-haired traveler had appeared in the forest. Struck by a shock that froze her heart, Taritta nocked her bow and became a huntress.

Calmly, calmly, the arrow's tip fixed its aim on the target. And yet her soul howled with a burning, exultant cheer. It howled. It howled.

It had been howling.

—The instant the Deer Human girl leapt out, sound vanished from the world.

Just before, the tremendous shockwave centered on the Great Disaster had literally shaken the world, detonating with enough force to blow the very city away in its bid to shatter the calamity. Struck by fierce wind and impact across her whole body, it was enough to make one certain that any being struck by an impact thousands, tens of thousands of times greater would be smashed to bits.

And yet, painting over even that certainty, the Great Disaster endured the annihilation. Beyond the drifting smoke, though hardly what one could call unscathed, sensing the presence of a Great Disaster that had escaped total destruction, Taritta—now merely a bystander—was aware of her own body trembling as she knelt on one knee.

She had watched Rui, Medium, Al, the people of the city, all fighting desperately in resistance. Unable to fix her own way of being, unable even to face the mandate that had jerked her around like a puppet, recalling Mariuli's blood-stained face, Taritta had been unable to move before the Great Disaster.

As if leaving that pitiful Taritta behind, everyone fulfilled their own role. Just as Yorna Mishigure, ruler of the city, had sacrificed her beloved city to unleash a single strike. Just as, seeing that strike wasn't enough, a Deer Human girl had leapt out from hiding.

"—————"

Fiercely, her right eye burning, the girl in a kimono who ran was the messenger girl who had appeared at the inn. Ahead of the girl who called herself Tanza lay the drifting smoke and, beyond it, the Great Disaster. What she meant to do, Taritta had no idea. Only—

"—Stop, if you please!!"

Yorna's cry, scolding her own trembling legs and shouting at the back of the running girl, made Taritta unwittingly understand Tanza's intent and resolve.

Tanza was risking her life, meaning to challenge the Great Disaster by some method. Yorna had some idea what that method was, and, loving the girl, was trying to stop her. But stopping that girl meant letting slip a once-in-a-lifetime chance to destroy the Great Disaster, which had been halted in its tracks by the blow that had cost the demon city itself.

Or perhaps, if Yorna, one of the Nine Divine Generals, put her mind to it, she might yet be able to corner and drive off the Great Disaster by some other means.

—Was that really true, though?

"—!"

Cursing herself for entertaining such wishful thinking, Taritta quietly held her breath. Casting her gaze around, Taritta sought some means, some trigger to change the situation. She even hoped that someone other than herself might take some action. But among the wreckage of blown-apart rubble, and the people tossed and battered among it, there was no strength left to deal with this situation. —Only Taritta had any left.

Taritta alone, who had hesitated to join the desperate struggle, who had been trying to simply endure until the storm passed, ended up being the only one on this field with any choice left to make. And then—

"—Taritta."

At the faint call of her name, she turned to see the man who had risen to his feet on the high ground. Abel. Bleeding from within his demon mask, he fixed his black eyes on Taritta, and rather than speaking, quietly pointed a finger skyward.

He wasn't telling her to look at the sky. What Abel indicated was the heavens—or rather, the stars. —In other words, the mandate.

Choose, he seemed to say, forcing the decision upon Taritta.

"—————"

Mariuli's blood-soaked final moments, and the mandate to kill the black-haired, black-eyed traveler, rose up in her mind. If she carried it out, ruin at the hands of the Great Disaster would be averted, and Mariuli's soul would be freed from its chains. And so—

"—————"

Quietly, without hesitation, Taritta drew an arrow from her quiver, nocked it to her bow, and took aim. The hunting technique she had repeated thousands, tens of thousands of times before transformed Taritta—from a foolish, frightened, faltering girl unable to fulfill her role—into a single huntress.

If she was to be told, so imperiously, to choose the way to save the world, then very well, she would do exactly that. From the start, Taritta's answer to the stars' whispers, to heaven's proposition, had already been decided. Which was to say—

"—I don't want to know anything about any mandate."

The string was loosed, and the nocked arrow shot through the air, cutting the wind with fierce force. And true to its aim, it pierced the leg of the tall woman who had risen and was about to run. Crying out in shock, the woman stumbled and pitched forward, falling flat.

Her white face etched with alarm and shock, the woman reached out in anguish, raising her voice. That it was a voice calling the name of the girl charging toward the Great Disaster, Taritta understood clearly, even in a world stripped of sound. And still, she had made her choice.

—To defy the mandate entrusted to her by her "soul sister," and let the girl who charged forward, risking her life, achieve her heart's desire.

"—Yorna-sama."

Just before her feet left the ground to leap beyond the drifting smoke, the girl's lips called that name as if in love. And then her small body was swallowed, beyond the cloud of dust, into the waiting Great Disaster—

—And a second impact, tremendous enough to rival the demon city's own detonation, finally blew the Great Disaster away for good.
