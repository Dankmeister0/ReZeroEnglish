Arc 7 Chapter 24: "Arrogant and Overbearing"

——With every passing moment, as the start of the banquet drew closer, the tension only grew.

"………"

In the waiting room where the five of them had gathered, there was no conversation, only silence. Abel, silent and honing his focus, was one thing—but Subaru couldn't get a word out either. When even Flop had fallen quiet, the weight in the air spoke for itself.

They'd made it this far without a hitch, so there shouldn't be any problem left. But people aren't built simply enough to just decide that and be done with it. No matter how flawless the cross-dressing had turned out, the tension only mounted the closer they came to the real performance. And no matter how loudly one's heart screamed, no matter how the cold sweat soaked through one's back, time refused to stop flowing, and the appointed hour arrived all the same.

"The banquet hall is ready. It's your turn."

The voice of the guard on watch called out, and the moment the taut tension finally broke. Exchanging glances, Subaru and the others each took up their instruments and followed the leading soldier out of the waiting room. They underwent a light body search, though there was hardly room to hide a weapon on people dressed in little more than thin, sheer garments to begin with. Passing the inspection under eyes full of curiosity and lechery, Subaru and the others were led toward the banquet hall. Except—

"—hh"

"Taritta-san?"

Subaru's brow knit at the sight of Taritta, her cheeks stiff, her steps heavy. Though Subaru had kept her voice low so the leading soldier wouldn't hear, it should still have reached Taritta—and yet she didn't answer. One look at her eyes was enough to guess the state of her mind.

Extreme tension had her entire body firmly, firmly in its grip. Leaving behind the settlement she'd called home, unable even to rely on her own sister the chieftain, and now marching into the very heart of enemy territory practically unarmed—the plan must have forced an immense strain upon her. Her color and her breathing looked ready to give out at any moment. Subaru tried to think of some way to encourage her, but nothing came to her in the moment.

Words like it'll be fine or leave it to me—those easy, shallow comforts. Would something like that actually soothe Taritta's spirit right now? Just as Subaru hesitated, unable to take that step forward for the anxiety in her own heart—

"—Taritta."

"………"

"There's nothing to fear. Just watch me."

It was a voice thick with arrogance, with haughtiness—and yet brimming with absolute confidence. Baseless, no different from the very same empty comfort Subaru had just been fumbling for. And yet the effect that easy comfort produced was immense.

Taritta let out a faint breath and blinked. She had forgotten to breathe, her eyes on the verge of losing focus—and that one word alone was enough to bring her back to herself. A single sentence carrying the power to reach so forcefully into another's heart.

Those who have, strip away in an instant all the desperate effort of those who don't. Having been made to feel that truth so many times before, Subaru felt it now driven home all over again, vividly.

"Well, I suppose I can't even be annoyed about it."

Subaru muttered that to herself, if only to make peace with it, using it as her own small comfort. But she immediately realized that not just Taritta's tension but her own had eased as well, and her lips twisted. She'd never say so out loud—he'd only lord it over her. And then—

"—So you've come. I hear you intend to show us a truly splendid dance and song."

In the great hall prepared for the banquet, nearly thirty sturdy men awaited them. Word was that ordinary soldiers weren't permitted at tonight's feast, so everyone present here must hold the rank of officer or commander. Among that gathering of "generals," the one who called out to them sat in the chair furthest back.

"...Is that Second General Zikr Osman?"

Subaru murmured the question only within her mouth, and when she glanced sidelong at Abel, the veiled dancer of their troupe gave the faintest dip of her chin, confirming Subaru's guess. Taking that nod, Subaru looked again at the man and, beneath her carefully shaped brows, frowned.

Zikr Osman, commander of the imperial soldiers garrisoned in the walled city of Guaral. Given that the man held the rank of Second General of the Empire, Subaru had expected some manner of formidable warrior—but his appearance betrayed that image on every count.

For one, he was short. Even compared to Subaru, who was by no means tall, he looked shorter by half a head. About on par with Kuna standing at attention. Of course, given the precedent of Garfiel—a man of imposing strength despite his own lack of height—stature alone was no reason to underestimate someone. Unfortunately, though, the man didn't look particularly skilled, either.

What left the strongest impression was his hair—an enormous, distinctively puffed-out style. If put into words, "afro" would probably be the most fitting description; and perched atop the small man's head, it lent him a strangely mascot-like charm. She'd heard beforehand that he was a tactician rather than a swordsman, and indeed, he didn't look like the type to win glory by force of arms.

"At present, we find ourselves saddled with a troublesome problem. Cooped up inside this city, our spirits sink lower with each passing day. And so, we've arranged tonight's banquet. You understand your role, I take it?"

"—Yes. We are most deeply honored by the invitation."

Resting his cheek on his hand against the armrest, Zikr tossed out his grandiose words, and Subaru knelt before him. Flop, Taritta, and Kuna followed suit, but Abel, standing at the rear, did not. For an instant Zikr's eyes narrowed, and an uneasy air spread among the "generals" as well. Sure enough, one of the soldiers who'd set his cup down at his feet rose and glared at Abel.

"Why do you not kneel? Do you not understand you stand before a Second General—"

"Hold. Don't get so worked up. This gathering was arranged for a drinking feast. What's wanted of those who make their living by their art is not proper etiquette, but relief from tedium, surely."

"Mm... if the 'General' says so..."

But it was none other than Zikr himself who reined in the soldier bearing down on Abel. At his show of magnanimity, the soldier reluctantly sat back down. All the while, Abel kept her face hidden behind the veil and did not so much as stir.

"Not even a flinch before a warrior's pressure. You must have great confidence in your dance indeed. But the first impression is a poor one. I'll be expecting you to overturn it."

"...My thanks for your generosity. But please, set your mind at ease."

Zikr, ever ready to interpret Abel's arrogance in the best possible light, raised an eyebrow with an interested "Oh?" at Subaru's reply from her kneeling position. Her chest ached to think of what was coming, but this was too good a chance to waste. She would make the fullest use of it, and see to it that he tasted utter defeat.

"—What we now present before you is a lovely dancer, come from beyond the Great Waterfall. Lustrous black hair that swallows the light of the sun, beautiful white skin blessed by the spirits, a peerless beauty that would put even the denizens of heaven to shame—tonight, she shall dance for you in full splendor."

Rising to her feet, Subaru laid out the opening words. An introduction grandiose to the point of excess—and as if to prove it wasn't mere bluster, the veil hiding Abel's face slowly lifted. And there, at last revealed, the dancer's—

"………"

Above all, the shock that struck Zikr, meeting Abel's eyes directly, was immense. Perhaps because he'd shown such a generous stance toward them beforehand, he'd been all the more prepared to receive this magnanimously—and so the blow landed like an unguarded punch. Quite literally, he seemed to forget to breathe, to lose his voice entirely.

"—If it would please you, grant us the opportunity to show you our dancer's dance."

Bowing her head, Subaru observed the proper courtesies. Flop did likewise, and together they raised their instruments, seeking permission to begin the feast. At that, Zikr nodded, and Subaru exchanged a glance with Flop. Then, with a shared nod that all was ready, they began preparing to play.

"—Tonight, what we shall show you is a dance from our dancer's homeland, from the very end of the Great Waterfall. A dance come from the farthest of the far—please, savor it to your heart's content."

"The end of the Great Waterfall" was, of course, an exaggeration. But in the sense that it was a dance the imperial soldiers had never seen before, it wasn't exactly a lie either. Both the music and the dance were things brought not from this world, but from Subaru's own. Naturally, they'd been arranged to suit accordingly, but given how fundamentally different the foundations were, the dance alone was more than enough to catch the crowd's eye. Though, admittedly—

"………"

Setting aside the elegance of the dance itself, with a beauty like that captivating everyone with her looks alone, perhaps a secret weapon like an otherworldly song and dance hadn't even been necessary.

"………"

There's an expression, "accompaniment to drink," which doesn't refer only to the food served alongside liquor—any manner of entertainment that serves to encourage the drinking is called by that name as well. Naturally, that includes performances of the sort, and dances especially are sometimes even called "accompaniment dances"—a proper part of the drinking experience. And yet, rarely does such an accompaniment prove this effective.

Eyes captured by the dancer's movements, throats scorched, souls parched as if thirsting—men reached for their drinks. Naturally, the pace and frequency of raised cups quickened, and the soldiers' cheeks reddened one after another. Most notable in the speed of his drinking was none other than Second General Zikr, seeking to soothe the numbness in his skull.

Once judgment is stripped away by drink and dance, blunders that would ordinarily be unthinkable start to happen. For instance—succumbing to the desire to see a sword dance, and offering up one's own blade at the dancer's request—an unprecedented, catastrophic mistake.

"—You have lost, Zikr Osman."

And so, even with the tip of the drawn sword pointed at his throat, even hearing the unmistakable voice of a man come from the dancer's mouth, "the womanizer" Zikr Osman's eyes remained lost in intoxication, unable to break free.

—We did it, Subaru cried out inwardly with the conviction of victory.

The sword in Abel's hand was positioned to pierce Zikr's windpipe with ease. Head thrown back, throat exposed, Zikr had no chance of counterattack. Without a doubt, they could say they had succeeded in seizing the enemy commander. And moreover, sooner than originally planned, and at a precise, opportune moment.

Originally, Subaru and the others' plan had been to become famous in the city as a traveling troupe and worm their way into Zikr Osman's inner circle. The ideal scenario was to be summoned to Zikr's private chambers, where they could catch him off guard, seize his person, and force the imperial soldiers garrisoned at Guaral to surrender. That premise had been overturned by the holding of this banquet.

"—At the banquet hall, we neutralize every officer and soldier remaining in the city, all at once."

Those were Abel's words, declaring the change of plan the moment they'd been called to the feast. A high-risk, high-reward gambit—if it succeeded, the payoff would be great, but so too was the danger. Subaru had agreed to the change of plan on the condition that they'd switch back to the original scheme depending on circumstances. Even so, she'd never imagined it would go this smoothly.

"………"

"I say it again, Zikr Osman. You have lost. Surrender at once, and have your men lay down their arms. Otherwise, your cup shall be filled not with wine, but with blood."

Abel, sword still leveled at the immobile Zikr, urged surrender once more. But there was no fear of a life-threatened man in Zikr's eyes. Nor was it the resolute gaze of one prepared to die because he was a soldier of the Empire. If anything, there was hesitation in those eyes. Hesitation, and doubt—that sort of light.

"Dancer, you... no, you are..."

As if he'd laid eyes on something too awe-inspiring to bear, doubt surfaced plainly in Zikr's eyes. It was right after that impression—that this was not the reaction of a man facing a mere dancer—that it happened.

"—! You scoundrels! How dare you—!"

Among the stupefied "generals," one who'd come back to his senses lunged at Abel on impulse. But before his hand could reach Abel's back, his arm—or leg—was pierced by a thrown knife first. It was Kuna, twisting her body, who'd loosed the blade.

"Sorry 'bout that, but the chieftain told me to protect Abel's face."

Having joined the fray carrying an instrument resembling a tambourine, she had secretly been gathering up knives from the meal throughout Abel's dance. Seeing a soldier collapse, limbs pierced by blades, the "generals" hesitated to press further. But there was one shadow that dodged Kuna's blade and leapt back a great distance.

"Ridiculous! You think something like this will stop the feet of imperial soldiers?!"

The roaring, bearded giant of a man took an additional thrown knife on his arm and knocked it aside with a mighty swing. Then, drawing his own greatsword, heedless of his own bleeding, he lunged straight at Abel.

"Abel—!"

Abel, having pinned Zikr down, was showing his back, standing motionless. Subaru screamed his name like a shriek, but the dancer didn't so much as flinch.

And then, as the greatsword swung down toward Abel's back—

"—Kh"

In that instant, gritting her teeth and taking aim at the man was Taritta, who had snatched a bow from one of the soldiers. Drawing back the notched arrow, she leveled it at the soldier closing in on Abel. A light—killing intent—lit up in Taritta's green eyes. Her stance was set to end the man's life, to save Abel, to stop this atrocity.

"Don't kill him!!"

"—hh"

But an instant before the arrow was loosed, Flop cried out on impulse. Hearing it, hesitation surfaced in Taritta's eyes, and the arrow's aim wavered ever so slightly. The loosed arrow struck not the man's back, but his right shoulder.

"Gaah!?"

Pierced by the tremendous impact, the man let out a cry of pain and toppled backward. But the greatsword gripped in his hand couldn't shed its momentum and spun toward Abel's back regardless. It seemed the greatsword would split Abel's head open from behind. But it merely grazed just short of the back of his head with its edge, and with a violent crash, buried itself in the floor. Abel had come within a hair's breadth of losing his life, and survived by the narrowest of margins. And then—

"—ah"

Abel's braided black hair loosened and fell open in a soft cascade. The greatsword's tip had sliced through the hair tie, undoing the carefully wrought knot. —No, it didn't stop there. The unraveled wig, its purpose spent, fell to the floor along with the false black hair. The dancer's long hair vanished in an instant, and there stood Abel, crowned only with his own true black hair.

—An emperor, cold and ruthless, who along with the hair tie had shed the soft, gentle impression of the dancer he'd played.

"Second General! These wretches must be dealt with at once—"

"—Stop! Do not defy him!"

Among the soldiers groaning, limbs pierced by knives and a shoulder pierced by an arrow, one officer still tried to press his protest, and Zikr silenced him. If they charged in blindly, heedless of however many might fall, Subaru and the others' forces would be crushed in an instant. That was the one counterattack they most needed to avoid. But Zikr wouldn't let his men do it. With himself pinned down and his fellow "generals" likewise rendered unable to move, he understood the outcome was already decided. And so—

"If we do as you say, will you guarantee my men's lives?"

"That depends entirely on your own conduct, 'coward.'"

"Guh...!"

Zikr, having shown a docile posture of accepting the demand for surrender, reddened at Abel's merciless jab and clenched his teeth. It was the face of a man tasting a humiliation greater even than being caught by their scheme and having his own person seized. Looking down at Zikr's humiliation, Abel snorted.

"I've heard, Zikr Osman. Before you were called 'the womanizer,' you were called 'the coward,' were you not?"

"...An epithet unbecoming an officer of the Empire. Is that why you thought this scheme would work?"

"………"

"That I, called 'the womanizer,' and reviled as 'the coward' besides, would have a sword pressed to me by a woman and, valuing my own hide, surrender...?"

If that were so, what humiliation it would be. Or rather, if that were true, it would be a disgrace severe enough that Zikr might have died of indignation on the spot. But the reason Abel had judged this plan feasible, as far as Subaru and the others had heard it, was—at the very least—a different reason from that. It was—

"A tactician skilled and steady in his use of arms, whose battle results may draw little attention, but who spares his men needless losses. An excellent commander, but lacking in aggression. Hence, 'the coward.'"

"Yes, that's right, that's exactly it. I, however..."

"You—what have you misunderstood?"

"Eh?"

Zikr's eyes widened at Abel's narrow-eyed question. Still with confusion plastered across his face, Abel continued, sword still in hand.

"When others revile you as a coward, it's either false bravado spoken with hindsight of the result, or the idle talk of fools who cannot foresee results at all. I made your very nature the basis of my calculated odds."

"………"

"You despise needless casualties. That is precisely why I judged that you, a tactician branded 'the coward,' would not resist in this situation. —Have I disappointed you?"

His gaze sharp, blade still held to Zikr's throat, Abel posed the question. To anyone unaware of Abel's true identity, this could only sound like reckless, nonsensical talk—the ravings of a madman, one might well suspect.

Trusting in the enemy's cowardice, and building it into the calculated odds of a plan. Saying such a thing straight to a man's face carried no guarantee it wouldn't provoke him to fury instead. If anything, it was the kind of remark that seemed almost designed to invite the other's rage, one that could well have made things worse.

Here was a man who, even dressed as a woman, could not bring himself to be humble in the slightest. And yet, faced with such an Abel, Zikr Osman drew in a sharp breath. What crossed his eyes was an emotion hard to put into words—if one had to try, it might be described as something close to a stirred, moved surprise.

It seemed almost like the fresh, innocent reaction of a maiden who had received some gift from the one she secretly admired.

"—I shall disarm. And issue the strictest orders to my men as well."

"A wise decision."

At Zikr's docile, bowed-headed answer, Abel gave a quiet dip of his chin. Despite his attire—still that of a dancer—no one could defy the dignity in that nod. With the commander Zikr's surrender, the imperial soldiers dropped their weapons one after another as well. And then—

"What are you gawking for? Go set fire to the flag atop the roof, quickly."

"Ueh? M-me?"

"You. Only you. The one who's contributed nothing whatsoever since things started moving."

Abel's cold eyes pierced Subaru, who pointed at herself, blinking. At his remark, Subaru looked around the great hall.

Kuna, who'd held the enemy in check with thrown knives; Taritta, who'd shot the giant; Flop, who'd kept that giant from being killed and had fought to preserve the "bloodless surrender"; and Abel, who'd forced the disarmament. Indeed, the only one who'd done nothing at all was Subaru—who'd fallen on her rear when the giant lunged.

"Go, quickly. Without Mizelda and the others here, even seeing to the disarmament is more than we can handle."

"F-fine, I get it! Yes, yes, forgive me, please!"

Saying so, Subaru headed for the balcony and nimbly climbed from there up to the rooftop. From the roof of the city hall, the night view of Guaral stretched out below, quite the spectacular sight. Then, bathed in the cold wind, Subaru retrieved a torch from the wall and set fire to the imperial flag—the flag of the sword-wolf—that flew atop the city hall's roof, letting it burn.

—To signal, to all, that the fall of the walled city of Guaral had been achieved.

The first to notice the anomaly was Jamal, who'd been keeping an eye on the city hall.

Jamal had been glancing back repeatedly, worrying about the person he'd left behind in the building. Todd, exasperated by how obvious his behavior was, continued his patrol of the city. Being a city with a fair bit of history, there was no shortage of ways to slip past the formidable defensive wall. Ordinary houses with dug-out tunnels beneath them, smuggling routes past the wall using children—Todd rooted these out one by one with precision, all while keeping his guard up for the coming attack.

"Pulling back entirely—that's not gonna happen. Which means they're definitely coming to take the city. Use a hidden passage to seize the city hall in one go, or set the town on fire... hmm, are there other options?"

If the goal were simply to bring down the city, plenty of methods came to mind that made no distinction between soldiers and civilians—a straightforward massacre. Fire, water, burying people in earth—any of those would do the trick. If one didn't care about means, a person could push things through with any amount of cruelty.

That black-haired boy—what manner of atrocity would he resort to, exactly? The fact that he looked, at a glance, ill-suited for such things, and yet was good at them anyway—that was what made him frightening.

No matter how many holes they plugged, it felt like the still-undiscovered one was the real threat. So even with a hole already blown clean through his gut, Todd couldn't rest easy sleeping in the barracks.

"Everyone around here's careless and sloppy, is the problem."

Not that everyone was on Jamal's level, but plenty of people failed to notice the finer details. Todd didn't think of himself as especially sharp or clever, either—but if you're aware you're a fool with plenty of gaps in your own judgment, there's no end to how many of those gaps you can go around patching. He couldn't understand how everyone else managed to live without ever doubting that they weren't fools.

Every last human being is a fool without exception—so the best a fool can do is give it his fool's best.

"—Ah?"

Thinking such things, just as he was about to head into the next house, Todd stopped short at the sound of a dumbfounded noise Jamal made behind him. Looking over, he saw Jamal had stopped in his tracks, staring off toward the city hall in the distance, his face slack with stupidity. Wondering what was going on, Todd frowned.

"Hey, hey, what kind of joke is that supposed to be?"

"What's up, Jamal? What's going on over at the city hall...?"

Todd trailed off before finishing—there is—and, lining up beside Jamal, looked the same direction. At the city hall right about now, there should be a banquet underway, meant to entertain and reward the "generals," with the invited dancers providing entertainment. So it wouldn't be strange if some of them got carried away and did something foolish. It wouldn't be strange, but—

"—no way."

Whatever else happened, having the imperial flag flying over the city hall set ablaze was well outside the bounds of acceptable. Even the unflappable Todd was struck speechless by an outrage that went far beyond mere breach of decorum. Burning in the flames, fluttering in the hot wind, the flag swayed, burned, and lit the night sky with red light. And right beside that burning flag stood the figure of a woman he'd seen inside the city hall.

Rich black hair, sharp eyes—a musician. Her name was, if he recalled—

"—Natsumi Schult."

He remembered now—how she'd answered when he'd asked. A frail woman who'd shrunk back, frightened, when Jamal bore down on her. And yet that very woman now stood there, brazenly setting the imperial flag ablaze with a torch in hand, in plain sight. There wasn't a trace of that weakness he'd seen back in that corridor.

Surely this wasn't some cute little drunken prank. No, that was unmistakably an act of aggression against the Empire. And when a stronghold's flag is burned, it means that place has fallen into enemy hands. Just as Todd's thoughts reached that point, a certain possibility struck his mind like a bolt of lightning.

"Don't tell me... it was you?"

Eyes wide, Todd scrutinized the black-haired woman holding the torch, Natsumi Schult, closely, and fell speechless at the minuscule possibility sprouting within him. But the more he thought about it, the more things lined up; the more he looked, the more they diverged. And yet, that was precisely why the effect had been so devastatingly complete.

Who on earth would even conceive of a trick like this? That anyone would try to break through the enemy's watch head-on, using a method like that, to bring down the very keep itself?

"I thought it was impossible. Trying to slip through head-on, right out in the open."

Who in the world could pull off such a reckless, life-defying method? Of course, they'd been thoroughly wary of the possibility of hiding among cargo or dragon carriages, and had reinforced the checkpoints against exactly that. But an infiltration on foot, and one drawing attention no less—that had been outside all expectation. Especially after the first infiltration attempt had already put them on heightened alert. The natural response, surely, would be to shift toward more secretive means of entry—

"Don't tell me... that was a setup too? Deliberately letting us spot them, planting the assumption that a head-on breakthrough would be unthinkable?"

And so they'd waltzed right into the city, been invited into the stronghold as dancers, brought down the city hall, and burned the imperial flag. —Exactly as Natsumi Schult had intended.

"...This is bad."

A chill ran straight down his spine at the sheer, meticulous scale of the planning. He'd thought himself someone who spared no effort, who did his utmost to find the best possible move. But his opponent had effortlessly surpassed that, a child of war mocking him from above. Todd was startled to find himself trembling.

"Damn it! What the hell is happening?! Anyway, we've gotta get back to the city hall—"

"Idiot, stop. You planning to die too?"

"Ah?"

Unlike Todd, Jamal hadn't realized the identity of the one who'd burned the flag. Given the distance, there was no way his eyes could make out the details atop the city hall's roof. Todd could only see it because of his special eyes. And precisely because he could see it, he held Jamal back.

With the stronghold's imperial flag burned, the fall of the city was plain. Most likely, by now, every "general" who'd attended the banquet—Zikr included—was already dead. Charging in there without a plan would only end in getting cut down for nothing.

"You scared, huh? And you call yourself a soldier of the Empire, huh?!"

"Pride won't win you victory or your life back. Besides, even you can see it—the city hall's done for. The Second Generals are dead too. Before long, the Shudrak will be marching into the city."

"………"

"If we don't run before then, dying bravely in battle is gonna be the only option left."

Jamal wasn't the sort of man who could docilely accept the humiliation of disarmament. His alternative would be to keep his weapon, charge headlong into the enemy camp in a fit of reckless valor, drag maybe ten of them down with him, and die fighting. It might sound like the death of a proper sword-wolf, but from Todd's point of view, it was nothing but a dog's death, plain and simple.

Life, too, is a finite card in one's hand. Spending it toward victory is one thing, but wasting it on a sore loser's last stand was a shame. They'd known each other long enough, more or less. Close enough a relationship, he figured, to offer this much of a warning. Probably.

"There's that hole in the wall, the one they just patched up. I'm getting out through there. You coming?"

"Gh, guh... you're telling me to live in shame all over again?"

"If you're alive, you get another chance to wipe away the shame. But if you're dead, that's it. So that's why I'm going. I'm not getting on board with a fight I've got no chance of winning."

Truthfully, it wasn't just fights with no chance of winning—Todd wouldn't get on board with fights with even low odds, either. But there wasn't time to waste splitting hairs and arguing back and forth with Jamal. Turning his back cleanly and taking off running, Jamal hesitated for a moment before letting out a curse—"Damn it!"—and, with no other choice, followed after Todd.

If everyone were this simple to deal with, life would be easier, but the world doesn't work out that neatly. Anyway—

"For now, guess I'll remember the name as Natsumi. —You, the child of war."

Once it became clear the imperial flag had been burned and the city hall had fallen, the imperial soldiers obeyed the call to surrender with unexpected meekness. The same held true of the guards defending the city, which was a great relief for Subaru—whose whole goal had been a bloodless surrender—though it was also a surprising turn of events.

"Whenever I watch period dramas or those Warring States battles, I always thought it was strange how the fighting would just end the moment the enemy general's head was taken, but..."

Once the general falls, ordinarily the next-highest-ranking person becomes general and the fight continues. The fight shouldn't be settled until one side is annihilated or close to it—or so an amateur might assume—but that's not how it actually plays out in real battles. Indeed, even the overwhelmingly stronger imperial garrison had complied with disarmament the very moment word got out that their commander Zikr had been seized.

"Still, that was quite the feat, Subaru—no, Natsumi."

"Mizelda-san."

Subaru, secretly relieved at both the results and the success of the operation, was addressed by Mizelda, who was taking hearty swigs straight from a wine bottle. Having lain in wait outside the city as a separate unit, she and the others had realized the city hall had fallen the moment they saw the flag burn, and entered the city. The guards, learning of the imperial soldiers' defeat, apparently hadn't bothered trying to stop them as they marched in boldly. Thanks to that, with the Shudrak's cooperation, they'd succeeded in capturing the majority of the imperial soldiers.

The great hall, once set for the banquet, had had its rows of drink and food cleared away, and in their place, the restrained imperial soldiers now stood lined up in rows. Thinking it a waste to toss it out, Mizelda was drinking straight from a bottle—doing so only made her look all the more like a plundering barbarian, Subaru thought.

"But even so, this many people... a head-on confrontation really would've been tough, wouldn't it."

"Even were the enemy many, the Shudrak's pride does not falter. ...Still, sheer numbers are not something to be overturned so easily. But you and Abel managed to overturn it anyway."

"………"

"Be proud, Natsumi. You showed valor through wisdom. Something we could not have done."

Slapping Subaru's shoulder firmly, Mizelda left behind a manly grin. With that same stride, she headed off to praise Taritta and Kuna, the two who'd contributed most to the city hall's fall. Even from a distance, Taritta's eyes lighting up at her sister's praise was a heartwarming sight. Kuna, meanwhile, was being crushed in a bear hug by Holly, who was checking her over for injuries—looking rather like she was being folded in half.

"As long as our hero Kuna doesn't breathe her last like that, we should be fine, I suppose... and..."

"………"

A little smile slipped out of Subaru, helped along by relief. Then, as she turned around, she nearly collided with someone standing right behind her. That someone was—

"R-Rem..."

"...Yes."

Being looked up at intently from close range, Subaru couldn't help but step back. Rem, leaning on a wooden cane, looked Subaru up and down with her pale blue eyes. Feeling uncomfortable under that gaze, Subaru cleared her throat lightly.

"W-what is it? I'm perfectly fine, you know?"

"So I've heard. But it seemed to me that even if you were injured somewhere, you would hide it and say the same thing."

Regarding what would happen to her and Rem once the walled city had fallen. But the conversation Subaru had been trying to bring up went, unfortunately, unheeded. Because—

At Zikr's single remark, Abel murmured that phrase, and the air in the room seemed to tremble. Before Subaru and the others, who stared wide-eyed at Abel's sudden change, he rose forcefully from his chair and strode with great steps back toward the great hall. And then—

Naturally, since even Subaru and the others, who'd been in conversation with him just moments before, had no idea what this was about, Mizelda and the rest couldn't grasp Abel's true intent either.
