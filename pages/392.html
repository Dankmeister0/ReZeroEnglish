Arc 5 Chapter 75: "The Battle for Priestella, Result 2"

Having finished his talk with Wilhelm, Subaru felt a quiet relief settle over him. Garfiel included, the two of them had been assigned to deal with "Lust" — thinking it over, that had surely been the pairing expected to face the hardest fight of all. In practice, "Lust" had abandoned the control tower, and what awaited them instead was Wilhelm's wife Theresia and some so-called hero of old named Kurgan standing in their way. Even so, it had already been confirmed that no casualties had occurred at any of those three sites.

"――――"

Of course, both Garfiel and Wilhelm surely had things weighing on their hearts. In fact, Garfiel had put his lingering unease into words, and it wasn't hard to imagine that some wound too difficult to voice had been carved into Wilhelm's heart as well. And yet. And yet, still.

Subaru was glad the two of them had made it back safely from the fight. If a casualty had come from among his own people, Subaru might have steeled himself to Return by Death. So their safe return carried with it the relief of not having to resort to that power. But that relief at not having to die was, at the same time, tied to a conviction.

That he really shouldn't lean on an unnatural power like Return by Death, again and again.

He had used Return by Death, challenging fate over and over in pursuit of a better future. Someone might scoff — you've relied on that power so many times, and now you say this? — but that was exactly why. Precisely because Subaru had died again and again, overturning the future each time, there was a conclusion only Subaru was allowed to voice.

By rights, Return by Death shouldn't be necessary at all. Even without Return by Death, there had to be a way to win the future you wanted. Just as everyone had, this time, worked together toward a single goal — and won it.

"Subaru, was Wilhelm-san all right...?"

Emilia greeted the returning Subaru, worry in her eyes as she glanced toward the old swordsman standing off in a corner of the shelter. At her words Subaru gave a nod, not bothering to glance back over his shoulder.

"Yeah, I think he's fine. Sure, he's banged up in a bunch of places... but the scariest wound, the one on the inside, seems like he took care of that himself."

"I see. ...He's a strong person. Well, of course he is."

"Yeah. He's a strong person. So it's fine."

Subaru nodded his head over and over at Emilia's words. Her eyes went wide at the motion, and then her lips curved into a smile. Caught off guard by the unexpected reaction, Subaru furrowed his brow, and she pressed a hand to her mouth.

"Sorry. It's just, Subaru, you act so differently when it comes to Wilhelm-san compared to everyone else. It's like... I thought, you're really smitten with him."

"Nobody says 'smitten' these days..."

Subaru gave a wry laugh at Emilia's rare, old-fashioned turn of phrase, then scratched his cheek with a finger. He'd tried to play it off as a joke, but he understood exactly what Emilia meant. And it was something Subaru himself was fully aware of, too.

"Wilhelm-san's kind of in a special category for me, y'know? I can respect him honestly, as someone amazing — it's that kind of thing."

"I get that he's amazing, but by that logic, wouldn't the same go for Reinhardt and the others? And yet."

"Well, you look at people your own age differently than you look at those who came before you. When I feel a gap with someone my own age, it stings, makes me feel pathetic — but the difference with someone older, that I can turn into a goal. Someday I want to age all suave and dignified and end up with that kind of gravitas, like Wilhelm-san."

"...Mm, okay. Heh. I'll let you have that one."

At Subaru's quip, half a cover for his embarrassment, Emilia nodded with a knowing look. Subaru felt like he'd been had. If he were honest, he didn't even know what to call, in words, the feeling he held. He was fairly sure it was the kind of thing that didn't need to be put into words, though.

"Betty doesn't think a beard like that would suit Subaru, I suppose."

"Pretty sure that wasn't the topic of discussion, but fine, I'll take it. I'll grow a beard whenever Bea-ko decides I'm worthy of one."

"...Now then, I wonder if that day will ever come. The delicate coexistence of loveliness and hairiness is a domain that can't be achieved without at least Wilhelm-nii-chan's level of refinement, I suppose. Keep training, I suppose."

"Yeah, yeah, sure thing."

With both Emilia and Beatrice each shifting his mood in their own particular way, Subaru turned his gaze toward one corner of the shelter — a spot that had suddenly grown noisy. That was the area where the evacuees sheltering inside had gathered, and every face there was uniformly free of the fear and anxiety of having had their city occupied. But there was a brightness on their faces that went beyond just that explanation. The cause was—

"And soooo! Allow me to sing for you once more! Please, listen to my new song — 'The City Ablaze, a Burning Ballad'!"

"That racket — is that Liliana?"

At the center of the fervor was a small girl with tan skin. Her yellow hair whipped about as she was glimpsed energetically strumming a lyulyre, singing away. An appearance too impact-driven to mistake for anyone else, an originality so sharply askew that even "Lust" surely couldn't have impersonated it — it had to be Liliana.

"She really is a noisy girl, I suppose."

"But Liliana was also one of the people who fought an Archbishop of Sin, wasn't she? Um... I really can't imagine how she fought, though..."

Noticing the same thing as Subaru, Emilia and Beatrice both slumped, the tension draining from their shoulders. Since she was in such high spirits, Liliana had clearly made it back with all her limbs intact. Her battlefield — the fight against "Wrath" — was, in terms of strategy and the balance of power, perhaps the one place where no one could have imagined even the mildest possible outcome. Subaru had considered the possibility that Liliana's singing might help contain Sirius's Authority, but exactly how it had been used in practice, he didn't know. He would like to hear the specifics of what had happened on that battlefield, if he got the chance.

"Approaching Liliana right now isn't gonna be simple. Let's save it for later."

"Right. ...Besides, I bet right now is exactly when Liliana's song is needed most. It wouldn't be good for us to take that away from her. Let's talk to her later."

"Agreed, I suppose. Betty would rather not talk to that exhausting girl anyway, I suppose."

Watching Liliana play her instrument and sing out in ringing tones, the three of them arrived at that conclusion together. The voice sliding into their ears was pleasant, seeming to seep into hearts battered and worn. Truly, if you isolated just the singing voice, the title of "Songstress" suited her. Subaru could nod along with Emilia's opinion — that she was exactly what the city needed right now.

"――――"

Looking closer, Subaru spotted Kiritaka standing near Liliana as she played. His expensive suit was stained here and there with blood and mud, with the marks of tears still visible in the fabric. Otto had said his fate was unknown — and this was the result of whatever hellish scene he too had come through.

"Guess nobody's battlefield was an easy one..."

Noticing Subaru's gaze, Kiritaka gave a small bow in his direction. Subaru waved back, then set off once more into the shelter in search of his next companion. As he started walking, Beatrice fell into step beside him.

"Not that it's a big deal, but it was that man who woke Betty while she was sleeping, I suppose."

"Kiritaka did?"

"He shattered a precious grand mana crystal just to do it, I suppose. Whether that was a sense of duty, or for someone's sake — Betty has no interest in that part. But that's how it happened, I suppose."

"...Is that so, is that so. Good man, Bea-ko. Glad you told me."

Hearing this unexpected bit of assist-story, Subaru patted Beatrice's head. She puffed out her cheeks in apparent displeasure, though it went without saying that it was only for show. From what Anastasia had briefly told him, Beatrice's contribution after waking had been indispensable to this outcome. If Kiritaka had played a part in bringing that about, then he could be said to have fulfilled a duty befitting his position as one of the city's key figures.

"Subaru. The injured people seem to be further inside the shelter."

As Subaru exchanged that conversation with Beatrice, Emilia, who had been peering into the depths of the shelter, spoke up. Following her words and looking toward the dim area, sure enough, that section still bore the marks of having served as a makeshift field hospital throughout the chaos.

Mats and blankets laid directly on the ground, with several injured people lying on top of them. Since Ferris should have been evacuated here first of all, the people lying here — even if not fully healed — were presumably those whose lives were in no danger.

"Guess even Ferris couldn't fully heal this many people?"

"No matter how skilled someone is with healing magic, there's a limit to how much mana one person can hold, I suppose. Treat everyone indiscriminately and you'd run dry in no time, I suppose. It's the wise call, I suppose."

Watching the row of the wounded, Beatrice responded with a faint trace of frustration. However much she hid it on the surface, she was a spirit girl of deep, gentle feeling. Beatrice's healing magic, while it couldn't match Ferris's, was still quite potent. But that was only true if there was mana to spare — and the amount Subaru could supply her fell far short of that. It was only natural that she'd feel regret, mourning her own insufficient strength.

"Honestly, I wish I could go around casting healing magic on everyone too..."

"Emilia-tan has a different job to do. So let's set that aside for now."

"Yeah, I know."

If they let themselves be swayed by momentary sympathy and failed to accomplish their objective, it would defeat the whole purpose. Subaru called on Emilia for restraint, and cutting across the rows of people groaning in pain, searched among them for his own people. Before long, he was able to find the person he was looking for.

"Natsuki-san, over here."

"Yo, Otto."

A figure at the very back of the row of wounded, waving a hand toward them. Spotting the familiar young man, Subaru and the others walked over to him, relieved. Lying on a makeshift bed, his face pale, wearing a weak smile — it was Otto Suwen, the Emilia Camp's proud, battle-hardened chief of internal affairs.

"I feel like I just heard a pretty unforgivable evaluation of me in your head, just now."

"Just your imagination, battle-hardened chief of internal affairs. Wasn't it you who went wandering all over the city hunting for prey 'cause you wanted to see some blood? You really love that stuff, huh."

"That kind of talk is how weird rumors start again — could you please stop spreading baseless slander!?"

Subaru traded barbs with Otto lying there, as a stand-in for a proper greeting after their reunion. As Otto cried out and then let his shoulders sag in resignation, Subaru crouched down beside him to check his condition. There didn't seem to be any life-threatening injuries, but his legs looked painful. He should have had healing magic applied and been bandaged, yet a damp red was seeping through from his thighs. He must have taken a serious wound indeed.

"Otto-kun, how are your injuries?"

"Looks like walking's going to be a struggle for a while until it heals, but other than that, no notable external injuries. ...Given the circumstances alone, Emilia-sama's ought to have been the more dire situation, so it's rather pathetic that I'm the one worse off."

"That's not true at all. It's the result of you fighting your hardest, right? Fighting isn't even your job, Otto-kun, so I'm glad it wasn't worse."

"Emilia-sama is currently the only person around here with a sane grasp of what an internal affairs officer's job actually is."

"Eh?"

At Otto's heartfelt mutter, Emilia tilted her head, looking puzzled. Setting that aside, Subaru asked Otto for a detailed account of how he'd gotten injured. By rights, Otto should have stayed at the city hall, waiting at command central for reports from around the city.

"You didn't get hurt getting caught in the hall collapsing, right? From what Anastasia-san said, the ones who stayed at the hall were Anastasia-san, Ferris, and Al."

"I don't actually know the specifics of what those three did. I left the hall myself and, in the waterway district, ran into... 'Gluttony.' And, well, this is the result."

"'Gluttony'... that bastard? Damn it, first 'Lust,' now this — they just keep messing with us."

Hearing the name of that despised enemy, something churned inside Subaru. The Witch Cult's malice, mocking and overturning every premise of the plans they themselves had prepared — those bastards ignoring the control towers entirely felt like they were laughing at the stage the rest of them had set up.

"Fortunately, thanks to Felt-sama and everyone from the 'White Dragon's Scales,' we managed to drive it off somehow. Though I doubt we'd know the outcome if Beatrice-san hadn't been there."

"It was bad, really bad — I could hardly bear to watch, I suppose."

"Mm-hm. Thank you sooooo much."

Emilia gently stroked Beatrice, who puffed her chest out a little. The exchange itself was heartwarming, but what concerned Subaru was Otto's own actions. Regardless of the outcome of the clash with "Gluttony," why had he left the city hall in the first place? Even if he'd been left out of the formation to intercept "Lust," he could have just stayed holed up in the shelter. There was no need to leave the shelter and go wandering around the city.

"I gave priority to securing... a certain book that was among the Witch Cult's demands."

Sensing Subaru's question, Otto said this quietly. That he'd been vague, calling it "a certain book among the Witch Cult's demands," was surely out of consideration for Emilia listening in behind Subaru. Subaru nodded at that consideration.

"The restoration artisan's place — what's-his-name."

"Dartz, yes. No one should know that I asked him to do the restoration, but... I wanted to make absolutely sure by retrieving it myself. In the end, I ran into 'Gluttony' before I even reached Dartz's place, and this is what came of it."

Now Subaru understood the reason Otto had left the hall and made his way through a city crawling with Witch Cult threats. Once again, Otto had tried to cover for something Subaru hadn't gotten around to. The attack on the city hall, the retrieval of the Book of Wisdom — Subaru hadn't thought any of it through nearly enough.

"Just say something next time, would you. That much, at least. Aren't we friends?"

"Emilia-sama gets kidnapped, on top of that I'm made to shoulder the fate of the whole city like some kind of hero, and now you want me to carry yet another troublesome burden on top of it? No thank you. I have no intention of dumping that kind of idiotic weight on my friends over and over."

"Tch."

What Subaru had meant as a jab came back at him as an unexpectedly warm answer, and he made a small sound in his throat. Watching the exchange between the two of them, Emilia and Beatrice looked at each other and let out a deep sigh.

"Neither of them is honest, I suppose."

"But I think that's just like them. Still, that means the book...? It'd probably be better if we went to retrieve it. Um, where is..."

"Ah, I'll handle that. Or I'll send Garfiel to run the errand. Emilia-tan, don't worry too much about that book, okay?"

"Is that so?"

Subaru didn't want to let Emilia get too involved with the Book of Wisdom, if he could help it. It was a superior version of a Gospel, and a kind of relic left behind by a Witch. Keeping Emilia away from such things as much as possible was one of the quiet resolutions Subaru held within himself.

"Anyway, so it was Felt and the 'White Dragon's Scales' who ran into 'Gluttony.' The white mercenaries, sure, but wasn't Felt supposed to be hiding out somewhere?"

"I have trouble picturing that girl staying quietly out of the way, though..."

"Same here."

Subaru had been told that Felt and the others were holding Reinhard's father — Heinkel — captive, keeping watch over him. So they shouldn't have been part of the effort to retake the control tower — but apparently that kind of reasoning didn't hold much sway with people like them.

"So, where's Felt now?"

"She was worn out, but had no notable injuries. Right now she's dashed off out of the shelter to retrieve the attendant she brought along."

"Tonchinkan, huh. Sounds like they actually managed all right, from what I'm hearing, that bunch."

A trio with an impression both good and bad, but there was nothing better than them making it out safe. There'd been that one time he'd been killed by them, but he'd square things properly someday and let bygones be bygones. In any case, confirming that camp's safety was a gain in itself. Setting the Book of Wisdom-related tasks aside for later, if there was a next problem to deal with, it would be—

"Natsuki-san. — Be careful about the shelter next door."

"The shelter next door...?"

Otto said this in a low voice to Subaru, who had fallen into thought. At the ominous feeling packed into that tone, Subaru's own voice, in response, dropped naturally as well. At Subaru's reaction, Otto gave a small nod.

And then—

"One of the Archbishops of Sin is being held captive there."

"What, it's you, mediocrity. My, my, the nerve you have, showing that gloomy face of yours before us. That shamelessness goes past exasperating, straight into admirable."

Leaving the shelter turned field hospital behind, Subaru followed Otto's words to the shelter next door. Compared to the previous shelter, this one was considerably smaller in scale. If the earlier shelter had been about the size of a department store parking lot, this one was, at best, a bicycle lot. Subaru was vaguely musing that the purpose of this shelter must be different, when the red-clad woman stationed at the building's entrance called out to him like that.

The woman's name was Priscilla Barielle. Among the Royal Selection candidates gathered in the city, she was the singular oddity, spectacularly lacking in cooperative spirit.

Even so, whatever else she was, she had proven a reliable ally in this whole affair. What's more, the one Priscilla had been assigned to deal with was the mysterious monster of unknown detail — "Wrath," Sirius. The skill with which she'd routed that threat and returned was, honestly, a result worth praising without reservation.

"That you don't like my face is your own personal opinion, so fine, whatever. Anyway, good work out there, both of us. I'm relieved you made it back safe. No flattery intended."

"My personal opinion, as an individual, is the standard by which the world's beauty and ugliness ought to be judged, above all else. By that measure, you don't even merit an evaluation... but never mind that. Though I have no words to defend the blindness it takes to look at me right now and call it 'safe.'"

"Huh? You hurt somewhere?"

Priscilla sat in an improvised chair at the building's entrance, fanning herself. Subaru scanned her figure up and down, but found nothing that looked like an actual wound. No — it wasn't even a matter of no wounds. Priscilla's white skin bore not a single scratch, and there wasn't so much as a speck of dust or dirt on the dress she wore. If anything had changed between before the retaking of the city and now, it was, at most, the accessory at her throat and her hair, now let down.

"Your collar and hairpin, did you lose them somewhere?"

"Hmph. Bring along a boor with no eye for such things, and even a woman notices, does she. I dislike the witless way you put it — 'collar,' indeed. But yes, to my great vexation, it was taken from me."

"'Not safe,' and you seriously mean the accessories...?"

Priscilla answered Emilia's simple, guileless remark with a snort. Indeed, she had now lost both the lavishly jeweled necklace and the equally ornate barrette that had bound up her orange hair. With her hair, usually done up, now let loose, the seductive allure about her had only grown more pronounced — a sinful woman, truly. Though Priscilla's particular allure was closer to that of a poisonous flower. Get too close, and you'd be stung.

"And I have no interest in being stung, thanks. So why'd you come all the way to a shelter like this? I wouldn't have pegged you as the type to volunteer for guard duty."

"Fool. As if I would ever stoop to such menial labor among the lowly. Even I find a place like this not to my liking, but my aesthetic sensibilities forbid me from standing before the public as I am now. This was a compromise, to avoid prying eyes. Besides, Al insisted, so."

"If the man himself were here, he'd probably deny that in a panic."

Overlooking the exaggerated denial the iron helmet would surely have offered, Subaru glanced toward the entrance of the shelter. The helmet in question was nowhere to be seen, but he'd been told the man was in this shelter. Which meant he wasn't outside, but inside the building — beside the Archbishop of Sin.

"Al's keeping watch inside, then?"

"Indeed. Leave a repugnant creature like that unattended, and there's no telling what it might do. Hence I have Al keeping watch over it. That man will manage well enough, I'd wager."

"...Didn't think you'd not try to kill it. That's a surprise."

"If you wish to, then do so. I won't stop you."

Perhaps bored by the exchange, Priscilla hid a yawn behind her fan. That, presumably, was her own way of declaring her indifference to Subaru's question. She made no move to stop him from entering the building, either. Subaru gazed at the shelter's entrance, one hand pressed to his chest as his heart began to beat faster.

"Subaru, if you're scared to go in, you don't have to force yourself..."

"That's right, I suppose. I doubt there's anything to be gained, I suppose."

Emilia and Beatrice each offered gentle words to Subaru, whose feet had stopped moving. Part of him wanted to lean on their kindness. But the moment that thought raised its head, Subaru caught, at the edge of his vision, Priscilla's cold, cruel gaze fixed on him.

It was a gaze that regarded all of Subaru's hesitation, his wavering, as nothing more than a tedious farce. Whether he retreated or pressed on, Priscilla's opinion of Subaru wasn't going to change. Subaru being saddled with her harshest possible rating — that, he could live with. He could live with that. But he refused to let the two standing with him be judged the same way.

"Let's go. Either way, running from this isn't going to solve anything."

"――――"

Neither of them voiced approval or objection to Subaru's decision. They simply stood beside him, respecting his resolve. And so, with the two of them in tow, Subaru stepped into the dim shelter. Priscilla no longer even bothered to watch their backs as they went. Typical of her, one might say.

His footsteps echoed dryly as he made his way through the stone building's interior. Before long, the end of the corridor came into view, and past a turn to the left—

"—Brother? Heard the princess's voice, figured someone must be talkin' to her."

Squatting in the corridor, a green dragon blade resting on his shoulder — the iron helmet, Al, was waiting. Seeing Subaru and the others approaching together, he turned his attention toward Emilia.

"Well, look at that, seems like you made it out okay too, missy. Nicely done, brother."

"Emilia-tan being okay was the bare minimum condition for clearing this thing. Heard you had it rough too, though. Especially with Priscilla's crazy demands."

"Ah, no lie, dead serious about that. Even I had my doubts about this one, for once. Well, I always have doubts about myself, so that's not saying much."

"But Al, you don't look like you mind it all that much...?"

"――――"

Al, who'd been about to let slip something like a complaint about his liege Priscilla, had the wind knocked out of his sails by Emilia's guileless remark. Though the helmet hid his face, Subaru could almost picture Al's mouth twisting into a frown behind it. In truth, he was a man who, despite constantly being jerked around by Priscilla, still chose to serve as her attendant regardless. There was probably some relationship between the two of them that outsiders couldn't fully grasp.

Al tilted his head for a moment, looking like someone who'd been had, tapping his own shoulder with the flat of his blade. Then, suddenly turning his gaze toward the depths of the corridor—

"Bit late to ask now that you've come this far, but... you here to talk to the Archbishop of Sin?"

"What other reason would there be, I wonder? Betty and the others don't have so much time to waste that we'd come all this way just for idle chat with you, the guard, I suppose."

"Harsh, this little lady. Don't get so worked up, Bea-ko, an—"

"――――"

At Beatrice's icy, sharp glare, Al shook his head in a show of feigned innocence. Subaru held back Beatrice, who looked ready to lunge at him regardless of the height difference, and glared at Al for the unnecessary provocation.

"I get you're in a mood for whatever reason, but please don't provoke her. And Bea-ko, don't take the bait either. Handle this with some grown-up dignity."

"Betty only allows Subaru to call her that, I suppose. Use that name again, and a punishment beyond imagining awaits you, I suppose."

"Yeah, yeah, got it. Man, you're cold."

As he spoke, Al stepped aside to the edge of the corridor, clearing the way. Straight ahead, further down the corridor, a door came into view. That, presumably, was where the Archbishop of Sin was being held. A faint tension began to prickle at the back of Subaru's neck.

"Archbishop of Sin's further in. Restrained so it can't cause any trouble, so I don't think it'll come to blows. — That said, one bit of advice."

"Advice?"

"Brother, missy, you too, spirit girl. You'd be better off not talking to it at all, just heading back. Nothing good comes from getting involved. Leave it be and go home."

"...Can't exactly do that."

Lowering his voice, his tone earnest and heavy with sincerity. Subaru shook his head at those words, rejecting the suggestion — he couldn't do that. And at Subaru's answer, Al let out a sigh and muttered, "Figured as much."

"Not like anything I say carries much weight anyway, huh. No excuses for how bad my attitude's been here today, either."

"That's not why. Sure, you weren't exactly cooperative, that's a fact, but that's not me saying I won't listen because of it. Don't get the wrong idea."

Chiding Al for his oddly self-flagellating words, Subaru pointed toward the door at the end of the corridor. Whatever business waited with whoever was beyond it was Subaru's own business, no one else's. Perhaps sensing that resolve, Al sat himself down right where he was, heavily. Then, gesturing toward the door at the end with just his head, he said—

"While you're talkin' — don't lose yourself in there."

"OK. If anything happens, feel free to jump in and help, no need to hold back."

"That happens, I'll send the princess in — so whatever happens, rest in peace, yeah?"

Trading one last, characteristic exchange, Subaru and the others left Al behind and headed for the door. Something like a strange sense of pressure seemed to drift from the sealed room beyond. No use hesitating here, Subaru told himself, and resolved to grip the doorknob, pushing the door open with force.

—It was a small, dusty-smelling room. Dim, with few light sources, its arrangement as a shelter kept to only the barest minimum. A cramped room that would fill up with just five or six people in it, stifling enough to make breathing feel difficult. And there, in the center of that room—

"—Ah, ha. You came, didn't you. Sorry to trouble you. Thanks."

Bound from head to toe in chains upon a worn-out chair, the monstrous figure — Sirius — was waiting.
