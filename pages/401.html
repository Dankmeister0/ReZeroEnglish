Arc 5 Interlude: "The Unfinished Great Vessel"

The menu offered at the dining hall was so lavish that one could hardly believe the entire city was frantically absorbed in its own recovery and reconstruction. The food at the infirmary had been the same way — its offerings had nothing to do with modesty, and his stomach growled despite himself. For Garfiel, still in the prime of his growing appetite, it was a welcome thing, but was such extravagance really permissible?

"Even though most of the city's functions have been paralyzed, if you set aside the casualties and the collapse of a few facilities, the actual scope of the damage is fairly narrow. If we let a bad situation become an excuse to lower the quality of life, the townsfolk's spirits will break before recovery even begins... or so Master Kiritaka seems to think, at any rate."

Wilhelm said this to Garfiel, who sat frowning before the meal that had just been brought out. He was the gentleman who happened to share Garfiel's table at the dining hall, quietly eating his lunch across from him. At the old swordsman's answer, Garfiel's eyes went wide, and the image of a slender young man rose in his mind.

Honestly, in Garfiel's memory, the man wasn't someone he'd seen much reliability from. But from what he'd heard, that same man had run himself ragged for the city's sake amid the chaos of the Witch Cult incident. He couldn't picture it, but the man must have done work worthy of the effort. If anything, the way his ordinary demeanor differed so sharply from how he acted in a crisis meant Kiritaka might share something in common with Subaru. — That thought left a knot of unease in him.

"Mm! Yummy, yummy! Tasty food means happiness, so Mimi thinks this is a reeeally good thing! Miss always said free food tastes the best, after all!"

"Ha ha, high spirits are a good thing. And your manners... forgive me, are quite proper. Whoever taught you must have done well."

Beside Garfiel, Mimi chattered away cheerfully as she dug into her meal. As for her hands, though — surprisingly, Mimi seemed to have been properly trained in table manners, and she handled her knife and fork correctly. Garfiel already knew this, but it apparently came as a surprise to Wilhelm, who blinked in mild admiration.

"And Sir Garfiel...?"

"Don't go expectin' too much outta me. This past year I've been tryin' to fix it up bit by bit, on account of my sis naggin' at me."

"An effort to improve is a fine attitude to have. Provided you keep at it until it bears fruit, that is."

Wilhelm nodded, but perhaps it was the sight of Garfiel's actual results that prompted him to add that second half. In his own hands, the knife and fork weren't being put to particularly refined use. Back when he'd lived in the Sanctuary, it hadn't bothered him in the least, but over this past year of living at Roswaal Manor, Garfiel had come to feel shame in the small corners of daily life. It wasn't just his table manners, either — plenty of other things were under correction as well. Though it was hard to say any of it was showing much progress.

"Still, gettin' shown up by a shrimp like her stung, I'll admit."

"Well, Mimi's been doin' this since she was itty-bitty, so it can't be helped! Miss and the Cap'n were both suuuper strict about mealtimes! But thanks to that, Mimi got good at it!"

"Everybody back at our place has got it down proper too, dammit. Never mind the ones who actually studied manners for real, but even the boss and Otto-nii..."

That everyone originally connected to Roswaal Manor — Roswaal included — was skilled at it made sense to him. Emilia and Beatrice too — whatever their origins, they were both quick studies. With practice, they'd have picked it up easily enough. What didn't sit right with him were Subaru and Otto. Otto, it turned out, actually came from a fairly respectable merchant family, but Subaru was a complete mystery. Then again, Subaru had picked up any number of strange skills, so in a way, that was explanation enough.

"The boss, that bastard, can eat with those two weird sticks, no problem. What's it called again... chopsticks, or somethin'."

"Chopsticks, yeah, those are a little hard! There were tons of 'em in Kararagi, but Mimi's fingers go all wobbly-wobbly, so she might not be able to use 'em!"

"Chopsticks, hm. Now that brings back memories. They are indeed difficult to handle."

Even Wilhelm acknowledged the difficulty — such was the mysterious implement known as chopsticks. To Garfiel's eyes it was nothing short of baffling, but Subaru, for one, could wield them freely to carry food to his mouth. Ram too, for that matter. Well, in her case, it was probably just because she had an abnormal knack for picking things up by watching, or so he figured.

"So even old man Wilhelm's no good with chopsticks, huh."

"I've traveled to Kararagi a number of times, and on those occasions I have tried using them. Though I'd say it remains a tool ill-suited to everyday use."

"Kararagi, huh..."

"Yes, several times before. — When I was pursuing the White Whale, among other occasions."

"—— "

At the sight of Wilhelm narrowing his eyes in nostalgia, it was Garfiel who found himself at a loss for words. As for the history between Wilhelm and the White Whale, one of the Three Great Witchbeasts, Garfiel knew at least the broad strokes, if not the details. That this old man had run himself across every nation chasing leads, all to avenge the previous Sword Saint — that much he knew too, if only on the surface.

Overcoming hardships of that magnitude, Wilhelm had finally struck down the White Whale. But the old swordsman, who by all rights should have completed his revenge, had in this very city crossed paths with the wife whose soul he'd believed saved — and had crossed swords with her—.

"Surely what Sir Garfiel wishes to ask me is not, in fact, how to use chopsticks?"

"Ah, uh..."

"Naturally, there are things I cannot speak of. But I owe you a debt — you yielded to me my confrontation with my wife. If it is something these old bones can answer, then I shall answer it."

"—— "

"My confrontation with my wife," Wilhelm had said, plainly. It was common knowledge by now, but the two dead who had sided with the Witch Cult had indeed been the previous Sword Saint and Kurgan of the Eight Arms. At the atrocity of treating the dead as anything but dead, an anger that refused to fade threatened to well up in him — but for now, he set that feeling aside. If there was something he had to ask, it was this—

"Was the guy I fought really Kurgan of the Eight Arms?"

"...Hm. What do you mean by that?"

"Ain't there a chance it was just somebody who looked a hell of a lot like him? A stranger's resemblance? 'Cause otherwise, no matter how you slice it..."

"There is something you cannot accept. — I see. Understood."

Wilhelm nodded at Garfiel's halting, unclear way of putting it. Wilhelm set down his utensils, and pierced him with those quiet blue eyes.

"Is it that winning, however imperfectly, has left a sense of wrongness within you?"

"...I'm the strongest. I've worked to become the strongest, and I'll keep workin' at it. That's my duty to the boss, and it's somethin' I need. But not like this. The peak I'm lookin' at ain't somethin' like this."

Hero, war-god, Kurgan of the Eight Arms. A legendary superhuman without equal in strength, passed down in stories throughout the Sacred Vollachia Empire.

He might be cursed as weak-hearted for thinking it mid-battle, but time and again during the fight, Garfiel had thought he couldn't win. He had felt death and defeat close at hand. And yet he had overcome every one of those moments, fought his way through, and survived. In the end, he had challenged Kurgan to a head-on duel and taken his head.

That achievement in itself was something to be proud of — even Garfiel thought so. The people around him mostly evaluated him favorably for it, too. Though not one of the Sin Archbishops, Kurgan had been an overwhelming force that couldn't be ignored. Defeating him single-handedly had, without a doubt, been a real contribution to the city's defense. But the fact of the matter and Garfiel's own sense of conviction were two different things.

"Old man Wilhelm — the 'Sword Devil' Wilhelm — you knew the Eight Arms firsthand, didn't you. From where you stood, how was it? Was that thing the real deal?"

"—— "

With an expression like a man clutching at something, Garfiel leaned on Wilhelm's memory. Even Garfiel himself hadn't worked out what answer from Wilhelm would satisfy him. Would being told "no, it wasn't him" be enough? But even if he were told it wasn't Kurgan, could he really take that denial at face value?

"It is true that, nearly thirty years ago now, I was acquainted with Kurgan. We crossed blades in earnest four times. I once cut off one of his arms, and in turn had my belly run through... ours was, one could say, a relationship built on repeated death-matches."

There had once been a time when relations between the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica and the Vollachia Empire soured worse than at any point in the past several centuries, and a ferocious clash broke out in a city along the border. To avoid provoking the intervention of the Divine Dragon, the Empire had invaded the city with only a bare handful of soldiers — and it was Wilhelm, then captain of the Royal Guard, who clashed with them.

In the end it became a fair, straightforward duel, an evenly matched fight that ended with both sides striking each other down. The outcome was carried forward unresolved, and after four decisive battles in all, the matter was said to have ended with Vollachia's forces withdrawing and the conflict laid to rest.

"He was a fine swordsman. Eight arms, four Demon Cleavers — parrying the eightfold assault he could unleash in a single blow was a matter of life and death..."

"Yeah, that's damn right. I nearly died, for real."

"Eight strikes against my one. So I was forced to choose: either whittle down the number of his strikes first, or slip past all eight to land a single fatal blow of my own."

"...Look, I ain't askin' for a lecture on how to beat him."

As a rare firsthand account from that time, he'd have been interested if it turned into a tactical discussion. But right now, more than curiosity, what mattered to him was an answer to his own question. At Garfiel's words, Wilhelm dipped his chin. "My apologies."

"As one grows old, the occasions to recall the past inevitably increase. These past few days especially, I've found myself doing little else."

"Can't say I care much for lookin' backward. Though I ain't one to talk."

"Less looking backward, perhaps, than retracing one's own footprints. Though either way, it may amount to the same maudlin thing. — But more to the point, we were speaking of Kurgan."

Wilhelm's expression, as he answered Garfiel's question, was serious in kind. The old swordsman recognized, reflected in his eyes, the trouble of a warrior still young. Of course he did. That trouble, that doubt, was a wall that anyone who placed themselves within battle was bound to run up against at least once. And the answer to that trouble was—

"—— "

"Unfortunately, during the recent conflict, I never exchanged words with Kurgan. I assumed it was likely him, but I had no room to spare him my attention. So I have no grounds to declare with certainty that the Multi-Arm Tribesman Sir Garfiel faced was Kurgan of the Eight Arms."

"No grounds, huh..."

"What you yourself felt, Sir Garfiel, in crossing blades with him — that should be the answer, in itself. Still, I understand why you remain unconvinced. So, if you will allow me to speak strictly from my own personal view... the wife and the Kurgan we each faced were those two people, and yet were not those two people."

Garfiel caught his breath at Wilhelm's flat declaration, then furrowed his brow. The idea was too abstract; it didn't quite reach him. Noticing Garfiel's confusion, Wilhelm prefaced his next words with "May I?"

"There is no doubt that their remains were defiled, and that the two of them had been made into puppets of the Witch Cult. Though I believe that, in their final moment, they reclaimed themselves, and words could be exchanged."

"—— "

The final moment — that meant the moment the one-on-one duel was decided. Slipping past the eightfold strikes, Garfiel's fangs had dealt the fatal blow, and Kurgan had met his end. In the moment he vanished, the words Kurgan had turned toward Garfiel still lingered in his ears, refusing to fade.

"—Well fought."

With only that single word left behind, the war-god's form turned to ash and scattered on the wind. In that moment, in the instant of that victory alone, there had been nothing but joy at having been acknowledged by a legend.

But as time passed, a negative thought had begun to simmer and rise within him. Was it really so easy a thing — for someone who had not yet reached the strongest, who couldn't even face down the tiger within himself — to have defeated a war-god once called a legend?

In an extreme state, with his life on the line, with a brother and sister behind him he hadn't yet said enough to— Was that — was that the reason he'd been able to win against such an opponent?

"Kurgan was a man of few words. Even had I been present at his final moment, he would never have tried to leave behind many."

"Yeah. Just one word. To me, just one word..."

"Then I shall not ask to hear it. — That was praise Kurgan of the Eight Arms offered to the warrior who defeated him. It is not something for an outsider's ears."

"—— "

Stopped by Wilhelm, Garfiel held his tongue. Praise, from a war-god to a warrior. But was it truly worth what he thought?

"I don't get what you meant, before."

"Hm."

"'Those two, and yet not those two.' I ain't in the mood for riddles. Just tell me. What you actually think."

"—— "

His tone turning rough, Garfiel pressed the question, aware even as he did of how poor his manner was. Deep in Wilhelm's eyes, a faint stir of emotion had risen. He leaned in, trying to probe its true nature, to seize it as a means of laying bare his own trouble. But—

"Garf, Mimi thinks that's a not-good thing to do."

"...Ah?"

"Old man, just now, weren't you lookin' kinda lonely? So Mimi thinks pushin' in like that is a not-good thing? Also Garf's eyes look reeeally mean right now. Not good!"

Sitting beside him, Mimi said this while jabbing Garfiel's side sharply with her finger. Pushed back by the poke of her finger, Garfiel frowned, wondering what she meant — and then it hit him at once.

"—— "

The gentleness in the way Wilhelm looked at Mimi — that same gentleness had, moments ago, been turned on him too. And there was one more thing besides. That he himself, unconsciously and without restraint, had been digging his fingers into Wilhelm's wound.

"...Sorry. I wasn't seein' anything around me at all."

He apologized, bowing his head. To the Sword Devil, who had been forced into an unwanted reunion with the wife he'd already lost, who had ended it by the sword, and exchanged final parting words — Garfiel had heaped on him a selfish violence of words, with no consideration at all for his heart. To Garfiel, ashamed of himself, Wilhelm shook his head.

"No, the fault is mine, for being so roundabout about it. At your age, it's only natural to be impatient for an answer. I must have been the same once, but as one ages, one forgets that."

"...Can't believe old man Wilhelm was ever like that."

"It's not so unbelievable. If anything, I was, at times, far more rash and foolish than the young men one finds around here."

At the comforting words of this composed old man, Garfiel felt nothing but awkward embarrassment. Famous as the epithet "Sword Devil" was, he'd never once heard that the man's character had been vicious. Being indulged by the generosity of his elder was, in itself, a good part of why Garfiel felt so much self-reproach. In any case,

"'Those two, and yet not those two.' I put it in a roundabout way, but I wasn't trying to pose you a riddle. It means exactly what it says, plainly."

"Exactly what it says... even takin' it that way, I still don't get it."

"In their final moment, standing at the brink of death, their will did indeed return. But before that, they were nothing more than puppets of some wicked faction, and their swordsmanship was never wielded to its full extent."

"—— "

"In other words, neither the previous Sword Saint nor the Eight Arms was, in their true strength, anything close to what you saw."

Wilhelm told this to Garfiel, who had gone speechless. To be told flatly that an opponent who had fought so fierce a battle hadn't even shown his true worth — that wasn't something one could nod along to so easily. He had, quite literally, won a life-or-death struggle. Though, since his doubt had begun precisely from the question of whether it had truly been an opponent he could win against even at the cost of his life, this was in a sense the answer he'd wanted. It was, but—

"Had you faced my wife at the height of her prime, even I, as I am now, would be overwhelmed after only a few clashes of the blade. It was I, back then, at my own height, casting everything aside, who barely managed to close the distance on her true strength. — It could never have been anything so slight as what you saw."

"If the same holds true for..."

"Kurgan too, I would think, was wearing only a husk, his true strength degraded. It may sound strange to say... but had you faced the genuine Eight Arms, I cannot imagine you'd have come away as anything but scraps of meat. That was the caliber of man he was."

"I-I could've—"

"—Do not grow arrogant, whelp."

An instant later, a piercing edge of killing intent sent every hair on Garfiel's body standing on end, and he instinctively kicked back his chair, his body flying clear back to the entrance of the dining hall. Amid the surrounding shock at the sudden commotion, only Garfiel — gasping for breath, down on all fours — looked out of place. Wilhelm, and Mimi too, simply sat there as if nothing had happened.

"Wha— what the hell...?"

"You do carry the makings of a great vessel, Sir Garfiel, but that vessel has not yet finished firing in the kiln. I am, myself, already well into the realm of an old relic — but I know the genuine article. If you felt even a fragment of it just now, then this cold splash of water will have served its purpose."

Having said his piece, Wilhelm wiped his mouth and rose to his feet. He had finished his meal. And his manner made clear that he had said all that needed saying.

If something was lacking, it wasn't Wilhelm's words. What was lacking, surely, was Garfiel's own heart. Because there was some deficiency on the receiving end, the understanding poured into him kept spilling out over the rim of the vessel.

"That understanding is something you need in order to grow stronger. Take care not to lose sight of it."

"I'll keep it in mind, sure... but you're a mean old man, y'know that."

"I'm not exactly firmly on your side, after all. A little check like this is only proper. Though—"

Wilhelm, having finished readying himself to leave, glanced at Mimi mid-sentence. Mimi, humming a tune as she kept eating, was in the midst of pilfering a favorite dish off Garfiel's plate. Noticing Wilhelm's gaze, Mimi—

"Hm? Somethin' you need, old man?"

"That you could tell, in that single instant, whether there was hostile intent behind my killing edge — that was well done indeed."

"Old man ain't got any reason to do somethin' bad to Mimi and them, right?"

"A keen eye. — With you at his side, there is little fear of him straying from the path."

Wilhelm nodded at Mimi's blithe answer, then turned his eyes to Garfiel. Gesturing toward Mimi with his hand, the old swordsman headed for the dining hall's exit, saying—

"You would do well to treasure her. A companion of the opposite sex like that will surely become a treasure in your life."

"—Tch! Like hell, with her! I already got a woman I'm—"

"Whatever the case, guard against losing it, with all your might. — Lest you end up like some rotted-away devil, somewhere."

With only that left behind, Wilhelm walked out of the dining hall. Unable to talk back, Garfiel watched his retreating back, gnashing his fangs in irritation. Then he stormed back to his seat and shoveled down the rest of his meal in one go.

"Aah, Garf, that's bad manners!"

"Don't want to hear that from the one who was stealin' food off my plate. Ah, damn it. Feels like I'm more pissed off now than before I even asked, huh."

Far from clearing his doubts, if anything, the seeds of his troubles felt like they'd multiplied. A path toward some ray of light might have been laid out for him, but the guidance toward it was so cryptic that Garfiel couldn't even find a foothold.

Both the conviction of his own strength and what he ought to do here — it was the maddening frustration of being just one step short.

"Damn it all, what am I supposed to do."

"Garf, you in trouble? What kinda trouble?"

As he touched the white scar on his forehead and grumbled, Mimi, having likewise wolfed down her own meal, asked. The girl, her mouth smeared with food, scrubbed at her face with her sleeve and said—

"If somethin's troublin' you, spit it out! Mimi'll answer it right up, ta-daa!"

"...I don't even know what the hell I'm supposed to do, right here, right now."

"Clear away your finished dishes! That's what!"

"That ain't — I don't mean somethin' that literal."

Even so, the two of them cleared their finished dishes to the kitchen and left the dining hall. His stomach was full. Should he go on and take part in the city's reconstruction work this afternoon, same as always? If moving his body like that meant he wouldn't have to think—

"Alright, so, let's go, Garf!"

"You're full of energy, ain't you. So, where we goin'?"

"Obviously — to see Garf's little brother and sister and mama!"

"—— "

Trying to follow Mimi as she strode off easily, Garfiel's feet froze at her words. Garfiel's eyes went wide, his fangs clicking together, and he cocked his head, straining as best he could to keep his inner thoughts from showing on his face.

"Come again?"

"We're goin' to see Garf's family now! That's what Garf needs right now!"

With no grounds whatsoever, Mimi puffed out her slight chest and stood her tail up straight. Then, pointing at the speechless Garfiel, she said:

"You oughta have a proper talk with your family! That's what Roshi taught me!"
