Arc 1 Chapter 5: "Your Name"

An interaction with a friendly party — the first one since arriving in another world. After that heartwarming exchange, and after he'd offered to help her out, about thirty minutes had passed.

"...Okay, hold on, what is going on."

The investigation had stalled out nicely.

The girl's gaze as she pressed him was cold, and being on the receiving end of that displeased look had brought Subaru to a new discovery. Namely—

"Being looked at with such cold eyes, and I'm getting chills... don't tell me. No way. Could it be that I... I am... an M...?"

"I don't know what an 'em' is, but I can tell you're saying something really, really stupid."

Arms crossed, the girl didn't even try to hide her exasperation as she let out a sigh. It was about as damning an assessment as they came, but it could fairly be called an accurate evaluation of Subaru's contributions over the last thirty minutes.

Subaru had volunteered himself, and so their journey to search for her missing item had begun — but with several problems surfacing along the way, the investigation had run into serious trouble.

First, he had no sense of the local geography. Honestly, having just been summoned to another world, all he could say was please forgive him for that — but it was no less of a blow to the girl, who admitted she wasn't familiar with this area either. The two of them had each assumed the other knew the way, and wasted about ten minutes wandering the back alleys — a funny story now, in hindsight. Except it wasn't funny at all.

Second, he couldn't read a lick of the writing. Since conversation worked fine, he'd let his guard down, but looking around again, there were hieroglyph-like handwritten characters everywhere. He hadn't noticed at first, but seeing them on signboards too, unless it was some kind of "trendy protective charm going around town," that had to be the writing system of this world. Which meant he couldn't tell shops apart by their signs, nor could he read a map to ask for directions. In other words, of the classic summoned-hero perks — "somehow you can understand the language and read the writing!" — only the former had applied to him. Granted, if even the former hadn't applied, he'd have been left to die in a ditch, so maybe this counted as the slightly better outcome. Even so—

"Even so, this is a pointlessly absurd difficulty spike..."

Far from the start of some pampered cheat-protagonist story, it was proving to be a shoddily prepared one, with holes turning up everywhere. And above all, the fatal problem that had earned him the girl's displeasure was—

"More to the point, you've just been hiding behind me this whole time. You haven't been the slightest bit of help."

"Well, talking to people I've just met is kind of scary, so..."

"You're not a child..."

She furrowed her lovely brows as if she'd developed a headache, and spoke in a voice that bordered on pity. Just as Subaru himself had feared, the hurdle of asking a total stranger something had turned out to be abnormally high. This past year, he'd been sprinting full-speed up an endless slope of social ineptitude. Right now he was nothing more than a shy little kid clinging wordlessly to his mother's side.

He wanted to protest her disappointment, if only a little, but his own uselessness weighed on him so heavily that the words just wouldn't come. Then the little cat, who had kept silent on her shoulder until now, spoke up while wagging its tail.

"Don't worry. ...He's got zero malice, at least."

"That's even more disappointing. I'd almost rather believe it was outright sabotage. ...Good intentions that are simply useless are harder to deal with than ill intentions."

"Well, that's a lesson learned, huh."

The girl let out a breath at the cat's detached, none-of-my-business attitude. Then she turned her gaze back to the street, and prefacing it with "even so," she said—

"This might actually be pretty rough."

She admitted her weakness in a small voice, just like that. Their location had shifted — away from the back alley of before, away from the main street that alley connected to, into a slum far from the bustle of the shops. Passive as it had been, this rough trail was the fruit of their continued questioning.

"They said if someone's fencing stolen goods, it'd be the slums or a poor district, but..."

"You already know the place and what the guy looks like — why not just leave the rest to the police, or, uh, whatever you call the guards around here? If we could throw a mob of people at it, that'd settle it in one shot."

A narrow alley leading into the slums, one they'd learned of from a shopkeeper two streets over. Partly because the sun was starting to slant, but also for reasons beyond the mere light, the atmosphere turned dim and murky the moment they crossed into this space set apart from the main street by just one block. A damp air and a musty smell drifted over, and Subaru couldn't help but grimace.

"The air, the mood, and probably the personalities of the people living here too — all bad. Calling in reinforcements would be the sure bet."

"No."

It wasn't that he'd chickened out or gotten cold feet — it was a proposal made after carefully weighing his own abilities against the girl's safety. But it was flatly, immediately shot down. Subaru blinked in surprise at how firm her refusal was, and the girl looked at him with a touch of apology.

"Sorry. But it's a no. I don't think the guards would move for a theft this small, and... besides, there are reasons I can't rely on the guards."

She pressed her lips together, and with a look that seemed to plead "I can't tell you why," she looked at Subaru. She probably doesn't want to be asked about it, Subaru thought, and raised a hand in response to that look. He wasn't the type to go poking around in other people's business to begin with, and getting too deeply involved was just a hassle besides. If he had it in him to be that proactive about human relationships, he wouldn't have become a shut-in in the first place.

"Well, so what do we do?"

Without pressing for the reason, Subaru asked her what their next move would be. Not digging into her reasons and deciding whether to keep helping her were two separate matters. He didn't know her circumstances, but he refused to be the kind of person who forgot a debt of gratitude.

The girl seemed surprised by his suggestion, raising her eyebrows slightly, as if she'd fully expected him not to cooperate. On her shoulder, the cat did a little step, and—

"See? Told you. Zero malice, that one."

Still looking utterly unbothered, it pointed a paw at Subaru with obvious delight. But then the cat's expression abruptly turned serious.

"Still, choose carefully. Night's coming soon, and once it does, I won't be able to lend a hand anymore. If it's just thugs you're up against, I'm not too worried, but... you'll still need to be careful."

"Right... yeah. I'll think about it. I will, but—"

The girl's answer to the little cat's suggestion was noncommittal. While she agonized over her answer, Subaru turned a sudden question toward the cat.

"So wait, from what you just said — what, you can't come out at night?"

"It's not that I 'can't come out,' exactly — I might look this cute, but I'm a spirit, you know. Just staying manifested all the time eats up a fair amount of mana. So at night I retreat fully into my anchor and focus on storing up mana. On average, nine to five is the ideal, I'd say."

"Nine to five, like a government worker... spirits have surprisingly strict employment terms...!"

Terms like "spirit" and "anchor" were being tossed around quite naturally, but that was something his reading comprehension — poisoned as it was by a modern otaku's diet of anime and games — could unpack well enough.

Anyway, the little cat in front of him — Puck, rather — apparently wasn't contracted for nighttime activity. Going by its appearance alone, it looked about as reliable as a throw pillow, but judging by how thoroughly those thugs in the alley had been spooked, whatever this thing called a "spirit" was, it clearly held some serious power — Puck himself notwithstanding. The girl who could summon blocks of ice with magic — a being even she seemed to fear. Contrary to its abnormally idyllic vibe, maybe it actually had some habit like "feasts on human flesh and craves the scent of blood!"

"Thinking about it like that, this is suddenly terrifying. Just so you know, I'm all sinew, I wouldn't taste good!"

"I can read minds a little, in a vague sort of way, and I gotta say... you're pretty dumb, huh."

He said it like he'd tried to wrap it in something soft and failed halfway through, and then Puck scrunched up his face, furrowing his overly fluffy eyebrows.

"Come to think of it, I still haven't heard your name. I don't think we've actually introduced ourselves, have we?"

"Oh yeah, huh. Guess not. Well then, I'll go first."

Clearing his throat, Subaru spun around on the spot and struck a pose, finger pointed at the sky.

"The name's Natsuki Subaru! Clueless about which way is which, and penniless besides — a man of guileless innocence! Nice to meetcha!"

"Just hearing that makes it sound like you're already doomed. Yeah, well, I'm Puck. Nice to meet you."

Puck launched his whole body at the friendly outstretched hand for a dynamic handshake. One side offering a hand, the other its entire body — from a bystander's view, it probably looked like Subaru was crushing Puck in his grip. Soothed by the same fluffiness he'd had even at his giant size, Subaru then turned his gaze to the girl beside him. She watched the exchange between the boy and the cat with a jaded look.

"Why can't you dial back that unnecessarily familiar attitude for normal situations?"

"I'm panicking and grabbing at whatever thread I can find, that's why! Damn it, I'm not letting this connection go... I'm gonna depend on it to survive...!"

"That's a really pathetic resolution. ...By the way, do you even remember what pretext you're supposedly traveling with us under right now?"

"Of course. Turning a search for a lost item into a search for a lost person. And I'm the only one who knows the characteristics of the person being sought... I won't let myself get cut loose, I'll never spill it, no matter what...!"

"You were muttering behind me during the questioning, something like 'blonde hair and... teeth, oh, never mind, that's fine' — so the general details are already out."

"You idiot me!!"

Subaru crouched down on the spot, clutching his head. It was like playing poker with his hand of cards shown face-up. Calling that "negotiating" would be laughable. Even though this was finally the world's first friendly contact, the thread he was clinging to felt like it was about to snap, and the impatience of it all had him on edge. But watching Subaru's inner turmoil, Puck gave a wry smile.

"Well, everyone's got their own circumstances, circumstances. We'll hear about yours later, Subaru — for now, let's settle the business at hand. Still, that's an unusual name. Got a nice ring to it."

"Right, it's not a name you'd hear around here at all. Now that I think about it, your hair and eye color, and your clothes too, are pretty unusual... where are you from?"

"If I go with the template answer, probably some tiny country in the east!"

A well-worn pattern for other-world stories — some hidden Zipangu-esque land tucked away in the east of the world. A nation with almost no diplomatic ties to others, from which he'd drifted here — that magic-word setup that usually satisfied most people who heard it. However—

"Lugunica is the easternmost country on the continental map... there's nothing east of this country."

"Wait, seriously?! This is the eastern edge of the world?! Then— my beloved Zipangu?!"

"Doesn't even know where he is, penniless, and too scared to talk to people. ...Somehow I'm starting to worry about this guy's future from all sorts of angles."

Faced with Subaru's flustered panic, the girl began to look restless and uneasy. For all that she wasn't honest about it, plenty of a caretaker's instinct leaked out from the girl at the edges. She was probably unable to stay calm, seeing how utterly defenseless Subaru was. Puck watched the two of them agonize with an amused, warm expression. He flicked at the whiskers on his cheek with a paw.

"Anyway, let's iron that stuff out later. Right now, more than anything, we need to head deeper... though, I've got less than an hour left before I can't stay manifested. I need a decision."

"—Let's go. Either way, I'm not letting this chance slip by. It'll be too late once it's carried somewhere out of reach."

Answering Puck's request, the girl then turned to face Subaru.

"Alright, we're going, but... from this alley on, be even more on guard than before. It's getting dark, so there might be people with bad intentions around, and to begin with, the people who live here are used to rough business. If it scares you, you can wait here, or just stay behind me like you have been."

"If I said I'd wait here, what kind of chicken would that make me?! I'm coming! Like a guardian spirit haunting your back!"

"Not once considering stepping out in front, huh... though that does mean I don't have to worry about you as much."

Another sigh from the girl — he'd lost count of how many by now. Since they'd met, Subaru thought, her expression had done nothing but cloud over. Come to think of it, he still hadn't seen her smile even once. And she looked this good even angry. If she smiled, she'd probably be the most adorable thing imaginable.

"There's a mikan orange sitting on top of an aluminum can!!"

"...What's gotten into you all of a sudden?"

"That's weird. It's not landing, why... Ah! Damn it! Does this world even have aluminum cans! Um, um, okay then, how about the stove flying off the handle... no stoves either, huh!"

"Suddenly acting so strange... don't tell me it's some effect of a 'curse.'"

"No, this one's all on me. Though I am putting a lot of effort into it. In a pointless direction."

Maybe sensing the root of Subaru's floundering, Puck seemed to take it in fairly good humor. Meanwhile, all it had accomplished with the girl — to whom Subaru looked like he'd simply glitched out — was to plant seeds of distrust. Lamenting that too many of his puns only worked back in his original world, Subaru racked his brain desperately. Getting too fixated on what was right in front of him and losing sight of the big picture — that was one of Subaru's bad habits too.

"My leg cramped up, so I guess I can't leg it home, huh?"

Finally, a joke came out that didn't rely on modern-world knowledge — but the girl's response to it was simply to turn her face away, cold as ever. Her gaze was fixed on the alley ahead — a sign, perhaps, that she couldn't be bothered to keep humoring him any further. His fleeting desire to see her smile had ended in complete failure.

"—Are you done, Subaru?"

The girl called out to Subaru, who was at rock-bottom self-loathing over both the process and the result. Being called by name for the first time, and by a girl no less, left him a little flustered, and he said—

"O-oh, yeah. T-totally fine. Seriously fine. Totally got this, easy peasy."

"Why are you so flustered... I get that you're scared, but you haven't even gone in yet. If you're that scared, you could just wait."

Worrying over entirely the wrong thing, the girl let out a short breath and, this time for real, gave up on being considerate toward Subaru. Following behind her as she stepped into the alley, Subaru suddenly realized something — that even after she accomplished her goal, she had no intention of abandoning him. "Wait here" meant she intended to come back once she'd found what she was looking for. He'd learned in this past hour or so that she wasn't the type who could tell a calculated, convenient lie.

"Damn. I'm so freakin' lame."

He'd volunteered to help, but in practice had been absolutely no use whatsoever, and on top of it all, he'd made the girl worry about what would happen to him even after she'd achieved her goal. That was basically being a kept man.

Slapping his own cheek, Subaru forced his mind awake through the pain and faced forward. The whining and weak-willed complaints still hadn't run dry. Anyone dropped into another world with no protection at all would end up lost like this. He still didn't think that, in itself, was a bad thing. However—

"Being carried around like dead weight is just lame. At least walk behind her on your own two feet, me."

However dependent he was, he'd stand on his own two legs while doing it. —For someone who'd just "woken up," Subaru's goal was pretty small-scale.

"Oh, hey, so, um."

Called from diagonally behind, the girl glanced back just slightly through the gap in her silver hair. Even that sidelong glance was unfairly alluring, and feeling a little intimidated, Subaru posed his question to her pale profile.

"I realized — I've heard your pet cat's name, but I never actually heard yours."

At the playfully teasing question, the girl turned her gaze back forward and fell silent for a moment. Seeing that, Subaru inwardly panicked — uh oh, did I mess up?! Her not speaking up back in that earlier moment might have been her silent way of saying she didn't want to give her name. Unable to read anyone's heart, let alone a woman's — my social ineptitude striking again, in full, unfortunate force—

"—Satella."

"Oh?"

At the girl's sudden murmur, Subaru — still tangled up in his inner turmoil — was caught off guard. Without turning back, the girl said it once more to him, flatly, without emotion.

"You can just call me Satella."

Even as she gave the name, her manner seemed to reject the idea of actually being called that. Subaru, who would have preferred a family name that was easier to use, fell silent, unable to bring himself to actually address her that way. For now, I'll settle for using second person, he concluded, in typically spineless fashion. Against the backdrop of that exchange between the two of them, Puck — buried in her silver hair — murmured a single line, almost in passing.

"—That's bad taste."

But it reached neither Subaru nor even the girl herself.

  • "アルミ缶の上にあるミカン" (a classic Japanese palindrome joke, roughly "a mikan on top of an aluminum can," which reads the same forwards and backwards) doesn't work as wordplay in English. I preserved the literal image/absurdity rather than inventing an English palindrome, since Subaru's subsequent lines explicitly reference the aluminum can and rely on this literal content.
  • "足が痙攣したから家にけえれん（帰れん）" is a pun on Kansai-dialect "けえれん" (can't go home) sounding like "cramp" (痙攣). Rendered as "cramped up... can't leg it home" to approximate the pun via "leg" wordplay rather than dialect.
  • "M" (Subaru's aside about being "M") is a reference to masochism (M/S kink slang in Japanese); kept as "M" per source since Subaru name-drops it directly and Ram's response plays on not understanding it.
  • The girl in this chapter is unnamed in the narration (referred to as 少女/"the girl") and gives the false name "Satella" at the end — this is intentional per the source (she is later revealed to be Emilia). Kept ambiguous/untranslated as in the original.
