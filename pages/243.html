Arc 4 Chapter 76: "≠Satella"

This was, in fact, the first time Subaru had ever come face to face with the "Witch" herself.

He'd heard the name Witch of Envy more times than he could count, and within the loops that had begun from this Sanctuary, he had confronted the very threat of that witch as well. More than once or twice he'd tasted the agony of having his heart crushed simply for defying rules that had been forced upon him without his consent, and it was hard to imagine holding anything but ill will toward the witch who had taken over Emilia's body and destroyed the Sanctuary. After the exchange he'd just had with Echidna, an aversion to the very word "witch" had already taken root in him — which made it all the more true now. And yet,

"Still, this one really is... on a different level from the other witches."

Faced with the pressure radiating off the witch standing before him, Subaru muttered in a hoarse voice.

A slender woman. Both arms hanging limply at her sides, she stood without a hint of tension, seemingly looking straight at him. Her body was wrapped in a jet-black dress — literally woven from the shadow rising up from her feet — and he could see it rippling against her skin as though keeping time with a heartbeat. The dress had long sleeves, but past the wrists, unnervingly white fingers were visible; like the other witches, it seemed likely that the "Witch of Envy" too possessed beauty befitting — or surpassing — her station.

The trouble was, the one piece of information most necessary to judge that was missing.

"I've seen it a few times now, but... what's the deal with that?"

Everything above the witch's neck was shrouded in jet-black shadow, impossible to make out. Unlike the pitch-black dress she wore, that shadow drifted and swirled like mist, veiling the "Witch of Envy's" face from Subaru's sight.

To Subaru's half-exasperated question, however, the witch gave no response. While the mounting unease inside him scorched at his chest, Subaru, sweat beading on his brow, glanced around at the surroundings — at the other four witches, who remained resolutely silent.

"—————"

Seeing the change in their expressions, Subaru felt something unexpected. As far as he knew, the relationship between the "Witch of Envy" and these women was that of murderer and victim. Coming face-to-face again with the person who had killed you — Subaru at least thought he understood something of the psychological weight that carried. But the witches' expressions were, every one of them, different from what he'd imagined.

One wore a gentle smile, one a look of pitying compassion, one a blankness that was pure indifference, and the last —

"So you've broken through my boundary and come all the way in here. To set foot so crudely even into my castle of dreams... how endlessly self-serving of you."

Echidna alone glared at the "Witch of Envy," hostility burning in her eyes. That Echidna, of all people, should wear something so akin to hatred came as a surprise to Subaru. Only moments ago he'd declared his parting words to her on the assumption that she possessed no such emotions. Seeing her now, baring feelings so openly, made him doubt himself — had he misjudged her?

As a practical matter, though, the moment for raising that had already passed. The problem now was how to deal with the still-unmoving witch before them.

"What the hell is that thing even doing here in the first place..."

"Isn't it because you went and blabbed about something you weren't supposed to say, and made her angry? Personally, I have my doubts about men who run their mouths like that. I can kind of understand that girl's indignation."

"Don't ask me, how should I know. And wait, are you actually taking that witch's side? Isn't she your enemy too, yours and the other witches'?"

"'Your own enemy' — that has such a stupid ring to it. ...Whether what you're saying holds up, we're about to find out."

Narrowing her eyes at Subaru's words, Minerva moved, her wavy blonde hair swaying. She stepped between Subaru's line of sight and that of the "Witch of Envy," who was staring fixedly at him, then squared her ample chest and stood boldly to confront the witch. And,

"Can you hear me? It's me, Minerva. Minerva, the 'Witch of Wrath.' If you remember me, if my voice is coming through properly, answer me."

"—! Hey, wait a second! As far as I know, there's no talking to that thing! If you provoke it with some weird stunt on top of that..."

"You'd best keep quiet, haah."

The reckless-sounding speech Minerva delivered looked like nothing but sheer folly to Subaru. But the words he'd tried to interject to stop her were cut off from behind, by Sekhmet, still a ball of fur on the ground. Glancing back only with his eyes, Subaru saw Sekhmet shift and swell the size of her reddish-purple fur-ball as she spoke,

"Many times longer than the short while you've had with it, fwoo. We spent our time together with That, back then, haah. Course it's only natural to be uneasy, fwoo. Best to leave this one to Minerva for a bit, haah. Sure, that girl acts without thinking sometimes too... fwoo. But this time, I don't figure she's acting quite so thoughtlessly, haah."

"I can hear you, Sekhmet! If you don't want all of us getting swallowed up because I fail at this conversation, don't say things that'll make her angry! I'll blow my top!"

"Somebody just breathing in front of you, fwoo, and you'll find a reason to get mad about it, haah. What am I even supposed to do with you, fwoo."

For all the scathing assessment she'd been handed, Minerva didn't take her attention off the threat before her. The "Witch of Envy," for her part, could have taken any number of actions during this exchange, yet she simply stood there doing nothing, her gaze fixed on Subaru past the "Witch of Wrath."

It might be fair to say this marked a clear departure from the witch's purely physical responses up to now. But that only meant she wasn't lashing out in open hostility right away — whether she was even in a position where conversation could work seemed like an entirely separate matter.

Minerva had taken it upon herself to talk; Sekhmet had left the whole matter entirely in Minerva's hands. So what of the remaining two?

"I-it's not like... anything, I mean, if, Minerva-chan wants... to, try her b-best, then... th-that's fine... by me? If, Minerva-chan... um, gets, hurt... I'll... k-kill it, though."

"A reassuring thing to say, but I've told you time and again — you're simply far too ill-matched against That. In this place, only Sekhmet stands a chance of countering it. — You understand that, don't you?"

Echidna, keeping her voice deliberately level, chided Carmilla for her halting yet combative remark. Then, glancing sidelong at Sekhmet, the white-haired witch found the ball of fur trembling all over, as if even answering were too much bother.

"Even I can't keep it pinned down continuously, haah. You know as well as I do my abilities just aren't suited to that sort of thing, fwoo."

"Of course I know that. Which is why all you need to do is crush its limbs and wring its neck off. Bind it physically, snuff out its breath, and I can finish the job of burying it out of this space with my own hand."

Echidna radiated such fierce hostility that it made Carmilla's remark seem almost endearing by comparison. Her tone remained breezy, yet nothing about it read as a joke, because a loathing she couldn't quite hide seeped through every word. It was enough to make one believe, without needing a reason, that Echidna's negative feelings — toward the "Witch of Envy" alone, of all people — need never be doubted.

Off to the side of that powder-keg exchange still going on behind her, the standoff between Minerva and the "Witch of Envy" continued. If anything, Minerva took another step closer, as if trying to keep the "Witch of Envy" from overhearing the other witches' conversation.

"—————"

Wordlessly, watching Minerva advance, Subaru held his breath. Minerva's actions looked like nothing but recklessness to him, but then again, he hadn't the faintest idea what the "Witch of Envy" had even appeared here to do in the first place. If this matched the pattern of everything so far, the reason the witch had shown herself was that Subaru had broken a taboo. But the worst the witch had ever done to him for breaking that taboo was to manifest only an arm and torment his heart — and the times she had actually appeared and interfered in reality, all she'd done was swallow up everything in sight into shadow.

Friendly contact was, of course, out of the question — more than that, the "Witch of Envy" had never even properly expressed a will of her own. What the witch was scheming, then or now, Subaru hadn't the faintest clue. So what action the witch might take toward Minerva was, to Subaru, a complete unknown.

— If Minerva were swallowed by the shadow, the three behind her would surely move at once.

If the trust placed in Sekhmet held true, the "Witch of Sloth" could crush the "Witch of Envy" with her Authority, and Echidna could drive off a weakened "Witch of Envy." And yet, if that were so —

Why weren't they doing it right now — that was what struck Subaru as inexplicable.

"—————"

If anything was strange, it was already strange that they'd left the first contact with the "Witch of Envy" entirely to Minerva. Carmilla, who wouldn't hesitate to strike back if attacked, was one thing, but Sekhmet had no wish for active hostilities, and even Echidna, brimming with enmity, hadn't rushed to defy Minerva's will and launch a preemptive strike. Just what were they all aiming for —

"You have the look of a man being tossed about, unable to see what the witches are scheming."

"..........."

"Mind you, having watashi — whoops, having boku's inner workings read that easily would be a disgrace to a witch's name. I'd hate to be thought so cheap on that front."

"Don't tack the boku-girl act on like it's an afterthought. — I'm just saying, if you're serious about keeping the 'Witch of Envy' away, now, while she's defenseless, would be your best shot."

"I see. So that's how the current situation looks to you. Well, well... yes, I suppose it would. For my part, of course, I wholeheartedly agree with what you're saying. In an ideal world, I myself would love nothing more than to unleash the full extent of my Authority and erase That without a fragment left behind, but..."

There she cut herself off, and Echidna narrowed her eyes. There was something in her manner unlike her — though at this point he couldn't say how much he really knew of what "like her" even meant — some shade of unaccustomed chagrin, and sensing it, Subaru waited for her to continue. After a brief silence, Echidna spoke.

"If I exhaust every means to eliminate That, only to have the other witches turn on me afterward, it would defeat the whole purpose. Minerva aside, gambling everything by making enemies of Sekhmet and Typhon is simply a bad bet."

"I'm not following. Why would trying to eliminate the 'Witch of Envy' turn them against you? In terms of being everyone's enemy, it's the same for all of them..."

"Th-that's... not... right...?"

It was Carmilla, who had kept silent up to this point, who cut into Subaru's question. Without looking at Subaru's startled face, she kept her eyes fixed on the standoff between Minerva and the "Witch of Envy," letting her words trail off flatly in fits and starts,

"For, everyone, 'Envy' being the enemy, is... mm, right, but that, and that girl... are, different, things, aren't, they?"

"...I don't get what you're saying. What are you all..."

"Far as we can't tell which one That standing there is, fwoo, striking first isn't just something we're reluctant to do — it'd flat out be the wrong move, haah."

"Which one...?"

Sekhmet's answer, picking up the thread. But even that only deepened Subaru's confusion. Understanding what they meant came to him from an entirely different direction.

One more step, and Minerva closed the distance to the "Witch of Envy" further. Then she spread both arms wide, forming a posture of complete defenselessness, and said to the "Witch of Envy,"

"— Are you the 'Witch of Envy'? Or Satella? Which is it?"

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

Subaru felt as though something he'd taken for granted, right down to its foundations, had just been overturned.

What Minerva had said diverged sharply from the facts as Subaru knew them. And yet, that it was neither a delusion nor some kind of joke on Minerva's part was affirmed by the silence of the witches around her, all of whom had lived through the same era. At Minerva's call, the "Witch of Envy's" shoulders trembled faintly for the first time. The black haze veiling her head stirred, and it seemed as if her body turned to face Minerva. — Only in this instant did the witch, for the first time, take Minerva into her awareness.

"—————"

Subaru wasn't given so much as a gap to raise the question of what that statement meant. Before he could, his throat had already gone bone-dry from the strain of the tension. Even with the witches' confirmation, it betrayed his own thinking from far above where he'd been standing. What Minerva had said, namely —

— The possibility that the person called Satella, the "Witch of Envy," was in fact someone else entirely.

No — that was reading far too much into far too little, this early on. How many times had he been burned before, judging things by surface information alone and clinging to fixed assumptions? He should always weigh the possibility, but he must not fixate on it alone. Above all, right now, he shouldn't let his attention stray from the scene before him for even a second.

"Not lashing out the instant I asked my first question... means there's still a chance, then."

As she spoke, Minerva closed the distance further. The gap between the "Witch of Wrath" and the witch was down to just five steps now.

"If you were the 'Witch of Envy,' it wouldn't be strange for you to turn your 'envy' on me the moment I stood between you and him — though I wasn't all that worried about that."

Four steps.

"Even so, if that's how it is, I wish you'd have said just one word to me first. I get it, sure — I know ours isn't a relationship where it's easy to face each other. I haven't forgotten the look on your face, right at the end, when you swallowed me."

Three steps.

"I like to think I was a lot better than the other five, though. Typhon aside, out of the rest of them I was the one who was closest to... a friend, or so I thought."

Two steps. She lowered her eyes.

"Yeah, I thought so... and because I thought so, that's exactly why —!"

She crouched. With two steps still between them, Minerva folded forward, power gathering in her back leg. And then,

"Do you have any idea how it feels to be ignored, after all this time apart —!?"

The ground exploded, and the mere two-step gap vanished in a single lunge. Kicking up a cloud of dust, Minerva surged forward, twisting her whole body and launching a fist from her shoulder with everything she had. It bored through the air, tore past the sound barrier, and rocketed — wrapped in a deafening roar — straight for the witch's head. It drove toward that darkness-veiled face —

"— See? Just as I thought."

Minerva's fist had come to a miraculous stop, a hair's breadth from slamming directly into the witch's face. It wasn't that the witch's shadow had stretched out and caught her arm. It was Minerva's own will that had halted her arm just short of full extension. Still holding the pose of her wound-up punch, Minerva straightened up, swaying her golden hair,

"There, look at that. The one who knew she didn't need to dodge my fist is Satella, not the 'Witch of Envy.' Echidna, you don't need to be so wary."

"...I wonder about that. I genuinely admire your resolve to prove it with your own body, but that's beside the point. If That simply rated you as too low a threat to bother with, naturally, it wouldn't have moved either way. So, Sekhmet —"

"Don't go dressing it up in reasons just to get me moving, fwoo. You're every bit as bad about giving up gracefully, Echidna, haah. Just admit it — That's Satella, fwoo."

To the speechless Echidna, Sekhmet threw back nothing but a sigh. The witches' ultimate weapon showed no sign of moving, remaining a ball of fur all the while. And Minerva, standing close enough to touch hands with the witch — Satella — turned back toward Subaru.

Pinned by those emerald eyes, unable to properly process the reality that she was standing right beside such a threat, Subaru could only stand there dumbstruck. Snorting at Subaru's attitude, Minerva's face took on a look of displeasure,

"What are you standing around gawking for. Get over here, quick."

"Get over here, you say... easy for you to say."

"What's that, not very manly of you. I proved it myself, showed you it's fine — shouldn't you come over here with your head held high too? Shouldn't you? Or is it that even after I've laid everything out for you like this, it's still not enough? If you say you can't cross a bridge even after someone's tested it for you and found it sound, then how exactly are you supposed to cross it!"

"Don't get all worked up on your own and boil over! It's not that I'm too scared to go over there! The reason I can't go over there is because I don't understand the reason I should!"

Raising his own voice to match Minerva's outrage, Subaru objected to being left out of the loop like this. Stamping his feet, he pointed at Satella — who seemed to have stepped outside the category of immediate threat — and swept his gaze over the witches, who were gradually standing down from combat readiness,

"In the first place, what does it even mean that the 'Witch of Envy' and Satella are two different things! You all talk about it like it's obvious, but as far as what I already know, that's flat-out different!"

"There's nothing 'how' about it. When a being unsuited for it forcibly absorbs a Witch Factor, side effects like that arise. Call it a clash between what might be called the witch-persona born of the Factor's influence, and the person's original self... I suppose. As far as I'm concerned, they're the same entity, so I've never quite understood the point of distinguishing between the two the way the rest of them do."

"A separate... personality!? So what, then? You're saying that swallowing you all up, all those historic atrocities, were the work of one of those personalities, and the other one's harmless..."

"That part isn't right."

Even as he tried to process what he'd just been told, Echidna stopped him. She shook her head, correcting the shape of his logic,

"Drinking down half the world, drinking down the six of us Witches of Sin — all of it was the doing of 'Satella,' not the 'Witch of Envy.'"

"Wha—!? No, that doesn't add up! If it was Satella who swallowed you all, and Satella standing right there too, then... the story—"

"It adds up just fine, that's the whole of it, haah. We don't forgive the 'Witch of Envy,' fwoo. But Satella, we hold no grudge against, haah. That's all there is to it, fwoo."

"I-I mean... even, Satella-chan, is... mm, still not great, but... better, than, the witch, at least... th-that's, all."

Sekhmet and Carmilla voiced their agreement in a way that only deepened Subaru's confusion further. It seemed to be the witches' unanimous view, but Subaru couldn't wrap his head around it. The one who had destroyed them possessed a split personality, and while they forgave the personality that had destroyed them, they wouldn't forgive the other — what was he even supposed to make of that?

"I've argued all along that the distinction is meaningless, but... we're never going to see eye to eye on it. Which is why I can't simply disregard their wishes and eliminate That. In my current state, a fragile spirit with no body to speak of, I wouldn't stand a chance if they turned against me after I'd gotten rid of it. Even I, once scattered as a mere soul, could never come back from that."

"B-but... isn't that a huge risk for the other five too? You're the one holding onto their souls, aren't you. If you disappear, the other witches would..."

"They've each made their peace with their own 'death.' Which is why they feel no attachment to lingering on like this, as nothing but souls. — Rather than bend themselves to survive, they'd far rather perish in service of their own convictions. That's the sort of thinking that makes them witches in the first place."

Neither Sekhmet nor Carmilla offered a word of denial to Echidna's flat declaration. The decisiveness of their judgment was too absolute to be called mere resolve, and Subaru found himself at a loss for words at how fierce the witches' way of living was. Anyone might admire the idea of being able to live — or die — that way, might wish they could. But no one could ever swear they'd carry it through, even after death, even in the afterlife.

"Minerva too..."

Was probably the same. Perhaps more than anyone, she could have been destroyed first, by the "Witch of Envy's" own hand. And yet Minerva had trusted a witch who, until she came within arm's reach, had done nothing at all — and as a result, she had proven that trust correct.

Subaru couldn't understand the bond between them. If there really was a tie of mutual trust between them, why had the "Witch of Envy" destroyed the other six witches in the first place? And why did the witches forgive her for it? Echidna's way of thinking, Subaru could still understand, at least a little better. And yet, even so —

"I get that that's how you all see it. Accepting it... is hard, but I can understand it. But I still haven't heard why that thing came out here in the first place."

"—————"

"I get that it's not going to attack us without a word of warning. Fine, I'll accept that much. ...But that doesn't mean it's safe. If what I've been seeing all this time really is the 'Witch of Envy,' then what does Satella want from me? To me, the 'Witch of Envy' has only ever been a hateful enemy. Being suddenly told it's different — I can't just wrap my head around that."

What's more, putting together everything the witches had said, it seemed certain that it was this very Satella standing before him who had drunk them all down. If that were true, then even if it was the "Witch of Envy" who had swallowed the Sanctuary, didn't that mean Satella was capable of doing something every bit as terrible? Feeling a sense of danger, being wary, keeping his distance — who could blame him for that?

"What does it want, what did it come here for? Until I know that...!"

"If you want to know that, why don't you come over here and find out."

Minerva cut off Subaru just as he was about to raise his voice further. Hand on her hip, she glared at him, not bothering to hide the irritation on her lovely face.

"I've had enough of you rattling off excuses and hedges. I'm standing right here beside her, and nothing's happened. And she came all the way here just to see you. If, even so, you're too much of a coward to so much as approach her, then I guess that just means we misjudged you."

"Misjudged, my ass! Don't go sizing me up on your own like that! Don't shove your own assumptions onto me! What do any of you even know about me!"

Being saddled with someone else's arbitrary image of him, and expected to move accordingly — he'd rather die. Once before, when he'd shouted the same thing, there had been a voice that answered him. He remembered the words from that time. He'd been leaning on those words ever since. — If he couldn't betray the self that had been saved by those words back then,

"Ah, damn it... I'm thinking something stupid, aren't I..."

It wasn't rational, an emotional decision swept along by momentum. No reflection on how much pain he'd suffered for doing exactly that, time and again. He should be paying closer attention to the small details of things, killing his emotions, moving coldly according to solid facts rather than the sway of his heart — keeping an unshakable heart of steel, always.

That was how he was supposed to want to be, wasn't it — and yet.

"You're slow to decide."

"The fear of getting close to someone you fought to the brink of life and death... damn it, you all understand that, don't you. This is hard to do."

"It's not like we don't feel anything about it either. It's just that Sekhmet and Carmilla are a lot more grown-up about it than I am. I have my own reasons for taking her side."

Saying this to Subaru as he approached, clicking his tongue, Minerva gave a shrug. Without giving him time to press her for what that reason might be, she stepped aside to make room for him. As the "Witch of Wrath" moved out of the way, Subaru found himself, quite naturally, standing face to face at close range with the witch — Satella.

"—————"

Unconsciously swallowing, Subaru found himself at a loss for words at the sheer strangeness of the presence before him. He'd seen her from a distance, and then closer still as he'd walked over, and he thought he'd already understood — and yet even so, he couldn't shake the pressure radiating off her, or the wrongness fed to him through his own eyes. The shadow-dress clinging to her body traced her limbs and curves in a manner disturbingly seductive, and the fact that nothing above her neck could be seen only lent the whole thing a perverse allure. And all of those impressions were drowned out entirely by the discord of that unreadable head.

"—————"

Seeing her up this close, Subaru realized the interference with his perception wasn't physical in nature. It only looked as though darkness were covering her face — it wasn't that shadow was literally covering that spot and blocking his line of sight. The reason he couldn't see the witch's face went deeper, rooted in something more fundamental to the mind. It wasn't physical obstruction keeping her face hidden. It was instinctive — something was making sure he "wasn't allowed to see."

"Everyone wants to look away from the ugliest delusions within themselves."

"..........."

"If you can't see her expression, then that's a matter of the state of your own heart."

From behind him came this unwelcome but validating piece of advice, confirming Subaru's own reasoning. Somehow swallowing down the urge to click his tongue, Subaru ignored Echidna — or rather, he had no spare attention to spend on her at all — and kept facing Satella.

Still, no proactive action came from Satella's side. All Satella had done, so far, was simply appear here. Everyone around her had made a fuss on their own, working themselves into a frenzy trying to prevent the damage her actions might cause from spreading. It might not even be an exaggeration to say that the sheer fact she was feared this much, merely by existing, spoke plainly enough to the danger of her presence. And then, just as Subaru's patience was wearing thin at the lack of movement, it happened.

"—!"

"—————"

Suddenly, seeing both hands stretched out toward him, Subaru's throat froze solid. Not sparing even the span of a single blink, Subaru had kept his entire focus locked onto Satella's every movement. He had no idea what might happen in the next instant — and that very tension left him at the mercy of a pair of hands that seemed to have appeared from outside the bounds of his awareness. It wasn't that he'd been startled by failing to see the movement. Subaru had, in fact, clearly watched Satella's arms move. And yet, that was exactly what startled him — that his own consciousness had quietly stood by and watched, all the way from the moment her arms were extended to the moment the gesture finished.

"What... are you, really? What do you want with me?"

The fact that he'd been unable to take any effective action against the hand held out to him. And half unconsciously grasping what that meant, Subaru forced out words on reflex. Just so he wouldn't have to acknowledge that fact, wouldn't have to face it, he wrung the words out —

"If you're the one giving me the power to start over... then why..."

He didn't understand Satella's intentions. And facing her, standing close enough now to touch, even as some part of him screamed over and over, consciously, that this was dangerous — he didn't understand why his body refused to listen.

— The meaning behind his body's unconscious urge to feel "relief," standing before her.

"—————nn."

"—Huh?"

Unable to accept his own body acting against his will, Subaru was slow to react to the sound that struck his eardrums. This time, without a doubt, it was the correct response to something that had come from entirely outside his own intentions.

Holding his breath, Subaru waited for what would come next. In front of him, Satella, her expression still unseeable, kept it turned toward him. And to Subaru — holding his breath, waiting once more for her words — she took her time, slowly, and at last she said,

"—————..."

"—————"

"I've loved you, all this time. Only you, all this time — I love you."
