Arc 3 Chapter 5: "When the Promise Is Kept"

Natsuki Subaru's heartbeat was now closing in on the danger zone.

His pulse raced fast enough to feel almost like pain, and there was no reining in its volume. If he wasn't careful, he had the sneaking suspicion it was thudding out loud enough to rise above even this noisy crowd.

The great avenue lined with open-air stalls was as bustling as ever, the tide of passersby just as vigorous as before. All manner of peoples mingled together, and with the wild variety of hair colors, the whole scene was awash in a rather gaudy splash of color.

Hair that would have vanished into the crowd back in his old world, without a hint of individuality — black hair, apparently rare in this world — was nowhere to be found no matter how far he scanned. The most common eye-catching colors were, as ever, gold, followed by white and blue, with green and pink cropping up here and there in far smaller numbers. And among them all, no glint of silver, either.

In short, Subaru currently stood out tremendously even amid the crowd. A pair with the rare colors of black and silver hair, no less — and on top of that, this pair was—

"Um, so, Emilia-tan... this is kind of complicated, but can we not do this, maybe?"

Subaru offered the suggestion with a placating smile and a cold sweat, glancing sidelong at "this" — the two hands clasped firmly together that he held up to indicate.

The two of them were walking through the crowd hand in hand. To any onlooker, they'd look like a doting couple, and under normal circumstances Subaru himself would be flaring his nostrils at the sheer good fortune of it. But—

"Absolutely not! Knowing you, the second someone looks away you'll go and do something ridiculous. While we're in the royal capital, acting alone is strictly forbidden. Understand?"

"I said I was sorry about the dragon carriage thing! I'm super sorry! Sorrier than the sea is salty, more dramatically than a mountain is tall! But but, at this rate my heart's gonna give out at warp speed!"

Shot down by a gaze that held zero trust in him, Subaru pleaded, swinging their joined hands up and down. The mere fact that he was holding hands with her right now was enough to make his heart feel ready to explode. Every joint in his body ached with tension, and he was seized with anxiety over whether his palm was drenched in sweat.

—Right after the "Subaru Attempted Mid-Ride Disembarkation Incident" aboard the dragon carriage, Subaru had lost consciousness, only to wake up cradled by the soft sensation of a lap beneath him.

He'd spat, metaphorically, at the owner of that lap directly above him — Roswaal's unwelcome kindness benefiting no one — and then pieced together what had happened. Rem had reeled in the falling Subaru with her Morningstar (he marveled at her precise skill, snagging him mid-flight from a speeding dragon carriage no less, and lavished her with the praise she wordlessly demanded — enough to cover both the rescue and the life she'd saved). Roswaal had then nursed the recovered Subaru back to health, during which time Emilia's mood had plummeted. And upon waking, Subaru had been treated to a very, very long lecture from Emilia that began with "Sit. Seiza position."

And above all else—

"Once you're dropped from a Divine Protection's coverage, apparently you stay uncovered after that too — that was the annoying part. An hour and a half of that speed and shaking... everything in my stomach came out."

Everything from the secret mayo-chugging session he'd snuck off to the kitchen for before departure had come right back out. After that, he'd experienced a ride so rough that most of Emilia's lecture failed to register at all, and as a result he'd come to fully, thoroughly appreciate the consequences of his own actions.

Having thus collapsed into something close to a groggy stupor, Subaru — who could respond to anything said to him with nothing but "yes for-in-rabu" — had a promise imposed on him by the dragon carriage's other passengers. That promise was now manifesting as his current state.

"I get it, I get it, this whole mess started 'cause I was careless, so I'm fine with a chaperone... but c'mon, can't we at least skip the hand-holding part?"

"Hmm, is that so. Funny, when you were saying 'let's go on a da-a-ate' back in the village, you sure seemed eager to hold hands then."

Emilia, apparently entertained by watching Subaru grovel, teased him in a tone that seemed to relish his reaction, dredging up that scene from a few days prior. Subaru let out a groan of "uguu—"

"That's different! Back then my inner manly-man power was, like, ringing off the hook! But right now the gauge is running on fumes, I'm weak, it's a no-go!"

He covered his face with his free hand, gritting his teeth at his own weakness for being unable to simply accept this situation as a gift served on a platter. But it was also true that he took the need for self-reflection seriously. As a result, his inner state was an absolute mess, an eyesore even to himself.

"More importantly..."

Emilia easily brushed past Subaru's inner turmoil and scanned the crowd. As she did, she used her free hand to pull her hood down deeper, careful not to forget to distance herself from the notice of those around her. It was a slightly nervous gesture, but perhaps not excessive caution — after all, she'd already had her insignia targeted here once before. With the location the same, it only made sense that her wariness would sharpen.

"Do you think you can remember where that shop was that you mentioned?"

"Now that we've gotten this far, more or less. I think... it was a right turn up ahead, probably."

Diverting his attention from the hand still clasped in his, Subaru focused all his effort on staving off the crisis of sweaty palms. He nodded along to Emilia's words and scanned the crowd, shaking loose his memories. Honestly, given how the scenery along this street repeated itself endlessly, he had nothing close to certainty. Still, this was a path he'd walked four times before. As he began to recognize certain landmarks along the street, the pace at which he led her by the hand grew steadier, his hesitation fading.

Following his memory, as the familiar shopfront drew nearer, the hazy, groping quality of Subaru's recollection sharpened into clarity. The weathered shop's construction was simple, lacking any flashy appeal, the sort of place that would seem to sink into the crowd at a glance. But a garishly colored sign — apparently repainted quite recently — asserted its presence, and as a result stood out from the din of the street.

The characters painted on that garish sign — which had once looked to Subaru like nothing more than a child's scribbling — now that he could read the I-script, he could see spelled out "Kadomon." A person's name, probably, borrowed for the shop's title. In any case—

"Made it there safe."

"Here?"

"Yep. Man, this is hitting me way harder than I expected."

Arriving in front of the shop, Subaru nodded in response to Emilia, who lowered her voice beside him. The proprietor, who had been working inside, noticed the two of them huddled together out front. He turned, and as his gaze fell on them — Subaru could feel Emilia's hand tighten slightly around his own at the sight of that man's face.

Subaru gave a wry smile at Emilia's easy-to-read reaction. Admittedly, at first glance the man did have a rather striking appearance. It was only natural she'd tense up. After all, running down the left side of the middle-aged man's face was a scar that cut a vertical line from forehead to chin, and combined with his powerfully built, hulking frame, he looked anything but like someone in a respectable line of work.

"Well, still, I happen to know that underneath all that gruff talk, he's actually the type who can't help but look after other people — a real tsundere scarface character."

Subaru had crossed paths with him four times now, once each time he'd looped back. He'd been treated rather coldly on occasion, but Subaru knew from firsthand experience that the man's character was, contrary to his looks, a genuinely good one.

Thinking back on it, this man might well be the very starting point of Subaru's time in this other world. He was, after all, the very first person Subaru had interacted with here — and who could blame Subaru for the wave of emotion that welled up unbidden in his chest at the thought of him?

"Welcome!"

Apparently taking the pair standing there as customers, the shopkeeper turned his voice — and his rough-edged grin — toward them.

Their eyes met. For a moment, all sound seemed cut off from the world, and Subaru's skin registered the slow passage of time. It felt as though the space around them had been carved away from everything else, as if he and the shopkeeper were the only two people left in the world.

And then—

"Do you... remember me?"

The question, released from trembling lips, carried within it a tangle of anxiety and hope.

The shopkeeper blinked at Subaru's words, narrowing his eyes as if searching his memory, staring intently. Peering into those deep, dark brown eyes, feeling almost drawn in, Subaru waited in silence for his answer.

Beside the tense Subaru, Emilia — still holding his hand — could feel that same tension transmit through him, and she too held her breath, watching to see how the exchange would unfold.

And then, the shopkeeper slowly parted his lips, and let the words ride out on his voice—

"Nah, don't know you. Who're you, kid?"

Subaru had been forgotten, just like that.

The reason Subaru had brought Emilia along on a walk through the royal capital was, simply put, to keep a promise he'd made.

When they'd first arrived in the capital and he'd announced his intention to go out for that purpose, the entire party had tried to stop him — but Subaru had refused to budge, and eventually they'd relented.

Having secured permission through all that, Subaru had been ready to dash out in high spirits, but a promise made aboard the dragon carriage (one of which Subaru, thanks to his nausea, had zero memory) shackled him, condemning him to a ban on solo outings. As a result, he found himself dragging Emilia along — or rather, being dragged by Emilia — tormented by both the awkwardness of the situation and a heart threatening to explode from tension, as the promise-fulfillment event proceeded.

And after all that accumulated stress, faced with an unexpectedly snapped flag for a touching reunion, Subaru now stood with his arms crossed, thoroughly sulking.

"I was standing here all dutifully intent on keeping my promise, and this is the treatment I get?! I demand — nay, I formally demand improved service!"

"Don't gripe at me about it, kid."

At the sight of Subaru's cheeks puffed out in indignation, the shopkeeper sighed, wearing a weary, wry smile. He eyed Subaru with something like exasperation.

"Two whole weeks ago, and just some passing chat, wasn't it? Feels like maybe something like that happened, vaguely..."

The shopkeeper traced the scar on his face with a finger, closing his eyes as if searching his memory. The very fact that he was making the effort to remember spoke to his good nature — though Subaru, still reeling somewhat from the shock, failed to register the kindness of it.

In place of the self-absorbed Subaru, it was Emilia beside him who spoke up, telling the shopkeeper, "Please, don't strain yourself."

As the shopkeeper opened his eyes, Emilia straightened her posture, her fine brows knitting as she lowered her voice.

"I'm sorry my boy is being so unreasonable. I never imagined it was such a one-sided verbal promise..."

"Hey, hey, Emilia-tan, that's just an excuse. A promise made man-to-man is a promise made man-to-man. No matter the conditions it was made under, a man's gotta do everything in his power to keep it, or else what kind of man is he... yeah, that's the Stalingrad of manly resolve!"

Seizing the moment while his opponent was busy being contrite, Subaru pressed his advantage. But Emilia shot him a sharp glare that silenced him at once, then, pursing her lips in irritation, said,

"Subaru, don't go making unreasonable demands either. Do you have any idea how many people a shopkeeper deals with in a day? This is the royal capital, not some little shop in the village near the mansion."

"Hey now, don't sell yourself short, old man. There's no way a shop run by someone with a face this scary wouldn't be thriving... ow, ow, sorry, sorry!"

Emilia's punishment finally triggered in response to his rude comment. As his ear was pinched, Subaru immediately declared his surrender, and the shopkeeper let out a breath, looking almost impressed.

"What a rude brat you are, kid... oh, wait, I remember now!"

He clapped his hands as if something had clicked into place, then pointed at Subaru.

"The broke kid. Left without buying anything, the ungrateful little punk."

"And that's when I promised I'd buy Appa next time I came, right? Right? Right?"

Delighted to finally be remembered, Subaru leapt up, and taking hold of the hand pinching his ear, clasped both of Emilia's hands and began dancing right there on the spot. As he pressed forward, escorting a flustered Emilia thrown off by the unfamiliar steps, the shopkeeper bared his teeth in a grin and said, "Right, right, that's the one,"

"Well, ain't that a dutiful thing to do. So you came all the way here just to buy it, huh. Alright, since I appreciate the gesture, I'll throw in a discount. Normally it's two copper coins per Appa, but how about twenty Appa for thirty-eight copper coins!"

"Whoa, what a deal! So if I bought two hundred, that'd be three hundred eighty copper coins? That's amazing, Emilia-tan!"

"That works out to paying for twenty extra, though. Also, don't make me dance in public, it's embarrassing."

Subaru played right along with the shopkeeper's sales pitch. Emilia calmly shot it down and released Subaru's hand, cutting the dance short. Though, true to her conscientious nature, she immediately reconsidered and took his hand again right after.

"Well, that aside, I'll go ahead and buy some Appa. I've been looking forward to it... though I've already jumped the gun and eaten some a few times already!"

After all, every previous loop, he'd wound up face to face with Appa at the very first breakfast table. There'd been no avoiding it. For what it's worth, Appa — which looked exactly like an apple — tasted more or less like one too. The difference was little more than the name.

Setting aside Subaru's private musings, the shopkeeper hauled out a wooden crate full of Appa and spread open a bag, ready to go. He'd apparently shelved any sentimentality and shifted fully into the mindset of a merchant simply hoping to make a sale. That was a little lonely in its own way, but—

"So, how many are you actually buying?"

"Let's go big — ten. Gotta pay for exceeding my promise, right?"

The shopkeeper laughed, "Every time," at Subaru's generosity and began bagging the red fruit.

Meanwhile, Subaru figured he ought to prepare payment too. Suppressing his reluctance (he'd grown rather used to the hand-holding, honestly) to let go, he turned to have Emilia release his hand — and noticed she was fumbling about in her clothes.

"What are you trying to pull your wallet out for, Emilia-tan?"

Emilia, having pulled a leather pouch from inside her clothing and checking its contents, tilted her head curiously at his words.

"What do you mean, why... I have to pay for it somehow, don't I?"

"No, I mean, isn't it weird for you to be the one paying? It's my purchase, so obviously I should— hey, scarface, what's with that look?"

In the middle of protesting Emilia's inexplicable attempt to pay in his stead, Subaru noticed the shopkeeper eyeing him with a flat, unimpressed gaze.

The man prefaced his words with an "eh," scratching his head, then shook his head slowly.

"I did say come back and buy something once you had the money, sure... but bringin' a girl with money to pay for you, that ain't somethin' I can respect, kid."

"You saw that whole lovers' quarrel just now?! I'm literally arguing that I should be the one paying! I am!"

"Yeah, but you weren't exactly reaching for your wallet either. Looked to me like you were just puttin' on a show of offerin' to pay in front of the lady."

"That's a hostile interpretation of me just being slow on the draw! I'm not some sweets-brained gold digger who thinks getting treated is just my due, y'know!"

Shouting what was, in its own way, an equally biased opinion, Subaru hastily pulled out his own wallet.

His wages — the actual payment for which was still a bit further off — were pocket money that had been advanced to him by Roswaal's own hand partway through the journey to the royal capital. It held a fairly substantial sum, enough to make Subaru shudder at just how well a noble household paid its servants.

Though in truth, that sum also included a reward for Subaru's role in resolving the incident with the Witchbeast in the forest, so for Roswaal it was merely a partial payment of thanks. In a world without any banking or savings system, there was no way to confirm the numbers, but in truth, Subaru had quietly become something of a minor man of means. Though it was a fact he himself had not been informed of.

"So if one Appa's two copper coins... ten of them would be, like, two silver coins? Good deal?"

"Hey now, don't you know the current exchange rate? Silver's worth nine copper right now."

"So that means two silver and two copper. Here."

Subaru briskly pulled coins out of his leather pouch and handed them to the shopkeeper. In response, the man fell into a sullen silence, and Subaru, puzzled by the reaction, tilted his head.

"What's up?"

"Not that it's my place to say, but, kid — you oughta be a little more suspicious of people. The exchange rate for currency's posted right there on the sign at the market entrance. Wanderin' in without even checkin' that... a shady merchant'd eat you alive."

The shopkeeper's warning sounded less like scolding an honest fool and more like watching something precarious. Hearing it, Subaru nodded in understanding with an "ah." Sure enough, he'd been about to trust the numbers and hand over payment without a second thought. Was he judging things too much by the standards of his old world, where trust in commerce was a given? In the village near the mansion, the community was so tightly knit the very idea of cheating someone probably never even crossed anyone's mind — but here was the royal capital, for better or worse, the largest city in the country. It would hardly be strange if people with ill intent were about. Which meant—

"Old man, you really are a super nice guy, huh."

Grinning easily, Subaru expressed his goodwill toward the scarfaced gentleman's character.

"Cut it out," the man said, waving a hand roughly.

"Just happened this time. A customer who came all the way to keep some half-remembered promise, gettin' robbed blind and left in a ditch somewhere right after shoppin' at my place — that'd give me nightmares. Got it?"

"Ah, the classic unappreciated tsundere man. I get it, I get it."

"Just take this and get outta here already! Payment's exact, thanks for your business!"

Rough at the start, and full "the customer is god" spirit by the end. Chuckling with delight at the two extremes, Subaru accepted the handed-over bag of Appa, took Emilia's hand, and stepped away from the shop front.

They'd overstayed as it was. Any longer and he'd be interfering with the man's business.

"Thanks, old man. If fate has it, we'll meet again."

"Welcome back anytime you're buyin'. Take care of yourself out there, y'hear."

A perfunctory wave shooed them off.

As the distance grew, the crowd gradually swallowed up the view, and soon the good-natured shopkeeper was out of sight.

Once he confirmed that, Subaru readjusted his grip on the bag and turned, suddenly noticing Emilia had gone quiet for a while.

"Huh, Emilia-tan, what's up?"

"..........."

"Are you mad I didn't let you pay? No, wait, that's not really something you'd need to pay for anyway. That'd be way too much like being some kept man... hold on. But wouldn't it be kind of nice if we each bought each other presents with our own money? Dang it! I should've let Emilia-tan buy the Appa and bought her something fancy myself!"

His head filled with nothing but ring-and-bouquet ideas straight out of some dating sim he'd played too much of, he clutched his head. Emilia gave a small shake of her head at him, saying "No, that's not it,"

"Subaru's pretty quick with numbers, isn't he."

"Huh?"

"That scary-faced— ah, sorry. When the shopkeeper was talking about the money, you calculated it right away, didn't you? I understood it too, but you were really fast."

"The fact that your true feelings slipped out at the start of that sentence really shows how deep your fluster runs, huh, Emilia-tan. But huh... surprised I'm quick at mental math?"

It probably wasn't that she thought his head was slow, unexpectedly. This, too, was purely a matter of the difference in educational standards between modern-day Japan and this other world. Even back in his old world, he'd heard people say the Japanese were quick at mental arithmetic — chalk it up to a difference in upbringing.

Understanding as much, Subaru nodded repeatedly as if satisfied.

"Heheheh, seems even my sheer intellectual prowess has your heart doing a little explosive throb, huh. Guess no matter the era, a man's gotta have both brains and guts. Well, well — looks like my very own isekai cheat skill has arrived!"

Despite understanding the situation perfectly well, Natsuki Subaru lacked the humility not to get carried away. Boasting proudly of an achievement that was in no way entirely his own, Subaru's triumphant charge through this other world was just now beginning—

"The mathematical isekai fantasy where I do battle with an abacus, opening curtain — hearing that the currency exchange rate changes all the time though, man that's a total drag! My spirit is broken!"

And promptly cancelled. In a world without abacuses, he had no knowledge of how to build one himself, nor the communication skills to popularize it even if he did — and besides, if anything, Subaru leaned more toward the humanities anyway.

"I haven't even begun to climb yet. This long, long slope of the abacus, I—!"

"...Subaru, I really can't tell if you're amazing or not amazing at all."

At Subaru's grand tale that opened and closed its curtain in the same breath, Emilia pressed a hand to her forehead with a weary sigh.

Then, as if shaking herself back into focus, she gave a small shake of her head and said, "Well then," by way of preface,

"We've kept the promise like you said, so, next is..."

"Ah, right, the Loot House."

Recalling the slums — the events of his very first day summoned to this other world — Subaru turned his gaze that direction, giving her hand a squeeze to draw her attention back.

"Still, is it really okay to drag you along that far, Emilia-tan? I'll be honest, the security's bad enough there that thugs strut around like they own the place. For someone in the Royal Selection, shouldn't we be, like, super careful..."

At Subaru's belated-sounding question, Emilia gave a wry smile, then lightly pinched the edge of the white hood covering her silver hair.

"This has an anti-perception effect, so most people can't tell who I am. It's not for nothing that it's handmade by the Chief Court Mage, you know. It's actually pretty impressive, this thing."

"Made by Roz, huh... somehow hearing that makes it feel a lot less impressive all of a sudden."

"Hey now, you shouldn't say things like that. ...Is what I'd like to say, but."

Unable to fully hide that she privately agreed, Emilia stuck out her tongue, looking a little embarrassed.

Caught off guard by the sudden burst of cuteness, Subaru staggered, feeling his heart give one massive, explosive throb.

"Ahh, geez, E-M-I—"

"Yes, yes, there you go saying something incomprehensible again. I know you're not happy it's me tagging along instead of someone you'd rather be with, but."

Brushing off Subaru's praise, Emilia said this with a resigned laugh.

Seeing that faint, weary smile, Subaru shook his head. "Hey, hey,"

"There you go again, saying stuff like that. I've told you like a million times, I wanna go on a date with you, Emilia-tan. Come on, get it already."

"I think it just won't work out as long as you keep saying something that important so lightly. For someone who really matters, you should choose a proper attitude and proper words. That's a promise, okay?"

Having judged his intentions to be nothing but empty flourish, Emilia swiftly cut the conversation short.

And once she'd built that wall so completely, Subaru had no way to push further. After all, to show his feelings any more plainly than that would leave him no option but to throw his heart's honest truth straight at her, unfiltered.

"There's no way I can pull off something that high-level, no strategy, no prep, just riding on momentum..."

To begin with, not fighting battles he couldn't win was the Natsuki Subaru way.

Making that excuse to himself, Subaru quietly retreated from facing Emilia head-on in that moment. Faced with a wall, neither going around nor breaking through — just retreat. That, as it stood, was Subaru's attitude toward the wall currently lying between him and Emilia.

"Well then, off to the Loot House... oh?"

Clearing his throat as if to shake off the mood, Subaru, having reset his sights on their destination, twisted his mouth. Seeing his reaction, Emilia's eyes asked, "What?" and Subaru said,

"No, I was just counting the Appa in the bag... there's eleven."

The count of the plump, deep-red ripened fruit came to eleven in total. The shopkeeper himself had packed the bag, and a miscount seemed unlikely from a professional merchant. Which meant—

"Yeah, that old man really is way too nice a guy."

Picturing the rough-looking scarfaced man, Subaru laughed as he straightened the tilted angle of the bag.

The faint unease that had just begun to take root in him seemed to clear away, just like that.

—Yeah. Keeping a promise really was, without question, the right choice.
