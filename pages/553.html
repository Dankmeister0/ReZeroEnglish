Arc 7 Chapter 55: "Magic City Rhapsody"

—At the heart of the Magic City, Chaosflame: the collapse of the Crimson Lazuli Castle, and the flood of shadow that followed.

That was the true source of the roar that shook the city, the nightmarish sight witnessed by all who were present.
Crimson lazuli — the beautiful gemstone within which red and blue intermingled as though dancing — had been piled high to build a resplendent castle, and now that castle was being crushed as easily as one built of sand.
To those who knew the Magic City, to its people, it was a shock equal to the sky itself falling.
Something that should never happen was happening, to the point where the line between dream and reality blurred — and this held true even for those less familiar with the Magic City.

「――――」

The tremor spread even to a room in the inn set aside in the Magic City for outside visitors.
Some froze, eyes wide with shock; some stiffened their whole bodies in wary alertness; some screamed and fell backward onto the floor; some sought to steady themselves by huddling close to their unease.
But however their reactions differed, what had struck them all was, equally, a "threat."
And even as they were seized by it, bound up in instinctive fear, the irreversible situation—

"—Everyone, be still!!"

In that instant, what tore through the frozen stillness gripped by threat was a sharp voice — two voices, overlapping.

Two voices rang out in the room — identical in quality, striking the eardrums and rousing even the brains on the verge of shutting down beyond them.
Before those who jerked their faces up as if struck, the ones who had spoken were both black-haired figures.
One bare-faced, one masked with a demon mask — the two men each extended an arm toward the overwhelming scene of collapse visible outside the window.

"Don't freeze! If we're slow to deal with that, every one of us will lose our lives."
"It has swallowed the castle whole and still means to devour the very sky — a great glutton. If we do not act now, the casualties will mount in the blink of an eye!"

「――――」

"—Kafma Irulux!"

As though following the same line of thought, the two men's gazes converged on a single figure.
The bronze-skinned man who found himself under their gaze — Kafma Irulux — widened his eyes, thrown into confusion over which of the two he ought to face.
But that confusion did not last long. Because—

"Stop that shadow in its tracks! Know that every moment you hesitate is paid for in the lives of the Empire's people!"

"—Yes, sir!"

The instant he was given a clear objective, an action to take, the hesitation in Kafma's eyes cleared.
Kafma set his fist to his chest and bowed,

"I take my leave of your side. May the Divine Protection of the Sun Sword be with you!"

"Your service will serve as my Divine Protection. Give it everything you have."

"As you command—!"

Answering with vigor, Kafma turned straight for the window.
Still carrying that momentum, he leaped, slashing the window open with his outstretched arm and bursting outside in a scatter of glass shards. An instant later, his back swelled, and from beneath the cloak he shed, six transparent wings spread wide.
Beating wings reminiscent of a flying insect's, Kafma's form cut through the sky above the Magic City.
His course was straight for the Crimson Lazuli Castle, even now being brought down by the overflowing shadow.

"How many did you bring."

Watching Kafma's flight out of the corner of his eye, the masked man — Abel — voiced a sharp question. Its target was the striking black-haired man who had received Kafma's bow: Vincent.
The man who held the throne of the Vollachia Empire narrowed his eyes slightly at Abel's question,

"My retinue is as you see it. Kafma Irulux, and Olbart Dunkelkenn, who is absent. The other one present would be of little use even were he here."

"No hidden pieces in reserve, then. Your light traveling has come back to bite you."

"To lay that blame at my feet would be more than a little unreasonable."

That was the thoughtless remark Abel let slip at Vincent, who had revealed his hand without hesitation. Vincent voiced his displeasure at it, but Abel paid it no mind.
Abel put a hand to his chin, tracing a finger along the lower edge of his demon mask, and thought for a few seconds before glancing at the others in the room — those who could not hide their shock at the threat.

The ones who could respond to that wordless gaze were two girls, both young in appearance.
One was Medium O'Connell, golden-haired and blue-eyed; the other was Tanza, the Deer Human girl in a kimono whose round eyes trembled — neither had a decisive card to play in this situation.
The only one who might possibly serve as some sort of card to play was—

"A-ah... why, why, why now, of all times..."

"Al-chin..."

There was a frightened voice, utterly unaware of Abel's quiet gaze.
Clutching his head with his one remaining arm, tormented by the dilemma of fearing the sight outside the window yet being unable to tear his eyes from it, was the black-haired youth whose face was hidden beneath haphazardly wound rags.
Among those Abel had brought along, this boy was, in a sense, the one who had shown the least of his true depths — but with fear this unfeigned, incorporating him into any plan felt like a stretch.
Not out of any consideration for his feelings. — Purely a judgment that he would be of no use.

"Then there cannot be many courses left to take."

Vincent's words, spoken from beside him, struck at the profile of the pondering Abel.
Standing side by side, the two men gazed out the window together — or perhaps it would be more accurate to call them two emperors.
Dismissing the sentiment that welled up for an instant as beneath notice, Abel let out a breath.

As Vincent said, there were not many options open to them.
And, as if answering Vincent, who had surely reached the same conclusion, Abel spoke a single line—

"—We rendezvous with Yorna Mishigure. We need her strength."

Sensing the shift in the situation, Taritta wet her parched lips with her tongue.

The change in the air, the scent of her pursuers, the felt sense of the killing intent and hostility directed at her — all of it spoke of something different from a moment before, and for just an instant her thoughts raced to make sense of it.
Whether it was for good or ill, which one it could possibly be.
But—

"—Either waY, it makes no differencE."

If the outcome would be the same either way, Taritta wouldn't waste time worrying over it.
She already knew her own head wasn't built for thinking things through. If it were her sister Mizelda, even with a mind cut from the same cloth, instinct alone would probably land on the right answer.
But Taritta didn't have her sister's nose for it. Probably no talent for it either. Lacking, lacking, all the way down.
Even if she took the time to worry, she'd just end up going back and forth forever over whether she'd chosen right.
So she'd decided not to worry. Once the results were in, she could agonize over the rights and wrongs of it then.
And so, no matter how the air around her pursuers changed, Taritta didn't change her approach.

"Gh, gk...!"

"It won't come offF. No matter how much strengtH you havE."

She choked the man whose airway her jacket had sealed off, his eyes bloodshot as he thrashed.
The ram-man with the burning right eye struggled desperately to break free, but Taritta, strangling his throat with the sleeve of the jacket she'd stripped off, had already severed the tendons in his arms to keep him from acting.
Frankly, the sheer toughness of these pursuers — getting back up even after a blow to a vital point — had given her real trouble.

"Once I knew how to deal with iT, even I could manage somehoW."

「――――」

Before long, the man's gasping breath fell silent, and the strength drained from his body all at once. Taking that as her cue, Taritta unwound her coat from around his neck and dragged his collapsed body to the side of the road.
Behind Taritta, who let out a breath at that, the bodies of the pursuers she had choked unconscious lay scattered in a row along the walls of the street.

Once she'd learned that strikes and knife-work weren't effective, and that chokeholds were, she'd stuck to that method alone for driving them off.
Fortunately, though her pursuers possessed abnormal strength and stamina, they seemed to be amateurs when it came to the technique of wielding that power efficiently.
Thanks to that, Taritta's technique worked on them, and she was able to keep neutralizing her pursuers by relatively gentle means.

— If she'd been allowed to let them die, things would have gone a good deal faster.

"—Try not to kill them."

That single line had crossed her mind, and it was what had forced Taritta into such a difficult fight.
It was what Abel had told her right before they parted ways, when she became the decoy to let the others escape the inn.
Given the situation she'd been placed in, it might have been a moment to protest that he was asking the impossible. But would a man with eyes as sharp as Abel's really give an order he knew couldn't be carried out?
Or had he given that instruction believing that Taritta, specifically, could manage it?
If so, that would mean Abel had seen through to the true extent of Taritta's skill.

「――――」

Just how far did Abel's sight actually reach?
Of course, the secret art that had struck Subaru, Medium, and Al made it clear that Abel wasn't someone who moved with perfect foresight of every possible event.
If someone could truly do that, it would no longer be the work of a human being — it would be the work of something beyond the ordinary.
She didn't believe any human could trespass into that domain of reason no one was meant to enter.
And yet, she couldn't help but think that a person might come close to its threshold.

As a member of the Shudrak, Taritta possessed the skill to bring down any prey.
And the fact that she — Taritta — had mastered a chokehold technique that should never work on beasts, and even the reason for it... perhaps Abel could know that too.

"—Well, well. Now theeere's a sight to behold."

「――ッ」

A voice slipped into the gap in Taritta's thoughts as she stood lost in contemplation.
Wrenched abruptly back to reality, she spun around with the dagger in her hand raised — meaning to strike down whoever had closed in behind her without a trace of presence—

"Wah wah wah, wait wait! Let's wait a moment! That's a liiittle hasty, don't you think?"

"...Who are You?"

"...Ah, are you all right? Calmed down? Sooo, is it safe for me to lower my arms now?"

Taritta stopped her knife hand an instant before crippling the man's limbs.
Before her eyes, awkwardly shielding his head with his arms, was a gentle-looking, gray-haired man wrapped in a hooded blue robe.
He had refined features, but his eyes lacked the flame common to her pursuers, and above all, the amateurish way he carried himself didn't register as dangerous.
Still, the fact remained that he had slipped past her vigilance to stand behind her.

「――――」

"Oh dear, seems I've earned myself a fair bit of suspicion. I'm just an ordinary passerby who happened to be walking through, really..."

"...An ordinarY person wouldn't be so calM, seeing thiS."

The self-proclaimed harmless man had just given her one more reason not to lower her guard.
He looked upon the row of pursuers' bodies lined against the wall — corpses, or at least things one could be forgiven for assuming dead without checking up close — and remained utterly unmoved.
Unless his heart was dead, the only explanation she could think of was that he was accustomed to such scenes.

At Taritta's wary gaze, the gentle-looking man offered something like an embarrassed smile.
For a moment she nearly let herself be distracted by the handsome man's smile, but her wariness of something dangerous won out over any reaction to good looks. One gentle-looking pretty boy was already plenty — Flop was enough.
— Though now was hardly the time to be thinking about him.

"No, no, this is a bit much. I won't claim it's a sight I'm used to back home, mind you. Quite the spectacle, just as I said. — Isn't that right, Taritta-san?"

「――ッ」

"Ah, don't gouge, don't gouge! I may be suspicious, but I'm not your enemy!"

The wariness that had, for just a moment, begun to ease snapped taut all at once.
The gentle-looking man before her had spoken her name as casually as if they were old acquaintances. Of course, Taritta had no recollection of ever meeting him.
There was no one here who knew her well enough to call her by name; it wasn't information he should have had access to.
The one possibility was that Abel and the others, after parting from her, had given him the information—

"Have You met my familY?"

"—? Your family is here? Hmm, I'd heard the Shudrak rarely leave their forest. A change in the chief's policy, perhaps?"

"How do you know I'm ShudraK...!"

"Oh dear, the more I say, the more I give away, it seems. I always do this."

The gentle-looking man wore a wry smile, his manner growing ever more familiar toward Taritta — but in stark contrast, Taritta's wariness of him, and her revulsion at his eeriness, only deepened.
He showed nothing resembling hostility.
But the mounting eeriness of him slowly, slowly planted in Taritta the dangerous impression that he ought to be eliminated here and now.
And Taritta had a good idea of just how that planted seed of suspicion would sprout, what kind of bud it would put out, and what flower it would open into.
Because—

"—The Shudrak's stain."

「――ぁ」

At the words that suddenly spilled from the gentle-looking man's lips, Taritta's mind went white.

「――――」

Why had this man spoken those words? — No, how did he know them?
After all, the man who had once spoken those very words to Taritta was already—

"Heaven's Mandate. That's what it's called, Taritta-san who became the Shudrak's stain."

"Heaven's... Mandate...?"

"You could call it the turning of the stars, or their guidance. Every person has their own fate, you might say. Reading it happens to be my line of work."

Telling this to the dumbfounded Taritta, the gentle-looking man shook his head. "No, well—"
Then he laid a hand on his own chest, and pierced Taritta with a straight, level gaze,

"Or perhaps I should say — our shared Heaven's Mandate."

「――――」

At the plural — "ours" — Taritta's throat clicked as she caught her breath.
The sound was loud enough to carry the illusion that the whole world itself had trembled. — No, the breath she'd drawn hadn't shaken the world.
And yet, as a matter of fact, the world was trembling.
That was—

"Oh my, my."

A leisurely breath from the gentle-looking man, one that gave off an almost carefree impression.
But what was happening was a calamity of such magnitude that his manner utterly failed to capture it.

— Far off, at the center of the Magic City, away from Taritta and the man, the beautiful Crimson Lazuli Castle that had towered there was collapsing with tremendous force, its red and blue light being swallowed beneath a black darkness.

Not as a figure of speech — a literal black darkness was engulfing the castle.
As if something filthy were painting over something beautiful, something cursed smothering something radiant.

"Oh dear, what in the world... This might be a spot I haven't read, after all."

"Who are You—"

The gentle-looking man shaded his eyes with a hand, watching the collapsing castle and the darkness spilling out of it.
His very calm made her suspect he might even be the cause of that abnormal situation. But as if sensing Taritta's suspicion, he shrugged his shoulders,

"Bit awkward timing, right after talking about fate and all that, but I've got nothing to do with that. That's a Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica problem, if anything. Not my department."

"Not your department... the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica...?"

"I wonder — does that girl not even realize she's a 'Stargazer'? If so, His Excellency is placing quite the bold bet... though I suppose it can't be helped, given the hand he's holding."

"Stop spoutinG nonsensE...! If you know somethinG—!"

Meaning to intimidate an answer out of him, Taritta closed the distance and seized the gentle-looking man's throat. She pressed his slender body against the wall and laid the blade in her hand against his pale neck.
If Taritta twisted her wrist, blood would come gushing from his throat in an instant.
The cold touch of the blade should have been more than enough to let the man sense that outcome.

"I don't imagine whether I live or die has much bearing on how this all gets resolved, though..."

「――っ」

"And I think you're the one who can't afford to waste time here. Mind you, I haven't read the stars on this — it's just a hunch."

And yet the gentle-looking man remained calm, ignoring the feel of the knife entirely as he met Taritta's eyes.
It was the moment her displeasure with him peaked more than ever before. With no clear reason she could put into words, an impulse to simply slit his throat right then sparked inside her.
But an instant before she gave herself over to that violent impulse—

"—Killing me won't make things turn out any better. Wasn't that true last time too?"

— That single line was what kept Taritta from swinging the knife she held.

"Whoa, whoa!"

"Get ouT of my sighT! RighT now...!"

Gritting her teeth, Taritta hurled the gentle-looking man, still gripped by the collar, out into the street.
The man managed to keep from falling with his long legs, and rubbed at his roughly handled throat as he studied Taritta's face. "Are you sure that's wise?"

"It's rather like strangling your own chances, quite literally... but you might never get another opportunity to run into me like this again, you know."

"ThaT would suiT me finE. Seeing your facE, it's likE..."

「――――」

"Like being made to face a nightmare I've seen over and over agaiN...!"

With something like a curse in her heart, Taritta waved a hand as if to drive the gentle-looking man off.
At that gesture, the man sighed, gazing off at the distant, collapsing castle,

"Seems like, unlike me, you've got work to do. And that just might be the trump card for calming that shadow down."

"Me...? I don't have any powER like thaT..."

"—Your Heaven's Mandate has already come down. You know it, don't you — the Shudrak's stain."

「――――」

"Whether you fulfill it or not is entirely up to you. Though, speaking as someone with no mandate of his own to follow, I can't help envying you, having a road sign to follow."

Leaving her with those words, the gentle-looking man pulled his hood up over his head, turned his back on Taritta, and ran off.
It wasn't a fast pace, by any means. She could have caught up in an instant if she'd wanted to, but Taritta felt no urge to chase after him.
Part of it was that she truly, deeply wanted nothing more to do with him.
But the biggest reason of all was the words he'd left her with at the end—

"HeaveN's MandatE has already come dowN."

She wished she had no idea what he meant. But she did.
And that very thing was what had been tormenting Taritta, endlessly, these past several days.
She didn't want to agonize before acting.
She didn't want to go on carrying some unanswerable anguish, dithering back and forth, and yet—

"Taritta-chan!!"

"—! Medium?"

A voice familiar to her ears called out to Taritta, who stood hugging her own shoulders, head bowed.
When she spun around in surprise, she saw a girl running toward her, waving — Medium, her tall frame shrunk down, closing the distance.

"Thank goodness! I found you right away, and you're okay, Taritta-chan!"

"I'm gladD you're safe tooO. ...Are Abel and Subaru and the others all righT? AnD, what in the world is thaT..."

With Medium hurrying up in front of her, Taritta fired off questions one after another.
That she was alone, and that she'd been out looking for Taritta like this, were both unexpected. Of course, with the Crimson Lazuli Castle collapsing, it would already have been strange for the "hide-and-seek" with Olbart to still be going on — yet it seemed unlikely they'd had time to discuss calling it off.
To Taritta's questions, Medium answered, slightly out of breath,

"Abel-chin and Al-chin are okay! Subaru-chin and Rui-chan got separated! And we've gotta do something about that shadow that came out of the castle, so come with me, Taritta-chan!"

"Subaru and RuI got separateD? AnD, where am I supposed to gO..."

"We're gonna go stop that shadow together with Yorna-chan! Abel-chin said we need your strength too, Taritta-chan!"

「――――」

Taritta's eyes went wide; she couldn't help her astonishment at what she'd just been told.
At the edge of her vision, the black shadow was expanding its damage on a scale so outrageous that even Taritta, still shaken by her exchange with the gentle-looking man, was forced to acknowledge the reality of it.
Stopping it, challenging it — neither was an idea that had ever occurred to Taritta.
And on top of that, to be told now that Abel was calling for her—

"Taritta-chan, are you hurt!? Can you still go? I'll do my best too, so will you do your best with me!?"

Medium's unhesitating words swept away Taritta's confusion.
Even with her height shrunk down, the straightforward heart Medium carried hadn't shrunk or bent one bit. As though led by the hand of it, Taritta nodded quietly,

"UnderstooD. —Let's head there righT awaY."

"Okay! Thank you!"

At the relief in Medium's emphatic nod, Taritta felt a pang deep in her chest.
The anguish that had been tormenting Taritta on and on, at such length — the thing she could forget by losing herself in battle — now asserted its presence before the greatest battle yet, pressing her toward a moment of decision.

"—Heaven's Mandate."

Taritta murmured, under her breath alone, the words that had been spoken to the now-vanished man.
As if pleased to have been called by name, the anguish in the depths of her chest seemed to give a small leap.

— Time winds back to the very instant the Crimson Lazuli Castle collapsed beneath the great flood of darkness.

"Child—"
"Uwaaugh!!"

In the nick of time, she pulled back the girl called Rui, who had been about to leap forward without hesitation.
Then, following the instinctive sense of threat crying out through every nerve in her body, she leaped backward with a powerful kick-off, not even registering that the keep's roof tiles were exploding.

Shattered beneath her thick heels, the shock to the tiles spread to those around them, and the destruction widened.
But the roof tiles breaking like that was far too trivial a thing next to what happened immediately after.

「――――」

With a rush, the overflowing shadow, the darkness, the pitch black, swallowed the keep whole, and the shattered tiles vanished along with it.
— No, it wasn't only the keep that was swallowed.
Yorna's eyes clearly caught it — the Crimson Lazuli Castle's keep, its upper floors, its middle floors, swallowed by the black flooding like a muddy torrent, vanishing from the world entirely.

That was a dark, dark blackness that must never be allowed to swallow anything.
A true darkness that no light could illuminate, that offered no means of salvation once one was drawn in, that painted over every hope with despair until it could no longer be seen.

She sensed it instinctively. — That was a Thing that should not exist.

"Uu!"

Restraining the struggling girl in her embracing arms, Yorna ran her eyes over the keep as it turned black.
At the instant the black shadow had come pouring out, the black-haired boy who'd been at its center, and Olbart, who had been touching him — neither was anywhere to be seen.

In that split second, she'd seen the arm Olbart had reflexively pulled back vanish into nothing.
That Thing had been overwhelming enough that even Olbart — greedy for life, superb at sensing any danger — could not avoid sacrificing his own arm to it.

Since she had prioritized grabbing Rui and leaping clear immediately after, whether Olbart lived or died was unknown.
Part of her thought that strange old man surely couldn't have died, while a cooler voice within her asked whether she could really afford such optimism after seeing that pitch black.
And what twisted Yorna's thoughts even more than Olbart's fate was—

"Child...!"

The safety of the boy who seemed to have been at the center of the black shadow that had swallowed Olbart's arm.
By Yorna's estimation, that boy had no especially outstanding ability. He was skilled at reading situations and never mistimed playing his cards.
If anything, that was the factor that had drawn him victory in his confrontation with Olbart. But while that spoke well of the boy's resolve, it hardly amounted to an outstanding ability.
So she had to conclude that, in that instant, there was no possibility whatsoever that the boy had gotten away safely.
That was, of course, assuming the boy had been nothing but a victim in all this.

"If this be that child's doing, then..."

Would that mean the boy was hiding somewhere within that vast black darkness?
Yorna admitted to herself that some part of her didn't want to believe this had been the boy's intent. Even acknowledging that, there was a judgment she had to make.
That, precisely, was the duty she had to fulfill as ruler of the Magic City, the position she had to bear, the object of love she had to choose—

「――っ」

The instant she thought that, a change came over the darkness before her eyes.
The shadow, having swallowed half the Crimson Lazuli Castle and now moving to draw its lower floors and outer walls into darkness as well, writhed at the part that had consumed the keep, and reached out a "hand" toward Yorna and the others suspended in midair.

「――――」

What extended toward them was, quite literally, a "hand" — there was no other way to describe it.
A black hand reached for Yorna and the Rui she held. And not just one or two of them — ten, twenty at once, reaching out together, an overwhelming sight.

They had the appearance of long shadow-puppets stretched out by the sunlight, and yet there was nothing pastoral or gentle about them — because those arms, which should have had no expression at all, overflowed with emotion.
The vast mass of shadow, the black arms, cried out with a single emotion: "hunger."
Not simply "hunger" in the sense of an empty stomach.
It was a bottomless "hunger," one that craved to consume absolutely every Thing there was.

"Child, this be going to throw you about. Mind you don't bite your tongue!"

"Uh, uwah—!"

To evade the reaching shadow, Yorna, suspended in midair, resorted to an extreme measure.
With the shadow's true nature unknown, and having witnessed firsthand what it had done to Olbart, she deliberately set as her rule that touching it would be fatal — leaving no option but to evade it entirely.

So Yorna opened her eyes wide, drew to herself the shattered tiles sent flying by her earlier leap, and used them as footholds to carve an escape route through the sky of the Magic City.

With a light, brisk sound, Yorna's figure rose high into the air, using the flying tiles as stepping stones.
Meaning also to test the shadow, she hurled one tile at the reaching arm, but it failed even to pierce the darkness — the spot it touched simply vanished, swallowed, gone into the void.

Leaving her assumption that it must not be touched unchanged, Yorna traced her path of footholds higher into the sky.
If the reaching shadow-arms couldn't keep up with her string of tile-climbing, all the better. Or if she could gauge the shadow's reach, she could work out a countermeasure from there.
That said—

"Being made to run like this without striking back, though — that be plenty infuriating."

Not the being chased, no.
That was humiliating enough, of course, but the greatest loss by far was the Crimson Lazuli Castle.

Crimson lazuli was beautiful, and above all, an exceedingly rare gem.
To build a castle out of it was the kind of idea one could only call absurd, even to consider. And that the castle had been built at all was not owed to any great wealth or power of Yorna's own.
To begin with, it hadn't even been Yorna who thought to build the Crimson Lazuli Castle.

It was the people of the Magic City who had raised the resplendent castle of dancing red and blue.
Believing that the residence of Yorna, ruler of the Magic City, must never look shabby, they had talked amongst themselves, gathered the precious gems, and built up that beautiful castle.

It was a rare gem, difficult to work with — outsiders could scarcely imagine what hardship had gone into building that castle. Yorna was no different; she had never once imagined that hardship.
There had been no need to imagine it — Yorna had watched the castle go up from start to finish, every bit of it.
So—

"You've swallowed this one's love, have you, you brute——!!"

Yorna extended her slender, long fingers toward the shadow devouring the castle.
Nothing was actually released from them, nor was the gesture strictly necessary. She simply wanted to thrust it at the shadow, to make it understand that this was the shape of her fury.

— In that instant, the massive shadow that had swallowed the Crimson Lazuli Castle was blasted through from within by a ferocious shockwave.

「――――」

Whether the shadow had a physical body, let alone a will of its own, was beyond even Yorna's knowing.
But she felt, with certainty, the sensation of having landed a solid, damaging blow.

What the shadow had swallowed was the Crimson Lazuli Castle — down to each single stone forming its walls, every piece of it something into which the people had poured their love for Yorna.
How could she ever think of that as just a castle?
How could she ever help loving it?

The shockwave of the bursting castle tore fiercely at the black darkness's momentum from within.
As proof, every black arm that had been clinging after Yorna scattered into mist, and none remained that could catch her as she fled into the sky.
Even Rui, stirring in her arms, stared wide-eyed in surprise at the explosion of Yorna's love.
But at the girl's reaction — one might even call it admiration — Yorna could take no joy.

"'Tis but a castle, some might say — build it anew. And that be true enough."

"Uu?"

"Even should a new castle rise, only that one castle ever loved through those days... Is it a failing in this one's heart, to curse the love now crushed away?"

Looking down at the Crimson Lazuli Castle, more than half swallowed by the shadow and no longer holding its original shape, Yorna's chest was struck with an aching itch.
But Yorna had no time to steep herself in sentiment.
Even if the clinging shadow-arms had vanished, the source of that darkness had not disappeared.
Much less—

"This one's own pale skin tells her the threat of that thing has not faded one bit."

There was no doubt that the shock of the swallowed castle bursting had carried the force of an entire castle being hurled at it. And yet, even having taken that blow and lost some of its momentum, the sense of pressure radiating off the black darkness hadn't wavered in the slightest.
The shadow remained intact — which meant, naturally, that its threat remained just as intact.
In other words, with the condition that it must not be touched still unchanged, she needed to somehow devise, or find, a means of driving off that pitch blackness—

"—Yorna-samaaa!"

As Yorna's nerves stood on edge, a voice from the ground below struck her ears from far off.
Looking down, she saw it was the people of the Magic City, gathering toward the fallen castle, calling out to her. They were lending a hand to the castle guards crawling out from the rubble they'd been caught in, helping with the rescue.
To the black shadow, that must have looked like nothing so much as prey to drool over.

"All of you, run——!"

Spurred by the alarm surging up within her, Yorna called down to the people below to evacuate.
But she had risen too high into the sky to leap down to them now. The distance was too great; her hand couldn't reach. And it was too much to ask that every one of them share the same sense of crisis that gripped Yorna.

— As a result, below Yorna, the shadow came surging toward the people who had gathered at the castle out of concern for her, their ruler, and there was nothing Yorna could do to stop it.
That is—

"—Don't think I'll let this happen so easily!!"

The black shadow-arms reaching toward each of the frozen, standing townspeople — an instant before they could swallow them into an eternal void, their bodies were snatched away from the side.
What accomplished it were dark green briars, reaching the townspeople's side from far off.

Briars Yorna had seen before, growing with fierce speed — faster than the shadow — catching the townspeople's bodies on their thorns and dragging them clear.
Even in the Magic City, full of people with the strangest of constitutions, there was only one person Yorna could think of capable of pulling that off.

"Kafma Irulux, lending aid on His Imperial Majesty's orders——!"

The man who grew briars from his outstretched arms and beat the transparent wings on his back — Kafma Irulux — appeared at the center of the crisis, and Yorna's eyes went wide.
It wasn't just his bizarre appearance; that he had protected Yorna's people was a surprise in itself.

In the past, under the pretext of putting down Yorna's rebellion, Emperor Vincent had once sent an army to bring Chaosflame under control.
Kafma had been one of the soldiers in that force who had rampaged through Chaosflame.
For him, of all people, to be acting to protect Yorna's people—

"Whoever they may serve, they are, all the same, subjects of the Empire."

「――――」

"First General Yorna! I know our views do not align, but this is something that must not be left unchecked! Allow me to lend my cooperation!"

Answering Yorna's watchful gaze with those words, Kafma beat his wings and flew about the sky with furious speed.
Dancing freely through the air, drawing the attention of the shadow consuming the castle onto himself, Kafma seemed intent on using his mobility and suppressive power to buy time.
The problem, though, was what to do with the time he bought.

"Uh! Ah-uu!"

"Child?"

"Uh!"

As Yorna considered what action to take next, Rui, held against her chest, began thrashing again. But this thrashing wasn't the reckless, impulsive kind from before.
The girl twisted around in Yorna's arms, pointing at a single spot on the ground below and growling.
Following Rui's pointing finger with her eyes, Yorna understood what the girl was trying to say.
And—

"Second General Kafma, this one entrusts the field to you a while longer."

"If that is what's needed, understood!"

"And also..."

"What is it!"

Kafma, circling to dodge the reaching shadow and no doubt wanting to focus fully on facing such a formidable foe, raised his voice slightly at Yorna's words.
Though she had no wish to distract him, Yorna thought it would be a problem to leave this unsaid,

"For protecting this one's children just now — you have this one's thanks."

"—. As a 'General,' I did only what was natural!"

Kafma faltered slightly at Yorna's gratitude, but answered so all the same.
As he returned to his duty and set about buying the necessary time, Yorna revised her opinion of him.

Her impression of him as a stiff, inflexible man hadn't changed, but carrying that single-mindedness through to the end was, if anything, a virtue. If circumstances and situation allowed, he too could be someone worth loving.
Though even so, Yorna's one and only would remain unchanged for all eternity—

"Down we go. Hold on tight now."

Taking the tight grip of Rui clinging to her as her answer, Yorna made her way to her destination, using the floating wreckage of buildings, torn-up ground, even clods of earth, as footholds.
Along the way, she told the townspeople clamoring to know what was happening to simply get away from the Crimson Lazuli Castle — or rather, what remained of it — while her steps carried her to the roof of a house set well back from the castle.
There, waiting for Yorna's arrival with unhurried composure, was—

"This one had rather thought His Imperial Majesty himself would come."

"Hmph."

Standing there with his arms folded, magnificent in his bearing, was the black-haired man whose face was hidden beneath a demon mask.
The instant Yorna landed before him, Rui slipped from her arms as if she'd been waiting for exactly this and dashed over to the man.
Rui seized the man's arm, her long golden hair swinging, and pointed toward the castle,

"Uwaau!"

"You needn't spell it all out; I understand. To begin with, I disagreed with him over how you were to be handled. It's bold of you to show your face before me at all."

"Uu! U!"

"Do you intend to follow my lead? If so, I'll count you among our number."

Even with his arm being tugged, the man answered Rui's appeal flatly. Rui made an impatient face at his response, but eventually let go of his arm as if resigned to it.
That she didn't simply run off regardless was, in her own way, a matter of consideration on the girl's part.
— No matter what, she meant to bring back the boy swallowed by that black shadow.

"Yorna Mishigure, you understand the situation, I trust. That is a Great Disaster that cannot be left unattended."

"This one agrees. A pitch darkness that returns everything to nothingness at the merest touch — as ruler of the Magic City, this one has no option to simply leave it be. And more than that, it has drunk down this one's castle whole. —Could it be, did you know something like this was going to happen?"

"I considered that something might occur. What, precisely, lay beyond the scope of my guesswork."

「――――」

Narrowing her eyes, Yorna tried to gauge the true intent behind the man's words.
But the man's heart was hidden behind his demon mask, impossible to see through no matter how she tried. Though even without the mask, she doubted she could have seen through to what lay within his heart regardless.
A solitary existence who let no one glimpse his thoughts, his heart, his intentions — that, precisely, was the nature of this man, the seventy-seventh Emperor of the Sacred Vollachia Empire.
Which is to say—

"—Your Excellency."

"Don't call me that so carelessly. Not unless you'd like your written petition torn to shreds."

"My apologies, then. ...Wherever did you come to learn of this one's wish?"

"I had no certainty of it. Not until you sent my envoy back safely."

Hearing that answer, Yorna found herself both admiring and exasperated by the man's way of thinking.
He'd apparently meant to gauge whether his own reasoning was correct by whether the envoy returned safely from yesterday's audience.
On that point, Yorna had to admit, with some bitterness, that she had danced entirely upon his palm.
But more than that—

"Your Excellency ought to be scolded, at least once, by that envoy's children."

"That too, only once all of this is settled. Yorna Mishigure. Follow my instructions."

"—. If that be the best course, this one has no choice but to obey."

At the man's overbearing manner of speech, her hesitation and resistance lasted only an instant.
Yorna had something she needed to protect: the Magic City of Chaosflame, and its people. And what was needed to protect it was someone with the ability to bring this situation to a proper close.
Not strength in the sense of fighting power, but rather what might be called the power to govern.
Yorna had her own pride in that power. But the man she was measuring herself against was a poor choice of opponent.
Who could possibly hope to match, in a contest of that power, a man who ruled and governed the greatest empire, holding the largest territory in this world?

"So then, what do you intend to do?"

With the resolve to go along with any plan, if it meant protecting the Magic City, Yorna asked.
Rui, who had come to stand beside her at some point, had her eyes lit with what looked like agreement to Yorna's resolve.

Receiving the gazes of both Yorna and Rui, the man — no, the ruler of the Empire — nodded.
And then—

"—We abandon the city and conduct a fighting retreat. There is no choice left but to let the Magic City be swallowed by that shadow."
