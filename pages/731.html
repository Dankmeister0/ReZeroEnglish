Arc 9 Chapter 35: "The Waking Star"

When Rem arrived back at Roswaal Manor, "arrived back" was not a feeling she could summon at all.

"Rem, can you see it? That's the manor where Ram and the others work, Roswaal-sama's estate."

"Our... workplace..."

At Ram's words, pointing out the scenery visible through the Dragon Carriage's window—a picturesque view bathed in bright sunlight—Rem blinked her pale blue eyes, her lashes trembling.

They had taken a public Dragon Carriage to the industrial city of Costuul, and after boarding a carriage arranged to fetch them there, some time had passed—and what came into view was a "home" she had no memory of at all.

A great gate, and beyond it a splendid manor. Roswaal Manor was even more opulent than Berstetz's residence in the Vollachian capital, where Rem had stayed with Katya—an elegant building unforgettable once seen. And yet—

"Well?"

"...I'm sorry, sister."

Rem shook her head slowly, apologizing in shame for failing to meet her sister's hopes. Along the way, after parting from Subaru, Emilia, and the others who had split off for various purposes, Rem had traveled the road back to the manor alone with Ram. The whole while, she had hoped that the many sights along the way might jog her memory, but not once had that hope been fulfilled.

And it seemed even this manor—so important to them both—was no exception.

"I see. But it can't be helped. After all, it's been quite a while since Ram and the others worked at the current manor. This isn't the manor most impressed upon Rem's memory."

"Eh!?"

"By the way, the previous manor doesn't exist anymore—Otto burned it down in a fit of pique. If it had survived the fire, we might have considered making a detour there."

"Otto-san... he didn't seem like that kind of person at all."

Rem's head spun at this belatedly revealed information, and Ram's account of Otto only deepened her confusion. Otto, the camp's minister of domestic affairs, gave the impression of a mild-mannered man—Subaru's friend and indispensable companion, someone Petra and Frederica described as never sleeping at all. And now, on top of that, came the information that he'd burned down a manor in a fit of rage. His personality had gone missing somewhere along the way.

Perhaps it was a case of people not being what they seemed, but was it really all right to lump it together like that?

"That man included, is everyone on Emilia-san's side like that? Garfiel, and Roswaal-san too..."

"Roswaal-sama, dear."

"...Roswaal-sama, too."

"Good girl. But it would be a bad girl who lumps those three in with Roswaal-sama, Rem. Roswaal-sama is wise and capable, the finest mage in this world. Compared to him, those three together barely add up to one whole person... no, half a person, more like."

"Three people, half a person..."

Rem mentally hoisted Otto, Garfiel, and Subaru up onto one another's shoulders, and lined them up beside Roswaal, who could fly with magic, hoisted up alone. Comparing the two like that, Rem couldn't say exactly how great the gap between them was, but surely Ram wasn't wrong.

For Rem, with no prospect yet of her memory returning, the trust and affection she felt for her twin sister Ram—born of that closeness—was the one thing beyond doubt.

—It was the one certain bond connecting Rem's past to her present.

"...This way of thinking would get me scolded by Priscilla-san, wouldn't it."

At the bitter feeling rising in her chest, Rem recalled the gaze of that woman the color of scarlet flame. In only a short span of time, she had left a great impression on how Rem thought and how she walked forward, and she had never once approved of clinging to the past, of trying to lean on it. In the same way, she surely wouldn't care for Rem surrendering herself to the honey of trust, currying favor with Ram.

Rem didn't want Priscilla to dislike her, and she didn't want to live in a way she'd be ashamed of.

So she scolded her heart for threatening to turn backward, slapped her own cheeks, and lifted her chin.

"But sister, from what you just said, doesn't that mean there isn't much of a clue here that would bring my memory back, even if I return to that manor?"

"Ha, quick on the uptake. Ram is quite proud, having such a clever little sister."

"No, that's... I don't deserve to be praised so much by you, sister..."

"Just who do you think Ram here is the sister of? Do you really think Rem's sister Ram would make such a blunder?"

Ram placed a hand on her own chest and declared this with a face full of confidence.

At Ram's dignified pronouncement, Rem honestly felt ashamed of her own misguided worry. Even without Rem pointing it out, Ram had already seen through that much. Which meant what really mattered now was just what Ram was thinking—

"If that's not the point of coming home to the manor... then is it a person you're after?"

"Who knows. We'll find out the answer... very soon."

Rem's guess and Ram's smile happened almost at the same moment the Dragon Carriage came to a stop. Startled by the shaking she felt for the first time in a while, no longer kept at bay by the Divine Protection of Windshielding, Rem looked up to find Ram already standing gracefully, offering her a hand.

"There, we've arrived. Let's go."

"—Yes, sister."

Taking Ram's hand, Rem pressed a palm to her chest, faintly tense with a fluttering heart, and stepped down from the carriage. While Ram spoke to the driver who had brought them this far, Rem stepped forward ahead of the carriage and, through the bars of the closed iron gate right before her, took in the sight of the manor and its beautiful front garden.

"So this is..."

Rem narrowed her pale blue eyes, but the manor reflected in them was still unfamiliar. Even so, she could tell that every corner forming the building—the flowerbeds, the hedges—was meticulously well-kept, and that quietly straightened her spine.

Memory or no memory, if Rem was going to spend her days at this manor from now on, her position would surely be the same as Ram's and Frederica's and the others': a servant.

No, it's fine if Rem just lives every day healthy and free and easy.

For a moment, she pictured the Subaru inside her head saying something like that, but Rem forcibly drove off that illusion trying to spoil her with excessive kindness.

True, it did sound like something Subaru would say—but the real him had no time for that right now. Precisely because, to comfort someone wounded far more deeply than Rem by the loss of Priscilla, he was right now—

"—O? Ooh, oooh, ooooh!"

The instant a growl suddenly reached her ears, Rem gave a startled "eep" and stumbled backward. Looking up, the voice had come from beyond the fence—that is, from inside the manor grounds. There stood a small girl, gripping the bars with tiny hands, her eyes wide with astonishment.

A lovely face, long pink hair. A small frame that looked about eleven or twelve, wrapped in a black tunic-like garment—the girl's round eyes were opened wide, staring straight at Rem.

Rem recognized that girl. —From amid the devastation of the Empire.

"Y-you're... the 'Witch' who caused the 'Great Disaster'!"

"My, Ryuzu-sama, thank you for coming to greet us."

The bearer of the "Great Disaster," trembling and pointing at Rem.

While Rem stood aghast at this sudden, unthinkable reunion with catastrophe, Ram peeked her face out from beside her and addressed the girl so casually. At that natural manner, Rem turned to look at Ram with an "eh," and the "Witch," as if struck, glared right back at her sister.

"Ram! Welcome home. Welcome home, but... you're being far too nonchalant about this, you!"

"...Would you rather Ram scoop Ryuzu-sama up in her arms, the way Garf or Frederica do?"

"That's not what I said! More to the point, that girl there..."

"Sister, this person is..."

The "Witch" who had schemed the ruin of the Empire, her name was Sphinx.

But Ram had called the girl before them by a different name—Ryuzu. Rem, meeting Ryuzu's gaze turned back on her once more, looked at Ram in confusion.

At their reactions, Ram gently placed a hand on Rem's shoulder.

"Introduce yourself."

"——"

Even without the barest minimum of explanation, Ram's quiet tone strangely calmed Rem's frayed nerves. Her confusion naturally eased, and Rem turned back toward Ryuzu.

Then, steadying her breath, she looked the other in the eye and said,

"—I'm Rem. And you're... Ryuzu-san, is that right?"

"...I see. So it is. Mm, that's right. I'm Ryuzu, Rem."

At being told the name and asked her own in return, Ryuzu's eyes briefly grew distant before she nodded, softly.

From that reaction, from Ram's manner, and from the fact that this girl stood inside the grounds of Roswaal's manor, Rem could finally be certain that the girl before her and the Empire's "Witch" were different people.

"Even so, they look far too alike, don't they..."

"True. There's a popular saying that everyone in the world has three people who look like them, but in Ryuzu-sama's case, it's nearly ten times that. Which means the tawdriness is also tenfold."

"To say such things while an old woman is moved to tears!"

At Rem's remark on the confounding resemblance, Ryuzu scolded Ram for her reply. Even from this exchange, it was clear the two of them were on quite easy terms. Judging by Ryuzu's manner, she too must have been someone with a deep connection to the previous Rem.

"I'm sorry. But I don't remember anything at all."

"Not at all, not at all, no need to apologize. I am—"

"—Her little sister. See, she has the same hair color as Ram."

"Th-that's true...! Eh, but if she's your sister, sister, that means..."

The shocking truth revealed by Ram, cutting Ryuzu off. Rem, stunned by the surprise, looked back and forth between Ram and Ryuzu and felt a certain persuasiveness in their matching hair color.

But since Ram and Rem were twins, that necessarily meant the relationship between Rem and Ryuzu was—

"...my sister too?"

"Stop it, Ram! You—this is it! This is the bad influence of Roz-boy and Su-boy!"

"Putting those two in the same category? Even from you, Ryuzu-sama, there are things one should and shouldn't do."

"Look who's talking!"

Ryuzu raised her lovely eyebrows and spoke louder, and Ram frowned at her. Rem, blinking at their back-and-forth, finally realized it was one of Ram's jokes.

"Now that you mention it, if she really were my sister and yours too, it would be strange for you to add '-sama' to Ryuzu-san's name, wouldn't it."

"Well, depending on circumstances, there's also the possibility of half-sisters through a different father or mother, descended from someone of exalted blood. So it's not a perfect counter-argument, is it?"

"That's true, too... So then, is she really my sister and yours...?"

"No. It is unmistakably one of Ram's little pranks. Honestly, what a troublesome girl."

At Ryuzu's great sigh, Ram stuck out her tongue just slightly.

Somehow she'd been led around by the nose, but come to think of it, along the whole way here, Ram's playfulness like this had troubled Rem quite a bit while also saving her, all at once. For Rem—prone to sinking at the smallest of reasons—Ram's presence, brimming with confidence and humor, was exactly like the warmth of sunlight.

"I think you're wonderful, sister. Including that playfulness."

"Judging by what the Bird Mail told us, it hasn't even been that many days since your reunion, and yet you're already this thoroughly wrapped around her finger..."

"Wrapped around her finger, how rude that sounds. I've simply given her nothing but sincerity and love."

"For all that, you're being unusually giddy for someone like you, Ram. Though I do understand the joy of your sister waking up after being bedridden all this time, and getting to spend time together like this."

"—. How impertinent, coming from you, Ryuzu-sama."

"Your embarrassment makes you awfully combative, so while I don't mind, I'd ask you go easy on Gar-boy at least. Now then..."

Ram frowned faintly. As Rem admired the rare sight of her sister being bested, Ryuzu, who had been leaning against the iron gate, stepped back from the bars.

"It's a strange thing to keep talking through the gate forever. I'd like to hurry and hug my granddaughters, safely returned, before too long."

"Granddaughters...?"

"That's Ryuzu-sama's best attempt at a joke. Do laugh for her."

"That just shows how dear I think of you both!"

At Ryuzu pouting her lips like that, Rem's own lips loosened despite her confusion. It wasn't because Ram had told her to laugh—it was Ryuzu's warmth that naturally smoothed away the edge in her.

Though, being treated as a granddaughter by someone who looked even younger than Rem and Ram themselves was, even accounting for the warmth behind it, something that made her want to tilt her head.

"Beatrice-chan was the same way—do small children have a tendency to want to act older than they are...?"

"That's right. Beatrice-sama's true self only comes out when she's sitting on Barusu's lap."

"With how adorable that must be, I can believe it."

Spica was the same way, too—children were best off laughing brightly and freely, the way children should. Not just Beatrice, but Ryuzu too—how much lovelier she'd be if she stopped straining to seem older and simply laughed as befit her age.

And as Rem was sinking into that reflection, the very next moment her thoughts froze for an instant.

"Now then, everyone, open the gate."

No sooner had Ryuzu said this than the manor's iron gate slowly began to open.

That much itself was fine. There was no problem there. The problem was how the gate was opened.

"「——」"

The gate's bolt was drawn back and the doors swung open inward—and the ones moving them, answering Ryuzu's call, were several people, each with their own distinct hairstyle and ornaments, dressed however they pleased—but every single one of them had Ryuzu's exact face.

Which was to say, they were girls with the very same face as the "Witch" Sphinx—

"—!!"

It was, understandably, too much to bear, and Rem's scream rang out loud and clear.

"I did wonder what that scream was about, but now I understand. Indeed, seeing Ryuzu-sama and her sisters the Pico-samas for the first time would naturally be startling."

"Hearing you say that helps a little..."

At the blonde maid's understanding words, Rem bowed her head in mortification.

The greeting with Ryuzu at the gate, and the shocking event that followed—the appearance of over ten girls all with Ryuzu's exact face—had left her so flustered she was embarrassed at herself now.

"Sister told me clearly that there were ten times three people with Ryuzu-sama's face, but I thought she was just joking..."

"Given how Ram phrased it, it's no wonder Rem thought that. I'd say it's Ram who's being blind, expecting anyone to take that as anything but a joke."

"You're saying that so casually, but perhaps you ought to reflect on yourself too, Ryuzu-sama. If you keep causing trouble at a frequency that can't be overlooked just because you're cute to look at, I might just have to raise a hand myself before long."

"Th-that's a frightening thing to say..."

Glared at quietly by the maid, Ryuzu shrank back in repentance. At the sight, Rem finally managed to relax her lips and straighten the back she'd been hunching.

She'd never have imagined causing such a fuss before even setting foot inside the manor grounds, but now that she'd properly been shown inside, she couldn't keep moping forever. Still, even so—

"I'm sorry for the trouble, causing such a commotion. Um..."

"—Sylphy Elmart. I was formerly Corneas, but I've since divorced. This time, while everyone was away, I was entrusted with managing the manor."

"The manor's... So then, Sylphy-san, were you a coworker of mine and my sister's too?"

Rem asked this of the maid—Sylphy—who bowed quietly at the waist in greeting.

Naturally, everyone working at Roswaal's manor was very likely someone Rem knew. She must have caused them no end of trouble by falling asleep like that.

But to that worry of Rem's, Sylphy shook her head with an "no,"

"I'm still quite new here, so I've never worked alongside you and the others, Rem-sama. And besides, I wasn't hired by the Margrave—I was hired by Milord-sama. What's more—"

"What's more?"

"My only master is myself. And there is no one but Emilia-sama who is master of my heart."

Overwhelmed by the strength of will in Sylphy's gaze, Rem couldn't help leaning her head back.

The extraordinary devotion to Emilia in Sylphy's words could be glimpsed too in the flawless upkeep of the manor's interior—the entrance hall, the corridors, and now the drawing room they were in—wherever her guidance had led them. Just as the manor had impressed from outside, the work here was superb.

Truly the fruit of a devotion that elevated Emilia to nothing less than master of her heart.

"Sylphy-san, have you known Emilia-san a long time?"

"No, we only met a few months ago. And even then, the time I was able to spend directly at her side was extremely short."

"And still you hold her in such regard?"

"Isn't it not the length of time spent together, but rather what that person has done for you, that determines how much of your heart they come to occupy?"

"That's..."

Startled by the unexpected answer, Rem lowered her eyes at Sylphy's following words.

At that moment, what crossed Rem's mind were the faces of Spica, Katya, and the other precious people to her. But the time Rem had spent with them—even accounting for the fact that memoryless Rem had built that bond up from nothing—could hardly be called long.

And yet, Rem still thought of them, so strongly, as irreplaceable.

What Sylphy said made sense. —Just as she was on the verge of accepting that, Subaru's face rose clearly to mind, and Rem pressed her lips together tightly.

"Rem-sama?"

"She's confused, surely. Sylphy, I told you of Rem's circumstances, did I not?"

"So you did. My apologies. I was lacking in consideration."

"No, it's not something you need to apologize for. —It's simply... my own matter."

Hearing Rem's answer, Ryuzu and Sylphy exchanged glances. It pained her, given how earnestly the two of them cared, but Rem had no intention of speaking of the conflict inside her right now.

Just as she'd said, this was, after all, her own worry, her own problem.

"—You look troubled, Rem. Did Sylphy bully you or something?"

"——"

At the sudden voice calling her name, Rem lifted her face toward the drawing room's entrance. Standing there was Ram, who had parted from them partway through their tour, saying she had some business to attend to.

Rejoining them a little late, Ram's appearance made Rem's eyes go wide, catching her breath. —Because the Ram who had appeared stood there in an unfamiliar maid's uniform.

"Sister, that outfit..."

"I've told you many times, haven't I? Ram is the manor's maid—Roswaal-sama's maid."

Smoothing her pink hair back with a flourish, Ram answered so, and Rem couldn't help staring, entranced. Watching Ram just as Rem did, Ryuzu let out a breathless "ho,"

"It's been a while since I've seen Ram in that get-up, hasn't it."

"It has. Ram's been in traveling clothes for so long that it feels fresh."

"Too fresh, it seems—the uniform's put on wrong in several places. May I fix it?"

"Oh? Then please do."

While Rem stared, wide-eyed, Ryuzu looked on with deep feeling, and Sylphy silently began helping straighten Ram's uniform. As Sylphy's hands adjusted her clothes, Ram winked one eye at Rem, who was watching her,

"This is the maid uniform Ram and Rem wear when working at the manor."

"...I think it's lovely. But—"

"But?"

"Compared to Sylphy-san's uniform, isn't there a bit more skin showing? What in the world is the difference... don't tell me that man had something to do with this."

Comparing Ram's and Sylphy's maid uniforms, Rem hugged her own shoulders and voiced that suspicion.

It was certainly a cute outfit, but the difference was too obvious for her not to wonder about the intent behind it. And come to think of it, even back in the Empire, Subaru had repeatedly commented on Rem's clothing, saying things like "it'd be cuter this way" or "adding this touch would be more appealing," giving her all sorts of complicated feelings. The memory of it now resurfaced at the sight of the uniform before her.

"I thought he had an unusual attachment to other people's clothing too, and it seems that's the case even here..."

"...Some kind of misunderstanding seems to have arisen, Ram-sama?"

"True. As for Ram, it doesn't matter in the slightest how far Barusu's reputation drops in Rem's eyes, but having credit stolen out from under her is another matter entirely."

"Credit?"

"Yes. This maid uniform is something Ram herself proposed and had remade. All of it was... because Ram wanted to wear a more mobile uniform herself? Was, I believe?"

"Why did you suddenly lose confidence there?"

At the tail end of that explanation, Ram's confidence unusually wilted, and Ryuzu tilted her head at the sight. At Ryuzu's pointing it out, Ram muttered "how unconvincing" to herself,

"It's unpleasant, not even being convinced by my own opinion. Ram's reason for proposing the uniform redesign probably wasn't something as thrown-together as what just came out of my mouth."

"E-even though it's your own opinion, saying such a thing!?"

"I sense someone else's intentions at work in this. ...It's irritating to think the same as Barusu on something, but it makes far more sense to say she wanted to dress Rem in a cute uniform."

"I see, indeed. That does add up."

With a finger to her lips, Ram deduced this, and for some reason Sylphy agreed with her. Judging from the flow of the conversation, Sylphy too must harbor an impulse to want to dress someone in cute clothes.

In truth, in a world where the memories concerning Rem had vanished from most everyone, there was probably no way to ever learn the true source of Ram's sense of unease, nor its correct answer.

"In that case, Ram will simply believe what she wants to believe. Without owing anyone an explanation."

"...I think you and Priscilla-san must have gotten along, sister."

Rem's circle of acquaintance was narrow and small, but in terms of being proud, strong women, Ram and Priscilla shared a dazzling quality Rem couldn't help but admire in both. Surely, had Priscilla still been alive, there might have been a future where the two grew close through Rem. Or else, they might have clashed like natural enemies.

Either way, it seemed suspecting Subaru over the maid uniform matter had been misguided after all.

"But then, all of this is because that man is always doing confusing things in the first place."

"If you mean Su-boy, the poor lad's getting talked about something awful."

"Ryuzu-san, you say that because you haven't seen him say incomprehensible things and dress as a woman."

"What!? Su-boy cross-dressed even in the Empire! What an incorrigible child."

"Cross-dressing? Even with someone like Emilia-sama in his life? What could he possibly have meant by it. Depending on his answer..."

From Ryuzu's reaction, it became clear that Subaru truly was a repeat offender. Hearing this, Rem resolved in her heart to press him further on the matter, alongside Sylphy, who displayed rather severe displeasure.

While that exchange went on, Sylphy finished adjusting Ram's clothes. Ram spun about on the spot, her skirt hem flaring, and swept her hair back with a flourish,

"Not bad. Good work, Sylphy."

"...I'll go ahead and say I'm honored by the praise."

"It's praise from Ram, so accept it honestly. And it's not just this. I had a light look around, but the manor's upkeep was thorough too."

"I spent my days in submission under a hopeless husband, you see. If even a single speck of dust was found on the floor, all his wives would suffer for it. It makes me angry just remembering it. Though he's recently received his comeuppance."

Having smartly straightened out Ram's uniform, Sylphy's remarks—about her former workplace, or rather, environment—carried a sense throughout of just how dreadful it had been, and Rem felt deeply sympathetic.

Just then, before Rem, Ram, now a perfect maid, gently set down a cup of tea.

"There, you must be thirsty after being grilled with questions by Ryuzu-sama and Sylphy."

"Making us out to be the villains just for pouring us a cup of tea."

"Ha."

Scoffing at Ryuzu's pouting lips, Ram prepared tea for her and Sylphy as well.

Ram, who had apparently brought a serving cart out into the corridor, deftly poured tea for the two of them while, with just a glance, urging Rem to drink first. Obeying, Rem enjoyed the tea's aroma only briefly before bringing the cup to her lips—and couldn't help widening her eyes.

"It's delicious..."

"Of course. Brewing tea is Ram's one and only skill as a maid."

"That's shameless enough to be almost admirable. ...Though in truth, there's no other chance to drink tea this good, so I have nothing left to say."

"Even Frederica always says, laughing, that this is the one thing she can never beat."

At Ram's triumphant look, Sylphy and Ryuzu both conceded defeat and sipped their tea.

Savoring the aroma rising through her nose and the flavor on her tongue, Rem nodded along with the others' words, when she suddenly noticed Ram's soft gaze watching her, tinged with affection and a faint trace of expectation.

"—Ah."

Ram never said it outright. But surely, some hope had been riding on the taste of this tea, too.

Given a flavor this fine—tea that no one but Ram could brew—it wasn't hard to imagine that the Rem before losing her memory had savored it too. In truth, the master's touch in brewing it, moving enough to bring tears to the eyes, seemed to seep tenderness into every corner of Rem's body.

And yet, it did nothing to stir her memory, brought none of the result she had hoped for.

"It was very, truly very delicious. But—"

Setting the empty cup down on the table, Rem let her words trail off, eyes lowered. But at the sight of her like that, Ram smiled and said "silly girl,"

"I only brewed some tea. Where's the need for adorable Rem to apologize for anything?"

"Ram-sama's eyes were rather sharp, perhaps that's what made her apologize. My former husband was quite the moody one, but apologizing right away always put him back in good humor, at least."

"...Ram, would you mind pouring a second cup for Sylphy? This girl is just so, so pitiable to me."

"Whyever would you think that? Far from pitiable, I'm enjoying a freedom I've never had before."

Frowning faintly, Sylphy told the sad-eyed Ryuzu that this was uncalled for. At Ryuzu's request, Ram had already begun preparing a second cup for Sylphy.

Watching Ram's back as she did so, Rem resolved, once more, with all her strength:

...I want to repay the people who are so good to me.

After leaving the drawing room, Rem wandered the manor here and there, following wherever her feet led.

Every time she stopped, Ram, who came along with her, would tell her the memory attached to that spot—"That's the flowerbed where Barusu got a face full of fertilizer," "This pillar has bite marks from Garf—I'll have to scold him later," "Oh, the portrait Emilia-sama and Beatrice-sama drew of the Great Spirit-sama on the ground is still there"—leaving Rem no time to feel lonely.

But despite all of Ram's closeness and consideration, Rem still couldn't manage to encounter anything that gave her that firm push forward she was hoping for.

"Oh, more children who look like Ryuzu-sama..."

"That's the Picos. They were let out of the 'Sanctuary' and are learning all sorts of things from scratch."

"...I suppose my situation is similar to theirs, and to Sylphy-san's, isn't it."

Unlike the strangely worldly Ryuzu, the girls—chief among them the one called Pico—looked as innocent as their appearance suggested, and could be seen throughout the sprawling Roswaal Manor, busily helping the maids who wore the same uniform as Sylphy.

These girls, who seemed so admirably earnest, had apparently been born into a fate unlike other people's, and were now, freed from it, right in the middle of living out a different way of life.

Meanwhile, the maids—many of whom drew the eye with their beauty and grace—had, according to what she'd heard, all been placed in the same dreadful circumstances as Sylphy, and all owed their escape from it to Emilia.

Ryuzu's sisters and the maids alike, in the sense that fate had toyed with and tossed around their lives, seemed to Rem to be in the same position as herself.

But to that thought of Rem's, Ram said "true enough" and then continued,

"Whether one has memories or not, once you decide to start walking a new path, in most cases there's nothing to do but build it up again from scratch. Rem is nothing special."

"Sister..."

"No, that's wrong. Rem is special. To Ram, unbearably precious."

Corrected like that, Rem turned her face away, embarrassed by the sheer weight of affection being given to her.

Of course, Rem too felt a strong closeness to Ram, a love overflowing from within—but where Rem's amounted to one, from Ram it felt like being given ten, a hundred, leaving her unsettled.

Carrying that restlessness, Rem and Ram's tour of Roswaal Manor continued for a while longer.

And then, eventually—

"—Here it is, Rem. This is your room."

The door opened by Ram's hand, and Rem stepped into the room she indicated.

Compared to the places they'd toured through the manor—the drawing room, the dining hall, the great bathhouse, the secret training grounds, the sitting room, the garden, the balcony—there wasn't much to note about this room, no different from any guest room. Given the sheer size of the manor, the room given to Rem, merely a servant, was solid enough in its own right, but that was about it.

"——"

A large bed, a bookshelf holding poetry collections and picture-tales. The writing desk was neatly tidied, with a vase of fresh flowers set upon it.

"There was a time I tidied away your personal things without realizing what they were, and Barusu pulled everything back out and restored it all, more or less the way Rem had it."

"That man did..."

Standing before the bookshelf, Ram told her of Subaru's efforts. Ram's remarks about Subaru tended, generally, to run sharp, and there were plenty of points Rem found herself agreeing with—but the words just added held nothing but sincerity, and it struck Rem's heart.

She pulled out a poetry collection and turned its pages. None of the poems written there were familiar to her, but they were surely verses the previous Rem had liked, had favored. Somehow, many seemed to be poems of love and romance, and she felt an odd mixture of amusement and embarrassment at her former self's taste.

"I was asleep for over a year, wasn't I."

After returning the poetry collection to its shelf, Rem sat gently down and stroked the bed, narrowing her eyes.

In place of Rem, fallen into a sleep from which she would not wake, unable to fulfill her duties as a maid, Ram, Frederica, Petra, and the others must have kept up the manor's upkeep, tending day after day to the bedridden Rem. Even now the bed's pillow and sheets were kept spotlessly clean, evidence of their careful work.

Beside the bed, the faintly sun-worn patch on the floor was proof that a chair had long stood there. A picture rose in her mind of whoever had visited the sleeping Rem, speaking to her from that spot.

That person surely was Ram, was Emilia and the others, and—

"——"

Beside Rem, seated on the bed, Ram silently sat down as well. Her gentle pale-pink gaze looked over at Rem's profile, but Rem closed her eyes and shook her head.

She hadn't been asked. But surely, Ram wanted to ask.

There was nothing in this whole manor, nothing in this whole room, that stirred Rem's memory. What was here was an abundance of love, things she could feel—nothing but things making painfully clear to memory-less Rem just how fortunate she had been.

She felt that so strongly, so deeply, that it brought tears to her eyes.

And so—

"...Sister, that thing over there is really bothering me. What is it?"

Sunlight filtered through the leaves into a corner of the room where soft air and consideration lingered—and there, a mysterious object caught the eye with its strangeness: an iron ball on a chain, studded with spikes.

At Rem's question, Ram let out an "ah," as if just remembering,

"That's a Morningstar."

"...mor-ning-star?"

"Yes. Barusu started calling it that, and somehow the name just stuck... apparently that, too, was something Rem treasured."

"I treasured that!?"

Told something so out of tone with all the memories shared so far, Rem found herself glancing back and forth, again and again, between Ram and the spiked iron ball—the Morningstar. Having done so, she nearly convinced herself this too was one of Ram's mischievous jokes.

"Ram understands the urge to doubt it. But watching Barusu prepare a special cloth and oil, polishing that thing every single day, Ram thinks the setup's gone on far too long for a mere pointless joke."

"B-but wouldn't that man do something that pointless anyway?"

"True. I won't deny it."

Whether this was an argument already thoroughly exhausted between them, Ram's answer to Rem's objection came tinged with resignation. At Ram's manner, Rem rose from the bed and walked over to the Morningstar.

"I don't understand it at all..."

Drawing closer, she found a hand-made stand set up so the iron ball's spikes wouldn't scratch the floor, showing careful thought put into it. The cloth and container of oil placed in the same spot backed up Ram's claim that Subaru polished it every day.

But even knowing that, so what.

"This too must just be his idea of a joke—"

Just like the cross-dressing, it was surely nothing more than something malicious reflecting Subaru's taste, delivered with just enough sincerity to seem convincing.

Thinking so, Rem crouched down and touched the iron ball with her finger. —That was the instant.

"—eh"

Suddenly, Rem's vision warped and twisted, and she found herself falling backward onto the floor. It was an unseemly position, but Rem, in that moment, had no room to spare for embarrassment or worry about it.

"Ngh..."

Even having fallen, the dizziness didn't stop, her vision spinning round and round.

Even as she remained where she was, she lost all distinction between floor and ceiling, the world's black and white inverted, and a confusion of the five senses struck her—what she saw and what she heard and what she smelled and the taste and texture of the air, all mingling together, so that air was seen, sound smelled, scent touched, color tasted, and things were heard.

"—Ah, uh"

Everything she could feel mixed together, churned, and Rem's past and present and even future intermingled.

A mountain village, a wolf banner pierced by a sword, the creaking sound of a wheelchair's wheels, a swarm of Flying Dragons blotting out the sky, a monstrous white whale flying through the night sky, a broken horn soaring up into the firelit dusk, and a boy pleading with a desperate expression, a boy angry, a boy in tears, a boy laughing—

"——"

Eventually, the shock, like the whole world being remade anew, burst all at once, like a bubble popping.

"——"

Sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch—all of them, seeing and hearing and smelling and tasting and touching in their proper form, taught her once more the true shape of being alive.

Feeling that searing, branding intensity of sensation, and the pulse it sent coursing blood through every last corner of her body, Rem blinked once, twice, three times.

She had no idea what had just happened.

Only—

"I... Rem is..."

Still sitting where she'd fallen, Rem touched her own cheek with a trembling hand, confirming its existence.

The sensation at her fingertip wouldn't fade. Fragile, uncertain, she was afraid it would crumble away like a sandcastle if she pressed any harder, and so Rem couldn't bring herself to add any more strength to her finger.

If she were greedy for even a little more, everything might vanish, she felt—

"——"

Rem, wrapped in that undeniable unease and fear, was suddenly embraced from behind by a warm touch.

She caught her breath and froze, unable even to turn around. Held by arms wrapped around her from behind, Rem found, in that head and heart that had been consumed entirely with herself, a chance to turn her attention elsewhere.

And the first place she turned it was toward the one who held her from behind—

"Sist—"

"—Wait."

"——"

"Don't turn around yet. Right now, Ram is disappointed in herself for the first time in her life."

Just as she'd begun to speak, begun to turn, stopped in both by the other, Rem's body went rigid. Ram, whose face she couldn't see, whose arms were wrapped around her, whose voice reached her—all of it was trembling. It was so utterly unlike the Ram that Rem knew.

Always, Ram had shown Rem nothing but a dignified, valiant, and confident self.

That had been true when they reunited in the Empire, true on their journey back to Roswaal Manor, the two of them alone, true even from when she first began working at the manor, right after her horn had broken and the burden was so heavy on her, true even when she fought so fiercely against the Witch Cult in the burning village to protect foolish Rem.

Whenever she appeared in Rem's eyes, Ram had always been so wonderful—

"I'm sorry, Rem. I'm ashamed of myself."

"Sister... what are you..."

"To have forgotten—"

"——"

"To have forgotten someone so dear, so precious as this, and lived all the way until today—what a heartless sister Ram must have been."

At Ram's voice, regretting herself precisely because her love was too great, Rem's eyes went wide. And shaking off every attempt to stop her, Rem turned her body around, bringing herself face to face with Ram, who held her.

"—!"

Her tear-dampened pale-pink eyes widened faintly, and Ram looked at her, startled. —At that face, the one Rem had looked upon more than any other, for longer than any other, in her entire life, her chest grew warm.

She felt certain of it now. Of just how fortunate she was, of just how surrounded by things dear to her she was.

"Sister, you're not heartless at all. If anything, it's Rem who should apologize. All this time, all this time, I've only ever caused you worry—I'm an undutiful sister."

"Rem."

"I understand it all, properly. How much you've worried yourself for Rem's sake, sister, how much you've sacrificed, to stay by my side all the way until today. And also..."

"Rem..."

"What weak, pathetic Rem, who's only ever received, needs to do from now on."

"—!"

Looking straight at Ram's face before her, Rem scooped up with a finger the tears running down her cheek, and smiled at her. Then, reaching out, she drew her sister's head close and held it to her chest.

Seated flat on the floor, the two sisters drew close together, gently, cherishing one another. —No, Ram had always been doing that. It was Rem who hadn't been able to.

"Sister, I love you so much."

Bringing her lips close to the ear she held, Rem conveyed her overflowing feelings to Ram. Feeling hot tears slowly soak into her chest, Rem kept holding the silently weeping Ram.

Finally accepting, from the heart, the reunion truly fulfilled in its real sense, Rem conveyed her love with everything she had—her past self and her present self both.

For now, she wanted to spend this time with Ram, just like this, for as long as her heart desired, until she was fully satisfied.

And once she had properly reclaimed all this unfilled time between the sisters, there were so many people Rem needed to—things she needed to do, gratitude and love she needed to convey.

Above all, the first to come to mind was a black-haired boy with sharp, unfriendly eyes—

"—Subaru-kun."

She had to stand up, in order to convey her love.

Just what a fortunate reason I'm alive for, Rem thought.

△▼△▼△▼△

"——"

Before the maid-uniformed girl who bowed deeply and then lifted her face, Petra caught her breath.

Her eyes went wide and fixed, her stiffened body refusing to move. It wasn't only Petra who fell silent, struck speechless at the sight of the one who'd appeared—Meili and Emilia were the same.

Amid that forced silence, the first to open her mouth was—

"—Rem."

The insubstantial Imaginary Subaru, looking at the same person as Petra, stood frozen in place.

His voice, dumbstruck, reached no one but Petra, and sadly, the very girl who was the sole focus of everyone's gaze couldn't meet the eyes of his half-transparent form either. Petra had no room to spare, right now, to help the "Subaru" that no one—not even her—could notice.

But she could vouch for one thing: the correctness of "Subaru's" cry. —The one standing there was, without question, that dear, precious girl named Rem.

"—Ah"

In that instant, a spark ran through Petra's head, and one by one, the haze clouding her field of awareness began to clear.

The gravitational pull drawing her in—its cause standing right there before her—unconsciously shoved into a corner of her mind, veiled under a cloth of forgetting: the picture-gallery of memory began, once more, to be put on display along the corridors of her awareness.

And at every vivid, colorful picture put back on display, she was struck with a shock like scattering sparks.

—The time Petra, as herself, had spent in contact with her had never been especially long. A maid girl who occasionally came from Roswaal's manor to the village to shop. Someone with almost no point of contact, someone she'd barely exchanged words with—but once Subaru began showing his face in the village, her expressions had grown rich with feeling, and above all, she had been the one who saved Petra and the others' lives. —Though it had been Meili, controlling a Witchbeast, who'd put those lives in danger in the first place, which made for an ironic tale. Or rather, at every crucial juncture, Meili and her sister seemed to target the lives of the people connected to them far too often.

In any case, that was the extent of the connection between Petra and her.

Afterward, at the same time the Royal Selection began, she had fallen victim to the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony, and Petra, hired to fill the gap she left, had joined the Emilia Camp as a maid.

Which meant that, without her tragedy, Petra's current position couldn't possibly exist—a thought that, in itself, left her with terribly complicated feelings—

"Rem... is that you, Rem?"

Like a face-down card being turned over, its picture and the words written on it confirmed anew, one by one inside Petra the things that had been incomprehension toward her were being replaced with understanding.

Meanwhile, it was Emilia, blinking her eyes, who called out to her in that halting voice.

Stepping forward, Emilia opened and closed her lips several times, as if hesitating over what to say next,

"No, sorry, that's a strange way to put it. I already knew, properly, from before, that Rem is Rem... but that's not what I want to say."

"It's all right, Emilia-sama. Please take your time, there's no need to rush. Rem understands this is sudden, too."

"—. Yes, thank you. There's all sorts of things I want to say, but, well—"

"But?"

"...That outfit really, really suits you. It really, really feels like—like Rem."

Pressing a hand to her chest, her violet eyes glistening, Emilia smiled at Rem like that. At Emilia's words, Rem raised her eyebrows slightly, then nodded.

Proudly, she puffed out her chest in the maid uniform,

"Because it's the uniform my sister tailored to suit Rem."

At Rem's answer, Ram, arms crossed, nodded with satisfaction.

"...What in the world is this, all of a sudden?"

Amid the exchange between Emilia and Rem, Meili grumbled with a scowl on her face.

The little crimson scorpion peeking its head out from her hair clicked its pincers, as if worried for its mistress's reaction, and Meili, closing one eye, put a hand to her forehead,

"The moment I saw Ram-onee-san's little sister, I remembered getting my whole pack of Urgalums crushed by that man. That was the reason my job failed, wasn't it..."

"Then you'd best be grateful to Rem. Thanks to Rem botching her job, there were no victims besides Barusu, which is exactly why you're allowed to be here now."

"That's such a complicated feeling, it's hard to feel grateful..."

At Ram's remark, hugging her own elbow, Meili made a face of distaste and stuck out her tongue.

Certainly the way of putting it was unkind, but Ram's point held truth all the same. In fact, the reason Meili—once an enemy—had been accepted into the Emilia Camp was undeniably in part because the damage the camp suffered from her actions had been kept to a minimum. —And, as it happened, Ram's observation that there had been no victims besides Subaru was ironically also correct from the standpoint of "Return by Death."

"As you can see, the moment Rem's appearance was witnessed, the memories of those who knew her from before were revived. Regeneration. I too, and Ryuzu-sama as well, could not contain our agitation. As a result, our joining you was delayed. Confession."

"Clind's reaction was too thin to be interesting, but Ryuzu-sama's loss of composure was exaggerated enough to be worth seeing. That's the privilege of having been present for it."

"I see, worth seeing, is it. Indeed, a rare sight was to be had. Acknowledgment."

"—. Ha."

The revival of "memory" that occurred in those around Rem at the sight of her—no, given the conditions of Gluttony's Authority thus far, this should properly be called the revival of "names."

When Clind, touching on this fact, turned a meaningful look toward Ram, she visibly looked away in displeasure and gave a small snort.

Looking closely, there might have been some connection between the faint reddening around Ram's eyes and Clind's remark about it being "worth seeing."

But Petra didn't mention it. —Not out of mercy, but because she simply couldn't manage to.

"—Petra-san?"

Rem, who had been the center of everyone's attention, suddenly noticed Petra's silence and widened her eyes. At her reaction, Emilia and the others turned to look this way too, and were startled at the sight of Petra.

Their reaction was only natural. Petra, who hadn't uttered a single word this whole time, had been desperately holding back a throat about to burst into sobs, tears streaming in torrents from her round eyes.

"Petra-chan!? Oh no, don't cry so much—"

"—!"

The instant her tearful face was noticed, Petra kicked off the floor and broke into a run.

Slipping past Emilia's hand as it reached to pull her close, startled by Petra's tears; dodging Meili, who'd reached for her sleeve; ducking under old man Rom, left behind by the whole situation—Petra, with swift, nimble movement, threw herself straight ahead, into the chest of the wide-eyed Rem.

And then—

"Rem—!"

"Kyaa!"

Caught in the arms of Rem, who hadn't dodged away, Petra clung tightly to her slender frame. As Rem, blinking, hugged back the clinging Petra who refused to let go,

"Rem... Reeem, Reeem..."

At Rem's response, Petra, unable to stop the tears streaming down in great drops, gave in to the one thing she'd absolutely sworn never to do in front of others—sniffling her nose—and still refused to pull away.

Supporting the weeping Petra, Rem looked around as if seeking help,

"Um, Rem and Petra-san... is it possible we were close enough that Rem simply doesn't remember it, close enough for her to cry like this over me...?"

—so she said, unable to hide her bewilderment, all the while gently continuing to stroke Petra's head.
