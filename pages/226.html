Arc 4 Chapter 60: "The Story of the End of the End"

Having finished his conversation with Frederica, Subaru stood in the corridor of the third floor of the mansion's main building, the floor where Roswaal's study and the like were located. Whether his head felt heavy because of accumulated fatigue, or because his heart was troubled over how to deal with the difficult problem waiting ahead, he couldn't say.

"And on top of that, I've got no choice but to wing it again..."

Scratching the back of his neck with a finger, Subaru grimaced at how meager his hand of cards was. Compared to last time, he felt he'd gathered far more information, but whether that connected directly to the problem ahead wasn't clear at first glance. He had to feel his way through, fitting unseen puzzle pieces into a picture he'd already assembled. That kind of anxiety, unable to see what lay ahead, hung heavy in his chest.

"Subaru-sama. What will you do?"

The one who called out to Subaru as he stood there was Frederica, waiting quietly at his side. Having finished their talk in the parlor, Frederica had accompanied Subaru this far. Subaru gave a vague nod of "Ah" to her question, then said,

"Since Frederica came back, you haven't crossed paths with Beatrice even once... right?"

"Yes. Even before, she rarely showed me her face, but this time, not even once so far. It's rather embarrassing, I must admit."

"Nah, in this case it's more that she's playing hide-and-seek way too seriously. Not finding her isn't your fault, Frederica."

In truth, if one seriously tried to find Beatrice, who had vanished through her Door Crossing to the other side, there would be no choice but to check every single door that might connect to the forbidden archive, one by one. If that were confined to just the mansion, it would be one thing, but recalling that she'd once connected doors to Arlam Village and the Sanctuary—

"The range is way too wide, there's no way to narrow it down. It's a mean thing to say, but this is the kind of riddle that definitely has no findable answer."

"Then what will you do? Subaru-sama, you have something to discuss with Beatrice-sama, don't you."

"If she's seriously hiding, nobody can find the right answer. That's a fact."

Frederica's brow furrowed at Subaru's repetition of his earlier words. In response to her doubtful look, Subaru cracked his neck and raised a finger.

"What matters is what comes next. If she's seriously hiding, nobody can find her. But if she's not seriously hiding, that's a different story."

"Not... seriously?"

"Nobody plays hide-and-seek wanting to never be found. Everyone hides while hoping, somewhere deep down, to eventually be found. And, well, there might even be an oni you'd rather have find you—that's the complicated part of the human heart."

As he spoke, Subaru walked a little and made a quick turn to the left. Turning ninety degrees down the hallway, he stopped in front of the room next to Roswaal's study—the archive room. It was a cramped room stuffed to the brim with paper documents, a room Otto had once organized while nearly losing his mind. Subaru put his hand on the door of that room, and through the doorknob, he felt the "correct answer."

"Weird thing is, the instant I open it—no, the instant I decide to open it—I just know whether it's right or not. Right now, though, I can't help wondering if I was just being made to know."

"Subaru-sama..."

"Anyway. Ta-da."

With Frederica's concerned voice at his back, Subaru made a little sound effect with his mouth as he threw the door open. And beyond the archive room's door, he felt the sensation of space warping—a peculiar odor drifting out from the room, the kind only old books that had endured a long span of time could carry. It was a rich fragrance of paper and ink that mere documents destined for quick disposal could never hope to produce.

"I don't plan to be back too late, but if it starts getting dark, don't forget to see Petra home to the village."

Still holding the doorknob, Subaru called out to the startled Frederica. Frederica blinked a few times at the address, then pinched the hem of her skirt and bowed.

"I shall await your return. Please, do take care."

"Nice. Almost makes me think I'm someone important."

Seen off dutifully by the maid, Subaru passed through the door into the room. As the door shut behind him with a sound, he seemed to hear the noise of a wind that shouldn't have existed, and the space twisted. The connection that had been there vanished, and the forbidden archive was once again isolated from the world.

And then—

"So you've finally come, I suppose."

A murmured voice, entirely unwelcoming, greeted Subaru. Letting a wry smile rise naturally to his face, Subaru raised a small hand.

"Yo, Bea-ko. Been a while since I've seen your face, and you're as tiny as ever."

"I could go my whole life without hearing your idle chatter and it would still be more than enough of a nuisance, I suppose. Truly... hopeless, beyond hopeless."

Seated on the middle rung of a wooden stepladder, Beatrice hugged a black-bound book to her chest. Seeing her like that, Subaru thought, as always, that she was sitting in that exact spot. There were proper desks and chairs inside the forbidden archive. And yet, the girl's fixed spot for greeting visitors was always right there. For some reason, something twinged in his chest, and Subaru clenched his back teeth.

"Your spineless face is only getting harder to look at, I suppose. Hurt yourself, torment yourself, that's your business, but doing it in front of Betty is unpleasant, so stop it at once, I suppose."

"How high-handed of you. Sorry, but I've got no reason to grant that request. Not until I can confirm that what's between you and me is solid enough for that kind of thing to hold."

Implicitly, Subaru conveyed that he already knew what he'd learned about Beatrice's circumstances from the previous loop. Beatrice, receiving that, let a trace of hardness surface on her composed face and murmured under her breath, "So that's... how it is, I suppose..."

"Fine, I suppose. Let's say we can both assume the cards worth playing are already in our hands."

"My cards, though, I've got plenty of doubts about whether they'll do any good. I'll be filling in the gaps with guesswork here and there as we talk."

"Do as you like, I suppose. In any case..."

Fwuh—Beatrice's taut expression suddenly loosened. When the stubbornness that had been plastered over her face peeled away, what showed from underneath was a gentle smile and a fleeting glimmer in her eyes—Subaru unconsciously choked, falling silent. And then Beatrice said,

"The long, long, long contract is coming to its end. —When the end of the end has ended, will Betty finally, at last, be freed from her stagnation, I wonder."

She said it, in a voice tinged with such loneliness.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

"'When the end of the end has ended'... huh. That's quite the poetic turn of phrase."

Before Beatrice, who already seemed fully prepared, Subaru shrugged and probed. His gaze flicked briefly to the black book in the girl's hands—if Roswaal's words were true, one half of the only two completed Gospels still existing in this world. That thing, which brought information about the future, seemed to Subaru to have the aspect of a prophetic book, while also carrying something like the character of a directive from the future, declaring "the future must be made this way." In fact, the Witch Cult's Petelgeuse had acted precisely according to the descriptions of his incomplete Gospel, growing frantic as if there were meaning in doing exactly that. Though, since the incomplete version hadn't recorded events all the way to the future's conclusion, that madman had ultimately been struck down by Subaru's own hand.

"Is it thanks to that book that you get to sit there looking like you know everything?"

"...You're one to talk. How much do you even know about this book, I wonder."

"Roswaal let slip a bunch of things here and there. I figure I've got a grasp of the outline, at least. ...That it has a similar nature to the Gospel the Witch Cult has, but a higher-tier version. That only two copies remain in the world, and you and Roswaal each hold one."

"Roswaal really is a loose-lipped man, I suppose. Given his aims, I can just imagine him telling you gleefully, I suppose."

Subaru's brow furrowed at the venom in Beatrice's tone. Ordinarily, Beatrice's assessment of Roswaal was scathing as a matter of course. But up until now, there had always been something like the easy familiarity of a close relationship underlying it. In what she'd just said, however, there wasn't a trace of that. Right now, from the bottom of her heart, Beatrice was speaking out of genuine loathing for Roswaal.

"I still don't quite get the relationship between you and Roswaal, either. You share the only two copies of a book that exist in the whole world, and yet he's got your whole bloodline under contract, living in his house."

"Say clearly what you're getting at, I suppose."

"Fine, I'll say it plainly then—your position in all this is way too unclear."

Beatrice narrowed her eyes. An intimidating pressure, ill-suited to her lovely features, radiated from her, and Subaru felt it almost like a gust of wind against his skin. As he cut into the heart of the matter, the atmosphere Beatrice wore shifted abruptly.

"Roswaal's position, I've got a rough idea of by now. A family line that made a contract with the Witch of Greed, carrying that role on and on. Managing the Sanctuary is part of that same thread, though why he's trying to make Emilia win the Royal Selection is still murky."

"............"

"But your role—I can't find it anywhere in that. Roswaal is the one in the position of having contracted with the Witch of Greed. You could say he's Greed's disciple."

From the way he stubbornly called her "Echidna" instead of "the Witch of Greed," it was somewhat easy to sense that Roswaal harbored no ordinary attachment to Echidna. Inadequate as it might be by comparison, wasn't his position, too, something like Subaru's own—a disciple of Greed? Though of course, Subaru had been made one against his will by Echidna's own hand, while Roswaal, presumably, had inherited the position much like a family headship.

"Whether the Gospel—the one the Witch Cult has—shares the same root as the completed version you and Roswaal hold, I don't know. My guess is the maker is different. And who's making the Witch Cult's Gospel, I've got no idea. But the two completed copies—I can guess who made those."

"...Who, I wonder."

"—Echidna."

The instant that name left his mouth, Subaru could tell Beatrice had held her breath. And that this name was, to Beatrice, by no means a small matter.

In Echidna's dream castle, Subaru had seen the artifact Echidna possessed, called the Book of Wisdom, said to rival omniscience and omnipotence itself. The essence of the Book of Wisdom differed from that of the Gospel, but they matched on the point of being bound tomes, grimoires beyond human comprehension. And if one connected the person who most recently held it with the Sanctuary where those involved were gathering, the answer all but presented itself.

"The Gospel you and Roswaal have—Echidna made it. Roswaal's is probably the one copy passed down through House Mathers, generation after generation. So then—how did you come to have yours?"

"............"

"Here's one question first. About your Door Crossing."

Raising a single finger, Subaru killed his earlier momentum and brought up a different topic. Beatrice blinked at Subaru's rhetorical maneuvering, bracing herself for whatever he'd say next. Subaru threw his question at her.

"—The range your Door Crossing can reach. In this case, what does the range of possible destinations look like?"

"...I don't know what you intend to do, hearing that, I suppose."

"If what I hear matches what I'm imagining, then it's going to confirm what I've been imagining."

Arms crossed, Subaru squared his shoulders at Beatrice, who kept her mouth shut. Beatrice hesitated, her lips trembling, then closed her eyes as if resigning herself.

"Betty's Door Crossing connects within the same building, or a nearby space. Or somewhere close by that she knows well. The distance—it can't reach very far, I suppose."

"There's another condition too, isn't there? At least one more."

"And you think Betty has any reason to just tell you that honestly, I suppose?"

"Then I'll guess it for you. —Even if the distance is far, you can connect your Door Crossing to a place you share a deep bond with. How's that?"

"――――"

Beatrice caught her breath, her eyes widening. That reaction was enough to confirm, in Subaru's chest, the suspicion he'd been harboring.

"If your focus gets disrupted and you still use the Door Crossing anyway—how does it choose where to connect?"

"...top."

"In a split second, anyone falls back on the actions or words that are second nature to them. For something like a Door Crossing, it's no wonder at all if the place that comes to mind is somewhere you feel strongly attached to."

"...'s not... I suppose."

"You, who received the Gospel Echidna made, connect the archive with your Door Crossing to a place in the Sanctuary. —That means—"

"—Just, stop it already, I suppose!"

The girl, rocking the stepladder as she stood, looked at Subaru with a face full of pleading. Her lips pressed tight together, her eyes welling with tears. He was certain, now, that he'd shoved his filthy hands into territory she didn't want touched. Feeling a bittersweet ache run through his chest, Subaru shook his head. "No," he said.

"I'm not stopping. I know for a fact that a Door Crossing connects here to a certain place in the Sanctuary. And the reason it happens—what you just denied so frantically—that's the answer."

"............"

"Beatrice. You're connected to the Sanctuary too, aren't you? What's your relationship to Echidna?"

Knowing full well it was an act of trampling with muddy shoes into the heart of a girl who wanted none of it, Subaru swallowed down his hesitation and crushed Beatrice with question after question.

Through a Door Crossing, Subaru had once been sent to the facility in the Sanctuary used for the immortality experiments. That the space which had opened in that split-second, desperate moment had connected there meant that place held strong significance for Beatrice. That she, a Spirit, held such a strong memory of the facility where Ryuzu Meyer's copies were made. And considering the circumstance of her having been given the Gospel by Echidna—

"Beatrice... just who is it you're contracted to, as a Spirit?"

"――――ッ!"

"I asked Puck about this once before. About the principle behind a Spirit's contract. I'll skip the finer details, but the gist is that equal terms are established between a contractor and their Spirit. You once said you're bound by contract to guard the forbidden archive. Bound to a contract with whom?"

"…………ah."

"Up till now I just assumed, on my own, that the contract was made with Roswaal. Given you live in this mansion, managing the archive inside it, it's a natural enough assumption... but right now, I'm starting to think that's a bit off."

Under Subaru's relentless barrage of words, Beatrice could no longer even manage a proper response, her trembling lips letting out only faint, weak breaths. Her small body seemed to shrink even smaller, and the arms cradling the Gospel, seeking something to cling to, tightened as if to confirm its weight. It was a posture almost too fragile, like someone enduring the unendurable. Even seeing that—even while seeing it—Subaru said it anyway.

"—You're the Spirit who made a contract with Echidna, aren't you?"

—And that was the beginning of the end of the end.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

—The instant the question left his mouth, Beatrice crumpled like a marionette with its strings cut.

"Bea—!?"

The girl fell from the ladder, knees striking the floor. There came a hard sound, followed by the rustle of scattering paper, as the Gospel dropped from Beatrice's hands and its contents spilled across the floor. Carried everywhere, constantly, opened again and again, traced over and over by fingers—the book's endurance had apparently long since reached its limit. From the impact of the fall, the spine came loose from the pages, scattering them across the floor like a carpet of white.

"The Gospel—wait, what?"

Seeing a few pages fly all the way to his feet, Subaru crouched and picked them up without thinking. What crossed his mind was the Gospel Petelgeuse had possessed—a book whose pages had been packed edge to edge with tiny, cramped handwriting, writhing as if the madness of its owner had infected the very letters. He remembered it well. Holding the pages he'd picked up, Subaru was lost in that recollection—until the memory was blown clean away by what he saw spread before his eyes. Because—

"What... huh? Blank...?"

Front and back, the page Subaru had picked up had nothing written on it at all. Flustered, he picked up another page at his feet, but that one, too, had no writing on it. He wondered if it was just chance—that these happened to be blank pages from the later, still-empty portion of the book—but,

"No, that's ridiculous."

Still crouched, Subaru looked over the pages scattered around Beatrice, and he realized. Every single one of the pages covering the area around her had not a single character written on it. Among a scattering of no fewer than a hundred pages, how likely was it that every single one happened to be showing its blank reverse side?

"It's a Gospel, and yet... nothing's written on it?"

Rather than believe in the miracle of every page happening to show its blank face, it was far more rational to conclude that every page's contents had simply been emptied out, as if the whole book had come unbound and lost its text. And following that rational line of thought led straight into a fact that was anything but rational.

"Only two completed copies exist, so why doesn't it record the future? Is it some kind of gimmick where only the owner can read the text? Is Petelgeuse's copy just not a valid reference point?"

If it came down to complete versus incomplete, the Gospel Subaru had for reference was the incomplete one. That one could be read by people other than its owner, and now that its owner was gone, Subaru was the one keeping it safe. Fortunately, there had been no sign, so far, of new text appearing after Petelgeuse's death. So Subaru had simply assumed that whoever owned a Gospel, its contents would be visible to anyone.

"It's already been... quite some time, I suppose."

"—Huh?"

"That Gospel—it stopped showing Betty any future at all, and it's already been... years..."

Slumped on the floor, her face lowered, Beatrice let the words spill out haltingly. Restraining the urge to ask what she meant, Subaru waited, without pressing her, for her words to continue. Beatrice's lowered hand pressed down on the scattered pages, crumpling them in her grip. Her fingertips trembled, and her voice sounded as though it were mixed with tears.

"The role given to Betty was to maintain the archive of knowledge. To keep protecting this place until the reunion that would eventually come... I suppose."

"The archive of knowledge... you mean here?"

Standing up, Subaru surveyed the mass of bookshelves filling the room. All the books slotted into place—the contents of the archive, some of which Subaru himself had reached for before—he'd always assumed were books House Mathers owned. But—

"So all the books here, too, are Echidna's books?"

"She was... someone who loved collecting knowledge, I suppose."

"Given that love took her so far she calls herself a Witch over it, I'd believe that."

She was someone who called herself the embodiment of the thirst for knowledge, after all. Boasting that she wanted to know everything in this world—it was, indeed, a fittingly greedy attitude, Subaru thought. The enormous number of books filling the shelves were, in that sense, the fruits of her collecting of knowledge. Beatrice had been the librarian entrusted with Echidna's storehouse of that knowledge.

"From the way you're talking... it sounds like, somehow, you've entered the mausoleum, I suppose."

"Yeah, I went in. Had a rough time of it, went through some pain too... but I'm glad now that I went. Echidna's a double-edged sword as a confidante, though."

As someone he could confide his Return by Death in, she was a precious resource—but acting on that ended up summoning the Witch of Envy as a result. Since it required dying at least once as a prerequisite, avoiding it if at all possible was his honest preference. Though he'd do it again if he had to.

"...Earlier, you said the Gospel hasn't told you the future in years."

"It's the truth, I suppose."

"I'm not doubting it, exactly. No—actually, I guess I am doubting it. Because, right? If that weren't true, then you wouldn't have..."

—Helped Subaru, over and over, all this time.

In the loop before last, there had been a parting between Subaru and Beatrice. Learning of the existence of the Gospel Beatrice possessed, for the first time, in that moment, had dealt Subaru a shock that was anything but small. Because it had nearly convinced him that everything—all of Beatrice's actions, her feelings, all of it—had only existed because the Gospel had dictated it, and that there was no room in any of it for her own thoughts or feelings to have entered in. And so, even standing now before a girl utterly crushed, Subaru felt, deep inside himself, a relief so clear he could recognize its presence with certainty.

Knowing that Beatrice's actions up to now reflected her own heart brought him a sense of relief. Why that should bring Subaru such comfort, he didn't know. Even without understanding the root reason, this much Subaru believed: that he wanted, without needing a reason, to keep being on the receiving end of Beatrice's goodwill. What it was about him that made him feel that way toward her, he couldn't say.

"Why did you... help me, all those times? It wasn't written in the Gospel, was it? You could've just left me be."

It was a roundabout, unpleasant way to ask, and he knew it even as he said it. It was as if he were leaving the answer up to Beatrice, but Subaru understood exactly what he wanted. And understanding that, he hated his own cowardice for choosing to phrase it this way. What Subaru had really wanted to ask Beatrice, simply and plainly, was this:

—Aren't you on my side?

"Betty... helped you... cooperated with you, because..."

"Yeah. You helped me out in all kinds of situations. The Jaguarnaut's curse, for one. Treating me when I was on the verge of dying, that too. Telling me the truth when I was supposed to just die from that curse, no questions asked."

There were other times, too, more than Subaru could count, when Beatrice had saved him. In the loop that had originated from that mansion, when he'd been killed by Rem and could no longer trust anyone living there, it was Beatrice and Emilia alone who had saved him. He hadn't forgotten how Beatrice had protected him through that time, tormented by isolation and fear. A bond no longer remaining anywhere in this world, existing only within Subaru now—an unforgettable, certain bond. So—

"You, apart from any Gospel or anything like that, you..."

"—At the very end, it told me something, I suppose."

Wouldn't Beatrice throw away every problem standing between them and take his side? Someone he could entrust with his whole, unreserved trust—having lost Rem, unable to show Emilia his own weakness, wouldn't Beatrice become that person for him?

It was, in a sense, far too selfish a wish. And then,

"That eventually, 'that person' would come to visit Betty's archive. To protect the archive until then."

"...That person?"

"That's what it told her, I suppose. Until 'that person' comes—that's the role given to Betty, to guard the forbidden archive until then. Whether you're 'that person' or not, Betty doesn't know, I suppose."

Subaru, who had been looking at Beatrice with heat in his eyes, felt that heat drain away at her gloomy tone, his brow creasing at the ominous turn things were taking. He had no idea what Beatrice was about to say. He didn't know. He didn't know—but he felt, somehow, that she shouldn't be allowed to say what came next.

"Betty doesn't know. Whether you're 'that person' or not... but"

"Wait, Beatrice. Both of us—we're rushing this too much. Let's calm down a little more..."

"Whether you're 'that person' or not... it doesn't matter anymore, I think, I suppose."

Beatrice, who had been looking down, raised her face. Her two ringlets swayed with the motion of her head. Wavering, uncertain, as if mirroring both her own heart and Subaru's. A bad premonition tightened around his chest. Unable to shake it off,

"Even if you're not 'that person,' it doesn't matter to me anymore, I suppose. So—"

"Bea—"

"I want you to kill Betty, and end this contract, I suppose. End the end of the end, and set Betty free, I suppose."

Beatrice's eyes glistened, her lips curving into a faint, powerless smile.

"You— become 'that person' for me—"
