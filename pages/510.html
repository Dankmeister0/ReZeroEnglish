Arc 7 Chapter 13: "Not Born to Be a God"

——Sway, stagger, and reel.

Consciousness swayed slowly.
Like being on a ship crossing the great sea, rocking left and right, back and forth.
The faltering consciousness felt heavy, and even with eyelids closed, the world seemed to spin.

Not enough. Everything was lacking.
Blood, flesh, bone, peace, anger, joy—all the various things that make up a person were lacking.
As if it had all spilled out somewhere along the way, dripping away, not enough.

I have to pick it all back up, came the hazy thought.
Pick it up again, pack it back in, and then, once more, stand up.

There was a place he had to go, and a wish to go there.
There was a reason he had to live, and a wish crying out to live.

So, so, so.
Even if everything was lacking, even remaining incomplete and lacking pieces, he had to go forward.
For that purpose, Natsuki Subaru——

△▼△▼△▼△

"——Ugh."

The instant consciousness surfaced, Natsuki Subaru twisted his lips at the sticky sensation on his own face.
There was a feeling of dampness clinging from his face down to his chest. The lukewarm, wet sensation was close to the feel of night sweat on the morning after a bad dream.
Combined with the dull, heavy feeling in his head, he might have been running a fever.

"...An unfamiliar ceiling."

Muttering that like it was practically obligatory, the sight before him was a crude wooden ceiling.
Far from any solid architectural style, it was a makeshift hut, its timbers combined with considerable boldness. A work born of brute force papering over unrefined technique—Subaru's thoughts stalled on it for a moment.
Trying to recall what had happened, why he was in a makeshift hut, he slowly reeled in his memories.

"Let's see, I stepped out of the convenience store, blinked, and I was in another world, and there I met Emilia-tan—and then it's kind of long so I'll skip ahead."

A monologue meant for no one's ears, but cracking jokes helped his head start turning again.
Yes, the boy Natsuki Subaru, summoned to another world, had met an ultra-gorgeous silver-haired girl, gone on to have all sorts of grand adventures, finally conquered the Sand Tower, and been sent flying to a neighboring country.

"Even saying it myself, it makes no sense..."

In any case, the situation had continued to be outlandish enough to make him want to groan.
And searching his memory, he still had no recollection of this shabby ceiling.

"The only real candidate was the Sanctuary, but even when Garfiel had me locked up, the building was at least properly built."

Garfiel, who now adorably called him "Boss, Boss," but the memory of being tied hand and foot and locked up by him remained firmly lodged in Subaru even in this lost world.
Not that he held a grudge, exactly, but being held captive was such a rare experience that it wasn't the kind of thing you could easily forget even if you tried.
Still, even that miserable environment didn't match up with the memory of this ceiling.
Somehow or other, he had apparently been living his otherworldly life in places of a reasonably high level of civilization—Subaru, a modern man at heart, felt grateful for one of his few strokes of luck.

I wish I could've realized that in a somewhat nicer setting, he thought ruefully——

"——!"

And then, as he sorted the jumbled contents of his mind piece by piece, with no particular trigger, a shock like lightning ran through Subaru.
Right—this wasn't the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica. This was the Vollachia Empire.
Flung unexpectedly into a neighboring country, with nothing they could rely on, Subaru and the others——

"Rem...!"

The girl he had to protect no matter what had come to this country along with him.
And on top of that, Subaru had been separated from her, and Rem was still under the control of dangerous men——

"Am I an idiot? No, I am an idiot...! This isn't the time for this..."

"——What are you making such a racket about?"

"——Ah."

Just as he abruptly sat up, about to charge off in search of Rem on pure momentum, a hard voice thrown from the side made Subaru catch his breath.
He'd sat up from what appeared to be a bed just as shabby as the ceiling above it, and beside him, a pair of blue eyes was watching him with a narrow, wary look.
It was a girl with short-cropped blue hair, her lovely eyes narrowed sharply as she looked at Subaru.

"R-Rem...?"

"—. Answering 'yes' wouldn't really be affirmative. I haven't yet acknowledged that I am the person you call Rem."

At her hard tone, her voice frozen as much as she could manage, Subaru's eyes went quietly wide.
But she was here, right in front of him, speaking—and as proof that this wasn't a dream or illusion, he could feel her warmth, her soft scent. Yes, even the warmth of her hand.

"Wait, hand?"

"————"

Subaru suddenly noticed, beneath the ragged cloth draped over him, that his right hand was clasped around the girl's—Rem's—hand, and he froze in shock.
He had absolutely no memory of what had happened to cause that.

"Don't tell me—you held my hand until I woke up?"

"Huh? Please don't say such disgusting things. Isn't it obvious just from looking? You were the one gripping my hand and refusing to let go."

"Ah, ah, right. Yeah, that's right. I'm the one holding your hand..."

Unable to tell expectation from reality, Subaru only added fuel to Rem's foul mood.
It seemed that while unconscious, Subaru had been gripping Rem's hand tightly all along. —Though he did have some feelings about the fact that she hadn't shaken it off.

"What's with that look?"

"N-no, nothing. Nope."

"Is that so. Then please let go already. My hand is getting slick with your sweat."

"A devastating blow to an adolescent boy...!"

Said by a cute girl, it was the kind of strike that could leave some men wounded for life.
Fortunately, Subaru was tough, so he only barely survived, but as he timidly released her hand, Rem pulled it to her chest and wiped it on her clothes, delivering a follow-up blow.
But setting aside Subaru's inner turmoil for the moment——

"Rem, are you hurt anywhere? If anything hurts, don't hide it, tell me."

"Huh?"

"Eh, what's with that face... Did I say something weird...?"

Setting aside their earlier exchange, Subaru wanted to make sure Rem was safe. But to his concerned question about her wellbeing, Rem's response was bitterly cold.
Startled, Subaru panicked, wondering if he'd put his foot in his mouth again, but he couldn't think of what.
It should have been just an ordinary expression of concern for Rem.

"...You were the one in far worse condition. You were on the verge of dying, and you're not even aware of that?"

"Aware of it, well, um..."

"I see, so you weren't. —As I thought, I can't trust you."

Bluntly, looking him in the eye, she threw the words of rejection at him, and Subaru swallowed hard.
Having lost her memories, with the scent of the Miasma clinging to Subaru only reinforcing the bad impression, Rem's attitude toward him remained consistently hard and cold.
And this time, there had been no chance for Subaru and Rem to close the gap between them.
All Subaru had done was desperately try to rescue Rem from the imperial camp——

"——Ah."

Another missing piece of memory slotted into place within Subaru, and his brain seemed to shudder with the illusion of it.
This shock, too, was every bit as significant as Rem's very existence.

Held captive in the imperial camp, he had gambled everything to rescue Rem.
As a result, he had encountered a tribe living in the forest—the "People of Shudrak"—and together with a male prisoner who'd shared his cell, he had undergone their tribe's ritual.
And then——

"...Not black anymore. My right hand's normal."

He lifted his right arm and murmured that, looking at the missing sleeve and the arm peeking out from it.
The hideous black pattern that had risen on Subaru's right arm—an aftereffect from his encounter with a Witch Cult Archbishop of Sin in the water-locked city of Priestella—had never faded before, but now it was gone without a trace.

——That black pattern had healed the tattered wound on his right arm.

It was a nightmarish memory, but it seemed that hadn't been a dream or a nightmare after all. Well, it had been a nightmare, but apparently a real event nonetheless.
His ruined right arm had been healed, and Rem was here at his bedside.
In other words, Subaru had survived and completed the "Lifeblood Ritual," and had succeeded in getting Rem back. —At the cost of many sacrifices, instead.

"————"

"...You look pale. I think you should still be resting."

Looking down at his unnaturally pristine right arm, Subaru fell silent, and Rem spoke up seeing him like that.
Even toward someone she didn't view favorably, if he looked unwell she would still show concern—likely a result of Rem's innate kindness. Or perhaps his expression had simply looked that much like a corpse's.

There was a part of him that wanted to indulge in that kindness.
But he couldn't allow himself to. There was far too much he needed to confirm.

"Thanks for worrying about me. But there's a lot I need to ask about. ...I want to ask first—this is one of the Shudrak's homes, right?"

"That's how they refer to themselves, it seemed."

"I see. ...Then I'd like to see Mizelda and the others. Also—"

It wasn't something he wanted to ask about, but there was no way around confirming it.
Rem, who had been held captive in the imperial camp, was here now. Which meant he had to wonder about the whereabouts of the person who should have been captive there alongside her—Rui.

To put it plainly, he was worried.
Not about Rui's safety, but about the damage that could result from leaving Rui unattended.

"I'll ask honestly—that girl, Rui, where is she?"

"—. What a bitter expression. Why do you keep that child at arm's length?"

"That's hard to explain, and there's a decent chance you wouldn't understand even if I did."

Any exchange concerning Rui was bound to earn Rem's displeasure.
Subaru felt that keenly, but he also didn't think explaining it would get through to her.

"Anyway, where is she? Don't tell me, at the camp—"

"—Look to your left and you'll understand."

"Huh?"

Wondering what she meant, Subaru's eyes went round, and he turned that same round-eyed gaze to his left—the side opposite Rem, who sat on his right.
And looking that way, his face stiffened with a belated shock.
There, was——

"Zzz, zzz..."

"Wha—ah..."

Rui, peacefully baring an innocent sleeping face.
She lay on Subaru's left side, wrapped in the same ragged cloth on the same bed. Drool dripped from her wide-open mouth, and finally, seeing that unseemly sight, understanding caught up with him.

The true identity of the sticky, wet sensation on Subaru's chest and face the moment he'd woken up was——

"————!!"

A wordless scream tore out of Subaru.

△▼△▼△▼△

"Ohh, Subaru! Looks like ya've woken up fine!"

Guided by Rem, Subaru showed his face in the plaza, and it was Mizelda who greeted him.
The young chieftain of the "People of Shudrak," her black hair dyed red, came over toward Subaru as he emerged from the makeshift hut and looked him up and down.
Her forthright manner, bordering on impudent but with a bracing energy to it, drew a faint wry smile from Subaru.

"Thanks to you, I managed to make it back alive somehow. Seems I had you worried too, Mizelda."

"Don' worry 'bout it. If ya'd died, we'd just have sent a brave comrade's soul back to the heavens and buried the body in the earth. Since that didn' happen, I'm glad yer soul stayed put."

"Mizelda..."

Crossing her sturdy arms, Mizelda puffed out her tan, towering chest.
Her unadorned words struck him in the chest, and Subaru felt a quiet swell of emotion. Honestly, when he'd been captured by the "People of Shudrak" and thrown in a cell, he'd braced himself to die all over again.
And to Subaru feeling so moved, Mizelda narrowed her eyes and said, "By the way—"

"Shame ya already got someone. How 'bout addin' one more, on top o' Rem an' Rui?"

"Mizelda!"

At Mizelda's somewhat mischievous proposal, Rem's voice rose sharply beside Subaru.
Leaning on a newly cut wooden staff, her legs still unsteady, Rem's expression was severe. Rui, just woken, clung to her arm, but at Rem's raised voice she blinked in surprise.
Stroking Rui's blonde hair, Rem called Mizelda's name once more and said,

"You go too far. I neither trust nor understand this man."

"Then, might I take him off yer hands?"

"Yes, of course. He's yours."

"My opinion isn't being factored in at all!"
"Ah-uu!"

With a prim, aloof attitude, Rem readily agreed to Mizelda's proposal to hand him over.
Subaru hastily called a stop to it, and just riding the momentum of the moment, Rui shouted along too.
Hearing that, Mizelda laughed, saying "Jokin', jokin'," but witnessing her easy manner up close, Subaru couldn't help but be amazed.

The period right after the "Lifeblood Ritual" was fairly hazy even to Subaru himself.
And what came after that, too—he couldn't clearly separate what was genuine memory from what might just be his own convenient wishful thinking. Still, Mizelda's manner didn't seem to be an illusion.

"Guess this means we managed to build a good relationship right from our first meeting, for once. That's nice, but..."

"Hm, ya look pale. Somethin' wrong with ya? I can cut off whatever part's bad, if ya want."

"I'll pass on making that request—the vibe suggests it'd be a bad idea to carelessly say 'please do.'"

Probably not a joke, but a genuine offer.
If he carelessly said "please," it seemed like things could become irreversible.
In any case——

"Last time, I didn't really have the chance to look around slowly."

Saying that, Subaru turned to survey the settlement of the "People of Shudrak."
The settlement, deep in the heart of a vast forest, starting with the makeshift hut where Subaru had woken, had a worldview where the level of what one might call civilization had simply stopped developing.
It was a scene that fit perfectly with the image of an indigenous people living in an unexplored jungle.
Amazons with few men, living by hunting—there wasn't a sign of civilization to be found in the settlement, every bit the picture of untamed wilderness.

"With so few people, you can live off daily hunting... but, I—"

"————"

Biting his lip, murmuring with his brows knit painfully, Subaru trailed off.
Rem watched his profile in silence, her expression complicated. What she was thinking, in the depths of those blue eyes, Subaru couldn't read right now.
Not being able to read it was frightening. He couldn't easily smile back even at Mizelda, who had laughed for him.
So, to break through that situation——

"Mizelda, I want to talk. —I think I need to talk to him, too."

"...Right. Good timin'. He should be at the meeting hall with Taritta an' the others right now."

Hearing Subaru's words, Mizelda nodded and jerked her chin.
What she indicated was a large building at the back of the plaza—what was called the meeting hall. Presumably a place that served the role its name suggested; whether or not it was the finest building in the settlement, it seemed to have been given the largest one for that purpose.

"—Let's go."

Fixing his destination, Subaru turned his feet that way.
As a matter of course, he offered his hand to help Rem, but she refused it. Bringing Rui along, she made clear through her actions that she would walk under her own power.
Subaru gave a wry smile at Rem's unchanging attitude and tightened his expression.
Then, Subaru and the others passed through the entrance of the meeting hall and went inside.

There——

"So you're finally awake. What a fine life you've been living, Natsuki Subaru."

He was greeted by that voice—a masked man sitting cross-legged on the ground of the meeting hall.

△▼△▼△▼△

In the center of the meeting hall, among the Shudrak gathered around a lit fire, one man was mixed in.
At the man's welcome, Subaru's cheek twitched, and words failed him.

"————"

"What's wrong? Surely you won't claim you lost your voice as the price for barely surviving? Though perhaps that would be a fitting cost to pay for such a result."

"No, I haven't lost my voice. I haven't lost that, but I have lost the words for this. ...What the hell is that mask?"

Saying that, Subaru pointed at the face of the man speaking with such insolence.
The face the finger indicated, covered by what looked like a red-and-white painted "oni mask." Though, since the "Oni Tribe" existed as an actual race in this world, it probably wasn't literally meant to be an oni's face.
Still, it was a mask modeled after something with a fearsome visage, and the man was wearing it.

At Subaru's remark, the man responded with a bored "Ah," and said,

"A gift I was presented with. I'd already intended to keep my face hidden going forward anyway. If anything, the timing was convenient. I, too, found it tiresome to rewrap my face every time I washed it."

"Sounds like it'd get itchy when it gets dirty... wait, that's not what I'm here to talk about."

Twisting his cheek at the man's nonchalant attitude, Subaru strode right up in front of him.
At that, Taritta, one of those sitting in the circle with the man, tried to stop him with an "Oi."
But——

"Taritta, stop. This one has business with me, it seems."

"Y-yes, sir... but..."

"If I say it's fine, it's fine. Is there some other word needed?"

Restrained by the man himself, Taritta shook her head and backed off.
In such a short time, he seemed to have won considerable trust from the "People of Shudrak." —No, that attitude was less trust and more, appropriately, domination.
In Taritta's manner there was not fear or trust, but reverence. And that was true, to no small degree, of the other Shudrak gathered in the same space as well.
What method he'd used, Subaru couldn't begin to imagine.

"For now, I want to talk with you, just the two of us. —Vincent Abelux."

"————"

Standing before him, looking down at the seated man, Subaru made that statement. Hearing it, how the man's expression changed, Subaru couldn't see behind the mask.
But the air of the meeting hall subtly tightened, and there was the illusion of the perceived temperature dropping.
It was a pressure that nearly made him gasp, but Subaru endured it through sheer force of will.
And then——

"The first time, I forgave you, since you were delirious. But do not make me say the same thing to you twice. Know that there will not be a third time. Refrain from carelessly speaking my name."

"...And if I say I refuse?"

"I will mete out an appropriate punishment. I know any number of ways to make you cry uncle."

The man rose to his feet there, glaring straight at Subaru face to face.
Judging from the atmosphere and bearing of his statement, there was no lie in the man's words. Nor was there likely any bluff to it. Even with limited means and methods, this man would surely make good on whatever punishment he'd voiced.

"You're one irritating bastard..."

"Then shall I make you say it a third time?"

"—. No, I'll pass on that. I didn't come here to argue. —Abel."

Realizing there was no point in being stubborn, Subaru relented first.
And so he decided that, for the masked man—following his warning, for now—he would call him Abel. At Subaru's decision, Abel gave a nod, saying, "Wise."

"Had you not backed down, blood would have been shed."

"Yeah, sure, whatever. Not to brag, but I think if you and I actually threw down, it'd be a pretty close, good fight."

"Then it's you who should look behind you."

"Behind...?"

Taking it as a provocation, Subaru wrinkled his nose at Abel's words. Following the instruction and turning around, Subaru trembled his shoulders with an "Ugh."
There stood Rem, watching the two men's exchange with a look of utter disdain.

"U-um, Rem? That face is..."

"Nothing? It's just that, despite nearly dying and sleeping for three whole days, you seem to be stubbornly refusing to take care of your body. Maybe you should just go ahead and die in a ditch somewhere."

"Sorry, my bad, I won't do it again!"

Crushed by Rem's icy look, Subaru desperately apologized.
In the end, he couldn't get Rem's cold expression to budge, but Subaru suddenly realized something.
Belatedly, but if there hadn't been some kind of special elixir, the reason a near-dead Subaru had managed to survive was most likely healing magic.
If that were the case, then the one who'd done it was——

"Including how he's being treated, I suppose I must speak with you about that as well."

"Abel..."

Apparently reading Subaru's inner thoughts from the way he'd caught his breath, Abel lightly shrugged and turned to face Mizelda.

"Mizelda, have everyone step back. Just me and this man will do."

"Ahh, well, bossy, ain't ya. If ya weren't such a looker, I'd be gettin' mad."

"Sister, please get mad even at a looker..."

At Abel's instruction, Mizelda readily complied, and seeing her sister's manner, Taritta's shoulders slumped.
Though, given her own recent behavior, Taritta wasn't exactly in a strong position to say so herself.

Once the chieftain Mizelda had been persuaded, there was no reason for the other Shudrak to remain.
One after another, everyone headed out of the meeting hall. The question was whether Rem, and incidentally Rui, would be swept along in that tide as well——

"It would surely be inconvenient for the conversation you wish to have if that woman were present."

"Ngh..."

Realizing even his hesitation had been seen through, Subaru caught his breath at Abel's words.
But in the end, what he'd said was correct. He didn't want Rem to hear what came next.

"Rem, could you go out with everyone else? It won't take long... well, I don't know if it'll be quick, but I don't intend to take too much time."

"The way you say that, it sounds as though I'm the one being unreasonable."

"That wasn't my intention... so, will you go out for me?"

"...And if I say no, what will you do?"

"Eh!?"

Having thought he had his hands full just dealing with Abel, Subaru was stunned by Rem's words.
He quickly realized it was a jab mocking his earlier exchange with Abel, but Subaru was weak against being pressed by Rem.
If he could, he wanted to grant Rem anything she wished for.
However——

"—. ——. This is something I'd rather you not hear, I think."

"I could stubbornly refuse to move, you know."

"Then... then it's unfair, but I'll run away."

At the pace Rem could manage with her staff, Subaru could outrun her if he ran.
That alone was clearly a point where Subaru currently had the edge over Rem. Though not being able to think of any other such point right off the bat was, he had to admit, rather pathetic.

"Uu—"

Before Subaru, agonizing over this, Rui tugged at Rem's arm with a low growl. It seemed she was trying to lead Rem out of the meeting hall.
Seeing Rui like that, Rem gave a small laugh and said,

"I'm sorry, I've worried you, haven't I. It's just that I felt a little like being sarcastic toward this smelly man, that's all."

"Aw-uu!"

"Smelly man..."

More than any sarcasm, that one line pierced deeper and sharper, but never mind that for now.
True to what she'd said for Rui's benefit, Rem readily withdrew with a "Well then," and left the meeting hall together with Rui, leaning on her staff.

Seeing off her back, which didn't even turn to look, Subaru scratched his cheek.
Whether Rem genuinely had no interest in him at all, or whether she'd said that out of consideration for him, he couldn't tell.
Of course, he'd be happier if it were the latter.

"I've come in with the worst possible impression. I won't aim too high—that's my lifestyle..."

"Pointless patience and conceit. —Sit. I'll hear what you have to say."

And so, with the meeting hall now containing just the two of them, at Abel's word Subaru plopped himself down on the ground, sitting cross-legged.
Across from him, Abel likewise sat on the ground, knees drawn up, watching him through the mask.
With the burning fire placed between them, the two faced each other across the flames.

"First, what I want to know is how much of it was a dream, and how much was real."

"Hah. That's exactly the kind of question no one but you could possibly know the answer to. What do you want me to answer? Would you be satisfied to hear that it was all a fleeting dream, and events proceeded peacefully?"

"There was a kid named Utakata, so 'fleeting' gets kinda confusing there..."

Among the "People of Shudrak," there had been one young girl, and she should have been Utakata.
It was a confusing pun with the word for "fleeting," but that wasn't the essence of Abel's remark. —No, rather, Subaru understood the essence perfectly well, and was fleeing into that flippant joke instead.
And Abel did not let Subaru's faintheartedness slide.

"I have no intention of indulging your cowardice, Natsuki Subaru."

"...Yeah, I know. —The attack on the imperial camp. Was that real?"

"Of course it was. The imperial camp deployed outside Badheim was utterly annihilated, by the strength of the Shudrak. What you saw was neither illusion nor anything of the sort."

——Once more, from Abel's own mouth, it was stated that it had been real.
Hearing that, something heavy sank into the depths of Subaru's chest. He bit down hard on his lip, using the pain to hold back the whitening haze threatening his consciousness.
Such a convenient escape was not something Natsuki Subaru was permitted.

"...I understand what you're saying. You led the 'People of Shudrak,' and attacked the imperial camp. And you drove them off. That's the gist of it, right?"

"Yes. But that alone is insufficient. Allow me to supplement it myself."

"Supplement?"

"I commanded, and the Shudrak destroyed the camp. And moreover, our side suffered no losses whatsoever."

"—. So, this is a brag about how amazing your command ability is?"

"No, that is not it. —This is your achievement."

Shaking his head as he said that, Abel drew a "huh?" of wide-eyed surprise from Subaru.
It was a statement he hadn't expected at all. Not just the fact that Abel was acknowledging Subaru's achievement, but Subaru couldn't even conceive of a reason he should be praised in the first place.
Wondering just what he was talking about——

"You don't understand? The reason Shudrak and I could win without a scratch is that we were able to know the enemy's disposition down to the last detail. From none other than your own mouth."

"——Huh?"

Resting his cheek on his hand atop a raised knee, Abel's words brought Subaru's thoughts to a halt.
Not merely unexpected—words that struck him from completely outside anything he could have predicted. Unable to properly take in the meaning, Subaru opened and closed his mouth several times——

"Disposition and placement, roughly knowing the enemy's personnel—that raises the certainty of a plan to bring down the enemy. In fact, we won without loss on our side. That is your contribution. I've had you rewarded for it, too."

"Re...ward..."

"Your woman was safely rescued. I repay those who work for me. There is no way to repay the dead. I thought to do it while you still had breath in you, but... hmph, you're a man of stubborn luck."

Even being told he had "stubborn luck," Subaru remained dumbstruck.
Perhaps, to Abel, that was meant as a compliment, a form of praise. But unfortunately, Subaru had neither the capacity to receive it as such, nor was such a culture ingrained in him.
Of course not. —Why should he be glad to have been useful as a tool of war?

"Why would I have talked about the camp at all..."

"A side effect of the medicinal herbs. Having completed the 'Lifeblood Ritual,' you were on the verge of death. It was necessary to keep you alive until the camp fell and the woman was rescued. To that end, the medicine I administered kept your mind fixed in a delirious state. Hence—"

"I just... babbled on, answering whatever I was asked...?"

Aghast, Subaru pressed both hands to his own face, his voice trembling.
Certainly, Subaru had had a rough grasp of the inside of the camp. He had once spent several days there as a servant boy.
He knew where things were, and roughly how many people there had been. He'd known the location of weapons and tools too, so he understood how a preemptive strike targeting those would create an overwhelming advantage.
He understood it—but so what if he understood it?

"Drugging me—give me a break! Using that without my permission! You..."

"But without it, you would have died without ever reuniting with the woman. In other words, the woman's healing magic would never have reached you. There is no cause to be reviled for keeping a dying man alive."

"O-of course there's cause...! I never wanted to be complicit in a war! All those people, dying... and yet, you!"

"—You are laboring under some kind of misunderstanding."

Breathing hard, in a posture of denouncing evil, Subaru was met with Abel's flat pronouncement.
Pierced by that cold tone, Subaru's cheek stiffened.

"Misunderstanding? What am I supposedly misunderstanding?"

"Suppose, hypothetically, your mind had not been clouded by the drug. Even so, you would still have needed the strength of the Shudrak to rescue that woman. Naturally, you would have divulged whatever knowledge you possessed and done your utmost. ...Which means you would have spoken of the camp's disposition regardless."

"Ah, ngh..."

"You understand, don't you. The result is the same. Whether or not you were dying, in the end it was your knowledge that exposed the camp's secrets, and led them to their deaths."

Faced with Abel's pointed logic, Subaru tried to search for some way to argue back.
But what he said was correct, and Subaru could easily picture how things would naturally have unfolded in that case as well.

In fact, had he survived the "Lifeblood Ritual" under better circumstances and the talk turned to devising a strategy to save Rem, Subaru surely would have told them about the imperial camp.
He would have shared the layout and personnel as material for working out a way to rescue Rem.

"But in that case, I'd have been part of the war council. If there was a plan that was going to cause deaths, I'd have absolutely opposed it. So..."

"You think you could have persuaded them? With no means but killing, could you have persuaded people you know nothing about, found some better method, and rescued the woman peacefully, without a single death?"

"That's..."

"I'll tell you what that is. —That is called a fairy tale."

Pierced by Abel's words, Subaru's soul screamed, bleeding.
He wanted to shout that it wasn't true, to deny Abel's words. But all that came out were baseless appeals to emotion, and they couldn't crack Abel's mask, couldn't move his expression at all.

What they were looking at was different. Their ways of living diverged.
An unbridgeable difference in outlook on life and death lay between Subaru and Abel, and the "People of Shudrak." And that wall could not be removed, could not be broken through.

At least, not in the short time before Rem would have been lost. Absolutely not.
Even Subaru himself had come to be convinced of that.

"...Even so, I didn't want to give up."

"In place of your refusal to give up, someone other than you dies. It may be a stranger with no connection to you whatsoever. Or it may be someone like your other half. To stop and wallow in foolish deliberation is to permit that outcome."

Gritting his teeth, cursing an intolerable reality, Subaru was pressed further still by Abel.
In exchange for that severity, Abel might have secured the result he did. But by what right did he have to choose whose life would be sacrificed instead?

"Who the hell do you think you are. You think you're some kind of god or something...?"

"Fool. I am neither god nor hero. Nor, of course, am I like that wicked Observer looking down on this world. —I am a king. A king among kings."

"————"

"The common folk call the one who stands at the summit an Emperor. —I am that."

Grandly, placing a hand on his own chest, Abel declared it.
Beyond the mask, his hidden expression could not be seen. But the one time Subaru had glimpsed Abel's bare face, he could picture it now—wearing a bold, fearless smile, eyes blazing.

Called an Emperor, Subaru even forgot to catch his breath.
With such overwhelming, imposing dignity, he had proven his own existence through words alone.
And to the frozen Subaru, Abel—Vincent Vollachia—continued, his voice retaining all its authority.

"—The Sacred Vollachia Empire's seventy-seventh Emperor. That is what I am."

"————"

"Although, at present, I am one who has been cast down from the summit, and fallen to the wilds."
