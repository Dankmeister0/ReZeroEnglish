Arc 3 Chapter 16: "Statement of Conviction"

Miklotov's declaration rang out, dignity incarnate, and tension drew taut across the hall.

It wasn't as though the exchanges up to this point had been slack, but it was true that the proceedings had gone forward with the Council of Wise Men — the apex of the nation — treated as something of an afterthought. Now, with Miklotov abruptly reasserting his presence, the eyes of everyone in the throne room were naturally drawn to him.

Sensing this, Miklotov stroked his beard, unshaken, and spoke.

"In proposing that the Council of Wise Men convene, I would first ask for the assent of my fellow councilors."

At the center of the nine seats arrayed on the dais, Miklotov surveyed the elders around him. At his words, even the old men who until now had said nothing and all but faded into the background nodded their assent.

"To Lord Miklotov's proposal, I likewise lend my assent, by the authority of the Council of Wise Men."

"Likewise."

"Likewise, let us assent."

At the elders' agreement, Miklotov dipped his chin, then looked down at the candidates below.

The five who had until then maintained their somewhat adversarial positioning hurried to assemble before Miklotov's silent gaze. Anastasia in particular, who had drifted furthest away, broke into a dead sprint.

As the candidates regathered, the two who had naturally drifted to their sides now peeled away as well, falling back in line with Subaru and the others on the knights' side. — With Al and Reinhard flanking Subaru, it was unbearably awkward.

"Do I really have to stand here?"

"Personally I'm glad you're here — makes for good padding, y'know? Honestly, picking a fight with the Sword Saint ain't happening. For real."

Al made a show of wiping his brow, the very picture of trembling dread. He had his helmet on and his voice hadn't changed tone at all, so it was presumably meant as banter — but for Subaru, who had seen Reinhard's combat prowess up close, it wasn't something he could just laugh off.

Al himself had gone out of his way to call himself a mercenary, serving as Priscilla's bodyguard despite having only one arm. He probably had some confidence in his own skill, but given that he was a fellow countryman from the same era, it was hard to imagine he'd stepped into inhuman territory the way Reinhard had.

Just as he'd said himself: if it came to a fight, he wouldn't last a minute.

"Come to think of it, maybe Elsa really was seriously skilled after all..."

He recalled the woman who, against Reinhard of all people, had managed to produce something that could, however tenuously, be called a proper swordfight. At the same time the pain of his slashed stomach seemed to come welling back, and Subaru grimaced — then quickly stopped himself.

"Her skill was the real thing. We still haven't picked up her trail since. I'm afraid we can't yet see her brought to justice for the grievous wound she dealt you."

"I just finished cutting off my own flashback scene, don't go and dig it back up. Ah, now even my eye — the one she slashed clean across — feels like it's throbbing again..."

Thinking back on it, of all his deaths so far, the second one felt like it had been the most gruesome. Though the ones that hurt more emotionally came later in the story.

At the sight of Subaru rubbing his eyes at high speed, working them bloodshot, Reinhard turned a look of concern on him.

"Are you all right? If it would help, I know a skilled practitioner of water magic. She's right nearby, in fact..."

"Mm, we're about to get into the important stuff, so hush now, nya. I'll heal you up later."

"It's a strange thing for me to say, but don't you two operate at a rather... unhurried pace of your own?"

Ferris picked up on Reinhard's remark, and Julius gently reproached him for it.

Subaru had always had confidence in his own unhurried pace, but he found himself thinking, with a distinctly detached air, that the four people around him were each pretty formidable in their own right too.

While the candidates radiated tension and their attendants exchanged this rather leisurely banter, the convening of the Council of Wise Men was formally declared — Miklotov nodded.

"My thanks for my colleagues' assent. Now then, shall we move to discussion. The topic, of course, is 'who shall be made king' — but—"

He paused mid-sentence, combing his beard, then the old man closed one eye.

"Hmm. The question is how we're to decide that. The Dragon History Stone instructed us to gather five candidates, but it made no mention of the method of selection thereafter. Put in extreme terms, we could have every faction here compete in arms, and make whichever faction remains standing the king."

"With that method, it does seem like one faction would come out on top rather thoroughly, doesn't it?"

Roswaal's glib remark answered Miklotov's jesting words from among the ranks of the civil officials.

The faction that glib remark pointed to was one and only one — the faction backing Felt, with Reinhard at its side. Surely aware of this, Miklotov laughed knowingly.

"Even the head court mage himself has someone he'd rather not face, does he?"

"There's a certain person against whom my one and only gift simply doesn't work, you see. Regrettably, the moment it came to facing off, that would be it — game over, sayona-a-ara."

Roswaal shrugged and shook his head. Even Reinhard, formidable as he was, ducked his head as if abashed at this — but the fact that he didn't dispute it suggested he had confidence of his own in the matter.

Knowing firsthand just how formidable Roswaal was as a mage, Subaru had his doubts as to whether there was really such an insurmountable gap between the two of them. But if the two people involved had both accepted it as fact, then it presumably was an immovable truth.

More and more, Reinhard's sheer inhumanity stood out in Subaru's mind.

And then—

"Well, given that the head court mage has also voiced his objection, let us set aside so violent a solution. Hmm, so then, what do you all suppose would be best, everyone?"

Withdrawing his greeting-like banter, Miklotov steered the Council of Wise Men toward discussion.

The council members party to the matter exchanged glances, and then, in voices somewhat less carrying than Miklotov's, spoke quietly — yet somehow audibly to everyone all the same.

"We ought first to hear what the candidates themselves have to say, surely."

"This is the first time good fortune has allowed all of us to gather and meet face to face like this."

"Before now, even we of the Council of Wise Men could not always manage full attendance."

"Hence, let them speak. Each one's position, their resolve to become king, and what they intend to do once they have — that would seem a reasonable line of discussion."

"Hmm, most sensible. Well then, Knight Marcos, might I ask you to proceed?"

As a result of the council's deliberation, the conduct of the proceedings was once more entrusted to the knight commander.

The lone figure in armor who had never left the candidates' side bowed once, then turned back toward them, his craggy features hardening as he spoke.

"If I may be so bold, I shall once more conduct the proceedings. Each of the candidates surely has their own claims and their own position. The members of the Council of Wise Men, as well as the knights and officials present in this hall — all wish to hear them. If you would be so kind."

Speaking for the sentiments of everyone in the hall, Marcos bowed respectfully to the five candidates.

Then he raised his head, his stern features opening wide as he spoke.

"We shall begin, then, with Lady Crusch. — Knight Felix Argyle! Come forward!"

"Very well."

"Yes, sir~!"

At Marcos's call, Crusch nodded with unhurried composure, and Ferris raised a hand with a light, breezy air.

As Crusch stepped forward, Ferris trotted along to keep pace with her toward the center of the hall. On the way there, she fixed her gaze on Marcos's face.

"Commander. I keep telling you — it's not Felix, it's Ferris. Call me that, would you~? Feri-chan's feelings get hurt, you know."

"I have no intention of treating any of my subordinates as a special case. Naturally, that includes you. Move forward."

Marcos coldly rebuffed Ferris, who pleaded with a finger pressed to her cheek, and jerked his chin toward the spot beside Crusch, hurrying her along. Ferris stuck out her tongue in a petulant "bleh" to vent her displeasure at Marcos, then took her place beside Crusch, who stood with her arms crossed.

"Royal candidate, head of House Karsten, Crusch Karsten. A pleasure."

"Crusch-sama's first knight, of House Argyle — Ferris."

"Knight Felix Argyle, if it please the Council of Wise Men."

Crusch declared herself with unhesitating, dignified bearing, and Ferris followed suit with her characteristic breeziness. Marcos corrected her declaration as if reproving her, and Ferris's gaze stabbed at him like a blade — but Marcos, stolid and expressionless, simply ignored it.

Watching this exchange from the sidelines, Subaru gave a small murmur of "huh," and said,

"So that Ferris kid's real name is Felix, huh. Sounds like a pretty manly name, that."

In Japan too, in old warrior houses, it apparently used to be that the eldest child's name would be passed down regardless of whether the next heir was male or female. In those gal-game-style historical gender-bender stories, female versions of male generals running around with men's names were practically a genre convention. Japan's a real mess that way, honestly.

While Subaru arrived at this bit of understanding beside him, Al tilted his head slightly. Was this territory not so easily accepted by the common sense in his head? There was also the gap between the eras Subaru and he had each been summoned from to consider — it might be worth deepening their mutual understanding on such points a bit more, at some point.

"Subaru, weren't you listening?"

As Subaru mused to himself, glancing sidelong at Al, Reinhard turned to him with a faintly surprised look. Not grasping the point of the question, Subaru answered back with a blank "To what?"

"'Sounds like a manly name' — Ferris is a fine, upstanding man, through and through."

"—Wait."

"What is it?"

"What did you... just say."

Unable to accept the content of what Reinhard had said, Subaru's brain temporarily short-circuited. Slowly, chewing over each word, Subaru demanded that Reinhard repeat himself. Reinhard nodded magnanimously.

"'Sounds like a manly name' — Ferris is a fine, upstanding man, through and through."

Word for word, without a single change, he'd gone and said the important thing twice.

"AAAAAAAAAAAAAH?!"

Comprehension caught up with awareness, and the twin screams of Subaru and Al rang out through the hall.

The whole room's attention turned to the two of them at their outburst, but caught up in flailing gestures of pure confusion, neither of them noticed.

Moving his whole body in exaggerated motion, Subaru pointed at Ferris standing in the center of the room.

"Wait wait wait, that's a guy?! Even the Knight among Knights can't pull off a joke like that?! That's not funny!"

Even as he spoke, he found himself studying Ferris, who was now looking back this way.

He had thought her rather tall for a woman, true, but strip away the facial structure, the slenderness of the body, and even the height, and she looked like nothing but a woman. Granted, one couldn't deny a certain lack of feminine... contours — but plenty of grown women in the world were flat-chested too. That wasn't proof of anything.

"Voice's high, too, and slim build. Skin looks practically translucent. Even I can't believe that's a guy... no, I don't wanna believe it!"

"I feel you, bro!"

"Yeah, I feel you, bro!"

Al, one-armed, threw an arm around Subaru's shoulder with a firm clap, the two of them aligning their sense of reality.

Two were so much more reassuring than one alone. Surely, faced with this much solidarity, even Reinhard's unfunny joke would show some sign of remorse — just as Subaru was half-convinced of victory—

"Ah, so you two are seeing this for the first time. My knight Ferris is a man — I, if no one else, can affirm it."

Crusch, who had kept silent until now, spoke up to the two of them with their arms around each other's shoulders.

At the unbelievable content delivered in that dignified voice, Subaru's and Al's heads whipped around with an audible snap. Still with arms locked around each other's shoulders, they began turning in opposite directions, and for one instant their shoulder joints locked painfully, a chain of undignified "Ow!"s erupting — then, pulling himself back together, Subaru stamped his foot on the floor.

"Y-you can say anything with just words! You're not gonna pull one over on us that easy! Proof! Yeah, we need proof!"

"Ferris and I have known each other a long time. We've bathed together, in fact — and without question, there was a male organ between—"

"STAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAP!! My bad, my bad!! I'll accept it, I'll take it all in, fully, completely, so please don't have a girl's mouth say the word 'phallus'—!"

"I never said phallus, bro?!"

Subaru corrected the overly direct phrasing, only for Al, at the height of confusion, to throw in a retort of his own on top of it. Buried under a pile-up of confusion, Subaru flailed and jabbed a finger firmly at Ferris, the root cause of the entire situation.

"And you're just as bad, dammit! What's the point of you having that, looking like this! And on top of it, you've even got cat ears, and you're secretly a dude, who's that for?! I don't have any of that trap fetish stuff, okay! I'm strictly normal, I like silver-haired heroines!"

"Even if you say that to me, it's not Feri-chan's fault Subaru-kyun went and got the wrong idea all on his own, nya. Feri-chan never once said she was a girl, nya~"

"Don't screw with me, you bi— correction, you bastard!"

Ferris stuck out her tongue with a playful "tehe" and a wink, and Subaru stomped his feet in a show of indignation, but it didn't look like he'd wring any more of an apology out of her than that.

Mom, Dad — the "trap" archetype was alive and well even in another world.

"Unbelievable... an outrage like this, I've never experienced in my life. Even I, famous for standing right next to gentleness itself walking around fully clothed while I watched fully naked myself, can't help but feel a surge of anger...!"

"That's just about the loneliest kind of naked there is—!"

Al, ever conscientious, threw in a retort even as Subaru, worked up beyond reason, seemed to be losing track of what he was even saying.

The two of them exchanged the reassuringly ordinary rhythm of straight man following punch line. After a while, as their shared shock began to settle—

"—Have you two settled down?"

A raspy voice tossed the question their way to confirm.

On the dais, it was Miklotov, hands folded in his lap. Having gone out of his way to be shown consideration by the very apex of the nation, even Subaru snapped back to himself with a genuine, sheepish "S-sorry about that."

Shuffling back, thoroughly deflated, he returned to his place in line, engaging in what was for him a truly rare bout of heartfelt self-reflection.

"Whenever people learn Ferris's sex, they always show it on their faces. That, at least, never gets old, no matter how many times I experience it. — Though few have been as shocked as those two just now."

"Hmm. Knowing full well, and yet you continue on regardless — that is rather ill-natured of you, Lady Crusch."

Miklotov gently rebuked Crusch, whose lips curved in satisfaction, but Crusch tightened her expression and shook her head.

"Lord Miklotov seems to labor under a misunderstanding. Ferris's manner of dress is not something I have ordered. It is entirely a matter of his own free will."

"Surely it is also a lord's duty to see that one's attendant is dressed befitting their station."

The one who countered Crusch's declaration was Rickert, who like Subaru's group had returned to the ranks. Whether he'd gained some standing to speak among the officials from the earlier exchange, several of those seated nodded along in agreement with his words.

Crusch surveyed those strange bedfellows, then turned her sharp gaze upon Rickert, piercing him through. Pierced, Rickert's face stiffened, but he still faced her head-on.

"D-do you have some objection...?"

"You are not like the nameless masses who look away. Setting aside your somewhat poor timing, the fact that your convictions never quite align with others', and that you are a touch too easily carried along by others — I do think well of you, Lord Rickert."

—Isn't that pretty thoroughly savaging him?

Subaru couldn't help thinking so privately, but by Crusch's standards, was that meant as praise? Rickert too wore an expression of someone not quite convinced.

But before he could press the matter further, Crusch continued with a "however"—

"You said it is also a lord's duty to see one's attendant dressed befitting their station. Then indeed, I would still wish for Ferris to remain as he is now. Do you understand why?"

"Why, might I ask?"

The question was directed at Rickert, but cowed by her gaze, he could not find words to continue. In his place, Miklotov posed the question again, and Crusch nodded.

"It is a simple matter. — Because that being should be granted the form that lets its soul shine brightest. Ferris suits his current attire far better than he would a knight's armor. Just as I myself prefer this attire to a dress."

Declaring this, Crusch drew herself up as if bearing her own soul upright.

Beside her dignified, commanding stance, Ferris fell in line — she, no, he too, smiling as he followed at his lord's magnificent side.

Looking down at the two of them standing there, Miklotov narrowed his eyes as if gazing at something dazzling. Then he dipped his chin slightly.

"Hmm, very well. Let us end this line of discussion here. Lord Rickert, are you content with that?"

"I—I have no objection."

"Nor do I. Commander Marcos, please proceed."

Rickert, still somewhat tongue-tied, sheathed his sword of argument, while Crusch, ever the picture of a sovereign's composure, lost none of it. It had the shape of an exchange of views, but which side had come out on top was plain without even needing to compare the two of them side by side.

At any rate—

"Though this is the first statement of conviction among the candidates, she is the frontrunner, after all. It's only natural, I suppose, that she inspires a different level of confidence than the others."

The voice that carried these words was pitched somewhat too loud to really call it a murmur.

Catching it, Subaru's ears twitched, and he asked Reinhard beside him, "What's that about?" Reinhard lowered his eyes slightly at Subaru's earnest question.

"House Karsten, of which Lady Crusch is head, is a ducal house that has long supported the history of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica. A history of loyalty to the crown, an unimpeachable pedigree, and Lady Crusch's own brilliance in commanding a ducal house as its head at so young an age — there could be no more obvious frontrunner for the Royal Selection."

"That's... how's that, really, though?"

At the content of Reinhard's fluent explanation, Subaru could only give an involuntary groan low in his throat.

Even Subaru, whose knowledge of peerage matters wasn't especially deep, understood that a duke's rank was among the highest offices in the land, counting from the top. A royal candidate — even in a state where the royal family had been wiped out, it stood to reason that whoever ascended to the throne next, it would be preferable the closer they stood to the original royal line.

Thinking of it that way, Subaru couldn't help but think of Emilia's disadvantageous position.

"It's practically decided, surely."

"The head of House Karsten, and above all, Lady Crusch's brilliance is a well-known fact."

"She makes somewhat bold decisions at times, but even that speaks to the largeness of her vessel."

The listeners around him were presumably reconfirming for themselves the very content Subaru had just heard. The whispered conversation exchanged around him was nothing but talk of Crusch's advantage, and it seemed almost tacitly understood that in this Royal Selection, barely begun, she was the one head and shoulders above the rest.

Even before Emilia's turn to make her appeal had begun, Subaru couldn't help but feel a rising sense of urgency as the mood of the hall tilted further and further against her.

But that anxiety gnawing at Subaru's chest was—

"It seems a good number of you are somewhat mistaken."

—brought to a temporary halt by Crusch's words, raising a finger to cut off the whispered conversations.

Every mouth closed, and Crusch waited for all attention to gather on her. Sensing her intent, silence fell over the hall, and she gave a single nod as if using it as a cue.

"I believe I understand, in my own way, what each of you hopes for from me should I take the throne. House Karsten is a pillar of the realm with deep ties to the royal family, and has borne a considerable share of responsibility in the nation's governance to this point. Precisely for that reason, were I to ascend the throne, there would be no disruption to governance or the administration of the realm. A smooth, waveless succession of the crown would be assured."

At Crusch's fluent words, several throughout the hall who had been listening intently nodded along.

Carefully spelled out as it was, even Subaru now understood, in clear terms, the reason she was said to be closest to the throne. It seemed all the harder, then, to counter. And yet—

"—Forgive me, to those of you with such expectations, but that is a promise I cannot make you."

At this statement — one that seemed to willingly discard the overwhelming advantage granted to her — a moment of silence fell over the throne room, followed, after a few seconds' delay, by a violent stir.

Voices rose one after another, asking "What do you mean by that?" Sweeping her gaze over them, Crusch stepped boldly into the still-simmering commotion.

"When I become king, the shape of this nation will inevitably become something different from what it has been under the kings before me. I would have you all understand that."

"—And might I ask what you mean by that, precisely?"

"Naturally."

The murmuring condensed into Miklotov's questioning voice and fell quiet, and in the hall, its initial shock beginning to settle, Crusch looked up at the dais.

Her long, deep green hair swayed, and with a dignified expression she gazed past the Council of Wise Men — beyond them, at the design of the dragon carved into the wall of the throne room.

"The Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica — this nation built its prosperity under the protection of a Covenant sworn with the Dragon long ago. War, plague, even famine — every crisis has been averted by the Dragon, and throughout the long history of this kingdom, the word 'Dragon' has never once vanished from it."

The Covenant with the Dragon — that too was exactly the story Subaru had read in a picture book. The outline of history: that the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, protected by the Covenant sworn with the Dragon, had continued to prosper and flourish.

Everyone listened intently to what Crusch was saying, chewing over its meaning.

The Covenant with the Dragon, which had always quietly supported the kingdom's history from the shadows — now, with the extinction of the royal line placing its continuation in jeopardy, it formed the very premise underlying the Royal Selection. Namely: the article that the next ruler must be one qualified as the Dragon's Priestess.

"Which is precisely why we must entrust the throne to a priestess capable of communing with the Dragon. Otherwise, the prosperity promised to this kingdom—"

"—That way of thinking. I don't care for it."

In the midst of Miklotov's discourse on the Covenant, Crusch's remark stabbed in, catching him off guard.

The old man's eyes widened in faint surprise, and Crusch crossed her arms and let out a breath.

"The prosperity built up through the Covenant with the Dragon — splendid, by all means. In times of war, the breath of the Dragon burns away our enemies; when plague strikes, the activation of mana heals the people; when famine comes, the earth steeped in the Dragon's Blood grants a bountiful harvest. Every hardship, without exception, saved by our noble Dragon. Glory to the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica—"

Though the content of what she spoke was suffused with brilliance, Crusch herself, speaking it, remained flat, her expression never brightening.

Sweeping her gaze over the silent assembly, she murmured quietly.

"Let me ask you this. — Do you not find it shameful?"

Even Subaru could tell that a tension surpassing anything before now had drawn taut across the silent hall.

But amid this hall now filling with all manner of violent emotion, if one asked who at this moment felt the greatest anger of all — it was, without question, Crusch, standing before the throne.

"No matter what hardship or trial may come, we are promised that we shall overcome it through the Covenant with the Dragon. And so we grow complacent in that Covenant, fall into decadence, and the moment its continuation is threatened, we panic and grasp at some alternative. Are we not to be appalled by this?"

"—You go too far, Lady Crusch!"

At Crusch's fierce declaration, one of the Council of Wise Men rose to his feet, anger plain on his face. A figure every bit as aged as Miklotov. The old man struck the armrest of his seat with a rasping voice.

"To hold the Covenant in such contempt cannot be permitted! Do you know how many sacrifices this kingdom has been spared, thanks to the Dragon's blessing? How many lives saved besides? Would you deny the whole accumulated weight of that history?"

"As to past prosperity, I have said it is splendid indeed. I myself would not dare claim I have not benefited from it. House Karsten, too, is a house whose birth was entwined with that of the kingdom itself. When the kingdom faced crisis, so too did our house. And if the nation was saved by the Dragon, then so too was our house."

But, she said, drawing a breath—

"The matter of the future is different. Do you feel nothing, looking at our present disgraceful state? In clinging so to the Covenant with the Dragon, have we not simply stopped thinking? When war, plague, and famine strike this kingdom again, will we have no recourse but to curry favor with the Dragon?"

"—That is..."

"If we have grown so accustomed to living beneath the Dragon's protection that we would perish without it, then let the kingdom perish. Excessive blessing breeds stagnation; stagnation leads to decadence; decadence brings ruin. That is what I believe."

"You... you would say you'd see this nation destroyed!"

The old man bristled with fury, veins looking fit to burst.

At his outcry, vigor blazed in Crusch's eyes, and she shook her head. "No."

"If we would perish without the Dragon, then it is we who must become the dragon. Everything the kingdom has, until now, relied upon the Dragon for — the king, the vassals, the people, must all shoulder it themselves."

Hence, Crusch said, pausing for a breath—

"When I become king, I will have the Dragon forget the Covenant we have held until now. If, as a result, we must part ways, then so be it. The Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica is not the Dragon's nation — it is the nation of us, its people."

"————"

"Hardship awaits us, I have no doubt. Perhaps even calamities surpassing all those we once overcame with the Dragon's aid await us still. But I wish to live a life I need not be ashamed of, before my own soul."

Lowering the tone of her voice, Crusch shook her head, her gaze dropping.

"I have long harbored doubts about the shape of this nation. I choose to believe that this present turn of fortune has been granted to us by heaven, as a chance to set it right."

Given the loyalty owed to the former king, it was a remark that could easily be dismissed as sheer insolence. Indeed, even the elders of the Council of Wise Men exchanged glances at her words just now, their expressions clouded with deep shadow. And yet, at the same time—

"It's definitely idealistic, but..."

There was a weight to it that couldn't be denied, Subaru found himself thinking, drawn into Crusch's words.

It seemed those around him felt much the same, for no voice rose in the hall to loudly counter her. She had gone toe to toe with the very history the kingdom had built up, and carried herself with a bearing that would not even permit rebuttal from the very pillars of state who had built that history.

That, Subaru could say without hesitation, was an undeniable, sovereign bearing.

"—I have heard Lady Crusch's thoughts well and clearly. Having received them, should you attain the throne, you may do as you see fit. That, indeed, is the choice of a king who bears the burden of the nation."

"Naturally."

Responding to Miklotov's words with just those three syllables, Crusch turned on her heel as if she had said everything there was to say.

A stir threatened to leak out once more at her boldly unhurried manner, but Miklotov cut it off before it could spread. The old man now turned the topic toward Ferris.

"Now then, Knight Felix Argyle. Have you anything to add, yourself?"

It seemed the intent was that not only the lord, but the attendant too, should speak to their lord's merits.

But at Miklotov's words, Ferris quietly shook her head.

"With respect, there is nothing for me to add. Crusch-sama's thoughts are precisely as Crusch-sama has spoken them. And the rightness of Crusch-sama's conduct — history hereafter, and we who follow her, shall prove it. — I harbor not the slightest doubt that my lord shall become king."

Solemnly, bowing her slender waist, Ferris declared this in ringing tones.

At Miklotov's nod of acceptance, Ferris gave a single salute, then went to Crusch's side. Exchanging a glance, when Crusch dipped her chin, Ferris's cheeks softened happily.

"Crusch-sama really is wonderful, always. Feri-chan's just melting, nya."

"At times, Ferris's words make little sense to me. — But I shall forgive it. You would never do anything to my disadvantage."

The absolute trust and the bond running between the two of them was plain to see in that exchange.

Ferris, offering a devotion so fervent it nearly broke character, and Crusch, receiving it with magnanimous ease, unfazed — a fine relationship, it seemed to Subaru.

"Well now, we have finally heard from one of you — hmm, it would seem we're off to a rather turbulent start."

With Crusch's statement of conviction brought to a pause, Miklotov briefly summed up the exchange just now.

For the Council of Wise Men, the officials, and roughly everyone hoping this affair might be settled peaceably, the frontrunner's declared policy must have come like a bolt from the blue.

Likely, in that exchange, she had lost a great many votes she might otherwise have secured. But by the same token, those who continued to support her even after hearing all this would presumably now hold a trust so unshakeable that nothing could sunder it.

How much this would ultimately affect the Royal Selection, over the long term, was something whose merits and demerits could not yet be measured at this stage.

"I still don't really get how this Royal Selection thing gets decided..."

The very point of this whole statement-of-conviction segment was to work that out.

To put it in terms of a group date: it was the opening self-appeal round to decide which girl to go after. Feeling out for landmines with a few jabs, then figuring out how to land the knockout blow and take someone home — the crucial branch point. Even by his own standards, that was a terrible analogy.

At any rate—

"Now then, let us continue. In order, from the one beside Lady Crusch."

"Hmph, finally my turn. Time for hyper-concubine-time, is it."

As the knight commander, having recomposed himself, moved the proceedings along, a remark that threatened to shatter that composure all over again rang out, and the orange-haired girl stepped forward.

"Did she just say hyper-concubine-time?"

At the katakana-mangled, garbage grammar of it, Subaru stared in blank astonishment, while Al, as if boasting of some great achievement, pointed a thumb at himself — no doubt wearing a smug grin behind his helmet.

Then, with heavy, plodding footsteps, he too fell in beside Priscilla as she stepped forward.

Unlike Crusch before her, she wore a gaudy dress and hair that seemed to reflect the sun itself. A profusion of vividly colored ornaments clinked with a metallic clatter, dressing up her already flamboyant appearance with yet more noise.

And standing beside this girl was a one-armed man in what looked, at a glance, like commoner's clothes, his face hidden behind a jet-black helmet — small wonder the surrounding gazes couldn't help but turn strange with curiosity.

"Hmph, it seems the lowly gazes of the nameless masses are already gathering upon this gorgeous self of mine."

"You're workin' that real nice, Princess. Got a good buzz going, real nice vibe."

Despite being looked at with eyes better described as gawking at a curiosity than mere strangeness, Priscilla thrust out her chest with inexplicable pride, and Al, playing the part of her attendant, offered praise that missed the mark entirely.

Facing this exchange between the two of them, evidently somewhat unhinged, Marcos cleared his throat.

"Now then, Lady Priscilla Barielle. If you would."

"How vexing, but I shall indulge you. I need only make those doddering old bones there aware of my magnificence, and then let them choose to follow me. A simple matter."

So saying, she drew a fan from between her breasts, snapped it open with a click, and laughed softly behind it, hiding her mouth. It was a smile ill-suited to her lovely appearance — sadistic, almost like some poisonous seductress.

Following on Crusch's bombshell declaration, an air of unmistakable unease began mingling into the already far-from-settled atmosphere of the hall. Even Subaru, no one's idea of perceptive when it came to reading a room, could feel the plain tension straining through the hall — and in the midst of it, someone murmured, low.

"—That blood-colored bride. How infuriating."

At that voice, deep, deep with something like simmering hatred, Subaru caught his breath.

Who it referred to, and what effect that voice — carrying further than one might expect — would have, he couldn't say. But he could feel, with unmistakable clarity, a premonition that things were not about to turn in a good direction.

With the second candidate now at its center, the prologue to the Royal Selection had only just begun.
