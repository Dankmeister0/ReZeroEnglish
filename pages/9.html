Arc 1 Chapter 8: "The Taste of Bitter Sake"

Having settled the score in the back alley, it was already evening, the sun well past its peak, by the time Subaru stood before the imposing sight of the Loot House, deep in the heart of the slums.

"F-finally found it... Took way too long, dammit."

Wiping the sweat from his brow with his sleeve, he slumped down in front of the destination he'd finally reached.

Between all the running around, nearly two full hours had passed before he made it here. He could at least check the elapsed time on the cell phone in his pocket. Two hours, without a doubt.

"I figured I'd just been there, so it'd be easy..."

The fact that he couldn't read the signposts had, indeed, proven a major obstacle for Subaru.

In the royal capital, lined with so many similar-looking streets, Subaru had no sense of direction to rely on, and couldn't even lean on the written names of places or shops.

As a result, all he could do was retrace his memory and brute-force his way through every street, but—

"I was busy talking with Satella and Puck here and there along the way. No wonder I only half-remembered the route."

That tendency had only grown more pronounced once he entered the slums.

Unlike a few hours ago, someone had thoughtfully given his tracksuit a thorough "cleaning," and thanks to that, the locals' reactions were nothing but cold and standoffish — thoroughly uncooperative.

Cheerfully diving into such an exclusionary atmosphere was well beyond Subaru's social skills, so even now he found himself utterly alone, with no one to help.

So, for now, he wanted to give himself credit for making it this far.

Which meant it was time for a reward for his hard work.

"Finally, time to crack this open — corn potage flavor. This is the whole reason I went to the convenience store in the first place. Wouldn't be an exaggeration to call this the actual cause of my getting summoned to another world. Sinful, that's what it is."

Muttering all manner of excuses, Subaru tore open the bag of snacks.

A soft, sweet smell was released, and his nostrils were overtaken by a sense of happiness. He pulled a piece of the snack from the bag and tossed it from his trembling fingertips onto his tongue. — Bliss. It utterly ravaged his sense of taste.

"So good... so damn good...! Come to think of it, I hadn't eaten anything at all. So damn good."

Munching away at the snack, he looked back over the road he'd traveled so far.

He'd been a little hungry ever since he missed his chance to eat that apple-like fruit. About six hours had passed since then — he'd held out well.

But even as he distracted himself from his hunger, Subaru realized he couldn't fully distract himself from his own emotions.

His heart was pounding, its rhythm abnormally fast. He could feel the blood coursing through his entire body at a pace beyond comparison to the excitement he'd felt back in the alley.

His limbs felt heavy, and his mouth had gone dry for reasons that had nothing to do with the snack. A sharp pain, like being struck in the head, shot through him again and again, accompanied by a high-pitched ringing in his ears.

— Inside this Loot House lay the answer Subaru was searching for.

He swallowed hard, and a scene he didn't want to remember flickered across his mind.

A room turned into a sea of blood, the corpse of an old man missing an arm. Himself, gutted and on the brink of death, and Satella's limp body, dragged into it all because of him.

"Don't chicken out, don't chicken out, don't you dare chicken out, me. Am I an idiot...? No, I am an idiot. But there's no way I'm turning back now without finding the answer, not after coming this far."

Besides, there was nowhere for him to go back to in the first place.

Steeling his resolve, he faced forward and tried to start walking — only to notice his knees were shaking.

His lower half trembled and refused to obey him. He drove a fist into his kneecaps, forcing the shaking to settle, and this time, after a deep breath, Subaru moved forward.

Bathed in orange sunlight, the door of the Loot House seemed to silently refuse his visit.

Suppressing that illusion born of his own faintheartedness, Subaru raised his fist toward the door and said,

"Is anyone there?"

Even thinking it was a fleeting hope, he gave the wooden door a light knock.

The sound that echoed, both inside and out, was surprisingly dull. But what came back was a silence so complete it was unbearable.

That stillness was somehow terrifying, and even knowing it was pointless, Subaru pounded on the door.

"Somebody... there's gotta be somebody in there! Come on, answer me... please."

He didn't want to face the despairing possibility that someone had died here.

Wanting to deny a reality he refused to accept, Subaru slammed his fists against the door hard enough to make it creak.

Unable to withstand the force of his emotion, the door gradually tilted out of alignment, its old hinges beginning to warp.

— It was right about then.

"—Would you shut UP already?! You don't even know the signal or the password, and you're trying to bust the door down?!"

The door in front of him flew open with tremendous force, and Subaru, who'd been leaning his weight against it, was sent flying.

Thrown back nearly five meters from the entrance of the Loot House, Subaru tumbled gracelessly across the ground before lifting his head, eyes blinking in bewilderment.

In his wide, startled eyes, he saw an old man standing at the entrance, face flushed red with fury, glaring at him.

A large, bald old man.

His jacket, which might once have been white, had turned brown from dust and years of sweat and who knew what else, giving him a thoroughly unhygienic look. The faint, pungent odor drifting off him was probably to blame.

Beneath those clothes was a body packed with muscle, and despite an appearance that betrayed his age, there was a toughness about him that didn't suggest the slightest frailty.

In short, standing there was a big, bald old geezer who looked like he had energy to spare.

"Who the hell are you?! Never seen your ugly mug before — what're you here for?! How'd you find out about this place, how'd you get here?! Who sent you?!"

The old man closed the distance with terrifying speed and hoisted Subaru up with one enormous hand around his collar.

Feeling his feet lift effortlessly off the ground, Subaru understood he'd let himself get too cocky. That business earlier had been a special case. He really was, after all, mediocre through and through — common, unremarkable, ordinary to the bone.

Even his baseline physical stamina, which rarely lost out to anyone, was no match against an opponent this far out of the ordinary.

Hoisted up by an old man standing nearly two meters tall, all will to resist thoroughly gone, —

"—Here, have one, as a token of friendship."

All he could manage was to toss a single piece of the corn potage-flavored snack toward that face consumed entirely by fury.

After what must have been a mutually terrible first impression, Subaru found himself invited inside the Loot House.

Just past the entrance, at the counter, he sat on a fixed guest chair and shifted his rear uncomfortably.

The seat's surface was rough with splinters, jabbing sharply into his backside with every shift. If his sphincter had been on the verge of an emergency, this would have been more than enough to set it off.

"What's with all that fidgeting... You that worried about where your balls are sitting?"

"I'm not worried about my junk's position, thanks. More importantly, give back the snack bag. I never said you could have all of it."

Standing behind the counter, in what should have been the proprietor's spot, was the large old man.

Given his powerfully built frame, the bag he held up looked so small it seemed to throw off one's sense of perspective.

"Stingy, ain't ya. Hoggin' something this tasty all to yourself — you'll end up in hell for that."

"And an old geezer who helps himself to someone else's tasty stuff without asking doesn't end up in hell? Conveniently forgetting your own faults while complaining about others is a bad habit of the baby boomer generation, you know."

"There you go again with words I don't understand. This is why young folks these days... mm, tasty."

"I said don't eat it!!"

Leaning forward, he stretched out his arm and somehow managed to wrestle the snack bag back from the old man's grip. But by then, most of the contents were already safely tucked away in the old man's stomach.

Checking the bag and finding only a few pieces left, Subaru's shoulders slumped.

"Ahh... my precious corn potage. And I might never get to taste it again."

"Huh, was it really that precious a food? Well, I'll admit I've never seen anything like it before. If it comes to that, why not just commission a Replication Magic for the rest?"

"Replication Magic?"

"Magic that turns one thing into two. With living creatures, you can only copy the outside, but with something like food, I'd wager it can be duplicated properly."

At the old man's words, delivered while stroking his bald head, Subaru was once again struck by just how convenient magic could be.

Impressed at how magic seemed to be a cure-all in every sense, Subaru let his gaze casually sweep once around the room.

The Loot House at evening — there was no trace whatsoever of the tragedy Subaru had experienced here.

As ever, an utterly mismatched jumble of goods crowded the shelves, haphazardly filling up the warehouse's spacious interior.

Perhaps noticing where Subaru's gaze had wandered, the old man before him narrowed his eyes meaningfully and said,

"What's this, boy — got an interest in stolen goods?"

—cutting straight to the heart of the matter.

Negotiations with the large old man — who had introduced himself as Rom (he'd said to just call him Old Man Rom) — had, thanks to the golden snack (golden in the literal sense here, not just a figure of speech) offered up in response to his shouting, gone unexpectedly smoothly.

Old Man Rom, utterly absorbed by the unfamiliar flavor tossed into his mouth, had promptly and safely released Subaru as a result.

After that, Subaru explained he'd come on business and that he'd heard about the place from a middle-aged man in the slums, which was enough to satisfy Old Man Rom, and so the two of them now sat together at the counter.

Across the counter, Old Man Rom rested his sturdy arms on the counter and, pouring liquor into a grimy glass, gave a small laugh.

"Well, folks who come here only ever want one of two things. Either bringing in stolen goods, or wanting something to do with the stolen goods themselves — it's always one or the other."

"Yeah, one of my reasons is exactly that."

"One, huh. So then, I take it there's another reason too?"

At Subaru's qualified affirmation, one of Old Man Rom's eyebrows rose. Subaru nodded, then, hesitantly, braced himself for ridicule and put together his question. It was this:

"This is gonna sound crazy, but... old man, you haven't happened to die recently, have you?"

His neck and right arm hacked clean off.

He decided against adding that part. As far as he could tell, there wasn't a seam anywhere on the man's neck or shoulder.

At Subaru's question and stare, Old Man Rom's grayish eyes went wide for a moment, and then, as if time had suddenly started moving again, his face broke into a grin.

"Gahahaha, and here I thought you'd say something worthwhile. I'll admit I'm an old geezer half in the grave already, but unfortunately, I haven't got any experience actually dying yet. Mind you, at my age, it's probably not far off."

Laughing as though he'd just heard a delightfully absurd joke, Old Man Rom offered the glass toward Subaru with a "Want a drink?" Subaru waved it off with a gesture at the sharp, nose-stinging smell of alcohol, and offered a brief "Sorry" in apology for his earlier remark.

He'd apologized, but the sense of wrongness inside Subaru only kept growing.

Here was Old Man Rom, exchanging words with him right now — but Subaru had seen his corpse.

In this very place, in the darkness, an arm and throat sliced open by a blade, this same old man turned into a silent corpse.

And yet, as if to deny that image burned into his eyes, Old Man Rom stood right in front of him, his large frame squeezed uncomfortably behind the counter.

His ruddy face, tipping back the glass, clearly had color and life in it — a stark contrast to the sickly pallor of death from massive blood loss.

Old Man Rom was, without a doubt, alive. And conversely, the same could be said of Subaru.

Looking back, Subaru too, just like Old Man Rom, had suffered a wound that should by all rights have killed him. And yet here he was, visiting this place with a body that bore no trace of it at all.

Wondering if he'd merely had some kind of waking dream, Subaru was starting to distrust the contents of his own head.

"All of that — all of that feeling — was just a dream...? Then where does the dream start and end, and why the hell am I in a world like this at all?"

The whining complaints that his sense of urgency had let him forget were welling up again now that he had a place to settle down.

That self-recrimination, that burning pain of a mortal wound, that faint warmth of the girl he'd barely touched — if all of it were nothing more than remnants of a dream, then why was he here at all?

If it came to that, he'd rather be told that everything, starting from the moment of his summoning to another world, had been a dream.

That would be far easier to accept than being told that everything he'd experienced after being summoned was the dream.

"Old Man Rom, you haven't seen a girl with silver hair around here, have you?"

"Silver hair...? Nah, can't say I have. A look that striking, I wouldn't forget it — no matter how much my head's gone to pot."

"Gahaha," Old Man Rom laughed heartily, but Subaru's expression in response remained clouded.

Perhaps sensing the seriousness behind it, Old Man Rom abruptly stopped laughing and said,

"Drink."

He shoved the glass toward Subaru again.

He tipped the bottle over the empty glass, filling it to the brim with amber liquid. As Subaru watched in silence, Old Man Rom repeated, curtly, "Drink."

"Sorry, but I'm not in the mood. Besides, I'm not some kid who needs to drink to act tough."

"Idiot. It's the ones who can't drink and act tough who are kids. Knock it back, let it set your insides ablaze. Once you can't stand the heat anymore, all sorts of things come spilling out."

"So drink," Old Man Rom said, pushing the glass toward Subaru a third time.

Cowed by his forceful insistence, Subaru picked up the glass and brought his nose close to the amber liquid. The thick smell of alcohol struck his nostrils sharply, nearly making him choke, and his face twisted into a grimace.

But even as he took that stance of refusal, he also felt an urge to simply obey Old Man Rom's words.

Despite the fact that he'd always thought of running to drink as the very picture of an uncool adult.

"Argh... screw it!"

He tipped the glass back, pouring the liquor straight down his throat in one go.

He had no idea how strong the drink was, but the instant he drained it, Subaru felt his entire body burst into heat, as if set alight.

The path the liquor had taken screamed as though scorched, and, still riding that momentum, he slammed the glass down on the counter.

"Pwah! Gah! Awful! Hot! Absolutely disgusting! Nnngh, awful!"

"Quit saying it over and over, you heathen! Anyone who can't appreciate the taste of good liquor is a fool who's missing out on half of what life has to offer."

As Subaru exhaled the heat welling up inside him, Old Man Rom shouted back while taking a drink of his own — boldly skipping the glass and swigging straight from the upended bottle.

He put away easily three times or more the amount Subaru had drunk, let out a rough belch, and laughed.

"Still, that was a fine drink you took! Feel like you can spit a little of it out now?"

"...Yeah! Just a little! Alright, old man, time to take care of that other reason of mine."

Turning his face away from the old man's grin, Subaru wiped up the spilled liquor with his sleeve and pointed toward the back of the warehouse. Unlike the shoddy junk scattered around here, that was surely where the higher-value stolen goods were kept.

Old Man Rom's face took on a serious cast. Watching him, Subaru too, clearly and plainly, —

"I'm looking for an insignia set with a gemstone. — I want you to hand it over to me."

—stated his purpose in words.

Separate from his initial goal — confirming Satella's safety — this was the true reason he'd come here.

The gem-set insignia that Satella had had stolen from her. He hadn't been told why, but it was an item important enough that she needed to reclaim it even at the risk of danger.

He'd heard that any stolen goods brought into the slums were bound to pass through here.

If nothing else, if the insignia turned out to actually exist, Subaru could confirm that the waking dream he'd witnessed was real.

But in response to Subaru's request, made with such a desperate, clinging hope, Old Man Rom's face turned troubled.

"A gem-set insignia... no, sorry, but nothing like that's been brought in here."

"...Really? Think hard. You sure your memory's not just going senile on you?"

"With the drink in me, I'm at the top of my game right now. If it's not coming to mind under these conditions, then I can only say I don't know. But—"

To Subaru, on the verge of having his last hope severed, Old Man Rom gave a meaningful grin and said,

"I heard ahead of time that there's a big delivery coming in today. — If it's this gem-set insignia you're after, there's a good chance it could be part of that."

"Don't tell me the one bringing it in is... a girl named Felt?"

"What, you even know the name of the one who stole it?"

At Old Man Rom's words, spoken with a kind of deflated surprise, Subaru couldn't help but pump his fist.

The thread he'd thought had snapped clean through had, at last, connected again.

The name of Felt, the girl believed to have stolen the insignia, had come up here. Which meant, naturally, it also proved the existence of the girl named Satella, who'd had it stolen from her.

At the very least, it was safe to say the possibility that the silver-haired girl was some convenient event character conjured up by Subaru's own delusions had grown much slimmer.

"For a second there I was sweating that this was just my thing for silver-haired heroines showing through..."

"Hate to interrupt your weird sense of relief, but whether you'll actually be able to buy whatever gets brought in is a whole separate matter, y'know? An insignia with a gemstone's gonna fetch a decent price."

"Ha! You can try to size me up all you want, it won't do you any good. See, I'm flat broke!"

"Then there's nothing to talk about, is there!"

Perhaps he'd been planning to drive up the price once negotiations began, because Old Man Rom shouted as though he'd had the wind knocked out of his sails. But Subaru just wagged a raised finger at him side to side.

"Tsk, tsk, tsk. Sure, I don't have any money. But! In this world, money isn't the only way to get your hands on things. There's this thing called bartering, y'know?"

Old Man Rom fell silent, offering no counterargument. Answering the silence urging him to continue with a nod, Subaru fished around in his pants pocket. And what the hand he pulled back out held was—

"—What in the world is this? Never seen anything like it."

"Behold — the magic vessel that cuts out and freezes the flow of time itself for all things: the 'Cell Phone'!"

A compact white cell phone. As Old Man Rom blinked in confusion at the sight of something he'd never seen before, Subaru quickly entered a command — and an instant later, a flash of white light sliced through the dim interior of the shop.

A click of a shutter sound rang out, and Old Man Rom, caught in the flash, jumped back in exaggerated shock and tumbled behind the counter. Subaru couldn't help but laugh at the sheer scale of his reaction, and—

"What was that just now?! Trying to kill me?! Pulling suspicious stunts like that — don't go underestimating this old geezer."

"Whoa, hold on, hold on. Take a deep breath, calm down, and then take a look at this."

To Old Man Rom, his face reddening for reasons that had nothing to do with the drink, Subaru thrust the phone's screen at him.

Old Man Rom drew back with suspicious eyes, then peered closely at the small screen — and his eyes went wide.

What was displayed there was Old Man Rom's own face, just photographed. Taken using the cell phone's camera function. Naturally, such technology surely didn't exist in this world.

Just as Subaru expected, Old Man Rom stared intently at the screen and said,

"This is... my face, isn't it. What in the world is going on?"

"Told you, didn't I? It cuts out a moment of time and freezes it. I used this thing to cut out a moment of your time just now, Old Man Rom, and trap it in here."

As he spoke, he turned the camera around and photographed himself.

When he showed the screen again to the curious Old Man Rom, this time it displayed Subaru's own face, flashing a peace sign.

"That's the kind of thing this lets you cut out of time. Though honestly, it's usually used for leaving behind more meaningful, commemorative pictures, not wasting it on stuff like this."

"I see... yeah, this really is... hmm."

Hand on his chin, Old Man Rom peered into the phone as though deep in thought.

Sensing the reaction was better than expected, Subaru felt he'd finally gained some real leverage in the negotiation.

As if to reinforce Subaru's growing confidence, Old Man Rom picked up the cell phone, turning it over in his hands as he looked it over, and said,

"Never seen one myself... but could this be what they call a 'Magic Tool'? I've heard tell of them."

"Magic Tool?"

Subaru tilted his head at the unfamiliar term. Old Man Rom nodded, "Mm," and said,

"It's a tool that lets even someone without an open Gate, unlike a mage, make use of magic. Mind you, they're a rare item, so this is the first time I've laid eyes on one myself."

Old Man Rom gave an admiring groan, and after examining the phone thoroughly, practically drinking it in with his eyes, set it down on the counter.

Then, turning to face Subaru properly once more, he said,

"This thing's worth is truly beyond measure. I've been in this business a long time, but... this is my first time handling a Magic Tool. Still... there's no doubt it'll fetch a price like nothing I've ever seen before."

He was probably excited both by seeing a Magic Tool for the first time and by the prospect of handling one as merchandise. His voice trembling slightly, Old Man Rom prefaced his next words with "Which is exactly why," and continued,

"Putting this up for barter is way too raw a deal for you, kid. I don't know what that insignia you're after is worth, but it can't possibly be worth more than this Magic Tool. If you're comparing purely in terms of money, you'd come out way ahead just putting this Magic Tool itself up for sale."

It was advice that seemed out of character for someone running an underground business fencing stolen goods deep in the slums.

For Subaru, there was no doubt it was tempting advice.

Subaru had no value whatsoever in this world.

He couldn't use magic, nor did he have any exceptional strength. He had no knowledge to speak of and couldn't even read the local script, and worst of all was the plain fact that he was flat broke.

If he could sell this cell phone, he could break through at least some of that worst-case, boxed-in-on-all-sides situation. At the very least, his money worries would be gone for the foreseeable future.

With a future in which he'd obviously be struggling just to find tomorrow's meal staring him in the face, it was something Subaru wanted so badly he could taste it — a choice as natural and obvious as burping after downing a cola. But—

"Yeah, that's fine by me. This Magic Tool's going toward trading for the insignia that girl Felt brings in."

"Why go that far? Is it worth more than this Magic Tool? Or are you saying it's got some value that can't be measured in money?"

Old Man Rom seemed exasperated by Subaru's stubbornness. Even while questioning the insignia's worth, there was a hint of mockery lurking behind that second remark.

Living in a place like this, he probably understood the concept of something mattering more than money, but actually acknowledging it as real wasn't so easy.

Acknowledging that such values existed, Subaru shook his head, "Nah," and said,

"Honestly, I haven't even seen the actual thing myself. And if I turned it into cash, it's probably not gonna be worth more than this Magic Tool, so I'm guaranteed to come out at a flat loss."

"If you understand all that, then why do it?"

"Isn't it obvious? — I want to take the loss."

Subaru watched, with no small satisfaction, as Old Man Rom's eyes went wide in bewilderment for the third time.

Yeah. That was the answer.

Even if he ended up struggling for tomorrow's meal, even if his future turned pitch black, even if some fantasy of filling a bathtub with stacks of cash slipped further out of reach — this loss was worth taking.

"I want to pay back what I owe. Debts get settled properly, down to the last cent. Otherwise I can't sleep easy at night. I'm a nervous type, see, being a kid of the modern age. — So, even if it costs me big, I'm getting that insignia."

"Hmm... hearing that, I take it the insignia was never yours to begin with, then?"

"It belongs to the beautiful silver-haired girl who saved me. Don't know why, but apparently it's something important to her."

"And this person you owe? Isn't she with you?"

"Currently searching for her! Or, well, maybe being saved in the first place, or even that beautiful girl's very existence, was just some delusion my dream cooked up!"

Clenching his fist tight, he spoke aloud the very anxiety he'd rejected earlier and laughed it off.

Things had connected this far now. There was no way Satella, that girl, didn't exist.

He'd get his hands on the insignia, and surely, meet that girl once more.

Looking down at Subaru as he steeled that resolve, Old Man Rom regarded him with eyes that seemed to be gazing, from the very bottom of his heart, at something bizarre.

"—You, kid, are a hell of a fool."

And with that, having shaken off every other emotion, he simply laughed, purely delighted.
