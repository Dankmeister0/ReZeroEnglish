Arc 4 Chapter 125B: "Starting with Revenge"

The wind was blowing. A particularly strong gust struck, and it sent the hair and hems of the two facing each other whipping violently.

The place was the edge of the Sanctuary, near the hideaway where a young Garfiel and Frederica once lived — an empty, unpeopled meadow. There were no houses in the area, and even without that, there was no chance of anyone passing through right now. By this hour, the residents of the Sanctuary should all have gathered at the tomb, waiting on the outcome of the Trial.

That was the pretext — cheering Emilia on when she emerged from the tomb — resting atop Ram's own practical reason: she wanted the area cleared of people.

"An obsession, is it-e-e?"

Ram, who'd had at the back of her mind some guilt over using Emilia as an excuse, was pulled back to the present when she saw Roswaal's lips curl into a smile before her. Roswaal let his long indigo hair fall down his back, closed one eye, and pierced Ram with a yellow stare.

"That you, who knows my feelings and my purpose, would say such a thing — well, no-o-ow, isn't that a sad thing to hear."

"I never said it aloud, but I've thought it all along. Isn't that only natural?"

"Natural... we-e-ell, I suppose it is, natural. From your perspective, it's been long days of humiliation, lying low."

"――"

At Roswaal's shrug, Ram lowered her eyes and answered with silence. She understood, in a dim, hazy way, what he was trying to say. Of course she did. Ram had watched Roswaal all this time. She understood, painfully well, how he — a man faithful above all else to his own feelings — must have interpreted the loyalty she'd shown him.

"And so, the first thing you did once free of the contract's yoke was to upend my plans. Working with Subaru-kun, joining in the beating of Garfiel — that was the same thing, wasn't it?"

"That had two purposes — Ram's own aims, and correcting Garf's foolishness. ...Without Ram there, those boys wouldn't have been able to do anything."

"I can't deny it turned out well, in the end. In a scene where so many important things were at stake, Subaru-kun really does make some shockingly reckless gambles. ...I myself could never, ever bring myself to gamble with something precious like that."

It was a phrase that mocked Subaru's decision while making the case for the rationality of Roswaal's own thinking. In truth, there was little in what Roswaal said that could be denied. Much of what Subaru did was made up as he went, and with Garfiel, fortune had favored him — Ram's help included. Ram's assessment of him — a man whose only virtue was good timing — hadn't changed even now. On the other hand, when it came purely to accomplishing a goal, Roswaal's way of thinking was extremely capable. Provided one added the caveat: so long as the Gospel can be trusted.

"You can't gamble... and the result of prizing certainty above all is the Gospel, then."

"Ju-u-ust so. Though it seems you've never trusted it, and I imagine you've found it distasteful for a long time now. That can't be helped. From your side of things, you must have prayed that the day would come — the sooner the better, even by a single line — when events would depart from its record."

"...I won't deny that."

How could she? It was true that Ram loathed the Gospel. But the reason for that loathing, in Ram's true heart, differed wildly from what Roswaal believed. That fact, though it never showed on her face, filled Ram with an unbearable sorrow.

"Do you remember? The contract exchanged between you and me, through the Gospel."

"—So long as the world carves its history in accordance with what is written in the Gospel that Roswaal-sama possesses, Ram will serve Roswaal-sama with her very life. In exchange—"

"Should time flow in a way that departs from the Gospel's record, my purpose will collapse. And if I lose sight of my purpose, I no longer have any reason to live. Should you find use for such an empty husk, you may do with it as you please."

"Whether to let you live or to kill you is Ram's choice."

"Su-u-uch was the contract."

Speaking aloud the terms of a contract neither of them could ever forget, Roswaal drew a black book from within his robes. Cradling the thick, bound volume with care, he let out a breath as he stroked its cover.

"For you, it truly must have been a long, long, painful stretch of time."

"――"

"After all... you were made to swear a loyalty you never wanted, to a man who bore some part of the blame for destroying your homeland. Your heart quickening in my presence, in spite of what you wished — how very painful that must have been. Forgive me for speaking of it as though it were someone else's affair, hm-m."

With malicious intent, Roswaal chose his words to wound Ram.

A man who bore some part of the blame for destroying your homeland — hearing Roswaal speak those words, what passed through Ram's chest, along with the pain, were memories of her burning homeland, and of her family.

Among the demi-human races, the Oni were few in number, but in exchange they possessed formidable strength. Ram's people, gathered in small numbers, had built their settlement deep in the mountains — and in the span of a single night, they were engulfed by the violence of flame and blade and wiped out to the last, leaving only Ram and "――" behind.

It was in the gray light of that morning, as she stared blankly at the burned-out ruin of the settlement, that Ram made the contract with Roswaal that let her go on living.

To survive, Ram had swallowed the contract that was offered to her. "――" had known nothing of it, and Ram, in turn, had told "――" nothing.

"――?"

Feeling a faint throb at the back of her skull, Ram's brow furrowed at an inexplicable sense of wrongness. Somewhere in the memory rising back to the surface, there seemed to be an unnatural blank. As though something that had to be there was instead being forced, by sheer will, into a lie that insisted no such thing existed. And yet without it, Ram's memory could not possibly hold together—

"An affection within yourself that you couldn't accept, and a vengeful heart that you kept storing away, deep down. Even living with those two contradicting feelings warring inside you, you were su-u-uch an excellent pawn. In tracing out the contents of the Gospel, how very much I came to rely on your obedience."

"…………"

Even as Ram tried to probe at the strange gap in her memory, Roswaal's talk went on. Telling herself this was no time for that, she abandoned her search for the blank and turned her attention back to him — Roswaal, praising her long history of loyal service in a honeyed voice. But little by little, other emotions began to intrude on the sidelong glance he cast her way, and—

"And now, that very same you, of a-a-all things, betrays me and sides with Subaru-kun. Do you have any idea how much heartache this has caused me, hm-m?"

"...I shouldn't have violated the terms of the contract. If the world diverges from what is written in the Gospel, then Ram is to follow her own heart, not Roswaal-sama's word. That was the contract... and if Ram had broken it, she wouldn't have come out of it unscathed."

Hand to her chest, Ram asserted the legitimacy of what she'd done. The contract exchanged between Roswaal and Ram was, of course, not merely a verbal promise. It was something far more formal — a magical sigil carved into both their souls, and if the contract were judged broken, it would inflict a penalty well beyond what the offense warranted. So long as that penalty hadn't triggered, Ram's heart had not violated the contract.

But hearing Ram's answer, Roswaal gave a large shake of his head.

"That's exactly it. In this situation, the fact that the penalty which should trigger upon breach of contract hasn't triggered means... your soul believes, without a shred of doubt, that it is upholding the contract. That judgment, I find, is nothing but regrettable."

"What do you mean by that?"

"It's simple. — The Gospel's record has not yet diverged. The moment when the contract between you and me is truly, in the real sense, severed still lies ahead."

Dropping the tone of his voice, Roswaal fixed Ram with his mismatched eyes and declared it flatly. Even Ram, who normally kept an expressionless face, felt her cheeks stiffen faintly at this. The situation her own contract's sigil recognized, and the record she had heard described, were worlds apart. Even laying out all these conditions side by side, Roswaal's heart refused to bend, refused to yield.

"It hasn't diverged from the record...? Barusu is not challenging the tomb to free the Sanctuary, and Emilia-sama isn't making it snow here either. In a state like this, that the record hasn't diverged — Roswaal-sama, what's come over you?"

"Nothing's come over me, we-e-ell, isn't it just as it's always been? True, neither of the things you just named has come to pass yet, but... it's still too soon to say."

"It's an impossible thing to hope for. Barusu will leave the Sanctuary, and Emilia-sama will conquer every one of the Trials. That it should still settle into that record from here... isn't that nothing more than a child's stubbornness, or a hopeless, losing struggle?"

"A child's stubbornness, well, I'll deny that much, being a grown man of a certain age — but as for a hopeless, losing struggle, that I won't deny. Ye-e-es, that's exactly what this is of mine — a hopeless struggle. — One I've kept up endlessly, going on for more than four hundred years now, on and on and o-o-on."

As though throwing all caution to the wind, Roswaal declared his own actions to be a "hopeless struggle." Laughing with a sound low in his throat, slapping his knee as though he'd made a masterpiece, the clown's expression twisted into something like a fit of ecstasy.

"A hopeless struggle, yes, a hopeless struggle! What a masterpiece this is! Is there any phrase more precise than this to describe this obsession of mine? N-o-o, there isn't! A hopeless struggle... a hopeless struggle... ahha-a-a, how delightful. I never once thought to phrase it so."

"Roswaal-sama!"

"A hopeless struggle clung to out of obsession, and a servant whose vengeful heart toward a madman was reshaped into loyalty. What you and I are, together, is truly warped and comical. But even if you call it a hopeless struggle, my resolve doesn't waver for it. What you're doing is simply premature."

Erasing the wild grin from his face, Roswaal thrust the Gospel forward as though displaying it to Ram, and—

"Whatever you believe, the contract has not yet been broken. Until that girl overcomes the Trials of the tomb, the record that Natsuki Subaru will free the Sanctuary does not diverge. Even if she doesn't bring the snow, so long as I make it snow myself, that too doesn't diverge from the record."

"――"

"Even if you yourself demand the contract's terms be enforced, I too will insist on its terms being upheld. And so we're at an impasse. For you to see your revenge through, the time is not yet ripe."

Tossing the raised book lightly and catching it in his other hand, Roswaal tucked it back inside his robes. Then, on the right arm he'd left extended before him, a wavering flame flickered into being. Showing off the fire as it shifted through red, blue, and green in turn, Roswaal narrowed his eyes.

"To punish a servant who's jumped the gun, when she hasn't even broken the terms of her employment. If you were truly convinced the Gospel and the world had diverged, all you had to do was wait two more days. Then I would have offered up this body of mine to you without resistance. ...Being short-tempered never does anyone any good, in any matter."

Shaking his head as though lamenting it, Roswaal added, "Though," and continued,

"I can't say I don't understand your wanting to destroy me even a single second sooner, hm-m-m."

"...As I thought, you understand nothing at all."

"――?"

To Roswaal, wearing his cynical smile, Ram murmured weakly and closed her eyes. Behind her eyelids lay a swell of complicated emotions she could never let show on her face. Ram, closing her eyes, revealed to herself alone the way of living she had sworn never to show to anyone, for the whole of her life. She raised her face. And mana gathered at the tip of the staff she had kept leveled at him the entire time.

"There's no meaning in this if it only comes after the contract is fulfilled and you're handed over to me. After you've already broken, none of it means anything at all."

"—Come, then."

"As you wish."

—Vivid, colored flame and a colorless blade of wind collided.

Waves of heat spread through a Sanctuary that held only the two of them, and a warped dance between an Oni and a Warlock began.

The instant the Trial began, Emilia sensed it at once.

The loss of her five senses, and the loss of the very concept of her own body. The organs through which she should have perceived the world had slipped from her control, leaving only a drifting sensation, as though her consciousness alone had been cast out into some uncertain, insubstantial space — right now, she was nothing but a soul.

This was clearly, unmistakably different in nature from any Trial before it.

"――"

No voice would come. No mouth. No eyes either — and yet it was strange that she could still perceive the world at all. No, "perceive the world" wasn't quite right — nothing solid enough yet existed to let her declare with confidence that this was a world.

In the darkness, only Emilia's consciousness floated, hollow and adrift. That she could still hold onto herself at all was thanks to the many lights scattered here and there throughout that darkness.

Countless pale lights of every color. They resembled the glow given off by minor spirits, and yet their presence was decisively different from that of a living minor spirit.

Faint though their hues were, resembling minor spirits, they were perhaps closer to the material light cast off by a magic stone. Whatever the case, the fact that they were scattered around, encircling Emilia's consciousness, let her sense that she hadn't lost the world entirely.

"――"

Relieved that she was surrounded by light and not simply left behind, Emilia nonetheless began, little by little, to feel confused by the lack of any change. The hazy lights remained unchanged, and no notable change began around her either. In every Trial before this, Echidna had always appeared first to explain what was about to happen — but this time, of all times, no guide showed herself at all.

Time merely passed, uselessly — Emilia had no way of knowing how much the flow of time inside the tomb differed from that outside it, but one thing was certain: doing nothing but drifting like this would accomplish nothing.

"――?"

I have to do something, Emilia thought, willing some change to arise from her own consciousness. At once, her awareness — which until then had been fixed in one place, unable to move — shifted, and she realized she could reach a position close enough to touch one of the surrounding lights.

Having no body and yet touching a light was a strange sensation. But there was no other way to describe it. Perhaps what she was now was, without her being able to see it, the mana that composed her body laid bare — a mass of Od.

If it was Od that housed consciousness, housed the soul, then that would explain, at least somewhat, her present state.

Reaching a measure of acceptance and affirming herself, Emilia moved toward one of the lights. The scattered lights numbered, at a rough count, no fewer than twenty. For no particular reason, Emilia extended her awareness toward one glowing faintly silver. And then, the instant the light and her Od made contact — she saw.

"I hate you, I hate you, I hate you so much. I really, truly hate you. It's true. Every word of it is true. Ever since the day we first met... I've hated you, unbearably, this whole time."

"――!?"

The instant the voice reached her, an intense, primal scene came flooding into her awareness. An abnormally huge sun, a scorched wasteland, a woman standing beside the ruins of some massive, crumbling structure, bathed in blood-red light, her silver hair soaked in blood — it was Emilia.

The grown version of herself she had just glimpsed in the second Trial. Wearing a terribly sorrowful expression, she was hurling those words at someone before the ruins.

"I've thought it so many times, and denied it so many times... but in the end, the nightmare caught up to me. So I'll say it."

"――"

"Maybe we really shouldn't have met, after all."

A single tear spilled from the corner of her violet-blue eyes. It traced down her cheek, and just before the drop fell from her chin to the ground, the world burst and vanished, as though it had shattered.

"――っ"

Emilia gasped. Od alone shouldn't have been capable of such a feat. All Emilia could do was simply take in the sight she had witnessed.

What had that light just been? What had that scene just now shown her? That had certainly been Emilia — and yet Emilia herself had no memory of any of it. Or was that, too, like the second Trial, a scene from some world that could never come to pass?

"――"

No, Emilia thought. Calming her bewildered mind, Emilia groped through her memory, casting her thoughts back. Back to the words that must have echoed in the tomb, when she stepped into this third Trial.

"First, face your own past." "Look upon a now that could never be."

The third phrase that followed those. Yes, that was it. "Face the calamity yet to come."

The calamity yet to come — did that mean the future? The past, and a present that wasn't truly the present, and finally, the future. Was that the baptism this thing called a Trial kept showing, over and over, to those who dared challenge another world?

If so, was the scene she'd just witnessed also a future that would, eventually, come to Emilia? That place where everything seemed to have sunk into twilight, weeping as she confessed to someone her regret at having ever met them — was that the shape of things to come?

"――"

Shaking off her unease with a feeling of denial, Emilia's consciousness regained a surface calm. But when her awareness took stock of the darkness once more, another wave of unease arose.

The silver light Emilia's Od had touched a moment ago was gone. Where the light had been, there was only a blank space, the light missing entirely. Puzzling over this, Emilia realized at once.

If each and every one of the lights around her presented some future yet to come, then Emilia's consciousness would not be released until it had touched all of these futures.

—If this was what a Trial was, then after seeing this multitude of futures, she would surely be made to choose something. If Echidna was waiting anywhere, it would be there.

In other words — Emilia would have to witness no fewer than twenty more futures.

"――"

Were they each a different future, or fragments of one and the same future? With the illusory sensation of a heart she didn't have shrinking inside her, Emilia extended her awareness toward the light beside it.

It was a blue reminiscent of something as deep as the sea, and—

"You're right. That kid was our enemy, and her wounds ran deep. Even if we'd pulled her out here, the two of us, without the means to treat her, might not have been able to save her."

"Then..."

"But that kid was still just a child. — Isn't that enough, all on its own?"

Once again, the scene of the world shifted. This time, against the backdrop of a deep forest, two figures stood at the edge of a sheer cliff.

Their faces — she couldn't see them. But she recognized both voices. One was familiar to her, close; the other not so close, perhaps, but certainly familiar all the same.

The two stood facing each other before the cliff, one kneeling, the other looking down at the one who knelt. Both wore expressions, Emilia thought, of deep, heavy gloom.

"You are... you're a hero. There's nothing you can be but a hero...!"

"I..."

"Thanks for saving me!!"

Turning his face away, the figure with an outstretched hand received the other figure's words of gratitude, thrown out almost carelessly. Between the two figures there, she could sense an unmistakable parting.

Words of farewell in which nothing but an unchangeable sorrow, and pure despair, lay. The world began to blur again, and Emilia's consciousness returned once more to that dark, hollow space.

"――"

In that world just now, Emilia's own presence had been nowhere to be found. As for the two who had appeared, she had some inkling they were people Emilia knew — but the fact that she herself hadn't been present in that scene left her uneasy.

She should be approaching each light in the form of a future — her own future. And yet in that future, she had had no place, or she'd been shown a scene where she simply didn't exist. What could that possibly mean?

—Was she being shown the futures that might befall the people around her, as a result of her own choices?

If so, was the scene she'd just seen also a fragment of one such possibility? Not only her own fate, but the changes her choices wrought upon those around her — was she meant to witness even that, and see it through?

"――"

The blue light vanished, just as the silver light before it had. Around Emilia, twenty lights still remained.

—Each and every one of them carrying the weight of a choice.

Steeling herself to that, Emilia extended her awareness once more, to see through the consequences of her own choices. The next future, and then the next, waited on Emilia's decision.

Swinging her staff, Ram conjured a blade of wind and unleashed it. The wind blade that formed, colorless and soundless, became an assassin taking aim at its target's feet.

"That much won't do it, will it."

But Roswaal evaded the invisible attack with an easy, light leap. Of course he did. As head of House Mathers, that storied line of magical scholars, and a peerless mage versed in all six of the great attributes, seeing through mana wielded by another was child's play for Roswaal L. Mathers. Even wind magic disguised as an unseen blade was, to Roswaal, as easy to spot as a fire arrow loosed in the dead of night.

"Allow me to return the favor."

Three colors of flame, distinct from one another, rained down on Ram with a single sweep of Roswaal's arm. Red flame, blue flame, green flame — every one of them chased after Ram as she leapt backward, a troublesome breed of fire that pursued its target. Running backward, breathing hard, Ram raised her mana and released it. A blade of wind intercepted the three kinds of flame, but rather than being canceled out as expected, each flame reacted differently.

"――!?"

The moment the red flame took the wind head-on, it surged in intensity as though doused in oil, blossoming into a pillar of fire. The blue flame, by contrast, was cleanly severed by the wind and scattered its heat in every direction on the spot. The green flame, seeming to be caught up in the wind, instead absorbed the wind's mana and changed shape, becoming a serpent of green flame that slithered across the ground in pursuit of Ram.

Buffeted by the pillar of fire, Ram kicked off a great tree to dodge the blue flame, then rolled across the ground to avoid the fangs of the green fire serpent, striking it again with a blade of wind. The fire serpent shattered apart in the blast, scattering, and the green flame that spread out continued to smolder here and there across the meadow.

"My, my... we've only traded a single exchange of magic, and already you're rather badly hurt."

"Ha... haah."

"If you acted believing you had a chance of winning, I have to say your estimate was far too generous, hm-m. It's true that right now I'm devoting the greater part of my mana to controlling the spell-formula for the large-scale weather magic. But I'm not so careless as to let that leave my hands and feet unguarded."

Watching Ram, who was breathing hard from her shoulders, Roswaal — surrounded by flame — tilted his head. The three colors of flame appeared once more, taking the shape of palm-sized fireballs that began circling around Roswaal's body. Their number grew with each rotation, their speed increasing, until within mere seconds a swirling vortex of three-colored flame encircled him entirely.

"And that's the result of just one of each flame. Ten of each of the three kinds — thirty fireballs in all. Your abilities as they stand now couldn't handle that, could they?"

"――"

"To begin with, if this was timed to catch me at a moment of weakened strength, then lending your aid in the showdown against Garfiel was the height of folly. Even if my power is diminished, it means nothing if your own strength is diminished as well. I can tell just by looking at the mana filling your body. — You've turned into an Oni, haven't you."

At Roswaal's low question, Ram answered only with her eyes, still working to steady her breathing. Perhaps he hadn't expected an answer; Roswaal merely shrugged.

"It stands to reason that if you transform without my support, this is the result. Challenging me with a body that's all but drained of mana, you'll be at your limit after barely a minute of combat. From the standpoint of doing your utmost to achieve your own goal, it's honestly hard to watch."

"Hard... to watch, is it."

"Ye-e-es, hard to watch. You said something a moment ago yourself. I told you that you'd have your answer either once the world fully diverged from the Gospel's record, or after waiting two days — and you said that would mean nothing. At first I wondered what you were talking about, but... I gave it some thought of my own, and I've arrived at an answer."

Her breathing was gradually calming, though of course that did nothing to restore her stamina or her mana. Roswaal understood this too, which was why he'd eased off pressing his attack and taken to exchanging words with her instead. If she became a nuisance, that would be a different story, but Roswaal had no intention of killing Ram. And that very leeway of his was, for Ram, nothing short of humiliating.

"Given that your goal is revenge, the answer came easily enough. Tormenting me once I've become a broken husk wouldn't truly satisfy you. I can think of no other reason you'd challenge me now, as I am, when you'd be forfeiting the sure chance to see your revenge through. You believe, don't you, that revenge is only truly achieved by cutting down the me who is still mid-way through his purpose."

"――"

"As for that, I imagine some of the fault lies with me as well, for forcing a choice on you back when you were still so young, in a moment of desperation. Perhaps, some time later, you came to realize that fact and grew impatient, hm-m. And so, unwilling to let this chance slip by, you rushed. ...The result, well, is plain to see."

"――ah"

A small sound leaked from Ram's throat. It was something hoarse, a breath that slipped free as though it had escaped her.

Roswaal's mismatched eyes bore into her, watching for the slightest movement, determined not to miss it. Bathed in that gaze, Ram found herself looking back over the whole of her life until now. And then, as though for the first time, she was struck once more — belatedly — by something she'd understood all along.

Feeling that truth keenly, Ram opened her mouth. Wide, wide open, tilting her face to the sky, mouth open —

"Ahahahahahaha—!"

"—Ram?"

It was a mirror image. Recalling Roswaal's own laughter from a moment ago, Ram, laughing now, found herself feeling an even sharper, more exquisite amusement. The reason was entirely different from Roswaal's own, but — indeed, this was funny. She couldn't help but laugh. Of course she couldn't. Naturally. Because — well, of course.

"For all that she's repeated it, for all that she's touched him with it, over and over — and still, the other party hasn't even noticed her feelings."

Calling him dense, calling him insensitive — this was on an entirely different level from that. He was obstinate. Rigid, calcified. He'd simply declared it impossible, and refused to budge from that.

That a feeling which began as revenge could, over time, turn into love — within him, such a thing was impossible.

"The reason Ram stayed by Roswaal-sama's side... was the contract."

"Ah, yes, of course it was. In that burned-out settlement, you and I exchanged a vow of fealty. Even after you lost your horns, I remember your eyes still, soaked in scarlet rage. And so I sealed that with the contract, and redirected the course of your fury into loyalty. Even knowing a day like today might eventually come..."

"That's right. So it was. Ram wanted to kill you. But that chance was taken from her, and she carried an incomprehensible loyalty within her as she lived on in the mansion... and then—"

"Today, freed from the contract's yoke, you sought to carry out that vengeful heart of yours—"

Roswaal, moving toward his conclusion. The sight of him looked terribly comical to her. This person really does see nothing at all, she thought, except his own feelings.

"Ram loves you, Roswaal-sama."

"――"

"I've come to love you. And so, there's no meaning in having you handed over to me broken. That isn't the Roswaal-sama that Ram wants."

Hearing Ram's answer, Roswaal's eyes went wide, and he froze. He looked utterly speechless, as though he'd been handed an answer he truly, genuinely, hadn't imagined even for a moment. He shook his head at once and tried to form words, but his lips only trembled, refusing to shape anything coherent.

"What's the matter?"

"What's the... matter, is it? Are you... mocking me? At a time like this, of all things? Realizing you can't reach me by force, so you try to shake my heart instead—"

"Why would you think such petty tricks would work on you, Roswaal-sama? Ram is only telling you her true feelings."

"If that's so, then there's all the less chance it could be true!"

Stamping his foot, Roswaal's voice rose. Reacting to his spiking emotions, the curtain of fireballs surrounding his body wavered wildly. Scattering the now-still flames around him, Roswaal glared at Ram.

"You, love me? What are you saying? I'm a hated man to you. A man you should despise. To you, I'm a man tied to the very destruction of your homeland. In fact, you must have hated me enough to want me dead!"

"That was true at first, but it's different now. Ram loves you now."

"Don't be absurd...! Who would let such a cheap emotion...!"

Feelings that began with revenge had to remain revenge. Feelings that reached love could only ever have begun as love.

Roswaal believed, stubbornly, that people's wishes and feelings did not change. That was precisely why he could not believe in a change of heart profound enough to shake the very foundation of how Ram lived.

"What of your revenge? Didn't you swear it! Before your burned-out homeland, to the souls of your dead kin, didn't you swear you would see your revenge through, without fail!"

"I do feel some guilt toward my kin, and my chest does ache when I think of my homeland. But there's nothing to be done about having fallen in love. Ram will put her own feelings above the feelings of the dead."

"――!"

"Besides, Roswaal-sama isn't even the one directly responsible. If my vengeful heart clouded my judgment there, that would be the more shameful thing... or so Ram will make her excuse."

Ram, brazening it out, left Roswaal unable to find a retort. Perhaps expecting him to understand right away was asking too much. Roswaal was a man who had carried a single feeling through, unwavering, across a long, long span of time. Consistently, devotedly, he had poured everything into loving one person alone, and into doing his utmost to see that wish fulfilled.

He held far too strong a conviction that emotion, that the heart, ought to be that way. And because of that, feelings that shifted and changed with time, and their strength, were things he simply couldn't comprehend.

That even this about him could seem lovable to her — there was nothing to be done about that now.

"That's exactly why Ram will never, ever let you become a broken husk."

"...It's contradictory. Whatever your feelings may be — no, if anything, precisely because they're as you've said — I don't understand why you'd need to clash with me here. If the Gospel diverges, I lose my purpose for living, and my mind breaks. Knowing that, why?!"

"Because it's now, this very moment. While Barusu, Emilia-sama, and Garf... are the ones shaking Roswaal-sama's heart — right now is Ram's one chance in a thousand years."

As long as the contract between Roswaal and Ram held, Ram could not defy him. And the reason Ram was able to defy him now, Roswaal had pointed out, was because Ram's own soul had judged that she was free of the contract's yoke. But was that truly so? That one party's perception alone should be enough to remove them from the contract's bounds — did a system like a contract really operate on so vague, so lax a standard?

And so Ram thought, and hoped, this: Ram was not the only one who believed the record had diverged from the Gospel, that the conditions for the contract's dissolution had been met. Somewhere in his own heart, Roswaal knew it too. That, she believed, was exactly why they'd arrived at this moment.

"――っ"

Facing Roswaal, who wore a look of incomprehension, Ram held her breath and kicked off the ground. Drawing her staff, she wrung out what mana remained to her and began shaping a spell.

"—It's pointless!"

Casting off his bewilderment at Ram's movement, Roswaal commanded the fireballs floating around him to strike, meaning to pin her in place. But not a single flame struck Ram directly — each passed by, grazing the ground or her face, doing nothing more than scorching her skin harshly.

Even now, at this juncture, he couldn't bring himself to make the judgment to cut down Ram — Ram, who had fulfilled the conditions necessary to keep tracing the Gospel's future. Part of it, too, was that he couldn't see what Ram was aiming for.

Perhaps, just perhaps, he'd come to think it a waste to kill her. If that was so, that alone was enough to make her heart leap, enough to make her forget all the frustration of moments before.

"—El Fura!!"

The force of the wind gathered and exploded, a colorless destruction, right before his eyes. Roswaal braced himself, but he wasn't the target. It was the ground at his feet, blasted apart, the churned-up clods of earth filling the air to blot out his vision.

"Such a crude blind!"

"――っ!"

The brief curtain of dirt that had formed was shattered with a single sweep of Roswaal's arm. Watching the barrier dissolve, Ram let out a sharp breath and gathered her strength at her brow.

"...Ah, guh"

Searing pain. Her vision flooded red, and blood-tears welled from the corners of her blood-shot eyes. She could hear the muscles and bones throughout her body groaning, and the sound of tendon after tendon tearing.

Ignoring all of it, gritting her teeth hard enough to crack her back molars, she stamped her foot against the ground. The earth shattered beneath her sole, and in that instant Ram's body moved beyond the limits of life itself.

Faster than an instant, Ram's body closed the distance and leapt in before the Roswaal who had just swept away the dirt. Ram's movement was faster than Roswaal noticing her and widening his eyes. Her outstretched arm reached toward Roswaal's torso, and Roswaal drew in a breath as her palm landed against his chest.

The Oni transformation — the acceleration of Ram's physical abilities — could be the only explanation. Ram's current strength had, if only for an instant, surpassed the limits of the human body. Roswaal must have realized his own misjudgment, in the possibility of his sternum shattering, his organs being burst apart within him. And yet—

"—Wh-what?"

The impact and pain that should have followed never came, and Roswaal let out a voice of pure astonishment. Ram, having covered the ten-odd meters between them in the blink of an eye, skidded to a sudden halt some distance from Roswaal, gouging the ground as she went. Her lowered face spat a great gush of blood from her mouth as she sank to her knees.

Unable to grasp the meaning of Ram's action, Roswaal's brow furrowed. But almost at once, seeing what the collapsing Ram held in her hand, his expression changed.

"That's...!"

"For Ram... the root of all evil... is this."

Roswaal's face drained of color as he rushed toward her. Ram, lifting only her eyes to answer him, swung her arm without a moment's hesitation.

—The Gospel that had been in her hand was hurled into the still-burning green flames.

"――!"

Roswaal let out a soundless scream, but with merciless indifference, the flames swallowed the Gospel and flared higher. With a crisp, crackling sound, the aged book turned to green ash.

Watching the sight as though it were the very thing she'd longed to see, Ram smiled and saw it through to the end, and—

"—At last, this is—"

A satisfied sigh, her cheeks flushed with color.

—It was immediately after that a fireball, loosed in the grip of fury, pierced clean through the girl's small frame.
