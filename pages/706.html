Arc 9 Chapter 11: "You Are No Longer Anywhere"

"I get that everyone's worried, but I think they're all being a little too obvious about it."

Inside the Pleiades Watchtower, Petra offered her opinion on the awkward atmosphere as she prepared dinner for everyone.

It had already been half a day since they'd arrived at the tower, and with a three-day limit on their stay, Subaru and the others couldn't afford to waste any time — they'd gone straight into the archive and were hard at work searching for the book they needed.

Petra, meanwhile, was busy fixing dinner for everyone who'd surely come back hungry — but the atmosphere in the archive just before that had been the worst.

"Both Subaru and Garf-san can't focus on finding the book at all. Hey, are you listening?"

To Petra's question, Meili gave a listless "Yeahhh" of a nod. She scooped up a spoonful from the pot Petra had on the fire and gave it a quick taste.

"Mm, tasty. Petra-chan, your cooking's gotten even better."

"Thanks. But I've still got a long way to go before I reach Frederica-neesama's level, so I've got to keep at it. Especially now that my formidable rival Rem-neesama is back..."

"Rival, sureee, but Ram-oneesan's little sister doesn't remember anything, does she? So she's not really a rival to you in cooking or, um, anything else, is she?"

"You don't know that. She fell in love once already. It wouldn't be strange at all if she fell in love again... actually, I already think something's a little suspicious."

Lowering her voice, Petra grew suspicious of the atmosphere between Subaru and Rem.

Petra was glad Rem, who'd slept for so long, had finally woken up. Subaru and Ram had always worried about her, and Petra herself had tended to her every day while she slept. She understood full well how precious Rem was to them.

And honestly, she'd also thought that if Rem didn't wake up soon, she'd never be able to gauge just how formidable a love rival she was.

Now that Rem had actually woken, and Petra had met her and spoken with her, she'd found herself liking Rem's earnest, hardworking nature — but still, something was suspicious. She'd heard there'd been quite a bit of friction with Subaru because of Rem's lost memories, and even now Rem seemed to treat him with relatively cold, harsh distance.

"But it's suspicious."

"Hmm, is that soo. But that Onii-san does have a way about him. No matter how hard you glare at him going 'what's your problem,' it starts to feel kind of silly, y'knoww."

"Right, right, exactly. I thought Subaru was a weirdo at first too... wait, didn't the way you just said that sound a little suspicious?"

"Come onnn, you're overthinking it."

Meili stuck her tongue out and raised both hands with a look of distaste. Petra glared at her with a "mmm" but withdrew her suspicions for the time being.

Before they'd left for the Empire, when they'd returned from the Pleiades Watchtower, Meili had also said Petra was overthinking things — but as far as Petra was concerned, keeping this much of a guard up around Subaru never hurt.

Anyone who spent even a little time acting alongside Subaru would come to see his good qualities. And once they saw those good qualities, the chances of that turning into special affection shot right up.

"Even in the Empire, while he wasn't with us, he went and charmed all sorts of people... he probably added like a thousand more people who like him!"

"That's an exaggeration. A thousand's a bit much, no matter how you look at it..."

"He did! It's a good thing most of them were men, but you can never let your guard down!"

Not having seen it for herself, Meili laughed it off as a joke, but Petra wasn't in the mood to laugh it away.

She clenched a fist bravely, while with her other hand she stirred the pot so it wouldn't boil over — her heart, all the while, devoted carefully and constantly to Subaru.

If she didn't keep at it like this, he would surely go drifting toward someone hurting somewhere out of her sight again, and wear his heart thin over them.

—Much like right now, coming all the way to the tower just to help Al look forward again.

"So, speaking of Al-san,"

"Ohh, you were saying something about people being obvious. What's that about?"

"What's it about, it's exactly what it looks like. Both Subaru and Garf-san are being way too obvious that they're keeping watch on Al-san..."

"True, those two don't really seem cut out for keeping watch on someone. ...Al-san is the one in the helmet, right? Was there anyone else it could be?"

"Meili-chan..."

Having finished her taste-test, Meili had left the pot and settled onto a nearby bag, propping her chin on her knee. At her exasperatingly indifferent answer, Petra puffed out her cheeks with a "come on."

It couldn't be helped that Meili had joined partway through and only had secondhand bits of what happened in the Empire, but even so—

"Don't make a face like your job's already over. You're one of us too, Meili-chan, so show a little proper interest."

"Even if you say that, I really don't have anything to do until the trip back. Telling me not to look done with it doesn't change that I am. ...And I'm not so sure about being 'one of you' anywayyy."

"Don't you go doubting that yourself. You got permission from the Council of Wise Men back in the royal capital, didn't you? Then act like it! Chin up, chest out, come on, cute face!"

"Okayyy, cute face."

Meili pressed both hands to her cheeks, wearing a prim, entirely unsmiling face, and Petra sighed.

Meili wasn't a bad kid at heart, but she'd spent so long trying to pass herself off as bad through and through that it had soaked into her completely. Probably even she herself could no longer tell whether it was genuine or an act.

"Haah... there are so many people I have to teach from scratch..."

"You always seem so busy, Petra-chan. Don't you get tired?"

"You're one of the reasons why! And besides..."

"And besidesss?"

"I can't go around saying I'm tired. Especially when the person I most want to rest won't take a break as it is. If I complained, it'd only make it harder for me to tell him to."

"—"

"Oh, and by the person I want to rest, I don't mean Otto-san. I want Otto-san to properly make time for himself and actually sleep. At least six hours a day!"

Otto had made a habit of not sleeping, but unlike Roswaal, it wasn't that his constitution let him get by on little sleep — he was simply overdoing it. Proof enough: once a big job was behind him, he'd occasionally sleep like the dead for half a day or more.

"He shouldn't be competing with a master who's cheating even at the constitution level, but he just won't listen at all."

"Heh heh... I'm not exactly one to talk, but you really do dislike that lord, don't youuu, Petra-chan. Weren't you relieved he isn't with us?"

"Honestly, yeah. Because of that I ended up separated from Frederica-neesama, but it also means I've been trusted with this."

A day or two would be one thing, but this was the first time Petra had been the sole caretaker on a journey of over ten days. Even at the western margrave's gathering, even on the trip to the Empire, either Frederica or Ram had always been along. This time, though, it had been left to Petra alone.

That alone made her resolve different. She had to keep a proper eye on everyone.

"That's why I'm worried about the thing with Al-san too. ...The man in the helmet. Since there's no one else it could be."

"Right, right, the conspicuous one. If I remember, we came to the tower for his sake, didn't we?"

"Right. Because there's a Book of the Dead he absolutely wants to read. ...If reading it would cheer him up, I think that'd be fine."

"—? That's a funny way to put it. Are you that worried about this Al person?"

"No, I'm more worried about Subaru and Garf-san than I am about Al-san."

At Petra's answer, Meili tilted her head. "The onii-sans?" It seemed to interest her more than the subject of Al, and she looked at Petra with a furrowed brow.

Petra met Meili's gaze and went on, "Well, because—"

"Both of them are taking it too much to heart, like it's their own problem. You know how sometimes, when family or friends or someone important is feeling down, you end up sinking right along with them while you're trying to comfort them?"

"...Who knows? Is that soo?"

"Maybe I was silly to ask you, Meili-chan..."

Meili's face showed no sign of understanding at Petra's plea for sympathy. Feeling like she was pushing against thin air, Petra's worry settled, at a ratio of six to four, on Subaru and Garfiel.

Petra, too, had been told by Subaru and the others about the suspicion that Al's purpose in seeking Priscilla's Book of the Dead might be to bring her back to life.

Whether that was actually possible was beyond Petra's imagination, but even supposing it were, she understood why Subaru and the others would want to watch Al's every move.

"But if they came here wanting to let Al-san read the book, and now they might have to do the exact opposite, then their heads and hearts are going to end up all out of sync."

Petra knew that when head and heart, reason and emotion, fell out of alignment, performance dropped dramatically. Not just Petra — anyone got better results when their sense of purpose and their actual actions meshed together.

Right now, could Subaru and the others really be said to have that alignment?

"—"

"If you ask me, you're overthinking things just as much, Petra-chan. —Oh?"

Meili, who had been watching Petra's pensive profile, noticed something and let out a sound. Drawn by it with an "Eh?", Petra caught sight of someone peeking into the room's entrance.

On the fourth level, with its many rooms, in the very room where Petra and Meili were preparing dinner, the visitor was none other than the person who'd just come up in their conversation—

"Yo. Figured it smelled good in here — you two handling dinner all by yourselves?"

So saying, Al raised a hand casually and came clomping over. Behind him, quietly, came Flam, seemingly there so he wouldn't be left alone.

At this unexpected pairing, Petra was momentarily at a loss for words, but—

"That's right, we're in the middle of making dinner... though, well, I'm just watching, and Petra-chan's the one doing all the cooking."

"What, slacking off shamelessly, huh? Is what I'd like to say, but you already did more than enough heavy lifting just getting us here, Meili. Not my place to talk big."

"I'd hope so. While we're here, nooo one gets to talk back to me."

"Ain't that the truth, ain't that the truth. Much obliged, much obliged."

Al gave a one-handed salute of thanks, bowing his head to the imperious Meili. Alongside their exchange, Flam approached Petra, who was stirring the pot.

"Thank you for your hard work. I'm sorry for leaving the meal preparations to you."

"Oh, it's fine, it's fine! You've been taking care of Ezzo-san the whole time before we got here, haven't you, Flam-chan? It's okay to let me handle a bit of this."

"Thank you. Ezzo-sama has this bad habit of forgetting to eat or sleep the moment he gets absorbed in whatever's in front of him, so it's quite a bother."

"I know how it feels to have someone like that around..."

At Flam's heartfelt words, Petra nodded deeply in sympathy.

Flam, likewise a servant, was probably a year or two younger than Petra. Having been born into a family that served House Astrea, she might actually have more seniority as a servant than Petra did, but—

"Let's do our best together as everyone's caretakers, okay? You can come to me about anything."

Petra thumped her chest and said this to Flam, playing the older-sister part.

No matter how much her skills improved, Petra's position as the youngest in the camp never wavered. No matter how hard she tried, age was the one thing you could never catch up on or overtake — that was the drawback of being the youngest.

Of course, the camp also had Beatrice, over four hundred years old and still adorable, and Emilia, over a hundred and still adorable, but that didn't mean Petra could play the older sister to either of them.

So opportunities like this, to interact with someone clearly younger — like Schult or Utakata in the Empire — were precious to Petra.

"Thank you, Petra-sama. Actually, if I may,"

"Mm-hm, what, what?"

"No matter how many times I tell him, the young master's habit of treating my sister and me like children won't go away. What should I do?"

"R-Reinhard-san, huh~"

The very first thing Flam consulted her about turned out to be a heavyweight subject right off the bat, and Petra hesitated greatly over how to answer.

Petra had no direct acquaintance with Reinhard van Astrea, but he was the knight of knights — anyone from the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica had at least heard the rumors about him.

Personally, Petra thought Subaru was more amazing as a knight, but it felt wrong to judge someone she'd never actually met that way.

In any case—

"But I do understand what it feels like to be treated like a kid when you don't want to be. For starters, I think you should get him to stop patting your head."

"My head... setting me aside, Grassis doesn't seem to mind being patted on the head by the young master. Setting me aside."

"It's not that I dislike it either, you know? The act itself isn't the problem — it's the sense of distance in the relationship when it happens that's not good."

Petra herself was, at that very moment, trying to somehow fix a similar deadlock in her own relationship, which was why her answer to Flam's question came out unusually specific.

Hearing Petra's answer, delivered with a clenched fist, Al let out a low "heh" in his throat.

"Mm, what is it, Al-san?"

"My bad, my bad. But as advice for Flam here, that's a little off the mark, isn't it? Seems like you and Flam have your feelings pointed in different directions."

"Eh, is that so? You're not in love with Reinhard-san?"

"I'm fond of the young master, but not as a member of the opposite sex — he fails there entirely."

"O-oh. I see..."

Embarrassed at having jumped to conclusions, Petra stuck out her tongue a little. Then she looked over at Al, who'd pointed it out, and added, "That's surprising."

"You're pretty perceptive about that kind of thing, Al-san."

"Oi oi, I'm a grown man of respectable age, y'know? I've tasted my share of the bitter and the sweet — other people's, that is."

"If it's other people's, that's nothing to brag about, is it?"

"That's exactly why I'm not bragging. It's self-deprecation, self-deprecation... whoop."

Al shrugged, and just then his stomach growled abruptly, as if pleading its hunger. Petra's lips softened at that.

"Just wait a little longer, okay? It'll be ready soon."

"Ahh, sorry for rushing you. That wasn't me, that was the bugs in my stomach."

At Al's ever-flowing quips, Petra nodded along with a "Yeah, yeah."

Even as she traded this light banter with Al, Petra couldn't help but feel, once again, that Subaru's and Garfiel's attitude toward him was excessive.

She understood why, with all sorts of preconditions tangled together, Subaru and the others' anxiety kept swelling. But Al, at the very least, was still able to put on the appearance of pulling himself back together. Someone who was truly despondent, truly sunk, wouldn't be capable of that kind of consideration for the people around him.

"So you actually eat properly, huh, mister."

It probably wasn't that she'd read Petra's mind, but Meili said this to Al. At her words, Al fiddled with the seam of his helmet and said, "Course I do," continuing,

"An army marches on its stomach and all that... don't you think going without food or drink and having your performance tank would actually take you further from your goal?"

"Sure. But there's also the option of not drinking, not eating, not sleeping, isn't there? You've only got three days, mister."

"Meili-chan...!"

"Nah, it's fine. I know there's a limit, and thinking 'do you actually have the will to see this through, taking it this slow?' is a perfectly natural thing to think."

Waving a hand, Al didn't reproach Meili for how blunt her words had been. Then he brought that hand to his head, tapping the helmet with a fingernail, and went on, "But,"

"I'm the princess's fool. Going at this bloodshot-eyed and desperate... I figure that's just a bit off from who I am."

"Al-san..."

"And besides. Sure, three days is the agreed limit, but hey, it's not like we're timing it precisely with an hourglass. If I cried and clung and begged, don't you think I could squeeze out half a day extra as a bonus?"

"—Pfft."

Just when he'd seemed to be speaking so seriously, he'd suddenly come out with something so conveniently self-serving that Petra couldn't help but burst out laughing.

It was true — if Al seriously cried and wailed and clung to them, they might well end up extending their stay by half a day.

But—

"There's no such convenient arrangement. Drink your water properly, eat your meals, make time to sleep, and manage within that."

If he counted on that from the very start, there'd be no point in having set a deadline at all. More than anything, it was Petra's role here to be the one who could speak the harsh words that the kind-hearted Subaru and the others couldn't.

Even knowing that, in the end, she might well be talked into it anyway.

"For these three days, I'll properly help you too, along with Subaru and the others, Al-san. —There, it's ready."

"Tough as nails. Whoop."

As she spoke, Petra scooped the contents of the pot into a bowl and gently held it out to Al to taste. Taking the bowl, Al lifted the visor of his helmet and brought it to his mouth,

"Ow, hot... but damn, that's good."

he murmured, in a soft tone, satisfying Petra to no end.

"Setting aside the Sage who built this great library, I daresay I've likely read more Books of the Dead than anyone else alive. Allow me to share my findings on the matter — I believe the archive's ultimate nature lies in the vicarious reliving of vanished history."

Dinner finished, Subaru and the others returned once more to the archive of Taygeta.

There, while helping Al search for the book he sought, and simultaneously keeping an eye on Al's behavior — their attention scattered every which way — Ezzo lectured to them.

In the high-ceilinged archive, Ezzo's voice, lower than his appearance suggested, carried well.

Despite belonging to a different camp with no shared purpose, his willingness to keep them company like this deserved real respect. Though, granted, it was also possible that his urge to talk was exploding precisely because Flam wouldn't indulge these kinds of discussions with him.

Regardless, it was an interesting enough subject that listening to it wasn't tiresome.

"Even so, 'vicariously reliving vanished history' is a rather grand claim, I suppose. I know a Book of the Dead lets you glimpse the life of the person it belongs to, but this sounds like a matter of an entirely different scale, I wonder."

"Strictly speaking, Lady Beatrice's understanding leaves nothing to be desired. But that's a view limited to a single book. When the matter spans multiple books, the circumstances change considerably."

Since they'd split up to search the shelves, conversation tended to come out louder than usual, carrying a faint thrill of transgression, like talking too loud in a school library.

Talking across the bookshelves like this, Subaru tilted his head at Ezzo's answer.

"Come to think of it, Ezzo-san, you've read more than ten of these, right? ...Since a Book of the Dead can only be read if it belongs to someone you know, isn't your batting average pretty crazy high?"

"Even holed up in here like this, ain't that the truth. Been at it more'n half a day now, and not a single name I recognize's turned up."

"That's a hopelessly low batting average in its own right, I suppose. ...I might spot a familiar name here and there, I wonder. But a familiar name and an acquaintance's name are two different things, I suppose."

Holding hands with Subaru, gazing at the same shelf, Beatrice murmured this quietly.

In fact, given that Beatrice, knowledgeable as she was, had lived four hundred years, it seemed she did occasionally come across familiar names. Whether out of consideration or not, she made a point of never mentioning it, but every time she found one, there was a reaction in the hand she held, so she wasn't hiding it from Subaru at all.

Subaru, for his part, had no intention of asking about the people she chose not to mention.

"I don't figure Ezzo-san's vain enough to lie here just to brag about being a lucky guy. If there's some secret to your good fortune, mind sharing it?"

"I'm sorry to disappoint what you're hoping for, but the secret isn't luck at all, Al-dono. You and the others, Natsuki-dono, ought to think a bit more flexibly... starting with questioning the premise."

"The premise—" "Question it?"

Whether it was Ezzo's skill as a speaker, Subaru and Garfiel both furrowed their brows at the question posed to them. Meanwhile, seemingly pleased by that reaction, Ezzo brightened his tone with a "Yes!" and continued,

"As Natsuki-dono says, for a Book of the Dead to fully exercise its function, one must be acquainted with the person the book belongs to. I can think of several reasons for such a restriction, but none go beyond speculation, so let's set that aside for now. What matters is that, because of that premise, it's difficult to vicariously relive the deaths of those from long, long ago — the age of the Witches, for instance."

"Not that I'd particularly want to see that even if I could... but, uh, question the premise?"

"The hell's that mean? Some kinda trick where you can actually read someone's book even without knowin' 'em?"

"If there were a cheat trick like that, I'd think Ezzo wouldn't have stopped at twelve books, I suppose."

"Ha ha ha! Having my thirst for knowledge acknowledged like that does leave one rather ticklish!"

It hadn't really sounded like a compliment, but Ezzo grew delighted at Beatrice's words all the same.

Ezzo, who seemed suited to something like teaching, had Subaru seriously mulling over his words, but no answer sprang to mind right away.

Instead—

"—I did think of one 'no way, could it be' kind of possibility, though."

"Ohh, splendid! Then would you tell me what possibility you've thought of?"

"The premise is basically the part where you can only read the book of someone you know, right. That's why you can't read the book of someone who died long ago. —But if you knew that person who died long ago, you could read it."

"Yeah, but that's the whole thing, that you can't do that, right?"

"Is that so? Here, though, you could, couldn't you?"

So saying, Al made a gesture of sweeping his gaze around the archive of Taygeta. At his words, Subaru's understanding lagged for a moment, and he fell into thought.

But between the talk of breaking down the premise and the way Al had just put it, he arrived at something like an answer.

Namely—

"If you read someone's Book of the Dead, you can relive their life. Which means, after reading a single book, the range of people you know has widened compared to before. So then..."

"Repeat the vicarious reliving and increase the people you know, and both the number of people you can view and how far back in time you can reach grow along with it, I suppose. So you've finally grasped what Ezzo meant, I wonder."

"The vicarious reliving of vanished history...!"

Picking up on Al's guess, Subaru and Beatrice had found this method within the use of the Book of the Dead, and turned to look at Ezzo.

At their gazes, he stepped out from the shadow of the bookshelves, spread both hands, and nodded.

"Splendid. There's a real pleasure in watching young people unravel a difficult question through their own thinking. As one walking a step ahead, my heart can't help but soar."

"Ah, sorry to say, but I'm older than you, Ezzo-san."

"Betty finds being called 'young' quite an affront too, I suppose. You underestimate a great spirit who's lived four hundred years far too much, I wonder."

"Ahem, my apologies! But surely Natsuki-dono and Garfiel-dono are young people? Looking at the faces they made upon reaching that understanding..."

"Gao..."

Triumphant, Ezzo's words came to a halt at the sight of Garfiel's awkward expression.

Counting something out on both hands, Garfiel's emerald eyes held nothing but confusion as he tried his hardest to chew through what Subaru and the others had just said, and his brain short-circuited.

"Read a book, and the people I know go up? Go up, and then... the books I can read go down? Go up? I don't get what the hell that means..."

"...It's fine, Garfiel. This isn't really about us right now anyway, and it's not like you have to understand it."

Subaru gave a wry smile, comforting Garfiel, whose head was swimming with question marks.

In any case, they now understood why Ezzo had managed to read as many as twelve Books of the Dead. He hadn't been blessed with the tremendous luck of drawing readable Books of the Dead — he'd tackled it through the brute-force method of increasing the books he could read, by using the vicarious reliving of the Books of the Dead itself to grow his circle of acquaintance among the dead.

And, as Ezzo had guessed, repeating this could presumably let one trace back through history a hundred, two hundred years, even to peer into the age of the Witches four hundred years ago, which he'd used as an example.

Unfortunately, Subaru had no interest in that era—

"—Satella."

A name flickered across his mind for an instant, and Subaru shut his eyes to drive the intrusive thought away.

He'd said he had no interest in four hundred years ago, but there was one name that did concern him. Except, that person wasn't dead — the story went that she was sealed away instead.

So her book wouldn't exist in this archive, and even supposing it did, whether Subaru could actually read it was anyone's guess. —To be precise, there was no way to find out, and truthfully, he had no wish to find out either.

"Even so, even knowing the method, reading twelve of them sounds like suicide to me. How do you manage it?"

"Naturally, even using a technique to empty the mind, it's no easy task. Every time I finish a single book, I need to work at separating my true self from the information that's flowed in. For safety's sake, I'd say a minimum of two days' rest is necessary."

Ezzo had been carrying on with something fairly reckless, but knowing that even this recklessness came with proper thought behind it put Subaru a little at ease. Though, if he was taking a two-day gap between each book, having already read through twelve, the pace was a bit questionable.

In any case—

"Calling it 'interesting' feels a bit off, but that was a fascinating talk. Thanks."

"Interest and enjoyment are a hair's breadth apart. So long as you're mindful of how you phrase it, there's no need to reproach yourself for how you feel about it. And thank you, in turn, for lending your ears."

"Thank you very much."

Watching Al respond so politely to Ezzo's lecture, Subaru felt, somehow, that Al was gradually regaining his composure.

At first, when Al had reached for a Book of the Dead so carelessly, right at the start, Subaru had thought it dangerous — but even now, with Priscilla's book still nowhere to be found, that sense of precariousness that had seemed to hang over Al's entire being felt as though it had faded a great deal.

He'd still occasionally let slip somewhat self-deprecating remarks, but little by little his characteristic quips were coming back, and honestly, Subaru felt relieved.

"I guess constantly having to doubt someone really does take a toll on you mentally."

As Subaru muttered this, his mind called back to those first days in the Vollachian Empire.

Back then, with not a single familiar face around, he'd had to spend his time constantly on edge, terrified over who could and couldn't be trusted — mentally, that time had been rough. In the end, the Pleiades Battalion had joined him from the Sword Slave Island, and eventually Emilia and the others had reunited with him too, so those anxieties had been resolved.

And so, having set that weight off his shoulders a little, Subaru pulled himself together and turned his attention back to the shelves.

—That was when he found a Book of the Dead with "Natsuki Subaru" written on its spine.

"—"

Holding his breath, Subaru stared fixedly at the Book of the Dead.

There had been that incident before. Of course his own Book of the Dead would be here in the archive — reading it had been the very key that let the memory-lost "Natsuki Subaru" return to being Natsuki Subaru.

Even so, being confronted with it again brought a heavy, leaden weight settling into the pit of his stomach. He thought he should look away from the book and leave the spot at once,

"No..."

Wait, Subaru told himself, calling a halt on his own impulse.

It was one thing for his own Book of the Dead to be here. But wasn't it dangerous to look away and just leave it as it was? —Here, after all, was Al, someone who, like Subaru, had been summoned from their original world into this other one.

The title, "Natsuki Subaru," was written in kanji — but Al might well be able to read it. And if Al were to find that book and carelessly open it, wouldn't that risk something enormously complicated occurring?

It would be one thing for Al to learn just how outrageous Subaru's life in this other world, started from zero, had been. —The problem was not knowing what kind of malfunction might occur if that overlapped with the penalty for having his Return by Death discovered.

"—"

Subaru had to admit that the Witch of Envy, Satella, who seemed to have granted him the power of Return by Death, bore him no ill will. But at the same time, it was also true that he couldn't trust the Witch enough to believe she'd go easy on that penalty.

Shouldn't he hide the book away, then, so as to prevent that kind of carelessness from happening?

"Subaru? What's with all the fidgeting? Do you need the bathroom, I wonder?"

"No, it's not that... um, actually, I found a Book of the Dead that's kind of inconvenient, and I want to move it somewhere less obvious if I can."

"Inconvenient... don't tell me you found Priscilla's book, I suppose?"

"Yeah, of course you'd think that. But it's not that."

At Beatrice's question, Subaru shook his head firmly.

Beatrice, too, was someone he couldn't confide in about Return by Death — up until now he'd gotten out of explaining through the sheer power of their bond, but this was a tricky moment for that.

Of course, Beatrice couldn't read kanji either, so she couldn't make out the title "Natsuki Subaru." Still, hiding the book while fobbing her off with some vague excuse felt dishonest.

So—

"Beako, this is a book that absolutely must never be read. Handle it wrong, and it's the kind of thing that could have the Witch of Envy reaching out from the shrine again."

"Y-you've suddenly come out with something outrageous, I suppose...! 'From the shrine again' — that would be a repeat of when Subaru got flung off to the Empire, wouldn't it! I absolutely refuse to allow that, I suppose!"

"I feel the same way, and honestly, it might really stop being a joke. So..."

As he spoke, Subaru pulled the offending "Natsuki Subaru" book from the shelf. Careful not to accidentally open it and end up reliving his own life, he leaned in and whispered quietly into Beatrice's ear.

Hearing this, Beatrice frowned but nodded along vigorously,

"Muraku."

Beatrice's Shadow magic activated, and Subaru's body turned as light as a feather. Freed from gravity's yoke, Subaru hopped up onto the top of a bookshelf with a light little leap, and there quietly tucked away the book he'd just pulled out.

"That's exactly the kind of thing a mischievous brat pulling a real prank in a library would do..."

It was the sort of underhanded move that troubles a library's librarian by not returning a book to its proper place — a downright godless act to commit in front of Beatrice, who'd served for years as librarian of the Forbidden Archive, but it couldn't be helped.

Three days from now, right before leaving the tower with Al, he'd have to remember to put it back.

"It'd be a real problem if Ezzo-san's thirst for knowledge led him to find it and read it. ...Actually, it'd be bad no matter who read it, so maybe I should just get rid of it outright?"

"Get rid of what, bro?"

"Waaaaaugh!"

Having hidden the book and returned to Beatrice's side, Subaru had been racking his brain over how to handle the "Natsuki Subaru" book when a voice suddenly called out from behind him, wringing a scream out of him.

Having let out a truly earsplitting shriek that echoed through the archive, Subaru turned around to find Al standing there, himself startled by Subaru's overreaction.

"Oi oi, that's way too jumpy. I just made small talk."

"Ah, ah, sorry for reacting like that. That came out way louder than I meant."

"Nah, I wasn't that bothered, but ain't that more of a problem over there?"

"Over there?"

Following the direction Al was pointing, Subaru let out an "Ah" and looked at Beatrice. Crouched down there, she had both hands pressed to her ears, eyes brimming with tears,

"W-w-w-what an enormous voice, I suppose...! My ears, Betty's ears are ringing, ringing, I suppose!"

"Sorry! It wasn't on purpose! I love you!"

"I can't hear you, I suppose! Say it louder, I wonder!"

"Beako lovely! Beako pretty!"

Subaru desperately professed his love as Beatrice, half in tears, closed in on him. Cheeks puffed out, she fell silent for a while at his words before letting out a long breath.

And then,

"Well, in consideration of your love for Betty, I suppose I'll forgive you. Be grateful for my generosity, I wonder."

"Thanks, thanks, many thanks, love you always. —So, um."

Petting the head of Beatrice, who'd promptly reeled her tears back in, Subaru turned once more toward Al. Having watched their little flirtation, Al fiddled with the seam of his helmet and prefaced with, "Ah, you two done?" before continuing,

"No matter when I catch you, you're always red-hot with your own spirit, bro. Sorry for interrupting the honeymoon."

"Don't worry about it at this point. Way more of an issue is me postponing time with Emilia-tan and the others after getting back from the Empire... no wait, sorry!"

Trying to answer Al's casual teasing in his usual manner, Subaru realized halfway through just how careless a remark it was, and clapped a hand over his own mouth.

But before Subaru, regretting the remark, Al gave off the air of a wry smile,

"Nah, you're not wrong. You're right, bro. If anything, let me thank you properly. Even just three days — carving out that time for my sake must've been rough."

Faced with Al's expression of thanks, Subaru couldn't come up with a good reply.

It was Al himself, on this journey to the Pleiades Watchtower in pursuit of the Book of the Dead, who had proposed the three-day limit for their stay at the tower. Of course, part of that was probably wanting to raise the odds of reliably reaching the tower by having Subaru and the others come along.

But Subaru also thought it was proof that Al hadn't been able to become quite so self-centered as all that.

"Sorry to you too, Beako-chan, for dragging bro into this. Thanks for coming along."

"—. It's only natural that Betty be with Subaru, I suppose. Also, Betty isn't merciful enough to give the same warning over and over, I wonder. See that you correct it yourself properly, I suppose."

"Got it, got it. —Real sweet, your girl."

"Yeah, that's right. Packed full inside this light, fluffy, cotton-candy body of hers is nothing but kindness and adorableness — that's Beako for you."

Answering Al's murmur like that, Subaru took Beatrice's hand again. At their exchange, Beatrice turned her face away as if to hide her embarrassment, muttering, "Hmph, I suppose."

Softening at Beatrice's heartwarming reaction, Subaru let out a breath and said,

"Al, how's the archive going?"

"—. Like you can see, no results to speak of. Figured three days would be enough, but honestly, this number of books has got me at my wit's end. Feels like a needle dropped in a desert, for real."

"—"

Al gestured around the archive with one hand, and his words weren't exactly wrong.

There was no telling how many lives had been lost in this world — no, since the great library of Pleiades had come into being. Checking through them one book at a time was enough to make one's head spin.

And what's more, as Ezzo had said, the books were increasing not by the day but by the second.

"But don't give up. We'll lend you a proper hand too."

"Bro..."

"That's right, I suppose. We've made this huge detour before heading back to the mansion — I won't allow it to end with your heart breaking just because you couldn't find it, I wonder. So don't you dare give up, I suppose."

"—"

At Subaru's and Beatrice's words, one after the other, Al bowed his head quietly. Watching his lowered right hand clench and unclench, Subaru sensed hesitation there.

It was, in fact, the very reaction Subaru had been hoping — calling it "hoping" felt too coldhearted, but something along those lines — to draw out of Al.

Al had come to the Pleiades Watchtower with the aim of reading Priscilla's Book of the Dead.

But by actually witnessing the archive for himself, and by engaging in the concentrated work of swallowing Priscilla's death, perhaps he could find calm even without reading the book.

As proof of that, self-destructive remarks had clearly decreased from Al, and his manner had softened as well.

And Subaru understood, too, that Al would probably feel guilty over becoming that way.

So he added,

"Al, when Beako and I say don't give up, we don't mean finding the book. We mean don't give up on reaching something you can be at peace with."

"At peace with...?"

"Yeah. Not because you got sick of it, not because you ran out of time — nothing like that. You reaching your own conclusion, through your own thinking. That's what being at peace with it means, I think."

If he couldn't convey it well, Subaru thought this too could come across as a horribly insensitive thing to say.

Depending on how it was taken, it might sound like he was pressuring Al to accept Priscilla's death. But that wasn't what Subaru meant. Rather, being at peace wasn't about whether Priscilla was alive or dead, but about feeling that you'd faced her death itself thoroughly enough.

Mourning her, thinking of her, and drawing a line somewhere — that was what was needed.

For that—

"Let's give these three days everything we've got. I will too — all of us will, all the way."

"That said, going without sleep or rest is an unhealthy way to do it, I wonder. You'll turn into Otto, I suppose."

Beatrice added this with a wink, to Subaru's forceful appeal.

At their words, Al drew in a faint breath, then slowly tilted his helmet upward and exhaled. It was a long, long sigh, like some great mass slipping loose and falling away.

"Three days, huh."

"That's right. No overtime. ...Well, no, if you seriously begged me I might reconsider, but no counting on that from the start, got it!?"

"If we get back later than planned, I wonder if Emilia will give you an earful, I suppose."

"Emilia-tan's earful! ...Well, that'd be adorable and worth seeing in its own right, but—"

No matter the emotion, the expression, the angle, Emilia would surely be blindingly adorable — but he'd already put her through more than enough worry.

If he could help it, he wanted to see nothing but smiling faces, bashful faces, positive faces from her from now on.

"Well, no matter the face, she's still E-M-T, that much never wavers, but..."

As he said this, Subaru shrugged at the exasperated look on Beatrice's face and turned back to Al.

And, meeting Subaru's gaze head-on, Al nodded—

"—Ol Shamak."

—The next instant, Natsuki Subaru's world faded to black.

△▼△▼△▼△

"Three days. Give someone three days, and they start bracing for something to happen on day three."

A sharp clack rang out as something dropped onto the floor of the Taygeta archive, and a toe pinned it down before it could roll away.

"So as a result, the first night — right after a good, heartfelt talk — is when the guard drops the most."

Crouching down on the spot, he picked up the black sphere he'd pinned beneath his toe.

Cold and hard to the touch, it felt like a marble of glass, but it was nowhere near so fragile. It would be a problem if it were. —After all, this was a prison that could ensnare even a Witch.

"I know, bro... no, Natsuki Subaru. —I'm not going to kill you."

There'd apparently been someone who, no matter what, threw themselves recklessly into trying to kill Natsuki Subaru — but they hadn't understood a thing. Doing that meant nothing at all.

Do that, and it becomes Natsuki Subaru's battlefield.

You must never fight Natsuki Subaru on his own battlefield.

Which is to say—

"Let's begin, Sensei. —So that I can be me."

Priscilla Barielle was no longer anywhere.

And so Al — Aldebaran — set himself into motion, for the sake of the true purpose he had once abandoned.

—A battle to remove Natsuki Subaru from this world.
