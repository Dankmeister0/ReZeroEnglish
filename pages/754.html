Arc 9 Chapter 58: "The Sound of Chains"

"Rigel. —Natsuki Rigel!!"

—That soul's raised cry, Roy Alphard had certainly heard.

Desperate, frantic, an appeal so charged with feeling it would strike the heart of anyone who heard it — surely, if it had been the "Gourmet" Lye Batenkaitos, who loved the harrowing passions and bittersweet longings of humanity, he would have gladly lent his ear to that wish, and answered the request while licking his lips.

"But the one here wasn't Lye — it was us, wasn't it."

The "Gourmet" Lye and the "Omnivore" Roy — twins who shared the same Witch Factor of Gluttony. To be precise, they were triplets, together with Rui, who had died in their mother's womb — but, unexpectedly, they were siblings who didn't get along all that well.

What each valued in a meal was completely different, despite being brothers made of the same flesh and blood.

But he'd never once lamented that difference in appetite. If anything, it was something to be glad of — it meant the brothers never had to fight over the same prey. Rui, who couldn't freely choose her own prey and had no choice but to steal bites from the "Soul's Stomach," seemed to find that frustrating, but—

"Can't help it, can't help it. We think our little sister's cute too, y'know. But, but, see? Eating is something you do for yourself — we're not skilled enough to go filling somebody else's stomach for them."

So even pitying his sister when she complained of hunger, that was still just a nearby stranger's hunger. Even hearing that Lye had met a fleeting end at the Sand Tower, that too was just a nearby stranger's failed meal.

He didn't particularly think "serves him right," nor did he grieve over how that meal had ended. All he felt was one thing — a sense that it was a waste.

"Ahh, from Lye's 'Gourmet' point of view, us being always starving probably made us failures, rejects, the ones who didn't make the cut... but we 'Omnivores,' we could've eaten Lye, y'know."

It was strange to realize he'd unconsciously excluded Lye and Rui from being food, and Roy found it amusing that his family affection wasn't such a bad thing after all. To think the "Omnivore," who cleanly devoured anything and everything that caught his eye, of all things, had been picking his plates.

"Ahh, what a real shame that is."

Too bad, but Lye had gone and died. And Rui — ever since that time spent at the Sand Tower, he hadn't felt her presence in the "Soul's Stomach" either, and had no idea what had become of her. —Really, truly a shame. If he ever got to touch his sister again, he'd surely take a bite out of her soul.

"After all, we're the 'Omnivore,' see."

No matter how ugly or dreadful a thing might be, if he wanted to devour it, he couldn't help but stretch out his tongue. Even something the "Gourmet" wouldn't spare a glance and the "Sated" would push the plate away from — he'd eat it, every last bite.

—Above all, giving up on something he'd once desired to devour was unthinkable.

"—!!"

Beyond the dust, Aldebaran, raising his voice, was in a state of absolute crisis — no, rather, he seemed to have been driven into a corner precisely by being put into the opposite state. That he'd remembered Roy's existence there and thought to feed him a "name" to flip the board over — Roy found himself impressed at how sharp that was.

The curse mark carved to keep Roy obedient and dragging along was being invoked, and Aldebaran was desperately calling him. A strange pain, like having his untouchable soul seared, assailed Roy, and his body jerked and bounced against the ground. It was abuse inflicted upon the soul, threatening through pain: obey, if you want to escape this agony.

"I get it, I get it, I get it, I really do, I get it, so I get it, even so, I've got no choice but to get it! Precisely because I get it! Gluttonous drink! Gluttonous feast!"

He understood what Aldebaran wanted to say. He understood what he wanted him to do. He understood that he was being driven into a corner. Honestly, even as his soul was being seared like this, Roy was grateful to Aldebaran. —Thanks to being taken out of the Prison Tower, he'd gotten hold of chances to eat that might never have come again. He didn't care for eating on command, but not choosing what he devoured was Roy's virtue, so that must have suited Aldebaran's convenience well enough.

"But, y'know, I can't have you getting the wrong idea."

Because he was grateful, he'd go along with being ordered around. —But that was only within the range Roy was willing to concede; anything outside that, and he'd do as he pleased. For example—

"—Found you."

It was his nature — an opponent he'd wanted to devour even at the cost of his own life, he would devour, without fail.

"Rigel. —Natsuki Rigel!!"

That blood-spitting scream had reached the ears of the "Witch of Melancholy" clearly as well.

A blast wind and violent pressure had surged in from beyond her awareness, and the damage it wrought upon the battlefield was great; buffeted by a shockwave that seemed to level the very earth, it happened just as she'd finally managed to raise herself up.

"—Petra! Are you okay!? Damn it, what was that all of a sudden!? Is everyone safe!?"

Even with a shrill, ringing tinnitus dominating the inside of her skull, Imaginary Subaru's voice, worried for her, came through clearly, quite apart from her eardrums. It was into that moment — "Subaru's" confusion and concern, worrying over the bodies of her fallen comrades — that Al's opening scream had arrived.

"——"

Amid the billowing sand and dust, the "Witch of Melancholy," spitting out grains of sand that had gotten into her mouth, felt surprise from two directions at Al's scream, mixed in with the raging wind.

One was the way Al had chosen to use the Authority of Gluttony to break open this situation. The other concerned the name Al had screamed out as his own true name.

Natsuki Rigel. —That sound clearly wasn't unrelated to Natsuki Subaru.

"If he's another otherworlder like Subaru, it was obvious enough that 'Aldebaran' was a fake name, but..."

For the name that popped out to be Natsuki Rigel was too strange a coincidence. Rigel was the name of a star, and the surname Natsuki was, of all things, the same as Subaru's. —Could it be that the condition for being summoned to another world was a set: the Natsuki surname plus a star's name?

"That guy is..."

Naturally, "Subaru" too knit his brow at the same question as the "Witch of Melancholy."

Al was full of mysteries, and even as the "Witch of Melancholy" and the others faced him — a man who'd resolved to make an enemy of the entire world — they remained ignorant of even a single thing about his true feelings or true intentions. This, too, was one more proof of that. But—

"—Sorry. I've already made my choice."

The "Witch of Melancholy" wasn't so childish as to prioritize a riddle that couldn't be solved here and now.

What she should prioritize above all else right now wasn't the newly presented riddle from Al, but a countermeasure against Al, who — even having played almost every card in his hand — still held a means of overturning the board. Al's scheme of having his own "name" devoured, if carried out, would leave no way to counter it. —That much had already been proven, by forgetting Rem, and then remembering her.

"So, I'm going to do it, Subaru."

As the "Witch of Melancholy" murmured that, "Subaru" was standing not beside her but directly in front of her. Not floating — his feet planted on the ground, he faced her head-on.

"——"

Staring straight into those black eyes, the "Witch of Melancholy" tasted a strange feeling. However much she struggled, the him standing here was nothing more than an illusion she was seeing the way she wanted to see it — never the real Natsuki Subaru. It was, no doubt, a terribly ridiculous dream if seen from outside, nothing but the most convenient sham for the "Witch of Melancholy."

"And yet, you still won't marry me, will you."

"Petra, I..."

"Just kidding, just kidding. Just a witch joke — I only wanted to fluster you a little."

Sticking her tongue out, the "Witch of Melancholy" apologized to the flustered "Subaru." She rather proudly took the fact that this "Subaru" wouldn't simply bend to her will here as a sign of how noble her own feelings were. It was pleasing, but complicated, when the person you loved turned out exactly as you'd imagined.

"Because, well, I fell in love with the Subaru who taught me that I was an idiot."

"...That's a real honor, then. I'm an outrageously happy giant idiot."

"—That's really true."

At "Subaru's" reply to her confession, the "Witch of Melancholy" let her lips curl into a smile. Then she turned around on the spot, showing "Subaru" her back. After a beat — a pause of thought or hesitation — "Subaru" embraced the "Witch of Melancholy" from behind.

This was a perfect hundred points, matching both her hopes and expectations. And besides, it was surely something even the real, non-illusory Subaru would do too, so it didn't affect the "Witch of Melancholy's" scoring. So, still held in those arms—

"—Found you."

That sticky voice sounded close by, and a threat approached from beyond the billowing smoke. It was Roy Alphard, the Archbishop of Gluttony, who had come charging furiously, guided by the white light released from the fingers the "Witch of Melancholy" had pointed skyward. Roy, baring his fangs, and the "Witch of Melancholy" locked eyes. And then—

"—It's Petra Baumann."

Still pointing her finger to the sky, the "Witch of Melancholy" declared that name. The shuffling of names carried out by the "Aldebusters" as a countermeasure against "Gluttony" — the fruit of a renaming rite, formally accepted as legally valid with the cooperation of Roswaal, their lord. As each of them freely chose their own name, the "Witch of Melancholy" had taken the surname of the person she most revered. But there was no other reason she'd kept this new name hidden until now. —It was her trump card.

"—Petra."

With arms she couldn't touch, "Subaru" still tightened his embrace and called out to her. At that voice, the "Witch of Melancholy" smiled, laying her hand over that likewise untouchable arm. Smiling, the maiden who had paid every price to become the "Witch of Melancholy" declared:

"—Subaru, I love you so much."

Setting it on his tongue, he activated the "Corrosion" — the instant he devoured it, a satisfaction seeped through him, and a shudder ran through his entire body.

"—!!"

This very peak of ecstasy from the "Corrosion" was the true essence of "Gluttony" — something even Roy, who had devoured countless "names" and "memories" and gorged on lives up until now, could imagine no substitute for.

Hunger, hunger, an all-too-excessive hunger — that starvation was the raison d'être of "Gluttony." —In the instant one could delude oneself that even a fragment of it had been sated, an unbearable fullness of life could be felt. And that was, precisely—

"—Exquisite!!"

The taste of the "Witch" who had kept her promise intoxicated Roy — licking his lips at the aftertaste — with the most immense love. Captivated by emotion beyond what he'd expected, an intensity beyond what he'd feared, a purity beyond what he'd hoped for, Roy savored, in the fullness soaking through his body, what a true "Corrosion" really was. Truly, truly, truly, truly, truly — he had lived for this one taste.

—Even if his soul was to be burned as the price of breaking the curse mark, he had no regrets about this "Corrosion."

In the "memories" flowing into him, the reason Rui had so thirsted for this was made plainly clear. But unfortunately, unlike his sister, what Roy's taste buds found the most exquisite was the girl's prayer. A "Witch" who had loved a single dish this much had magnificently exceeded his expectations from before he'd even tasted her.

And so, Roy Alphard, the Archbishop of Gluttony, laughed. Burning in the blue flames that marked his breaking of the curse seal, he laughed.

"—Now that was a feast!"

And then, in the instant his very soul was to be consumed entirely by the blue flames—

"—That's far enough."

A crimson flame, suddenly arising on the battlefield, had mercilessly cut through that blue flame in a single flash.

—If asked for the byword of the "Sword Saint" Reinhard van Astrea, naturally the "Sword Saint's Divine Protection" would be the first thing to come up.

But anyone who knew Reinhard's absurdity better than that would also know that the many other Divine Protections he possessed were part of what made his name known as the strongest "Sword Saint" in history.

In the past, when he fought a battle against Regulus Corneas, the Archbishop of Greed and one of the strongest beings in history, Natsuki Subaru, who was present there and fought alongside him, called the "Phoenix's Divine Protection" — among the many Divine Protections Reinhard possessed — especially outlandish.

To begin with, despite being a being for whom taking even a single life was already difficult given his overwhelming skill, the power of the "Phoenix's Divine Protection," which let him revive even after death, was quite literally a foul move.

But if Natsuki Subaru, who'd judged the "Phoenix's Divine Protection" a cheat ability with equal parts exasperation, knew its true outlandishness, his assessment would surely have been overturned all over again.

"—'The Phoenix's Divine Protection.'"

Since it had already been used once in that fight against the Archbishop of Greed, this was, precisely speaking, a new, subsequent "Phoenix's Divine Protection" newly granted to him — but the phenomenon it produced was the same, so the distinction meant nothing. What mattered was that, when this Divine Protection activated, the lost life of its bearer, Reinhard van Astrea, was revived, and at that moment, every wound he bore, fatal injuries included, was completely healed.

So at this moment, every wound Reinhard had sustained over three days and three nights of battle against the afterimage of the "Witch of Envy" — fought to stave off the world's ruin at the very brink — was wiped away. That included the injury Aldebaran, in cooperation with the "Divine Dragon," had inflicted, rendering both of Reinhard's arms useless — even the exhaustion of fighting on without sleep was burned away by that flame of vitality.

That alone would be more than enough proof of the outlandish power of the "Phoenix's Divine Protection," but the true value of why Reinhard had relied on this Protection lay further ahead.

The threat of the "Witch of Envy," revived into the present age through some mechanism Aldebaran had contrived — the instant it met a sudden vanishing, just as abruptly as its appearance, Reinhard recognized the end of that battle — and reached the decision to immediately inflict a fatal wound upon himself.

If the only goal had simply been to heal his own wounds, Reinhard wouldn't have bothered to injure himself — he would have waited for those wounds to heal naturally. In other words, what Reinhard sought from the "Phoenix's Divine Protection" wasn't the healing of wounds, but the other effect that Protection carried. And that was—

"——"

—Wreathed in crimson flame, in an instant, Reinhard was reborn dead-center in the battlefield.

That was precisely one facet of the outlandish effect the "Phoenix's Divine Protection" held — the flame of resurrection, which reborn a dead bearer at the location of his choosing. In the fight against Regulus Corneas, it was a power that had revived him right in front of Natsuki Subaru, who'd fought alongside him — but where Reinhard had revived at this moment needed no explanation. It was at his liege's side — the one who should be cleaning up after Reinhard's own mess, fighting Aldebaran.

"——"

Reinhard, manifesting on the battlefield from within flames swirling against the wind, grasped in an instant that this was an abnormal space struck by cataclysm. Even Reinhard couldn't help but be startled by that sight, but—

"Reinhard!"

Turning at the sudden call, Reinhard witnessed his friend — lovely of face, desperation lodged within her — and, cradled in that friend's arms, his liege — Felt. At a glance he registered that this was no ordinary matter, and Reinhard tried to instinctively rush to his liege's side. Felt, held in the arms of his friend Ferris, hung her head, drained of strength. There couldn't be anyone more suited to this than Ferris, but he tried at least to call out to her—

"—Oi."

"——"

Faster than Reinhard could step in, that weak, rasping voice was spun out. Amid the dust, Felt — wrapped in the pale glow Ferris emitted, her head bathed in concentrated healing light — slowly raised her face, her focus wavering again and again, and yet still caught the First Knight in her red eyes. Then, scraping together every last bit of strength left in her body, she formed a single phrase. It was—

"—The rest, I'm leaving to you."

At that one line, Reinhard understood that what he ought to do wasn't to worry over his liege. Reinhard's liege was strong — far stronger, in fact, than Reinhard himself. And so—

"—That's far enough."

Leaving his liege, who had done what needed doing, to his friend, and pushed onward at her back, Reinhard went forth into the battlefield. In an instant he grasped the positions of every presence scattered across the area, and among them, made for the enemy he needed to deal with, cutting down Roy Alphard of "Gluttony," wrapped in blue flame, erasing him from this world in a single flash.

"—Now that was a feast!"

What had been burning Roy Alphard as he laughed like that was a blue flame that never went out, kindled by his own soul as fuel. And so, Reinhard delivered the mercy stroke, so he wouldn't suffer long. And then—

"—Sir Al, this time, I will stop you."

Against Aldebaran standing amid the dust, Reinhard kicked off the ground and flew. It was a movement swift enough to seem to crush space itself, without even borrowing the power of "Compression." Between that Reinhard and Aldebaran, a girl instinctively spread her arms wide. A red-haired girl moved at top speed to shield Aldebaran. With a single kick, Reinhard's hand-blade — allowing not even the time to react — struck her neck, mowing her consciousness away into the distance in an instant.

And then, against the now-defenseless Aldebaran, Reinhard drew his arm back once more—

"—That is my duty."

—In an instant, the "Sword Saint's" true strength struck Aldebaran's torso in a single blow.

What psychology had driven him to instinctively reach out toward her as she fell? Either way, Aldebaran's hand hadn't reached her. It was always like that. And then, without even a moment to blink, a flash of white swept across Aldebaran's torso—

"—Ohh."

It happened. Cut in a single flash of a hand-blade above the waist, Aldebaran was, without a doubt, split cleanly in two. But from the very moment he was split in two, regeneration began, and with a force just like a video playing in reverse, Aldebaran's flesh restored itself — his severed torso mended instantly. It was a body-severing magic trick made real by the abnormal potency of the "Blue" healing magic cast upon Aldebaran, combined with the sharpness of the "Sword Saint's" hand-blade, which far surpassed any ordinary famed sword.

But—

"I won't die...!"

Did that outcry ring out as affirmation, or denial? Which was it? Reinhard's blow — yes, Reinhard's. His appearance, when Aldebaran had supposedly neutralized him by summoning the "Witch of Envy" herself, and the merciless hand-blade he'd taken — and yet, he didn't die. Having severed his torso, Reinhard must have attacked meaning to kill Aldebaran. But the "Blue" healing arts had surpassed even that "Sword Saint's" murderous intent, and blocked the Authority from activating.

"——"

Ignoring a pain so brief he could scarcely be sure it had even happened, Aldebaran thought. Reinhard was here. Bad. Dangerous. Everything would be wiped out. And moreover, if Reinhard's being here wasn't because he'd abandoned the world, then it meant the "Witch of Envy" had withdrawn. And if the "Witch of Envy" had withdrawn, then there was no other conclusion than that the reason for silencing him had vanished. —Which meant, in other words, proof that Roy had broken the Oath of his curse mark and used his Authority against the "Witch of Melancholy." Knowing full well he'd pay the penalty for doing so with his life.

Was he saying an Archbishop of Sin had thrown away even his life, as revenge for being forced into obedience? One thing was certain — Aldebaran, through his own actions, had severed the possibility of breaking through this situation.

"—!"

The possibility called "Gluttony" had slipped through his fingers. Acknowledging that fact, Aldebaran gnashed his teeth as his thoughts sparked, deciding to spend every action from here on out on breaking through this situation. Not a breath, not a heartbeat, not even a single drop of sweat trickling from one finger — none of it could be wasted, not one bit. Sternly admonishing his own soul with that, Aldebaran wrapped the falling Yae in his arms. —Wrapped Yae in his arms?

"—Ah?"

Only a moment ago, Aldebaran had just resolved what he ought to do. He'd meant to spend even the merest instant of every instant from here on out on surviving this accident — he was certain he'd made up his mind to that — so why had he reached out for Yae and pulled her into his arms? She had no consciousness, and there was zero chance this was one of her signature feigned-death tricks. Holding Yae up wouldn't break through the situation. And yet, why had Aldebaran—

"You know it too. —That this is as far as it goes."

To Aldebaran, standing in bewildered shock, unable to make sense of the meaning of his own actions, Reinhard's voice called out — hale and hearty, as though he hadn't just fought a death-match against the "Witch of Envy." That this was as far as it went, that he should give up — Reinhard said it so easily. Said it, so easily.

"Sure, I bet the 'Sword Saint' is carrying plenty of his own burdens too... but I sure as hell didn't start this with cheap enough resolve to just let it slide."

"——"

"Not yet, not yet. This much... you think I'm going to give up over something like this!?"

Putting up a bluster, Aldebaran glared at Reinhard. He wanted, at the very least, to overpower his opponent through sheer will, to grasp even one single thing with his fingers. But against that force from Aldebaran, Reinhard raised the hand he'd swung the hand-blade with, in answer. The "Sword Saint's" hand-blade, which had once severed Aldebaran in two, was clutching something tightly.

—A black orb, woven of magic, that had no business being there.

"Even now?"

Eyes wide with shock, speechless, Aldebaran understood that the aim of the "Sword Saint's" earlier slash hadn't been his life, but had been, precisely, to sever his torso in two. Reinhard had done it on purpose. —Trusting in the power of "Blue," he had literally split him open. He'd split Aldebaran's stomach open, and pulled that out of him.

The thing he was supposed to have thrown away into the Grand Mogolade Geyser, the hole that led to the far side of the world—

"But, y'know, you can't crack it open!!"

The shock hadn't left him. Even so, the initiative was still his. It was a Forbidden Art devised by the "Witch," one that surely sealed away anything drawing power from a Witch Factor — not even Reinhard's monstrous strength, not even the breath of a world-destroying "Dragon," could break it. And no matter what happened, Aldebaran would never release that seal—

"No — what's sealed in here is Subaru, isn't it? —If that's the case, it can be drawn out."

In that instant, the atmosphere trembled with a torrent of extraordinary power, and the world itself gave a distinct shudder. That was how great an event it was for that sword to be drawn — great enough that the world itself couldn't ignore it. It was—

"—The 'Dragon Sword,' Reid."

An impossible flash of light split the small magic orb in the "Sword Saint's" hand clean in two.

The "Witch" who named Aldebaran the "Trailing Star" and set everything in motion had said that.

The "Sun Princess," who gave Aldebaran a meaning beyond just the "Trailing Star," had said that.

And then—

If it had been a lament at being overwhelmed by the water, unable to hold it back, he could have borne it. If it had been a sigh of relief at Reinhard's arrival, he could have borne it. If it had been satisfaction at seeing that hateful Aldebaran driven into a corner, he could have borne it.

Leaping out from within the light was a black-haired boy cradling a Great Spirit dressed in a gown — a figure who could be no one else — dropping to one knee under the momentum of his release from the black orb. And then, his lips parting, the very first thing he cried out was—

"—Rem!!"

As though answering that voice, the sound of a chain pierced Aldebaran's eardrums.

—Rem loves Natsuki Subaru.

This, by now, was an unshakable fact, one that even the Rem from before she'd fully regained her "memories" would admit to, however reluctantly. Or rather, whether she had her "memories" or not, it was inevitable that Rem would be drawn to Subaru, so honestly, it makes her wonder what she'd even been holding back for.

"No, that's not right. That's a bit too much of a leap, or rather... first of all, that would make it sound as if I'd fall for him even if he'd never done anything for me at all."

Rem thinks her own feelings aren't something so convenient as that. If anything, she's a difficult, suspicious woman who doesn't easily let her guard down — that's how Rem sees herself. Even she has to shudder at herself, wondering what an unapproachable girl she is. Quite different from the honest, compassionate Ram, or the adorable Katya, whose true feelings show right through her insults.

Yes, the "original" Rem was standoffish, ill-mannered, ugly in character. Narrow in view and narrow in world, so consumed with guarding her own limited sanctuary that she had no room to spare for consideration toward those around her. —And yet, Natsuki Subaru stepped in.

He stepped in, trampled through, took her by the hand, drew her out — and Rem fell completely for him. And those who've become like this, those who go on becoming like this, will only keep growing in number from here.

"But, even so, the fact that Rem was the very first one to become like this doesn't change."

Rem here was the first one to understand what was good about Natsuki Subaru. Emilia might give her a good fight for it, but in depth and weight, Rem will insist on being first. That, too, is unshakable. —A precious thing, bound up with Natsuki Subaru.

"The mansion, the royal capital, Flugel's Tree."

The path that Rem, who loved Natsuki Subaru before losing her "memories," walked together with him.

"The dense forest, the walled city, the imperial capital, the Coupled Dragon Carriages."

The path that Rem, who began loving Natsuki Subaru after losing her "memories," walked together with him.

"The past, the present, and what's to come."

The path that Rem, having now regained her "memories" and fallen in love with Natsuki Subaru all over again, will walk together with him.

Even without Yae telling her so, over and over, Rem understands well enough that it's crude to bundle all of that up under the single word "love." Even so, if asked what drives her, what fuels the hope that lets her declare she'll have no regrets no matter what happens — she still can't think of anything besides "love."

Because of love, Rem can believe in Natsuki Subaru. Because of love, Rem can devote herself for Natsuki Subaru's sake. Because of love, Rem can wish for the same future as Natsuki Subaru.

"—Sister Rem, let me ask you just one thing."

It was an exchange she'd had, before heading into battle, with a girl who, just like Rem, loved Natsuki Subaru.

She, too, Rem understood, was one who'd met Natsuki Subaru and stepped off her "original" course — one who thought herself fortunate for it, and had decided to walk the thorny path he trod alongside him. One who knew full well that his eyes looked straight at someone other than herself, and yet couldn't help but wish for that loving gaze to be turned her own way regardless.

"Sister Rem — if it's to help Subaru, is there anything you couldn't do?"

Later, Rem would think back on it. Just how much courage had it taken to voice that question? Just how much deliberation had it taken to convey the words prepared beyond it?

And if, hypothetically, Rem had given an answer that didn't meet her expectations, just how much resolve and how many ideals had that girl been prepared to shoulder, to walk that path alone?

"Yes, of course. —Because I am Subaru-kun's Rem."

So being able to answer that without hesitation is, for Rem, a pride beyond anything else. If she had to put it into words, that was a contest of whose feelings for Natsuki Subaru ran stronger. And a secret vow that, of all people, could absolutely not be left to Emilia, who also holds feelings for him.

What will happen from fulfilling that vow, Rem honestly doesn't know. All she knows is that it's a help to Natsuki Subaru — no, the reverse. That she can become the first of the many feelings that Natsuki Subaru, that kind-hearted boy, will go on carrying.

To wish for anything more would be extravagant. But now that her "memories" are back and she's grown greedy, even knowing it's extravagant, she finds herself wishing, just a little, for something beyond that. For instance: that when the boy, pushed forward by Rem and the others, walks that path, thinking of Rem's and that girl's devotion — a wave a little bigger than a ripple, just a little, rises in his chest.

That alone is Rem's—

"—Rem!!"

Beyond the white light, the instant she saw his figure appear, Rem's touching reverie shattered to pieces. In that instant, tossed about by an endless torrent of emotion surging up from within herself, Rem was seized, without meaning to be, by the urge to fling everything aside and run straight into his chest. She held herself back.

Right after, the earth her soles bit down hard into burst apart, and a rising rock wall was about to sweep across her field of vision — but a slash from the "Sword Saint," still trailing the afterglow of flame on his body, cut it away, and a path opened. Straight, in one line, the gazes of Rem and her beloved boy met.

"——"

Her eyes crossed with the black eyes of that boy — desperate, and yet unable to entirely wipe away his innate gentleness. Swallowing down a thousand words she wanted to say, ten thousand feelings she wanted to convey, a hundred million prayers she wanted to offer, Rem says:

"Please, let this me, right now... let Rem, fall in love with you all over again."

In that instant, she swung her arm with all her might. —An iron ball flew straight, in one line, across the sky.

—The sound of a chain rings out.

In that moment, none of it mattered to Aldebaran at all — not his own blood-spitting scream, not the earth heaving up from the colossal magic he'd unleashed in a frenzy, not the fact that the supreme slash the "Sword Saint" had swept through the freezing air had even canceled out the cataclysm itself, not that the man who'd appeared cradling a spirit seemed to have grasped the whole situation using the Authority of "Greed," not even that he'd, without realizing it, tightened his hold on the warm-bodied woman in his arms. None of it.

He only reached out, recklessly. —No, he tried to reach out. He couldn't. His right arm held the woman who'd betrayed him, and his lost left arm had been left behind, along with the "Witch's" expectations, in that bygone era — Aldebaran couldn't reach out. Instead—

"—Al."

He heard the man who'd drawn the attention of everyone present staring at him, calling to him. The emotion held in those black eyes, that hue that belonged to Natsuki Subaru, who seemed as though he might, if he could, even want to save Aldebaran himself — that, most of all, most of all, most of all—

"I hate you, goddamn it—"

—The sound of a chain rang out.
