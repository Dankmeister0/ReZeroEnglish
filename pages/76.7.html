Side Story: "The Best Day of Rem's Life"

1

Her heart raced, and she could distinctly feel her skin taking on a faint warmth.

Even as she was kept busy with cooking, cleaning, laundry, and all the other sundry daily chores, most of her mind kept carving out space to think about something else entirely.

Yet if you asked whether her hands grew careless and her efficiency dropped as a result, quite the opposite — she was praised for work more careful and precise than ever before. There was no room for self-reproach.

Amazed at her own fickleness, she quickened her steps in search of the person she was looking for—

"—Ah."

The moment that back came into view, a sound slipped from her throat.

The unexpected tone of her own voice had gone slack with joy, clear even to herself, and her cheeks colored faintly. How unseemly. But even so — the girl, Rem, walked toward that back.

"Oh? Don't tell me you've finally got a break from work too, Rem?"

Noticing the presence behind him, the owner of that back turned around with a smile.

Short black hair, sharp sanpaku eyes, an expression more charming than dignified, innocent and endearing like a mischievous little boy — though perhaps that was Rem's partiality talking.

Oblivious to what was going on in her heart, the boy tilted his head, and Rem stopped in her tracks, smiling back at him.

"Yes. —Shall we have some tea, Subaru-kun?"

And then, with the face of a girl in love, she spoke the name of the one she yearned for.

2

These days, Rem was well aware that she had been struck by a fever.

She couldn't get one boy out of her head; before she knew it, she'd find herself thinking of nothing but him all day long. Literally, whether asleep or awake — that sort of thing.

"Was Rem always such a flighty girl...?"

She had, in fact, once questioned whether there was something abnormal about how far her feelings had gone.

For Rem, the person closest to her afflicted with the same fever was her beloved elder sister, Ram. The devotion Ram held for Roswaal was of the same kind as the feelings Rem held for Subaru.

And yet, Rem could hardly imagine her sister spending her days as flustered by her own emotions as Rem herself was.

—Sister truly, truly is amazing.

That was Rem's unwavering affection for her sister. Not once had her respect for her sister ever wavered, and even after losing her horn, it had never once crossed her mind that she herself had become the better of the two.

Which was precisely why Rem suspected her own fever might be even more severe than Ram's.

"Hey, Rem?"

The voice slipping into her eardrum pulled her back to reality, and Rem blinked, going "ah." The voice had come from right beside her, close enough that she could feel his breath—

"Yes, what is it, Subaru-kun?"

"'What is it,' she says... isn't this a little close? I mean, it's fine, but doesn't it make it hard to drink?"

"Is that so? I don't think that's the case at all."

At Subaru's wry words, Rem pressed herself even closer as she answered, feverish.

The two of them were in the parlor of Roswaal Manor, sitting side by side on a single sofa, enjoying tea. Rem's right side was pressed snugly against Subaru's left.

Though, to be fair, they'd started out sitting a little further apart — this was the result of Rem gradually inching closer.

"Oh, Subaru-kun, would you like a refill of the baked sweets with your tea?"

"Oh, if there's more, I want some, I want some. These things you make, Rem, are seriously delicious."

"Since you praised them before, Subaru-kun, I got a little carried away. Will a hundred or so be enough?"

"Are you plotting to fatten me up and eat me!? Two or three is plenty!"

The pastries filled with thick, sweet cream were Subaru's favorite. They'd made repeated appearances at their tea gatherings over the past several days, and today was no exception.

"Well then, I'll go prepare some. Subaru-kun, I won't forgive you if you wander off on your own."

"I wouldn't do something that awful. If you're worried, want to make a promise? A promise."

"Yes, let's. Let's make one."

As if she'd been waiting for exactly this, Rem held out her pinky finger, and Subaru, with a faint wry smile, offered his own in return. Their fingers linked together, and with a short chant, the promise was sealed.

"—Pinky, swear."

—It was probably this promise that had pushed her fever into its terminal stage, Rem thought vaguely.

The witchbeast incident that had occurred a few days earlier — once that disturbance had settled and the injured Subaru had woken, Rem had apologized to him, and in turn, had had her heart utterly ensnared by a sweet, gentle punishment.

How thoroughly that future — the promise they had come to share — had melted her with sweetness.

Lately, she found herself seeking out promises over even the smallest, most trivial things. Indulging in Subaru's willingness — even if he only accepted with a wry smile — she let her heart burn ever brighter each time a future was promised.

"Oh right, speaking of promises—"

Still feeling the warmth of his pinky, Rem was about to rise from the sofa when his voice stopped her. After that preface, Subaru ran his fingers roughly through his own black hair and said—

"—You promised you'd cut my hair for me, right? Think I could ask you to make good on that soon?"

Subaru smiled, utterly unaware of just how much his words had set Rem's heart racing.

3

—Rem, cutting Subaru's hair with her own two hands.

That promise was, for Rem, one of her most treasured and unforgettable promises.

"After all, it's the very first promise Rem ever made with Subaru-kun."

In the midst of the witchbeast incident, in the forest they'd entered to rescue the village children, Subaru and Rem had made that promise. Back then, Rem hadn't yet felt anything in particular toward Subaru.

Which was exactly why it meant so much for the Rem of today to fulfill that promise.

"It's not like I forgot about it, it's just things have been hectic this whole time."

"That's not very like you, Subaru-kun, being so considerate. You could just go on acting on impulse the moment something crosses your mind, like usual, and cause Rem trouble — that's fine too."

"Huh!? Somehow, after a while, Rem's venom really stabs right into my chest!?"

At Subaru clutching his chest and hunching over on the chair, Rem's soft smile only deepened. Even that reaction looked adorable to her, but she firmly resolved in her heart not to say so.

In any case, to make good on the promise to cut his hair, the two of them found themselves in the manor's courtyard. A chair was set up on the neatly trimmed green lawn, a towel wrapped around Subaru's neck, and the preparations were complete.

"Getting a haircut out in the garden feels kind of strange. You see it a lot in anime and manga, though."

"Doing it indoors is a hassle, both setting up and cleaning up afterward. Outside, all you have to do is brush it off and rinse your head later, and that's enough. How short would you like it?"

As far as Rem could tell, Subaru's hair already looked plenty short. There was just a bit of unevenness in the length, so simply trimming that up should make it look considerably better.

"That's a question I always struggle with even at the barber's. Ah, just — whatever length looks coolest to you, Rem, that's what I'll go with."

"Subaru-kun looks wonderful no matter what."

"At that rate, haircut time's over before it even starts!"

Rem held down the shoulders of Subaru, who was rattling the chair, and studied his head carefully.

There wasn't a shred of a lie in what she'd just said, but it was also true that it wasn't a satisfactory answer.

How could she get every person in the world to share the feelings Rem held for Subaru?

"This is a heavy responsibility... But then again, it'd be a problem if everyone noticed how charming Subaru-kun is."

"Um, Rem-san? You really don't have to agonize over it so seriously, okay? Look, like you said before, just trimming the ends and thinning it out a bit would be fine."

Subaru made the suggestion hesitantly, but Rem wasn't in any mood to listen. Lightly misting Subaru's hair with a spray bottle, she took up the silver scissors and prepared to face this challenge head-on.

"—————"

Just as she moved to begin, her hand froze. —No, not froze. It was shaking terribly.

Rem stared, stunned, at her own hand trembling and clattering. Even as she concentrated on keeping her composure, the tension from her entire body seemed to have gathered in her right hand alone, and the shaking wouldn't stop.

"Subaru-kun, this is terrible. My hand — my hand won't stop shaking."

"Huh? I mean, a little hand-shaking isn't— whoa, that's shaking a lot!? What is that, are you okay!? You're shaking like you're about to gouge out my skull — aren't you used to cutting hair!?"

"Rem has done Sister's and Roswaal-sama's hair before... but this is Rem's first time with Subaru-kun."

"If that's how it works, barbershops would never do any business at all!"

It was certainly true that a barbershop refusing first-time customers couldn't stay in business. But irrespective of any such logic, Rem's hand kept trembling the moment she tried to cut Subaru's hair.

"Ah, Rem, well, it's not like I can go so far as to say 'don't worry even if you mess up!' but you really don't need to be this tense. Even if you cut a bit too much, it'll grow back to normal in about a week."

"But, for Rem's own hands to leave Subaru-kun without hair for a whole week..."

"I'd stop you well before it got to a full buzzcut, though!?"

Holding back Subaru, who had started rattling the chair again, Rem struggled to steady her trembling hand, and then voiced a sudden question. It was—

"Subaru-kun, aren't you scared, entrusting your body to Rem like this?"

It was a question that had simply slipped out, somehow, of its own accord.

But the moment she spoke it, the question settled onto Rem's shoulders, heavy with meaning.

—No matter how much she might explain it away in words, the fact that Rem had once done Subaru harm would never disappear.

In the witchbeast forest, Rem had come close to letting Subaru die twice over. The first time, by abandoning him to death; the second, nearly by her own hand. And if she counted the time at the end, when Subaru had made himself a decoy for her sake, that made three.

"Twice, in the forest, Rem showed Subaru-kun her demon form. ...And even before that, Rem—"

There had been a time when she'd suspected Subaru's very presence might be a dangerous element that needed to be eliminated. That suspicion had surely been eating away at Rem's weak heart, and would have exploded eventually.

It was only by the outcome that she hadn't killed him — surely, Rem would have gone through with it.

The guilt of that unrealized crime still tormented Rem, the would-be perpetrator, even now. And for Subaru, the would-be victim, it was only natural that it should be an even more painful memory.

And yet, why did Subaru smile at her so defenselessly, so openly, like this?

"Rem."

In a quiet voice, Subaru spoke Rem's name.

Her throat tightened, and no answer came right away. Fear and anticipation for what he might say next leapt about in her chest.

"Back where I'm from, there's a fairy tale called 'The King Has Donkey Ears.'"

"...Huh, what?"

"There's this king in some country whose ears are long, like a donkey's. So whenever the king gets his hair cut, he calls for a barber from the kingdom — but every barber, the moment he realizes the king's ears are a donkey's, laughs, and gets his head chopped off for it. Then, among them, there's finally one barber who somehow manages to hold back his laughter. But even he can't stand carrying the king's secret around forever, so he digs a hole up in the mountains and shouts into it—"

Subaru cupped both hands around his mouth and shouted at the top of his voice.

"'The king has donkey ears——!' Like that. And every single time he did it, a nearby tree ended up memorizing it, and when they made a flute out of that tree's wood, blowing it would make it play, 'The king has donkey ears.' And once the whole kingdom's blowing that flute, there's no way it stays a secret anymore. That's the story."

"Um, er...?"

She thought it an amusing tale, but couldn't grasp what Subaru meant by it. Looking up at Rem's questioning face, Subaru twisted his cheek into a grin, and—

"Rem's got demon horns on her head——!"

"—————"

Subaru shouted out loud once more. Both the voice and its content startled Rem enough to make her shoulders jump. Pleased with her reaction, Subaru slapped his knee with exaggerated flair, and—

"The king was so ashamed of his donkey ears that he cut off the head of anyone who spread the secret around. So, is Rem ashamed that she's a demon?"

"N-no, Rem isn't ashamed of her birth... not anymore, at least."

"Or maybe you can't forgive me for spreading your secret around like that?"

"No. Rem doesn't think she needs to hide the fact that she's a demon."

She looked back into Subaru's eyes and answered clearly. At her answer, Subaru resettled himself in the chair and said—

"Then there's no problem at all. If whether or not Rem might hurt me has nothing to do with her being a demon, then I've got no reason to be scared. Ah, that's a relief, a relief."

"B-but, Rem really did nearly...to Subaru-kun..."

"Every guy gets taught by his dad from the time he's a little kid that making a girl angry gets you into scary trouble. I forgot that and messed up, so it's on me. We're even."

At such a reckless piece of logic, Rem was left speechless with astonishment.

Turning only his head back toward the now-silent Rem, Subaru continued, "So, then—"

"How's that shaking hand of yours doing?"

"Ah..."

Glancing down, Rem realized her hand had long since stopped trembling. Even with the tip of the scissors pointed at Subaru's head, her fingers no longer shook at all.

She couldn't say exactly what effect Subaru's words had had on her heart. After all, these days, every single word of his left an enormous impact on her heart without fail.

Still, she understood one thing — that her cowardly heart had been swallowed up entirely within the fever, and had vanished completely.

Before Rem, her trembling now stilled, Subaru slouched his weight lazily back into the chair. And then—

"Make me look cool, alright? The kind of cool that'd make Emilia-tan go weak in the knees without even meaning to."

Though those words carried a meaning that pricked at Rem sharply, very sharply indeed, she found the boy who couldn't even notice such a thing utterly dear to her, and smiling, she said—

"—Subaru-kun is wonderful, always."

Fin
