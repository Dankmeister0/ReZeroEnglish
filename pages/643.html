Arc 8 Chapter 27: "Berstetz Fondalfon"

"Berstetz, I'll leave this place to you. Hold your ground, even if it kills you."

"Yes. And you, Your Excellency—please stay safe."

That had been their last exchange.

It was a businesslike master-and-servant relationship; asked whether it was what one might call a strong bond, he would answer no.
He had sensed a great vessel in her talent and bearing, put to use the discernment he'd acquired with age, smoothed her path, and sought to lead her to the throne the Emperor should occupy.
It was a lord-and-vassal relationship that held together without any loyalty or passion directed at her personally.

He was a man who, given one thing, could grasp a hundred, and from a hundred, produce a hundred and one—no need to spell out every instruction from one to ten.
A man who could hold both himself and others to duties befitting their abilities and obligations befitting their station.

Blessed with no martial prowess, yearning for the rank of Sword Wolf of the Empire yet never reaching it.
Never respected by others, capable of living only a life of contempt—the fact that she had called him useful was, surely, something worth being grateful for.

And so there had not been a shred of falsehood in the words they had last exchanged.

In the midst of the Imperial Selection Ceremony, he had been sternly ordered to hold his ground even at the cost of his life, and he had been prepared for that.
Unable even to call himself a Sword Wolf, a man who had simply grown old and withered—this had been his final service.
Even in a lord-and-vassal bond without passion, there had been the resolve to die at his post.

And yet, Berstetz had survived.
And the lord whose safety he had prayed for had lost her life.

Even now, Berstetz Fondalfon continues to expose himself to a shameful life.
Having failed to fulfill his duty, he pursued the position of an Emperor set to lead the Empire to ruin, failed to notice the Great Disaster creeping forward behind his own machinations, and remained a disgrace unworthy even of calling himself a Sword Wolf.
Unable to carry out even one of the orders given him, he goes on, aimlessly, exposing himself to a shameful life.

"General Ralfon, what's wrong?"
"Aren't you being a bit unlike yourself, considering how fond you are of the Vollachian imperial family?"
"Or is it that a woman like me, who lost the Imperial Selection Ceremony, just isn't to your taste?"

The same voice issued from different mouths, and he found himself fixed by countless pairs of golden eyes.
Goz Ralfon, clad in golden armor the same color as those eyes, twisted the scars covering his face and ground his back teeth at the horror his own eyes were showing him.

He drove his warhammer down with all his might.
The blood of the Empire's Sword Wolf flowing within Goz screamed in protest, but he endured it, and struck down Lamia—one of the imperial family he ought to revere—as an enemy to be felled.
Tasting a pain like his very soul being torn apart, Goz understood, in the truest sense, the result of what he had done.

—That the Great Disaster was, without question, a threat aiming to bring about the ruin of the Vollachian Empire.

"For what reason...!"

Goz ground his teeth; before him, Lamia—no, the Lamias—tilted their heads.
He watched orange hair spill smoothly down over their slender shoulders, and something in Goz's gut burned.

"For what reason would you permit such a thing!! There is no blasphemy greater than this! You mock Lady Lamia's very life!!"

Riding his voice with rising anger, burning away the rising tears with hot blood, Goz appealed to the countless duplicated bodies of Lamia before him.
But at that appeal from the depths of Goz's gut, the Lamias, as one, pressed the backs of their hands to their lips. And then, voices lilting with amusement,

"Don't misunderstand, General Ralfon. Nobody made me do this—I did it myself."

"...What... are you saying?"

"That Witch is so rigid in her thinking. If something can be revived after being shattered, then it can just as well be revived before it's shattered. Vessels can be made in any number—all that's left is to thin the source and fill them. Once you grasp that intuitively—well, can't you do something as dreamlike as this?"

With an alluring, blood-tinted smile spreading over her pale face, Lamia Godwin—her demonic cleverness unchanged from when she lived—spread her arms wide to flaunt her multiplied existence.

"—————"

Dreamlike, she'd called it, and even in his heart Goz could not help but agree.
Lamia, multiplied without limit, defiling her own existence of her own will. And every single one of these Lamias held the Sunlight Sword, the Empire's symbol.
If this was not, as Lamia herself said, a dream—a nightmare—then what was it?

"Say, General Ralfon, in reality you can't smash your way out of a dream. Why not accept that... and come over to our side?"

"—. What exactly do you mean by that?"

"It's not complicated. Once you die, you'll be in the same position as me and the other girls. If it's going to happen anyway, why not side with the winners early?"

Pressing the hand that held her sword to her chest, Lamia tilted her head and made the invitation.
If one fell in battle, that person would join the ranks of Lamia's army as one of the Undead. It was already happening across the battlefield, but to actually imagine it placed an incalculable strain on the mind.

Even Goz, who clung fiercely to loyalty as an imperial soldier, and the officers and men who surely felt the same, would—once fallen—join the side bent on destroying the Empire, and think nothing more of it.
Just as Lamia Godwin, who had once had her fingers on the throne of this Empire's Empress, now did.
What would be completed there was an empire of the Undead. —A shadow of the land of the Sword Wolf, nothing more.

"Words too generous for the likes of me, Lady Lamia!"

After squeezing his eyes shut, Goz lifted his face and turned to face Lamia head-on.
Before Goz's fighting spirit, Lamia's shapely brows furrowed.

"Sounds like you're about to refuse."

"For Lady Lamia's magnanimous mercy! I, Goz Ralfon! Must respectfully decline!!"

"Just as I thought—refused."

At Lamia's narrowed golden eyes, Goz thought of the vision he had seen behind his closed eyelids just now.
A vision of himself, life extinguished, become an Undead like the Lamias, pale-faced with golden eyes lodged in them, swinging his warhammer to bring this Empire to ruin—that vision, he smashed to pieces.
Smashing it, shaking off the vision that clung to him, he roared.

"I retract what I said before! I said that not one of the Nine Divine Generals would bend to the Emperor's will as he pleased! But I am His Excellency's faithful pawn! Whatever the others may be! I alone! Wish to remain so!!"

He refused to die and become a mere vanguard of ruin; he wished to remain a living pawn of His Excellency the Emperor.
That was Goz Ralfon's way of being the Empire's Sword Wolf, the path he had chosen to want.
Deep thought, the creation of ideals, all such things he would leave to Vincent, the ruler; Goz would simply fulfill the role expected of him.
Namely—

"I am the 'Fifth' of the Nine Divine Generals, chosen by His Excellency Emperor Vincent Vollachia! Goz Ralfon!!"

Raising his golden warhammer high, he declared he would not yield a single step against the countless Lamias.
The instant he finished his bold declaration, mouth wide open, the Lamias raised their Sunlight Swords and came dancing at him. A single graze of that blade, and Vollachia's crimson flame would burn a soul to nothing.
Before it could reach him, Goz's muscles surged, and his warhammer swept out in a wide arc.

"—!"

Goz's swing, all hesitation gone, caught the leaping Lamias broadside, striking down as many as five of them together in midair, turning bodies as soft as porcelain into powdered dust.
Witnessing that single blow, the remaining Lamias' eyes widened slightly, their expressions shifting.
They gazed at the almost absurdly stubborn Sword Wolf, and smiled cruelly.

"No matter how bravely you roar, once you die, you'll be my slave, you know?"

"If that is an unavoidable fate! Then I shall turn to stone before my life is spent! By not dying, I shall offer that as my final loyal service to His Excellency the Emperor!!"

Swinging his warhammer in a great arc overhead, Goz took up a stance to let none of the Lamias gathered in the lead carriage escape.
If Lamias bearing the Sunlight Sword scattered throughout the coupled dragon carriages, that sunlight might yet burn Vincent's eyes. That, he would prevent.

For that very purpose the massive body of the "Lion Knight" existed—so Goz Ralfon raged, fiercer and fiercer.

"—My, such fierce resolve, but I'm afraid it's for nothing."

In the lead carriage of the coupled dragon carriages, Goz Ralfon roared, staking his very reason for being on not letting the carriage heading toward its destination be robbed of its legs.
His spirit was impressive, but the goal he cared most about would not be achieved.

Unfortunately for Goz, determined to keep the Undead Lamia from going elsewhere, Lamia's form had already reached other carriages of the coupled train.
Of course it had. Even multiplying, there was no need to multiply in the same place. No such restriction had been set. Goz's valiant struggle was not meaningless, but its contribution was slight.

And on a dragon carriage carrying, above all, those who bore vital roles among the Empire's living, it went without saying how important it was to tie up the hands of a powerhouse like Goz Ralfon.

"Sorry, but it's not that you're pinning me down, General Ralfon. It's that I'm the one pinning you down."

Sparing a glance at Goz's lone struggle against the Sunlight Sword, dampening his fervor, Lamia walked on toward the central carriage, gazing into the distance.
In the far sky, there, a Wolf Human clashed with the vast Blight Dragon, black as a storm cloud.

"It's outside my calculations that Valgren's useless, but pinning down that one's hands is a fine result."

Even to Lamia's narrowed golden eyes, the Wolf Human who appeared to be Valgren was in a class apart.
Goz, one of the Empire's nine generals, was without question one of the world's foremost powerhouses. And yet this thing, surpassing even Goz, was contending with the strongest living being in the world.

—No, if anything, the Blight Dragon was the one being pushed back.
That it hadn't been overwhelmed was only because the dragon's recovery from the blows it took outpaced the damage. Shattered again and again, the dragon restored itself, and Shinobi could not finish it off.
Merely not being killed was more than enough to make it an outrageous sight.

"But it's a shame that Wolf Human's no use to me even if he dies."

"—Now that's somethin' I can't let slide."

A small footstep sounded, and at that rough voice Lamia slowly turned around.
Facing the same direction as Lamia, glancing sidelong at the battle between the Blight Dragon and the Wolf Human, stood a golden-haired man—no, a boy.
Lamia gave a small snort and smiled at the boy.

"I thought you smelled like a beast—you're a half-beast, aren't you?"

"...Ain't keen on bein' told that by a dirt-stinkin' woman like you. Nah, that ain't even the half of it."

"—?"

"A woman linin' up a bunch o' the same damn face—my granny was more'n enough of that for me!"

Bang, the half-beast boy struck his fist to his chest, and at that outburst Lamia and the other Lamias narrowed their eyes.
The meaning of the boy's outburst was unclear. Only that, just like the present Lamia, he had before experienced a swarm of identical beings.
And besides that—

"I ain't exactly buddy-buddy with that gold-plated old man, but that old man's got a big damn mouth, so... I already know you're the toughest one here among the ones that came!"

"—————"

Just as the boy bared his fangs and roared that, Lamia sensed a shift in the air.
It was in the individual carriages of the coupled train—if the whole line were divided into fifths, Lamia faced the boy in the third carriage. The change was in the second and fourth carriages, apart from Goz's struggle in the first.

The roof of the second carriage was wrapped in flame, while the fourth froze over under an icy wind, conversely.
Both were carriages carrying different Lamias from this one, along with the Pruning Corps.
Sensing that fact directly, Lamia pressed the back of her hand to her mouth and laughed.

"I seee. Kingdom folk really do hate to give up, huh."

"...How the hell d'you know we came from Lugunica?"

"Just found out now. It's not very Imperial of you, so I was just asking. How cute."

"Nah, that ain't it. Ain't just to mess with me."

Watching Lamia's mocking smile, the boy shook his head.
It wasn't sour grapes. Nor, for that matter, was it insight. If anything, call it the boy's instinct—the fruit of an animal sense for sniffing out truth from falsehood.
He connected that sense of his with Lamia's earlier words in his head.

"Talk about the 'Worshiper' bein' useless, and disappointment 'bout where I'm from tossed in together..."

"—That's quite enough chitchat."

For all the world, a boy not suited to using his head, straining to use it anyway.
Watching someone struggle hard at something they're bad at is, frankly, unpleasant to witness.

Lamia shrugged her slender shoulders, and in response another Lamia lunged at the boy with a blade.
The boy lowered his body to dodge the red blade tip and struck that Lamia's stomach with a backfist. But another Lamia slashed the body he'd sent flying, and the split-in-two Lamia caught fire; from behind the burning curtain of flame, a lethal thrust aimed at the boy.

Barking sharply, the boy kicked off the roof and leapt into the air.
There, an Undead Flying Dragon closed in gliding, and the boy, having fled into the sky, was bitten by the dead dragon's mouth. Carried up into the sky by the Undead Flying Dragon still clamped onto him, other Undead Flying Dragons swarmed toward him.

"General Ralfon too, but the kids who have to get in close have bad compatibility with the Sunlight Sword."

Regripping the unused Sunlight Sword, Lamia murmured this, watching the boy being swarmed by the Undead Flying Dragons.
A single graze, and the flame of the Sunlight Sword burns until it consumes the soul entirely. The effect of the Enchanted Sword, which enhances physical ability just by wielding it, turns even a Vollachian imperial who has coasted on privilege into a first-rate fighter.
Needless to say, the benefit for one who has spent time honing swordsmanship is even greater.

"Now then, the beast-smelling one's dealt with. Next is to find my brother's whereabouts..."

Just as Lamia, moving as if strolling, tried to take a step forward, a sudden roar echoed overhead.
Looking up, the swarm of Undead Flying Dragons, having clustered so thickly they'd formed a round mass, exploded to bits in midair—a dozen-odd of them, all at once. From the center of that explosion appeared a form utterly unlike the slender, supple boy from before: a mighty great tiger.

Swinging arms as thick as bundled logs, the great tiger, having blasted apart the swarm of Undead Flying Dragons, dove down all at once. Its weight groaned against the roof of the coupled dragon carriage, and in an instant it closed on Lamia.

"—————"

Turning, Lamia swung the Sunlight Sword up from a low stance.
There was a moment's surprise. But having taken beast form, the target had grown larger. Against a wielder of the Sunlight Sword, for whom even a graze meant victory, that was a poor move.

The great tiger, leaping in headlong, had the Sunlight Sword rip upward from beneath its chest in one stroke—

"—!!"
"—Oh my."

There came the sound of the Sunlight Sword's tip scraping steel, and Lamia's golden eyes went wide.
The charging great tiger, rather than slamming its thick arm into Lamia, drove its claws into the roof half a step in front of her, forcibly tearing it free and using it as a shield.
Lamia's blade slid across its surface, and then the tiger's opposite arm, spinning around, struck her down.

The impact tore off Lamia's upper body, and the shattered body was knocked out of the carriage.
The great tiger had exceeded Lamia's expectations. But, same as with Goz, it was futile.

"Even if I die..."

Scattering to dust before hitting the ground, Lamia's lips spun out those words.
Even if this body died, it would simply be restored, same as her other selves. And moreover, the advantage of this restoration wasn't merely that dying didn't mean the end.

The next Lamia to be revived could carry over every experience of this Lamia.
Namely—

"—————"

As she fell, sensing the other Lamias and the Pruning Corps about to leap at the great tiger, her shattering vision caught sight of the interior of the coupled dragon carriage, leaving her behind.
Inside that carriage, her eyes met those of someone staring fixedly out the window. Having confirmed which carriage her quarry was in, she could carry that over to the next Lamia.

"—Big brother Vincent, found youuu."

"—Your Excellency."

At the sight visible through the carriage window, Berstetz's ever-narrowed eyes opened slightly, and a voice escaped him.
Just now, shaken from the roof and shattered against the earth, had been none other than Lamia Godwin, revived as an Undead.

Berstetz had already confirmed that fact with his own eyes.
Given that those participating in this ferocious assault on the coupled dragon carriage were the Pruning Corps, whom he knew well, he had also been fully aware that Lamia was the one leading them.
And yet, the sight of Lamia's form shattering, turning to dust, shook Berstetz all the same.

"I had hoped to see it once with my own eyes, but I hadn't expected it to take this form."

"Marquis Dracroy..."

"Don't make that face, Chancellor. You saw the same thing I did, didn't you? If Lady Lamia was shattered like that, perhaps the Pruning Corps, her private army, will stop. Though..."

"—————"

"I doubt we can count on it."

At the Undead Lamia meeting her end outside the carriage window, Serena, wearing a smile, shrugged her shoulders.
Just as her low-expectation remark predicted, even with the Lamia to whom they'd pledged loyalty struck down, the Pruning Corps' ferocious assault showed not a shadow of faltering.

Given the exchange in the earlier war council, death was not the end for the Undead.
Sphinx, likely the mastermind behind the Great Disaster, had used even death itself in her aim to destroy the Empire. If the Witch could do it, why should the "Poison Princess" be incapable of the same?
Even without watching the Pruning Corps' undiminished fighting spirit, it was certain that Lamia's end would come later, not sooner.

"Lady Anastasia, please fall back!"

Raising a dignified voice, the young man in a wasō wielded his flowing sword.
The slash, drawing a rainbow-colored arc, cut through the sturdy black armor the Pruning Corps wore as easily as heated steel through ice.
Even to the eyes of Berstetz, who had never been blessed with martial prowess, it was clear that the young man—Julius—was a match for the Empire's finest warriors.

"But even my Julius can't fight forever. We gotta get things movin'."

Protected by Julius's valiant struggle, Anastasia murmured, stroking the scarf wrapped around her neck.
Julius was holding off the enemies flooding into the carriage, while Garfiel fought them off on the roof, but combined with the Lamia who had just fallen, both inside and out had become fierce battlegrounds.
Indeed, every person capable of fighting, including the guard soldiers, was fighting.

"Even so, don't you think we're bein' targeted a bit excessively?"

"Probably 'cause you're here, don'tcha think? That spot on your chest that was hurtin'—it's got a mark on it, plain as day."

"A mark...? Ohh, wow, you're right!"

Ubilk, having been brought out of the confinement room, peered down at his own loose collar and cried out.
Just as Anastasia said, on his glimpsed white skin was the trace of a red welt, and if it had appeared at the same time as the assault—

"An Evil Eye that tracks the target's location... could it be Lord Palladio Manesque?"

"Well, well, will this turn into a full gathering of the Imperial Selection Ceremony's participants? In that case, I'd love to reminisce with Lord Barthroy, who sent me flowers when I seized the family headship from my father."

The Evil Eye of Palladio Manesque, the Vollachian prince who inherited the blood of the Evil Eye Tribe, had locked onto the target—was that the reason the Pruning Corps kept relentlessly concentrating their attacks here?
As Berstetz surmised this, Serena, keeping enemies at bay with her own sword, licked her lips.

"Whether we move from here or not, I'd rather not die by choosing wrong between forward and back. If we're struck down, the Empire loses not just its head but everything from the waist up, practically speaking. Whether a creature in that state has any path besides dying is a matter of some interest, but..."

"...Please don't say such irreverent things. Setting aside an old husk like myself, there is no replacing His Excellency or you."

"There's no one, anyone, who can't be replaced, Chancellor. Awfully bold of you to say that, considering how you arranged a stand-in for His Excellency the Emperor himself. In any case—"

Serena, never losing her caustic composure no matter the situation, gave Berstetz a little jab about his own doings in the capital, just as things began to move.
It appeared, from the very direction of the choice between forward and back.

"There you are! You held the fort here, huh!"

"Natsuki-kun!"

The door connecting to the rear carriage, already broken open by the Pruning Corps' assault, was trampled over as small shadows tumbled into the passenger car where Berstetz and the others stood.
At the sight of the black-haired boy leading them, Anastasia raised her voice, welcoming the reunion.

Rushing in with a clatter was a boy named Natsuki Subaru, and a girl in a dress holding his hand. Following behind were a Deer Human girl, a blonde girl, and girls with pink and blue hair, identical in face—

"Subaru! What happened to Emilia-sama?"

"Emilia-tan's fighting hard in the rearmost car! She's freezing the carriage to reinforce it so enemies can't get in, but if we'd stayed, we'd have been frozen solid too!"

"How forceful. ...But it's the best move, huh."

Knocking down an Undead breaking through the window head-on, Julius asked this, and Subaru answered. At the boy's grimacing answer, Anastasia closed one eye and murmured, then turned her pale blue-green gaze past him—to the blonde girl.

"Aaugh..."

"Guess that means the talk got interrupted?"

"—No, I've reached my conclusion. What got interrupted was my chance to tell everyone. Where's that bastard Abel!? Has the same mark showed up on him as on me?"

Standing beside the blonde girl, Subaru pulled open the front of his own clothes to show his chest. There, the same welt as on Ubilk, the "Stargazer," had surfaced.
At once, seeing it, Ubilk let out a loud "Ah-ha!"

"See, just as I thought! You've got the mark showing too! That makes us fellow 'Stargazers,' doesn't it?"

"I told you that's wrong! Not just me—even cute little Bea's got the same thing, poor girl! What's the connection!?"

"—That would presumably be an obstacle the Great Disaster needs to eliminate."

That voice leapt into the passenger car from the opposite door Subaru's group had come through—the passage leading to the forward carriage.
Appearing side by side were Otto, the Kingdom's strategist, whose presence had been notable even in the war council, and the girl who had been actively participating in healing the soldiers.
At the appearance of these two, Subaru turned, eyes wide.

"Otto! Petra! You're both okay! Sorry about earlier!"

"Let's save that conversation for later. Right now, sharing important information matters more."

"The mark—the one Subaru and Beatrice-chan had—the master's got it too! Right now he's drawing the zombies' attention in the front carriage, but—"

"Roswaal-sama has the mark too..."

At the report from the girl called Petra, the pink-haired girl who was presumably Rem's kin lowered her eyes.
But if the mark had surfaced not just on Ubilk, Subaru, and the girl called Beatrice, but also on the Kingdom's court mage, the conditions for the branding began to come into hazy focus.
Namely—

"—Margrave Mathers, who came face to face with Sphinx directly on the plain, and Beatrice-chan. Plus Natsuki-kun, who stopped the ambush on the dragon carriage, and the 'Stargazer,' was it?"

"There's no way to confirm it here, but it's quite possible the same mark has been carved onto Halibel-san, who's fighting the black dragon outside."

"And then our Emperor, presumably. There's more than enough chance of that. Every name mentioned so far is someone irreplaceable for what they contribute."

Glancing sidelong, teasing Berstetz's earlier remark, Serena agreed with the intellectuals' conversation.
Berstetz was of the same opinion as what Anastasia and the others had begun discussing. And as long as the effect of Palladio's Evil Eye continued, assassins would be sent, endlessly, after those marked as targets.
However—

"Wait a moment. If that guess is correct, I don't understand why this child—Spica-chan—doesn't have the same mark showing."

The one who raised her voice was Rem, holding the small girl's shoulders from behind.
He had not been told the details of the importance of this girl she called Spica, but he had heard that she was one of those named in Ubilk's prophecy.
On that point, she was undoubtedly important for the sake of the Vollachian Empire, but—

"Spica, is it?"

At Rem's appeal, Otto murmured this with what seemed a different kind of emotion from Berstetz and the others.
Subaru, catching his murmur, fixed his eyes on Otto, his expression turning serious.

"I've decided where I stand. I'll prove it through how I live from here."

"—. What a coincidence. I, too, just happened to decide where I stand."

A quiet exchange, and how complicated the feelings tangled within it, outsiders had no way of guessing. Besides, in the middle of this assault, that was a matter to set aside for later.
What mattered most right now was—

"Garf's roaring away up there—we should move before he turns into roast tiger."

"Don't put it like grilled chicken... But there's a point to what Rem just said."

Nodding at the pink-haired girl's words, Subaru turned around, and everyone's gaze converged on the girl named Spica. Under that concentrated attention, the girl growled in her throat, looking around in confusion.

"If we're to believe this talk of a 'Stargazer,' that girl—Spica—is the natural enemy of the Great Disaster, I suppose. And yet, why is she being overlooked?"

"—? Is that so strange? I mean, we wouldn't know either if that man hadn't told us, right? So maybe it's the same for the other side too?"

"...You mean there's no 'Stargazer' on the Undead's side?"

At the exchange of questions between the girls, Subaru murmured, looking struck by the thought.
Ubilk, addressed by that remark, shook his head weakly and unreliably.

"Nooo, sorry. I don't know anything about things unconnected to fate either."

"Then at least tell us what it is about Spica-chan that constitutes the ray of hope you speak of. Just naming her and leaving it at that is irresponsible, no matter how you look at it."

"Sorry for being irresponsible, then~"

At Ubilk's brazen response, Rem's expression darkened with hostility. But even though Ubilk's answer was undesirable, the topic Rem had raised might be worth examining.

"With the materials we have now, all we can do is protect those who've been marked while we crash the coupled dragon carriage, which might fall apart in midair, straight into the fortress city. That city should still be under repair, too."

"I don't think we've got that much time to spare."

Even as they spoke, the dragon carriage, exposed to the Pruning Corps' assault, was steadily losing its original shape. If their means of movement stopped, annihilation was inevitable—that much had already been discussed.
To stop this battle, they had to deal with the commander presumably leading the enemy forces—Lamia.

"—————"

At present, with a need for more than symptomatic countermeasures, Berstetz noticed, within the narrow field of his thin, slitted eyes, Subaru wearing a deeply conflicted expression. The anguish occupying that expression was born of having noticed something, and hesitating to put that something into words.
In this situation, a remark the boy hesitated to voice. His conflict, different from Serena's insufficiently considered remarks—its content was—

"—Barusu."
"Natsuki-san."

Two people called out to Subaru at the same moment.
Like Berstetz, they too had noticed the change in the boy's expression. And these two, who knew the boy far better than Berstetz did, seemed to have grasped even the substance of his worry.
Closing his eyes to the intent in their gazes, Subaru took a deep breath, and steeling his expression, said—

"—Spica's Authority might have the power to break this situation open."

"Oryaaaaa~!!"

Wielding the broadswords gripped in both hands, Medium forcibly cut down the Undead closing in from the front.
That wide swing left her back wide open, and another Undead's massive shears nearly struck there. But a slender longsword cut across its path.

"Don't go gettin' casual with your hands, you overgrown lumps!!"

Roaring that fiercely, letting his voice—and his slashes—run just as wild, was the one-eyed Imperial soldier who called himself Jamal.
Cooperating with this Jamal, Medium fought unceasingly to protect her brother Flop, Jamal's sister Katya, and the willful Abel from the relentless attacks of the Undead.
Even now, Medium halted an enemy's advance with a front kick, swept its head off with her broadsword, and turned it to dust. Jamal, too, severed an enemy's knee with his twin swords and finished it off by skewering its chest.

"Empress-sama! Leave this to me and get back!"

"I~ ha~ven't~ said yes yet!"

"Empress-candidate-sama! Please fall back!"

"Ohh, come ON!!"

Treated with such deference, unaccustomed to such treatment, Medium couldn't hide her confusion.
It had been like this since even before, back when Flop and Abel were discussing things. Cautioned by Abel, Medium had cooperated with her brother without hearing a word of Flop's plan.
Come to think of it, Abel had been right—she should have listened to the story first.

"If I had, I wouldn't have been so shocked...!"

"Sister! Fretting doesn't suit you at all!"

"Whose fault do you think that is, bro!"

Putting her anger into her broadsword, she locked blades with the thrust-out shears, pouring strength into her legs. Medium planted herself, refusing to lose the contest of strength, and pushed back. A blade thrust in from under Medium's arm, and Jamal, having circled behind, pierced the enemy's chest to finish it.
Having done so, Jamal scraped his twin swords against each other in midair.

"Katya! Don't you dare stick your neck out! I won't let a single one of 'em near you!"

"S-stop it... it doesn't matter anymore, honestly... it's not like living, whatever happens, has anything good in it anyway..."

"Don't talk nonsense! If you die, Todd won't rest easy!"

"—! Y-you idiot brother...! How could you—? Y-you wouldn't normally say that! Die! You should just, j-just die, brother...!"

Katya, seated in her wheelchair, began crying in fits at the mention of a name Medium didn't recognize. To Medium's eyes now, that Katya looked terribly pitiable. If it weren't for this situation, she'd want to listen to her, fellow sister-swept-along-by-her-brother that she was.
But there was no room for that kind of leisure anywhere.
Because—

"—'Empress,' hm? Now that's something I can't let slide."

"—!"

"Do you have the qualifications to walk alongside the Sword Wolf among Sword Wolves?"

The one who tilted her head so was an Undead who, even in Medium's eyes, was strikingly beautiful compared to the swarming black-armored Undead soldiers.
If not for the pale, cracked skin and the eerie golden eyes, she would surely have been a beauty worth losing oneself in.

Whether that overwhelming presence came from being dead, or had been there since she was alive, Medium couldn't tell, but her words carried an intimidation Medium had no way to talk back against.

"Whatever answer this girl might give, it is of no use to one already dead as you are."

So it was not Medium but Abel who answered back.
At the front and the very rear of the passenger car, with Medium's group and the Undead soldiers caught between them, Abel and the beautiful Undead glared at each other. —No, gazed at each other.

"Well, if it isn't big brother Vincent. Still as dashing as ever, I see. ...Though have you gotten a little thinner?"

"Just as I think myself free of the bother of siblings, you and Palladio come lost and wandering back out. It's only natural my cheeks would hollow somewhat."

"Fufu, you'd make me want to come lost and wandering too. —You saved Prisca, didn't you, big brother?"

"—————"

"Does big brother, who upended the very premise of the Imperial Selection Ceremony, have the right to punish me and big brother Palladio? If people knew the truth, wouldn't no one accept you as Emperor?"

Giggling, hand at her lips, the Undead princess laughed.
At that single remark, Abel's black eyes wavered slightly. With those same eyes, Abel tried to say something back to her—

"It's not 'no one'! I think Abel-chin is the Emperor!"

"Me too, Your Majesty! No need to listen to what a dead person says!"

Medium, unable to hold back any longer, and Jamal, riding on her coattails, shook the passenger car with their voices.
At those two remarks, Abel's eyes widened even more than before, and the princess narrowed hers. With her golden eyes peculiar to the Undead, she looked at Medium and Jamal.

"Well, aren't you bold. Do you soldiers even know who I am?"

"Huh? Looks like a Vollachian royal to me, but what's it matter, seein' as you're dead! The dead are the losers, and the living are the Sword Wolves! That's! The damn! Imperial way!"

Shouting with the world's simplest logic, Jamal resumed his melee against the enemy soldiers.
At his vigor, Medium blinked and then laughed. Laughing, Medium too, like Jamal, resumed the fight.

"You're cooler than Abel-chin, Jamal-chin!"

"I'm honored, Empress-sama!"

At Medium's praise, Jamal wore a wild grin, and his twin swords raged all the fiercer.
With that battle sandwiched between them, the standoff between Abel and the princess continued. At the princess's slightly displeased look, Abel narrowed his black eyes.

"I sense the presence of many Sunlight Swords. You are not alone, Lamia."

"And if I am? Does it make you happy that your cute little sister has multiplied? Or are you uninterested because it's not Prisca?"

"If you are exploiting the mechanism of the Undead to bring about a phenomenon beyond reason, why have you not shown your numbers before me?"

Against the princess's—Lamia's—mocking attitude, Abel had found no purchase. Yet within Lamia's silence, giving no answer, Abel found a clue of his own accord.
With his narrowed black eyes, looking at the sister who had surely changed beyond recognition, Abel said,

"There is a limit to your numbers. And moreover, the bulk of them are being held in check. —Goz, is it."

"You say that so calmly, big brother. If that's true, General Ralfon's efforts deserve a medal, don't they? Poor thing, saying he's content just being a pawn—"

"—That is precisely why I chose him."

In a quiet tone, Abel cut off Lamia's words.
Abel naturally crossed his arms where he stood, taking Lamia's gaze and words head-on.

"That one is one of the 'Generals' I chose. That degree of effort is only to be expected."

Having stated that with such composure, Abel went on to call her name—"Lamia."
And to Lamia, whose eyes widened slightly, he said,

"I have never once thought you a trivial being."

"—————"

At that single remark thrown her way, Lamia's expression changed drastically.
Until then she had worn only expressions of allure, of sadism, or else of discontent—but at Abel's words, she wore a different face.
Her golden eyes went wide, and she bit her lip.

"—Vincent Vollachiaaaaa!!"

The next instant, the expression Medium had just seen vanished as if it had been a lie, and a different face surfaced.
Her bloodless face now holding unmistakable fury, her golden eyes flaring, she drew from midair, with her outstretched hand, a sacred sword blazing red.

Holding the brilliantly glowing sacred sword, Lamia herself charged forward, kicking off the floor, off the wall, slipping through gaps between the Undead, and leapt at Abel.

A dazzling light, as if flame or light itself were being swung down, lit up brightly the interior of the car covered in Undead, seeking to erase Abel from the head down.
Abel, facing it head-on, had the sacred sword come swinging down before his very nose.

"Abel-chin!!"

Kicking off an Undead and leaping backward, Medium caught the sacred sword with her broadsword. A moment's stalemate—Medium's broadsword melted, and the sacred sword's trajectory flowed on unimpeded.

"Your Majestyyy!"

An instant later, Jamal's twin swords, like Medium's blade, went to knock the sacred sword away. This too was swallowed by the sword's light, its blade vanishing in an instant.

Breaking through Medium and Jamal's interference, Lamia's slash reached Abel.
Just as Abel's whole body was about to be swallowed by red light, Medium was about to let out a scream.
That was the moment.

"—————"

No one could tell what had happened.
It wasn't Flop and Katya behind the standing Abel, who together pulled at his coat to sit him down onto the floor; it wasn't Medium, whose heart had ached tremendously at the thought Abel would die; it wasn't Jamal, his face despairing that all was lost.

—It was simply that, right there, as if struck by wind, Lamia's stance had crumbled.

"Still alive, are you—how stubborn, Berstetz. —Say, who won the Imperial Selection Ceremony? Big brother Vincent? Or was it Prisca?"

—Those had been the first words thrown at him upon their reunion.

A reunion with a lord who had changed into something unrecognizable.
Even after nine years, behind his eyelids he could recall her living form as clearly as if it were yesterday. When people grow old, it is often the things from long ago, more than yesterday's events, that grow vivid.

So he had known, at a glance, that something was wrong with his lord.
The cracked, pale skin, and golden eyes that no longer sought the light—these were the marks of a dead thing that had forgotten life.
Even from her very first question, he could tell. Her time had stopped. That was as it should be. Was that not how it ought to be? The living and the dead must be kept apart.
Therefore—

"—Pruning Corps, staaand down!!"

Straining his dry throat until it seemed it might tear, he shouted that command.
Thanks to the benefit of the Divine Protection of Wind Evasion, there was neither the fierce wind that should have been blowing nor the violent shaking the carriage should have had, and his voice carried far, far, and high.

And the instant that command was heard, the Undead wielding their great shears stopped moving.
The Pruning Corps halting reflexively—he did not know how to feel about that fact. Should he grieve, or should he take pride?
It was he himself who had entrusted their hearts to the "Poison Princess" and remade them into symbols of terror with cold blood flowing in their veins. They had answered that purpose, met that expectation.

And even after death, their bodies had responded to Berstetz Fondalfon's command.

"The Undead's time is stopped. Then, to them, that battle of the Imperial Selection Ceremony is but an event of yesterday... what has soaked into the body cannot easily fade."

Even in death, they had proven, with their very bodies, a way of being that remained faithful to their lord.

"—And? You can only seal my beasts for an instant, can't you?"

"...Indeed. But that was enough for you to come here."

Turning at the voice from behind, Berstetz went to greet her alone.
The roof broken, the walls destroyed, the carriage now a shadow of its former majesty. And yet, even so, in one of the coupled dragon carriages still running, carrying the Empire's hopes, Berstetz and Lamia faced each other.
Confirming his own reflection in Lamia's golden eyes, Berstetz let out a breath.

"I have been awaiting you, Lady Lamia."

"Yes, so it seems. But why are you the only one left here, I wonder?"

Tilting her head, Lamia spread both hands and looked around the empty carriage.
Neither Serena nor any of the Kingdom's people were here besides Berstetz. Berstetz alone had stayed behind, claiming he had a secret stratagem to hold Lamia and the Pruning Corps in check.

Indeed, he had achieved a momentary halt of the Pruning Corps.
His command would likely never take effect again, but the few seconds of opening it had created would surely have been put to good use by those aboard this dragon carriage.
With that, Berstetz had kept his promise to those who had gone on ahead.

"I am no longer of an age to make promises I cannot keep."

"No need to put yourself down like that. Nine years have passed since then, and you haven't changed a bit. Just like when I died."

"—Just as Your Excellency says, I imagine."

At Lamia's teasing words, Berstetz answered in a low, rasping voice.
Hearing that, Lamia's brow knit. Before her, Berstetz clenched his bony fist, and clenched, hard, his miraculously still-complete set of teeth.
Nine years gone, and yet unchanged. What Lamia said was correct.

"Since that time, my own time, too, has been stopped, Lady Lamia."

Murmuring that, Berstetz took one step forward.
A great step, filled with resolve. Having taken it, the next step. Moving his aged body, Berstetz stepped forward, forward.

"—————"

As if exasperated, Lamia's golden eyes narrowed.
Slowly—the flow of time felt slow, sluggish. Not merely felt—it was, in fact, slow. Compared to Julius and Garfiel, who had until moments ago been fighting to protect Berstetz and the others, it was so feeble a thing that comparison itself seemed presumptuous.

Amid a pack of wolves, he had been a sheep who painted himself black. He had become a crafty old goat. Making his horns look large, desperately asserting that even within a pack of wolves, there was a role for him.

"—Sunlight Sword."

In Lamia's hand, outstretched to the sky, the hilt of that red sacred sword took form.
Her slender fingers gripped it tightly, drawing out the sacred sword, the symbol of Vollachia. That blade, red and blazing, sealing crimson flame within it, seared Berstetz's eyes.

It was well that his eyes were already narrowed. Thanks to that, even with his eyelids scorched, his eyeballs were spared.
Holding such a trivial, useless thought, Berstetz raised his fist. On that fist was fitted a ring—proof of the Vollachian Empire's Chancellor.
It was a Meteor imbued with the power of Fire Mana.

"I've seen that one already."

Already in the capital, already in the Crystal Palace, already in the throne room she had seen it once—the color of Lamia's cold gaze did not change.
Berstetz's advance, too slow by far; the trump card already broken once before; and what the opponent wielded was one of the world's ten mightiest Enchanted Swords—

"—Your Excellency."

He knew he would be cut down, reduced to ash, in less than a single second.
And yet, in a life of nearly seventy years, Berstetz used that single longest second to the fullest.
And he spoke, once more, the counsel he had once given his lord.

"We have been defeated...!"

As he said it, Berstetz swung down the fist he had raised, pointing the ring at the floor.
There, he discharged the Meteor; the swelling flame burst beneath Berstetz's feet, adding the force of fire to the too-slow advance of an old man.

Fiercely, Berstetz's whole body charged at Lamia.
Before that aged form hurtling toward her, Lamia's golden eyes went wide. Reflected in those widened golden eyes was Berstetz Fondalfon.
Watching that reflected face, Lamia, sword still raised,

"—You... you could make a face like that, so full of regret."

A lord and vassal who had served long, without deep intimacy, who had never spoken their true hearts to one another.
At the wrinkled face, the thin, slitted eyes that never showed their color, at the first expression the inscrutable vassal had ever shown—the look of an old man on the verge of tears—Lamia's hand stopped.

—Head-on, Berstetz's and Lamia's bodies collided.

Unable to soften the shock of the explosion, the two bodies, grappling, went toward the wall of the passenger car—the wall already broken by the Pruning Corps' incursion—and were flung straight out.
Tightly, the withered old man's fingers gripped the beautiful girl's dress and would not let go.

Never letting go, both their bodies were flung, greatly, out of the coupled dragon carriage—

—At that instant, a shockwave struck every one of the countless Lamia Godwins that had appeared throughout the coupled dragon carriages.

"—————"

The Lamias fighting Goz Ralfon, the Lamias fighting Garfiel Tinzel, the Lamias fighting Roswaal L. Mathers, the Lamias fighting Emilia, wherever in the dragon carriages a Lamia stood, all at once were struck by the shock.

A Blessing is a gift that life born into this world may sometimes receive, and its full nature has never been elucidated—much of it remains shrouded in mystery.

There is only one certainty most people hold intuitively about Blessings.
That is, a Blessing affects the soul—both of the one who receives it, and of the one it targets.
Whether that is fact has never been proven. But this much can be said.

—That the moment Berstetz Fondalfon and one of the Lamia Godwins were flung outside the dragon carriage, and the two of them fell outside the range of the Divine Protection of Wind Evasion, coincided exactly.

And that was also the very instant a tragedy occurred: one of the Lamia Godwins struck at Vincent Vollachia, and Medium O'Connell and Jamal Aurélie failed to fully block it, before the eyes of Flop O'Connell and Katya Aurélie.

"—!"

As if struck by wind, the body of Lamia, sword raised, was pulled backward.
Neither Lamia nor Medium and the others knew what had happened in that moment. Only this was fact: Vincent, having been pulled down, extended his leg and kicked Lamia backward.
And so, behind the kicked-back Lamia, the door leading to the neighboring carriage was broken through—

"—————"

A rainbow-colored gleam danced, and the Pruning Corps soldier standing before the door was cut down.
Slipping through that rainbow light, three small shadows came tumbling into the car.

One of them held out a hand, and a faint light wrapped around the three shadows, increasing their tumbling speed. The one in the middle of the three shadows, hand joined with the one who had done this, raised her voice.

"—Lamia Godwin!"

A raised voice, the cry of the black-haired boy holding hands with the girls on either side of him.
The girl in the dress had produced the light, the black-haired boy had called out the name, and the last one, who had come leaping in, reached out with the hand not joined to the boy's—

"—Iaaiau"

—and, shaking off the hand that touched the back flying toward her, stripped away the name of the "Poison Princess."
