Arc 7 Chapter 108: "The Coming Great Disaster"

—The final act of Chisha Gold, who had disguised himself as the false emperor, lay beyond comprehension.

"——"

Driven from the throne, Vincent Vollachia had taken the name Abel, and after gaining the cooperation of the People of Shudrak in the great forest to the east, had stood himself at the head of a rebellion and thrown the empire into chaos.

It was not a desirable turn of events when one considered what lay ahead, but it had been Chisha's design all along — going so far as to stage something resembling treason in order to drive him from the imperial capital. Such a scheme could not easily be reversed.

And so Chisha had laid his hand to make certain that action and intention alike would be resigned to futility.

The unavoidable end brought about by the Stargazers — for Abel, this was something he had steeled himself for long, long ago, from the moment he first became conscious of himself as a prince of Vollachia.

At the hour foretold as heaven's mandate, the reigning Vollachian Emperor seated upon the imperial throne would die, and that death would serve as the trigger for the ruinous Great Disaster to begin.

He himself had not been named specifically.

But he had held the conviction that, when that foretold hour arrived, it would be he who occupied the imperial seat.

He had held the conviction that no one but himself could save the Vollachian Empire.

That great task could not be entrusted to any of his other brothers and sisters.

Pouring in every ability and possibility he possessed, Vincent Abelux would seize the imperial throne, and as Vincent Vollachia, lay the groundwork to preserve the empire.

That was the plan upon which Vincent Vollachia — upon which Abel — had staked his entire life.

In the Vollachian Empire, where the strong were revered, he had revived the system of the Nine Divine Generals — a body that all could look upon as an object of faith — and placed at its pinnacle one whose supremacy was beyond doubt.

He had bent the empire's ways, rife as they were with reckless violence, and guided its people to lose their resistance to violence bound by order.

He had done everything to leave behind, after his own death, as much possibility as he could for the Vollachian Empire to endure.

All of it had been a plan upon which he had staked his life.

"——"

A body collapsed, helpless, endlessly spilling blood from a chest whose heart had been pierced through.

Black hair, black eyes — the face that, every time he glimpsed it in a mirror, stirred anger at his own insufficient strength and insufficient knowledge, had lost all color, and its unseeing expression was steeping now in blood.

The borrowed form he claimed to have obtained through the power of Noh at the moment he had faced death and lost his own coloring — even with that life now spent, it did not return to its original shape.

Never again would that figure be seen; never again would that glib, unyielding tongue strike against an eardrum.

Never.

"Why."

A question that should never have been asked spilled from his lips.

The one who could have answered that question would never again open his mouth. And yet lips that should have known to avoid such futility let the words spill out regardless.

It had been easy enough to imagine that, once he learned of the Great Disaster's prophecy and its conditions, he would try to overturn it.

Abel had searched beforehand for a way to do so, and told him there was no hand left to play, meaning to make him accept it. And even so — because he had not, in fact, accepted it — this treason had followed; and even so, Abel had thrust the futility of it back in his face.

Facing him here in the throne room like this, he should have proven there was no way to walk around the Great Disaster.

He had trained Chisha so that, after his own death, the empire could be entrusted to him — trained him into someone who thought as he thought and would arrive at the same conclusions he would, someone able to serve as Vincent Vollachia's proxy.

So — if he were truly Vincent Vollachia — he should have accepted defeat cleanly.

He should have acknowledged defeat, laid down his spear, put an end to this fruitless battle, returned the throne to Abel — who had come back to accept his own death — and stood against the ruinous Great Disaster.

That was not what happened. It had been a foolish gamble.

A foolish gamble — dying as Vincent Vollachia in Abel's place, only for the Great Disaster to refuse to recognize it, and for it all to end as nothing but a dog's death.

That, of all things, was the very pinnacle of inefficient death that Abel despised.

And Chisha Gold should have known that better than anyone.

"Why."

Inexhaustible doubt and confusion dyed themselves red, redder still, with the flowing blood.

No matter how many times he repeated the question, only an answerless silence piled up in reply. That too was the very extreme of inefficiency that Abel loathed, and then——

"—Well now, that'd be poor strategy, that would."

An instant later, a second bolt of white light was loosed, drawn straight and true toward Abel, standing frozen where he was.

△▼△▼△▼△

True to its description as light, the death-dealing blow aimed at Abel faster than any arrow.

Abel had never been a man of arms to begin with.

And with the shock of what had just happened still gripping him, reacting to a flash of white light that grazed the very corner of his vision for a mere instant should have been all but impossible.

And so, what saved Abel's life in that moment was not any judgment of his own.

"——"

It was an instant before Abel would have been pierced by the light, his heart destroyed head-on.

A thunderous roar and violent tremor shook the throne room — no, the entire Crystal Palace — and the sturdy wall was destroyed by a tremendous impact. Whatever had caused that impact did not stop even after breaking through the wall, and thrust itself between the frozen Abel and the white light hurtling toward him.

There came a sound like water bursting apart, and the white light shattered against the obstruction. And what saved Abel's life by the barest hair's breadth was——

"—A giant arm — Moguro Hagane!"

"The man on the throne — protect. His Excellency — gave me the order."

The wall-like thing that had suddenly appeared before Abel's eyes was Moguro Hagane's right arm, forced bodily into the throne room.

Moguro, who was himself the Meteor known as the Crystal Palace, had taken the ramparts of the imperial capital of Lupugana into himself as his own body, and should have been locked in fierce battle out on the field.

And yet——

"Chisha... !"

Moguro's words just now were proof that he had acted on orders to protect Abel.

And the only one who could have issued the order for the distant Moguro to return was Chisha Gold, who had been seated on the throne with its function of directly commanding the Meteor.

He must have read that, the moment after he himself was shot through, Abel's life would be targeted as well.

"—Moguro Hagane! Get me outside!"

"You — who. I — His Excellency — priority——"

"The Emperor is dead! If I die now, that death becomes a waste!"

"——"

At Abel's forceful words, Moguro peered into the throne room and noticed the fallen shadow there.

It was hard to tell exactly where Moguro's eyes were in his current state, but as the green gem embedded in the rampart flickered, he too acknowledged the fallen Emperor's death.

"The remains——"

"Unnecessary! Pay the dead no mind!"

Shouting down Moguro, who had moved to recover the Emperor's body, Abel leapt onto the huge right arm before him. Clinging to one of its hand-shaped fingers, he was drawn out into the sky and the raging wind as Moguro withdrew the arm from the throne room.

"——"

Moguro, one with the ramparts, had reshaped their structure however suited his own movement.

Moguro's massive humanoid frame stood over fifty meters tall, and Abel, riding the raised arm, found himself at roughly that same height.

From that height, the streets of the imperial capital spread out below — here and there, buildings and roads trampled flat from the Third Peak that Moguro had been guarding, showing that he had come running straight here without regard for anything in his path.

Beyond that, the battles at each of the Peaks still raged on — those fighting them had already lost their reason to fight and needed to be preparing for the next battle instead, yet were squandering their lives in vain.

In vain, their lives——.

"—!"

His thoughts fraying, Abel tightened his grip on the massive stone bulk he clung to.

This was no time to be caught up in what had already happened, scattering his thoughts in a thousand directions. What was done was already done. Just as he had told Moguro — to die now would be a dog's death.

At the very least, he had to determine whether that death had held any meaning.

"I cannot die."

He forced the words out, clenching his back teeth and glaring down at the scene below.

Even if he raised his voice here, it would never reach those clashing their lives against each other on the battlefield below. But somehow, he had to make them understand that this battle's meaning had been extinguished.

"Danger."

As Abel thought this, Moguro's voice struck his eardrums like a gale.

The difference in size between them had grown so vast that every one of Moguro's actions was like a human's toward an ant. But Moguro had not shaken his eardrums for nothing.

He twisted his enormous body, turning his back to the Crystal Palace to shield the right hand that enclosed Abel. A string of impact sounds echoed against the outside of the hand wrapped around him, and Abel's cheek twisted.

The relentless attacks did not come from a single direction, but rained down from every side at once.

That alone would suggest the enemy's numbers were great. —No; gathering magic users capable of targeting an enemy with this degree of precision would be difficult on Vollachian soil.

In other words, this was not a matter of assembling numbers — it was a single extraordinary talent, prepared alone.

A master skilled enough to create the illusion of a great many attackers striking simultaneously from every direction — the memory surfaced, from earlier, when his thoughts had frozen under the numbing shock to his brain, and Abel raised his head.

He recognized that style of fighting — recognized it, though it was neither the answer nor the voice he had been seeking.

It was——

"——"

Moguro twisted his body, shielding Abel from the rain of shots pouring down, and fell back.

Looking up at the sky from inside the enclosing fist, Abel saw an afterimage streak past at a speed too fast to follow — an exceedingly rare sight, yet one that could still be seen within the Vollachian Empire.

Flight on the wings of a Flying Dragon, and the acrobatic maneuvers of the Flying Dragon Rider controlling it — no, there was no rider left in the present-day empire who could glide through midair at such speed.

That skill, which could only be called the very extreme of flight, was the sole domain of the empire's strongest Flying Dragon Rider.

In that instant, white light scattered, an impact and the sound of Moguro's massive body being shattered rang out, and something like a shockwave grazed Abel's cheek. Without letting the pain twist his expression, Abel looked.

A Flying Dragon Rider passed through the black-eyed field of vision behind his demon mask — ashen brown hair, cracks running across a face drained of all color, and, in eyes as black as the dead of night, a golden light.

The figure was almost unrecognizable, and yet there was no mistaking him.

"—Balleroy Temeglyph! Why are you alive!"

"That I won't be telling you, good sir of the demon mask."

A beautiful voice, refined in its impression, spun the words, and though it refused an answer, it confirmed his existence all the same.

Vanishing from sight once more, the enemy who cut through the sky astride his Flying Dragon — Balleroy Temeglyph himself was the culprit who had taken aim at the throne room through the Crystal Palace's walls and pierced the false emperor's heart.

Known as the Magic Bullet Marksman, he was the man who had reigned at the very pinnacle of every Flying Dragon Rider.

Once the Nine of the Nine Divine Generals, during a civil war that had struck the empire in the past——

"Balleroy — died. You — fake."

Sharing Abel's same understanding, the massive Moguro launched a counterattack.

Still enclosing Abel in his right hand, Moguro swung his massive left arm, quite literally sweeping the sky aside as he hurled an attack at the Flying Dragon.

Large things are often assumed to move sluggishly, but that is merely an illusion born of viewing them from a distance.

On top of shielding Abel from the magical sniping, Moguro had blocked every one of the enemy's attacks up to this point. His movements were swift and precise.

"Fast. Nimble."

But even with Moguro's agility, pinning down Balleroy, who ranged freely over the whole of the sky as his own domain, was no easy task.

It was not only speed — the ability to move in every direction, up and down, left and right, front and back, was the Flying Dragon Rider's greatest advantage, one that could never be stripped away short of bringing him down to the ground.

And more than that, Balleroy was no ordinary Flying Dragon Rider — he was the very pinnacle of them.

"He's wrapping himself in wind...!"

Balleroy's tactics excelled at sniping while in motion through the use of Magic, but his strength as one of the rare Magic users in the empire was not something put to use in attack alone.

He wrapped his own Flying Dragon in wind, adjusting his speed and softening impacts, achieving maneuvers no other Flying Dragon Rider could manage, and never once allowing himself to be caught.

That said, achieving this was impossible without a Flying Dragon perfectly attuned to him.

"So that Flying Dragon, too, has danced back from beyond death!"

The greatest weakness of a Flying Dragon Rider, for all his overwhelming combat prowess, was that raising a dragon took time, and that a Flying Dragon would never carry anyone on its back save the partner it had chosen.

To kill a Flying Dragon Rider, one struck the Flying Dragon first. Balleroy's own death had been no exception to that rule.

And yet Balleroy had danced his way back. Quite literally, dancing through the sky together with his beloved dragon.

"The Great Disaster——"

There was no damage occurring that could clearly, definitively be pointed to as such.

But given that Chisha had scattered his life as Vincent Vollachia, Abel could not help but suspect a connection to the Great Disaster in whatever events beyond human understanding followed after.

Even so, it did not sit right that Balleroy Temeglyph should be the one to bear the Great Disaster.

Certainly, Balleroy was the pinnacle of Flying Dragon Riders, and one of the empire's mightiest.

If he set his mind to it, snuffing out Abel's life would be no trouble at all.

And yet——

"This will shake. Bear it."

"Never mind that — do it!"

The inorganic voice was loosed like a raging gale, and Abel, answering it, raised his own voice.

In the next instant, Moguro, who had been thrusting his arm out toward the enemy, plunged it down beneath his own feet instead. "Beneath his feet," at Moguro's present scale, amounted to an entire street.

His left arm, driven into the imperial capital's streets, groaned as it met a single beat of resistance before tearing the street free, and with a great heave, hurled it skyward.

"——"

Abel had seen the sight of part of a city being hurled through the air once before, some while ago, in Chaosflame. That had been a ridiculous enough sight in its own right, but it had merely been the city's residents banding together to fling dismantled buildings.

But what Moguro did was raw brute force stripped of any trick, an outrage beyond any mistaking.

A sight that laid bare, beyond any standard, that Moguro Hagane was indeed one of the Nine Divine Generals.

Balleroy, too, was certainly one who stood beyond any standard — but so did Moguro, and so did every other of the Nine Divine Generals; the conditions were the same for all of them. And that was precisely why Balleroy could not be the one to bear the Great Disaster.

Of course, simply hurling the torn-up street would end with the nimble Balleroy dodging it.

And so Moguro took the street he had thrown, that single cannonball, and——

"Shatter."

He slammed his left arm into it, turning it into countless scattering fragments that sealed off Balleroy's escape routes through the sky.

Even scattered as they were, each fragment of rock and earth was larger than a full-grown man. Sent flying by Moguro's single blow, they could deal a fatal wound to an ordinary person with the merest graze.

Naturally, even Balleroy, versed as he was in the Flow Method, would show a fatal opening in midair if struck. To avoid that,

"Carillon!"

A sharp voice cut through the raging wind, and the Flying Dragon flew as though slipping between the raindrops of stone.

Beating its wings and slicing through the sky, the Flying Dragon made for the one corner of the world, amid the raining stones, where the rain fell just barely the weakest——

"A trap, of course."

Moguro had deliberately prepared that one weaker corner. But even knowing it for a trap, Balleroy and his dragon had no choice but to dive in — they could not afford to be struck by the shrapnel.

What remained was simply whether Balleroy, driving his beloved dragon, could evade the blow Moguro had waiting for him.

Wrapping his dragon in wind, Balleroy accelerated, shattering the obstructing fragments with light as he raced through the sky.

Moguro spun the left arm he had drawn back, and a blow that could carve stone from the world swept across the sky of the imperial capital.

Both of them Nine Divine Generals — a duel that had never come to pass while Balleroy still lived unfolded now, both men's full might crossing high above, about to decide which of them would prevail——

——And in that instant, faster than the collision itself should have come, an explosive roar and shockwave blew away the clouds over the imperial capital.

"—!"

Though he escaped the direct force of that bomb-like power, even the aftershock alone nearly tore Abel's body loose from where he clung to Moguro.

But no matter how brutal the situation grew for his slight frame, Abel never once closed his eyes. And so he caught this moment's events fully, with his own two eyes.

"Moguro's arm——"

Moguro's spinning left arm, which should have crushed Balleroy between its fingers, took a blow from the side an instant before it could deliver its full force, and was shattered at what, on a human, would have been the upper arm.

Spinning with tremendous force, Moguro's arm — long enough to rival a castle — went whirling through the sky. Passing leisurely beneath the very heart of the devastation, Balleroy escaped the rain of shrapnel alive — no, he had gotten away without a scratch.

And the one who had destroyed Moguro's arm and rescued Balleroy from his predicament was——

"O-o-o-ohhhhhh—!!"

Letting out a low voice that shook the very air, an impact slammed violently in from the side. Moguro, his balance broken by the destruction of his arm, blinked his green gem at the thing that had come hurtling in.

Even Abel had not anticipated this intrusion. And yet it was something he should rightly have expected.

If the man he faced was Balleroy Temeglyph, danced back from death.

"You! Get away from the Dragon's groom, ya hear meee—!!"

A swung dragon's claw tore effortlessly through Moguro's body of rampart-stone, and a tail strike, slammed down with a roar, destroyed an entire section in a single blow, whittling away Moguro's mass before Abel's eyes.

It happened all too easily — but of course it did. The one they faced was the strongest living creature in this world.

"The Cloud Dragon, Mezoreia... and the one riding inside her now is Madelyn Eschart!"

"Aaaahhhhh—!"

The roaring Dragon thrashed her whole body, and Moguro's massive frame reeled violently under the impact. With every blow, the ramparts cracked, and shattered pieces rained down upon the imperial capital.

The gardens of the Crystal Palace, called the most beautiful castle in the world, were trampled to ruin, and the imperial capital's orderly, meticulously planned streets died a little more with every passing second.

And on top of it all, the calamities besieging the capital were not yet finished.

"——"

Amid the thunderous roar of Moguro's body being torn apart under the Dragon's rampage, what reached Abel's ears, mixed in beneath it, was a shattering sound like the world itself splitting apart, arising from somewhere far, far away.

Looking, he saw that behind the Crystal Palace — in the retaining wall of the reservoir that served as the water source for the entire capital, fed by springs from the mountains — the left arm Moguro had just had torn from him was buried deep.

A beat later, a crack spread out from the point where the arm had struck, and the reservoir's water began to seep out through the fissure and flow. It would gradually gain force, and before long, would collapse the entire retaining wall and pour into the capital as a churning flood.

Right this instant, the capital's residents, along with the imperial soldiers and rebels caught up in the siege, needed to be guided to evacuate——

"—Failed."

The moment after that not-so-small mutter of Moguro's reached him, a sensation of floating struck Abel's body.

Turning at the sensation of his very insides floating up, he found that he had not been thrown clear — rather, Moguro's arm, the very hand enclosing him from the wrist down, had been bitten off by the Dragon.

It had not been targeted deliberately; it was merely part of the Dragon's reckless, all-out assault.

And yet——

"—Ohh."

Swallowed by the momentum of free fall, Abel's body flipped over in midair.

The hand that had clung to Moguro's arm was torn free — though even had he managed to keep clinging on, what had once been rampart-stone would serve as no cushion, only as a cause of death.

But even if Moguro's arm were not to be his death, at this rate, something else just like it would deliver Abel to the very same fate.

"Something——"

He swept his gaze around, searching for anything, and his eyes were drawn to a single point.

Though not as any hand of salvation.

What entered Abel's view was the great hole torn in the Crystal Palace, where Moguro had pulled himself free.

A hole connected to the throne room — he could not see inside it, yet his awareness was drawn there all the same.

The very man who had said to pay the dead no mind had, in that instant, swallowed the same poison himself.

"——"

Whether that single second would have let him find some way to break the deadlock, he could not say.

But that he had failed to spend that one second was, for Abel, proof of a whole heart he had failed to give, and something that would carve an undying wound into his soul forever.

Though of course, if he simply fell to his death right here, a wounded soul would hardly be worth discussing.

"Pull baaack—!"

"—!?"

Still uncertain what measure to take, Abel had resolved to disperse the impact by landing on his limbs one at a time — but before he could, something soft caught him, powerfully, faster than that.

Abel's body, breath caught involuntarily, bounced and fell back down onto that same sensation. After a few short repetitions of this, Abel realized it was the bounce of a spread cloth.

Someone had spread layered cloth beneath the falling Abel and caught him.

As he took in the fact that he had survived by the narrowest of margins, Abel immediately rolled across the cloth and set his feet on the ground from its edge. Going down on one knee, he lifted his face to confirm who had done this——

"—Now then, it's here—the hour of heaven's mandate!"

"——"

"Your Excellency, or Your Excellency? Either way, the Great Disaster is coming. Why don't we stand against the Great Disaster together, shall we-e-e!"

Was the fulfillment of prophecy truly such a joy to him?

With a voice and manner absurdly, inappropriately bright, the man — Ubilk — spread both arms wide, the crumbling city and the battle between the giant Moguro and the Cloud Dragon that was causing it all at his back, and smiled.

Celebrating this certain moment of fulfillment of the heavenly mandate that the Stargazers had brought upon him.

△▼△▼△▼△

When the thunderous roar of shattering walls rang out, the Crystal Palace shook violently, and it became certain that something decisive had occurred, Berstetz Fondalfon pressed his way into the throne room.

And then——

"—Master Chisha, is that you?"

Amid the collapsed wall of the throne room and the freshly billowing dust, Berstetz found the black-haired figure of the Emperor collapsed atop the red carpet, and the corners of his narrow eyes drooped in gloom.

Approaching, he found a hole opened in the chest of the body lying face-down, the heart that should have been beating within it burst apart, its life's thread severed.

He was no military man, but it was obvious at a glance. Instant death. There would not even have been time to feel the pain.

"That, given all we ourselves have done, might well be called a mercifully gentle end."

From the moment he raised the banner of revolt and staged treason against Emperor Vincent Vollachia, Berstetz had resolved himself never to expect an easy death.

Chisha, too, as a co-conspirator, should have made that same resolution.

Though——

"From what the Stargazers say, it seems you were looking toward something different than I myself was."

That had been a clear act of betrayal, yet Berstetz had no intention of blaming him for it.

If anything, admiration was closer to his true feeling.

To spend every ounce of one's own power in pursuit of what one wanted — that was the Vollachian way.

Neither Berstetz nor Chisha possessed the martial strength to carry through the Vollachian way on those terms, but in that they both filled what they lacked with cunning, the two of them were birds of a feather.

That this, too, was a form of strength — proven and acknowledged as such — was the very shape of the new empire that Vincent Vollachia had built as Emperor.

Following that way of the empire, one that Berstetz himself acknowledged, Chisha had proven it magnificently.

Proven his own strength. —That was an act of honor, for a man of the Vollachian Empire.

"Though it seems there is little time to spare for words of praise."

Berstetz, who had been looking down at the corpse, turned his eyes to the hole in the wall through which the raging wind poured in, and confirmed the presence of the stone-colored giant moving beyond it — Moguro Hagane.

Given the circumstances, it made no sense for Moguro to have killed Chisha.

By his very position, Moguro should have been an ally to Chisha in his guise as Emperor, and while the cause of Chisha's death was a single blow through the chest, that massive body of Moguro's should have been incapable of such a delicate strike.

If there was anything troubling him, it was the strange words left behind by the Stargazer — Ubilk — who had passed Berstetz on his way out of the Crystal Palace.

"The Great Disaster, and the ruin it brings... just what, unknown to myself, is happening to this empire?"

"—Oh my, you haven't been told? Then shall I be the one to teach you?"

"——"

At the voice that reached him without warning, Berstetz's cheeks stiffened, and slowly he turned his gaze back.

From the great hole, his eyes moved to the fallen corpse in the guise of the Emperor. And then, lifting his gaze further still, to the innermost depths of the throne room — to the throne itself.

The throne, unshaken from its rock-solid place even amid the impacts rocking the entire castle. There, where the national flag bearing the sword-wolf hung behind it, fluttering in the wind, a shadowy figure sat with chin propped upon one hand.

A figure who, with all due awe set aside, had seated itself upon the throne that belonged to His Imperial Majesty, looking down upon Berstetz.

"—Wha—"

Under any normal circumstance, Berstetz should have sharpened his voice and rebuked whoever had committed the disrespect of sitting upon that throne.

But he could not do it. Shock had certainly robbed him of his voice, but more than that, Berstetz simply had no right to rebuke this particular person.

The position of Prime Minister carried, within the Vollachian Empire, the greatest authority next to the Emperor and the imperial family.

The generals celebrated as the Nine Divine Generals might be said to hold equal rank, but what mattered was this: there were almost no people within the country before whom Berstetz, in his position as Prime Minister, could not speak a word of rebuke.

And yet, in spite of that, Berstetz could not rebuke the one before him.

Because——

"Still alive, are you? My, aren't you a stubborn one, Berstetz. —Say, which of them won the Imperial Selection Ceremony? Brother Vincent? Or was it Priscilla, I wonder?"

The one who spoke these words, her beautiful, bloodless face crazed with cracks, was of the Vollachian imperial line.

The princess Berstetz Fondalfon had once served as his lord, the one who should have died in defeat during the Imperial Selection Ceremony — Lamia Godwin — sat with legs crossed upon the throne, utterly at ease.
