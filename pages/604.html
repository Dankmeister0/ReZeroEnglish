Arc 7 Chapter 99: "That Which Rules the Sky"

――There's a word: "war cry."

Put simply, it's nothing more than a battle roar. It's the voice raised to rouse oneself, one's comrades, and morale when charging into combat, into a match, into any kind of contest — it cuts off the mind's retreat and grants the strength to push forward. Forming a huddle before a match, raising a battle cry before charging onto the field, the magic words that bind everyone into one — in this world, those were, quite literally, words of "Magic."

"…Unbelievable, I suppose."

Beatrice was struck speechless by the sheer destructive force of the "Pleiades Battalion," the amateur mob centered on Natsuki Subaru, as it charged furiously into the enemy forces and smashed through Imperial soldiers braced in sturdy armor.

Red Galewind Horses bounded forward, shaking the ground with their thunder, reins held by a dull-looking bearded man named Idra; ahead of him rode Beatrice and Subaru in an unglamorous, ungainly riding posture. Under that banner, the whole group advancing was under the influence of a ferociously intensified sun magic.

And the method making that possible was—

"—Weapon-Spirit Awakening."

That was the phrase Beatrice murmured under her breath: a phantom technique that had existed in name for centuries, yet had never been taken seriously, let alone brought anywhere near practical use. Even Echidna, the "Witch" who had systematized every branch of magic, had judged it a technique no single person could ever reproduce, and had left it as nothing more than a provisional name — an ancient "flight of fancy."

"Weapon-Spirit Awakening" was, in essence, a method of forcibly achieving the "Flow Method" — the technique of channeling the mana circulating through the body into physical enhancement — by an alternate route from sun magic, one that induced the same effect through a different approach.

There's a saying about the strength born of desperation in a burning building — a way of describing how, in a crisis, the body's normal limits come off and it produces power beyond anything ordinary. And in this world, that phenomenon is recognized as something that genuinely occurs. Under sufficient mental and physical strain, the Gate within the body — normally dormant — opens, and as a result the person falls into the same state as one who can wield the "Flow Method."

Those with exceptional aptitude sometimes acquire command of the "Flow Method" on their own through such experiences, but explaining that further would stray from the point here, so it will be set aside. What matters is that "Weapon-Spirit Awakening" is a method of forcibly stripping away the human body's limiters — and that it was, in fact, active in every single one of the several thousand members of the Pleiades Battalion.

To begin with, the only time in the past Echidna had ever witnessed a phenomenon that could be called "Weapon-Spirit Awakening" was, unsurprisingly, on a battlefield — and the ones producing that effect had been a small tribe raising battle cries.

As was their custom in war, they kept raising their voices before the fighting, during it, and after it was decided. Those morale-rousing war cries carried an effect close to sun magic, lifting the strength of the whole tribe — and as a result, that tribe, though small in number, had achieved results far beyond anything the others managed. Even so, they could not overcome the disadvantage of numbers, and vanished amid the chaos of war; afterward, similar groups arose here and there only to be ground down in turn, and none of them survived intact.

Gather people at random and the effect never appears; let the numbers grow too large and the effect fails to take hold; a lone master of the art has no reproducibility at all. And so "Weapon-Spirit Awakening" faded away without ever seeing the light of day. In the present age, it remained confined to rare individuals of exceptional skill mastering the "Flow Method" on their own — while for anyone else, raising a battle cry on the field amounted to little more than a small boost of courage. And yet—

"""Strongest! Strongest!! STRONGEST——!!!"""

Shouting those words, everyone in the Pleiades Battalion advancing alongside Subaru blazed with the radiance of what should have been a lost art: "Weapon-Spirit Awakening."

Bound together by unshakable trust centered on Subaru, numerous enough to be counted as a proper army, with no lone expert around to throw off the balance with careless sun magic — it was, in every sense, an ideal environment. The effect of the triggered "Weapon-Spirit Awakening," expressed in terms of sun magic, was an outrageous, multi-layered enhancement: sharpened senses, dramatically improved physical ability, a sharp rise in bodily toughness, and even a dizzying boost to thinking speed and reaction time.

Each and every soldier became, quite literally, a warrior worth a thousand — and the Imperial troops, caught utterly unprepared, were swept away by the momentum; the front line simply "melted" away. Like ice touched by heated iron, the enemy's formation crumbled from its leading edge, and the amateur mob tore through the heart of the enemy line at a terrifying pace. More astonishing still—

"—Knock 'em senseless, but don't you dare let 'em die! Make 'em believe they can't win even if they fight back!"

"""OOOOH——!!!""" """We hear you, boss!""" """It's just like when you saved us!!!"""

On a battlefield where this many people were colliding with their lives on the line, here was a declaration of no-kill from the very one leading the charge. Anyone who knew war would laugh it off as absurd, as childish nonsense — but this amateur mob, who had apparently broken through with this same overwhelming force all the way to today, took it in stride without knowing any better.

Blow after blow, tackle after tackle sent Imperial soldiers flying one after another, but what piled up wasn't corpses — it was mountains of wounded, broken in body and spirit alike. Leave the enemy alive and their comrades must divert effort to rescue the fallen — that would be the tactical reasoning, except it isn't. It's simply that Natsuki Subaru lacks the nerve to let anyone die. And that lack of nerve, that wish of Subaru's, was being granted by this overwhelming force.

"Subaru, how much of this did you actually think through!?"

"Huh? Oh, sorry for everybody yelling so loud! But you know, when everybody shouts like this together, all 'let's go, let's go, let's go,' it really gets you pumped up!"

"~~! That's absurd, I suppose!"

Unwittingly, Beatrice found herself echoing the exact same conclusion that anyone who witnessed this battlefield anomaly would inevitably blurt out. But there was no reason to blame her for it. It would only be natural to want to say as much. From Subaru's answer just now, Beatrice understood at once — Subaru, and everyone around him, knew nothing of "Weapon-Spirit Awakening," understood none of it.

They were simply fighting, weapon in hand, on the nonsensical logic that shouting your lungs out somehow makes you strong — and in doing so, perfectly reproducing a theory even the "Witch" herself had abandoned. And, though Beatrice had no way of knowing it, the only person in the entire world capable of putting that theory into practice was Natsuki Subaru, bearer of the Authority of the "Little King."

"Well, of course you would…"

"Hm?"

"Of course you would be Betty's partner!"

In the arms of Natsuki Subaru, who was pulling off what no one else could, Beatrice affirmed the situation even as her mana — depleted by their long-awaited reunion — was replenished. Amid a state of affairs that almost no one in the amateur Pleiades Battalion understood, and had no need to understand, Beatrice put her mind to work as befit Subaru's partner. How could she intervene in this — perhaps unrepeatable — historic moment?

"If the enemy's momentum's been broken, I suppose I'll see to it they can't regroup."

Beatrice shifted, turning her small palm toward the mass of Imperial soldiers sent flying. Keeping in mind Subaru's wishes, the battalion's valiant fighting, and the very rules of engagement that governed the Empire's way of war, what Beatrice did was—

"—El Shamak."

As one member of the Pleiades Battalion, Beatrice wielded her own power, swollen beyond its usual bounds. Black mist descended one after another over the heads of the Imperial soldiers defending the Fourth Apex, stealing their thoughts, freezing their fighting spirit, robbing them of any chance to strike back.

——Within mere minutes of the Pleiades Battalion joining the fray, the Fourth Apex — which until then had suffered the least damage in this Battle for the Imperial Capital — collapsed with no means of resistance left to it.

Word of the Pleiades Battalion's unstoppable charge reached Abel's ears at headquarters almost immediately.

Though at that point, the name "Pleiades Battalion" hadn't reached him yet — only a report that a group appearing from the west had collided with Imperial troops at tremendous speed and shattered their defensive line. In any case—

"—Well, well. Here I come like the trump card making her grand entrance, only to find our moment already stolen. How thoroughly delightful."

A vigorous voice rang out through headquarters, and a beauty strode in with unhurried confidence, taking the fact in with evident good humor. The one who appeared was a woman with rippling hair, a white scar carved across her fine, resolute features. Clad in garb better suited to a rough brigand than her noble station, this heroic woman who now set foot in headquarters was named Serena Dracroy. She held a position among the foremost High Counts of the Vollachia Empire, and this time had thrown her lot in not with the Emperor's side, but with this band of rebels against the ruling order — one of Abel's true trump cards.

At Serena's arrival, Abel, still gazing out over the distant battlefield, crossed his arms.

"I had been given to understand that the heart of your reinforcements was your Flying Dragon Squadron."

"Relax. I've no intention of changing the fact that my own elite corps is the Flying Dragon Squadron. Whatever's stealing the battlefield's attention right now is something unexpected — even to me."

"Not your doing, then?"

"If they wanted to come under my wing, I wouldn't turn them away, but unfortunately those people seem to look up to only one person. They haven't so much as glanced my way."

As she answered, Serena's long strides brought her up alongside Abel. She studied his face with her narrow, keen eyes, gaze lingering on the demon mask that concealed his features.

"Don't tell me you refuse to join hands with someone who hides their face?"

"I've no intention of saying anything so tiresome. Those who hide their true feelings behind makeup and those who hide their true face behind a mask are hardly so different. If the reason for concealment is a scar, I'd wager mine is no worse than yours."

"I won't tell you the reason, but this mask isn't due to any wound. It's a necessity."

"I imagine so. Your letters made it clear enough that you're not one to do anything without necessity. Having exchanged words with you directly now, that impression's only deepened."

Hand on her hip, Serena smiled fiercely as she took Abel's measure. Of course, in her dealings with the true Emperor, Abel and Serena were already acquainted. The mask's "Perception Obstruction" effect was subtle enough to smooth over any such minor discrepancies, but Abel's own assessment of Serena did not change for it. Fierce, forward-thinking, willing to bare her fangs at the Emperor himself if necessity demanded — a formidable general who lived up to the epithet "the Scorching Duchess" in every respect of her character.

Indeed, had she not possessed exactly that temperament, she never could have been counted among the hand of cards thrown in with this rebellion.

"Is that your assessment — that there's no one else quite so eccentric as me?"

"There was some thought required in how to prepare the material to draw you in. But the greatest reason I chose you was to raise the odds of victory in this war by bringing you into it."

"To think my Flying Dragon Squadron, left out in the cold by the capital, would be valued so highly."

Serena shrugged, her tone dripping with irony, but her true reaction — belied by both her words and her expression — was one of simmering fury at the underlying fact itself. The existence of the Flying Dragon Squadron under High Count Dracroy's command was the very core of why the Dracroy domain was feared and revered in the Vollachia Empire, a nation that prizes strength above all. In other words, for Serena Dracroy, it was the wellspring of pride she could least afford to see wounded.

Even if someone from the Dracroy domain committed some outrage — even conspired in secret to assassinate the Emperor himself — their skill and prowess must never be called into doubt. That was how far it went. Which was precisely why—

"You accepted this invitation. Can you drive back those flying dragons ruling the battlefield's sky — the ones obeying the dragonkin Madelyn Eschart — and seize control of the airspace?"

"Your enticing proposal wasn't the only reason I took the bait, I'll admit… but seeing that through is, at present, an important role of mine. I accept the charge. —My Flying Dragon Squadron will handle it."

"——"

"It irks me, after all, to keep having our moment stolen by those upstart reinforcements."

Having accepted the role expected of her, Serena steered the conversation back to the western battlefield. Abel's gaze, drawn once more by her words, narrowed behind the demon mask, his black eyes sharpening. As Serena said, that band was a complete departure from everything he had planned for. —Honestly, straying from the path he had laid out was not something he welcomed, even if the tide of battle happened to be turning in his favor.

"That group has been stirring up trouble west of the capital. Surely word of them had reached your ears?"

"It had. But I couldn't read the faction's purpose, and by the position reported, I judged they wouldn't make it in time for the decisive battle. So I didn't count them among our forces."

"Then they've overturned your prediction. From what I hear, they kept moving day and night without rest just to make it to this battlefield in time."

"…I can follow the logic, but it's an unrealistic feat. How far would one have to run in a single day to make it? And even supposing they did, with that many stragglers falling out along the way, there's no way they could actually fight."

The more a group's numbers swell, the more grueling even mere movement becomes — that goes without saying. The longer time passes untouched by logistics, supply, and combat for a force that size, the more its frenzy cools; accumulating fatigue saps morale with ease, and hearts drift from the banner raised over them. Bringing such a force to a battlefield intact is no small feat, and even if they manage it, there's no reason to expect they could fight competently once they arrived. And yet—

"Then tell me, masked commander-in-chief — does that look to you like the fighting of men with low morale?"

Put that way by Serena, Abel had no choice but to deny what his own eyes were showing him. As far as he could make out from a distance, the way that group was storming the enemy line, kicking up clouds of dust, defied all reason. Even without hearing the full extent of the war cries they raised, the aftershocks of it had reached headquarters unmistakably. To call that low morale would be the empty prattle of a fool blind to reality.

"I hear the one leading them is one of the 'Black-Haired Crown Princes' making a stir in various regions. I'd taken the talk of Lord Vincent's bastard children being scattered about as nothing more than a joke — but it seems, unexpectedly, one of them is proving himself the genuine article."

"…I see. So that's it."

"Hm?"

Watching the trailing dust from afar, something clicked into place in Abel's narrowed thoughts. Serena, who had merely repeated information she assumed he already knew, tilted her head at his words. But Abel did not answer her puzzlement, only closing one eye at the understanding taking root within him.

Abnormal morale, remarkable cohesion, a distance that should have been impossible to cross, all traversed to come crashing uninvited onto this battlefield — the true nature of that group had finally come into view. Namely—

"—So you've finally decided to make proper use of your own power."

If that were so, it would explain the sheer presence of this unforeseen force that had come charging in from the west. And that, presumably, would be just as unexpected to the false Emperor sitting on the throne in the capital. Assuming that "Black-Haired Crown Prince" possessed abilities worthy of the rumors — or, at the very least, was someone capable of playing the part convincingly enough.

"There's no need for us to intervene in the western battlefield ourselves. But the vital point still to be struck remains the Third Apex. I have no intention of easing off. —High Count Dracroy."

Called by name, Serena nodded, "Understood." Then, following the direction of Abel's gaze toward the sky ruled by darting flying dragons, she said,

"Let's give His Imperial Majesty — who so favors his wild, ill-mannered dragonkin — a good look at the difference between a feral flying dragon and a properly trained Flying Dragon Squadron."

And with that, wearing a wild, feral grin, she took on the task — never for a moment imagining that the genuine article of that very Emperor stood right beside her.

——Headquarters, positioned to look down over the whole battlefield from the rear, able to keep watch over developments in every quarter.

The reinforcements that arrived there — the flying dragons that made Serena Dracroy's blood boil — were commanded by the dragonkin who was also one of the "Nine Divine Generals": Madelyn Eschart. Ferocious creatures that, without special secret arts, would never suffer familiarity with humans, the flying dragons unleashed their innate savagery and danger without restraint, rampaging as a tremendous threat to the humans even in this Battle for the Imperial Capital.

Madelyn did seem to make some effort to spare her allied Imperial troops from harm, but between her own rather slapdash explanations and the flying dragons' inability to distinguish friend from foe, the indiscriminate devastation seen when they had assaulted the walled city of Guaral did not repeat itself here. Even so, the threat of claw and fang wheeling through the sky lunged at the rebels as if remembering itself now and then, and the occasional dropped stone struck the earth like a bombing run — damage too significant to ignore.

That said, the only ones the flying dragons could inflict such attacks on were fighters who valued strength in numbers over individual prowess; they could not force their way into clashes between beings of overwhelming individual power. Between the "Nine Divine Generals" guarding each Apex and the rebel elites who clashed with them head-on — a battle on an entirely higher plane — the flying dragons had no way whatsoever to intrude. And so—

"—I, Mezoreia. In answer to my beloved child's voice, I shall become the wind from the heavens."

"—Icicle Line!!"

Sweeping both hands, Emilia redrew a white line of ice across the world she had traced, and leapt. An instant later, a tail swept across the snow-blanketed ground where Emilia had stood only a moment before, at ferocious speed — the ground stripped bare in an instant, snow flashing into vapor.

The one who had done it was an overwhelming presence, a massive white-scaled body wrapped as if in the very clouds that streamed across the sky — a threat to the world itself, called down to the earth by the dragonkin Madelyn.

"Mezoreia……!"

Somewhere in the back of her mind, Emilia recalled that there was a city in the Vollachia Empire that shared this exact name, even as she gritted her teeth at the force of the attack she had barely dodged, steeling her resolve. Along the line of ice redrawn by Icicle Line, an ice wall rose from the ground with a resounding crack. It didn't match the height of the wall encircling the capital, but it was tall enough to keep the airborne Mezoreia from escaping the battlefield.

"I can't let you go anywhere near everyone else."

In a battlefield growing colder and colder — no, one she herself was making colder — Emilia declared her resolve, breath white in the air. Honestly, when Madelyn had summoned Mezoreia, Emilia had been quite at a loss. Dealing with Madelyn alone was already more than enough to keep her scrambling, and it was plain to see that adding Mezoreia to the mix would only make things far worse. And yet Emilia's instincts told her that having someone else help her here would be difficult.

"If it were someone who could match pace with me like Priscilla, that'd be one thing, but…"

Emilia isn't especially good at fighting in sync with people around her. She's not good at thinking while she fights. That's why she respects people who can think while they fight, and figures she could combine her strength with someone like that. But in this situation, fighting Madelyn and the others, even that was difficult.

"So I'll just keep making it colder, and colder, and colder."

Creating the ice barrier with Icicle Line wasn't only to keep Mezoreia from escaping — it was also to let Emilia confine the space in which she was dropping the temperature. This had been Petra's suggestion: most Earth Dragons are weak to cold. If Flying Dragons and Water Dragons shared the same roots as Earth Dragons, maybe they'd share that weakness to cold too. In truth, unlike Earth Dragons, Flying Dragons that soar through the frigid upper sky and Water Dragons that swim through cold water have widely varying tolerance to cold — but if the cold is overwhelming enough, that misunderstanding becomes irrelevant.

And at present, the extreme cold Emilia was generating had long since exceeded any such limit.

"—It's like the time of Puck's Magic Release Period."

There had been a time when Puck's mana discharge was delayed and nearly encased Roswaal Manor in ice before it could burn — the current battlefield's chill rivaled that. An ordinary person's hands would go numb, unable to grip a weapon; their whole body would become terribly hard to move. Even strong fighters would feel the effects to no small degree. Having chosen to fight this way, Emilia had no choice but to fight alone. At least, this time.

"—I, Mezoreia. In answer to my beloved child's voice, I shall become the wind from the heavens."

From the low, thick-clouded sky, a voice rang down with such gravity it seemed as though the heavens themselves had spoken, and Emilia's violet-and-indigo eyes wavered.

That declaration from the transcendent being was followed, in the next instant, by a blow beyond human comprehension. A mere sweep of the tail tore up the earth; a swipe of claws cleaved space itself; and its great breath carried enough force to blast away nearly half a city — utterly beyond any normal scale. That was what a Dragon truly was — a legend Emilia had, in a mere short span of time, now encountered twice.

"——"

Its spread wings flashed, and the ice wall Emilia had raised was cleaved through with ease. Though she'd worked hard to harden it — not quite to the strength of iron — it was destroyed effortlessly, and before disappointment or shock could catch up with her, Emilia scrambled to dodge the bullets of ice now hurtling toward her. Countless fragments of ice, large and small, sent flying by the wing and raining down like shrapnel — colliding carelessly with a chunk as big as her own head would completely rob her of the mobility she needed against a dragon.

For so small a being fighting so vast a dragon, that would be a fatal blow.

"Hyah! Yah! Whoop! Watch out!"

And so, moving almost like a dance, Emilia weaved between the chunks of ice, deflecting what she couldn't dodge with a sword and shield of ice she conjured on the spot, holding her ground. In that moment, never breaking eye contact with her opponent — that instinct came from her direct battle against the "Divine Dragon" Volcanica at the Pleiades Watchtower. A dragon's power far exceeds anything Emilia and the others can imagine, so even the slightest motion that doesn't even look like an attack can turn out to be terrifyingly dangerous.

"With Volcanica, I almost got blown away just by its breathing."

Remembering that, Emilia was struck again with relief that it was she who was here now, and not someone else. Without knowing how to fight a dragon, one could easily be undone by the very first careless slip. But as Emilia fought Mezoreia, that wasn't the only thing on her mind.

Summoned by Madelyn's call, parting the sky to descend upon the battlefield, the "Cloud Dragon" Mezoreia — its uniquely dragon-like way of fighting was, of course, an enormous trial. But—

"Mezoreia! Please, listen to me! I don't want to fight you, or Madelyn either……"

"—I, Mezoreia. In answer to my beloved child's voice, I shall become the wind from the heavens."

"Ohh, I knew it……"

Emilia's voice rang out, pleading — but her words were brushed aside; Mezoreia, deaf to them, only swayed its long whiskers and looked down at the small figure of Emilia below with its eyes. Meeting that gaze head-on, every nerve in her slender body thrumming with resolve, Emilia gritted her teeth at a truth she could feel pressing in on her more and more. And that truth was—

"—I, Mezoreia. In answer to my beloved child's voice, I shall become the wind from the heavens."

"Just like Volcanica — it's too old, and it's forgotten everything!"

The dragon, encountered for the second time in her life as a transcendent being — the "Cloud Dragon" Mezoreia, ruler of the skies — was, just like the "Divine Dragon" she'd met at the tower, senile, having lost its wits entirely.
