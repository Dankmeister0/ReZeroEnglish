Arc 4 Chapter 126B: "Next Time, We'll Surely Have a Tea Party"

—I saw the future.

'—Without that, you can't even swing a sword, can you. Thief!!'

'There, look. Once again, the victory is mine.'

'Subaru and Emilia-neesama must be so tired. I'm sorry. And now I've become a burden too. I'm sorry. I wanted to keep saying thank you, over and over, for as long as it took to make up for how little I've said it...'

—The lights of every color, each time she touched one, kept showing Emilia a different future.

'That the person I wanted to kill this badly turned out to be someone kind — what an awful nightmare.'

'There are feelings that must never be spoken aloud. If revealing them led to this, are you satisfied?'

'Do you think you've kept your promise with this? If so… if so, then I should have just died back then, trussed up in a straw mat in that pit! If it meant seeing a dawn like this… I should have ended it there! Damn it, damn it!'

'I'm sorry. Because I'm weak, I'm sorry. For not being able to kill him, I'm sorry. Because of this, — will be alone forever now. Because I'm weak, I'm sorry…'

—Wailing grief, roaring fury, endings, rebirths, partings, meetings — presented in every form imaginable.

'Mm, mm... my treasured grandchild... has grown into a fine child, hasn't he...'

'I will absolutely not be killed by some incomprehensible thing called a curse!'

'I just realized, that's all… that all these days, I hadn't been walking alo-o-ne this whole time.'

'Why… won't a soul dwell in it, whyyy?!'

—Is there truly nothing but despair in the future? Nothing at all besides sorrow and suffering?

'Just like I promised, I'm gonna kill ya! Ahh?! Natsuki Subaruuuu!!'

'Am I really asking for so much? Is it such a selfish thing to want? Nobody dies, nobody cries... What's so hard about that?'

'In the end, we must spill every last drop of our blood in atonement, mustn't we.'

'Good, evil, like, dislike, right, wrong — who gives a damn. You can just stand there stomping your feet. Me... we don't care if it's a Witch or a Dragon — if it's blocking the way, we're smashing it down.'

—Then is this path wrong? Is wishing itself a mistake?

'—I think praying in order to ask for something is arrogant. Prayer is for when you seek forgiveness.'

—In the world of the last light, a girl she had never once seen awake and speaking had said this.

She thought she wanted to properly exchange words with her, someday. That feeling alone was enough to refuse denying it all.

When her vision cleared, Emilia realized she was standing in a grassy field where the wind gently stirred.

Atop a small rise stood a white table. Her own body was seated in a white chair, and she couldn't recall what had happened in the moments just before she blinked. Still, she understood that she was in the dream world.

"Echidna?"

If someone were to greet her like this at the end of the Trial, it could only be Echidna, the overseer. Emilia cast her gaze around, searching for her.

But though the endless grassland stretched clear to the horizon, there was no sign of another presence, nor anything to block the view, no matter how far she looked. It felt as though, were she to leave this spot and start walking, she would never again be able to find this white table.

"...................."

Even though her body was certainly here, she felt a strange sensation of being about to fall away toward the far horizon. Emilia took a deep breath to steady herself, and decided that if no one was around, she would move.

Perhaps there was an exit somewhere. She would find it and leave this place. Spending time here doing nothing would gain her nothing.

And then—

"It's been like this since way back, haa. Why is it that, in cases like this, fuu, the short straw always lands on me, haa. What do you make of that, fuu?"

"...Ah."

Seeing the ball of fur that had suddenly appeared across the table, Emilia froze.

Still half-risen from her seat, she stared, stunned, at the being before her.

She gasped, unable to help herself.

"Mm... that's an easy reaction to read, haa. I suppose that's the correct one to have, fuu. That boy was just a little bit dense, that's all, haa."

"—————"

With every sentence she formed, the figure let out a gloomy sigh.

A listless woman with an enormous mass of reddish-purple hair, wrapped in a single well-worn black robe. Her words carried no venom — she seemed entirely at ease, natural.

—And yet Emilia felt a pressure as though her throat were being gripped.

If the woman so much as had a whim, Emilia's head would vanish from her shoulders instantly.

Emilia had reclaimed her past and could now wield a vast quantity of mana too great to fit within a single body. Even by the standards of Emilia, whose strength as a solo combatant had grown by leaps incomparable to before, there was simply no contest between herself and the person before her.

Emilia understood — she could be killed as easily as one might swat away a fly.

"There's no need to be on guard, fuu. I've got no intention of doing you harm, and none of letting harm come to me either, haa. That kind of thing's just a bother, fuu. And yet that Echidna, dead set on not showing her face to you, went and pushed this whole thing off on me, haa. That's the story, fuu."

"I-Is that so..."

Timidly, Emilia gave a small nod at the woman's words.

Even now, the overwhelming presence surrounding the woman hadn't faded. She remained a being who could destroy Emilia with ease. But if being killed, should the woman feel like it, was unavoidable either way, then whether Emilia cowered or not made no difference.

A deep breath — hold it, release it. And Emilia forced herself to calm down.

"So, if you're standing in for Echidna... are you a Witch too?"

"—. I see, haa. You've got more nerve than I expected, fuu. Not flinching when it counts — haa — maybe that's something you get from your mother, fuu."

"You know about my mother?"

"Can't say, but I know her, haa."

Emilia caught her breath at the unexpected connection, but the woman said it flatly, her face utterly worn out. Even if pressed, she likely wouldn't give anything more.

Though she felt a "someday" welling up in her, Emilia set the matter of her mother aside for now. She had seen the past, seen an impossible present, and now the radiance of Fortuna and Jus lived in her heart.

For now, that alone was enough.

"What should I call you, since you're standing in for Echidna?"

"I like a girl who doesn't whine and fuss, fuu. I'd like Typhon to take a lesson from you, haa. My name's Sekhmet, fuu. As you've probably guessed, I'm the so-called Witch of Sloth, haa."

Sekhmet, her body slumped against the table with only her face propped up, smiled faintly.

The dark circles under her eyes and her unhealthy complexion were concerning, but she was a beautiful woman with well-formed features. Even so, the word "Witch" and the uncanny aura she gave off made it easy to imagine she was no ordinary person.

"How our names, us Witches, get passed down in the present age, fuu — I've got no interest in that, haa, so it's all the same to me, fuu. All I want is to finish up this favor quick and settle back into my lazy sleep, haa. That's all, fuu."

"Um, if it's such a bother... couldn't someone else have done it? If Echidna didn't want to, it didn't have to be her, but... aren't there other Witches?"

"With the rest of that lot, haa, you can't hold a conversation, fuu. And Minerva, the one who might actually manage a decent conversation at a time like this, haa — she says she hasn't got the face to meet you, fuu."

"Minerva..."

Sekhmet's speech, broken up with sighs between every phrase, had an awkward rhythm. And yet, if even she counted as easier to talk to than the other Witches, imagining what the rest were like was frightening.

But even that thought was overwritten by the feeling the word "Minerva" stirred in her upon hearing it.

"Minerva..."

Emilia murmured it again, only under her breath, and tilted her head at the sound of it.

For some reason, it felt as though it stirred a deeply nostalgic memory. And yet, she had no recollection of having heard it before — not among her memories from before, nor among the ones that had been restored to her.

Still, it was a strange name, one that powerfully called to mind someone she had once been close to.

"Well, no use talking about someone who isn't here, haa. Anyway, all I'm here to do is pass along Echidna's message, fuu. Once you've heard it, whether or not you decide to end the Trial is left up to you, haa. Being used like an errand girl isn't easy either, fuu."

"Um, thanks for your hard work...?"

"I'll just take the thought and leave it at that, haa. Now, listen close, fuu."

Calling Emilia back from her thoughts, Sekhmet tipped her head the other way on the table. Then, eyeing Emilia with a flat stare, she set her right hand on the table with a sigh.

"In the third Trial, haa, you should have seen the future, fuu. That future — once you decided to overcome this tomb, haa — is a future of possibility, one that could come to pass, fuu."

"A future... of possibility."

"There's a chance you saw all of it, haa. Or, the opposite — a chance you saw none of it, fuu. But that the futures you did see were bad ones, haa — well, knowing Echidna's personality, I can tell you that much for certain, fuu."

As for how Echidna was regarded by the other Witches — at the very least, Sekhmet seemed to have concluded she had a mean streak. Emilia couldn't really say much to that.

In truth, Sekhmet's assessment of Echidna was a bit harsher than Emilia imagined, but it was a tall order to expect a worse verdict than that from Emilia, who had already filed Echidna away as "the mean Witch."

"Futures branch endlessly, and possibilities spin off from possibilities, haa. Even so, the future you just saw is a seed of tragedy with a strong chance of coming true, fuu. What kind of bloom it grows into once it sprouts and buds… haa. Are you prepared to deliberately choose to walk a path that might bloom into a poison flower that makes everyone miserable… fuu?"

"—————"

Falling silent, Emilia looked at Sekhmet with serious eyes.

Sekhmet's face showed the fatigue of having delivered such a long speech, but when she noticed Emilia's gaze, she knit her brows.

"...I thought I'd already asked my question, haa."

"Eh, wait? That was the question? If I answer it, the Trial's over?"

"That's how it works out, fuu. ...Though honestly, given what you're after, the Trial itself was more or less finished the moment you made it this far, haa."

At Sekhmet's air of treating this like a dead formality, Emilia gave a wry smile.

There was no ill will in it. She was simply surprised at how anticlimactic the question had been. After all, how Emilia would answer a question like that was already a foregone conclusion.

"A world where everyone ends in tragedy. I'm not prepared to watch that."

Memories surfaced, ones that felt like her chest being torn open, her heart being clawed at.

In the world of darkness, amid lights of every color, Emilia had heard cries of grief again and again.

"A future where everyone might meet a sad end. In the pitch-dark world before this one, I saw a lot of that too. Everyone was crying, and suffering, and angry. I don't know the details of what happened, but I thought I never wanted to see a future like that."

"...But if you choose to stay on this path as it is, fuu, then that outcome is one I can practically guarantee, haa. If you don't like it, will you run, fuu?"

"No. Because I don't like it, I'll stand and face it."

Shaking her head, Emilia squared her shoulders at Sekhmet, whose eyes had narrowed.

Even as she felt on the verge of being swallowed by that overwhelming, otherworldly aura, Emilia's resolve did not break.

When she felt like buckling backward, the memories of her mother and father held her up. And when she felt like giving up and looking down, there were people who would call out to keep her from doing so.

"If there's a sad future ahead, I'll run right up and dodge around it. And if that doesn't work, I'll build up speed and jump right over it. If someone's fallen down, I'll pull hard and haul them back up. If I keep doing that, over and over, I'm sure I can wipe away every one of those tears from before."

"That's a mighty confident way to talk recklessness, haa. Nothing but convenient words, all talk and no substance, the type that breaks the moment something goes wrong, fuu. Isn't that how it usually goes, haa?"

"If it were just me alone, maybe it would."

Emilia answered without flinching at Sekhmet's faintly mocking words.

In a sense, it was a stance of relying on others, of leaning on outside help. But that was, for Emilia, the hardest thing of all — a choice she had never once managed to choose for herself, until now.

"—————"

At that, Sekhmet's face went blank for a moment.

Then, almost immediately, she lowered her face, hiding her expression behind the table and her hair,

"Pfft, kuhu... ha, hahahaha! Ah, that's right! That's exactly right! Of course, of course, the you standing here now would give that answer! Ahh, this is too much!"

"Was it really that funny an answer?"

"It's downright delightful to me, haa. Listen, fuu. That Echidna, haa — the one who challenges this so-called Trial, who watches the challenger agonize and suffer alone over their past, present, and future, fuu, and takes her pleasure in seeing what answer they come up with, haa — she was an incorrigible pervert even after death, fuu. And the day her little scheme falls apart, coming like this… haa. Isn't it just delightful, fuu?"

Sekhmet laughed loudly, speaking on in high spirits even as she caught her breath with difficulty. She lifted her face, straightened her body, and though she leaned back against her chair, she looked straight at Emilia.

With a nostalgic light kindling in her eyes, Sekhmet, still smiling, said,

"To a Trial premised on facing it alone, haa, your answer is not to face it alone, fuu. —If Echidna heard that, she'd say sore-loser things with a perfectly straight face, haa."

"Really. So that's the answer I gave. ...Yeah, I'd really, really like to see that face myself."

"That woman never admits defeat gracefully, so I doubt you'll get to see that face, fuu. On that count, I'll let it stay a privilege reserved for us dream-dwellers alone, haa."

"That's not fair."

When Emilia pouted, Sekhmet's face grew even more delighted.

To an outside eye, it might have looked like two old friends of ten years' acquaintance exchanging words, so full of warm, easy joy was the scene.

"Still, as a kind of compensation, I'll hand down the result of the Trial by my own discretion, haa. Naturally, no complaints — full marks, a passing grade, the works, fuu."

"Is it really okay to decide that so easily?"

"Did you want some fumbling answer, some grand production, haa? If so, sorry, but you're expecting the wrong thing from me, fuu. Either way, right now I'm the overseer, and my answer is the answer, haa. ...The Trial, safely, is over, fuu."

Letting out a long breath, Sekhmet snapped her fingers lightly. It didn't come out clean on the first try; only after the second and third attempt did a proper sound finally ring out, and a wind blew from behind Emilia.

As the wind stirred her silver hair, Emilia turned to look and saw a single door had appeared at the base of the hill. Though it looked as though it led nowhere at all, Emilia knew instinctively that it was the way out of the dream world.

"If I go outside through that door, the Trial is over... is that it?"

"That's right, haa. Congratulations, fuu. For four hundred years, haa — ever since the tomb was built and Echidna's Trial began to function — no one has ever cleared it, fuu. Well, mind you, there were hardly any challengers to begin with, haa."

"...I guess so. Not many people come into the Sanctuary, and the conditions for someone to get trapped inside the Sanctuary are surprisingly strict."

"That's part of it, but... ah, well, never mind, fuu. Either way, it's over now, haa."

The way Sekhmet had subtly hedged her words nagged at her, but Emilia didn't press. More than that, the exhilaration of hearing the Trial was over outweighed it.

Honestly, she didn't yet feel anything like a sense of accomplishment. It hadn't sunk in. This was the Trial where she had stumbled so badly on the first one, the Trial of the Past, where she had, for a time, nearly given herself over to despair, convinced it was impossible.

She had thought she'd steeled her resolve, telling herself she wouldn't lose. But even so.

"You've got a face that says you're not convinced, fuu."

"Um, just a teensy bit. I guess it's been bugging me."

"Whatever else you say about her, Echidna doesn't set questions that can't be solved, haa. Saying that's all there is to it wouldn't be quite right, but it's more or less like that, fuu."

If a Witch — a fellow Witch — said so, then that was probably how it was.

Reluctantly, Emilia dipped her chin, settling into acceptance. Sekhmet glanced at her for a moment, then gave a small wave of the right hand she'd rested on the table.

"Once you go out through the door behind you, haa, it's goodbye to this dream castle, fuu. Which means, of course, the end of the Trial, haa. And in the tomb, right now, in the chamber at the back of the room where you took the Trial, fuu — it also means you've earned the right to look inside the door there, haa."

"The door inside the small chamber. Right, I remember it. I can go inside there... what's inside it?"

"There's a mechanism there that maintains the tomb's function, fuu. Only once you stop it, haa, will the Sanctuary's role finally come to an end, fuu. How to stop it, you'll understand once you're inside, haa."

"Stop the tomb's function, and end the Sanctuary's role. The barrier will disappear, then."

If that barrier vanished, then not only Emilia but the residents living within the Sanctuary would also be able to leave the forest.

When the barrier opened, she had no way of knowing how many people would actually go out into the wider world. Nor whether living outside would truly, in a real sense, be good for them.

But they couldn't go on shutting themselves away any longer.

Just as Subaru had won Garfiel over, Emilia too would have to tell them. The time when they could go on hiding away forever, in a place where time had stopped, was over.

How to carve out a place to live within time that had started moving again —

If possible, Emilia wanted them to search together with her for that answer, one she herself hadn't found yet either.

Even if leading them by the hand, or pushing them from behind, was difficult, she could still walk beside them.

An unreliable, groundless way of showing them the right path — one that had only just begun.

"That'll do just fine."

Sekhmet said it as though she had seen straight through to Emilia's inner thoughts.

In that one line, there was none of her characteristic weary sigh mixed in. At Sekhmet looking straight at her and saying it, Emilia caught her breath slightly.

Then she smiled back.

"Yeah, thank you. That's what I want to do too."

With that answer, Emilia rose to her feet before Sekhmet.

Brushing back her silver hair, she bowed her head to Sekhmet, who sat in a posture of seeing her off.

She wasn't quite sure why she did it.

She just somehow felt that a simple word of farewell wasn't enough. Why there was a feeling of gratitude mixed in — Sekhmet, surely, wouldn't tell her.

Pushing her chair back in at the table, Emilia went down the hill toward the door.

The door standing alone in the grassland looked somehow fragile, and Emilia noticed in herself a small pang of loneliness at leaving this dream castle.

A white table, a cool breeze. Gentle sunlight, perfect weather.

If only they could have gathered around that table for a tea party — surely it would have been such fun.

"Sekhmet-san. Can I ask you to pass a message to Echidna?"

"...I'll give it a try, haa."

"If we ever get the chance to meet face to face again, let's definitely have a tea party. Even if she has to come haunt my dreams to do it, I'll welcome her, I promise."

"—Ah, that sounds good. I'll be sure to pass that along."

With her hand on the doorknob, Emilia looked back over her shoulder, and Sekhmet laughed at her words.

Emilia smiled back too, and opened the door.

Beyond the door, darkness spread out.

But somehow, she felt no hesitation, no doubt, about stepping into it. She already knew perfectly well where it led.

It was a door that led beyond the Past, through the choice of the Present, and on into the Future.

—Feeling short of breath, Emilia pushed herself upright on the hard ground.

Waking from the unconsciousness of the Trial was not like waking from sleep.

It was less that her body had fallen asleep, and closer to her consciousness alone having been drawn away from her body to somewhere else. Since the body and the soul had been in different places, and the soul itself had not been asleep, it was only natural that it took a different form from ordinary sleep.

If waking from this were no different from waking from sleep, Emilia, who was bad at getting up in the mornings, would need a considerable amount of time to fully come back. Before, Puck would have woken her, but now that she couldn't rely on him for that, it would eat up an awful, awful lot of time.

Going forward, she would need to be able to handle situations like that on her own too.

"—Ah, oh no."

Emilia, drowsily, had already let out a thought that sounded half asleep even at that point.

She shook her head and stood up, hand against the wall for support. There was nothing wrong with her physically. The lack of any real sense of having overcome the Trial was the same as before, too.

But if what Sekhmet had told her in the dream castle was true—

"That door at the back of the room should be able to open now."

Turning her gaze toward the back, she caught sight of the stone door across from the small chamber.

Before, the door hadn't budged whether pushed or pulled, but now, to Emilia's eyes, it seemed to be faintly wrapped in light, the same as the tomb's walls, which glowed dimly.

Something like an unlocking, perhaps.

Emilia walked toward the door, her footsteps echoing, and standing before it, held her breath for a moment.

Beyond this, there was something to release the Sanctuary.

Sekhmet had said she'd understand what to do once she saw it, but honestly, the possibility that she wouldn't understand made her a little anxious. Emilia didn't have much confidence in her own head.

Would it be no good to bring someone along? Not only were there few people who could even get this far, but she also had the feeling that the door wouldn't open at all if anyone besides herself were present.

Perhaps because things had gone so smoothly, Emilia couldn't shake her suspicion about the problem in front of her.

Suspecting she might be getting set up spoke to a wariness greater than before — though this was a wariness limited strictly to matters involving Echidna. It was a caution born precisely because Emilia understood the personality of the one who'd rigged the whole thing.

"Anyway, I have to go inside and see. Okay, here I go."

Clenching her fists lightly to psych herself up, she reached for the door. Just as her fingers were about to reach it, while she was still wondering whether to push or pull—

—With a sound, the stone door slid sideways, opening a path for Emilia.

"...Ah, somehow I get the feeling Echidna's laughing somewhere with a wicked look on her face."

Her momentum thrown off, Emilia muttered the words with a discontented pout.

She had the vague feeling that this door's little gimmick was one of Echidna's petty harassments, and it eased her tension just a bit.

Letting out a breath, she refreshed her resolve and stepped into the room.

The room beyond the door was smaller — by more than a full size — than the chamber of the Trial.

It was smaller still than the small antechamber — if two of the large beds from Roswaal Manor were set side by side, there would be no floor space left at all.

Her eyes went wide at how much more cramped the room was than she'd expected, and then, catching sight of what lay at the far end, Emilia gasped and pressed a hand to her mouth.

—At the back of the room was something like a transparent coffin, and inside it lay a single woman.

The woman looked so beautiful, so utterly untouched, that Emilia might have thought her merely asleep — as though time itself had frozen around her.

The coffin appeared to be crafted from processed mana crystal, its purity so high that Emilia was astonished when her fingertips touched it — a crystal of such density that it surpassed even the one that had served as Puck's Anchor.

A mana crystal capable of more than sealing away a Great Spirit on the level of Puck — and within it, a single sealed woman. —Naturally, she wasn't breathing. There was no sense of life force; this was already an empty husk of a life.

Long, lustrous hair, white as snow. Her cheeks, the nape of her neck, every inch of visible skin held a beauty reminiscent of virgin snow — a beauty that nearly made one's breath tremble.

Wrapping that beautiful body was a jet-black garment utterly without any excess coloring — a dress-like thing that held a miraculous harmony, as though tailored precisely for her.

A beautiful woman expressible in just two colors: white and black.

That true beauty needs no excess ornamentation at all — standing before that beauty made of nothing but white and black, one could feel the truth of it, along with something like fear.

"What a beautiful person..."

An admiring breath escaped Emilia's lips before she could stop it.

If she looked in a mirror, she herself was also someone of peerless beauty, but there was not a fragment of that kind of self-awareness in Emilia's admiration.

It was simply, purely, a sensibility intoxicated by the beauty of what lay before her — nothing more.

A beautiful woman of white and black.

The one she had met in the dream castle, the one she had exchanged words with so many times — the Witch of Greed.

At the very depths of the tomb, waiting beyond having overcome the Trial of the Witch of Greed, was—

"She looks like Echidna, but... who is she?"

Despite an appearance that called to mind the very embodiment of intellectual hunger, she was a woman Emilia had never seen before.
