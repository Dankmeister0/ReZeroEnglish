Arc 4 Chapter 58: "Granny"

Accompanied by Ryuzu and Pico, Subaru stepped out of the isolated cottage and noticed the eastern sky beginning to pale, and only then became conscious of his own drowsiness.

"Whoa, morning's about to break… Guess it's been an eventful night, in more ways than one."

By Subaru's own sense of elapsed time, tonight hadn't felt continuous—it had begun with Emilia's first Trial, then Subaru's intervention in that Trial, a Return by Death, a return to reality, a skirmish with Garfiel right after the Trial's outcome was settled, then uncovering the location of the facility, and finally Ryuzu's long, drawn-out confession about its inner workings.

It had been such a dense stretch of time that he hadn't been conscious of its pace, but unlike his awareness, his body—which had kept operating regardless—couldn't hide its fatigue. The subtle lack of coordination between mind and body was, if he thought about it, yet another shortcoming of Return by Death, however belatedly he was realizing it.

"If things were normal, I'd want to head straight back to the cathedral and sleep till noon…"

"There's no reason you can't, you know. I'll simply have the next Ryuzu take over for me, and I'll get a proper rest."

"That enviable shift system of yours sounds great and all, but I don't have the time. Can't afford that."

Six days from now—no, since a full day had already passed, five days remained. Factoring in that the round trip to the mansion alone would eat up a full day, he had, realistically, three days in which to actually act.

He couldn't afford to burn away a precious half-day here, and yet, even though Ryuzu stood in a position to know about the Witch, he hesitated to confide in her about the future he'd witnessed.

"Can't go doing anything reckless right now that might set off the Witch of Envy…"

A clammy sweat beaded on Subaru's forehead as the shadow-Witch drinking down Sanctuary flashed across his mind.

He had a vague suspicion that the disaster stemmed from Subaru openly speaking of forbidden things at Echidna's castle. It was precisely because of that place that the Witch's restraint hadn't been in time, and such wrath had been invited.

So, in the real world—where the Witch's hand could reach Subaru directly—he could probably assume the penalty would, as before, fall on him alone, but—

"I'd rather not bet someone else's life just to confirm that."

Subaru muttered weakly, looking down at Pico, who stood vacantly holding his left hand. At his glance, she gazed back, her round eyes widening further still, as if awaiting the next command to fall from his lips.

Having received one order from Subaru already, she seemed to have clearly recognized the transfer of command authority. Now, Pico's demeanor was as obedient as a chick following its mother hen.

"So, what's Su-bou planning to do from here?"

"For now, I'm heading back to the mansion. There's someone I need to talk to, and… I want to see Frederica too. There's a lot I want her to fill in for me."

"Frederica, is it…"

Picturing the tall maid in his mind, Subaru grew suspicious watching Ryuzu, upon hearing the name, knit her brow and repeat it with obvious meaning. It seemed out of character for her.

"You got something on your mind? About Frederica?"

"…It's nothing much."

"Ryuzu-san. I'd honestly rather not use command authority if I can help it. Making you obey orders—frankly, I'd like to be spared that."

Shrugging, Subaru made his plea with an air of entreaty. But contrary to his words, his narrowed, sharp three-whites eyes grew even keener, silently but loudly asserting, if it's necessary, I'll use it. Ryuzu let out a sigh.

"Well, now that I think on it, it does seem like the gears of Sanctuary started slipping little by little, ever since Frederica left."

"The gears started slipping?"

"Given its origins to begin with, whether it could ever be called a healthy state is doubtful. Even so… mm, the residents, and the Ryuzu Meyer duplicates, and even Gar-bou—none of them used to waver as much as they do now."

"…………"

"Su-bou. I'm counting on you."

Ryuzu murmured this, looking up at Subaru, who had fallen silent.

At the word counting, Subaru became aware of a sharp ache twisting in his chest. To have such feelings, beginning with that word, directed at him was—for Subaru—

"For a long time now, Sanctuary has barely managed to keep drawing breath, clumsily stitching together a role it lost sight of long ago. That strain has, at long last, begun splitting open in places all around. So, I will hope, Su-bou."

"Hope for what, from me…"

"That you'll bring the Witch's obsession, Sanctuary's reason for enduring, Ryuzu Meyer's wish—to an end, in the form everyone hopes for."

It was a hope resting entirely on someone else, and far too heavy a one at that.

Subaru moved to answer at once with a single word—"impossible." But his mouth, having looked Ryuzu's earnest gaze full in the face,

"――――"

Would not form the words.

"For now, that's fine. For now, that's still fine."

Ryuzu nodded, as though she understood every bit of Subaru's hesitation and wavering.

Despite her appearance as a young girl, in this one moment Subaru clearly understood that she possessed values befitting all the years she had actually lived.

"It's about time my turn came to an end."

Leaving those words behind with a tone tinged with lingering regret, Ryuzu's body began to emit a faint glow.

It called to mind the sight of a spirit on the verge of vanishing, and Subaru instinctively reached a finger toward her body. But—

"Be at ease. I'm not returning to mana. I'm merely entering a brief period of dormancy, to replenish the mana I've spent. Another Ryuzu will be along shortly."

"E-even if the appearance and way of speaking are the same, that doesn't mean it's the same existence, right?"

"That's right. The appearance, the manner of speech, even the personality—we deliberately keep them alike, but… we are different people. Which is why, right now, the only one who could speak these words to Su-bou like this is me, and me alone. Does that make you lonely?"

"It's not about loneliness or whatever. Ryuzu-san… doesn't it hurt, Ryuzu-san? Don't you hate having to keep playing the part of one Ryuzu Meyer split among four? Don't you ever think, where is my own life, or something like…"

Even as he spoke, Subaru understood that this was far too cruel a question to ask.

Even supposing Ryuzu herself had feelings on the matter—even if, deep down, she truly harbored pain and sorrow over her present state—what could Subaru possibly do, knowing that?

Unable to unravel even the surface principles of magic, the details of mana, the mere outline of the formulas involved, what meaning was there in continuing to reach a finger toward a grief he could never touch?

Ryuzu must have understood Subaru's inner conflict. She smiled faintly, her pale pink hair swaying gracefully in a wind wrapped in morning colors.

"What do you think, Su-bou?"

"――Eh?"

"I'll set the answer to this question, too, as one of the things I'm counting on you for."

With those parting words, Ryuzu's body, thinning like mist, dissolved into the glow of dawn.

It's not the same as dispersing, she'd said beforehand, but it was a sight too fantastical to believe even so. A single person, melting away into the morning sun as though absorbed by it.

That it wasn't the extinguishing of a life—however much he was told so, it wasn't something he could simply believe.

And yet, after a single blink, a new figure had appeared in the very spot where Ryuzu should have vanished. In build, in everything, a being utterly identical to the Ryuzu who had disappeared.

She shook her head once, then looked up at Subaru.

"No need for me to introduce myself, is there, Su-bou. The previous 'me' and I have properly synced up on what was discussed with you."

As if resolving Subaru's doubts, the new Ryuzu briskly ironed out the gaps in their shared understanding. Then, at last, she tilted her head.

"Now then, Su-bou… first, how will you move?"

"Right, well…"

He looked up at the sky.

Taking in, at the edge of his vision, the way the glow of dawn slowly encroached on the night sky, he let his consciousness slide through the gap between the time that had passed and the time that remained.

Then Subaru lowered his gaze, looking at Ryuzu and Pico in turn.

"First, I want to get out of Sanctuary. I'll need you both to lend me a hand."

That was what he proposed.

Just after dawn broke, Subaru's figure stood before the dragon carriage shed.

He opened and closed the creaking door as quietly as he could manage, searching within for the sight of his partner. A jet-black frame that seemed to melt into the dim gloom waited at the very back of the shed for its master's call.

"Sorry for leaving you to your own devices, Patrasche."

At Subaru's greeting, Patrasche stretched her neck out and rubbed her nose against him in reply. The gesture carried both affection and a hint of sulking at having been left alone, and even at a time like this, it brought Subaru an oddly ticklish sense of fondness.

"Sorry to hit you with this right when you've woken up, but it's work. Think you could carry me back to the mansion again?"

He stroked the muzzle pressed against him, and Patrasche rumbled in her throat, seeming to say, with some resignation, "Can't be helped, can it, Subaru-kun…"

Relieved that he'd at least gained her consent, Subaru let out a breath and set about removing the fence to lead Patrasche outside.

With no dead weight to slow her down, Patrasche's own legs could shorten what would normally be a half-day journey even further.

If they set out now, in the early morning, it should be possible to reach the mansion before evening.

"Well, it's a pretty slapdash plan built on the assumption of a do-over, but…"

Having resolved this time to return to the mansion, Subaru, unlike the times before, had no intention of bringing along the evacuees from Arlam Village. Bluntly put, with a do-over always in the back of his mind, he was resolved to strip away every unnecessary variable and focus purely on gathering information.

Having heard Ryuzu Meyer's story, having built something of a friendly relationship with Ryuzu in this current timeline—it was true that letting that go felt like a waste, but—

"My relationship with Garfiel is already the worst it can be, and I just bit off a chunk of stuff that's only gonna make it worse."

The transfer of command authority—if it turned out that what had apparently belonged to Garfiel originally had passed to Subaru, it wasn't hard to imagine him searching bloodshot-eyed for wherever it had gone.

Before long, he'd surely trace it back to Subaru. Subaru hadn't sworn Ryuzu to secrecy, either—he'd told her beforehand that she should answer honestly if asked. All of that, too, had been thought through.

"――And even so, I'm the lingering fool who leaves a note behind for Emilia and no one else."

If he simply accepted that this was a world destined to vanish with the do-over, then logically there was no reason to agonize over how to handle Emilia in this current world.

However much she might suffer, however much she might grieve—or conversely, however much he might make her happy—it would all be abandoned in a world that would simply disappear. He understood that much, in his head, and yet—

"This kind of thing isn't about logic."

Even knowing it was a vanishing world, a world he'd be leaving her behind in, Subaru still couldn't stand the thought of Emilia wearing a sad face.

By silently vanishing on her, he'd surely cause her some keen, piercing grief. She might lose what she clung to, might even lose her footing entirely. That Emilia leaned on him that much made him glad, even as it pained him.

So that it wouldn't come to that—or, even if it did, praying that it wouldn't stay that way—Subaru left a letter for Emilia.

That said, its contents were harmless, the usual string of reassuring platitudes. Unable to convey the truth, all Subaru could do was paper over things with a surface-level comfort.

"Better than nothing… or, I guess, better if Emilia weren't so dependent on me to begin with."

With Puck absent, the degree of Emilia's reliance weighed rather heavily on Subaru alone.

He knew this current thinking was just a way of soothing himself, that things probably wouldn't go that smoothly.

Even so, Subaru would leave Emilia behind and go out of Sanctuary. Hardening his heart, telling himself it was a necessary sacrifice to rewrite an unsalvageable present into a future that could be saved.

"Well then, before anyone spots me, let's get—hm?"

In the process of leading Patrasche out of the shed, Subaru pulled the saddle for riding the Earth Dragon directly out from the removed carriage. He lightly brushed off its surface and was about to set it on Patrasche's back when something caught the edge of his vision. It was—

"Petelgeuse's Gospel, huh…"

The thing bound in thick black covers was the Gospel, tucked away as if hidden in a corner of the carriage.

It was Petelgeuse's keepsake, and honestly, Subaru's real preference was to dispose of it as soon as possible, but he was also wary of it falling carelessly into someone else's hands. More than anything, he'd kept holding onto it in hopes it might prove some clue to the aims and inner workings of that inscrutable group, the Witch Cult.

"Come to think of it, thanks to what Roswaal told me, my view of this thing's changed quite a bit."

Setting the saddle down on the seat, Subaru absently picked up the Gospel.

At its weighty heft and texture, the image of a blood-soaked madman rose up in his mind.

A man who'd harbored an excessive, obsessive attachment to the Gospel, utterly convinced it was proof of his devotion to the Witch.

It was ironic that its contents, to Petelgeuse its owner, had apparently looked like hints of his own future actions.

"Roswaal, Beatrice, even Petelgeuse… every single one of them, a book only they themselves can read—what's with that…?"

Muttering curses under his breath, he flipped idly through the Gospel's pages.

His finger stopped, and a strange groan escaped his throat—surprise arriving before anything else.

"I—I can read it?"

The characters inscribed on the Gospel's white pages were, to Subaru's own eyes, perfectly legible.

The handwriting was messy, almost like a child's scrawl, but there, properly arranged, were words that carried actual meaning. And what's more, in the form of the I-script, so that even Subaru could read them.

"What is this… don't tell me the book's recognized me as its owner? But I haven't done anything special…"

Haven't done anything, he thought—and then it struck him, something did come to mind.

Previously, the time when Subaru had been unable to read the contents of this Gospel was before he'd set foot in Sanctuary. It had been in the royal capital, and at Roswaal Manor after returning from the royal capital. Since then, he'd had no opportunity to open the Gospel, so he couldn't confirm anything past that point, but he couldn't believe there was no connection between this and what had happened in Sanctuary.

Or rather—not the events in Sanctuary, but a more direct cause came to mind.

Which was—

"Did that Echidna woman do something to me…?"

Just as he'd been able to undergo the Trial at the tomb, it was highly likely that Echidna was exerting some kind of influence over Subaru's body. He couldn't shake the feeling it had something to do with that tea Echidna served during their so-called tea-party discussions.

Echidna had joked about it being bodily fluid or something, but might it not be something else entirely—

Something that could, in fact, dramatically alter Subaru's body from what it had been before.

"Actually, I don't know if that's the reason, but the change last time was worse than ever before."

Perhaps confessing his Return by Death to Echidna hadn't been the sole cause of that Witch of Envy's rampage of fury after all.

That he had no time, right now, to press her for an answer was, for Subaru, a bitter regret.

"…Still, maybe it's not that ownership passed to me exactly."

Being recognized by a Gospel that had belonged to the Witch Cult was chilling enough on its own, but as Subaru's eyes ran over the recorded contents, he judged that this apparently wasn't a message addressed to him—it had simply become possible for him to read what had been conveyed to Petelgeuse all along.

Since no dates were recorded, there was no telling when the events on the first page had taken place. Still, the Gospel's entries continued on for a considerable number of pages from there, and Subaru could easily picture Petelgeuse crossing off each item one by one.

For the most part, the content was to the effect of go here, do this, without specifying how it would concretely tie into results. Petelgeuse had probably rationalized to himself that, regardless of outcome, every act was steering the Witch Cult toward a good direction.

Skimming along like that, he reached the final recorded section.

Pages still remained beyond it, but from there on it was nothing but blank paper. On the very last page, the word "END" that Subaru himself had once scrawled in blood, huge, remained there in dark reddish-black.

Just before that, in the Gospel's own original hand, this remained written:

"In the Mathers domain, trial upon the silver-haired half-elf… huh. Doesn't make any sense."

With the content of the Trial unknown, this entry offered no more information than the fact that Petelgeuse had tried to attack Emilia.

All it did was give Subaru certainty that the atrocity he knew Petelgeuse for had been set in motion by this very entry in the Gospel.

"…Ah, sorry, sorry. I'm coming, already."

Impatient, Patrasche pressed her nose against Subaru, who had been leaning back against the carriage. Wryly smiling at the gesture, Subaru returned the Gospel to its original spot in the carriage.

Keeping what he'd just seen in memory, he shifted his awareness to a separate matter—namely, his escape from Sanctuary, and his return to the mansion.

"Gonna try to slip out without causing a fuss in Sanctuary, as much as I can. Quiet, quiet, sneak-mode—I'm counting on you."

"――――ッ！！"

Straddling the back fitted with the recovered saddle, clinging on as he made his plea, Subaru got a magnificent, sky-high whinny from Patrasche, as if to say leave it to me.

Feeling a certain reliability in a partner who understood not a single thing he'd said, Subaru soothed the excited Patrasche and turned her course toward the outside of Sanctuary.

In the eastern sky, the glimmer of the sun—already beginning to show its full form in the distance—lit the tops of the forest trees. If he didn't hurry, the early-rising residents would soon be up and about, raising the difficulty of a nighttime dash.

He urged Patrasche on, letting Subaru feel the force of that initial burst of acceleration as she broke into a run. The Earth Dragon's galloping body was immediately enveloped in the effect of the Divine Protection of Wind Evasion, and the shaking and wind no longer reached Subaru's body.

They shot straight out of Sanctuary and into the forest. Along a path more like a game trail, Patrasche raced without hesitation toward the mansion. From past experience, Subaru already knew that, without him needing to so much as hold the reins, Patrasche would choose what was best for him of her own will.

There was a touch of loneliness in that, but leaving things to Patrasche was the wiser course, ability-wise. Holding the reins only for form's sake, clinging with his whole body to her hard hide, Subaru became one with Patrasche as she tore through the forest. If nothing interfered, they'd likely clear the forest and complete their escape from Sanctuary within less than an hour. But—

"Hold it… hold it right there, damn it!!"

A vertically spinning heel came falling from above, plunging into the ground in their path and blasting the earth apart.

Amid the raging dust and flying wood fragments, Patrasche slammed on the brakes, her turned legs gouging into the ground to kill her speed. With almost divine balance, protecting the central axis of her body so as not to throw Subaru from her back, the now-stopped Patrasche glared straight ahead.

At the same moment, Subaru—who had braced himself against the shock on Patrasche's back—saw the very same thing Patrasche was glaring at.

"You bastard… just what in the hell do you think you're doing, huh, what exactly do you think you're pulling, huh, oi!!"

It was Garfiel, stamping the ground with an expression of unbridled fury.

Wrinkling his nose, radiating irritation and displeasure, Garfiel bared his fangs up at Subaru, who looked down at him from atop Patrasche.

"Don't you go lookin' down on me, get down here. Talk face to face, at eye level. Start there. Or I'll smash you flat, you bastard…!"

"I kind of figured you'd show up to get in my way, Garfiel."

"I never for a second thought you'd pull some half-assed stunt like this, though! Tail between your legs, runnin' outta here now, is that it? Don't screw with me! Not you! Not this Sanctuary! Not the half-elf, not Roswaal! All of us! Every last one! We're all in this together, ain't we?! Till the Trial's over, there ain't no way in hell I'm lettin' you out of here…"

"That's just your excuse, isn't it?"

"――――"

Garfiel, his expression twisted with rage, changed it at Subaru's short question.

His eyes, sharpened to a point, cooled from their furious heat, and only the sound of his grinding fangs punctuated his quiet, controlled breathing.

"Garfiel, you keep acting like you want to shut us up in this Sanctuary and make us clear the Trial, but… that's not really it, is it?"

"The hell's that supposed to mean, huh?"

"Nothing complicated about it. If you truly, genuinely wished for Sanctuary's liberation, you should be letting my actions slide. The fact that you're not, that you can't—means there's some troublesome agenda tangled up in this. Am I wrong?"

"Hah, don't talk outta your ass. I just don't like some guy reekin' of the Witch, like you, wanderin' around loose, that's all…"

"Do you really smell the Witch on me?"

Once again, at Subaru's relentless question, Garfiel lost his words.

His eyes darted, his lips trembled. This guy's no good at bluffing, Subaru thought, watching his reaction—he nearly felt a wry smile rise up.

"The thing that clinched it was last night, when I came out of the tomb. Honestly, there was no moment I braced myself more for the possibility of you killing me than right then."

"…Ha? What the hell are you talking about?"

"Since you don't get what I'm saying, that's exactly why I'm suspecting your claim about your nose being so sharp was a lie."

Fresh off a Return by Death, and moreover, the cause of that death had been contact with the Witch.

Subaru should have been trailing an intensely dense Witch's Lingering Scent, and yet Garfiel had treated him no differently from before he'd entered the tomb. Then, after they'd parted ways once, Garfiel had called him back as if suddenly remembering something—that was last night's exchange. It was, to put it mildly, far too unnatural.

"I did consider the possibility that you just pretended not to notice, right then, so as not to complicate things… but given how straightforward and hot-headed you are, I decided that wasn't it."

"You've got a hell of a lot of nerve, sayin' whatever the hell you please. My bein' able to smell the Witch off you is a lie? Hah, ridiculous! What the hell's the point of a lie like that, huh? There ain't no point at—"

"There's a point, all right. If you say that and get people's guard up around themselves… then it draws attention away from the one who actually can smell it, the one that matters."

"――――"

The remark that had probably struck at Garfiel's true intent.

The moment he heard it, Garfiel's expression changed in a real, fundamental sense.

From the stance he'd held onto so far—still trying to settle things through talk—into something short-circuited, willing to resort to violence.

Garfiel's arms swelled, growing a size larger. Golden beast-fur began covering his exposed skin, and his already-stooped posture curled further, shifting from a forward lean into something closer to a four-legged crouch.

"I ain't listenin' to your words no more. Seems like you found out somethin' you shouldn't have. Didn't wanna do this, but I can't let you live."

"Don't say that, Garfiel. You'd do better to hear a bit more of what I've got to say. Otherwise, you won't even know where your own scheme leaked from, will you?"

"My scheme…?"

Garfiel raised his voice in a question, his glinting eyes fixed on Subaru.

To wipe away his distrust, Subaru, from atop Patrasche, raised a hand skyward and snapped his fingers, loud and high. And—

"Ah—ah?"

Garfiel's throat made a sound, his eyes doubting the sight before them.

Where his gaze landed—surrounding the Earth Dragon, appearing one after another—were Ryuzu Meyer duplicates, gathered at Subaru's command. Their number: twenty-one.

Recreating with his own hand a scene he had once witnessed, Subaru pointed a finger at Garfiel.

"Seeing this, you understand what position I'm in right now, don't you?"

"Why the hell… how'd you find that place?!"

"I summoned the truth, with regret and pain as sacrifice. And it's still my turn."

Palm turned upward, Subaru looked down from atop the dragon at the shaken Garfiel.

His throat catching at that gaze, Subaru drove the exposed truth home, drunk on the sensation of it.

"Command authority's been transferred to me. Without you noticing, I gave the order that, for one night, you were to carry out Garfiel's orders and listen to him just as before."

"――ah"

"That cover's done now. Listen up, Garfiel. I'm about to leave Sanctuary and head back to the mansion. There's something I need to do. And for that, I can't afford to have you get in my way."

Faintly grasping what order Subaru was about to give, Garfiel's expression crumbled.

The resolve that had, until moments ago, coated his face like solid armor melted away, and the confusion peeking through his features made him look as weak and helpless as a lost child.

His body's beastification halted, the swollen frame shrinking back down to its original small stature.

"Don't come after me, Garfiel. There's plenty I want to ask you too, but that'll have to wait for now. Command authority included, there's too much I need to hear."

"Screw… screw this. You think I'm gonna give up over somethin' like this...!"

"You'll stop. Deep down, at the root of it, you're too soft-hearted for that."

At Subaru's provocative words, Garfiel roared and leapt. Baring his fangs, he closed in, aiming to crush Subaru, dragon and all, beneath him. But a small figure cut into his path.

A duplicate. Garfiel swung his arm up, meaning to fling her aside and clear his path. But just before that descending arm connected—

"――Gar-bou."

"――!?"

At the sound of his pet name, Garfiel's expression transformed utterly, and the arm that should have struck home cut only empty air. Then, his body, adrift midair, was seized from behind by multiple arms reaching out, and he was pinned helplessly to the ground.

The duplicates had reached out to every part of Garfiel's body, restraining him. And standing at their head, looking down at Garfiel with a sorrowful face, was—

"That should serve well enough as a delaying tactic, Su-bou."

"Yeah, you saved me. I figured Garfiel would never in a million years think of this move."

In the battle against the Witch of Envy, Garfiel had ruthlessly used the duplicates as pawns, waging his fight without mercy. But there hadn't been a single sentient Ryuzu Meyer present at that scene. Part of it, no doubt, was that the active Ryuzu at the time had been swallowed by the Witch's shadow early on—but Subaru suspected there was another reason besides.

It was, in a sense, a calculation that deserved to be called crueler than cruelty itself.

"You can't treat Ryuzu-san, someone you treat like family, the same way as the other duplicates. If there's a difference between how you and I use command authority, that's the reason for it."

"Y-you bastaaaaaard!"

"Even setting that aside, there's no way you could destroy a Ryuzu Meyer duplicate with your own hands, right? Just be good and let me go, this once. I won't do anything to make things worse for you."

"How the hell could things get any worse than this?! Screw this, screw thiiiis!"

A howl rang out, but Subaru deliberately ignored what he could hear and slapped Patrasche's back. Sensing Subaru's intent, the Earth Dragon let out a small cry, turning her back on the pinned-down Garfiel and pointing her head toward the forest's exit.

Before setting off, Subaru turned back to Ryuzu.

"Sorry for making you do something so awful."

"You judged it necessary, didn't you. Even hating it, I couldn't refuse—there's no need to spare a thought for me."

"Even so, I'm sorry."

Leaving those words of apology for Ryuzu, whose eyes held sympathy as she looked at Garfiel, Subaru treated it as his farewell and set Patrasche running.

Once more, the unfolding Divine Protection of Wind Evasion left sound and wind behind.

"Wait! Wait, damn it! You bastard, this ain't no joke, oi!!"

The receding voice chased after Subaru.

Shaking it off, accelerating harder, Subaru tore through the forest, out of Sanctuary.

"Let go! You can't let him out there… why, why the hell, why?! Is the old hag on his side more than mine? Huh, why, why the hell...!"

"――――"

"Granny――!!"

A voice of anguish, a voice betrayed by affection, echoed through the forest.

As if leaving all of it behind, Subaru simply drove straight on through the forest, without looking back.

A necessary sacrifice, a necessary sorrow, all of it a cornerstone for the future.

Biting through the edge of his own lip, feeling the blood well up, Subaru resolved himself—for the sake of a grand, happy ending—to write off Garfiel's grief in this present moment as a sacrifice.
