Arc 7 Chapter 72: "A Ridiculous Rumor"

—Hanging a sheet outside the office window had been insurance against the worst-case scenario.

During the meeting in the office between Gustav and the envoys, Arakiya and Todd—conducted almost entirely between Todd and Gustav alone—it had taken Subaru and Hiain, eavesdropping from outside, a great deal of trial and error to get to the heart of why Todd and the others had come to the island.

What they needed was to track the events as they unfolded and the words that were spoken, to find blind spots to hide in, to keep Hiain calm, and to secure an escape route.

They had to deceive that meticulous man Todd, and Arakiya, one of the Nine Divine Generals.

These were opponents who could casually read a person's presence like something out of a manga. To throw them off, Subaru had thrown every trick he could think of into the mix.

Weitz bought them time; Tanza laid down strong-smelling herbs along the path to the office; Idra gathered people up on the high ground, hoping to distract their targets even a little.

In that way, they'd piled up just enough suspicious activity to brush right up against the edge of Todd and Arakiya's vigilance without tripping it.

That fortress, built on ice thin enough to crack, was what had let them scale the enemy's walls.

But—

"Schwartz! What do we do now!?"

"I'm thinking!"

Clinging to Hiain's body, having barely avoided falling to his death, Subaru snarled back.

Being a Lizard Human, Hiain's hands and feet could cling to walls just like an actual lizard's. That said, his body was much larger than a real lizard's, and on top of that he was carrying the extra weight of Subaru.

More accurately, with the help of the hanging sheet, they'd scrambled down without getting hurt.

Lowered from Hiain's arms into the courtyard, Subaru checked the feel of the Curse Tool in his hand.

The black sphere, still slick and wet with blood, had been inside Gustav's body—Subaru had no idea where exactly—but in any case, it was something he'd dragged out from within the man.

"Ngh."

Fighting down the nausea rising in his throat, Subaru desperately worked out his next move.

The instant Todd let go of the Curse Tool, Subaru had moved on pure instinct, thinking now or never. Since he'd actually managed to get his hands on it, it hadn't been the wrong call.

But everything after that had undeniably been done without a plan.

"Anyway, hide in the bushes and camouflage!"

"S-shouldn't we run?"

"If we just run blindly we'll get spotted right away! Hurry!"

Between the two options that came to mind—'run' or 'hide'—Subaru chose to hide, right away.

Having already made an enemy of Todd and the others, either choice put death right next door no matter what. So at the very least, he picked the option whose next step was easier to see.

If they ran, they'd have to keep running without pause.

But if they hid, they could keep thinking while they held their breath.

"—————"

Pulling the arm of a bewildered Hiain, Subaru dove behind a shrub in the corner of the courtyard. There, Hiain took on his camouflage and draped himself over Subaru, hiding them both from view.

Under the weight of Hiain's body pressing down on him, Subaru could feel his heart hammering wildly against his back.

He was terrified, frozen stiff. But he needed him to hold on.

Or else—

"Not here."

With a light thump, a girl cleared in a single leap the height Subaru and the others had barely scrambled down.

The one who landed barefoot in the courtyard, a slender tree branch in hand, was Arakiya. With one eye covered by an eyepatch, she swept a sleepy-eyed glance around the courtyard.

Would Hiain's camouflage really be enough to fool the eyes searching for them?

When no one suspected they were there, they could hide behind the blind spot of another's awareness—but once their presence was known, there was no slipping into a blind spot. Even a slightly careful search was enough to swell the fear of being found.

There was nothing more Subaru could do from here. But they couldn't afford to be found.

Not because of the Curse Tool now in his hands—the thing that could kill or save everyone on the island.

For a far more urgent reason, one tied directly to what it meant to be Natsuki Subaru, he could not be found. He could not die.

"Mm—"

He wanted her to turn her back and go searching in some other, wrong direction entirely.

But Subaru's wish went unanswered; Arakiya gave a small growl, furrowed her brow, and started toward the shrub where they were hiding.

Presence, scent, breathing, the sound of a heartbeat—there was no telling which of these had given them away.

Or maybe it was something Subaru and the others couldn't perceive at all—some kind of aura only the strong could sense. If that was what she was picking up on, it was flatly unfair. Cornering someone with a sense there was no way to counter—that was practically cheating.

Not that "rules" were a concept Arakiya would do anything but scoff at—

"Whoa, hold on, what is this? The Warden's room's been blown to bits!"

"Who's the hot stranger? A guest?"

"What happened? What's going on?"

Just before his head filled up entirely with resentful curses—which was also just before Arakiya reached the shrub—the gruff voices of several men cracked through the air of the courtyard.

Looking over, Subaru saw five sword slaves come tramping into the courtyard—probably men from the Union, drawn by the noise of Arakiya blowing up Gustav's room, coming to gawk.

Their attention was fixed on Gustav's room with its blown-out wall, and on the unfamiliar, beautiful girl standing in the courtyard—Arakiya.

Arakiya, too, turned to face the newcomers, tilting her slender neck in thought.

From her expression, she seemed to be wondering whether these new arrivals were allies of the Subaru and Hiain she was hunting. Please, by all means, let her keep wondering.

Their appearance now was completely outside anything Subaru had anticipated—interlopers summoned purely by chance.

Interrogating them would get Arakiya nothing on Subaru and Hiain's whereabouts. If anything, while she was busy questioning them, Subaru and Hiain might get a chance to slip away.

A thread lowered from heaven, different from the one they'd prepared themselves—these men might be exactly that—

"—Listen!!"

"—!"

Just as Subaru, determined not to let this windfall chance slip by, strained his eyes to watch Arakiya's every move, a voice rang out from directly above them—from the very room with the broken wall.

The voice belonged to Todd, the last person left in that room.

Fear of Todd, mixed with the perfectly good excuse of having to focus entirely on Arakiya in front of him, had let Todd slip from his awareness for even a moment—Subaru, trembling, snapped himself back into focus.

He steeled his cowering heart, readying himself to handle whatever Todd was about to say.

"Warden Gustav Morello has been murdered! The culprits are a black-haired child and a gray Lizard Human, the two of them together! Kill them on sight!!"

"—————"

"I repeat! Warden Gustav Morello has been murdered! General Arakiya will be taking over the Warden's duties! If you don't want to die by the Curse Law, kill the two of them!!"

It wasn't a rough shout, nor an emotional bludgeoning—it was a carrying voice, its emotion deliberately arranged just enough to be heard by a great many people and move them to act.

Even if the listener's understanding lagged behind, it was a voice that lodged itself in the eardrum, impossible to forget what it had said.

And the moment he heard it, Subaru's eyes went wide. "He's got us," flashed through his mind.

Gustav's death had been pinned squarely on Subaru and Hiain. Protesting that it was a false charge would get them nowhere—there was no hoping for a proper investigation or trial on this island.

To begin with, the moment either of them was caught even once, they were finished.

In other words—

"—Now then, let the 'Sparka' begin!!"

The vile custom of the lone island of sword slaves, Ginunhive—the Lifeblood Ritual—was about to begin, with the entire island as its stage.

"Arakiya!"

Having declared the start of the cruel ritual, while those who'd heard it still hadn't managed to move, Todd called out to Arakiya, his voice no longer pitched to carry as it had a moment ago.

When his eyes met hers as she looked up, Todd silently pointed down at the courtyard below. For a moment Subaru's heart lurched—but Todd wasn't pointing at him and Hiain.

He was pointing at the sword slaves, the ones who'd arrived late in the courtyard—that thread from heaven.

After pointing at the sword slaves, Todd brought the same finger up to his own throat.

'Do it.'

Even without hearing a sound, Subaru could tell from the movement of his lips what he'd said.

Drawing a finger across the throat, side to side—it was a cold-blooded signal to cut the other's throat. Arakiya, seeing the same thing Subaru had seen, slowly turned her gaze toward the sword slaves.

Pinned by the stare of Arakiya's uncovered red eye, the sword slaves faltered in confusion.

They still hadn't finished processing the meaning of Todd's words from a moment ago.

"Hey, girlie, you know something about this? This business about Gustav being dead…"

"Sorry. I've been told."

"Huh…? Told what?"

The sword slaves tilted their heads, baffled by the combination of a sudden order and an unfamiliar face. In answer, Arakiya swept her right hand, still holding the branch, in a wide arc to the side.

That was all she did. And it was more than enough.

"Gbugh."

In the next instant, all five of the sword slaves facing Arakiya had their heads burst open from the inside.

To be precise, the effect varied: some heads simply popped, some died with the contents of their faces pushed out through their eyes and noses, and some died with their heads swollen to a grotesque size.

Since the result was the same either way, it probably made no real difference to any of them.

"Told not to talk to people who're about to die."

Their heads destroyed, the sword slaves crumpled lifelessly to the ground.

It was only after a moment that Subaru realized her murmur, spoken while watching the sword slaves fall, had actually been the answer to the earlier question. His mind had been too overloaded to notice sooner.

His head had been too busy processing what the monster called Arakiya had just done.

Probably water.

It was the advice Todd had given Arakiya when Gustav was killed—to make water appear inside a person's head. That, probably, was exactly what she'd just done.

That was why the sword slaves' heads had burst from the inside, blown apart by water that had suddenly appeared within.

"One cup's worth…"

As she said this, Arakiya toyed with the tip of the branch in her hand with her other hand.

Her tone as she repeated the words was nearly devoid of emotion, but to Subaru it somehow sounded like a complaint—the kind of dissatisfaction that came from something not going quite right.

To kill people and feel nothing more than that was simply wrong.

Which was exactly why she was a monster. Arakiya was. And of course, so was Todd.

"Anyone here!? Did you hear that loud voice just now!?"

While Arakiya's inhuman handiwork stained Subaru's heart black, and Hiain froze solid, forgetting even to breathe, yet another voice came into the courtyard.

Another sword slave. Word had spread that the voice had come from the Warden's office, and since everyone understood it was the place people would most likely gather, sword slaves kept climbing up one after another.

And that meant—

"Hey, you, where'd you come from!? The Warden—"

"I've been told. —Not to talk to people who're about to die."

Arakiya turned around—and it meant the ranks of Spirit Eating's victims were about to grow.

And so, it had begun.

—A massacre on the lone island of sword slaves, born of a cause entirely different from the activation of the Curse Law.

△▼△▼△▼△

The situation Todd had engineered was so catastrophic it hardly seemed like something improvised on the spot.

One after another, indiscriminately, Arakiya kept killing sword slaves.

Naturally, not a single person facing off against a Nine Divine General like her could land even a single blow. Simply entering her field of view was disaster enough to mean instant death.

If Subaru remembered right, Arakiya could also command tornadoes and roaring flame, and the fact that she wasn't unleashing anything on that scale, something that would engulf the whole island, was at least the barest sliver of mercy.

Probably Todd had ordered her to hold back, not wanting the bodies rendered unidentifiable.

And Arakiya wasn't the only threat—the island's guards were trouble too.

The guards, it seemed, genuinely believed Todd's claim that Arakiya had taken over Gustav's role after his death. Subaru had no idea how succession to such a post actually worked, but he was fairly sure Gustav would never have wanted the position handed to the very person who'd killed him.

Of course, as far as anyone knew, Subaru and Hiain were the ones who had killed Gustav, so the guards had no reason to question any of it.

Being misunderstood like that by the guards was bad enough, but the bigger problem was—

"Damn it! They actually did it! The guards let the gladiator beasts loose on the island! They're coming! Run, run, run, RUN!!"

All across the panicking island, shouts and screams like that kept rising.

The gladiator beasts that had been let loose—the ones used as the enemy in Sparka—were, as far as Subaru could tell, all Witchbeasts with their horns broken off, made obedient to the guards.

Following the orders of the guards under Todd's command, the gladiator beasts were hunting for Subaru and Hiain somewhere on the island. The catch was, the beasts had no way of telling one sword slave from another.

So the gladiator beasts, too, had begun attacking sword slaves indiscriminately, one after the next.

"Where are you!? Get out here already, Schwartz! You creepy little brat!"

A guard with eyes glinting with hostility bellowed as he stalked through the corridor, a huge two-headed dog padding alongside him.

The guards normally made no effort to communicate with the sword slaves, so this was the first time Subaru had ever seen one of them with emotions so nakedly on display. Realizing that this kind of hostility had been simmering underneath all along left Subaru feeling, of all things, a little sad.

But it wasn't only sadness. There was something he'd noticed, too.

In the way the guards searched for Subaru and the others, there was impatience, irritation, and fear. That was probably not unrelated to the fact that the guards, too, would have died the moment the Curse Law activated.

The guards, too, were scrambling to think their way through this, obeying orders purely to keep themselves alive.

Arakiya and the guards, dangerous enemies both, roamed the island.

Todd, who had them all as pieces on his board—was he moving on his own, or purely directing from the shadows? Not knowing where he was cut deep into Subaru's capacity to think clearly.

Having to stay on guard against Todd without let-up left his taut nerves on the verge of cracking.

And piling on top of all that—

"Hey, where's Schwartz!? You see him!?"

"Ain't seen him! Forget that, the guards are the real problem! If they find us, we're dead!"

"So if we just hand Schwartz over…"

"Don't be stupid! They're going after anyone! They can't tell one of us from another!"

"Then what are we supposed to do!? Just shut up and die!?"

"B-but, if that rumor about Schwartz is true…!"

The voices of sword slaves thrashing with everything they had just to survive.

Exposed to the threat of both Arakiya and the guards, the desperate sword slaves were split over what to do.

Some argued for standing up to the tyranny, others for caving in and handing Subaru over; some had already given up, convinced it would make no difference either way; some simply lamented the sheer unfairness of it all; and some clung to a flimsy, baseless hope with nothing behind it. All kinds of people, all kinds of reactions.

Even among the sword slaves, there were plenty who were already Subaru's enemies, or who could easily become one.

In a way, that made them tougher to deal with than the guards or Arakiya, whom he could at least write off entirely as enemies.

Not knowing who was friend and who was foe meant he couldn't afford to rely carelessly on anyone.

Arakiya. The guards and their gladiator beasts. Sword slaves whose loyalties were unclear. And Todd.

With all of them now hunting Subaru and Hiain, the lone island of sword slaves was hurtling toward ruin.

It had happened. The massacre. No matter how desperately they'd struggled to stop it.

"Old man Null!"

Racing through the island now steeped in catastrophe, Subaru dashed into the room ahead of him.

The infirmary in the island's lower level was old man Null's workplace, the man who'd looked after Subaru more times than he could count. Its entrance had been smashed to pieces, and Subaru rushed in with a sick feeling of dread.

And that dread turned out to be justified.

"Schwartz-sama…"

Waiting for Subaru as he burst into the infirmary was a hoarse girl's voice calling out. It was Tanza, sitting slumped at the back of the room, and beside her small frame lay the corpse of a winged rat.

Probably a Witchbeast Tanza had brought down on her own. Subaru thought that was genuinely impressive.

But before Tanza had arrived, that Witchbeast had already torn through the infirmary and killed someone.

At Tanza's crouched feet lay a body with its throat torn open—the frail, skin-and-bones body of an old man.

"Sc-Schwartz…"

Hiain, who had rushed into the infirmary alongside Subaru, saw the same sight and his voice trembled.

Back in that courtyard, through Arakiya's atrocity, killing every sword slave who happened by until she finally left—the only reason they'd survived hiding there at all was thanks to Hiain.

Without him, there was no way Subaru could have made it back this far.

And yet, no matter how many times they ran and hid, there was simply no winning this.

"Schwartz, you're back!"

"Hmph. So you didn't die either, lizard bastard…"

Idra and Weitz came running up to where Subaru and the others stood, heads bowed before old man Null's body. The whole crew of the Union that had helped sneak into the office was gathered now.

Given the state the island was in, regrouping without losing anyone felt like a miracle.

But shouldn't a real miracle have pulled in a much, much better outcome than this?

"Schwartz-sama, is what's being said true? About Warden Gustav's life?"

"Me and Hiain? There's no way we could've. The one who killed Gustav-san was the envoy from the capital…Arakiya. No—Todd, who used Arakiya to do it."

"Todd…? Who's that…?"

"A guy scarier than an Archbishop of Sin."

At least, that was how Subaru saw it right now.

The Archbishops of Sin he remembered were no good people either, but set against Todd, they almost started to seem endearing by comparison. No, they didn't. Scum was scum no matter what you measured it against.

Still, from the way Subaru said it, Tanza and the others seemed to grasp just how dangerous Todd was.

"Please don't make comparisons like that so lightly. Even knowing it's not literal, hearing 'Archbishop of Sin' is bad for the heart."

"Ah, sorry, it's just what came to mind. Ran into them a lot, you know."

"You ran into Archbishops of Sin often? That would mean, after all…"

"'After all,' what? What do you even know about me?"

"—————"

Partly out of irritation, Subaru's words had come out sounding like an accusation against Tanza.

He regretted it immediately, but Tanza, lowering her eyes, didn't seem bothered by being accused so much as by something in what Subaru had said just before that.

That struck Subaru as a little strange.

Surely no one here could know about Subaru's condition—that every time he underwent Return by Death, the Miasma somehow connected to the Witch grew stronger.

"So, what should we do? If Warden Gustav is dead, then the Curse Law binding us should be gone too. Do we raise the drawbridge and head off the island?"

"D-don't be stupid! We don't have the key for that! Besides, the second we try moving the drawbridge, we'll get picked off!"

"Then what, we keep fighting with the whole island against us…? With a Nine Divine General among our enemies, sooner or later… we have no way out…"

As Idra and the others debated a way to break the deadlock, Subaru's attention was drawn along with them.

Idra's suggestion, escaping via the drawbridge, wasn't a bad idea in itself. The conditions had changed in various ways, but with the Curse Tool now in Subaru's hands, and the Curse Law no longer active, they could in principle leave the island.

The trouble was, as Hiain had said, fleeing across the drawbridge meant risking ambush in two places—the control tower that operated it, and the bridge itself while they were crossing.

Then again, staying on the island and slowly bleeding out, as Weitz had put it, wasn't an option either.

And besides—

"—Gustav-san."

Old man Null, and everyone falling one after another to Arakiya even now across the island—if things stayed as they were, they'd all remain dead in the world Subaru went on living in.

That was something Subaru couldn't bear.

Even if he'd succeeded in recovering the Curse Tool and the threat of the Curse Law was gone, settling for this outcome as "the best he could do" and moving on from it was not what Natsuki Subaru truly wanted.

That wasn't the path the son of Natsuki Kenichi was supposed to choose.

"If we could at least cut down the number of people we're up against…"

Subaru squeezed his eyes shut, caught in a maze of his own thoughts.

Beside him, Tanza murmured, biting her thin lips. It was true—if the situation of having the entire island against them could be eased even a little, a different set of choices might present themselves—

"…H-hey, so, then, maybe it's fine to stop hiding it already?"

"—————"

"Hey, Schwartz, I mean you. I'm talking to you here."

"…Huh?"

Subaru blinked in surprise at Hiain, who had spoken up out of nowhere, sounding almost timid about it.

Partly it was because he'd been lost in thought, but more than that, the remark simply didn't connect to anything he could think of, so his reaction came late.

Stop hiding it, Hiain had told him.

He'd said it, but—

"Stop hiding it… hiding what?"

"C-cut it out already! This isn't a card you get to keep hidden in a situation like this!"

"Hey, knock it off, lizard bastard…!"

Hiain snapped at Subaru with sudden ferocity, though Subaru couldn't imagine what he meant. Weitz stepped in to pull him back, but Subaru was too startled to even manage a thank-you.

Then, with the same seriousness, Idra addressed him too. "Schwartz."

"Whatever you think of how he put it, I understand how Hiain feels. Won't you tell us, at this point?"

"No, seriously, I have no idea what you're all even talking about…"

"—We're talking about your father."

"…My dad?"

Idra said it to him in earnest—unbelievably, deadly earnest.

But no matter how seriously he said it, Subaru's confusion only deepened. He had no idea why his father would come up here of all places.

Subaru's father was, from the eyes of his son, from the eyes of his wife, an admirable man deserving of respect, the very embodiment of what Subaru aspired to be—but—

"But he's just a regular salaryman…?"

"Don't try to cover it up! We already know! You're His Majesty the Emperor's secret child, aren't you!?"

"—————"

Subaru tried to explain that this wasn't information of any use here, but his voice was drowned out by Hiain, still shouting even as Weitz held him back in a bear hug.

Drowned out—and the color of the paint used to drown it out was so thick that Subaru's mind couldn't make sense of the picture it left behind.

What had Hiain just said?

"…Me, His Majesty the Emperor's child?"

"Yeah, that's right! Outside the island, it's all anyone's talking about! Black hair, black eyes—His Majesty Vincent Vollachia's illegitimate child, hiding out somewhere in the country! And that's…"

"—Schwartz. That's you, isn't it."

Picking up where Hiain left off, Idra said it to Subaru, his voice and his eyes both quiet.

Faced with Hiain's pleading eyes and Idra's utterly convinced ones—neither of them looking like they were cracking some unfunny joke in a moment like this—Subaru turned to look at Weitz.

Weitz twisted his tattoo-covered face and said,

"I told you before…I know exactly who you are…. But whatever the truth is, I'll lend you my strength regardless…"

It was a solemn line, one that, in any other situation, would have struck Subaru far harder.

Only now did Subaru fully grasp the weight of the resolve behind those words, spoken back when Weitz had first said them to him down in the lower level.

Along with the fact that they'd been rooted in a conclusion that was completely, utterly wrong.

"W-wait, hold on…why would I be the Emperor's child…"

"That judgment, that command, that sheer will to survive—none of it fits someone your age. In the Sparka, it was you who saved us, and Hiain's people too, Schwartz."

"That's…"

It was true that Subaru had fought against fate using every ability he had.

But he'd never once imagined it would lead Idra and the others to such an outrageous misunderstanding.

And if they'd already been laboring under that misconception all along, then—

"Tanza, why aren't you saying that's wrong…"

"—Schwartz-sama. I saw it with my own eyes."

"—Ah."

"I saw you, Schwartz-sama, together with His Majesty the Emperor."

Tanza said it quietly, one hand pressed to the hem of her kimono, and Subaru was left speechless.

She was talking about what had happened in Chaosflame. It was true that Subaru, shrunk down in size, had been together with Abel while he wore that ridiculous mask.

Since Tanza was Yorna's attendant, it wasn't strange at all that she'd know the truth.

She had seen Abel, the real Emperor, together with a shrunken Subaru.

Had she connected that with Idra and the others' misunderstanding?

"But Tanza, you were working together with Olbart-san…no, wait."

Subaru's guess was that Tanza had been conspiring with Olbart, moving behind the scenes on various things.

It had probably all been to protect Yorna, so he didn't hold much of it against her. And Olbart wasn't the type to show his hand even to an accomplice.

Which meant Tanza had no idea that Subaru was really a shrunken "Natsumi Schwartz."

Which meant—

"Tanza, what do you think I am…?"

"—…The child born between that black-haired woman and His Majesty the Emperor."

"—————"

Subaru's words failed him.

Tanza said nothing more, but he understood exactly what she'd concluded. Understanding it was precisely why his words had failed—and he understood, too, just how hard this would be to explain away.

He had no proof to offer. Short of suddenly growing back to full size right here and now, there was nothing he could do.

"…Why is this whole 'secret child of the Emperor' thing even a topic of conversation?"

"—…Apparently a rebel army has risen against His Majesty the Emperor outside the island right now. And the one being put forward as the rebellion's figurehead is…"

"The Emperor's secret child?"

Idra nodded at Subaru's question.

Even in Subaru's own head, halting as it was, the logic began to connect. A power struggle between the Emperor and his child. That was apparently how the war Abel and the others had started outside the island was being framed.

The truth was a war between the real Emperor and a false one.

But what the public knew was a war between the real Emperor and a rebel army led by the Emperor's own son.

Subaru could easily guess who had arranged things into that shape, and how.

"That bastard…!"

He understood at once that this was a situation Abel had engineered, a rumor Abel himself had spread.

And that it wasn't just Abel being spiteful—it likely served as a legitimizing banner for this entire civil war, and doubled as a way to search for Subaru after he'd vanished from Chaosflame.

It probably meant Abel was operating on the assumption that Subaru wasn't dead. Though he'd sooner bite his own tongue off than admit he "believed" Subaru was alive.

"—————"

Tanza and the others watched Subaru, one hand pressed to his mouth, fall silent.

Weitz let go of Hiain, who had finally settled down, and all four of them turned their attention on Subaru.

Even as they stood there, Subaru could tell that outside the infirmary, all across the island, fighting was breaking out, and sword slaves were falling into danger or losing their lives outright.

There was no room anywhere for staying quiet, staying calm, and taking his time to think things through.

Which meant—

"—Schwartz-sama."

Tanza called out to him, stating the obvious—that there was no time—if only to make sure he knew it.

Feeling just the faintest trace of reverence woven into the way she said it, Subaru looked around at all the faces of the Union.

He looked around, and he decided. —Whatever was being asked of him in this situation, he would deliver it.

△▼△▼△▼△

Sensing a shift in the wind, Todd Fang quietly stroked his own chin.

"—Bad feeling about this."

He'd blown the head clean off an uncooperative Warden in the office, swapping out the island's "head" in the most literal sense possible, and yet everything since had somehow felt sluggish.

Granted, if the goal were simply to wipe out everyone connected to the island, Arakiya alone was overkill.

That Nine Divine General, who had no head for thinking things through herself, possessed overwhelming power in stark contrast to how empty-headed she otherwise was—if she felt like it, she could wrap the entire island in flame.

Since that would be a problem for Todd, he'd told her not to.

To begin with—

"And here I never even wanted to come to a place like this."

Every action he'd resolved himself to take out of pure necessity had backfired one after another, and it left Todd in a sour mood.

Rescuing Arakiya when she'd been captured in the walled city had been, in his own mind, both a way to redeem the failure of letting the city fall and a means of latching onto her to get back to the capital and secure a transfer.

In fact, the transfer itself had gone through—just not at all in the way he'd hoped.

Todd had been installed as the sole subordinate of General Arakiya, who otherwise commanded no one.

And it was all because of a single lie he'd told to manipulate her.

"Jamal. A pain in the ass even after he's dead."

It was a situation his own lie had invited, but Arakiya believed without a shred of doubt that Todd genuinely wanted revenge for Jamal from the bottom of his heart.

Since it was a lie he'd needed to win her over in the first place, there was no way Todd could deny it now.

Honestly, she was a woman whose trigger points were impossible to predict. Given the real possibility of being reduced to a pile of ash the instant he touched a nerve, he couldn't carelessly walk anything back either.

The upshot was that Todd had ended up as Arakiya's subordinate.

Now, as the tool of Berstetz Fondalfon, the Imperial Prime Minister who gave Arakiya her orders, he'd ended up dispatched here, of all places, to this paradise for the deranged—the lone island of sword slaves.

Berstetz's order was to deal with an internal problem on the island. The means to do it were supposedly in the hands of Warden Gustav, so it was framed as an easy job—just give the order and let it happen.

That, too, had turned out to be a complete lie.

An "easy job" had never once actually gone easy in Todd's experience. He'd expected as much. Which was why he'd prepared for it.

Most things could be handled with enough preparation beforehand. Only genuinely unlucky accidents were truly unavoidable, and even those could be minimized by avoiding the situations where they were likely to occur.

And yet, an accident had happened.

In that exact moment, an invisible child leaping out from a blind spot—the kind of accident you get caught up in through no fault of your own.

"If it were killing someone stronger than me, I've got plenty of methods worked out already…"

He'd hardly ever bothered thinking through how to kill a child.

It wasn't a pleasant thought, and the occasions to use it weren't likely to come up often. So, working from a fairly short list of options, he'd combined it with a method suited specifically to a place like the sword slave island.

Drag in the guards, the gladiator beasts, and even half the sword slaves, and any single person on the island could be picked off with precision.

Mix Arakiya into that as well, and it would be stranger for the targets to survive than to die.

Although—

"—There's no such thing as a flawless plan."

Todd muttered this, tilting his head, as he walked down a corridor littered with corpses.

Every single body lying there belonged to some shabbily dressed sword slave of the island. Whether they'd died fighting each other, at Arakiya's hands, or torn apart by a gladiator beast held no interest for him.

A corpse was a corpse. Once dead, they could be struck from his mind entirely.

"Corporal Fang, was it really necessary to go this far…"

"Of course it was necessary. Warden Gustav was killed. The sword slaves had been organizing, preparing for this. Their plan was an armed uprising to flip the whole island. I don't know who's been drawn into it, but tell me—can you actually tell the enemy apart from everyone else?"

"Ngh…"

At Todd's question, one of the two guards trailing behind him fell silent.

They were two of the guards who'd come running the moment Todd sent Arakiya out from the office and declared the Sparka. He'd kept them in line by dangling the Curse Law's grip over their heads, but given they had a perfectly serviceable excuse to fall in line without complaint, their odd stubbornness struck him as unnecessary.

It was Todd giving the orders, after all, so they could just pin all the blame on him.

They were going to do what needed doing anyway, guilty face or not, so their hesitation accomplished nothing at all.

"Quit playing the decent man. In the first place, the fact that you two got stuck as guards here means both you and your buddy have got something shady in your past. Strip off the uniform and there's barely any difference between you and them."

"—!"

"That uniform's your safety valve, though. You'd do well to really appreciate that."

Todd clapped the shoulder of the young guard, whose bitter expression hadn't faded, and gave him a grin, sighing inwardly all the while. —Wearing the uniform or not, that really was the entire difference, and they ought to be grateful for it.

Every guard on the sword slave island was an Imperial soldier with a record that could just as easily have landed him as a sword slave himself.

It was a penal colony for the half-baked failures who'd disobeyed orders or brawled within the ranks, unable to abide by the army's iron code.

Slow learners who'd only finally figured out obedience once their lives were bound by the Curse Law. Between that and their half-hearted attempts at playing decent men, it really was almost comical.

"Well, not that it matters much either way."

What Todd needed wasn't their competence as guards, but the gladiator beasts assigned to them.

If he could just cut straight to the beasts and skip the guards entirely, he'd have been happy to dispose of the lot of them at once—but presumably the guards were kept in the loop precisely to prevent that from happening.

That, too, was probably a safety valve the now-dead Gustav had set up—or if not his doing, then a decision from the Emperor whom Gustav had so stubbornly obeyed.

"Both the people itching to start a war and the ones willing to answer the call are out of their minds. Please, just don't go disturbing the peace Katya and I have going…"

The first stirrings of rebellion in the walled city, and everything that followed in the demon city of Chaosflame—to Todd, all of it was nothing but tiresome noise.

If life got more livable, fine, let whoever sat at the top of the Empire change. If it wouldn't, then things could just as well stay as they were.

So long as nothing happened, and his days with the woman he loved simply went on—that was all he wanted.

"—Now there's something unpleasant I just remembered."

Thinking of the rebellion had summoned a terrifying presence into the back of Todd's mind.

A "child of war," you might call her—someone who loved war and was loved by war in turn. Ideally she'd have died somewhere along the course of some brutal battle already, but there was no telling how things had actually gone.

Given her position, if she'd sided with the rebel army, then once its calamity spread far enough, she would show up.

And if she went on a rampage, then when that time came, he'd take Katya and get as far away as possible—

"—Todd Fang!!"

"—————"

At the sound of his own name called out in a high voice, Todd felt, for some reason, an unsettled stirring in his chest.

As if the very worry he'd just been turning over in his head had seized the chance to make itself real.

And then—

"…What are you thinking, kid."

At the end of the corridor Todd turned to face, standing at the far wall, was the boy who was supposed to have made an enemy of everyone on this island and be on the run for his life.

A black-haired kid with a nasty look in his eye. He was smeared with blood and mud all over, but all his limbs were still attached, and he didn't look like he'd lost anything.

Which meant that Arakiya and every single guard alike had somehow all let him slip past.

"I can tell there's something going on here that 'lucky' doesn't even begin to cover. But what'd you come all the way here to show your face for? Doesn't add up."

"—————"

"And it doesn't look like you're invincible just because you've got backup you can count on, either."

As he spoke, Todd took in the four people who'd come along with the boy.

A gray Lizard Human. A man covered head to toe in tattoos. Next to those two, someone of unremarkable medium build and height. And, for whatever reason, a young Deer Human girl in a kimono.

Even to Todd's eye, none of the four looked like they wielded any particularly exceptional power. All amateurs, judging by their bearing—only the tattooed man's eyes and the way the deer girl carried herself warranted any real caution.

The tattooed one showed no hesitation, and the deer girl carried a strange kind of confidence.

And then—

"Hard to read the look in your eyes, kid. Whether you're confident or not…"

"Todd. Surrender. And make that woman—Arakiya—stop, too."

"—Well, for starters, mind telling me why?"

Faced with a demand so unreasonable it wasn't even worth listening to, Todd's brow furrowed.

For now, he played along, feigning interest and prompting the boy to continue, while silently signaling the two guards flanking him with a hand gesture.

The two guards each had a gladiator beast with them—a great black eagle, and a frog like a chunk of solid rock.

Right beside Todd and the others as they faced off against the boy's group, out past the corridor, the eagle cut through the sky and the frog crawled along the wall, both closing the distance inch by inch.

If the gladiator beasts could finish them off and recover the Curse Tool, that would be the best outcome.

Of course, there was a chance the Curse Tool had been hidden somewhere else, which would leave some hassle behind. Still, taking care of the boy and the Lizard Human here would at least settle things quickly for now.

If Arakiya were here, this would be even simpler—

"Where's she gotten herself stuck…?"

Surely, of all people, Arakiya wasn't just enjoying the sword slave hunt too much to bother coming.

Some soldiers took pleasure in killing, but Arakiya wasn't that type. She was a soldier whose entire purpose was devotion to someone precious to her—nothing more, and nothing less.

If she was failing at the one job that mattered, then something had to be getting in her way—that was the only reasonable conclusion.

But if something like that really existed, the whole picture would change.

"So, what's this story you're about to tell me?"

"Simple story. —The Curse Tool that controls this island's Curse Law is in my hands."

"Wha—!?"

At the boy's answer to Todd's shrug, the two guards flinched.

Understandable enough. The single biggest reason they obeyed Todd at all was the inescapable fact that their lives were held hostage.

But Todd dismissed it flatly. "Bluff."

"A pointless bit of leverage. And besides, a Curse Tool? If a thing like that actually existed…"

"—Here."

"Well, well."

Without even having to be asked, the boy pulled something out from inside his clothes. A black sphere, wiped clean of blood, unmistakable even from a distance.

He'd only held it briefly, but there was no mistaking it.

That was the Curse Tool Gustav had kept implanted inside his own body. The genuine article.

If it were Todd, he'd have hidden it somewhere on the island without fail, keeping even its location as leverage to negotiate with.

So what exactly was the boy's goal, bringing the actual thing with him instead of doing that?

"So what if it's some filthy ball? Don't tell me you think you can actually activate the Curse Law with it. If you really can, go on, try it. Thin out a few of these irritating guards for me."

"Wha—!? S-stop, don't! If you do that, we'll—"

"Told you. It's a bluff. Or don't you trust the word of an envoy from the capital?"

Todd pressed the point further at the flustered guard, who went pale and fell silent.

That reaction caught Todd's attention, just slightly. He could understand being unnerved and doubtful when shown a black sphere claimed to be a Curse Tool—but something about the reaction was off.

It was as though the guards had some reason, some grounds, that kept them from simply writing off the children's—no, the child's—claim as an empty bluff.

"What is it? Something about that kid—"

"—Compose yourself and listen well, you insolent man."

"—————"

Todd made to press the guard for an answer to the thing nagging at him—but before he could, a high, clear voice rang through the corridor, and the Deer Human girl drew everyone's attention.

She wound the sleeve of her kimono round and round her arm, then quietly gestured toward the boy at her side.

"If you, too, have come from beyond the island, then surely it has already reached your ears—the countless rumors circulating amid the great upheaval currently shaking this entire Empire."

"—————"

At the girl's appeal, dignified enough to feel almost valiant, Todd closed one eye.

However she'd managed to secure a source of information from inside the island, the girl was right about one thing—the wider world was gossiping freely about all manner of things.

As for the rebel army, there were already all sorts of stories going around, including one about a Nine Divine General siding with them.

Some of them were thoroughly ridiculous, of course—

"Hey, don't tell me—"

"—The one standing before you is the very core of the crisis now shaking the Empire. A noble personage of rightful and legitimate standing, who has risen to correct the Empire's wayward course."

The girl paused there, just a beat, and looked straight at Todd.

In the depths of her round eyes, Todd glimpsed an intent that swelled the misgivings already forming in his mind—

"—Natsuki Schwartz-sama, beloved son of His Majesty the Emperor Vincent Vollachia."

An illegitimate child of the Emperor, holding a Curse Tool in hand.

The Deer Human girl had recited such a ridiculous story with all the composure in the world, as though this were the kind of scene she'd found herself in countless times before—and that bearing carried a persuasive force, one that made even so absurd a claim feel as if it might, after all, be true.

It was true—among the absurd rumors, there had been just such a story.

That the figurehead of the rebel army was the black-haired, black-eyed illegitimate child of the Emperor—that particular rumor. Naturally, any Imperial soldier with a shred of good sense would laugh it off; taking it seriously was, on the face of it, absurd.

Even so, Todd had privately thought that if such a person really existed, it would be a real headache.

But the idea that such a person would just happen to be here, on this very island, was too absurd even to entertain.

And yet—

"—!"

Todd didn't miss the way the guards sucked in a breath, their faces changing color.

They had probably already known something close to what Todd had just been told. They'd heard it somewhere, somehow, and let the seed of suspicion grow quietly in their chests.

That was why, when the boy—the child called Natsuki Schwartz—declared he was holding the Curse Tool, they hadn't been able to simply laugh it off as a bluff.

They'd been possessed by a fantasy that, to them, might have carried more credibility than the position of Todd himself—envoy from the capital, subordinate to Arakiya.

And so—

"Now then, guards, if you would swiftly—"

The girl made to press on, about to say something further.

But before her demand could even be voiced, Todd's hand swept back to his waist, seized the handle there, and swung it sideways with all his strength.

"—!"

The arcing blade of his axe tore into the two guards standing beside him. The guard on the right had his head sent flying; the one on the left had his skull split open and his body slammed into the wall.

Both died instantly—cutting off, in that same motion of their confusion, any chance they'd side with the enemy.

And then—

"—Now!!"

Lunging at the severed head of the guard, Todd booted it with all his strength straight ahead of him. Scattering blood as it flew, the head hurtled toward the wide-eyed children.

Aimed straight at the group, who had no idea what purpose any of this could possibly serve—

"————!!"

In the next instant, the massive body of the black eagle came slamming in sideways through the corridor, swallowing the flying guard's head—and the children gathered around it—into utter devastation.
