Arc 5 Chapter 31: "The Price of a Mistake"

"Crusty-crusty!"

"Shut it, quit sayin' that already!"

The next morning, Garfiel walked through town with Mimi, wearing an awkward, embarrassed expression.

Giggling, Mimi tugged at the front of her robe, fidgeting with the spot where it had dried stiff with Garfiel's tears, snot, drool, and general grime.

The stench reached Garfiel's nose too, walking beside her, sharp enough to sting.

For Mimi, whose sense of smell was just as keen, the stink up close should have been unbearable, but when Garfiel gently suggested she wash it off, she said,

"Hmm, isn't it fine? I'll go back to the inn and change. Since I didn't come back yesterday, Miss's definitely gonna yell at me! And Hetaro and Tivey too!"

"...Sorry 'bout that."

"I don't mind though? Mimi was just petting Garf, and Garf was just sleepy-sleeping."

Mimi answered with an innocent, carefree smile to Garfiel's quiet apology.

Last night had brought both a reunion with his mother and, in the truest sense, a farewell.

Overwhelmed by it all, Garfiel had wailed shamelessly, held in Mimi's arms on an open rooftop.

Worse still, pathetically, he'd cried himself to sleep right there, and by the time he woke with a shout loud enough to carry across the whole city, it was already morning — and he was on Mimi's lap.

Garfiel tried his best to act composed, but against Mimi's ever-unshaken normalcy, his fluster only showed all the more plainly.

Before he could even manage an excuse, the owner of the building where they'd spent the night found them on the roof and gave them a thorough scolding — hence this walk back.

"Feel a little better now?"

"Yeah, a little. I mean, what's it... y'know. That."

"That, huh. I see, I see."

"...Yeah. That."

Having missed his chance to say thank you, Garfiel scowled sulkily. Mimi's attitude, seemingly unbothered by any of it, left Garfiel at a loss for what to say.

He recalled how, last night, Mimi had blurted out that she was in love with him.

Without meaning to, he found his eyes following her.

He'd thought she was oddly clingy, but he'd never imagined she actually harbored romantic feelings for him. He'd assumed it was more like the affection you'd have for a pet liger, but apparently that wasn't it.

And despite having said something like that, she'd been completely her usual self from this morning right up to now.

It was the pathetic part of Garfiel that, despite having been so forward about his own feelings toward Ram, showed a glimpse of weakness the moment he found himself on the receiving end instead.

"So, what're we doing this morning? Goin' to see your mom?"

Lost in thought as he was, Garfiel was startled to find Mimi had stopped in front of him, looking up at him — and startled again by what she'd said.

"H-hold on. Why the hell would I need to go see her again?"

"Hmm? I mean, Reala's Garf's mom, right? So shouldn't you talk to her properly?"

"Seriously, did this girl not get it at all?"

Apart from instinctively hitting on the most important point, Mimi seemed to have missed all the nuance. Garfiel considered whether he should explain his own difficult position, and that family's, to her, but quickly gave up on it as pointless. Besides, this was a matter of Garfiel's own heart.

"It's fine. That person... it's better she never knows I'm her son."

"That so?"

"Yeah, that's how it is. ...Ah, though I gotta think about whether to tell sis, at least."

Frederica was a sensible woman.

No doubt, even while wavering, she'd arrive at the same conclusion Garfiel had, only faster.

Still, even if the conclusion were the same, his sister had the right to know their mother was alive. Whether he should burden her with the same pain he now carried was another matter to agonize over.

"Guess I'll just have to take my time decidin'."

"I see. Sounds hard. Mimi doesn't know her mom, so she kinda thinks it'd be nice if you just got along."

"...You don't know your mom?"

At Mimi's unexpected words, Garfiel's ears trembled as he asked back.

Mimi nodded, unburdened, with a "Yup."

"Mimi and Hetaro and Tivey, none of us know our dad or mom. See, we're triplets, right? Raising us looked like it'd be super hard, so we got abandoned. Then the Captain picked us up. So the Captain is family! And Miss is family too!"

"...I see. Heh, quite the big family, huh."

On impulse, Garfiel patted Mimi's head lightly, without much thought.

Instantly—

"—Hyah!"

Mimi swatted his hand away and jumped back.

Seeing her face turn bright red, Garfiel's eyes went wide. Mimi immediately turned her back to him, muttering "uu-uu" while scrubbing at her own face,

"Something's weird, since yesterday. When Garf gets too close, I get weird."

"T-that so. Well, my bad. ...Want me to walk further off?"

"That's kinda no. So, like, not far but not too close is good."

With quick little steps, Mimi came closer and fell in step beside him, just far enough that their hands couldn't touch.

When Garfiel knit his brow in confusion, Mimi grinned her usual loose grin. Though her face still seemed a little red.

"Oh, that reminds me, soirie! I've got soirie! Let's eat soirie!"

"O-oh."

As if suddenly changing the subject, Mimi rummaged in her robe and pulled out a small bag of sweets.

It was what Reala had given Mimi last night, as they'd parted ways.

For a moment, a numbing pain flickered across his chest, but as the wrapped sweet was set on his outstretched palm, he gave a small sniff at its fragrant smell.

Soirie was a baked treat made from bread dough kneaded with sweet seasoning, the kind of light snack filled with cream or sweet bean paste.

There seemed to be two plump, generously sized soirie in the bag. Garfiel and Mimi each took one and bit in, making it their substitute for breakfast.

"Mm! Sweet! Tasty! Yum, yum!"

"Yeah, it's good."

As much as Mimi sang its praises, Garfiel too savored the flavor.

Sweet, but not too sweet, the dough baked soft — a real delicacy. It would surely have been even better fresh from the oven. Maybe this, too, was something his mother made well.

If so, then perhaps, in another life, he might have had countless chances to enjoy it himself.

"Garf?"

"Ain't gonna go soft on you, not me. It's nothin'. Let's go."

Answering Mimi as she peered up at him, Garfiel started walking as if to shake something off.

Turning his back on the place where his mother lived, he headed toward where his companions ought to be waiting. Like Mimi, Garfiel too had stayed out all night without leave.

First, he figured, he'd have to take a proper scolding for that—

"—Um, so? Is this workin' okay? Can you all hear me proper? It'd help me out a whole lot if the trash-meat who can hear this screamed, and the trash-meat who can't just went and got crushed to death, y'know. Kyahahaha!"

"Ahh?"

"Whazzat?"

The instant he took a step, the voice abruptly struck Garfiel's and Mimi's eardrums.

The two exchanged a glance, then looked up at the sky at the same moment.

Because to both of them, the voice had sounded as though it were falling straight down from the sky.

"What the hell was that idiot voice..."

"So, so, so, is there any idiot who up and died just from that? If not, well, that's fine too, but ignorin' little old me's words takes some real nerve, don'tcha think, so right from the start of talkin' I'm already good an' offended!"

Ignoring even Garfiel's musing, the voice kept right on blaring.

Looking around, he saw that the other people walking through the morning streets wore startled faces too, all of them looking up at the sky in blank astonishment, just like Garfiel and Mimi.

It felt like a tremendous, booming voice — but that wasn't quite right.

With hearing far beyond an ordinary person's, Garfiel could tell the voice wasn't a loud shout coming from any single point, but something echoing throughout the whole town at once.

But understanding that told him nothing else. All he understood was—

"Just breathin' in and out and already offendin' little old me — you creatures really are worthless, trash trash trashy trash, aren'tcha. If all you're gonna do is breathe your stinkin' breath, think filthy thoughts, and drool your way through life, you'd be way better off shovin' your heads in the gutter right now and turnin' into nice bubbly drowned corpses! Or better yet, just go ahead and die, seriously! It's my one little wish! Kyahahahaha!"

—Whoever owned this voice was, purely and simply, the possessor of a wicked, hideous soul.

"The hell is this bastard. Don't screw around..."

"Garf... this is, like, super gross."

Mimi murmured that, anxious, to Garfiel as irritation edged his voice. At her manner, Garfiel traced the scar on his brow and clacked his fangs together.

It wasn't a face that suited her. He didn't want her wearing a face like that.

"Now then, now then, that's little old me for ya — but even you dull-witted trash-meat oughta be catchin' on 'bout now, right? The meanin', meanin', meanin' of why it's ME broadcastin' this!"

"Meaning of... broadcastin'?"

"Meanin', I've — no, no, WE'VE got a hold on this city's very core, that's what it means, right? Oh, and just so ya know, it ain't only here — those control towers out on the edges of the city, was it? Got a hold of those too."

"Control towers... the ones Otto bro was talkin' about!?"

Setting aside the speaker's true intentions, Garfiel felt a jolt of threat catch in his throat.

Around the time they'd arrived in the city, Otto had explained Priestella's structure. As a water-gate city crisscrossed with waterways, Priestella had once served as a trap designed to bring down enemies of overwhelming power — and that function still lived on today.

And it was the control towers at the city's four cardinal points that governed the operation of those waterways — meaning if they'd been seized, then this voice with its rotten personality had taken the entire city hostage.

Others around him arrived at the same conclusion Garfiel had, and shock and panic spread through the crowd.

Amid everyone crying out, thrown into confusion over what was happening, the booming voice let out a wicked, delighted cackle.

"Kyahahahaha! Only NOW! Only at THIS point! Only just now realizin' your lives are like a candle 'bout to gutter out — your carefree stupidity's got me so speechless I don't even know what to say! Ahh, trash trash trash trash trashy trash! Kyahahahaha!"

"――――"

"Oop, my baaad. Guess I oughta introduce myself 'bout now, or types like you'll start runnin' off into denial, huh? So, bein' the clear, plain, kind, and merciful gal that I am, I'll go ahead and spell out reality nice an' easy for ya!"

As the city teetered on the edge of panic, Garfiel took hold of Mimi's hand.

And he listened seriously for what the voice would say next.

"I'm a Witch Cult Sin Archbishop, in charge of 'Lust'—"

"Witch Cult...!"

"—Lady Capella Emerada Lugunica, that's me! Kyahahahaha! Revere me, worship me, weep and beg and die in miserable rottenness! Trash-meat, all of ya! Kyahahaha!"

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

The moment the broadcast ended, Garfiel found himself forced to choose.

Panic spread at the broadcast declaring itself the Witch Cult, but even so, the citizens of Priestella could be said to have acted in an orderly fashion. Though wracked with confusion, they'd clearly absorbed the city council's regular instructions, and were quickly organized to flee to their nearest shelters.

Nearby people even offered to guide Garfiel and Mimi to a shelter. But the two of them firmly declined the offer and resolved to act at once.

Even here, though, another choice assailed Garfiel.

And the result of that choice—

"Oh, Gorgeous-san!"

"—!"

At the shelter they'd made their way to, Garfiel found Reala hurrying toward him.

Her lips relaxing into a smile at the sight of a familiar face, Reala approached, and Garfiel somehow managed to face her while enduring the ache in his chest.

—This was the result of his choice.

The moment Garfiel had refused the offer to be guided to the nearest shelter, he'd been forced into a decision.

Between returning to where he belonged — to Emilia, Subaru, and the others — or confirming the safety of the mother he'd resolved, just last night, to leave behind.

Rationally, Garfiel knew he ought to return to Emilia and the others.

And yet his feet had carried him here instead, because from where he'd heard the broadcast, this was the closer distance, and he could tell himself he'd check first and then return—that was just an excuse.

"I'm glad you're safe too, Mimi-san. That broadcast made me so anxious."

"Mm, I'm fiiine! Oh, the soirie was yummy, thanks for the food!"

"Oh, no, not at all. I'm glad it suited your taste."

And Mimi had followed along with Garfiel's choice without a word of objection.

Really, Mimi too must have wanted to return to Anastasia and her brothers. To her, Reala and her family were true strangers — she had no reason at all to come back here.

"――――"

Listening to his own voice condemning himself, Garfiel at least reached a stopping point in having confirmed Reala's safety. Now he had to hurry back to Emilia and the others, join in planning what to do next, and put his strength to use.

If the Witch Cult's name had come up, then he had to fight to protect Emilia. That was the promise between men Garfiel had made with Subaru — one he could never break.

He, who had failed to keep hold of being a tiger, could not afford to forget being a man too.

"Glad you're safe. Then, we oughta be..."

"Yes... I'm fine. Um, Gorgeous-san."

As Garfiel made to say his farewells and leave the shelter, Reala hesitated, seeming reluctant to speak. When Garfiel frowned at her uncharacteristic manner, she asked, timidly,

"Have you... seen my children, by any chance? They went out to play this morning... and they're not here at this shelter."

"...!?"

Garfiel's head snapped up, and he scanned the surroundings. But the figures he sought didn't appear anywhere in his sight. The siblings weren't anywhere in this place.

"And my husband too... no, never mind. That was uncalled for."

"W-what. Ain't gonna help me none if you go hidin' stuff now. Spit it out."

"—That broadcast, it came from the city hall. My husband's at work there today too, at the city hall, so... I keep thinking something might have happened to him."

"――――"

City hall was the tall building at the very center of Priestella.

It was the place said to govern the city's central functions, and sure enough, in that broadcast, the Sin Archbishop had declared she was there.

Garek was supposed to be right there.

"Ha... ha, ha..."

His heart pounded like an alarm bell, and Garfiel nearly staggered under the weight of it all.

The siblings nowhere to be found, and Garek supposedly in what was now a danger zone. In exchange for confirming Reala's safety, he'd learned of the family's crisis.

"Boss, Emilia-sama..."

Subaru and Emilia flashed through Garfiel's mind. Beatrice and Otto flashed through it too.

The people he needed to hurry back to, to lend his strength to, flashed through his mind.

But close on their heels, the images of the siblings and Garek flashed through his mind too.

"I'm sorry. I've put you in a difficult spot. Gorgeous-san, please forget I said anything."

"...Ah."

"That was very unfair of me just now. It's fine. Both the children and my husband know this city's rules perfectly well."

Reala smiled bravely, but her two hands, clasped together as if in prayer, trembled in small tremors.

It was plainly clear she was forcing herself, desperately putting on an act so as not to worry Garfiel.

"――――"

Silence, silence, silence.

Sealing his words inside, gritting his teeth, Garfiel thought.

Mimi said nothing to Garfiel in that state, silently waiting for him to speak.

Just holding Garfiel's hand, saying nothing at all.

"...Don't worry. I'll go bring back your kids and your husband both, safe."

"—! Gorgeous-san!?"

At the unexpected answer, Reala's voice caught in surprise.

Giving her a firm nod, Garfiel looked down at Mimi, still holding his hand.

"From here on, this is just me bein' selfish. You go back—"

"Hyah."

"Ow!"

Just as Garfiel tried to tell her to go back, Mimi stomped sharply on his foot. As Garfiel yelped in pain, Mimi puffed out her chest right there and said,

"Garf just said something cool, so there's no way Mimi's runnin' off! Let's go, I'm totally comin' with you!"

"You... nah, fine. Sorry."

"That's an 'thank you,' not a sorry!"

"—Thanks."

"You're welcome!"

Mimi grinned her loose grin, and Garfiel smiled back, looking as though he'd been saved.

Then, turning back to Reala, who stood stunned,

"We'll go find 'em. So you stay put here. Don't move with the others, just wait quiet 'til this whole thing's over."

"B-but... why go so far for us?"

"――――"

Why would he do so much for them?

Reala's eyes wavered as she asked after the true meaning behind Garfiel's decision.

At her words, Garfiel bared his teeth in a grin,

"That's 'cause I'm the Golden Tiger! Gorgeous! Tiger, that's why!"

"And Mimi's Gorgeous Mimi!"

They shouted it at the top of their lungs, drawing the startled eyes of everyone at the shelter.

As Reala looked on, dumbfounded, Garfiel and Mimi struck a pose. Then, spinning around dashingly, the two dashed straight out of the shelter.

"Garf, what now?"

"We track by smell. Got both those two's scent, and Garek's too, memorized solid!"

The trouble was, this was a wide city, and on top of that, water flowed everywhere through it.

Conditions were somewhat poor for tracking a scent cleanly. There were too many people around, and their sense of smell fell short of a real dog's. Still, tracking by the two beastkin's noses could yield decent results.

They first went back to Reala's house, and from there traced the scent to search for the siblings' whereabouts.

Meanwhile, the citizens' evacuation seemed to be proceeding smoothly, and the city had taken on the emptiness of a small ghost town. One might expect a bit of looting amid the chaos, yet the absence of such low behavior was, perhaps, a testament to the sheer weight the name "Witch Cult" carried.

"Mm, this, is it this one? I think it's this smell, Garf!"

"...Yeah, that's right. Goin' by this scent, direction-wise—"

Garfiel picked up the scent Mimi had found too, and pinned down the direction the siblings had moved. It turned out to be the road heading from their home on Third Street toward First Street.

The moment he realized it was First Street, a guess flashed through Garfiel's mind.

"I see. Those two, headin' to the park again today to hear the songstress sing...!"

The boy had said yesterday they'd set out too late and missed it. So today, in its place, he'd probably decided to head out early in the morning, determined not to miss the songstress's song.

This time, no doubt, his sister was going along with him too.

"Then if we head toward First Street...!"

And going that way at the same time would let them hurry to rejoin Subaru and the others too.

The situation seemed, unexpectedly, to be working in their favor, and Garfiel's expression brightened a little as he lifted his head. —Just then, a scent brushed past his nose.

"――――"

"This... their dad's scent...?"

Mimi noticed it at the same moment Garfiel did.

Along the road toward First Street where the siblings had gone, the scent branched off midway. Heading in a direction different from First Street was Garek's scent — heading toward the city hall at the center.

Once again, a moment of choice assailed Garfiel.

If he headed straight for First Street as things stood, he could probably find the siblings. If they'd made it in time for the songstress's song, they'd surely have made it to a shelter too.

But what about city hall?

At city hall, where the Witch Cult had certainly already taken hold, every passing moment further endangered the lives of whoever remained inside.

Every second, Garek remained somewhere the odds of death only grew.

"Garf... what do we do?"

"――――"

Garfiel, forced into a choice.

One path: confirm the siblings' survival, then rejoin Subaru and the others. But this raised the likelihood of abandoning Garek, still at city hall.

Just what sort of person was Garek supposed to be, to Garfiel, when it came to making this judgment?

Unlike Reala — who was Rishia — and the children born from her, there was no clear tie between Garfiel and Garek.

If blood ties were the grounds for a rescue, then Garfiel had no obligation at all to save Garek.

But what would become of Reala and the others if they lost Garek?

They would spend their days drowning in grief, weeping. Perhaps, all because Garfiel had abandoned city hall here.

That family might end up in tears.

"...The Sin Archbishop's holed up at city hall."

"Mm, that's what she said."

"I know the other control towers are dangerous too. But — whoever's callin' the shots, it's gotta be the Sin Archbishop, no doubt about it. So if we take her down..."

"Everyone's saved? Amazing! That's amazing!"

Bouncing up and down on the spot, Mimi praised Garfiel's idea.

But she soon stopped bouncing and said,

"But, is it really, really okay? Somethin's givin' me the creeps."

"The creeps?"

"Like, a dangerous feelin'. Dunno. But a bad feelin'."

Without any real grounds, Mimi voiced that she sensed a bad premonition.

Garfiel felt a flicker of irritation at her words, sounding as though she were balking now of all times.

Up until now, Mimi had always respected Garfiel's judgment. He'd assumed she'd back his decision here too.

"Pathetic... takin' it out on her? Ain't like me at all."

"Garf, what do we do?"

"Your bad feelin's, can't just laugh those off either. And the boss has told me till my ears hurt how dangerous these Sin Archbishops are. But still..."

To turn tail and back off over that alone would shame the name of the Emilia Camp's greatest fighting force.

And no matter how he struggled against it, there was no changing the fact that this was an enemy he had to face to save the city.

"First, we check out city hall. Whether there's a lookout, whether the people inside are safe. Whether we go rip the Sin Archbishop's neck out — that comes after."

"So like, recon? Mm... mm! Got it. Let's recon!"

Mimi agreed, though reluctantly, to Garfiel's cautious plan.

Watching her draw her beloved staff from her robe, Garfiel fitted the two shields at his waist onto his arms.

Covering both arms with what looked like silver gauntlets, he was fully prepared.

"Let's go."

"'Kay."

Mimi answered Garfiel's brief words, and the two of them headed for city hall.

From what he'd heard from Subaru, the Witch Cult's "Sloth," whom they'd fought, had apparently brought along a large number of subordinates. Battle-hardened Witch Cultists, while not on Garfiel's level, could still be a real threat if gathered in sufficient numbers.

Wary that such enemies might be deployed along the way, the two of them advanced toward city hall with the utmost caution, layer upon layer of it. And yet—

"...The hell's it mean, there's nothin', not a single one?"

Far from an overwhelming force of Witch Cultists, there wasn't even a single lookout to be found.

An oversight on their part — given how hard it would be to fool their noses, that wasn't a likely explanation. Which meant—

"So it's like that, huh — they're lookin' down on us somethin' rotten."

"…………"

Recalling the voice from the broadcast, Garfiel couldn't hold back his fury at the Sin Archbishop's mindset.

Was she saying she wasn't even wary of being attacked? Perhaps, having already seized the control towers, she'd already decided she'd won.

He wanted to tear that arrogance apart with claw and fang, and grind it to pieces.

"Mm, mm."

Beside Garfiel, gritting his back teeth, Mimi kept letting out small, repeated groans.

Restless, she kept rubbing the back of her neck over and over, sniffing again and again with a troubled look on her face.

"What."

"Dunno. But somethin's off. Garf, up ahead, somethin's really off."

"Cut it out!"

Grabbing at the hem of Garfiel's clothes, Mimi suddenly blurted out something close to a whine.

Garfiel snapped at her, sounding as though she might tell him to retreat, of all things, now that they'd come this far.

The Witch Cult's attitude — not even a single lookout posted, no wariness at all.

There was no way he could pull back just because it felt unsettling.

If he backed off here, it would only increase the odds of Reala's family being plunged into grief.

"If you don't like it, stay here. I'll go rip that damn voice's owner's neck clean off myself!"

"Garf!"

Shaking off Mimi's fingers gripping his hem, Garfiel burst out from their hiding spot.

He tore straight across the plaza along the canal, meaning to plunge into the city hall building now visible just ahead.

The distance closed. No presence anywhere around. Whether they were looking down on him or just messing around—

Nothing came at him. Ten steps left. Nine. Eight. Seven. Maybe he'd run up the wall and hit the top floor in one go. Six steps. Five—

"Garf—!!"

"—!?"

The instant he gathered strength into his knees to leap, Garfiel threw his body sideways instead of forward.

Right after, a soundless flash of a blade gouged the paving stones at a sharp angle with fearsome sharpness.

The destruction was so smooth it made no sound at all.

The paving stone, sliced at an angle, remained sitting there as if unaware it had even been cut. The faint white smoke drifting up was the residue of friction born from the strike's sharpness.

"—!"

Without Mimi's voice, Garfiel would have taken that strike across the neck.

The brilliant strike would have sliced diagonally through his medulla, leaving his severed head dangling by a single strip of skin — some avant-garde art piece put on display in the plaza.

A chill ran through him.

At the same moment, landing and spinning around, Garfiel saw them.

"――――" "――――"

Right before Garfiel's eyes, two figures stood, having dropped abruptly into the plaza.

One was a giant of a man, so massive he had to be looked up at, holding a large greatsword in each arm, his attention calmly turned this way. The other was a slender, feminine figure, gripping a long single-edged sword with a lengthy blade.

Both wore black garb covering them from the head down, making it impossible to make out their faces.

"...Quite the greetin' you two just gave me."

A cold sweat beading at the back of his neck, Garfiel spoke with deliberate bravado so as not to let on that he'd nearly been overwhelmed.

But the two shadowy figures gave no reply at all to Garfiel's words.

"Garf, those two..."

Giving the pair a wide berth, Mimi ran up to Garfiel's side.

Without looking at Mimi, Garfiel kept glaring at the two figures and said,

"Yeah. Strong, ain't they..."

Mimi's voice was tight with tension, and Garfiel, answering her, couldn't keep the strength from tensing in his own shoulders.

From the two figures standing before them radiated a dreadfully ominous, murderous air.

So evenly matched were the two, on a level almost impossible to rank, that their danger seemed to balance out entirely. Under the tremendous pressure stabbing into his skin, Garfiel felt his throat go dry with startling speed.

These enemies had clearly stepped past the boundary of the human realm — transcendents.

And at a single glance, it was obvious they surpassed even the murderous woman he'd once crossed blades with.

"So, there's two of 'em..."

No other figures were around.

Only these two had come out to intercept them. Since they'd kept their presence hidden from even Garfiel until the very last instant, there was a chance other lurkers could go undetected too — but it seemed unlikely there'd be more fighters of this caliber still hiding.

Which meant these two before him were exactly the wall he had to clear to seize city hall.

The instant he understood that, Garfiel shivered and twisted the corner of his mouth.

"Ha, now that's interestin'...!"

"Garf?"

"I'll get past 'em. I'm gonna blast right through this hell..."

Rousing a heart on the verge of cowering, he clashed the shields covering his fists together in front of his chest. A high ringing sound and a shower of sparks flew, igniting the fire in himself before he could freeze up entirely.

But as Garfiel rose, his fighting spirit bared, Mimi grabbed his hem and shouted.

"N-no! Garf, no! Those two, no good! Super strong! Just me and Garf, we can't win, no way! No!"

"—! Whether we can win or not, we won't know 'less we try. And I sure as hell ain't acceptin' a word like 'no way.' Besides—"

Mimi's words, holding him back, seemed to stir up a weakness of his own, and Garfiel clicked his tongue, jerking his chin toward the two hostile figures.

"Even if we tuck tail and run, those two ain't gonna let us go. Nothin' for it but to fight."

"T-then, just once! We hit 'em, push through, and run. Anything else, no good! Just us two, no good! Without the Captain or Julius, it's impossible!"

"――――"

At Mimi's continued hesitance, Garfiel bit his lip and fell into thought.

Of course, Garfiel understood Mimi's argument was reasonable. Two opponents, each with fighting power that might even surpass Elsa's.

Taking on two enemies like that at once — being called suicidal for it wouldn't be unfair.

Unfair or not, was really pulling back here the right answer?

The two before him were a wall. A wall blocking the way with overwhelming power, one he had to get past.

Defeated by Reinhard, Garfiel had felt in his very skin just how distant the summit of the strongest truly was.

The path to becoming the strongest, the journey required to truly claim the name Golden Tiger, the self-awareness of being a shield for those precious to him.

And then, the mother he'd been reunited with, though not in the form he'd wished for, and her new family.

If he backed off here, then at the very least, Garek would—

"――――"

As dizzying thoughts overtook Garfiel, Mimi kept clutching at his hem, her face still anxious. He suddenly remembered that night, protected by her warmth.

At once, the stubborn, rigid feeling knotted inside him began to thaw.

"...Fine. You're right. One strike, then pull out. Bring the others back and hit 'em again. —That work for you?"

"Mm! Yeah! Let's do that! Okay, let's do our best!"

Mimi's face relaxed with relief as Garfiel withdrew his reckless plan.

With their agreement settled, as Garfiel and Mimi steadied their breath on the spot, the two shadows that had come out to intercept them waited silently.

They could have attacked in the middle of the argument, yet hadn't — whether out of pride, mercy, or simply confidence.

"—Raaah!"

"Hyaaah!"

—If it was confidence, he'd shatter it to pieces.

Without any signal, Garfiel and Mimi lunged at the two figures simultaneously.

Garfiel went for the woman, Mimi for the giant.

Against Garfiel's bullet-like charge, the woman swayed her upper body loosely, then in the next instant brought her gripped blade down at tremendous speed.

The beauty of that straight-falling flash of the blade carried a sharpness fierce enough that Garfiel might have lost himself gazing at it, forgetting time itself.

"—Gah!"

But he wasn't foolish enough to get cut while staring, entranced by beauty.

He caught the strike with his raised right shield, then loosed a kick straight at the woman's torso. She dodged it with a frighteningly supple body, and, spinning, sent the blade — stopped by the shield — flying again.

Garfiel caught the strike bearing down on his neck with his left shield. A front kick landed square on the woman's now-stilled body, sending her light frame flying all at once.

"What the—?"

Watching her sail so lightly backward, Garfiel felt something like an anticlimax.

Glancing back, he saw Mimi, showing off her nimbleness against the giant, slip beautifully through the greatsword's attack and land a single spell — right as she moved to break away from the plaza.

It looked like Mimi could make a clean escape as things stood.

The giant lacked the speed to catch up with Mimi as she pulled away. And the woman wasn't in any position to counter Garfiel's follow-up attack. So then—

"Right here, one down—!"

If he took her down here, everything after would be easier.

For that matter, once he'd dropped the woman, he could tear into the giant behind her too.

With the woman still off-balance, Garfiel kicked hard off the ground and leapt at her. Her blade still hung far off where the shield had knocked it aside, leaving her torso wide open.

If Garfiel drove his right fist in there, a slim body like hers would shatter to pieces. Surely this woman, too, wouldn't turn out to have some undying constitution like Elsa's.

"Got—"

The woman's life — taken.

The instant Garfiel gave voice to that certainty, "death" came from behind.

—There should have been distance between him and the giant, and yet, at the sense of "death" swelling up in an instant, Garfiel's whole body reacted like a coiled spring.

He instantly abandoned his attack, swinging his left arm around to his back, and leapt straight up on the spot.

But the blow closing in from behind shattered his left arm's defense in a single clash, slamming Garfiel into the ground with a groan and making his bones creak.

"A-agh!?"

"――――"

Swallowed by the impact, Garfiel groaned at the weight of a blow he couldn't comprehend.

Bouncing off the ground, his body still airborne, another sideways slash closed in — his two arms, moving just barely in time, blocked the shock — and his body went flying with terrifying force.

His body flew, hovering level with the ground, and as Garfiel glided through the air, the giant and the woman both exploded off their feet at once, closing the distance.

"――――" "――――"

"GAAAAAAAAH!!"

With Garfiel sandwiched between them, the two figures ran alongside him left and right, attacking simultaneously.

He deflected the long sword's strike coming straight at him with his shield, and just barely dodged the greatsword's ferocious blow rushing from directly behind by driving a kick into the flat of the blade. He matched his shield against the long sword rising once more, his cheek singed by the sparks, and right after, the greatswords struck his body from above and below at once.

"Guh—!"

His hip bone and sternum creaked, and the force of the blow dyed Garfiel's vision bright red.

He'd taken the two greatswords' strikes head-on, but the fact that they were essentially blunt weapons, lacking any real edge, was what barely saved Garfiel's life.

Groaning and spitting blood from his mouth, he somehow used the spring of his whole body to try and break free of being pincered. But the two formidable foes wouldn't allow it.

"――――"

Silent still, a soundless strike closed in to take Garfiel's head.

The weight of the blow was incomparable, but the sharpness of the blade the woman wielded carried a beautiful "death," heedless of raw power. Between her skill in handling the blade's length as if it were nothing, and how even a graze from its tip would cause damage equivalent to being run through halfway.

"――――"

He too remained silent, but the giant's way of fighting had a ferocity to it.

But that ferocity had nothing to do with a lack of skill; it was the crystallization of destruction optimized to a degree only true mastery could achieve. He swung around, with one hand alone, a weight an ordinary man would struggle just to carry, and the two blades struck freely, like the arms of a giant.

"Gh, o, AOOOOH!!"

A strike like flowing water, and a strike like a raging whirlwind.

Caught head-on by two different kinds of skill, each the pinnacle of stillness and motion respectively, Garfiel, feet planted on the ground, had no choice but to fight purely on the defensive.

Desperately backing away across the paving stones, he relied on instinct and gut feeling to receive, sweep aside, deflect, and block — with the shields on both arms — the strikes that grazed the edge of his vision and the blows that came crashing down, punching through the very air.

—At this rate, sooner or later, he'd be crushed by the sheer volume of blades and cut down.

"――――" "――――"

The two shadows before him weren't even out of breath despite driving Garfiel back this far. In contrast, he couldn't find even a moment to breathe, wringing out an oxygen-starved head in search of a glimmer of hope while he was reduced to desperately dodging fatal blows, at best.

If his stamina ran dry, his concentration would falter.

If his concentration faltered, his hands wouldn't keep up, and he'd take a blade.

These were the strikes of two transcendents, beyond even master swordsmen.

Even Garfiel's life, for all his confidence in his own physical toughness, they would take with ease.

The more time passed, the fewer options remained in hand, and the more any breakthrough vanished.

A decision. A decision was being forced on him.

This very moment was the only chance to break free of this state and sink his fangs into them. This moment, where it was plain to both the enemy and to Garfiel just who held the overwhelming advantage.

Right now, this instant, was Garfiel's chance to turn the tide from the brink of death.

He needed one breath. That was what he sought.

"—Haah!"

The fatal blade was the woman's strike. He beat it away with a two-armed guard, and against the giant's blow, twisted his body to make do with only the barest minimum of evasion.

Sure enough, the giant's blow shattered Garfiel's left shoulder, and cracked his right kneecap. But damage on that scale, he could deal with, given this one breath.

"Gaaaaaaah—!"

"――――" "――――"

Letting out a roar, he unleashed the seething heat within himself.

With a sensation like his blood boiling, his vision went white for an instant, and his face's very shape began to shift with the audible sound of bone reforming. His mouth split wide, his fangs lengthened, the muscles of his trained arms swelled, and golden fur spread to cover his entire body.

A half-beast form, confined to his upper body.

The taste of blood nearly sent his reason flying, but in this state, his thoughts weren't yet dead. A man had transformed into a beast right before their eyes. His enemies couldn't stay unshaken by that either.

"――――"

Against the two silent figures, Garfiel let loose a roar meant to burst their eardrums, and confirming that the enemy had stopped in their tracks as though caught in a sudden gale, he moved to gouge into their bodies with his thickened arms and blade-like claws.

The tip of his claw was about to tear into the woman's slender frame — but just before it did, the giant cut in front of her.

No matter. Even a thick slab of muscle was nothing but a paper shield against his claws now. And whatever his intention, the giant stood in the way with his greatsword-wielding arm outstretched — a stance chosen not for defense or counterattack, but simply to shield the woman.

Honorable. But this was the end.

—His claws would tear through the giant's body, and the follow-through of that claw strike would tear through the woman's slender one too.

"—!?"

The sequence that should have connected stalled out at its very first stage.

The claw strike of Garfiel, now a great tiger, failed to tear through the giant man. Before it could, both of Garfiel's arms had been stopped by the arm the giant extended.

The front of the giant's garb split open, and from within it, six arms extended.

They pinned down Garfiel's thick arms, and the greatsword they held caught the very tips of Garfiel's claws firmly, absorbing the fierce blow head-on.

—In total, a complete defense from eight arms.

"――――"

Struck dumb, Garfiel lost his words entirely.

The giant simply held him in place, not letting him budge an inch.

Which meant this was the very instant that left Garfiel utterly defenseless—

"――――"

Circling around from behind the giant, the woman appeared at the exposed back of the defenseless half-beast.

Garfiel's back, now covered in fur and grown broad and huge, was, to the woman raising her long sword in one flowing motion, no more difficult a task than cutting down a wooden puppet.

The tip of the blade streaked forward, and Garfiel felt an unseen "death" closing in from behind. But his movement remained locked down by the giant, and he found himself captivated by the sensation of approaching "death"—

"Choyasaaa!!"

"――――"

An instant before Garfiel would have been cleaved diagonally in two, a spirited shout cut in, and the woman's strike was blocked by a blue-glowing magical barrier that unfurled into place.

There came a grating sound like a blade striking ice, and the woman's strike slid across the surface of the barrier, gouging into the ground.

The one who'd pulled that off, saving Garfiel by a hair's breadth, was a small orange-furred cat.

"Garf, you said we'd run right away!"

For the first time, a voice tinged with reproach for Garfiel came from Mimi's mouth.

Still in his half-beast form, hearing that voice at his back, Garfiel — his mind on the verge of losing all reason — became aware of his own foolishness.

He'd rushed for results, let his guard down, and underestimated his opponent, landing himself in a dangerous state. Mimi had saved him from an absolute dead end.

Garfiel caught his breath, too, at the sheer resilience of the barrier she'd deployed.

The fierceness of the woman's blade, contrary to its appearance, was beyond anything ordinary, and Mimi's skill in blocking it was considerable in its own right. He genuinely thought — thank god she was here.

"—O, GAOOOOH!"

"――――"

The instant relief spread through his chest, Garfiel forcibly wrenched his captured arms free. He drove a kick straight at the giant's torso, watched it get blocked by an arm, then wrapped a hand around Mimi's slender waist and leapt back a great distance.

He'd take Mimi and pull out of here as he was. Following the original plan, they'd redo the assault on this place from scratch.

"――――"

The woman lunged after them as they fled. Right in front of her, Mimi deployed another barrier once more, larger in scale than the last. Seeing the woman's strike deflected, Garfiel coiled strength into his legs, ready to break away from here at full force.

One breath. The woman's shadow stopped, drawing her arm back lightly before the barrier. An instant before leaping—

"—Ah."

"――――"

"Eh?"

A faint, rasping voice, and a light impact.

Even as his mouth opened, wondering what had happened, Garfiel's body had already completed its leap. Shattering the paving stones, the half-beast soared through the air, and bright red blood traced a scarlet line across the sky in its wake.

Fresh blood. Where was it coming from?

"Kiddo?"

His concentration for holding the half-beast form broke off, and Garfiel's shape rapidly reverted to human. But a chill ran down his spine strong enough to make him forget even the sensation of his fur falling away.

Mimi's body hung limp and lifeless in his arms. He looked down. Looking up at Garfiel soaring through the air, he saw the woman's shadow drawing back the long sword she'd thrust out.

He saw red blood coating the blade halfway up its length.

Hot liquid trickled down around Garfiel's lower stomach. Mimi remained limp and unmoving in his arms, and he saw her beloved staff slip from her hand and fall.

"――――"

Landing, then leaping again, Garfiel jumped up onto a nearby building, and without so much as looking back, fled with all he had. Being caught would spell trouble, but fortunately, the two enemies from before didn't chase after them.

Whether they had no interest beyond defending the plaza, they'd felt utterly inhuman. No — none of that mattered right now. Putting distance between himself and the plaza with four, five all-out leaps, Garfiel landed hard enough to crack the rooftop of some building, and there he set down Mimi's limp, unmoving body from his arms, and checked on her.

Mimi's eyes were closed, blood pouring in great quantities from a wound that had pierced through her chest.

Frantically, he pulled back her clothes to examine the wound. It had just barely missed any vital organ that could have made it instantly fatal. Of course, that was no reason for optimism about her condition. There was also the massive blood loss to consider. He needed to apply healing magic at once and keep her still.

"――――"

Placing his hand over the wound, Garfiel sent healing mana flowing into Mimi's body.

Garfiel's skill at healing magic was among the finest even in the Sanctuary. He'd always boasted that healing magic wasn't his style, but when something happened to someone, he'd wanted the power to somehow pull them back from a dangerous state. So Garfiel had poured his entire aptitude for magic into healing spells, and had mastered them to a respectable degree as a healer in his own right.

So long as it wasn't a mortal wound, he was confident he could heal even a deep injury.

Mimi's wound too should have closed at once under the wave of healing. Sweat beading on his brow, hand pressed to the wound to stem the flowing blood, he kept sending in power to heal the torn skin, the muscle, the injured organs beneath. Kept sending it. Kept sending it. Kept sending it.

—The wound wouldn't close.

"Why... why..."

Someone murmured in a faint, rasping, feeble voice.

Watching this scene, he wanted to punch whoever had let out a voice like that. He lifted his head and searched around. No one was there. He realized it at once. That voice just now had been his own.

Could he really make a voice that weak? Had he really let it out?

That was just like, just like, just like—

"—! Close! Close, close up, damn it, heal, heal, heal, heal already!!"

With everything he had, he commanded every last drop of mana circulating through his body and drove it into the healing spell. The wave of healing flowed through Mimi's entire body, filling it with gentle power.

And yet the wound that had to close simply would not close.

"—No... way."

Unable to accept the reality before him, Garfiel let his weakness slip out again.

Right after, he struck his own cheek, biting through his lip with his fangs, jolting his mind awake with sharp pain. This was no time for weak words. There had to be some way.

Something, something, something — there had to be.

He didn't understand. But not understanding wasn't a reason to give up. Whatever it took, right now, no matter what, he had to save the girl in his arms.

Because wasn't this the girl who'd let him cry?

A girl like this — because of him, trying to save him — had no right to die.

"――――"

Gnashing his fangs, Garfiel leapt up in a desperate frenzy. Hand pressed to the wound of the girl in his arms, he kept trying to stanch the bleeding while continuing to cast healing magic that did nothing.

The smell of blood, the smell of "death." The streets, empty of anyone. Even what had happened had vanished completely from Garfiel's mind by now.

Someone, anyone at all. Save this girl. Whoever, wherever you are, work a miracle. Tell me. If there's anything to be done, if anything at all can save her—

"――――"

Grasping at straws, Garfiel sharpened his sense of smell.

The smell of water. The smell of blood that had, before he knew it, filled the air around him. His emotions surging, the smell of scorched flesh. Amid the stench hanging thick in the air, Garfiel caught a familiar scent and threw himself after it, chasing it, running, running, running with everything he had.

He burst into the shelter he found, and small screams went up at the sight of his blood-soaked form. He had no time to spare for that. Eyes bulging, he searched for the figure he needed. Searched, searched—

"Garfiel!?"

The other side found him first.

At the very back of the dark, cold underground facility, stood the figure he'd been searching for.

It was Subaru. Natsuki Subaru.

To Garfiel, he was a symbol of miracles, the one who could surely find a thread of light even in the worst of situations — his last hope.

His legs wobbled. His head swam.

His throat closed at the light weight in his arms, and Garfiel staggered forward, running as best he could.

Seeing him approach, Subaru and someone nearby caught their breath. They'd noticed Mimi hanging limp in his arms.

Reaching him at last, Garfiel bowed his head to Subaru. Holding Mimi out to him, he cursed his own foolishness over and over.

"Sorry, boss...! I'm useless! Worthless...!"

He hadn't protected his family, hadn't fulfilled the role he'd sworn to as a shield, had recklessly challenged the enemy on his own judgment and failed to produce results, and now, on top of it all, he was about to let a kind girl die.

"Garfiel, what's... no, this isn't the time for that! Ferris!"

"I know! Quick, hand her over!"

Mimi was taken from his outstretched arms, and Garfiel saw the slender figure standing beside Subaru lay her down on a bed.

In the next instant, an overwhelming wave of healing poured out, incomparable to Garfiel's own. If Garfiel's was a single drop of rain, this person's power was like the Great Waterfall itself.

Witnessing healing power that seemed capable of even reviving a lost life, Garfiel watched the treatment with a hollow, soul-drained expression.

Subaru gently set a hand on Garfiel's shoulder. When he slowly lifted his gaze, Subaru, bearing his own painful leg injury, nodded to him and said,

"Can't say it's a good situation, but you made it here, and that's somethin'. Comin' to where Ferris is — that was the best call you could've made. Thanks to you, that girl's gonna make it."

"...Thanks to me?"

What was Subaru talking about?

Mimi saved, thanks to Garfiel? How could something so absurd be true? It was Garfiel's fault, in the first place, that Mimi had suffered a wound like that.

And yet, why — as if it were only natural she'd be saved, as if his own judgment counted for nothing at all—

His thoughts wandered emptily, an endless self-reproach and awareness of his own folly.

And the world would never forgive Garfiel for that foolishness.

The price of a mistake was always, without fail, collected.

And in the most horrifying form imaginable.

"Ferris, what's wrong...?"

Sensing something was wrong, Subaru's expression changed.

Dragging his leg, Subaru made his way to the bed where the treatment was underway, calling out to the person casting the spell there with all their might.

Amid the tremendous surge of mana, the healer shook their head and said,

"Why...? The wound — the wound won't close, nya...! At this rate, I can't save her, nya!"

Listening to that anguished voice ring out through the underground space, Garfiel looked up at the sky.

He couldn't see it. The underground sky told Garfiel nothing at all.

—Nothing, except that the price of the mistake he'd committed would be paid in blood.
