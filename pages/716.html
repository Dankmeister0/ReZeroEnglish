Arc 9 Chapter 21: "Valga Cromwell"

"—Domain Expansion, Matrix Redefinition."

In response to the shifting situation, the world is carved and advanced second by second, moment by moment, instant by instant. For Aldebaran's Authority, which could not reach far back through time, this was a gamble that could reel in certain victory just as surely as it could let victory slip away for good.

"—Domain Expansion, Matrix Redefinition."

Step by step, move by move, action by action, verifying, fixing, closing in on the board. That the superhuman warriors of this world built up their skill within a time that only ever moved forward, in a world of unrepeatable, one-shot bets — it was, truly, beyond imagining.

"—Domain Expansion, Matrix Redefinition."

Not just warriors — the great sages of superhuman caliber, too, were the same in the sense of possessing thought beyond ordinary dimensions. If anything, they might feel even more monstrous, since they assembled the world's unfolding entirely within their heads, without practice, without ever knowing the true scent of blood or the pain of a wound. Either way—

"—Domain Expansion, Matrix Redefinition."

They had every right to trample the training of the warrior and the deep deliberation of the sage alike, to ignore the truth that it was all a one-shot bet, to ignore the principle that one cannot witness the outcomes of two choices at once, and to curse, loathe, and spit upon Aldebaran's way of being — snatching victory alone while disregarding all of that — and call it contemptible. Let them cut him to pieces with all the vile names they liked. In exchange—

"—Domain Expansion, Matrix Redefinition."

By this filthy method, he would steal victory, and victory alone, without fail.

"...At the very least, I'd like a bit of the charm a pig has when it's begging for feed."

The joke, spun in a low, growling voice from the pig-faced man — Dolteo Amule, the "Pig King" — came out hoarser than the crushing sense of despair he'd radiated up until now. Naturally. His entire body was scored with lacerations from steel wire, and he was bleeding profusely from countless wounds. If anything, it was stranger that he was still conscious at all, and that his face showed no trace of grief.

"I get that you're not scared of dying, even after all this. But not being scared of dying doesn't mean it doesn't hurt like hell."

"Can't you see it yourself? Death and pain both, in the end, only bring fear. So the only thing we need to swallow down is the fear itself."

"...I don't get it."

Dolteo's argument treated the fear brought on by death and the fear brought on by pain as one and the same. But to Aldebaran's mind, they were different things. The fear born of death and the fear born of pain were not the same. More than that — pain was, after all, a stimulus to the flesh, so it seemed different again from fear itself.

"—. Whatever you say, it's just a pig's howl."

Before Aldebaran, white foam beading at the corners of his mouth, Dolteo muttered those words. Around the thick neck of the "Pig King" was wound, layer upon layer, an impossibly fine steel wire, crushing his windpipe. Dolteo had thrust his fingers in to resist the strangulation, but the two fingers driven into his own throat had lost their purpose, and at last his massive body, its brain starved of blood, dropped to its knees.

Around the kneeling Dolteo lay the fifty-five men he had brought with him, all fallen. Taking on the "Pig King" while knocking down the other men one by one had, without exaggeration, cost bone and flesh and life — over and over. In total: twenty-three thousand and eight times.

That number of attempts ranked among the very top even within Aldebaran's vast, hard-won catalogue of defeats. Of course, part of that was because the coordinated attacks of the "Pig King" and his "Black Silver Coin" subordinates had been extraordinarily meticulous. But the biggest factor was simply Aldebaran's own lack of talent.

The steel wire wound around Dolteo's neck hadn't extended from Yae's hand — it extended from Aldebaran's, using the ring he'd borrowed from her. Manipulating a single finger's worth of steel wire, closing off Dolteo's ferocious assault move by move, he had at last managed to loop the wire around that thick neck and bundle enough strands together to crush it. It had taken more than twenty thousand cycles of time, within this brutal domain, just to learn the Steel Thread Technique from scratch and make it usable.

"————"

With the sound of a fist striking the earth hard — thud — Dolteo's consciousness went completely dark. Even so, the "Pig King" who reigned over the underworld did not let his body fall sideways; he braced his massive frame with a fist pressed to the ground, proving a pride that would not break. Even as an enemy, it was a bearing one couldn't help but call admirable.

"Haaah, honestly, Al-sama. You bragged that nobody in the village but me could ever learn it, and now look — this is downright embarrassing, isn't it~"

So said Yae, standing beside a winded Aldebaran before the now-silent Dolteo, having already strung up every other enemy but him into the air. Her eyes, narrowed like a cat's, were fixed on the Steel Thread Technique Aldebaran had used to bind Dolteo. Still, there was no way he could take that praise at face value.

"Don't talk nonsense. Using just one finger, I feel like every muscle from the top of my head to the tips of my toes is about to cramp. You're doing this with ten fingers — ten times the work — are you secretly ripped under there or something?"

"Eh~, could it be that Al-sama is curious about what's under my uniform~? I've given you plenty of chances to check, and you turned every single one down, you tease."

"...Lend me the ring a little longer, would you."

Responding to Yae's playfully teasing remark, Aldebaran ran a hand over the ring fitted onto his right ring finger. He'd tried various things, but for him, wearing it on the ring finger simply felt the most natural. Honestly, he'd had some reservations about putting a ring on that particular finger, but then again, he had no left arm to begin with. Bringing that kind of maidenly sentimentality onto a battlefield was ridiculous enough on its own. In any case—

"Old man, you all right? Sorry I couldn't spare the attention to check on you."

"N-no, don't worry about that. I'm — I'm fine."

Heinkel nodded, though he stumbled over his words, at Aldebaran's check-in. During the fight with Dolteo, to put it plainly, Heinkel had been reduced to a nonfunctional deadweight — but he had contributed by knocking out the men Yae had bound and by smashing the mirror serving as the mirror-bearer's link, so Aldebaran counted that as work done. As expected, once opponents of a certain caliber — Dolteo, of course, but also someone on the level of Gaston — got involved, it became hard to count Heinkel as combat strength. Aldebaran couldn't bring himself to blame Heinkel for that kind of psychological fragility.

Everyone has someone their very soul balks at facing. For Yae, that was Aldebaran; and for Aldebaran — well, that was fine, leave it there.

"...Aldebaran, what in the world are you, really?"

"Hm?"

"You had no chance of winning against that Pig-Human. He had you beat in skill and in power both. And yet, in the end, you won. Barely a scratch on you, and it wasn't even close."

"————"

Heinkel said this with eyes that looked upon something almost too awesome to face. Listening to those words, Aldebaran found himself revising his opinion of the man — he really did watch closely, all things considered. Precisely because he'd watched from the outside, Aldebaran's strangeness must have struck him all the more vividly. Aldebaran hadn't grown stronger, nor had he simply been well-matched against Dolteo. If anything, put bluntly, it looked like he'd won by stacking up a hundred lucky breaks in a row.

"—He's a monster."

Yae stated it flatly, refusing to elaborate further, and there was something almost refreshing about her attitude. She answered Heinkel in Aldebaran's place, and her face showed no trace of either dread or fear. In Yae's mind, her impression of Aldebaran had already been settled long ago.

"Al-sama is a monster. We're traveling together with a monster, you know."

"...I know a whole lot of guys who seem like monsters. But this guy—"

"Men who seem like monsters and an actual monster are different things. Please don't insult him by comparing him to a knockoff~"

Her tone was mild, her expression smiling, but her way of speaking left no room for argument. Cowed by Yae's outwardly gentle but unyielding force, Heinkel found himself unable to say anything more.

"Whether I'm a monster or not, we're not changing the plan going forward. Yae holds down the numbers, I take on the strong ones. Old man, follow orders and keep working the mirror."

"R-right, got it."

"Yes~ma'am~. Whatever Al-sama commands~."

As Heinkel nodded hesitantly, Yae threw both hands up in a cheer, her fingers moving in small, precise motions to manipulate the steel wire. Guided by that dexterous handiwork, the wire wound around the unconscious Dolteo, binding the limbs of the "Pig King" who had shown his stubbornness by refusing to collapse, and hoisted him into the air alongside the rest. Even knowing it was a safety measure in case Dolteo woke up, it didn't feel good. Not just Dolteo — the same went for Rachins, whom he'd had to beat until unconscious.

"My head hurts..."

Honestly, he'd underestimated Felt's people. Aside from Reinhard, he'd assumed everyone but Ezzo could basically be written off as non-combatants. And there was still that giant of an old man to deal with, plus Grassis, Gaston, and of course the main event, Felt herself—

"If Al-sama would just remove one restraint, I could be so much more useful, you know~?"

"————"

"Needless to say... I'm better at strangling than at binding, myself~"

Every so often, Yae would say something that, for whatever reason, sounded like she was trying to seduce Aldebaran, and that whisper struck his eardrums far more sweetly than any invitation to a bedroom ever could. Unlike Heinkel, who couldn't perform at full strength for psychological reasons, the reason Yae couldn't show her true colors was, plainly, that Aldebaran was forcing his own ego on her. If he removed that restraint, a forest this dense with cover would become Yae's solo stage in an instant — even if the enemy numbered five hundred, it wouldn't take long at all to stain the green leaves red.

Still, Yae wasn't complaining that she was dissatisfied at being unable to do that. No matter how unjustified an order might be, obeying was simply her nature. So the reason she whispered this to Aldebaran now was—

"—Thanks. But no thanks."

"Oh my."

Yae stuck out her tongue, seemingly not even shocked that her proposal had been shot down. It was a proposal she hadn't expected to be accepted from the start. Surprising, and yet — no, not surprising at all, given that she wanted Aldebaran to accomplish his goal — it seemed she'd simply been trying to fire him up. Thanks to that sweet bait, Aldebaran found his spent will restored.

"There's an option to just push south out of the forest as we are, but thinking ahead, that's a no-go. Sorry, but Miss Felt and her people are going to have to lose here."

"Understood. Shall we proceed as planned, then?"

Erasing the tone of the exchange up to that point, Yae tilted her head, and Aldebaran nodded. Then, picturing Felt and the old giant beyond the forest, no doubt scheming for a way to win, he murmured quietly.

"You lot are strong. It's just — your stars were bad."

—Upon having the third "Dialogue Mirror" broken, Rom-jii grew certain.

"So he's doing more than simply seeing through our tactics."

Rom-jii removed the one "Dialogue Mirror," among those fitted into his gauntlet, that had lost its partner and become useless, tossing it away, and turned his mind to the optimal behavior of the helmet bastard — the true nature of the movements by which he instantly identified whichever mirror-bearer held a "Dialogue Mirror," denying them the chance to gather information.

Rachins had gone down, and even Dolteo, who had rushed to the scene, had been defeated. It was a possibility Rom-jii had accounted for, but even so, the "Pig King's" defeat was something that, given their long acquaintance, he could not help but find astonishing.

"Had he been born ten years earlier, that man might have left his name in the history books of the Demi-Human War."

The man himself would likely laugh that off as exaggeration, but in Rom-jii's mind, it was a fair assessment. Granted, Dolteo alone would not have been enough to flip the outcome of the Demi-Human War — that fight hadn't been close enough for that. Even if he had left a name in the history books, the likelier scenario was that he'd be remembered as one more man cut down and his life scattered by the "Sword Demon" or the "Sword Saint" of that era.

Regardless — that Dolteo had lost. So then, was the enemy on the level of the "Sword Demon"?

"No, that's not it."

Shaking his thick neck, Rom-jii cast off that pessimistic thought. Honestly, the existence of the "Sword Demon" was a kind of trauma for Rom-jii. Against the "Sword Demon," even if his side numbered a thousand against the man's one, he could see not the faintest sign of a path to victory. This wasn't a claim that the "Sword Demon" was the strongest being on earth — it was about the sheer stature that made one believe so.

Even when it isn't actually true, there exist those whose stature alone convinces an opponent that victory is impossible. Felt herself, whether in raw strength or in cunning, couldn't plausibly go toe-to-toe with the underworld's biggest names — and yet they still looked to her as their king. That was an immovable thing called "stature." And that — the helmet bastard did not have. Bluntly put, he wasn't frightening. Though one could argue that was precisely what fed into his unfathomability.

"That unfathomability is something we need to strip away, piece by piece. —Launch it!"

At Rom-jii's raised voice, some twenty soldiers he had kept in hand moved. Igniting the fire mana-stone and firing the contents of the tube — the magic cannon, a precious device portable by hand, capable of repeated use once cooled — but the mana-stone launched was more precious still. It was—

"—Night Banisher."

Immediately after, a white light sliced across the sky of the battlefield like a bolt of lightning, and in an instant the clouds cleared. The blue sky glimpsed beyond drove off the approaching dusk, rewriting time itself. —No, that wasn't quite right. The effect of "Night Banisher" was, strictly speaking, a reproduction of daytime.

Launched at night, it rewrote the night sky, reproducing a daytime sky in its place. Reproduce a clear sky on a rainy day, and you could even stage the inexplicable sight of raindrops falling from a cloudless blue. Ordinarily, "Night Banisher's" effect was used to drive off the night that plunges a battlefield into chaos, but this mana-stone had another effect, one few knew about. It was the effect of painting reality over with illusion, just as it rewrote night into day, a dark day into a bright one. Namely—

"—If something's been watching us from the sky, that eye is now useless."

The "Night Banisher" mana-stone, prepared as a contingency for when the fight dragged on into night, found its use here. Judging this a rare stroke of luck, Rom-jii took a countermeasure against one possibility in the helmet bastard's arsenal — surveillance by an eye in the heavens. Though, to be fair—

"Six, I've found the helmet bastard! Right now I'm— guoaah!?"

From the report of the mirror-bearer assigned as Number Six, and the way it abruptly went silent afterward, Rom-jii concluded that the helmet bastard had broken the fourth "Dialogue Mirror," and that the possibility of an eye in the heavens had vanished.

Still, there was no disappointment. He'd rated the "eye in the heavens" possibility as low to begin with. Against the helmet bastard's optimal play, the first instruction Rom-jii had issued was rotating the mirror-bearers at set intervals and relaying the next move via written text through the mirror. But the helmet bastard had countered this, seizing the mirror-bearer first two mirrors running. Even if he were visually observing their movements from the sky, that alone wouldn't let him respond so completely. That left—

"An arch-spirit I don't know of, or a powerful Divine Protection, or perhaps he's reading the contents of his opponent's head — or else, what he's watching isn't the contents of anyone's head at all—"

So he listed the possibilities that occurred to him, one after another, and set about eliminating them one by one. Given the helmet bastard's behavior, an arch-spirit seemed unlikely. A spirit's true strength lies in Magic, but there was no sign that the helmet bastard was drawing on a spirit's power to break through the situation. Spirits of the sun and shadow attributes might offer support that was harder to spot compared to spirits of other attributes, but such spirits were themselves rare in existence. The chance he had one in tow wasn't zero, but—

"Low priority. — Seven, advance east. Nine, advance southeast. Rush to Six's engagement point. No — never mind reinforcing Six. Instead, align the pace of the two units. Combine mana-stones and thrown stones — whatever it takes, force him to use two or more Divine Protections."

A Divine Protection, by its nature, resides in only one person at a time. Those who carry two or more Divine Protections in one body number few enough in history to count on one's fingers, and among those, the number who can actually use both is smaller still, few enough to count on one hand. — That two such freaks of fortune, the "Sword Saint" and the "Mad Prince," should both exist in the present age was already strange enough, but there was every chance the helmet bastard was a third. Hence—

"Bundle up the hardships, layer them, and how he unravels them will tell you his Divine Protection. — It isn't for nothing that I'm said to have killed more Divine Protection-holders than anyone else alive."

Ever since the sudden "Night Banisher," Aldebaran had felt the situation take a dramatic turn for the worse. The enemy's movements had begun to include an obviously deliberate kind of inaction, and even identifying the mirror-bearer had become that much harder — meaning the order of magnitude of his attempts changed.

"Because he keeps shuffling the mirror-bearers, if my timing engaging them shifts even slightly, the one holding the mirror changes too...!"

On top of that, attacks purely intended to pin down, defeat, and kill Aldebaran's group had dropped off, replaced by a marked rise in attacks that seemed designed to test his reactions. Setting aside their intent, coping with attacks that didn't directly threaten his life was proving a torment for Aldebaran. If it were a fatal attack, one certain to take his life, he could crush it mercilessly and cut off its origin without hesitation. But when he couldn't grasp the intent, or when the impact was minor, having to clear each one away like a pebble on the road demanded an enormous number of actions.

"Put bluntly, we're talking about redoing things over a mere scratch."

Even so, he couldn't ignore attacks that, like a slow-acting poison, might by their very reception force Aldebaran into a checkmate save. As a result, once he began to suspect even one, he found himself forced to deal with every single attack, and the order of magnitude of his attempts shifted up another notch.

"Well played, you damned old geezer."

△▼△▼△▼△

As the number of broken mirrors piled up, Rom-jii grew increasingly wary of how the situation was developing. Thanks to notices sent in advance from the heads of each organization, the rough men of the underworld had followed Rom-jii's orders with startling obedience. There must have been those among them who fell without any clear idea of their role, and yet the way they went down without a word of complaint was, frankly, humbling. Thanks to that, his own hand of cards was dwindling at a dangerous rate, but in exchange, the true nature of the enemy's hidden cards was, gradually but surely, being exposed.

"Men who've made their peace with the risk. The tie to Flanders was Felt's doing, but truly, the heavens do watch over those who struggle with all their might."

Using the effect of "Night Banisher" to blind the eye in the sky, and shifting the mana density of the area through repeated volleys of mana-stones to create a space that would intoxicate spirits, he'd confirmed the absence of any cooperating spirit. He'd suspected some highly versatile Divine Protection effect, mixing direct and ranged attacks, physical and magical, throwing every Divine Protection he knew of — limited, of course, to those whose properties he could verify, with Reinhard as his test subject — against the present situation, and had whittled ninety percent of the candidates away.

"It's not like the 'Divine Protection of First Sight' or the 'Divine Protection of Resurrection.' If it's not the 'Divine Protection of Foul Weather' or the 'Divine Protection of the Tiger's Den' either, then there aren't many candidates left."

The remaining one-tenth might, depending on how they were used, prove useful for some narrow specialized purpose, but none seemed capable of universally handling the cornered situation at hand. He set them at the back of his mind and effectively ruled them out. From here on, it wasn't Magic, nor a spirit, nor a Divine Protection he needed to suspect — it was an Authority.

"To warp the very laws of the world — that's arrogance beyond what any human body should hold, isn't it, helmet bastard."

As the pattern of the volleyed attacks shifted, and the enemy's movements changed once more, Aldebaran ground his teeth as the worst-case possibility, long simmering at the back of his mind, hit its mark.

Most likely, that old giant — Valga Cromwell — had realized that Aldebaran was using an Authority, breaking through the encroaching crisis by stacking up countless matrices. The continuous volleys and the variety-rich barrage of attacks that had come in quick succession just before were meant to strip away the possibilities of exactly how Aldebaran was escaping his overwhelming disadvantage. Surely he couldn't have grasped the true nature of Aldebaran's Authority — the near-infinite number of attempts made possible through the existence of the Domain and the redefinition of the Matrix — but Aldebaran had a distinct sense that he'd been cornered quite far.

The likely candidates in Magic and Divine Protection had already been picked over thoroughly, and there was a palpable sense of entering a turn where the enemy would begin to suspect an outlandish, unreasonable Authority. And, just as Ezzo Cadner had at the Pleiades Watchtower, the chance that a sharp-minded person would see through Aldebaran's Authority was high. That said, even seeing through it, cornering Aldebaran to checkmate was difficult. Ezzo's judgment — flooding every escape route with water magic — had been one form of optimal solution, but pulling off the same thing in a space this wide open would be no easy feat.

Even so, that lingering trace of an unpleasant premonition never quite vanished from the depths of his chest, and it made Aldebaran hesitate over updating his matrix within a short span of time, gradually, steadily building up just how difficult it would be to recover from a single failure.

To begin with, why was Valga Cromwell — a famous figure from the Demi-Human War — even alive, let alone cooperating with Felt? Valga was a notorious misanthrope, the great strategist of the Demi-Human War who had taken thousands of lives through merciless tactics. The sheer senselessness of the combination made his head feel ready to boil over with complaints. Something was seriously, seriously wrong here.

By this point, the worst premonition had reached a stage beyond doubt, and Rom-jii, suppressing the sickening feeling welling up in his chest, rubbed his bald head hard, pointlessly.

He'd concluded that the helmet bastard was most likely a wielder of an Authority — not a Divine Protection, not Magic, but that outlandish power once said to have been wielded by "Witches," a power that might well warp the very laws of the world. Sphinx, the successor of the "Witch" he'd once had dealings with during the Demi-Human War era, had claimed herself a failure who could not reproduce the Authority in full — but a Noh whose bearer even that "Witch" would have coveted enough to reach out and grab? Even if the helmet claimed he wanted achievements for Felt's sake, this was a far too troublesome type of opponent.

And, though he hated to admit it, he'd narrowed the helmet bastard's Authority down to either "reading the opponent's mind" or "short-term precognition of the future." Though at this point, he judged precognition the more likely of the two, rather than mind-reading.

Given how thoroughly the helmet bastard kept dodging their attacks, if he really were reading someone's mind, Rom-jii reasoned it would almost certainly be his own — and he set a trap to confirm it. He gave two units differing orders — the first to advance without attacking, the second to launch a full assault — and yet the helmet bastard tried to deal with the first unit, which he should have been able to safely ignore, and thereby placed himself into the desperate bind of a pincer attack with the second unit. If he'd been reading Rom-jii's mind, or the mind of anyone in the first unit, he'd taken on trouble he needn't have shouldered at all.

At that point, it was confirmed that the helmet bastard's Authority was, most likely, not mind-reading. Which meant his true Authority had narrowed down to almost certainly being "precognition of the future." Having narrowed it down was no reason to cheer "we've got it now," though. Sphinx had spoken with a knowing air about a power that warps the world's laws, but "seeing the future" was simply going too far. What on earth was one even supposed to do about something like that? Something was seriously, seriously wrong here.

"—Anyone who can't use the Flow Method, fall back! But don't stop moving!!"

Bellowing that in a booming voice, Gaston burst out from the group and threw himself at the helmet bastard. Through the mirror-bearer, Gaston had already heard Rom-jii's theory. — The helmet bastard, apparently, knew a little of what was about to happen ahead of time, or something like that.

"Then it's just a matter of making sure knowing doesn't do him any damn good!"

Roaring the words out, Gaston abandoned any thought of a single decisive blow and switched to a rapid string of finely chopped strikes, prioritizing cutting off the helmet bastard's escape routes and landing a fist. But against this flurry, the helmet bastard stamped hard into the ground, and—

"Used your head there, Gaston. But this is exactly the wrong place for that."

A wall of rising earth shielded the helmet bastard, and Gaston's power-restrained strikes were blocked by the wall of dirt. Just as the helmet bastard said, the moment Gaston ground his teeth at the backfire, that same wall of earth folded itself into a gauntlet over the man's arm, and his fist punched clean through Gaston's forehead, sending him reeling backward. With Gaston still reeling, there was a sense that the helmet bastard's follow-up was coming.

"Oraaa!!"

Toward that presence, Gaston launched a kick with the momentum of a backward somersault. The nimble kick, spun in a swift rotation, was an imitation of the cat-like acrobatics Felt used when trying to slip free of Reinhard's grip. No one would have expected Gaston's massive frame to pull this off — a technique that had even landed a hit on the old gardener of House Astrea who sparred with him—

"Landed. Four times, even."

Along with that baffling line, Gaston's kick cut through empty air. Even his ultimate technique, the move he'd trained to the point of calling it a finisher, hadn't worked. But before he could regret it, a wave of wrongness hit Gaston's body. A pressure like being seized by a giant's hand, and his right leg was hoisted up. — Looking down, it was an impossibly fine thread extending from the helmet bastard's right hand, binding him. Not just the redheaded maid traveling with him — the helmet bastard could use it too?

"Mine's just one strand. She's the real genius, honestly."

Hearing the praise for his companion in that spiritless voice of the helmet bastard, Gaston ground his teeth. His right leg, suspended by the thread, had been lifted to head height. His supporting left leg was up on its toes, and no strength would go into his body at all. In this ridiculous posture, Gaston had been rendered helpless. And to that helpless Gaston, the helmet bastard turned his back, and—

"Old man, he's yours. I'm going to go back up Yae."

So saying, the helmet bastard began walking off, as if dealing with Gaston were already finished business. Stepping forward in his place was the father he'd heard about from Reinhard. Same color hair and eyes as Reinhard, and yet the air he wore about him felt entirely different from his son. And above all — that face, reluctantly, grudgingly following the helmet bastard's orders — Gaston hated it. So—

"—Hey, you, don't try anything reckless— boeh!"

Gaston punched the face that had stiffened its cheek at the sight of his movement, and lunged with everything he had straight at the retreating back of the helmet bastard. But before his arm could grab him, his footing collapsed, his half-turned opponent dodged the tackle with ease, and Gaston crashed hard onto the ground. He'd failed. — Yanking his leg free of the thread by force, his ankle, half torn through, had failed to support his weight.

"You, with that leg..."

"So what if it's just one leg?! I'm out here putting my life on the line! You think this is some kind of game you quit the moment it hurts a little?!"

"————"

"And what pisses me off most is that you people are doing this reluctantly! Don't go picking fights with people if you can't even laugh and say 'well done' to your own comrade's work!"

Hurling his fury at the silent helmet bastard, Gaston wrung out every last drop of strength and hauled himself upright. Blood still poured profusely from his right ankle, and past the gouged-out flesh, white bone showed through. Even so, Gaston clenched his back teeth and stood — thanks to the weight of what he carried on his shoulders.

Gaston had a woman he loved. A woman who, fearing that a villain traveling with a "Divine Dragon" might come for them, had told him not to worry, held him close, and then pushed his back as he set off with Felt.

Gaston had men he loved. A friend who griped endlessly with his mouth while forever fretting over everyone around him. A troublesome bond of fate who, cowardly at the root as ever, still never once ran even with his knees knocking. A mentor who, for all his tedious, meddling lectures, looked out for everyone and swore that anybody had it in them to become great. A cursed tie who loved the girl who threw out her chest and swore to make her big talk come true, and turned even a past worthy of a curse into strength.

Gaston had a man and a woman he admired. A strong man who, being that strong, could surely protect anything he wanted to protect. A strong woman so dazzling that once you'd seen her shine, you could never forget her, no matter where you went.

—Gaston had every reason to fight with his chest held high.

"People without that have no business stepping onto the ground where someone's fighting with everything they've got!!"

Roaring that from the bottom of his heart, Gaston charged at the helmet bastard again, deliberately planting his weight on his right leg. Even if this one step shattered that leg, tore it off, left him unable to walk ever again — fine. If it came to that, Gaston would find something else he could do, and he knew, without a doubt, that his comrades wouldn't abandon him even then. — Right now, he wanted to beat the helmet bastard.

Leaping off, Gaston's massive body rose into the air, and a knee strike launched upward with his left knee. Even if it were stopped by a wall of earth, there was a second stage ready — a downward hammer blow with both upraised arms. Either way, he'd crush that helmet hiding the man's face and—

"—Honestly, that one hurt."

Matching his lunge and leap, the helmet bastard closed the distance as if he'd known it was coming all along. The instant Gaston's throat gave a faint groan at that, a rock-encased fist met the airborne Gaston's face head-on, snapping his neck backward.

"Screw... you..."

In the end, not a single one of Gaston's attacks had landed. And yet, hearing "that one hurt" — whether as a joke or as sarcasm, there wasn't a shred of humor to be found in it. As blood loss from his leg took its toll as well, Gaston's consciousness rapidly began to fade—

"—Rraaaahhhh!!"

—Last of all, along with a familiar high-pitched voice, he saw white light flash across the edge of his vision.

△▼△▼△▼△

—That "Meteor" is called the "Star Staff."

Most of the "Meteors" currently existing in this world are said to have been made by a single "Witch," fashioned on a whim, following nothing but curiosity and desire — but the "Star Staff" stands apart from those. It is a crystallization of wisdom that the "Witch" built with real purpose and real intent to see that purpose through — and even if that purpose was something as trivial as silencing a "Dragon" too free with its mouth, its power is the genuine article.

Drawing up the mana of its holder, the force it unleashes bypasses formulae entirely, becoming Shario, starlight itself. Naturally, it's a mere flicker compared to Al Shario, which can kill even a "Dragon" — but even so, as a power an individual can wield without any training whatsoever, it's an outrageous force.

Chased by starlight that would not stop pursuing until it struck its target, Aldebaran took every defensive measure he could think of, but not one of them let him weather it without taking a wound. This type of attack, too — one that cut off every retreat — was Aldebaran's natural enemy. So, weighing it against his priorities, there was only one course of action available to him.

"Sorry, old man."

Voicing that apology, Aldebaran raised the ground beneath Heinkel, who had taken Gaston's counterattack and fallen on his backside, and used him as a shield between himself and the starlight. Naturally, Heinkel sensed the intent and his face twisted with fury as he shouted "Aldeba—" but at that instant, the surging white light struck the red-haired swordsman directly, and the impact that could silence a "Dragon" tore straight through his whole body.

"—Ka, o, guoaaah."

By experience, Aldebaran knew this was the kind of damage that would sear brain and organs to a crisp. And yet Heinkel, eyes rolled back white, still had enough composure left to fall to his knees and writhe in place, right there. From what had happened in Vollachia, and from the man's time as "Aldebaran," and now, through this whole grueling struggle, Heinkel's abnormal toughness had been proven beyond doubt time and again. — Saved by that endurance, which seemed to bring Heinkel himself not a scrap of joy, Aldebaran turned his gaze toward the source of the light. There—

"—So you're Camberley."

A man of the Dwarf Tribe, blood dripping steadily from his nose, leaning against the "Star Staff" that had unleashed the light. By now, this fight had already repeated itself twenty-nine thousand two hundred and twenty-one times. Aldebaran already understood the assembled rough men better than they understood themselves. By this point, there wasn't a single opponent he'd faced whose name he didn't know. In a way, he could even say he'd come to feel a kind of unbidden closeness to them. Sorry, that's a lie. He was sick to death of these tenacious enemies. Even so—

"...Even knowing how to activate it, that's not a 'Meteor' someone unqualified should be able to use. How the hell did you manage to fire it off?"

"How, do you think, I did it...? Heh heh, worry about it for the rest of your life."

"—. Don't tell me — Hexcel?"

Seeing the blood leaking from eyes and nose, and the veins bulging along the man's neck and forehead, Aldebaran realized this was the side effect of a certain forbidden drug. The substance called "Hexcel" was a bona fide banned drug made from Bokko Fruit, which activates the mana within the body — a substance whose use is prohibited even in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica. — The drug does carry effects like those of a narcotic, but its true nature is the excessive generation of mana through activation of the Gate. Under the effects of that doping, even someone lacking the qualifications could handle the "Star Staff."

"Even so, it'll wreck the inside of your body. Doing something like that..."

"Like I'm the only one gonna hold back?! Rachins, Gaston, all of 'em fighting with everything they got... Reinhard too, y'know? Heh heh, man, I'm in some crazy company..."

With a face pale as death, not even bothering to wipe away the blood-tears and nosebleed that looked oddly vivid against his skin, Camberley looked every bit like a man on the verge of dying — and yet his smiling remark carried none of the recklessness peculiar to someone acting out of despair.

"Every single one of them..."

Beyond Heinkel, still writhing, seared by starlight, Gaston lay collapsed. He too had clashed with Aldebaran head-on, and had dealt him a wound with words rather than fists. There had been so many like that in this fight against the Felt camp. By knocking down every single one of them, one by one, carefully and without fail, Aldebaran had made it here.

Twenty-nine thousand two hundred and twenty-one attempts, layered attempt upon attempt—

"—Helmet bastard, you've never once fought a battle betting your own life on it, have you."

"————"

"I have. Right now, this is it. ...I've got a reason to bet my life on a fight, and I've got the guts to do it. I've just proven that, I have..."

Saying this in a trembling voice, Camberley gripped the "Star Staff" in his hand tightly, trying once more to summon its starlight. But that was impossible.

"Gafuh."

He lacked the mana to control it. — As if punishing him for the attempt, backfire from the "Star Staff" seared Camberley's Gate, and white smoke billowed from his small frame. When someone unqualified went through the activation steps, not only did it fail to fire, there was also a minor penalty. It was a safety valve the "Witch" who made the "Star Staff" had built in, meant to keep children from playing with it. That she thought this counted as a safety valve was proof enough that the "Witch" truly had no understanding of the human heart. In any case, struck by that very safety valve, Camberley's eyes rolled back white and he began to fall backward—

"Ka-kun!"

—and a woman who'd rushed over caught his small body before it could hit the ground. A woman with dark skin, wrapped in a provocative dress — by her features, unmistakably the female boss of the "Flower Prison Garden." She held the unconscious Camberley close, turning sharp, hardened eyes on Aldebaran.

"...No need to glare like that. I don't have the hobby, or the spare energy, to kick a man once he's down."

"You people are..."

"Seven days... no, six now. I'm not planning on stirring up trouble for more than another six days. — Not that any of you would've believed that."

The boss of the "Flower Prison Garden" — Toto, was it? He'd been given plenty of grief by Dolteo, the boss of the "Black Silver Coin," too, but the sharpness in the eyes of this woman clutching her precious man was every bit as keen.

Under that gaze, Aldebaran narrowed his eyes behind his helmet — asking, "You want a piece of this?" — testing Toto's will to fight. Come to think of it, this was the first time in this whole battle he'd run into a woman. He still hadn't seen the face of Grassis, who'd landed the first painful blow, nor of Felt herself, their leader. Oblivious to Aldebaran's little reflection, Toto kept her limbs taut, and jerked her chin at him as if to say, "Go on, then."

At that show of defiance, a wry laugh slipped from Aldebaran, and he turned his back. And then—

"—Ku, ah."

Behind Aldebaran's back, he heard the sound of Toto — who had raised an iron fan to strike — clawing at the steel wire wound around her own throat, failing to tear it free, and falling. At that, Yae, who'd come up beside the fallen Toto, picked up the iron fan that had dropped to the ground, and—

"Wow, there's poison worked into this. Judging by the smell and color... Jiranme, maybe? A fast-acting deadly poison, so someone here was really planning to go all out, weren't they~"

"Don't get mad about it."

"How rude, calling me mad when I'm being so adorable about it! Besides, isn't this Al-sama's fault, in the first place? Turning your back so carelessly on a woman just because she's a woman. Though, I suppose for Al-sama, dying a few more times doesn't really matter either way..."

"—That time was zero deaths. Because you showed up."

Not flattery — genuinely, thanks to Yae, he'd made it through without Toto landing so much as a single surprise blow. Hearing Aldebaran's answer, Yae let out an "hmph" and fell silent, wearing a complicated expression. It had been Aldebaran's own way of thanking her, but after all her complaining that he didn't show enough gratitude, getting this reaction the moment he actually did — it made him wonder why he'd bothered saying anything at all.

"Anyway, this bunch here is all secured too. ...And the old man?"

"He's a bit charred, but very much alive and kicking. That man's not right in the head either, is he?"

"Well, of course not. He's from House Astrea."

"Even someone like that counts as House Astrea?"

Even so, it seemed even a member of House Astrea felt the impact of a "Star Staff" strike. Yae called out to check on Heinkel, who looked to be suffering more than at any other point in this fight, all while shooting Aldebaran a look that suggested "let's leave him behind while we've got the chance." Ignoring Yae's look without a second thought, Aldebaran's attention turned northwest.

"————"

The fight had begun with them being chased south, ever south, through the forest, driven by smoke. Steadily whittling down over five hundred enemies, Aldebaran and his companions had finally circled back. Circled back, and—

"—Huh, funny, feels like the first time we're meeting, somehow, helmet bastard."

"Same here. Guess it's because I've been thinking about you this whole time."

In the plain where they'd first been chased, Aldebaran finally, formally, faced his true enemy — Valga Cromwell — head-on, at the far end of twenty-nine thousand two hundred and twenty-one cycles of trial and error.

△▼△▼△▼△

Sitting heavily cross-legged in the middle of the wide-open plain, Valga Cromwell rested his cheek on a fist propped on his knee. Looking at him again, he really was enormous. Despite sitting, the top of his head was hardly any higher than Aldebaran's own — a testament to just how massive his frame was, though it might also be because of how short his legs were.

"I feel a certain kinship. I've got a complex about my short legs myself."

"I'll have you know, I've never fretted over the length of my legs. Back when I was younger than I am now, though, I did sometimes wish for a bigger body, or longer arms — wanting what I didn't have."

"You still look plenty young to me, gramps. How old are you, anyway?"

"Who knows. I stopped counting past a hundred."

Valga's answer was indifferent, but then, the Giants were famously long-lived. Setting aside the Elf race, said to be unable to die unless killed, the Giants were probably the longest-lived race in this world. And yet even the Giants stood on the brink of extinction, all because they'd angered a single "Witch" — the people of a generation past really had no sense of limits when it came to holding a grudge.

"A swordsman who slaughters left and right just because he likes the taste of 'Dragon' meat, a 'Witch' who tries to wipe out an entire race to the brink of extinction over one grudge, an Elf race catching the fallout from the 'Witch of Envy' — I can't take it."

"Quite the knowledge of history, for a young pup. And I hear you're on friendly terms with the 'Witch of Envy' yourself, aren't you."

"Friendly? Now that's a joke that doesn't land."

Tit for tat, with Valga's remark clearly meant as provocation — even so, Aldebaran couldn't let it pass without objection. That he'd be described as friendly with the "Witch of Envy" — whether it was meant as sarcasm or a joke, it wasn't something he could let slide.

"That woman despises me spectacularly. We've never once even made eye contact."

"...Making eye contact with the 'Witch of Envy,' is it. Now that really is a joke that doesn't land."

"If that's not funny, then what I've got is on a whole other level. Every single one, hitting exactly the moves I hate most... So where's Miss Felt?"

"Pinning that girl down would've been the quickest way for you to settle this, wouldn't it. Did you really think I'd keep her within your reach?"

At that answer, Aldebaran clicked his tongue, and Valga, snorting, glanced past him — toward the forest where their brutal battle had unfolded thus far — and continued, "So then."

"Fifty—no, five hundred, and you killed every last one of them, did you."

"Compared to your kill count back during the civil war, that's cute stuff. And besides... ah, no, never mind that. Forget it."

"————"

Tit for tat again, and Aldebaran forcibly swallowed the words he'd been about to let slip in the flow of the exchange. — In this fight, Aldebaran and his companions had not taken a single life. They'd gone as far as inflicting near-fatal wounds to strip away combat strength, but every one of those opponents should survive, provided they received proper treatment. Still, saying so here wouldn't leave Valga with a good impression.

He'd only be questioned over such an unnatural choice — why bother doing that? Of course, exactly as Yae had suggested to him time and again, there had been more than a few moments where taking the enemy's life outright would have let him get through more easily than sticking to a no-kill policy. Even as that pushed the order of magnitude of his attempts up further, Aldebaran had stubbornly held to that restriction. He'd decided this. Aldebaran had. Never again would he let anyone slip through his fingers — anyone within the scope of what he touched — from the world she had loved. With the sole exception of Natsuki Subaru. And for that, Aldebaran had taken no one's life—

"—You can't bear it anymore, can you, watching a life get snuffed out right in front of you."

—. ——. ————. ——————— huh?

"—Huh?"

"I had my doubts. Ever since I heard that Ezzo and Flam had been beaten at the Pleiades Watchtower, and yet no lives were taken."

"Hey..."

"Having Flam use the 'Divine Protection of Telepathy,' then ambushing the 'Sword Saint' who came in response at the Augria Sand Dunes, where he'd be weakest — that plan itself makes sense. But it gives no reason to keep Ezzo alive. Once she's used the Divine Protection, Flam serves no purpose either."

"Hey, gramps... old man..."

"Cutting off future troubles is only sensible. And that, you didn't do. — You asked me where I'd sent Felt off to, didn't you. I had that girl trace out your group's escape route. And—"

Ignoring Aldebaran's trembling voice, Valga lifted his own arm — the gauntlet fitted with "Dialogue Mirrors" — and tapped a finger against the one whose cover had not yet been shut. The mirrors Aldebaran and his group had broken so far numbered twelve; the one fitted in the gauntlet was the thirteenth and last. Beyond that "Dialogue Mirror" was Felt.

"You and yours haven't taken a single life, and once the bindings come off, no small number of them will still be able to move. Five hundred might be a stretch, but do you have it in you to face down a hundred or more, one more time?"

"You damned old geezer...!!"

Aldebaran ground his teeth behind his helmet at Valga, who spoke with a smile on his face. That the man had been quietly, deliberately preparing to use their no-kill restriction against them, readying to send the incapacitated back into the fight — that came as a shock. But Aldebaran and his companions hadn't been thinking nothing, either. He hadn't merely bound them — he'd broken limbs, made returning to the field as hard as possible—

"I've had every last man dosed with 'Hexcel,' just in case. This time, you won't be fighting people who fear no pain — you'll be fighting people who feel none at all."

"—! Fine, bring it on! A hundred, two hundred, whatever you've got. I'll do the same thing all over again."

He was about to shout that, letting momentum carry him, when— Watching the enraged, raised-voice Aldebaran, Valga suddenly narrowed his eyes. At that gaze, which seemed to peer straight into the helmet itself, Aldebaran couldn't help but catch his breath. And into that, Valga drove his words home.

"I see. — This conversation, it seems, is a first for you."

"————"

For a moment, unable to grasp what that meant, Aldebaran froze. But it was only a moment. The instant a violent chill shot up his spine, Aldebaran finally, belatedly defined the presence before him as a danger perhaps greater even than the "Sword Demon."

"Yae——!!"

At that instant, Aldebaran wrung the shout from his throat and ordered the waiting Yae to attack. Using Yae's Steel Thread Technique, he had to bind Valga Cromwell and shut the mouth that spoke his thoughts aloud. The fastest way to do that was to take his life. But he wouldn't. Absolutely not for the reason Valga had spoken of — he would do it of his own will, and for that reason alone.

"I'll put you to sleep for six days straight."

He hadn't worked out the specific method yet, but that was the best plan available. To carry it out, he ordered Yae into motion—

"—Right There."

What reached his ears was a man's voice, out of place on this battlefield, its very tone carrying a wickedness like a pot of countless poisons simmered down together. The man lying prone in the grass of the plain — a hideous figure covered head to toe in tattoos of a balance scale — turned his gaze to a single point in the forest, his abnormally large right eye rolling grotesquely in its socket. With a wrongness as if he'd forced an eyeball that wasn't his own into place, that gaze skewered — Yae, who had just leapt out to obey Aldebaran's order.

That man — surely Manfred, of "the Balance" — had seen through the shinobi's stealth. And Manfred, pressing a palm against his right eye, caught Yae with his left — the eye whose size, too, didn't match — and,

"Don't Move."

"—!"

The instant that command flew out, a faint disturbance ran through Yae's leaping motion. It was the effect of the "Divine Protection of Coercion," which interferes with others through the sheer weight of words. But if it was the "Divine Protection of Far Sight" that had pinpointed Yae's location, then that meant two different Divine Protections at once.

"A disciple of Typhon's...!"

Ignoring Aldebaran's grinding teeth, Yae, however, severed the coercive force of the Divine Protection in an instant. Her next step recovered its composure, and with a shinobi's mobility she moved to extend steel thread toward Valga. — But into that one moment of disturbance, the transcendent ones intervened.

"—For my sister, Flam."

Just like Manfred, Grassis, hidden in the grass, sprang up and drove a kick into Yae's torso. Yae caught it hastily with crossed arms, but the blow from the young girl who had already mastered the Flow Method was terrifyingly heavy, and Yae's slender frame was sent flying hard.

"I have nothing to do with your older sister!"

"Basically everyone's doing it for Flam."

Under Grassis's blunt reprisal, Yae's attention was split for a moment. Heinkel, whom Yae had left rolling in the forest, still hadn't recovered from that "Star Staff" strike earlier, and even if he had, he couldn't be counted on in this situation. Which meant—

"—Domain, Re-Expansion. Matrix Redefinition."

From here, Aldebaran alone, by his own strength, would have to seal Valga Cromwell's mouth. To that end—

"—You lost your composure there. That's the losing side's undoing, in every age."

The moment he activated his Authority, spreading the sensation of warping the world by his own rules, Aldebaran realized that Valga, before him, had made a motion as if crushing something between his teeth. He knew the gesture, because he himself had the habit of gulping down the poison tucked in his own back teeth. — And in this situation, there was only one thing Valga might be taking.

"Hexcel."

The next instant, an enormous palm spread across Aldebaran's field of view, and he reflexively reeled backward on the spot. He landed on his backside. In his place, the giant frame that rose up seemed swollen a size larger, perhaps from the effect of the forbidden drug he'd taken, and Aldebaran caught his breath.

Aldebaran didn't know Valga Cromwell's direct combat strength. But in this moment, Valga's presence carried a ferocity equal to that of the countless warriors he'd faced so far — the kind of intensity that dwells only in those who have truly staked their life on the line.

It was at the instant that ferocity nearly overwhelmed him that—

"—Aldebaran!!"

A man from whom he'd expected absolutely no contribution let out a shout, pointing at the sky. Aldebaran only caught a glimpse of it at the very edge of his vision, but there was only one reason Heinkel would point at the sky with an urgency that wasn't fear or terror, in this situation.

From beyond the sky, the very time limit that would end this battle was approaching.

"With this—" "—Time's up, is it."

Amid the frantically shifting situation, Valga's voice overlapped with Aldebaran's own words, certain that its end had come. But something was wrong. There wasn't a trace of resignation or urgency in that voice. If anything, it was as if Valga, too, had been waiting for this very moment.

—The very next instant. In Valga's hand, the light that had once lit up the sky flickered to life once more.

△▼△▼△▼△

—At that instant, with his body's Gate activated by Hexcel, Rom-jii crushed the "Night Banisher" mana-stone in his hand and played his final move to bring down the helmet bastard.

"The helmet bastard's behavior doesn't quite add up, whether it's mind-reading or precognition."

He'd ruled out the line that the man was reading minds to counter their tactics. So he'd wondered if the man was reading the future and dodging accordingly, but this too didn't quite fit. The future, after all, exists only at the far end of countless accumulated choices. And not the choices of any one person, either — only at the far end of the countless choices of countless people. To read that out and respond perfectly to it felt, frankly, unrealistic, lacking any grounding in actual possibility. To begin with, even seeing the future shouldn't reveal the location of a mirror-bearer.

But if he'd already seen the outcome once, that changed things. If the helmet bastard's Authority wasn't foreseeing the future, but observing the results of events that had already happened in some future, then his behavior up to now made sense. Probe someone's pocket to identify the mirror-bearer. Confirm whether it succeeded or failed. Move to the next. Repeat that, and the helmet bastard's actions and their results all line up perfectly.

"What in the blazes!!"

Now that it made sense, Rom-jii found himself furious that it made sense at all. Something that made no sense as either mind-reading or precognition — going back after having seen the result — he didn't have a name for it, but in any case, it was an outright cheat. How was he supposed to stop something like that?

"But there is a hole."

To begin with, if it were a truly all-purpose ability that could observe every outcome and respond to all of them, the helmet bastard should have been able to avoid even encountering them here in the first place. He'd successfully countered and trapped Reinhard, but surely dodging Reinhard entirely would have been the far safer option compared to actually fighting him. That he hadn't done so meant there was a reason he couldn't do so.

—The window in which he could go back and redo things, having seen the result, was not, it seemed, very long.

And if he could choose, of his own will, when to use that Authority, then by creating a rhythm of safety and danger, the helmet bastard would naturally want to carve out a safe haven in the gaps between them. — There, Rom-jii would lay his trap. He would take the place the helmet bastard believed was safe and turn it into an inescapable crucible of a snare. And at the final point of the trap he'd assembled, he would place—

"—The 'Divine Dragon,' your ally, whom I've called back."

"————"

The instant that presence appeared beyond the sky, both the helmet bastard's camp and, naturally, the Felt camp were certain of what it meant: the end of the battle. It was, after all, the absence of any single overwhelmingly powerful being that had made this fight even possible in the first place. Should the "Divine Dragon" return and sweep the plain with a single breath, not even five hundred, nor a thousand, resolute rough men could hope to stand against it.

To prove that, a single blast meant to crush the will to fight would be unleashed from the "Divine Dragon." — Without a doubt, this was the breath that would always be loosed, as a ritual meant to bring the fight to its end.

And Rom-jii would make it misfire, under conditions where it couldn't be dodged.

"—The effect of 'Night Banisher' is to make night appear as broad daylight. By extension, it's the power to blanket the true scenery in illusion."

Ordinarily, giving a "Night Banisher" mana-stone deliberate directionality was difficult even for a seasoned mage — but for Rom-jii, who had pushed his own Gate to its limit with "Hexcel," it was possible. Why? — Because this was, originally, one of the plans that had gone unused during the Demi-Human War.

—It was a variant of a scheme that traded life for outcome: targeting a figure the Royal Army could not afford to ignore, and unleashing a great spell to wipe out the enemies gathered around that person, that very person included, all at once.

"Back then, it was a plan to make someone the target, and have magic aimed at that point. But today is different."

The illusion produced by "Night Banisher" spread outward, enveloping both Rom-jii and the helmet bastard. —As a result, the "Divine Dragon" Volcanica, descending from beyond the sky, tasked with the duty of ending this battle, should now be seeing nothing but a vast, empty, uninhabited plain.

Without letting the "Dragon" realize that Rom-jii and the helmet bastard were standing there, he would provoke a misfire. Making it fire on a spot it believed empty — that was Rom-jii, Valga Cromwell's, method of sealing off the helmet bastard's safe haven.

"—!"

The helmet bastard tried instinctively to flee, but Valga Cromwell seized his leg. Realizing there was no escape, the helmet bastard caught his breath and looked up at the sky. In the "Divine Dragon's" eyes, they weren't there at all. Its mouth filled with white light, and the breath was loosed, aimed at the "uninhabited" plain.

"—Felt."

At that instant, the image of his beloved granddaughter flickered through the mind of Valga Cromwell — no, of Rom-jii. From her days as an infant, through her childhood, all the way to the woman she'd become now — it all passed by in an instant, and thinking, so this is the scenery one sees right before death, Rom-jii found an out-of-place smile rising to his lips. For all that this needlessly enormous body should have been filled with hatred and rage, not even the faces of Libre or Sphinx, who had crossed through death's threshold alongside him, so much as flickered through his mind.

With that thought as his last, Rom-jii, along with the helmet bastard, was swallowed by the "Dragon's" breath—

"—Don't you dare screw around!!"

In that instant, a bolt of starlight fired off blasted straight through the side of the "Dragon's" face in the sky, sending the breath that should have been unleashed veering off course, and it detonated instead across the truly empty plain.

"Wha..."

Buffeted by the tremendous plume of smoke and shockwave, Rom-jii couldn't hold his footing and was sent tumbling. Caught up in the same fall, the helmet bastard let out a groan of "Guoah!" as well. The sheer, unbelievable power of that "Dragon's" breath — had things gone according to his design, it would, without question, have driven the helmet bastard into a corner with no escape and broken him there. And yet—

"Had a bad feeling and look what happens. Being ready to die and just wanting to die already are two completely different things, damn it! You old bastard, Rom-jii!!"

Smashing Valga Cromwell's scheme to pieces, and shouting that with her red eyes burning even redder, was his adorable granddaughter, "Star Staff" in hand, tears welling in her eyes.

—And so it was, to his adorable granddaughter, that Valga Cromwell had lost.
