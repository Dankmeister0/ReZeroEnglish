Arc 4 Chapter 10: "The Embodiment of Thirst for Knowledge"

—About fifteen minutes after leaving the building where Roswaal had been convalescing.

"We're here. This is what they call the Tomb. Kinda a shabby-lookin' grave, if you ask me."

So saying, Garfiel jutted his chin toward a weathered ruin at the edge of the Sanctuary. Built up out of stacked stone, its architectural style was about as far from anything magical as could be—crude and primitive. There was no telling how many years had passed since it was built, but between the cracks running through the walls in places and the unnatural density of the creeping ivy, it was unmistakably over a century old.

Only the entrance of the ruin faced the forest; the bulk of the structure had been swallowed up into the woods, so that at a glance there was no telling how large it really was. If this really was a tomb built for the "Witch of Greed" alone, then perhaps it was fair to think of it the way one might think of the pyramids back in Subaru's old world.

"Guess people in power wanting to sleep in some huge grave is the same no matter the era or the world, huh..."

Touching his chin, Subaru let that observation slip out, tilting his head at the sheer scale of the thing. Being something of a live-for-the-moment type, Subaru didn't find his own posthumous reputation a particularly gripping topic. Though admittedly, a certain resignation—the sense that he wasn't important enough to leave a mark on history—helped that attitude along. In any case—

"Okay, we're at the tomb. So what are we supposed to do here?"

Standing beside him, Emilia looked up at the ruin and threw the question at Garfiel with a lost expression. Subaru, harboring the same question, likewise turned his gaze to the back of the blond youth who'd led them here. Garfiel clicked his fangs together and turned around at that.

"The nitty-gritty you can hear from that bastard Roswaal once we get back. For now, all I need Emilia-sama to do is go inside."

"I just need to go in? Not do anything in particular once I'm there?"

"Sun's still up right now, see. Even if you go deep into the tomb, the 'Trial' won't start. Can't get anything ready or nothin'—first thing's gotta be checkin' whether you even qualify."

"Wh-wh-whoa, hold on! You're jumping ahead again. Trial, preparation, qualify—there's been zero explanation on any of that!"

Subaru planted himself between Garfiel and Emilia, who looked liable to shove her inside by force, and called on him to explain himself. Garfiel, however, wrinkled his nose in the same exasperated expression he'd worn several times already this past hour.

"Ahh, c'mon, does it matter? Once she's inside and we get back to Roswaal's place, it'll all make sense. I ain't good at explainin' stuff in order, so if I try it just gets all tangled up and don't make no sense."

"You're basically asking her to sign a contract without reading it—there's no way I can be okay with that, it's terrifying. If you're bad at explaining things in order, then just answer our questions one at a time, carefully."

"Aah... well, fine, I guess. Can't keep at this past sundown, though, so let's make it quick."

Garfiel spread his hands lightly, accepting the proposal. Relieved that they could finally have a proper discussion, Subaru pondered what to ask first—and then,

"This is the 'Tomb'... meaning, the grave of the Witch of Greed, right?"

"That's what I've heard it called. Truth is, nobody knows whose bones are actually buried here. This is the grave of the Witch of Greed—that's what the village folk taught me, and me too, at least."

It was an answer that left something oddly off, but Subaru swallowed that discomfort for now. Then he picked out a few words from Garfiel's earlier remarks that had caught his attention. The two that seemed important were "Trial" and "qualify."

"What's this 'Trial' that's supposed to start inside the tomb? To be honest, based on the last few weeks of experience, I don't have anything remotely like a good impression of that word."

"Relax, I hate bein' tested too. So, well, about the 'Trial'... I don't actually know what it is."

"Hey."

"Don't get mad. I ain't messin' around. I just know it's a thing that happens inside the tomb. And that if you can't clear that 'Trial,' you don't get released from this dead-end proving ground."

"Released... who does?"

"That's where 'qualify' comes in. Whoever qualifies can't leave the proving ground. Not till the 'Trial' is over—the Witch's possessiveness or whatever, it won't let go."

It was a wishy-washy answer, but Garfiel didn't seem to be deliberately twisting his words. He was putting into his own terms what he understood, chewed down as best he could. That it still didn't add up to a coherent answer was probably because even in his own head, it was hazy. Still, piecing together the fragments he'd been given, Subaru tried to form some kind of answer in the shape of a working understanding of the situation. And the answer he arrived at, fitting the scattered pieces together, was—

"Only people who qualify can enter the tomb, and those qualified people can't leave the Sanctuary unless they clear the Trial... is that it?"

"Ahh...? Somethin' like that... I guess?"

"I chewed it down as much as I could and it's still no good, huh..."

Garfiel tilted his head, giving an unreliable reply that suggested he probably didn't fully get it either. Setting aside his attitude toward that for the moment, Subaru glanced at Emilia beside him. Catching his gaze, she voiced her own conclusion about the answer so far.

"When I entered this Sanctuary earlier, my consciousness cut out for a moment... was that what this was about?"

"That was the boundary line, and you passed the point where you could leave, so you got knocked out? No wait, but Otto and I were both fine at the time..."

"That's 'cause you two ain't qualified, obviously."

Garfiel cut in the moment they arrived at an answer for Emilia's sudden loss of consciousness. He pointed at Subaru, then pointed at Emilia with his other hand.

"Half-elf Emilia-sama qualifies. But Subaru here, bein' full-blooded human all the way, don't. So he's free to come and go. But he can't take the Trial."

"Wait wait, wait. So basically, given what you just said, is it this?"

Subaru held his breath, sorting through his thoughts. Then, recalling what Garfiel had said when they first met, and again when he led them to the Sanctuary, he realized something.

"The ones who can take the Trial are half-elves... no, humans and Demi-Humans who are half. And everyone living in this Sanctuary is in that same position."

"—Ah, huh, hadn't mentioned that part yet, had I."

Hearing Subaru's answer, Garfiel nodded, satisfied, and blinked. In the next instant, his open eyes tinted gold, his pupils narrowing into something close to a carnivorous beast's. The tips of his fangs seemed, somehow, to lengthen, and the claws on his raised hands sharpened to something like blades. It looked, for a moment, as though his small frame had grown a size larger—no, that wasn't an illusion. His short golden hair had, before Subaru knew it, grown long enough to cover his back, and golden fur the same color as his hair now covered the skin of his exposed arms and legs.

"I got a fair bit o' the blood left in me, see. 'Throwback' ain't nothin' hard for me."

"...Whoa. Can I pet you?"

Struggling not to let his shock show in his comment, Subaru wiggled his fingers to hide his agitation. But his request was flatly cut off as Garfiel shifted back to his original form. Having witnessed his transformation, Emilia let out a breath and stepped forward.

"So then, this village really is a gathering of Demi-Humans..."

"To put it right, it's a gathering of mixed-bloods—Demi-Humans and humans mixed together. Rounded up on purpose, all sorts of races stuck in similar spots. That's probably why they call that bastard Roswaal a 'Demi-Human enthusiast.'"

"So that's why Roswaal did that. For me, this place is..."

At that, Emilia fell silent, pressing a hand to her lips, sinking into thought. For his part, this new information wasn't light for Subaru either. In other words, the people living here were, details aside, people who shared Emilia's origins. People capable of understanding the pain she carried—the past of being shunned, of being cast out. It might turn out to be nothing more than mutual wound-licking for her. But faced with the possibility of finding others who could share that wound-licking, what would she feel?

Even if he thought he understood and tried to touch her wound, Subaru—who had never tasted the same pain—didn't know how to heal it without widening it. That fact gnawed at him, bitterly, frustratingly.

"Wasn't where I expected this to go, but I've got a rough grasp of the village situation and the qualification thing now. So that leaves... the problem is the Trial. You said you don't know what it involves, but at least it happens after sundown, right?"

"That's the story. Details, I don't know either. But makin' sure whether she qualifies is why we came here now, I figure. If you show up at night and the Trial just starts, you'd be goin' in blind."

Gesturing at the ruin with his thumb and at Emilia with his chin, Garfiel laid out their present purpose. Nodding along, Subaru quietly looked toward the tomb, its mouth open and waiting for them. Thick ivy grew wild, and a foul-aired half-darkness beckoned from within. Combined with the gravity of the word "Trial," it was hard to believe this would end as a simple stroll through some old ruin. Above all, sending Emilia into a place that might hold danger—that fact was, for Natsuki Subaru, unbearable.

"Sorry, Ram. Looks like I can't take your advice right off the bat."

"Did you say something?"

"My heart's about ready to burst just thinking about sending Emilia-tan in first. So how about, as a check and as a bit of a sacrificial offering, Garfiel goes charging in first?"

At Subaru's suggestion, finger raised, Garfiel blinked blankly for a moment before breaking into a grin. Slapping his knee again, producing another round of that dry sound,

"Ain't that usually the part where you say you'll go yourself, tryin' to look cool?"

"I'd love to say that and look cool, believe me, but thinking about survival odds if something actually happens, you're better suited for it than me. Seems like you could stomp straight through the ground or whatever and come back out fine. You're the strongest, after all."

"Ohh? Well, yeah, guess I am the strongest. Trial or whatever, don't matter what kinda danger comes rainin' down, 'Penny-penny don't budge'!"

It wasn't entirely clear what exactly he intended to protect, but with Garfiel rubbing under his nose in high spirits, Subaru decided against raining on his parade. But Garfiel's good mood evaporated almost immediately with a "but—"

"Sorry, but I can't go inside. Contract, see."

"...Contract?"

"Ahh, damn annoying thing it is. And it ain't even like I made it myself."

Kicking at the dirt, Garfiel spat the words out with a click of his tongue. This wasn't the sort of joke or idle remark—as a matter of fact, it seemed he really couldn't go inside. What would happen if he broke the prohibition—that wasn't a question he could ask in front of Emilia, who held contracts in such high regard. In any case, this left them boxed in on every side. Sending Emilia in alone was out of the question, and the Garfiel-as-scout plan had been nipped in the bud right out of the gate. That left only one remaining option.

"Give me a sec, I'm gonna go find Otto, wait here?"

"By the time you do that, the sun'll be down. —It's fine. I'll go."

Subaru had been about to set up yet another sacrifice, but Emilia gently shot the idea down. Having apparently steeled her resolve, her gaze was fixed on the tomb's entrance, her indigo eyes holding a wariness for whatever might be waiting inside. She too, it seemed, was anticipating that something less than peaceful awaited within, given both the weight of the word "Trial" and the ominous stage of a "Witch's tomb." Sharing that same anxiety and unease, and yet lacking the strength to hold her hand back—Subaru found himself pathetic.

"What if I just go a little way in first... no, even just around the entrance, to check it out first?"

"You'd best not, I think? Subaru ain't qualified. If someone who ain't invited by the Witch tries to go into her tomb, they end up like Roswaal."

"Like Roswaal, meaning... don't tell me his injuries were from going in here?"

Recalling the sight of Roswaal wrapped head to toe in bandages, Subaru suppressed his shock and looked up at Garfiel. Arms crossed, Garfiel nodded, confirming it.

"Someone without qualification steppin' in at night, yeah, that's what happens. Him bein' who he is, he probably got off easy with just that—normal unqualified folk might burst if they went in."

"So it wasn't that someone did that to him—that's what that meant..."

Now Subaru finally understood the meaning behind Roswaal's evasive way of putting things. He'd said he hadn't been hurt by any particular person—and this was exactly what he'd meant. But that raised a different question. Why had Roswaal entered the tomb in the first place? —An unqualified man. Surely he, of all people, would have known that about himself.

"...I really am going to check inside first."

Setting the question of Roswaal's suspicious behavior aside for later, Subaru, who had been looking down, reached that conclusion. Hearing his answer, both Emilia and Garfiel looked momentarily stunned.

"Oi oi, were you even listenin'? I just said it's dangerous if someone unqualified goes in. Roswaal endin' up like that was 'cause it was nighttime, but daytime ain't exactly safe either."

"That's right. It's dangerous, so please don't, Subaru? I'll be fine, I'm sure. I've never really been grateful for it, but maybe being a half-elf will actually be useful here..."

"I'm glad you're both worried, really."

The two of them tried to hold him back, especially Emilia, who tugged at his sleeve. Subaru turned a gentle look on her and gently pried her fingers loose.

"If we calmly divide up roles, this is the obvious way to go, right? Whoever goes inside, the danger's the same either way. It's just that from what we've heard, there's a slightly higher chance it's more dangerous for me. So after that, it comes down to what each of us is actually capable of."

"Capable of?"

"If, worst case, something dangerous happens inside and someone gets hurt, I can't heal Emilia-tan. Unless Garfiel turns out to be secretly amazing and a top-tier healing mage."

"Wounds like that, mostly you just spit on 'em and they heal up."

"Says the suspect in his statement to the police... something like that. Whether it's me or Emilia-tan, if there's a chance of getting hurt, I want to keep Emilia-tan, who can use healing magic, in reserve as insurance."

With Garfiel declaring that shamelessly off to the side, Subaru worked on persuading Emilia. Subaru's reasoning shook her resolve somewhat, but on the crucial point she seemed to decide she couldn't concede, shaking her head with a "but—"

"A serious injury... especially one that threatens your life, I can't heal that. Puck won't even respond to me, there are limits. Even Roswaal, calm as he seems now..."

"Yeah, well, that wound was pretty dangerous, wasn't it. ...Even so, well, trust in my stubborn refusal to die, okay? I think I'm pretty high up there in tenacity, even in this world."

Smiling at Emilia as she pressed her point, Subaru answered with words that, for once, weren't a joke at all. As a matter of fact, he thought to himself that there probably weren't many people as bad at giving up as he was. If given infinite chances to try again, he probably would try again, infinitely. No matter how many times his heart was broken, no matter how many times it was shattered, he kept struggling to reach the answer he sought. That was, for Natsuki Subaru—

"How about I make you a promise? That'd put you at ease, right? I'll definitely come back to you, Emilia-tan. I won't leave your side or anything."

"—I'll do it."

Holding out his pinky finger, half-joking, he got a response far more eager than he'd expected. Surprised, Subaru watched as Emilia held out her own pinky in kind, tilting her head slightly.

"What do I do with my finger for this?"

"Huh? Ah, right, you link your pinky with mine like this... whoa, Emilia-tan's finger is so thin and white and cute...!"

Linking their fingers together, Subaru was moved by the unexpected contact. Then, following her indigo eyes as they urged him on, he gave a small cough.

"Pinky promise, cross my heart, hope to die, swallow a thousand needles if I lie—" "Pinky cut!"

Their pinkies parted at the same moment, sealing the promise between Subaru and Emilia. This time, it was a promise made with full understanding of just how heavy such a promise was to Emilia. There was no way he could treat it as lightly as he once might have.

"Okay, I'll go take a quick look inside. I'm planning to keep calling out while I look around, basically, so keep calling back to me from outside so I don't get lonely."

"Can't tell if you're bein' cool or pathetic, you."

"I'm just cautious. Even though I'm apparently just going to casually break Ram's advice..."

The latter half trailed off into a mutter under his breath, and inwardly Subaru bowed his head in apology to the pink-haired girl. The Witch's obsession—that ominous phrase she'd spoken—was not something he wanted Emilia to hear. She would surely insist on going in herself all the more firmly if she did.

"Subaru. If you think it's dangerous, please, really, turn back right away."

Clasping her hands in front of her chest, Emilia saw him off with worried eyes. Flashing her an original thumbs-up with his pinky raised, Subaru bared his teeth in a grin and then turned forward—toward the tomb.

Stepping over the ivy trailing across the ground, he strained his eyes into the darkness that swallowed everything just a few meters past the entrance. The tomb was filled with stillness; for now, at least, there was no sense of vengeful voices or vicious creatures lurking within. Still, there was no real way of knowing what waited beyond the dark.

"Ahh, whatever. Well, here goes. Nothing ventured, nothing gained. Not that I need a tiger cub or anything!"

As a devoted petting enthusiast, he did feel some desire to dote on a baby tiger to his heart's content, but that wasn't something he'd risk danger for. In any case, as if infected by Garfiel's habit, he rallied himself with an idiom and, resolve set, stepped into the tomb.

And then, the instant his foot touched the tomb's cold floor—

"—Eh?"

A strange sensation, as if the ground beneath his feet had been swept away. Startled, Subaru looked down and lost his voice. —The floor had vanished.

"Wai, hold... no way, seriously..."

—The flag had been collected way too fast.

The footing he'd meant to step onto simply wasn't there, and there was nothing to catch his pitching-forward body. He shot a hand out on reflex, but it touched neither wall nor floor, and Subaru's body was sucked straight down into the darkness below—

"Aaaaaaah—!?"

Down, down, endlessly down, he fell into the depths of what felt like a bottomless abyss.

—About ten minutes after waking at the bottom of the abyss.

Wandering through a darkness that seemed to lead nowhere in particular, Subaru had finally arrived somewhere, and there he met a girl. And now, prompted by her questions, he'd recounted everything up to this point.

"So anyway, after falling I wandered around shaking with loneliness and a growling stomach, and found you. ...That satisfy you?"

"Mm, satisfied indeed. It seems you're more interesting than I expected."

Pressing the back of her hand to her lips, the girl standing before him let out a low chuckle, and Subaru watched her with wary eyes. His legs were tensed, coiled, ready to bolt at any moment, and his hands opened and closed, ready to pin her down the instant it was needed. Though, admittedly, such a crude ambush plan of Subaru's was—

"There's no need to be so wary, is there? Besides, you yourself have judged that your odds of winning against me are slim, haven't you? Bravery and recklessness are similar, but not the same thing."

"Sorry, but losing and then submitting isn't really my style. And you told me not to be wary, but... can I really afford not to be, with someone who's introduced herself as the Witch of Greed?"

"Fair point. You're quite right about that. That was an oversight on my part."

Receiving Subaru's answer, full of irritation and rebellious spirit, the girl—who had called herself Echidna—still didn't drop her composure. It wasn't the sort of thing you could chalk up to mere confidence, or amusement at the futile struggling of a weaker opponent. It was something transcendent, on another plane entirely. It was like reading a manga and looking down at the characters drawn within from some outside vantage. To her, Subaru wasn't even standing on the same stage.

Precisely because of that, Subaru faced her with the utmost caution. Here was a person radiating a pressure that outstripped even the White Whale. And that person had called herself the "Witch of Greed." Whether that was true or not was a trivial matter. The problem was her overwhelming presence—so overwhelming that even Subaru couldn't simply laugh it off. And yet, casting a sidelong glance at Subaru, sweat beading on his brow, she said,

"Still, being treated so coldly really does hurt, you know. As you can see, I'm just a frail little girl. Being looked at like that by a boy isn't nothing to me."

"That 'girl' of yours got some furigana reading 'death flag' next to it? I'll have you know, the warning sensor in my head has been going haywire since a while back."

It was a skill born of the many "deaths" Subaru had experienced since coming to this world. Despite having that skill, his death count only kept climbing regardless, but the ever-present resolve never to feel that again made him keenly aware of that wariness. By that measure, the danger level of the girl before him rivaled even Petelgeuse's. But—

"This won't do for proper conversation, will it. Can't be helped, I suppose. —Well then, how about this arrangement?"

So saying, Echidna raised her right hand lightly before her face. The moment Subaru caught his breath at the motion, she snapped her fingers at the tip of her raised hand. A light sound rang out, and immediately after—the world in Subaru's field of vision transformed completely.

The cold stone space that, until a moment ago, had made him believe he was at the bottom of the tomb, vanished, and in its place unfolded a field of grass swaying green in the wind—atop, specifically, a small rise of a hill.

"Wha—!?"

"Why not come over here instead of playing around over there?"

Laughing at Subaru as he looked around in stunned bewilderment, Echidna sat in one of the chairs surrounding a white table set atop the hill, gesturing with her hand toward the seat across from her, inviting him over. At a loss, Subaru approached her, shrinking back, and took the seat facing her. On the table sat teacups with steam rising from them, and as Subaru looked at them in silence, she said,

"No need to worry, there's nothing dangerous in there. If you like, I could drink first, to demonstrate. Though if you're doubting whether poison works on a Witch, that wouldn't really prove anything, would it."

"...I give up. Ever since I stepped in here, my common sense has been getting overturned nonstop. What is this place? Can you use teleportation magic too or something?"

The teleportation magic Subaru had previously experienced had been Beatrice's doing. Cast out of the Forbidden Archive by her hand, he'd been sent flying all the way to the livestock shed in Arlam Village. According to Julius, that was supposedly magic on a level considered lost to history, but if the person before him really was a Witch, it wasn't so surprising after all.

"Teleportation... ah, the shadow magic. No, if that's what you're thinking, you're mistaken. That magic has all sorts of drawbacks. It's not something I much care to use. What just happened was a bit of an amusement. To some extent, I have free rein here. This is my castle, after all."

"Your... castle?"

Furrowing his brow at Echidna's words, Subaru looked around once more. The wind-swept grassland stretched on endlessly, and no matter which direction he strained his eyes, there was nothing to be found all the way to the horizon. Setting aside whether such a blank expanse of land could exist in reality, it was, admittedly, a rather fantastical sight. Conscious of that, Subaru swallowed and then shrugged, putting on a smile.

"Sorry to say, no matter where I look, I don't see so much as a hut, let alone a castle. What, is your castle under renovation right now? Or did they repossess everything but the chair and table over unpaid debts?"

"Fufufu. You really are amusing. Standing before me and still cracking that many jokes—aside from other Witches, I could count on one hand the number who've managed it. I never imagined that number would grow after my death."

Laughing at his joke, Echidna counted off on her fingers, seeming quite pleased to add Subaru to that tally. Subaru, meanwhile, frowned at catching a word in what she'd said that he couldn't let slide. She had, indeed, just said "after my death."

"If you really are the Witch of Greed, then by my understanding, you should be dead. That's the whole reason I came in here—to visit your grave, in fact."

"How thoughtful of you, thank you. If you'd like to offer flowers, please leave them at the entrance. As I don't indulge in drink, if you're bringing an offering, something sweet would be appreciated."

"There's a whole offering culture in this world?... Sorry, I didn't bring any souvenirs, and I forgot to buy flowers too. You'll have to make do with my smiling face."

A smile like a field of blooming flowers—though of the poisonous variety. Displaying it for her, Subaru drew a pleased chuckle from Echidna's throat. Then she brought the cup on the table to her lips, wetting her throat, and said,

"I never enjoyed tea this much even while alive. Death does have its merits after all, it seems. There's no end to the new discoveries."

"I'm starting to doubt whether this even counts as a conversation between us anymore. ...Damn it, fine, I'll drink it. I'll drink it!"

Feeling foolish for treating an apparently harmless opponent with such prickly suspicion, Subaru snatched the cup off the table and downed it. It wasn't water, wasn't tea, wasn't black tea—some strange, indefinable flavor. Not unpleasant, though.

"Drinking down what a Witch offers you—quite brave of you."

"Ha. Can't afford to be scared, not after coming this far. Besides, if you wanted to kill me, I'd be a pile of ash by the next panel anyway. No point being wary of a cup of tea."

Waving a hand, he set the emptied cup down on the table and added "thanks for the meal" before continuing,

"Wasn't great, wasn't bad, but what kind of tea was that, anyway?"

"Something brewed within my castle. To put it plainly, it's my bodily fluid."

"What the hell are you making me drink!?"

Kicking his chair back as he shot to his feet, Subaru struggled desperately to bring back up what he'd just swallowed. But Echidna only chuckled at his exaggerated reaction.

"How rude. I don't think I'm all that bad to look at, myself."

"Even a pretty girl's bodily fluids, I don't want to drink without being warned first! Actually, even if I was warned, I don't want to drink anything called 'bodily fluid'! My tastes are normal, thank you!"

He wasn't the type to get excited over secretions like saliva or sweat, he thought—probably. Quietly hiding away the thought that, well, if it were Emilia's or Rem's, maybe he wouldn't mind so much, he said,

"Damn it, can't spit it out now. —Hey, this isn't bad for my body or anything, right?"

"Relax. It's about as absorbable by the body as anything could be. It is a bodily fluid, after all."

"That's not making it sound any better, quit that face!"

Echidna's faintly self-satisfied expression left Subaru exasperated. Unfazed by his continued protests, she tilted the cup further, cool as ever, and added, "Still, though—"

"You really are a curious individual. The proof of it is that you can stand before me like this, quite normally."

"What's that supposed to mean. That if I'm not blinded by how pretty you are, that's abnormal or something? I'll have you know, I'm my own personal ideal of a beautiful girl at all times, feasting my eyes on myself. So the number of times I look at you and think 'wow, kinda cute' is pretty low, comparatively."

"No, ordinarily, people throw up just standing in front of me. Amusing, isn't it?"

"There's nothing amusing about that at all!?"

Continuing an exchange that kept producing nothing but anxiety-inducing phrases, Subaru took another look at the girl seated in the chair.

Hair and skin white as snow. Her black clothing looked almost like mourning attire, lending an odd allure to her beauty, still touched with youthfulness. A beautiful woman in mourning clothes has a certain magic to it, he thought—and yet the ever-present, never-fading pressure kept her existence feeling like a threat all the same.

"Now, then—"

Looking up at Subaru, still unable to let his guard down, she set the cup she'd emptied down on the table and traced its rim with a finger.

"Talking like this is a fresh sort of pleasure for me... but I imagine it isn't the same for you, is it? You must have things you want to say, things you want to ask?"

"...That's... right. Right! I completely forgot, swept up in the atmosphere, but you're right. You're... no, before that, where even is this? Is this really inside the tomb?"

For Subaru, this was a place connected to the fall he'd taken right after stepping into the tomb. He'd been willing to believe it was the bottom of the tomb up until that dim space a moment ago. But now, invited into this grassy field, even that felt questionable. At Subaru's question, Echidna lightly smoothed her white hair with her hand.

"That question is half right and half wrong. Your body is, without a doubt, inside the tomb. But your mind is inside my castle. To put it plainly, this is inside a dream."

"A dream...? But I don't have any memory of your face, not enough to dream about you."

"Being inside a dream doesn't necessarily mean it has to be your dream, does it. This is my castle—in other words, my dream. Don't you know a space resembling this one already?"

Subaru held his breath at Echidna's probing question. Then, shaking his head slightly,

"Wh-what makes you think something like that..."

"No certainty to it. I simply had a feeling. Your manner reminded me of someone averting their eyes from something they know."

"...It's true, I don't know. But what you're saying isn't wrong, either."

It wasn't a harsh way of putting it, but her words made Subaru feel as if he were being indicted. And indeed, he couldn't control the wave of conflicting emotions warring within his chest. Echidna's words weren't wrong, but neither was Subaru's answer a lie. When told this was inside a dream, Subaru had felt surprise and, at the same time, a sense of recognition—as though his heart already understood the sensation as something familiar. Why he should feel that way, though, he couldn't find anywhere in his memory, no matter how he searched.

"Fine, I'll accept for now that this is inside your dream. So, how do I get out?"

"The way to wake from a dream is either to decide to wake, or to be woken from outside. Though, trying to work on it from outside won't do much good, since my body no longer exists, and waking oneself from inside someone else's dream is difficult. If I don't decide to wake you, I don't imagine you'll be able to wake at all."

"—! Then, don't tell me you're..."

Subaru shuddered at Echidna's flat, matter-of-fact words. The meaning of "her castle" took on a far more vivid shape. Subaru's soul, trapped here, now rested entirely in the palm of her hand. Ram's talk of the Witch's obsession—those words gained a fresh weight of reality.

"You're planning to keep me from getting back outside...?"

Bearing the utmost caution, Subaru threw out words that might create a fatal crack with a Witch—understanding full well that if she revealed her true nature, he would stand no chance at all. Echidna let out a small breath at his question.

"No, not particularly. If you want to go back, I'll send you back, you know? It's not as though I called you here—you came in entirely on your own."

"What am I supposed to do with all this tension you keep dismissing? Mr. Serious over here isn't even breathing anymore."

"Mr. Serious can't stand before me the way you can, you know. Probably off vomiting in the bushes somewhere."

Deflating at Echidna casually spitting venom, Subaru sighed. In the end, what did she even want from talking with him? Even after their brief exchange, he couldn't get a read on her character. Though, expecting to fully understand someone called a Witch in this short a time was probably asking too much to begin with.

"Anyway, if you can send me back, please do. There's a girl up there who's probably worried about me. Rather than sitting here drinking your bodily fluids, I'd rather go put her mind at ease."

"That's fine, but are you sure that's all right with you?"

"What's that supposed to mean."

"Leaving my presence—that's what I mean. An opportunity to hear the words of the Witch of Greed—not many people, besides you, could ever hope to obtain such a thing."

Hearing that, Subaru registered the meaning of her words for the first time. That was right. That was exactly right. So fixed on her as a threat, Subaru had overlooked something crucial. If she really was the Witch of Greed, a being who had truly lived under that name—

"You... do you know the answer to what I want to know?"

"Asking me, of all beings, where knowledge may be found—is that it?"

At Subaru's strained words, Echidna let out another low chuckle. She laughed, but that laugh was the most delighted one yet, and it pressed down on Subaru with more weight than any before it. The air warped, and the blue-sky atmosphere of the grassland began collapsing all at once. The sky split, the grassland burst into flame, and the world beyond the horizon crumbled away.

Feeling a tremor that shouldn't have existed, Subaru scrambled to grab hold of the one thing that still seemed solidly real—the table. The instant he touched it, it dispersed in his hands like sand turning to dust. And then,

"You truly are a fascinating being."

As Echidna lifted her face, the scenery around her transformed, and ghastly patterns wrapped the world in their coils. Darkness spread, its tendrils reaching out and wrapping themselves around Subaru's entire body. He struggled desperately to escape that horror, but the world's collapse had already spread to right beside the two of them. There was no footing left to flee to. And just like that, the world continued to vanish, and—

"For an exchange of questions and answers, this much space will suffice. To know what you wish to know. That desire—that greed—I shall affirm it."

All that remained between the two of them was just enough space for the chairs they'd been sitting in. Close enough to reach out and touch, a world existing only for the sake of two people seated in chairs, talking. Everything beyond that had been lost entirely. At the bottom of that footing-less darkness, there was no visible limit. Surely, and this was no joke, if one fell in, there would be no coming back.

Subaru's spine turned to ice at the realization, but before him, seated in her chair, Echidna was in high spirits. She clapped her hands, gazing at Subaru with shining eyes.

"Now then, what would you like to hear? So long as it is something knowable, I shall answer anything. Shall it be of Daphne, the 'Witch of Gluttony,' who birthed beasts unlike gods to save the world from famine? Of Carmilla, the 'Witch of Lust,' who gave emotion to things not human, wishing to fill the world with love? Of Minerva, the 'Witch of Wrath,' who lamented a world filled with strife, and struck down and healed all people alike? Of Sekhmet, the 'Witch of Sloth,' who drove the Dragon beyond the Great Waterfall, for the sake of bringing peace, and peace alone? Of Typhon, the 'Witch of Pride,' who went on judging the guilty with the innocence and mercilessness of childhood?"

An unfamiliar recitation of history—no, one that, in the present world, ought not to survive in anyone's knowledge at all. Before Subaru, left speechless by the sheer scale of information pouring in, Echidna kept laughing.

"The embodiment of thirst for knowledge, who sought after every conceivable wisdom, leaving even lingering regrets in the world of the dead? The 'Witch of Greed,' Echidna—is that who you mean?"

Pointing at herself, speaking as if mocking her own self, she went on, "And then—"

"The one who destroyed all those other Witches, and made the entire world her enemy as fuel for herself—the 'Witch of Envy'—her, is that who you mean?"
