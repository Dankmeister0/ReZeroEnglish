Arc 1 Chapter 14: "Fourth Time's the Charm"

"Wallet, check. Cell phone, check. Corn potage and cup ramen, no problem. Jersey and sneakers, no tears. And of course..."

He flipped up the hem of his jersey and craned his neck back, twisting awkwardly to check his own back.

There wasn't a scar to be found anywhere around his waist or the center of his back, and the emergency situation of two knives sprouting from his spine hadn't occurred either.

"Phew, good. A scar on the back is seriously a swordsman's disgrace. As someone who was at least nominally enrolled in the kendo club, I may have lost my way as a human being, but I refuse to lose my way as a swordsman."

Grumbling to himself, he patted down his unharmed back over and over. He found a single long hair growing near his shoulder, and after a valiant struggle managed to pluck it out, finally letting out a breath.

"Which means this is, well... hard to believe, but..."

He blew the plucked hair away with his exhaled breath, touched his chin with his free hand, and looked around the street.

He hadn't changed location — he was just a little ways from the vegetable stand, in the corner of the same row of market stalls. The shifty-looking proprietor selling suspicious pots and plates eyed him with annoyance, but Subaru, arms crossed and leaning against the wall, showed no sign of adjusting his behavior.

The sun hung high, the wind was gentle. The main street bustled with crowds, and every so often a lizard-drawn carriage rumbled through, pulled by its lizard. Subaru had more or less gotten used to the dust in the air by now — the most he did was give a light wave of his hand in front of his face.

His body showed not a single trace of injury, not even a hint, and the corn potage he should have finished eating was somehow available to enjoy all over again, a bargain deal if there ever was one. And as for the feel of his chin under his fingers — smooth, without a single snag to catch on—

"Man, forgetting my own specialty — that's pretty pathetic of me."

He possessed the craftsman's skill of gauging roughly how much time had passed by the growth of stubble on his own chin. Subaru, brimming with a self-satisfied confidence in this rough-and-ready technique that only applied a certain male hormonal privilege, was now certain of one thing.

—That the stubble on his chin showed no change whatsoever from the moment right after he'd shaved, before heading to the convenience store.

"Which means, well..."

He brought the hand that had been touching his chin forward and snapped his fingers, making sure the gesture was visible to the crowd — including the stall owner — who were all watching him.

"—Sounds like I go back to square one every time I die."

He settled on that as his conclusion — the very idea he'd dismissed as ridiculous only moments ago.

"Return by Death, huh... I mean, this is basically an ability that says 'lose, then die.'"

In the source material, the ability was more like "defeat, then rewind" — but given the pattern of losing and then coming back, his current state seemed to fit the wording better the other way around.

"Put more properly, this would be... 'time regression,' I guess?"

Limited in scope, sure, but it seemed he was pulling off something like what people called "time travel." In short, Subaru had become the stuff of dreams — a "time traveler."

Honestly, up until just a few minutes ago, this had already surfaced as a possibility, but he'd shied away from it given how demanding the conditions for it seemed. He'd found it easier to believe in the scenario of some wandering superhuman healer happening to pass by — that felt like it required fewer leaps of logic.

Though, when it came to the conditions he'd dismissed as impossible—

"Time magic is like, the strongest-tier ability, so it's weird for me to have it right from the start. And besides, time regression might be a pipe dream, but there's no way it's actually possible. Common sense, right?"

The moment he said it, he found himself wondering just what exactly was "common sense" about "getting summoned to another world" in the first place.

Once he thought about it that way, the resolve with which he'd been stubbornly denying it all up until now started to wilt.

"And on top of that, if I assume I've been doing this 'Return by Death' thing, all the weirdness up till now lines up perfectly, neat and tidy..."

His first death was, in essence, the time he and Satella had gone into the Loot House together.

His unguarded stomach sliced open, unable to even cry out a warning, dragging Satella down with him — an inexcusable, worst-case scenario with no room for excuses.

And his second death would be when old man Rom and Felt were killed, and after his own futile resistance, he'd been butchered by Elsa. During that first "Return by Death," he'd assumed the difference in the sun's height meant a different day entirely — but in fact, he'd simply been sent back to "that same point on the same day."

His third death he'd experienced just moments ago, barely ten-odd minutes back.

There could hardly be a death more deserving of the term "died for nothing."

To think he'd be killed by some minor mook this early on. It was like some poorly made adventure game that rigged a bad-end event behind every single choice — leaving nothing but a rotten aftertaste.

"I mean, that makes it — I've died three times in the span of, what, half a day..."

Considering that a normal life only affords you one death, dying three times in a mere half-day overturned common sense in every conceivable way.

He'd figured he'd lived a fairly ordinary seventeen years, but thinking that he'd apparently been dodging life-and-death odds at a rate of seventeen years times three hundred sixty-five days times three within that span was, in its own way, a bit moving.

"Or maybe I'm just hopelessly bad at staying alive."

He still hadn't shaken off the lukewarm-bathwater air of his old world, and his body simply couldn't keep up with the rapid-fire instant-death events of this one.

Part of the reason he kept dropping dead so casually was probably that he kept waltzing into places he already knew full well were dangerous.

"Given the cause and effect between the first and second times... I've probably been killed by Elsa twice."

The first time, it must have been Elsa lurking in the darkness of the Loot House.

Which meant the corpse of the large, elderly man lying there really had been old man Rom. Though he couldn't picture a scenario in that room where Rom gets killed with nothing to do with Subaru's own slip of the tongue.

"Felt was inside the storehouse at that point... I couldn't confirm that far, could I."

Naturally, Rom must have gone along with Felt's negotiation.

Which meant Felt's involvement was indispensable to however Rom ended up killed. Matching it against how the second round played out, it seemed likely that Felt's greed caused the negotiation to fall apart.

"Maybe she threw out some needless provocation and got silenced for it."

That was exactly the kind of thing a stubborn, hot-tempered girl like Felt would do.

And with that silencing already carried out, Subaru and Satella arriving at just the wrong moment was probably the gist of the first round.

"The second time's simpler. I just happened to be there for that same silencing scene."

Which made him wonder — after he and the others were killed, did Satella end up visiting the storehouse after all?

He knew her magical skill, but whether that murderer would give her the time to chant a spell felt like a pretty dicey proposition. Nine times out of ten, the odds would favor Elsa.

"Anyway, I've been killed by the same person twice now. Simply put, Elsa's a death-flag character — run into her and you're confirmed dead. Final answer. And besides..."

What need is there to work out a countermeasure, Subaru heard his own detached inner voice ask.

That's right — what need was there to even think about what would happen if he ran into Elsa?

The only place he was likely to encounter her, put simply, was the "Loot House."

And the reason for going there at all was "Satella's insignia" — and the goal of recovering Satella's insignia was rooted in "repaying the debt of having been saved by her."

But for Subaru, sent back by "Return by Death" all the way to right after his summoning, the whereabouts of that debt of gratitude had literally vanished into the far reaches of time.

Satella's icy reaction when he'd made contact with her in the third timeline said as much.

She hadn't known Subaru. Which was, in and of itself, proof that in this current timeline, Satella and Subaru had no relationship whatsoever — meaning the debt he owed her had ceased to exist.

So then, shouldn't he just forget all about the insignia, clean and simple, and avoid the bad-end flag of running into that threat again?

He had no idea why a situation like "Return by Death" had been set up for him, but he did have the ability to know what was coming. Avoid the landmines you can avoid — that was the correct move.

It was practically standard procedure for any time-travel story. Otherwise, what would even be the point?

"Lucky for me, I already know the cell phone's worth money. I don't need to rely on old man Rom — if I just find some shop that seems halfway trustworthy and sell it off, I can put together some starting funds."

He wasn't sure exactly how much twenty-plus gold coins amounted to, but it seemed like enough to cover several nights at a decent inn. After that, all he had to do was sharpen his fangs and wait for the day to sink them in.

"Well, I've got zero plan yet on what exactly I'm gonna sink my teeth into."

He had no particularly outstanding knowledge, nor any deeply devoted hobby. Broad and shallow knowledge of everything — that was the modern stance, and Subaru, without exception, was one of those modern people.

"Guess once I sell the phone, before that money runs out, I'll have to get myself hired as some kind of errand boy at a shop or something..."

He was more than a little worried about whether he, with zero work experience, could actually pull that off, but even a somewhat shady labor environment had to beat getting cut down by a blade. At the very least, he wouldn't have to face dying three times in half a day.

"If it comes to that, easy enough. Gotta get moving before the sun goes down. Right, old man?"

"I figured you were muttering to yourself over there, but now what's this all of a sudden? Don't go asking me 'right,' I don't know anything about it, me."

The proprietor running the stall next to him answered with an annoyed look, in response to Subaru's bid for agreement.

A turban wrapped around his head, selling what might've been pots or plates. Regardless of Subaru muttering away beside him, the setup didn't exactly look like the kind of stall that drew in customers.

"Maybe I should sell my stuff like this too... though, would it even sell for much lumped in with these sad-looking pots? What do you think, old man?"

"Don't go calling other people's merchandise sad while you're looking at it! What is with you, kid!"

"I'm a man destined to make his name as a tycoon someday. Call me the Cell-Phone Nouveau Riche. ...Hey, doesn't your head get itchy wrapped up like that? Why do you wear it? Are you bald?"

"That makes no sense and it's incredibly rude besides! Go on, get out of here, you're ruining my business!"

The proprietor's manner, faintly effeminate in speech, was decidedly cold.

The hearts of people dealing with random passersby — same the world over, Subaru sighed inwardly.

"But you know, there are good-hearted fools out there who help other people even when they're in a tight spot themselves."

Right after having something precious stolen, in the middle of chasing down the one who stole it.

Helping some useless stranger who had nothing to do with any of it, spending time and effort to treat him, and trying to walk away without even accepting thanks for it.

A good-hearted fool who indulges some useless guy's self-satisfaction, only to meet a horrible end for it.

"Try repeating something three times, and you start figuring a few things out. Well, if I still hadn't figured anything out by then, that'd be pretty sad for my head — but mine's not that far gone."

"What are you on about now?"

"There's probably a pattern to this. Call it fate, if you want. —No matter how many times I redo it, this development is bound to happen. For instance..."

First time, second time, third time — in every one, Satella has her insignia stolen by Felt.

Both the first and second times, the incident of Rom being killed in the Loot House had occurred. Presumably, Felt had been killed in the first Loot House scene too.

So then — in the second timeline, once she surely arrived at the scene at the storehouse, what became of Satella?

There'd be no useless deadweight tagging along that time, so — had she managed to win against that Elsa?

"That I don't know. Don't know at all. But there is something I do know."

If he left things as they were and let this fourth round play out unchecked, Felt and old man Rom would, without question, end up killed. And then Satella and Elsa would end up clashing.

So what if those two died? A petty crook fencing stolen goods on the side, and a headstrong little girl trying to sell what she'd stolen at a jacked-up price without an ounce of shame.

Two criminals, gone — if anything, wouldn't the world be better off?

"Ahh, guess this just proves I really am a kid of the modern age, huh. Even though I used to mock feelings like this something fierce, sitting in front of my computer."

Sympathy, mercy — he'd always played it off as ridiculous.

At the very least, Subaru himself didn't think he'd been faking that attitude. He genuinely believed himself to be a person of shallow feeling, and figured every modern person shared that same thinned-out emotional register.

So he'd assumed that no matter what situation he found himself in, he'd take it in stride without much emotional reaction. A few acquaintances dying didn't seem like it should fall outside that.

"And yet — I hate this. It makes me feel sick. Neither of them is anywhere close to a good person, those two. —But knowing that people I've once gotten to know are about to be killed, and just looking the other way — I can't do it."

In the end, maybe that's all it ever was — just an act he'd been putting on.

All of it just virtual-reality-level stuff. The moment it carried real weight, real substance, he'd flip his convictions without much resistance — that shallow.

Not that it was some deeply held creed to begin with, so peeling it away didn't leave much of a scar.

"And besides, there's still Satella... I mean, that girl — I can't just abandon her either."

He called out her name and thought to himself that it was probably a fake one.

Come to think of it, back in that first world, she hadn't seemed all that willing to say that name out loud. And now, on top of that, there was the exchange from the third world. Even if he didn't want to, the realization stung.

In short, it probably came down to a lack of trust. Insufficient affection points — he'd taken a failure result on the pass/fail check for the "obtain her name" event.

"Well then, guess this time I'll just have to work hard enough that she'll properly, actually tell me her name."

Right there on the spot, Subaru crouched and stretched, letting out a big "Ohh!" as he extended his whole body.

Bursting into a full "Cha-cha, cha-cha-cha-cha-cha-cha!" radio calisthenics routine, Subaru left the stall owner completely speechless.

In front of the man, still struck dumb, Subaru finished off the entirety of Radio Calisthenics No. 2, worked up a good sweat, then turned back to the shopkeeper, snapped his hand up sharply, and said:

"There are times a man's just gotta do what he's gotta do. —Right, old man?"

"Right, right, sure, right, whatever you say, that's right, now would you just get going already."

Subaru had meant to strike a fairly determined pose, but the tepid response nearly made his cheek twitch.

Given a perfunctory shooing wave, Subaru scurried off at a trot, leaving behind the stall owner sunk deep in long, long thought.

He wove through the crowd for a while, running maybe two hundred meters or so.

"Now then..."

Subaru came to a stop, sweeping a hand up through his short bangs in a needlessly stylish little flourish, casting his gaze left and right, then leaning a hand against a wall with practiced nonchalance, closing his eyes and running a hand through his hair once more. And then—

"Where do I even go to find the fake Satella, huh?"

And with that thoroughly uncertain declaration, he set off — engine running before the plan was even finished.

  • Title: 四度目の正直 plays on the Japanese idiom 三度目の正直 ("third time's the charm"), swapped to "fourth time." Rendered as "Fourth Time's the Charm" to preserve the idiom while keeping the numerical joke; the pun on the altered number is inherently visible in English too, so no separate substitution was needed.
  • "コンビニ" (convenience store) rendered simply as "the convenience store" — assumed to refer to a real-world store Subaru visited before being summoned, consistent with early Re:Zero web novel content.
  • Radio calisthenics ("ラジオ体操") is a real Japanese institution (NHK's daily radio exercise routine); kept as-is with brief context via "routine" since it's a recognizable enough concept and central to the comic beat.
  • "ケータイ成金" rendered as "Cell-Phone Nouveau Riche" — a coinage to preserve the joke of naming himself after his one asset; flagging as a translation choice rather than an established term.
