Arc 4 Chapter 50: "The Distant Roar"

Shadow surged forward like a great wave, a jet-black devotion bearing down on Subaru and the others.

Clinging precariously to a branch atop the great tree's trunk, Subaru had no way to flee. He glanced desperately at Garfiel, who should have been beside him—

"Garfiel!?"

He'd let go of the branch he'd been gripping and, for whatever reason, dropped in freefall to land on the ground below. The shadow-corrupted earth tried to slither up and swallow both his feet, but Garfiel paid it no mind, swinging both arms down to stab into the ground, planting all four limbs against the earth.

"If it's before I get swallowed up—how's this, huh!!"

With a roar, Garfiel wrenched upward the arms he'd driven into the soil. Following the motion, the ground itself peeled up and over—the shadow-cloaked earth flipped in a reversal of staggering scale, tearing away the ground that had been on the verge of being swallowed by the black wave and slamming it into the shadow in a brute-force gambit aimed at cancellation.

Clods of earth flew into the air, and amid a roar the upturned ground collided with the shadow. For one instant the impact held, weight against a thing that should have had none—but just as with the building before, the shadow swelled in scale, swallowing the dust storm whole to grow larger still.

The wave of shadow deepened—in height, in breadth, in the intensity of its blackness. The more it consumed, the more monstrous it became. And yet, however faint, a stall had been bought.

"Get your ass down here or I'm leaving you behind!"

"Waaaugh—!"

Subaru, who'd been watching the spectacle in dumbstruck silence, couldn't withstand the sudden jolt and was knocked from the tree to the ground. An instant before he slammed into the earth, Garfiel's outthrust hand caught him by the waist, braking him hard. Head spinning, Subaru registered what had happened and sputtered.

"Y-you didn't have to kick me down!?"

"Yer judgment's too damn slow. Seems like it's real fixated on you. Me, I can take a hit, but if it grabs you, yer done in a second flat."

Gripping Subaru, Garfiel jerked his chin toward the shadow swelling before them and grinned ferociously. Following his gaze, Subaru saw, beyond the wave, the shadow's source—a vaguely human silhouette, arms outstretched toward them with single-minded devotion.

"I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you—"

The muffled voice reached him, even at this distance, as if whispered directly into his ear—an obscenity of proximity that ignored physical law entirely, as though the gap between them meant nothing at all. Subaru found it revolting.

Looking at that shadow, on the receiving end of such dark, unrelenting passion, all that welled up in Subaru's chest was an unbearable disgust and revulsion.

That thing was the cause of his Return by Death—in some sense, even a benefactor. But it was impossible. His body wouldn't accept it. On a visceral level, his soul refused. He'd rather leap into the mouth of the White Whale than be embraced by that shadow.

"Garfiel, what do we do...!"

"Ain't nothing to do but fall back! Can't count on that Roswaal bastard either. Ram, the old hag... the rest of 'em too... don't think any of 'em could've stood up to that shadow."

Garfiel clicked his teeth, growling in frustration. Unlike Subaru, he had seen with his own eyes the people close to him—Ram, Ryuzu—swallowed by the shadow. His anguish was easy enough to imagine.

For Subaru, who held memories of Garfiel that he could only hate, seeing him now consumed by grief made for an unbearably complicated moment.

"—!"

If the writhing shadow was a massive palm, then its fingertips suddenly stretched out, reaching for the two of them. Garfiel dodged with a backstep just in time, still gripping Subaru. The shadow soaking the ground they'd landed on was still thin—if they could put distance between themselves and the shadow's center, they might at least avoid being dragged straight down into a bottomless mire.

"Falling back just means slow death by attrition... What happened when you hit it head-on?"

"Can't tear the damn shadow's dress. A full-power strike might do it different, but I can't get the openin' to land one."

Leaping in great bounds, weaving through gaps in the trees, Subaru and Garfiel exchanged words as they fled. Their speed was enough to leave the shadow behind them in the forest, yet the thing—which should have been slowly pursuing—refused to be shaken off entirely.

Was this inability to gain real distance some Authority of the shadow's, or something else? And there was another anomaly besides.

"...Damn it all."

Garfiel spat and growled irritably in his throat. His shoulders shook with ragged breath. Sweat beaded heavily on his forehead, and something unfamiliar, something wrong, had crept into every one of his movements.

It didn't look like exhaustion from carrying Subaru's weight. Subaru frowned at the sight, and seeing his reaction, Garfiel clicked his tongue.

"My body's heavy as hell. Unnatural heavy. —That shadow's suckin' the life force outta everything around it."

"This ground beneath us—the shadow's doing that!?"

Rattled by Garfiel's answer, Subaru—feet not even touching the ground—shuddered as he looked down at the shadow below, at the sheer reach of it swallowing the grassland no matter how far it stretched. And only now, far too late, did he grasp the shadow's true, terrible meaning.

"Hey, don't tell me—"

—The forest had grown lower.

The trees ringing the Sanctuary stood tall, their thick canopy dense enough to hide the moon and stars. But now the sky above that forest was plainly, clearly visible. The trees hadn't been felled. The branches and leaves hadn't been burned away. The green of the forest stood unchanged, rustling faintly in the wind.

—But the forest's height had sunk so low that Subaru felt he could clear the treetops with a single jump.

"The forest's sinking—!?"

"Happens if it ain't always on the move. The more it swallows, the more power it's got compared to before, I'd guess—!"

The shadow's power, spreading across the entire Sanctuary, was intensifying—the whole forest was beginning to sink into jet black. In every direction, front and back, left and right, there was no range the shadow hadn't reached. Even past the barrier, even past the forest, there seemed to be no end to it at all—a despair thick enough to choke on.

Caught up in this unprecedented turn of events and the appearance, at last, of the Witch of Envy herself, Subaru had misjudged the scale of the threat and everything else about it.

That thing was the Witch of Envy—the calamity that had once swallowed half the world, and whose scars of terror still ran deep even now.

"It can't seriously be able to scale up to half-the-world levels... can it?"

"There's a story it swallowed a whole country once, whole. If you're gonna laugh that off, better make sure you never heard it."

Garfiel gave a bitter laugh of agreement at Subaru's fear. The exhaustion plain on his face came both from the ill effects radiating off the Witch's shadow and from the accelerating encroachment—the sinking sensation of the ground beneath them growing stronger by the moment. Each step taken to flee sank deeper, and the leg strength needed for the next stride climbed exponentially. Under normal circumstances, Garfiel alone could probably have outrun it—but.

"Garfiel, its target is me. So..."

"If yer about to tell me to drop you and run, I'll gnaw yer fingers off one by one with these fangs, y'hear me."

Subaru was left speechless, his proposal rejected before he'd even finished it. But he shook his head to recover his flagging nerve, glaring at Garfiel's sweat-streaked profile.

"This isn't the time for that! At this rate we're both getting swallowed whole! If I face it myself, I can buy us at least a little time. In that gap—"

"You sayin' run? Or go fetch that Roswaal bastard? That shadow's first target was the village center... The villagers, the folks who fled there, Roswaal—all of 'em, swallowed."

"—! Y-you sure about that?"

"Everywhere you ain't seen, the whole damn Sanctuary's been swallowed by that shadow. Unless by some miracle every last one of 'em happened to wander off into the forest to watch the moon, there ain't no doubt about it."

Garfiel's flat, emotionless delivery—so unlike his usual excess of feeling—only underscored the truth of his words. If refugees with no combat ability, Sanctuary residents who avoided violence, and even Roswaal had all been swallowed, the situation could only be called hopeless.

For Garfiel, whose primary means of attack was closing in and striking, the Witch of Envy—who attacked from a distance by extending her shadow—was the worst possible matchup. If Roswaal or Ram, or anyone skilled at ranged attacks, had still been with them, they might have combined close and long-range strikes to some effect.

"All the more reason—if you go down, we lose our only countermeasure..."

"The old hag too! Ram too! Everybody else, swallowed, all of 'em...!"

"—!"

"And on top of that, you want me to throw you to that thing too and just live with the shame of it...? Absolutely not. Not in a million years. I gotta pound it into that thing that 'Pararagurara's scars never fade,' or I ain't gonna be able to live with myself!!"

Garfiel bared his fangs and roared. What showed on his face was boundless rage at the shadow—but was it only Subaru's imagination that something else lay beneath it too?

Someone who'd had everyone dear to him ripped away and yet didn't simply howl in pure fury—if that was the kind of man Garfiel truly was, then—

"Then why did you do that to everyone else, like that..."

Why had he killed the villagers who'd fought back so fiercely, so desperately?

Garfiel knew the pain of losing something. The agony of things vanishing. He had the capacity to feel that, to empathize with it. So why, then, had he been able to commit such cruelty?

Garfiel didn't seem to understand the meaning behind Subaru's strained question. He said nothing, only tightened his grip on Subaru—a wordless declaration that he had no intention of letting go. If anything, he drove his strides down harder still, fleeing the shadow whose encroachment kept accelerating, tearing forward through the steadily sinking forest.

Torn between the threat behind them and his uncertain feelings toward Garfiel, Subaru was startled when the view suddenly opened wide before him, and he lifted his head.

They burst through the sinking forest and out into open space, the two of them flying free. Here, at least for now, the shadow's encroachment was weaker. Bare earth, low-growing grass and flowers, and—the thing that truly took Subaru's breath away—

"—Eh!?"

The instant it registered, Subaru's body was flung out toward the open field.

He yelped in surprise, tumbling across the ground, clawing at the dirt to stop himself, shaking his head. But any complaint about being tossed aside so roughly was swallowed by his urgent need to make sense of what he'd seen. Namely—

"Why is Ryuzu-san here—?"

Voice trembling, Subaru stared ahead at a girl with long, pale-red hair swaying in the breeze—in that girl's form, but with the soul of someone aged and worldly within: Ryuzu. She stood motionless deep within the space ringed by trees, her gaze distant and unfocused.

Subaru reeled. Because he had just, only moments ago, heard from Garfiel's own mouth that she'd been swallowed by the shadow.

What was in front of him didn't match what he'd just been told. And if he had to choose which to believe, it would be the reality before his eyes. Which meant—had everything said just now been a lie?

"Garfiel, what is this—"

"...Don't go jumpin' to conclusions. I get what you wanna ask, what you wanna say, but there ain't no time. Took everything I had to lure it out this far."

Garfiel waved off Subaru, who was ready to press him, and swept his gaze around the clearing. Then he tilted his chin slightly upward, drew a deep breath, and—

"—OOOOOOOWWW!"

He let out a howl—not loud enough to tear through the whole forest, but one that cut straight, oddly, through the strangely still air.

Hearing it, Subaru had the wildly inappropriate thought, this guy can do a proper beast howl—but at the result of that howl, he lost his words for real this time.

"—!?"

With a rustling of underbrush, figures began pushing through the plants and pouring into the clearing one after another.

Every one of them was short in stature, their impossibly long hair trailing nearly to the ground. Pale red hair, translucent white skin, round eyes utterly devoid of expression. They wore oversized robes that didn't fit, hems dragging on the ground, worn directly against bare skin—and through the gaps that wouldn't quite close, bare feet showed plainly.

The figures shuffling out numbered close to twenty, by Subaru's rough count. Lining up to fill half the clearing, every single one of them shared the same face. Not merely the same expression—the same face, identical.

"What kind of joke is this..."

"Wish I coulda spared you the sight, if I could've."

Garfiel's pained mutter didn't reach Subaru's shell-shocked ears. No—it reached them, but it failed to register properly, failed to shake his brain into meaning.

Girls with identical faces—figures that were spitting images of Ryuzu, standing in rows—the sight gave Subaru the illusion of watching some kind of dream. In fact, he'd had this particular shape of nightmare more than once before. Part of him wanted to write this off as just another one of those. But—

"The cut on my arm from that branch still hurts, and this pain in my chest... that's real."

Pressing a hand to his blood-welling upper arm and his sharply pounding chest, Subaru let out a long breath. Bracing himself to accept the scene before him, he studied the girls again.

The girls with Ryuzu's face shared not just her features but her expression as well—every one of them. Which was to say: no emotion, no animation, doll-like faces. The Ryuzu Subaru knew hadn't exactly been lively, but she'd still been a person of rich emotional expression, with a living human quality in every gesture, however subtle.

"—"

That distinctly human quality was utterly absent from the girls before him. "Doll-like" wasn't just an apt description—it was, in fact, precisely accurate. They might as well have been dolls outright. Breathing, marking out the moments of life, yes—but dolls that merely moved. And here were twenty of them, sharing one face, lined up together, an abomination.

"Clones... no, there's no way this world's technology could do that. Some kind of duplication magic, copies...? But why so many, why all Ryuzu-san...?"

As the phrase "somatic cell cloning" flickered through his mind, Subaru suddenly understood. The fact that this Sanctuary was called a testing ground. The reason Echidna, the mistress of that testing ground, had spoken so evasively. And the reason Garfiel had cursed this place, over and over, as a dead end.

"Don't tell me this is the result of the Sanctuary's experiments...? Copies of Ryuzu-san. But no, what would even be the point of..."

"Hate to interrupt all that deep thinkin', but looks like it's about time."

Beside Subaru, still racing through possibilities at full speed, Garfiel's arms had swollen enormously. Covered in golden fur, they'd torn through his clothing from the inside, bulking up to nearly three times their original mass.

Atavism—if Garfiel's true form was the great tiger, then this partial transformation was likely the preliminary stage before his trump card.

"Surround it, crush it. Simple, but with everyone else swallowed, that's all there is."

"...I sort of get that you're the closer here, but—those kids—"

"Don't worry 'bout it. Ain't like the old hag—there's nothin' inside 'em. Still, they can follow orders enough to move where I tell 'em. If that buys an openin', that's a win."

There was far too much Subaru wanted to ask, both about the plan and about these Ryuzu duplicates. But there was no time to press for details, no time for a calm conversation.

With his thick arm, Garfiel shoved Subaru further back into the clearing. Stumbling, Subaru followed the rough instruction, and the mass of Ryuzus stepped forward as if to shield him from behind. That put Garfiel at the center of the clearing, the Ryuzus behind him, and Subaru at the very rear. And then, swallowing the trees Garfiel had been glaring at—

"—I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you—"

With a slow, gliding motion, words of love came pouring out, sweeping the forest before them.

An unceasing revulsion, an alarm ringing danger, hammered ceaselessly against Subaru's skull. The Witch's shadow raised what was probably its head, and its gaze fixed on Subaru—

"—"

Visibly, unmistakably, something like jubilant momentum surged through the shadow's movement. The swirling black mass, dragging the forest's trees into its spiral, snapping trunks and grinding them to compact rubble with a chorus of cracking sounds, came pressing forward, whispering love all the while.

The clearing's meadow was violated, the spreading shadow soaking the earth in jet black in an instant. At this rate, it wouldn't be long before this clearing too sank beneath the shadow just as the forest had. So if Garfiel had any chance at victory at all, it would be right before the shadow's power swallowed the clearing whole—which meant right now, this very instant.

"—GRAAAAAAAAH!!"

Looking up at the sky, Garfiel's throat let loose a roar that shook the very air.

At the violent pulsing of the atmosphere, Subaru felt an instinctive terror clench even his innards. As Subaru cowered before him, Garfiel transformed not just his arms but his legs into a beast's limbs, and slammed his feet down against the ground with tremendous force.

An instant later, the earth exploded outward, and with Garfiel's planted footfall as the fulcrum, the ground bearing the Witch buckled like a seesaw, flinging the shadow into the air. It was a replay of the moment Garfiel and the others had first met—when he'd torn the very earth out from under Patrasche's dragon carriage.

While the launched shadow, carried aloft with clods of earth, lost all sense of up and down, Garfiel dropped low, planting all four limbs, roaring furiously as he pushed his transformation further still.

His remaining clothing, unable to withstand the swelling of his body, tore completely away, leaving scraps caught and swaying in his golden fur. A body over four meters tall, thick and powerful limbs, and a head with a jaw lined with fangs like sharpened blades.

It was none other than the manifestation of the great tiger—the very thing that had once given Subaru despair and unforgivable fury.

"—!!"

With a roar, the monstrous beast's body tore through the wind and hurled itself at the shadow.

The ground beneath the massive beast's leap caved in, and the speed of its launch was savage beyond what its huge frame should have allowed. Jaws opening wide, fangs capable of biting through iron aimed to tear through the shadow's slender waist—

"—"

—at that instant, a shadow surging up from directly below wrapped around the leaping great tiger and bound it fast. Robbed of its momentum, the great tiger froze in midair, then its throat shuddered with a scream.

The shadow coiled around the tiger's limbs sent up a mist of blood, cinching tight as though intent on wrenching its thick arms and legs clean off. Limbs as big around as Subaru's waist began to tear apart with the sound of rending flesh. Subaru couldn't look away from the screaming, helplessly suspended great tiger. The shadow, showing no mercy, was about to tear the body apart entirely, spilling out organs and blood—

"—ah—"

But that didn't happen.

Before Subaru's stunned eyes, two of the standing Ryuzu duplicates suddenly broke away and hurled themselves into the clash between tiger and Witch. Mouths hanging open, spilling meaningless gasps, the young girls ran—moving with unexpectedly swift speed, they slipped past the shadow, touched down on the ground, and closed in on the Witch, who was looking up at the tiger she'd bound in midair.

"Uu—"

"—"

They lunged, arms spread as if to embrace the Witch. But an instant before contact, the Witch noticed the pair closing in and effortlessly foiled them, shadow lancing out to pierce them both.

Its tip honed to a sharp, spear-like point, the shadow slid toward its prey with a whip's suppleness, severing the legs of the two running Ryuzus, then skewering their torsos and hoisting them up, displaying them beside the still-screaming Garfiel as if for show.

A scene of excessive cruelty—but it was a mistake born of the Witch's own overconfidence.

"—OOOOOON!"

Garfiel, throat shaking with agony, let out a roar of a different color the moment he saw the mutilated duplicates hoisted beside him—a sound that made Subaru's brow furrow in confusion at the change in tone.

While Subaru puzzled over what that difference in voice could mean, a pale blue light rapidly filled the bodies of the two suspended Ryuzus—

"—!?"

"—"

The next instant, the two Ryuzus burst apart in a blinding flash of light.

It wasn't the gruesome carnage of a living thing exploding, blood and organs scattering. Their bodies became particles of light, blowing away the surrounding shadow along with them and, for one instant, bringing the world back to life. An explosive dispersal—but nothing like a death by detonation.

Subaru, his eyes seared by the white flash, rubbed at them roughly. As his vision cleared, the Ryuzus who had formed a wall in front of him were already sprinting forward, all at once, just as the first two had.

Scattering in four directions, eighteen Ryuzus surrounded the Witch with coordinated, varied timing. Moving as though they had no other means of attack, arms spread wide, they charged into the shadow's domain with the same aim as the first pair: to grab hold of the Witch.

But no matter how coordinated their assault, the duplicates' movements never exceeded human bounds—and their opponent was the worst calamity of all, the Witch of Envy. She spared the encircling Ryuzus barely a glance before the shadow surging skyward split into eighteen points. In an instant they became sharp blades, and even as each of the eighteen Ryuzus attempted evasive action, the shadow pierced, sliced, and ravaged skulls, torsos, and abdomens with a precision that mocked their every dodge.

Even staggered in their timing, the Ryuzus who'd challenged the Witch were wiped out entirely, and after a single beat, every one of them burst apart in pale blue light—momentarily driving back the clearing's shadow, the swirling darkness around the Witch vanishing.

"—RRRRRRRAAAAAAH!"

The battered, wounded great tiger did not let that opening pass.

Freed from the shadow's binding during the Ryuzus' charge, the massive beast coiled its limbs, and the instant the eighteen duplicates burst apart, it let out its greatest roar yet and dove headlong at the shadow.

Tearing through the wind as it closed in, the great tiger met the Witch's countering wall of shadow—but against that wall, the tiger slammed down a human figure it had kept hooked on its claws, hidden until now: another duplicate, used as a literal decoy to blast the wall apart, and past the pale blue light, fang and claw came down upon the shadow.

Got it—even Subaru was certain of it, such was the flawless execution.

Twenty-one Ryuzu duplicates, spent without hesitation in an act of utter inhumanity. If the claws of the tiger that Garfiel had become landed true, then surely, whatever the Witch was, her life could not continue—

"—I love you."

That near-prayer of certainty in Subaru's mind was shattered to pieces, in an instant, before the sweet, shadow-voiced call and the sight of Garfiel's corpse bursting apart from the inside.

"—Subaru-kun."
