Arc 1 Chapter 19: "Battle of the Spiritual Arts User"

The merciless crossing of icicles churned up a white mist within the room, veiling the black-cloaked shadow inside a low-temperature storm. The launch speed of the icicles far exceeded what Subaru had seen in the alley — by his common sense, they were putting out pitch speeds beyond even major-league level, the kind you'd only see in a video game. Their sharp tips would pierce a human body with ease, staining the transparent projectiles crimson with fresh blood. And there were fully twenty of them. A hit meant death was unavoidable. And yet—

"Did she pull it off!?"

"Yep, one bad flag successfully raised, right here—!!"

The bald man, who'd kept his silence up till now, had to open his mouth at exactly the wrong moment. As if in answer to Subaru's outcry at that,

"—Always good to come prepared. I hated how heavy it was, but I'm glad I wore it."

Slicing through the white smoke, hair streaming, Elsa leapt out.

Cocking back her kukri knife and stepping lightly, there wasn't a wound to be seen on her body. Aside from having shed the black cloak she'd been wearing, leaving her in only the black garb fitted to her skin beneath, nothing looked different from before.

"Don't tell me — the coat itself was heavy, and taking it off is what let her move light on her feet!?"

"That's an amusing thought, but the truth is much simpler. My cloak was woven, just once, with a formula for warding off magic. Seems I got to keep my life."

Responding politely to Subaru's worry, Elsa brought her blade around to the front from a low stance. Standing there was the fake Satella, who'd just unleashed her big move. The blade drove straight in, its weighted tip aiming for the girl's chest. Subaru nearly cried out without meaning to. Before that cry could take shape—

"Don't underestimate a wielder of the Spiritual Arts. Cross one, and you'll find out how scary that is."

A multiply-deployed shield of ice easily stopped Elsa's blade cold. The fake Satella pressed her hands together before her chest — the ice shield was surely her doing. And Elsa, her strike halted, immediately somersaulted backward to evade. Chasing after her, a barrage of icicles — somewhat smaller in size this time — struck the ground. That follow-up attack came from right beside the silver-haired girl, from Puck, swinging his arm like a conductor.

"Division of labor between offense and defense — effectively, it's two against one."

"That's the annoying thing about spirit users, that is. One attacks while the other defends. Depending on the situation, one can buy time with simple magic while the other lets loose with a big spell... 'If you meet a spirit user, throw down your weapon and your wallet and run' — that's the rule of thumb on any battlefield."

Beside the awestruck Subaru, old man Rom, gripping his club, muttered gravely. Subaru nodded along readily to his point. Indeed — in a one-on-one situation, that kind of advantage would be absolute. Not something easily broken.

"By the way, old man, what exactly are you planning to do?"

"Wait for my chance, then lend the elf girl a hand. She still seems the more reasonable one to talk to."

"Wait wait wait wait wait wait wait! Don't! Just don't do that! You'll only get in the way! You'll end up losing an arm and your head, that's how it'll go — just stay put!"

"Don't go predicting my defeat so specifically! Somehow, hearing you say it, I actually feel like I've been cut!"

Given that Subaru had, in fact, seen him get cut twice already, the statement carried a certain ring of truth. Whether it was some sense of having been sliced apart in another dimension bleeding through, old man Rom, clutching his arm and neck, seemed to genuinely, seriously dislike the idea. Even amid this exchange, the battle raged on. Despite his glib rebuke of Rom, the truth was that Subaru could find no opening to interject at all in this fight.

—Countless chunks of ice were being conjured one after another, dancing wildly through the room in every direction. Their sizes varied — the largest about the size of a head, the smallest still fist-sized. And every single one of them flew at a speed not far off a full-power pitch, so their danger level went without saying. And yet, amid that violent hail of ice, Elsa — having lost her magic-warding cloak — moved with an agility that existed on no ordinary plane.

She spun her body, dropped flat as if crawling along the ground, and at times even used the walls as footholds, defying gravity in her evasive maneuvers. And when she judged she couldn't dodge fully, her supple blade would shatter the ice, pulverizing the white crystal into mist. Making up for overwhelming numbers with sheer, terrifying skill.

"She's a battle-hardened one, for a girl."

Even Puck, the one attacking her, let out a note of admiration at a skill that could only be called divine. At that murmur, even in the thick of the fight, Elsa's lips curved into a smile.

"My, my. It's been quite a while since anyone treated me like 'a girl.'"

"From where I'm standing, most opponents look like babies. Even so, it's almost a shame how strong you are."

"Being praised by a Spirit — how humbling."

Accepting the compliment with genuine pleasure, her keening blade batted away the surrounding chunks of ice. The number of ice shards fired must already be nearing a hundred, but aside from that initial encirclement, not one had directly reached Elsa's body. In sheer volume, Puck had the offensive advantage, but the fact remained that he lacked a decisive finishing blow.

"At this rate, won't he run out of MP and lose?"

Picturing a scenario where offense and defense reversed, Subaru voiced his concern about the disadvantage of a drawn-out fight. But it was old man Rom who shook his head at that worry.

"I don't know what this 'em-pee' is you speak of, but there's no need to worry about a spirit user running out of mana."

"What do you mean, no worry about running out of mana...?"

"Unlike a mage, a spirit user doesn't draw on mana from within himself, but from mana that exists outside him. So long as the world itself doesn't run dry, a spirit user never runs out of ammunition."

"Unlimited gas, rev the engine all you want. Talk about a cheat code."

Rom seemed to have understood absolutely none of Subaru's response, and tilted his head. Still at that angle, the old man prefaced his next words with an "although," and continued,

"How long the Spirit itself can stay manifested is a separate matter, mind you. Without the Spirit, the balance of the fight could tip in an instant."

"Ugh, right, that's a thing. ...Is it getting on toward five o'clock or something!?"

In the first world, Puck had fallen asleep sometime a little after sunset, if Subaru recalled right. Not that much time had passed since the fight began in the evening, but with this much magic being thrown around, surely all that stored-up mana was being burned through in grand fashion. Subaru's worry was—

"Ah, uh oh. Getting a bit sleepy. Actually, I think I was just fighting half-asleep just now."

"Hey, Puck! Get it together!"

"...Huh! I wasn't asleep! Not asleep at all! I'm totally, completely not asleep!"

"Why does that exchange, delivered in such a suspiciously quiet voice, make me so nervous!?"

It seemed the fear had been spot on. Puck seemed to be nodding off rather badly, and even the expression of the girl with the kitten perched on her shoulder betrayed a flicker of alarm.

"And just when it was getting fun. How cruel of you, to have your mind wander off elsewhere."

"That's the hard part of being a popular fellow. The girls just won't let me sleep. But hey, look — staying up too late is bad for your skin, you know."

Bending her knees lightly, Elsa took a crouch to leap. Just ahead of that, in the line of her gaze, Puck gave a deft wink and said,

"Time to bring down the curtain, don't you think? Even the same old show gets tiresome to watch, doesn't it?"

"—My leg—"

The instant she tried to push off, Elsa pitched forward on the spot and caught herself with her hand. Before Subaru's eyes, startled by her unbelievable slip, lay the answer to her fall, right at her feet.

Elsa's right foot — it had been pinned to the frozen floor. The shattered ice fragments had piled up ever so slightly, playing the role of a wedge ensnaring her leg.

"It's not like I was just scattering that stuff around for nothing, y'know?"

"...I've been had, is that it?"

"Chalk it up to the difference in experience, and feel free to just go ahead and praise me for it. Nighty-night."

Puffing out his chest, Puck's small body trembled in short bursts atop the girl's shoulder. Striking a pose as if unleashing some ultimate finishing move, both his little paws thrust forward, and from there, magical power concentrated on a scale greater than anything before — Subaru saw it — and then was unleashed.

The magical force no longer took the shape of ice at all, but was fired as pure, unadorned destructive energy.

A pale blue light froze everything in its line of fire, painting the entire Loot House white in one sweep. The energy, in a straight line, passed through Elsa and slammed into the entrance of the Loot House, blasting the ill-fitting door clean off its hinges — a scale so far beyond normal that the aftershock of the freeze even reached outside.

In the wake of that pillar of light, nothing remained but ice — stolen goods, furniture, the counter, all of it swept up wholesale into a frozen wasteland. Naturally, a direct hit would turn even a human being into a statue of ice, no question.

Except—

"No... way..."

"It's not a lie, you know. Ah, how lovely. I really thought I might die."

That would only be true if she'd taken a direct hit.

"...You're a girl, and yet, honestly, I can't say I approve of that sort of thing."

Having had his trump card dodged, there was no anger beyond the words themselves in what Puck said. It was, purely and simply, a tone of displeasure at the act itself.

—Blood dripped, and faint steam rose off the frozen floor; even Subaru could see it.

The source of the blood was Elsa's right leg. Standing barefoot, having stepped just slightly clear of the beam's line of fire, an enormous amount of blood poured from her right foot. Small wonder — after all, the sole of her right foot had been sliced clean away.

"I very nearly cut it off myself in my haste — that was a close call."

"Even just that much has to hurt like hell."

"Yes, that's right. It hurts. How lovely. It makes me feel alive. And besides..."

Nodding with a look of rapture at Puck's concerned words, Elsa lifted her bleeding foot and, without the slightest hesitation, pressed the sole against the nearby chunk of ice. A sound rang out like the air itself cracking, and Elsa let out a sultry "Mmmnnh," and immediately after, her swung kukri knife sliced smoothly across the surface of the ice.

With just that, a crude bit of first aid was complete — a fragment of ice fixed to the sole of her foot.

"A bit hard to move in, but this'll do fine."

Her footsteps ringing hard against the floor, Elsa, now shod in ice, laughed with even greater delight. Subaru was rendered speechless by her battle-junkie readiness to injure even herself, but what was even more dire was the state of the fake Satella and the others facing her.

"Puck, can you keep going?"

"Sorry, I'm super sleepy. Got a little too cocky there. I'm gonna vanish — out of mana."

Puck answered the girl's murmur, and for the first time, the ease had vanished from his voice. Beside the silver-haired girl, on her shoulder, the little cat's form glowed faintly and dimly, flickering as if it might fade away at any moment. In other words — time was up.

"I'll handle the rest here somehow, so rest for now. Thank you."

"If anything happens to you, I'll follow the Covenant. —If it ever comes to that, wring out every last drop of Od if you have to and summon me."

As though pressed by the fake Satella's gentle, loving voice, Puck's body abruptly turned to mist and vanished. Everyone in the room mourned his departure, but among them, the one whose shoulders dropped most visibly at it was—

"—Ah, he's gone. How dreadfully disappointing, that is."

None other than Elsa, who had, quite literally, been locked in a fight to the death with him. Readjusting her grip on the kukri knife, Elsa advanced on the fake Satella, the hard clack of her ice-shod footsteps ringing out. Around the fake Satella facing her, icicles were conjured one after another in response, but the volume was considerably reduced compared to what Puck had unleashed. Even against Elsa, whose mobility had been hampered, the odds had likely been pulled back to fifty-fifty.

"Guess it's about time I stopped just watching."

Perhaps judging the situation unfavorable, it was old man Rom who gripped his club and hauled himself heavily to his feet. Receiving Subaru's silent gaze, he shrugged and said,

"I can't say what the odds are without lending a hand now. Keep quiet and just watch, and we'll only miss our chance. ...You understand too, don't you, Felt?"

"I get it, alright. Whether we run or not, it's about time we made a move."

Felt, who hadn't spoken a single word since Elsa's initial threat, now stood beside Rom, who seemed to have readied himself for battle, and glanced over at Subaru for a moment.

"Earlier, what you did... helped, a little."

"—Huh?"

"Just a little, though. And — don't go calling me a brat. I'll have you know I'm fifteen. Barely any different from you, big brother."

"...I turn eighteen this year. Old enough to drive and get married."

"No way! Your face is way too kid-like for that. You oughta get a few more years carved into that mug of yours."

Having been a shut-in, and having lived life so far without any real ups and downs to speak of. Even so, he had to admit the present situation seemed to be making up for that lack of drama with a vengeance.

Subaru could only watch as the two of them, resolve steeled, kept their eyes on the tide of battle. He was, in all likelihood, the least combat-capable person present. And if it were only a lack of combat prowess, that would be one thing, but—

"My legs are shaking too much to move. ...Seems my resolve's coming up short too."

Before the question of qualification even arose, Subaru simply had no way to involve himself in this fight. Old man Rom could fight with brute strength, Felt with her legs, and the fake Satella with her magic. But Elsa's abnormality surpassed every one of those in spades. And indeed—

"She's starting to push them back."

Old man Rom's blunt assessment said it all. The barrage of ice kept firing without pause, but every last shot was shattered before it could land, broken apart by Elsa's blade-dance. Elsa's slashes, leaping in as if dancing, were blocked by an ice shield when she had to interrupt her attack; her following combo strikes were dodged by razor-thin margins, sliding across the freshly-frozen floor beneath her feet. Whenever distance opened up, the barrage would once again reverse the balance of attack and defense, but this was no opponent against whom the same trick would keep working forever.

The next time those fangs closed in on her, Subaru would be forced to witness a sight he never wanted to see again.

"Here I go——!!"

Having likely pictured the same thing Subaru had, old man Rom let out a battle cry and threw himself into the fray. His raised club cut through the air with a fierce gust as it swung down; Elsa ducked to avoid it, her hair caught only slightly in the wake, and the front of battle widened.

"My, my — isn't it rather rude to cut in on someone's dance uninvited?"

"If you want to dance that badly, I'll give you the finest dance of your life! There, spin and twirl!"

Thrusting out his spiked club, he shifted his attack range from a line to a single point. The unexpected, improvised strike aimed straight for Elsa's throat, and as a result, it was old man Rom's own throat that froze in place.

"Wha... the hell is that!?"

"It's only because you're strong enough that a trick like this could even work."

There stood Elsa, having lightly perched the tip of her toes atop the very end of the thrust-out club. It was an acrobatic feat that could only work through a razor-thin balance of forces and an almost divine sense of equilibrium. Before that delicate balance could collapse, Elsa's blade swept sideways. Its position: right at the height of old man Rom's temple — a direct hit would send his head flying with that force behind it.

"Not gonna let that happen—!"

Clang! A small thrown knife struck the horizontal slash from below. The impact from directly beneath threw the blade's trajectory slightly off course, and the flat of it struck the massive man's head at full speed. A dull thud of steel striking bone rang out, and old man Rom's body was knocked sideways as if flung.

"Naughty girl."

"—Guh—"

Landing lightly, Elsa turned only her gaze back to look at Felt. What had saved old man Rom from that fatal blade was the small knife Felt had thrown. She'd surely been aiming for the arm, but in the heat of the moment, her aim had fallen short. Though in this particular case, it could be said that very shortfall was what had barely saved Rom's life.

"No resolve, no strength to fight. If that's the case, the least you could have done was make yourself small in a corner of the room."

The sound of her footwork rang out sharp and high, and in an instant Elsa's black shadow seemed to glide right up before Felt's eyes. Old man Rom lay unconscious from the blow to his head; the fake Satella had kept her distance, and that now worked against her, leaving too much space between them. And Felt, frozen like a frog caught in a snake's stare, couldn't move at all—

"Heeeeeyaaaaaaaaaah!!"

The only one who could scoop up that small body of hers on the spur of the moment and leap clear was the one coward who'd been huddled trembling right beside her.
