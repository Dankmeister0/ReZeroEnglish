Arc 2 Chapter 26: "The Third Option"

He had run blindly, desperately, and he had no idea how much time had passed.

Breath ragged, knees buckling, sweat streaming down his chin, his mind churned into a mess—and still he couldn't stop, couldn't do anything but keep running. If he stopped running, the incomprehensible emotion chasing him from behind would catch up. And if it caught him, then this time, surely, everything would truly be over—that conviction alone was what drove Subaru onward now.

Ram's scream, her raging howl of resentment, the cry that had marked Subaru as an object of hatred—it still hadn't left his ears.

Ram, who never dropped her rude, arrogant, faintly mocking manner. That same Ram had bared such raw emotion, had screamed her hatred at the top of her lungs. Just remembering it made his heart seize up, made an inexpressible sorrow well up inside him.

He had run. He had actually run away.

He'd dashed out, burst from the mansion, cast off every noise chasing him from behind, and fled, abandoning everything.

He could never go back to that place now. Ram and Roswaal would never forgive Subaru for running. Emilia and Puck, too, wouldn't be able to fully trust a Subaru who stubbornly kept his mouth shut. And Beatrice, bound to him by their Contract, wouldn't become an active ally either, unable to read the true meaning behind Subaru's actions.

If only he could have thrown it all out into the open, that would have been fine. But to do that, a black mist rose up and barred the way.

In a world where time had stopped, in a world cut off from sound, the pain and terror of having his heart crushed in a fist—before that, Subaru's half-hearted resolve to run had been scattered without a fight.

In the end, unable to bear either the pain or the grief, Subaru had had no choice but to flee.

"It couldn't be helped, could it...! What could I have... I mean, I—!"

He understood nothing. Why had things turned out this way? What was he supposed to do? What could he possibly do to make the world forgive him?

"It was so... fun... and yet—"

Suddenly summoned to another world, forced to live in a world he knew nothing about, a world rooted only in anxiety—and yet this had been the place that had welcomed him in. Those days, that time—time that had passed no more than a single week—now felt further from Subaru's reach than he could bear.

Redo after redo, returning again and again, repeating over and over, and each time the world bared its fangs at him anew. The world that had once been so kind had changed color; its once-vivid brilliance had faded until everything looked dull and gray.

—It's no use anymore.

The thought brushed across his mind, unbidden.

—There's no need to try any harder than this, is there?

At the voice urging him to give up, at its sweet temptation, Subaru's thoughts nearly ground to a halt. If he simply did as it said, surely he'd be at ease. That was what he thought. To begin with, Subaru had always been the type to drift toward whichever path was easier.

And not just Subaru. Wouldn't anyone be the same? When two choices are laid out before you and neither can be discarded, you're forced into a stretch of bitter, agonizing time. And in the midst of struggling through that agony, what if a third option suddenly presented itself?

As though that option were divine revelation, a heavenly blessing, who could blame the hand that reached for it, drawn by its allure?

If he could neither resist nor outrun it, then why not just throw it all away?

"Throw it away... he says."

The blood drained rapidly from his head, and even the heartbeat that had been pounding so violently now felt distant, unreal. His limbs grew heavy as lead, and the legs that had been driven forward slowed their pace, gradually shifting into something more like a dragging shuffle.

Suddenly the field of vision that had narrowed to nothing widened again, and Subaru realized he was standing in a forest, surrounded by trees. It seemed that in his headlong flight, he had burst from the mansion, strayed from the road, and wandered lost into the mountain paths.

Surrounded by dense, overgrown greenery, in a dim half-light where even the sky was blotted out, Subaru thought that this place resembled the site of his last death. The moment that thought crossed his mind, a clear vision of the third option rose up before him.

"If I died..."

Would he be saved—from this situation?

"Ah, yeah. That's right. If I just died, things would change."

The foolish words of weakness slipping from his mouth felt so achingly sweet that a twisted smile crept onto Subaru's lips.

He had already lost his life three times. A fourth death was something Subaru had never experienced. And so he'd been tormented by a baseless fear that if a fourth death came, everything might simply end there. But—

"If it ends there, what's so bad about that...?"

Each time he repeated it, the situation Subaru found himself in only grew worse. Death by exhaustion. Death by exhaustion followed by being beaten to death. Killed by someone he'd trusted, and now, this time, that person's death, and the loss of every shred of trust from everyone else involved.

This time, all he'd managed to salvage was his life. This time, life was the only thing he'd been left with. Losing everything like this, struggling on in a situation worse than the one he'd been in when he was first summoned to this other world—what meaning could there possibly be in that? No—even if there were some meaning in it, why did it have to be Subaru who walked that path?

"If somebody wants to do it, let them do it themselves. ...What does it have to do with me? With me?"

He bit his lip and spat his hatred at whatever being had dragged him into this situation. A thick, dark emotion boiled up in his gut, filling his mind with every curse he could think of, pouring black fuel onto a heart already withered to nothing.

—The choice called "death"—to Subaru now, it seemed like the finest idea imaginable.

If dying meant it ended there, then so be it, that was that. But what if, by dying, he could return once more to "Day Two"?

"The first time... right. Getting along with Emilia-tan, petting Puck, teasing Beatrice, trading banter with Roswaal, being with Ram and Rem..."

He might be able to trace that first loop over again, and have fun doing it. Come to think of it, that first time had been nothing short of ideal for Subaru. Even if he couldn't change the fate that awaited him on the night of the fourth day—dying of exhaustion—that death had come without pain or suffering, and above all, it had promised peace and tranquility.

If he could return to those days again, if he could touch their smiles once more, then dying was nothing to fear at all. This present situation, this mess he had no way of untangling, was far more frightening to Subaru—and above all, the sorrow of it was more than he could bear.

Holding his breath, Subaru dug through the pocket of his pants. Inside, the thin object his hand closed around was the quill pen he hadn't let go of once in these four days. Its sharp nib had pierced the back of his hand more times than he could count, staining it each time with fresh blood, until now the blackish-red crust clinging to it could probably never be fully wiped away.

Gripping the quill pen backhanded, Subaru pointed its tip at his own throat. The sharp point was more than capable of tearing into a human body—Subaru himself could already attest to that from firsthand experience. If he built up momentum and swung it down with full force, his goal would be achieved.

"Haah... haah... haah...!"

His vision flickered and flashed, and his whole body burned as though his blood were circulating far too fast. And yet no blood seemed to reach his head at all; his thoughts froze over more and over, as if the spinning of his brain knew no limit.

His gaze fixed on the pen's tip, and the trembling point crept closer, bit by bit. His breathing, ragged even beyond what a full sprint could produce, came with a wave of nausea so strong that the moment his concentration slipped, he felt he might retch on the spot.

His knees swayed, his hips gave out, and his arms hung lifeless from the elbow down as if the nerves had stopped answering. And yet only the right hand gripping the pen trembled, absurdly, almost like some kind of joke.

"Damn it..."

He clicked his tongue and shook his head, as if trying to shake off the emotion welling up before him. He grabbed his trembling right wrist with his left hand to steady it. But the instant his left hand touched his right, the shudder spread to it as well. Both hands, gripping the pen, trembled in short, uncontrollable bursts, and never carried out their intent.

"Stop... just stop already!"

He raised his voice in irritation, but even that voice couldn't hide the trembling. At last the quill slipped from his grip, and as he bent to retrieve it, the knees that had been failing him gave out entirely, and he collapsed right where he stood. Both hands pressed to the dirt, Subaru stared, stunned, at the ground inches from his face, and hot tears suddenly welled up and spilled from both eyes as he bit through his lip in frustration. A sharp pain, the iron tang of blood filling his mouth—he tasted it as he sobbed.

"I can't even do something this... this simple..."

—Is it because I don't have the courage?

If he could just tip over to that side, he'd be at ease. And yet, even as he tormented himself with that thought, Subaru couldn't summon the resolve to actually fall.

He couldn't give in to impulse and let momentum carry him through; he couldn't carry out even an easy, weak decision. His resolve, his resignation—even those were too fragile, and all he could manage was to curl up and wail. And exposing himself in such an unsightly way changed nothing about his situation. It didn't even make things worse. Nothing changed at all. It only told him, flatly, unceasingly, that the situation he found himself in was rock bottom, and that nothing about it would change.

Pain, suffering, sorrow—everything pressing him toward a decision, all of it, showed him no kindness at all. Even the choice he had longed for so desperately—he lacked the resolve to carry it through.

If he was going to choose an ending either way, he thought, maybe he should have just let Ram kill him. Subaru had no direct connection to Rem's death. So even if Ram had managed to kill him, it would have been nothing but a completely misdirected act of vengeance.

Still, this was Ram—who had wailed in agony as if half her body had been torn away, who had screamed such hatred. If it could ease even a little of the impulse scorching her from within, then why not offer up his own life, since all he'd chosen to do was rot away here anyway?

It looked, on the surface, like concern for another person. But its true nature was nothing more than escapism—a way to distract himself from his own inability to settle on any resolve. And yet Subaru, who couldn't even bring himself to face his own shame, never noticed how that very thought insulted the Ram he was using as an excuse.

This selfish emotion, one even a Spirit might abandon him for—perhaps what was sent to him then was some arrangement of heaven, unable to bear watching such an unsightly display any longer.

"—Huh?"

At the sudden shift in the air around him, Subaru, who had been crawling on the ground, lifted his face. Nothing changed in his tear-blurred view of the dirt beneath him. But in the dim half-light, blocked from sunlight by leaves and branches, something snagged at Subaru's nerves.

It was a warning summoned up from instinct. As the alarm bells rang in his mind, he pushed himself up, rising to one knee, and scanned his surroundings. He rubbed his eyes, cleared his vision, strained his ears, and held his breath.

The sound of grass being trodden rang out, followed by the dry, repeated crackle of shaking branches. Fast. And sharp. Something was closing in, keen and swift.

Unconsciously wetting his dry lips with his tongue, Subaru snatched up the quill pen that had fallen at his feet. He held it backhanded, raising it before him out of some inexplicable unease.

—In that instant, the thing pushed through the brush and appeared before Subaru.

"You're..."

Not pursuers from the mansion. The weakness that had, somewhere, hoped it might be exactly that was betrayed. His eyes dropped for a moment at that selfish hope—but the thing before him stood its ground, unhurried.

Going by what Subaru knew, it most closely resembled a wolf. Its whole body was covered in black fur, its four legs planted firmly in the earth as it crouched low. Saliva dripped from a great maw lined with sharp fangs, and at the center of that pitch-black face, the only thing that asserted its presence were a pair of gleaming red eyes, burning bright.

Its body was roughly a meter long, its weight probably somewhere around thirty or forty kilograms. In his original world, that would put it around the size of a large dog, but its overall lean, sharp build didn't suggest anything so overwhelmingly powerful. And yet—

"Come on, seriously..."

Could anyone who knew nothing but the tame, domesticated dogs of human society really stay calm in front of a creature with eyes gleaming at the sight of prey, breath ragged before the kill? Facing down its bared fangs and unmistakable hostility, cold sweat rapidly soaked Subaru's brow.

Before Subaru, frozen and unable to move a muscle, the creature lowered its head and snorted through its nose. It had probably tracked him here by scent. Subaru's body, after running so recklessly, was covered in scratches from branches, plenty of them still oozing blood. On top of that, he'd been letting out pathetic sobs the whole way. There had been no shortage of clues to pinpoint where he was.

The black dog lowered its head, baring its teeth in warning. Then Subaru noticed something—a yellowish horn growing from the top of its head—proof that whatever this creature's biology was, it was definitely not the same as what he knew.

Faced with the ravenous, horned ferocity before him, Subaru felt his heart seize with terror—and yet, at the same time, he noticed something like relief welling up in him from some unknown source. Perhaps because the beast before him, unlike himself, would affirm Subaru's decision without a moment's hesitation. Cornered as he was, that was how it seemed to him.

"Here I was thinking about letting Ram kill me... and now this, right away. I really am fickle, aren't I."

Because he couldn't hold onto any firm resolve, he kept drifting along with whatever situation was in front of him. Just as it had been until now, he was letting himself be carried along again by the easy option right before his eyes. He didn't think making that choice was wrong. Thinking so would be pointless anyway, and it wouldn't change anything even if he did.

Spreading both arms wide, Subaru declared his surrender to the horned creature before him. For a moment, it seemed the beast, crouched low, couldn't quite grasp the meaning of its prey's behavior, and the way it flinched, trembling as if startled, struck Subaru as faintly amusing.

"Something's got you spooked, when I'm already beaten down this bad?"

A laugh slipped from his lips. He was almost stunned that he still had the strength left to laugh like that.

—It was strange—laughing like this somehow made him feel lighter.

The black beast standing before him, clearly regarding Subaru as prey—this, surely, was heaven's messenger, come to grant his wish. Since Subaru couldn't even end his own life himself, it would guide him gently, sweetly, into stillness, with those fangs, those claws, that horn.

Either death would be the end, or he'd return and sink back into a lazy kind of happiness. He didn't know which fate awaited him, but either way, he would escape this hopeless dead end pressing down on him now.

That was enough, for now. That alone was enough. He wanted nothing more than that. So, if anything, better to make it quick—

"Huh...?"

Just as he'd settled into that thought with something like serenity, Subaru was startled out of it by the sound of something hard clicking together, right beside him. Even as he kept his attention on the threat before him, he searched for the source of the sound, but he couldn't find it anywhere in sight. And then it hit him at once. Of course he couldn't find it. Naturally.

The small, hard clicking sound was his own teeth, chattering, unable to stay still.

"—Eh?"

His trembling lips let slip a murmur, blood rushing so hard into his fingers that it hurt, his heart pounding at a speed that felt like it might burst. Subaru realized the change happening in his own body only belatedly, and it left him stunned. Why was his body so desperate to flee this place? It was as if—

"This is like..."

—as if he didn't want to die.

Betrayed even by his own body, Subaru found himself in the humiliating position of being unable to trust even himself. And paying no heed at all to the turmoil roiling inside him,

"Grh...?"

The beast suddenly vanished from view, and a foolish sound escaped him, dropping into the forest. Dazed, he tried to track the pitch-black shadow that had been right there a moment ago. He couldn't find it.

An instant later, his right thigh, where it met his hip, was seized in a massive set of jaws.

"Gh...!"

Fangs tore through flesh, reaching the nerves, the wound widening with a sharpness like a blade. Blood sprayed out, the world's motion slowed, and as Subaru stared down, transfixed, at the source of the pain, his eyes met the red pair of eyes sinking their fangs into him.

—As if to loudly proclaim that he was still alive, Subaru's scream tore through the forest.
