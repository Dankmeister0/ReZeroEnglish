Arc 4 Chapter 86: "Disregarding the Odds"

Even the pain of being struck was forgotten as Subaru stood dumbfounded at what had just happened. Knocked flat onto the ground, he lifted his face to find Otto glaring down at him. In those features that never showed strong color—usually so pathetic, or wearing an ingratiating smile—there now burned, unmistakably, the flame of violent emotion.

Otto Suwen looked down at Subaru with anger kindled in both eyes.

"You don't know what to do about anything, and your head's a complete mess, isn't it."

"――――"

"Everywhere you look there's someone who needs your help, and you don't have the arms or the head or the strength to manage it, and time just keeps rushing by while you fight desperately against it, don't you."

Otto flung the words at Subaru's silence while closing the distance between them step by step. Still sprawled on the ground, Subaru couldn't move, and could only watch Otto while wincing at the heat now belatedly asserting itself in his left cheek.

"Silence is affirmation without denial. At least, where I come from, that's the lowest thing you can do—it's just an invitation to be taken advantage of. ――Are you listening?"

Otto reached out toward the still-speechless Subaru, grabbed him by the collar, and hauled him up.

"If you can hear me, answer!"

"――――!"

Something hard and sharp struck his forehead, and sparks scattered across Subaru's vision. Reeling, Subaru realized he'd just been headbutted by the Otto in front of him. And Otto, seeing him still dazed, delivered a second headbutt before shoving him back by the chest.

The pain in his forehead, the pain in his cheek, and Subaru staggering backward from the shove—even he couldn't just stay silent after all this.

"What the hell... are you doing..."

"Oh? For someone who just took it and took it without fighting back, you were conscious after all. I figured you were out cold, so I went a little further than I'm used to."

"The hell did you—!?"

Tears welling from the second headbutt to the bridge of his nose, Subaru lunged at Otto in a fit of rage. But his outstretched arm was dodged by a sideways slip of Otto's body, and his legs were roughly swept out from under him instead, sending him tumbling.

"Gah!?"

"Blood rushes to your head, and this time your footing goes unguarded. That's practically the story of Natsuki-san's every action—pathetic."

"Is that... so!"

Springing up from his fallen position, Subaru hurled the dirt he'd grabbed on the way down straight at Otto's face. But Otto, as if he'd read this coming too, guarded his face with his arm, and Subaru—delayed by the shock of having his blinding trick anticipated—found Otto closing in. Otto seized him by the back of the collar as he gasped, planted a hand against his stomach, and threw him in one motion.

Slammed back-first into the ground, Subaru bounced and the breath was knocked out of him by the impact and pain. The spot he'd been thrown onto was piled with fallen leaves, but of course that didn't absorb the full force. A numbness ran all the way to his fingers and toes, and Subaru, gasping, couldn't even stand.

"Trying a little trick like that—well, I suppose that's very Natsuki-san of you. Though it wasn't going to work on me, since I had that in mind the whole time we were facing off."

"...ka, hah..."

"Hey, Natsuki-san. That's the extent of your strength. Nowhere near the knights, or Roswaal-sama, let alone Garfiel. Not even up to me, as you can see."

As Subaru desperately fought to force oxygen into his convulsing lungs, Otto's words—shaking his head as if exasperated—pierced him. Otto approached the side of the Subaru who couldn't even manage a rebuttal, crouched down, and brought his face close.

"Even picking a fight with the White Whale or the Witch Cult is out of the question. Natsuki-san is weak—if it actually came to blows, you'd be lucky to be crushed by a single fingertip. You must know that yourself."

"――――"

"So then, do you make up for what you lack in strength with wisdom? From what I've seen, Natsuki-san's head does work in a somewhat cunning way... but as for having consistently high thinking or judgment—no, that's definitely not the case. You're even short on common sense."

Subaru had no idea what Otto was trying to say, and irritation crept into his ragged breathing. The convulsion in his lungs, the shock of being thrown, the pain in his forehead and cheek—all of it was faintly fading. In its place came a returning calm, and with it, no clearer understanding of what Otto's barrage of words was really driving at. Looking down at Subaru, whose black eyes held nothing but incomprehension, Otto continued.

"Lacking in both strength and wisdom, and if you ask whether there's something else to make up for that... there isn't, particularly. Natsuki-san is small, short-armed, an ordinary person you could find anywhere. And yet, for all that, you hope for things far above your station."

"Y-you... what are you even... going on about..."

"Unable to reach, aware of your own inadequacy, and then I watched to see what second-best plan you'd come up with—and instead you just drove yourself harder, scraping away at yourself trying to pull out something that doesn't even exist... I finally understand how Patrasche-chan feels."

"Patrasche...?"

At the mention of the Earth Dragon's name, Subaru's eyes widened in surprise. Patrasche. The jet-black Earth Dragon was a beloved companion far too good for Subaru—one who would let herself be wounded to save a master helplessly cornered, and who, when Subaru shook his head unable to understand why he deserved such treatment, had taught him something important. A benefactor. A dragon benefactor, even. And Otto had just said he understood how she felt.

To Subaru, blinking in surprise, Otto ran his fingers through his own gray hair and, with a barb of irritation in his voice, said, "Now then..."

"Playing it cool in front of the girl you're in love with—fine. I think that's a necessary kind of vanity, so I'll respect it. Wanting to say and do things above your station, well, that's unavoidable, I suppose. I'll overlook that much."

That would be about Emilia. About Subaru's attitude toward Emilia.

"Playing it cool for a girl who's fallen for you—I'll allow that too. That's also necessary. I think the one who's loved bears some responsibility in that kind of relationship. Playing it cool for someone who cares about you—that matters too. I'll allow it."

That would be about Rem. Once, Subaru had said the same thing in front of Otto—that he wanted to look good for Rem, because she was a girl who cared for him.

"But you know—that's as far as it goes."

Having said his piece, Otto leaned his face in closer. Subaru flinched, shoulders hunching as if braced for another headbutt, but Otto, with an expression like he was about to bite, said—

"You know you're lacking, don't you. You know you can't reach it, don't you. You want to look good for the girl you like, don't you. You want to be someone worth being proud of, for the girl who cares about you, don't you."

"――――"

"Then for the parts those girls, those people, can't see—why not just borrow someone else's hands to fill the gap? ――Your friends, for instance."

Otto pulled his face back, pressed a palm to his own chest, and delivered that last part. At his words, Subaru let out a small "hah" of breath.

Honestly, what Subaru thought in that moment was a flat, unremarkable is that all it is. It wasn't as if he'd never thought of relying on someone, of leaning on someone, in that way. Of course he had. Just as Otto said, Subaru was well aware of his own weakness, his own inadequacy. He wasn't so arrogant, didn't think so highly of himself, as to believe he could do everything alone.

In fact, hadn't Subaru rushed around seeking cooperation from Echidna, from Roswaal, precisely to make up for his own shortcomings? And in the end, he'd gotten cooperation from neither of them—worse, he'd been made to learn truths he'd never wanted to know, and had only been wounded further for it. Even so, it wasn't as though he hadn't tried the solution Otto was proposing. Otto's argument missed the point. That road was already closed off.

"――Hah."

"What's so funny about that?"

Once the dry conclusion settled inside his chest, it took shape on his cheek as well. Seeing it, Otto's brow furrowed with displeasure, and Subaru, his face reddening and swelling, fixed his gaze on him.

"That's your reasoning missing the point entirely. ...It's not like I never relied on anyone. I did what I could think of, tried to do something about it. I tried to rely on someone I believed I could rely on... and then..."

Betrayed by that expectation, and still unable to let it go, he'd simply been left holding his head in his hands. And in the end, Emilia—whom he'd been thinking he had to protect, had to save—had denied that very notion, and it had made him realize he'd been looking down on her, treating her as a weak thing that needs my protection.

Through all sorts of experiences, meeting all sorts of people, saying all sorts of cool-sounding things, roaring that he'd somehow make things right, rejecting a resolve to die and choosing a resolve to live—putting on the appearance of moving forward, even a little, through all of that—in the end, Subaru hadn't advanced a single step, forward or back. He hadn't found a single means of changing his deadlocked situation.

Even his dry smile ran out of stock, and a cold, hard expression settled over Subaru's cheeks. At that quiet, wordless anguish of Subaru's, Otto's lips trembled.

"...But I don't recall ever being relied on by that Natsuki-san, even once."

"――――"

"Is that because someone like me isn't worth relying on, or it wouldn't mean anything...? Is that how you've written me off? Or am I, too, in your eyes, Natsuki-san... someone you feel you have to protect?"

His trembling voice, trying to kill the emotion in it, only stirred that emotion up all the more. It was Otto's anger, his sorrow, a fragment of feeling with nowhere left to go. Touched by the overflow of Otto's emotion, Subaru realized he was about to wound Otto with unintended words, and shook his head reflexively.

"That's not it."

"What's not it. It'd be strange if it weren't. If it weren't, why else would you say nothing and just curl up alone like this?"

"The reason I haven't told you anything, haven't... explained things to you, isn't because I don't trust you. It's not that. It's not like that."

"――――"

Otto said nothing to Subaru, who shook his head, eyes wandering. He simply stared, fixed, at Subaru with those green eyes.

Under the pressure of that gaze, Subaru lowered his eyes, hedged his words, and pressed a hand to his forehead. It wasn't that he didn't trust Otto. He was genuinely grateful for the man's good-natured willingness to risk his life to protect Subaru during the loops, to stay with him through circumstances with no direct payoff. The feeling of calling him a friend—that wasn't a lie either.

But how could he explain the current situation to Otto in a way that would actually get through? With people like Echidna or Roswaal, who already understood Subaru's circumstances, it was fine—he could speak while avoiding anything that touched the forbidden conditions, and the conversation would still hold together. But Otto was different. And not just Otto. Emilia, Ram, everyone else in the Sanctuary—none of them knew a single thing about the circumstances Subaru was carrying.

Without telling them about the Witch, about Return by Death, there was no way to explain the crisis Subaru was facing. Even if he could convey what was going to happen—the Great Rabbit's assault, the attack on the mansion—he had no way to show how he'd come to know it, no grounds to point to at all.

In a situation like that, how was he supposed to get someone to believe his words? How could he expect that?

He was aware his strength was lacking. Aware his wisdom was lacking. Aware he himself was weak. So ultimately, Subaru was sure, he couldn't achieve what he wished for without borrowing someone's strength. He believed that laying the groundwork to obtain that necessary cooperation was his role to play. It was just that he hadn't been able to fulfill that necessary role, and his feet had simply stopped moving.

"I just... can't explain it properly. My head's a mess... just like you said, it's a total jumble, and I can't... give a coherent explanation."

"............"

"Even if I talk, it's all stuff no one would believe... I don't know what to say or how... and so, to you, to anyone, nothing—"

"...Try saying it anyway."

"――Huh?"

To Subaru, who murmured that he had no evidence, no grounds to offer to be believed, Otto said that. Subaru looked up despite himself, and Otto, looking down at him, crossed his arms.

"So try saying it. Even if it doesn't hold together, even if the middle parts are a total jumble, even if your head's such a mess you can't lay it out logically—I'll hear you out to the end without cutting you off."

"No, but, that would just—"

"That's exactly—! I'm telling you not to play it cool about this!"

Otto kicked the ground and raised his voice as if he could no longer stand it. To Subaru, wide-eyed at the sudden shout, Otto jabbed a finger.

"If you've got time to sit there stewing over having no proof that'll get you believed, no grounds solid enough to earn trust, no way to lay it out logically—you'd be a lot better off spitting out everything in your head and just dumping it all out than curling up like this!"

"Even so... I! I need that mess to be believed...!"

"――Then say the whole mess! And at the end, just say 'believe me!' That's it! Because we're friends!!"

――It felt as if everything crammed messily inside his head, every last piece of that emotional clutter, had been blown clean away, roots and all, by the force of Otto's shouted words.

The statement was hopelessly short on grounds, and viewed logically, held not a shred of persuasive power. And yet there was something in it, more than enough to push at the back of a Subaru who'd been standing frozen, unable to move.

"You might not... believe me, but..."

Bit by bit, it didn't take long before Subaru began voicing the problem he'd been carrying alone.

"And on top of that, Roswaal's going to have assassins attack the mansion... to try and corner me and Emilia somewhere with no way out... that's how it looks."

Fearing the emergence of the Forbidden Arm, Subaru finished explaining the situation with the utmost care. The whole time, Otto hadn't interrupted once, his brow furrowed, silently listening to every word Subaru said.

"That's everything—all the information I've got right now... though it's not exactly what you'd call reliable. But that's all of it. Nothing held back. All of it."

Of course, the parts he couldn't tell—the tea party with the Witch, the matter of Return by Death—were separate. With those parts left blank, the grounds for the rest were riddled with holes, to put it mildly. Even Subaru himself, hearing the connections between the pieces of information come out so tenuous, had felt slightly sick just saying it. All the more, then, did Otto's reaction while listening weigh on him. Otto, who had flatly declared that all Subaru needed to do at the end was add "believe me"—how had he judged what he'd just heard?

"Natsuki-san..."

"............"

After a long silence, Otto unfolded his crossed arms and looked at Subaru. Seeing himself reflected in those green eyes, Subaru swallowed involuntarily. He felt his own heartbeat oddly loud, waiting to see how that first word would come out. And to the tensed Subaru, Otto said—

"Could we pretend I never heard any of that and just run away?"

"Wha— hah!?"

The response that came back wasn't just unexpected—it came from a completely different direction, and Subaru raised his voice. But Otto piled his own voice over Subaru's surprise, shouting "I mean, think about it!"

"We're trapped in a place the Great Rabbit is about to attack, and the only way to escape depends on Emilia-sama, who may or may not even be able to clear the Trial—and when we try to at least move the people who aren't caught by the barrier, some stubborn idiot gets in the way, and then when we finally manage to get back to the mansion, the master of said mansion sends assassins after us by his own order... what kind of situation is this!?"

"I want to know that myself!! Why do I have to be dragged into a situation this insane! I knew it, but does God seriously hate my guts! Well I hate him right back!!"

If there were a god who governed fate, that god unquestionably hated Subaru. He didn't feel he'd done anything to deserve such hatred, but there it was—an unreasonable state of affairs. Still, resenting God over it wasn't going to make things progress, or regress, or change, or get any easier. And before any of that—

"Wait, Otto. I get why you're kicking up a fuss like that, honestly, but... do you actually believe this ridiculous story?"

"――――"

"A witchbeast that's way too much trouble is bearing down on us, and there's no way to escape unless Emilia stands up, and Garfiel's getting in the way of evacuating everyone, and Roswaal's betraying us for some reason I can't even figure out... you believe all that?"

Saying it himself, what an appalling pile-up of circumstances it was. The two biggest problems in particular were the Great Rabbit's approach, which he had no grounds to prove, and the fact that the mastermind behind the attack on the mansion was Roswaal. Both were fatal issues, and for both, he had absolutely nothing to offer that would convince anyone else.

As for the Great Rabbit—how was he supposed to explain catching wind of a witchbeast's approach that everyone in this world was desperate to know about? As for Roswaal—why would he be scheming something that would even work against Emilia, whom he was supposed to be supporting in the Royal Selection? Subaru could explain neither.

"Natsuki-san."

After a moment with his eyes closed, Otto answered Subaru's question. He raised a single finger.

"I've traveled through quite a lot of places up to now, you know, and for what it's worth, I've had dealings with a fair number of people."

"...Don't tell me it's some kind of 'I can tell by looking someone in the eye' thing."

"No, I don't put stock in superstitions like that. Running a trade, you get more than your fill of experience with just how clear and untroubled a person's eyes can look while they're deceiving or betraying someone. I've got no shortage of experience on that front myself."

He said it with a note of pride, though wasn't that just another way of saying he'd been deceived plenty himself? In the middle of such an important conversation, though, Subaru wasn't about to interrupt with a jab, and held his tongue as Otto went on.

"Well, meeting all sorts of people that way, I've done my share of negotiating in my own fashion. It's been about four years since I left home and started peddling, and for better or worse, I've managed to survive somehow."

Otto spoke in a light tone, but it had probably not been an easy road. There must have been situations, more than once, tied directly to life and death. In a world where simply crossing a plain could mean running into the White Whale, there was no end to the dangers a traveling merchant might have faced—wild dogs, bandits at night, threats of that sort.

"Through all those days, I've lived as something of a merchant in my own right... and up to now, I've had the confidence that I was throwing my lot in with the side I calculated had the better odds. Not that the results have always followed... actually, if anything, whichever side I judged had a chance always seemed to run into some unbelievable disaster afterward, so the results haven't followed at all."

"Hey, hey, hey..."

"Good results or bad aside, I've always tried to choose in a way I wouldn't regret—when it comes to staking something of my own, betting something of my own, I think that's clearly a necessary thing to do."

What standard Otto used to choose, Subaru didn't fully know, but up to now, by his own calculations, he'd always sided with whoever had the better odds. Even coming along to the Sanctuary in search of a connection to Roswaal had been, in the end, precisely so he could meet Roswaal face-to-face with an eye toward future dealings. In that respect, Otto had been acting, thoroughly, as a realist. Which was exactly why—of course there was no way he'd lend an ear to words from Subaru that had neither grounds nor any visible chance of winning—

"So this is a first for me, Natsuki-san."

"――Huh?"

Wondering what he was about to be told, Subaru gaped at Otto. To that Subaru, Otto, wearing a ridiculously bright expression, said—

"Disregarding the odds and throwing in with the side that has no visible chance of winning—this is a first."

And with that, he'd said his piece.

His feet raced ahead. His breath came in gasps. As if chasing after his heart, which was already sprinting across the grassy field, Subaru's body cut through the wind, dragging its own impatience behind it.

Slicing through the crisp morning air, swinging his arms wide, taking one great stride after another. Kicking up dirt, stepping over stones, straight down the crude little path that had been leveled only as a token gesture.

Before long, the building he was making for came into view in Subaru's racing sight. An unintended surge of exhilaration twisted his cheeks, baring his teeth. Panting like a dog with his tongue out, Subaru threw himself forward, reaching for the entrance of the building as if leaping at it.

And then—

"――Roswaal!"

Throwing the door open violently, Subaru practically tumbled inside. He crossed the entryway, tore through the living room connected to the bedroom, and shoved open the thin door with enough force it nearly broke.

In the bedroom was Roswaal, propped up in bed, and Ram, attending to him with devoted care—and the two of them, together, turned toward Subaru with surprise coloring both their faces.

For the usually easygoing Roswaal and the ever-composed, poker-faced Ram to both react as if caught off guard—that was rare indeed. And now, about to attempt something he'd never attempted before, that felt like a good omen.

Tying the pair's surprise to that hopeful thought, Subaru pointed a finger at the master and servant, still without words, and said—

"――Let's make a bet. Your wish against mine, as the chips."
