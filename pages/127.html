Arc 3 Chapter 50: "Where the Sin Lies"

Ever since he'd fallen into this world, Subaru had experienced death again and again, in layer upon layer.

Considering that for most living things, life was a single, one-shot gamble, Subaru—who had already earned his tenth attempt—was something of a veteran when it came to "death." And from having been forced into such an intimate acquaintance with death, whether he willed it or not, Subaru alone had honed a certain sharpened sense.

—A nerve that could clearly scent out a death bearing down, unmistakable, right before him.

"What a fine mess you've all made of things, every single one of you."

That voice, carrying a bone-deep, freezing pressure, poured down from the sky to the earth below. Looking up, he saw icicles hovering as if to blanket the sky, their sharp points aimed downward, and commanding these freezing blades was one very, very small shadow.

Only by straining his eyes and focusing could he tell that it was some cat-like creature with gray fur. Though it was a strange sight—its upper body raised as if standing on two legs, short arms stretched and folded as far as they'd go, its oddly long tail swaying, and its nose an unusual pink.

Before this being that wielded human speech, Petelgeuse and the rest of the Witch Cult were struck speechless. And Subaru, likewise collapsed where he lay, found his throat closed off by a shock all his own, separate from theirs.

Because this was the first time he had ever seen this being—this spirit—display its wrath so openly. And from the mere aftershock of that wrath alone, he could tell the world was dying.

"...Puck."

Around the floating spirit—Puck—the air fogged white, and the forest all around began to transform with a sound like cracking. The trees' green dyed itself white, the leaves, the branches, the trunks all froze over on the surface, their mana drained away as they withered and fell. The same effect began to spread across the earth as well—first the plants died, then the soil, and at last that malevolent reach extended even to Subaru and the Witch Cult standing upon that soil.

His skin prickled, a burn-like pain stabbing through his entire body. A creeping listlessness welled up from deep within him, even breathing became a chore, and his consciousness began to grow hazy. Mana collection—a sensation Subaru knew all too well—was the debilitating drain caused by a spirit's forced seizure of mana. What had once been done to Subaru alone, the enraged Puck was now carrying out on a world-spanning scale.

Indeed, in the field of view of Subaru, who was biting back groans, Petelgeuse broke into a cold sweat and staggered backward, while the kneeling Witch Cultists gasped like fish starved for oxygen. Puck looked down at their state and gave his adorable little nose a "hmph,"

"The Witch Cult, is it. —Four hundred years, and not a thing about you has changed. No matter the age, you always do the one thing that grieves me most."

So saying, he moved his dark eyes, and Puck's gaze fixed on a single point in the forest. Drawn to follow that gaze, Subaru realized there remained, in just that one spot, a space untouched by Puck's mana drain. In the midst of everything freezing over and beginning to wither, that one space alone remained hollowly green. —It was the place where Emilia's body lay collapsed, resting against the roots of slender trees.

"Ah, poor Lia. ...You died without understanding anything at all, didn't you."

Into that voice, which had been deliberately stripped of all inflection, there suddenly bled a grief too plain to hide. After gazing at Emilia with lonely, sorrowful eyes, Puck slowly turned that gaze toward the survivors gathered there, and said,

"The sin of costing my daughter her life is a heavy one. Think of it—not a single one of you will make it back alive."

"What does a mere spirit—what, what, what, what, whawhawhawha—have to say?! A half-devil who failed the Trials of the Tomb is nothing but a filthy half-breed, DESU! It is your own 'Sloth,' unable to protect that fool, that ought to be condemned, DESU! Ahh! Ahh! Ahhhhh! My brain, it trembles, tremblesssssss!!"

Against Puck's threats, Petelgeuse looked up at the sky, flung out both hands, and spat as he raged. His bloodshot eyes, wild with frenzy, rolled asymmetrically left and right as his murderous intent swelled. Fully aware of how immense the power of this hostile spirit was, Petelgeuse resolved to resist regardless. This was not a choice he could make because he was brave. For a man like him, the difference in strength meant nothing whatsoever.

Because—

"Every event, every event that could possibly occur, is written in the Gospel, DESU! The Witch loves me, DESU! And so, before the diligence with which I repay that love, you Sloth-ridden creatures have no means to resist, DESU!!"

—Love unto death; love, and be gone.

For Petelgeuse, faith in the Witch was nothing other than an act of repaying her love, and he believed without end that demonstrating his love for the Witch through action was the one absolute truth in the world. Any act that displayed his love for the Witch took precedence over all else, and whoever hindered such acts would be broken and swept away before that love. —For love of the Witch surpassed everything.

"The half-devil is dead! And you too shall receive the wages of your Sloth, DESU! To the Witch's favor! To the love that shakes the heart to trembling! You would do well to die a martyr, DESU!"

Waving his arms, shrieking without restraint, Petelgeuse stamped his feet. Looking down at Petelgeuse's mad display there on the ground, Puck's eyes were coldness itself, through and through. It was neither pity nor anger, but the clear, transparent gaze of one who found no value whatsoever in his target. And even as he basked in that gaze, Petelgeuse reached a hand overhead, and behind him—where his exaggerated gesture had drawn attention—the kneeling black-robed cultists stirred—

"Die."

—Raining down without mercy, blades of ice drove into the backs of the black-robed figures, bursting out through their bellies to pin their bodies fast to the earth. Like insects pinned to a specimen board, torsos and limbs alike were skewered and staked to the ground, and the black-robed cultists all died as one, without so much as a cry of agony. From the ice stakes plugging their wounds, the freezing spread instantly through their pierced bodies, and rows of ice statues in grotesque poses lined up throughout the forest.

With no warning motion at all, in a single instant, close to twenty lives had been taken, and Puck's gaze had not so much as wavered. But out of the corner of his eye, Petelgeuse—hand raised to his head—was no different. Sparing not a glance for the followers who, at his own command, had become literal sacrificial pawns, he made use of that split second when Puck's attention had left him, and—

"—My brain... trembles."

The instant Subaru saw those lips twist into a gruesome smile, he saw Petelgeuse's shadow burst outward as if exploding. In his fallen, ninety-degree-tilted field of view, black palms burst forth in layer after layer from Petelgeuse's shadow, surging toward Puck floating in the air.

Given his own abilities, those black palms should never have been unavoidable. And yet Puck, arms folded, paid the approaching threat no mind at all. —He could not see it.

"Puck—!"

"Hush now, Subaru. —You alone are different."

Puck told Subaru this in a quiet voice as Subaru groaned out a warning of danger. Cut off by that voice, Subaru's warning never reached the spirit, and the approaching palms closed in, enveloping his small body.

"Mmf?"

Puck's small body was of a size that could be hidden entirely within a single adult's palm. With as many as seven black palms converging on him, his body was already entirely hidden from Subaru's sight. More than that, even one of those palms alone carried force enough to tear a human body apart with ease. Spirit or not, what would become of him under such physical force?—especially when the owner of those black palms was Petelgeuse, the Witch Cult's mad fanatic.

"Carelessness! Negligence! In other words, Sloth! You ought to have finished me off at once, DESU! You had the power to do it and yet you failed to do what needed doing, DESU. And that is what has produced this result, DESU! DESU! DESUDESUDESUDESUDEEEESU!!"

The Unseen Hand, visible only to Subaru, crushed down on Puck's enveloped body. Packed tightly together into a single black mass, within it the spirit's body was being erased by that malicious force—

"How pointless."

In the next instant, the black haze was blown away all at once, and Subaru saw.

"Borrowing the Witch's name for something this trivial—four hundred years too early for that. If you truly mean to kill me—"

Trees, unable to bear the sudden weight, snapped and broke, a long tail sweeping among the fallen trunks. The remains of the black-robed cultists, turned to ice statues, were shattered into splinters, and the forepaw that did it drove the ground beneath it into a world of absolute zero. A single quiet breath alone rivaled a blizzard, and within the white haze, golden eyes blazed brilliantly, glaring down upon the dying earth with a merciless light.

What stood there was—

"Stretch out a thousand shadows—half of what Satella could—and show me."

A four-legged, cat-shaped beast with gray fur, its body so massive it straddled the forest. The lord of ice who, in another world, had brought down the mansion and driven Subaru to his death—here was its magnificent, full manifestation.

Transformed into a towering form one had to look up to see, the gray beast now looked down upon the world in the truest sense. The cold intensified all at once the instant the beast appeared, and in the low temperature that had begun to dye Subaru's vision white, even keeping his eyelids open brought pain.

"————"

Enduring that faint pain, Subaru's mind, however, was already elsewhere. Blankly, or perhaps frozen in place, with the feeling of having seen something unbelievable, he could only stare in silence at the beast—a beast so vast there was no way to take it in except by looking up.

"What... are you..."

A trembling voice rang out abruptly in that world of extreme cold. The trembling in that voice surely wasn't caused by the skin-piercing chill. The one who had spoken it, after all, could have no more room in his mind to spare for the cold than Subaru did.

"What is it that you have brought here, DESU!?"

It was Petelgeuse, shrieking, tossing his head back to look up this way. His parched lips split lengthwise from the force of that scream, a trace of red blood welling up from the crack—but even that froze over and stanched itself in the space of a blink. Amid a wind so fierce that closing his eyes even to blink made him fear they might never open again, Subaru turned Petelgeuse's outcry over in his mind and looked up once more at the beast.

However many times he turned it over, there was only one answer to Petelgeuse's words. Namely—

"Puck... is that you?"

"Can't you tell just by looking? Though I suppose that's a mean thing to say."

To Subaru's hoarse question, the gray beast answered, moving a mouth far too large. Each word carried a gale of wind with it, and the giant beast, without laughing, delivered its irony and confirmed Subaru's question. At the same time, Subaru arrived at a single understanding.

—Of just why, at the end of the previous loop, he had met with a death of his own.

"It... can't be."

Leaving the silent Subaru aside, Petelgeuse wrung out those words. He put the fingers of his right hand into his mouth one after another, biting down without hesitation to crush their tips, as though forcibly anchoring a consciousness being dragged away by the cold,

"It can't be, it can't be, it can't, can't, can't, can't, can't be, DESU! A mere! Spirit! A mere spirit having...having power like this—it cannot be, DESU! Power like this..."

"—Echidna."

"————"

With bloody foam spilling from the corner of his mouth, his eyes wide open, Petelgeuse's movements froze. What had cut into the madman's words of denial was some word Puck had murmured. The instant it reached his ears, sounding like a person's name, Petelgeuse fell silent.

"A member of the Witch Cult ought to understand the meaning of that name, wouldn't he?"

As if to push forward the thoughts of the now-silent Petelgeuse, Puck posed the question. Petelgeuse's reaction to it was dramatic. There came the sound of something hard shattering, and Petelgeuse spat something out. What he spat out was a white fragment—a molar, cracked and broken from clenching too hard. And then—

"How filthy...!!"

Glaring at Puck with bloodshot eyes, Petelgeuse let blood dribble endlessly from his mouth, dyeing his chin and the collar of his priestly robes crimson, as he said,

"To even speak that name aloud is abhorrent! Ahh! You fearless, foolish, pitiable creature of Sloth! To speak, before me—before us, the Witch Cult—the name of any fallen witch besides Satella, the Witch of Envy...!!"

Whether from burst capillaries or not, Petelgeuse's eyes, stained with blood, painted themselves crimson through and through. Tears of blood ran from the corners of his eyes, and the madman, flinging bloody foam, pointed his mangled, finger-shortened hand at Puck,

"You do nothing less than insult our faith, our love! The very time we have devoted to it, DESU!"

"—Don't you dare speak of time to a spirit, when you're a human who's been alive for a mere few decades."

Puck said this coldly to the stamping, raging Petelgeuse, and let his golden eyes flare bright. That alone was enough to still Petelgeuse's frenzied trembling. No—it wasn't that he'd stopped himself deliberately. He was being forcibly frozen, starting from the tips of his feet.

Lying there, in a field of vision hazed white, Subaru watched his sworn enemy draw ever closer to death. Petelgeuse, as he froze, must surely have realized that the moment of his own death was drawing near. Before that death, he looked up at Puck with eyes ablaze,

"Time has nothing whatsoever to do with the depth of one's faith, DESU! Do not lump me together with idlers such as yourselves, who—simply because you possess something akin to infinite time—squander the greater part of it for nothing, DESU! Ahhhhhhh, my brain, it trembles, trembles, trembles, tremblesssssss!"

Even with his own end staring him in the face, there was not the slightest wavering in Petelgeuse's fanaticism. To Subaru, who knew the terror of that absolute event called "death" better than anyone could, Petelgeuse's attitude was nothing short of insane. To be able to carry one's own way of being through to that extreme, even in the face of death—that was proof, purely and simply, of madness itself.

"Death doesn't even function as punishment for you. —That's exactly why I hate the likes of you."

"The Trials of the Tomb have been fulfilled, DESU! In time, my body shall arrive at the side of the noble Witch and join her ranks. ...Ahh, how I look forward, DESU... to that moment!"

Spreading both arms wide and looking up at the sky, Petelgeuse burst into cackling laughter. The blizzard grew fiercer, the buffeting wind dyeing his gaunt frame white, and little by little his voice, his movements, slowed and began to fade. Even so, Petelgeuse never stopped laughing, and until the very moment his cackling cut off, his expression remained possessed of a madness bright enough to be called serene.

"—So he got away with it, in the end."

Muttering this, the gray beast swung a forepaw and shattered Petelgeuse's ice statue. A single blow split him into upper and lower halves, and the weight from above smashed the upper body, already crumbling as it fell, to pieces on the ground. What remained of his lower abdomen was mixed in among the shattered fragments of the black-robed cultists from before, and there was no doubting that he was dead.

—Just as Puck had said: even in death, no one had managed to break his will.

Even as he watched the man he'd hated, shattered before his very eyes, his life snuffed out so mercilessly—what filled Subaru's chest was nothing but an overwhelming emptiness. This was a man he had hated so much, wanted so badly to kill. He'd believed that man's very existence was the root of it all, that he sat at the center of every problem—that if only he could kill that man, surely everything would work out.

But what had actually come of it?

Petelgeuse had fallen, and it could be said that the threat of the Witch Cult had been removed. And yet Rem, who should have shared in that welcome result with him, had vanished from the world entirely, and Emilia—with whom he should have been able to reconcile, good news in hand—had been let die by Subaru's own doing. Faced with the two women's deaths, Subaru himself had abandoned all responsibility, and even when he'd tried to throw away his own life, he couldn't manage even that; his enemy had been struck down by another avenger's hand—and even then, that man's spirit could not be broken. In the end, he had done nothing at all. That alone was the result of all this.

"Now then—"

As Subaru lay crushed beneath his own helplessness, Puck—having quietly watched Petelgeuse's end to its finish—looked down at him. Realizing all over again that this giant beast was Puck, Subaru understood, in his very bones, just how immense that power was. Once, at the royal castle, he had watched the Knights and the Council of Wise Men tremble in fear at the sight of Puck, and had thought of it as none of his concern—

"Shall we talk?"

—but now, he understood how they must have felt, painfully well.

From the sheer cold, his consciousness had grown hazy. Every trace of the pain that had been raging so violently through his body had vanished somewhere, and he felt nothing at all of his injured spine, his shattered ribs, or the organs pierced by bone fragments. He was hearing the footsteps of death drawing near. Before those quiet, dark footsteps, small sensations like pain simply receded somewhere far away. But—

"If you think you can just slip away that easily, you're sorely mistaken."

"—Aaahh!!"

In the cold, his eyelids—drawn nearly shut by an enveloping drowsiness—snapped open as the pain sense that should have faded away was seared awake by a monstrous heat. His throat closing up with agony, Subaru turned his eyes to the site screaming out in pain and saw that everything below his right knee had been riddled through and through with slender needles of ice.

The information his eyes fed him about the wound reached his brain, and his hyper-alert brain correctly propagated the pain across his entire body. Blood seemed to explode behind his eyes, dyeing his vision red. It hurt, it hurt—no, "hurt" wasn't even the word for it. Transcending mere pain, this was hell itself, occurring within his own body.

"Subaru, you have three sins."

Looking down at Subaru as he writhed in agony and screamed, the gray beast went on speaking as if nothing at all were happening. Even though this was a being grown gigantic, its mouth full of countless sharp fangs moving as it spoke, even its very voice changed—the fact that only its manner of speaking remained the same was, conversely, terrifying. To Subaru, who was trying to distract himself from the agony by racing through useless thoughts, deceiving his own mind, Puck swept his long tail and mowed down the frozen trees, saying,

"The first is that you went against your Contract with Lia. It seems you don't understand nearly well enough how heavy a promise sealed between a Spiritual Arts User and their spirit truly is. You broke that Contract so carelessly—you probably don't even know how badly it wounded Lia's heart."

The instant those words were finished, the tip of his left leg was now the one skewered. An icicle's tip, entering through the sole of his foot, burst out from his knee, and the ice stake, still buried inside his body, branched apart, churning through muscle fiber and nerves, bursting the pink flesh open from within as it thrust out.

—Pain seized control of his brain. He couldn't see, couldn't hear, didn't understand, didn't even want to know. "The second is that you ignored Lia's request and came back anyway. If you hadn't, the Witch Cult would likely have held off a while longer, and there should have been more time before the fog appeared. If not for that, perhaps something might have been in time after all."

Just as with the black-robed cultists, Subaru's outstretched arms—spread-eagled—were pinned down from directly above by icicles. What differed from those men frozen solid outright was that the icicles, their sharp cross-sections showing, having pierced clean through the backs of his hands, then traveled on toward his elbows—cleanly severing the flesh, bone, blood vessels, and nerves straight through the middle of each arm, from wrist to upper arm, and laying them bare to the air.

"And the third is that you let Lia die."

All his wounds froze over at once, and the pain that had been grinding away at Subaru's very soul like a file receded all at once. That searing red pain, as if his nerves had been steeped in magma, was abruptly stripped away, and Subaru—who had forgotten even how to breathe—convulsed in his lungs and throat, desperately seeking oxygen.

After gasping for a while, he finally grasped the knack of breathing again. As his lungs expanded and oxygen spread through his whole body and brain, his consciousness at last began to return. Until just a moment ago, he'd thought that agony and despair, that pain, ranked somehow lower than "death"—but that thought was nowhere to be found in him now.

"Pain," "death," "fear"—all those things human beings shrink from held, each of them equally, the power to break a weak human being named Natsuki Subaru.

In a world where the tears welling at the corners of his eyes turned instantly into drops of ice, Subaru grasped that his own limbs were already in a state beyond any recovery. Frozen solid, wounds and all, they had already become one with the ground; if he forced them to move, they would simply snap off, easily, somewhere around the shoulder or thigh. In effect, it only looked as though his limbs were still attached—it was plain even to himself now that he had been reduced to something like a limbless daruma doll.

"—In accordance with the Contract, I am going to destroy this country now."

Puck told this to Subaru, whose sluggish consciousness had begun to grasp the fact of his own death. It was the first moment since arriving at this place bearing his wrath that he had let slip a glimpse of any feeling clearly distinct from that anger.

"I'll bury everything beneath ice and snow, and let that be my parting gift to Lia."

"...Something, like that..."

"It isn't about whether that girl would be glad of it. What I've decided, a promise, cannot be broken. Even if it's a Contract made with myself."

In response to Subaru's voice, which failed to form words, Puck went on, "But,"

"I know it will end unfulfilled. Even if I try to spread a world of ice, the way Lia and I once lived in that forest, and blanket the whole land beneath it—Reinhard, the Sword Saint, will surely stand in my way. And him, I cannot defeat."

Speaking the red-haired hero's name, Puck murmured it as though lamenting the gap in strength between them. Subaru couldn't believe those words, their meaning. Even Puck, who wielded power this immense, this overwhelming, was flatly declaring he had no chance of victory against that Sword Saint. And yet, even understanding that, even knowing he would be cut down partway through, he clung to a stubbornness that would not let him stop.

"Why... would..."

"—Lia—Emilia is my reason for existing."

Puck answered Subaru's question. Rubbing his nose against the ground, twisting his body as if to hide that huge face from Subaru's sight, Puck said, in a voice somehow lonely,

"There's no meaning in my existing in a world without that girl. So let the world just vanish, in a fit of my own spite. —Everything I was ended the moment that girl died."

Shaking his head, Puck closed off his words there, and the force of the wind suddenly grew stronger. The wind's cold sharpened remarkably, stabbing at his skin, freezing his eyelids, even beginning to freeze the blood circulating within his body. —The end was near.

"If I were to freeze someone gradually, gradually, starting from the tips of their fingers and toes, at what point would they die, I wonder. Are you curious, Subaru?"

"————"

"I'll take silence as a yes. And as for the answer—why don't you find out for yourself."

Slowly, slowly, the encroaching ice began to eat away at his flesh. Beyond the limbs already frozen solid, his shoulders and thighs now began to freeze as well, and destruction equal to what his limbs had already endured came on, trailing agony and despair behind it.

"—So the fog's come, has it. Seems you've gone and called something troublesome, right before you die."

If pain could drive a man mad, then hurry up and shatter this sanity of his already. Tear it apart, smash it open, break his heart into pieces—please. Otherwise—

"Gluttony... ah, though these days it goes by the White Whale, doesn't it. You called that thing here, let Lia die, and threw away your own life besides... You really are hopeless, aren't you."

He couldn't hear it. Someone's voice was far away, and only his own screams went on echoing endlessly. Gradually the screaming faded, and in its place came laughter—cackling laughter.

Cackle, cackle.

It was a laugh he recognized. The voice of the man he'd hated enough to kill. Feeling that he absolutely had to find out where it was coming from, he sent his scattering consciousness searching for the source of that laughter. And then, he realized.

It was his own mouth, cackling with laughter he could no longer hold back.

The pain stopped registering, and pleasure began to take command of his brain instead. A world of madness spread out before him, and he stepped into a landscape twisted just pleasantly enough to feel good.

The laughter wouldn't stop. It was laughter mocking himself.

He'd let Rem die, killed Emilia, and even his own death had been for nothing. Ah, truly, what a thing, indeed.

"—How slothful of you, Subaru."

With a small snap, his consciousness cut out.

And surely it wasn't only things like consciousness or life that had been cut. —Something more, something he had somehow, for so much longer, managed to keep holding onto, had just now snapped, with that same small sound.

With a snap, it had cut out.
