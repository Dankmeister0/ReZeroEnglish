Arc 8 Chapter 58: "Proof of Existence"

——He circled behind the watchful sentry and struck the pressure points running along the centerline of the man's body.

"—————"

In an instant, the man collapsed without so much as a sound. Olbart caught the falling body, not out of any kindness, but because he couldn't afford to let the clatter of armor hitting the floor echo through the hall.

"Yare yare, holding up a grown man with one hand is rough work for an old-timer, eh. —And the fact that he's hollow inside but still weighs a ton is nothing but trouble, eh, now."

Grumbling so, the white-haired, white-browed old man—Olbart—held the fallen enemy up with just his left arm and gave the castle wall beside him a light tap with his toe. At that, the stone wall rippled like the surface of water where it had been struck, swallowed the enemy's body as he shoved it in, and sealed itself back up.

Fortunately, the Undead didn't seem to need to breathe to stay un-dead, so burying one in a wall wouldn't kill it.

Its heart wasn't beating either, so no blood spilled. And if you did kill one, all you had to do was sweep up the shattered pieces with a broom afterward—convenient, as far as disposal went.

"Only trouble is, nothin' else about 'em is good. Kill one, and some of 'em just get right back up soon as they're dead—so killin' careless-like just makes more trouble for yourself, eh."

Of all the Undead's peculiar traits, the one Olbart considered most dangerous was their ability to revive after being struck down.

Not because being resurrected over and over would break the spirit of even the most battle-hungry Vollachian soldier—no, the problem was that an Undead who revived after being killed carried over its memories from before death.

If that happened, the enemy could trade a life that was replaceable anyway for the instant sharing of every scrap of information it held.

As head of the Shinobi, Olbart knew all too well how vital freshness and accuracy of information was in war. Over his lifetime, the enemies he'd killed the most of were scouts and messengers, running neck and neck.

Normally you killed someone to prevent information from getting through—but here, killing them risked passing that very information along anyway. It was nothing short of absurd.

"Well, Shinobi got their own tricks—like blowin' yourself up when you die and sendin' word through the color of the smoke, so I reckon we're even, in a manner of speakin', eh."

Being a nuisance was the Shinobi's true specialty, but at this rate, business was going under.

According to findings made during the delaying actions of the retreat from the imperial capital to the fortress city, some of the Undead had a weak point called a "Core Insect," apparently the source of their revival. Some Undead reformed even after losing their heads, and that, it turned out, was because the Core Insect hadn't been killed.

On top of that, the Core Insect's power supposedly traced back to the great spirit known as the "Rock Mass"—so killing too many Undead, Core Insect and all, would drain Muspel's strength until it could no longer hold up the empire's land.

That was why Olbart labored, with great care, to enforce a strict policy of "no killing" against the Undead.

The Undead he'd just embedded in the wall, too, he'd disabled by precisely striking the pressure points of the human body, robbing it of its ability to move. At this rate, Olbart had walled up some fifty Undead so far—and he was relieved that, hollow papier-mâché bodies or not, the pressure points still worked as vital points.

Though against the Undead's overwhelming numbers, that was no more than a modest act of resistance.

"Tellin' a Vollachian—folk who're best at killin' and gettin' killed—not to die and not to kill, now that's just cruel, eh."

It was an opinion that would make the allied troops from the Kingdom and the city-state—cooperating to save the empire—frown if they heard it, but one that a fair number of Vollachians would probably sympathize with.

As a result, the enemy leading the "Great Disaster" had forced upon Vollachia the one kind of defensive war it was worst suited for: one premised on not killing. —No, calling it a result would be wrong.

"Always imaginin' the worst is just a bad habit of old age, I reckon. But this enemy's usin' every drop of blood the empire's ever soaked into its own soil against it… goin' straight for the weak spots, through and through, eh."

A battlefield where seasoned veterans rose again one after another—that was an environment only possible because Vollachia was a land where strife never ended.

Given the same conditions, another nation likely wouldn't have suffered a catastrophe on this scale, which meant the enemy was employing the most fitting strategy possible to bring Vollachia down.

Which was exactly why their own side had to understand this was a board where they couldn't afford to misread a single move.

"—————"

Narrowing eyes shadowed beneath his white brows, Olbart held his breath within the Crystal Palace, where he infiltrated alone.

His stealth, erasing his presence as thoroughly as possible, was a true showcase of a Shinobi chief's skill—including thinning out the sentries without any of them noticing. He could not afford to be detected.

If his presence were caught and he were included among the targets of the "Thorn Curse," even Olbart would be rendered helpless. He was weak to pain. Just imagining thorns piercing his heart made him want to roll over and give up.

To avoid that fate, Olbart moved with the utmost caution.

—Strike the imperial capital with a small elite force, and swiftly cut down the enemy's leader.

This was the strategy adopted for the final phase of the war against the "Great Disaster," a move that both Vincent and Olbart agreed, without objection, was the best option available.

The Undead outnumbered the Vollachian army's total forces, and even a war of attrition would only whittle away at the Rock Mass's life, shortening the empire's own lifespan in turn—a thoroughly underhanded bind.

That they needed to force a swift conclusion was obvious to anyone.

"Course, the enemy's surely expectin' that too, eh."

But even if the enemy knew, if there was no other move to make, they had to make it anyway. All that was left was how much they could pack into that already-read move to betray the enemy's expectations.

For that reason, the small elite force had been split further, preparing multiple flashy decoys to draw the enemy's eye.

While those decoys did their work, all Olbart had to do was fulfill the role assigned to him.

—Olbart's role was to slip into the Crystal Palace and bring back intelligence on the castle's murky interior.

Chief among these priorities were locating the whereabouts of the one called Sphinx, mastermind of the "Great Disaster," and freeing the "Steelfolk" Moguro Hagane, presumably confined somewhere within. After that, if there was room to spare, recovering Cecilus's beloved swords, the "Dream Blade" and the "Wicked Blade."

"Well, Cecil's swords probably ain't gonna turn up. The Wicked Blade runs off on its own, and Lord knows where he's even stashed the Dream Blade, eh."

Cecilus loved to run his mouth about anything and everything, but while he'd boast about how amazing his beloved swords were, he wasn't foolish enough to blab about their actual properties. He might well answer if asked outright—but asking about an enemy's hidden cards would only make them suspect there was a reason behind the question.

Olbart wasn't fool enough to carelessly provoke Cecilus over it.

In any case, given the low likelihood of success, Olbart put recovering the swords on the back burner.

Ideally, he'd pin down Sphinx's location and secure her person. If he could manage that, then with the power of that peculiar shrunken boy who refused to go back to his old size—Schwarz—and the girl accompanying him, they could destroy the Undead, soul and all.

Achieving that would be the most desirable conclusion possible to this war against the "Great Disaster."

Though, that was no easy task either.

"—Tch, they've made this a dead end too, eh."

Contrary to his wish for a swift conclusion, Olbart's feet kept getting stopped.

Grasping a scene perfectly from a single glance at a map, and never forgetting a place once visited, was a basic Shinobi skill—and to Olbart, the Crystal Palace was a familiar old workplace.

And yet his search wasn't going smoothly, because the interior of the Crystal Palace—which should have been thoroughly familiar—had been completely rebuilt from what Olbart knew.

"This kind of trickery's supposed to be our exclusive specialty, eh."

Olbart himself saw no value in it, but the Crystal Palace was renowned as one of the most beautiful castles in the world. Thanks to the battle for the capital, there were traces of partial wall collapse here and there, but even so, its appearance from a distance hadn't changed much.

Step inside, though, and it was an entirely different story.

The layout of the corridors, the placement of the rooms, the size of the doors—all manner of elements had been altered, transforming the Crystal Palace into an entirely different castle, one that could confound even Vincent, who had lived here.

Precisely because the exterior showed no change, anyone entering without a plan was guaranteed to be led astray.

Just bringing this information back would more than justify Olbart having gone in ahead of the others. Or had Schwarz, acting as strategist, anticipated even this?

If so, that foresight was as reassuring as it was, in the future, a threat.

Even the Kingdom and the city-state, cooperating with the empire for now—there was no telling how they'd behave toward it once the Undead "Great Disaster" had been stopped at the water's edge.

And when that time came, Chisha would no longer be at Vincent's side.

"—Still, you sure pulled somethin' off, Cheshi."

The flames of war, the reek of blood, the air of "death" that clung on for both the living and the dead alike.

He'd had no time in his heart to mourn amid all that, but Olbart offered that assessment of Chisha regardless—the man who had stolen his own techniques through "Noh," and who had, on top of that, kept his impersonation of Vincent hidden without anyone noticing.

Olbart liked stealing others' techniques, but he did not like having his own stolen.

After all, growth was the privilege of the young. They had every possibility of creating something new with their own hands, without needing to steal. The old, on the other hand, had no room left to grow. That was why Olbart disliked having things stolen from him.

What would happen if the young acquired a taste for stealing rather than creating?

Nothing new would ever be born into the world again, and there would be nothing left for Olbart to steal, would there?

"And that bastard Cheshi went and pulled a lazy trick like that, eh. —Though I can't say I don't understand the feelin'."

Within his limited time, Chisha had sought the best possible outcome.

In the end, Chisha's choice and Olbart's were the same. Olbart was an old man with death's welcome drawing near, and Chisha was a soldier who had fixed the timing of his own death close at hand.

So the story went that, in order to increase the cards in hand, neither had been picky about the means.

Gambling with what few options he had, Chisha won this present situation—

—Keeping Vincent alive, and bringing Olbart onto their side.

"—————"

—Olbart Dunkelkenn had ambition.

It was his final goal in life: to leave his name in the history of the empire in a form unmatched by any other, to carve into that history proof that his existence, as a single life, had truly been.

Taken in as a Shinobi, raised as a Shinobi, and living his life as a Shinobi—that was Olbart.

His was a role in which most lives were used up and discarded, where it was only natural not to live long, and yet Olbart had lived nearly a hundred years now. But across a life lived long both as a Vollachian and as a Shinobi, the greater part of Olbart's days had been spent fulfilling the purposes of others.

If that was simply the fate of a life lived as a Shinobi, then so be it—but Olbart had lived exceptionally long, even among Shinobi. —So then, perhaps his ending didn't have to be Shinobi-like either.

If it was the Shinobi way to leave no name behind, to vanish silently into history's darkness without a sound, then why shouldn't Olbart deliberately aim for the exact opposite?

For that reason, Olbart had kept watching like a tiger, biding his time, waiting for his chance.

To make one last blossom bloom at the end of his life, he had even considered that taking the head of the "Sage Emperor" Vincent Vollachia would be the most rewarding option—

"But if the enemy's out to destroy everything, there ain't no point in betrayal, is there."

With the enemy being the "Great Disaster"—an army of the Undead—and its goal the total destruction of the Vollachian Empire, Olbart's scheme had no choice but to crumble away, fleeting and fragile.

Olbart wanted to leave his name in the empire's history.

If the empire itself were destroyed, there was no way Olbart's wish could ever come true. Which was why he had to take his hat off to Chisha's act, for keeping Olbart from acting rashly until things had come to this point.

"Just hope there's still room left for me to pull off somethin' after all this ruckus settles down. Reckon my own life might run out first, if I ain't careful."

He hadn't ruled out, say, assassinating the emperor amid the postwar rebuilding of a spent empire, before the Kingdom or city-state could make their move—but it felt too much like piggybacking off the "Great Disaster," and he wasn't keen on it.

In any case, unable to find the perfect opportunity, Olbart tucked away his premature calculations and ambitions until the "Great Disaster" itself was driven off, and looked ahead. —Ahead of Olbart's gaze stood the great doors leading to what, in the original castle, would have been the audience chamber.

"—————"

In Olbart's silent heart, two contradictory sensations welled up.

The sense that he should not go beyond this door, and the sense that he should—the former rooted in instinct, the latter grounded in years of a Shinobi's honed intuition.

Ordinarily, Olbart's judgment would settle on the former without hesitation. But his gut was telling him: beyond this very door lay the reason he'd infiltrated at all.

Whether that meant Sphinx, or Moguro, or the swords—or none of them—he couldn't say, but—

"…Drag this out too long, and an old man with not much life left might miss his one shot at overthrowin' a nation—now that'd be somethin' I couldn't die easy over, tch, honestly."

Muttering that in his rasping voice, Olbart decided which way to move—forward or back.

Reckoning that, if this really was the audience chamber's location, he ought to be able to check on things through a hole in the wall without going through the door, Olbart made his way along the wall from a corridor window to reach the room he sought.

Without opening the door, he peered inside—

"—Oi, oi, even I wouldn't go this far, eh."

At the sight of the "Witch's" handiwork within that transformed hall, the head of the Shinobi known as the "Wicked Old Man" muttered those words, making no effort to hide his displeasure.

—That Thing's soul had long since lost sight of its original form, torn to shreds.

Recalled to the earth by forbidden arts, forced to submit to the banner meant to destroy the very empire it should have belonged to—a pitiable soul held captive. That was the situation in which the Undead found themselves.

To a greater or lesser degree, the Undead had had their mental structures altered from what they'd been in life. Otherwise, there was no way every single resurrected dead would end up aiding in the empire's destruction.

The degree of alteration ranged from light to severe, but the more heavily one's original character was burdened, the more faithfully it obeyed orders—at the risk of being unable to bring out its true strength.

For that reason, it was only natural for the caster to want to keep the burden light on those who had been powerful in life.

In fact, skilled fighters like the "Thorned Emperor" or the "Magic Bullet Marksman" suffered a marked decline in ability under mental strain, so the "Witch" had steered toward preserving their strength even at the cost of granting them some measure of free will.

Had these powerful figures been made into mere obedient puppets, they would likely have shown a pathetic performance, unable to bring out even half their true strength.

In any case, aside from such exceptionally powerful individuals, or Things like Lamia Godwin who had shown initiative in cooperating with the "Witch," the souls of the Undead were being toyed with mercilessly.

Whatever wishes they had held in life, however noble a warrior they might have been, whatever they had once held dear—all of it was trampled underfoot and put to use.

And they were forced to move as the vanguard of calamity, working to destroy the empire.

That Thing, too, was no exception.

Robbed of free will, it had become a tool to be used up and worn down for fighting, and no matter how many times it tasted "death," its soul was summoned back again, reborn into a new vessel of earth as its anchor.

Sadly enough, that Thing matched rather well with the conditions the "Witch" had set for Undead transformation.

Skill high enough, an attachment to battle strong enough and then some, no real disadvantage in ability even if turned into an obedient puppet, and a fierce regret and hatred seared into memory as its final moment—

For that reason, it rampaged fiercely, driven by an endless, unchecked passion.

Dragged along by a soul that was being torn apart and losing its original shape, its body had forgotten it was ever human-shaped, transformed into a monstrosity ghastly to behold.

Long, oversized arms, too many legs, a paper-thin torso of nothing but skin and bone—even having been reduced to such a shape, it could not escape being used by the "Witch."

No matter how many times it was killed, its soul would be stretched out and it would revive again as yet another monster.

To end that cycle, there was nothing to do but grant the "Witch's" wish. Grant the "Witch's" wish, become a herald of the "Great Disaster," and burn the empire to ash.

For the Thing that had become an impossible shape, that was now its only wish. That alone was its wish.

That alone, that alone, that, that, its wish was that alone—

"—Izmail, the 'Giant Eye.'"

—. ——. ————————.

Suddenly, amid the dreadful roaring and the sounds of fierce battle, it heard that.

Into ears that no longer had the shape of ears, all manner of other sounds should have been pouring in as well, and yet that single voice alone came through strangely clear, oddly solid, mysteriously whole.

What meaning it carried, the Thing did not know.

Did not kno—

"Izmail!"

"Iaa iau."

The question brought its thoughts to a standstill, and all its many arms and legs meant for killing came to a halt. Through that opening, a hand reached out, stroked its thin, flat torso, and snatched something away.

There was a sensation, smooth and sudden, of something slipping free.

Whether it had been something important, or something large, it couldn't even tell. But the instant it slipped away, the violent impulse that had filled every inch of its body receded all at once.

Hollow. Hollow. Its insides had gone hollow.

The countless reasons to rampage, forced in from outside, drained away, and deep within its hollowing self, only what had been there from the very beginning remained.

That was its reason to fight. You could call it ambition, or a hunger for glory.

The reason it had rushed to join the fight was to fight. It fought in order to carve its name into history. To prove that it—the "self" its clan had once taken pride in—had truly, certainly existed.

That its own existence, that Izmail the "Giant Eye," had been there—

"Ah... so it came true, after all..."

The golden pupil floating within its huge, black single eye reflected the man's face with perfect clarity.

That striking man, black-haired, with sharp, narrow eyes, didn't so much as flinch at the blade bearing down on his own life, staring back with unshaken composure, calling out to Izmail in his transformed, unrecognizable form.

The one who stood at the very peak of the Vollachian Empire had recognized Izmail.

And that—

"—Is the honor of a sword-wolf, Your Imperial Majesty."

Jamal blocked a blow from the arm fused with a battle-axe, while Subaru and Beatrice used magic to keep in check its movement as it darted freely through the streets, exploiting the mobility of its many added legs.

All the while, attacks of lethal force were unleashed again and again, but Spica, leaping and bounding, forcibly knocked them down each time, narrowly preventing harm to her comrades.

And then—

"—Izmail, the 'Giant Eye.'"

Eyes sharp, Abel stared down the transformed Undead and called out its name.

For a moment, Subaru thought, no way—but he'd decided from the start that if Abel called out an Undead's name here in the capital, he'd believe it wholesale. And in fact, up to now, Abel had never once mistaken an Undead's name.

Still—

"Izmail!"

"Iaa iau."

At the end of their joined hands, Spica—flung forward as if swung into the fray—slammed her palm against what looked like the chest of the monstrous being he'd called Izmail.

Not a punch, not a strike—her "Star-Eater" activated, and Subaru and the others held their breath.

If the conditions were met, then no matter how far its shape had strayed from human, it should meet the same end as the other Undead.

And indeed, before Subaru and the others as they watched with bated breath, Izmail's massive body slowly trembled, and with the golden pupil in its large, round single eye, it fixed its gaze on Abel.

And then—

"—Ah."

Leaving behind some unvoiced word, its monstrous, massive body crumbled all at once into dust.

"Ah, damn it! Unbelievable! What the hell was that monster just now!"

"A warrior of the Cyclops Tribe, called Izmail, the 'Giant Eye.' Exactly as I named him, at the end."

"The Cyclops Tribe—so it's not just havin' one eyeball, they turn into monsters like that too?! ...I mean, is that so."

Having finished the fight with the powerful Undead, Jamal grumbled while wiping the blood and sweat running down his chin.

He caught himself for having spoken so roughly, forgetting for a moment he was addressing Abel, but his point wasn't off the mark.

The person known as Izmail hadn't looked like that from the start, after all.

"Beako."

"I know, I suppose. That was a victim of the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King,' I'd wager. Revived over and over again until he was in no proper state at all, I suppose."

"Uu, au..."

As Beatrice gave a firm nod, Spica stared down at her own palm with a downcast look. Resting a hand atop Spica's head to give it a pat, Subaru let out a deep breath.

Subaru had encountered Izmail once before, during the battle for the capital, while fleeing with Rem, Flop, and the others—and the transformation of his form back then had already been too horrific to look at directly.

Whether he hadn't been able to die even after being blasted by a dust explosion, or whether he'd been revived again after dying, his transformation into a monstrosity had only progressed further.

But that endless hell should now have been ended, by Spica's "Star-Eater."

"Still, how'd you even tell who that was, lookin' like that?"

"The moment one is revived as an Undead, one's features are already affected to some degree. What matters, then, is distinguishing the individual's characteristics. That one fell into an easily recognizable category."

"Easily recognizable..."

Barely a trace of its original human form remained, and it was only because Subaru had already seen it once before, after it had strayed from human shape into monstrosity, that he could match it to the enemy from the retreat—but that wasn't the case for Abel.

And yet, that he could name it so confidently anyway made Subaru suspect it was less a monstrous memory and more something bordering on outright cheating.

"One keeps track of the empire's notable soldiers. Whether they end up an enemy or an ally, having more material to judge by is never a bad thing."

But the words Abel added after that revealed he hadn't identified the man from simple physical traits alone—he had assessed other qualities in him as well.

"—————"

Hearing that, Subaru recalled Izmail's final moment, just before he'd crumbled to dust.

That single voice, wordless and impossible to make out, had nonetheless seemed to carry something more solemn in it than resentment or grudge.

Perhaps, just perhaps, it had been a feeling of reverence toward the emperor who had named him correctly.

Either way—

"—Guess this is crunch time."

Subaru murmured, looking up at a sky from which a booming roar echoed without pause.

Within Subaru's field of view, an iceberg tore through the cloud-covered sky and fell toward the Crystal Palace—one Roswaal had had Emilia make for tactical use.

Further off in the distance, swirling clouds had become an ominous weapon, and in yet another direction, thunder roared noisily up from the ground, while cityscapes could be seen sliced apart by clean sword strikes and collapsing.

Proof that, at each point, everyone was keeping to the conditions Subaru had laid out and waging their own fierce battles.

"That Roswaal's really goin' all out, I wonder. Overdoin' it, I suppose."

"Seein' that reminds me he really is a scary bastard. Hope he doesn't go and crush the castle with Olbart-san still sneakin' around in there."

"If it's necessary, he'd crush it, I'd wager. That's the kind of man he is, I suppose."

With a sound like the sky itself splitting, the shattered iceberg scattered countless glittering fragments across the heavens.

Roswaal, together with Medium, was currently facing off against the "Flying Dragon Rider" enemy—since dealing with that foe was one of the hardest tasks of all, there was nothing to do but trust in Roswaal's victory and leave it to him.

Of course, the "Thorn Curse" and the "Cloud Dragon" remained threats beyond anyone's control as well.

"The question now is whether Emilia-tan and Tanza can pull it off..."

In an imperial capital descending into a state that the word "fierce battle" didn't even cover, Subaru worried over Emilia and the others—among all the comrades sent out to different fronts, the ones whose outcome remained unseen.

He believed the current distribution of forces was as good as it could get, but Emilia's placement alone hadn't been pinned down clearly, left in a vague, uncertain form. Still, considering the role entrusted to her, having her act together with Tanza for now had to be the right call.

"Honestly, when Izmail showed up I thought we were done for, but..."

That the current force, without Emilia, had managed to hold back that monstrous Izmail was undeniably thanks to everyone fighting together as one.

Jamal's contribution in particular had been huge, and his movements were visibly improving by the moment. Maybe, standing on the brink of death, he was leveling up at an incredible pace.

That in itself was something to welcome—setting aside the mental discomfort of Jamal turning out to be ridiculously reliable.

"Jamal Aurélie, you can still go, can't you?"

"Yessir! If it's by Your Excellency's word, I can take on a hundred, two hundred of 'em!"

"So he says. The rest of you had best exert yourselves greatly as well."

"Damn it, actin' all high and mighty just 'cause you're doin' a job nobody else can."

Cursing at Abel, who hadn't joined the fight and looked cool as ever all by himself, Subaru then checked on how Beatrice and Spica were doing.

It was one tightrope walk after another, but the fight against the Undead was always a total commitment of whatever forces were on hand. Given his intent to proceed carefully, it wouldn't do to press on with anyone left as a weak point.

"Betty's no problem, I suppose."

"Aau!"

At Subaru's confirming glance, the two of them nodded back gamely. At this rate, Subaru himself might end up the one with the most reason to worry about stamina.

Though, since Subaru's connection to the "Pleiades Battalion" was still ongoing, perhaps everyone would unanimously agree that Abel was actually the one to worry about most, stamina-wise.

In any case—

"The battle situation ought to start shifting soon. If Halibel-san's doing her part, there should be a path opening up toward the Crystal Palace by now. From there—"

He wanted to gather information from the other fronts, material to weigh this loop and what lay ahead against.

Just as he was about to call out to his companions with that—

"—I see. What a curious phenomenon. Observation required."

Suddenly, a voice from beyond everyone's awareness made them all whip around as if struck.

In Subaru's wide-eyed field of view, there stood a third party who hadn't been there before—a short, pale-skinned Undead crouching down, checking the dust piled on the ground with a fingertip.

Toying with the dust of Izmail, destroyed by "Star-Eater," was a pink-haired Undead they knew all too well—

"—You—"

"From what I can see, it didn't directly interfere with the core insect. The insect has died from losing its host... Loss of soul? Seizure, plunder, retrieval... Fascinating."

Murmuring so, licking the dust that had soiled her fingertip to confirm it, was a being who looked the spitting image of someone close to Subaru's group, while carrying in her eyes a coldness utterly unlike them—Sphinx.

The mastermind of the "Great Disaster," the very operational objective of this final battle, had appeared right there before them.

"—————"

At an entrance so brazenly composed, Subaru's mind went blank for an instant.

With the "Thorn Curse" and the "Magic Bullet Marksman" both in play, it was fraught with difficulty for Subaru's group—carrying their trump card, Spica—to come face to face with Sphinx, who should have been inside the Crystal Palace. That was why they'd sent Olbart, a master of concealment, to scout ahead, intending to keep hunting Undead and whittling down enemy strength until a path opened, so as to fight the coming decisive battle from a position of advantage.

But to have that entire premise collapse this easily—

"—No."

This is a chance, Subaru thought, suppressing his shock, gripping Beatrice's hand tightly. Exchanging a brief glance with Beatrice, Subaru also took hold of the startled Spica's hand.

The Sphinx before him was the very source behind this "Great Disaster."

And like the other Undead, she too had been revived by the power of the "Sacrament of the Immortal King"—which meant Spica's "Star-Eater" should work on her as well. By becoming Undead, she had gained the advantage of "fleeing into death," but in exchange, she had also taken on an inescapable weakness in the form of Spica.

Strip away that chance of revival, and the battle would end.

Even without knowing the enemy's intentions or aims, they could bring the fight to a close.

So—

"Sphin—"

"What's necessary is a match between the target's 'Name' and 'Soul.' I'm quite surprised you went so far as to make use of the Authority of 'Gluttony.' Countermeasure required."

"—!"

Their attempt to strike first was crushed by Sphinx's calm analysis.

Caught off guard at hearing the Authority of "Gluttony" spoken aloud, at the fact that the true nature of Spica's "Star-Eater" had been seen through at a glance, Subaru held his breath. He'd let himself hold it.

Forgetting the strength of an Authority that, even if its aim were seen through, could not be escaped so long as it landed.

And so, he found himself paying the price for that.

"—Idiot."

A voice, cursing coarsely, cut in between Sphinx and Subaru's group as they were shoved aside.

Sphinx had raised one slender finger, pointing its tip at Subaru's group. Before the white heat ray unleashed from that fingertip could pierce through Subaru and Spica's heads, a man leaped in the way.

The sword Jamal had held up was gouged clean through in the shape of the beam, and what it failed to stop tore through the torso of the rough-hewn warrior of a man, the strong, raw stench of scorched blood striking hard against their nostrils.
