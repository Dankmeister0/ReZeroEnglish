Arc 8 Chapter 35: "A Job Worth Dying For"

"Huh? What did you just say?"

"I won't say it a third time. Any citizen of the Empire I've been able to account for since I took the imperial throne — I can match every one of their faces to their names."

"Are you out of your mind!?"

Subaru's voice cracked at Abel's absurd answer, delivered with arms crossed and a grave expression.

The scene was inside a Dragon Carriage, on the road from the fortress city of Garkla toward the imperial capital of Lupugana, now the Undead's stronghold.

The strike team — dubbed the "Save the Vollachian Empire from Ruin Squad" — was split roughly between Kingdom-affiliated members and Empire-affiliated members, riding across three Dragon Carriages along with the necessary supplies. Within that arrangement, Subaru had deliberately chosen to ride in the carriage carrying the Empire's people.

The reason wasn't some betrayal of the Kingdom — there was simply something he had to ask. He wanted to know why Abel, the Emperor himself, had joined the strike team.

Of course, given that this was the decisive battle for Vollachia's very survival, it made sense for Abel, given his position, to stand on the front lines. But speaking as someone who'd weathered more unwanted crises alongside Abel than he cared to count, Subaru knew Abel had wits, but no fighting strength.

As far as the raw combat power that would be needed in the capital going forward, there was no reasonable justification for his coming along.

Subaru wasn't about to guess that even Abel was Vollachia-brained, so if the man was coming along, there had to be a reason that overturned that assumption. But there was also the chance it was the sort of thing Abel wouldn't want to say in front of Kingdom folk — which was exactly why Subaru had gone out of his way to climb into this carriage.

And so, skipping the roundabout talk, he'd simply asked, "What good are you, anyway?" — which brought them to this.

"Matching every citizen's face to their name..."

The answer was so absurdly off the charts that Subaru's jaw simply hung open. At being called out like that, Abel himself frowned, displeased.

"Don't misunderstand. Not all citizens of the Empire. Those with no public exposure, like the People of Shudrak who stay holed up in the forest — their identities are beyond my grasp. Hence, accounting for the entirety of the Empire's citizenry is impossible."

"That's not the part I'm mad about — it's plenty crazy either way! I mean, what you're saying is you remember every single one of your own Imperial soldiers, right?"

"It's necessary."

He said it flatly, in a single line, but that wasn't how a normal person thought.

Of course, Subaru had never thought Abel was a normal person to begin with, and the fact that he had every Imperial citizen's face matched to a name mattered a great deal, given their current situation.

Because—

"For that girl to strip an Undead of its yoke, a name is required, is it not."

At Abel's flat remark, Subaru glanced over at Spica, seated at the edge of the carriage.

Given her position, Spica couldn't simply be left unattended, so she too rode in this carriage along with Subaru. Right now she sat quietly being coddled between Medium and Beatrice, who never strayed far from Subaru's side.

To bring Spica's Authority to full effect, Abel's abnormality — his memory — proved useful.

'Star-Eating' — there was still far too much unclear about how that power actually manifested.

When she'd succeeded in 'Star-Eating' the imperial family member Lamia Godwin, no one had forgotten her, which meant Spica apparently hadn't devoured her "name" as such. Even so, there had been a definite sense that matching a face to a "name" was the key to that Authority.

Which was exactly why, for now, they had no choice but to cling to the one method that had already worked.

"But it still pisses me off that your stupidly perfect memory is useful for it..."

"Schwarz-sama, isn't that a bit of an unfair accusation, even so? No matter how much His Excellency the Emperor treats Yorna-sama coldly time and again."

"Is that counsel for this one? Or a grievance against me, wearing that skin?"

"Naturally, it's a warning for Schwarz-sama, isn't it?"

The one who answered so coolly, turning her face away from Abel's gaze, was Tanza, the girl in the kimono.

As one of the members accompanying the strike team, she was, it seemed, someone who had feelings about Abel where Yorna — her single greatest reason for going to this battle — was concerned.

From what Subaru had heard, Abel had flatly rejected Yorna's proposal of marriage, so between that and his cold treatment of the Divine City, Tanza had exactly zero reasons to think well of him — if anything, the number was negative.

"Let it be known, the one Yorna Mishigure held feelings for wasn't me, but a former Emperor."

"Eh, really? Like your old man or something?"

"Further back than that. If you want the details, go ask her yourself. At the very least, that chance still remains. Isn't that right, girl."

"...Yes. Even now, I can feel Yorna-sama's power."

At Abel's words, Tanza gently laid a hand over one of her eyes and answered quietly.

It wasn't wishful thinking, but a clear certainty born of the blessing of Yorna's power, lent to Tanza. As long as that remained, Yorna's survival was guaranteed.

Still, there was no telling what state she was in within the capital, and that uncertainty was maddening.

It wasn't just Yorna — Subaru was worried too about Priscilla, whose whereabouts were unknown, Al, who'd stayed behind to help her, and Cecilus, who'd wandered off on his own. His impatience only grew.

"But now that I think about it... isn't our fighting power seriously lopsided?"

Suppressing his impatience, Subaru pictured the makeup of the "Save Them from Ruin Squad" once more and finally voiced the thing he'd been debating whether to say.

Abel closed one eye at the remark, and Tanza tilted her head instead.

"Lopsided fighting power, you say?"

"That's right. I mean, look at us — there's me, Bea-ko, Emilia-tan, Spica and Roswaal, and our great hope Garfiel."

"Did you just brag about Betty, I wonder?"

"Yeah, I did. Lovely Bea-ko, I love you."

"Betty loves you too, I suppose."

Having confirmed their mutual love with Beatrice, who'd cut in partway through, Subaru held up one finger.

Outside this carriage, Emilia and the others were riding together in the one running behind.

"And Halibel-san over there is a secret weapon Anastasia-san sent us. For a battle that's supposed to be for the Empire's sake, it's practically all outside help."

Of course, it wasn't as if the strike team had no Imperial personnel at all.

But chief among them were Abel, whose brainpower had just been revealed to be inhuman all over again, and Medium, who'd negotiated her way directly to Abel to join up. Then there was Jamal, who'd somehow been promoted to General and picked as a bodyguard, now driving the carriage in high spirits.

There was no doubt Medium and Jamal both fell into the higher-combat-power category among Imperial citizens, but even so, neither could be called stronger than Garfiel.

"Tanza belongs to me too, so she's basically on our side, practically speaking, right?"

"—"

"Huh? I figured I'd get smacked right away and got kind of let down... delayed reaction—OW!"

"Please don't say such presumptuous things."

Subaru cried out, struck with far more force than he'd braced for as payment for his little joke. He crouched down, eyes watering despite himself, but all he got in return was Tanza glaring down at him with icy eyes.

Sure, he'd said too much, but wasn't this an awful lot of anger for it?

"Ka-ka-ka! Reckon you got told there, Your Excellency. Says he can't rightly see how serious the Empire is about this. Can't say I blame the boy for sayin' so, eh?"

"Yeah, yeah, can't blame me for saying so— GYAAAH!?"

"Subaru!? What's wrong, I wonder!?"

Just as he moved to nod along with the sudden gravelly voice, Subaru let out a shriek from the depths of his throat.

He tumbled away and clung to Beatrice, who rushed over in alarm. As Subaru held onto her, a small, bent-backed shadow stood within his line of sight.

Appearing out of nowhere as ever — the Wicked Elder, Olbart Dunkelkenn—

"Subaru, calm down, I suppose. It's just a small old man who appeared out of nowhere, I wonder?"

"If a small old man just appears out of nowhere, that's either Olbart-san or a Japanese horror movie! And the thing they've got in common is they're both terrifying! Insane! Off the rails!"

"Oh-ho, listen to this whelp run his mouth. Still small as ever, but you been keepin' well?"

"Whose fault do you think it is that I'm small!? I've been fine! What about you!?"

"I been keepin' well too. Though I did go an' lose my right hand, just a bit."

Saying so, Olbart gave a little flutter of his folded-up empty sleeve where his right hand used to be. Shown that fluttering fabric, Subaru found himself unable to say anything careless, and fell silent.

For an absurd old man, showing off his wound to earn sympathy was a dirty trick.

"But here I am, falling right into his trap anyway...!"

"Put your inner conflict on hold. — You've heard the particulars, haven't you, Olbart Dunkelkenn."

While Beatrice stroked the head Subaru held in his hands, Abel called out to Olbart, ignoring his anguish entirely. At the address, the strange old man nodded with an "Aye, well," and said,

"There's some sorcerer in the capital puppetin' the dead, and you're headin' off to kill him, that it? I can just picture Goz bawlin' away, left behind by Your Excellency. Ain't that a pity, now?"

"That one has a role only he can fulfill. Naturally, the same goes for you."

"I'm an old geezer past ninety, y'know? Workin' me like this still — could stand to show an old man some mercy."

"If you want to discuss retirement, do it once this crisis is settled. And let me add this—"

Olbart never once dropped his breezy manner, even facing Emperor Abel himself. Overlooking the strange old man's thoroughly disrespectful attitude, Abel added one final thing.

At that preface, to Olbart, who raised an eyebrow with an "Oh?"—

"From here on, consider it settled that there will be no occasion, until your life runs out, for your bad habit to show its face."

"—"

"Therefore, I command you. Don't get any funny ideas — simply fulfill your duty, Olbart Dunkelkenn."

At Abel's words — brief, but laden with clear intent — Olbart pressed his lips together.

It touched on the considerable concern Subaru harbored toward Olbart, and it was also a gamble meant to head that concern off in advance.

If Olbart were to fly into a rage here, there was no one in this carriage capable of stopping him.

But Olbart stayed silent a while, tapping his own lower back with his remaining left hand, and said,

"Well, well. Young folk have too much room to grow, and that's just what I don't like about 'em. Never figured even Your Excellency would turn on me too — hard world for an old man, ain't it."

"I am generous."

"Ka-ka-ka!"

Olbart laughed as though he'd just heard the best joke of the century.

Sensing that this marked the conclusion of the quiet duel between Abel and Olbart, Subaru finally felt the needless tension drain from his shoulders.

Setting aside how funny Abel's joke actually was, the powder keg between the two of them had been avoided.

"...So, will Olbart-sama be accompanying us to the capital as well?"

It seemed Tanza had felt the same tension as Subaru. At her question, her voice slightly stiff, Olbart narrowed his eyes beneath his bushy brows and said,

"His Excellency asked me himself, and it'd rankle me somethin' fierce to just take it lyin' down from this whelp. Besides, a country that won't play its cards when its own hide's on the line ain't even worth a laugh. And besides—"

"Besides?"

"There's others in the capital too, ain't there. At least that Moguro, and that brat Seci."

"...Yorna-sama as well."

A temporary alliance in the Divine City — Subaru didn't know the full details, but Olbart, who'd Infantilized him and the others, and Tanza had acted together on a shared interest.

Theirs was a complicated relationship, but for now, whatever friction remained between them had been set aside in this exchange.

Of the names raised, Subaru had never met this Moguro person, but at the very least Yorna was someone he could trust both in ability and character, and Cecilus was more than adequate in ability — enough to make up for however much his character fell short. Add Olbart to that mix, and the Empire's fighting strength really would be substantial.

"But we've got Halibel-san on our side too, so even with Ceci and the rest, we've got a slight edge... I mean, Ceci's one of my friends anyway."

"You think you can put a leash on that one? If so, it shows just how little you understand him."

"Ahh, so Halibel's comin' along, is she. That one's the only shinobi in this whole world stronger than me, so I flat-out hate her."

Each of them had plenty to say about the strongest of the cards in the other's hand.

In any case, with Olbart joining them, Subaru now had a clearer picture of the reason and stance behind Abel's coming along, as well as what to expect from the Empire's fighting strength. His questions — aside from a few things he still wanted to dig into, like the matter of Medium being a candidate for Empress — had, for the time being, been mostly resolved.

"I'll save the Medium-san question as something to look forward to once we're back. — Rem told me the same thing, after all."

Feeling the softness of Beatrice's hand held in his own, Subaru recalled the words Rem had sent him off with from within the great fortress at their departure.

It was something he could, at maximum positivity, interpret as her hoping he'd come back and praying for his safety.

"I might get chewed out something fierce with a vengeance once I'm back, but until that actually happens, I'm gonna take it positively as Rem worrying about me."

"Y-you don't have to be so self-deprecating about it — Betty thinks she really was worried, I suppose?"

"I took it the same way as well, but..."

"C'mon now, I know it's not your fault, but neither of you understand Rem at all. Sure, Rem's kind, so I bet there's a little of that in there, but mostly it's anger at me... no, no, positive, stay positive...!"

Subaru, having nearly undone the suggestion he'd cast on himself by accident, recast the spell of positivity on himself once more.

Beatrice and Tanza both watched him with looks of pity, but to a Subaru freshly re-enchanted, the color of their gazes meant nothing at all. Clang — he deflected it.

"—Your Excellency, we'll be reachin' the relay point shortly, so the carriage'll stop for a spell."

Just as Subaru finished re-arming his mental fortitude, the carriage's small window slid open, and Jamal's report came in from the driver's seat, peering into the cabin.

Jamal, who'd been keeping watch around the moving carriage, startled at the sight of Olbart — who had no business being there — sitting beside Abel as he nodded at the report. Olbart waved back cheerfully at his reaction, which was tasteless in the extreme.

In any case, once they reached the relay point Jamal had reported, Subaru — his questions now resolved — intended to head back to the Kingdom's carriage where Emilia and the others were. But before that, thinking the timing was right, he called out "Hey" to Jamal through the little window.

"I happened to catch a bit of this — you've apparently been saying you're gonna die for Katya-san's sake?"

"Ah? So what if I am, kid."

"No, I mean, that's messed up, isn't it? Katya-san's already devastated over losing her fiancé — can't you imagine how she'd feel if her family died too?"

Hearing that Jamal had proudly declared to Katya that he'd go die bravely and gloriously, Subaru had questioned his sanity — dizzy at the thought that Vollachia-brainedness ran this deep.

So he wanted to make Jamal take those words back, and meant to have him swear all over again that he'd absolutely come home alive to Katya.

But Jamal only snorted, displeased, at Subaru's words, and said,

"Listen, kid, don't go spouting nonsense."

"Nonsense? What's nonsense about it?"

"Katya crying because I die? Who gives a damn about that. What matters is how much use I am to His Excellency and the country when I die, and whether that gets a reward or not."

"That's exactly it — you people go on about honor and treat lives so cheap—"

"You don't get it, kid! She can't stand, and she can't bear children! Without Todd around, there's no man who'd take her! Katya needs money to live!"

Jamal raised his voice, spit flying, at Subaru as he tried to press further.

Jamal then looked straight past Subaru, over toward Abel behind him, and said,

"Right now I'm a Third General. If I'm of use to His Excellency here, I can expect to be treated as a Second General when I die. That'll set Katya up for good. As Katya's brother, I can do more for her dead than I ever could alive!"

"—"

At the sheer intensity of Jamal's reasoning, Subaru couldn't help but hold his breath.

"Subaru-chin..."

It was Medium who looked at the silenced Subaru, her brows drooping with worry. But that was as far as her reaction went — she raised no objection to Jamal's logic. And it wasn't only Medium. The same went for Abel and Olbart too.

Jamal's argument made sense. From the perspective of the Empire's people, it was a shared premise.

Only Beatrice, as if considering Subaru's feelings, took half a step closer and pressed her shoulder against his.

"Uau."

—No, Spica too, gently laying a hand on Subaru's back, tried to offer him support.

"—"

Against Jamal's logic, Subaru's was an emotional argument that knew nothing of the Empire's reality.

But even so, the fact that Abel and the others said nothing to what Jamal was saying — that was wrong. If dying earned a reward that would secure Katya's future, then Jamal should live, and live, and keep on living, rise through the ranks, and secure Katya's life with his own salary instead.

"That'd work fine, as long as Abel's not paying his Generals some ridiculously stingy wage. — I've decided, Jamal."

"Ah? What now..."

"That future you've got planned out — I'm absolutely going to stop it. — You are absolutely not allowed to die."

Clenching his back teeth hard, Subaru declared it like a challenge.

Hearing this, Abel gave a small snort, and Olbart bared his teeth in a low, amused laugh. Tanza hugged her own arms tightly, and Medium blinked wide-eyed before nodding.

And Beatrice and Spica, the two of them, stood alongside Subaru and stared Jamal down.

At the gazes of those three children lined up before him, Jamal narrowed his uncovered left eye and said,

"You're one creepy kid, you know that."

"Your friend told me the same thing. — Not that I'm gonna stop."

With that final exchange before the small window closed, Jamal returned to his lookout duties, set on bringing the carriage to a stop at the relay point.

Watching him go, Subaru closed his eyes and faced his own heart honestly.

He understood that Jamal had his own reasoning. And that it was one of the ways of thinking considered correct within the Empire.

Keeping all that in mind, he still thought: to hell with it.

"Dying to save someone — something like that—"

That was nothing but a means so agonizing even Abel couldn't bear it.

Which was why Natsuki Subaru had to go on denying that method, no matter what.

Contrary to the hurrying speed of the carriage, the capital in question remained frustratingly far away. Thinking of what was happening there, his heart burned with impatience — hurry, hurry.

Hurry, hurry — Subaru's heart went on burning with impatience all the same.

—The scene shifts, under that same sky, to a situation that could hardly be called peaceful.

Black clouds hung low, a sky that seemed to foretell the Empire's advance toward ruin.

Soaked in the enormous volume of water that had poured out from beyond the shattered floodwalls, the imperial capital of Lupugana — having driven out the great many living souls once inside its star-shaped ramparts — had become a city of the Undead.

Survivors kept fighting against the wandering of the resurrected dead, and the terror of being found while holding their breath in hiding — though among them were also some bold souls who sought to win their lives through combat rather than mere concealment.

But most of those who took up arms would come to regret so rash a decision.

A battle, once begun, meant an endless race against Undead who kept reviving without end — pitting one's life force against those whose vitality had already run out entirely left no room for victory.

Ironically, it was the bravest who lost their lives first, joining the ranks of the Undead to go hunting for the next of the living.

The imperial capital had now become a spiraling city where death swirled endlessly, the Undead calling forth more Undead — a manifestation of hell in which the despair of this world had been boiled down to its essence.

But—

"—It's certainly a rare sight to behold, but the trouble is that once you get used to looking at it, it's not all that interesting. If only a slightly more worthy opponent had returned once more to the stage, now that would give me something to look forward to."

So said the boy, his bundled blue hair fluttering in the damp wind that carried a faintly rotten scent.

The boy, whose appearance stood out for his wasou robes and zōri sandals, shaded his eyes with a hand and swept his gaze all around, letting slip that impression.

His blood-warmed skin and blue eyes were unmistakably those of the living, far removed from the most obvious traits of the Undead.

And yet, his impression, gazing down at the scene of screaming horror across the transformed capital, was in a sense an opinion even further removed from the living than any ordinary Undead's would be.

Or perhaps, depending on who you asked, one might have defined a true transcendent being as one granted such license to do as he pleased that even the distinction between living and dead became meaningless.

Either way, the boy's eyes held something brightly, cheerfully insane — and it wasn't insane simply because he felt no fear whatsoever toward this situation.

It was insane because, in all his life since birth, he had never once known fear at all — that was the light held within them.

"Now then, cutting down every enemy in sight one after another, sword swishing away, and repainting this whole battlefield single-handedly would be quite the feat — a magnificent, legendary achievement only I could pull off, but..."

The boy, whose gaze would surely strike terror into anyone else, lowered his hand, stopped staring off into the distance, and cocked his head.

"Why is that, I wonder? Somehow I just don't get the feeling that leads anywhere good!"

Throwing both hands up toward the sky in surrender, the boy stayed honest about what he felt.

Given his ability, without exaggeration, the boy could probably mow down most of the Undead infesting this capital single-handedly. And yet he wasn't doing it. And he was agonizing over the fact that he couldn't quite put into words, even to himself, why he wasn't.

To the great many people suffering in this capital, it was a worry so far off the beaten path that it wasn't so much a luxury as something that might make them keel over dead from sheer indignation. A concern no one but the boy could understand, that meant nothing to anyone but the boy — and yet, to the boy himself, it was of the utmost gravity.

Groaning over it, the boy sighed in a troubled way and said,

"I can't quite explain it well. What do you think about that, there?"

"I don't, really get it, but..."

"Mm."

"Is that... something we could talk about after you pull me up...?"

The boy crouched down where he stood and peered below.

What leaped into his view was a man in an iron helmet, clinging desperately to the edge of the rooftop of a tall building he'd chosen as a temporary perch, doing everything he could not to fall.

The man was holding up his entire body weight with a single hand, but there was no helping that — after all, he had only one arm, and supporting himself with both hands was simply not an option for him.

In any case, looking down at the man in such a dire situation, the boy — Cecilus — smiled.

"No good, Al-san. Since I haven't the faintest idea what you actually are, I simply can't decide whether it's right to let you tag along for the rest of my History!"

Below Cecilus's beaming face, the dangling man — Al — made a thin, strangled sound in his throat.

The imperial capital of Lupugana had by now become a hell where a spiral of death, Undead calling forth Undead, swirled endlessly on—

"Ahh, damn it, this is seriously the worst..."

But at least, in this one moment, this one scene, it had come down to two living people — who had no reason to fight each other at all — interrogating each other over the whereabouts of a life.
