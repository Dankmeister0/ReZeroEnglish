Arc 5 Chapter 11: "An Unexpected Reunion, an Inevitable Reunion, an Unintended Reunion"

After that, the walk back—taken at a leisurely pace as they strolled through the water capital—went smoothly enough. Every now and then, Emilia would gaze out at the water's surface as though lost in thought, but the moment anyone tried to point it out, she'd quickly mask it with a smile.

The person from before—a hooded man close to their own age, though beyond that Subaru couldn't recall a single distinguishing feature—what connection did he have to Emilia? For someone who apparently knew Emilia, the man had seemed remarkably at ease. If anything stood out, it was the way he'd said something about her silver hair—positive words about silver hair were rare enough on their own—

"Come to think of it, the perception-obstruction coat..."

The thought struck him suddenly—Subaru realized that the perception-obstruction effect hadn't worked on the man at all. It was the spell woven into Emilia's coat, the one that kept people from consciously perceiving her distinguishing features. When the spell worked properly, all a stranger should be able to tell about Emilia was her gender, at most. But the man had clearly seen her silver hair. That meant the man was, at the very least, skilled enough that the spell couldn't fool him.

"Beako."

"Betty noticed, I suppose. Emilia and Garfiel are both too scatterbrained to have caught on, it seems. Such troublesome children, I suppose."

Sensing the concern Subaru had picked up on, Beatrice, walking beside him, gave a faint dip of her chin. A little ways ahead, Emilia and the others walked on; behind them, Subaru lowered his voice.

"He didn't try anything, but that guy back there is a little suspicious. Perception obstruction isn't something that just gets broken willy-nilly, right?"

"Either he's well-versed in magic, or he holds rank enough to match it... Either way, he's certainly no ordinary citizen, I suppose. Nothing but trouble, I suppose."

"Guess I should give Emilia a heads-up later, without making a big deal of it."

"Betty thinks that's fine, I suppose. Emilia should at least realize the feeling she sensed was something ill-intentioned, I suppose. She's not the type to go looking for it herself, I suppose."

At Beatrice's certainty, Subaru accepted it with a short "Yeah, I guess." If she said so, her judgment could be trusted. Beatrice, for all her manner, watched people closely. If she'd seen enough reassurance in Emilia's demeanor, that was good enough for now. No need to stir up needless worry.

Still, at the very least, he and Beatrice ought to stay on guard. In a water capital this vast, the chances of crossing paths with that man again didn't seem high—but the possibility that he might make contact was one that warranted constant vigilance, and then some.

"We're 'bout there, boss. Keep pace with Beatrice's little steps and the sun'll set on us."

"No one asked for your commentary, I suppose, you little brat."

Walking ahead, Garfiel turned just his head back, and Beatrice, needled by the jab, snapped back roughly. Garfiel laughed cheerfully at that—then, abruptly, his expression changed. His ears twitched, and his muzzle wrinkled.

"What's wrong?"

"Nah, it's just... sounds like some kinda argument comin' from the inn."

No sooner had Garfiel said he could hear voices from around the corner than the commotion reached Subaru and the others too. Sure enough, it sounded like two men arguing.

"Sounds like a real showdown. This town never runs out of trouble, huh."

"Says the boss who set off a magic-mineral explosion in someone else's workshop. Can't really talk, can ya? If the merchant guild folks hadn't cleared the area, you'd be surrounded by guards right about now."

"I still think that one was outta my control... Emilia-tan?"

Feeling a bit awkward at having his own recent incident brought up, Subaru noticed Emilia, walking beside him, break into a light jog, and called out to her. Without turning around, Emilia answered his call.

"One of those voices just now sounded familiar... actually, I think it's Joshua-kun."

"Ah. Now that ya mention it, might be that scrawny guy's voice."

"If someone we know is causing trouble, that's a problem. Let's go too."

Passing the not-especially-tense Garfiel, Subaru chased after Emilia. He quickly caught up with her as she rounded the corner ahead, and at last the commotion in front of the Water Feather Inn came into view. There—

"Forget it, quit makin' me say the same thing over and over, kid! Cut the smart mouth and go fetch your master already!"

"I wouldn't dream of calling out my master—or even my brother—in front of ruffians like you. Please leave. While I'm still handling this politely!"

"Man, you just don't listen, do ya. I'll knock your block off, I swear!"

A purple-haired young man—Joshua—stood in front of the inn with his arms spread wide, locked in an argument with the man. His opponent had his back turned, but he had a sturdy, muscular build. Given the roughness of the words he was hurling at Joshua, things could turn violent any moment.

"That's enough!"

Before Subaru could even gauge the difference in strength between the two, Emilia had darted forward and inserted herself between them. The man flinched back, and Joshua's eyes went wide with surprise.

"E-Emilia-sama!?"

"We just finished our errand and came back. You can't go making a scene like this in front of the inn. What's this fight even about? Calm down and talk it out."

Her tone—the kind you'd use to break up a squabble between children—drained the tension right out of what had been a hair-trigger standoff. Subaru let out a relieved sigh at the sight, while Garfiel, catching up at a leisurely pace, said with evident disappointment, "Aw, ain't gonna be a fight after all?"

"Everyone's back one after another... Good work out there."

"Thanks. Now then, the cause of the fight. Don't hide anything, tell me."

"It's nothing that should worry you, Emilia-sama..."

Joshua, stubbornly refusing to let her get involved, might have been worried that having this settled for him would leave him holding some awkward debt. If so, he was overthinking it—Emilia scheming something that calculating wasn't going to happen even if she aged another hundred years.

"Ain't nothin' complicated about it. This brat here wouldn't let us through even though we came 'cause we were called for. So I was protesting, that's all."

Just before the frustrating standoff could take hold again, it was the man arguing with Joshua who spoke up. His narrow, upturned eyes sharpened, and he flung his complaints about Joshua's handling of things in a voice edged with hostility.

"And that's exactly why I've told you again and again. If you're going to impersonate someone of rank, at least prepare properly and know your place. Putting on a slightly nicer set of clothes doesn't hide the lack of refinement that seeps right through. Enough with the lies already!"

"Sorry for bein' so tacky! It ain't like I dress like this 'cause I want to! Same with runnin' errands like this. Argh, damn it, this is pointless!"

With Joshua stubbornly refusing to accept a word he said, the man finally clawed at his own head in frustration. Even though Subaru and the others had stepped in to calm things down, they'd been left completely out of it—the two men were locked in a world entirely their own.

"Honestly, this is getting nowhere. Subaru, what should we... Subaru?"

Troubled by the two men's refusal to listen, Emilia turned to Subaru and tilted her head. In front of her, Subaru had a hand to his chin, brow furrowed in thought. His gaze was fixed on the rough-mannered man arguing with Joshua.

"What is it, Subaru?"

"No, it might just be me, but... I feel like I've seen this guy somewhere before..."

"Ah? What, now you're gonna start picking a fight with me too...!?"

Overhearing Subaru and Emilia's exchange, the man turned to redirect his anger their way. But the moment his gaze landed on Subaru, his expression stiffened. His lips trembled, and he pointed at Subaru.

"Y-you... the Reinhard scam guy...!"

"'Reinhard scam,' that's an awfully specific kind of scam to be known for... ah."

The words "Reinhard scam" jogged something loose in Subaru's memory. And with it, the man's identity too. He was cleaner-cut than Subaru remembered, and dressed far more respectably now, but the shifty look in his eyes hadn't changed a bit.

"Chin! It's Chin, isn't it! Whoa, why are you here of all places? You been doing okay?"

"Don't go callin' me like we're buddies! And who's 'Chin,' anyway! I got a name, and it's Rachins!"

"But you're totally Chin."

"Shut up!"

Overcome with nostalgia, Subaru tried to throw an arm around his shoulders, but Chin—Rachins—roughly shook him off. Pouting at the cold treatment, Subaru got a question from Emilia: "You two know each other?"

"Yeah. A blast from the past—someone I knew from the royal capital, back when Emilia-tan and I first met. Cornered me when I wandered into an alley and tried to rob me blind."

"Oh, is that so... wait, rob you?"

"Next time I went to the capital, they tried to rough up Priscilla too. Then they came back later with reinforcements for revenge. Real memorable bunch."

"As far as Betty's concerned, they sound like nothing but trash, I suppose."

Emilia and Beatrice reacted to Subaru's wistful reminiscence like that. Behind them, Garfiel cracked his knuckles, and Joshua's glare grew even sharper. Sensing the tide turning against him, Rachins lost even more color from his already pale face.

"W-wait a sec. Sure, maybe somethin' like that happened, but both times it didn't even go through, and it's ancient history, right? Let's let bygones be bygones and just talk this out. C'mon?"

"Garfiel. What do we do in a case like this?"

"No mercy for scum who oughta die."

"W-wait, wait a second! Seriously, wait! I said wait!"

Sensing an aura from Garfiel that was clearly a cut above any two-bit thug, Rachins seemed to realize he stood no chance in a real confrontation. In the year since their last meeting, it seemed he'd had at least some opportunity to grow. He clutched his head and backed away several steps, then pointed at the inn.

"It's true! I was called here... no wait, not me, my employer was called here! But she insisted on touring the town before heading to the inn, so I was told to run ahead and let the inn know. I ain't lyin'!"

"Ah, yeah yeah, got it, got it. We'll hear the rest inside... nah, in the courtyard, nice an' slow."

Garfiel advanced on him mercilessly as Rachins pleaded his case for all he was worth. Subaru felt a little bad for how earnest Rachins was being, but he had nothing in him to make him believe the guy either. Dressing the part meant nothing if the substance didn't match up—trust wasn't won that way. No wonder Joshua had turned him away. It was a story that hit uncomfortably close to home.

"Bad timing for you, pal. Turns out this happens to be a day when this inn's crawlin' with celebrities—!"

Garfiel backed the retreating Rachins against a wall and reached for his collar. But the motion stopped short—an instant later, Garfiel had spun around with no windup at all. His eyes went wide, pupils narrowing to sharp points of alertness in an instant. Every fine hair on his body stood on end, and it was plain that his fangs, claws, and muscles had all snapped into combat readiness.

It was a reaction almost absurdly sudden, almost absurdly instinctive and decisive. It was something primal, summoned up by Garfiel's fighting instincts, and precisely because of that, it spread its tension to Subaru and the others without a shred of doubt. And as Subaru and the others turned to follow the direction of Garfiel's glare—

"Rachins. I was wondering why you hadn't come back—what's all this noise about?"

For a split second, Subaru could have sworn there was a flame standing right in front of him. The flame flickered red—no, it was a raised hand, giving a light wave. A humanoid shape. No—a human. Hair like a blazing crimson flame, and clear eyes that seemed to hold a captured blue sky. A tall frame draped in white, with features so fine that once seen, they could never be forgotten. The shock that ran through his entire body was surely the very thing an ordinary person felt upon coming face to face with a hero. Because that encounter was, without question, exactly that kind of moment.

There was no mistaking him. The man's name was—

"—Reinhard."

At the breathless, hoarse call of his name, the young man approaching them smiled gently. There was a warmth to that smile with the power to forcibly set at ease the heart of whoever it was directed at. Just being smiled at was enough to give Subaru the peace of being held in the arms of an absolute guardian. And it wasn't only Subaru—everyone present felt that same sense of calm.

"It's been a while, Subaru. To think we'd meet in a place like this—I'll have to thank Julius for inviting me along this time."

"O-oh. Yeah, it's been a while. Been doing okay?... Wait, you too, invited by Julius?"

"To be precise, it wasn't me Julius invited. Felt-sama received Anastasia-sama's invitation. I simply came along, intending to enjoy a reunion with friends while I was at it. I never imagined you'd be here too."

Subaru was inwardly startled to find himself so overwhelmed by the presence of a hero. He'd been overwhelmed by the sheer scale of Reinhard's presence before, sure. But he hadn't always dealt with him while feeling like every pore in his body had just sprung open. That he could now sense a degree of Reinhard's brilliance he hadn't been able to perceive before was simply because Subaru himself had honed his skills enough to notice it. A man who felt further away the more you trained—now that was something to be humbled by.

"I see. Maybe it's because a whole year has passed. You seem far stronger than the last time we met, Subaru. That makes me happy."

"Don't say that so much, it starts to sound like sarcasm. Except it doesn't sound like sarcasm at all, which is the scary part. I had some confidence I'd grown too, but looking at you, that confidence kinda fades."

"That's not true at all. If anything, I'm the one disappointed in my own lack of growth. It feels like it plainly shows just how differently we each spent this past year—I'm embarrassed by it."

That sounded less like a real problem and more like the kind of complaint you'd hear from someone who'd hit a growth cap, or whose level was just hard to raise because it was already so high. And even before that—the fact that someone with this much skill still refused to stop growing was, frankly, terrifying to Subaru.

"By the way, Subaru."

"Hm, yeah. What's up?"

"That fellow who's been glaring at me this whole time—he's your friend, isn't he? I'd appreciate it if you could tell him not to be quite so wary of me, if you don't mind."

At the end of Reinhard's wry gaze stood Garfiel, still unable to stand down from combat readiness. Garfiel had curved his already-hunched back even further, poised to spring at the slightest trigger. Subaru had, over the past year, seen firsthand—and relied on—just how easily those fangs and claws could tear a prey apart as weapons. And yet, even if Garfiel were to snap and attack, Subaru couldn't picture him managing to land so much as a scratch on the young man before him.

"Garfiel, stop it. This is Reinhard. My... friend. He's not gonna hurt anyone, and I won't let anyone hurt him either."

When it came to saying the word "friend," Subaru hesitated, just slightly. Part of it was having just physically felt the sheer scale of the Sword Saint and shrinking from it. Part of it was the memory of their last parting—the training grounds, the day of the Royal Selection—flashing before him, the memory of swatting away the hand Reinhard had extended to him there. But beside Subaru, lost in that thought, Reinhard nodded, apparently untroubled by any of it.

"Just as Subaru introduced me. I'm his friend, Reinhard van Astrea. I'd be grateful if you'd tell me your name as well."

"—Garfiel Tinzel."

"A fine name. And you're well-trained, too. Impressive, for someone so young."

At Reinhard's offhand remark, Subaru felt a delayed jolt of shock. Over the year since leaving the Sanctuary, Garfiel had gained experience of the outside world, and despite his manner, his appearance now carried even more of a settled, seasoned quality than before. If he kept quiet and composed, he'd pass for someone around twenty. No one could guess his true age of fifteen now any more easily than they could have a year ago—if anything, less so. And yet Reinhard's remark had seen through that fact effortlessly.

"I've heard the rumors. The twin pillars guarding Emilia-sama—'Garfiel the Shield' and 'the Spirit Knight, Natsuki Subaru' are both well known. As your friend, it makes me proud."

"Guess it's a small mercy that name's the one that stuck."

"I know of another nickname too, but I suspect you wouldn't be too pleased to hear it. By the way—the partner behind your title of 'Spirit Knight,' would that be the young lady there?"

Reinhard's attention next turned to Beatrice, who had made herself small right beside Subaru. Without anyone noticing, she'd taken a firm grip on Subaru's hand, and now she stared straight back at Reinhard as he knelt to bring his gaze to her level.

"I take you for a Great Spirit of high standing. It is the utmost honor to be granted the chance for an audience."

"...Betty is Subaru's Contract Spirit, Beatrice, I suppose. Your admirable manner isn't bad, I suppose. But don't come any closer than that. You should already know why, I suppose."

"I understand. My apologies for the burden I place upon you."

Unlike Garfiel, she wasn't showing open hostility. But the grip Beatrice had on Subaru's hand was in dead earnest, and she couldn't fully hide an unusual trembling. And yet, that wasn't her fighting off fear. It was something else entirely. And even after being treated in a manner that was, if anything, rather much, Reinhard turned to the last person standing there, Emilia, and offered her a reverent bow.

"Emilia-sama, it has been too long. Word of your many accomplishments since then reached me even in my own distant domain."

"Yes, it's been a while, Reinhard. It really has been a whole year since that castle, hasn't it. We've heard plenty about you all too, you know."

"Compared to your own brilliant accomplishments, Emilia-sama, mine amount to little. I've found myself almost hating my own inability to lend proper support to my liege—especially upon hearing of Subaru's exploits."

"Fufufu. That's right, Subaru's been amazing. He's my knight, and I'm proud of him."

At praise from Reinhard that didn't sound like mere flattery, Emilia puffed out her chest, looking proud. Having her speak of him that way, so proudly, left him half pleased and half embarrassed. In any case—

"Anyway, we were getting pretty off track, but—you called out to Rachins earlier, right?"

"Ah, that's right. Is he an acquaintance of yours too, Reinhard?"

"Yes, that's right. He... currently works as an attendant under Felt-sama. He still falls short in many respects for the role, but Felt-sama is fond of him."

"She hired that guy!?"

At the unexpected news, Subaru's eyes went wide. Hearing that, Reinhard's brow drooped apologetically, and he turned a sorry look toward Subaru.

"Thinking of how you must feel, I'm filled with a desire to apologize myself. I was there, after all, when they cornered you in that alley. Later, when we crossed paths with them again, a number of things happened, and... on the spot, Felt-sama declared she'd take them in."

"Well, I mean, saying I don't care would be a lie, but... what kind of coincidence is this. Of all people, those guys... wait, did you just say 'them,' plural?"

"She hired three of them, including him. All of the ones who tried to attack you back then."

"The whole gang of bumbling fools, all present and accounted for!?"

At this bizarre twist of fate, all Subaru could do was clutch his head and shout. The three of them—people he'd ended up having a series of memorable run-ins with almost the moment he'd arrived in this world. He'd never given a thought to what became of them afterward, but to think he'd meet them again like this.

"Um, it's fine that Subaru's really, really surprised, but... so basically, Rachins-kun is one of Reinhard's group, and Felt-chan's subordinate, right?"

"That's right. Felt-sama wanted to tour the town, so I sent him ahead to let the inn know first, but he never came back."

With Reinhard's account tracing exactly what Rachins had said earlier, everyone nodded in understanding. Seeing that, Rachins, who'd frozen up, recovered his momentum.

"S-see, told ya! I said it over and over, didn't I! And yet you all ganged up to doubt me! Apologize and pay up damages, c'mon!"

"Rachins. As I've told you many times, your way of speaking shows a distinct lack of self-awareness for someone acting as an envoy. I've grasped the general situation, but it seems difficult for me to defend you regardless."

"Whose side are you even on!?"

"The side of justice. And in this case, I think it's only natural that my friend's younger brother misunderstood you."

Saying this coolly to the shouting Rachins, Reinhard turned a smile toward Joshua. At Reinhard's smile, Joshua nodded with a somewhat awkward expression.

"It's been a while, Reinhard-sama. This time, due to my own mishandling, toward your envoy..."

"That fault lies on our end, Joshua. And please don't do something as itchy as adding '-sama' to my name. It's been a while since we've met, and feeling that distance makes me a little sad."

"My brother and you are friends, Reinhard-sama, but the two of you are political rivals now."

"You haven't changed. You don't need to imitate Julius that closely, even down to that."

At Reinhard's wry smile, Joshua fell silent with an expression like he was grinding his back teeth. In any case, it seemed the trouble here had settled down safely. A relief—but in its place, a different question surfaced. Namely—

"Still, calling not just us but you all too—what's Anastasia-san even thinking?"

"The invitation we received was framed as arranging a venue for beneficial exchange of information. I expected Anastasia-sama to have some clever scheme cooked up, but I never imagined even Emilia-sama and the rest would be invited too. Though given how things are going, it might turn out to be about more than that."

"You mean she's got something even more surprising prepared?"

"I think that's a possibility, wouldn't you say, Joshua?"

Turning the question to Joshua, one of those responsible for organizing this, the young man adjusted his crooked monocle and played dumb, saying, "Now, who's to say." Reinhard, closing one eye at the attitude that seemed to have regained a bit of composure, turned his gaze toward the inn.

"So this is what they call wafu architecture. The Water Feather Inn, is it. An unusual shape. Though I hear it's not so uncommon a style over in Kararagi."

"Huh, that's surprising. So even you'd never seen it before, Reinhard. Have you ever been to Kararagi or anywhere like that?"

"No. I'm forbidden from leaving the country, you see. There's a risk of running afoul of international treaties, so I even avoid going near the border. Which is why Priestella, so close to Kararagi, is about as far as I can push it."

Subaru and Emilia both blanched at the ordinance apparently banning the export of Reinhard. They thought it might be his way of joking, but as he laughed "hahaha," he never quite claimed it was a joke. Pressing further felt too frightening, so for now, confirming the truth of it would have to wait.

"Man, I'm kinda worn out. We can't keep making a racket out front forever, it's a bother to the inn—let's just head in if we're heading in. Felt and the others will still take a while, right?"

"Since I, her chaperone, have stepped away from her side, yes. She's probably cutting loose right about now. She does need to rest her mind every so often, so I won't rush her."

"...'Every so often'? Feels like she's always cuttin' loose."

"Rachins, did you say something?"

"Nothin' at all, sir. So, what should I do? Bein' here is makin' me die of discomfort, ya know."

Muttering a curse under his breath, Rachins was, without saying so outright, asking for permission to leave. Reinhard let out a resigned sigh.

"Go rejoin Felt-sama, and watch over her together with Gaston and Camberley. There shouldn't be any danger, but Rom-dono isn't with us this time. If Felt-sama tries to do anything reckless, put your body on the line to stop her."

"Got it. What about you, sir?"

"I'll go on ahead with Emilia-sama and the others to pay our respects at the inn. If anything happens, send up a signal flare. I'll be there in five seconds."

"That doesn't sound like a joke, which is exactly why it's scary."

With that parting shot, Rachins took off running, weaving through the group. On his way, he shot a glare at Joshua, whom he'd been arguing with, without forgetting to flinch, small-fry style, at a glance from Reinhard. A model petty crook if there ever was one.

"Well then, let's head in. We've already greeted Anastasia-san, so I guess we should go tell her Reinhard and the others are here."

"Pretty sure that's technically Joshua's job. Ah well, bit late for that now—let's just go together."

"...Yes, let's do that. My apologies for the trouble I've caused."

Perhaps because he hadn't managed to smooth things over well, Joshua seemed somehow downcast. As if to comfort him, Subaru, then Reinhard, then Emilia, then Garfiel, and finally Beatrice each patted his shoulder in turn as they filed into the inn.

"Feel like that little chain just made me feel more pathetic, somehow."

"Since Subaru did it, I thought I'd do it too."

"Since those two did it, oh, I thought I had to do it too."

"If the boss and Emilia-sama are doin' it, no way I can not do it, right?"

"Betty didn't want to be the only one left out, I suppose. ...Not that Betty cares about being left out or anything, I suppose. It was just a coincidence, I suppose."

"There, there, cute, cute."

Subaru and Emilia, from either side, patted the head of Beatrice walking between them. Beatrice swatted the hands away irritably, then walked along pinching the hems of their clothes between two fingers.

"This way. Right now, Anastasia-sama and the others are in the middle of receiving another guest."

Leading the way down the hallway, Joshua guided Subaru and the others to a room separate from the main tatami parlor. Following along, Subaru's eyes sharpened at Joshua's last remark.

"You said they're receiving a guest. Who else are they welcoming besides us?"

"...You'll find out soon enough without giving me that beastly look."

"'Beastly' is a bit much. My eyes aren't that hungry-looking."

"You'll find out soon enough without making that Witchbeast-like sound, either."

"That's even worse! Which Witchbeast, exactly? Dog, whale, or rabbit—pick one."

Those were the top three unpleasant Witchbeasts lodged in Subaru's memory. He had a feeling there were others too—some Witchbeast burned to a crisp had a face like a lion, maybe—but it hadn't left much of an impression. Subaru made an effort to dig up those thin memories, but that effort was interrupted by Reinhard's profile as he murmured faintly, "A whale, huh..." Noticing Subaru's gaze on him—

"By 'whale,' I take it you mean the White Whale, Subaru?"

"...Yeah, that's right. Worst whale there ever was. Thought I was gonna die more times than I can count, and I still think it's a miracle I didn't."

In truth, that his death count against the White Whale hadn't climbed even higher was nothing but a product of sheer miracle. That Witchbeast had been that much of a monstrous threat, and the damage it wrought was far from ordinary. The scale of the sacrifices paid still pierced through Subaru's chest even now, keeping the pain of it real.

"Would you mind telling me more about the White Whale later? That Witchbeast isn't unrelated to me either, as it happens. It's a story that runs a bit long, though."

"—Sure. As for your side of it, you don't have to tell me if it's hard to talk about."

Somehow, Subaru had a vague sense of what circumstances Reinhard held regarding the White Whale. For Subaru, the fight against the White Whale had also been the fruit of one old swordsman's obsession, a man who'd chased that shadow for over ten years. And Subaru knew that the identity of that sword demon was deeply connected to this red-haired young man. What exactly had happened between them in the past, though, he didn't know.

—This wasn't something to press for out of mere curiosity. That much judgment, at least, Subaru could manage.

"Thank you."

So, to that consideration from Subaru, Reinhard responded with only that brief word. And Subaru asked for nothing more than that.

At Reinhard lowering his eyes, Subaru let out a long breath of his own. Seeing Subaru like that, Emilia and Beatrice sent him concerned glances. To show them it was nothing, Subaru turned a smile their way.

"We're here. Please wait in this tea room for now, until the conversation finishes."

In front of Joshua, who'd guided them to their destination, stood a sliding partition in the style of a fusuma door. The fact that it was properly papered over with something like washi showed real attention to detail, and Subaru found himself faintly delighted—amused at his own Japanese soul for reacting that way. But that brief window of optimism lasted only a few seconds.

"Excuse me. Would it be all right if I brought some other guests to share the tea room?"

Apparently there was already someone inside, as Joshua called out through the fusuma. At that, there came the sound of someone shifting inside.

"—Please, go ahead. I myself was just at loose ends in here."

At the calm voice that answered, Subaru's brow furrowed, and then he was struck with surprise. It was a voice he recognized—one he couldn't possibly have forgotten. More than that, he'd only just been thinking of that very person moments before. No one but Subaru seemed to have caught on—no, Reinhard alone was different. His soft features had taken on a faint stiffness, and his blue eyes wavered with confusion. Unaware of that hesitation, Joshua slid the fusuma open. There came the quiet sound of the wooden door sliding, and the room designated as the tea room lay revealed before them. And the person within, sitting formally on a cushion, was looking their way.

"—Grandfather."

"Reinhard, is it."

The first words exchanged between grandfather and grandson overlapped one another. It was a reunion neither Reinhard van Astrea nor Wilhelm van Astrea had intended.
