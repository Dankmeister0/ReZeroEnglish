Arc 6 Chapter 56: "A Talk About What Comes Next"

"――――"

Caught just before her body could topple down the spiral staircase, the girl—Meili—widened her eyes in surprise. With those large, round eyes so close before him, Subaru couldn't help but feel a tangle of complicated emotions at the confirmation of a situation he'd already half-suspected.

He was glad that, by making use of Return by Death, he'd managed to avert a tragedy as a result. But it had also now been proven, twice over, across two separate timelines, that this young girl had pushed him from behind. The culprit who shoved Subaru's back, sending him plummeting from a great height to his death—that had been Meili Portroute, the very same girl who, in the previous loop, had been swiftly dispatched by Natsuki Subaru's own hand.

"...Blaming me for killing you? You're saying weird things again, Onii-san."

For a moment, a flicker of unease crossed her widened eyes, but Meili quickly let her lips relax, tracing a finger along the arm Subaru had wrapped around her torso, and smiling coquettishly. Then she took a step back, putting some distance between herself and the spiral staircase, and faced Subaru squarely.

"Maybe losing your memory knocked loose a few other things too. Otherwise you wouldn't go around making such a silly misunderstanding."

"A misunderstanding?"

"Yes, right? ...Me, trying to kill Onii-san? That's an awful thing to accuse me of."

Clasping her hands behind her back, Meili declared this with an innocent smile, utterly without malice. Seeing her brazen, bald-faced denial, even Subaru was caught off guard. He hadn't expected her to keep bluffing even after being caught red-handed. But then again, that kind of resistance made perfect sense—if not for "me," then certainly for Meili.

To put it kindly, she was resourceful; to put it unkindly, she acted purely on impulse. Choosing whatever seemed like the best solution in the moment, for that moment alone—that was the compass by which she lived her life. Put plainly, it was the very essence of a "beast."

"Being suspected like this really hurts my feelings. I mean, if I really wanted to kill you and everyone else, wouldn't it have been way easier out in the dunes than in a tower like this? Ahh, but you don't remember, so maybe you wouldn't know."

"Yeah. That is a strange point. If you'd been planning to kill us all along, you've had plenty of chances. But you didn't take them."

"Right? So then—"

"But it's a different story if your motive to kill me only sprouted up this morning. Or—actually, more accurate to call it a chain reaction that started last night, wouldn't it be?"

"――――"

At that, Meili's expression changed. Pressing her lips together, she let her confident smile fall away, then let out a long, deep sigh. And then, with a world-weary shrug that didn't suit her appearance at all, she said—

"I wonder if I got set up."

"Set up? What do you mean by that?"

"Weren't you testing me? Lying about losing your memory, to see if I'd try to push you off... isn't that the standard move, flushing out a traitor if you think there's one among your allies? And I've got no more use for you all, now that we're through with the tower, right? ...Perfect timing to cut me loose."

She listed her disadvantages one by one, methodically confirming the situation against her—it was a sad thing to watch. In truth, even if there hadn't been quite that much malice behind it, it was true that Subaru had, in a sense, tested Meili's actions. Denying that point wouldn't change her impression of him one bit. Still, there was one thing he could say for certain. And that was—

"So then, how exactly are you going to deal with me? Since we're at it, why not push me off from here, as payback? Without your nasty little animal friend around, I bet you could take care of me all by yourself easily enough, Onii-san."

"Don't get the wrong idea, Meili. My memory loss isn't some lie to trick you. It's the real thing, so it's a real problem, seriously."

"Well, that's its own kind of messed up, but... in the end, what is it you want, Onii-san? Are you the type who needs to actually feel it to believe revenge really happened?"

"—Ugh."

As she spoke, the girl wrapped her hands around her own slender neck and stuck out her tongue. For an instant, Subaru's heart lurched hard in his chest at the gesture. But it wasn't a jab referencing the manner of her own death—it was simply a sardonic bit of pantomime for murder in general. Thinking about it that way, she really was a tiny assassin with an uncanny knack for hitting Subaru right where it hurt. So, though it wasn't meant as payback—

"I wouldn't recommend a method that drags things out too long. Not just 'cause I don't wanna suffer, but... Onii-san doesn't seem like the type who's good at hiding things..."

"—I don't intend to kill you. I don't intend to hurt you, either. Not now, not tomorrow, not any day after that. I intend to keep on with you exactly the way things have been."

"...Huh?"

At Subaru's answer, Meili's expression shifted again. But this time it was fundamentally different from before, when she'd instantly settled on the next best response. Clear confusion swept across her face, and she looked at Subaru with eyes that held no comprehension at all. Subaru nodded back at that look.

"Luckily, your attempt got stopped before it happened, so if we—the ones involved—just keep it quiet, it'll be as if nothing happened at all. If I'd just dodged it and left it at that, though, you might've just gone and tried some other way to kill me, so I did need to actually catch you in the act. If that makes me a bad person, I can't really deny it. Sorry."

"Ah... w-what are you..."

"But hey, this ought to have shown you something, right? Trying to do something to me is pretty risky for you too. And if that's the case, then—let's actually talk this through, properly. If you've got complaints, I'll listen as much as I can—"

"Complaints? Complaints—"

Meili murmured, her voice small, trembling. Then she pressed her lips tightly together, and—

"If I've got complaints, this—right now—is one! I don't believe this!"

Subaru had chosen his words carefully, trying his best to talk his way toward some peaceful resolution. In response, Meili turned eyes on him that looked at something unbelievable, and howled.

"I don't believe it, I don't believe it, I don't believe it..."

As she howled, her hands kept fretting at her braided hair. Subaru could tell that was a kind of mental self-defense against her spiraling confusion—a sign of dependence on someone else who'd worn her hair the same way.

"You don't even understand what I was just about to do, Onii-san! That's the only way this makes sense! If it wasn't that, then..."

Her words grew halting, and Meili desperately insisted something was wrong. This was the first time Subaru had ever seen Meili this distraught—no, that wasn't quite true. He had seen it once before, inside the Book of the Dead. Her actions last night—encountering Subaru at "Taygeta," her panic, and then, after a conversation with "Natsuki Subaru," her resolve to kill the memory-lost Natsuki Subaru.

But that hastily assembled murder plan must have been a double-edged sword for her as well. Subaru himself, pushed and killed in that fall, had no way of knowing—but after his death, how had she planned to talk her way out of suspicion as the murderer? Of course, there was a good chance it could've been written off as an accident. —No, would it have, though?

Now that he knew Emilia, Beatrice, Ram, and the others, Subaru was certain: they would never let his death, its true cause, be glossed over. In that case, Meili's crime being exposed would have been unavoidable. Ram, Julius, Echidna—any of them would have uncovered the truth far more cleanly than Subaru could, without ever dying even once. It wasn't hard to imagine Meili's mind eventually turning to that possibility too. So this was—

"That was just an impulsive urge. You're just used to killing as an answer. You just can't imagine any other option when it comes to solving problems. That's not your fault."

"—! Don't talk like you understand! What could you—could you possibly understand about me!?"

"—I do understand."

"――――"

Flaring up, having snapped back at him, Meili went rigid, as if doused in cold water. Looking straight at her, Subaru said it firmly, decisively.

"Meili. I understand you. It might creep you out, but maybe there isn't a single other person in this world who understands you better than I do."

At Subaru's remark, delivered with a shrug, Meili's face twisted into one of deep fear. He thought that fear was only natural, but at the same time, he was struggling with how to convey the undeniable truth of it—struggling with this twisted "self-love," deep in his own chest.

He recalled it now—the temptation of "me," repeated over and over again. A voice that whispered ahead of every one of Natsuki Subaru's actions, again and again trying to lure him toward the most consequence-free, worst possible resolution to any problem: murder. The temptation of killing. The temptation of the ghost of the dead Meili Portroute, assimilated into Subaru through reading the Book of the Dead—

"—No. That's not it."

Shaking his head, Subaru rejected the idea of shifting that blame onto the girl he'd killed. Even now, he was being ridiculous. Look at the confusion, the desperation, on the face of the girl before him. More than anything—recall Meili's own Book of the Dead, the one Subaru himself had witnessed.

Given the anguish Meili carried, there was no way a personality that conveniently urged murder could have been born from her. That voice hadn't been some Meili assimilated through the Book of the Dead at all. It had been an illusion conjured by Subaru's own weak heart.

Proof of that: not once had that voiced girl ever actually appeared before him.

"――――"

Though in a terribly abbreviated, one-sided way, Subaru had relived Meili's life through the Book of the Dead. He had witnessed her days from the time she'd first become self-aware—establishing her own identity, deciding how she would live—all the way to the unreasonably early close of a life that could never be called long.

Within those days lay a void that no one else could ever hope to fathom, and an experience of "terror" great enough to match that overwhelming emptiness, and, alone, one shining attachment.

The name of that attachment was—

"—Elsa Granhiert."

"—!"

"That's the reason you tried to kill me, isn't it?"

At the question, Meili's expression twisted with bitterness. It was anger—anger at having a place she never wanted touched, being forced open; at having someone else tread with muddy boots into a place she would never allow it. But Subaru did it anyway, deliberately. Because—

"When you visit someone's house you take off your shoes, but stomping muddy-booted into other people's hearts—that's the Natsuki family way."

As a proud member of that family, Natsuki Subaru marched right into the heart of the little assassin. Into the factor rooted deep in her heart, the one that drove her toward impulsive violence.

A black executioner Natsuki Subaru had never known, yet somehow felt intensely, unbearably close to. Thinking of her, what welled up in his heart was relief and adoration, grief and burning fury, and a blankness—that was the complicated, and yet utterly simple, tangle of feelings Meili had held for her.

—Meili had adored Elsa. She had loved her. She had longed to be like her.

And so, having had that taken from her, she grieved, she suffered, she hated, she raged, carrying both murderous intent and despair. That she'd traveled with Subaru and the others all this while without letting any of it show wasn't because she was skillfully acting out some grand plan for revenge—she wasn't nearly that adept.

If anything, it would be fair to say Meili's clumsiness at handling this was extreme.

A sad girl so ignorant of her own emotions that she didn't even understand the depth of the wound in her own heart. —A killer raised in captivity, shaped entirely by her environment. That was Meili Portroute.

"You wanted revenge?"

"...I don't know."

"Even if you did, Elsa wouldn't have—"

"She never wanted that. I know that much."

To Subaru's question, Meili shook her head twice in a row. Subaru could understand what Meili was feeling. And Meili, in turn, understood that Subaru's words had struck true. In this moment, Meili and Subaru stood as equals.

And so it was a sad thing—that she, unable to bear her own emotions, lost in a dead end with no answer in sight, had no way to clear that confusion except through "murder." At the same time, he felt something close to hatred for a world that had given Meili no other option.

Not even the very way Elsa lived, the thing Meili yearned so desperately for, could undo that. Elsa might have been a light to Meili—but the path illuminated by that light was too brutal a road for an ordinary person to walk.

"I know you can't handle these feelings of yours on your own. But the answer to that probably isn't something you can come up with right here, right now. So—"

"――――"

"Leave this to me for now. I won't let it turn out bad for you. At the very least, I'll do everything I can to make sure it doesn't. If you're willing to want that too."

"...I can't believe you. Talk's cheap, Onii-san. Anyone can say anything with just words."

Even hearing Subaru's persuasion, Meili, her face lowered, wouldn't nod so easily. Of course not. What he was asking of her was to find a way of being that existed nowhere in the dictionary of the life she'd cultivated across her entire existence. And on top of that, the one proposing it was—for reasons she couldn't fathom—this Natsuki Subaru, lecturing her with a face far too knowing. Even Subaru himself was a little shocked at how suspicious he must seem—a guy who'd lured her out here fully prepared to be shoved off, no less. So, having expected to be told exactly that, he'd prepared a fallback plan for this too.

"If you can't trust my word alone, well, fair enough, honestly. Apparently up till yesterday I was a habitual promise-breaker. So—"

"So?"

"Let's not make it a promise just between you and me. Let's make it a promise between you and both of us."

"――――"

At Subaru's declaration, Meili frowned, not understanding his intent. But the answer to her doubt soon became clear. It was—

"—Mm, don't worry. I heard everything too. I'm a witness to the promise."

"—!"

A voice—Meili flinched, her shoulders jumping, and spun around, frozen stiff. There, appearing before her eyes, was a girl of rare, otherworldly beauty, so lovely it seemed almost unreal. At the sight of her, Meili's eyes widened, her lips trembling in shock.

"Onee-san... were you listening?"

"Subaru asked me to be here for this. ...He said, if things got dangerous for Meili, to stop it."

Speaking, the one walking toward them, her silver hair swaying like the shimmer of moonlight, standing there like something out of a page of myth, was Emilia. Hearing Emilia's words, Meili tilted her head, still full of surprise—

"If things got dangerous for me...? Not—not for Onii-san...?"

"That's right, if it got dangerous for Meili. That's what you wanted, wasn't it, Subaru?"

"—. Ah, yeah, that's right. That was honestly the one thing I was genuinely worried about."

"—? Why are you turning your face away? Subaru, is something wrong?"

"No, it's just, uh, too cute to look at directly..."

At Emilia's puzzled question, Subaru mumbled his answer in a small, indistinct voice. Unable to catch what he'd said, Emilia tilted her head even further, but Subaru covered his embarrassment with a cough and turned his attention back to the still-bewildered Meili.

If he'd known in advance she'd try to push him, Subaru had had enough confidence he could stop her. The real problem was what came after—the behavior of "Natsuki Subaru." Hypothetically speaking, if in the loop where Meili was killed, "Natsuki Subaru's" purpose had been an act of self-defense against a Meili trying to kill him, then there was no telling what "Natsuki Subaru" might do—against Subaru's own wishes—the moment after catching her in the act. So Subaru had chosen to hand that problem over to someone clearly, unambiguously stronger than himself. Trusting that even if his own consciousness faded and "Natsuki Subaru" surfaced, Emilia would surely handle it—no, that his companions would find a way.

"Listen, Meili. I believe what Subaru said. If you can't believe it, then just watch Subaru closely, together with me. And if he ever breaks his promise, I'll get angry right alongside you."

"Watching Onii-san...? That's strange. That's backwards. It's supposed to be Onii-san and Onee-san watching me, isn't it..."

"If Meili's ever about to do something bad, it'll be because Subaru broke his promise to Meili first. So then, what we should be watching is whether Subaru keeps his promise. That's the right order, isn't it?"

"――――"

Bending down to meet Meili's eye level, Emilia's words left the girl bewildered. Inside her head, fact and circumstance and countermeasure and solution churned together in a chaotic tangle.

"You were about to do something you shouldn't have. But thanks to Emilia-chan and me making a nice save, it got stopped before it happened. So now you've got a chance to learn what you should've done instead. In that chance, can you find some path other than killing me...? Let's call it a match."

"A match...?"

"When all that jumbled-up mess of feelings inside you finally gets sorted out—will you still think you have to kill me, or will you realize you don't...? I'll do my best at teaching ethics class too."

Scratching his head, Subaru laid out this path forward for Meili's confusion. Honestly, calling it uninvited meddling would be fair. Meili had a way of life she'd built up over all this time, and Subaru was, uninvited, trying to graft some new possibility onto it. But if she refused it, her path would be sealed off, here in this tower of sand. With the way of being she'd cultivated up until now, she wouldn't be able to make it through this tower together with them. And so—

"I'm not going to let you get off partway through. I don't know exactly how old you are, but... when I was around your age, I had plenty of adults around me who helped me out more times than I can count."

"――――"

"So I'm going to try to help you, even if you don't want it. Being too proud to accept help when you don't know what to do—you're still way too lovely for that, kiddo."

As he spoke, Subaru closed the distance between himself and Meili by one step. Seeing that gap close, Meili's shoulders trembled with a start. Still, she looked up at him with anxious eyes, and Subaru, without any hesitation, reached out and stroked her head.

He wouldn't strangle that slender neck. He would offer "Natsuki Subaru" some other solution, entirely different. Meili was a child who had no need to die. And—

"Please, Meili—believe in Subaru... believe in us."

"—Ah."

As Subaru stroked her head, Meili, unable to move, was embraced from behind by Emilia. Wrapped gently in her arms, Meili bit her lip faintly, and Emilia pressed her cheek close to her, saying—

"This tower of sand is too small a place for you to decide something this big."

"――――"

"Get out of here, and in a bigger, wider place—find your answer. We'll try really, really hard too."

Letting emotions with nowhere to go fester inside a closed-off space like this wasn't good for her, in any sense. Even choosing his words with care, that had been the only way Subaru could think to put it—but Emilia spoke to Meili in gentler words, filled with sincere feeling.

"――――"

At that, Meili let her gaze wander, thinking, again and again, before finally—

"Elsa... I don't want to forget her."

"Yeah, that's fine. You don't have to forget someone you loved. Just, well..."

There, Subaru paused, thinking of the black-clad beauty he had seen through the Book of the Dead. A strange impression, that woman—someone he'd never actually crossed paths with, and yet who felt intimately, familiarly close. Recalling her, Subaru found himself, unconsciously, stroking his own stomach as he said—

"—Even if she's someone you loved, I'd rather you didn't copy her methods."

That was the weary, earnest request he made.

—It was a good long while after that, a good many dozens of seconds of silence, before Meili, weakly, still lingering with doubt, finally nodded.
