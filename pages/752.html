She was born blessed with extraordinary looks and natural dexterity, but beyond that there was nothing else particularly noteworthy about her. By rights, she should never have had any occasion to become entangled in the sort of events that get carved into history—she would probably have ended her days as an ordinary villager, still dreaming of someday going off to the city.

Which is why the present Petra—who strayed from her "proper" route and became caught up in one history-making catastrophe after another—exists in a shape that has been warped by outside interference.

Petra herself was well aware that her fate had been bent out of true.

By "rights," Petra had been an embarrassing girl, smug about her own cuteness, who mistakenly believed she could wrap every adult and child around her little finger. No doubt the "proper" Petra would have gone on living selfishly, playing at ruling over her own tiny world, that misconception never once corrected.

Not that she thinks that would have been a bad life. In its own way, it probably would have been fun, and happy.

It's only that the values of the present Petra—the one who strayed from "proper"—can't see it as happiness.

"I mean, none of the people I love now would even be there, right?"

But more than all that hardship she's been put through, there is something she was given. Something she managed to grasp.
