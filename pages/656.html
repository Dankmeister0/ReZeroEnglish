Arc 8 Chapter 40: "A Threat Not Worth Mentioning"

—Heinkel Astrea wasn't even a Stargazer.

As everyone knew, he was no Sword Saint either, nor had he been granted the sword-name "Van"—bestowed upon those who earned their achievements through their own swordsmanship—and even his post as vice-captain of the Royal Guard of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica was nothing more than a figurehead title.
He was a man who had been chosen for nothing among the things that could only be attained by being chosen—that was who Heinkel was, and coming to the Vollachia Empire hadn't changed that.

Swept along, he'd joined the fighting; broken by a sight that didn't seem of this world, he'd fled, and in his flight he'd scraped by with his life, only to be swept along again back to the place he'd run from, where he now clung to a feeble hope.
Even that hope was nothing but an escape route, conveniently laid out by someone who'd expected Heinkel to serve as a sacrificial pawn—and his failure to even notice the scheme was the stupidity born of a mind that had given up thinking.

—And so, unlike Rowan Segmunt, who had defied the battle strategy for reasons bordering on madness, he himself, trying earnestly to fulfill his role, had been made to draw the worst short straw of all.

"Damn it all..."

Of the five vertices of the wall that formed the linchpin of the imperial capital's defense, Heinkel had ended up shouldering both the south and southeast points alone—points he was supposed to have split with Rowan—and he headed south, intent on securing the First Vertex, where the main gate used for entering and leaving the capital stood.
Put simply, it was the sound call to make, a reasonable choice with nothing eccentric about it.
And at the end of that sound, reasonable choice, Heinkel came face to face with—

"—I am Mezoreia. In obedience to the voice of my Beloved Child, I shall become the wind from the heavens."

—the awesome majesty of a white Dragon, wings spread as if to guard the First Vertex.

The room was cluttered with jewelry and ornaments piled in disarray.
Craftwork lavished with gold and silver, garments and hairpieces studded with gems, all manner of exquisite pieces crowded together, scattered and rolling about—a room strewn with a brilliance that assaulted the eyes.
In the middle of that dazzling room, Madelyn Eschart opened her eyes.

"—"

Dazed, her golden eyes—still loosely tethered to reality—blinked several times; the two black horns jutting from her sky-blue hair swayed as her small frame rose to its feet. At once, the jewelry around her clattered and rolled, scattering roughly across the floor, but Madelyn paid it no mind.

Jewelry, gold and silver treasure—that sort of glittering thing, well, she liked it.
Humans were weak and fragile yet noisy, and she had no love for them, but the crafts they made using gems and gold held a charm nothing else could replace.
Carrying out tasks humans ordered of her and receiving such things as rewards wasn't so bad.
Piling and packing them into her den, sleeping surrounded by them, did help her rest easy. —But neither jewels nor gold could fill the hole in her heart.

"—"

Unsteadily, she turned her head where she stood and pushed open the door of her den.
This wasn't the den she'd lived in for a while after becoming one of the Nine Divine Generals—it was a new one, hastily put together after everything that had happened this time.
It didn't carry enough of her own scent, and the treasures were only what she'd scraped together from around the castle, so it was far from satisfying—but it was better than nothing.
More than anything, there was a reason here worth abandoning her familiar, long-lived-in den for.
And that reason was—

"—Carillon."

Stepping out of the den and crossing the corridor, she emerged onto a balcony where a tepid breeze blew, and there she found a single Flying Dragon resting its wings.

"—"

Called by name, the Flying Dragon turned its head toward her—its whole body scaled with painful-looking cracks, its murky black eyes floating with gold, plainly showing the state of a Vessel from which life had drained away.
Just like the humans reviving one after another, no small number of Flying Dragons had been pulled back from beyond to here.
Only, communication wasn't possible with those that had lived wild in life—but with a limited few, like the Flying Dragon before her now, Carillon, it was.
Indeed, responding to Madelyn's call, Carillon quietly lowered its head where it stood.

The other dead Flying Dragons—the Undead Flying Dragons—would attack without mercy anything they judged to be a living thing, even if it were Madelyn herself.
Naturally, she had no mercy to spare for creatures that didn't know their place, and no hesitation in shattering them.

"The Dragon has no wish to shatter you, cha."

Stroking the neck of the bowed Carillon, Madelyn's mouth stiffened at the coldness of it.
To begin with, a Flying Dragon's body, lean of excess flesh for flight, ran cool. Even so, the warmth—or rather, the cold—conveyed to her fingertips was the same as touching a jewel on display.
The difference was that, unlike a jewel, she felt neither brilliance nor beauty in it.
Even so, nothing could replace the preciousness of Carillon moving, being here before her, like this.

—To Madelyn, Carillon was the first Flying Dragon she'd ever seen tamed by a human.

Madelyn, the last living Dragonkin, had lived without any contact whatsoever with anything beyond the sea of clouds where she was born, owing in part to the nature of Mezoreia, whose Dragonhusk she was.
Setting Mezoreia aside, the only ones who'd ever had the chance to interact with Madelyn were the wild Flying Dragons that made their home around the sea of clouds—and even they were nothing but beings that served the Dragonkin.

The gulf that separated her, a Dragonkin, from the Flying Dragons as living creatures.
It was something rooted in instinct, something that left no room to wonder why or to what end. In a divide that simply existed as a matter of course, there was no room for either pessimism or doubt to arise.
A creature that had never laid eyes on the beauty of gold would never covet a golden crown.

But Madelyn's fulfilled days, ignorant as they were of anything beyond the sea of clouds, came to an abrupt end.
What brought that end was none other than this very Carillon—the stray who, coaxing and cajoling his beloved dragon, reluctant as it was to rise into the presence of Mezoreia, the "Cloud Dragon," had plunged into the sea of clouds.

"—Where is the Dragon's good husband, Balleroy, cha?"

Tickling its neck with her fingers, Madelyn asked Carillon just that.
The name she spoke was one familiar to Carillon as well, and as the sound settled on Madelyn's tongue, it also seeped through her chest like a slow-acting poison, throbbing dully.
This poisoned ache, one that seemed to corrode even her Soul, was also the very reason Madelyn had flown out of the sea of clouds and descended to the ground where humans lived.
But now, that poisoned pain was beginning to turn into something different from before.

"—"

Madelyn tasted that poisoned pain within her chest. Beside her, Carillon, who had been stroked, slowly lifted its neck and gave a small rumble in its throat.
Realizing that its gesture and cry pointed behind her, Madelyn turned around.
Just then, a figure came from beyond the corridor connecting to the balcony. The figure waved a hand breezily, taking a familiar, easygoing attitude toward the Dragonkin.

"...If it were anyone but you, the Dragon would crush such insolence between her fangs, cha."

"Well, if it weren't me, I reckon nobody'd dare take such a tone with the venerable Dragonkin. That's what I'd like to say, anyhow, but surprisingly enough, I'd wager there's no shortage of impudent folk among the Generals, all the same."

"True enough, cha. Humans really are tiresome."

"Ha ha, can't argue with that."

At the wry, shrugging gesture of the man, Madelyn let out a breath through her nose as if exasperated.
But it seemed to her that some hard-to-hide feeling had mixed into that breath, so she chastened her unsettled heart and waved a hand as if to hide the tinge of color in her sigh.
A hard-to-hide feeling—an unending, overflowing warmth toward the man before her.

Even if his complexion and the shape of his eyes had changed completely, feelings couldn't lie.
The preciousness she felt even for Carillon, now an Undead Flying Dragon, she felt all the more strongly for him.

"Balleroy, where'd you go, cha?"

Closing the last half-step of distance as he came right up to her, Madelyn pressed him with the question.
He'd been by her side when she fell asleep in that den full of treasure. If possible, she'd wanted him beside her the whole time until she woke.
Unable to say as much, Madelyn's question still seemed to let Balleroy sense the feeling left unspoken, and he raised a hand to his own head, saying, "Sorry 'bout that,"

"Seems there's some rats scurryin' round the capital. Lord Palladio got wind of one and kicked up a fuss about it, so I went and had a look and made my report."

"Rats... did you kill it properly, cha?"

"Nah, this one's a stubborn rat. Told him I let it slip, and Lord Palladio was madder than mad—been gettin' chewed out for it."

Balleroy shook his head slowly, and at his words, the pupils of Madelyn's eyes narrowed.
The Palladio he'd mentioned—if she recalled right—was one of the revived Vollachian imperial family. A man of the Evil Eye Tribe who'd lost in the Vollachian custom of siblings killing one another for the throne, to Vincent, who'd fought his way through it all to become Emperor, and who had since been revived.

"If he means to trouble the Dragon's good husband, the Dragon will gladly tear him apart with her own hands."

"Now don't go sayin' such scary things. Even if he's died once already, he's still Vollachian imperial family... there's no small number among the revived who follow him without question. Cross him, and you'll be makin' enemies of the lot of 'em too."

"—Are you saying the Dragon and Balleroy would lose to the dead, cha!?"

If so, that was a grave misunderstanding.
There was no way the Undead could win against Madelyn and Balleroy working together. If they meant to get in their way, then such people should simply be smashed to the last one.
Madelyn's breath grew ragged as she said so, but Balleroy gently rested a hand on her thin shoulder,

"That ain't it. This is a problem from before the question of whether we'd win or lose, ain't it?"

"What do you..."

"You know full well. Our freedom, us corpses', is held right in that Witch's hand."

"—"

At Balleroy's admonishing words, Madelyn bit down hard on her back teeth and fell silent.
The "Witch" was the very one who had turned this capital into a place overrun by the Undead—the being responsible for the reason Balleroy and Carillon now stood before Madelyn like this.
That unnatural "Witch," whose body was built in a way similar to Mezoreia's Dragonhusk, had touched upon mysteries of Od Lagna unknown even to Madelyn, a Dragonkin.
That was precisely why she was able to call back so many Souls and recreate them in their living states.
On the other hand, if the Witch were to interrupt that miracle on a whim—

"Like Madelyn says, if it comes to a fight, I reckon we'd beat most anyone. But it's just the two of us and one dragon against Lord Palladio, who can order around hundreds, thousands. Can't say which one the Witch'd find more useful to keep around."

"She'd choose the humans over us dragons?"

"Can't say. After all, nobody's got a clue what that grand wish of the Witch's even is."

It was nothing but frustrating, but there was more than a grain of truth in Balleroy's answer.
The Witch, whose thoughts were utterly unreadable, had placed Madelyn within the occupied Crystal Palace on the condition that she cooperate. Being ordered about at another's whim was infuriating, but she'd already swallowed that humiliation the moment she came down to the ground and accepted Berstetz's proposal.
What was hard to swallow was one thing alone: not knowing the other party's true intentions.

"—"

Just as Balleroy said, as long as the Witch's purpose remained unknown, it was no different for Madelyn than having a blade's edge pressed endlessly against her one vulnerable scale.
If not for that, Balleroy surely wouldn't have resigned himself to a situation like this—he'd have fled with Madelyn beyond the sea of clouds and sworn the wedding vow they'd promised.
Yes, the promise that had never been fulfilled—

"We finally got to meet again like this, cha."

"...Madelyn."

Giving in to the swelling impulse, Madelyn threw her arms around Balleroy's body right in front of her.
Balleroy was much taller than the petite Madelyn, and as she leaned her body against him, her black horns very nearly stabbed into his neck. Deftly dodging them, Balleroy patted Madelyn's back.
That gesture brought back the memory of the one time, overcome with emotion, her embrace had driven a horn clean through him, resulting in a massive bloody disaster—and her eyes grew moist.

The body she clung to was cold, and the skin against hers held no softness.
His blue eyes, once the same color as Madelyn's hair, had likewise become black eyes floating with gold that gave off no sense of warmth, making his feelings impossible to read from his appearance alone.

Even so, they shared their memories. Even so, he knew her wish.
He had died, they'd been parted by death, and the body she touched again now carried neither blood nor warmth. So what.

"Balleroy is here. ...The Dragon wants nothing more than that, cha."

Living or dead made no difference.
The dead didn't necessarily have to stay beneath the earth. If, by some twist of fate, the dead had overflowed up onto the earth's surface, and among them was someone precious to her, then who had the right to deny this ghastly miracle and declare it a mistake?
What creature would dare say such a thing before Madelyn Eschart, this Dragonkin?

"—It's here."

Burying her face in her beloved's chest, savoring their cold reunion, Madelyn let out a low growl.
Sensing what that tone meant, Balleroy, who had been gently patting her back, stopped his hand and turned his gaze out past the balcony—far, far to the south of the capital.
Something had snagged Madelyn's senses—no, the senses of Mezoreia, the Dragonhusk.

Whatever might come, Madelyn had been ordered to guard that point.
Let it be said again: it galled her. But Madelyn did not waver. What she wanted was right here.

"I'll be off, cha. Balleroy, this time—"

"I know. I'll be right here by your side 'til you call for me."

"—That's what makes you the Dragon's good husband, cha."

He never said things he couldn't do, like that he'd never leave her side, or that he'd stay by her forever.
Because Balleroy, easygoing as he was, understood the limits of what lay within his reach, Madelyn could love him just as he was, without excessive hope or disappointment.
Confirming that once more—suddenly, the strength drained from Madelyn's body.

"—"

Limp, her limbs hanging slack, Madelyn crumpled where she stood. Balleroy pulled her body to him on reflex, firmly supporting a weight unimaginable from her small frame.
And so he lifted up Madelyn, who had lost consciousness—no, who had returned her consciousness elsewhere.

"—"

"I know, I know. I'll carry her nice and gentle, don't you worry."

The beloved dragon's small growl showed its concern for Madelyn's limp form.
Nodding at Carillon's cry, Balleroy turned his gaze once more toward the state of the capital, and

"Even bein' dead, we can still meet again. I don't ask for more than that. Same goes for me too, Madelyn. —So long as we can just keep on meetin'."

—The battle began one-sidedly, and ended one-sidedly.

To begin with, a "battle" was properly called such only when it was a clash, full-souled, between parties both possessed of fighting will, lasting as long as that will did not run dry.
By that definition, what ended in an instant, one side's fighting will spent, was not a "battle."

"—"

It was something too feeble to even be called a threat.
And yet, for something that came bearing hostility and ill intent, no other fitting word came to mind. "A threat not worth mentioning" was the most apt way to put it.
In fact, that threat not worth mentioning was sent flying like a leaf with a single sweep of the tail, as though brushing a street with a broom, and buried in clouds of dust along with the surrounding buildings it dragged down with it.

It was an anticlimactic result, to be sure. But it was nothing to particularly lament or pity.
That was simply the gulf between a Dragon and a human, as beings incomparable as living creatures.

Face a Dragon, literally different from them in the very quality of its existence, and a human easily turned to dust.
For some reason, one had to admit there existed, on the rarest of occasions, exceptional beings for whom that wasn't so—but those were mutations that had appeared within the human species, or to put it plainly, something other than human entirely.
And whatever that something else might be, ultimately, as a living creature, it still fell far short of a Dragon.

A fact that would eventually have to be proven in some certain form—by driving off this threat not worth mentioning, she reaffirmed it all the more strongly, recognizing anew that this was how things ought to be.
In that sense, even this threat not worth mentioning had value in having existed.
It had produced the result of impressing anew upon the Witch the might of Mezoreia, the "Cloud Dragon," and had given the Dragon herself the chance to look again upon the value of her own existence—

"...Damn... it all."

Suddenly, a voice that sounded as if it were cursing the world reached her, and the Cloud Dragon's movement stopped.
The Dragon had been flapping her wings, turning her back on the street she'd swept clean, moving to return to the wall—to show, as a result, that no matter what ill intent approached, it could not break through there.
The motion of trying to do so stopped. Because a voice sounded that should not have.

"—"

As the Dragon slowly turned back the way she'd come, a mountain of rubble collapsed with a great clattering roar.
From within the street leveled by a single strike of her tail, and the towering pile of rubble that had once been buildings, there crawled out, battered and tattered, a grimy human with red hair and blue eyes.

A human she had judged a threat not worth mentioning, and who had indeed proven to be exactly that.
By the definition of a "battle," if the thing that had crawled out drew the sword at its waist once more and turned it her way, then the curtain thought to have fallen on this "battle" had not actually fallen yet.
But there wasn't a shred of fighting spirit or vigor anywhere in the human that had crawled out.

"It's always... like this..."

Coughing, perhaps having breathed dust into his lungs, the human muttered under his breath.
Once again, it was a voice like a curse. And what it cursed wasn't the Dragon, but the world—the ground he stood on, the air enveloping him, everything surrounding him, and above all else, himself, standing at the center of it all.

"When it matters most, luck always abandons me—"

Finding it thoroughly repugnant to listen to that litany of grievance, she drove her tail down vertically this time.
Before, he'd only been caught up as she swept the street clean, but this time the Cloud Dragon's tail scooped upward, striking directly at the feeble figure staggering to its feet.
Struck by the tail's blow as it whipped through a full backward arc, the human's body, hit dead-on, went flying like a kicked stone, punching through walls and buildings, tearing through three, four streets in a row.

Seen from above, the capital's streets were laid out with an orderliness pleasing to behold.
And this human, wrecking that order haphazardly with the single flying wreck of his body, was ugly and graceless in his very being, in the way he flew, even in the way he died—grating on the Dragon's nerves through and through.

That something beautiful should be marred for the sake of a thing like this was more than she could stomach.
And on top of it all—

"—Ngh."

Far off, past the many streets he'd been driven through, a groan could be heard.
No matter how immense the damage he'd taken, no matter that it was barely more than the breath of someone half-dead—it should not have been possible to hear it at all.

There should be no such thing as a being whose life did not simply burst apart under a blow from the Dragon's tail.

"...I..."

There should be no such thing.

"All of you—every last one—vanish and be gone, cha—!!"

Welling up, an uncontainable impulse riding on her breath, white ruin was unleashed from the mouth of the Cloud Dragon—Madelyn Eschart, clad in the Dragonhusk—and rained down upon the human who was supposed to have been nothing but a threat not worth mentioning.

This was not a "battle."
It was the beginning of a slaughter—one-sided, one that was meant to be one-sided.

  • Madelyn's verbal tic だっちゃ is rendered as a trailing "...cha" throughout, applied consistently wherever it appears in the Japanese, similar to how Beatrice's のよ is fixed as "...I suppose."
  • Balleroy's rough, working-class Edo-style speech (あっし/でやす/でやんす) is rendered with dropped g's, "reckon," "ain't," and similar colloquialisms to mark a distinct register from other characters.
  • The source distinguishes 龍 ("Dragon," used for the true Dragon Mezoreia/the Cloud Dragon in narration) from 竜 (per the glossary, "(Lesser) Dragon," used in terms like Dragonkin/Flying Dragon and in Madelyn's self-referential dialogue "竜は..."). For naturalness I rendered both as "Dragon" in Madelyn's self-address ("the Dragon has...") rather than the more awkward "(Lesser) Dragon," since she is using it as a proud title rather than a taxonomic term. Flagging this simplification in case a stricter distinction is wanted.
  • "屍飛竜" (lit. "corpse Flying Dragon") was rendered as "Undead Flying Dragon" by extension from the glossary's 屍人 → Undead; not itself a catalogued term.
  • 逆鱗 (the mythological "reverse scale," a dragon's one vulnerable point) was translated descriptively ("her one vulnerable scale") rather than literally, to keep the idiom's meaning clear without requiring the reader to know the Chinese dragon-lore reference.
