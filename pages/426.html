Arc 6 Chapter 24: "The Contrary Examiner"

——There was a woman. A woman was once alone.

The woman was emotional. The woman was always crying. Sensitive to pain, she went on crying, endlessly. The reason for her grief was singular: she could not forgive her own powerlessness.

Around the woman there was always conflict, always battle, always something being torn away. No matter how many times she raised her voice, no matter how she clung and pleaded, no matter how much she cried or screamed, the sorrow never once came to an end. And so the woman cursed fate. She cursed fate, cursed it, cursed it again, and at the end of all that cursing, she realized something: that crying, no matter how much of it, was useless.

Having realized that, what the woman next desired was purely, single-mindedly, power. She wanted power to overwhelm others, power to sweep everything aside, and she drove herself past her limits, tormenting her own body, running herself ragged in pursuit of every last scrap of strength she could obtain.

What was needed was not the power to wound. Nor was it the power to take. She sought a strength so overwhelming that no one could ever catch up to it. She believed that was what would stop the fighting. The woman who kept on shedding tears wanted, more than anything, the power to no longer have to cry.

A battle where strength collides with strength cannot be stopped by someone who remains powerless.

Her voice went unheard. Her wishes went unanswered. Her lamentations were pushed away, and sorrow spread to cover the sky. Why can people bear it so calmly? Why can they wound one another? Why can they go on living while still wounded? Why, why, why can no one imagine that there might be another way?

"Children are crying. The elderly are crying. Men are crying. Women are crying. Everyone is crying. And yet, why——!!"

To put a stop to it, she desired power, and nothing else. She forged herself, endured any agony, and drove her will through like steel. And at last the woman arrived somewhere: at a place of unrivaled strength, an overwhelming plane no other could approach.

Standing upon the battlefield, the woman cried out, loud and clear, for the fighting to stop. She crushed every kind of strength with her own strength, crushed every lamentation beneath her power, beat down every malice with force, and ran herself ragged for the sole purpose of stopping the tears that flowed. She struck down those who gripped swords, kicked aside those who relied on magic, shattered those who bared their fangs, and pulverized, without exception, every last one who sought battle.

But the more the woman resisted, the stronger she became, the more the swords and the magic and the fangs multiplied. It was like a spiral — a spiral of war.

No one held any answer for how to survive except to meet force with force. And so no one knew that there might be a path other than fighting to win.

"Why——!!"

Even as she thought this, she herself, in the end, was only committing violence. Lowering her blood-soaked fists, drenched in the blood of others, the woman looked up to the heavens and wailed.

The fighting would not stop. Every effort, every mile she ran, all of it was for nothing, and the tears — hers and theirs — would never cease. Never stopping, ever running, at last despair came calling upon the woman's heart.

Tears fell. They overflowed. Not the hot tears that had streamed ceaselessly before, but cold tears of helplessness and disappointment. And yet, at the very same time, another feeling welled up within her.

A passion that dyed the inside of her chest a filthy black, and beyond that, turned her vision crimson red, and her mind pure white. Crying, the woman would come to know the true nature of that emotion. Knowing its name, and knowing where it had begun, the woman understood.

She had not, all this time, been crying out of sorrow. She had, purely and simply, been raging the entire time. The name of that emotion — people call it anger. No — this, people call "wrath."

Wrath at a world that forced tears upon her. At people who would not stop fighting. At the irrationality of a life that must, someday, inevitably end.

——She would deliver an iron fist.

Before she knew it, the woman had risen to her feet, brushed the dirt from her soiled knees, and begun running once more. She dove headlong into the very center of those still locked in battle, and she smashed her fist into their faces, and she shouted.

Stop fighting. Look at the sky. Listen to the wind. Smell the flowers. Live — with your families, with your lovers.

At the sound of the woman's voice, for the first time, unrest rippled through the battlefield. Fists that split the earth, kicks that made the sky groan — every one of them let people live.

Wounds closed. Screams stopped. Knees buckled at the touch of warmth, and fighting lost its meaning. Life returned to the ordinary day-to-day, and the wailing voices vanished from the battlefield.

The people's tears stopped. The people thanked the woman. They raised their voices, waved their hands, and smiled. But by then, the woman's form was already nowhere to be found.

Of course it was. The woman still had work left to do. She had no time to look back, no reason to stop her feet. She sought a world where no one cried, where there was no conflict, where nothing was ever taken away.

She ran, and ran, and kept on running, and the woman kept on swinging her fists. Until, someday, every last tear stopped falling. Until the hot droplets wetting her own cheeks stopped falling.

——The "Witch of Wrath" burned with anger at sorrow itself, and ran on and on, forever.

"――――"

Having found yet another book belonging to a knowable dead soul, Subaru returned once more to reality. What flowed in from the opened book was only the barest surface — a fragment of the battlefield the person named within had once run through in life, and the convictions they had held aloft. Knowing that much, even so——

"...Heavy."

There was a sensation, thick and sinking, of shapeless lead pooling inside his chest. A human life — whoever the person, one second is one second, one minute is one minute, one year is one year. There is no ruler to measure the density of a life, and it is precisely because of that, that time always flows equally for everyone. Such an accumulation of time could never possibly be conveyed in full by mere writing left behind on such flimsy paper, or by memories transcribed through some special technique. And yet, even if it was no more than a mere touch of the surface, Subaru still had to endure, to keep himself from being dragged along by the consciousness flowing into him.

That was how immense, how heavy, the footprint of a single human life could be.

"Subaru, are you okay?"

"...Yeah, I'm fine. I'm okay. Just got a little dizzy for a second."

"That's... not what 'okay' means..."

Emilia watched Subaru with concern as he closed the open book and returned it to the shelf. Closing one eye at her gaze, Subaru pointed to the book he'd just put back.

"Found another one. A witch's book. This one's, well, one of the more decent ones among the witches. Still kind of a weird kid, though."

"Weird kid... Sekhmet?"

"That one's plenty weird, all right. Wait, you know about Sekhmet?"

At the mausoleum in the Sanctuary, during the dream tea party, Emilia had presumably run into the witch Echidna too, but he hadn't heard anything about her having contact with any witch besides Echidna. He'd rather assumed Echidna hadn't let anyone but Subaru meet the other five, so it came as a surprise to hear Sekhmet's name from Emilia's own mouth.

The "Witch of Sloth" Sekhmet — a self-indulgent woman who let her reddish-purple hair grow out unchecked — but setting her conduct aside, in terms of personality she was, among the witches, the closest thing to normal.

"If you know Sekhmet, what about the other witches? Like, say, Minerva, from this book?"

"Um, sorry. I only met Echidna and Sekhmet at the mausoleum. And even with Sekhmet, we only talked a little."

"I see. No, it's fine, really. Even if it had been someone you knew, it's not exactly a pleasant thing to see."

Subaru gave a wry smile at Emilia, who drooped her shoulders, and lightly tapped the side of the bookshelf. The shelves that sealed away the memories of the dead in life were made of some strange material, neither quite wood nor quite metal, and didn't budge even under Subaru's palm.

Right now, Subaru and the others, ransacking the archive of the third floor "Taygeta," understood that the countless books lining the shelves called up the memories of the dead, and were pressing on with their patient search all the same. What exactly they were searching for was, in fact, quite simple.

"Still, I wonder where the staircase to the floor above is."

"Zero leads so far, honestly..."

Emilia tilted her head one way, and Subaru tilted his the other, letting out a sigh. Right now, the thing troubling the whole group wasn't clearing the "Trial" of the third floor "Taygeta" and moving on to the next problem — it was, more fundamentally, the whereabouts of the staircase needed to even attempt the second floor.

The white space unfurled by the monolith had vanished, and the great archive of "Taygeta" now lay open before them. Thanks to that, they'd reached a space employing what some would consider a positively enviable technique — the ability to touch the memories of the dead — but,

"Honestly, it's not really relevant to us right now."

The information Subaru and the others wanted — searching it out from the memories of the dead whose faces and names happened to match, and moreover from memories they couldn't even actively choose — would take an enormous amount of time and luck. Neither of which they could currently count on. Time, obviously not, and Subaru had no confidence he could claim, even in jest, to be blessed with good fortune.

"So the information we actually want is probably up above, I'd guess."

"And yet we can't get up there. I even tried jumping on top of the bookshelves, but no luck."

"Emilia-tan's kind of bold, huh..."

Pointing at the ceiling, Emilia recalled the little demonstration she'd given just now. Growing impatient with the search for a staircase leading upward, she'd leapt onto the top of the outermost bookshelf in the circular arrangement — the archive of "Taygeta" was structured so that the floor rose in tiers from the center outward, making the shelves at the outer edge the tallest — and even so, she still hadn't been able to reach the ceiling.

Incidentally, when she jumped atop the bookshelf, the hem of Emilia's short skirt had fluttered rather boldly — and yet, oddly enough, the motion had somehow never once revealed what was underneath. Worth noting for the record. At Subaru's roundabout way of pointing this out, Emilia touched the hem of her own skirt.

"Puck taught me. Um, a ladylike, modest way of protecting myself... I think that's what he called it. I always make sure to keep that in mind."

"I have complicated feelings about Puck right now, somewhere between wanting to praise him and wanting to scold him. ...Well, anyway."

Emilia had already tried, and failed, to force her way to the upper floor through purely physical means. Which meant the staircase was some kind of gimmick that appeared through a certain method — not just the archive's appearance, but even getting to the floor above required some extra trick. Overly elaborate, every step of the way. Subaru felt like being this elaborate would just sap a challenger's motivation, but presumably Flugel, the tower's designer, had accounted for that too when he built the Watchtower. Infuriating.

"I'd like to see the parents who raised him... though honestly, the guy's own face is a mystery too."

"—Subaru, do you have a moment?"

A voice called out to the back of Subaru's head as he grumbled. The speaker was Julius, who'd been ransacking the archive on the opposite side from Subaru's group. Seeing him approach with Anastasia at his side, Subaru raised a hand.

"Hm, find something?"

"Unfortunately, no results. Since the very first book, I haven't been able to turn up another book with a name I recognize. It seems your side got the same result."

"Not exactly. Subaru here found another one and went all woozy over it. Right, Subaru?"

"Since Emilia-tan's bragging on my behalf, I'll ride along — yeah, that's right."

"I see. Take care of yourself."

Julius gave a level nod to Subaru, who scowled and rubbed at a temple where a faint ache still lingered. Watching the exchange between the two men out of the corner of her eye, Emilia added, "But—"

"You guys haven't found the stairs either, huh, Julius. This is really, really troubling."

"I sympathize with how you must feel, Emilia-sama. ...That said, if this archive truly does record the names of every deceased person from the past up to the present, then frankly, this is far beyond what the few of us can manage alone. This is a matter that calls for far greater numbers — indeed, one the kingdom itself ought to take up."

Prefacing his remarks with the shortage of hands, Julius made the proposal with a perfectly straight face. Subaru, for his part, had no objection to the idea in the ultimate sense. The Augria Sand Dunes had already been conquered, and simply reaching the Watchtower now required nothing more than passing through the Witchbeasts' den. That alone still counted as a great adventure, but——

"Compared to before, the road to the tower is now essentially open. Making good use of this strange archive, too, I think it's worth reporting to the kingdom."

"The value as a historical resource is no joke. ...I'm sure it'd fill in plenty of holes in that history stuff you're so fond of, too."

"That is, strictly speaking, a secondary consideration."

Julius replied quickly to Subaru's jab, then fell silent for a moment. But soon he exhaled, closed his eyes, and said, "No—"

"It would be a lie to say I have no expectations of that at all. I apologize. There was self-interest in it."

"Don't get so down about it, what's wrong with acting out of self-interest? If you go and repent that hard over it, what does that make me, running around a hundred percent on ulterior motives?"

Basically, Subaru expected something in return, and didn't believe he could act purely out of a sense of duty, setting self-interest aside. Always thinking of himself first — that was Subaru's guiding principle, his policy. Which was exactly why he could never come close to the image of a noble knight like Reinhard or Julius.

"There's no need to go out of your way to distance yourself from it, either. To begin with, as far as I recall, Anastasia-san's reason for wanting to become queen was self-interest through and through. Wasn't it?"

"Mm-mm, no argument there. My reason for wantin' to be queen is that I want my own desires satisfied. Turns out that brings benefit 'round to the folks near me too. That's all there is to it."

At Subaru's rude, tactless remark, Anastasia laughed without any sign of taking offense. Then she touched her fur stole, stroking its white fur.

"But still, it's kinda funny, the two of us — Julius here and me — endin' up lord and vassal. That's how I see it, anyway. You two don't feel that way?"

"Personally I'm relieved the others aren't marching in lockstep, so please just keep going with the 'musical differences' and break up already — ow! Emilia-tan, ow!"

"Don't say mean things. —I still don't really understand Anastasia-san and the others as well as I should, so I can't say anything careless. But no matter how splendid the other side might be, I'll do things my way, together with my knights, and do my best."

At the words "my knights," Emilia pinched the sleeve of Subaru's clothes and puffed out her chest. His heart swelled at the sight of her profile, and Subaru let out a long breath through his nose.

"We might be a bunch of weaklings, but we won't lose."

"Nobody says 'weaklings' these days— ow! Ow!"

The moment he teased her, his arm got pinched, and Subaru hopped away to escape Emilia's punishment. Watching the two of them go at it, Julius and Anastasia looked thoroughly disarmed. Julius in particular seemed to have completely lost the reflective mood from a moment ago.

"...Sometimes I genuinely find the two of you frightening. I can never tell how much is sincere and how much isn't."

"Me aside, Emilia-tan's mostly sincere and serious about it. Isn't that cute?"

"I'll keep that in mind."

With that, Julius gave a small nod, and the tangent finally reached its terminus. It was Anastasia, clapping her hands together, who pulled things back to the main topic.

"So, gettin' back to what we were sayin' before... we're short on hands, so it'd be better to call in people from outside — that was the gist, right?"

"Yes, exactly. As things stand, most of the concerns regarding the dunes have been largely alleviated, and it should be possible to bring a larger-scale investigative party into the tower. If we could make some discovery in this archive, beyond just the path to the upper floor..."

"That's the thing, though — that's what's got me a mite scared."

"Scared, you say?"

Cutting off Julius mid-speech, Anastasia shook her head slowly. To her own knight, who furrowed his brow at that answer, Anastasia held up a finger. "See, now?"

"More hands, that part I welcome plenty. Just makin' the rounds of this archive is hard enough work. Especially for me, bein' short as I am — just lookin' up at the shelves makes my neck ache."

Tapping her own nape, Anastasia went on, "But still—"

"I do wonder if it's really wise to let a whole crowd of people into this tower."

"――――"

"The basics of trade are thinkin' from the other fella's shoes. Well, not just trade — it's a basic of life, useful in any situation. So, let's think on it that way, shall we?"

"Think about it how — whose shoes?"

"The one who built this tower, prepared the 'Trials,' and put Shaula here on watch... that fella. Think on it from their feelin's. Then, don't things start comin' clear?"

"You mean their personality is twisted?"

"Bent clean out of shape, if you ask me."

When Subaru's mouth curled, Anastasia nodded along, evidently in full agreement. At those words, the earnest Emilia and Julius wore troubled looks. But Subaru and Anastasia, belonging more to the side of poor personalities, if anything, found themselves naturally in sync.

Anastasia's reasoning seemed sound. If someone with a genuinely twisted personality really had wanted to raise the difficulty of clearing this tower...

"Shaula! I've got something to ask. Get over here a sec."

"Master-sama? Yes ma'am, comin' right up, ma'am!!"

Calling loudly toward Shaula on the far side of the bookshelves brought a spirited reply, and immediately a figure came bounding up in a high leap into view. Shaula. She stretched both arms wide, her long ponytail streaming behind her, and came barreling straight into Subaru's chest with a face full of smiles——

"Master-sa-maaa!"

"Whoop."

"Nginyaaah!"

Subaru just barely dodged that overenthusiastic embrace with some exquisite footwork. Unable to kill her momentum, Shaula bounced off the ground and ended up sprawled on hands and knees on the floor, glaring up at him resentfully.

"Uu, Master-sama's seriously mean."

"No, that wasn't because it was you — it's just, the momentum was so intense I dodged on reflex."

"Then if I come at you slow, will you hug me!?"

"Huh? No, I don't want to?"

"Why not! What's wrong with me!? With a body this glamorous! Being this much of a bitch!"

"You're a bitch, huh. Well, there's your reason."

Shaula, thoroughly dissatisfied, grumbled at Subaru's cold response. Just then, from where the sulking Shaula had bounced off to, two girls came walking over toward them. Beatrice and Meili — appearances aside, a rare combination. Perhaps Subaru's thought showed in his eyes, because Beatrice, noticing, put a little distance between herself and Meili.

Petra was likewise around the same apparent age as Beatrice, but the way Beatrice treated her and the way she treated Meili were drawn along a fairly clear line. Of course, it would be hard to treat Petra — an ally in good standing on the same side — the same as Meili, who had originally been sent in as an assassin from somewhere or other, and who'd been involved in the burning of the forbidden archive and the mansion.

"Keep being that standoffish and you'll never make any friends."

"What are you suddenly saying that for, I wonder. Besides, Shaula got dragged off partway through, so I could hardly finish talking, I suppose."

"Ah, my bad for cutting off your conversation there. Did you two find anything?"

"...Nothing in particular, I suppose. Besides, now that I think on it, it may not have even been much of a conversation to begin with. Yes, that must be it, I suppose."

"——?"

Turning her face away with a huff, Beatrice curtly dismissed Subaru's question. Subaru felt a faint pang of exclusion at her oddly stubborn attitude, but before he could press Beatrice on it, Shaula, who'd collapsed in a heap, was already recovering. Having gone down flat on the ground, Shaula pushed herself upright with nothing but abdominal strength, and stretched.

"Well, Master-sama bein' mean ain't exactly news, ma'am. Bouncin' right back. Quick recovery's my specialty, ma'am."

"On that point, your cooperativeness is genuinely a help. Shaula, if I may — I'd like to ask you something."

"What is it, ma'am? Can't go easy seducin' anyone who ain't Master-sama, ma'am."

"You are indeed a charming woman, but let's save the invitation to dinner for another occasion. —There are several things I'd like to confirm regarding the circumstances surrounding this tower."

Julius elegantly deflected Shaula, who was declaring a chastity unbefitting a self-proclaimed bitch, weaving in a courtly line of flattery. Shaula, apparently not expecting that kind of response either, blinked wide-eyed in a way quite unlike her usual self, and answered obediently, "Ah, yes ma'am."

"I appreciate it. Now, first of all — do you know where the staircase up to the second floor is, from here?"

"Stairs to the second floor 'Electra,' ma'am? Dunno, ma'am. I ain't never been above the fourth floor, ma'am. No idea, ma'am."

"Well, that's a shockin' thing to say, all on its own."

If Shaula's self-report were true, the years she'd spent at the Watchtower would amount to roughly four hundred years. If, in all that time, she'd focused entirely on watching over the dunes without ever exploring the very Watchtower she lived in, that had to be called, at the very least, an excessively lofty sort of loyalty. Honestly, for Subaru, being treated as "Master-sama," it left him with distinctly mixed feelings.

"Julius, mind if I have a go?"

Now, in Julius's place, Anastasia stepped up to face Shaula. Claiming the right to question after the knight, Anastasia reflected Shaula in her pale blue-green eyes.

"Shaula-san, you're that, right? Not exactly the Watchtower itself, but — you're the keeper of the Great Library Pleiades, correct?"

"Eee-zact-ly, ma'am."

"I see. Then, by my reckonin'... four. Hm, or maybe five?"

Anastasia raised her right hand and held up five fingers. At that gesture and remark, Shaula, unable to read her intent, blinked in bewilderment. But then—

"The secret rules Shaula-san's under, for protectin' the tower. Ain't it five?"

"――――"

At Anastasia's serenely smiling words, Shaula, for the first time, was struck speechless. Her eyes went wide, her shoulders jumped — and that reaction, more plainly than any words could, showed that Anastasia's suspicion had struck true.

"To begin with, it ain't like I was hidin' anything on purpose, ma'am. I just didn't say 'cause nobody asked, ma'am. I'd like that put down in writin', properly, ma'am."

"Never mind that, just spill everything."

Subaru crushed Shaula's flustered attempt at self-justification, now that Anastasia had seen through her secret. Faced with that hardline stance, Shaula pressed the fingertips of her two hands together.

"Like, just for example, ma'am — if Master-sama and the others tried to leave the tower without tellin' me, I'd have to kill you all without mercy, ma'am."

"That's a hell of a way to open!?"

"It ain't like I want to do it, ma'am! Just an example, ma'am! To begin with, this is a matter I can't go against, ma'am!"

At that unexpected declaration of hostility, Subaru's eyes went wide, and Shaula shrank her tall frame down, shaking her head. Sitting flat on the floor and hugging her knees, crushing her ample chest against them, she went on,

"There ain't no way I could actually kill Master-sama, ma'am. I'm the one who'd end up gettin' killed instead, and that'd be way too much of a hassle, ma'am..."

"If you hate it that much, just refuse... don't tell me — a Contract, is it?"

With a bad feeling creeping up on him, Subaru put that word to Shaula. It was something he should have thought of from the start, really, given Shaula's background. Cooped up in the tower for four hundred years, standing watch over the shrine where a witch was sealed, in place of the "Sage." Entrusted with such an absurdly long-term role, and moreover, actually following those instructions faithfully for literal centuries. — Neither her lifespan nor her nature was anything human.

"Aren't you a Spirit too, like Bea?"

Bound to the forbidden archive for four hundred years, over a promise that had never even existed — that was Beatrice. If, like her, Shaula too had spent four hundred years cutting down anyone who approached the Watchtower, one after another, waiting for someone capable of surpassing the conditions to finally arrive. Perhaps she, too, was the same kind of being as Beatrice——

"No way, ma'am. Don't lump me in with Spirits, all that fluffy lot, ma'am. Absolutely refuse... wait, why's everyone's eyes suddenly so scary, ma'am!"

"Eighty percent of the people here have some connection to spirits, that's why!"

After all, there were three Spiritual Arts Users present, counting even the inexperienced ones. On top of that, a little girl who was a Spirit herself, and one more person whose body was currently occupied by a provisional Spirit. The only ones unconnected were the demon sisters waiting downstairs, and Meili, who for some reason was watching Shaula with a mischievous look on her face. Shaula's remark wasn't exactly welcome to a group like that. Still,

"Fine then, so what exactly are you? If you're not a Spirit, there's no need to be this desperate about keeping a Contract."

"What are you sayin', Subaru. Whether you're a spirit or not a spirit user, if you made a promise, you have to keep it, obviously. Promises are important. Here, repeat after me."

"No wait, that one was my bad, but it's just a figure of speech—"

"Promises are important. One, two, three."

"Promises are important promises are important promises are important."

Getting scolded by Emilia out of nowhere, he had to repeat it three times to earn forgiveness. In any case, setting aside Subaru and Emilia's ridiculous exchange, Shaula's stubborn stance was utterly incomprehensible. It really didn't seem like something explainable by sheer, dogged loyalty alone.

"What matters right now is what Shaula-嬢 has been ordered to do. Wandering wildly off-topic is a bad habit of yours. You'd do well to be mindful of it."

"Like it's somehow all my fault... fine! Fine, it was my fault! I admit it was my fault, so separately from that, cough it up already, you!"

"Waaah, that's a total case of takin' it out on someone else, but that's just like Master-sama, ma'am. I'll talk, ma'am."

Shaula clapped her hands together, looking thoroughly pleased, at Subaru's pathetic, cornered expression. Then she cleared her throat and, with an oddly formal air, opened her mouth. "Well then, ma'am."

"Unworthy as I am, allow me to briefly explain what I've been told, ma'am. First — challengers of the Great Library Pleiades are absolutely never to be let outside anymore, ma'am."

"That's completely impossible right off the bat."

"It's fine! There's a proper loophole, ma'am! Buuuut, only if you properly finish the Great Library's 'Trial' and make it down to the first floor 'Maia,' there's no problem, ma'am. All-okay, ma'am."

Shaula raised a thumb and gave a firm thumbs-up.

"By the way, ma'am, if this condition gets violated, I turn right into a bloodless, tearless killing machine, ma'am, so my promise with Master-sama becomes void, ma'am. I'll come after you, ma'am."

"So it outranks my promise with you. That hurts, man."

"Ooh, managin' to hurt Master-sama — guess I've leveled up, ma'am! This must be evolution, ma'am! The culmination of four hundred years, ma'am!"

"That was sarcasm!"

"So was I, ma'am!"

They traded sharp words, and then Shaula held up a second finger, wagging it rhythmically as she continued, "Moving right along, ma'am."

"I'm already sick of this, so let's speed through it, ma'am. One: leavin' before finishin' the 'Trial' is forbidden. Two: breakin' the rules of the 'Trial' is forbidden. Three: disrespectin' the archive is forbidden. Four: any act of destruction against the tower itself is forbidden. Five... ah, five is... ah, there ain't one, ma'am."

"Four rules in total... but—"

Emilia picked up on Shaula's oddly halting words, turning back toward Subaru and the others with a troubled expression. Subaru nodded along with the unease and concern she carried. The content Shaula had laid out was, broadly speaking, within the range of what they could manage to observe — but there were a few things that bothered him. In particular, the real problem was——

"'Breaking the rules of the Trial is forbidden' — that's the one that bugs me."

"It implies there are some rules hidden from us that we don't yet know about."

Julius seemed to share the same opinion as Subaru, who had a hand to his chin. Regarding the third floor "Taygeta" "Trial" — at least the puzzle involving the monolith and the asterism — Subaru and the others hadn't been informed of anything resembling such rules. If anything came to mind, it was only that the moment they'd touched the wrong monolith, they'd been disqualified on the spot——

"Rules, decisions... somehow, that's got an unpleasant ring to it, don't it."

"...At the mausoleum, the 'Trials' — if you failed, you couldn't try again until the next day. That's a little similar to redoing the 'Trial' just now, and maybe..."

There, Emilia cut herself off, biting her lip hesitantly. Her trembling violet eyes flicked briefly toward Subaru, and at that glance, Subaru nodded back.

"If you've thought of something, go ahead and say it. Nobody's going to laugh at you, whatever it is."

"Okay, got it. Um, you know how Subaru and Anastasia-san were saying that whoever built this tower has a reeeally twisted personality... right?"

"The way you put things is awfully cute, but yeah, that's right. And?"

"It's important to think from the other person's perspective too. So, thinking about what Shaula just said while putting myself in the shoes of a reeeally twisted person, I thought it might be something like this."

All eyes converged on her, and Emilia licked her lips once. Then she pressed her hands together, pointed her fingertips toward the ceiling, and said,

"There are rules you have to follow, but you're not told what they are. ...Don't you think that, in itself, is the twisted part?"

"...Meaning?"

"Meaning maybe you're supposed to guess at what the rules are, and proceed without breaking them."

"――――"

At Emilia's uneasy words, Subaru and the others exchanged glances. At their reaction, Emilia lowered her eyes, framed by long lashes,

"I just thought, if it were Echidna, she seems like the type who'd do something like that."

"...Emilia-tan, the first person who comes to mind for you too when you think 'someone with a twisted personality' is her, huh. We're on the same page."

The remark added on afterward only made it feel more credible, at least to Subaru. Emilia's unaccustomed foray into twisted, mean-spirited human logic — and, moreover, one that struck Subaru as quite possibly correct.

Setting rules that must not be broken, without ever telling the challenger what those rules are. Quite the nasty, tasteless little trap.

"By the way, is it you who decides if one of these rules gets broken?"

"Somehow it just becomes clear, apparently, ma'am. If any of the conditions I just named get broken, apparently I just know it, ma'am. So there's no fakin' it, ma'am. — Not about me, and not about Master-sama and the others, ever, ma'am."

In the latter half, there was a strange gravity to her tone, and real weight behind the words as she declared it. That wasn't weight in the sense of Shaula's own strength. If anything, it was the opposite.

——There was a force with enough weight to make even Shaula, a being of Shaula's caliber, say such a thing.

"It's just one more headache stacked on top of headaches we already had. Nothing new."

"I sometimes envy how you can say that so plainly."

Letting out a breath, Julius spoke up at Subaru's muttered remark. When Subaru glanced back at the handsome man's profile, he shrugged.

"Perhaps it's the fact that you can only ever stand against opponents stronger than yourself that cultivates that kind of mindset. If so, that's quite the gap in experience — one I can't easily replicate."

"You should be more scared of what's under your feet. One of these days you're gonna stub your pinky toe on a table corner. Go ahead and stub it."

"Alright, alright, fightin' friendly's all well and good, but let's not forget the main topic, yeah?"

Cutting in between Subaru and Julius, Anastasia looked down at the seated Shaula.

"So is that really the end of it? Nothin' else we oughta worry about?"

"I swear, ma'am. No more lies this time, ma'am. And as long as none of these rules get broken, my body's my own, ma'am. Wait, that's wrong, ma'am. It's Master-sama's, ma'am."

"Don't want it."

"Rejected, ma'am! But my heart's always by Master-sama's side, ma'am!"

"Don't want that either."

Pressed further by Anastasia, hand on her hip, Shaula firmly stated her position, along with a heap of unnecessary embellishment. Amid all the extra flourishes, it seemed, at least for now, that the promise made with Subaru downstairs — not to harm him and the others — would be kept. Conditional, of course, on none of the rules being broken in the meantime.

"Still, not being able to leave until we finish the 'Trial'... somehow, this feels more and more like the mausoleum's 'Trials.'"

"Nah, worst case, if we beat this one here when she turns hostile, we get to go home, right? That's more lenient than the mausoleum."

"Master-sama wouldn't do somethin' like that, ma'am! You've got the biggest, kindest heart of anyone, ma'am! Aw crap, my body's gettin' itchy from lyin', ma'am!"

In front of a sighing Emilia and Subaru, Shaula flailed about, entirely her own fault. For now, that was more or less everything they could get out of her.

"Are you alll done talkin'? Is it okay to keep cuddlin' up to the naked big sister?"

"Done, we're done. Sure, go ahead, do what you want. ...Man, you're really attached to her, huh."

Sensing the conversation had wrapped up, Meili, who'd been left out of it until now, ran over to Shaula. Clinging to her slender shoulders, she climbed right back up onto her back. Fully settled now into her designated position within the tower. For Shaula, strong enough to haul a Dragon Carriage, a weight like Meili's was practically nothing more than a feather.

"Bein' near big sis makes me feel really calm, somehow."

"Well, I don't mind none, ma'am. I'll look after the little runt number two, ma'am."

"Number two?"

"Number two's this little runt here, and number one's that little runt over there, ma'am."

Still carrying Meili piggyback, Shaula pointed at Beatrice, calling her "number one." At that thoroughly rude remark from Shaula, Beatrice showed no reaction at all. Normally, she'd have gone red in the face and lit into Shaula on the spot — that was her nature.

"Wait, actually, you've been totally out of the conversation for a while now too — is something wrong?"

"――――"

"Bea? Hey, Beatrice. Snap out of it. I'm gonna kiss your forehead."

"...Do as you please, I suppose."

"――――"

It wasn't that she hadn't heard him, but the reply came out listless, indifferent to Subaru's words. Somehow that rubbed him the wrong way, and Subaru's brow furrowed. Then,

"Mwah."

"Ngyaaah, I suppose!?"

Somehow irritated, he actually went and kissed her forehead for real, and Beatrice snapped back to herself instantly, clutching her kissed forehead and leaping backward. Stumbling. Getting up. Stumbling again.

"You're way too flustered..."

"Wh-wh-what do you think you're suddenly doing!? That's far too abrupt, I suppose!"

"There was context, and I got your permission. Are you actually okay?"

Feeling a bit stung by the sight of her frantically rubbing her forehead, Subaru grew worried about the girl. Come to think of it, this was a tower in the middle of an ominous, storied sand dune. There was probably some kind of miasma or whatever drifting around that Subaru didn't fully understand, and maybe that was the cause.

"If you're not feeling well, hold my hand. That'll settle you down, right?"

"Not with things as they are right now, I suppose! Give me a moment to calm down, I suppose!"

Beatrice, red in the face, screeched and flailed, and Subaru just shrugged. Getting turned down even on holding hands stung a little, but at least this was her usual self again. If she had something weighing on her mind, he'd find another chance to draw it out of her later.

"Well, so then, the remaining problem is..."

"In the end, it seems the location of the stairs to the second floor can only be found by searching blindly."

Turning back toward the bookshelves once more, Julius picked up where Subaru's words left off. The faint air of dejection about him came from the fact that his idea — that they needed more hands — had been half derailed by the rule Shaula had been bound by. Unless they finished the "Trial," they wouldn't be permitted to leave the tower. Naturally, that also meant they wouldn't be allowed to go back out to the dunes to call for reinforcements.

Hence, there was no option left but to continue the search with the current group as it was.

"You know that feeling of searching for a single grain of gold you dropped somewhere in the dunes? Get what I mean?"

"An uncharacteristically poetic turn of phrase from you, but I find myself in honest agreement."

Subaru and Julius, uncommonly, found themselves in genuine, straightforward accord in the face of the challenge before them. Standing around bracing for it wasn't going to get them anywhere. Better to just dive headlong into this sea of books and start searching for a way upward — just as he thought that——

"Hey, I just thought of something, a little bit."

To the two of them, freshly resolved to throw themselves back into the archive, Emilia raised a small hand. As the two turned back, she tilted her head and pressed a finger to her lips.

"I've been thinking this whole time about the part where an insincere, twisted person built this tower."

"The way you put things is awfully cute, but yeah, that's right. And?"

At the end of an exchange that felt, eerily, like an exact repeat of before, Emilia added, "So—"

"About where the stairs might be — could it possibly be——"

"Yeah, this tower-builder's personality really is the absolute worst!!"

Standing before a staircase stretching high and long — the one leading up to the second floor "Electra" — Subaru let loose an enormous outpouring of anger he could no longer hold back. The staircase to the second floor, its hiding place as Emilia had guessed, was——

"I just thought, maybe it'd show up somewhere on the fourth or fifth floor we hadn't looked at yet."

Emilia's words carried a complicated mix of pride at being right and something else entirely. Her guess had hit the mark dead-on: the staircase to the second floor had appeared on the fourth floor, in the room right next to the green room where Ram and Rem were waiting — in that very blank space.
