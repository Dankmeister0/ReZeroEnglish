Arc 8 Interlude: "Ubilk"

——As you well know, Ubilk was a Stargazer.

The heavenly mandate he'd been granted was to save the Vollachian Empire from the ruin of the looming Great Disaster.
To that end, he — once nothing more than a common male prostitute — had acquired an intellect beyond all expectation, until at last he'd earned a measure of recognition from Vincent Vollachia.

Of course, Ubilk wasn't naive enough to credit himself, to preen over it as though it were his own achievement.

Even so, while most who were given the role of Stargazer were thought to walk a miserable path, he considered his own position to have been a fortunate one.
Most Stargazers, in being granted their heavenly mandate, had their prior humanity twisted out of shape and were forced into a new way of living. That was sometimes called misfortune, something to be pitied — but to Ubilk, the very premise of such a judgment felt like a luxury, a viewpoint only the blessed could afford.

A humanity that could be bent out of shape, and people around you who would notice the change — without those things to begin with, such a perception could never arise in the first place.
Ubilk was exactly the sort of person who had neither. He was deeply, sincerely grateful to the heavenly mandate that had changed a life that should have been spent, used up, as no one in particular.

——That much hadn't changed, even now, after he had finished fulfilling his mandate as a Stargazer.

"I sti-ill haven't managed to save Vollachia, you know..."

In the fortress of the citadel city of Garkla, where the reinforcement of arms, the proper placement of personnel, and war preparations were being carried out through the night, Ubilk — with no post of his own — murmured this as he looked down upon the bustling people below.
There was a sensation of a fog that had always occupied a corner of his mind clearing away, its influence draining smoothly from his thoughts, and Ubilk realized that he was no longer a Stargazer.

As mentioned before, the heavenly mandate Ubilk had been granted was to save Vollachia from the Great Disaster.
Despite that, being released from his mandate at this point in time amounted to being told, implicitly, that there was nothing more Ubilk could do about the Great Disaster.
At once, he woke from the fervor that had been driving him forward, and Ubilk felt as though he'd been stripped bare.

"I am grateful, you know? I really a-am grateful, bu-ut—"

It had taken nearly ten years to bring to fruition — truly the gamble of a lifetime.
If the ladder was kicked out from under him right before the results came in, well, he'd be entitled to complain that this wasn't what he'd signed up for. To prove to Vincent that he harbored no divided loyalties, Ubilk had gone so far as to crush the Evil Eye he'd been born with as a member of the Evil Eye Tribe, declaring his position by that very act.
Even now, there was an old scar at the center of his chest from that ruined Evil Eye — a lonesome thing.

"Well, I-I was pretty useless as an Evil Eye user anyway, so it's fine, I suppose."

The problem was the borrowed conviction that, having had fuel fed into it without pause until today, now had that fuel taken away and continued to send up only smoldering smoke — and where to place the self that remained once it was gone.
Until now, he had kept moving in whatever direction his mandate pointed, fulfilling his role as a Stargazer.
Since that had been Ubilk's way of fighting, he'd never learned a warrior's way of fighting — swinging a sword, drawing a bow, anything like that. Even during his days on the Sword Slave Island, his top priority had been not dying so he could fulfill his mandate; he had exactly zero experience of actual combat.
And so, in this fortress where everyone had resolved themselves to fight, Ubilk was utterly alone.

"Maybe I shouldn't have been so honest with His Excellency, I wo-onder?"

Vincent had left the citadel city of Garkla and headed for the imperial capital of Lupugana for the decisive battle.
Before his departure, Ubilk had been asked directly by him — as a Stargazer, was there still anything he could do to help avert the Great Disaster?
If only he'd been able to lie there, to say his mandate still had something left in it, perhaps he wouldn't have been left behind in this fortress.

"But-ut, it's not like I want the Empire to fall, you know."

Lying because he had nowhere else to belong, and thereby leading Vincent's judgment astray, would have been an act of aiding the enemy.
Weighing which — Vincent or Chisha — surviving would be better for the Empire, Ubilk had chosen to go along with Chisha's plan, helping Vincent live.
He himself had been granted a heavenly mandate, but Chisha, who had determined his own way of life without one, was admirable.
He didn't want to sully that choice. And yet, at the same time—

——

In the bustling atmosphere of the fortress, Ubilk — with nowhere in particular to go — passed by person after person.
Every single one of them understood the dire straits they were in, and yet still they could take on what needed doing. Regardless of how their abilities measured up against each other, he respected those who could give their all to their proper duty. At the same time, he envied them. Until just a little while ago, Ubilk had been on the same side as them.
And so, Ubilk found himself thinking.

"If the Empire were ever in danger again... would-d I be granted a mandate once more, I wonder?"

If his role had been taken from him because it was no longer needed, then what if a hand were needed again?
At this point, there was nothing Ubilk could do for Vincent and the others who had set out for the imperial capital. But suppose, for instance, that even if Vincent and the others managed to remove the root cause of the Great Disaster, someone holding a key post in the Empire lost their life, or someone from the allied Kingdom died in the process — what then?
Wouldn't that, too, be a crisis of the Empire's ruin, in a form different from the Great Disaster?

"The chance of a Stargazer who's already been granted a mandate once, and fulfilled that role, being granted a new one — that's close to zero. But-ut, it's not zero."

If heavenly mandates were things brought forth to save the Vollachian Empire from ruin...
...then if a new crisis were born, a new mandate to avert it might come down as well.

——It was his desperate hope for that possibility that turned Ubilk's feet toward the back of the fortress.

——

Ubilk, and the dignitaries of both Empire and Kingdom alike, were all gathered in the sturdiest of Garkla's great fortresses.
For the Undead to reach this fortress, they would need to bring down the walls surrounding the city and the several fortresses along the way — and by the time things got that far, it would already amount to a crushing defeat from a defensive standpoint.
But the citadel city had no further line of retreat beyond this; the soldiers would have to fight to the very last man. This great fortress was, quite literally, the Empire's final bastion.
——If there were a single flaw in it.

"Just a small one."

A single flaw would be enough.
He wouldn't even need to fully remove the bar from a gate — leaving it just slightly loose would make it easier to knock free. Or he could rig a simple timed device using a magic stone on the fortress's outer wall. Anything that gave the Undead's back one small push.
If he could arrange that, then into Ubilk's heart, its fire gone cold, the next piece of kindling—

"—Hey, what are you doing over there?"

Startled by the voice that suddenly rained down from above, Ubilk stopped in his tracks, his cheeks stiffening.
Turning around, he met a gaze looking down on him from a second-floor passage of the fortress — a woman in a wheelchair, dark brown eyes narrowed in a nasty look.
She was the woman who'd been aboard the Coupled Dragon Carriages too, someone whose role he'd never quite worked out. She glared at Ubilk with suspicious eyes, at him standing alone in the deserted back of the fortress, and said—

"Don't tell me... you're slacking off? If you are, that's the worst. Listen, even a woman with bad legs like me is working, so are you trying to get yourself killed, or what?"

"...N-no, I'm not slacking off—"

"Then why are you standing around there? Don't give me some bad excuse. You think you can fool someone naive like me, don't you? Well, I can tell."

Mixing in an oddly self-deprecating angle, the woman laid into Ubilk like that.
It was an awfully one-sided attitude, but anything he said back seemed likely to just make her angrier for nothing, so Ubilk seriously agonized over what to do. And as he agonized, a thought struck him.

"Ca-an I ask you something... A young lady with bad legs, who probably can't fight — why do you go on living?"

"Are you picking a fight with me!?"

"Ah, no, that came out wrong. I-I'm not trying to say anything bad about you, miss, it's just—"

To the woman glaring wide-eyed and leaning out of the window, Ubilk raised both hands in apology. While apologizing, he assembled in his mind what he'd actually meant to say.
In this situation, in a great fortress where warriors came and went, here was a woman with a disabled body, of no use to anyone.

"How is it that a young lady like you can go on looking for something you can do?"

"Obviously it's because it feels awful to just sit here doing nothing!"

——

Startled by the blunt, angry shout thrown back at him, Ubilk was taken aback.
At Ubilk's surprise, she bit at her fingernail in irritation, muttering "Ugh, seriously." Biting away, she glared at Ubilk like he was her mortal enemy, and said—

"...You know, my fiancé died in this war. He went out of his way to come save me, and he died."

"...That mu-ust have been hard on you."

"Don't act like you understand. Well, it's not just you — everyone acts like they understand, but it's none of their business. But, you know—"

"But?"

"If a woman in a wheelchair just sits around moping and useless, what do you think people will say? Oh, he died because he bothered with someone like her — that's what they'll say about Todd, mocking him for it."

The woman said this with her voice and lips trembling, tears welling in her furious eyes.
Was that name that had leapt out without explanation her fiancé's? What she'd voiced was, unmistakably, a persecution complex. But however much it was exaggerated, it also hit close to the truth.
Without knowing the circumstances, without knowing her relationship with her fiancé, most people whose lives brushed briefly against hers as someone else's business would probably feel that kind of pity for her.

"I don't need anyone's pity. If I-I can just carry myself with a little dignity, if I can find even a little bit of work I'm able to do, then that idiot — that idiot won't be looked down on by people who never even knew him."

——

"I'm going to live with my head held high."

Gritting her teeth, tears in her eyes, her voice trembling painfully, she said that. Having said it, she once more glared at Ubilk with those glistening eyes.
Sharply, from her higher vantage point, she jabbed a finger down at him, and—

"I-if you get it, then go find work and get to it. Are you saying you're worse off than a woman in a wheelchair?"

Whether that was meant to spur him on, or was simply her mouth being foul by nature, Ubilk — who didn't even know the woman's name — couldn't tell her true intent.
He couldn't tell, but what kind of life she had resolved to live came through more than clearly enough.
And that was—

"That's admira-able, miss. You've just killed off one reason the Empire might have fallen."

——the trigger that, in the truest sense, freed Ubilk — no longer a Stargazer — from his heavenly mandate.

"He said this utterly incomprehensible thing, trying to smoke-screen his way past me, that man. Well, afterward it looks like he actually went and got work from the fortress soldiers properly, so..."

Walking alongside Katya, who was huffing with unresolved indignation, Rem sighed deeply at the story of the man she'd apparently scolded.
Even in a situation like this, where everyone needed to pull together as one, a certain number of troublemakers inevitably showed up — the sort who sapped the morale around them and disrupted the circle.

"But please be careful. There's no telling if the other person might lose their temper and turn violent against you, Katya-san."

"Ugh, that's..."

"It's a good thing he listened to you honestly this time. From now on, please don't do anything reckless when I'm not by your side, okay?"

Making a sour face, lips pursed, Katya couldn't say anything back to Rem's concern.
She didn't want to nod along obediently, but neither did she want to say something needlessly nasty. Glimpsing that very Katya-like struggle, Rem's eyes softened at the corners.

With Subaru and the others having set out for the imperial capital, Rem and those who remained in the city were kept busy preparing for the defensive battle.
Those who could fight took up their respective posts, and those who couldn't lent support to them. Rem, who could use healing magic, was in high demand — it was, quite literally, an all-out effort.
Katya, who had just talked one unmotivated worker into shaping up, was one of those giving her all toward that effort too — though of course, Rem understood that her fighting spirit didn't mean she had actually recovered from her sense of loss.

Katya, who had lost her fiancé Todd, and who had even been told outright by her own brother Jamal that he was heading off to a place of certain death — imagining what was in her heart, even Rem's own chest felt fit to break.
Katya's stubborn front, her effort not to let Rem worry over her, only made it worse.
That's why—

"Katya-san, could I ask for your help gathering clean cloth? I'm sure we'll need a lot once the fighting starts."

"Sure. See, my lap can actually hold a surprising amount of stuff."

Without pointing out that show of bravado, Rem asked her for help with the work, believing it was what Katya needed right now — and what was needed for even one more person to reach tomorrow.
And then—

"—Ah, Rem-neesama!"

Called by name like that, Rem looked up, and a girl waving from down the corridor came into view.
A lovely girl with a large ribbon in her hair — Petra. Beside her stood tall Frederica, and there was yet one more—

"Neesama's here too — what are the three of you all doing together?"

Catching sight of Ram leaning back against the wall, Rem tilted her head.
The three of them, apparently, had been Rem's coworkers back when she'd had what Subaru called her "memories." She had, for now, come to accept her relationship with Ram, but with the other two she still hadn't quite figured out the distance between them.
To Rem, who tensed up just slightly at that, Frederica smiled, her eyes crinkling gently, and said—

"Our work reached a stopping point, so we came to invite you to take a break with us. Though without letting our guard down too much — a minimum of tension is necessary, of course..."

"You can't keep up being tense forever, and the more time spent with Rem, the better."

"I agreed with Ram-neesama too. Besides..."

Laughing at Ram's blunt honesty, Petra looked up at Rem with a meaningful expression.
At Petra's gaze, Rem fell silent for a moment and understood her true meaning. They were people whose relationship with Rem she was still feeling out — but the same was true from their side as well.
Still, the fact remained that this was an emergency, so—

"I appreciate the thought. But this isn't really the time for..."

"No, Rem. It's the other way around."

"Neesama...?"

"The situation is what it is. You might think this isn't the time for something like this. But it could also be said that now is the only chance we've got. Ram can believe in Rem without question. But—"

At that, Ram narrowed her pale pink eyes and indicated the two beside her with a glance.
Rem had thought that, precisely because the situation was so pressing, building closeness should be set aside for later. Ram and the others, however, thought that trust mattered all the more precisely because of a situation like this.

"...What do you think, Katya-san?"

"Eh!? D-don't ask me about something like that. Besides, I wasn't even invited..."

"What are you talking about. You're Rem's friend, aren't you? You're coming too."

"Wha— y-you look so alike, but you're so much pushier than Rem... no, wait, come to think of it calmly, she's plenty pushy in her own way too...!"

Addressed simultaneously by the sisters, Rem and Ram, Katya was thrown into great confusion.
In any case, having been asked her opinion, Katya let her eyes wander for a while before saying—

"...Why not just get along? As long as you actually talk about work too, it's not like it counts as slacking off, and I bet they wanted to see you, is what I think."

"Katya-san!"
"—Katya-sama."

"D-don't come at me like that! I don't even know your names!"

Petra and Frederica were delighted that Katya, after agonizing over it, had come down on Ram and the others' side. Watching Katya out of the corner of her eye, flustered by the two girls' enthusiasm, Rem resigned herself as well.
It wasn't as though Rem herself minded, not at all. It was only that she simply couldn't help feeling rushed—

"—Are you that worried about Barusu?"

"Yes. That person is always doing something reckless...ah."

Ram asked this quietly, looking at Rem's profile as she held a hand to her chest.
It came so naturally that Rem's true feelings slipped out reflexively before she could stop them.
Without thinking, she moved the hand from her chest to her mouth and stared fixedly at Ram. But her sister only winked and smiled proudly, and Rem was made to realize, once again, that she was no match for her.
And it was right then — that a great, enormous booming sound rang out in the sky above the great fortress.

"—!"

In that instant, as if struck by something, Rem spun toward the window and saw a flash of light tearing through the dawn-lit sky.
It was the signal that had been arranged and announced beforehand, from the city's lookouts—

"—Frederica!"
"I know!"

Right after, before Rem could even move, Frederica reacted to Ram's sharp call.
She swiftly moved around behind Katya and grabbed the handles used to push the wheelchair.

"Katya-sama, please be careful not to bite your tongue!"

"Wai— h-hold on a—— gyaaah!"

"Frederica-neesama, this way!"

Katya, in the wheelchair, let out a scream as Frederica took off running at full tilt. As if guiding the wheelchair, Petra dashed ahead down the fortress corridor with quick little steps of her small body.
A beat behind them, people began stirring one after another throughout every part of the great fortress — no, across the entirety of the citadel city of Garkla — and in that instant the city transformed into a single living creature.
A creature bound by shared fate, all in the same boat, living or dying together as one.

"Rem, are you ready in your heart?"

Rem caught her breath at the illusion that the whole city was shaking, and Ram fell in step beside her.
At the question, Rem looked back at her sister's sharp, striking profile, let out a small breath, and said—

"What would you do if I said I wasn't ready?"

"In that case, rest easy — Ram's already finished getting ready, enough for both her heart and Rem's."

"...As expected of Neesama."

"Naturally. Ram is the kind of older sister who makes you want to puff your chest out and say so."

Needless to say, Rem nodded to Ram, who had shared her courage with her, and together with her sister, she took off running through the fortress growing louder and louder around them, chasing after Katya and the others up ahead.
The battle was beginning — the final battle that would decide the fate of this Empire.

"Please. —You're the one we're counting on."

Picturing the boy who wasn't here, Rem prayed, holding back a bitter, gnawing feeling.
Whether that feeling came from relying on the boy, or from not being able to help him at his side, the source of her frustration remained unclear even to her.

Rem chased after Ram's back as it went on ahead, and together they ran on through the shaking fortress.

The large-scale evacuation from the imperial capital of Lupugana, and the retreat battle of a scale unprecedented in history that accompanied it.
Even as it produced no small number of casualties among both civilians and soldiers, the fact that the current toll still fell drastically below what had originally been anticipated owed much to the everyday mindset instilled in the Empire's people.

——Let the people of the Empire be strong.

That teaching and philosophy had permeated to the very edges of the Empire's populace, and even in extreme conditions, many had succeeded in taking the best possible action to survive.
In addition to such individual resolve and action bearing fruit, the fact that preparations for emergencies — starting with the Coupled Dragon Carriages — had functioned to their fullest was also significant. It was the achievement of the person who had treated the coming of the Great Disaster, originally prophesied by a Stargazer, as a threat destined to arrive and prepared accordingly.
That the man himself had never made it to this battlefield against the Great Disaster could only be called a bitter regret.

"But that selflessness is what kept the Empire alive! I pay him my respects, Marshal Chisha...!"

Roaring that out at the top of his voice, Kafma Irulux swept away swarms of the Undead with thorns unleashed from both arms, fighting fiercely to defend the road to the citadel city at his back.
The Imperial Army, deployed in stages along the highway running from the imperial capital to the citadel city, was supporting the retreat of the fleeing Imperial citizens while gradually falling back toward the city, aiming to link up with the fortress contingent.

Among the Marshals who had taken part in the decisive battle for the imperial capital, the role given to Kafma was of the highest importance; though he himself wouldn't admit it, it could be said his efforts had swayed the very survival of Vollachia.
The resolve of the Imperial citizens had a great effect on the casualties among refugees, prior preparation on the citadel city's defenses, and Kafma's fierce fighting on the high morale of Imperial soldiers and their minimized losses.

His service as rearguard at every stage of the gradual retreat toward the citadel city, and his continued use of the power of the Insect within him to slow the Undead's advance, was an achievement that deserved to be recorded in the history books.
But all of that only mattered if the Vollachian Empire actually escaped ruin.
And Kafma's extraordinary feats were also the fruit of a battle fought without any consideration for his own survival.

"—!"

As far as the eye could see across the great plain, an army of the Undead filled the land.
Slamming thorns, orbs of light, and blades of wind into them, Kafma gritted his teeth against the appetite of the Insect gnawing at him from within, swallowing back his own scream.

Members of the Insect Cage Tribe, who drew on the power of Insects living symbiotically within their bodies, displayed varying degrees of ability and combat strength depending on the type of Insect they carried. But the relationship between the Insect Cage Tribe and their Insects was, first and foremost, one of symbiosis.
It was neither one-sided command nor full assimilation.
Naturally, the Insect demanded compensation for its labor from its host. That meant the host's mana and vitality — and if that wasn't enough, their very flesh and blood.

More than fifty hours had already passed since Kafma had resolved to fight to the utmost of his strength, and throughout that time he had been forced to go, quite literally, without food or drink.
His mounting battle spirit had sharpened his awareness like never before, but the host's mental state meant nothing to the Insect inside him. It simply pressed its hunger upon its host, and, having lost patience with a host who wouldn't listen, began trying to chew its way through his flesh.

"It's I who's breaking our agreement. But even so...!"

He couldn't afford to pull back from the front lines here, nor could he afford to collapse.
The Empire had many other reliable warriors besides himself, but Kafma had the pride of believing he could die more bravely than any of them. He had to prove that pride was real.
To that end, too—

"I cannot afford to fall here—"

—was what he told himself, bracing his resolve, about to step forward even as his innards were being devoured, when—
a large hand seized his shoulder from behind.
And the owner of that large hand—

"Fall back, Vice-Marshal Kafma! You've done more than enough!"

The shout, loud enough to sound like scolding, was in fact this man's ordinary speaking volume.
Buffeted by that voice like a gust of wind, Kafma's eyes went wide with shock as he looked at the giant in golden armor who had drawn up beside him — Goz Ralfon.
Goz, a war-hammer that only he could wield gripped in his hand — his arrival caught Kafma by surprise.

"Why have you come to the front, Marshal Goz? You were to command from the fortress—"

"Senior Count Dracroy is there for that! Even without me holed up in the fortress, that formidable woman will manage the soldiers well enough! More than that, I can be of better use to His Excellency out here on the battlefield!"

"...Then I'll fight alongside you."

His heart, cracking under desperation and sheer effort, tried once more to armor itself firmly at Goz's arrival. But Goz stopped Kafma's plea with a single "No."
With that, Goz pressed a raised, thick finger against Kafma's chest, and said—

"I can hear something stirring and devouring inside you. Your Insect must have lost control. If you were to be eaten alive, that would be a great loss!"

"That's... but everything I have built up has been for this very battle!"

"Kafma Irulux! Dying is not atonement!!"

"—!"

Struck head-on this time by a true, honest shout of rage, Kafma's whole body shuddered.
Just this once, even the Insects, absorbed in devouring Kafma's body, stopped moving, cowering before the ferocious presence of Goz right in front of them.
Looking down at Kafma as he went still, Goz's scarred, bearded face twisted with anger, and he said—

"I understand you're still trying to atone for the treason your kinsman committed alongside that Balleroy. But! That is not a debt paid by dying! You will live!"

"Marshal Goz..."

"Even once this Great Disaster has passed, the Empire won't simply return to how it was overnight! Both you and I still have much more we can do for His Excellency! And so—!"

Packing his roar so full of loyalty it seemed ready to burst, Goz pounded away at Kafma's resolve.
Having had even his own inner heart seen through — that this was no place to throw away his life — Kafma shut his eyes tightly and bowed deeply before Goz.

"Bah, either way, this fight isn't easy enough that it'll be over just because you fall back. Your turn will come again soon! Eat, sleep, and get your strength back before then!"

"Understood. Marshal Goz, I leave this to you...!"

"No need to say it. —I've got plenty of pent-up frustration of my own to work off!"

Breaking into a grin at Kafma's strained words, Goz leveled the war-hammer from his shoulder toward the enemy lines.
Wielding that impossibly heavy weapon, one no one but him could ever handle with ease, Goz's entire body overflowed with such an immense fighting spirit that it seemed almost visible to the eye.
Feeling once again the Insect within his own body cower in fear, Kafma thought to himself.

Both Chisha Gold, who had unleashed a fiendishly brilliant scheme to forestall the Empire's ruin, and Goz Ralfon, who made Insects cower merely by standing there brimming with overwhelming presence — those who had risen to the rank of Marshal of the Vollachian Empire were, every one of them, transcendent beings, none inferior to the rest.
Picturing the past in which he himself had nearly been counted among them, and the future in which he would have to stand among them once more—

"—The Empire is strong. We're not so easily taken from, not by the Undead, not by some calamity."

"Well, you say it plainly. Much more to my liking than someone who can't voice a proper opinion for fear of reading the other person's face. Though if your martial skill could catch up to your wits, you'd suit my taste even better."

"Countess Dracroy, let's keep the digressions to a minimum."

Chastising her like that, Berstetz glared at Serena with eyes narrow as threads. At that look, Serena merely shrugged, while Otto — the one who'd just been flirted with — let out a small sigh.
The setting was the command room of the great fortress, and those gathered face-to-face were the ones entrusted with the role of commanding the city's full-scale defensive battle.

Following the lookouts' report, the citadel city of Garkla, centered on the great fortress, had entered a state of full battle readiness.
The delaying force that had been holding back the enemy's advance had also joined up with the city, and now, on the stage of the city boasting the strongest defenses the Vollachian Empire had to offer, the largest siege in Imperial history was about to begin.

"Still, the usual win condition for a siege is reinforcements arriving, but we can't really count on that this time. The only way we can say we've truly won is..."

"—Only if Natsuki-kun and the others, together with His Imperial Majesty, manage to strike down the enemy's mastermind. Until then, our job is to fight to keep Vollachia from falling."

"And if Vollachia falls, Kararagi and Lugunica would both be in danger too, wouldn't they... Geez, this really is a fight for the survival of the whole world, isn't it."

At Anastasia's words, spoken with a soft, graceful smile as she rested a hand against her pale cheek, the fox stole chiming in beside her let out an exasperated sigh.
Odd-looking as it was, the fox stole was apparently a Spirit that traveled with Anastasia. More usable minds were always better than fewer, and this was, in every sense, shaping up to be an all-out war effort.

"First is the question of how long the city's outer walls will hold. Marshal Ralfon has gone out to switch places with Vice-Marshal Irulux. For all his looks, he's a man who does surprisingly delicate, skillful work. As long as it's possible, he shouldn't be taking the lives of the zombies, in line with His Excellency's orders, but..."

"Holding to a strict policy of non-lethality is impossible. That goes for anyone, not just Marshal Goz. If there's anyone we can pin real hope on, though..."

"That would be Natsuki-kun's group of friends, I'd guess."

"That's what I think too."

Anastasia and Otto looked at each other, aligning their opinions.
What the two of them had brought up were the members of the Pleiades Battalion, who were being counted on as an unusual but promising defensive force for the citadel city.
The black-haired boy who had struck up a relationship with Vincent that bordered on a miraculous kind of friendship, and the group he represented, had lent no small hand — one, two roles worth — to supporting the refugees, thanks to their strong sense of solidarity and inexplicable combat strength.
At present, the boy who represented them had gone with Vincent to the imperial capital, and leading the group in his place was Gustav Morello — with the stiff, upright Multi-Arm Tribesman at the fore, the Sword Slaves were running riot.

"High morale, and high non-lethal capability too. I keep thinking it, but Natsuki-kun really is a genius at bringing the necessary thing to the necessary place."

"Yes, it's practically his one and only redeeming trait. Though I do wish he'd just stick to deliveries and bridge-building, and do something about his bad habit of charging straight into the most dangerous spot there is."

"But isn't that more Emilia-san's problem, really? If Emilia-san goes charging on ahead, Natsuki-kun's got no choice but to run after her."

Anastasia shook her head with a sigh of "Honestly," and Otto slumped his shoulders in agreement.
Watching this exchange and keeping an eye on the shifting web of relationships had its own charm, but spending too long on it drew a sharper look from the chancellor.
Serena cleared her throat, drawing everyone's attention, and said—

"As arranged beforehand, our combat policy remains, first and foremost, a siege — a defensive battle. If there were some single opponent we could take down to settle this, I'd want to factor in assassinating them, but..."

"As things stand, His Excellency and the others who've headed to the imperial capital are, in effect, that arrow of assassination."

"That's how it stands. —This is an all-out war."

At Serena's words, there was a sharp, crackling sense of the air drying out, and the heat in everyone's expressions shifted.
The lighthearted exchange from just a moment before came to an end; from here on, it was a world in which everyone would have to give their all, to the death.

Officers and soldiers of the Imperial Army, warriors of the rebel forces, the Sword Slaves of the Pleiades Battalion, the People of Shudrak, and even the Kingdom's collaborators and the envoy of the city-state — a battle fought with every single asset that existed.
And as the first gate to be passed through—

"I'll be counting on you then, envoy of the city-state."

"Sure, leave it to me. —As far as I can remember, she's never once let me down."

——In the wars of the Vollachian Empire, where does it differ most from other nations?

Setting aside things like the Imperial soldiers' mindset or the sheer strength of spirit with which they threw themselves into war, the answer is simple and clear: the presence or absence of aerial forces.
The technique of Flying Dragon handling, found nowhere else, and the Empire's unique abundance of Flying Dragons, brought their advantage fully to bear in war as well.

It went without saying: the mere ability to fly through the sky gave an overwhelming advantage in war.
In fact, the walled city of Guaral, said to boast high defensive strength among cities of comparable size, had been reduced to ruin by a swarm of Flying Dragons led by the "Flying Dragon Marshal," Madelyn Eschart.
Merely having things dropped from a position beyond one's reach was enough to make a battle utterly one-sided.
That threat held true even for the citadel city of Garkla, ringed as it was by towering, towering walls.
However—

"Loose——!!"

Lined up along the city walls, bows drawn, the Shudrak loosed their arrows into the sky all at once.
An inescapable barrage of arrows blanketed the sky, and what took the brunt of that countless assault head-on was a swarm of undead Flying Dragons, ordered to strike the fortified city from above and closing in through the air.
Struck by merciless blows, their heads shattered, the undead Flying Dragons crumbled into dust one after another and fell to the earth.
Among them were a few that shrugged off a few hits and kept flying regardless—

"Finish it off!"
"Hope it comes crashing down, no~"

Struck by a shaft with a thickness and length incomparable to an ordinary arrow, such creatures met their end shattered to splinters in midair.
The Shudrak, who boasted unrivaled strength against aerial forces — the way they fought owed a great deal to the experience of that earlier crushing defeat at the walled city.
Without that defeat, the Shudrak would not have been able to fight the undead Flying Dragons here.

Giving meaning to every battle they'd fought, the People of Shudrak, too, had fought through the Empire's upheaval from the very start — the upheaval that had begun with Vincent Vollachia's exile.
As if answering their spirit in kind—

"Go get 'em! Pretty-face man!"

"I'll live up to your expectations. —Aru Clauzeria."

A graceful, elegant voice wove an incantation, and as the tip of the swung knight's sword traced a line across the sky, a rainbow light born chasing that trajectory blocked the undead Flying Dragons' path.
Contrary to its beauty, the rainbow's brilliant light became a barrier rejecting everything before it.

"Different from when they were still buds, this is the flicker of my ladies now come into full bloom."

For scarcely longer than the blink of an eye, that brilliant rainbow light covered the sky above the citadel city — yet the scale and breadth of that rainbow was enough to shield fully half of the sprawling citadel city.
At that off-the-charts offensive and defensive power, the Imperial soldiers fighting on the front lines, along with those posted to defend the city from the walls, were left speechless — and then couldn't help but draw courage from how reassuring it was.

Feeling that surge in morale against his skin, the knight who had conjured the rainbow — Julius Juukulius — let the hem of his kimono flutter as he looked up at the sky once more.
The young man, a white scar beneath his left eye only adding to his keen good looks, faced what needed doing without a trace of hesitation in his gaze, even in this battle he'd joined out of duty. Watching the sky and the earth where the dead pressed ever closer, he trusted in the fierce fight being waged by the friend who had gone on beyond it, and said—

"—I shall fulfill my own role. Do give it your all as well, Subaru."
