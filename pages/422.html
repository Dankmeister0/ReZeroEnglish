Arc 6 Chapter 20: "Shaula ≠ Sage = Flugel"

—The great "Great Sage" Flugel.

At Shaula's declaration, delivered with her ample chest thrust out and an open, guileless smile, Subaru and the others all exchanged glances, and a delicate atmosphere descended over the group.

"Huh? What's with that reaction? Did I say something weird?"

"No, well, I mean, right?"

At the less-than-favorable reaction from Subaru and the others, Shaula blinked, her eyes going round in bewilderment. It was unkind to her, confident as she was that there was nothing at all strange about what she'd said, but the feelings that name—"Flugel"—stirred up in Subaru and the others were, to put it mildly, complicated.

The "Great Sage" Flugel. The title attached to his head was unfortunately a different one, but Subaru had heard that name before. It belonged to a person with whom, ever since being summoned to this other world, Subaru's fate had crossed paths exactly once. Granted, he'd never actually met the man in person—he simply owed him a one-sided debt. After all, it was thanks to that man's achievements that Subaru had once had his life saved.

"Flugel's Tree… you mean the Flugel who—that Flugel, right?"

At the keyword Subaru spoke, everyone but Shaula ducked their chins in acknowledgment. Meili, however, apparently hadn't heard of it, and tilted her head in question. "Whaaat's that?"

"Never heard of it. Taiju—that means tree, right?"

"That's right. Ah, um, in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, there's a place called the Lifaus Plains, and there was this absolutely enormous tree growing there, called Flugel's Tree. I mean, it was so huge it looked like it might touch the clouds—the kind of thing that really tickles a guy's fancy."

"Ohh, really? Hearing you talk about it like that makes me want to see it."

"Sorry. I'm the one who cut it down."

"Mister, you're so mean!"

Subaru's rapid-fire crushing of Meili's wish earned him treatment as something less than human.

Strictly speaking, the felling of the great tree wasn't Subaru's responsibility alone. That said, it was Subaru who'd proposed cutting it down and putting it to use, so if the question came down to who bore the heaviest share of the blame, well, it was still Subaru.

—Flugel's Tree had been the trump card in the Subjugation of the White Whale a year ago.

In subduing the "Mist" Witchbeast, the White Whale, the existence of a great tree that surpassed even that giant beast's body had proven immensely useful. In the end, they'd felled the tree and pinned the White Whale beneath it, sealing its movements and delivering the finishing blow—truly the decisive stroke.

"I always felt kind of bad about it, toward this Flugel guy who apparently planted it… but I never imagined I'd hear his name again, of all places, here."

"That's the spot we went to see the stump, isn't it, after the commendations for defeating the White Whale. They said the wood that got cut down was used for all sorts of things…"

"These days it remains only as the 'Flugel's Tree Ruins,' with just the stump left standing. It's protected as an important historical site, but it will be several centuries before it returns to what it once was."

At Subaru's exclamation, Emilia and Julius chimed in about what had happened afterward. Right after the subjugation of the White Whale, there had been the defeat of "Sloth," on top of the Great Sin Archbishop's attack that had drawn in Rem and Crusch, one thing piling on another. On top of that, the Emilia Camp had immediately been thrown into chaos by the incident centered on the "Sanctuary"—but the commendations came after that whole string of upheavals had settled.

The main topic there had been the achievements against the White Whale and "Sloth," but naturally, compensation for the resulting damages had also been discussed. The effective use of the felled timber from Flugel's Tree, and the protection of the remaining stump, were part of that. Incidentally, the White Whale's carcass had had no use—its massive body had been incinerated at considerable expense of manpower, and only the skull had been presented to the royal castle as proof of its defeat.

In any case, setting aside what became of the tree, the crucial matter was Flugel himself.

"Come to think of it, I did hear he was called the 'Sage' Flugel."

"But he's a 'Sage' whose actual accomplishments are pretty unclear. …Which, come to think of it, makes it kind of strange that he was treated as a 'Sage' to begin with, I suppose."

"Judging by achievements alone, it's true that his results fall short of the title 'Sage.' Either he was remarkably good at publicizing his own accomplishments, or… he's like Barusu, that way."

"When have I EVER exaggerated my own accomplishments!"

Subaru grew thoroughly displeased at the unwarranted assessment. Ram, meanwhile, kept a perfectly cool expression. As she listened to the exchange, it was Anastasia who said, "I see," nodding to herself as though she'd worked something out.

"True enough, goin' by what's been passed down, this Flugel fella might not quite measure up to bein' called a 'Sage'… but seein' as how Shaula-san, who was treated as the real 'Sage,' turned out to be a person like this, well, maybe that explains it, don't it?"

"Explains what, exactly?"

"The achievements that got passed down are the opposite… nn, no, that's not quite right either. Not so much opposite as skewed. Mm, might be more likely they were deliberately pinned on someone else."

"You mean, Flugel-san made it look like the things he did were things Shaula-san did?"

At Anastasia's speculation, Emilia's eyes went wide with surprise. Anastasia nodded at her understanding, then turned back toward Shaula.

"That's the theory I'm eyeballin', anyway—what do you think, Shaula-san? Was your Master the sort who'd do somethin' like that?"

"Hmm, well, that's a hard question, I don't really get the tricky stuff."

"You…"

"No, no, no! Hold on, don't jump to conclusions! There's more, I swear!"

At the unreliable answer, Subaru narrowed his eyes, and Shaula shook her head as if abashed. Then she raised both hands, opening and closing her palms meaninglessly, and said,

"Honestly, there's a lot about what Master was thinking that even I don't get. But Master wasn't really the type who liked standing out. So, pinning the awkward rumors on me and slipping away—that kinda seems like something Master would do, I think."

"I'd think a person who doesn't want to stand out wouldn't go planting a tree named 'Flugel's Tree' in the first place, but…"

"Subaru, that's a misunderstanding. Flugel's Tree wasn't a great tree from the moment it was planted. It grew into that magnificent stature over the course of many years. If the name of the person who planted it had simply been recorded somewhere, it wouldn't be strange at all for later generations to start calling it that, and for the name to stick."

"…Can't argue with that."

At Julius's correction, Subaru pursed his lips in acceptance. Indeed—what had originally been just an ordinary bit of tree-planting had, over the long years, grown into that magnificent great tree. At some point people must have thought, "We ought to give it a name," and naturally arrived at, "Then let's name it after the man who planted it." Even so, it did feel like something of a slip on Flugel's part, leaving his name behind like that.

"If he wanted to stay hidden, you'd think he'd avoid leaving the name 'Flugel' lying around in the first place. Was he surprisingly careless?"

"I read in a book—the reason they knew it was Flugel's name, I think it was because near the top of the great tree, there was something carved that said 'Flugel Was Here.'"

"That's way beyond careless! What is he, a kid on a school trip?!"

Subaru was stunned by this episode of self-display, far beyond what he'd imagined. He recalled having half-wanted to do something similar when he'd been near the great tree himself, but actually going through with it was another matter entirely. And besides, at the time Flugel had left that mark, the great tree surely hadn't been a great tree yet.

"So that name took on a life of its own, and in the end, the 'Sage' Flugel came down through history as a mysterious great figure about whom nothing was known beyond the fact that he'd planted a tree."

"So there wasn't even solid confirmation that Flugel was the one who planted it!?"

"And now, after all this time, it's been proven true by the mouth of a different 'Sage.' Thinking about it that way… hm. Being present for the moment a gap in history gets filled in—it does make my heart beat a little faster."

"Don't go sounding like a history nerd…"

Having learned the truth behind centuries of historical fact, Julius seemed somehow moved. Julius tended to have deep knowledge when it came to magic, or rather, tended to grow talkative when explaining it—but perhaps there was a knowledge-nerd side to him as well. The suspicion Subaru had harbored about him from before grew a little more suspicious, and he felt his spirits sink.

"But even if we find out that the 'Sage' of ancient times was actually Flugel… that doesn't really change anything major, does it?"

With everyone's views on Flugel now more or less aligned, Emilia looked around at everyone's faces, a little uncertain. Subaru nodded in agreement. As Emilia said, the fact that the true credit for a past great achievement belonged to Flugel didn't, in the current situation, carry much weight. —Or so it should have been.

"No, Emilia-tan. Actually, there's a huge problem."

"Eh?"

"Think about it, right? Originally, our goal was to hear all kinds of things from this supposedly all-knowing 'Sage.' But the rumored 'Sage' turns out to be someone else entirely, and all that's left is this one here, who's obviously got an empty head, which means—"

"Aah, Master, please don't shake your head. It makes a rattling sound."

Shaking Shaula's shoulder produced that playfully exaggerated reaction from her. Without mincing words, however you looked at it, she was about as far from all-knowing as could be.

"Our whole original objective can't be accomplished. —If there's no 'Sage,' then coming all the way here was for nothing."

"————"

At Subaru's conclusion, Emilia and the others fell silent. Watching their reactions, Subaru himself was gritting his teeth against the conclusion he'd just reached.

It had taken over a month, and as for Subaru, he'd already died four times. After overcoming that much hardship to reach the Pleiades Watchtower, all that remained was a guardian who was a "Sage" in name only. At this rate, they had nothing to bring back. The truth of history, the reality behind the "Sage"—none of that held any value for Subaru. All Subaru wanted was a means of saving the person who mattered most to him. That was all.

"Shaula. It's a long shot, but let me ask this much. Your Master… the real 'Sage,' Flugel—where is he?"

"If I said 'right in front of me right now'—I feel like I'd get yelled at! But I'm gonna answer anyway! The one standing right in front of me is Master Flugel!"

"Figured you'd say that."

At Shaula's declaration—looking like she might crumple but never quite crumpling—Subaru could only sigh. In fact, his inner state right now was desolate enough that he almost wanted to take his frustration out on the ever-cheerful Shaula. It was, one might say, a situation where the lead had run completely dry.

The light of the "Sage" had gone out, and they'd simply been thrown back to groping in the dark—that much was true, but the darkness after once glimpsing a dazzling light within it carries a different weight than darkness alone. Hope gives you the strength to keep walking, but when that hope is painted over, the darkness that follows can blanket a person's vision even blacker than if they'd never left the dark at all.

But then, to that Subaru—

"Subaru, listen. About that—actually, we're not completely stuck."

"Eh?"

A hand came to rest on Subaru's shoulder, and Emilia's voice reached his downcast profile. When he turned to face her, Emilia's violet eyes held a clear and certain hope. Chin drawn in, her hand still on Subaru's shoulder, Emilia turned to Shaula. "Isn't that right?" she continued.

"Up until now you haven't really told us much, but now that you're like this, don't you think you could tell us a bit more in detail?"

"Sure thing. I don't like Master being all down either. And besides, I've been alone for a really long time, so I wanna talk."

At Emilia's meaningfully pointed question, Shaula responded cheerfully, her attitude unchanged. Sitting cross-legged, she rocked her body back and forth, raised her right hand toward the sky, and pointed overhead.

"I already told the others a bit, but this is the very bottom of the tower. Sixth Floor, 'Asterope.' Going up, it's the fifth floor, 'Celaeno,' then the fourth floor, 'Alcyone,' and the very top is the first floor, 'Maia.'"

"You gave each floor its own name? Isn't that a hassle?"

"Master's the one who named 'em, y'know?"

"Not a taste I dislike, but that's not what I'm asking about right now. So?"

There was hope in Emilia's eyes, and the flow that encouraged it made Subaru's heart quicken. Perhaps sensing that shift in his expression, Shaula's cheeks loosened into a delighted, teasing grin. She thrust out her left hand—the opposite of the one pointing up—showing off all five fingers.

"Fifth floor, 'Celaeno,' is right above us—that's the floor with the entrance connecting to outside. Sixth floor, 'Asterope,' is below that, so it's actually underground. If you mess up and break something, you could end up buried alive, so it's best not to go around trying to break walls or anything."

"Incidentally, regarding the walls and floor—at a glance they look like stone, but the strength is anything but ordinary. I'll say up front that neither I, nor even Lady Emilia's magic, could leave so much as a scratch on them."

Julius wasn't the one who'd tried to break anything, presumably, but he added this supplement to Shaula's explanation. Subaru had no intention of destroying the tower to begin with, but nodded in acknowledgment anyway.

"Okay, so the fifth floor's the entrance, got it. What's above that?"

"Fourth floor, 'Alcyone,' is easy to get in and out of—it's kinda like my living space. I've made a real mess of it, doing whatever I want, so it's a little embarrassing if you stare too hard~"

"————"

"Master, your eyes are serious. Scary. …N-normally, this is where I keep watch over the sand dunes. And anyone who comes near the tower, I shoot 'em down, one after another, no exceptions!"

"So it really was you."

Half of it he'd already more or less known, but hearing it confirmed by her own testimony finally sealed his conviction. The white light from the Pleiades Watchtower that had killed Subaru twice on the dunes, and had also been one of the causes behind the team's split afterward—the culprit behind it was, indeed, Shaula.

"Because of that, we went through hell over here, you know. What are you gonna do about it?"

"What am I gonna do—I was just followin' Master's orders, y'know. And if you're gonna complain about that, you're basically pickin' a fight with what I've been doin' for the past few hundred years~"

When Subaru glared at her with a stern voice, Shaula scrunched up her face and shrank back defensively. There was no malice in that face, and, further, not a trace of remorse either. It wasn't so much that she lacked a sense of guilt—she simply had no concept of guilt to begin with. That wasn't an emotional deficiency, or that kind of problem at all—

"Subaru, it's pointless to say anything about this, I suppose. There's no guilt or sense of wrongdoing here to speak of. This was simply something she was ordered to do… interrogating a tool about how it was used is meaningless, I suppose."

"Yeah, yeah, I'm Master's tool! Good one, shorty!"

At Beatrice's ruthless assessment, Shaula beamed as if she'd found a kindred spirit. Her expressions and moods shifted so readily, and her understanding of her own current existence—Subaru's values were, most likely, simply different from Shaula's. That was probably why their conversations kept talking past each other. In short—

"Talking to you is exhausting."

"People used to tell me that a lot! Been a while since I heard it!"

"I see. I used to hear that a lot too, back in the day."

He still felt like he heard it sometimes even now, but Subaru let that point slide. In any case, even having learned the white light from the watchtower was Shaula's doing, he understood that no apology or reflection could be expected from her. If so, there was no point in cutting the conversation short. All that was left was to press on.

"The fourth floor is your living space… well, 'living space' sounds off too, your living quarters. So what's above that?"

"From the third floor, 'Taygeta,' up, it's the testing grounds. —Testing your right to enter the archive."

"…Archive?"

At that word, Subaru furrowed his brow and asked back. Shaula, paying no mind to the change in Subaru's questioning tone, nodded back easily. "Yep."

"An archive. From the third floor, 'Taygeta,' upward, there's a test, and an archive corresponding to it. As long as you meet the conditions to enter the archive, you're free to read whatever books are inside."

"What's written in the books inside?"

"Dunno."

"DUNNO!?"

After all that build-up, being brushed off with "dunno" was too much to bear. As Subaru's lips twisted, Shaula shook her head and hair in a reluctant "well, I mean~" gesture.

"I can't even read books and stuff, and I've never been told anything beyond 'guard the tower.'"

"That's also from Master… from Flugel?"

"Yep!"

Being proud of that gave Subaru nothing to say in response. Still, while Subaru could only tilt his head at Shaula's statement, Emilia and the others' expressions remained thoroughly serious. The hope in their eyes hadn't wilted; it remained just as it was.

"Subaru, the books in the archive—are their contents knowledge, I wonder?"

"Knowledge… not just the literal meaning of the word?"

At Beatrice's words, Subaru asked back the obvious question. What's written in books is, in most cases, either a story or knowledge. What one gains from a book ought to be either emotional resonance or the satisfaction of wisdom. And that wasn't quite what Beatrice meant to say.

On Subaru's lap, Beatrice shook her head. Her long ringlets swayed the same way, and as Subaru's eyes idly followed the motion, she said,

"This is a place where the knowledge recorded by the 'Sage' called all-knowing sleeps, it seems. The Pleiades Watchtower is apparently just the name used for it from outside, I suppose."

"That's right. If Master's come back inside, then this place returns to its original role. A great library where you can search for anything you want to know, anything you want to realize—Pleiades."

"So basically, third floor is beginner level. Second floor, intermediate. First floor, you get access to advanced-level information. And the 'tests' are like qualification exams for obtaining clearance—that about right?"

"Ohh, as expected of Master! Even I'm gettin' more and more lost following what you're sayin'! But I think that breakdown's probably OK!"

—The Great Library, Pleiades.

With the watchtower's name transformed into something rather more approachable—and, conversely, rather more suspicious—Subaru's group left the sixth floor and began climbing the stairs toward the upper level in question. The spiral staircase carried Subaru and the others upward as it wound around the tower's circumference, but with no handrail, the higher they climbed, the more it chilled the nerves.

"Scary."

"Subaru, if you're scared, want to hold hands? You're still recovering, so there's no telling if you might get dizzy and fall…"

As the sixth floor's ground grew distant and Giant and the dragon carriage shrank considerably smaller, Subaru peered down and caught his breath, prompting Emilia's offer. Honestly, he'd love to take that hand and savor the feel of her white fingers, but—

"Thanks, Emilia-tan. But I'm okay for now. The stairs have decent width, so it's not like I'll fall, and, well, I've got some impatience driving me too."

"Really? But if it gets hard, tell me right away, okay? If it comes down to it, I can even carry you piggyback."

"I see… let's not let it come down to that."

Being carried up the stairs on Emilia's back—the visual would be an absolute disaster, no question. Rather than resort to such an unmanly act, it would be better to owe Julius a favor.

"Something wrong?"

"That's a terrible option too, that's all. It's nothing. Let's keep going."

Noticing the gaze, Julius turned just his head back, and Subaru waved him off. Julius shrugged, then took Anastasia's hand again and resumed leading the way up the stairs.

At present, Subaru's group was making their way toward the tower's upper levels on foot, at an ordinary pace. It wasn't a journey they needed to rush, so despite the impatience gnawing at them, their speed was moderate. Julius and Anastasia led the way, with Ram and Emilia behind them, and Subaru and Beatrice walking behind those two. And bringing up the rear were Shaula and Meili—

"Nooo, Miss Naked Lady, don't shake me around so much, pleeease."

"Ehh, you're the one riding on someone's back and you're bein' all bossy, shorty~"

"But I'm tiiired. I've been walking this whole time, and I can't keep going up and down all these stairs."

"Just 'cause of that, why do I gotta carry— ah! Don't pull my hair!"

Noisy and rowdy, yes, but there was nothing sharp in the exchange. At the very back, Shaula and Meili made for a curious pair, with Shaula—of all people—carrying Meili piggyback up the stairs. It had started when they'd decided to head up, and Meili had whined about her tired legs; Shaula herself had then volunteered to carry her, so it was left as her own decision.

"Honestly, physically speaking, Shaula's probably the toughest one here…"

"No arguing that, I suppose. —Subaru, don't let your guard down. As harmless as she looks, there's no telling when she might bare her fangs, I suppose."

"Her? From that vibe?"

"If she finds out Subaru isn't Flugel, there's no telling how she'll react, I suppose."

"That's…"

At Beatrice's low-voiced warning, Subaru fell into a hesitant silence. It wasn't as though he hadn't considered it, but it wasn't a problem he could afford to set outside his thoughts, either. In truth, the reason Shaula was so friendly toward the group—no, toward Subaru specifically—was, in the end, nothing more than her mistaken belief that Master equaled Subaru.

And Subaru and the others knew full well that the connection was a complete misunderstanding. They'd denied it outright, in words, more than once already, but Shaula only laughed it off and refused to engage. Or rather, "refused to engage" wasn't quite right—it simply didn't cause her the slightest discomfort to hear it. In any case, it never got through to her. That could be taken as a kind of guarantee that Shaula's attitude wouldn't change—or—

"You mean there's no telling what might trigger it to collapse."

"If she ever does turn hostile, there's no doubt it'll be a serious struggle, I suppose. Emilia and that knight… Julius included, it would take all of us together with Betty and Subaru, I suppose."

"Shaula's strength, huh."

Glancing back, Subaru observed Shaula roughhousing playfully with Meili. A figure so friendly and unguarded, out of proportion to her appearance. —Given how their first meeting and subsequent exchanges had gone, she was a difficult opponent to judge in all sorts of ways. Still, honestly speaking, while Subaru hadn't yet settled on how to feel about Shaula, he didn't harbor any particularly negative feelings toward her, if he was being truthful. Even though it was a case of mistaken identity, being treated with such open friendliness made it hard to feel anything but bewilderment, let alone hatred.

But that feeling of goodwill and a lack of alertness were two things that shouldn't be conflated.

"————"

Watching Shaula, his eyes drifting over that unguarded frame, Subaru thought it over. By her own admission, it had been her hurling light down from the tower at Subaru and the others on the dunes. The speed of that light's rapid-fire barrage and the precision of its aim had been extraordinary, more than enough to suggest just how formidable Shaula's abilities truly were. Subaru still had no idea whether that had been magic or something else entirely.

And further—the final moment beneath the sand sea. In the hazy, fading edges of his consciousness just before it cut out, what lingered was the image of Shaula colliding with that grotesque, centaur-like thing. That monster—something that looked like nothing so much as unreasonableness or absurdity given physical form—had been overwhelmed and blown away, effortlessly, by a stronger power. Subaru had seen it with his own eyes.

He didn't want to admit it, but he couldn't picture himself winning against Shaula in direct combat. Regulus, or Petelgeuse—those two would honestly be far less troublesome opponents by comparison.

"…Thinking about those guys puts me in a bad mood. Well, of course it does, but this feels more personally unpleasant somehow."

"I don't know what Subaru's thinking, but caution is warranted, I suppose."

"Yeah. In that case… might be faster to just ask."

"Eh, it is, indeed."

Beatrice's dim-witted-sounding exclamation and Subaru's turning his whole body around happened simultaneously. Setting his feet on the stairs backward, Subaru faced Shaula and Meili, stacked directly behind him. Shaula stopped at the motion, tilting her head.

"What's up, Master? Don't tell me even you got tired? I don't mind carryin' you too, but my back's occupied, so it'd have to be a princess carry?"

"That'd make for a hell of a visual on its own, so I'll pass. Ah, sorry to interrupt while you and Meili are having a nice time with the ponytail…"

"It's not a ponytail, it's a Scorpion Tail."

"————"

"Scorpion Tail."

For whatever reason she was so particular about it, Shaula repeated herself to the silent Subaru. Pressing a hand to the bundle of her ponytail, Shaula thrust her face forward.

"Scorpion Tail…"

"Fine, I got it! What's with that specific hang-up! Anyway, sorry to interrupt while you're getting along with Meili, but I've got something I want to ask."

"Mm! I'll listen to anything Master's got to say! I'm happy Master's talkin' to me this much~"

Shaula swished her ponytail—correction, Scorpion Tail—happily around. As a result, the Meili on her back was catching collateral damage from the tail's swings. It was surprisingly soft and pleasant, as Subaru already knew from personal experience, but that aside—

"You listen to what I say, right?"

"No lewd stuff, okay?"

"Don't confirm that first thing. Way to kill the setup."

"You're one to talk, Master, killing setups left and right. Takes one to know one."

Faced with Shaula's easy composure in the back-and-forth, Subaru found himself flustered, unable to seize control of the pace. Normally, most opponents fell out of rhythm once they got dragged into Subaru's conversational pace, and from there he'd feel out an opening to steer the talk—that was his usual method, but it wasn't working here. This particular kind of difficulty was different from dealing with abnormal types like the Great Sin Archbishops. With them, the difficulty was that conversation didn't function at all to begin with. Shaula was different.

Somehow, he felt at a disadvantage—being known by someone he himself didn't know. As if he and this Flugel person really did have that much in common.

"Anyway, it's a request and a question. Answer honestly and straightforwardly as much as you can."

"A request doesn't sound like you, Master. You should just order me around."

"I can't quite manage that bossy a tone. So, the question is… you attacked us from the tower, right? What was that about?"

"A sniper attack to keep pests from gettin' close to the tower. Hell's Snipe."

"…What?"

"Hell's Snipe."

Said cheerfully in loanwords, Subaru's face soured. I mean, well, it was a name he understood, sort of.

"Ahh, but now that I think about it, good thing Hell's Snipe didn't hit Master. If the Dimension Gate hadn't gotten cancelled, I mighta kept flingin' attacks 'til it hit you."

"Wait wait wait wait, too many new words! Dimension?"

"Dimension Gate. A little trick to keep people from reaching the tower."

From the way Shaula described it, Subaru understood that this "Dimension Gate" was the spatial distortion that had cut off the path to the flower field via that "sand wind," and had ultimately been undone by the effect of E.M.T.

"But hey, thanks to that, we figured out Master was really Master, so all's well that ends well. If it'd hit you, even you'd probably have gotten mad at me, right, Master?"

"Ah, yeah, well, I wonder. Would just getting mad have covered it."

In fact, he'd been hit and died twice from it, so he wasn't sure he'd even have made it to the "getting mad" stage. It was rare not to feel rage boiling up when face to face with the very culprit who'd killed him. Part of the reason was, in a sense, knowing this had been an accident, and that holding Shaula accountable for it would be pointless.

"But if somethin' like that had hit him, wouldn't Mister have just died? If that happened, I don't think it'd be a question of gettin' mad or not."

Meili, riding on Shaula's back, cut in on behalf of the half-resigned Subaru. At her words, Shaula burst into laughter, a sound with absolutely nothing ladylike about it.

"What are you talkin' about, shorty. There's no way my Master would die from somethin' like that. To begin with, Master's some incomprehensible person who I don't even know whether dies or doesn't die."

"But but, the sand earthworm thing got attacked a ton and died, so…"

"Don't care about worms or bears. My Master doesn't die—that's the important thing. —If he'd died, then he just wouldn't have been Master, that's all."

Grinning easily, Shaula gazed at Subaru with delighted eyes. Faced with that innocent smile, a chill ran down Subaru's spine. An innocent, childlike trust, unguarded to the point of recklessness. The faith she placed in Flugel was, more than he'd imagined, an ideal built firm and unshakable at the very core of who she was. Just as Beatrice had warned—if that ever came into conflict with the present Subaru—

"Also, sorry it took so long to come pick you up at the Sand Palace. While I had Master's other group in the tower, you all went on ahead deeper in, so I got kinda flustered."

"Ah, no, that's fine… wait, did you just say Sand Palace?"

"Yep. That place is seriously dangerous, better not go in. Even for Master, gettin' close without all the keys gathered would be reckless. The Hungry Horse King's wanderin' around there too."

"The Hungry Horse King… you mean that horse-like monster?"

Sand Palace, keys, Hungry Horse King—there were a lot of concerning words, but Subaru latched onto the last one, the Hungry Horse King. That was presumably the centaur-thing in question. At the question, Shaula nodded.

"That one wasn't around when Master was here, so I bet it was a shock. It basically just wanders around inside the Sand Palace, so you won't run into it unless you go there. Any that occasionally wander outside, I take out without mercy."

"There's a lot of those things?"

"Loads."

At Shaula's unhesitating affirmation, Subaru felt genuine, deep disgust well up from the bottom of his heart. Setting aside the Hungry Horse King's—the centaur's—combat strength, its appearance and nature carried a revulsion beyond words. He'd hoped it might have been a special, one-off specimen, but that apparently wasn't the case. The word "loads" made that all too clear.

"The Hungry Horse King… I've never heard that name either. Might wanna see it too~"

"Don't! That's not something for kids to look at…"

"M-mister…"

At Meili's careless remark, Subaru cautioned her almost sternly. It might strike a chord with her Witchbeast-collector sensibilities, but trying to make contact with that thing just to fill out an encyclopedia entry was not something he could recommend. At Subaru's words, Shaula nodded in agreement as well.

"I think you'd better not, too. The Hungry Horse King's a Witchbeast built around the concept of its horns regrowing endlessly, so none of the usual rules for Witchbeasts apply to it. See it, kill it—that's justice."

"Booo, fiiine."

Pursing her lips in a gesture fitting her age, Meili reined in her curiosity. Relieved at that, Shaula said "So, anyway," turning the conversation back to Subaru.

"Master, is that all you wanted to talk about?"

"Ah, for now… no, that's not all."

"—?"

Head still tilted, Shaula kept her posture open, as if to say "bring it on." Continuing to be wary of someone who had opened up her heart to him this much felt genuinely dishonest. But no matter how much it stung, there were times one had to steel one's heart regardless. If saying it once could change something, then it was best to say it.

"Shaula, this is a request from me. Don't harm me, or my companions."

"————"

"Master's order was: attack anyone who comes near the tower, right? We're inside now, so we're outside that scope. Which means there's no more need to attack. Don't harm us. Ever."

"————"

At Subaru's words, pressed once more for emphasis, Shaula narrowed her eyes. Looking at those narrowed eyes, he noticed their color was a deep, rich green. Shaula, who had kept her expressive face full to the brim without a gap, had for the first time let her smile fade, as if lost in thought. A brief silence followed, and Subaru, holding his breath in the strange tension, finally couldn't hold it any longer and let out a breath. Then—

"Mm, OK. Got it, added to Master's new orders, all locked in~"

"————"

"Master?"

The moment it was accepted, Shaula's face broke back into its usual loose grin. Subaru, unable to keep up with the sudden shift, blinked in surprise, and Shaula brought her face close enough that he could feel her breath. At that close range, their eyes met, and in that green gaze there was no trace of deceit or distrust to be found. At least, not as far as Subaru could tell.

"Is that alright?"

"Nothin' alright or not alright about it. It's what Master says. And it's not even a hard thing to do. Non-violence, non-compliance."

"You're complying by following the order, aren't you."

"My body can be free to command, but you won't take my heart!"

"Ugh, annoying…!"

Subaru flicked Shaula's forehead, her expression far too pleased with herself, and pushed her back. As Shaula recoiled with an "Owww," the Meili on her back panicked, but nothing came of it. In any case, it seemed Subaru's request had been accepted rather easily. There was no telling how far its effect would extend, but at the very least—

"As long as I don't betray your expectations, the promise holds, huh."

"That's the only condition, so it should be fine, I suppose. Subaru betrays predictions, but not expectations, I suppose."

"I appreciate the incredibly high praise and all, but in this case, isn't the part about the 'expectations' I'm not allowed to betray way too vague? What am I even supposed to do to be Flugel? Should I sprout wings or something? The German-sounding kind?"

Incidentally, to Subaru, "Flugel" brought to mind the German word for "wing." He doubted that was actually where the name came from, but even that was suspicious in its own right. After all, this whole Flugel business, Shaula's name included, reeked of something fishy.

"Oh, right, Shaula. One more question, just one last thing."

"What's uuup~"

Wearing a face utterly drained of tension, Shaula was having her cheeks kneaded by Meili. Still facing away from her, Subaru spoke with deliberate casualness.

"Maia, Electra, Taygeta, Alcyone, Celaeno, Asterope."

"…Subaru?"

At the words Subaru murmured, Beatrice beside him gave a puzzled look. Heard purely as sound, it was a string of unfamiliar-sounding words. Of course, about four of them he'd only just heard moments ago, but they weren't the kind of thing easily memorized well enough to recite—not if they were truly unfamiliar.

"Going from top to bottom, all of these are the names of the floors of the Pleiades Watchtower—or rather, the Great Library Pleiades, right?"

"Yep~"

"Then where's Merope?"

"————"

At Subaru's question, Shaula fell silent again. But this silence was different from the earlier, thoughtful one—this was the silence of surprise, of being caught off guard. There was the faint sound of an indrawn breath, and Subaru judged that he'd touched on something confirmed true.

"Subaru, what did you just ask? What's Merope?"

"The name of the last of a certain set of seven sisters. If it's Pleiades, there ought to be seven."

From the first floor down to the sixth, six floors had been assigned six names. But the motif being drawn on was originally seven sisters—there should be a seventh, named floor somewhere.

"A seventh floor… or if not that, a zeroth floor."

"Floor Zero. Master's the one who named it, so of course. …Only, it's a place that came into being after Master disappeared, so I shouldn't even know where it is."

At Subaru's speculative guess, Shaula answered in a hoarse voice. Beatrice's presence beside him betrayed surprise, but Subaru licked his dry lips and narrowed his eyes. There was no sense of achievement in it, no feeling of having uncovered something hidden. After all, to Subaru, this was practically something that had never been hidden at all.

"If the first floor is advanced level, then Floor Zero is super-advanced? To get in, you'd…"

"—No, you can't."

As Subaru began to ask how to enter, Shaula cut him off quickly. At the sharpness in her tone, Subaru glanced back, but Shaula's expression hadn't changed. Still that same smile, that same trusting gaze. Only now, there was a faint trace of loneliness at the corners of her eyes.

"The conditions aren't met yet. Master came back partway along the road, to see me, and that's enough. So, Floor Zero's a no."

"————"

Her tone of voice hadn't changed from before, but there was, instead, a strangely rigid wall he could feel behind it. It carried a precariousness that threatened to shake even the very foundation of the promise they'd made just minutes ago, and it strongly warned Subaru of the danger of pressing further. And so, Subaru gave up pursuing the matter further, here and now.

"Got it. I won't push for anything unreasonable. Just—hold to that promise from before, please."

"Got it, will do~. I'll keep it, super keep it~"

At once, Shaula's face broke back open, delighting as though she'd already forgotten the exchange that had just passed. Listening to that bright, cheerful voice at his back, Subaru let out a long, deep breath.

"Subaru, tell me if it's hard, okay?"

"Mm, I'm fine. Lots to think about, though."

At Beatrice's considerate words, Subaru smiled faintly and gently stroked her head. That left Beatrice unable to say anything more, but this too was a kind of ritual for Subaru to settle himself.

The abnormality of Flugel, brought into sharp relief through his conversation with Shaula. It was nothing complicated, really. He, too, was the same as Subaru.

Subaru. Al. Hoshin. And Flugel. Bringing knowledge into this world that shouldn't exist here, and leaving it behind as if flaunting it, ostentatiously. There was no longer any room for doubt—Flugel was a person from the same homeland as Subaru.

"Several hundred years ago, huh."

Letting his thoughts drift across that long, long stretch of time, Subaru scratched roughly at his head. In this other world, what had he thought, what had he considered, what had he aimed for, what had he sought—

A man who abandoned the name of "Sage" to go plant a tree—what had he wished for? Had he gone on living in this world, wondering that all the while? And then, to that Subaru's back—

"Master."

"Hm?"

Shaula called out to him in an easy, familiar tone. Subaru didn't stop walking, only glancing briefly back over his shoulder, but Shaula looked up at him and gave a small, bashful smile. Her expression was one of truly heartfelt joy, and she said,

"Welcome back again, Master. —The return of the 'Sage' Flugel—this Shaula has been awaiting it, from the bottom of her heart. …Yep."
