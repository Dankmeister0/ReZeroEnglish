Arc 5 Chapter 52: "The Stars and the Sin Archbishops"

Collapse came surging in. Blood rained down, and every single drop became a savage, destructive hand tearing the city apart.

Wherever a drop of blood touched, cohesion was lost more helplessly than paper meeting a blade. The spreading destruction brought buildings down, and the aftershocks of collapse rippled outward, toppling the cityscape one street at a time.

"Ooooooohh—!!"

"—!"

Knowing full well it was pointless, Subaru wrung the breath from his lungs and kept running with everything he had. Beside him Emilia ran just as hard, silver hair streaming, lips pressed tight. But the picturesque City of Water was famous for its waterways, crisscrossing the city in every direction.

Which meant, in short, that even securing a straight, shortest escape route was in practice difficult. Ahead of the two runners spread a great canal, while behind them the destruction threatened to swallow them whole.

"Oh, hell!"

"It's a gamble, but—Subaru, grab on!"

At the shock of finding their escape route cut off, Subaru cried out, and Emilia made an instant decision. The moment Subaru grabbed her outstretched hand without hesitation, cold air blanketed the surrounding atmosphere. Emilia's brief incantation, and at the same time, countless tiny points of light rose into view.

It was the simultaneous use of Magic drawing on Emilia's own mana, and Spiritual Arts borrowing the power of minor spirits.

"—Everyone, please!"

At Emilia's command to the minor spirits, blue light burst from the points of light and ran along the ground. An instant later, the ground beneath their running feet turned white in a flash, and in the blink of an eye a world of silver spread out before them. The street froze over, and the soles of Subaru's shoes slid wildly, nearly wringing a scream out of him. Still in that stumbling posture, his whole body was suddenly yanked forward.

"Whoa!? Emilia-tan, you're amazing! Genius!"

"It's hard to control, so don't let go of my hand!"

Subaru looked up to find his left hand joined with Emilia's right. And in her raised left hand, Emilia was gripping an ice pillar that had shot out ahead of them. She'd frozen the ground and launched an ice pillar, borrowing the magic's propulsive force to boost their escape speed. Even more astonishing was the icy course the minor spirits had carved out.

At the edge of the great canal ahead, a curve formed like a ski jump ramp, and Subaru and Emilia sailed off it at full acceleration, soaring clean over the canal.

"Yeeeehaw!"

An ice track formed on the far side of the canal too, and they touched down and kept skating—Subaru marveled honestly at Emilia's skill.

"Nice, Emilia-tan! You're so amazing I'm falling for you all over again!"

"But I can't think how to stop! What do I do?"

"Eh?"

Emilia had already let go of the ice pillar, but even the remaining momentum alone was enough that a full-on collision with a wall would mean serious damage. Emilia's ice magic couldn't conjure some convenient cushion soft enough to absorb the impact for two people. Even as they spoke, a wall loomed ahead of them. On the verge of collision, Subaru felt Emilia's hand squeeze his tight, and made an instant decision.

"Emilia-tan! Set up a curve!"

"A-a curve?"

"Like a wall that curves around gently! In a big loop!"

At Subaru's urgent voice, Emilia's magic obediently complied. A gentle curve rose up in front of the sliding pair, and their bodies followed it around, avoiding the collision in one great arc.

"Keep the curve going, don't let it break! Loop it! Loop it!"

"L-loop it, loop it—!"

Round and round they went, the icy curve forming an unbroken wall the whole way. Seen from directly above, it traced a shape like a coiled mosquito-repellent spiral, an ice wall built as they went, and by the time the two of them reached the center, their momentum had finally bled away and they came to a natural stop.

"Whew, managed it by wasting a ton of Emilia-tan's mana…"

"Never mind that—the attack from before!"

The moment Subaru caught his breath, Emilia clapped her hands and shattered the ice wall completely. Watching the pulverized shards turn to particles of mana, Subaru narrowed his eyes at the traces of the destruction bearing down on them, a chill running down his spine.

Centered on the tower where Regulus's assault had begun, the city's landscape had utterly transformed. The devastation at the center, which had taken the brunt of the bloody onslaught, was especially horrific. The circle of destruction grew patchier the farther out it spread, but even so, far fewer buildings retained anything like their original shape than didn't. It was, once again, an attack power beyond all reckoning.

The attack had reached even in Subaru and Emilia's direction, crossing the great canal to their side. That it hadn't quite reached them was thanks not merely to luck and a desperate escape—no.

"Reinhard!"

There was no one atop the building where Regulus had been standing moments before. In its place, thick smoke billowed nearby, and the sound of tremendous destruction echoed through the air.

Tearing apart the Water Dragon's corpse and scattering its blood, Regulus sneered.

Below, he could see two small figures desperately fleeing along the streets. How diminutive. How petty. How utterly trivial.

Sneering, he savored the sight of the city being trampled by the scattered blood, waiting eagerly for the moment that destruction would catch up to the fleeing pair. A rain of blood, fitting for the deaths of a whore and a rapist.

"Scatter! Fly apart! You villains who toyed with my heart!"

"—Sorry, but that's not going to happen."

The instant he'd draped his victory and rage in words, a voice sounded right at his ear, and he flinched. Turning, he saw at the edge of his vision red hair that seemed to blaze brighter, as if fanned by the wind.

"You're ridiculous, you know that! How devotedly you keep interfering in a man's romance!"

"If your methods were honest, if you respected the other party's wishes, and if you could promise to withdraw gracefully upon being refused, I wouldn't be averse to cheering you on."

To Regulus's furious shout, Reinhard responded, still mocking, still smiling. His endlessly unshaken composure was infuriating enough, but what truly seized Regulus's mind in that moment was a baffling suspicion—namely, the state of the leg Reinhard had used to leap here in a single bound.

That right leg, surely, had exploded near the shin only moments ago. It hadn't been severed outright, but it would hardly have been an exaggeration to say it was hanging on by a strip of skin below the ankle—nowhere near a state fit for combat, let alone walking. That he had recovered from that state meant—

"How thoroughly detestable. Not just swordsmanship, but healing magic too, right up your sleeve? With every gift life could bestow more generously than on other men, how many hearts have you trampled underfoot? Crushing others' spirits without even trying—it must feel wonderful, doesn't it!"

"Let me clear up just one of your misconceptions."

Twisting his body in midair, Reinhard's form wound the wind about him with a roar. The roundhouse kick he unleashed cleaved the air and struck the Water Dragon's corpse Regulus had been swinging around, smashing what was already a mere lump of flesh into splinters—

"Wha—!?"

"I can't use healing magic—I can't use any kind of magic at all. The minor spirits of the air simply took pity on me and rushed to heal my leg."

The leg that seemed poised to obliterate the corpse with its momentum instead used a twist of the ankle to scoop the Water Dragon's remains up out of Regulus's grip. With that same deft footwork, the Dragon's body was handled without violence and tossed gently onto the roof of a half-collapsed building.

And then—

"Good timing. —Next verification: executing Plan J."

"Guhack!?"

Just as he ground his teeth at Reinhard's self-righteous little act of mercy, the hilt of the Dragon Sword slammed into the side of Regulus's head again. Struck down, his body was knocked from atop the tower.

Sent hurtling diagonally toward the ground, Regulus heard, once again right at his ear—

"Let me test something."

"—!?"

Leaping like a bullet along the same trajectory, Reinhard's body caught up with him. Midway through the fall, Regulus's inverted leg was seized, and he was swept up into the motion of Reinhard twisting his own body. Reinhard then leapt off in the same direction as the fleeing Subaru and the others, dragging Regulus along. A ferocious gale, an acceleration so violent that any ordinary person's leg would have been torn clean off at the grip.

"Just what are you—!"

"Nothing particularly special."

Saying so, Reinhard came to a stop and swung Regulus's body upward. It was the handling of a child roughly playing with a doll's leg. Just as Regulus's fury was about to explode at the treatment, the details of what Reinhard was doing became clear.

Swinging Regulus's body around, Reinhard slammed it into the falling rain of blood. The rain, tainted by Regulus's own influence, was blood that could bring down even buildings of stone. If Regulus's ability was a power that granted invincibility to whatever it touched, then perhaps—Reinhard must have reasoned—it could even break through the very invincibility protecting Regulus's own body. If so, the thought was shallow.

"You think I'd be hit by my own attack? I don't know how richly gifted you were born, but enough with the bad habit of underestimating everyone else. There's no way someone like me gets taken down by such an idiotic method!"

"No effect here either—"

The drops of blood that touched Regulus's body simply became ordinary droplets and bounced off him. Naturally. The priority was different.

At the same moment, Reinhard reflexively released his grip on Regulus's leg. A sharp instinct, that man. Had he stayed carelessly in contact any longer, Regulus would have mangled that palm of his until it could never grip a sword again.

The effect of being swung around vanished. His body landed on the street below, and once more Regulus faced Reinhard. Wary, Reinhard narrowed his eyes.

"It seems I can't touch you again."

"You've got a good nose for it, but do you want to feel that same pain again?"

"Next time I'll be mindful of breath and gaze as well. If there are other precautions I should take, I'd be glad to hear them."

"Disappear from in front of me, right now!!"

Stepping forward, Regulus thrust out both hands and lunged at Reinhard. Reinhard evaded with an incredible burst of speed, circling wide around him. Keeping a considerable distance, he began landing strikes with hit-and-run thrusts of the Dragon Sword.

"You just keep skittering around, don't you…!"

"I'm quite powerless against problems that can't be solved by cutting. I'm ashamed of myself."

"You haven't even drawn that sword once, have you!"

Regulus flailed his hands wildly at the darting Reinhard. But such a careless attack could never reach the hero Reinhard. If anything, Reinhard's blue eyes stayed sharp even against Regulus's cunning ploys of blowing his breath at him. Neither side landing a decisive blow, the standoff continued.

"Ah?"

But then, out of nowhere, an interruption came.

Regulus, standing his ground opposed to Reinhard, found an ice pillar thrusting up from directly beneath him, aiming to skewer his body. But the ice spike forming beneath his shoe sole was crushed underfoot before it could break the surface, shattered to pieces without ever getting the chance to properly take shape.

Glancing over, Regulus spotted, across the canal, a silver-haired girl with her hand raised toward him. The ice magic was surely her doing. A sensation of his innards boiling over with irritation.

"Every last one of you, just get it through your heads already! That you're different! Everyone's born with different things! Not one of you—not a single one—can match or even reach me, complete as I am on my own! Accept your own inadequacy, be satisfied, and just die already!"

He was sick to death of dealing with people who wouldn't stop refusing to give up, no matter how many times. The absolute gap between them wasn't closing. Why couldn't they understand that?

"—Plan A failed too. What next."

Regulus, stamping his foot hard enough to crack the street, had no room to spare to catch that mutter of Reinhard's.

"No good! That attack didn't work either, it seems!"

"Damn it! So the sole of his foot being a weak point is out too…"

Following Subaru's instructions, Emilia had attacked the sole of Regulus's foot—but the ice pillar was crushed underfoot, with no sign the damage had gotten through at all.

Plan J is also ineffective. Sorry, not enough strength on my end.

For a moment, telepathy came flying in from Reinhard via his Divine Protection of Telepathy, and Subaru found he'd already lost the capacity to be surprised by the man's sheer absurdity. Looking closely, the wound on his right leg seemed to have closed up at some point too, but even if it had been severed and reattached, Subaru wouldn't have blinked. After all, Subaru himself had already experienced having a leg torn off and reattached.

"If he's invincible and any attack gets through, then the parts touching the ground might slip past the barrier—that was the idea behind Plan A (for Ankle)…"

If the mechanism meant the invincibility at the point of contact with the ground never lifted, then in the worst case his body might just sink straight down into the earth. That was the hope, anyway, but it seemed to be a miss too. And from Reinhard's message, it was safe to say the hypothesis behind Plan J (Jibaku, self-destruction)—that Regulus's own invincible attack might pierce his own invincibility—had also missed.

With Plan I (Ike-Pocha, "into the pond") having already missed as well, the reality was that most of the pre-planned guesses at weaknesses in the invincible ability had come up empty.

"Something else, is there something else? A weak point in an enemy that looks invincible, a weak point…!"

Hand pressed to his mouth, Subaru wracked his brain desperately. From before he'd even engaged Regulus, and continuously ever since, he'd been agonizing over it, but what other means could there possibly be to defeat the wielder of what was, in effect, the strongest shield ability in existence? Knowledge from all manner of subcultures raced through Subaru's mind, straining to produce an answer, but nothing panned out.

"Is my approach wrong? A different way of thinking?"

Maybe what was needed wasn't a way to pierce the invincible ability. Something more fundamental—just what, exactly, was Regulus's Authority?

"Subaru, what else can we do? What should I do?"

Emilia asked this of Subaru, lost in thought. Ahead, across the great canal, the fierce battle between Reinhard and Regulus continued, and unable to lend her strength to that fight, she felt the frustration keenly.

Both Emilia and Reinhard trusted Subaru, placed their hopes in him. And it wasn't only the two of them here. The comrades fighting at the other control towers, the citizens of the city Subaru had called out to over the broadcast—it was the same for all of them.

"————"

Think, think, think, think, think, think, think. It was an unpleasant memory, but Subaru cast his mind back over everything—from his very first encounter with Regulus up to this instant, the man's actions and words, the moves Subaru's side had made, the attacks Regulus had launched. There had to be something. There had to be some reason. It didn't have to be Regulus alone. It could include the other Sin Archbishops too—there had to be something. They were all trash. That much he knew. But beyond that—

"It's the names. The stars."

"Subaru?"

Subaru suddenly realized. He'd arrived at this same thought once before, and dismissed it as nonsense. But now, here, he reconsidered whether it had truly been an idea worth throwing away.

Regulus. Capella. Alphard. Sirius. Petelgeuse. With this many names tied to the stars lined up like this, could it really, truly be dismissed as coincidence?

Remember the Water Pergola Inn. Remember Kararagi's customs. Remember Hoshin of the Wilderness. In this world, the influence of the world Subaru had once lived in showed up in the corners of things far too often to laugh off. Why, at this point, could he possibly think the Witch Cult had no connection to it?

Petelgeuse—Betelgeuse. "The Hand of the Hunter." "The Unseen Hand." Regulus was Leo—"the Little King." And it had another name besides. "The Little King"—that was, indeed, a fitting crown for that man, he thought—but—

"Emilia, I need to ask you something."

At Subaru's quiet voice, Emilia's eyes widened, and then she nodded. Feeling her pale face gazing back at him with utter seriousness, Subaru closed one eye.

"He grabbed you by the neck, right? Back then—"

"Mm."

"Was Regulus's hand hot? Or cold?"

"————"

At Subaru's question, Emilia's eyes went round. She touched her own slender neck, paused a beat, and then answered.

"No… Now that I think about it, I didn't feel anything. Not hot, not cold—no warmth at all."

Hearing Emilia's answer, Subaru held his breath. Slammed into the canal, breath undisturbed, body untouched by water. Attacks on the sole of his foot, attacks on his person, none of them getting through, an ability that left no opening to exploit in either offense or defense.

If this wasn't simple "invincibility"—

"Reinhard!"

He called out the hero's name, across the great canal, to the man facing down a monster. In the midst of a battle allowing no pause, Reinhard nonetheless surely glanced Subaru's way. So that his voice would reach him, Subaru shouted.

"—Check whether his heart is beating!"

At Subaru's loud voice, Emilia's eyes went wide, and so did Reinhard's. And Regulus—

Regulus was—

Once the simultaneous assault on the four control towers had been carried out and he'd confirmed that each faction's strike team had departed, Otto too left the city hall to fulfill the role assigned to him.

"Truth be told, I feel like it's my place to try an' stop ya… but it's also true we want to pin down where the 'Book of Wisdom' the Witch Cult's after actually is. Rough hand you've drawn, Otto-kun."

So said Anastasia, seeing Otto off as he departed. In her heart, Anastasia had probably wanted Otto to stay behind at city hall. With Subaru and the others clashing against the Sin Archbishops, this was a battlefield in the truest sense. With city hall expected to function as something like command central, there was no such thing as too many minds to process the information streaming in from every corner.

And yet, leaving the recovery of the "Book of Wisdom" to some other party wasn't a simple matter either. They were cooperating for now to counter the Sin Archbishops, but once the crisis passed, they would return to being rivals once more. When that happened, he wanted to avoid a situation where the "Book of Wisdom's" true power had already been seen through by another faction. If he were honest, he'd have preferred to avoid even revealing the "Book of Wisdom's" power at the negotiating table at all—but Subaru and Garfiel wouldn't stomach that kind of maneuvering. Feeling as though his own character were somehow rotten, Otto wanted to sigh.

"Just when did I end up running around like this for other people's sake…"

Hand to his gray hair, Otto brooded once more over a question he'd pondered many times over this past year. His position unexpected, his relationships with people unexpected, even his own current feelings unexpected. If his family knew he was doing something like this, what would they think?

"If this all wraps up safely, maybe I'll try writing a letter…"

Muttering something that, had Subaru been present, would undoubtedly have been flagged as a "death flag," Otto pressed on toward the city's Third District. The Sin Archbishops ought to be concentrated at the control towers; by their reckoning, no Witch Cultists should be stationed within the city itself. None—that was the assumption.

"Haah… hah."

Clutching his chest, Otto felt his own heartbeat quickening. The Witch Cult, the Sin Archbishops, Witch Cultists—for Otto, those words connected straight to memories of terror.

A year ago, the memory of meeting Subaru and the others was, for Otto, inseparable from the memory of nearly having his life taken. The horror of the Sin Archbishop he'd encountered that day was something he still couldn't forget.

The eyes of a madman who thought nothing of taking a human life. The sight of fanatics who, following that madman, offered up blood and flesh without a will of their own. The silence, the soundless isolation that ruled everything, in the moment he had prayed with all his heart for someone to save him.

He had never been so afraid. He had never felt so hollow. Compared to the terror of that time, even fighting Garfiel, even facing down the Bowel Hunter, even encountering a pack of Witchbeasts, had been nothing much at all.

That was how deep a dark shadow his encounter with the Witch Cult had cast over Otto's heart. And even though he found it this terrifying, that dark hand never once let go of him.

Emilia, unable to escape the Witch's slander due to her resemblance to the Witch of Envy. Subaru, fated to serve as her knight and fight on and on against the Witch Cult that hunted the half-elf.

Beatrice, sharing Subaru's fate, ready to spend every last bit of her small body for it. Garfiel, pouring the whole of himself into his fists to protect his family. Ram, sharp-tongued yet soft enough underneath that she could never abandon anyone. Frederica, living with guilt over her brother and a sense of responsibility for her position. Petra, treated like a child and yet cheerfully doing her best for the sake of everyone's smiles.

He loved them all. He'd told himself he wouldn't settle down in one place, and yet before he knew it, it had become too comfortable to leave. Even knowing he stood opposite something terrifying, he couldn't bear to part from their side.

If it meant protecting this place, if it was necessary to stand shoulder to shoulder with them, he wanted to crush even the very symbol of terror underfoot and make up for everything beyond their reach. And so—

"Somehow, I have to fulfill my role."

Stepping into the Third District, Otto said this aloud on the street. Ahead of him stood a small silhouette.

Beyond the stone bridge spanning the canal lay a plaza, and the small figure stood there. Besides that figure, Otto could see multiple other shadows scattered around. But in that moment, the only thing Otto's eyes glared at was that one small shadow.

Sound had vanished. It was terribly quiet, nothing audible at all. Living things holding their breath, desperately trying to hide their own existence. Otto Suwen knew this state well.

And so, even as the figure before him let both arms hang limp and lifted its face, shaking its long, dark-brown hair loose, his heartbeat remained astonishingly calm.

"Welcome, mister."

"————"

"Witch Cult Sin Archbishop in charge of Gluttony… Welcome to the feeding grounds of Lye Batenkaitos."

Opening a mouth full of fangs, the Sin Archbishop who should not have been there grinned, malignant and vile.
