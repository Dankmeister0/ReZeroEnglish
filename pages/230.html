Arc 4 Chapter 64: "A World Crumbling Away"

When he came to, the first thing Subaru registered was an unbearable, searing pain wracking his entire body.

The pain was especially severe from the neck up, radiating from his face. His left cheek, the area around his right eye, his molars and front teeth, his left ear—the list of aching wounds went on without end.

Probing the inside of his mouth with his tongue, Subaru realized two of his molars were gone, along with one front tooth and one canine. Then, opening his eyes to look around, he understood that his right eye was swollen shut.

"Th-this is..."

Blood in his mouth and the discordant gaps where his teeth should have been garbled the words as they left him.

Every breath sent a cold ache through his exposed nerves, and even trying to breathe through his nose was useless—it was clogged with dried blood. Gasping, he spat out the blood pooling in his mouth.

"Don't tell me... I couldn't even die?"

Dragging his ravaged body, Subaru was forced to confront the fact that he'd somehow scraped through that hopeless, life-or-death situation alive.

Taking in his surroundings with only half his vision, he found himself sprawled in a dim corridor. No sign of anyone. He recalled the exchange from just before he'd blacked out.

"Elsa... is..."

Not here.

At least, not anywhere Subaru could see.

She was a woman who made her living lurking in shadows. It was possible she was within view yet using some technique to keep herself from being noticed—but there was no reason for her to bother with that now.

Elsa was gone. She had left. At least, without killing Subaru.

"For what... no, more importantly..."

Every movement of his mouth brought fresh bleeding, and he spat it out irritably, shaking his head. He tried moving different parts of his body, gauging what hurt and what wouldn't respond at all—and that was when he noticed it.

—There was a warm presence in his arms, breathing faintly.

"—Rem."

The beloved blue-haired girl. The one who kept sleeping, the one who spurred Subaru onward.

Rem's heart beat quietly within his arms. Her shallow, repeated breaths, the blood clearly still coursing beneath skin flushed with color—proof that the rhythm of life continued in her.

"—"

Overcome, he unconsciously tightened the arms holding her.

Taking advantage of her lack of response, he clutched her small body close, savoring its warmth, as if trying to feel through her skin the proof that she was alive.

"Why... didn't she kill me, or Rem... why did she just leave..."

Holding Rem's body, Subaru mused on the inexplicability of Elsa's actions.

The murderer had killed Petra, killed Frederica, even erased Beatrice from existence—and yet had left without taking the lives of Subaru and Rem, who'd both been right there.

It was true that, right before losing consciousness, Subaru had done something like beg Elsa for Rem's life. Elsa's reply could have been taken as consent—but could she really have honored that so faithfully?

He couldn't imagine understanding the thought process of a psychopath like the Bowel Hunter, but maybe that was, in fact, the reason Rem had been spared.

"Then... why me...?"

He'd thought he would be killed.

At the very least, Elsa had surely swung her knife at him with clear intent to harm. The shattered places, the flesh carved into—all of it told Subaru so, plainly, through the pain.

So then, just what was the reason Elsa had let him live after all?

"In any case, right now..."

There's no way I can know, he thought, shaking his head, and forced his aching body to lift Rem up.

Holding light Rem in both arms, he looked to the end of the corridor—where Frederica's corpse still lay, discarded—and decided on what he had to do.

—First, he had to give Frederica and Petra a proper burial.

"If I've already accepted this world is going to end, then none of this even matters..."

A self-mocking murmur slipped out, at how sentimental, irrational, and pathetically shallow the act was.

Subaru had already decided to redo this world through his own "death."

He had lost too much. He had gained things too, but with what he'd gained, he hadn't been able to protect a single thing he wanted to protect. Just as before, or worse, Subaru had lost. He had no courage left to go on living in a world he'd lost so much in.

If paying with his own life could win it all back, there was nothing to hesitate over.

This world was a world that would end.

Petra's death, Frederica's death, Beatrice's death—all of it could be redone.

His promise to Petra, his apology for having doubted Frederica, a real answer to Beatrice's grief—all of it could be delivered in the next world.

If he accepted that much, then truly, there was no meaning in mourning the dead.

Sentiment left behind in a world doomed to vanish wouldn't remain in anyone's memory, so long as Subaru alone could bear it.

—If he could really accept things that thoroughly, Natsuki Subaru probably could have overcome this looping world much sooner.

"Resolve, determination, ability... no matter how far I go, none of it's ever enough. Why am I so weak? Hey, Rem."

There was no answer from the girl in his arms.

Being able to voice his weakness, being able to show his frailty—for Subaru now, it was only here, only in front of this sleeping girl, that he could do such a thing.

—It was only a few dozen minutes after Subaru decided at the mansion to give Petra and the others a proper burial that he found it.

"What... is this?"

Looking at the object sitting before him, a dumbstruck sound escaped Subaru's throat.

But no one could blame him for that. The thing before him now was that unfathomable, that impossible to identify.

A pinkish lump of flesh—that seemed like the closest description.

A round lump of flesh, misshapen like a mud ball a child had molded. That description alone should convey the strangeness of it well enough, but there was more to why it bewildered Subaru.

"It's huge—"

Simply put, the lump of flesh was enormous beyond reason.

It towered over him, and even from a glance, its density and weight were evident—packed solid. In color and texture it resembled the fresh pork or poultry you'd see at a butcher's counter. Though Subaru had no courage to actually touch it and confirm as much.

As far as he could see, there were roughly twelve of these enormous lumps of flesh. All of them were about the same size, scattered here and there as if put on display.

"What in the world is this...?"

Unable to find an answer amid his confusion, Subaru repeated the same question for what felt like the umpteenth time.

Then, turning his head,

"Where did everyone in the village go?"

In the empty center of Arlam Village, surrounded by lumps of flesh, Subaru muttered blankly.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

—The reason Subaru had come down to Arlam Village was to borrow hands to help bury Petra and Frederica, and, above all, to tell Petra's family of her death.

He'd braced himself to be struck, braced himself to be cursed at.

It was the same sentiment as at the mansion. Here too, Subaru could have avoided that pain. He could hide Petra's death, redo the world without ever telling the villagers.

If he did that, he could keep the responsibility for letting Petra die locked away in his own chest, and be saved just by cowering under nothing but his guilt.

But when he considered whether he could forgive himself for doing that, he found he couldn't.

"In the end, I guess that's just self-satisfaction too, though."

He planned to tell Petra's family, and then bury the two of them.

As for mourning Beatrice, he didn't know how. Spirits left no remains. The way she'd vanished, almost refreshingly clean, had ironically robbed Subaru of any real sense that she was dead.

Maybe—that was as far as his lingering, wishful thinking went.

With thoughts neither forward-looking nor backward-looking, Subaru made his way down to Arlam Village.

He brought sleeping Rem along with him, intending to ask someone to watch over her during the burial.

And so, after arriving in the village and wandering around in search of the villagers, what Subaru had come across was those lumps of flesh.

"—There's no one here."

Temporarily setting Rem down under the eaves of a house, Subaru ran all through the village and reached his conclusion.

Sweat running down his brow dissolved the dried blood, staining his face in red blotches—Subaru was a dreadful sight. If a villager saw him now, a scream of alarm would be the natural greeting.

But there wasn't a single resident around to scream at the sight of him, and Subaru sat down beside sleeping Rem, at a loss.

—When Elsa had vanished from the mansion, the thought had crossed his mind: surely not.

This was the same Elsa who, back in the royal capital, had immediately decided to kill everyone involved. Perhaps she hadn't been satisfied with just the mansion's residents and had come down to Arlam Village to wield her murderous blade there too.

Amid all the reasons he'd told himself for coming to Arlam Village, that anxiety had been part of it too. But what greeted Subaru upon arriving was a situation beyond anything he'd anticipated, and worse.

Scattered lumps of flesh, and villagers nowhere to be found.

—An unpleasant thought naturally rose in his chest, but Subaru unconsciously pushed it away.

"If there's no one here, there's nothing for me to do. ...I need to hurry and bury the two of them."

Muttering something like an excuse, Subaru lifted Rem up and left the village.

The motionless, gigantic lumps of flesh were left as they were. He felt no guilt at all about leaving them behind. He didn't even want to keep them tucked away in a corner of his memory.

His mind was already on the verge of bursting.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

Burying Petra and Frederica ended up, unexpectedly, being simple.

He arranged their features into peaceful expressions, wiped the blood from their skin and faces with clean cloth. Feeling apologetic about it, he changed their clothes into fresh ones. Naturally, no impure feelings arose in him.

More than that, as he threaded sleeves onto their cold, stiffened bodies, he was gripped by a baffling feeling—wanting to cry, yet finding the depths of his eyes only drying out.

"...At least, rest peacefully."

Praying for the two of them's peace over the mounded earth, Subaru offered words that were little more than a comfort to himself.

He didn't know the customs of prayer in this world, nor, for that matter, the proper way to send off the dead back in his original world. All his relatives were still alive; he'd never attended anyone's funeral, and being irreligious in the typical Japanese way, he'd never taken any interest in such rites.

Now, he regretted that.

—Not knowing the proper form or words to send the two of them off filled him with an unbearable frustration.

"Sorry for putting you through this too. Thanks for helping."

As Subaru said this and reached out a hand, the jet-black Earth Dragon pressed her muzzle against his fingers.

Her legs caked with dirt from digging, Patrasche nuzzled close as if worried about Subaru.

Finding Patrasche safe in the stable, having been spared the disaster, Subaru had borrowed her strength to bury Petra and the others. Clever as she was, she'd quickly understood his clumsy appeal, and while Subaru dug with a shovel-like tool, she dug out the larger hole needed for Frederica's bigger frame with her own feet.

Even with her powerful legs—the ones that ran the earth and became the wind—caked in mud, the jet-black Earth Dragon remained noble and beautiful. He felt a fresh, deep gratitude for her existence.

The hole for Petra, Subaru dug himself. Though she was small, he wanted to make sure she wouldn't feel cramped, and he worked the unfamiliar tool until the skin of his palms split open, again and again.

As he covered her with earth and watched Petra's form disappear from sight, the tears he could no longer hold back finally ran down his cheeks, but Subaru didn't wipe them away, letting them dry on their own.

He saw Frederica off the same way, and after fashioning simple grave markers, considered the burial complete.

Though he'd brought this one task to a close, his shoulders only grew heavier instead of lighter.

"...There's no point staying here anymore."

He murmured it under his breath.

The irreversible tragedy at the mansion had already run its course.

He carved the whole chain of events firmly into his memory. The regret of having seen the two of them off like this, too—he etched it deep, so as never to forget.

This regret, now branded onto his soul, he would make certain to erase come the next chance.

Only by achieving that could Subaru truly take responsibility for their deaths this time around.

"Once I've confirmed what needs confirming, I'll head back to the Sanctuary. —And Rem's coming too, since I can't just leave her here."

The sunlight had slowly begun to fall.

The darkening world was, if Subaru's reckoning was correct, about to enter the night of the third day. If he confirmed what he needed to confirm and left the mansion the next morning, he could make it back to the Sanctuary by the night of the fourth day.

That left a day and a half of grace before the fateful sixth day. And this time was also his first experience of leaving the Sanctuary and then returning to it again.

Protect the mansion, and break through the Sanctuary as well.

Since these were the two hurdles he'd determined he absolutely had to clear, the experience gained here was something he had to secure before attempting the run that would finally succeed.

What sort of changes would come over the Sanctuary while Subaru was away from it?

He suspected it would likely follow much the same course as when he'd been struck down by Garfiel and kept imprisoned. If so, then on the night of the fifth day, Otto and Ram should move to get the refugees from Arlam Village to escape.

"Before that happens... right."

Garfiel, whom he'd left behind, weighed on his mind too.

Using forceful means, Subaru had used Ryuzu as a shield to stop Garfiel's pursuit. How badly that had struck a nerve with him, Subaru couldn't fully imagine.

And he'd have to tell Garfiel of his sister Frederica's death, too. Suspecting her of being an informant and failing to take any steps to save her was Subaru's own failure.

He would have to accept Garfiel's anger, all of it, without complaint.

"I'll go back to the Sanctuary. —I want to see Emilia."

Thinking of the string of events weighing on his mind, Subaru let his true feelings slip out in a daze.

It might have been close to a weak admission.

But right now, he wanted to be honest about it.

He wanted to see Emilia's face. He wanted to touch her.

He wanted the feeling of Emilia's presence to heal his heart, which felt ready to break right now.

That was how utterly exhausted Subaru was.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

—It was when Subaru entered the forest close to the Sanctuary that he noticed something was wrong.

Riding astride Patrasche, holding sleeping Rem against his chest, the two of them shared the saddle. It was a precarious, nerve-wracking position, liable to lose balance at any moment, but fortunately Rem didn't stir even once, and Patrasche perfectly compensated for her master's ineptitude, letting them make the journey without incident.

He couldn't have her run at full speed the way she had returning to the mansion, of course, and the journey back took roughly seventeen hours, bringing them right up to the night of the fourth day.

The day and a half he'd planned to have at the Sanctuary had shrunk down to just one day, it seemed.

It was time necessarily spent. Of course, he had no intention of blaming Patrasche for it.

But what proved to be a miscalculation for Subaru, who had proceeded so carefully along the way, was this:

"This isn't funny at all... What the hell is going on...!"

Along the path to the Sanctuary, in the forest where the barrier stood—partway through it, a cold that pierced the skin had begun enveloping the world.

Frost coated the leaves of the green trees, and the surface of the trunks was faintly frozen white. The moisture pooled in low ground had frozen over, forming small patches of black ice here and there.

The abnormal drop in temperature—cold like the depths of winter, or worse—made Subaru tighten his hold on Rem, and he looked around, breathing out white puffs of air.

The forest, as ever, held only a faint trace of living things, but even the vitality of the trees themselves was being sapped away before this cold. The fact that the forest had made no preparations at all to meet such extreme cold was, more than anything, proof that this chill was nothing like a natural phenomenon.

"Cold enough to turn everything white in front of me... I've got nothing but bad feelings about this. Patrasche."

"—"

"Hey, Patrasche?"

Driven by the bad feeling rising in his chest, he tried to urge Patrasche onward, but there was no response from her at all.

Frowning, he looked down at his beloved dragon, and saw that the jet-black Earth Dragon had stopped moving, her breathing badly ragged and labored.

"Patrasche!"

Startled, Subaru pulled the reins to call for Patrasche to stop, dismounted from her back, and reached a hand to her neck. The rock-like texture of Patrasche's neck felt about the same as usual, but it was frighteningly cold to the touch. And then Subaru realized.

"Don't tell me Earth Dragons are weak to the cold...? They look kind of reptilian—what do they even do in winter?"

Many reptiles, like lizards and snakes, hibernate through the winter to survive the cold. Patrasche, an Earth Dragon with many outward similarities to reptiles, might just possibly share that same trait.

If that were true, then having Patrasche march on in this cold would be nothing short of suicidal.

Because if Subaru's guess was right, this cold was likely to grow even more severe the closer they got to the center of the Sanctuary.

"It'd be tough to go together... yeah. At this rate, I'm getting worried the Earth Dragons who stayed behind in the Sanctuary might be in danger too."

He stroked Patrasche, who was trembling in small shudders. It probably did no more than offer some small comfort, but Patrasche leaned into the feel of his palm as if clinging to it.

Heading to the Sanctuary without her meant an unavoidable further loss of time—but if they pressed on together like this, the best they could hope for was collapsing somewhere along the way.

"Patrasche. Sorry, but could you wait outside the forest... no, could you head back to the mansion?"

At Subaru's decision and instruction, Patrasche let out a sorrowful snort.

But clever as she was, she understood Subaru's thinking, the state of her own body, and the conditions of the forest ahead. After he repeated a few soothing words, she eventually lowered her head to Subaru as if giving up her resistance.

Petting her head firmly, Subaru took clothes and simple provisions out of the baggage Patrasche had been carrying, layering on as much as he could to prepare for the cold. He dressed Rem in a coat the same way, tied the remaining baggage to her body, and lifted her up in his arms.

"The road to the Sanctuary is straight ahead... right."

"—"

"Don't make that worried face at me. I'm the one who's worried about you. You must've been suffering, and I'm sorry for not noticing something was this bad. I'm really sorry for being so thoughtless."

As Subaru bowed his head, Patrasche gave a small whinny, as if to say there was no need for that. Then she began walking off toward the edge of the forest, and Subaru watched her go until she was out of sight.

Not once did she look back along the way. She was a dragon in whom a pride that wouldn't allow showing lingering attachment coexisted with a kindness meant to spare Subaru from feeling guilty.

Truly, he thought, she was a far better dragon than he deserved.

"Patrasche should be able to make it out of the forest safely. ...More than worrying about her, I need to worry about what's ahead of me first, damn it."

Readjusting his hold on Rem, Subaru pressed on, his footsteps crunching over the frost-heaved ground.

His breath showed white, and exposed to a cold that would set his teeth chattering the moment he let his guard down, he pushed onward, ever onward, toward the Sanctuary.

"What happened, Emilia...?"

Calling out the name of the girl who surely lay at the heart of this cold.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

Forcing his numbed feet forward, moving lips too frozen to even tremble anymore, he kept up his shallow, repeated breaths. Prying open eyelids whose lashes had frozen shut, he forced his eyes open, somehow securing a line of sight through the hazy white mist, and pressed on through the forest.

—The bitter cold enveloping the Sanctuary far surpassed anything Subaru's naive imagination had prepared him for.

With every step deeper in, it felt as if the temperature dropped further still.

The feeling in his skin had long since vanished, and what kept Subaru's body moving was a ghost-like sense of mission, the sheer will to keep pressing forward, and—

"—"

—the presence of Rem in his arms, her vital signs continuing at a steady, unwavering pace that made it impossible to tell whether the outside environment was affecting her at all.

He should be moving straight ahead, aiming only for the Sanctuary.

He had no way to visually confirm whether his own path was correct. All he could do was trust, from the way the extreme cold kept intensifying, that he was indeed heading the right way.

Snow had piled up as high as his shins, and before he knew it, the forest had transformed entirely into a wintry landscape.

An influence powerful enough to alter the very nature of the world itself. Subaru knew of it.

"—"

His lips trembling, he gasped for breath. The skin of his upper and lower lips, stuck together, tore apart with a sharp little sting, and blood welled up. Feeling its faint warmth on the tip of his tongue, he could tell his body hadn't frozen through to its core just yet.

He could still go on. He could still manage.

He hadn't confirmed anything yet. If he stopped here, like this, he wouldn't even know what he'd sacrificed everything for.

So—

"—Ah"

Suddenly, Subaru noticed something cross his all-white field of vision and stopped in his tracks.

Rubbing his half-closed eyelids open, Subaru strained his eyes at the foreign shape intruding on his vision. As its outline gradually resolved, it took the form of a person—and one he recognized.

"Ryuzu-san?"

"—"

To Subaru's call, the girl responded only with a silent stare.

Seeing her manner, Subaru immediately recognized that the girl wasn't "Ryuzu" herself, but one of the replicas of Ryuzu Meyer.

At the same time, he realized that if she was a replica, then now that Subaru had returned to the Sanctuary, he should hold command authority over her.

"Just... in time... please. Guide me... to the Sanctuary..."

"That request there, that ain't one she's gonna listen to, y'know?"

Breath coming in broken white puffs, Subaru called out to the replica girl. But cutting across his words, a voice dropped down from above.

He raised his head. At that instant, someone dropped down and landed on the snowfield beside the girl. The snow sank with a crunching thud, and a young man appeared there.

Short blond hair, sharp eyes, radiating hostility from every inch of him as if ready to gun Subaru down on the spot.

"Garfiel."

"Well now, look what dragged its face back here. Damned if I ain't surprised you managed to crawl on back. 'Even a lyin' Beatoon bows fine' — ain't that what they say?"

His incomprehensible turns of phrase alive and well as ever, Garfiel bared his fangs at Subaru with a click.

He glared down at the winded Subaru with irritation, then caught sight of Rem in Subaru's arms and widened his eyes in obvious shock.

"Huh...? Why the hell are you with Ram... no, that ain't Ram. Huh? What's goin' on here. Who the hell is that woman..."

"Not sure explaining it'll even get through to you, but... this is Rem. Ram's honest-to-god little sister."

"Never heard anythin' about Ram havin' a sister... but callin' it a lie's a bit of a stretch too, ain't it, hey."

Faced with a girl whose features were the spitting image of the woman he loved, Garfiel's aggression flagged slightly. Since he wasn't moving in to kill him outright, Subaru judged he was still being reasonable enough, and put off deciding whether to fight back or flee.

Then Subaru glanced at the replica standing silently beside Garfiel,

"What's this about her not listening to what I say?"

"...Simple enough, ain't it. After you left the Sanctuary, I went straight into the testing grounds myself. Overwrote the command authority and took it back, that's all. Made me remember some unpleasant shit all over again, though."

"Command authority can really just be handed off that easily, back and forth?"

"Basically just gotta touch that thing. Same as it was for you, wasn't it."

"That thing," as Garfiel called it, was presumably the crystal sealing away Ryuzu Meyer's true body. If touching that was the condition for transferring command authority, then it was only natural it had passed back from Subaru to him.

In any case,

"Coming all this way to greet me—how thoughtful."

"Ain't got no intention of playin' along with your nonsense. Look at the state of things now—should be plain we're way past the point where we can laugh this over, ain't that right?"

"Yeah... you're right. ...Then let me just ask you straight."

Nodding at the menacing Garfiel, Subaru gave a small shake of his head, drew a breath, and said:

"—Is this Emilia's doing?"

"Can't even say for sure on that one. She ain't even come out of the Tomb, see."

"She hasn't come out of the Tomb?"

Subaru frowned at the unexpected answer. Irritated by his reaction, Garfiel clicked his tongue and kicked up a great spray of snow at his feet,

"Ever since the day you disappeared, the half-demon's been actin' strange. Thought she'd settled down, and then she goes and shuts herself up in the Tomb, and now this. Before anyone knew it, the Sanctuary got covered in ice past the point of no return. —Same as the Elior Forest."

"You... you know about Emilia's hometown—!"

"You think I ain't heard? Roswaal's a slimy bastard, but he answers the questions that need answerin'. That's exactly why I don't trust this so-called Emilia-sama."

Spitting that out coldly, Garfiel then closed the distance to Subaru, whose expression had hardened. Unable to react to his sudden movement, Subaru could do nothing against Garfiel now standing right in front of him.

"What a pathetic mug you're makin'."

"Wha—!?"

Shoved in the chest by his palm, Subaru fell backward gracelessly.

He hurriedly tried to shield Rem in his arms, but his hand grasped at nothing. Because—

"You—Rem, you—!"

"Give her back, huh? Hey now, ain't you awful attached. Thought the woman you fell for was s'posed to be Emilia-sama."

Landing a jab that stung Subaru too, Garfiel snorted.

Rem's body, which had been in Subaru's arms only a moment ago, had been snatched away into his.

Forcing his numbed body to move, Subaru desperately tried to grab hold of Garfiel, but he leapt back to put distance between them, and Subaru couldn't catch up.

"What are you planning to do with Rem...!"

"Ain't got no intention of hurtin' her. That'd be missin' the point entirely. I stick to what's right. Stuff that don't make sense just makes my skin crawl."

Saying so, there was indeed no malice in the eyes with which Garfiel looked down at Rem.

At the very least, Garfiel's nature wasn't so twisted that he'd bring harm to a girl who shared the face of the woman he loved.

Then why—Subaru began to form the question, but Garfiel spoke first.

"Get into the Tomb. —And drag that half-demon out of there."
