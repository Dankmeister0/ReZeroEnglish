Arc 6 Chapter 7: "Aiming for the Sea of Sand"

Miruula, the town nearest the Augria Sand Dunes, was — to put it plainly — a run-down post town. The town had a certain size to it, but naturally, it was incomparably smaller than the water-gated city of Priestella, the industrial city of Costuul, or the royal capital of Lugunica. The roads laid throughout the kingdom were all well-maintained, and the journey to Miruula by dragon carriage had been a stable one, but for the terminus at the kingdom's eastern edge, it was a lonesome sort of place.

"But I guess it can't be helped. Looking at the map, there's really nothing east of here but the Augria Sand Dunes... and to the south is the kingdom's biggest forest, right? It's far from the five major cities, so I can see why there aren't many people passing through."

Walking beside Subaru as they looked over the town, Emilia let slip that observation. She had a white robe pulled over her head, faintly hiding her beautiful silver hair and face. When a beautiful girl like Emilia turns up in a country town, it deals a blow to the townsfolk's sense of aesthetics — or so went Subaru's joke, but she also held an exalted position. It was a small precaution, meant to avoid inviting trouble not just over her appearance but her identity as well.

Though that wasn't the only reason for covering her head and mouth with cloth. Aside from concealing her identity, there was another reason: the effects of the sand wind, Miruula's famous "disaster."

"No room to develop east or south, and to top it off, this sand wind. Rough."

Standing Emilia on the leeward side, Subaru — who, as a small gesture of resistance, took the windward position himself — pulled up the cloth wrapped around his face, hiding his mouth as he muttered that. When he moved his tongue, he felt the grit of sand scraping between his teeth, and it left him feeling thoroughly unpleasant.

This sand-laced wind came pouring into town as if blowing straight down off the eastern dunes. On windy days, the sand wind apparently blew without pause, so this kind of anti-sand fashion was indispensable for going out and about in town.

"—? Subaru, something wrong?"

"Nah, when I heard 'desert,' this isn't quite the scenario I pictured."

Emilia tilted her head at Subaru, who was glancing sidelong at her. She had hidden every last bit of bare skin, her form swallowed from head to ankle by a cloak. Subaru's basic philosophy was that cute girls ought to delight the eyes of those around them with all manner of outfits, but with defenses this thorough, there was no eye candy to be had. Speaking of deserts, what had happened to the dancer-girl outfits meant to guard against the sun?

"Still, I guess we should've listened to what the innkeeper said."

"It's exactly 'sand hour' right now, isn't it? That's why there's no one on the streets."

The wind wasn't especially strong, but the sand, being light, formed itself into a faint haze of dust. In the yellow, hazy visibility, Subaru and Emilia were the only two people to be seen along the town street in either direction. The townsfolk, thoroughly accustomed to this environment, held up inside during "sand hour," when the sand wind blew, sealing even the gaps in windows and doors — that thoroughly. In fact, amid the sand wind, not just visibility but even breathing demanded considerable effort. The innkeeper had gone so far as to firmly try to stop the two of them, saying they shouldn't go out during "sand hour." Still, there was a reason they'd pushed past the innkeeper's objections and come out anyway.

"This is what it's like inside the town. The sand wind out on the dunes themselves must be nothing like this."

"...Yeah, that's true."

It wasn't exactly a dress rehearsal, but the reason for deliberately going out during "sand hour" was to prepare themselves mentally. Surrounded on all sides by sand, the route across the dunes had sand wind blowing even outside of "sand hour," and it wouldn't be anything so mild as this. This was the visibility inside a town with buildings packed close together, at a distance from the dunes themselves. They needed to properly steel themselves now for how brutal the route across the dunes would be.

"Anyway, should we duck into some shop? At this rate I feel like both you and I are going to turn into lumps of sand."

Brushing off the clinging sand, Emilia pointed at a building across the street. What looked like a tavern or something of the sort seemed, for the moment, to be functioning as a shelter. The other shops had an air of "not open for business," but at least this one's entrance wasn't sealed shut.

Following Emilia's suggestion, the two hurried toward the building at a brisk pace. They shook off as much sand as they could in front of the push-door, then slowly made their way inside the tavern.

"...Welcome, in this sand wind."

Upon entering the tavern, the proprietor, who had been polishing a glass behind the counter, called out to them in a low voice. The lack of welcome in his tone was probably because he'd noticed at a glance that they were caked in sand. They'd shaken off as much as they could, but the amount of sand still clinging to them was immense. Feeling somewhat guilty, they scattered sand as they stepped into the shop.

"What'll it be?"

"Milk, the cold kind."

"Milk, the warm kind."

"——"

At the two orders from the pair seated at the counter, the proprietor narrowed his eyes but said nothing. For the record, the cold one was Subaru's order, and the warm one, Emilia's.

"Doesn't look like business is booming... slow going during sand hour?"

Pulling off his hood and taking a sip of the milk as it was brought to him, Subaru tried asking the proprietor. The gruff proprietor gave a short "Yeah" in response, then continued,

"Even without the sand hour, this town doesn't get much traffic from outsiders to begin with. I keep the place open while the sun's up, but it's more like a hobby."

"I see. Then as outsiders and customers both, we're your best patrons, huh."

"Not much to be proud of, ordering milk at a tavern. Here, this one's yours, missy."

"Wow, thanks."

The warmed milk was handed over, and Emilia took hold of the earthenware cup with a sigh of relief. Watching her out of the corner of his eye as she blew on the milk, Subaru leaned forward over the counter.

"No other customers around, so you've got a bit of time, right? Mind if I ask a few things?"

"Even if I really do have time, it's bad manners to tell a man on the clock he looks bored. ...What's an outsider want to know?"

"Straight to it — about the Augria Sand Dunes."

Subaru raised a finger, leveling the question at the proprietor like a written challenge. At that, the proprietor's expression cracked for the first time. The gruff, gray-haired, close-cropped proprietor narrowed the eyes beneath his thick brows, suspicion plain on his face. He looked back and forth between Subaru and Emilia, then let out a small sigh.

"I don't know what kind of joke this is, but if you're going in for a sightseeing lark, drop it. You'll just die."

"Whoa, hey, what's that all about? You think we look like we're out for fun?"

"What else would you look like, to make me give advice like this? Nobody who goes there comes back alive — it's a sea of sand and death. It's no place for a lovers' rendezvous."

"I'll grant the thrill of danger's an effective approach to courtship, but I'm not that desperate. Back me up here, Emilia-tan."

"Foo, fooo, hot... huh? What? Sorry, I wasn't listening."

"See, look at this E-M-T display."

"No offense meant, but go home before you get yourself killed."

Watching the exchange between Subaru and Emilia, the proprietor's trust in them dropped even further. Still, it wasn't as if the proprietor meant any ill will by his words. In fact, the danger of the Augria Sand Dunes was already known even from rumor alone. But—

"Turning back isn't an option for us. Since the only road is forward, all we can do is pick the safest path forward that we can. You get that, right?"

"You're the one who doesn't get it. Listen. That dune is beyond hopeless. It's a den of Witchbeasts, the Witch's miasma hangs over it, and you can't even get near that tower you can see off in the distance."

Irritated by Subaru's persistence, the proprietor began describing the dune's threats. Pointing at the window sealed shut against sand, gesturing east toward the edge of town, he twisted his lips.

"Every year, reckless fools like you come here chasing the dunes. Their goal's the Sage's tower out in the sea of sand... but nobody ever reaches it. Making it back alive is the best you can hope for — most of them either dry up out there in the sand sea or end up as feed for the Witchbeasts."

"Has anyone really never made it there?"

"You have any idea how many hundreds of years it's been since that tower went up in the sand sea? The number of fools aiming for that place hasn't dropped off, and if anyone had ever reached it, there's no way they wouldn't have come forward. Don't you know? That would be an achievement even the 'Sword Saint' failed at."

"——"

The conquest of the Pleiades Watchtower was a challenge even Reinhard had failed. Subaru had heard as much from Reinhard himself, and it spoke to just how absurdly high the difficulty of conquering the Watchtower was. And yet, even so, it was something Subaru and the others now had no choice but to overcome.

"Obviously we're not the type of fools to charge in with no plan. That's what this information gathering is for. Doing it at a tavern's the standard move, right?"

"Is it? Why at a place where people drink alcohol?"

"Couldn't tell you the specifics, but probably something like, everyone loosens up once they've had a drink, so all sorts of rumors come flowing in?"

"So in other words, it's drunkards' idle chatter. Charging in with no plan is bad, but I'd say leaning too hard on foolish plans isn't much better."

"Guaah! A direct hit of sound reasoning!"

Struck down head-on by plain good sense, Subaru drained his milk and went under. Subaru's usual conversational pace faltered whenever he was up against a proper grown-up. The proprietor, too, was probably well used to dealing with people as reckless as Subaru who challenged the dunes. In truth, Subaru's attempt on the dunes wasn't reckless at all, but as long as the proprietor across from him didn't know the reality, he'd be treated the same regardless. At this rate, they'd end up heading back with no leads whatsoever—

"Besides, dragging a girl along into that kind of hell..."

"I'm sorry, sir. You've been worried about us this whole time, and Subaru's done nothing but complain."

As Subaru sank into a slump, out of leads, Emilia cut off the proprietor just as he was starting up another lecture. At Emilia's words, opening with an apology, the proprietor's eyes went wide. Emilia gave him a small bow.

"Thank you for everything, even though we're complete strangers to you."

"No, well— I know I sound like a nag myself. But I don't think I've said anything wrong. It's just, young folks like you two, every single time..."

"Do that many people really try to go meet the 'Sage'?"

"There aren't many with the real guts to try meeting the 'Sage.' Most of them are after the glory of conquering the Augria Sand Dunes. I suppose there are some who hope the Sage might grant them wisdom if they're lucky, but even the rumor that you get some reward for reaching the tower sounds pretty fishy to me."

Shrugging, the proprietor sighed with a weary expression. Just as his words suggested, he had probably seen off any number of challengers to the Pleiades Watchtower. Perhaps he was kinder than his face let on, or perhaps there was some bitter regret behind it.

"Does that mean... even if you reach the tower, you can't meet the 'Sage'?"

"I've never heard of anyone even getting that far. Word is the Sage still looks down over the dunes from atop the tower even now, striking down divine punishment on the impudent... but it's not like anyone's actually seen it. To me, that whole dune feels like nothing so much as a trap, dangling bait to lure in prey."

"You mentioned Witchbeasts and miasma. What's that about?"

"Just what I said, no more, no less. That place is a den of Witchbeasts... Witchbeasts come in plenty of varieties across the kingdom, but strangely enough, that one dune has every single kind."

Perhaps warming to the topic, the proprietor grew talkative even as he kept his voice low. Emilia held her breath and nodded, and the proprietor's gaze went distant.

"The Spot-King Hounds and the Black-Winged Rats, those are almost cute. The native unicorns and the sand earthworms — you won't see those anywhere else. And if there's ever a flower field out on the sand, that's hunting grounds for the Oiran Bears. Consider it a nightmare."

"Oiran Bears..."

"Supposedly they're only supposed to live over in Kararagi. Witchbeasts with flowers blooming all over their bodies. Get fooled by the looks and go in close, and it'll slurp your guts right out."

"Witchbeasts sure are something... brutal..."

Witchbeasts are beasts made to kill humankind — whoever had told him that. The proprietor snorted, as if to say "see, I told you," at Subaru, who was frowning as he listened in.

"But more than the Witchbeasts, what you really need to watch out for on the dune is the miasma. The sand wind and the shifting environment are dangerous enough, but the miasma takes priority over those."

"That miasma thing doesn't quite click for me."

At the proprietor's voice, clouding over again, Subaru cocked his head. Miasma — he'd heard the word plenty of times, and he could half-picture it from the characters alone. Basically, it must mean air that looked bad for you, the kind that had some harmful effect on body and mind. He figured it wasn't quite like toxic gas, but maybe something close to it.

"Um, well, Subaru. Miasma, to put it simply, is what you call mana that's been contaminated by something evil. You can't see it, but it's everywhere, right?"

"Wait, mana is the miasma?"

Subaru was startled by Emilia's remark. It was an easy-to-follow explanation, but it shifted the meaning slightly from what he'd been imagining. It was true that mana held an enormous influence over mages and spirits. But just living normally, or moving around on dragon-back, shouldn't be much of a problem. Yet Emilia, as if she'd seen right through Subaru's letdown, slowly shook her head.

"You can't underestimate miasma. Normally, ordinary mana has no color or direction to it at all — it's pure. But miasma... mana that's been fouled by something evil, just taking it in through the Gate is enough to poison a living creature's body from the inside. Since the Gate is an organ that naturally draws in mana, there's no avoiding it."

"Because you can't exactly hold your breath forever, huh."

"That's the right idea, missy, but the miasma drifting over the Augria Sand Dunes is a cut above that. —The miasma there doesn't just harm living things, it spoils food and drink too."

"You mean it makes them inedible?"

"I mean eat it, and the contamination spreads through you all at once. Taking in even a trace amount through the Gate is enough to drive you mad. Try taking it in straight, through your stomach. You'll be swallowed whole by the corruption."

"And then what happens? Does it stop at just going crazy?"

"Common belief is you go mad and die. As for the reality of it... well, I can't deny it."

The proprietor shook his head, saying little more, his complexion poor. He hadn't said it outright, but he had probably seen it himself — the final moments of someone driven mad by the miasma, meeting their death.

"A place like that, if you can avoid going, that's a whole lot better. Do that."

"—Thank you for the milk. And thank you for the conversation, too."

As the proprietor moved to wrap up the conversation, Emilia drained her hot milk and offered her thanks. Partway through, the proprietor had probably realized he was being gently drawn out. Even knowing that, he had kept talking this far, trying to make Subaru and the others change their course. But sadly, even so, the place Subaru and the others were aiming for wasn't going to change.

"We'll leave the payment here. Subaru, let's go."

"Yeah... guess so."

Setting a silver coin on the counter, Emilia tugged at Subaru's sleeve. It was an excessive amount for two cups of milk, but it included a tip for the information and the concern. They stood, thanked the proprietor, and made to leave the shop.

"—It's been about a year now, but people have started seeing birds flying off toward the dunes."

"——"

As they pulled their robes back on, bracing for the sand wind, the voice called out to their backs. Turning around, they found the proprietor with his back to them, wiping a glass, continuing on in a tone like he was talking to himself.

"According to the folks who've seen them, there's this joke going around that the birds are flying toward the tower. Not a shred of evidence behind it, but..."

There, he cut himself off and set down the glass. And then,

"If you ever lose your bearings out on the dunes, try looking for the birds. If you're lucky, they might just lead you all the way to the tower."

"——"

"Though the moment you're lost out on the dunes, your luck's already about as bad as it gets."

Without a reply to the proprietor's words, Subaru and Emilia left the shop. Outside, the force of the sand wind had eased considerably. Confirming that visibility had opened up somewhat compared to when they'd entered the tavern, Emilia turned back to Subaru.

"Let's head back to the inn for now and regroup with everyone. Anastasia and the others might be back by now too."

"Yeah. I'm curious how their end went too."

Standing in front of Emilia, who had covered her face again, Subaru started walking, serving as a windbreak. Once they'd gotten far enough that the tavern was out of sight, Emilia murmured quietly.

"That shopkeeper just now, he was missing a leg."

"...I didn't notice."

"I don't know where or how he got hurt... but I have a feeling it's related."

At Emilia's pensive words, Subaru understood what she meant too. The proprietor, who had gone to considerable lengths to warn two total strangers of the dunes' dangers.

If that had been a warning born of his own experience, then the two of them, brushing off his concern to challenge the dunes anyway, were terrible people. That was the feeling that came over him.

"Looks like Natsuki-kun and Emilia-san made it back safe."

Back at the inn, having shaken off the sand and come up to their room, Subaru and the others were greeted by Anastasia, who should have also been out and about, just as they had been. Changed out of her outdoor clothes and seated on the bed, Anastasia wore a soft, elegant smile.

The inn where the group was staying was the finest one Miruula had to offer. Even so, it fell short compared to something like the "Water Wing Inn" in Priestella, but that was simply a gap in city-level standards there was no getting around.

"Where's Julius?"

"After seeing me back to the inn, he said he'd go look around town a bit more. Probably getting his eyes used to the sand. Same as you two, seems like. How'd it go?"

"Honestly, I'd kill for some dust goggles. Something like clear glass that covers this part of the face, like swim goggles. Think you could make some?"

Picturing swimming goggles, Subaru asked while gesturing around his own eyes. Anastasia tilted her head at that, then nodded, "I see."

"Glasswork would have durability issues, but it's worth considering... The sand wind problem isn't unrelated to Kararagi either, so it might be a usable idea."

"I'm not trying to talk future patent rights here. If we don't have that... well, I guess as long as we avoid 'sand hour,' we shouldn't be completely unable to move."

"Sounds like the bigger problem isn't the sand so much as the Witchbeasts and the miasma. Beatrice and I will try working with the minor spirits about the miasma, but... for the Witchbeasts, we're pretty much relying entirely on Meili."

Saying this, Emilia glanced through the wall toward the next room over. That room was currently, presumably, under Beatrice's watch over Meili. Emotionally, they wanted to believe she wouldn't betray them, but Meili's track record was what it was. They couldn't just trust her outright, and there was no guarantee her claimed severed connection to her mother was really true. Nor could they rule out the possibility that her mother might try to silence her.

"And on top of that, we're relying on Anastasia for navigation. Quite the tightrope walk."

"If you want to call it that, I guess it's business as usual."

"Can't argue with that."

At Emilia's rare bit of teasing, Subaru's lips curved, and he turned to look at Anastasia. She straightened her back on the bed and continued, "So, about that—"

"You can rest easy, I got done properly what I went out to do. The wheel adjustments on the dragon carriage, and how we're to be treated going into the dunes — both should come through exactly as we asked."

"Really... I'm sorry. It feels like we're leaving everything to you."

"It's fine, it's fine. In exchange, I'll be counting on Emilia-san for our fighting strength."

Anastasia waved a hand magnanimously. The purposes she'd mentioned — one was the adjustment of the dragon carriage for the dunes. Normally, a dragon carriage was only ever meant to run on a maintained road, or at least some kind of path. But this time, most of the route would be over sand, and an ordinary dragon carriage wouldn't be able to travel it properly. Leaving the carriage behind and traveling by Earth Dragon alone was an option — but with so many women and children in their group, and the still-unconscious Rem besides, it wasn't realistic. For that reason, adjusting a carriage suited to desert terrain, and switching out the Earth Dragons, had become unavoidable.

"We can't leave Patrasche behind, though. Leaving her behind would be unthinkable."

"That girl really is super smart, huh. If you started talking about leaving her, Subaru, I feel like she'd bite you..."

In fact, that seemed likely enough to happen that he couldn't really argue. Not that he had any intention of leaving her behind.

"So, the negotiation that wasn't about the carriage..."

"It wasn't much of a negotiation to speak of. I just calmed the other side's worries and explained things properly, is all."

She smiled charmingly as she said it, but sensing that the smile wasn't quite what it appeared to be, a chill ran down Subaru's back.

The carriage, and the other matter. Put simply, the latter was a rite of passage for facing the dunes. Namely: if Emilia and Anastasia, both Royal Selection candidates, were to go missing in the Augria Sand Dunes, where would the responsibility for that lie?

As things stood, it was common knowledge that the two of them held positions of near paramount importance to the kingdom. If, of all things, the two of them were to be buried in the sand of the Augria Sand Dunes, it would be a major incident — and naturally, there would be a great deal of commotion over where the responsibility lay. The Augria Sand Dunes — setting aside whoever administered the dunes themselves, for the lord who governed the area around Miruula, the last town they'd stopped at, the weight of that responsibility would be more than he could bear.

So, in advance, Anastasia had gone to see the lord in order to get him to accept the point that "whatever happens, it is the candidates' own responsibility," and to get it down in writing.

"Even so, he might get blamed for not stopping us hard enough... our lives aren't just our own, after all. Heavy responsibility, isn't it?"

"That's always been true, and it's not just because of the Royal Selection or being candidates. There are a lot of people who won't be saved unless we meet the 'Sage' and find a way to undo everything. That's not something new I'm bringing up now."

"Now that's reassuring, isn't it."

Anastasia looked back at Emilia, who declared this with something like pleasure in her expression. Then she tilted her head and turned her eyes to Subaru. "So then?"

"Did you two get anything out of your walk around town? Don't tell me you really just went out to get used to the sand."

"Well, it's not like we were out on some sandy date. For what it's worth, I think we managed to get some information out of someone who knew what he was talking about."

"It was just a happy coincidence that the shopkeeper happened to know a lot."

"The brilliance of my foresight shone through, picking that tavern."

"I feel like it was actually me who picked the shop..."

In any case, whoever's doing it was, or however the credit shook out, had nothing to do with the information itself. When they relayed the tavern story to Anastasia as well, she wore a thoughtful look.

"People seeing birds... hm."

"What, is that what's bugging you?"

"Well, the rest of it is roughly half a step above common knowledge when it comes to tackling the Augria Sand Dunes. What's more interesting is that shift, I'd say. Birds, of all things — you'd think they'd be the most sensitive to a wind laced with miasma."

"Now that you mention it, that's true..."

At Anastasia's words, Emilia touched her own hair, falling into thought. Glancing at Emilia as she sank into contemplation, Subaru leaned in quietly toward Anastasia.

"—Don't tell me you actually have some idea what that's about?"

"Nothing specific comes to mind. But it's a genuinely interesting point."

"——"

"Don't make that suspicious face. Look, she'll start wondering about you."

Eridna's true self peeked through, but once told that, there was nothing to do but back off. Fortunately, Emilia, still lost in thought, hadn't seen the exchange just now, but for some reason, the relief in Subaru's chest felt exactly like that of a man frantically hiding an affair.

"What about departure?"

"Sooner's better, right? Since the concerns about the carriage and the talks are settled... once we've restocked the cargo we need to load, I think we might as well head out as soon as we can."

"Tomorrow's too soon, but the day after?"

"Let's do that."

At Anastasia's words and clap of her hands, Emilia nodded in agreement. Then she turned her gaze to the room's window — the glass firmly sealed shut against the sand — and narrowed her eyes.

What Emilia's violet-and-indigo eyes gazed at was the view to the east, beyond the window. There, a vast, towering shadow rose up, tall enough to loom over the town itself.

"The Pleiades Watchtower."

Quietly, that bell-like voice named the structure. The tower they sought was so tall, so massive, that it could be seen looming even from within Miruula.

—Could something that huge ever really go unnoticed?

Ever since the Pleiades Watchtower had come into view along the road before their arrival in Miruula, Subaru had been carrying that question with him the whole way. What could possibly go wrong, to make a landmark of that scale impossible to find? And yet, even as he thought that, the tavern proprietor's warning stuck with him too.

"No plan is out of the question, a foolish plan is the lesser evil, huh."

Whatever happened, turning away from the challenge was never an option. That was exactly why he'd tried to choose every means available to him, anything that might increase their odds. They weren't like the reckless, foolish adventurers the proprietor had described — or so he thought.

And that was precisely why Subaru didn't notice, why he couldn't notice.

—Whatever happened, turning away from the challenge was never an option.

At this point, he had already, by his own hand, sealed off the very first path forward.
