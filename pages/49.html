Arc 2 Chapter 25: "The Cry of Resentment Never Fades"

How many times now had his consciousness been overtaken by this blankness?

Faced with a sight beyond comprehension, Subaru stood frozen, robbed of words, just as he had so many times before. He had been beaten down. Again and again, until now. It was starting to feel fair to whine, just once, that he deserved to be saved for a change. Because over and over, again and again, all he had witnessed was tragedy.

In front of Subaru, who stood rooted to the spot, a blue-haired girl lay on the bed. Her face, drained of life, was pale, and her closed eyes would never open again. The thin nightgown she must have worn as sleepwear — an odd thing to notice at a time like this — suited her with a childish air that contrasted with her usually composed impression. Subaru realized, with a start, that he had never once seen her in anything but her maid uniform. And now, the first time he saw her in anything else, it was her burial clothes.

"Why... why Rem—"

He muttered, running a hand through his hair, bowing his head. Faced with a situation beyond comprehension, his head spun uselessly, aching terribly. Exhaustion from lack of sleep no doubt played a part. Thinking at all felt unbearably heavy.

Four times now, he had repeated this cycle, and of those four, he had been killed three times — which made her, in his eyes, the person he needed to be most wary of as the culprit. Roswaal, who might well have ordered her silenced, and Ram, who surely shared her position, were under the same suspicion, but it was without question his firsthand experience of being killed that led him to judge Rem as the greatest threat this time around.

That was why this time he had kept them at a distance — even talking a reluctant Beatrice into helping him — and pushed the situation off again and again, buying himself more time to live. As a result, he'd managed to reach the morning of the fifth day. That much had been good news.

"Why has this — why has this flipped completely...?"

It should have been Rem trying to kill Subaru. Never the other way around. And he couldn't think of a single reason anyone would want Rem dead. Almost without thinking, Subaru stepped forward to see for himself whether she really was gone. The girl laid out on the bed had her hands folded over her chest, and whoever had done it had arranged her face peacefully, as though in sleep. He reached out, meaning to touch that unmoving body. But—

"—Don't touch her!"

An arm swung out and struck his hand away; Subaru gave a small groan and looked up. Across the bed, Ram clung to Rem, tears spilling down her face. Still crying, showing no sign of stopping, she said,

"Don't touch Rem... don't touch Ram's little sister."

There was no room in those words for anything else. Pure rejection. Having spat that much out like a slap, Ram clung to Rem's body again, whispering through quiet sobs. It looked exactly like a devoted, painful image — a mother telling a fairy tale to her sleeping child.

Even her sister's pitiable display drew no stirring from the girl, no sign of waking to scold her. The pale profile. The bloodless lips. And the body whose heartbeat had stopped. Seeing it all up close, Subaru finally accepted the reality.

—Rem really was dead.

"Magic, most likely," ne. Though it feels closer to the Curse Arts than ordinary magic."

Roswaal, standing beside the door, offered that guess to Subaru as he stumbled unsteadily out of the room. He raised a finger, his long indigo hair framing a face whose usual slack expression had tightened somewhat.

"Cause of death: exhaustion. Life drained away while she slept, her heartbeat pulled slo-o-owly further and further off, until the flame of her life was snuffed out as though she'd simply drifted deeper into sleep."

At the word "Curse Arts," Subaru's head snapped up. It was a familiar-sounding guess. And Roswaal's description of the cause of death struck a chord with him too. It was, precisely, the kind of abnormality Subaru had experienced firsthand in his second death. Assuming his first death had shared the same cause, then this time Rem's death matched his first death, and indirectly his second.

Which meant—

"I figured that weakening spell was Rem's doing too, in the second loop."

Weakened by magic, then had his skull crushed by an iron ball — that was his second death. Thinking back on that scenario, no one could blame Subaru for assuming a single culprit. But in reality, Rem had been killed by the Curse Arts, meaning Subaru had somehow survived not only the Curse Arts' influence but the baptism of the iron ball as well. In other words—

"The Curse Arts User and Rem are separate...? Are they working together, or what?"

In the case of his second death, the attack had come on top of the weakening effects of the Curse Arts — it seemed natural to assume they were working hand in hand. But what about this time? A falling-out between allies, Subaru thought, then shook his head. To begin with, he had no idea where the Curse Arts User even stood. Given the conditions, it only made sense if the Curse Arts User was someone close to the mansion. Otherwise there'd be no opportunity to have contact with Rem, and more than that, the person would need motives and circumstances that overlapped with Rem's own reasons for acting.

Judging by what he'd been told in the third loop, Rem's attempt to silence Subaru had been, in her mind, for Roswaal's sake. Indirectly it also removed an obstacle in Emilia's path — an act of loyalty taken too far, but loyalty all the same. That premise — that this outcome might be unavoidable — was what had driven Subaru to the edge mentally before. And now it was being shaken loose by the question of her relationship to the Curse Arts User.

Unable to arrive at a satisfying chain of logic, Subaru couldn't hide his irritation as he stood there, hand to his chin, thinking. On top of his accumulated exhaustion, the blood wasn't exactly flowing well to his head right now. He knew no decent conclusion would come of it, but with nothing else he could do besides think, think was all he could do. And so—

"Don't tell me... it's not cooperation, it's not related at all, is it...?"

What if there was no relationship whatsoever between Rem and the Curse Arts User? In the first loop, Subaru had been the Curse Arts User's target, and died from it directly. In the second, Rem had killed Subaru while he was already caught in the Curse Arts User's spell, for some reason of her own. In the third, it was pure runaway loyalty — Rem killed Subaru with the Curse Arts User having nothing to do with it. And now, this fourth time—

"Rem became the Curse Arts User's target because I didn't do anything...?"

He couldn't grasp the causality. To begin with, he didn't even know why the Curse Arts User would want his life in the first place. Perhaps the Curse Arts User's targets were everyone in the mansion — but if so, he couldn't think of a reason to skip over important figures like Roswaal and Emilia in favor of prioritizing lower-standing people like Subaru and Rem.

In the end, without knowing the Curse Arts User's identity, he was just going in circles. Feeling cornered on all sides, his thoughts stalled, and Subaru clicked his tongue in frustration and cut them off. Just then—

"You seemed to be thinking rather hard about something."

From slightly above, Roswaal looked down at Subaru and murmured that. His mismatched blue and yellow eyes narrowed, as though appraising him, and Subaru frowned at the unpleasant sense of having his inner thoughts seen through so easily. Roswaal must have noticed his own rudeness, because he quickly straightened his posture and gave Subaru a slight nod of the head.

"Forgive me. It seems I too am somewhat on edge. Understandably so, I think, given what's been done to a servant I'm so fond of."

Even as he spoke the apology, Roswaal's manner never truly softened in the way that mattered. He crossed his arms, tapping lightly on them with his fingers, and said,

"Scorched by fire, violated by water, sliced apart by wind, buried in earth. I feel that would be the bare minimum needed to answer for this. It's an odd thing to ask, but... my guest, wouldn't you happen to have some idea?"

The low murmur carried not a trace of a joking tone. Voicing what was surely a pure, unvarnished threat, Roswaal fixed Subaru with a testing gaze. Subaru, abruptly aware of just how bad his position was, hesitated over how to respond. But before he could open his mouth, another voice cut in.

"Last night, you told me to be careful. You said that, didn't you, Subaru?"

Turning to look, in his shocked, wide-eyed field of vision, Subaru saw a gray cat moving its short forelegs as though washing its face — Puck. Left speechless by this unexpected remark, Subaru could only stare as Puck tilted his head.

"Emotionally, and out of a sense of obligation, I'd very much like to take your side. But if I let that get in the way of judging things clearly, there's a girl who'll go unrewarded for it."

Puck turned his black eyes gently toward the room and rubbed his pink nose with a paw. Even with his usual playful manner unchanged, he must have had his own feelings about Rem's death. His movements were smaller than usual as he shifted his weight.

"If regret or hesitation linger, a soul isn't saved — it falls to ruin instead. As a mere spirit, and as someone who had at least some contact with that girl, that's a sad thing to me."

Shaking his small head once, as if to say he'd said all he had to say, Puck curled himself up again. Beatrice quietly watched him from her palm, and she too gazed at Subaru without a word. Whatever emotion was held within that gaze — even though she was normally such an expressive girl — Subaru, as he was now, couldn't manage to make it out.

"...Subaru."

Anxiously, Emilia tugged at Subaru's sleeve, turning moist eyes on him as though pleading for something. If there's something you know — that unspoken appeal came through in that single call of his name. Even as part of him wanted to throw everything he had into answering her plea, another part of him — his true feelings — wanted to shake off the girl clinging to him.

If there's something you know — everyone was saying it so lightly. But if anything, that was the very thing Subaru himself wanted to shout at the top of his lungs. If there was anyone who knew the truth of this situation, he wanted them to step forward this instant. He wanted to hurl every single one of the countless questions piling up like a mountain and be dragged out of this dead-end alley with no way out.

But in reality, no such convenient mastermind would ever appear, and the meaning behind the four pairs of eyes fixed on Subaru wasn't about to change either. His warning to Puck, meant well, had backfired this time. Even Puck must have grown suspicious of Subaru, who knew something and yet wouldn't put it into words. And Emilia, so closely bound to Puck, naturally harbored no small amount of doubt toward Subaru as well.

Loop after loop, he had struggled each time hoping things would turn out better. And yet every single time he did something, it backfired, dragging him back into a worse situation than he could have imagined. Surrounded on all sides — how many more times would he have to repeat this before anyone else felt the same heartache he had?

"Subaru."

Clearly, distinctly, Emilia called his name. It was proof that his silence had planted a doubt in her that she couldn't shake off. At her all-too-natural reaction, Subaru felt a pang of sorrow even as he thought it through. In a situation where he practically knew nothing, how could he possibly speak in a way that would satisfy them? Though he supposed they'd be disappointed in him either way, given that he truly knew nothing.

His thoughts were a tangled, formless mess. Just as he thought that maybe it would be easier to simply say it, to let it all out—

The stillness of the world and the black mist — as though drawn out by Subaru's own resignation, it once more fed on his despair and encroached upon the world.

He lost his words. No — his lips, meant to form words, wouldn't so much as twitch. Naturally, neither hands nor feet nor eyes nor tongue would move, and even the air itself was severed completely from the world. Sound and motion vanished; in the return of that absolute space of silence and stillness, Subaru was, quite literally, struck speechless.

It was the same unreasonable phenomenon that had forcibly cut him off the other day, when he'd decided to tell Emilia about Return by Death. The black mist formed its black arm more quickly and more gracefully than before. Where before the hand had only extended from wrist to about the elbow, this time it took shape with the faint outline of an upper arm as well.

Just as before, the arm showed no hesitation as it crept into Subaru's frozen chest. It gently stroked past his organs and breastbone, reaching his heart—

—and in an instant, his thoughts were blown away, and a pain that could kill his very mind outright tore through his entire body.

"Subaru!?"

Hearing that anguished, sorrowful voice, Subaru realized he had returned to reality. He also realized he'd been pressing a hand to his chest, sweat pouring down his forehead, down on one knee, breathing raggedly.

Raising a hand slightly toward the worried Emilia, Subaru looked back over what had just happened. When he'd tried to tell Emilia about Return by Death, that black mist had appeared to warn him off, obstructing him. And if it had shown itself again this time for the same reason—

"I can't touch on the details of the loop... is that it? Because explaining it is impossible...?"

At the very least, given his state of mind just now, there was no way he could have fended off their relentless questioning. But if that was indeed the case, this situation was as bad as it could get. Subaru wanted to spill everything inside him, while the people around him wanted to pry open exactly that. For the black mist, there could be no better stage to act on.

Questions with no end meant torment with no end. The instant he realized that, Subaru's lips trembled with an unbearable fear of the pain, and he took a step back. That act—

"—If you know something, I won't let you run."

—looked, to the girl who had been weeping and crumbling in that room, like an unforgivable act of folly.

In that instant, a sudden gust violently rattled the door of the room, its aftershock slapping against Subaru's bangs. He closed his eyes against the natural force of the wind, and right after—

"Gah...!"

A sharp pain stabbed his cheek, and Subaru instinctively covered his face with his palm. What he felt against his palm, wet and clinging, was blood dripping from a freshly opened wound. Realizing it was an injury from the wind just now, Subaru turned his shock toward the back of the room.

Gently stroking her little sister lying on the bed, the pink-haired girl held her other hand out toward Subaru, her eyes burning with fury as she glared at him.

"If you know something, spit it all out. Every last bit."

"Wait, Ram! That's—"

The one thing he stopped himself from saying was the foolish word "I can't." The instant he said that, there would be no way to salvage this scene. But even holding it back, no words came to break the deadlock. Subaru had no idea what would trigger that warning to strike him again. His fear of that pain robbed him of any sound judgment where Ram was concerned.

As a result, his words trailed off into nothing, and he did nothing to douse her mounting fury. To Subaru, who had fallen silent, Ram sent another gust of wind, meant once more as a warning.

If a hackneyed expression could be forgiven, it truly was a phenomenon like a "blade of wind." Likely some wind-related magic. A spell conjuring a whirlwind slashed through the air with the sharpness of a blade, tearing through the walls and door in its path toward Subaru, about to deliver a blow like the one that had split his cheek before — and then, abruptly, it vanished. Both Subaru and Ram froze, voices catching in their throats. And between the two of them—

"—I make it a policy to keep my promises, I suppose."

Murmuring that, a girl with cream-colored hair stepped in front of Subaru. Beatrice gave a light wave of her raised palm, and without any hint of pride at having so effortlessly dispelled the whirlwind Ram had unleashed, she said,

"While he's in this mansion, Betty will guard this human's safety, I suppose."

"Beatrice-sama...!"

At Beatrice's languid pronouncement, Ram bit her lip in outrage. Glancing sidelong at her reaction, Beatrice looked up at the tall figure standing beside her.

"Roswaal. Your servant is being rude to your guest, I suppose. As master of this mansion, how do you intend to handle that, I wonder?"

Put on the spot, Roswaal's brow furrowed for just a moment. But he quickly regained his usual composure, closing one eye and shrugging his shoulders.

"Truly a regrettable thing, I agree. If I could, I'd like to welcome him back as my guest again right away. If he'd just empty out what's in his chest and lighten his load, I'd do so immediately."

"...This one was in Betty's forbidden library last night, I suppose. So he can't be connected to this incident, I suppose."

"That's no longer where the weight of the matter lies. Beatrice, surely even you understand that much perfectly well, don't you-u-u?"

The negotiation broke down, and Roswaal, shoulders still shrugged, turned both palms upward. In those palms, multicolored lights suddenly appeared, several of them at once.

Even Subaru, who had no knowledge of magic whatsoever, realized this was condensed magical power made visible. Red and blue, yellow and green — four faint colors of light floating there. Even without knowing just how far outside the norm that was, he could tell it was extraordinary.

At Roswaal's display, Beatrice gave a small, cute sniff of her nose.

"As dexterous as ever, little upstart, I suppose. A bit of talent, a smidge more effort than others, a sliver of good fortune in family and teacher... and you think that's enough to escape being a jack-of-all-trades, master of none, I suppose?"

"You're as sharp-tongued as ever too-o-o. Though I confess it wouldn't be a lie to say I've wondered, more than once, just how different you — spending your days in a room where time has stopped — really are, from the rest of us who keep walking ever onward."

A surge of magical power rose between the two of them, giving Subaru the illusion that the air itself was warping. Without so much as sparing him a glance, the two of them, leaving Subaru — the very person at the center of all this — behind, stoked their fighting spirit against each other. Subaru himself had no idea why things had come to this. And yet the fact that he couldn't get a single word out to stop them was something he could only grit his teeth over.

"But to think you'd go so far as to put your own body on the line to protect him. Have you really taken such a liking to him, I wonder-r-r?"

"Save your jokes for your own particular tastes, Roswaal. Betty's ideal is Onii-chan, and no one else, I suppose. That human falls short both in loveliness and in hairiness, I suppose."

Against Roswaal, whose four visible colors of magical power kept climbing, Beatrice remained composed, taking no particular action of her own. And yet Subaru, standing close by, could feel it — an overwhelming phenomenon occurring around the girl, who should have simply been standing there, powerful enough to warp the very space around her. It was merely the aftershock of gathered power, and imagining just how much effect that power could have once given direction sent a chill down his spine.

"Why go this far?"

"A Contract is absolute, I suppose. Break it, and Betty stops being Betty. Being faithful to a Contract takes priority over all else."

Responding to Roswaal's breathless remark, Beatrice added, "Although," and continued,

"If he aimed for this very situation and used his Contract with Betty to bring it about, then he'd be an outrageous fraud indeed, I suppose."

"I! That's not what I meant, I—!"

Beatrice glanced sidelong at Subaru, then let her emotionless eyes slide smoothly away. What Subaru had asked of her had only ever been out of concern for the phenomenon tied to his past deaths. He had never, ever wanted things to come to this.

"I don't care, none of that matters, I don't care at all!"

Stamping her foot, it was Ram who broke into the exchange. Under everyone's gaze, she clutched the hem of her skirt tightly and said,

"Don't get in the way, let Ram through. Rem's killer... if you know anything, tell me everything. Save Ram... save Rem..."

It was an anguished plea, but Subaru had no words to give back. What his silence earned him was an invisible blade forged of fury and disappointment. But that blade, too, failed to reach him this time, intercepted—

"Sorry, Ram. Even so, I'm going to try trusting Subaru."

Stepping up beside Beatrice, it was Emilia who held Ram back. She turned her palm toward Ram and, with Puck perched on her shoulder, walked closer to Subaru. Then, bowing her head slightly as though searching for words, she said,

"Subaru, please. ...If you can save Ram, if you can save Rem, then tell me everything?"

At the tenderness held in those words, Subaru felt ashamed of his own smallness. Even now, even at this point, Emilia was still trying to stand by his side. Even toward Subaru — who had shown nothing decent this loop, who even now was keeping his mouth shut about the one thing that mattered.

And at his own weakness, unable to give anything back for her consideration.

"I'm sorry—"

Leaving those words behind, Subaru let himself stumble backward, putting distance between himself and her. For a moment, Emilia's hand reached out as though to stop him. But before it could reach him, it was redirected to intercept the blade of wind unleashed at him, and in the end, no one there was able to stop Subaru's feet.

"Subaru—!"

Shaking off the voice calling him back, Subaru tore down the hallway, all pretense abandoned. Behind him, he could sense the clash of magical power, but he couldn't summon even the courage to look back.

Despite how much Emilia had poured her heart into it, Subaru lacked the courage to face that fear. He was weak. Hopelessly fragile. And so, trampling over every bit of goodwill and kindness offered to him, he fled, thinking only of himself.

He no longer knew what he should do. Where he should go, how he should even think — none of it, not anymore.

Only—

"—I'll kill you. I swear I'll kill you!!"

A girl's blood-soaked scream, chasing him from behind. Only the cry of a girl who had lost half of herself, a scream of vengeance that tore at her own body, followed after him.

Fleeing as though escaping it, fleeing and fleeing, covering his ears, shaking his head, letting out a voice that wasn't even a voice anymore, Subaru abandoned everything and ran.

He kept running.
