Arc 1 Chapter 13: "End and Beginning"

"Enough already! There's shameless, and then there's whatever the hell you three are!"

Subaru stamped his foot against the ground, hurling his irritation at the three faces he'd seen more than enough of by now. This was their third encounter, and every single time it had been three against one in some back alley. Even after the first two meetings ended in nothing but wasted effort for them, Subaru couldn't help but marvel at their sheer persistence in making him their prey. If the situation had been any different, he might have even given them a round of applause. But—

"I don't have time to deal with you guys right now. I'll take you on once things settle down, so let me through."

There was urgency in him, sure, but having already won once against three-to-one odds, Subaru had a certain margin of confidence. If he threatened them a bit more forcefully, an opening might show itself—that was the calculation. But—

"'Let me through,' he says. What do we do with this guy?"

"I don't like that attitude. He doesn't get it—who's in charge of giving orders here."

"You lost pathetically three-on-one and you've still got the nerve to run your mouths... even a beaten dog howls with a little more shame than you three."

Honestly, given the current situation, Subaru himself couldn't quite shake the feeling of being a beaten dog either. In the end, wasn't this whole spot just a gathering place where losers and stragglers skulked around together?

"Too much negative thinking! I'm supposed to be the type who looks on the bright side a little more!"

"No good, this guy's completely off his rocker. We picked him 'cause of that weird getup, but maybe we picked wrong."

The men talked among themselves, not bothering to hide their irritation at Subaru's loud muttering to himself. The machete and the bare-handed one were subtly losing interest in Subaru, but the gaze of the knife-wielder—the biggest threat—stayed sharp. His eyes seemed to have given up on Subaru's value as a wallet, but they still wavered with a dark, cruel emotion of some other kind entirely.

At that look, Subaru muttered "this is bad" in his heart. After two rounds of fighting, Subaru had concluded that the knife man was the most troublesome of the three. Not just in terms of the lethality of his weapon—his temperament made him the quickest to "snap," too. Getting into a scuffle, let alone even getting close to him, was best avoided.

"Fine. I won't resist. Just tell me what you want."

Raising both hands to show he had no hostile intent, Subaru answered them like that. A complete about-face from his earlier bold diplomacy—now it was weak-kneed diplomacy, a stance of simply swallowing whatever the other side demanded. The judgment came from prioritizing two things: not touching a nerve with the knife man, and getting out of this spot right now. He resigned himself to accepting some minor losses, thinking only of how to escape this place.

—Well, if this was about revenge, there was a chance he'd get beaten to a pulp regardless. If the conversation started drifting that way, he'd land one hit before they could stab him and make a break for it. If he could get to the main street, they surely wouldn't try anything too flashy there.

While Subaru quietly plotted his own self-preservation, the men's attitude softened. As if mocking Subaru's cowardice for so readily bracing to comply with their demands—

"Tch, if you were gonna cave from the start, shoulda just done that, idiot." "Damn it. Don't run your mouth if you're just gonna chicken out." "Whatever, it's fine. He's not gonna do anything, and he's gonna listen, right? Coward."

A few words came flying that struck a nerve, but Subaru managed to brush them off with a dry, forced "haha." In his heart, he secretly dubbed the unrepentant trio "Ton, Chin, Kan," and quietly savored a small measure of satisfaction as he said,

"So, what exactly does this idiot, this bastard, and this coward want from me?"

"For starters, strip everything you've got. That weird kimono and those shoes too, all of it. You can keep your underwear on, though—we're not demons, y'know!"

Merciless mockery was hurled at the now-submissive Subaru. So the concept of "demons" exists in this world too, he thought, somewhat beside the point—but at the same time, their words struck him with a strong sense of déjà vu.

Everything he had, and the kimono and the footwear—these exact demands, he was sure he'd heard before—

"You guys took a hard hit to the head somewhere I don't know about, didn't you?"

Because it was the exact same demand they'd made during their first encounter. Either they'd hit their heads, or they were pathetic enough to lack the memory to reflect on their own words, or—the worst possibility—they were the type who recycled the same tired lines so often they couldn't even remember who they'd said them to. The further down that list, the more hopeless it got, but considering how bewildering their attitude was toward Subaru, the man who'd just beaten them down, the last possibility seemed most likely—which was, again, hopeless.

"Between this and Felt, there sure are a lot of people around here with terrible memories..."

"Quit running your mouth. You not gonna do what we say? Or is your head too empty to manage it?"

"Coming from you guys, 'head' is really the last word that should—"

He kept that last mutter to himself, and outwardly Subaru showed a stance of going along with them for now. As he considered whether he might negotiate to keep his clothes and shoes at least, or whether he should just take out all the approaching men at once, he reached his hand into the plastic bag he was carrying—

"Huh—?"

His brows knit at a wrongness unlike anything he'd felt since arriving in this other world.

"Why... is this...?"

Muttering almost like a groan, Subaru slowly checked the inside of the white plastic bag. There was the tonkotsu-soy-sauce cup ramen, along with the phone and wallet he'd stuffed in there because carrying them in his pants had been a hassle. And then—the golden snack that most matched his personal taste. Yes, the golden snack—the corn potage-flavored snack food.

It was the first thing he'd eaten since being thrown into this world, the diplomatic tool that had done wonders in soothing old man Rom's fury at the Loot House, and as a result of that, it should have been empty. —And yet that snack bag was sitting in the convenience store bag, packed full again.

"I definitely ate it. There was barely anything left. Definitely."

The contents of the bag should have been reduced to about a third. And yet the contents of that very bag had returned to full. There wasn't even a trace of it having been opened. No matter how he looked at it, it was abnormal.

The healing of his wounds, he could explain. There was precedent—Satella's magic. And so, as for the outcome of the two catastrophes at the Loot House, Subaru had settled it in his own mind as some kind of treatment by someone.

—But even granting that a master of some superclass healing magic existed, was it really possible for them to restore something that should have been gone?

"That's right—duplication magic..."

He remembered something old man Rom had muttered offhandedly. Duplication magic—magic capable of duplicating something that exists, even if only its outward form. If that magic were used, this situation could be reproduced too.

"What about the seal on the bag, then? Don't tell me healing magic can even fix the glue."

That didn't strike him as a realistic thought. And in a world where, seemingly, magic-based civilization had developed at the cost of the kind of mechanical civilization Subaru knew, he couldn't imagine the concept of "resealing a snack bag" even being understood.

His thoughts were boxed in on all sides. But even within that boxed-in thinking, Subaru had half-concluded that this phenomenon wasn't healing magic. The reason his conclusion went no further than that was that the other possibility that had occurred to him was so far outside common sense that his reason flatly rejected it as impossible.

"Oi, you. What the hell are you doing?"

"Huh?"

Suddenly addressed from close by, Subaru let out a startled, blank sound. One of the men—the bare-handed one, third in line, whom he privately called "Kan." Subaru's brows furrowed at the sight of him, who had somehow closed the distance without Subaru noticing.

"What, coming in close like that? For the record, I can take my own clothes off without help."

"Nobody's tryin' to help you with anything! You're the one who came wandering over here!"

Only after being shouted at did Subaru realize that he'd drifted from the depths of the alley all the way toward the street. Apparently, while sinking into his thoughts, he'd moved without realizing it. But to the men, Subaru's dazed demeanor looked like nothing but hostile action.

"You not gonna do what we say? Want us to rough you up a little?"

"Screw it, this is already a pain. Let's just do it here."

Watching the men start to move toward a rash, short-sighted action, Subaru pulled himself out of his deep thinking for the moment. What he needed right now was—

"Go fetch, boys!!"

"What!?"

He swung up the plastic bag in his hand and hurled it far into the depths of the alley. Drawing an arc through the air, the bag sailed straight toward the darkness. Naturally, the men who'd been eyeing it as their prize had their gazes dragged along with it.

Seizing that opening, Subaru slipped past the men and broke into a full sprint. As he'd concluded again and again, escaping these men was the top priority right now. After that, he needed to head to the Loot House and get answers to the suspicions that had surfaced.

Even he thought it was a ridiculous idea. That the healer was also a superhuman-level master of duplication or restoration magic or whatever, that they'd happened to stumble upon the carnage at the Loot House, that out of pure innate compassion they'd healed Subaru and the others for free, then released only Subaru in front of the vegetable stand, and vanished without even giving their name in one straight shot of hard-boiled cool. Being told some absurd, rambling tale like that would still be easier to swallow.

"No, even that's far from convincing, though."

That version was still, at least, more internally consistent. At least compared to the baseless, foolish thought that had just crossed his mind a moment ago.

Holding two contradictory lines of thought at once, Subaru dashed across the filthy ground of the back alley. If he got out to the main street and ran around making a lot of noise, surely they wouldn't be able to chase him down. Losing the contents of the plastic bag stung, but he'd already pulled out what mattered beforehand, so all that remained inside was the snack bag and a bit of small change to weigh it down—minor damage, all things considered.

Having settled on that conclusion, he took a step forward—and then, with a jolt of panic, realized that step had landed nowhere near where he'd aimed. His body swayed unsteadily, and the leg he'd meant to plant staggered instead. Then, this time, the strength drained out of his knees, and he sank to the ground. He caught himself with his hands, pitching forward, scolding himself for being idiotic enough to trip at a time like this. But—

"Huh, that's weird..."

Trying to gather strength to stand, the arms he'd planted on the ground trembled violently. He couldn't, by any means, lift his own body. Worse, he realized he'd already dropped whatever he'd been holding in his hand.

"That's why I told you to just listen, idiot."

A mocking voice came from directly behind him, and Subaru managed to tilt his head that way. Behind him, standing right beside him, was the man who'd been second in line—the one he'd privately nicknamed "Chin," the most humiliating name of the three. Still wearing his crude demeanor, he twisted the corner of his mouth and pointed at Subaru. Following the direction of that pointing finger with his eyes, Subaru understood the reason he'd fallen.

—Buried in his back, near his waist, as he collapsed, was a knife.

"Guh... gah..."

The instant he registered it, unbearable, searing pain shot through him and choked off his throat. He was robbed even of the freedom to writhe against the purely primal, sharp agony.

—Stabbed! Stabbed, stabbed, stabbed, stabbed, stabbed, stabbed.

Unlike the other two, "Chin," the one with the knife, was the only one whose attention had never gone to the plastic bag. He must have been the one who, even at that earlier point, found more appeal in hurting Subaru than in the value of any belongings. As he'd thought, more than Ton and Kan, dealing with Chin should have been the priority. This was the price for neglecting that. Over these past few hours, it was a kind of searing agony he'd now tasted more times than he could count. And yet no matter how many times he tasted it, he could never, ever get used to it.

"Oi, you stabbed him?"

"Couldn't be helped. What if he'd gotten out to the main street? That'd be way more than just a pain in the ass."

"Ahh, man, this is bad. His guts are hit, he's gonna die... ...his kimono's soaked too."

Even after stabbing a man, was there really nothing else on their minds? Subaru griped from the edge of his agony. It was a pain that would drag his consciousness away if he didn't split his thoughts onto something else. And judging by Ton's words, this was the kind of wound where, once he lost consciousness, there'd be no coming back.

He had to act while he was still conscious. Reaching that decision, he gathered up what strength remained across his whole body, scraping together enough for one good howl. He put it on his tongue, ready to let out a roar, and—

"Here y'go, number two before you can pull anything!"

A second knife, mercilessly, was driven straight into the center of his back.

"————g'ah"

The scream he'd been about to unleash was canceled by a numbness like an electric shock that raced clear to the tips of his hands and feet. The shock had already gone past the dimension where enduring pain or letting out a scream was even possible. There was nothing left for Subaru to choose.

The wound in his back must have reached his lung. No matter how many ragged, rasping breaths he drew, his lung wouldn't inflate, and breathing grew harder and harder. The lack of oxygen took a heavy toll on Subaru's functioning, and his thoughts grew so faint they seemed on the verge of cutting out entirely.

The feeling in his hands and feet vanished, and he couldn't even tell if he was lying face down or face up. This time his eyes hadn't been cut, and yet his vision was pitch black, showing him nothing at all.

—This time. What do you mean, this time?

It was pitiful, clinging to a thought he'd already dismissed as ridiculous. And if he was going to cling to that pitiful thought, he decided he might as well cling to it with everything he had.

—Turn your mind away from dying. Before you die, grasp the world around you.

His eyes were dead. His hands and feet were finished too. What remained was his nose and ears, more or less. Then he'd use both of those to their absolute limit. Any lingering scent would do, even if all he got was a mouthful of curses. The smell of mud in the alley. The coppery reek of blood welling up. Now—his nose had died. Died. His ears, too, had only a scrap of function left.

"...got anything worth... even..."

"...! Guard's coming, we gotta—"

"...run! This is bad! If they... it won't be a joke anymore...!"

That was all he managed to catch—just fragments of conversation. He'd caught them, fine, but the brain needed to make sense of them was already dead. He'd only heard because he'd heard. Whether he could remember what he'd heard, he didn't know. What did it even mean, to remember. What would remembering even accomplish. Accomplish—what was that supposed to mean. What was—

As if obeying the brain that had died before anything else, one function after another breathed its last, and at the very end, letting out a faint, rasping sound as it slipped away, Natsuki Subaru lost his life for the third time.

When his consciousness surfaced, Subaru was in darkness. Realizing it was a darkness of his own making, he gently pried open his closed eyelids—and the dazzling sunlight burned his eyes with such pain that Subaru let out a small groan and cupped a hand over his brow as a visor.

"Hey, kid. The Appa?"

A familiar voice was addressing him from right in front, asking that very question. His ears worked fine. The bustle of the main street was as noisy as ever, worlds apart from the brutal stillness of that back alley. And yet the distance between them was nothing more than turning off one street, over just the width of a single road.

"Not even able to turn off one street—pathetic, huh."

The vegetable stand owner with the conspicuous white scar heard Subaru's self-deprecating mutter, but not recognizing it as a reply meant for him, scowled with displeasure. He had a forbidding look about him, but Subaru knew from firsthand experience that he was, in fact, surprisingly attentive underneath it. Though he supposed the man himself had no memory of that. Thinking that, Subaru turned back to face that scarred face once more.

"How many times have you seen my face now?"

"What do you mean, how many times—you're a new face, kid. I wouldn't forget someone dressed like that."

"What's today's date, by the way?"

"The fourteenth day of the month of Tammuz."

"Thanks. —I see, the month of Tammuz."

Hearing it told him nothing. To begin with, how exactly was the calendar kept track of in this other world? Given that talk of a specific month and day made sense, he supposed some kind of solar calendar must exist here. It was probably common knowledge, but not the kind of thing he could easily ask about—especially not to a vegetable seller who, even now, was pouring his passion enthusiastically into making a sale.

The shopkeeper had patiently put up with Subaru's silence, but he'd apparently decided he couldn't keep dealing with a customer who took this long just to buy a single piece of fruit. He thrust the Appa he was holding out in his palm forward, urging Subaru, for who knew how many times now, to make up his mind.

"So, kid, the Appa?"

It was a business smile, his rough features forced into the shape of a grin with all his effort. The white scar twisted as he did, and though it was meant to be a friendly smile, the result looked like something no child would warm to—not exactly funny to watch. To that man, Subaru answered, hand on his hip, chest puffed out—

"Sorry, but I'm eternally, infinitely broke!"

"Get the hell outta here—!"

Struck by a bellow loud enough to make him reel back, Subaru fled in an undignified scramble. Guess I won't be able to show my face at that stand again for a while, he thought—meaning it in two different ways at once.

  • "Appa" (リンガ) — per the provided glossary this is the wiki term; in context it functions as a fruit sold at a vegetable stand, retained as-is.
  • "天壌無窮の一文無し" is a play on "天壌無窮" (an idiom meaning "eternal as heaven and earth," lit. used for the eternity of the imperial line) mashed with "一文無し" (penniless). Rendered as "eternally, infinitely broke" to preserve the comedic grandiosity rather than translate the idiom literally.
  • "トン・チン・カン" (Ton/Chin/Kan) is a pun on "頓珍漢" (tonchinkan, meaning "nonsensical" or "absurd/foolish"); the individual syllables are kept as nicknames since the pun is more in the collective sound than individually translatable — flagging in case the wiki has an established rendering.
