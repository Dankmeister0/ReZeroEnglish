Arc 4 Chapter 33: "The Wind-Swept Path"

Frederica lowered her eyes as she spoke of her brother that way. The feeling wrapped up in it seemed at once like a negative emotion and like affection—a complicated thing. Though perhaps, as feelings go toward one's own blood, it's not so unusual after all.

"He can breach the barrier on a physical level, but something in him won't let him cross it on an emotional one... is that how you see it?"

"In fact, he didn't answer when I, his own sister, called out to him. He followed me right up to the edge of the barrier, but in the end he chose to stay with Grandmother inside it rather than come with me."

"Grandmother... you mean Ryuzu-san?"

"That child has a foul mouth, but he truly adores Grandmother. Unless her long-cherished wish is fulfilled, there's no chance he'll ever come outside."

He's conspicuous for cursing at her—old hag this, old hag that—but it's plain to anyone watching from the sidelines that what Garfiel feels for Ryuzu goes beyond mere familiarity into real affection. Subaru had once called him a tsundere, and it seemed that was exactly right. In any case, uncovering that didn't change the situation one way or another.

"So in the end, breaking through the 'Trials' to release the 'Sanctuary' is still the one non-negotiable condition. Disappointing, if I'm honest."

"I'm sorry I couldn't meet your expectations. ...If there's anything else you'd like to ask?"

"Within what you're allowed to answer, right?"

"I'm afraid so."

Answering her spare words with a huff through the nose, Subaru mentally lists off a few more questions. But given the pattern so far, probably—

"Is Roswaal's true intentions the kind of topic I can ask about?"

"My master intends to support Lady Emilia and see her become the ruler of Lugunica. On that point, I can state without a shadow of doubt."

"The fact that you lead with that disclaimer the moment I mention his 'true intentions' means even you think what Roswaal's been doing lately doesn't line up with it, doesn't it?"

"That he's choosing roundabout, hard-to-follow means—that much, I don't think either Ram or I would deny."

Weaving in, without quite saying so, her own assessment of Roswaal's conduct, she answers with a pained expression. As though she accepts Subaru's doubts as only natural, and yet is forbidden even from showing him the key that would unlock them. Which is to say—

"Beyond that point, you can't speak without Roswaal's own permission."

"I'm sorry. Only this much I can say... my master is on Lady Emilia's side, and on yours, Subaru-sama. As long as the two of you hold to your resolve to win the Royal Selection, that much at least is certain."

"That phrasing is driving me nuts, but... fine, whatever. Setting Roswaal aside, I'm starting to feel like I can trust you, Frederica. If it were someone as head-over-heels devoted to Roswaal as Ram, my feelings aside, I'd have a hard time knowing what to make of their judgment."

As one person to another, Subaru holds Ram in fairly high regard, but that doesn't automatically translate into absolute trust—it's a difficult relationship in that sense. At the very least, so long as her order of priorities never wavers from Roswaal being absolute, and so long as that same Roswaal can't be fully trusted right now, Subaru has no choice but to reserve judgment on Ram as well.

"If you can't tell me Roswaal's true intentions, then... what did Garfiel mean, calling the 'Sanctuary' a testing ground?"

"A testing ground—is that what he said?"

"Also called it a dumping ground for people with nowhere else to go. The 'nowhere else to go' part, frankly, I can guess at from the whole demi-human conversation earlier. And how Roswaal's supposedly got this thing for demi-humans, housing the half-bloods who have no place to go in the 'Sanctuary.' But—"

The ominousness of the word "testing ground," and Roswaal's aim in gathering the half-bloods there—which, in a certain sense, amounts to confinement—remain a mystery to him. Or, if things have gone badly, there's a chance Emilia is allied with someone she shouldn't be.

"In the first place, if it got out that your family's been keeping charge, generation after generation, of a facility with ties to a witch—even if not the 'Witch of Envy'—wouldn't that be a fairly serious problem? I've heard there aren't records left or whatever, but if the fact of it survives to the grave regardless—"

"The word 'witch' itself has come to carry an evil meaning, hasn't it. I imagine people wouldn't take kindly to the 'Witch of Greed,' the one my master is in Contract with, either. That concern of yours is, I think, entirely justified, Subaru-sama."

"Glad we agree that the 'Sanctuary's' existence is a problem, at least. So, does 'testing ground' overwrite that shared understanding any further?"

"...Originally, that place was a hidden village where the 'Witch of Greed' gathered half-bloods in order to conduct a certain experiment. What sort of negotiation passed between the witch and House Mathers, who owned the land at the time, is unknown, but it's said that, on the strength of that agreement, House Mathers has managed and maintained the 'Sanctuary' ever since, generation after generation."

Nodding along as Frederica speaks haltingly, Subaru works to organize the information. He'd more or less pieced together that much already, stitching together what he'd heard from the people in the 'Sanctuary.' Which leaves the real questions—

"What the witch was experimenting on the half-bloods for, and why Roswaal's kept honoring that agreement even after the witch died... huh."

"The latter reason is simple. The terms of the Contract are: 'maintain the Sanctuary in accordance with the Oath sworn to the witch, until the Sanctuary is released.' The environment of the 'Sanctuary' can't sustain itself without people being periodically brought in."

"So that's why it's treated as a hideaway for half-bloods with complicated circumstances. Hearing just that much, what Roswaal's doing almost sounds like a form of charity."

Given that discrimination is a real, present fact, securing a place where they can find peace is a necessity. If Roswaal is the one fulfilling that role, Subaru's assessment of him needs revising. Although—

"Doesn't seem like every single half-blood there is exactly clamoring to stay, though. Actually, the fact that a lot of them are following Ryuzu-san's lead and hoping for the 'Sanctuary' to be released kind of says as much, doesn't it?"

"...The prejudice against demi-humans has faded a good deal, in truth. My brother and I ending up in the 'Sanctuary' had less to do with this blood of ours than simply having nowhere else to belong. And eventually, the 'Sanctuary' will be released. —Which is precisely why I—"

Frederica shuts her eyes tight and cuts herself off. Subaru falls silent at her manner, and after a moment, ventures, hesitant—

"Might just be me reading too much into it, but... is it possible you left the 'Sanctuary' with what happens after it's released already in mind?"

"...Why would you think that?"

"Why? Come on, you get this sad look on your face whenever you talk about the 'Sanctuary,' and yet you still tore yourself away from your hometown and came out here—there's only two reasons anybody does that: for themselves, or for somebody else. And on top of that..."

Scratching his cheek, what comes to Subaru's mind is a fierce-looking young man with short-cropped golden hair. Compared to the gentle woman in front of him, they're a mismatched pair, alike only around the mouth—both stubbornly unable to say what they mean—

"If Garfiel's habit of hiding his true feelings runs in the family, it wouldn't be strange at all if everything you've been doing turns out to be embarrassingly full of consideration for others underneath. You... when the 'Sanctuary' finally does get released someday, aren't you out here building a place so the people who come out won't be left stranded? Working here, I mean—sure, you probably owe Roswaal a debt of gratitude, but I don't think that's all there is to it. ...That's the kind of thing I've been thinking, anyway."

Aware, even as the words tumble out too fast, that his logic has taken something of a leap, Subaru endures the embarrassment and steals a sideways glance at Frederica. If this turns out to be wildly off the mark and gets laughed off, well, it'll just be plain embarrassing that Subaru got so worked up over it, but—

"When a new world eventually opens up... I wanted to be someone who could reach out a hand and lead them into that place."

Murmured quietly, and a smile rises on Frederica's face as she says it. But it isn't sarcasm aimed at Subaru for voicing something wide of the mark—there's nothing in it but the openness of someone re-examining the shape of her own heart and, for the first time, confiding it to another.

"I was raised by that place. Now I want to build the kind of environment where I can nurture, in turn, the will in others to step out from it. If I could even be some small part of building that environment, then surely there's meaning to the fact that I was born—even if I might have been born unwanted."

"Unwanted, that's not... something you should—"

"I don't need comforting words. Given the circumstances, I can hardly imagine my mother wanted to be carrying me. In fact, she abandoned both my brother and me at the 'Sanctuary.' That's the answer—and yet, because I didn't want to let the story end there, that answer alone, I'm standing here now."

It's a question to which she has already, within herself, arrived at an answer. Which is exactly why Subaru's sympathy, knowing only the surface of it, can't reach her heart. More than anything, she carries the answer she's arrived at, and still chooses a path that doesn't end with that answer alone. —She's strong, Subaru thinks, plainly. Strong enough to admire. A strength of conviction like that.

"...Does Garfiel know? This, what you really feel? If he knows, and still didn't come with you, then—"

"My brother is the only one I've told everything to—all of it, everything I carry. That he still didn't come with me is simply... the choice he made. Rather than go and obtain something hard to gain, he chose to stay and protect something easily lost. That our paths as brother and sister diverged is nothing more, and nothing less, than that."

"Protect... protect, huh. Going by his looks, he didn't strike me as the type to pick that. Well, you can't really tell what's in someone's heart from the outside, I guess."

Touching his chin, Subaru tips back his teacup in one go, drains it, and stifles a burp. Then, wiping his mouth lightly with the back of his hand, he prefaces with "So," and continues—

"Feels like you're trying to dodge, so let's circle back—what about the word 'testing ground'? Can you tell me what the experiment was... or do you even know what it was?"

"I'm afraid I don't know the content or the purpose of it. To begin with, once the 'Witch of Greed' died, continuing the experiment became impossible. Only the facility itself remained, and House Mathers has simply been maintaining it since."

"When you put it that way, it just sounds pointless. I get it, keeping a promise matters, believe me—but is there really any meaning in still honoring it four hundred years after the other party's dead?"

"At the very least, if my master and his line hadn't kept honoring that promise, my brother and I would have had no peace at all in our childhood."

"Ugh... that's— ah, right, I didn't think that through. Sorry."

At Subaru's frank apology, Frederica lets out a short laugh. Then she empties her own cup as well, gathers up the one Subaru finished, and rises to her feet.

"I've gone on far too long. Let's call it here for now. What will you do from here, Subaru-sama?"

"Originally I just came along to see the Arlam Village people back to the village. Once I get the answers I want out of everyone, I'll head straight back... though it looks like it's too late for today, so tomorrow morning, probably."

"I see. Then tonight and tomorrow morning, Petra will be quite fired up, I imagine. Given how it might scatter her attention, whether that's a good thing is another matter."

"Good to hear Petra's education is coming along. Where's Petra now?"

"Back in the village, I should think, showing her face to everyone who's returned. I told her to do just that."

Even before Subaru conveys his intent, she's already anticipated it—that's Frederica for you. Watching her back as she carries the cups off with a clatter, Subaru rises from his seat as well, counting off on his fingers what's left to do.

What he managed to draw out of Frederica amounts to maybe half of everything he wanted. Even so, it feels like enough information to push his reasoning a bit further forward. All that's left is to track down the one remaining person who seems to know the rest.

"Gonna take time, but let's just comb through the whole mansion for now..."

Subaru's shoulders slump before the heavy labor looming ahead of him. Glancing sideways at his back as she leaves the room, Frederica murmurs, in a small voice—

"The content and the purpose, unknown... and yet the result alone, known. That testing ground. When he learns of it, when it's learned of him... I wonder how he'll feel."

That murmur never reached Subaru at all, lost in thought as he was.

Everyone in the mansion knew how troublesome Beatrice's "Door Crossing" could be, but it wasn't until after returning from the royal capital, Subaru thinks, that he truly felt it in his bones.

Intuition, when faced with multiple options—purely a matter of gut instinct—was one of the few things Subaru could genuinely boast about. His knack for pulling the correct answer out of a no-hint multiple choice with no particular reason for it was, in a sense, a skill about as socially useful as being unable to "read the room"—but in this world, it had at least served him well in terms of his odds of running into Beatrice. That intuition, ever since coming back from the royal capital, simply hasn't been working right.

"Weird. I should've gone through every single door in this mansion by now..."

Here's the last one, he tells himself, pinning his hopes on it, opening the door of a bathroom stall—then closing it again, expressing his disappointment at falling short of his goal with a sigh and a tilt of the head. That makes it, cumulatively since returning from the royal capital, one win against a miserable string of losses in the Guess-Bea-ko game. Considering his batting average before had been close to a flawless ten-for-ten, "slump" doesn't even begin to cover it. Faced with this state of affairs, Subaru has no choice but to admit it.

"That little brat's straight-up avoiding me, seriously."

No one can break through Beatrice's "Door Crossing" when she's serious about it—so Ram once said—but Subaru had overturned that piece of conventional wisdom before, with no particular reasoning behind it. And somewhere along the way, Subaru had come to feel a certain sense of superiority over Beatrice regarding it—not so much about beating the "Door Crossing" itself, as about having, of everyone in the mansion, the biggest advantage when it came to her.

"Just because things ended badly the last time doesn't mean you have to hole up this hard. ...If you won't show your face, I can't even fight with you, let alone apologize."

He has no idea what it was about his words back then that made her choose this much rejection. He doesn't know—but as long as he's kept at a physical distance like this, whatever he doesn't know is going to stay that way. And he hates that. Setting aside the pressing need to ask her things, Subaru simply wanted to see Beatrice and talk to her. Snide remarks would be fine. Being talked down to and mocked would be fine too. There is a everyday life that's been lost. Having learned that, Subaru couldn't bear for another piece of that everyday life to vanish in front of him. Even knowing that, too, was a selfish way to think.

"Neither Puck nor Bea-ko will give me so much as one conversation when it actually matters, huh."

Puck, still nowhere to be found, and Beatrice, who's holed herself up along with her entire pocket dimension—neither of them will show themselves to Emilia when Subaru needs them most. Being relied on only when there's trouble—that's a rotten thing to do, same as some god up in heaven.

Still, the situation is too pressing to spend time wallowing in sentiment like this. With Elsa, a plainly visible threat, bearing down on the mansion, and no way to drive her off, evacuating everyone connected to the mansion is the top priority. Surely even Elsa wouldn't extend her blade to Arlam Village, which has nothing to do with the mansion. All the more reason securing the safety of everyone connected to it is, for Subaru, the single most important matter at hand.

Getting Rem and Petra out won't be hard. Frederica, too, can probably be moved if he appeals to her sense of professional duty. The only problem is Beatrice, whom he can't even manage to meet. Once before, in a loop that began in the royal capital, Subaru had failed to get her out of the mansion. That he'd still been able to leave her behind in the mansion at that point was only because he'd known the Witch Cult's target, the threat back then, wasn't the mansion itself. But this time is different. Elsa has broken into the mansion, and shows no hesitation about swinging her blade once inside. Regardless of whether Emilia, her true target, is present or not, that blade of hers is thinking of nothing but slitting open the belly of whoever's in the mansion.

He has no idea just how much fighting strength Beatrice actually has. He doesn't know, but Elsa is someone who fought Puck, who's in a Contract with Emilia, to a standstill—a monster who, in Subaru's imagination, might even be able to go toe-to-toe with Wilhelm. If Beatrice were to face Elsa directly, Subaru simply cannot picture a scenario where Beatrice comes out on top.

"Maybe I've just got too strong an aversion to Elsa at this point. ...Not that it's unreasonable, after getting killed by her three times. —Wait."

Hand pressed to his stomach where a phantom pain flickers, Subaru, walking down the hallway, comes to a sudden stop. Ahead, in his line of sight, is a door noticeably more lavish than any other in the mansion, located in the central room of the mansion's top floor—Roswaal's study. With the master of the house currently absent, Subaru is fully aware that stepping in uninvited goes well beyond a mere breach of manners, but—

"Come to think of it, there's something I wanted to check in this room too."

Saying so, he takes hold of the door without much hesitation and steps inside. The scene inside the study, once he steps in, is naturally unchanged from earlier, when he'd gone around opening every door in the mansion. With Roswaal himself, who does the actual work here, not having returned, the room remains exactly as it was when Subaru had Otto working himself to the bone organizing the documents. The documents and bookshelves that had been piled up haphazardly have been tidied by the meticulous Otto, leaving the room with the cleanliness of a freshly cleaned space. Looking around, Subaru surveys the room, then heads toward the back, to the two bookshelves standing side by side next to the ebony desk.

"Behind this bookshelf, there's a hidden passage."

A hidden passage he's confirmed the existence of twice now—likely serving as an evacuation route or something similar—but how to activate it and where it led were, to Subaru, still unknown quantities.

"It was open the last time Elsa attacked, so I'm pretty sure you can escape somewhere from here... but the last time I went in, I froze to death partway through."

A memory that ended with him turned to an ice statue, alongside the Witch Cultist who'd apparently touched a nerve in Puck. A ghastly memory of fingers snapping off and limbs shattering, but since it ended without pain and much of it is hazy, it doesn't quite reach the point of making him shudder. Still, death is death. He has no intention of taking it lightly. In any case—

"If I know where the evacuation route leads, I can at least plan for the worst case. Or, follow the evacuation route the other way, and get into the mansion that way. ...Not that I can imagine that situation coming up, though."

With securing everyone's safety as the top priority, confirming the evacuation route is essential. It's probably safe to assume it leads somewhere in the mountains behind the mansion. As far as standard evacuation-route etiquette goes, it'd be even better if there's an emergency kit stashed somewhere along the way, for disasters and the like.

"For confirmation's sake too, let's get right to the evacuation route... is what I'm thinking, but—"

Just what, exactly, does one need to do to trigger the mechanism for the evacuation route? For now, he grabs the bookshelf and tries moving it with all his strength, but the shelf, packed solid with contents, doesn't budge an inch even with Subaru putting his full arm strength into it. If he cleared the contents out, leaving just the empty shelf, there might be some chance of moving it, but—

"There's no way something this leisurely is allowed in the exact moment you're supposed to be hurrying to evacuate—there's gotta be a switch or something somewhere to move it."

Thinking so, he rummages behind the desk and in the recesses of the bookshelf, but finds nothing resembling a mechanism. Though there was one moment where he found a false bottom in a drawer stuffed with jewels, and quietly decided to pretend he hadn't seen it.

"Guess I give up... in the worst case, maybe it's not even in this room."

"Not in this room—what isn't?"

"Oh, obviously, the hidden switch-type something. I want to peek at the hidden passage behind the bookshelf, but if I can't find the thing, this isn't going anywhere."

"Ah, you mean the escape route. That's this statue, over here."

Petra tugs at the sleeve of Subaru, who's tilting his head. Pulled along by her, Subaru looks where the girl is pointing and nods, "Huh"—

"That statue in the corner of the room, the one that looked kind of ordinary... this thing has the trick to it?"

The statue, modeled after a seated human figure, is small enough to sit on a desk. In a study with so few furnishings, it carried a somewhat uncanny presence, but Petra, without a trace of hesitation, walks right up to it—

"Hyah."

With a small grunt, she twists the statue's head. As if the neck were being wrenched clean off, the statue's head spins a full hundred eighty degrees to face backward. Subaru frowns, feeling like he's just watched someone's shinbone snapped—but right after that,

"Oh—oh, oh—"

The sound of something heavy sliding sideways echoes through the room, and before Subaru, who turns at the noise, the bookshelf in question splits and moves apart to either side. And once the bookshelf has finished moving, what's revealed is an entrance opening into darkness, with just enough space secured for one person.

The evacuation route he'd been after has revealed itself, and Subaru gives a small fist pump in triumph.

"This, this, this is it. This is what I was looking for—you're a lifesaver."

"Fufu, right? I was properly taught by Frederica-neesama. She told me to remember it, as a way to escape if it ever comes to that."

"Nice, thanks a ton. Now let's get right to it... Petra, when did you even—!"

"Only now!?"

She'd joined in so seamlessly that Subaru, lost in deep thought, was slow to notice her presence. At Subaru's all-too-rude reaction, Petra puffs out her lips, pouting.

"I hurried all the way back here just to help you out, and... I think that's a little mean, Subaru-sama."

"No, I mean, I was thinking to myself, feeling like I was talking to no one, wondering who I was even talking to partway through—but I got so caught up in the joy of reaching my goal that I was slow to notice. My bad, my bad."

The girl turns her face away, sulking. Apologizing, Subaru gives her head a light pat, then turns his attention properly to the passage they've been calling the evacuation route.

"By the way, Petra, did Frederica tell you where this leads?"

"Yes. Frederica-neesama said it connects to a small hut partway up the mountain out back. There's a barrier separate from the one that wards off Witchbeasts, so it can't be seen from outside."

"I see. So it really is a hidden passage. But I've gotta see it with my own eyes to be sure."

Once he confirms where it comes out on the mountain, it should prove useful both as an evacuation route and as an infiltration route. Rolling up his sleeves in a show of motivation, Subaru heads for the entrance with a face full of resolve. And behind him, small footsteps follow—Petra tagging along.

"Wait, you're coming too, Petra?"

"Is that no good?"

"Not no good, exactly, but it's probably not gonna be interesting even if you come? I'm just gonna go look where it connects to out of pure curiosity, and then come straight back."

"It's my break time right now, so I'm free too. Is it okay if I stay with you?"

Pinching the hem of her clothes, Petra looks up at him with puppy-dog eyes. Being clung to so much makes it hard to push her away, and Subaru lets out a breath, smiling wryly.

"You're really something, wanting to tag along on a trip that's literally just there and back, Petra."

"If I wasn't something, I wouldn't be here in the first place... and I'm glad I'm something."

Petra answers Subaru's words that way. He's not quite sure what she means by it, but Subaru papers over it with a smile for now, takes the hand offered to him, and heads for the evacuation route. The dark, continuing hidden passage takes the form of a spiral staircase, its walls themselves made of some material that glows faintly, palely blue. There's little risk of losing the path down to the lower floor, but Subaru, who knows it leads all the way underground, turns back—

"The stairs are pretty long and dark, so watch your step so you don't slip, okay?"

"If I slip, will you catch me?"

"I'd just end up tumbling all the way to the bottom with you in my arms, so please spare me that... if that leaves you crippled for life, I really couldn't live with myself."

"Then I'll take care of you for the rest of your life."

"I appreciate the sentiment, but the process sounds terrifying!"

Trading remarks like that, with Subaru leading the way, the two begin descending the stairs. A chill, cold wind rises up from below, and the image of Puck, who shouldn't be down there, flickers through Subaru's mind, sending a shiver up his spine. Not that he's afraid of a future freezing-to-death that shouldn't even exist, but—

"Going down in silence feels pointless, and Petra might get scared, so let's talk about something."

"Subaru-sama, your palm's a little sweaty, you know?"

"Petra might get scared, so let's talk! How was everyone in the village?"

Petra, meeting the way Subaru insists on framing things as being for the sake of the younger one, indulges him with a look of fond amusement and plays along. And so, keeping up small talk enough that silence doesn't fall between them, they descend for several minutes—the stairs come to an end, and they reach the narrow passage Subaru already knows. A little further along that passage is a door, beyond which lies territory Subaru has never experienced.

"Judging by how it's felt so far, we've only gone down under the mansion itself. If the passage keeps going from here all the way to the mountain out back, this tunnel's gotta be pretty long."

"Evacuation route, escape route, tunnel—we can't seem to settle on what to call it."

"True. In the spirit of the wind blowing from Mexico, let's just call it Santana."

Petra beautifully ignores Subaru's nonsense. Subaru feels a mix of joy and loneliness at how quickly her skill at handling him has improved. Carrying that nostalgic feeling with him as they proceed down the passage, they eventually reach a somewhat wider space. Ahead, a door floats dimly in the darkness, confirming this is a small chamber. Once, this space had been crowded shoulder to shoulder with the ice statues of Witch Cultists, but this time, naturally, there's no trace of that. Letting out a quiet breath of relief, Subaru murmurs—

"Guess as expected, no trauma switch got flipped this time. For now, we've made it this far—about a third of the way to the mountain hut, maybe?"

"The wind's cold... is it beyond that door?"

Heedless of Subaru's relief, Petra can't quite hide her anticipation for the door ahead leading to a new stage. Subaru nods, "Yeah," too, and—

"Last time I touched the door, everything went dark on me right then and there, so from here on it's completely unfamiliar territory... well, let's just see where the road takes us."

We'll decide from there, he tells himself, and without much tension, Subaru puts his hand to the door. Then he pushes it open, and as a cold wind rushes forcefully into the small chamber, striking him full in the face—

"—guh?"

At the same moment as a light sound, Subaru realized something had struck his stomach. He drops his gaze, looking at his lower left abdomen where the impact hit. And there, something like a skewer stands embedded, its tail end still trembling, proof of just how recently it struck.

—Seeping slowly, watching blood soak into his clothes, Subaru's throat freezes solid, and—

"Aaahh—!?"

In place of Subaru, his throat sealed shut with shock, it was Petra's throat, noticing the same wound, that let out a high, piercing scream. It rang shrill through the narrow passage, striking hard against Subaru's eardrums. In that instant before the pain came rushing up, Subaru, with no idea what had just happened, turned his mind over at full speed toward what had to be done.

Petra's scream trails on and on. In the echoing passage, all other sound is swallowed up; only her voice remains. And within that world where nothing should be audible, Subaru heard it anyway.

The sound of footsteps, and a blade being drawn from its sheath, and—

"Now then, let's make good on our promise—"

The voice of a murderer, wetting her lips with a red tongue, trembling with the anticipation of slaughter.
