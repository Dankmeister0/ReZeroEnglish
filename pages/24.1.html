Side Story: "Emilia's Royal Capital Sightseeing Record"

1

"—Wow, the royal capital really is huge, isn't it."

The girl spoke in a bright, bouncing voice, hands clasped before her chest, eyes shining at the sight before her.

She was a beautiful girl. Long hair like silken silver thread, reaching down to her waist. The braided silver locks were adorned with white flowers, quite literally lending blossoms to her charm. Skin white as fresh snow, and long, slender limbs wrapped in garments done in white — the eyes that surveyed the cityscape shone a beautiful violet-blue, like a jewel set in place.

Hers was a beauty one could call without-equal without the slightest hesitation. And yet, the girl surveyed her surroundings with a restless, wide-eyed manner too childish for such a description.

Left and right, her eyes chased the flowing tide of people. One glance at those eyes, alight with curiosity at all the novelty around her, made it plain — this was the excitement of the sort people call a wide-eyed country visitor.

"You're cute enough having fun like that, Lia, but I think it's about time you faced reality."

The voice that threw cold water on things arrived as a whisper against the girl's ears — ears just a touch longer than an ordinary person's. And yet there was no one else in sight around her. But the voice had, quite literally, come from right beside her ear—

"—Mm, I know we're in trouble, okay. I only stopped for just a teensy bit, and you're being so mean about it."

"Well, I don't want my girl hating me for nagging either. But if we leave things as they are, there's a certain someone who's going to end up in trouble because of it — and you don't want that, do you, Lia?"

"That's... true, but..."

At the voice's unassailable logic, the girl drooped, crestfallen. And there, at the shoulder of the drooping girl, a small shadow parted her silver hair and popped into view with a hop. It was—

"Anyway, let's go look around the places we might find her. No use moping about it."

The one making this forward-looking suggestion while fiddling with its whiskers was a tiny kitten, small enough to sit on a palm.

Gray fur, round, wide eyes, a small pink nose — setting aside its size, that about summed up its features. What set it apart most from an ordinary cat, though, was that it walked on two legs.

Perched on the girl's shoulder, the cat swished a tail nearly as long as its own body, and purred.

"The last resort is working up the courage to ask someone. Can you do that, Lia?"

"Don't make fun of me. I can manage something that simple just fine, thank you very much. Honestly, Puck, you're always treating me like a kid."

"If getting so worked up you lose track of your escort isn't being a kid, then what is it, I wonder?"

At the easygoing yet pointed remark, all the girl could do was pout, lips pursed in a sulk.

At the girl who denied being treated like a child only to immediately act like one, the kitten — called Puck — laughed.

"Well, it'll be trouble once the sun sets. Let's get you un-lost while I can still help, Emilia."

"I'm not lost. I just don't know the way for a little bit, that's all."

Stubborn against his words, the girl — Emilia — set off walking with a prim, aloof look on her face, toward the street ahead, where the tide of people came and went without pause, to search for the attendant girl she'd lost.

2

The royal capital of Lugunica, with a population exceeding three hundred thousand, was one of the great cities of the world, and the beating heart of the Dragonfriend Kingdom.

With the royal castle set at its center, the city was a prosperous capital where all manner of races mingled together — the royal capital brimmed with vitality at all hours, and one could watch the people passing along its streets without growing bored for a whole day.

Especially in the commercial district where peddlers and shops gathered in great numbers — along Merchant Street — rare curiosities and fine goods brought in from other countries lined the way, and the district was forever awash in a dizzying clamor.

"...Wow. So many people."

Having somehow crawled her way out of the crowd and taken refuge at the edge of the street, Emilia let out a breath. She smoothed down her tousled silver hair, and after a moment's rest, took another look at the street.

Countless people came and went along Merchant Street, talking, haggling, over and over. This many people, in just one street, one commercial district. Just thinking about how hard it would be to find a single person she was looking for among them was enough to make her spirits sink.

"That girl was dressed distinctively enough, too... but here in the royal capital, that counts for nothing."

Puck, hidden within the waves of her silver hair, spoke Emilia's inner thoughts aloud for her. Emilia poked at the kitten through the gesture of touching her hair, and let the corners of her brows droop in dismay.

"Yeah, this is a problem. ...Also, Puck, you need to be more careful. If someone spots you it'll cause a huge fuss, so if you don't have a reason to be out, you could just go back into the crystal."

"I do have a reason — keeping you company, Lia. Wouldn't want you crying from being all alone right now. Anyway, what should we do? Wander around blindly, hoping we stumble on her by chance?"

"Put that way, it sounds like we'd never, ever find her. We have to use our heads more."

Shaking her head at Puck's suggestion, Emilia tapped her own head with a finger, once, twice.

Puck groomed himself and waited quietly for the brilliant idea to emerge from Emilia's deep thought. But what came out of her next was not a brilliant idea at all—

"—Look, Puck. Over there, someone's having an argument."

"Liiia—"

"Just a bit! Just a little bit! I'm curious, so I'll just go take a tiny peek!"

Across the street, a little way off, she could see a crowd of onlookers gathered around a street stall. At Emilia's remark upon noticing them, Puck held his small head in his paws and sighed.

"Listen, Lia, you're the one in trouble here too, you know?"

"N-No, that's not it. It's not that I want to go help or anything... yeah, I'm just feeling like a rubbernecker."

Putting on the most mischievous face she could manage — which was still adorable, of course — Emilia trotted off toward the commotion. Pushing through the crowd, she leaned herself right to the front of the onlookers. And then—

"That's what Mimi's been sayin' this whole time! Mimi wouldn't dine and dash! It's just that Mimi got separated from Hetaro, who's got the wallet, so Mimi's in a real bind, see!"

"You're the one making me repeat myself, missy. I don't know anything about this someone you got separated from, but you already ate the goods, so you gotta pay what's owed. Ahh, if your companion's not around, then I'm saying maybe your belongings could cover it instead..."

"All Mimi's got is this staff and stuff, but if Mimi hands that over, Boss's gonna be super mad at Mimi—"

Surrounded by onlookers, the two arguing figures made Emilia's eyes go wide.

One half of the argument was a tall, dark-skinned man — by his appearance, likely the proprietor of the stall in question. And facing him, arguing on and on, was a much smaller, much shorter figure.

A beastfolk girl with orange fur and round ears, shaped like a small cat — likely of the race known as Kitten Humans. She stood no taller than Emilia's waist, her entire body swallowed up in a large white robe. With perfectly round eyes and an adorable little nose, she was a girl whose entire being seemed composed of nothing but cuteness.

From the gist of the conversation, it seemed the girl was unable to pay the stallkeeper for something, but—

"Mimi doesn't wanna be thought of as a dine-and-dasher, y'know. Hey, mister, you want this staff?"

"Even I'd feel bad taking that! This is worth something, isn't it? Man, what a mess..."

"Geez, Hetaro's always getting lost, no good! Mimi's the big sister, so Mimi's gonna give him a proper scolding."

"Um..."

Just as things between the two parties began to unravel into a mess, a figure raised a hand and cut in. Standing between the surprised girl and shopkeeper, both of whom turned startled faces toward the interruption, was none other than Emilia.

Timidly stepping between them, Emilia met their gazes and continued, "Um..."

"I was listening, and... I just need to pay for what she ate, right?"

"Ah, yeah, that's right, but... miss, are you an acquaintance of this little lady here?"

"Eh, are you and Mimi acquainted, big sis? First Mimi's heard of it! Never met her before!"

"No, I don't know her at all either. This is the first time I've seen her."

"Whaaat—!?"

Caught between the kitten girl's and Emilia's twin airheadedness, the shopkeeper let out a despairing cry. But Emilia paid his reaction no mind, quietly slipping a hand into the folds of her dress.

"But I'll pay the money in her place. That solves the problem, doesn't it?"

"Well, that'd be a huge help for me, sure... but isn't she got nothing to do with you?"

"That's true, we're not related, but... she's just so incredibly cute."

It was hard to deny that this reason had been made up on the spot, but the shopkeeper apparently took it as some kind of thoughtfulness on Emilia's part. Smiling at the shopkeeper, who nodded in surrender, Emilia reached to pull a coin purse from her robes—

"...Huh? Huh?"

Her searching hand couldn't seem to find what it was looking for. Tilting her head, Emilia thoroughly searched every corner — her robes, her waist, the sleeves of her garment — and then nodded deeply. "Yep."

"—Looks like I dropped my wallet somewhere."

The girl's forlorn murmur — having lost both her companion and, now, all her money, in the middle of the royal capital — hung heavy over the scene.

3

Having declared she'd cover the payment, only to discover her wallet missing, Emilia found herself in dire straits.

All from trying to help — not a person, but a cat — and this was the result. She could feel, close by, Puck letting out an exasperated sigh from within her silver hair, but with nothing to say in her own defense, Emilia only fretted.

She had to find some way to break through this situation. But just what method could— As silence began to settle over the scene, Emilia's mind arrived at a single ray of hope. And that was—

"—I'll perform a trick. If you find it entertaining, please toss some money."

"Eh?"

At this sudden declaration of street performance, every onlooker present tilted their head in unison.

Amid the dumbfounded faces, only Emilia — who began preparing as though this had been her plan from the very start — and the Kitten Human girl, delighted and cheering, "Ooh, a trick! Can't wait!" — stood out as the odd ones out.

Naturally, even Puck couldn't quite keep up with the idea that had leapt clean out of left field from Emilia. The kitten shuffled about, repositioning himself within her silver hair, and whispered softly into her ear.

"Lia, Lia, I'm scared to ask, but what's suddenly gotten into you?"

"I remembered, Puck. In places with lots of people like this, if you show off some rare trick, people give you money, right? I thought it'd be perfect for me and that girl right now, since neither of us has any money!"

"Lia trying so hard is cute and all, but sometimes I do wonder if I raised you wrong. Like right now, for instance."

Emilia, who could no longer back down — and had no intention to, besides — paid no heed to Puck's lament.

Leaving everyone else's mental preparedness behind entirely, Emilia stepped forward and took a deep breath. Standing right in front of the stall, right in front of the onlookers, a stage belonging entirely to Emilia naturally came into being.

"I will now have the rarest of animals perform a trick."

"I've just gotten a very bad feeling that I'm about to be dragged into this out of nowhere."

"And — one, two—"

Before the onlookers, Emilia thrust her right hand forward and called out the cue.

The audience wore looks of confusion at the girl showing off an empty, upturned palm. Undaunted even by their reaction, Emilia sent a signal to Puck, who was taking his time making his entrance within her hair.

"C'mon, please. Come on, Puck. Come on, I said."

"I haven't lost my cue — I'm just reluctant, that's all. Can't leave you up here to be made a spectacle of, so I'll come out, but only because I have no choice."

Sighing and shrugging, thoroughly unwilling in every gesture, Puck crawled dejectedly out of her silver hair. His appearance drew a brief stir from the crowd, and in that moment, Puck made his way down from Emilia's shoulder, along her upper arm, over her elbow, and at last, atop her palm, raised both paws and declared, "Victory."

In that instant, the crowd erupted. At their reaction, Emilia felt a solid sense of success—

"This is the rarest of animals. Now, I'll have him..."

"A-A Spirit! It's a Spirit! She's a Spirit Arts user!"

"Crap, run! Drop your weapons and all your cash and run!"

Before Emilia, who had been thinking of having Puck do a little dance for the act, the onlookers scattered and fled in every direction. As they threw down the bundles in their hands, the panic spread beyond just the onlookers themselves. The commotion swelled all at once, and by the time the cloud of dust it had kicked up finally settled, the street had gone utterly empty.

To Emilia, left stunned by the sight, Puck — still perched on her palm — said, washing his face with a paw,

"Everyone runs the moment they see a Spirit. Just like you said, Lia."

"I mean, yeah, but... there was no need for them to be that startled."

In response to Puck's words, Emilia glanced around, just a touch sulkily. All around her lay the bundles the onlookers had thrown down in their flight, and by the rules of street performance, every one of them would technically count as Emilia's to keep as found goods. Of course, she had no intention of doing any such thing.

"...Your dancing is so cute, Puck, they should've stuck around and watched before running off."

"I do love how modestly you describe that reaction as them 'running off,' Lia."

So the two of them traded this deadpan little exchange. And then—

"Ohh! This cat, it talks so much! Why? Why can it talk? Same as Mimi? A Kitten Human? So small! Super small! Ufu hahaha—!"

"This one's got some nerve of her own, huh. Sure, we're an even match in the looks-department when it comes to prettiness, but you and I are different kinds of creatures."

"Is that so? Where'd you get that info?"

"I'm Lia's father, and you're that Hetaro kid's big sister, aren't you?"

"Fer real!!"

Whatever it was that struck her as so funny, the girl in question burst out laughing at Puck's quip. In any case, Emilia was honestly glad this girl, at least, had stayed behind.

If even this girl had run off, it surely would have hit Emilia hard.

"Huh?"

Then, all of a sudden, as Emilia scanned over the scattered bundles, her eyes went round. The cause was one of the packages fallen at her feet — a small white pouch resting atop a slightly grimy wrapped bundle.

Crouching down to pick it up, Emilia checked its feel and nodded.

"It really is my missing wallet. ...But why would it be here?"

"—Some kind soul must've brought it back to you, don't you think?"

Puck said, sounding bored, yawning as he slipped back into her silver hair.

It was a clear sign that his part was over. The Kitten Human girl looked disappointed at this, but the moment Emilia patted her head, she perked right back up, tail swishing side to side.

"Ufuhihihi, big sis, you're super good at petting heads! You'd give Boss a real run for her money! Way better than the captain, even!"

"Really? Fufu, thank you."

"Big sis, you're such a super nice person, Mimi's gonna remember you real good! If you ever got trouble, or some jerk you wanna wreck, you just tell Mimi and Mimi'll cut you a deal!"

"Wreck... I don't quite follow, but okay. I'll remember you too."

She hadn't really understood the meaning, but Emilia accepted the adorable Kitten Human girl's words with open-hearted grace.

Then, still stroking the girl's head, Emilia turned back around. There behind her stood the stallkeeper, who — left behind by the whole situation as he was — couldn't very well abandon his shop.

To the stunned shopkeeper, Emilia opened the mouth of her recovered wallet and said,

"Um, so, how much was it again?"

—and so, once more, resumed her rubbernecker's spirit of "helping people."
