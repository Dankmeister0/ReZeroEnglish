Arc 4 Chapter 6: "The Road to the Sanctuary"

"I'm afraid I cannot accompany you, but please do take care on the road. And if you would, please tell the master that Frederica is keeping watch over the manor."

On the morning of departure, with the dragon carriage drawn up before the mansion's front gate, Frederica—who had come out to see them off—said this and bowed at the waist.

Her bow, performed with impeccable posture, was disciplined to a beauty that left one entranced, carrying a kind of pressure that made whoever received it want to straighten their own spine in turn.

In any case, Subaru and Emilia, on the receiving end of it, exchanged glances.

"No, we're the ones who should apologize, leaving during such a busy time. With Roswaal gone, I really should be the one staying behind as acting lord..."

"Well, when it comes to that kind of practical work, both me and Emilia-tan are hopeless. We can handle chores fine, but the numbers stuff—total amateurs. We dumped it on Otto to sort out, but even that's just a drop in the bucket."

Recalling the fruits of the past two days, Subaru gave a wry smile at how little headway they'd made.

The result of the two of them prowling around the cluttered study was information that amounted to little more than "impossible without an explanation from the person in charge."

Frederica seemed to have some knowledge of it, but she'd need time to fill in the gaps left by her months away from the post, and above all, given the burden of simply keeping the mansion running, they couldn't ask her to take on more work than that.

Emilia handled a few of the simpler matters with the utmost care, while for the rest, they had no choice but to close their ears and pretend not to know that putting it off would come back to bite them eventually.

"Feels like showing up to school without finishing my summer homework. Weirdly enough, I was never actually the type to turn in homework late."

"I don't really get it, but isn't that a good thing? If anything, right now my chest feels really tight. It's not guilt exactly, but... knowing something's wrong and just leaving it be."

"It's not our fault, so—that's how I can just brush it off, but maybe that's hard for Emilia-tan. I get it too, that unease of watching things get worse right in front of your eyes."

The frustration of having no choice but to leave unresolved a problem that shouldn't be left unresolved. Subaru could agree with the guilt Emilia carried over it, but it was also true that they had no means to make up for it.

In the end, they simply lacked the strength for it. So there was nothing to do but go and fetch someone who had strength enough.

"The earth dragons are ready. It's only been a few days since that huge ordeal, and yet Natsuki-san's Patrasche looks raring to go."

"She's a good, hardworking girl, right? Though that's probably more thanks to her original owner's temperament than her current one's. Think she can handle the route guiding okay? Getting lost and stranded partway there would be no joke."

The one who cut into the conversation was Otto, who until then had been up on the coachman's seat talking to the two earth dragons—Patrasche and Frufoo.

The Divine Protection of Word Spirit only worked one way, on Otto alone, so from an outside perspective, the sight of him conversing with the earth dragons looked exactly like a man who'd lost his marbles—but no one was going to mention that.

Unaware of the tepid look Subaru was giving him, Otto took the question at face value and nodded, "Yes."

"Frederica-san's teaching seems to have paid off—there shouldn't be any problems. I think we can arrive in under half a day."

"I see, I see... But wait, are you seriously coming along too?"

"Of course I am!"

After nodding at Otto's confident assurance, Subaru voiced the question, and Otto stamped his foot loudly on the wooden floor, eyes wide.

"After all, this will be my first meeting with the margrave. I'm the one who asked you to introduce me, but when I imagine how you'd introduce me if I weren't there... it's just too terrifying. There's no way I could leave it entirely up to you."

"Whoa, whoa, that kind of trust is embarrassing."

"Yes, indeed, short as our acquaintance has been, I trust you more than enough—trust that you, Natsuki-san, are exactly the sort of person who'll unerringly do the one thing I'd least want you to!"

At being spoken of so harshly, Subaru's mouth twisted, and Emilia, who had been listening quietly until then, suddenly burst out with a "Fufu."

Both men turned their eyes to her laughter, and she raised a small hand.

"Honestly, you two are really such good friends. Every time I see you, you're bickering away so amicably—it almost makes me jealous."

"If you want, I'll get real friendly with you too, Emilia-tan, minus the bickering—just flirting instead. I'll hand my quota of bickering over to my Otto communication time, and pinch some of the friendliness from that Otto time to make up for it."

"If you do that, all that'll be left between you and me is name-calling and fistfights!?"

Subaru had fair confidence in his own way with words—glib or nasty, take your pick—but when it came to physical combat, he had his doubts about taking on Otto. That was because he knew firsthand that despite his gentle-looking exterior, Otto was a man who'd trained his body to a decent degree.

In a previous loop, an exchange of insults had ended with Subaru being thrown bodily. Even now, knowing his opponent wasn't an amateur, he suspected he'd lose in a straight fight.

"Thinking about it like that, my combat stat really is pathetically low. I knew it, but it still stings."

As mentioned, he lost to Otto, and of course he couldn't beat Emilia with her magic either. He'd been handed a beautifully clean defeat by Frederica, and there was no need to even mention the sleeping Rem or the shut-in Beatrice. Which meant, in the mansion's power rankings, the only opponent Subaru could beat was—

"Huh, wait, now that I think about it, Petra—the only one I could probably beat—isn't around. What happened to her?"

"I'm too scared to ask how we got onto the subject of winning or losing, but Petra-chan was in the mansion just a moment ago... ah, there she is."

Exasperated at Subaru, who was about to claim a hollow victory by process of elimination, Emilia looked toward the mansion, her violet eyes flickering faintly. Following her gaze, Subaru saw a girl running toward them, the hem of her brand-new serving uniform flapping with each step.

"Wait, please wait. Su—Subaru-sama...!"

"No need to rush like that—leaving without warning would be a nasty trick, and I'm not Otto, so I wouldn't pull it. Right, Otto?"

"Do you remember what you said three seconds ago!?"

Subaru smiled at the girl, who was bent over with her hands on her knees, catching her breath, and killed time with a light jab at Otto's expense while she recovered. After lightly wiping her brow, Petra raised her face.

Her flushed cheeks and round, sparkling eyes were adorable. She let out a long breath to shake off her fatigue, then shaped her angelic features into a smile.

"I wanted to give you this before you left. Please take it."

So saying, what she held out was a single, unremarkable handkerchief.

Its white cloth bordered in gold conveyed, to the fingertips, both fine material and meticulous craftsmanship. Taking it and turning it over, Subaru found—

"Embroidery, huh. Wow, that's some skilled work."

On the surface of the white cloth was stitched an embroidery that Petra had presumably sewn herself. Rendered in gray, pink, and black thread, it was something Subaru found familiar at a glance, and Emilia, peeking at it from the side, gave a small laugh, "Aha."

"That's the picture of Puck that Subaru drew, isn't it? Amazing, amazing, it's so well made."

"My chibi-fied Puck came out great. You shouldn't even have had a chance to see it, though."

"You stamped it on my card every morning after radio calisthenics, remember?"

Forgetting her polite speech, Petra pulled out from around her neck the stamp card Subaru had made and handed out to the village children—the one he'd stamp with a picture carved into a potato-print seal each time they showed up for the morning radio calisthenics.

Its most recent entry, stopped at the chibi Puck titled "Blue Monday," must have served as her model, which she'd painstakingly stitched, needle-poke by needle-poke.

"Still, this really is well made. As a fellow sewing meister, I can't let myself lose to this."

"Will you take it... no, will you please take it?"

"'Would you please take it,' you mean. —Of course, I'll accept it gladly. Using it to wipe away blood, sweat, and tears feels a bit like a waste, so I'll keep it tucked away as a good-luck charm instead."

He folded the handkerchief carefully, tucked it away with great care into his chest, and looked at Petra. Then, as gently as he could manage—though from the outside it looked more like narrowing a pair of vicious eyes—he gave her a wicked, fang-baring grin. In response, Petra pressed both hands to her reddening cheeks and turned her face away.

"I'll be waiting for you to come back safely. Oh, and Onee-chan and the noisy person too."

"Somehow I feel like an afterthought."

"Wasn't my rating just now pretty harsh!?"

Subaru gave a wry smile at how casually Petra had tacked on Emilia, then tacked on Otto as even more of an afterthought. Her fearlessness was endearing in its own way, but seeing Frederica behind her, watching her while radiating a dense wave of pressure, made it hard to keep thinking so.

No doubt, after Subaru and the others departed, Frederica's maid training would intensify and come crashing down on the girl. Petra, live strong.

"Well then, much as I hate to say it, I guess it's about time to head out."

"If we keep talking here too long, there's no point in leaving early in the morning."

Cutting off an atmosphere that seemed like it could go on chatting forever, Subaru put his foot on the carriage and climbed into the cargo bed. Then he reached a hand down.

"After you, Emilia-tan. Right into my arms."

"Maybe it'd feel nice to enjoy the view from the coachman's seat for once?"

"Ohh, so cold! E・M・K — Emilia-tan, Majorly, Koakuma (that's 'little devil')! —Whoa!"

Just as he was about to withdraw his hand at being brushed off so coolly, it was seized and pulled hard, and Subaru, pitching forward, found Emilia sweeping lightly past him.

Her dancing silver hair tickled the tip of his nose, and with a small sound her slight frame landed inside the carriage. She settled quietly into the seat across from him, and, noticing Subaru staring, tilted her head slightly.

"What's wrong?"

"Nah, nothin' really."

Saying this, Subaru—stomping just a bit harder than necessary, it seemed—moved over to sit beside her instead, plopping himself down heavily. Emilia smiled at how, despite the roomy interior, he'd chosen to make it feel so cramped.

Otto, who had been watching the exchange between the two of them, muttered, "I can't keep up with you people," as he took his seat on the coachman's box, gripped the reins, and looked back up at the front gate.

"All right, we're off. Please be careful not to bite your tongue or anything."

"You're one to talk—don't shake it too much. When you slam the brakes, give me the signal we agreed on beforehand. Otherwise I won't be able to properly push Emilia-tan down."

"Is that what you've been thinking about?"

"Me!? I never agreed to that scheme—when did I get made an accomplice!?"

Emilia turned a gaze bordering on contempt on both Subaru and Otto, and Otto cried out his innocence, but no one paid him any mind. Thinking to himself, as if it were someone else's problem, that Otto really did suit the role of crying foul over some unreasonable accusation, Subaru raised a hand.

"Right then, onward, bound for the Sanctuary—!"

"Who exactly do you think you are to say that!?"

Grumbling "such a killjoy" at Otto, who twisted his mouth up at the coachman's seat, Subaru poked his face out the window and turned toward the two who'd come to see them off.

"All right, I'm counting on you two while we're gone. And... take good care of Rem, please."

"Leave it to me. In exchange, please do take good care of Emilia-sama and the master."

"Take care, and come back safe."

At Subaru's voice, stripped now of its playful edge in this moment of parting, Frederica and Petra both pinched the hems of their skirts and bowed.

After that absurdly formal exchange, Subaru, if not quite able to leave his mind entirely free of the mansion, at least managed to shift his attention away from it.

"Hey, we still haven't left yet, Otto? You're slow."

"I really can't accept being treated like this!!"

And so, with that exchange easing whatever tension remained before departure, they set off.

"It seems I'll need to properly teach you your polite speech and bowing manners all over again."

"I'm terribly sorry. I just... let myself get a little too comfortable, just for a moment."

Petra bowed her head with a neat little dip, her form following Frederica's teachings almost to perfection. Quick to learn and sharp to grasp things—truly a student worth teaching.

Though her behavior just now had only been age-appropriate, so there was no real need to make a fuss over it.

"I understand that until recently you were close with Subaru-sama and Emilia-sama. Even so, right now the relationship is that of servant and employer, and you must not let that slip from your mind."

"...Yes. I truly am sorry."

The girl understood perfectly well, including the fact that her earlier behavior had been selfish.

She could have behaved exactly as taught, in a manner befitting her position, and seen Subaru and the others off that way. And yet she had deliberately set aside her role as a servant to see them off simply as a girl—there must have been a reason for that. A small reason, but one she couldn't give up.

"It would be crass of me to pry into that. Instead, I'll be adding the appropriate amount onto your afternoon dictation practice."

"Waah... my assignments are getting bigger?"

"You knew to expect at least that much, didn't you. The fact that you've already calculated it'll be settled with just that much makes you a student I look forward to watching grow."

Having said this, Frederica clapped her hands and continued, "Now then—"

"Even with Emilia-sama and the others away, Beatrice-sama is still here at the mansion. We can't cut corners on meals or cleaning either. If you don't finish quickly, you won't have any time left for your studies. Petra, hurry along to your work."

"Yes, teacher. I'll go finish up right away!"

Seeing the girl scamper off, Frederica's mouth loosened faintly, baring a hint of fang. Hiding that smile with a practiced gesture of her hand, Frederica looked toward where the dragon carriage—already long out of sight—had carried Emilia and the others away from the mansion.

"This much fulfills your instructions, my lord. Whether they can cross the Sanctuary or not is up to Emilia-sama and Subaru-sama now."

Closing her eyes, she let the wind wash over her.

"I wonder, will Emilia-sama be able to cross it? That paradise bound by the Witch's blood, that inescapable dead end—"

"So Puck really hasn't shown his face this whole time, huh."

"Yeah, that's right. I've called out to him many times, and I can sense his presence in the crystal, but... this is the first time he's stayed out of sight for this long, so I'm a little worried."

Inside the smoothly speeding carriage, the only sound was the conversation between Subaru and Emilia, seated side by side.

Under the effect of the Divine Protection of Windshield, the carriage largely shut out noise of any kind—the sound of wind, outside clamor, all of it. Traveling at considerable speed like this, and yet with barely any shaking and no sound at all, gave a sensation almost like being caught in some kind of dream.

Perhaps a first-class seat on the Shinkansen offered a similarly luxurious feeling. Though, granted, Subaru had never even ridden in an unreserved seat, let alone first class.

In any case, within such a quiet cabin, the only certain sound was the words exchanged between the two of them. And what they were discussing was the anomaly of the past several days—the absence of the small cat playing the role of father figure, who ought normally to be always at Emilia's side, thoroughly interfering with every one of Subaru's approaches toward her.

"Thinking back, he hasn't shown up since before we got back to the mansion. The last time I saw him was..."

"For me, it was in the royal capital, when we were staying at Crusch-sama's mansion. I thought everything was normal, but for some reason he was nowhere to be seen starting that morning. Even when I called out to him, he wouldn't show his face... I started worrying I might have made him angry."

Emilia looked down, keeping her face turned away from Subaru as she toyed with the tip of her hair between her fingers. For the past several days, her hairstyle had remained fixed with her silver hair braided.

As Subaru watched her intently, she seemed to sense the intent behind his gaze and nodded, "Mm."

"The last thing I agreed to with Puck, the day I last talked with him, was 'keep your hair in a braid.' Since then he hasn't told me to do anything different, so I've been keeping to that for now."

"So your hairstyle really is your contract with Puck. That's pretty light... or is it? They say a woman's hair is her life, so maybe Puck's contract holds the same weight as holding your life in his hands."

"I think it's a really, really light price. I didn't know this until we left the forest, but Roswaal was surprised too—said conditions as light as what I have now are way too light for a contract with a spirit on Puck's level. Apparently a proper one would need a vastly greater amount of mana, or much more complicated terms."

At Emilia's weak, loosening smile, Subaru nodded once, thoughtfully. But then, to shake off that faint, fragile smile of hers, he quickly hooked up the corner of his mouth.

"Well, just tying up a piece of Emilia-tan's time—from where I'm standing, that already counts as an outrageously heavy price, if you ask me."

"If you say things like that too lightly, they'll start to feel hollow. If it's something important, I'd rather you save it for a more important moment."

This was Emilia's complaint about Subaru's increasingly blatant approaches. In response, Subaru raised both hands lightly and shook his head.

"I've got my real ace-in-the-hole line saved up separately, for when it really matters. What I just said is just part of the everyday stock of sweet nothings I'm allowed to say to you."

"Honestly, Subaru, you really are glib. ...Ugh, don't look this way, my face might be a little red right now."

Subaru laughed as Emilia held up a palm as if to hide her face, confirming his goal achieved. Then, to steer the conversation back on track, he continued, "Anyway, though."

"With Puck missing, I'm seriously worried about our combat strength on this trip. Can't rely on Otto for muscle, and needless to say I'm useless too. And you without Puck would be rough, right?"

"Oh, so that's how it's going to be. Well, I'll have you know I can still use magic just fine without Puck. I'm not only contracted with Puck—I have contracts with the minor spirits too. Communicating with them isn't a problem, so I can fight. If something happens, I'll protect you."

"Whoa, so cool. And meanwhile I'm pathetic. Someday I'll be the one saying that line to you, so just wait a little."

"I'll wait without getting my hopes up, then."

As if to prove her words, glimmers of light began drifting to gather around the fingertip she held up—minor spirits, resembling Julius's quasi-spirits but with a somewhat fainter presence than those. Their output was doubtless worlds apart from Puck's, but at the very least, it seemed certain this group wasn't a company with zero combat power.

Though it was a fairly pathetic story that the group's only source of combat strength was the sole girl among them—and the very girl he was in love with, no less.

"Well, if we're being honest, I haven't changed a bit since the White Whale—still leaning entirely on other people. I relied completely on Rem, and before that too... wait, have I actually never once made it through on my own strength!?"

Of course, this was only from his own point of view, a case of excessive self-deprecation.

In any case, since no one present had enough information to call him out on it, Subaru's belated shock was simply left to pass, having at least driven him to a strong bout of self-reflection.

"Still, the Sanctuary, huh. I wonder what kind of place it actually is."

Having finished his round of reflection, Subaru gazed absently out the small window at the passing scenery and murmured this.

He'd first heard the word "Sanctuary" itself probably during a loop at the mansion—in the midst of what, in this world, had been his second loop. He'd missed his chance to bring up the Sanctuary as a topic then, and the result of dragging things out ever since was now.

There had also been a time when, fleeing the Witch Cult, they'd once nearly set out for the Sanctuary, but that too had been thwarted by Petelgeuse's atrocities.

As a result, the Sanctuary remained nothing but an unknown quantity to Subaru. Going by the sound of the name alone, it carried no image of danger, but the handful of warnings Frederica had voiced alongside mention of the Sanctuary tightened his thoughts back up just as they started to slacken. Chief among them—

"Watch out for Garfiel, huh."

"You've never met him either, right, Subaru? I've only ever heard the name too. Frederica wouldn't give us any details either."

Joining in on Subaru's murmur, Emilia furrowed her fine brows uneasily. The scene she pictured was probably the same one Subaru had in mind.

The scene of Frederica bringing up Garfiel's name as someone to be wary of, yet refusing to say anything further about him.

Even when Subaru protested at being fed information in dribs and drabs, she'd stubbornly refused to budge from her one line: "Because it's an Oath." Emilia, for some reason, had let the matter drop at that.

"I really should've pressed her for more. Knowing someone's dangerous and only knowing their name—that seems way too complacent to me."

"It can't be helped, it's an Oath. A pact is sacred and inviolable, never to be trespassed upon. Contracts, Covenants, and Oaths—though they differ in weight, they should all be treated as equally unbreakable."

Wagging a raised finger, Emilia said this as if lecturing Subaru.

Taking in these terms—Contract, Oath, Covenant—that sounded to him like nothing so much as wordplay, Subaru let a question that had suddenly occurred to him slip straight off his tongue.

"So what's between you and Puck is a Contract. What Frederica has with Roswaal-cchi, whatever obligation she won't budge on, that's an Oath. And what the kingdom made with the Dragon is a Covenant... is that right? Or is it different somehow?"

"It's not something people distinguish quite that precisely, but the way I think of it: a Contract is a pact between individuals. An Oath is a pact sworn from one side toward the other. A Covenant is something formed beyond individuals, beyond time itself. That's how I was taught, anyway."

"I see. Yeah, with that understanding, it does fit the pattern."

Nodding in agreement at Emilia's explanation, Subaru scratched his head roughly and continued, "But you know—"

"You dressed that up in some pretty grand language just now. 'A pact is sacred and inviolable,' was it?"

"A pact... a promise, is a precious thing. An Oath, of course, but even a Contract has no real force that compels you to keep it. It doesn't—but you keep it anyway. That's what it means to make the effort to keep it, isn't it? You keep your promise whether anyone's watching or not, whether anyone notices or not. Both sides—yourself and the other person—do everything you can to make sure of that."

Emilia pressed a hand to her chest and gazed steadily at Subaru, who had tossed out his question so lightly. Her tone was soft, with not a trace of blame in it. Precisely for that reason, it made his chest ache.

"Because I believe that, I can work hard to fulfill the pacts I've made. A promise is meant to forge that trust between two people, isn't it?"

"I am truly, deeply sorry for what happened back then—!"

Inside the carriage where no jolting could be felt, Subaru dropped flat to the floor and bowed his head all the way down.

Faced with Subaru's full DOGEZA, forehead grinding against the floor, Emilia blinked in surprise, and a few seconds later, having replayed her own words in her head, seemed to understand what had prompted him.

"Oh, I wasn't trying to blame you or anything. It's true you didn't keep your promise to me, and you didn't properly apologize for it either, you just got defensive about it, so I really did feel like, 'honestly, what is with you!'—but still."

"Ow, ow, ow, my ears hurt!"

"Afterward, I reflected on how one-sided I'd been about it too. Not going to make up with you right away was partly my fault too, for being so stubborn. I really am sorry."

"Ow, ow, ow, my chest hurts!"

"And about pacts, too—maybe I was thinking about it too heavily, too deeply. See, since I'm a Spiritual Arts user, contracts and things like that are closer to me than they are for ordinary people. For a Spiritual Arts user, a contract with a spirit is something to be honored above all else, so I get oversensitive about it... yeah, for me, a promise is a really, really important thing. So really, Subaru, reflect on it."

"Ow, ow, ow, my heart hurts!"

As she spoke, the real turmoil of that time seemed to come welling back up in her, and Emilia began to sound a bit sulky, while Subaru remained prostrate before her.

This made clear at last the reason she had been so furious back in the waiting room at the royal castle.

It hadn't just been anger over a broken promise, simple as that. For her, a promise itself carried an immensely heavy meaning. Even someone as kind-hearted as her couldn't have stayed calm in the face of Subaru breaking one so carelessly.

Without even realizing it, Subaru had trampled on something precious in Emilia's heart.

"Have you reflected on it?"

"I've reflected. Deeper than the sea, higher than the mountains, wider than the sky, grander than the universe."

"Then all right, I'll forgive you."

She poked Subaru's forehead lightly with a finger as he raised his face, then touched that same finger to her lips and smiled faintly. Relief that there was no anger left in her, combined with how adorable the following gesture was, left Subaru unable to get a word out.

Not noticing Subaru opening and closing his mouth like a fish gasping for breath, Emilia suddenly turned her head toward the direction they were heading.

"The Sanctuary, Garfiel. And then Roswaal and the villagers too... there are so many people I have to talk to, I'm already getting nervous just thinking about it."

"Don't worry, I basically won't let you run into any danger. Trust in me, Shield Number Two."

"Number two? Then who's number one?"

"Right now he's up front driving the carriage, flirting with my Patrasche."

At Subaru's straight-faced, casual designation of Otto as a meat shield, Emilia couldn't hold it in this time and burst out laughing. Satisfied at having drawn that laugh out of her, Subaru turned his thoughts, as she had put it, to the many gates awaiting them ahead.

Ever since coming to this other world, problems had followed one after another without a moment's rest.

Even on the road to the Sanctuary, what swirled inside him was less anticipation for a place he'd never seen than outright unease. After all, Frederica herself had stoked some of that unease, and there were plenty of suspicious points too, like how none of the villagers, Roswaal included, had returned. And on top of that, Subaru and the others had their own share of lingering worries—Puck not answering their calls, unresolved concerns left behind at the mansion.

"Were you thinking about Rem-san?"

"...You could tell?"

Emilia gazed at Subaru's silent profile and tilted her head at that question.

With that motion, silver hair slid down off her shoulder, and pinching the tip of it between her own fingers as the tail of her braid swayed, she said, "Of course I could tell."

"You could at least give me credit for watching you just about as much as you watch me, Subaru."

"So does that mean you're gazing at me and thinking about me around the clock, Emilia-tan?"

"Ah, well, more like a half of a half of a half of that, actually."

"So that's three whole hours...!"

"A half of a half of a half of that..."

"Hearing the exact number is gonna hurt, so let's just stop there!"

Calling out to Emilia, who was busy whittling it down to a realistic figure, Subaru let out a small breath and scratched his cheek.

"I've left it to Frederica and Petra, so there's nothing to worry about. There isn't... but even though there shouldn't be, I can't quite put into words this uneasy feeling that keeps welling up anyway."

"If you're worried, you're worried, there's nothing to be done about that. It just means she matters that much to you. Being thought of that much is almost a little enviable."

"For the record, I worry about you just as much, Emilia-tan... wait, you were trying to get me to say that just now, weren't you?"

"Yep, I was being mean. Sorry about that."

Just the sight of her sticking out her tongue in apology was enough to forgive everything.

As Subaru, unable to help himself, swallowed audibly, Emilia looked up at him from below, "But," she said.

"What you're worried about even more is Beatrice, isn't it?"

"...Hold on, could it be that our hearts are actually connected, Emilia-tan? I can already see the ending clear as day."

"Normally you'd play tough and say 'like I'd be worried about her,' but that didn't even come out today. Proof that you're really, really worried."

Struck right on the mark, unable to muster a single retort, Subaru bit his lip with a frustrated expression.

But he quickly painted over that expression with another.

"It's not really worry, exactly. It's just, the last time we talked kind of ended in a fight, and I haven't seen her since. Leaving the mansion like this without seeing her again just sits a little wrong with me. Yeah, just a little bit. Just this much, just the very tip of it."

"That sounds kind of lewd somehow, or is it just my imagination?"

"Well, it's not so much your imagination as it is my fault."

Hiding his pleasure at getting a bit of a reaction on the part he'd been aiming for, Subaru watched with a fond smile as Emilia tilted her head, not getting it at all.

"If that ends up making Bea-ko's shut-in tendencies even worse, then as a former shut-in myself, I can't help but feel pretty responsible, in a manner of speaking."

"Shut-in... that's right, you're kind of an expert on that, aren't you, Subaru. Do you think Beatrice will be able to come out?"

"That's the tricky part, honestly. Dragging her out forcibly without any real trigger isn't good, but on the other hand, taking it slow and easy is coddling her too much. Seriously, shut-ins, every single one of them, are such a handful... ah! I was one too!"

With that predictable punchline landing, the conversation's direction corrected itself.

In any case, now that they were on their way to the Sanctuary, settling that matter would have to wait.

"Once we're back, I'll have to talk things over with Bea-ko properly. In the end, I never managed to get out of her the things I needed to."

"I get the feeling both Beatrice and Puck know something they're hiding."

"Same here. Frederica too, come to think of it—everyone connected to that mansion seems to have this habit of dropping suggestive hints and then postponing the actual answer forever. It's basically a disease at this point. And not a good one either. Bea-ko went and shoved the Gospel back at me while only saying the stuff that'd bug me most."

The Gospel, shoved back at him by Beatrice along with all her suggestive remarks, was still safely in Subaru's keeping even now. If it were only going to be dead weight, he could have left it behind, but he'd brought it along in case, as a worst-case option, he needed to press Roswaal about it directly.

Since it unnerved him, though, it was tucked away quietly at the very bottom of his bag, as if hidden.

"...Looks like we've entered the forest."

Emilia, suddenly lifting her face, brushed aside the bangs falling over her forehead and, glancing around, let this remark slip out. Subaru raised his head too, following her lead, but from inside the carriage there was no way to confirm the truth of it. Walking up to the small window and looking outside, sure enough, he could tell the green hues had deepened.

"You figured that out without even looking outside, huh."

"I'm mixed blood, but I do have Elf blood in me. Elves and forests are so inseparable that they call us the forest folk, so—"

Just as Emilia was speaking, wearing a fragile, fleeting smile—

A sudden sensation lightly prickled Subaru's skin, and he glanced around, wondering what it was. Of course, nothing visible had affected the surroundings.

The inside of the carriage remained cut off from the outside by the Divine Protection of Windshield, as before.

But—

"—!? Hey, hold on!"

"..........."

Emilia's slender body swayed unsteadily, and just as she seemed about to collapse defenselessly, Subaru slid in and caught her.

Catching her with a slide across the floor, he found Emilia gone limp in his arms, her eyes shut, her face twisted in pain as she gasped in short, faint breaths.

"E-Emilia-tan!? What—Emilia, what's wrong!?"

Emilia seemed unable to even answer. Though she looked to be in pain, aside from her shallow, rapid breathing and her expression, she had no fever and wasn't sweating.

Lifting her light body into his arms, Subaru instantly judged that he'd get nowhere on his own. He dashed to the front of the carriage and shoved his head roughly through the small window connecting to the coachman's seat.

"Otto! Something's really wrong, Emilia suddenly collapsed! Do you have any medicine or—"

"Ah, Natsuki-san, I'm sorry about this."

Subaru's panicked words trailed off. Otto, who should have been the one answering, had sweat beading on his brow, and turned toward him with all the strength drained from his voice.

Two changes registered at once. First, the carriage had stopped. Patrasche and Frufoo had halted, stranded among the crowding trees. That he hadn't even noticed the stop spoke to just how sensational the commotion moments ago had been—but a problem bigger than that had now arisen.

That was the second major change—

"Comin' at me head-on again, bold as brass—you got some nerve, outsider."

The line, spat out, held not a shred of friendliness despite what the words on their face might suggest.

A single remark, and yet one that told you everything about the character of the person who'd said it.

That impression proved correct, and the appearance of the figure standing feet planted before the carriage didn't betray it either.

Short blond hair stood up in spikes, and a white scar stood out prominently on his forehead. His sharp eyes were every bit as ferocious as Subaru's own, the ones people had teased for years as sanpaku, and his canine teeth, like a great cat's, were startlingly white. His frame, hunched and rounded, was short for a man, but a savage, demonic aura leaked from his entire body, fierce enough that no one would dare underestimate him for his small stature.

"Don't know who the hell you are, but they say 'the nail that sticks out furthest wears down to nothin' and snaps easiest.'"

"Huh, wha?"

At the unfamiliar turn of phrase, Subaru let out a dumbfounded sound, and the other party, apparently deciding this meant he was scared stiff, huffed out a laugh, "Hah."

"Ahh? Don't go gettin' scared on me, now. Sure, you two got some bad luck. I mean, of all the places to go sneakin' into, and on top of that, who you run into is yours truly."

The man grinned ferociously, clacked his teeth together like he was baring fangs, slammed his fists together hard, and dropped into a low combat stance. Still in that posture, he glared up at the pair, who had gone silent.

"Showin' your face in front of this Garfiel-sama is where your luck runs out. You're gonna end up like a 'wishy-washy waffler, blown left and right'!"

The thug, having thus introduced himself, spat out that inscrutable bit of bravado and stamped his foot into the ground.

An instant later, an impact like the world itself flipping inside out came crashing down on Subaru and the others.
