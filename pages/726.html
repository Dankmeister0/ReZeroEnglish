Arc 9 Chapter 30: "Plan B"

"—When you're talking to me, look at me properly!!"

The instant he took the brutal kick, alarms blared in Aldebaran's mind. The cause of the alarm was the kick itself, and the various factors surrounding it.

For one, the kick's power was outrageous, and the damage was massive. For another, he was startled by just how long the leg that had kicked him turned out to be—"wait, all of that is leg!?" long. And he couldn't leave out the dire predicament of the ice forming on his chest, where the toe of the ice boot on that kicking leg had pierced through his shattered stone breastplate and driven into his creaking ribs.

But the biggest reason for the blaring alarm was, without a doubt, the inexcusably on-the-mark accusation that had come out of her mouth.

—Look at me properly, she'd said. And before that: that he looked lonely whenever he talked to her.

"—!"

Honestly, it was a shock that she'd picked up on the complicated feelings Aldebaran was carrying.

It wasn't that he'd been arrogant enough to think he'd hidden it well—he'd simply assumed she was oblivious to that kind of subtlety. But thinking about it, she was only bad at directing malice back at people; she was plenty sensitive to malice directed at her. —Having been born into this world as a silver-haired half-elf, that much was only natural.

"That was one hell of a tuition fee...!"

Just below where the ice had begun creeping across his aching gut, an ice man modeled after his infuriating opponent clung to him.

Aldebaran grabbed the head of the ice man that had gotten in his way, seizing it with the stone left arm he'd shaped with magic, and tore its neck off without mercy. He heard its maker cry "Ahh!", and the now-headless ice man's body shattered pitifully, scattering into flecks of ice.

It wasn't just the looks that matched the original—it died just as easily, too.

"There, catch!"

"Kyaa! Honestly, that's mean!"

Unlike the shattered body, the severed head was still trying to bite at him, so he flung the ice man's head back at her—Emilia—who'd been about to press her attack. Out of the corner of his eye he watched Emilia catch the head against her chest like a dodgeball, then looked down at his own stomach.

The frostbite from the kick was spreading. At this rate his whole body would be frozen solid—

"That'd be the end of you, wouldn't it, old man? You know what to do about that, right?"

Crucified upside down on a pillar of earth, the spectator watching the exchange between Aldebaran and Emilia—Roy Alphard—hinted at the cure for the ice with an infuriatingly smug grin.

He didn't need advice from a Sin Archbishop. Without being told, Aldebaran gritted his teeth and gouged into his own freezing abdominal muscles with the tip of his sword. Agony, blood—he forcibly sealed the wound, which came within a hair of disemboweling him, with a stone plug, avoiding the bad end of becoming an ice sculpture.

His vision went red with pain, and Roy's laughter—"Ahahaha! That's it, that's it!"—rang in his ears, thoroughly amused and thoroughly grating.

"—! Enduring it that way, that's just cruel."

"Oi oi, you're the one who forced the either/or, don't gimme that. If you don't like pain, just be a good boy and freeze solid. Get it through your head that that's what your attacks are, missy."

"—. That's true. I think so too. But don't you get the wrong idea either, Al."

"Wrong idea?"

"You can't grit your teeth and gut your way through the same trick with your whole body, can you?"

That declaration was a statement of intent: she meant to drive the same blow she'd landed on his gut into his entire body.

As if to back that up, the pained crease vanished from Emilia's beautiful face, and cold wind began to blow around the Prison Tower, the air turning hazy white. Fine white powder snow scattered and swallowed up the battlefield, and Aldebaran felt Emilia's seriousness in the numbing chill spreading through his fingertips.

"I'd like to say your angry face is cute too, but honestly, that's just plain scary. And besides..."

Right in front of Aldebaran as he cracked wise, the air creaked, and a body began reforming around the ice man's head cradled in Emilia's arms. As if declaring "a new face!"—no, "a new body!"—the ice man fully revived within mere seconds and did a squat on the spot to show off that it was alive and well.

That pointlessly provocative vigor faithfully reproduced the original—thoroughly annoying.

"...You made this thing well enough down to the little details that it's downright creepy."

"That's the same kind of compliment Ram gives. Everyone at the mansion says it's realllly a good likeness too."

"Huh. But I bet the guy himself made a face like he hated it."

"How did you know that?"

"I'd know. It's the last face anyone wants to see."

At his answer to her simple question, Emilia's eyes went wide and then sharpened with anger. She might have thought she was being made fun of, but it was simply Aldebaran's honest, unreserved opinion.

In the first place, if you were going to make someone out of ice, you should've modeled it on someone more dependable-looking and strong—Garfiel, say—but of all people, Natsuki Subaru?

Thanks to that, at least Aldebaran felt zero hesitation about smashing the thing.

"If you're gonna make a soldier, you'd think you'd model it on the most reliable guy you've got."

"—? That's exactly why I used Subaru as the model, isn't it?"

"...Is that right. Shouldn't have asked."

Sticking his tongue out inside his helmet, Aldebaran felt like he'd just bitten into the world's most bitter bug.

The damage from getting kicked was one thing, but for Aldebaran, the mental damage of standing here talking face-to-face with Emilia like this was far more grueling.

Maintaining his Authority's "Territory" required mental stability above all else. And yet Emilia was practically a meta-character purpose-built to rattle Aldebaran's heart with ease.

"God damn it."

Honestly speaking, running into Emilia here was outside his calculations.

After returning from Vollachia, Aldebaran had manipulated the membership headed to the Pleiades Watchtower to suit his own purposes. Naturally, he'd known Emilia and the others were heading to the royal capital, but even so, his plan had been to swiftly retrieve Roy and slip safely out of the capital, avoiding any turmoil. —Who could have guessed a Zodda Bug would sweep his feet out from under him.

"...The whole thing started going sour thanks to little miss Felt."

In order to intercept Reinhard in the worst possible environment—the Augria Sand Dunes—Aldebaran had deliberately had Flam, who was in the tower, use her Divine Protection to relay word of his betrayal to her younger sister. As a result, Reinhard had appeared at the dunes, and it had also drawn Felt and the others into an ambush there—but Felt hadn't kept that information to herself and her group alone.

With her comrades left at the Pleiades Watchtower having been hurt, and on top of that an emergency in which the "Divine Dragon" who was supposed to protect the kingdom had fallen to the enemy, it was impressive that she'd managed to exercise such calm judgment. He'd wondered if that was Valga Cromwell's wisdom whispering in her ear, but he shook his head at the thought.

Felt had been acknowledged as an enemy by Priscilla. That Royal Vessel of hers deserved honest praise.

Either way, Felt had chosen the best option amid a dire situation, and Emilia, who'd received that information from her, hadn't let it go to waste either.

The result was this worst-case present: his escape from the Prison Tower blocked, standing face-to-face with Emilia.

"—————"

In front of him, Emilia had fitted ice gloves over both hands, and beside her stood the infuriating ice man striking some kind of "Gear Two" pose, both of them eyeing Aldebaran warily.

Her violet eyes practically radiated the resolve that whatever Aldebaran tried, she wouldn't lose—but even without that wariness, there wasn't actually much Aldebaran could do.

He'd copied her Ice Brand Arts, calling his own "Earth Brand Arts" and fighting back with a stone sword, but the precision of the weapons he could produce was worlds apart. There was no comparing their durability, and Aldebaran couldn't shape a lump of earth into a dependable soldier the way she could ice.

What's more, even the thought of hurting Emilia, let alone harming her outright, weighed on his conscience. —This was, at this point, a fatal flaw built into his very being from birth.

And on top of that, Emilia's techniques, specialized as they were for capturing rather than killing, were the worst possible match against Aldebaran's Authority.

And so, now that he'd managed to remove Natsuki Subaru and Reinhard, the two obstacles he'd considered the greatest threats to his plan, Emilia had become Aldebaran's natural enemy—save for one other person, the single worst "enemy" he could face.

"Whatever the case—"

No matter how he sorted through the situation, he couldn't find a single good card in his hand, enough to make him want to cry—but no dead end had ever been broken through by stringing together complaints.

If he couldn't manufacture a finishing blow on his own against an opponent who, without even meaning to, countered his every move, then he'd have to rely on outside help for that finishing blow. —And just as he was about to play that trump card—

—that was when he saw the "Dragon" pierce through the clouds, descending toward the noble district.

"You've gotta be kidding me."

Watching it cut vertically across his field of vision, Aldebaran couldn't help but mutter that.

The pale blue line of light streaking vertically through the hazy white sky was "Aldebaran"—the very trump card Aldebaran had been counting on. If it was heading toward the noble district with that kind of momentum, toward where Yae and the others had been standing by, then nine times out of ten its purpose was to rescue them.

Which meant something Yae couldn't handle had been sent to the Barielle villa.

Given that "Aldebaran" had intervened immediately, Yae's chances of survival were fifty-fifty. —And that, in turn, meant Aldebaran couldn't borrow "Aldebaran's" hand to get himself out of this predicament.

"You've gotta be kidding me..."

Repeating the same words, Aldebaran keenly felt the situation had gotten even worse than before.

He could use his Authority to try again, calling "Aldebaran" to his side ahead of Yae and the others. But if he did that, Yae, left without backup, would surely fall to the unidentified enemy.

Neither Yae nor Felt was, strictly speaking, indispensable to Aldebaran's goal.

He couldn't call them indispensable, but—

"Damn it!"

Giving up the chance to down the poison in his mouth, Aldebaran cursed.

Plan A, the trump card, had been to use "Aldebaran's" draconic aura to blow away the cold snap Emilia had used to seize control of the entire battlefield with snow, then withdraw. Now that it was unusable, there was nothing for it but to hurry and execute Plan B.

And in order to execute that Plan B, he'd first have to hurry up and come up with a Plan B.

Even as he thought that—

"Did you see that, missy!? At this rate my partner's gonna go on a rampage in the royal capital—"

"I've been told that's not my department to worry about!"

Emilia charged in boldly at Aldebaran, who was trying to buy himself more time to think.

Even while sensing "Aldebaran"—the "Divine Dragon's"—advance beyond the walls, Emilia's nerve in trusting her allies enough to ignore it entirely was beyond reason. It no doubt proved just how much she trusted whoever had laid out the plan, but that wasn't something you could do just because someone told you to.

And the fact that doing exactly that happened to be the best possible move against Aldebaran was more than he could stand.

"Eya! Tei! Toutou! Soyaa!"

As if remembering herself, Emilia reverted to her original policy, abandoned conversation, and unleashed a raging flurry of consecutive attacks.

Countless fists in ice gloves flew at him, smashing his hastily-made rock shield head-on. Amid the flying fragments, he caught the head of an ice spear with his stone prosthetic arm, discarded it as it began to freeze over, and leapt backward. —Right into the path of the ice man, who'd circled around to slam a Tie Shan Kao into him.

"Guh, ah—!"

Struck by that copycat secret technique of Baji Quan, Aldebaran was sent flying forward. Waiting for him where he landed was Emilia, who had reshaped her ice spear into an ice hammer and wound up with all her might. Her slender, supple arms drew back taut, and she unleashed a full swing of unbelievable force.

The spirited, freezing blow beat the very air to death, and the instant Aldebaran resigned himself to the fact that he couldn't dodge it, he formed earthen armor covering his entire body without a gap—and took the tremendous force full-on.

"—!"

Unable to even manage a groan or a scream, Aldebaran was sent sailing through the air.

The earth he'd wrapped himself in had stopped the ice from spreading, but as protection it amounted to little more than the comfort of a handkerchief slipped between himself and a deadly weapon. His vision spun round and round, and after being flung through the air with no idea where he even was, he was caught by a hard impact and dropped to the ground.

"Uhyaa, that looks like it hurt. Talk about a hopeless situation, old man."

As he planted his hands in the freshly-settled snow, he heard the spectator's amused voice raining down from above, and Aldebaran realized he'd crashed straight into the earthen pillar Roy was crucified on. He'd been sent flying more than ten meters, and his whole body ached as if every bone in it were creaking. He didn't even have the wherewithal to snap back with an insult.

"Damn it..."

Dragging this out any longer wasn't something Aldebaran wanted.

Emilia's way of fighting, relentlessly dropping the surrounding temperature, was a brutal setup where the longer things dragged on, the more debuffs piled up like falling snow, and while he dithered, enemy reinforcements might show up.

He'd already learned the hard way, in the fight against Felt and her group, that the more opponents there were, the more exponentially the difficulty of overcoming them rose. —And there was no guarantee his "natural enemy" wasn't among any reinforcements.

"Can't really see with all this snow, but the city over there looks like it's in for a rough time too, doesn't it? Wanna try shouting your lungs out, appealing for someone to save you, old man?"

"Shut, up. Just stay there and keep quiet—"

Ever so considerately, the upside-down Roy kept rubbing the increasingly desperate Aldebaran's nerves the wrong way.

Anger welled up in him, tempting him to throw away a life just for the sake of punching that smirking face once—and beyond that irritation, Aldebaran spotted a glimmer of light for Plan B.

"If that guy is me, then..."

Far off, through the sensation of the hazy white sky trembling, he could feel that "Aldebaran" was locked in a fierce battle.

As far as Aldebaran's knowledge went, there weren't many candidates capable of putting up a death-match against "Aldebaran," but he had no choice but to trust in the "Dragon's" victory and leave that to him.

And what he had to believe wasn't only the "Dragon's" victory, but also that "Aldebaran," realizing he couldn't lend Aldebaran a hand, must already have laid the groundwork for an as-yet-unknown Plan B.

In order to make that unconfirmed Plan B a reality—

"Roy! If you don't wanna die, lend me a hand!"

"—! Al!!"

At Aldebaran's shout, Emilia's eyes went wide and her voice turned sharp.

At the disbelief packed into that voice, Aldebaran was struck with surprise—that despite having already dragged Roy out of the Prison Tower—no, despite already thinking his own stock had hit rock bottom the moment he'd trapped Natsuki Subaru—there was still room for it to fall further.

"But if it's necessary, I'll borrow a Sin Archbishop's power too. In the Empire, Natsuki Subaru was doing the exact same thing, wasn't he? What's wrong with that?"

"You know full well that's not the issue!"

"Yeah. I know, and I'm saying it anyway. Feel free to hate me for it."

He was used to being hated by Emilia. —A lie.

The truth was, before it was even a matter of being hated, he'd been someone she never even registered.

"You—!"

At Aldebaran's answer, Emilia, sad and furious at once, conjured ice stakes into the air.

Emilia, capable of more than just ice-fueled hand-to-hand combat and able to fight like a proper mage too, aimed the floating ice stakes at Aldebaran and Roy and rained them down like a carpet bombing. It was, at any rate, an expression of her intent to stop whatever mischief Aldebaran and Roy were up to, but Aldebaran met her frantic attack by peeling up the ground with magic like a carpet and flipping it back at her, tatami-toss style.

"Cooperate, huh, easy for you to say~"

The shock punching through shook the earth, and this jury-rigged earthen shelter wouldn't hold for long.

Roy, despite being exposed to the exact same predicament, paid it no mind whatsoever, and while Aldebaran's attention was divided by keeping the shelter up, twisted his lips into a discontented frown and said,

"As you can see, our arms and legs are broken, and we've been forbidden from eating. So what exactly do you want us to do about it? Or... are you saying you'll give us permission to eat that pretty lady over there?"

"Nope. Not gonna let you eat, and not gonna let you rampage either."

"Then there's nothing left that we can do—"

"There is. —Spit it out."

Aldebaran said this in a low voice, meeting the eyes of the upside-down Roy on the earthen tower he'd sheltered behind him, offering "Gluttony" a third option beyond eating and rampaging.

At the proposal, Roy's eyes widened, and then he immediately curled into a leering grin,

"Geez, old man, just how much do you know about us? You're not secretly our actual biological father or something, are you?"

"If I raised brats like you two, I'd be too ashamed to show my face to the world and keep on living."

Theirs was a relationship bound by a curse mark, forcing obedience. Aldebaran's attitude showed not the slightest intention of building any kind of friendly relationship, and yet Roy welcomed it, licking his lips.

This was Roy Alphard, who had earned the name of "Gluttony," and beyond that, "Bad Eater," entirely on his own merits. This glutton who'd put any kind of grotesque thing into his mouth—how had he sized up Aldebaran? —There was no time to demand an answer that was bound to be nothing but trouble anyway.

"—Stop this!"

The next instant, what broke through the earthen shelter was a mighty ice boomerang—thrown in, it was the spitting image of the Flying Winged Blade that Madelyn Eschart of the Empire had used—and through the gap torn open in the ice, Emilia came charging in at him.

Aldebaran blocked Emilia's path with his stone prosthetic arm, made a size larger than before, covering her from above with a palm shaped like a baseball glove, softly and surely cutting off her route.

But—

"—! A decoy!"

The ice man planted its hands on that stone glove and vaulted over the obstruction with a handspring.

Emilia had used herself, charging in the lead, as bait, sending the ice man ahead of her. Normally you'd think the roles of decoy would be reversed between herself and a construct, but he'd fallen for it completely.

The ice man cleared Aldebaran's head and closed in on the earthen pillar. Rocks Aldebaran conjured pinched in on the ice man from both sides, trying to shatter it—but—

"It's harder than before!"

It seemed she'd raised its ice density rather than bone density, and the sturdy new body of the ice man deflected the rocks.

Unfazed by the interference, the ice man reached its hand toward the crucified Roy, closing in to stop whatever Aldebaran might try—and in that instant, its neck and torso parted ways in tears.

"What!?"

Emilia, having sliced through the rock glove with an ice sword and then witnessed the ice man's head go flying through the gap, cried out.

She likely had no idea what had destroyed the ice man she'd rebuilt to be so sturdy. —That it had been the work of a steel thread extending from the ring on Aldebaran's right ring finger.

A silver thread, glinting amid the icy mist, had pierced through the ice man's defenses and taken its head. It was the Steel Thread Technique, worth a single finger's length, that he'd mastered in the midst of the fight against Felt and her group.

A skill mastered is a life saved. Right after embodying that lesson—

"—Well, if I'm gonna do it, this looks like it'll be the most fun."

A voice thick with dark delight rang out, and Aldebaran—no, the world itself let out a scream.

△▼△▼△▼△

In that instant, what struck Aldebaran was a nausea of the soul.

"Blegh."

He'd thought he understood the mechanism. He'd even been able to imagine what would happen.

But once it actually happened, neither his understanding nor his imagination did him a bit of good.

It was an attack on neither body nor mind, but on something untouchable—"memory" itself.

"Blegh."

There's a phenomenon called déjà vu, where something you're seeing for the first time feels as though it isn't the first time. But what Aldebaran—no, everyone caught in this effect—tasted in that moment was "Rumination": that same déjà vu intensified tens of thousands of times over, made so vivid it could not be ignored.

As proof of that—

"—Ah."

A ragged breath escaped her, and the ice javelin slipped from Emilia's hand. Poised to throw it a moment before, it now dropped limply, losing its shape before it even hit the ground, returning to mana.

Her wide violet eyes shook with violent turmoil, and without meaning to, she sank to her knees right where she stood. Watching this, Aldebaran clenched his back teeth and leapt backward.

"Blegh."

There was a ferocious sickness to it, yes. But not enough to buckle his knees.

"Rumination's" effect varied from person to person. Of course it did. Attachment differs for everyone. The way Aldebaran and the way Emilia were invested in that "memory" simply differed.

As infuriating as it was, Roy's choice had been precise. Precisely calibrated to hit Emilia hard while leaving Aldebaran comparatively unscathed—that was the "memory" he'd chosen.

Which was why—

"Blegh."

Enduring the nausea, Aldebaran raised his hand toward the heavens.

The sky over the royal capital was still hazy white with icy mist, so thick he couldn't properly see it. And yet, up there, Aldebaran believed "Aldebaran" had already laid the groundwork for Plan B.

—It was a gift born of the combat experience gained fighting Reinhard at the Augria Sand Dunes.

In that battle, Aldebaran and "Aldebaran," being two entities sharing the same human ego, had exercised magic by sharing a magic crest—taking the form of Aldebaran borrowing "Aldebaran's" mana to cast his own magic. This time, it would be the reverse.

Into the Plan B that "Aldebaran" had prepared above the clouds—a spell formula waiting to be triggered—Aldebaran, tracing the magic crest, would pour in mana in his stead, taking on the role of flipping the switch.

Because it was the same ego, they'd arrived at the same idea—a Plan B that had never existed until now activated—

"—Blegh."

The instant it activated, the full picture of the Plan B "Aldebaran" had prepared came into view, and Aldebaran let out a groan of a different quality than before, yet still continuous with Roy's "Rumination."

Plan B, in its entirety, was—

"No... !"

Sensing the intense torrent of mana, Emilia's lips trembled as she remained on her knees.

Still unable to stand, she lifted her face anyway, and her violet eyes caught sight of it. —Floating in the sky over the royal capital, a massive rock mass on a scale of a hundred meters.

It had a scale as if someone had yanked an entire small hill up from somewhere and set it floating there. Aldebaran had never encountered it himself, but perhaps anyone who'd seen the fabled White Whale up close, overwhelmed by something so vast hanging in the sky, would feel the exact same way.

Either way, "Aldebaran's" aim in floating that rock mass in the sky was obvious—

"Blegh."

The next moment, a sound like the sky itself cracking rang out, and anyone who'd noticed the anomaly in the sky could tell it was caused by the countless fissures now running through the rock mass.

The hill-sized rock mass split into ten, twenty rock shells, and just like that, headed for the ground—

"No...!!"

Foreseeing the outcome, Emilia berated her trembling knees and rose to her feet.

She must have understood: even shattered into pieces, rock masses more than ten meters in size and weight, falling on the city, would cause massive damage and a great many casualties.

And to stop it, the only way was for someone capable of stopping it to go and stop it.

"Even so, I'm doing this meaning not to let anyone die. So—"

"—! Al!"

"I'm counting on you not to let a single one of those hit the city."

Foisting off this one-sided, self-serving wish, Aldebaran ignored Emilia's voice cracking with intense emotion and dashed to the earthen pillar to secure Roy.

As one last act of defiance, the now head-only ice man came rolling in to try to obstruct him, but Aldebaran ruthlessly crushed it underfoot and tore the crucified Sin Archbishop down from the pillar.

"You're a bad man, old man."

Not answering Roy's needless remark, Aldebaran hoisted the small body over his shoulder and ran.

Without so much as glancing back at Emilia, whom he'd been trading blows with only moments before, he ran headlong.

"Al! Don't! Don't run away... Al!!"

He could hear Emilia's anguished plea, but no attack came to stop him. Of course not. Her power was, right now, being poured entirely into saving people.

If she could target Aldebaran here without a thought for the consequences, her life would be easier. Surely, if she'd had the kind of personality that could do that, it would have been a mercy—for both Emilia and Aldebaran.

But Emilia couldn't allow herself that selfishness; she used her magic to save people instead, and let Aldebaran get away.

"Don't go! Give me back Subaru, and Beatrice—!"

As if in response to that surge of emotion, the sound of the sky itself freezing over rang out dramatically.

Aldebaran raised the ground beneath his feet, using it as a foothold to clear the wall surrounding the Prison Tower, breaking free of the white battlefield Emilia had made, and still without stopping, he ran, and ran, and ran.

"She's crying, y'know, that lady."

Something he could have ignored if it hadn't been pointed out, the contemptible Sin Archbishop went out of his way to point out.

His heart torn apart by her tear-choked voice, Aldebaran kept running without stopping. For now, he simply prayed that all his accomplices would gather safely at the rendezvous point, and kept on running.

The rock masses falling toward the royal capital were held back one after another by towers of ice conjured in succession, staving off damage to the city.

Neither the very one who'd caused it, nor the one who'd stopped it, took any joy in it.

Only—

"—Ahh, that was quite the feast!"

Only the "Bad Eater," licking his lips as he savored that whirlwind of joy and sorrow, was left laughing.
