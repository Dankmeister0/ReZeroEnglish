Scorpion Tale Chapter 4: "Memories of Kaka-sama"

1

"Aaand-a one-two, three-four."

So she went, bathing her body in the sunlight, briskly and supplely bending and stretching her arms and legs.

To maintain her Glamorous & Dynamite Body, and also because it had become thoroughly habitual, she ran through the morning routine she couldn't do without. —And as she ran through it, she brooded.

"Am I doing this Star Watcher gig against my will, I wonder..."

What kept circling round and round in her head was her own heart, laid bare by Leilani in that stone chamber carved with tally marks — the unexpected frustration that had accumulated inside this ample bosom of hers over the long stretch of time spent alone in the tower.

Honestly, to Shaula those tally marks were something like proof of mounting feelings and accumulated time; she hadn't intended to load them with all that much meaning. But that, if you let Leilani tell it, was precisely the expression of the inner Shaula's scream, fit to burst.

Put that way, she started to feel it might be so, and when 'his' return was actually achieved, the thought of those tally marks being witnessed made her spine go cold.

If 'he' recoiled with a "Whoa..." she might never recover.

On that count, she was grateful to Leilani for pointing out ahead of time, "This is quite bad, it is."

"That doesn't mean going into that room gets a free pass, though."

"Harsh words indeed... And yet it was the doing of my base and witless self. Your esteemed wrath I shall humbly and gratefully receive. Only — that wrath, I beg you spare these children at least..."

"Gehh, there it is again. That's less a mother's strength and more a mother's dirty trick, so you'd be wise not to pull it on anyone but me."

Leaning her weight on the balcony railing, the stretch of it beside the tower entrance, Leilani watched her and stroked her swollen belly; Shaula stuck out her tongue as she delivered that advice.

Whatever the original instructions had been, they were nowhere to be found now; Leilani had gone entirely native, wearing the face of a tower resident and doing exactly as she pleased, and Shaula had spent the last several days with her hands unavoidably full.

Using her status as an expectant mother as a shield, Leilani demanded this and that, and as Shaula went along with it, she cocked her head and felt it keenly.

"I am so not cut out for all this devoted-caretaker business."

Real talk, considering the purpose she'd been made for, this was in a sense unavoidable too.

After all, the one who designed Shaula's nature and biology and all that was Kaka-sama. And Shaula's Kaka-sama had her deficiencies when it came to understanding hearts and emotions, on top of a strong efficiency-freak streak. Her way of thinking affirmed functional beauty over aesthetic beauty, and she'd clashed with those around her plenty.

"If Master or 'Leilani' hadn't talked her out of it, I might've turned out some misshapen grotesque monster built to fulfill the objective at max efficiency and nothing else..."

The frightening thing about Kaka-sama was that this didn't even feel like a one-in-a-million chance, so as far as Shaula was concerned, being made into her current Beautiful & Wonderful good looks earned major thanks.

Thanks to that, she'd surely been finished into a fine woman exactly to 'his' taste.

"I wanna prove that by knocking Master dead with my charms, soon as possible~"

Apparently this long, long wait had been hard on her, so to take back double for all that hardship and misery, 'his' return was something she looked forward to even more than before.

Speaking of looking forward to things—

"I want the baby in 'Leilani's' belly to hurry up and get born too."

"...I am astonished. Never once did I imagine you would deign to wish to meet these children."

"Nah, I've got no interest in brats. It's just, until that belly deflates, I can't kick you out even if I want to, so it's more like there's just that one hurdle I wanna clear."

"—. Am I still a hindrance to you, Shaula-sama?"

Her voice dropping slightly, Leilani stopped the hand rubbing her belly and asked, and Shaula gave a light "Hmmm," continuing her twisting exercises as she went on. "Well, having Leilani around, coming all the way back around, I don't really think of it as a hindrance anymore, but when Leilani goes into labor, or when I'm taking Leilani out past the Sea of Sand after the baby's born, there's no guarantee I won't accidentally cross paths with Master and miss him, right? The difficulty on 'Sand Time' is very hard, so what I'm saying is I wanna keep the chance of overlooking Master at zero."

"...Your thinking is unshakable, it seems. Even knowing, as you do, that I am the 'Leilani' who filled in so much of your memories."

"—? That's that, and this is this, isn't it?"

True, the memories shared between Shaula and 'Leilani' were many, and she did think they were an utterly indispensable component in forming the Shaula of today. But the root of the root, the base of the base, the ultimate core that made Shaula Shaula — that held not a single speck of cloud.

That alone was absolute, even if 'Leilani' piled up a hundred memories.

"Come to think of it, Kaka-sama was yammering on about that at her usual mile-a-minute pace..."

The Kaka-sama who had brought Shaula into the world was clever, and possessed an obsessiveness that bordered on clinging. So when she designed Shaula's biology, Kaka-sama's intent must have worked its way into every last part.

Shaula's convictions, which 'Leilani' had called unshakable, and the never-fading love for 'him' — all of it lay along the same line, designed with intent by Kaka-sama, surely.

"Well, for all her brains Kaka-sama's got a few screws loose, so I get the feeling there's plenty of stuff that didn't go her way at all."

"...You hold your esteemed mother in respect, it seems."

"'Respect' makes my skin crawl. What I feel for Kaka-sama is love and hate, gratitude and rebellious phase, respect and contempt — all that contradictory junk mashed into one big mush~"

"A splendid bond, one to be admired, I do believe. ...Might I, too, build such a bond with these children? Inept and misguided, indecent and unrefined, undiscerning and improper as I am."

Perhaps her anxiety kept mounting as she spoke, because the speed of the hand rubbing Leilani's belly climbed little by little. With all that belly flesh in between they probably didn't notice, but rubbed at that pace, even a baby might cry uncle.

Besides, Leilani's pessimism didn't ring any bells for Shaula.

"Straight up, aren't you overthinking it? Real talk, no matter what a parent thinks or does, kids grow up however they please."

"Shaula-sama..."

"Same with me. Kaka-sama probably thought through all kinds of complicated stuff and designed me without sleeping, or whatever. But even if she could set the direction, there's no way in hell her shabby imagination got all the way to me becoming this glamorous-bodied, universally-adored girl! I am me, therefore me! That kinda thing!"

Thrusting up a tightly clenched fist, Shaula declared her emancipation from Kaka-sama — strictly speaking, she'd gotten over Kaka-sama fairly early in her Shaula-life, but she asserted it anyway.

True, Kaka-sama's design had its influence on how Shaula lived and how she loved, but—

"—How I love, that's something I chose for myself."

"—"

"Hm? What's up? Moved by my declaration of emancipation from Kaka-sama?"

At the clammed-up Leilani's reaction, Shaula spun around atop the railing. Leilani stood a little way off, but Shaula's eyes were a special make, able to see clearly for several kilometers. She wouldn't miss even the slightest change of expression — well, there was the defect that she didn't remember the original expression all that well.

At any rate, to Shaula's eyes it was plain as day that Leilani had faintly bitten her lip.

But Leilani immediately erased it, slipping in a "My apologies," before adding, "The sunlight seems to have gotten to me a little. This body of mine is riddled with defects, like so much dust and refuse."

"For real? You lose to sunlight? I knew 'Leilani' was the it's-rough-being-alive frail type, but you blew past my imagination sideways. ...And yet for all that you jerk me around however you like — you're one incomprehensible gal."

There was a 'Leilani' against whom Shaula was utterly helpless, and then there was a 'Leilani' who couldn't even walk through a town unless Shaula lent a hand — a truly confounding opponent.

Rising unsteadily, precariously to her feet, Leilani put a hand to the wall and made to head back into the tower. Shaula asked that figure, "Want a hand?" but — "There is no need for that. Please, Shaula-sama, continue your esteemed daily routine. There is no call to exert yourself for the sake of one as vulgar and base as I."

"That so? Then I'll take you at your word — aaand-a one-two, three-four."

Nodding to Leilani, who answered with a feeble smile, Shaula returned to her performance atop the railing.

For a while she felt Leilani's gaze stabbing almost painfully into her back, the back of her head, her swaying scorpion tail, but eventually even that vanished, and Leilani dragged her feet listlessly back inside the tower.

Feeling that lingering, clinging reaction, Shaula suddenly remembered.

"Now that I think of it, Kaka-sama was another one who couldn't just come out and ask for help~" — so she mused.

2

"—All right, Shaula, let's stop here. We should have satisfied the necessary conditions sufficiently."

"Haaah? Again? We've only done the stretch-way-up-and-reach exercise so far, y'know? Kaka-sama, your feebleness blows past my imagination sideways."

If anything, by the second round of the very first exercise her arms would no longer rise above her shoulders, so she hadn't even seen the stretch-and-reach exercise through.

At Kaka-sama's sorry showing, Shaula — feeling not so much like she had indigestion as like she hadn't even finished chewing — puffed out her cheeks in a pout.

But at Shaula's reaction, Kaka-sama shut one eye in that excessively white face of hers. "Listen. The amount of exercise required differs from person to person, and to begin with, I am in the position of having been dragged out here by you and made to go along with this 'Rah-jee-oh Tai-soh' business. What I require right now is not to see this exercise through, but the achievement of having exercised in your company. That has been sufficiently satisfied. Therefore I withdrew. That is all there is to it."

"Kaka-sama, you seriously talk a mile a minute when you're making excuses."

"That, too, is someone's bad influence! Him, perhaps! Or is it Reid?!"

"Whaaat are you saying? The one who designed me as me is Kaka-sama, so it just goes all the way around and full-counters right back to you."

"The way you corner me properly with logic I can't rebut is thoroughly infuriating, honestly."

Shaking her head limply, Kaka-sama lamented from the bare ground in sour-grapes fashion. —Arms that wouldn't rise was one thing, but crumpling at the knees was too far gone. If it were anyone but Kaka-sama, Shaula could declare outright that it was an act put on to needle her, but considering Kaka-sama's preposterous feebleness, there was a real chance she'd genuinely run out of gas, which was frightening.

"I talked about this with Alec too, but Kaka-sama, why are you alive?"

"You discussed such a thing with Alec?! I had thought he and I were building an amicable relationship!"

"I got it wrong. It was, how is Kaka-sama able to stay alive."

"That's much the same, isn't it! Is my being alive so very wrong? —Wrong, perhaps. After all, I am a 'Witch' born and bred."

"Nah, sorry to spoil your cool pose, but that's a separate matter."

"I know! Even as I said it, I had the feeling you'd say that!"

For all her mysterious-beauty looks, a Kaka-sama squawking and yelping was business as usual.

Overwhelming magical power and technique, a vast store of knowledge spanning all times and places, a peerless and genuine 'Witch' at whose feet many knelt in search of the boons of her beauty and cunning — such was the reputation that had apparently spread, but to Shaula, who knew the real article, it was a rumor that made her want to snort.

"The reality can't get through even ten percent of Rah-jee-oh Tai-soh. That's Kaka-sama for ya..."

"Be quiet. What does it matter? Rah-jee-oh Tai-soh, of all things. Or what, then? Does being able to perform Rah-jee-oh Tai-soh make one great? Does Rah-jee-oh Tai-soh house some special power surpassing even Divine Protections and Authorities? It does not, does it?"

"That's enough already, Kaka-sama! It's too painful to look at, so that's enough...!"

Mentally independent though she already was, watching her own creator expose such a wretched sight did damage to Shaula's heart as well. Honestly, if it were anyone else she wouldn't feel it this strongly, so even if all the circumstances behind it diverged considerably from the norm — "Guess this really is what they call parent and child. When I think of Kaka-sama disgracing herself somewhere, my whole body gets unbearably itchy too."

"Why is my disgracing myself the premise? That's absurd."

"But it's seriously itchy and it's rough on me, so Kaka-sama, could you hurry up and die?"

"Is that any way to speak to your parent?! Not that I'm one to talk!"

Just as the wide-eyed Kaka-sama said, in the sense of verbal abuse toward a parent, maybe it was hereditary.

In Shaula's case, compared to 'him' it was like a speck of snot, but her abuse did properly contain a trace amount of love for Kaka-sama. Whereas in Kaka-sama's abuse when she spoke of her own parents, nothing could be felt but hatred with zero affection, which was almost refreshing.

"Seriously toxic parents, huh... poor Kaka-sama."

"By that logic they would be your toxic grandparents, so you become pitiable as well."

"Isn't it dragging on this long precisely because you lot didn't clear it up promptly? A toxic parents' bloodline, leaving the seeds of trouble down to children and grandchildren..."

"Kindly don't casually lump me in as a toxic parent as well. Thus far, apart from not having seen Rah-jee-oh Tai-soh through, I should have no demerits."

Saying she hadn't seen it through rather than that she couldn't see it through — phrasing it as though she'd have managed if only she'd tried — struck Shaula as cheeky.

At any rate, for Kaka-sama, the 'Witch' with sublime power, this sort of barren squabbling was all in a day's work, so Shaula no longer argued.

"After all, conflict only breaks out between opponents on the same level."

"...That makes me curious as to how my level and yours are treated inside your head. Terribly curious. You see, I am the incarnation of the thirst for knowledge."

"Now, now — fuss over the fine details and it's not just your hair going white, the color'll drain out of other places too."

"For the record, my hair color did not fall out from stress!"

To Kaka-sama, yapping with her voice raised, Shaula said, "Easy, girl, easy," then stretched way up and gazed at the sight below — the vast world visible from the floating castle.

The spectacular view from a floating castle flying at the same height as the clouds or higher had an extra-special quality even compared to every landscape Shaula had laid eyes on until now.

And the one who had made such a sight a reality was the Kaka-sama still looking at her resentfully.

"For real, though, I've got mad respect for how amazing Kaka-sama is. Nobody but my Kaka-sama could make a castle like this fly."

"...The praise is appreciated, but don't overestimate me. Construct the spell formulas for levitating objects, arrange them properly, and cover the requisite amount of Mana to keep the target structure afloat, and this is achievable by someone other than me. It is far from a miracle."

"I misspoke. Nobody but my Kaka-sama would have the dumb idea of making a castle like this fly."

"You simply cannot get through a moment without getting a jab in at me, can you!"

Shaula wanted to say it was Kaka-sama who wouldn't let her praise her straightforwardly, but that twisted 'Witch' Kaka-sama would never listen meekly. They were parent and child. Shaula figured she understood that much, at least.

And so—

"You going along with my invitation for once — that's some kind of staged special-occasion sorta thing, right?"

"—"

"Did you think I wouldn't notice? If so, Kaka-sama really doesn't understand the human heart. And I'm not a human, so all the more so~"

At Kaka-sama's reaction — her cheeks stiffening at both the question and the observation — Shaula felt like slapping her knee over having guessed right. —No, it was a bit iffy.

After all, this guess hitting the mark also meant it was a death flag for Kaka-sama.

"A work-obsessed type who normally doesn't keep her kid in even a corner of her head suddenly bonds with said kid at the last possible opportunity... Kaka-sama, may you rest in peace."

"I am not dead, nor do I intend to die! ...Though as you say, I cannot deny that it is a plan with a higher probability of death than any before it."

"Aaah, that's exactly the thing you didn't need to say, and going and saying it anyway is so very Kaka-sama~"

No need to worry. I'll be back for sure. —That show of spirit alone would have done, but the reason she couldn't just leave wishful thinking behind was probably that Kaka-sama had a personality bad at looking at hope.

On that count, Shaula didn't resemble Kaka-sama in the slightest — a lucky disposition, if she said so herself.

"I mean, did Kaka-sama design me so I wouldn't turn out that way? If so, then I'm the ideal way of being that Kaka-sama came up with, looking back over her own life..."

"Well, who is to say? There is no mistaking that I designed you optimized for the objective, but whether my own arbitrary emotions were mixed into it, that lies beyond my awareness. But supposing that conjecture is correct... I would no longer be able to trust myself."

"How come? Kaka-sama, covered head to toe with zero exposure, and me, baring my dynamite body without the slightest stinginess... Kaka-sama's two-sidedness comes out with a bang."

"I really would no longer be able to trust myself, after all!"

Raising her voice like that, Kaka-sama stared at Shaula with a look as if she wanted to say something. Incidentally, Kaka-sama had remained this entire time in a knees-on-the-ground posture, worn out by the full-body fatigue she'd sustained from Rah-jee-oh Tai-soh, but—

"You'd be better off if you could manage a simple 'lend me a hand,' Kaka-sama."

"You yourself could stand to have enough sense to notice promptly and lend the hand."

Shaula sighed at Kaka-sama, giving such an uncute reply even as a hand was extended to her, then hauled her light body up as she was and got her onto her feet.

Then, pulling Kaka-sama by the arm, she turned her steps toward the floating castle's balcony, toward its railing.

"The view's way better than from here. Worth a look even for you, Kaka-sama."

"...No, I shall pass. I have no interest in my own altitude or the scenery I can see from it."

"Don't tell me you're chickening out? If so, that's crazy lame."

"Is that any way to speak to your parent?! Not that I'm one to talk!"

Trading the customary exchange with a stubbornly rooted, thoroughly cowed Kaka-sama, Shaula found herself wondering whether Kaka-sama had managed to accomplish the purpose that brought her here.

The Rah-jee-oh Tai-soh she would absolutely never do under normal circumstances, the maximally out-of-character death-flag remarks — those were presumably actions she'd settled on, in her own Kaka-sama way, as things she wanted to do with Shaula.

"Kaka-sama, I belong to Master. But..."

"—That is fine. I ask of you no role beyond the mission I have set. If casting your net wide caused the original objective to be neglected, that would put the cart before the horse. She would not wish for that either."

"...Roger, got it and understood."

White hair streaming in the wind, Kaka-sama narrowed those black eyes, and Shaula did not defy her words. She did not defy them, but she did think. —Kaka-sama had spoken of 'her wish.'

"Then what about Kaka-sama's wish..."

What of it? Shaula did not press her on that.

Her interest, compared to that in any other outsider, was by far greater. Even so, Shaula couldn't ask. Probably because it was none other than Kaka-sama, that 'Witch,' who had designed Shaula that way.

3

"You'd be better off if you could manage a simple 'lend me a hand,' 'Leilani.'"

Catching up to the small back that had been walking unsteadily with a hand against the wall, Shaula gently took the woman's arm and softly supported that far-too-precarious body.

Having that done to her, 'Leilani' widened her eyes and stared hard at Shaula.

"Looks like you wanna say something along the lines of 'why.'"

"...That is precisely the very thing I was about to put into words. I left behind insincere, faithless words and took my leave of your presence, Shaula-sama, did I not?"

"If you actually meant it, then 'Leilani' has trouble not just with her life force but with her acting too. Your back was moping and calling out to me."

With the hand not supporting her, Shaula jerked her thumb toward her prized scorpion tail.

That attitude, as if to say a hand extended to her wasn't what she'd wished for, was thoroughly like 'Leilani.' Her life force was already so poor she'd have trouble living without relying on others, and now she was in a state where lives other than her own dwelt in her belly.

Measured against 'his' words instructing Shaula to treat expectant mothers with care, that way 'Leilani' dug in her heels was not desirable.

Therefore—

"Objecting won't get you anywhere, so I'd rather you didn't object."

With that preface, Shaula slipped her arms around Leilani's body and, ignoring the resistance of a woman startled by the contact, lifted the expectant mother up. A princess carry — a Princess Hold, you might call it.

Shaula peered into the face of Leilani, whose eyes had gone wide in stunned astonishment. "Isn't there something you oughta say to me?"

"—. Thank you... very much."

"Oho, you managed to say it straight out. Does having a baby in your belly round off your personality or something?"

Cackling, Shaula started walking with Leilani held in both arms. A hand reached out timidly and circled Shaula's neck, the woman holding on so she wouldn't be dropped.

Truly obedient, she marveled inwardly. If she'd be this meek about it, Shaula even thought she'd just as soon have 'Leilani' pregnant around the clock — and then she shrugged.

"That wouldn't be one bit like 'Leilani.'"

The 'Leilani' Shaula knew was outrageous and merciless, brimming with curiosity and beloved by everyone, a self-aware and selfish recluse from the world, a creator who was capable and yet terrible at living — a being who occupied an indispensable, important, far-too-complicated share of Shaula's history.

"—Somehow, this is just endlessly complicated."

That strange sentiment did not last long inside Shaula as she carefully carried that light weight.
