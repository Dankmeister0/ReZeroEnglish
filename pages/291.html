Arc 4 Chapter 122: "A Roaring Reunion"

Holding her breath, the little girl crept through the darkness, killing the sound of her own footsteps.

"――――"

She made her small body smaller still, mindful even of the whisper of her clothes brushing against the air. She walked with a hand pressed to her mouth, because without physically holding it down, her breathing would come out ragged.

If she was honest, she wished even the obnoxiously loud pounding of her heart would go quiet.

"――――"

The young girl with reddish-brown hair swaying as she moved—Petra—made her way through a mansion she had only just begun to grow familiar with, her heart as uneasy as if she'd wandered into some unknown world.

That the floor was laid with plush carpet was, just for now, something she was grateful for from the bottom of her heart. Normally she privately thought it made walking difficult, but it was thanks to that carpet that her trembling legs could move without making a sound.

She swore to herself that the next time she had the chance to wash it, she would do so with the utmost care, pouring in all her gratitude.

If she didn't occupy her mind with something else like that, the legs she was barely managing to move one after the other, in alternation, would surely stop moving altogether. She was already progressing at a pace not much different from a caterpillar's crawl—if she stopped now, she was too afraid to even imagine what would happen.

The long, long corridor, its end nowhere in sight, felt hateful to her right now.

Petra remembered how her heart had leapt when she'd been told she could work at the great mansion.

Even though it stood so close to the village, that mansion had always been, to Petra, a place terribly far away. Not a matter of distance. A matter of standing.

The margrave, lord of the land and master of the mansion, would come by Arlam Village whenever he had a spare moment. Despite being a noble, his manner carried no airs, and he had the largeness of spirit to laugh off even a child's rudeness. Petra had never once heard the villagers speak ill of the margrave, apart from his choice of attire.

Petra herself had never given the margrave much particular thought.

But she had always longed for the sheer size of that mansion. To Petra, born to ordinary parents in a small village, it was a place utterly beyond her reach. Even when she said she wanted to go to the royal capital and become a dressmaker when she grew up, that was only a dream she'd sized to fit herself—she had learned young the resignation of reaching for something out of reach.

And then, unexpectedly, Petra had been given the chance to work at that mansion of her longing.

What was more, there in that mansion was also the person who had saved her life, and toward whom she harbored a faint, quiet affection. Which joy won out between the two—well, it's a secret that the latter was slightly the stronger.

In any case, for Petra, working at the mansion had been the beginning of days like something out of a dream. The vastness of the corridors, the sheer number of rooms, the staggering amount of time cleaning took—all of it made her head spin at times, but even so, those dizzying days had brought Petra the joy of being alive.

And now, that place of longing and dreams was, to Petra, terrifying enough to freeze her heart solid.

What had happened, and how things had come to this—Petra didn't know.

What she did know was that she had finished her work as usual, and finished dinner as usual, alone with the senior maid Frederica—the woman she called Frederica-neesama.

Petra had used a step-stool to clear the dishes at the sink, and in the meantime Frederica had gone to collect the meal meant for the Lady Beatrice said to live in the mansion. It had gone untouched. Petra had still never once laid eyes on this Lady Beatrice who was supposed to live in the mansion. She couldn't help but wonder if such a person really existed—but since Frederica, Emilia, and Subaru all seemed to know of her, Petra simply went along without a word.

The masters of the mansion were off somewhere on a long journey.

Aside from Petra and Frederica, the servants, only two others remained in the mansion. The unseen Lady Beatrice, and the girl named Rem whom Subaru had brought back.

Neither of them would eat, which left Petra a little dissatisfied.

Still, the ever-sleeping girl named Rem seemed pitiable to her, and above all, Petra hadn't forgotten how tenderly, how carefully, Subaru treated her. The profile of Subaru's face as he watched Rem sleep had been so achingly earnest that Petra had seen in it a depth of feeling and anguish that made her hesitate even to feel jealous.

And so—

"...I have to save Rem-san, at least."

The resolve slipped out of her mouth unbidden, because that alone had become the whole principle guiding Petra's actions.

After Frederica brought down Beatrice's untouched dinner, Petra put away the dishes and received from Frederica the rundown and instructions for tomorrow's work. She really wanted to help Frederica, who still had work left to do, but Petra's still-growing body couldn't hold up to labor late into the night. As always, Frederica accepted only Petra's enthusiasm and sent her off to her room.

But this night, on the way to her room, something different happened.

—All the lights throughout the mansion went out at once.

Startled by the sudden dark, Petra clung to Frederica beside her. Frederica gently caught and held her, speaking reassuring words over and over—and then held her breath.

Petra would never forget the feeling of the air growing taut around them.

A dry air she'd tasted somewhere before. As Petra, swept up in rising unease, tightened her grip, Frederica's hands gently pried her loose.

"Petra. Be a good girl and listen carefully. Use the stairs behind us and get outside. Without making a sound—quietly, as fast as you can—run."

"Fre-Frederica-neesama is..."

"I'll follow right after you. Once you're outside the mansion, run for the village. Once I've safely caught up with you, we'll clean things up come morning."

Speaking gently, Frederica turned her gaze forward—Petra could tell. Then, with a light push, she was sent backward, the distance between them widening. The clouded sky hid the moon that night; there was no light source at all.

Petra sensed Frederica moving forward with quiet steps.

Aware of it, Petra did as she was told, moving through the mansion's corridors in the direction opposite Frederica. She somehow made it to the stairs, and was about to head down—when she remembered.

"This... it's the same as the forest."

She remembered now where she'd tasted this dry, taut air before.

It was the air from just two months ago, when she'd gone into the forest with the other children of the village.

The air she'd felt when her life was threatened, in the midst of a forest where a Witchbeast's murderous intent raged.

"—I have to go."

The moment she realized it, Petra's feet turned not downward, but up the stairs.

She remembered Frederica's instructions. She felt guilt at breaking them.

But she couldn't leave Rem behind in a mansion that had become just like that forest.

Because Petra hadn't forgotten that time—when Subaru had carried her out of that terrifying forest.

"—Ah"

Finishing that recollection, which nearly brought her to tears, Petra sensed by the rhythm of her own strides that her destination was near. By stubbornly keeping to silence, to going unnoticed, the journey that had only allowed her the barest minimum of speed was coming to its end.

Once she reached Rem's room, there was no way small Petra could carry her and escape. But Petra's mind was so dominated by urgency that such a thought didn't even occur to her. If she could just reach the room where Rem slept and confirm she was there, everything would work out.

A young girl, tormented by a sense of duty too large for her frame, and by the fear of death drawing near.

No one could blame Petra for failing to notice something so obvious.

A few more steps, a few more meters, past two more doors she walked straight by.

"――――"

She was almost there, almost at the room she was aiming for.

Her heart pounded fit to burst, and her breath, impossible to fully suppress, leaked out through the gaps of her fingers.

Just a little more, just a bit more, just—

"――――"

I've arrived, Petra thought, lifting her face.

It was exactly that moment. Outside the window facing the corridor, the clouds over the moon drifted apart on the wind.

Moonlight poured in through the window, and color came into a world that had been nothing but darkness.

And Petra saw it.

"My, what a cute little maid."

A woman in black, as if fused with the darkness itself, stood right in front of her.

Only three steps away, with the door to the room she sought between them.

A tall woman with long hair.

Her clothing showed off her voluptuous body without the slightest hesitation, terribly seductive. She swayed the tip of her braided hair with one hand, approaching with unhurried steps that carried an extraordinary allure.

If only Petra hadn't noticed that in the hand opposite the one holding her hair, the woman held a large, dully gleaming knife.

"From what I heard, the targets were two, plus one extra. You must be the little maid."

"...Ah"

"Trembling, are you? It's all right. —I'm sure your intestines are lovely. The insides of a child with a future ahead of her are always so beautiful."

Petra had no idea what she was talking about.

She only understood, without being able to say how, that the smiling woman drawing closer was synonymous with the footsteps of death itself.

Even understanding that, Petra's legs, stiff with terror, wouldn't move.

A knife too large for the woman's slender hand.

When it was swung, surely her life would be reaped without a second thought.

And yet—

"Good girl. ...I'll let you meet an angel."

Mercilessly, the woman raised her knife, aiming for the trembling girl.

The blade cut through the air, and just before it swept across Petra's torso—

"Petra——!!"

A large shadow leapt in from the far side of the corridor, wedging itself between Petra and the knife, sparks scattering with a high, ringing clang.

In front of her, long golden hair streaming, was the back—familiar now, and so reliable—shielding Petra.

There was only one person whose broad, dependable back could belong, impossibly, to a woman.

"Frederica-neesama!"

"You're a bad girl, Petra. I told you to run... you'll be punished for this later."

"Y-yes, ma'am!"

Shielding Petra with her back, Frederica turned only her head and spoke in a stern voice.

Called a bad girl, Petra trembled, nodding over and over against that back, nearly in tears.

"You're the big maid? Really, you are quite big."

Watching the exchange between the two before her, the woman, her knife blocked, tilted her head slightly from where she'd stepped back. Her long braid swayed with the motion—a movement so out of step with the woman's abnormality that it seemed almost comical.

"I am rather self-conscious about my size, you know. My father's blood, most likely."

"I see, your father was big. If you're that large, your intestines must be quite a sight too. I look forward to it."

"That is not what I'd call good taste."

"A woman's intestines are brighter and more vivid in color than a man's. I'll compare the two of yours and let you know."

At the woman's remark—abnormality taken to its utmost extreme—Frederica thrust both arms forward into a stance.

On both hands she wore gauntlets fitted with sharp claws; it must have been that weapon which had blocked the woman's first strike. A weapon well-suited to Frederica, making use of her large, powerful frame—but even so—

"Much as it galls me to say it, you're considerably skilled."

"You're not bad yourself, but I doubt you can match me. I nearly died in the royal capital not long ago, and somehow my skills have only sharpened since."

"Is that so. Then I find myself wishing to resent whoever it was that failed to finish you off."

Reading the skill of the woman before her, cold sweat beaded on Frederica's brow.

The gap in power she sensed just from facing her could be gauged from the extraordinary killing aura the woman gave off. Though she appeared to be standing there quite casually, a dense presence of death drifted from her.

Just how many lives must one have reaped to wear such a killing aura?

"Petra. This time, get outside for real. I'll hold that woman here."

"B-but, neesama..."

Petra's voice caught, her eyes flicking again and again toward the nearby door.

Just seeing that was enough for Frederica to understand exactly why Petra had broken her instructions and come here.

And so—

"I don't know who hired you, but... it seems Petra and I are both included among your targets."

"Yes, that's right. You, the little maid, and then a Spirit girl as well. I have some complaints about the number, but I haven't had the chance to open up a Spirit's belly before, so I'm rather looking forward to it. Last time I fell one step short and never got to see."

"You give up information rather easily. Isn't that a failing, for a professional?"

"It doesn't matter. You'll soon be unable to speak at all, and if my employer complains, I can just silence them."

"That's completely broken logic."

It was a conversation enough to give one a headache.

Frederica realized there was no further point in talking with this woman. Even so, she had managed to learn what she wanted to know.

"Petra. That woman's targets are myself, you, and Lady Beatrice. Understand?"

"—! Yes."

Petra wiped her tears and nodded.

From the exchange just now, and from that confirming question, she'd grasped Frederica's intention.

A clever child, Frederica thought. A good student, too. I don't want her to die.

"Go!"

"Yes!"

At Frederica's call, Petra stumbled forward and broke into a run.

At the same instant, the black woman spun and threw something. Cutting through the air, four thrown knives aimed for the back of the fleeing Petra. Aimed with vivid, hideous precision at her arms and legs, Frederica swept her right claw through the air and barely managed to deflect them.

A high ringing sound, and every thrown knife was knocked away.

The fleeing Petra didn't even look back. Complete trust in Frederica. And that trust had to be answered.

"Good girl."

"Yes—my pride and joy!"

Frederica struck her left claw at the woman as she closed the distance. The woman avoided it by tilting her body slightly—but aimed at that off-balance torso, Frederica's real attack, a front kick, launched out.

A straight kick that could pierce the wind and shatter walls. Unlike her human mother, Frederica's father was a half-blood carrying the blood of a thoroughly battle-oriented race. She couldn't wholly embrace the blood flowing through her own body, but right now, she was grateful for this strength.

The straight kick struck home in the torso of the woman, whose eyes widened slightly. She'd thrust her left hand, the one not holding the knife, forward in a reflex—but the force of the kick was more than enough to snap that slender arm and still crush what it was truly aimed at—

"Wha—!?"

"Is that so surprising?"

To Frederica, gasping in shock, the upside-down woman relaxed her red-lined lips into a smile.

The instant the woman's left hand had touched Frederica's kick—in a situation that allowed not the slightest miscalculation of force—she'd pulled off an impossible acrobatic feat. Putting her weight onto the tip of the kick, her body had inverted into a handstand on one hand. Frederica, even as she held the woman balanced atop her own leg, shuddered at how feather-light she was.

"A spider woman—!"

"That's what I was called just recently, too."

Speaking in a tone of offended surprise, a fierce slash utterly at odds with that voice cut through the air.

A silver flash reflecting the moonlight shot straight for Frederica's throat; she reflexively raised her claw to block it, but even blocked by both arms, Frederica's arms screamed in protest.

Despite using only one arm—and one far thinner than Frederica's own—the woman possessed an extraordinary, unnatural strength.

Sparks scattered, the grinding sound of blade on blade close at hand, and Frederica lowered the leg the woman had been perched on, aiming her last-ditch kick straight at the inverted woman's face—

"That would be unwise."

With the blade still locked against Frederica's claw as a pivot, the woman's body spun further in midair. The woman, who should have dropped into the path of Frederica's kick, instead sailed leisurely past beneath it, and her free hand reached for her own long leg. From beneath her flipped-up skirt, another sinister blade came into view—

"Show me your lovely insides."

Still upside down, the woman's crossed blades closed in on Frederica's torso from both sides, with enough force to cleave her clean in two.

"Hah! Hah! Haah!"

Flying down the stairs, swinging her small arms hard, Petra ran.

From above came the ringing chain of clashing sounds, and Frederica's faint cries.

Petra was not so childishly stubborn, nor so foolish, that she couldn't grasp the feelings behind Frederica fighting so hard to let her escape.

But even Petra, who understood nothing at all about fighting, could tell one thing.

That woman in black was a monstrous thing beyond reckoning.

Even with Frederica standing against her with such a fierce expression, that woman's smile hadn't wavered in the slightest. It wasn't that she failed to grasp the difference in their strength. She understood it clearly.

If things were left as they were, Frederica would be killed.

"If it were Lady Bea... Beatrice..."

The other presence said to be in this mansion.

That woman in black apparently didn't know about Rem's existence. Of course, if Rem were found, she too would likely be added to that woman's targets—but so long as Petra and Frederica didn't carelessly go around mentioning her, there was no way to notice Rem was there.

"Here... no, this isn't it, this way!?"

Descending to the lower floor, Petra threw open door after nearby door, checking inside indiscriminately.

It was hard to believe, but Beatrice was said to live in a room that moved within the mansion. Among the many doors throughout the mansion, if you kept opening them, you would eventually reach the room where she lived. That was the kind of power the mage possessed—or so Petra had been told.

Right now, Petra needed that mage's power.

If such a being existed, surely she could help Frederica. Surely she could defeat that woman in black and protect this mansion of Petra's dreams.

"She's not... not here either... neesama...!"

Her breath ran short, and as she nearly collapsed on the spot, tears spilled from Petra's eyes.

She had opened every nearby door in the servants' wing. And yet Beatrice's room refused to show itself to her. How long had it been since Frederica's fight with that woman began?

She had to hurry. She had to hurry, and yet—

"Nee... samaaa..."

She had to run, and yet Petra's legs wouldn't move.

Striking her legs over and over with hands she couldn't even properly close into fists, Petra tried to rouse her own shrinking heart. But it wasn't enough. She couldn't summon the courage. Hope was on the verge of withering away entirely.

"—Subaruuu"

When weakness took hold of her heart, the name that slipped out, clung to like a lifeline, was the name of someone who wasn't here.

To Petra, it was the name of the bravest person in this whole world.

Someone amazing, who had courage—who could scold his own trembling legs into standing against an opponent he couldn't beat.

When Petra, when everyone in the village, was truly in danger, on the verge of dying—everyone's savior, the one who came to help—that was the name that came out.

Even knowing that person wasn't here.

"Subaru, Subaru... help me, Subaruuu"

"Right, got it, Petra."

"—Eh"

Sobbing, her face buried in her hands, Petra looked up despite herself at that voice.

Through her tear-blurred vision, someone was standing right in front of her.

That person knelt down on the floor to meet Petra's eyes as she crouched there, and said,

"Sorry I'm late. But I came to help. ...Glad you're okay, Petra."

With that familiar, shifty-eyed face, he gave her a troubled sort of smile.

That face, doing its very best to reassure her, wasn't gentle in the slightest—and that was exactly why Petra felt relief flood through her from the bottom of her heart.

"Subaru... is it you? You came?"

"It's me, and I came. It's okay now."

At his reassuring nod, Petra reached out her hand.

She patted his cheek over and over, then let her weight fall forward, and he caught her, supporting her.

Not an illusion, not a dream—he was really here. He was here for her.

She wanted to savor that relief. —But there was no time for that now.

"Subaru... Frederica-neesama's fighting a woman, upstairs."

"Frederica is?"

"A woman in black with a big knife... and she's really, really scary."

"A scary woman with a big black knife, huh... yeah, I know her."

Subaru frowned at Petra's words.

Sensing that they were apparently on the same page about how dangerous this was, Petra tugged at Subaru's arm.

"Please, save Frederica-neesama! Subaru, beat her!"

"Alright, leave it all to me!—is what I'd love to say, but if I charge in against someone Frederica can't beat, I'll just end up a corpse in an instant!"

"—!"

For a moment, despair began to spread through Petra's chest at that answer.

But Subaru gently patted her head with his large palm, and said,

"So I already sent in super-strong reinforcements in my place."

Subaru turned his gaze up toward the floor above, his expression that of someone imagining the scene unfolding there. It was an expression somewhere between unease and reassurance, mixed together.

"Kind of a rowdy night for a reunion, what with all these party-crashers around."

As her torso was on the verge of being cut clean in two, Frederica began to accept it as an established fact.

"Sorry 'bout this, but... you ain't invited."

Amid the grating clang of steel meeting steel, that voice carried a cheerful sort of irritation.

It sounded contradictory, but it was the truth.

The voice's owner was in high spirits, exhilarated, while at the same time feeling thoroughly displeased with the one before him.

Which, all things considered, was only natural.

"You..."

"How long you gonna stay stuck upside down there, huh!? —Get lost!"

A kick thrust straight up, striking the woman dead center while her arms were still pinned mid-strike. The man watched her fold in half and go flying, then brought down his raised foot and slammed his two arms together.

On both arms, gleaming silver shields were firmly strapped in place.

One-handed shields worn on both arms, fitted to cover his fists.

"The boss man's got a saying—'the best defense is a good offense,' or somethin' like that."

As he spoke, the man bared sharp canines, clacking his teeth together to make a sound.

"So then. Turn a defensive shield into somethin' that can attack too... and you got your best offense and best defense livin' together—two 'bests' makin' one 'strongest,' ain't that right?"

It was a theory dumb enough that a child might have come up with it.

But this man had gone and put that childish theory into practice, wielding two shields as his weapons.

Standing with his feet apart, glaring warily at his opponent, the man with short golden hair standing on end glanced back over his shoulder—just his head turning—toward Frederica.

"Don't you think so too, sis—wait, huge!?"

In an instant, the air of a seasoned warrior he'd been carrying shattered completely.

The man—no, the boy—widened his eyes, staring openly in shock as he looked Frederica up and down.

"Wh-whoa, seriously!? This is sis!? The sis I remember was a bit smaller, a bit thinner, and her mouth was a bit gentler, wasn't it!? This is more like bro than sis— gaAH!?"

"Don't say something so rude."

Frederica's knee drove into the boy who'd been rudely staring and running his mouth off.

Struck in the side, the boy rolled across the floor and staggered back up. Watching his wobbling face, Frederica noticed it—the white scar on his forehead.

"Is that you, Garf?"

"And you're really Frederica...? Unbelievable... guh!"

"Don't call your older sister by her name alone."

Mid-rise, Garfiel took an elbow to the back and writhed in agony.

Watching him squirm, Frederica was reminded of their childhood. There had been no toys in the Sanctuary—nothing to do but wear off boredom using each other's bodies. Frederica used to fling Garfiel about, ignoring their nine-year age gap whenever he leapt at her. It hadn't changed a bit since then.

"No. Garf... you've gotten big."

"Comin' from you right now, that just sounds like sarcasm, damn it! Lemme tell ya, I'm still gonna get way bigger from here! Don't think you can keep lookin' down at the top of my head forever!"

"Fufufu. Let me correct myself, then. Your body may have grown a little, but your capacity for grace remains as small as ever."

"The hell'd you say!?"

Baring his fangs, Garfiel protested Frederica's words. Through this direct exchange with her brother for the first time in ten years, Frederica was filled with an almost unbelievable happiness.

To think a day would come when she could exchange words with Garfiel like this, outside the Sanctuary.

—Surely, whoever had gone to the Sanctuary had managed things well.

Ram, or Emilia, or perhaps Subaru. Someone among them.

"And Otto-sama was there as well."

"Hah, that bro never gets what he deserves either. Guess that's just how it is when you figure 'a Migrudo Clan bridge always falls apart eventually.'"

She could practically picture the gray-haired young man's dejected face.

Just as brother and sister arrived at that shared conclusion, a voice came from deep in the dark corridor.

"So, may I go ahead and get back to business now?"

"Waitin' around for us like that—you're pretty considerate, huh. Why don't you just forget the whole job and head on home. I ain't too keen on hittin' a woman, myself."

"My, how kind of you."

The woman smiled radiantly at Garfiel, who tried to wave her off like brushing away a bug. Frederica slapped the back of the utterly unguarded Garfiel.

"Garf. If you think that woman is just what she looks like, you'll pay for it dearly."

"I know that, obviously ain't normal. Besides, the only woman I treat like a real woman in this whole world is Ram, anyway."

"If you think that's cool, it's really not, at all. Ram would just laugh you off."

"The hell'd you say!?"

Garfiel whirled around, indignant, at Frederica's exasperated look.

—In the next instant, a silver disc came flying from the woman's hand at tremendous speed.

A disc—no, not a disc, but a large knife spinning rapidly along its long axis. Its velocity outstripped the limits of visual tracking, leaving the sound of wind-cutting behind it as it closed on Garfiel, aiming to split his skull and burst it into a spray of blood.

"Now, listen here."

"――――"

A scraping screech of metal on metal, and a burst of sparks bright enough to sting the eyes.

The attacking blade shaved across the surface of the right shield raised to meet it, and through a skillful shift of angle, was flicked straight up. The flying blade buried its tip in the ceiling, and without even watching it land, Garfiel's body slid straight forward, gliding across the floor toward the woman, his left shield swinging up.

"I told you real nice and proper to get lost, didn't I?"

"Yes, and this is my answer."

Just before his fist could be thrown, the woman stepped lightly back and yanked her left arm sharply.

In the next instant, the blade Garfiel had deflected and left buried in the ceiling was pulled free, and on the momentum of its return, spun again to strike Garfiel from behind.

A cord had been tied to the hilt of the blade, connecting it to the other knife still in the woman's hand.

"Garf!"

Sensing the threat, Frederica cried out—but too late.

The spinning blade closed in, aiming to sever Garfiel's left arm—the one on the verge of striking the woman—from his body. But—

"—Don't gimme that crap!!"

"—!?"

Whether Frederica's voice reached him or not, in that split-second instant, Garfiel roared.

Immediately after, the left arm Garfiel was swinging through erupted, ballooning explosively. Wrapped in golden fur, transformed into something as thick as a log, it was a beast's arm—one that could never belong to a human.

Even that woman's eyes flickered with a hint of unsettlement at the sight.

"RAAAAAAH!!"

With a burst of fighting spirit, Garfiel's left fist, shield and all, punched clean through the woman's torso.

Naturally, Garfiel, who hadn't spared a scrap of attention to defense, took the blade from behind straight into his left arm—but it had grown too thick, sheathed in wire-like fur, for the blade to sever it.

"—Guh!?"

"Get lost, you black bitch!!"

Ignoring the pain of flesh being carved away, Garfiel sent the woman flying with the force of his swing, and unable to kill the momentum, she was slammed into the ground and went bouncing and rolling across the floor.

Watching her tumble, Garfiel pulled the knife jutting from his left shoulder free. He bit through the cord at the hilt with his fangs and hurled the knife out a nearby window.

"Hah! That's what they mean by 'even without an arm, Kurgan still took down his enemy'! If you thought I'd flinch and curl up over a little pain, you got another thing comin', idiot!"

"You're the idiot here, obviously!!"

"OW!?"

His sister's fist drove into the back of Garfiel's head as he gloated.

Struck, Garfiel crouched down, shooting her a look of protest at the unexpected scolding, but—

"Fighting in a way that hurts your own body like that... Grandmother would cry if she saw you."

"Ugh... wh-whatever the old hag thinks don't matter to me none..."

"What kind of way is that to speak of your grandmother!? I don't recall raising Garf to talk like that!"

"You ain't seen me since I was like four years old, and you show up after all this time and lay into me like this—that's what I can't believe! ...And—"

Having been struck where it hurt, Garfiel, at a disadvantage in the exchange, caught his breath. At his reaction, Frederica too turned to look ahead, drawn by it, and saw the black shadow slowly rising to its feet.

The woman quietly hoisted herself up, spun the knife still gripped in her hand within her palm, scooped up the blood dripping from the corner of her mouth with a finger, and licked it off. Then, she wore a look of rapture.

"—Lovely, you. Just lovely. Such a vigorous, lively boy."

"Honestly, I didn't think you'd be standin' back up that quick after takin' that. My bad, I underestimated you a little."

The woman, smiling as she licked the blood, and Garfiel, pressing his palms together in apology.

At an exchange that looked nothing like it belonged to a fight beyond human comprehension, Frederica momentarily forgot the passage of time.

But quickly composing herself, shaking her head, she said,

"Garf! In any case, she's some kind of unfathomable woman. Don't let your guard down, and show her no mercy..."

"I know, I know. More importantly, sis... sis, you know a woman named Rem?"

"...? Yes, she's in the mansion. She's said to be Ram's little sister, I believe."

Even on that point, Frederica had no real certainty.

In Frederica's memory of Ram, whom she'd known since childhood, there was no sister. And yet, Rem, whom Subaru had explained was Ram's younger sister, was a girl who resembled Ram to an astonishing degree, nearly identical. The girl, still sleeping even now, had apparently been erased from everyone's memory due to the effects of harm inflicted by the Witch Cult.

"She look like Ram?"

"The spitting image. But that doesn't mean I'll allow her to be treated as a substitute."

"I wasn't plannin' anything that low. Just wanted to confirm it. —I see, huh."

While brother and sister talked, the woman lightly rotated her arms and legs, checking the condition of her own body.

Whether she was deliberately giving them time to talk, or simply hard to read, it was hard to say. In any case—

"If she's somewhere on this floor, sis, find your chance and get her out. I'm probably gonna have my hands full with this one."

"Wh-what are you saying. I intend to fight as well. With the two of us, we'd have an even greater advantage..."

"Would you, though?"

Frederica moved to stop Garfiel from taking the woman on alone, but the woman's voice cut in. Frederica turned a sharp look toward her, and the woman hid her mouth behind the tip of her blade.

"I'd rather you didn't make that face at me. Besides, I don't think what I'm saying is so far off the mark—I believe your little brother here can prove that himself."

"...Garf?"

Frederica knit her brow in suspicion at the woman's words and called her brother's name.

At her voice, Garfiel adjusted the angle of the shields on both arms.

"Sorry, sis. This ain't the kinda opponent I can handle while keepin' half an eye on watchin' my back."

"Wha...!"

Frederica fell speechless at being told, in no uncertain terms, that she would be a hindrance.

Of course, she acknowledged that her own skill fell far short of that woman's. But even so, to be told she couldn't even help enough to avoid being a burden was humiliating.

"And, don't get the wrong idea, sis. I ain't sayin' you're a hindrance."

"...Then, what are you saying?"

"I'm sayin' if me and her go at it for real, everything around us is gonna get wrecked to hell."

Garfiel pointed at himself, then pointed at the woman, who wore an expression of amusement, as if affirming his words. She toyed with the tip of her long braid between her fingers, leaning forward slightly.

"That's right. ...So it would be best if you stepped back."

"――――"

The sense shared only among true warriors, understood only by those in that realm.

Understanding that she herself was nowhere near that level, Frederica burned with frustration.

To have nothing at all to offer her brother, reunited after ten years apart.

"Don't go thinkin' somethin' stupid like that, sis."

"Garf..."

"Look at both my arms. These shields—these are the ones me and you used to play around with, back when we were kids. The startin' point of how strong I am now is runnin' around with you, sis."

At Garfiel's words, Frederica's eyes widened.

In a tone carrying something like concern, something like comfort, or perhaps some other feeling entirely mixed in, Frederica felt she could sense the sure growth of the brother who had been apart from her, and warmth spread through her chest.

"The boss beat me down through sheer numbers, sure. But unfortunately, in a life-or-death situation, that's a whole different story."

As he spoke, Garfiel stepped forward, clashing shield against shield, snapping his fangs together.

And then—

"Come at me, black bitch. This is a celebration for gettin' outta the Sanctuary. For starters, I'm gonna smash that first wall of yours to absolute pieces——!!"
