Arc 8 Chapter 17: "The Magical Approach"

The more this was needed, the deeper the conviction grew: this was an unprecedented crisis.
Among the history books recording events that had occurred in Lugunica in the past, there had been cases closely resembling this abnormal situation — but within the broader sweep of history, that precedent's treatment had never been a major one.

—The 'Demi-Human War.'
A large-scale civil war that had once occurred in Lugunica, and, just as in the present situation, the sole recorded history of an incident where the dead had risen and rampaged as enemies.
However, the true focus of the history books had been the friction between the human race and the Demi-Human race, and the details of the outlandish attacks carried out as part of that civil war had gone unrecorded.
That was maddening. If only the details of that time had been recorded in greater depth—

"Ain't no way I'm fallin' behind to bastards like these!!"

With a roar, a fist swung, and a cluster of pale-skinned Imperial soldiers went flying together.
The blow, closer to a sweeping backhand than a punch, was thrown carrying vitality drawn up hand over hand from the earth beneath his feet, hurled with all his strength like something flung bodily.

He kept it up, striking a second time, a third, each time scattering the enemy formation. —No, "formation" was too grand a word for it.
The enemy figures did press forward in a rough line, but there was nothing like discipline or coordination — nothing tactical about it at all. Which meant it was nothing more than a mass of individuals.
Against a mere mass of individuals, Garfiel wasn't the type to show himself falling behind.
Except—

"Zea! Zua!"

In time with a sharp lunge, a double slash grazed past Garfiel's neck.
He glared at the source of that hair-raising sensation, and what leapt out, kicking through the fragments of a shattered Undead, was a single figure whose bearing differed from the other Undead around it.

"Tch."

Clicking his tongue, he raised his shoulder and checked the neck the blade tip had barely grazed.
Between the first strike and the second, the reach of the slash had differed by about the width of a finger. A deliberately easy-to-dodge first blow, meant to lull him, followed by a second aimed to take his head off — the technique of a master.

It was merely a mass of individuals with no formation, but every so often, one among that mass carried this kind of skill mixed in.
This was unexpectedly troublesome — the rough estimate was that one in twenty was a skilled fighter. Because of that, he couldn't carelessly reassure his allies that coordination alone would see them through.
A single lapse in judgment could let one skilled fighter collapse a formation of a hundred.

"Zea! Zua—!"

Garfiel's nose creased, and the Undead let loose the same double slash again.
Having shown the technique once, this time its reach shifted more boldly. Before, the gap had been a finger's width; now it was a fist's width. If cut a fist's depth deeper, even the shallowest wound could turn fatal.
But the Undead that unleashed the technique widened its eyes — an expression at odds with any certainty of victory.

The reason was simple — the enemy's own right arm, the one holding the sword, had been shattered at the wrist.

"However much fancy trickery ya pull, the grip on that extendin' sword don't never move. 'Three-Headed Valgren's still got just the one body,' that's the sayin'... yeah!"

Flipping the left hand that had shattered the wrist, he sank his fist straight into the astonished face before him.
The instant the shock of the blow passed through to the back of the skull, the Undead's head burst apart like a fruit exploding. In step with the head's collapse, cracks raced down toward the torso and legs, and the whole body shattered to pieces.

"Don't like it."

Looking down at the wreckage of the foe he'd struck down, Garfiel spat out the words.
What stirred his irritation was this manner of the Undead's defeat — its "death," though "death" wasn't quite the right word for things already dead. More than anything, this way of being beaten, ending in nothing but fragments, robbed Garfiel of any sense that he was fighting something with life in it.
All that remained was a chilly, hollow sensation — just what exactly was he fighting?

"——"

Wiping the sweat trailing down his chin with his arm, Garfiel swept his gaze around. Across the nighttime plain that had become the battlefield, Undead soldiers appeared without letup.
To intercept them, the delaying-tactics unit, Garfiel among them, fought hard.
At first, Garfiel had been assigned to the healing team, making use of curative magic, but a policy of preventing casualties suited his temperament better than treating the wounded after the fact.

"Garf-san, the way you keep fidgeting is buggin' me! If you're that restless, go help out the people fighting up front directly!"

So Petra, who had been fighting hard alongside Garfiel on the same healing team, had said.
It wasn't exactly that he was obeying her — but Garfiel had leapt out onto the field. In fact, he thought he was managing results worthy of having volunteered.
And yet, he couldn't flatter himself that he was performing at his very best.
Because—

"Loose——!!"

A sharp command rang through the night darkness, and immediately after, the sound of taut bowstrings snapping loose chained together.
It was, at the single command of Taritta, chieftain of the "People of Shudrak," a hail of arrows released with astonishing density.
Falling not as individual points but as a single unbroken sheet, there was no way to dodge such a barrage — the only options were to take the hit or deflect it. In fact, any Undead capable of choosing something other than being showered with arrows tried to do exactly that, using their weapons or shields.
But—

"I see ya!"
"Let's do this!"

The Undead concentrated overhead were swatted away by powerful bows loosed with rousing shouts.
Literally — calling it "shot through" would be far too tame a word. It was a shock like being run down by a Dragon Carriage at full speed, sending the Undead who'd taken up a defensive stance, along with the cluster behind them, flying all together.

"I ain't exactly slouchin' either, but that there's on a whole other level."

The arrow-rain suppression wiped out the weaker Undead across the field, while the strong bows sniped the tougher ones that survived that barrage. The hunting rhythm of that hunting people, working in perfect concert, filled Garfiel with genuine admiration.
Even Garfiel, if pinned down like a pincushion full of arrows and then hit by one of those heavy bows, couldn't avoid taking serious damage. A tactic he was glad was on his side.

"Well, that lot over there's makin' just as little sense in their own way, though..."

In contrast to the Shudrak's high level of skill, another group earned Garfiel's admiration for entirely different reasons — the ragtag bunch that Subaru, who was supposed to be fighting a lone battle within the Empire, had gathered around him, and who called themselves the "Pleiades Battalion."

"Gonna do it, gonna do it, gonna do it, gonna do it——!"
"Strongest! Invincible! Excellent! Excellent!!"
"OOOOOOH——!!"

Even in the middle of a night battle, their sheer noisiness was abnormal, and even to Garfiel's eye, not one of them stood out as particularly accomplished in the martial arts. Certainly, plenty of them had honed skills roughly on par with an average Imperial soldier, but even so.
Most of them favored a way of fighting that could hardly be called refined, leaving everything to instinct.
And yet — they were strong. It was practically the difference in physical ability between an adult and a child.

"Magnificent. There's somethin' downright refreshin' about how strong those ones are."

As Garfiel watched the Pleiades Battalion fight, a voice spoke to him. Before he could even turn, the one who fell in beside him was a woman with dark skin and hair dyed red — Mizelda.
One of her legs was a wooden prosthetic, and Mizelda wore a blood-red smile at the sight of the battalion's rampage.

"Don't make no sense to me, but they're the boss's people, so — even when I think 'what the hell are they doin',' I ain't worried it's somethin' bad."

"The boss... Subaru, is it. Along with Emilia and the others, you place a great deal of trust in him, don't ya."

"Ha! Trust? That word ain't near enough. That man, the boss — he pays back a hundred times whatever expectation and trust I put in 'im!"

Not exaggeration, not bravado — Garfiel could praise him like that with absolutely zero doubt in his heart. At Garfiel's answer, the quality of Mizelda's smile shifted.
From something battle-hungry to something faintly gentle, tinged with understanding.

"I understand how ya feel. Subaru proved it to us, both durin' the 'Lifeblood Ritual' and in the fightin' after. The soul of a warrior. His face ain't much to look at, but I wouldn't mind him as a husband for Taritta."

"Quit talkin' about his face! The boss himself's plenty sensitive about them eyes of his! And besides..."

"And besides?"

"No matter how much the boss gets doted on, the one he's gone and fallen for is already decided, y'know."

"——I see. Fair enough."

Rubbing his nose with a finger, Garfiel got a deep nod from Mizelda in return.
He couldn't tell how much of what she'd said had been in jest and how much had been pure admiration, but the fact that Subaru was held in such regard even here in the Empire was something Garfiel himself felt proud of.
Wherever he went, Subaru dragged the people around him into producing great results.
But even though Subaru could surely make his way anywhere, Garfiel's own wish — and the shared will of the whole camp — was still to want him to stay with them regardless.
That was why they had come all this way to the Empire, crossing dangerous bridges together, to rush to his side.

"And yet, here they go interruptin' me and the boss havin' a good time roughhousin'...!"

Garfiel was a warrior, and he loved competing over strength. But he wasn't some battle junkie who wanted to fight anybody, anywhere, anytime, regardless of place.
A reunion with someone precious to him, a chance to share in that joy together — and yet the enemy kept spawning without letup, interrupting exactly that. Far from happiness, all it stirred up in him was pure irritation.

"Still, they really are a bunch that don't make no sense."

"That the dead should rise again is unnatural. I understand what you're sayin', but..."

"Nah, that ain't it, is what I'm sayin'."

"——?"

Grinding his clenched canines, Garfiel muttered, and Mizelda raised an eyebrow at his doubt.
To answer that doubt, Garfiel jerked his chin, turning his gaze toward the cluster of Undead still being pelted even now by Shudrak arrows.

"Watchin' 'em close, ya can tell... how these guys get taken down ain't all the same neither. Some of 'em break from just one arrow, and some of 'em don't break even with five stuck in 'em."

"Even among beasts, some are tougher than others. Same with people. Ain't that just the same thing?"

"I get there's a difference in strong and weak. But it ain't that, exactly..."

He couldn't explain it well, but even to Garfiel's eye, among Undead of roughly the same strength, there seemed to be a difference in the limit of their toughness.
Whether the arrow struck a vital spot or not didn't seem to matter much. Some were fine even with an arrow buried in the eye, while others shattered from one lodged in the shoulder.
It felt like something that couldn't be measured by simple strength of life force alone—

"Damn it! Thinkin' about it's makin' my head itch! 'Guiltilau of one-more-step,' that's what this is. Long as we smash 'em all up, we'll manage regardless, so..."

"——That very way of thinkin' is exactly what's meant by 'Guiltilau of one-more-step.'"

The instant that voice dropped from overhead, Garfiel's shoulders jumped hard.
Looking up in a hurry, he found a shadow slowly approaching from a night sky heavy with thick clouds. It swelled larger and larger in Garfiel's field of view, taking the shape of a man he did not care for.
And then—

"Sorry to interrupt your hard work, but I'm going to have to butt in he-e-ere."

"You, Dudley...!"

"Oop. Bad timing — you'd only just gotten used to the alias, but there's no longer any reason to hide the name after the meeting just now. As before..."

"Damn Roswaal...!"

"There's an unnecessary title attached, but I don't mind if you address me that way too-o-o."

So laughed Roswaal, having descended to the ground, provoking Garfiel's irritation.
Ever since his illegal entry into Vollachia, the makeup on his face and his outlandish clothing had stayed muted, but that distinctive way of speaking, which should have likewise been sealed away, had come back.
Hearing that the false name had been lifted too, the talks aboard the Dragon Carriage had presumably reached some conclusion.
The settlement in relations with the Empire that Otto and Frederica and the others had been discussing beforehand — reached, presumably, without bending Subaru's and Emilia's feelings on the matter.
That in itself was a welcome thing, even to Garfiel.

"If the talks're over, I'd like to hear how it turned out... Beatrice! Switchin' sides? Ain't like you to be tagging along with this bastard."

"Out of necessity, I suppose. Betty didn't want to leave Subaru's side even for a second, if it could be helped. But once relied upon, there's nothing for it, I suppose."

So saying, it was Beatrice who hopped down away from Roswaal's arms.
This past month, Beatrice had been active even without Subaru present — that memory was still fresh — but now that she'd finally managed to reunite with Subaru, Garfiel had assumed she'd resolved never to leave his side again.
In fact, just before everyone had mobbed the newly-woken Subaru aboard the Coupled Dragon Carriages, she'd been muttering exactly that, with her eyes completely glazed over.
For her to have left Subaru's side and come to the battlefield like this with Roswaal, of all things—

"Garfiel, you too felt something was off, I suppose. Have you come to pin down what that oddity is?"

"The... oddity's true nature?"

"Going into battle without knowing your enemy nets you less than half the results you'd hope for. To make up for that, one must know the enemy. Especially given how much of a mystery this particular enemy remains, is that not so-o-o?"

Roswaal, his arm swinging as if faintly lonely now that Beatrice had left his hold, spoke alongside her. From their words, Garfiel felt a surge of positive resolution about the meeting's outcome and a rise in fighting spirit.
Even if it came as a message brought by that Roswaal he disliked.

"Not Dudley, but Roswaal... so that's your name, then, is it."

"Yes, Mizelda-jō. For reasons of my own, I owe you an apology for having falsified my name. Not just I — the same is true of Emily, that is, Lady Emilia, as well-e-ell."

"A thing done by a fine-lookin' man. I'll forgive it."

"What an outrageous standard to judge by, I suppose..."

Mizelda digested the matter of the false name with her own unique set of values.
Meanwhile, Garfiel looked back and forth between Roswaal and Beatrice.

"So basically, the fightin' here in the Empire's still on, and the reason you two're here is..."

"For pinning down a phenomenon outside the bounds of reason, those with the arts of magic to interfere with reason itself are the most fitting, I suppose."

"A historic joint effort between Lugunica and Vollachia. The most valuable thing the Kingdom can offer them is precisely a magical approach toward getting to the bottom of the cause, that's what this i-i-is."

If magical knowledge could help pin down the cause of these Undead that kept spawning without limit, then indeed, within the Emilia Camp, no one was better suited to it than Roswaal and Beatrice.
Emilia was a Spiritual Arts User, Ram worked more by instinct, and Petra was still very much a student.

"So, having actually fought a good long while now, how's it look? Grasped anything?"

"——. Can't tell the difference between the ones that go down easy and the ones that don't. Figure the reason's somewhere separate from just strong or weak, but..."

"Hmm. A difference in survivability, in vitality, between individuals, is it."

Roswaal, pressing a long, slender finger to his chin, fell into thought, and inwardly Garfiel clicked his tongue.
It wasn't that every gesture Roswaal made rubbed him wrong — though that was true too — but this particular click of the tongue wasn't born of irritation at the man. It was born of the fact that he found himself hoping for something from that man's thinking and knowledge.
In this situation, he'd honestly, without meaning to, found Roswaal dependable.

"Roswaal, staring off vacantly won't connect you to any answers, I suppose."

"Agreed. Now then, in that case... Beatrice, how much mana do you have left?"

"Having met Subaru again, I'm in peak condition, I suppose."

"Splendid. Then he-e-ere we go—"

Roswaal, laughing faintly, narrowed his mismatched eyes, and immediately, four lights of differing colors — mana — rose up around him.
When Roswaal snapped his fingers, the four colored lights raced through the night darkness at arrow-like speed, plunging into the distant cluster of Undead, each unleashing its own particular force.

One Undead burst into flame; another was frozen solid. One had its limbs severed by a blade of wind; another was skewered from below by a mass of rock that burst up out of the ground.
All fatal blows, each with enough destructive force that a cracking sound rang out a second later and the target shattered to pieces. Even Garfiel, even Mizelda, raised an eyebrow slightly in surprise at the result.

"Just as reported, fire is the most effective element, is it not-o-ot. Wind doesn't penetrate well, and earth handles no differently from a blunt blow. Freezing seems inefficient for the cost."

But Roswaal, the one who'd carried it out, wasn't gauging things by the fact that he'd defeated the Undead — he was gauging the strength of each element by how well the attack had gone through on the Undead he'd struck down.
And Beatrice, too, had begun a verification of her own, in a form different from Roswaal's.

"——Vita."

Beatrice, raising a hand to the night sky, cast a spell that interfered with something slicing through that very sky as it flew — the hail of arrows loosed by the "People of Shudrak."
The countless arrows carrying out area suppression against the Undead, through the effect of Beatrice's shadow magic — magic that alters the weight of its target — had their weight multiplied several times over as they rained down on the enemy.
The magnitude of that increased force could be guessed just from the tremendous sound of the ground being gouged out along with the Undead struck.

"Just as Garfiel says, changing the weight of the arrows changes the force of impact, and yet there's no explaining why some zombies fall and some don't, I suppose."

"If ya make every last arrow heavier, won't that make it impossible to tell the difference?"

"Don't take Betty for a fool, I suppose. It's not that every arrow was made the same weight — I varied the weight arrow by arrow and tested it, I suppose."

Beatrice puffed out her cheeks like a scolded little girl, angry — but the statement she'd made in answering "don't take me for a fool" was, if anything, far more outlandish than the accusation itself.
Garfiel, too, though specialized in curative magic, was in a position to use magic himself, and he could tell just how precise Beatrice's manipulation of magic here was.
What she'd just done with magic was, in terms of difficulty, on par with threading a great many needles all at once without using her hands.
And what's more, the two of them, Beatrice and Roswaal—

"The cause of their collapse isn't the loss of limbs. Some are fine even with a vital point gouged out. In appearance they're humanoid, but it's probably best to stop thinking of them as living things at a-a-all."

"Even things that laugh, get angry, and talk being treated as not living — nobody but you could do that so easily, I suppose. ——It might be a matter of a fixed amount of accumulated damage, I suppose."

"What an unflattering assessment. Even I like to think I've regained quite a bit of my humanity, spending time with you all. Some of them go down from just losing one leg. If the condition is accumulated damage, that doesn't quite add u-u-up."

"Settling for simple individual differences in endurance would be too crude an argument, I suppose."

"There must be some other reason. The flow of mana is uniform."

"Uniform indeed... wait, I suppose. Too uniform, perhaps, I suppose."

Trading barbs back and forth all the while, Roswaal and Beatrice's analysis of the enemy pressed forward.
Remarkably, even as they bantered like that, the two of them were checking the properties of the pouncing Undead by making full use of magic in their own respective specialties.
Flame, wind, and violet arrows raged, and the Undead could not close the distance on the two, who fought back to back.

Of course, Garfiel and Mizelda were also attacking the Undead to keep them from closing in on the pair, but even without that, Roswaal and the others likely wouldn't have so much as flinched.
This was something Garfiel would never say aloud, since Beatrice would surely hate it absolutely—

"These two are in about as perfect sync as it gets."

"Ain't never lettin' her hear that."

Mizelda arrived at exactly the same assessment Garfiel had.
While showing off coordination that could only be described that way, Beatrice, unaware she was being thought of like that, drooped the corners of her eyebrows and, calling out "Roswaal!" to him—

"Go touch it, just the once, I suppose."

"——What an unreasonable thing to ask."

Leaving those words behind, Beatrice lightly kicked off the ground with her feet and leapt forward.
The hem of her dress flared lightly, and Beatrice's small body soared through the air with ease. This too was an unnatural leap achieved through shadow magic, erasing her own weight.
Beatrice's course led straight toward a single Undead with its back turned to her — which, sensing her approach, turned to face her.

"Jiwald."

In that instant, the right hand of the Undead about to swing its sword at Beatrice evaporated.
Roswaal, pointing at the Undead, had loosed a white light from his fingertip that burned away the enemy's arm. As the Undead froze in shock, Beatrice pressed her hand to its forehead.
And then, Beatrice's eyes, marked with their distinctive pattern, widened.

"As I thought, I suppose."

The moment Beatrice let that slip, her body was pulled back down, an arm wrapping around her slender waist.
Roswaal, drawing Beatrice's body close and swapping their front and back, threw a sharp punch with the arm not holding her, shattering the head of the Undead before it could break free of its paralysis.

"Good grief, and here it's me who's going to be scolded by Subaru-kun, isn't it?"

"And it's Betty who'll be praised by Subaru, I suppose. ——Restoration magic, I suppose."

"——So that's how it is."

Roswaal, who had voiced complaint at Beatrice's recklessness, closed his yellow eye at the words offered in place of an apology.
From that contact with the Undead just now, Beatrice had gained some kind of certainty. It seemed to have gotten across to Roswaal in just those few words, but unfortunately, to Garfiel it was utterly incomprehensible.

"Oi, I don't get a damn thing! Explain it so even Lady Emilia could understand, would ya!"

"Roughly speaking, Emilia's and Garfiel's level of understanding are about the same, I suppose. ——The structure of the zombies' bodies, the mechanism behind it — I think I've grasped it, I suppose."

"That's what I'm askin', what it is!"

Garfiel pressed his question, grinding his teeth.
Garfiel, too, knew of the existence of something called restoration magic. It was magic for repairing broken objects, and he'd heard that first-rate practitioners could even restore a burned book from its ashes back to its original state.
However, practitioners were few, since it required an exquisitely precise degree of magical control, and there were notable drawbacks too, like restored items being prone to a drop in quality.
And above all, restoring life was impossible. ——That wasn't restoration or repair; that fell squarely into the domain of a Forbidden Art, like the "Sacrament of the Immortal King" that had come up so often in conversation.

"Sensei chose mana as the vessel; I chose blood as the vessel. ——But has this 'enemy' chosen earth as the vessel, unconcerned even if the contents spill out?"

At the revelation Beatrice had granted him, Roswaal, pressing a hand to his mouth, let that much slip.
Garfiel's understanding still hadn't caught up with the two of them, but he could sense intuitively that it led down a path terribly dangerous, and toward an answer that would be unpleasant for him too.
And then, leaving Garfiel aside, Roswaal turned a grim expression on Beatrice.

"Beatrice, this isn't the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King,' is it?"

"...The origin point is the same; the approach differs, I suppose. With the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King,' the vessel comes first, the soul after, I suppose. But with these zombies—"

"The soul comes first, the vessel after. ——The flesh changes shape to match the soul."

At that one remark from Roswaal, Beatrice nodded deeply.
As ever, Garfiel couldn't follow the crucial part of the exchange between the two of them. Swallowing that bitterness, he then, without meaning to, doubted his own eyes.

"——"

There before him stood Roswaal, wearing an expression biting down on something even more bitter than Garfiel's own.
That he could twist his face like that was an expression Garfiel had never imagined seeing. ——No, he had thought, at some point, he'd love to plant a punch in that man's gut and watch him wear a face of agony, but entirely apart from that wish of Garfiel's, Roswaal was genuinely tormented.
With that torment still swimming in his eyes, Roswaal opened his mouth.

"——I might have some idea as to the 'enemy's' true identity."

"——! For real!? Then..."

"But wait. There's no way that could be. Because he, with these very hands—"

The composure he'd carried until just a moment ago vanished, and Roswaal's voice was now filled with hesitation and doubt.
At his evasive manner, avoiding a clear statement, Garfiel blinked, then bared his teeth. If this had been Garfiel himself, there was a chance whatever came to mind would be some Ton-Chin-Kan nonsense.
But the one who'd had this thought wasn't Garfiel — it was Roswaal.

"You, this ain't the time to be sayin' weak-ass things like that."

"——"

"Roswaal, let me hear one thing, I suppose."

Garfiel leaned forward, on the verge of grabbing him by the collar. But before his hand could lash out roughly, Beatrice's voice struck the silenced Roswaal first.
Beatrice gazed at Roswaal, and after waiting for him to look back at her—

"The reason for your hesitation — is it because it concerns Mother, I suppose?"

"...Am I really that easy to read?"

"There's hardly anything else that would rattle you this much besides something to do with Mother. Aside from that, perhaps something to do with Ram lately, I suppose."

"I do have confidence that I'd fret over you too, should anything happen to you, you know."

So answering, with a wry laugh, Roswaal shut his eyes tightly and tightened his cheeks. Then, opening his eyes, he pushed past the hesitation and weakness of a moment ago and nodded.

"Beatrice's read is correct. The mechanism by which the zombies are revived without their original corpses is an application of restoration magic. As a precondition for that, one needs to bring down a soul, and for this, the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' must have been applied."

"Neither restoration magic nor the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' is something you can just use the moment you understand the theory behind it, I suppose. To begin with, combining magics whose principles differ this drastically is such an outlandish feat that it's not even a matter of a handful of geniuses being capable of it, I suppose. The one who could pull that off is..."

"——Of Sensei's lineage. But it can't be Sensei herself. Which means"

The "Sensei" on Roswaal's lips and the "Mother" on Beatrice's lips were one and the same person — and someone who, for Garfiel too, could hardly be called a total stranger.
It was confusing, since multiple people bore that same name, but hearing that the name of that "Witch" was involved, a sense of understanding sprouted suddenly in Garfiel as well.

Beatrice's single remark had cut through Roswaal's torment, and the way he braced himself to accept something he didn't want to accept may have played its own part in bringing Garfiel to that understanding.
However—

"——What crude eyes."

Abruptly, Mizelda murmured in a low voice.
Owing to her unfamiliarity with magic, Mizelda, likely following Roswaal and Beatrice's conversation even less than Garfiel, had given up trying to understand the cause of Roswaal's torment and had focused single-mindedly on attacking the Undead.

Now she stopped, looked up overhead, and narrowed her keen eyes with hostility.
Garfiel, too, turned his gaze toward whatever the huntress's sharp eyes had pierced, and his throat tightened with a swallow. And it wasn't only Garfiel — Beatrice and Roswaal reacted the same way.
Though the reasons behind the three of their reactions likely differed, each in their own way.

For Garfiel, it was because he had seen the face of someone dear to him, someone who had no business being there.
For Beatrice and Roswaal, the reason seemed to stem from something far more negative.

——In the night sky, there was the figure of a girl in black robes, her long pink hair streaming.

It was the same face as someone Garfiel had adored since before he could remember — and yet it looked down on him now with cold eyes it had never once turned on him before.

"It was not what I would have preferred, but the practical trial succeeded... it seems this world has recognized me as a life, after all."

The familiar face murmured that in a familiar voice, gently tracing the crack that had run across her own face with a hand, glaring down at Garfiel and the others with golden eyes.
At that, there came the sound of someone swallowing hard, and Roswaal opened his mouth.

"So you were alive after all... Sphinx."

"No, I am dead. ——Observation required."

In a flat, detached voice, as if mocking them, that girl — the "Witch" whose appearance was the very image of Ryuzu, Garfiel's grandmother — spoke those words with the face of an Undead.
