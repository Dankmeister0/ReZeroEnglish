Arc 5 Chapter 48: "Someone I'll Come to Love Someday"

For a moment, Emilia couldn't grasp the meaning of what had been said to her. She caught her breath despite herself. At that, the young man before her—Regulus—smiled and raised a hand lightly at her reaction.

"Ah, sorry for springing that on you. I may have startled you a bit. I'll apologize honestly for that much. As I keep saying, I'm a man who can apologize. There are unsightly people in this world who can't even admit their own faults and just pile up messy excuses instead, but honestly, not being able to admit a mistake like that just shows such a small vessel of a person, it makes me sick. I think it's because they're under the delusion that they've never been wrong about anything, that they've been right about everything from the moment they were born until this very instant—but really, how arrogant is that? If they just took a good, hard look at their own feet, properly recognized how small a being they are compared to something as vast as the world, none of that would happen. A single apology, don't you think, reveals a person's character? Their humanity, so to speak. Don't you agree?"

"Um, apologizing is important, right?"

"Yes! That's right, apologizing is important. Good. It's an obvious thing, but I'm glad we can agree on it. There are, surprisingly, a lot of people in this world who can't agree even on something this obvious. It makes me sick. Well then, it seems our understanding of apology within marriage aligns without issue. I have high hopes we'll get along well from here on out. Now, about the apology—the issue was that I was, perhaps, a bit hasty..."

Having said that much, Regulus's gaze traveled over Emilia, up and down. Wrapped only in a towel, Emilia stiffened slightly under that look.

"Yes, modesty matters even between husband and wife. On that point too, I think very highly of you. That said, about my earlier question—I don't want you to misunderstand. It's not that I was trying to confirm whether you're a virgin from some vulgar angle. As I've said many times, I am your husband, and you are my wife. A married couple must be bound by strong love and consideration for one another. Since we'll be tying ourselves to each other with the long, long chain of love, it's only natural to offer everything of yourself to your partner. So, I want the certainty that no other man has touched you."

"Certainty that... no one else has touched me...?"

"Of course, treating the presence or absence of a hymen as solid proof would be nonsense. But as one touchstone among others, I think it holds very important value. So I dared to ask, even knowing it might make you feel unpleasant. I want you to understand—I ask because I love you. I couldn't care less about the virginity of someone I don't love. It's because I love you that I confirm it."

Fluently, so fluently, Regulus went on rattling off the grounds for his own thinking. Tossed about by that wave of words, Emilia felt something eerie in the way Regulus spoke so evenly. There was a nagging sense of déjà vu about him that gnawed endlessly at her chest, and the content, slipping past like poured water, refused to catch in her memory. Only one thing became clear. The word he placed such importance on—virgin. That was—

"So, let me ask again. —Hey, are you a virgin? Well?"

"Um, what's a 'virgin'? I'm sorry. I've never heard that word before."

"...What?"

To the question released with such fanfare, Emilia answered apologetically. She understood that he held some strong attachment to it, but she had no idea what it actually was. Probably something to do with girls, she thought.

At Emilia's words, Regulus's expression clouded as he repeated the question back in a low voice. He lowered his eyes and fell completely silent. Anxiety welled up at the pensive gesture, but the silence was shorter than expected. Regulus's eyes snapped wide open, and he extended his hand toward Emilia. And then—

"Wonderful. —You are my ideal maiden."

"Um?"

Taking Emilia's hand, Regulus wore a bright, beaming smile on his face. It was different from the smile he'd worn until now—a truly delighted expression. The kind of radiant look a child gets when handed the toy they'd been longing for by their parent. Regulus shook Emilia's captured hand up and down, nodding repeatedly.

"Yes, that's how it should be. I'd had a vague sense that physical virginity wasn't really fitting as a touchstone. But true purity, in the real sense, is something that dwells in the heart. Of course the body is a virgin! What truly matters is that the heart, too, remains a virgin. I feel like I've reached the truth. Isn't this amazing? You've brought something new to me, even though I should have already been fulfilled."

"——"

"Mm, mm, understood. Rest easy, I'll welcome you as my wife. And thanks to you, I've realized something important too. From now on, when taking a new wife, just asking whether she's a virgin won't do. Unless she's a girl who doesn't even know the meaning of 'virgin,' it lowers a wife's value. Adultery of the heart is unacceptable. Not fitting for my wife."

Releasing Emilia's hand, Regulus stepped about in evident satisfaction. The meaning of his words didn't quite land for Emilia. To begin with, the true meaning behind this "husband" and "wife" talk was unclear. In Emilia's understanding, a married couple meant one father and one mother, but from what Regulus said, the existence of multiple wives could be inferred.

That was far removed from Emilia's common sense of what a married couple should be. Were they perhaps talking about something different that merely shared the same sound?

"Oh, right, I can't very well leave you dressed like that forever, can I? I'll have a change of clothes prepared right away. —Number one hundred eighty-four! Come here."

"——"

While Emilia remained bewildered, Regulus suddenly spoke a number aloud. At that, from beyond the corridor appeared the woman from before, the one who had left Emilia alone in the room. The long-haired blonde woman took her place beside Regulus with a demure bearing and bowed respectfully on the spot. Regulus dipped his chin at her gesture.

"Get her—Number seventy-nine—changed. Once preparations are complete, we'll hold the wedding ceremony. She'll be in the same position as the rest of you. Get along with her, and look after her, would you."

"——"

"Yes, you've stopped smiling. —Good girl. A fine wife."

To the woman who only lowered her chin without a word, Regulus murmured in satisfaction. Then he walked over to Emilia, who was still left behind by the circumstances, and gently slipped his fingers into her silver hair, stroking the long strands.

"Well then, see you later."

"Yes..."

Emilia's instincts urged her not to defy him. Nodding at Emilia's short reply, Regulus turned and, with the sound of footsteps, vanished down the corridor.

Once his figure was out of sight, Emilia noticed her body released from tension. That was how unconsciously her guard had been up against Regulus. Regulus, who had seemed to be merely relaxed, just standing there—and yet the sheer, abnormal pressure of his presence had made Emilia think of a threat comparable to facing the Great Rabbit.

"This way."

The woman beside her suddenly called out to Emilia, who had kept her eyes fixed on the vanished figure. Hearing the woman's voice for the first time, it was a clear, beautiful tone reminiscent of a stringed instrument. But that voice, too, was stiff and frozen of any emotion whatsoever, just like her expression.

"Um, I'm sorry. I have a lot I want to ask..."

"Your change of clothes."

"The clothes too, but I want to talk... do you know where here is? I was in Priestella's main plaza with someone from the Witch Cult and... ah, geez."

Ignoring Emilia's attempt at a question, the woman simply began walking. Hurrying to follow behind her, Emilia tried firing off questions in rapid succession to somehow grasp the situation, but the woman's ramrod-straight back silently rebuffed every one of them. Thus guided by the woman walking ahead, Emilia was led to the room next to the one she'd been laid in—also a simply built room, but one crammed forcibly with furniture into an odd sort of space.

"This place... wasn't it originally a different kind of room...?"

"The master brought in things like the wardrobe for garments. Number seventy-nine, your change of clothes."

"That number seventy-nine—that's me? Earlier, I feel like Regulus called me that too. And you're..."

"Number one hundred eighty-four. His wife, the same as you."

"The same as me..."

Closing the door, the woman—the one who called herself Number 184—finally responded to conversation. Her voice remained just as frozen of emotion, but at last there seemed a chance for actual conversation.

"That 'wife' thing, I've heard it a few times now—does it mean wife, like, a married woman? Because I have no intention of becoming Regulus's wife..."

"Whether or not you intend to, he does. And the fact that he intends it means your own will is irrelevant."

"That's strange. To become a wife, you have to marry a husband, don't you? I haven't married Regulus, and I have no intention of doing so. Marriage is when a man and a woman like each other enough to want to stay together forever. I can't make that kind of promise to anyone yet."

"As for the wedding, it will be held shortly. And that will be the end of it."

Number 184 had no ear for Emilia's words. Sensing that the conversation seemed to be happening while not actually happening, Emilia's confusion only deepened. Meanwhile, Number 184 approached Emilia and reached to strip away the towel blanket wrapped around her body.

"Wait. Hold on, what are you doing?"

"Getting you into the bridal gown right away. Fortunately, the garments are ready. Your size was confirmed when you were undressed to be laid on the bed, so rest assured."

"Was it you who undressed me?"

"Did you think it was the master? Rest assured. He has no interest in peering carelessly at women's skin, and to begin with, he has no interest in women. Even checking whether you're a virgin, he won't do anything more than that."

"Do you talk about that 'virgin' thing too?"

"...I'm surprised. Don't tell me you really don't know, and it's not an act?"

For the first time, something like emotion showed faintly on Number 184's face. Emilia's eyes widened at the faint glimpse of surprise, and then she smiled thinly.

"Huh. So you can be surprised too. Then I wish you'd talk to me smiling. I think it'd suit you so much better."

"...The master doesn't wish it. I'll give you a warning too—the master is fond of your usual face. It's wiser not to change your expression, whether smiling or sad. If possible, I'd suggest not even opening your mouth."

"You mean don't talk? Why?"

"Because I don't know what might infringe on the master's rights."

Stripping the towel blanket from Emilia, Number 184 held out undergarments. Putting them on as handed, Emilia found the fit, indeed, exact. As Emilia turned her limbs about in her underwear, Number 184 suddenly let out a long breath.

"Is something wrong?"

"...No. I just thought you were beautiful. Your slender limbs, your white skin, that long silver hair."

"—? Thank you. Even as flattery, that makes me happy. There's really only Subaru and Anne who say things like that to me."

"Subaru... a man?"

"Yes, my knight. He's probably worrying about me so-o much right now. So I want to find out where he is as soon as I can..."

He was probably worrying an enormous amount, no doubt. In Emilia's mind, there was no worry that Subaru might have been done in. He had Beatrice with him, and to begin with, Emilia couldn't imagine a situation dire enough to put Subaru at death's door. Subaru would, probably, somehow make anything work out. Precisely because of that, being caught here, unable to say anything to Subaru, felt so pitifully inadequate.

"Please, never mention that man Subaru in front of the master."

"Eh, why?"

"To borrow the master's own words—because it would raise doubts about the virginity of your heart."

"That 'virgin' thing again?"

Being given a reason without an explanation was no help at all. Without further explanation to Emilia, who pursed her lips, Number 184 took a white dress from the closet and held it up against Emilia's body. It was a beautiful dress, dazzling in appearance, adorned with a number of showy ornaments.

"But it looks hard to move in."

"It's also wiser not to voice complaints. Let's get you changed."

Wondering, with a tilt of her head, whether such a beautiful garment would even suit her, Emilia let herself be guided by Number 184's instructions, threading her arms through the sleeves and changing into the dress.

"—Ah, isn't this lovely. Just as I thought, white suits you."

"...Thank you."

Seeing Emilia now changed, Regulus said so with a bright expression. His own appearance had changed too, from when they'd crossed paths in the corridor earlier. When Emilia's face showed she'd noticed, Regulus lightly spread the collar of his own garment to show it off.

"It's an important wedding, after all. Staying my usual, unadorned self is one way to think about it, but I couldn't bear to embarrass you over such a trivial point of stubbornness. A married couple's ideal is a relationship where caring for one another isn't a burden. I don't intend for you to feel weighed down thinking I made some accommodation over a matter this small, but I do want you to understand I'm a man who can accept a bit of change for your sake. The venue, too, will be ready soon."

"The venue... you mean here, right?"

Following Regulus, dressed in a white tuxedo, formal wear worn with ease, as he turned his head, Emilia too looked around the space she was in. It was a cathedral—properly speaking, a cathedral used for important rites, weddings among them.

Changed by Number 184, Emilia had only realized, upon stepping outside the building, that she had been in a room within what was a control tower. From there, she'd been led by Number 184 straight from the control tower to this cathedral nearby.

Inside the cathedral, several figures bustled about busily, decorating the interior, quietly proceeding with wedding preparations. And carrying out the venue's setup in silence, without exception, were all beautifully dressed women.

"They are my wives, girls in the same position as you. My wives number two hundred ninety-one in total... sadly, many have died and parted from me. Even so, I intend to pour equal love into those who still stand by my side now. Naturally, right? A way of loving that favors one person over another is a terribly warped thing for a husband to do. I would never do something so disloyal. A fixed love, in a fixed amount, delivered in a fixed way. No bias, no inequality, no unfairness in that. Rest assured. I'll love you equally too."

"What you're saying, it's somehow..."

"—Master. A word, if I may."

Just as Emilia was about to continue with "strange"—

Number 184, who had been standing in line, stepped forward and spoke to Regulus. Regulus's eyebrow twitched faintly at her words.

"Say. You can see I'm talking with her right now, can't you? Speaking up at a moment like this—don't you think that's an act of scattering poison over the sprout of love she and I are cultivating? Don't you think so? That kind of small consideration matters between husband and wife, I think. Isn't that exactly what I've told you all before? And yet interrupting like this is treating my one small wish with contempt. Number one hundred eighty-four, what do you think?"

"My apologies. But it is an important matter. I know it's presumptuous of me, but I say this out of concern for the master as well. Please, I beg you to hear me out."

Speaking quickly, his whole body radiating a dangerous air, Regulus loomed. Yet Number 184, exposed to that sharp presence, pressed her point without breaking her resolute bearing. Naturally, at that posture, Regulus's own thorny air receded somewhat.

"...Fine. Go ahead, speak. Showing generosity to a wife is a proper measure of a husband's tolerance too. I'm not so petty a man that I can't manage that much."

"Thank you very much. About the broadcast from a little while ago—will everything be alright? If, during the wedding, there should be some interference..."

"The broadcast? Ah, that trembling voice, mumbling something incoherent. Doesn't matter, does it? Leaving it alone won't cause any problems. Sounded like some sniveling coward listing off nonsense without knowing his own limits. Whether it's Capella or Sirius or those trash, I don't know what would happen to them... but it has nothing to do with me. Or is it that you don't trust my strength? Doubting your husband's abilities, as a wife?"

"No, I believe in you. With the master present, we have nothing to worry about. I only wished to have my anxiety wiped away by the master's own words. Please, forgive an unworthy wife who can't even honestly rely on you."

Number 184 tried to deflect Regulus's pressing questions with what seemed like a prepared answer. Weaving the words of a weak woman with an emotionless expression and voice. At her words, Regulus blinked as if moved, shaking his head.

"So that's how it was. Then it's my fault too, for not thinking it through. I should have noticed that anxiety in your heart, even unspoken. One should be able to consider their partner without needing words, and here I was so self-centered. I've reflected on that."

"It is I who should apologize. Your words have given me courage, master. I too will set to work on the venue at once."

"Ah, please do."

Bowing, Number 184 turned her back to Regulus. At the same time, her eyes flicked to Emilia with some kind of signal. It was, most likely, a warning about the carelessness of Emilia's earlier interrupted remark. She must have meant to say that Emilia was underestimating how dangerous Regulus was. Because that had come across, Emilia didn't hesitate in her judgment for what came next.

"—Look out!"

"Eh?"

As Number 184 tried to pass beside her, Emilia yanked her arm hard toward herself. Pulling the tall but light body into her chest, Emilia took one large step backward. Right before Emilia's eyes, in the space where Number 184 had been standing an instant before, a wind swept through, tearing up the cathedral floor, ripping it open, destruction cutting through in a straight line. The wind didn't stop there but slammed directly into the cathedral's great doors, shattering the entrance to splinters and blasting the destruction outside.

"——"

At that overwhelming, straight-line destructive force, Emilia, still holding Number 184, couldn't even find her voice. Number 184 too, perhaps sensing the destruction that had erupted behind her, had gone stiff, having made herself as small as possible. And to the two of them, Regulus, still standing at the point of origin of the destruction with his right arm raised, said—

"Sorry, sorry. I got a bit careless there. Glad nothing happened to either of you."

"——"

"I'll be in the waiting room until it's time, so call me once things are ready. Ah, you should probably have your hair done up in the waiting room too. I think that would be far more attractive. You're beautiful as you are, of course, but I don't think one should skimp on the effort to be beautiful. If anything, continuing to be beautiful, striving to become even more beautiful—I think that's the bare minimum courtesy owed to someone who's fond of you. Of course, I'm content with the fulfilling circumstances I already have, but I have no intention of placing limits on what's offered to me. If your way of showing affection tends toward making things even more fulfilling, I won't be the one to stop you."

Speaking of the destruction that had just occurred, an instant ago, as though it were nothing at all, Regulus smiled at Emilia and disappeared through a door at the side of the cathedral into what he called the waiting room. Emilia let out a deep breath, staring blankly at the traces of destruction.

"What was that, just now...?"

"...Thank you for saving me."

From within Emilia's arms, Number 184 slipped free, saying so. Having been stiff with tension, she smoothed her disheveled hair and immediately stepped away from Emilia's side. Her feet carried her toward the women setting up the cathedral's interior. Looking at those women, Emilia saw they too continued their work with faces utterly indifferent to the destruction that had just occurred. If anything, several of them were already gathering, in a line, toward the ruined floor and door, evidently starting work to make the traces as inconspicuous as possible.

"Wait! This is so strange! Don't any of you think something's wrong here?"

"——"

At that matter-of-fact attitude, Emilia raised her voice in confusion. But the women paid no heed to Emilia's voice, simply continuing their silent work. Getting nowhere, Emilia went to the one person who seemed capable of conversation—Number 184.

"You almost just got killed, you know? If I hadn't pulled you back, you'd surely have been torn apart right there. Weren't you scared? And yet—"

"And yet what? I already thanked you for saving me. What more do you want from me beyond that? Wouldn't asking for more be an infringement of rights?"

"I'm not talking about rights or obligations! I'm talking about something more important, something much more important!"

Emilia gestured with her hand around the cathedral, pointing at the women continuing their work.

"Regulus called you his wives. Are all of you his wives? Is that why you obey what he says? Because you're wives, even when you nearly get killed, you just silently accept it...? That's strange. It's wrong!"

"That is simply one shape that a marriage can take. Once you're in the same circumstance, you too will grow used to it eventually. ...And if you don't grow used to it, then that's that."

"That's not right... isn't marriage supposed to be something happy, happy people do, out of happiness? To me, neither you nor anyone else here looks happy. Am I wrong?"

"Yes, you are wrong. One can marry without being happy. A husband and wife can become one without loving each other. Stay together long enough, and you become a married couple. You get used to being a married couple."

Number 184 didn't deny that she was in this position against her will. But without denying it, she affirmed her current position all the same. That was warped, and wrong. Marriage, being a married couple—that should be something one wants to become, not something one merely gets used to.

"I have no intention of going along with a wedding. I'm leaving, right now."

"——"

The women, who hadn't listened to Emilia's words until now, raised their heads at that declaration. Still in their gowns, they stared at Emilia, who had just announced her boycott of the wedding. Exposed to a storm of emotionless eyes, Emilia squared her shoulders.

"There are people worrying about me. There's a lot I have to take care of. So I can't let myself be held up in a place like this. I'm going to rejoin everyone right away and do what I need to do."

"The master will never allow such a thing."

"I don't intend to become Regulus's wife. So I don't want his permission. I'll rejoin everyone, and then... I'll definitely come back to save all of you."

"—!"

"I understand now that none of you are with Regulus because you want to be. So I'll tell Regulus to let all of you go. Even so, if there's anyone who wants to stay with Regulus, they can go on being his wife. But anyone who wants to leave, I'll let leave. A marriage forced like this—if it can't make both people happy, there's no meaning to it at all."

What Emilia pictured in marriage, what she hoped a bond ought to be, was two people who loved each other. What rose in her mind was the figures of Fortuna and Juice, seen in that dream she'd once had. Those two never married, never became husband and wife—but even so, she'd thought it was good. It was those two whom Emilia had wished could marry. A happy marriage, a loving married couple—surely that relationship was exactly where such things ought to exist. And so—

"I know of people who loved each other but couldn't marry. So I hate the idea of a relationship where you're married but can't be happy."

"............"

At Emilia's declaration, unease spread among the emotionless women. But Number 184 was the first to break free of that unease. She looked straight at Emilia, then turned her gaze to the shattered entrance.

"If you say you'll leave, that's your freedom. But in that case, the master will not forgive us. Without fail, all of us will be slaughtered."

"Even though you're his wives...?"

"A wife who cannot fulfill her husband's wish is, by the master's standard, no different from a wife who has failed her role. If you leave, we die. And if you leave anyway, it is you who will have killed us."

"——"

To Emilia, Number 184 spoke while holding her own life hostage. As if to demonstrate that her opinion, in its extremity, represented the consensus, the women within the cathedral rose to surround Emilia, restraining her movement. Of course, not a single one among them could stop Emilia by force. They were, without exception, ordinary women. Ordinary women who'd grown up in ordinary households, cultivated ordinary morals, and lived envisioning ordinary happiness.

Women who, somewhere along the line, had simply had their gears go wrong, and ended up taken in by Regulus as wives.

"——"

The reason Emilia couldn't say anything back to their grim resolve was that she herself had witnessed Regulus's outrage firsthand. If Regulus, over nothing more than being talked back to a little, had so easily tried to snuff out a life, then there was no guarantee he wouldn't take out his anger on them upon learning of Emilia's escape. And that was something none other than his wives themselves understood best.

"How many of Regulus's wives are here?"

"The master's spouses number two hundred ninety-one in total. Of those, two hundred thirty-eight have already died and parted from him, so what remains is all fifty-three of us present here."

"Those wives who died and parted..."

"Is an explanation necessary?"

The hoarse question felt almost like it was mocking Emilia's own question. From that answer, Emilia understood the answer even without asking further. And that answer was, in a roundabout way, an answer to the fate awaiting these women should Emilia herself flee.

"If I leave, you'll all be made to suffer terribly..."

Terrible suffering—no, more likely it would be an unavoidable death. Beyond doubt, these women were hostages to Emilia's own free will. Considering the harm that would come from escaping this place, acting carelessly wasn't an option.

She thought of Subaru and the others outside the cathedral, in Priestella, surely worried about her. Thinking of them, Emilia apologized only within her heart. And then—

"I understand. Let's hold the wedding."

After that, preparations for the venue proceeded at a rapid pace. Repairs to the traces of destruction, though amateur work, were mended into a reasonably inconspicuous state. Just from the deftness of it, one could guess that these women had, many times before, dealt with the aftermath of Regulus's tantrums.

Having resolved to go through with the wedding, Emilia too had her hair done up in the waiting room by Number 184 and the other wives of Regulus, and was fitted with ornaments beyond the dress itself. Having her hair styled so elaborately was something that hadn't happened since Puck had shut himself inside the crystal stone—aside from the one time she'd borrowed Annerose's hands.

Her long silver hair was gathered up, styled with braiding worked in. Ornaments were added, just enough not to overpower the modest elegance of the white dress without becoming gaudy, and with that, Emilia's bridal attire was complete.

"——"

Looking at her reflection in the mirror, Emilia marveled at the women's skill. Indeed, it was a world apart from her usual self. These days, with no one around to fix her hair up nicely, she just tied it back simply whenever Subaru wasn't there to do it for her, and she didn't wear accessories, prioritizing ease of movement instead—all of which, it seemed, played their own part in feminine charm. She could only think that all of it was too much, wasted on her.

"Well then, let's go. Please, whatever you do, don't upset the master's mood."

Number 184 tossed repeated warnings at Emilia as they left the waiting room. Heading toward the cathedral, they found the assembled guests already waiting for Emilia's arrival—every one of them women in the position of Regulus's wives—and, before the altar, Regulus himself, standing in white formal wear.

Not knowing the proper procedure, Emilia nonetheless walked the red carpet laid from the entrance to the altar, heading toward Regulus, who awaited her there. Regulus, having waited, nodded in satisfaction at the sight of Emilia dressed up.

"Even in just the dress you looked transformed, but all decked out, you're on another level entirely. As I thought, it was the right call to leave seat number seventy-nine empty. I'm impressed with my own judgment."

"Seventy-nine... why was that number left empty?"

"Hm? Ah, that's because, some time ago, there was a woman I'd taken a fancy to, thinking she might suit that number—but sadly, before I could welcome her in, I judged her unfit at that stage. Still, the most important thing, her appearance, was close to my ideal. So, with some lingering regret, I left that seat empty. If it means I was able to find you thanks to it, I suppose even that mistake had its worth."

"Empty before, that..."

What was it? That phrase sharply provoked the sense of unease that had faintly faded in Emilia. The unease was taking on a shape, but it still refused to become clear. Meanwhile, Regulus straightened his own garments before the bride-attired Emilia.

"Well then, shall we exchange the rite of marriage? I imagine it'll be a simplified affair, but that's fine with you, isn't it? The essential thing is that the ceremony proceeds properly—the outer trappings of the content are nothing but garnish. It seems like such a cliché, fussing over the shell while neglecting the substance, but I won't stumble into such a foolish rut. It's rather comical, not seeing the true essence of a thing like that, isn't it? The sort who just skim the obvious, superficial part, the surface layer, and feel satisfied with that? People like that don't even notice when they're being sneered at from behind, so I suppose, as long as it's all self-contained, they're happy enough."

"——"

As Regulus pressed on, speaking without pause, Number 184 moved to the far side of the altar. It seemed she held something like the role of officiant in this rite of marriage. Indeed, among the fifty-three wives present, she seemed to have taken on something like a role of coordinator. That he'd tried to kill such a person on a whim showed that Regulus had no grasp of the power dynamics holding his wives together. In that sense too, Emilia thought, he truly was an unforgivable man.

"Hey, Regulus. Before the wedding, there are a few things I want to say."

And so Emilia, in this moment where she could face him directly, said so straight to Regulus's face. Not having discussed it beforehand—not that any satisfying discussion had taken place at all—Number 184's cheek stiffened at Emilia's unplanned remark. But Regulus, unexpectedly, nodded favorably at Emilia's words.

"Ah, right. Actually, there were quite a few important things I wanted to tell you too, about becoming my wife. I thought about explaining things one by one after the wedding, but I think a proper mindset is needed before we're bound together too. Isn't it a tragedy when, after marrying, it turns out 'this is different, that is different'? To prevent that kind of unhappy thing from happening, I think we should clearly put our thoughts into words for each other. Even calling it husband and wife, we're still two individual people, so that matters, doesn't it?"

"Yes, that's right. Since we're individual people, it matters."

"Right? I'm glad we see eye to eye on this. Now then, there are a few things I've had the other wives promise me too, so let's go over those. First of all—after you marry me, I forbid you from smiling."

"...?"

Furrowing her brow, Emilia showed her lack of comprehension at Regulus's proposal. At that, Regulus raised a finger and continued, "This is important."

"This is important. See, I love your face. I love it tremendously. I make it a rule to choose my wives by their faces. Beautiful, lovely, well-formed, attractive faces—that's how I choose my wives. All two hundred ninety-one women I've made my wives had beautiful faces. Your face is lovely too. That's why I'll make you my wife. Understand?"

"——"

"You know, I think there are more selfish people in this world than one would expect. You hear stories all the time about love cooling off after becoming lovers or getting married, don't you? Even though they're supposed to like each other, once they actually try living together, there turn out to be all sorts of things that don't match. Their tastes in food don't match. Their lifestyle habits don't match. Their hobbies don't match. Their schedules don't match. There are so many trashy people out there, using such self-serving excuses to grow disillusioned with someone they supposedly loved. I, honestly, despise those hopeless idiots from the bottom of my heart."

Still smiling, Regulus spoke of his own shape of love with intense enjoyment. Innocently, without restraint, letting seep through a righteous indignation toward those who trampled on love.

"Everyone's so selfish. Why grow disillusioned? Why get disillusioned just because you and someone you like have slightly different sensibilities? How could such a stupid thing even be allowed? It's absurd. That's why I choose the one I love by their face. As long as it's a face I love, I'll never be disillusioned, no matter what kind of person that face belongs to. Because it's the face I love. As long as it's that face, my love will never, ever cool."

"——"

"Even if she doesn't put away the clothes she's taken off. Even if she's a murderer who's killed several children. No matter how disastrously bad her cooking is. Even if she's sold off her own parents and siblings as debt collateral. Even if she can't be bothered to sort the wash and everything comes out dyed the wrong color. Even if she's some deranged person who secretly enjoys killing animals for fun. No matter how terrible her taste in clothes. Even if she has the filthy nature of someone obsessed with money. Even if she never bathes and reeks like filth. Even if she's seriously plotting the destruction of the world—I will not hate her."

One after another, in succession, Regulus pointed at the fifty-three women present and shouted this. It wasn't clear how many of the conditions he named applied to any of the women here. It wasn't clear, but he declared, without exception, that he would love any of them by their face regardless.

"There's no talking about it in the past tense, like 'I loved you.' I love your face. So even if you were, say, a witch plotting to inflict the utmost agony on every person in the world and kill them all, I wouldn't be disillusioned. As long as it's that face."

"...What does that have to do with not being allowed to smile?"

"It's simple. There are girls who are cute and pretty when they're just normal, but turn ugly when they smile, right? I can't stand that. So I said 'smile,' but the same condition applies to crying faces too. Basically, I hate the possibility of your cute, cute face turning unattractive. So don't smile. Don't cry. Don't get angry. Just keep that cute face."

Gripping Emilia's chin, Regulus forced this on her in a low voice. What would happen if she defied him—the answer to that had already been demonstrated by what just happened. What Emilia couldn't understand was the logic by which a man who spoke so much of "loving by the face" could still commit such an atrocity against a wife.

"You said you're fond of faces and never get disillusioned... so then why did you attack her just now?"

"Hm?"

Regulus tilted his head at Emilia, who pointed at Number 184. Still pointing at Number 184, Emilia slipped free of Regulus's fingers at her chin.

"If I hadn't pulled her arm, she would surely be dead right now. You liked her face too, didn't you—that's why you made her your wife? So why would you do something like that?"

"Ah, that's simple too. It's because she offended my, otherwise gentle, mood. I don't ask for much, and yet there seem to be quite a lot of people who can't spare a thought for others. I don't even want to imagine that a wife of mine could be like that, but what's happened has happened. Since it can't be helped, she needs to properly take responsibility for it, don't you think?"

"So you're saying you were disillusioned? But that contradicts what you just said..."

"I'm not disillusioned at all. I still love her face, still love her, unchanged. So even if she had died, I would go on loving her all the same. Isn't it a common saying? Even if the person you love dies, they go on living within your heart. Your love for them lives on, undiminished. That's exactly what I am."

Regulus's logic was perfect. Perfectly, without a single cloud, complete entirely within himself. There wasn't a sliver of room in it for anyone else's intentions to intrude. A flawlessness born of total absence.

At Emilia's silence, Regulus knit his brow. He peered into the eyes of the tight-lipped Emilia, with a color of suspicion.

"I've been thinking, since a while ago... don't tell me you have some complaint against me? If you do, that's rather hurtful, isn't it? I've shown you this much consideration, made this much concession, taken this much care as I could—and you don't understand that thoughtfulness? What does that say about a person, really? If you had even the slightest bit of consideration for others, even the capacity to think from another's standpoint, I don't think that would happen. Not being able to manage even that small courtesy means you don't see value in the other person. That means you're belittling them. Which means, in essence, it's an act of disrespect toward me, as an individual. That, I can't forgive."

"You know, I think marriage is something so-o wonderfully happy."

"Huh?"

"A ceremony that gives shape to two people who like each other wanting to be together. Liking someone is such an enormous thing—finding just one person among so many, and having that person like you back in return... I think that's an amazing thing."

Placing a hand on her chest in her bridal attire, Emilia spoke, and Regulus's face turned uneasy. In her place, the expressions of the wives lined up in attendance, and Number 184's at the altar, began to cloud over. It was a judgment born of alarm at the way this was heading, of concern for Emilia. —Proof that they were good people, capable of caring for others.

"Why do you call your wives by numbers?"

"Fussing over how to address someone? That's the same kind of judgment as fussing over appearances—it clearly shows you don't understand love. It's precisely because you lack confidence in your ability to keep loving, or a real sense of loving each other, that you have to decorate it up with such unnecessary things. On that point, I'm not swept along by such shallow self-flattery, so there's no need for me to fuss over such things. If loving equally means stripping away unnecessary elements, isn't that only natural?"

"...I see. But I don't dislike it when Subaru calls me 'Emilia-tan.'"

"Subaru...?"

Regulus's face colored with displeasure at the name, as if it were something he couldn't let pass unremarked. But Emilia, ignoring the change in Regulus's expression before her, continued.

"When he calls me Emilia-tan, Subaru's feelings are packed into it. And when, sometimes, he just calls me 'Emilia' plainly, I know right away it's a special moment. I don't think that's wasteful at all. A name should be able to carry that kind of feeling."

"Hey now, this conversation's moving along on its own, but who's Subaru? That's a person's name, right? A man's name, isn't it? For a girl about to get married to bring up a different man's name in front of the man she's about to marry—isn't that a bit lacking in common sense, no matter how you look at it? Even if he's someone with no real connection to you, it hurts, you know. You're hurting him. You get that?"

"He's not someone with no real connection. Subaru is my one and only knight, the person who says he likes me while calling my name."

"—Huh!?"

At Emilia's answer, Regulus's murderous aura swelled up. At that motion, Number 184, as well as the other wives, immediately tried to withdraw from the spot. But—

"Don't move!! Whoever moves, I'll blast everything below their neck away."

"——"

"I'll hear your explanation. Choose your words carefully, and please, put in every effort not to let me misunderstand. I have no intention of turning this wedding into someone's funeral. You understand, right?"

Trembling, his shoulders shaking, Regulus spoke in a voice straining to hold back emotion. Kept in check by Regulus, the assembled guests couldn't move. But Emilia met that swelling murderous aura with an expression as calm and unchanged as ever.

"Marriage is something a man and a woman who like each other do. But I—I still don't have any right to it at all."

"——"

"Because I don't understand what it means to like a man, as a woman. So even though Subaru says he likes me so much, I can't give him either a yes or a no to the answer he's looking for. That's such a terrible thing to do, and I know it hurts him, troubles him. But—"

Regulus fell silent. But Emilia's heart wasn't turned toward him. Everyone understood it clearly. That, in Emilia's eyes, he did not exist at all.

"Even though I still don't understand what it means to come to like someone, I know that someday, surely, I'll come to like someone. Surely, I'll love someone, as a woman. And when that happens, I've already decided who it is I'll come to like. So—"

Drawing breath, fixing her gaze on Regulus, Emilia said—

"—I will never be yours."

"—! Ah, is that so! Well, I don't want a selfish, unfaithful woman like you for a wife either! What a relief!!"

At Emilia's declaration, Regulus's face reddened with fury. As Regulus, in his rage, extended his fingers toward her, Emilia let magical power surge from her whole body, taking up a stance to intercept—first, meeting the incomprehensible destruction head-on...

"—!?"

Just as the two attacks were about to begin, a violent sound rang out through the cathedral. The sound came with tremendous force, an object flying like a bullet to strike Regulus directly. What struck Regulus's whole body, clad in his white tuxedo, was a wooden door scattering from the impact—one of the great doors of the cathedral's entrance, only just repaired. It had been blasted from the entrance side, striking Regulus. And then—

"Dude, we kicked it on the same count, but the result's totally different. What kind of leg strength is that?!"

"Sorry, I couldn't hold back. I did pick my target carefully, though, so I hope that counts for something."

"It's gonna throw off how cool this rescue looks, y'know? My kick just opened the door, but yours nails the enemy dead-on..."

Grumbling back and forth at each other, two figures appeared inside the cathedral. A black-haired boy, and a red-haired young man.

Emilia's eyes went wide, and Regulus, standing across from her, brushed away the wood splinters with a gesture like swatting off an insect. Unharmed, but his eyes now holding displeasure, he glared at the two intruders.

"Tearing through a sacred wedding uninvited—that takes some nerve, doesn't it? I don't recall any man's name on the guest list. Who are you, where are you from, and what kind of congratulations did you bring? Hm?"

At Regulus's intimidation, the two at the entrance glanced at each other. Then, nodding in agreement—

"Spirit knight without a partner spirit—Natsuki Subaru."

"Of the Sword Saint's lineage—Reinhard van Astrea."

Announcing themselves, Reinhard stepped forward. Beside him, Subaru winked at Emilia, pointed at Regulus, and said—

"We've got some objections to this wedding. —That bride, we're taking her with us."
