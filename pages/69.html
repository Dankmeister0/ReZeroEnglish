Arc 2 Chapter 45: "Effects of the Lingering Scent"

Kicking off the ground, he leapt forward. Thick roots writhed underfoot, trying to trip him, but he stamped down hard, driving his sole straight through them and breaking past.

When running through forest or mountain, along unpaved paths, it's actually a mistake to be cautious about the uncertainty beneath your feet. When running a true animal trail through nature, the right approach is decisiveness—pressing on without hesitation no matter what lies underfoot—and that's what clears the way.

Trusting the sturdy soles of his shoes, he trampled through the piled-up branches, leaves, and moss, and leapt again. His breath came ragged. Sweat trickling down his forehead threatened to get in his eyes, and he blinked hard, carving out an escape route for the sweat. He had neither the leisure nor the reason to look back. To begin with, the odds of actually getting away were next to nil. Even now the footsteps of his pursuers ran parallel just beside him, dogging his desperate flight as if mocking it.

Leaning his body forward to shrink the surface area catching the wind even slightly, he sprinted at full speed. His lungs ached, his whole body groaned, and he gasped for oxygen like a drowning man. And to Subaru, gaping his mouth open and shut as gracelessly as a fish hauled up onto dry land—

"What a shameless face... it gives away your upbringing."

"—Hey, you're gonna pay for that later!?"

Regretting the extra oxygen he'd wasted on that outburst, Subaru shifted the arm wrapped around the girl in his arms—Ram—and hugged her small frame more securely.

Roughly ten minutes had passed since the launch of "Operation Natsuki Subaru: Bait," making use of that black haze likely connected to the Witch. Just as Subaru had calculated, the battle against the Witchbeasts that came flocking had grown ferocious in the extreme, and now—finally—the two of them, with no other option, had chosen to flee and were tearing through the forest.

"You said you could fight, so I trusted you, and this is what happens!"

"I was fighting, wasn't I. In fact. It's just that Ram's stamina didn't hold up as well as expected."

"You said something cool like 'walking a dangerous tightrope,' didn't you!?"

"It was more dangerous than Ram imagined, I suppose. Ram nearly fell off before even starting to walk it."

Ram's replies to Subaru's shouting were insufferably composed at every turn. Given repeated bouts of combat had drained her Mana to the point where she couldn't properly move her limbs, her attitude was almost unbelievable.

Lured in by the Witch's Lingering Scent that Subaru was giving off, the Witchbeasts were being drawn out right on schedule. Their sheer numbers, though, vastly exceeded anything Subaru had imagined—frankly, the appearance rate had reached a level so far beyond manageable that nothing but regret came to mind.

Making full use of wind Magic, Ram had dispatched as many as seventeen Witchbeasts. After clearing that many in rapid succession, she'd suddenly lost her strength and collapsed. Subaru, right beside her, had been utterly stunned, and in a panic he'd scooped her up and begun to flee—

"—and now, here we are. Talk about winging it in the worst way, I suppose."

"You're the one being carried, so shut up! And don't make me talk so much! I might bite my tongue... besides, my stamina's, not, gonna, hold—!"

Subaru was a shut-in student who nonetheless excelled at every kind of physical feat, but his body, unaccustomed to going outside, suffered from a serious lack of stamina. In a long-distance race, he'd have been in the running for last place at school. Even with such feeble stamina, when his life was on the line, he could squeeze out a little more. Still, it was only a matter of time before the well of his endurance ran dry.

The pursuing Witchbeasts, too, seemed to sense that fleeing Subaru was nearly spent. As if savoring the process of wearing down their prey, every time Subaru's legs began to falter they'd gouge at the ground beneath his feet as a warning, stoking the flame of his flagging survival instinct.

"Barusu, there's one right to your left, I suppose. Swing your sword and you'll hit it."

Ram, in his arms, turned her head and, from within the blur of passing scenery, advised him on a convenient opening. Glancing that way, sure enough, he saw the muzzle of a Witchbeast running parallel to them, in a position that showed it had completely written off Subaru as a threat. Toward it, he drew the one-handed sword at his waist and swung in a single flash. The aim was true, and the slash should have split the arrogant beast's face vertically and horizontally in one stroke, adding a fresh scrap to the forest's compost—except that isn't what happened.

As if fed up with Subaru, who showed no sign of actually drawing his sword, anger kindled in the eyes of Ram, looking up at him from his chest. But Subaru glared back with equal intensity, and—

"Why won't you draw your sword?"

"There's a whole person! Held in both arms! You think I can swing a sword while running like this!? Even if I could swing it, it wouldn't land, you'd fall, I'd trip—strike three, you idiot!"

Snorting in disdain, Ram took his ragged breathing in stride, and Subaru forced his legs to keep moving, sweat pouring desperately from his brow.

Even as small as Ram's frame was, the weight he felt in his arms surely wasn't much under forty kilograms. Just managing to run flat-out while carrying that much weight was already quite an achievement. On top of that, drawing a sword and fighting too—that kind of feat belonged only in manga, and Subaru was no match for it. For an ordinary student with no training whatsoever, carrying a girl in his arms, the options were severely limited. Naturally, that meant carrying her while running was about the only choice Subaru had.

"Any minute now, it'd be about time for my hidden power to finally awaken—OW!!"

The voice eagerly anticipating the moment of awakening abruptly switched to a scream at the sudden, sharp pain shooting through his shoulder. Twisting to look, he saw a Witchbeast's fang buried deep in his right shoulder—it had lunged at him. One strike from a beast grown tired of toying with him, and the flesh of his shoulder was gouged out raggedly, his throat trembling at the pain of the fang's tip brushing against his collarbone. But—

"Carelessness—is your worst enemy, I suppose."

Still held in his arms, Ram reached a hand toward Subaru's waist, drew the one-handed sword from there, and unleashed a sharp diagonal thrust. The blade's tip drove in expertly beside the Witchbeast's nose, destroyed its brain, and burst out the back of its skull. The strength in the fang buried in his shoulder went slack, and it was thrown off by the momentum of Subaru's run, falling to the ground—leaving the corpse tumbling behind, Subaru glanced at the blade that had just swept past right beside his face.

"Don't do something that dangerous, you idiot!"

"I prevented another curse from stacking on top, I suppose—you should be thanking me. Barusu, if you're bitten by an Urugarum, you must kill it no matter what. Let it escape, and your life slips that much further away."

Deftly flicking the blood off the blade even while cradled in his arms, Ram glared at Subaru as she issued the warning. In response, Subaru pretended to focus on running and refused to answer.

Inside his closed mouth, he was aware of his teeth faintly chattering. He couldn't tell whether it was from a warrior's thrill or from fear. That was a lie. He admitted, with something close to resignation, that it was surely the latter.

He was afraid of Witchbeasts. Their fangs, their claws—terrifying beyond bearing. Just now, the bite at his shoulder had been shallow, but even so, its sharpness had been more than enough to stir the fundamental terror in Subaru's heart.

He had been torn apart, every inch of his body, by teeth like that once before. How could he possibly forget that pain, that sense of loss, that despair?

Even holding a sword, in this joint battle alongside Ram, Subaru had not once actually driven that blade into a Witchbeast's body. He'd only swung it around as a deterrent, keeping them at a distance. As a result, the burden had fallen entirely on Ram, and now he was forced to carry her, exhausted, as they fled. Including the fact that he'd failed to imagine the Witchbeasts would flock in such numbers, every bit of this was Subaru's own fault.

The wound on his shoulder throbbed. Blood trickled steadily through the torn fabric, soaking his right sleeve wet all the way to the elbow. The strength in his right arm threatened to give out; adjusting his hold on Ram's body again and again, he pressed desperately forward. Anything to change their location, even a little.

"Ram!"

"Already on it."

Responding curtly to his call, Ram's eyes dyed the color of blood. She periodically activated her clairvoyance, searching the surrounding area for any sign of Rem. During that time, protecting Ram—who became completely defenseless—was Subaru's job. He swept his eyes around, watching sharply for any sign of a Witchbeast lunging in. Fortunately, whether it was because the loss of that last one had left a mark, none of the remaining beasts seemed willing to make an overt move. They merely kept pace, neither closing in nor falling back, circling like predators biding their time for his stamina to run out.

At the eerie, unsettling quiet of it all, Subaru couldn't quite suppress a bad feeling, and he swallowed hard. Every so often, as if to keep him in check, they'd swing left or right, forcing him to change course to avoid an ambush. Each time it happened, he lost track a little more of the direction he'd originally been heading. He'd long since lost all sense of the way back to the village, the path deeper into the forest. Since Ram, using her clairvoyance, said nothing about it, he could only trust that at the very least they weren't running backward, and keep running.

Abandoning that line of thought, running on and on with his mind blank—the result was—

"Oh, sh—!"

The forest suddenly opened up, and the ground beneath his feet vanished, delivering its comeuppance. His legs clawed at empty air, and a sensation of weightlessness seized him, as though his very organs were being yanked upward. An instant later his heels scraped down a slope, and, his balance broken, he shifted into a slide.

His heart had leapt into his throat, but it wasn't a cliff he'd run into. More simply, he'd stumbled into a hollow nestled between the trees. Still holding the girl, he slid down the slope, nearly tumbling, and arrived gracelessly at the bottom. Grateful he hadn't fallen flat, he looked around—only to realize his gratitude had come far too soon.

The place he'd landed was a ravine in the forest, a V-shaped gouge in the ground whose hollow split into two exits, left and right. But from both of those paths came the sound of multiple Witchbeasts' footsteps. At the same time, from the top of the slope Subaru had just fallen down, came the sense of more beasts sliding down after them.

"We got set up...!"

He shouldn't have underestimated the Witchbeasts as mere brutes. Despite having sensed, through repeated encounters, that they possessed something resembling intelligence, he'd let the situation and their appearance convince him not to dwell on it. And so they had been allowed to choose the path exactly as they pleased, and it had become the very trigger that lured Subaru and Ram into this place with no way out.

In his arms, Ram, unaware of the gravity of the situation, still had her sight cast far away. There was no way to hand off the task of breaking through this crisis to her, when she hadn't even grasped the current state of things. Which meant that surviving this predicament here was the proof of the trust she'd placed in Subaru.

"Guess I've gotta do it...!"

A tendency toward snap decisions was one of the few virtues Subaru actually possessed. Somehow cradling Ram with just his left arm, he snatched the one-handed sword she held with his right. The wound on his right shoulder throbbed dully, but it wasn't the kind of injury that would interfere with his grip.

Letting out a deep breath at the sense of danger closing in from left, right, and behind—three directions—Subaru raised the one-handed sword, and—

"Here we go, HAAH!"

He drove it into the slope right in front of him, mobilizing every muscle in his legs and right arm—setting out to conquer the slope ahead. The method he chose was, once again, flight—there was no heroic charge to be made here, no cutting a path through by breaking one of the three routes; that wasn't a course of action worth even considering. With his cheat for brute strength apparently not granted, it was anyone's guess whether his blade would even reach the very first beast.

Driving the blade in, biting the mossy slope with the soles of his shoes, he mobilized his entire body to climb the incline. If he tumbled back down, that would be the end, but wherever he waited, an end was coming regardless—that much was self-evident. So if nothing else, let it come after he'd struggled with everything he had; otherwise nothing would be worth it.

Twisting his body, scraping his right arm—from elbow to shoulder, the very arm driving the blade—against the slope for whatever extra friction it could buy him, he pressed on toward the top. Behind him, the Witchbeasts that had meant to catch Subaru and Ram in a pincer within the hollow butted noses together, looking up at the shadow of their escaping prey and letting out a roar.

But realizing his intent now was far too late. After a good dozen-odd thrusts of the blade, Subaru and Ram's bodies finally reached the top of the slope. Leaning his upper body over the edge, he hooked himself over and somehow clambered up, and—

"Alriiight! I did it! Not bad, actually! Nice one, me! Me, nice! This time-honored one-handed sword's chipped to hell and totally busted, but—OOOGH!"

In the middle of his unending self-praise, he immediately yanked his body, leaned over the top, back down against the slope. An instant later a tremendous blast of wind tore past overhead, dragging earth and debris with it—too slow to dodge fully, the nape of his neck caught the backlash and was gouged, scorching heat and agony striking at once.

"—You little—!"

Grimacing in pain, he leaned out over the top again, and there, standing far off in the distance, blocking his view, was a small silhouette—the puppy-like Witchbeast, whom Subaru had now encountered a total of five times.

Its four limbs stretched out, belly pressed to the ground in a crouching stance. Those twin eyes glowing red, turned toward him, swelled with undying hostility for the prey it had failed to finish off. In short, it was already preparing its second shot.

"No way, like hell I'm eating that again!"

As the surge of Mana naturally began to interfere with the air, Subaru scrambled in a panic and leapt over the top. Leaving the sword sheathed and readjusting his hold on Ram for later, he fled from the beast's line of sight—and, chasing after his fleeing back, the forest was mowed down by a horizontal torrent of earth and stone.

He felt the outrageous force just behind him, trees flattened, the ground itself peeling up. There was no room to look back; his calf, lagging behind in the flight, was torn open by the backlash of the devastation, blood spurting out.

"Damn it aaall!"

He screamed, and leapt. The direction he sprang toward and fell was to the right—throwing himself toward yet another slope, different from before. Judging he couldn't afford to be caught by the torrent of earth, and facing the cliff's baptism waiting to greet him, Subaru again drove the sword—held in a reverse grip—into the ground, and—

"Owowowowowow, it hurts, it hurts, it hurts!"

Scraping the ground with the right side of his body, twisting the buried blade to dig it in and control his slide. Half in tears from the mounting scrapes and cuts, Subaru looked up at the cliff edge he'd just fallen from, and—

"Whoa there."

Unable to stop with all that momentum, he knocked aside the body of a Witchbeast tumbling down the slope. Letting out a shriek fit for some pampered house pet, several Witchbeasts fell straight down. Unlike the hollow from before, the bottom of this slope was a ravine floor jagged with exposed rock. The fallen beasts were embraced without mercy by that unyielding earth, and fell silent with the crack of breaking bone.

"Nearly joined them in condolences myself, but—"

"Barusu—! Ram found Rem!"

In his arms, Ram cried out in joy, and her bloodshot eyes returned to reality. After making a face of delight, she then knit her brow at the fact that the scenery around them was tilted at an angle, and said,

"It's only been a little while since I last looked—what on earth is this situation?"

"I'm in the middle of an unavoidable cliffhanger! More importantly, I'd love to hear your good news—if only to look away from reality for a bit."

Feeling his arm muscles trembling, Subaru shifted his focus toward distracting himself with the good news. Without remarking on Subaru's growing list of wounds, Ram gave a small nod.

"Not far from here, another vantage point caught sight of Rem's shadow. The timing was bad, so I couldn't grasp Rem's own field of vision, but she should be heading this way."

"How do you know that?"

"The field of vision Ram seized was that of an Urugarum. It was moving somewhere together with several others of its pack when it encountered Rem. It got crushed, and the connection was severed."

"I seeee..."

He had no idea what kind of shock Ram experienced when the person whose vision she'd shared died. But he imagined it couldn't have been a pleasant sight. In any case, confirming Rem's safety was a stroke of good fortune. Just as they'd predicted, she seemed to be out there in the forest happily hunting—which was excellent news too.

"Problem is, my pile's a lot bigger than yours right now... I rushed over here thinking Rem was in danger, and now it's not even 'the cure is worse than the disease,' it's way past that."

"If you fall to the bottom of this cliff, both of you will be in danger, Barusu, that much is certain. Can you climb?"

"With sheer guts, somehow. More importantly, the problem is the Witchbeasts still chasing us from up top..."

As he floated the question of what to do, he tightened his grip with his right arm, supporting both their weight. Leaning his weight onto the diagonally driven sword, trying to ease his stance a little, and—

"'—Ah'"

Their two voices overlapped, and at the same moment, a sharp, high-pitched sound of steel snapping rang out.

The blade of the one-handed sword driven into the cliff had snapped, leaving roughly a third of its tip behind in the slope. He hastily jammed the bent blade into the ground again, but with the tip now flattened, the force with which it dug in was weaker—simply put—

"—Is there any chance the bodies of the Witchbeasts that fell down there could cushion us?"

"There's a much higher chance that Ram and the rest end up cushioning the Witchbeasts that fall after us."

To Subaru's words, half a prayer to the heavens, Ram replied with utter resignation. At that instant, the blade, its grip too weak, was released from the slope, and their fall resumed. Resigned to having his flesh shredded, he pressed the entire right side of his body against the slope, but there wasn't enough friction to support the weight of two people, and their bodies were flung down the cliff with nothing to be done.

"Gaaaah, oh no—!!"

"—This is going to cost you dearly, Barusu!!"

At the sensation of falling headfirst straight down, Subaru's whole body bristled as he recalled that time he'd once thrown himself off somewhere. Still, the fact that he unconsciously hugged Ram tight in his arms, if only to shield her from the impact of the fall, showed he hadn't entirely given up being a man about it. Feeling that forceful embrace, Ram shifted, stretching an arm down toward the ground, and—

"—El Fura!!"

At the shout, Mana surged, and the ground at their predicted point of impact erupted with a blast of wind. The updraft from directly below caught Subaru's falling body, flipping his headfirst plunge right-side up, and slowing their rate of fall somewhat besides.

This should work, he judged, in a world where his vision spun half around, and pouring every last ounce of strength into both legs, he braced for the coming impact by clenching his back teeth hard enough to nearly crack them.

"Nnnngh, aaaaaaah—I held it! I held it!!"

—He'd held it. Soothing both legs, screaming with a ferocious numbness, by hopping in place, Subaru looked up at the cliff they'd fallen from and stood dumbstruck at its height. Over ten meters—roughly equivalent to a four-story building, like at a school. Thrown down from that height onto solid ground, and somehow he'd come out with his life intact.

"Seriously, thank god for Ram-sama, Buddha-sama, and perfect timing. If not for that wind Magic just now, I'd be..."

Just as he was about to voice his gratitude for having escaped with his life, Subaru noticed that Ram, in his arms, wasn't stirring even a little. Looking down, he saw a thin line of blood trailing from her nose as she hung her head, her eyes closed, breathing shallow and repeated, groaning only in pain.

"Huh, hey, Ram? Uh oh, this—hey."

He gave her body a light shake, calling out to her, but there was no response from Ram whatsoever. Her body had already been worn down from the battles against the Witchbeasts. On top of that, the Magic used to cushion their fall just now had been the final straw, grinding away what remained of her spirit.

"Ahh, damn it, my timing sucks like hell."

Cursing his own carelessness, Subaru shifted his hold on Ram, cradling her more carefully. The sword, snapped halfway, went back into its sheath awkwardly, and he looked up. At the top of the cliff, the Witchbeasts that had been giving chase seemed to have taken a detour and begun searching for another route to pursue Subaru and Ram, who'd taken the shortcut down. While he had the chance, he wanted to put as much distance between them and the beasts as possible, but—

"Not like I've got time to spare here—!?"

Fleeing meant losing time, and that loss of time led directly to death. And yet, with fleeing the only option left to him, Subaru held back the urge to click his tongue. But that moment of sentiment was immediately snapped away by a tremendous roar echoing down from the top of the cliff.

"Oh come on, you gotta be kidding me."

Looking up, down the very slope Subaru had just slid down, an enormous mudslide came pouring down—and riding atop it, the puppy Witchbeast, along with a dozen or more others.

He leapt back on reflex, at the very least avoiding getting caught up in that torrent of earth and rock. But the moment the Witchbeasts touched down on the same ground alongside all that debris, the advantage Subaru and Ram had risked their lives to buy vanished in an instant. And the very act of creating that opening had sunk Ram's consciousness beneath the surface, with the added bonus that Subaru's already unreliable weapon had become even less reliable still.

"Curse you, Witch-sama... your perfume's way too damn strong."

Whenever things went well according to plan, he was quick to give thanks; the moment he realized it had led to the present disaster, he flipped his opinion just as fast. Filthy, truly filthy of him, Subaru thought, rating his own pettiness even as he did it, and though he knew it would only postpone the inevitable, he began preparing to run again.

Turn tail and get out of here, praying the beasts would keep underestimating the two of them and not make an immediate move—

"Wait, huh?"

Right before he set off running, Subaru sensed something off and cocked his head. Something was wrong with the Witchbeasts that had slid down. He'd assumed they'd crouch low atop the mudslide and, the instant they landed, pounce straight at him, but—

"Huh, hey, I'm over here, y'know?"

Just as Subaru's bewildered voice suggested, the moment they landed, the Witchbeasts scattered in every direction, bolting away down the cliff bottom. The sight, like spiders scattering from a disturbed nest, was pathetic enough to remind him of his own flight not long before.

"What's gotten into you all—"

The tension of being under attack loosened slightly, and that question filled his mind instead. An instant later, the answer exploded at the top of the cliff and rained down upon him.

"Eh"

Looking up once more, Subaru's eyes clouded with confusion at the change atop the cliff—and then, right before his eyes, he saw reddish-black chunks of flesh scattering one after another, and understood.

—Far above, atop the distant cliff, a single human silhouette had appeared.

A girl in a maid's uniform, swinging a blood-soaked iron ball wildly, glaring down at the cliff bottom with eyes that had lost all sanity. The instant his eyes met that gaze, ruled entirely by murderous intent, Subaru felt his back go drenched in cold sweat at the worst premonition imaginable.

An instant later, she leapt, and with effortless ease from that towering height, the "Oni" descended to the ground below.

Surrounded by Witchbeasts deep in the forest, facing down an "Oni," still holding the girl he had to protect, a broken sword in one hand—Subaru held his breath, realizing he had arrived at the final confrontation.

"Given all that, isn't it a little unfair how pathetic I look by comparison?"

His protest reached no one, swallowed up by the wind blowing through the forest. Now then, here it was—the so-called moment of truth—.

Reflected in the blade of the drawn one-handed sword, his own distorted profile looked, to Subaru, terribly, terribly weak.
