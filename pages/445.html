Arc 6 Chapter 40: "Abandoned by the Starry Sky"

Everything, without exception, had gone wretchedly. Wretchedly — written quite literally with the characters for scattered and scattered again, a wreck twice over.

Found, dragged back, interrogated about what had happened. With every halting word he muttered, he could tell for himself how much worse the situation was getting.

"So Subaru really doesn't remember anything at all?"

The silver-haired girl looked down at him, at himself hugging his knees, her eyes shadowed with worry. Beside her, the young, lovely girl couldn't quite absorb his words either, and simply stood in stunned silence.

"I was at my wit's end when I heard you'd vanished from sight... but I never imagined it would come to this. This seems to be, I daresay, an extremely difficult problem we now face."

The girl wrapped in a white fox stole murmured this in a voice that sounded oddly stiff. The one who couldn't even spare the composure to nod along was the knight with the elegant, refined features.

"Just how much more of a pathetic sight do you intend to make of yourself before you're satisfied, Barusu?"

So said the girl who'd just finished cleaning up the filth he'd spread everywhere, glaring at him. And yet there was less ill will in it than her words suggested — more something clinging, something uneasy and forlorn.

"Master, Master. Somethin's gotten real gloomy 'round here, huh~. Gotta smile more, don't ya think? Also... huh, what's that. Smells like pee!"

The black-haired woman, oblivious to the mood, laughed on cheerfully, unbothered. Beside her, the young girl smoothing down her own braid only cast an indifferent, sidelong glance his way.

"――――"

Every one of these reactions, broadly speaking, was one he'd already lived through and let pass. The first time, the second time — and now this, the third. Three times now, he had let these women down. Though, of the three, this time — found soiling himself, fleeing in a panic, curled up sobbing alone — was without question, by a wide margin, the worst.

—Though he alone, in this room, knew just how wretched a state that truly was.

"Heh."

It was almost funny. That he — no, that the same stretch of time — was repeating itself for the third time.

He had seen the same sight once, then twice. And now, with the third time arrived, Subaru finally, properly grasped the situation he'd been placed in.

—He had died twice.

Both times, most likely, from the same fall from the same place — the first time, he'd lost consciousness partway down and never tasted the moment of death itself, so the memory of it had simply slipped away. But the second time hadn't gone that way. Falling clumsily, with no talent whatsoever for dying, he'd struck against everything on the way down, his whole body wrecked, and only then had he finally died.

Finally died — and been flung right back. The instant he died, he was flung back into that green room, and after waking once more, made to live through today all over again.

Die, and come back. — Return by Death. That was the blessing bestowed by some god upon Natsuki Subaru, here in this other world.

"Heh."

A second smile leaked out. Whether it deserved to be called a smile was, objectively speaking, very much open for debate — but at least to Subaru, that was what it was.

There was nothing left to do but laugh. His tears, honestly, felt like they'd run dry. Crying wastes stamina too. Dying and coming back seemed to reset whatever stamina he'd spent before dying — but this time, quite literally, he'd bled far too much of it out just from waking up.

"In any case, let's have Subaru rest until he settles down. Given some time, maybe something will change."

By the silver-haired girl's proposal, his own treatment was decided out from under him, by default. Perhaps out of fear he'd act out again, Subaru's person was, with great ceremony, escorted back to that green room under everyone's watchful eye.

"The thought of leaving Barusu with Rem right now doesn't sit well with me. Ram objects."

"...Still, setting aside the matter of pinpointing the cause of this, the best hope for Subaru's recovery ought to lie with relying on the spirit in that room."

"Then... then Rem simply has to be moved out of that room. Ram will watch over her, staying by her side the whole time. Ram is the only one left worrying over that girl, after all."

Please, take your arguments and your clashing opinions somewhere I'm not. Whatever the case, none of this was his to choose. Right now, Subaru simply didn't have it in him to muster the will to bravely overcome this situation.

"...Poor Rem."

Carrying on her back the sleeping girl who was her spitting image, she said this to Subaru before leaving the room. He didn't understand the true meaning behind those words. Nor did he particularly want to.

"Subaru, behave yourself and stay quiet here. I'm sure Betty will go and find some way to bring your memories back, I suppose."

"――――"

"I won't let you just curl up here all alone, I suppose."

Before leaving the room, the young girl spoke to him gently like that. Her voice carried a pained tone, and yet also a noble resolve — a strong voice, one that believed without a shred of doubt that this was the right thing to do. And to someone offering him such kindness, such concern—

"...Ah."

Subaru shrank his shoulders back, trying to dodge the finger she'd reached out toward him. Seeing that flinch, the girl's eyes, marked with their distinctive pattern, wavered with pain.

"――――"

They were strangers. No matter how far this went, they were strangers to him.

But it wasn't that, to Subaru, they were strangers. It was that, to them, the Subaru standing here was the stranger.

The familiarity they extended, the concern, or something closer even to affection — all of it was meant for the original "Natsuki Subaru," not for this wreck of a man left behind. And in just the same way—

"I've got no reason to be killed either, damn it..."

Left alone in the green room, Subaru ground his back teeth and muttered as much. Being on the receiving end of goodwill he had no memory of earning, an affection and trust he'd never built, having the accumulated time and bonds blown away and replaced with an uncomfortable warmth directed at him — that much, at least, he could stomach. But why should he have to clean up after the murderous intent that "Natsuki Subaru" had built up?

The good and the bad, lumped together — none of it was his. Not one bit of it belonged to him. And yet, why did he have to flail and struggle, half-drowning, in a place like this?

・

"I refuse," Subaru said, alone, hours later, pressing his back to the wall and forcing himself, inch by inch, to his feet. He spat out the mix of blood and saliva that had pooled in his mouth from clenching his teeth too hard. Then he began walking, slowly, toward the door of the green room.

"――ッ"

At his back as he moved, the cry of the black lizard — his sole companion in that room — rose up. It sounded, somehow, thin and threaded with loneliness, but he shook his head immediately. There was a limit to how ridiculous it was to imagine a mere oversized reptile feeling something like loneliness.

"Somebody'll bring you food. Just behave yourself."

Even as Subaru said this and left, the lizard's thin cry kept chasing after him. Deaf to it, Subaru shook off the tug at the back of his mind and stepped outside the green room. Checking both directions of the corridor for anyone, he crept forward.

"Water, and the food stores are..."

He knew. He'd gone along to fetch water before, so he knew where that was. He knew where the food was kept too. All that remained was how much he should take.

"――――"

Honestly, Subaru didn't know. He had no idea just who had pushed him off that ledge, who had taken his life.

But Subaru remembered clearly. That in that place, someone had pushed his back. It wasn't some absurd excuse like a hand clapping his shoulder from behind, or a strong gust of wind. Nothing so foolish. He had been shoved off, unmistakably. Under clear and deliberate murderous intent, Natsuki Subaru had been killed.

The suspects were Emilia, Beatrice, Ram, Anastasia, Julius, Meili, Shaula — seven people in all. How many of them were enemies, how many were allies, Subaru had no way of knowing. For that matter, he couldn't even judge, right now, whether every single one of them was truly an acquaintance of his to begin with.

—What if, in truth, every one of them was an assassin, gathered in this tower for the sole purpose of killing Subaru?

"If that's the case..."

Then his conscience wouldn't sting one bit for taking as much food as he could carry. But that judgment, in the same breath, meant doubting that it had all been an act — Emilia, who had spoken to him kindly both the first time and the second, unchanged; Beatrice, who had said she would search for a way to restore his memory; Ram, who had cried out, unable to fully believe he'd truly lost his memories.

"――――"

He couldn't do it. Even after suffering through something so terrifying, even after being killed not once but twice, Subaru could not, from the bottom of his heart, doubt them and simply run for his own safety alone.

"Damn it, damn it, you spineless bastard...!"

Cursing himself for his own indecisive, pathetic state, Subaru quietly packed food into a sack. It was all so-called emergency rations, with no thought given to taste. Of course, what mattered now was how filling it was — taste came a distant second, or third. By rough estimate, he decided to take enough for three days, portioned for one person alone. He packed water the same way, into a leather waterskin. And besides that—

"They said it's a desert out there, right..."

He threw on the cloak that had been stored in the same place as the food. Given its size and design, his own was easy to pick out. The cloak was designed so that, once closed in front, it could be pulled up over the neck to cover the mouth — clearly meant as protection against desert sand. With water, food, and desert gear all gathered, his preparations were complete.

"Have I passed the time I died twice already, I wonder..."

Given how long he'd spent fleeing, stammering out his account of what happened, and curled up in the green room afterward, he was probably now standing further along in time than when he'd been pushed off before. The effect of Return by Death, already at work. As if to say: keep overcoming every flag for death that falls upon you, one after another, and keep walking this life-or-death tightrope.

"――――"

He didn't want that. If that was what awaited him, he'd rather not be in a place like this at all.

Breaking into a jog, Subaru left the room and headed, by the map he'd drawn in his head, toward the spiral staircase. The place where he'd been pushed off, twice now. Naturally, his heart screamed in protest at even approaching it. And yet—

"――ッ"

Reaching the spiral staircase, his eyes were caught for just a moment by the sight below him, and then he immediately turned, carefully checking behind him for any assassin. Fortunately — whether because the timing was different now, or because whoever it was hadn't yet pinpointed his location — there was no arm reaching out to shove his back.

By now, they were probably headed either for the archive where the memories of the dead slept, or up to the floor above where the insufferable examiner waited — the memory of having his innards gouged out surfaced, and nausea welled up in him.

"Fight... fight something like that? No way in hell..."

It hadn't been human. Not in skill, and not in personality either. There was no way anyone stood a chance challenging something like that. —Then what did that make him, letting them walk off into a fight he already knew they couldn't win?

"Like hell I care!!"

The riddle that kept surfacing and vanishing in his mind had been trying to stop his feet this whole while. So what. So what if it had.

They might have been kind to him — but it could all be a lie. They might have worried for him — but behind that, they might just as well be sharpening a blade of murderous intent. Then at the very least, shouldn't he warn them that a dangerous person might be lurking among them?

"And what if the person I warn turns out to be the enemy?"

Enemy, enemy — what a joke. In ordinary life, in a world free of conflict, the only place you'd ever use a word like "enemy" was inside some game. And here he was, in a world that made using it, as a matter of course, seem natural. Being made to use it. It was wrong. He didn't want to be in a place like this. He couldn't stay in a place like this.

"――ッ"

Grinding his teeth against the unbearable irritation welling up from deep in his chest, Subaru ran. He ran down the spiral staircase, heading toward the floor below, too far to even see — toward the fifth floor. The steps were countless, the staircase running on and on along the wall, its end nowhere in sight. Gasping for breath, having once desperately climbed upward, he now found the absurdity of himself desperately fleeing downward almost laughable.

Even so, he didn't want to die.

"Made... it..."

He arrived at the floor below — the fifth floor. Inside the round tower, unlike the fourth floor divided into rooms, the fifth floor used the same full width as one single hall, undivided. The main features of the fifth floor were the staircase leading further down to the sixth floor, and—

"A huge... door..."

A door, towering enough to have to look up at, stood there with a terrible, oppressive weight.

"――――"

Faced with the door, a sense of being overwhelmed by its strangeness caught in Subaru's throat. He felt a faint, sandy wind blowing. Probably, wind from outside was slipping in through the gap in the door, carrying grains of sand quietly into the tower. Which was to say, unmistakable proof that the door connected to the outside, and—

"If Emilia and the others' explanation was right..."

Through that door, he should be able to reach the desert outside. The desert — he'd forgotten its proper name, but in any case, he could cross the sand and reach human settlements. Do that, and at least he wouldn't have to fear some cold-blooded assassin threatening his life.

The basics of moving through a desert: travel at night. Avoid sandstorms. Fix a direction and head straight for it. — That was about the extent of what he knew.

"Better to gamble on saving myself than to stay somewhere I'm certain to be killed."

Call it an unsound decision, and maybe it was. But even if this was an abnormal judgment, he didn't think a decision made in an attempt to survive could be called wrong. In this situation, the moment he couldn't even trust himself anymore, all that awaited him was darkness.

He would absolutely not sit in that darkness and simply wait for death. If he was going to die anyway, he'd fight back first.

"――――"

He set his hand against the massive door in front of him and slowly pressed forward. The door's size was easily ten times Subaru's own. Under normal circumstances, even if Subaru threw his whole body against it with everything he had, it shouldn't have budged an inch.

And yet, pressed by Subaru's palm, it swung open as easily as some mechanical contraption.

"Heh."

A breath of relief slipped out, and Subaru stopped the door before it could swing wide open. Even if, right now, they hadn't yet noticed he was trying to slip away, if the door ended up standing fully open, that alone might well tip them off.

Even if it got found out that Subaru had slipped away, he at least wanted to put enough distance between them first that they couldn't catch up.

"――――"

Stopping the door partway open, Subaru quietly peered outside through the gap. Instantly, what leapt into view was a vast sea of sand, faintly being swallowed up by the coming of night.

"...It really is a desert, huh."

He narrowed his eyes, looking off toward the far horizon, but he couldn't see the end of it. In an environment with nothing at all to block the view, and still the horizon's end wasn't visible. Just how far it was to reach it, Subaru had no way of knowing. But no matter how far it was, it couldn't be an infinite distance, one that would never close.

Take one step forward, and he'd be one step closer to the outside world. Get closer to the outside world, and — wouldn't that mean getting one step closer to his own original world?

"――――"

For a moment, Subaru's feet nearly stayed rooted inside the tower. It was the guilt of leaving behind, in this place, the people who — probably, surely — bore him no ill will, that made him hesitate like that. But he shook it off. His lingering attachment to the outside, to his original world, was what made him do it.

He didn't want to be here. Natsuki Subaru's home to return to was that house, where his father and mother waited.

"So..."

He slipped through the gap in the door and stepped forward, firmly, into the outside. The moment his foot pressed down on the sand, he felt the sole of his shoe sink into it more than he'd expected. Trampling through it by sheer force, Natsuki Subaru strode powerfully out into the world beyond. And then—

"...Eh?"

The sole beneath his foot burst violently, and Subaru's body was flung high into the air.

Slammed back-first into the ground, Subaru was thrown into total disorientation. Complete panic — though by now, it was unclear whether panic or a state of normalcy was more the standard condition for Subaru.

"What—"

happened, he meant to say, spitting sand out of his mouth, but he had no time to finish the thought. Because the answer to what had happened was already presenting itself, standing boldly right in front of him.

"――――"

Ahead of him, against a backdrop of a world dyed in night, a massive shadow loomed over him. A gigantic body with no limbs, slick, glistening skin, and rows of vicious, brutal fangs lining its mouth — to Subaru, it looked like an earthworm. A horrifyingly gigantic earthworm. A monstrous worm, no less than ten meters long.

"Wha—"

Was this the kind of world that had monsters like this in it? Every single time, some sight too surreal for reality struck his mind sideways, refusing to let him regain his composure. And the imminent, mortal danger bearing down on him wouldn't allow him to crouch and lament, either.

"You goddamn son of a—"

Covered in sand, Subaru forced himself back up and shook his head hard. Probably, whatever had just sent him flying was a strike from this worm, lurking beneath the sand. Or maybe he'd simply been standing right above the spot where it burst out to breathe. Either way, this worm had knocked him rolling across the sand, and now, on top of that—

"I'm not about to let this slide..."

Realizing the situation was overwhelmingly against him, Subaru swept his eyes around. He was only four steps out from the tower. Pathetic didn't begin to cover it, but the option of diving back inside the building to save his life was still very much open.

But sweeping his gaze around, Subaru cursed his own rotten luck. Sent flying by the worm's blow and rolled across the sand, he'd ended up further from the tower. The gigantic worm now stood squarely between him and the tower — if he wanted to get back, he'd have no choice but to slip past its enormous bulk, or it was hopeless.

"Even so..."

Running off into the sea of sand with no destination at all, he'd eventually be caught by the worm regardless. And for that matter, there was no guarantee this worm was the only one of its kind. Given its size, there was every chance it was the ruler of this desert — but even something half this size would be more than enough of a threat. And if he was ever set upon by a whole pack of them—

"So running was never a realistic option to begin with, huh."

He hated how thoroughly, how efficiently, the world was kind enough to teach him that. And it seemed the worm, too, had settled on its own attitude toward Subaru, fumbling to sort out the situation.

Slowly, the eyeless worm turned its head toward him, its enormous mouth splitting open. On that worm's head, a twisted horn jutted out, giving off a bizarre, alien wrongness all its own.

"One shot. One shot. One shot, one shot, one shot..."

Pulling the sand-cloth up over his neck, Subaru tracked the worm's every movement with his eyes. He held his arm out lightly in front of him, timing it. He caught sight, briefly, of the torn nail on his right hand — the wound closing, the nail already, faintly, beginning to regrow. Healing magic, or perhaps some lingering effect of the green room. Into that small gap in his awareness—

"――ッッッ!!"

Letting out a horrifically grating, unearthly shriek — impossible to imagine from such a massive body — the worm lunged, throwing itself bodily at Subaru. A roaring howl of wind sounded, and for an instant, Subaru felt the strange sensation of his own body being left behind by his awareness.

"――――"

He rolled to the worm's right flank as it lunged, dodging the first strike. He thought of nothing else. And he saw, in his mind, an illusion of himself pulling it off cleanly. All he had to do was let his body — lagging behind his awareness — trace the motions of that illusion. And so, he moved, exactly as it showed him.

"—Shiii!!"

Dust billowed up, and a shockwave erupted right beside him. Catching the edge of it, Subaru rolled across the sand, tracing the illusion in his mind.

"...Haah!"

His body moved far more nimbly than he remembered it being able to. In that instant, what flashed through his mind was a year — a whole year — of memories that didn't belong to this Subaru, memories of the "Natsuki Subaru" who had survived in this brutal environment. The residue of that experience remained. He didn't let himself think, for now, about being kept alive by it.

"Just like this—!!"

Turning the momentum of his roll into forward momentum, Subaru sprinted with everything he had, straight ahead, toward the tower's entrance. In this moment, and this moment alone, everything came down to clinging to the life right there in front of him.

He knew that going back inside the tower meant being tormented again by an environment thick with suspicion that would never quite fade, and by the fear of death. Even so, he couldn't choose to end up as worm excrement.

"――――"

He had no room to look back, but the worm, having plunged headfirst into the sea of sand, shouldn't have been nimble enough to instantly pivot and come at him again. Just another dozen-odd meters, and he'd somehow make it through—

"...ru"

In that instant, not the worm's head but its tail burst up, splitting the sandy ground open, and sand exploded upward as if to mow his feet out from under him — his body was flung into the air once more.

"Oh, ah—"

Tumbling through the air, clawing at limbs that grasped nothing, Subaru looked down. And there, beneath him as he spun and tumbled through the sky, was the worm's mouth thrown wide open, its filthy maw ready to devour him.

—He'd underestimated it.

He'd been naive to think he could outwit, in this soft, hothouse world he'd lived in until now, the raw wildness that had let this brutal environment grow something to this monstrous size. His shallow, careless thinking — the price for it, once again, would be paid with his life.

"No... don't want to."

Falling, thrashing his limbs like some insect with its wings torn off, the words spilled out of him. Die. He was going to die again. Even if he died, he didn't even know whether he'd manage to die all the way through. What would even happen if he died here, if it all ended here? Could he endure it?

Even if he were thrown, feeling exactly like this, into an endless darkness that never ended. Could he endure it?

"I don't want thiiiiiiis!!"

He screamed, reaching a hand up toward the night sky, begging for help. There was nothing there to touch. In the sky's far reaches, blurred as if veiled by thin cloud, not even the stars that should have been there were visible, and Subaru fell, alone. Abandoned even by the star that shared his name, he would be swallowed into the belly of a monster, and vanish, erased. That despair—

—a flash of white light burst forth.

"――――"

A flash of white light burst forth, and it blew apart the face of the worm's gaping mouth. Quite literally, the impact drove straight into the side of the worm's face, warping its massive head like taffy, and in an instant, it burst.

Scattering filthy-colored flesh and blood, the worm's head burst apart and was blown away.

The head that should have swallowed Subaru whole was gone, and the worm's massive body swayed, wobbling. But before that body could even collapse, Subaru's body collided into the now-headless worm.

It was the sensation of slamming his whole body into raw ground meat before it's cooked. Cushioned by what were wounds, what were organs, what was flesh and blood itself, Subaru bought his life in exchange for a stomach-churning, hair-raising disgust. A liquid mixed into his mouth, triggering both a searing sting and nausea, and he coughed desperately. All the while, the worm's corpse slowly began to topple onto the sand — and never got to finish.

Again and again, every time a light flashed at the edge of his vision, another part of the worm's corpse was blasted through. Shot through, burst apart, shortened, scattered — the worm was sliced apart, torn to shreds. That none of it struck Subaru was pure luck, good fortune buried within terrible misfortune. Eventually—

"...Ah."

Subaru realized that his body, tossed about by the impacts, had been thrown down onto the sand. Limbs sprawled out, he lay on his back atop the sand.

"――――"

Overhead was the same starless sky as when the worm had sent him flying. By some twist of fate, even now that he'd been given his life back, the sky remained unchanged, still abandoning "Subaru" all the same.

Exasperated with him, longed for, forsaken, resented, held dear, kept at arm's length. Did he want to live, or did he want to die? Did he want to be here, or did he not want to be here?

"What am I supposed to do?! If there's an answer, then tell me...!"

He covered his face and shouted up at the empty sky, where no one was, where nothing could be seen. With no reply coming, who, of anyone, would hold the answer Subaru wanted to hear the most? That was, most likely—

"—Answer me, Natsuki Subaru."

He said it pathetically, in a voice like a sigh, and let his body roll onto its side. Idly, meaninglessly, he had simply twisted his body — nothing more.

—That small, aimless movement was what barely saved Subaru's life from the white light that grazed past his ear and drove into the ground.

"Guh—"

Something shaved past his right ear with terrifying force. Clutching his throbbing ear, he rolled to the side. Blood dripped down, and he saw the cause of the pain.

A thin, white-glowing needle-like object stood jutting from the sand.

"This...eh?"

Wondering what it was, he reached out to touch it — and the moment he did, the white needle crumbled into dust. And immediately after, Subaru's vision swam.

"――――"

It swam violently. From above, downward. Whether it was because of the worm's earlier thrashing, or because that last white needle had been the final blow, the ground beneath Subaru's rear gave way, and along with the surrounding earth, he plunged downward, underground.

"Ah, aaah, aaaaah!"

He tried to leap clear, tried to grab hold of something, but his fingers slipped straight through sand. Like an insect falling into an antlion's pit, unable to resist, Subaru's body fell down through the bottom of the sand.

His limbs buried, unable to move, he looked up and gasped desperately for air. His whole body swallowed by sand, he struggled with all his might against the premonition of being buried alive, just like that.

"Somebody, somebody, help—"

The words that followed never took shape, and Subaru's body was swallowed by the sand, and fell.

And so, not a single fragment of the stars in the sky took the least notice of "Subaru," flailing pitifully below.
