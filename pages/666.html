Arc 8 Chapter 49: "The Thorn King"

—Halibel, called the "Praiser," who wields undisputed claim to the title of strongest in the Kararagi City-States.

A man said to have no equal as a shinobi across the whole of the Four Great Nations, an expert whose skill even Olbart, the empire's strongest shinobi, acknowledges with a bitter face. Ranked alongside the "Sword Saint," the "Mad Prince," and the "Blue Lightning" as one of the strongest in each nation, he possesses a record worthy of the comparison.

If there was one point where Halibel differed from those other three, it was that he belonged to no nation, styling himself instead a masterless ronin.

The "Sword Saint," bound to the Kingdom by treaty; the "Mad Prince," imprisoned in the northernmost tower as a national traitor — of course they had standing. Even the "Blue Lightning," whose selfishness had made him infamous even in other countries, had a position.

Only the "Praiser" Halibel lived as a free man, unburdened by any title.

Of course, Halibel himself held gratitude toward and a sense of belonging to his home country, and when serious trouble arose in Kararagi he would sometimes take on requests to investigate or resolve it. But he loathed, to an extreme degree, the idea of his own existence being owned by anyone, used as a bargaining chip in some deal or negotiation.

—He sided with whoever he wanted to side with, and turned his nose up at whatever failed to interest him.

That was Halibel's motto as the strongest, and precisely because of that capricious nature, opinions of him among those who knew him split into two extremes.

On one side, those rejected with a "Nah, you lot'll be fine without me, no worries — good on ya."

On the other, those accepted with a "Wow, gettin' someone like me to move — that's impressive, good on ya."

Whichever it was, he'd offer his praise so easily that people took to calling him the "Praiser." Naturally, that wasn't the only reason for the name, but evaluating any opponent or any deed from an objective standpoint was simply Halibel's way.

Which was precisely why—

"Well now, somebody sure had a rotten personality, this caster. —No love in it at all."

Smoke curling up from the pipe in his mouth, Halibel spoke those words with no trace of praise in his voice. His tone dropped slightly, carrying something jagged — anyone who knew him would have been startled by such rare displeasure.

Halibel himself held no particular fondness for the Undead in general, but even so, he'd found something to admire in the scale and effect of the technique, in the sheer skill of the enemy who'd cast it. But looking at the one before him now, not a shred of admiration welled up in him.

"—————"

His eyes, thin as threads, opened just slightly, and with golden pupils Halibel regarded the man in question — Eugard, who had discarded his tattered coat and now held an unbelievable famed blade in one hand.

The formidable foe who, until just moments ago, had been trading blows with Groovy, one of the Nine Divine Generals, had a soul entangled with more than mere skill at arms.

Seeing that same thing, Groovy beside him wiped his blood-smeared mouth with one hand.

"Oi, Ade..."

"No need to force yourself to talk. I'm seein' the same thing you are. —Though all I heard from that kid was that this one should be left to a curse specialist."

Stroking his chin with one hand, Halibel recalled the boy who had appointed him to this Fourth Apex position. A boy who had earned even Vincent's respect, whose judgment commanded the overwhelming trust of everyone around him — so much so that even Anastasia had told him to respect the boy's opinions as much as possible.

That boy had entrusted him, as a man skilled even in curses, with handling the opponent said to determine the outcome of this decisive battle for the capital. Having talked big to Anastasia besides, Halibel had come in fully fired up to show off the full measure of Kararagi's strongest without holding anything back.

"Well, this is unexpected..."

Halibel, caught flat-footed, and Groovy beside him were both fixed on the same point. Both of their attentions were locked on Eugard's left breast — where a heart would be, on a human — and the translucent, venomous-looking thorns entangled there. The fact that the same "Thorn Curse" that had been indiscriminately scattered across the wide expanse of the capital was now eating away at Eugard himself.

From that bizarre fact, two possibilities arose.

One: that the "Thorn Curse" was, in the true sense of the word, indiscriminate — ensnaring even Eugard, its own caster.

But Halibel weighed the other possibility as far more likely.

Namely—

"—You there, Demi-Human. Answer my question."

Having finished testing the feel of his regenerated right hand, Eugard tossed out those words. Naturally, the gaze that pierced through at that was aimed squarely at Halibel, the sudden intruder. When Halibel pointed at himself and asked "Me?" Eugard nodded deeply.

"If my assessment does not err, are you a Wolf Human?"

"Ah, yeah, that's right. I'm a Wolf Human... Vollachia's been raisin' such hell that we're squeezed thin the world over. Practically on the verge of dyin' out."

"I see. —I see."

It had been an all-out extinction-level joke, but Eugard merely nodded quietly, as if confirming a fact, and the joke fell flat. As Halibel eyed him with suspicion, Groovy raised a hoarse "Oi—"

"So I'm tellin' you not to push yourself..."

"Zore dokoja de, guzo! Geigyogu de iha...!"

"—I bear you, as an individual, no grudge."

Before Groovy's blood-flecked voice could finish, that declaration took shape and was loosed.

The casually swung slash of the "Evil Blade" ran black through the air, ignoring Groovy's existence entirely, tearing through to split Halibel from crotch to crown. In the space of a single blink, there stood Halibel, cleaved vertically in two.

"—!"

At that ghastly sight, Groovy, unable to voice his shock in time, fell speechless, while Eugard, on the cutting side, remained in the posture of one who had just swung the "Evil Blade" upward.

"However, your race once participated in the death of my Star. As such, the same as the Mole Humans who share that guilt, you shall be exterminated as an example."

"...Ah, now I remember. Old man Emperor, you're the reason we're nearly wiped out."

"Hm."

At the reply to words directed at a man he'd just cut down, Eugard's brow furrowed. Before Eugard's eyes, the one who answered was Halibel, split left and right down the middle of his body. As the halves slowly drifted apart, Eugard blinked.

"I am surprised. To not die even in that state — is this where excessive training leads?"

"Quite the amusin' theory, that. But nah. —Just ain't the real me."

The moment Halibel said that with a mischievous grin, the two halves of his body collapsed all at once, a great mass of black fur spilling onto the ground. Drawing attention to that surreal sight, from behind Eugard came—

"Carelessly taking my rear is not permitted. I allow that to no one but my Star."

"Heh, quite the somethin', ain't ya."

Behind the shock of the collapsing decoy, the torso of Halibel who had circled around to Eugard's back was cleaved clean in two by a slash of the "Sun Blade," once again gripped in Eugard's right hand.

But the body, split top from bottom and instantly bursting into flame, wasn't the real one either.

"Nu," Eugard exclaimed in surprise, his body responding to the ambush from behind by suddenly sinking down. The cause was a third Halibel, gripping both his legs and dragging him down into the street. In exchange for the Halibel pulling free of the ground, Eugard sank in up to his waist. Ideally he'd have been buried up to the neck, completely immobilized, but—

"Never does go quite that smooth, does it."

The grumbling third Halibel was severed atop the burning corpse of the second Halibel. Cuts ran vertically, horizontally, diagonally, carving the body into a lattice pattern that blew apart into pieces — and from the same slashes that had torn open the ground, Eugard leapt free.

Combat stance restored: "Sun Blade" in the right hand, "Evil Blade" in the left. Wielding those enchanted swords beyond human comprehension in a dual-wield stance, Eugard braced himself as two fresh Halibels attacked from left and right. In a sense, it was payback for the relentless assault the Undead had been inflicting — but when the ones multiplying were Halibel, they were a different breed entirely from your average Undead.

"Nothing but menace, through and through."

Taking the fact of Halibel's multiplication in stride, Eugard swung both enchanted swords simultaneously. Against the Halibels coming from left and right, each blade poised to deliver a fatal strike — but a third Halibel appearing from behind seized both his arms and stopped them.

"Sorry 'bout this, but I can go up to three, y'know."

With both arms restrained, Eugard held his ground as the two Halibels' knife-hands arrived a beat later, driving into his head and torso respectively. Halibel's piercing hand strikes, honed through mastery of the "Flow Method," cut sharper than any half-baked blade. They struck true, piercing Eugard's right eye and solar plexus with lethal force.

However—

"Do not think to deceive me. To do so to any but my Star would be disrespect."

His unpierced left eye met Halibel's gaze, and immediately after, the "Sun Blade's" glow intensified. In the next instant, the red glow became flame, and the whole area, Eugard included, erupted into fire all at once. The three Halibels in contact with Eugard were engulfed and burned in that explosive spread of flame.

Then, a beat later, as the fire vanished as if it had been an illusion, all three Halibels had become nothing but ash, and Eugard, likewise scorched, walked out unconcerned, his burnt surface regenerating.

After that, he swept his golden eyes around — and noticed that Groovy, who should have still been there, had vanished.

"I would not have thought you the type to withdraw obediently. —What are you after?"

"—Ugh, hate that. Sees through whatever we're schemin' the second we start."

Far to the rear, glancing back at Eugard left behind on the battlefield in the distance, Halibel shook his head wearily, sighing at the man's troublesome nature that went beyond mere swordsmanship. Eugard wielded his powerful enchanted swords without being overwhelmed by them, handling them masterfully. On top of that formidable skill, his Undead immortality and sharp insight. He'd even seen through the lie that their clones capped out at three, showing excellent adaptability even in a fatal situation.

"As expected of the man who flipped an unfavorable battle almost single-handed. Bet his nose is pretty high, huh?"

"Not the dime, to be sayin' that, guzo...!"

Carried in Halibel's arms and hauled away from the battlefield as he asked that, Groovy thrashed about. Had he been left there, he'd have been caught up in Eugard's final blaze and ended up in an even more unsightly state — yet there was not a shred of gratitude in him. Though Halibel understood the sentiment of a warrior hating to be pulled away from a fight.

"But you might die if this goes on any further, y'know? Forcin' your body to move with poison in the first place... reckless don't even cover it."

"—————"

At the remark, Groovy startled slightly, and Halibel flicked a finger against his own nose. The faint strange odor mixed into Groovy's blood — its identity was something a shinobi versed in every manner of poison could guess at. As for its use, it was something even Halibel, though he could imagine it, could never bring himself to try — just thinking about it made him shudder.

"Really, you Vollachia folk are scary with how set your resolve is. Olbart-san too, grinnin' away after losin' a hand — ain't that a bit much? Old man like that shouldn't be able to keep up."

"Whad aboud you, den...?"

"Hm?"

"Guzo's mulduplyin', doin' it like id's nothin', whad aboud the curse...!"

"—That, is the problem, isn't it."

As Groovy, tending to his throat injury with astonishing speed, asked in his hoarse voice, Halibel answered with a note of confusion in his tone. The reason he was carrying Groovy away from the battlefield like this wasn't to shirk his duty and flee the capital — it was, ultimately, part of a groundwork laid toward victory.

Charging in blindly, without knowing your opponent, was the act of a fool. Against most opponents, Halibel's own strength was such that he could still win through brute force even so — but this opponent wouldn't allow that.

And that wasn't simply because the opponent was a superclass fighter.

"Well, if it came down to a straight fight like that, I'd probably win. But—"

Still, defeating Eugard alone wouldn't accomplish the task given to Halibel. What Halibel had been entrusted with was dealing with the "Curse" that would determine the outcome of this decisive battle for the capital. Defeating Eugard alone wouldn't resolve that.

That fact was the biggest problem.

"Your, thorns...?"

"Vanished clean away. That's clue number two."

"...Number one bein' that damn thorn on the Emperor?"

Nimbly kicking off the ground as he ran, Groovy, cradled in Halibel's arms, peered at his chest. In the loosely folded front of his wasou, the "Thorn Curse" that should have been on Halibel's chest had disappeared. That throbbing, aching thing being counted among the greatest obstacles to be eliminated in this fight against the Undead made sense. And yet, Halibel had done nothing active to remove it.

Without doing anything, the thorns had vanished. Turn that around, and it meant Halibel hadn't been the one to make them vanish.

What that implied was—

"Goddamn it..."

"By the sound of it, seems you've landed on the same thought as me. Good to know I'm of the same mind as the 'Curse Tools Master.'"

"Shid and shid mixin' together, and comin' oud a shidty answer thad turns my sdomach...!"

Spitting the words out, Groovy turned his directionless fury into curses. Sharing that same sentiment, Halibel reclaimed his earlier displeasure in full, now certain that the second possibility conjured by the sight of the thorns on Eugard's chest was the correct one.

The logical reason the "Thorn Curse" was entangled around Eugard's chest — it was simple, really.

"Those thorns ain't the Emperor cursin' everyone around him..."

"Lord Eugard got cursed by some damn bastard somewhere, and it's spreadin' like shit ever since."

—The curse that the "Thorn Emperor" Eugard Vollachia had received was part of a sabotage operation within the Vollachia Empire's long-standing imperial succession ritual, the "Imperial Selection Ceremony."

Now, it was no longer clear at whose bidding among the rival imperial siblings it had been, nor how renowned the Curse Arts User involved had been. But the cruel scheme meant to thin out a rival ahead of the eventual "Imperial Selection Ceremony" had gone on to drastically twist the fate of the young royal who would later become Eugard Vollachia.

—The "Thorn Curse" placed upon Eugard was a thing horribly cruel in its simplicity.

Namely, that the binding of thorns would inflict unbearable agony upon the heart. And further, that this would also be applied to anyone in Eugard's vicinity.

If young Eugard cried out in pain from the curse, his family and servants would try to save him. But anyone who approached in that way would, one after another, get caught up in the curse themselves, unable to draw near him.

A curse meant to leave Eugard to die alone, in isolation and agony — that was the true nature of the "Thorn Curse" cast upon him.

Through this dreadful, merciless "Thorn Curse," Eugard should have been a tragic royal, unable to even reach the "Imperial Selection Ceremony," his short life ending there. That should have been the script written by whoever had ordered the curse placed on him — but here, a great, an enormous coincidence occurred, one large enough to alter the very fate of the empire.

Eugard had been born, by nature, unable to feel pain — with congenital analgesia.

Because of this, the ever-active "Thorn Curse" inflicted no pain upon Eugard himself, and instead continued inflicting the pain of thorns upon those around him — with the result that he ended up alone. Unable even to have family or servants approach him, spending his childhood alone in the estate given to him, with no chance for contact with others, Eugard believed this to be the reason his own expression had frozen as it had, though whether that was truly the cause, or simply his innate nature, was anyone's guess.

In any case, Eugard and the "Thorn Curse" went on to share a miraculous coexistence. Contact with others was kept to an absolute minimum, but there were still occasions where contact was unavoidable. Each time, those caught up in the "Thorn Curse" naturally came to whisper that "it's Eugard casting the curse," and Eugard never denied it.

In truth, Eugard himself had no way of knowing whether the true nature of the "Thorn Curse" was something cast upon him by another, or something he himself triggered for some reason. What's more, the Curse Arts User who had cast the curse — who would have held the answer — never again appeared in Eugard's life, and the identity of whoever had ordered it remained forever unknown.

And so, the "Thorn King" continued walking his long, long path, never knowing the true nature of the thorns that dogged his every step.

That path, too, should have — barring anything unforeseen — come to an end somewhere within the "Imperial Selection Ceremony." The thorns had made Eugard solitary, and the aim of whoever had cursed him had been fulfilled, if not in quite the form originally intended.

"Wouldn't your heart find more peace counting the stars in the sky, rather than staring into pitch darkness?"

—Except for that one encounter, with the girl who stepped across Eugard's thorns and drew near.

"I thought you might have fled, just like that, Wolf Human."

"Can't say the thought didn't cross my mind... nah, sorry, that's a lie. Didn't cross my mind one bit."

"Why did you attempt to deceive me?"

"Crackin' jokes is kinda a habit of mine. Hopin' you'll forgive it, seein' as it's all in good fun."

"To attempt to deceive an emperor — that would be disrespect. However, I judge favorably that you admitted your fault at once, and so I will let this most recent lie pass unquestioned."

Literally having flown back into the fray, Halibel found himself received face to face by Eugard, who gave a magnanimous dip of his chin. Rather than showing displeasure at Halibel — who had left the battlefield once and then returned — Eugard's steady bearing as he spoke carried an emperor's dignity. Setting aside his combat strength, one could rightly say his character was every inch that of an emperor.

"Gettin' that guy mad enough to boil like lava, huh — just what've you gone and done, old ancestor."

At that unbreakable connection tying him to the "Thorn Emperor" Eugard, Halibel scratched his cheek. To Halibel, a Wolf Human, Eugard was a mortal enemy to his entire race. Squeezed thin the world over, even now within the Vollachia Empire, Wolf Humans — and even half-Wolf Humans of mixed blood, the Werewolves — could not escape execution if discovered. And it was none other than this very Eugard who had left behind the decree in the empire that made it so, for all eternity to come.

The Wolf Humans and the Mole Humans, the two races, had been designated as hated traitors to the Vollachia Empire, and across its long history countless kin had had their lives taken, hunted to near extinction. Likely, that climate would never entirely disappear, even going forward.

So it was only natural that Halibel should harbor an equal measure of hatred toward the man before him — and yet—

"—I will not permit that look to be turned upon me."

"What kinda look am I givin' you, exactly?"

"The look my Star showed, on occasion. I do not permit that to be turned upon anyone but my Star. ...No — I do not care for it."

Hearing Eugard's answer — a man surely no stranger to falsehood or deceit in that reply — Halibel let out a long breath, then struck his own left breast with a hand.

There was no "Thorn Curse" there. To Eugard, a curse that should be raging against the hated, despised Wolf Human standing before him was not activating. —That, more than anything, set Halibel's temper further on edge.

"If that kid sent me here havin' foreseen even this — that I'd end up feelin' this way — then Ana-bo, you'd best watch yourself."

Picturing, behind closed eyelids, the girl he'd known since she was small, who'd never grown much in height, Halibel muttered that and put the pipe back to his mouth. Then he dropped fire onto the tip, savored the smoke thoroughly, and exhaled.

Exhaling, he made up his mind.

"Don't need your permission, old man Emperor. You and me, we've got that kinda relationship, don't we?"

"What kind of relationship is that?"

"Wolf Human. And the 'Thorn Emperor.'"

Two who had reason to hate each other, reason to destroy one another. Pointing at himself and then at his opponent, Halibel declared it, and Eugard's expression did not change. What lay sealed behind that unchanging expression, there was no telling.

There was no telling — but Halibel had made up his mind.

Simply defeating him wouldn't fulfill the role he'd been entrusted with, nor his own resolve.

So—

"I'll free you from that rotten, foul curse, and make you nothin' but a plain old king, 'Thorn King.'"
