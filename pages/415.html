Arc 6 Chapter 13: "The Sand's Mockery"

—Space warped, the world tore open, and he fell headlong into the rift that formed.

The world split apart as easily as tearing thin paper, baring the seam of its rupture. As Subaru was swallowed headfirst into the distortion, no sound at all reached his eardrums—a strange contrast to the spectacular visual display. All he could hear was his own scream, and someone's voice calling out to him.

Even that grew distant, and before long it was gone. The feel of contact faded too—fingers torn away, the distance widening.

Was he going to be cast out into a place with no one, nothing, left utterly alone?

Tormented by that loneliness and sorrow, Subaru's consciousness slowly rose to the surface. And then—

"—How long do you intend to sleep? Get up already, Barusu."

"Pororoca!?"

A sharp sensation stabbed into his side, and Subaru yelped at the impact. He jerked upright without meaning to, and the sand clinging to his whole body rose up as dust. He coughed, spitting out the grit in his mouth.

"Ugh! Ptoo, ptoo! Kah! Ptoo! What—what happened!?"

He rubbed roughly at his eyes and checked his surroundings as he stood. His knees wobbled, unsteady on the poor footing—beneath him, unchanged, was a sea of fine-grained sand. The moment before he'd lost consciousness came rushing back, and Subaru concluded this must be an extension of the Augria Sand Dunes. But he couldn't make out the scene around him. It was too dark—far too dark. In a darkness so deep even his own hands were uncertain, the only thing Subaru's eyes could make out was the presence of an artificial light.

"Sleeping so soundly in an emergency like this. I'm too appalled for words."

The biting voice rained down, and the light's owner gave a small huff. The light source was a type of magic-stone craft—a lantern-like tool made from processed Lagmite Ore. Seeing the face lit up by its glow, Subaru's brow furrowed.

"—Ram?"

"Who else would I look like? I hope you weren't expecting a dull answer like 'Rem.'"

"...You and Rem may look alike, but whatever seeps out from the inside couldn't be any less similar."

Responding to words from Ram that seemed even more caustic than usual, Subaru brushed off his knees again. Then he took the lantern she held out and looked around, lighting the area faintly.

"Where are we? What happened?"

"Ram would like to know where we are just as much as you. As for what happened, it's three steps worse than whatever you're imagining."

"Is that assuming I'm an optimist? Or figuring I'm a pessimist?"

"In this case, I wonder which would be better."

The reply had probably come with a shrug. In a darkness so thick he couldn't even be certain of Ram's state right beside him, the space illuminated by the light was strangely vast. It seemed they'd wandered into some kind of cavity. Overhead, a faintly visible high ceiling; the unbroken walls testified to the depth of the space.

"No outside air, and I can't see the sky. So we've been thrown into somewhere...?"

"If what Lady Beatrice said before the split is right, a spatial distortion is the cause."

"Split... that's right, split! What about the others!?"

Listening to Ram's flat, emotionless words, understanding finally caught up to reality. Subaru swung the light left and right, searching for any figure besides Ram. But no matter how he tried, the shapes he hoped for never appeared in the light.

"As you can see, we've been split off. Your magic nullified the illusion leading to the watchtower... whether that means we've ended up on the correct path, or been trapped in a spatiotemporal distortion to wander here forever, I couldn't say."

"Is now the time to say that!? Why are you so calm about it! You and me ending up paired means... we're being scattered completely at random!?"

At Ram's serene explanation, Subaru's face paled and his voice rose. He could easily recall Beatrice's scream and the space before him tearing open. That rift had swallowed the whole party whole and dragged them into another space. At the time, Subaru and Ram had been traveling companions, but should have been in separate groups. Subaru had been astride Patrasche; Ram had been inside the dragon carriage.

"With this kind of split, physical contact doesn't seem to be the condition for staying together. At the moment we entered the rift, Ram should have been holding Rem... and yet, here we are."

"Beako should've been clinging to me too. And yet she's nowhere to be found... she really isn't here, right? Don't tell me it's just me and Ram here? What kind of grouping is this?"

If contact wasn't the condition for the split, was there some other reason? The group this time numbered eight in total. There should be no notable reason unique to just Subaru and Ram. Was it truly a completely random grouping after all?

"No, guessing at the conditions can wait! More than that, we need to hurry and regroup with everyone... no, wrong! There's something more urgent I have to do! Rem!"

"—"

"You and me being together, that's still better. If everyone else got split off with somebody too, there's still hope. But if someone ended up alone, and it's Rem... that's the worst-case scenario."

Emilia and Julius, the two who'd shouldered most of the group's combat strength, would surely be fine. Meili and Beatrice could each likely manage alone with their own abilities. As for Anastasia—Eridna—she should have whatever trump card drove off the Lust archbishop. The rest of the group could honestly be said to be better off than the Subaru-and-Ram pairing.

—But Rem alone was a different matter.

Rem alone, unlike everyone else, was entirely incapable of acting on her own. Even if she was with someone else, protecting her while she remained endlessly asleep would be no easy feat. And in the worst case, if the split had left Rem all alone, she could end up nothing more than something waiting for "death" in this lonely sea of sand.

"Regrouping with everyone else matters too, but the top priority is securing Rem! I can't leave her behind in a lonely place like this. That much is absolutely... absolutely not allowed. Absolutely not..!"

"...Barusu."

"Because I said I'd bring her along... thinking that if we could just get her in front of the Sage, maybe we could wake her up, and now this happened. No. Absolutely not, damn it, not Rem, of all people...!"

"Barusu, calm down. Panicking now won't—"

"Why are you so calm about this!? Just because your memory of Rem has been wiped clean, doesn't mean you're not worried, does it!?"

"—! As if that could be true!"

Subaru, imagining the worst possibilities, rattled off hopeless futures and regrets in a rush. At his words, Ram suddenly snapped, as if she could bear it no longer. She grabbed the collar of the bewildered Subaru and shoved him bodily against the sand wall behind him.

"Do you think you're the only one worried about Rem? Don't get carried away, Barusu. Even without the feeling of it, Ram is Rem's sister. Don't mock me."

"—"

"...There's a faint sense of connection with that girl. So at the very least, she's safe right now. Believe just that much, and calm down for the time being."

It wasn't an expression baring raw emotion. But seeing the anxiety and worry that her eyes, feigning composure, couldn't fully hide, Subaru let the tension drain from his stiffened shoulders. Sensing it, Ram withdrew her arm. Freed from the wall, Subaru hung his head.

"...Sorry. My bad. Really, that was me being stupid and awful just now."

"That's more or less how it always is with you. If you spent every moment you're merely alive dwelling on your failures, the whole day would be gone. Stop wasting time on it."

"...Yeah, my bad."

Sensing that her sharp tongue was Ram's own way of calling a truce, Subaru offered one last apology. Then he slapped his own cheeks hard, steeling himself anew. Ram had vouched for Rem's present safety, but that didn't erase the anxiety of her being alone. The need to regroup with everyone quickly hadn't changed.

"Anyway, let's hurry and regroup. Ram, can't you use that shared sense of yours to find where Rem is?"

"That's difficult. Since she's asleep, all that comes through is about her heartbeat. As for other means, there's Farsight, but... that's not very reliable either."

"Why not?"

"This dune is crawling with witchbeasts, and there's hardly any ordinary flora or fauna. Farsight only lets me borrow sight from something whose wavelength matches mine. It doesn't overlap with witchbeasts. Given the lineup this time, about the only eyes Ram could borrow are your Earth Dragon's."

"Patrasche, huh. No, that'd still be plenty. I want to check on Patrasche and Jaian too, if either's with someone. Especially if we've still got the whole dragon carriage, that'd help a lot."

"...That wouldn't be much use."

At Ram's unenthusiastic attitude, Subaru felt a flash of frustration and tilted his head. Now, of all times, was when he wanted every scrap of information he could get toward regrouping. And yet she was hesitant here—he couldn't understand what she was feeling. But before he could put into words a question to confirm her true meaning, the answer arrived first.

"—?"

A light other than the lantern in Subaru's hand suddenly flickered at the edge of his vision. The memory triggered by a light crossing the edge of his sight was vivid and unpleasant, and Subaru instinctively flinched—but the light that appeared had nothing to do with that; it swayed with a gentle, rocking motion. The light gradually drew closer, and as its outline slowly came into focus—

"Ram-san, Natsuki-kun, looks like you two are done talkin'?"

"—Anastasia, and Patrasche?"

The familiar Kararagi accent rained down, and Subaru raised his head slightly in surprise. Looking ahead, there she was—Anastasia, swaying atop Patrasche's back, a lantern different from Subaru's own in one hand. At Anastasia's question, Ram pinched the hem of her robe and bowed where she stood.

"Thank you for your consideration, Lady Anastasia. Now then, how does the area look?"

"I only went a bit further in to look, but there's no sign of the others. Seems like it's just us three... and this one here, who got sent this way."

"I see."

"Gotta not let your spirits drop, y'know. Might not be much comfort, though."

"Thank you for the concern. Yes, I understand... or so I intend to."

Ram answered Anastasia, up on the dragon's back, with polite courtesy. From the flow of the conversation, Subaru just barely gathered that Anastasia had been out scouting the surroundings. But that was as far as his understanding went—he wasn't at all satisfied with the lack of explanation.

"Hey, Ram. Wasn't it just the two of us here? What's going on, explain."

"Ram never once said it was just the two of us, Ram and you. You simply jumped to that conclusion and got yourself worked up. How unseemly."

"'I was too worried about Rem to think it through' would've sounded a lot cuter, big sister."

"Hah."

At Subaru's remark, Ram scoffed in her usual mocking way. Feeling reassured by the return of her usual manner, Subaru walked over toward Anastasia.

"Being tricked aside, it's good news that you're safe, Anastasia. That's a relief."

"I had nothin' to do with any tricking, so don't go misunderstanding, alright? I'm relieved not to be alone too. And Natsuki-kun's Earth Dragon, Patrasche-chan here, being such a good girl really helped."

Anastasia put a foot to the stirrup and began, gingerly, to climb down from Patrasche. At that, Patrasche slowly crouched down where she stood, helping her dismount. As expected of a capable Earth Dragon. A real dashing Earth Dragon. Even if she's female.

"There's no one else hiding around, right? Genuinely, just the four of us?"

"Including Patrasche-chan, four's right, no mistake. No reason for Emilia-san or Julius not to show themselves if they were here... well, can't say for Meili-chan, though."

"Meaning she might've used the chaos to slip off, huh. ...Can't say it's impossible."

Picturing the braided girl in his mind, Subaru turned his thoughts over what Meili might choose to do. Just before the split, Meili had been exposed to Subaru for secretly keeping a witchbeast under her control as insurance. Having Returned by Death, Subaru knew that Meili had actually used that witchbeast to protect the dragon carriage from the watchtower's attacks and from a swarm of oiran bears. But that was something that hadn't happened this loop, and in the first place, the answer to what purpose Meili had been keeping a sand earthworm for existed only in her own heart. Given how things had turned out, it wouldn't be strange at all if Meili tried to slip away from the group and leave the dunes alone.

"But I want to think... that she hasn't done that."

"Hope? Or don't tell me—trust?"

"Let's just call it a wish. More importantly, what's the area like?"

Setting aside his stance on Meili, Subaru asked Anastasia for her scouting report. She tucked her hands into her robe's sleeves and tilted her head. "Well, about that..."

"Far as I can tell, we're definitely inside the dunes still... but I think this here's underground."

"Underground? Like, further down than the dunes themselves?"

"Under the sand of the dunes, is what I mean. Not a cave, more like a hollow, I'd say—but compared to up top, the temperature's clearly lower, and the air's heavier."

She tugged at the front of her closed robe, the gesture appealing to the chill in the air, and Subaru nodded. Indeed, the chill of the space they were in now was far stronger than in the scene just before. It fit with the guess of a space with a high ceiling and no sky in sight.

"Underground beneath the dunes... I have a bad feeling about this. Let's pray it's not a sand earthworm's burrow."

"Ugh... that's..."

It was a plausible thought. In fact, Subaru had seen firsthand the existence of sand earthworms living underground, and considering the sheer size of the one Meili had commanded, this cavity was plenty large enough. Given the ecology of the eyeless sand earthworm, the lightless dark of this hollow also matched perfectly.

"I mean, seriously, this team's combat power is way too lacking... me, Ram, and Anastasia—did someone go out of their way to stack us with non-combatants!?"

"A knight sworn to Lady Emilia, boldly declaring himself a non-combatant... this is hopeless."

"Call it knowing my own reality. I'm not conceited enough to trust my whip skills without Beatrice backing me up."

Compared to the members who had some means of self-defense, this really was a lineup lacking in combat strength. Ram with her restrictions, and Subaru without Beatrice—there was no need to even say it.

"By the way, what about Lady Beatrice? As her Contractor, can't you feel some connection?"

"Unfortunately, Beako and I are bound tight in the heart, but it doesn't come with any real physical sense. No, wait, Beako said before that she could feel me, but it doesn't work the other way for me."

"Useless."

"Shut it."

Turning his face away from the sighing Ram, Subaru quietly leaned toward Anastasia. He murmured softly to her composed profile.

"So, can you fight? What's the story there?"

"—If it comes down to it, of course I'll fight. Though it would mean shaving away at Ana's life. As for me, I'd rather avoid that if I can. I'll be counting on the rest of you."

"That expectation's gonna get betrayed. Can't say if that's a good or bad thing, though."

Subaru gave a snort at Anastasia, whose face had, for just a moment, become Echidna's. Then he turned to Patrasche, who waited quietly for instructions, and stroked her nose with his palm. The jet-black Earth Dragon leaned in wordlessly, rubbing her hard scales against him.

"Ow, ow... but I'm relieved. Without Emilia or Beatrice around... still, having you here puts me at ease. Man, though, this grouping really does seem meaningless."

"—Barusu."

Sharing his unease with Patrasche, he turned at Ram's call. She stood with her arms crossed toward him, jerking her chin in the direction Anastasia had come from.

"We can't just stay put forever. Let's get moving, if only to regroup with the others. Fortunately, thanks to Lady Anastasia, we have light. Ram and the rest of us can proceed."

"Oh... wait, come to think of it, did Anastasia bring this light out with her?"

"Right before the dragon carriage got swallowed up, I just grabbed the emergency bag Natsuki-kun had prepared. Thanks to that, I managed to bring along the light, a knife, and some emergency rations."

Anastasia pointed at Patrasche, where a small emergency bag hung from the saddle. It was, quite literally, the equipment Subaru had stocked in the dragon carriage as an "emergency grab bag." He'd always hoped it would never need to prove useful, but felt relief now that it had.

"This kind of foresight really isn't something to scoff at, huh. ...From now on too, I'll make sure not to forget checking the emergency exits first, wherever I stay for the first time."

"Nonsense aside, this is credit to you as well, Barusu. Now then, as a reward I'll let you carry the light—so hop to it and lead the way."

"Yeaaah... wait, this is a reward?"

Handed the lantern, Ram and Anastasia both climbed onto Patrasche's back. No matter how he looked at it, this arrangement put Subaru squarely in the position of a servant.

"Three of us riding at once might be a bit much... hmm, maybe if me and Ram-san squeeze in it'll work?"

"Let's not, Lady Anastasia. If close contact becomes possible, I can just imagine Barusu taking it as a golden opportunity to press himself against us."

"Imagine all you like, but if that's how you're gonna play it, I'll go imagine something way more extreme! This isn't a threat! Don't underestimate puberty!"

He raised his middle finger, insisting hard, and even Anastasia managed only a wry smile. Ram's reaction remained as ever, but Subaru, lantern in hand, started forward into the depths of the cavern. The banter and bickering between Subaru and Ram, as if they'd forgotten their situation entirely—was, surely, nothing but bravado spun so hard it spun in place, just to avoid looking straight at the unease each of them carried within. That, too, both of them probably knew—even though neither said a word of it.

Struggling with the difficulty of walking, Subaru and the others pressed on through the great sand cavern. As expected, Patrasche was well accustomed to it, moving without any trouble even with two weights riding on her back. Subaru too, aside from the feel of sand working into his boots, managed to advance without too much difficulty, applying the technique for walking the sand sea he'd learned these past few days.

"Is there wind... or isn't there..."

"There is. Though, judging by how faint it is, wherever it connects to the surface must be a long way off yet."

Along the way, Subaru wet his fingertip with his tongue to feel for wind, but he couldn't tell for sure. Ram, more sensitive to it, searched in his stead—but the exit they sought still seemed a long way off. Besides the exit, what they wanted to prioritize was regrouping with their companions. And unfortunately, relying on the wind offered no clue at all toward that.

"Lady Emilia and Lord Julius shouldn't ever lose their way, guided by minor spirits. On that point too, I'm tempted to suspect malice in how we were sorted."

"Right. Come to think of it, minor spirits guiding the way is pretty effective. Damn it, in my case Beako's presence is too overwhelming and the minor spirits run off. No capacity for it."

What became clear was a strength of Emilia's and Julius's beyond mere combat power. With a lineup that fell entirely short even in scouting, the search couldn't proceed as they'd hoped. All they could do was press on carefully, scanning their surroundings so as not to miss anything.

"About Ram-san—before you woke up, Natsuki-kun... right after we got sent here, she was pretty shaken up. Couldn't find Rem-san anywhere. She's hiding it bravely now, though."

"...I see."

Hearing that quietly from Anastasia only made him feel worse about the argument he'd had with Ram right after waking. Subaru had watched Ram live through a full year with her sister's existence erased from her memory. Between the two of them, something of what had vanished surely lingered faintly still. Of all people, Subaru especially should have been the one to believe that.

"Well, having everyone go gloomy and mopey together isn't much fun either. If you're gonna feel bad about it, you gotta look straight ahead, y'know? Otherwise you'll go and miss something important."

"...You're throwing me off my rhythm. Don't go saying good things like that. I nearly got won over by your other half in a similar situation, you know."

"I'd rather you start thinking of that 'other half' and me as separate things, one of these days. Push too hard and girls start disliking you, you know. That's a rule of thumb I've picked up watching the times go by."

"Don't know any other approach. I'll keep it filed away in a corner of my mind."

They traded quips laced with a click of the tongue, papering over the impatience and unease of their lack of progress. Interspersing such talk, the group pressed onward into the depths of the great cavern. Walking a path with no change whatsoever—sand path, sand walls—a few thoughts occurred to him. Namely—

"For all we were on guard about this maybe being a witchbeast's den... we haven't run into a single one."

"Ram's been wondering about that too."

Kicking at the sand underfoot, Subaru let that slip, and Ram agreed. They'd been pressing on into the great cavern for nearly an hour now. There was impatience in finding no one, nothing—but what welled up even more was unease and a bad premonition. Of course, the absence of hostile enemies was without question something to welcome, but the sheer silence couldn't shake off its own bad premonition either. —As if they'd been cut off from the world entirely.

"Don't tell me we've actually been swallowed into some pocket dimension that doesn't connect anywhere?"

"If that's so, then where's the wind we've been relying on coming from, I wonder. Should we imagine it's the breath of this cavern's owner—some abnormally huge, ferocious witchbeast?"

"The scary part is I can't laugh that off."

In truth, nothing that happened now would be strange. He'd already seen the world itself tear open with his own eyes. Wherever that rift had led, was there really any cause for surprise?

"Setting aside the malice behind how we were sorted, it's hard to imagine whoever set this up anticipated the illusion being broken. It wasn't a deliberate nullification on our part—it must be a product of chance."

"Meaning what, exactly?"

"Imagining the worst—that this path leads straight into a witchbeast's stomach—is a waste of effort. —Here, look."

Ram rationally dismissed the pessimism that their lack of progress had led to. Then, at the end of her explanation, she urged Subaru, who had stopped walking, to look ahead. Following her cue, Subaru squinted and held the lantern out ahead. The illuminated cavern passage narrowed, and before them—

"A fork in the path."

"Right or left, it seems. What do we do?"

"According to what I know, Kurapika once said to pick right in a situation like this."

"Who's that now."

He recalled something about behavioral science—that people, when lost, tend to unconsciously favor going left. Factoring in dominant eye, arm, and leg probably complicated the matter a good deal, but it was, at least, one way of thinking about it. So, going by the logic of the brainy character type Subaru knew, he wanted to believe heading right was the correct answer—but.

"So, which way do you actually want to go, Barusu?"

"...Honestly speaking, left."

"What about this Kurapika, then?"

"Who's that now."

Ram followed Subaru's answer with another question, and Anastasia teased him over it. Taken at face value, that exchange could be read just that lightly.

—But in truth, the color had drained from all three faces trading those words, their expressions stiff. What's more, even Patrasche, who understood no language, glared at the right-hand path with a fierce look in her eyes.

The cause was the overwhelming sense of negativity radiating from the right-hand path. In abstract terms, a bad premonition. Choosing words more carefully, it was closer to outright fear. Instinct rang the alarm bell: choosing the right path was dangerous.

"Right is... bad, I think. Feels like nothing good would come of it."

"Rare as it is, Ram agrees with Barusu on this. Lady Anastasia?"

"Kararagi-style majority rule says two votes settles it, no overturnin' after that... and honestly, I don't have the nerve to pick right here either."

Anastasia waved both hands as if to say she had nothing to give, and that gesture was everyone's answer. It wasn't just Subaru. Ram and Anastasia both, same as Patrasche, felt that same indescribable sensation from the right-hand path. And so, the right path could not be chosen.

"Left, then, huh. ...Even this feels like being forced into it—not a choice I much like either."

"So, turn back, then? There's nothing to be gained by that."

"Though sayin' there's nothin' to lose might be a bit too faint-hearted, y'know."

At Subaru's hesitance, Ram and Anastasia both spurred him on. Shrugging in resignation, Subaru stepped forward to walk ahead of Patrasche—straight down the left-hand path. Advancing that way despite his doubts, the distance from the right-hand path likely grew steadily within the cavern. The pressure he'd felt even through the sand walls receded, and he could feel the tension naturally draining from his shoulders.

"—What an awful place."

Perhaps she'd felt the same thing—Subaru heard Ram murmur that, and silently agreed. That overwhelming sense of negative intent he'd felt from the right-hand fork. Choosing left had partly been instinctive avoidance of it—but there was one more reason.

—Somewhere deep inside Subaru's chest, something had cheered, elated, at the right-hand path.

Following whatever that thing wanted was what truly terrified him. That, too, was undeniably a major factor in why Subaru had chosen the path he did.

Team "Non-Combatant" pressed on through the cavern, and several more hours passed after the fork in the road.

"—" "—" "—"

Slowly, as the three continued their search through the great cavern, they'd all fallen far quieter. Part of it was exhaustion, but the silence also owed to a mental wearing-down brought on by the unchanging darkness, where even the passage of time had grown uncertain. In truth, the sense that hours had passed was only Subaru's impression; the real truth was unknown. It was possible they'd been using their time more efficiently than he thought, or, just as possible, that they'd been squandering far, far more of it than that.

What he did know was simply this: there had been no progress at all.

"...Maybe that fork, we should've gone right."

"First words in a while, and it's weak talk? Stop it. It's unsightly."

He'd muttered it quietly, moving his dry lips, and Ram's retort came stabbing back sharp. But even her venom lacked its usual spirit. He could tell that she too was, faintly, in the grip of the same anxiety—that they might find nothing at all, at this rate.

Even on foot over the poor footing of the sand path, he was certain they'd already covered several kilometers. If the end of the cavern still wasn't in sight after that, a bad premonition brushing past was only natural. It was only reasonable to start suspecting they'd taken the wrong path. If only they'd hit a dead end already—at least then he could give up and turn back with some peace of mind.

"That barrage of light, back on the surface... what do you think it was?"

Fearing the conversation would keep sliding negative, Subaru steered it in a different direction. What he raised was the light from the watchtower—the very thing that had triggered this whole situation—the dunes' defense mechanism, presumably prepared by the Sage.

"They were needles."

"Needles?"

Ram answered Subaru's question with few words. She let out a breath at his asking back, gently smoothing her pink hair, and—

"I don't know the finer mechanics, but they were firing slender needles using magical power. The heat the needles carried was probably an effect of the magic propelling them. Though the needles themselves seemed to be specially made too—after being deflected and falling onto the sand, they crumbled and vanished."

"...You saw all that, in the middle of that chaos."

"Just a glance was enough to tell that much, surely."

She said it lightly, but it was simply that Ram's eyesight was anything but ordinary. Indeed, when Subaru glanced over, Anastasia shook her head, looking thoroughly exasperated. In Ram's case, the remark came from someone fully aware of her own talent, so it was pure sarcasm—but even so, it was quite the analysis.

"What do you think the purpose was?"

"Cutting down anything that approaches the watchtower... if so, it's a mystery why it targeted you so persistently. Culling the weaker threats first is basic tactics, so maybe that's it."

"Damn it, can't deny it!"

If, seen from a distance, that judgment had been made, then it was simply true, nothing to be done about it. Even so, thanks to that, none of his companions had become casualties. He could settle on the conclusion that it was a good thing Subaru, with his invincible magic, had been judged weak. Only, in that case—

"That means a friendly encounter with the Sage is a tall order, huh."

"I think that's jumpin' to conclusions too quick. From their side, they went and attacked without knowin' a thing about who we are or what we're after, right? I figure there's still room for a conversation to happen."

"And besides, worst case, what we need is the Sage's knowledge, not their personality. If they won't talk, we can just tie them up and rough them up enough to make them."

"Big sister, that's straight-up villain logic!?"

"If there's something you want, you take whatever measures are needed. This isn't some child's errand."

Ram remained utterly composed while Subaru sat there stunned. Precisely because of that, her seriousness came through all the more, and Subaru felt made painfully aware of how soft and shallow his own resolve was. Whether she'd truly go through with it or not aside, there was no doubt Ram carried that resolve. It was proof of just how serious she was in devoting herself to Rem.

"—"

Touched by Ram's resolve, perhaps Subaru too needed to settle his own. Not the resolve to dirty his hands for Rem's sake—that wasn't something he should pin on her. What he had to do was resolve himself to wrenching results loose by force. When he stood face to face with the being called the Sage, whatever kind of opponent it turned out to be, the resolve to cut through mercilessly if need be—that kind of resolve.

"—!"

"Patrasche?"

The moment Subaru bit his lip, thinking that, was the same moment Patrasche went rigid, holding her breath. The Earth Dragon glared down the path ahead, her eyes sharpening as she gave a low growl. Noticing this, Subaru stopped too and went back to calm her, stroking her neck and calling out, "What's wrong?"

"Did you notice something? What—"

"—This must be the cause."

Cutting his words off mid-sentence, Subaru fell silent, and Ram spoke in his place. Anastasia frowned, peering down the passage's depths just as Patrasche did. Of course, nothing could be seen in the spreading darkness ahead, but in place of sight, another sense made its appeal instead. —Slipping into their nostrils was the savory smell of something burning.

"...You think that's the smell of fire?"

"I can't sense it being anything else. Though I hardly think there's proper cooking facilities set up down here."

"If it's really fire... then the source of the smell has to be someone civilized, right?"

"—"

Neither Ram nor Anastasia could nod easily at Subaru's pleading question. He understood their judgment. But the drifting smell was clearly the scent of something being burned with intent. Ram had said cooking, and the smell drifting to them was close to that. A bonfire, or perhaps cooking a meal. If so—

"You think there's a chance it's Emilia and the others?"

"If we assume they got sent to the same place, then sure, plenty of chance. Though whether usin' fire in a situation like this is careless or the sensible thing, that's not somethin' I can rightly judge."

"—"

Whoever was up ahead, no amount of discussing it here would produce an answer. So the idea occurred to him of calling out to confirm the other party's intentions, but—

"Even if whoever's up there is human, no guarantee they're friendly."

If it wasn't Emilia and the others, then the possibility left standing was the Sage. It was hard to imagine the Sage, who should be up in the watchtower, coming down here—but it wasn't impossible either. In that case, if hostilities continued, a corresponding level of offensive power was to be expected.

"...Let's hide our light and move in, then. If they're using fire, that means there's light on their end. Worst case, we can keep ours hidden."

"Not even checking the cause would be a strange way to go about it, so I think that's the sensible move."

Anastasia nodded at Subaru's proposal, and Ram followed a beat later with quiet agreement. Of the two lanterns, they extinguished the one Ram and Anastasia carried, and the group set out, relying only on the light in Subaru's hand, heading toward the depths of the passage where the smell of fire drifted.

"Barusu."

"Hm?"

"If something happens, we're leaving you behind and running straight for it. Don't go holding a grudge."

"In that case, let me curse you at least."

Perhaps because she'd sensed his nerve about to fail him, Ram's words gave him courage. Seeing Ram's lips ease into the faintest of smiles at his quip, Subaru led the way straight down the passage once more. Advancing while feeling Patrasche's breathing behind him, the scent of fire grew gradually stronger, until—

"—! A light."

He spotted a faint, flickering red light further down the passage. Subaru immediately extinguished his own lantern, signaling the three behind him to keep silent. Then, crouching low, he crept forward through the sand, treading softly, to confirm the source of the light. One step, two—as he advanced, it became clear the light was falling on the far side of a bend. The passage curved faintly to the left, and the fire was coming from beyond it.

"—"

He reached the bend quietly, pressing his back to the wall as he peered around. A faintly heated breeze tickled Subaru's brow, and he shut his eyes without meaning to.

—The very next instant, the ground beneath Subaru's feet abruptly gave way, and a slide of sand sent his body tumbling down the slope.

"Wha—!?"

Unable to withstand the surprise, Subaru forgot even to brace himself and tumbled down the slope. The fine-grained sand offered nothing to hold onto, and he slid headlong all the way to the bottom. Plunging headfirst into a mound of sand after rolling down some ten-odd meters of steep slope, Subaru once again spat out sand and pulled himself upright.

"Ugh! Ptoo! More sand... no, wait, more importantly..."

Shaking his head, Subaru surveyed his surroundings. In his shock, he'd let go of the lantern without meaning to. Hoping it might be nearby, he swept his arm around his body and felt his hand brush something hard. For a moment he thought he'd found the lantern, but the feel was different from the hard object he'd been holding until just before. The surface was dry and brittle, its texture like a wooden stick. Lifting it, it was light, though it had a fair amount of thickness and length.

"What is this...?"

Even squinting and bringing it close, he couldn't tell what it was in the darkness. Sniffing it cautiously, what he faintly detected was a smell like charcoal—

"—"

The moment he thought that, a sudden light flared up directly behind him. What appeared behind him was a red, flickering, primal glow—the thing called flame. And in the world it illuminated, Subaru saw the true identity of what he held in his hand.

—It was, most likely, the leg of some living creature. The end result of something that had once been an animal, scorched until every part of it had become charcoal.

And scattered around Subaru, sitting there, were countless more of those charred former living things—he had slid down right into the middle of innumerable burnt corpses.

"Ohh!? Wah! Waaah!?"

He flung away the charred thing in his hand and instinctively scrambled backward. But what awaited him behind was the terrible flame itself, and its heatwave scorched the back of his neck, sending him pitching forward instead. Only then did he finally turn and look it straight on.

"—!!"

Bathed head-on in the resounding roar, Subaru's mind drifted, half in escapism. Why is it, he wondered, that every single witchbeast's cry is always this uniquely repulsive, stimulating human discomfort as much as it possibly could?

A cry rose up, high and cracked, as if countless infants were all wailing at once. Blazing red amid a space filled with charred corpses was a blasphemous creature the likes of which Subaru had never seen—unmistakably a witchbeast, yet unlike any he'd encountered before. As far as Subaru knew, witchbeasts were all warped and hideous, but most of them took forms that seemed like some crossbreeding of existing creatures. The White Whale, the Great Rabbit—same story. Each had some animal foundation to it. But the witchbeast before his eyes now—he didn't want to apply that same notion to it.

"—!!"

The wailing witchbeast, at a glance, shared traits in common with a horse. Slender, but sturdy legs built for powerfully racing across the earth—four of them. A torso supported by those four legs, and a long tail swaying from its haunches, just like a horse. But where a horse's neck should have led up to its head, there was instead a human torso. That human torso had, further, two arms of its own—making it the owner of four legs plus two arms. And above the neck of that human torso, there was no head at all. In place of a head, a "horn" grew straight up. The proof of a witchbeast—the clear, unmistakable mark that set it decisively apart from ordinary animals. That horn grew in place of a head. There was no head. So then where was the mouth that let out its cries? The human torso split vertically down its chest to its belly, and a sideways-oriented maw, lined with fangs, asserted its presence there.

"...A monster."

It was too presumptuous even to call it by the category "witchbeast"—here was a blasphemy against life itself. By Subaru's own knowledge, it was closest to the mythical creature called the centaur—half man, half horse—but what stood there was a distorted image, as if the attempt to recreate one had been abandoned partway through. And the centaur, of all things, grew a mane of flame from the back of its human torso, scorching the sand cavern with unbelievable heat and letting red light spray forth. Seeing that firepower made it plain who'd created all the burnt corpses scattered about. Hoping that "if there's fire, it must be someone civilized" had been a mistake.

"—"

The centaur's human-portion arms were, exactly, human arms complete with five fingers each. The centaur's horse-portion legs were, exactly, horse legs with formed hooves. Its overall body stood some six or seven meters tall, a size just right for striding through this cavern. —In other words, the owner of this cavern was this thing right in front of him.

"...This too, a trap, huh."

Subaru's breath caught, and despair swept over him. There was no countermeasure to take. He reflexively reached his hand around to the small of his back and grasped the whip's handle, but he couldn't imagine what that could accomplish. And to Subaru, who held his breath, the centaur tilted its headless head and let loose a roar from its mouth, deliberately loud, stoking his terror.

"—!"

In that instant, Subaru moved as if to catch it off guard. He spun on the sand and violently kicked up one of the charred remains, flinging it at the centaur. The corpse, thoroughly cooked through to its core, was far too light—there was no impact from the throw whatsoever. But even so, if it served as a distraction, that would do. He scrambled at the steep slope, trying to get back to the passage above. It meant retracing the path he'd come by, but that couldn't be helped now. The problem was he had no idea how far this centaur would chase him. Going around burning its prey to death like this, its appetite was doubtful. Of course, given that witchbeasts were said to have an instinctive nature to torment and kill humans, that fact wasn't necessarily a point in his favor. Even so, if it offered even the slightest chance to turn things around, then that was—

"Kah—"

Just as he thought that far, Subaru realized his body hadn't moved a single inch. Kicking up the charred remains as a diversion, turning to try and climb the steep slope, even reaching for the whip before that—none of it, Subaru had actually managed to do.

—Because before any of that could happen, the witchbeast's breath had already burned him.

"—Gh!"

The instant understanding hit him, Subaru screamed at the pain and agony of his entire body being scorched. But his throat was burned, the oxygen in his lungs consumed entirely, and not a fragment of a voice could escape him. His scorched skin bubbled up in an instant; his flesh swelled and burst, and fluids he'd never seen before flooded across his body, evaporating almost instantly as his blood genuinely began to boil.

"—"

He couldn't even arch backward, couldn't writhe in agony on the ground. He could feel his muscle and fat searing under the scorching heat, melting, turning to mush. The pain grew distant, and Subaru experienced the illusion of drowning within the flames.

Burns. Yes, burns. He'd seen something, somewhere, about the depth of burns. Burns had stages, and once you reached the third degree, scars remained and you'd need a skin graft—he'd heard that. And he'd also heard that when a human suffered burns across thirty percent of their skin, that alone made cutaneous respiration difficult and could lead to death.

"—"

His short hair burned down to the roots; his eardrums and brain matter, melted, ran out from his earlobes. His lips and gums evaporated, baring his teeth, and in the agony of the heatwave he bit clean through his own tongue. It didn't matter anymore. He was already drowning. Burns—would this even heal? He hadn't had much confidence in his face to begin with, and now, suffering burns on top of that—wouldn't they all give up on him? Emilia. Beatrice. Rem.

"—rusu!!"

Burning, burning, his vision was burning. Everything was burning, turning red, then fading white. Under the tremendous heat his blood was scorched away entirely, his eyelids melted away, and the moisture in his eyeballs evaporated, clouding them white—he could see nothing.

Only, he heard something. He heard someone's voice calling. He heard a baby crying. Someone came down beside him. Idiot. What are you doing. You said you'd run. Why come down to a place like this now. Coming down—it didn't make sense. More than that—left, right. A horse, a person. No, wrong. Hot. Melting, burning, sleepy.

Melting, vanishing—

※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

"—How long do you intend to sleep? Get up already, Barusu."

"Grand Blue!?"

Not a single limb would move any longer, and as he felt his charred thoughts crumbling apart, a sharp impact wrenched Subaru back to reality. Bolting upright at the suddenness of it, Subaru wiped away the cold sweat on his sand-crusted brow.

"Eh, ah... eh?"

He looked around. Dark. He could see nothing. That, in itself, summoned back the terror of mere seconds before.

"Hii—"

"...Unbelievable. How frightened can you get of mere darkness. You're not a child."

"—Ah."

Subaru, who had instinctively shrunk down, curling small, snapped his eyes wide open at that voice. Ram let out a small breath, lighting her own face with the lantern's glow. Then, quietly kneeling down where she was, she reached out and stroked Subaru's cheek with her hand.

"What a pathetic face."

"...My face—it isn't melted, is it?"

"—I've never once seen it melt, except before Lady Emilia."

The warmth of the palm against his cheek was, incomparably, gentle—nothing like what Subaru had just experienced. And so Subaru understood, thanks to that warmth so different from the sensation of flame, exactly what had happened.

He had, once again, Returned by Death. And that this was a starting point separate from the two deaths he'd met in the dunes so far. That he would have to challenge the labyrinth of sand once more, with his very life on the line.

—He felt as though the cold sand flowing through the sea of dunes was watching him, the boy who had been burned, as if mocking him.
