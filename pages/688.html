Arc 8 Chapter 69: "I'll Forgive You"

The silver gauntlet roared as it smashed apart the head and torso of the Undead standing in the way.
Inside an Undead's body lurked a core insect, and unless it was crushed, blowing away the head or heart wouldn't be a fatal blow. —If that was the case, then all he had to do was strike so the shock spread through the whole body.

"Like that!"

Until now, priority had been on landing fists swung with brute force, without any thought spared for the result after impact. But he was revising that thinking now.
He pictured the wave of shock born from a fist's impact propagating through his opponent's entire body. Doing so changed both how he threw his punches and what came of them.

"—Ngh"

There was a shuddering give, like a shiver running down a back, and the struck Undead's body crumbled into dust all at once, the destruction spreading a beat behind the point of impact. Proof that the shock had traveled through the whole body and killed the core insect.
So as not to let go of what he'd finally grasped, he met the swarming, countless Undead with his fists.
One, two, three — the number of Undead smashed apart increased at an accelerating rate—.

"Oi, brat! Enough already, there's no end to 'em!"

A rough voice called out to the back of the one standing firm, throwing punches at the Undead.
Wielding a finely-made sword, Heinkel — dealing with the Undead Garfiel missed — kept up his desperate resistance even as his hair and face were battered ragged.
At that voice, cracking into something like a whine, Garfiel twisted the corner of his mouth into a grin.

If he wanted to run, he could've just run — and yet here he was, dutifully holding his ground.
He'd insisted that having Garfiel around was safer than fleeing alone, but it was hard to believe that claim still held water in a situation this dire.
After all, this place was — the Grand Front Gate, the entrance to the Imperial Capital, the critical point holding back the great horde of Undead pouring in, the key to capturing the capital itself.

"Ain't movin'. If we don't hold this spot down, it'll mess up the boss's whole plan."

And so, holding this ground no matter what was Garfiel's duty.
At Garfiel's answer, fists pressed together in front of his chest as he psyched himself up, Heinkel clicked his tongue. Scowling bitterly, he raised his voice in anger, "But—!"

"If all you gotta do is your job, it'd be easier doin' it further back! There's no reason to hold the line here... forget about that dragon!"

Cutting down an Undead, Heinkel pointed with his raised sword tip at the 'Cloud Dragon' Mezoreia, lying collapsed on the ground after her death struggle with Garfiel.
Mezoreia should have technically been on the Undead's side, but the Undead were showing no mercy even to the fallen 'Cloud Dragon,' turning their hostility on her all the same — if left alone, the Dragon's scales would surely be stained with blood.

"Can't do it!"

"Why not! She's the enemy, ain't she! Just let 'em eat each other!"

"Don't wanna disappoint the boss!"

At Garfiel's childish answer, Heinkel's face stiffened and he fell speechless.
But it was the honest truth, no strings attached. Even in this battle, Subaru wanted the casualties kept low — as close to zero as possible. Garfiel wanted to help with that too.
And besides, he still hadn't exchanged real words with the 'Cloud Dragon' Mezoreia.
So—

"I ain't movin'! Come at me, you zombies!"

Roaring at the top of his lungs, Garfiel slammed his gauntlets together, ringing out a great sound.
Let the Undead who heard it, hunting for the vitality of the living, gather here instead.

"That's right, that's it. — All you strong ones, come to me!"

Because the harder he dug in and held his ground, the easier it would be, even a little, for the people precious to Garfiel.

"...You're outta your mind."

Heinkel listened to Garfiel's battle cry with his face twisted — not noticing that behind him, the collapsed 'Cloud Dragon' had stirred, just slightly.

The moment she was gently lifted up from the bed, Madelyn knew with certainty that this was the end. — Because in the chest of Balleroy, standing there before her, a painfully large hole had been opened.

It was the body of an Undead. Madelyn knew that a fatal wound wasn't simply fatal for one of them. And yet, she sensed Balleroy's will in the fact that a wound that should have closed up immediately had not.
That her beloved — the man she loved — was once again going somewhere beyond her reach. Somewhere far beyond even the clouds, beyond what Madelyn, child of the Dragon who commanded the clouds, could ever reach.

"—Sorry 'bout this, Madelyn."

His eyes crinkling downward, Balleroy said this, and Madelyn's eyes widened just slightly.
That expression of his overlapped with the one he'd worn in life, before their reunion as an Undead. Of course, his eyes still held black with gold floating in them, his complexion still that pale Undead color.
And yet, it felt as though a moment's warmth of life still dwelled in Balleroy's gaze, in his voice.
Just like all those times he'd come to see her, at the peak of Mount Palzoa.

—That it hadn't been her who restored that warmth to him — that filled her with genuine regret, down to her very core.

"—!"

Her voice caught with helplessness and inadequacy, and Madelyn's golden eyes welled with tears.
Balleroy — dead, yet with eyes full of strength — looked at Madelyn, alive yet with such weak eyes, reflecting her in the same-colored gaze, and said:

"Right down to the very end, I've been selfish. And with that selfishness of mine, I've put Madelyn through quite a lot."

Having settled on what he would do, having decided on this farewell, Balleroy stood at Madelyn's side.
This was merely the opening line. Madelyn could already sense that what followed would be words of apology and gratitude from Balleroy.
Yes — because she could already sense it.

"Stop it, ya..."

"Madelyn?"

"Apologizin', thankin' me, all that — stop it, all of it...!"

Her teeth chattering, forcibly evaporating the tears welling in her eyes, Madelyn pleaded.
Half her consciousness, still tethered to the Dragonhusk, wouldn't let strength fill her limbs. But she overpowered that reasoning through sheer will, and grabbed hold of the arm of Balleroy lifting her up.
She gripped his arm — an Undead's arm — hard enough to crack and crush it.
But what she wanted to convey with that wasn't anger, or hatred, or anything like that. It was simply, purely, the feeling Madelyn held, the will she wanted to convey.

"If you're gonna say you were selfish, then the most selfish thing Balleroy ever did was disappearin' on his own. Came to the dragon's nest on your own, became somethin' precious to the dragon on your own, stopped comin' on your own, died on your own, disappeared on your own... and now you're plannin' to disappear on your own again...!"

"——"

"You've been plenty selfish already. So... so! I won't allow any more selfishness...! Not Balleroy's goodbyes, not his thanks... I wanna hear the dragon's own story instead...!"

Even as his arm was crushed in her grip, Balleroy silently kept his eyes on Madelyn.
Meeting his golden eyes with her own — the same color, but a different light — Madelyn told him.
It was—

"—Balleroy brought the dragon outside."

"—!"

At Madelyn's words, Balleroy's eyes went wide.
Told something unexpected, Balleroy looked taken aback. Madelyn had never seen that face on him before. Even if it was an Undead's face, she was glad to see a new expression on the face of the one she loved.
Wanting to sear all of it into every fiber of his Dragonkin body, Madelyn went on.

"The dragon came outside. Balleroy made the dragon do that. Balleroy brought the dragon out beneath the sky."

"——"

"The dragon wasn't dragged around by anyone. That the dragon could come down from the mountain like this, that the dragon can talk with Balleroy again like this now... all of it, all thanks to Balleroy."

If only she'd chosen sooner. There was no end to regrets like that.
If only she'd talked with him more, touched him more. There was no end to laments like that.
And yet — she didn't want to let go of even a fragment of having chosen him, of having talked with him, of having touched him.

"Everything Balleroy gave me... Balleroy brought the dragon outside."

She wouldn't let the time Balleroy had come to offer his thanks and farewells be spent on anything like that.
She didn't want to spend what little time remained with Balleroy listening to, or arguing against, such trivial talk. If ten seconds remained, then Madelyn would spend those ten seconds—

"I love you. Balleroy is everythin' to the dragon. There's nothin' the dragon loves more than Balleroy. Balleroy is, the dragon's, everything..."

"Madelyn..."

"Balleroy, Balleroy, Balleroy...!"

—She would spend every one of those remaining ten seconds telling the one she loved that she loved him.

She wanted him to answer her. To want her back. She wanted him to hold the same depth of feeling for her that she held for him.
Casting aside all of that troublesome part of love, Madelyn became a pure mass of feeling.
Whether it was someone else trying to belittle Balleroy, or Balleroy himself wishing it — Madelyn would absolutely never allow it. She didn't want to allow it.

With the entirety of her life as Dragonkin, Madelyn loved Balleroy Temeglyph.

"A Dragonkin's lifespan is incomparably longer than a human's."

Through that long, long span of time — a span that, compared to a human life, might as well be eternity — Madelyn would live, thinking of Balleroy, carved into her very soul.
She would make sure to convey to Balleroy Temeglyph just how much she was leaving behind.

"...I wonder what you see in a man like me. Not worth fallin' for."

Balleroy said this quietly, self-mockingly, and Madelyn said nothing in reply.
She was too busy pressing her forehead to his chest, expressing her own feelings. As for a man who didn't even understand his own worth — he could go find the answer to that on his own.
Her intent seemed to get through to him. Balleroy let out a long breath.
And then—

"Can't forget somethin' just 'cause someone tells you to forget it. Yeah, I know that better than anyone. So you don't have to forget. Just—"

"——"

"Be happy, Madelyn. My beloved, precious dragon princess."

For the first time in her life, Madelyn had been clearly, plainly rejected by the one she loved.

"—So, what do you think, Your Excellency? My little sister's quite something, ain't she?"

At Balleroy's voice, unable to hide its wry amusement, every consciousness at the ruined gun emplacement was drawn to him. — No, not everyone. Everyone except one: Medium.

Her nerve, holding down Vincent's hand as he gripped the 'Sunlight Sword,' was on another level entirely.
Should someone unworthy carelessly grip the 'Sunlight Sword,' they would burst into flame. That was a plea made with death on the line. Though, it probably took something that drastic for Vincent to understand —
for an emperor who understood that his own life was the most important thing in the Empire, yet understood the value of that life the least of anyone in it.

"There ya go!"

Medium — the only one who hadn't fallen in line with everyone else — drew the machete at the small of her back and used it to deftly strike the green glow on the pedestal, Moguro's magic core, knocking it up off its base.
Spinning as it flew, it traced a gentle arc and landed in the raised hand of Balleroy. Feeling its warmth and weight, Balleroy looked at Vincent.
Vincent lowered the tip of the 'Sunlight Sword,' standing there facing Balleroy in silence.

"——"

For an instant, Balleroy witnessed Vincent's black eyes weave a complex, indecipherable color, and thought that this emperor needed Medium.
He needed someone to narrow down his choices, to support him from behind so the choice he made would be the best one, someone to stand beside him, laugh, and look toward the future with him. — Though Balleroy, who had taken Chisha from him, had no right to say such a thing.
Or perhaps, if it were Chisha Gold, he wouldn't be surprised if even the guilt Balleroy — now an Undead — carried had been calculated for.
Thinking that was probably overthinking it, Balleroy gave a wry smile, and said,

"Medi."

At Balleroy's call, Medium, catching his meaning, came trotting over. Into her arms, Balleroy entrusted Madelyn, whom he'd been holding in one arm all this while.
The precious Dragonkin girl who, drifting in and out of consciousness, had kept telling him of her love for him.
The girl who had cried herself back to sleep, exhausted — the girl who would now bear an eternal wound because of him.

"Bal-bro."

"What's up?"

Carefully taking Madelyn's body into her arms, Medium looked at Balleroy up close. Then, with the face he knew so well, she broke into a grin like sunshine.

"Do your best!"

"—Yeah. I'll do my best."

Who would believe there was vitality filling an Undead's body?
Drawing strength from something no one would believe in, Balleroy turned his back with vigor. Then, mounting the back of his beloved dragon waiting at the edge of the collapsed emplacement wall, he beat its wings.
In that instant, the rising wind pushed Carillon skyward, and their altitude climbed all at once.

Rising higher and higher and higher, Balleroy and Carillon surged upward toward the clouds above.
The sound of wind vanished, and he was wrapped in that sensation unique to flight — a vacuum of pure concentration.

"Balleroy and them, not just two. I, here too."

"Ah, right, right. My mistake."

Receiving that protest from the magic core clutched in his hand — Moguro — Balleroy smiled wryly.
Listed among the 'Nine Divine Generals' as the 'Steelfolk,' Moguro, despite that inorganic appearance and manner, was conscientious and understanding — and, probably, kind.

"I, Empire's 'Meteor.' That, my Vessel's purpose."

"—Vessel's purpose."

The words Moguro chose to break down that reasoning were, ironically, the same words Sphinx had so often used.
To fulfill their purpose as Vessels — Sphinx, who sought to destroy the Empire, and Moguro, who sought to protect it. And himself, wavering back and forth, lending sympathy to both.

"Why'd you betray, Balleroy?"

"Kuh."

At the question, thrown straight down the middle, Balleroy burst out laughing — so utterly like Moguro.
Whether that question was about his betrayal in life or in death, or whether Moguro made no distinction between the two at all — if anything, Balleroy would rather it be the latter.
In life and in death both, the reason Balleroy had chosen to stand against the Empire was the same.

"—It was for someone precious to me."

Maybe it was because the end was closing in before his eyes, or maybe it was because he'd come to think of this inorganic 'Meteor' as something like an easy friend to be around — Balleroy found himself saying it aloud.
Probably the first time he'd ever said it with real feeling behind it, and he'd never meant to say it to anyone at all.

"—!!"

Perhaps that sense of shame Balleroy felt got through to her, because when Carillon, soaring through the sky, glanced back to check on him, he nodded in return. Given the nature of a Flying Dragon Rider's bond, only Carillon should have been able to see through him. — No, it seemed Medium had seen through him too, apparently, but in any case, it was something he'd kept hidden.
Having that laid bare, Moguro must have found it more than she bargained for too, but—

"I see. Understood. We, the same. — Someone precious, for their sake."

"——"

"Balleroy, I, forgive you."

Moguro's forgiveness — inorganic, and yet somehow reaching out to draw close — made Balleroy's cheeks go stiff.
Onto those cheeks of his came the color of a living man, not an Undead, and his eyes regained their true blink, different from the black with gold floating in it. — That was the final radiance of Balleroy Temeglyph.

"I'm grateful, Moguro."

Having said that, as a dead man no different from the living, Balleroy looked up at the sky.
Still accelerating higher and higher, he and Carillon pierced through the clouds. And those pierced clouds began swirling as if possessed of will, enveloping the two of them as they raced through the sky.
Balleroy had seen this before, long ago, at the peak of Mount Palzoa — not meant to crush, but this. This was a Dragon's nest, meant to keep whatever was within the clouds from escaping outward—.

"——"

Many faces rose up in Balleroy's mind.
Vincent, the 'Nine Divine Generals,' Madelyn, Serena, Medium, Flop, Miles.
Proof that Balleroy had lived. The meaning of Balleroy's life.
Holding all of it, together with his beloved dragon Carillon—

"—It's been a life of wild ups and downs, but... it wasn't so bad."

"—!!"

That even his last words couldn't come out with proper conviction, and he ended up getting scolded by his own beloved dragon for it — well, that was a fittingly undignified end for a man like him.

In that instant, driving her dragon claws into the ground, the body that had been lying collapsed sprang up with force. The red-haired man nearby let out a yelp of "Whoa!?" and fell on his backside, but she paid it no mind.
More than such trifling things, right now—.

"Balleroy——!"

The shock of the blow she'd taken was too great; she couldn't possibly stand. But she didn't need to stand. What was needed wasn't the strength to stand, but the power of a being from the heavens above.
Trembling her long whiskers, filling her sharp eyes with power, she focused her consciousness on the sky directly above the Crystal Palace. A radiance stretching straight upward traced a line of green light across the sky. At the very tip of that line of light, still climbing, was the person precious to her.

She forgave his intent, his resolve, his determination, his selfishness.

"——!"

Power surging from her massive body reached into the heavens, and clouds began to swirl in the sky above the Crystal Palace. Taking the green light in within the swirl, the clouds rapidly thickened, and the whirl's rotation accelerated.
And so the 'Cloud Dragon' Mezoreia — Madelyn — wrung out every last bit of her strength.

"—Ah."

In that instant, the world flashed white all at once, and the swirling clouds were torn apart from within.
What that meant, what had been lost from the world, what had drawn away from her — she understood it clearly, understood it with an almost unbearable pain.

"Ah, aah, aaah, aaaaaaah——!!"

Looking up at the sky where the clouds had scattered to nothing, thinking of the beloved who left not even a trace behind, with the 'Cloud Dragon's great mouth and thick neck, Madelyn cried out without restraint.

—The dragon maiden, heartbroken in love, kept crying beneath a hatefully clear sky.
