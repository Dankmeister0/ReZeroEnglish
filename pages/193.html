Arc 4 Chapter 27: "Whisper"

—To Subaru, it was a furious voice that stirred an unpleasant, overwhelming sense of déjà vu.

"The witch's... stench..."

This was the second time Subaru had found himself on the receiving end of hostility for that very reason. Garfiel could smell something Subaru himself could never detect, and he turned on Subaru with a look of hatred fit for a man staring down his parents' killer. Subaru already knew the sharpness of that gaze, the torrent of that hostility, all too well.

The witch's foul stench. The lingering scent of a sinner. One who had been captivated by the Witch.

She'd said those very words while cursing him, once — and their relationship had soured so badly it had cost him his life.

"The hell's with that dumbstruck mug? Struck a nerve and now you can't even talk, huh?"

With his throat closed up by shock and confusion, Subaru could only stand there while Garfiel kept hurling out his still-unspent fury. His hands hung limp at his sides, but his guard hadn't dropped for a second. The way his eyes tracked Subaru's every twitch banished, far out of reach, any hope for the small sense of closeness that had genuinely existed between them until now.

"That, um... this witch's stench you're talking about..."

"Hah?"

"This stink coming off my body — it started after I left the tomb. After the Trial. That's what you're saying, right?"

"...Yeah. Wasn't even worth noticin' before, but the second you came back out you're reekin' to high heaven. Dunno what the hell you pulled off in there, but I ain't nice enough to go trustin' some bastard walkin' around stinkin' like that."

At the confirming nod to his question, Subaru let out a short breath and closed his eyes. The witch's foul stench — the fact that it clung to him more strongly now — had begun, almost without a doubt, right after his most recent Return by Death. It was a mystery he'd suspected for some time, one whose answer he'd unconsciously kept avoiding. Only now, belatedly, did Subaru admit to a piece of that answer.

—That it was the Witch who was making Natsuki Subaru Return by Death.

He didn't know why. There shouldn't be any connection between them at all. And yet there was a strange sense of understanding, of acceptance — like standing before a puzzle with just one last piece left to fit, having stalled in place all this time, and finally arriving at an answer whose completed shape he'd somehow already known all along.

"Just what kind of connection could I possibly have with her...? Before I came to this world, I had nothing to do with the supernatural, not one bit. And even since coming here, I've never once come face to face with this famous Witch everybody's on about... if anything, I died about six hours after getting summoned."

The very ability called Return by Death had been granted to Subaru the moment he was summoned to this world. If the Witch had a hand in that, then the Witch must have had a hand in the summoning itself, right from the start. He'd never once sought a clear answer to that question before, but —

"Guess I finally can't keep lookin' away from that one either, huh..."

"The hell are you mutterin' about. If you got time to rack your brain over shit that don't make sense, get your ass back to the cathedral and sleep it off. Don't go makin' more trouble for me."

"...You're just gonna let me go? By your own logic, I'm some suspicious bastard reeking of witch's stench head to toe. It's dead of night, nobody around, just the two of us. Perfect setup for a secret rendezvous or an ambush."

"Hah. I got a short fuse, but I ain't THAT much of a brainless idiot. ...Sure, I could bite your head clean off right now, easy. But what'd that get me? Even I know that if the half-demon's companion turns up dead, it's just gonna cause more trouble than it's worth."

Unable to read Garfiel's intent, Subaru tilted his head, and Garfiel went on, "But,"

"—I ain't lettin' you anywhere near the tomb and havin' that stink get any worse. Right now it's just me who's got a nose sharp enough to catch it, but... that old hag stuck in that dead end, or the rest of 'em — could notice any damn time. And worse company than them, too."

"Worse company..."

"You got one or two guesses of your own, don'tcha? That stink of yours ain't somethin' that just started up now. Bastards who catch that scent and come sniffin' around after you."

At Garfiel's words, punctuated by the click of his fangs, Subaru sucked in a breath — there were far too many possibilities that came to mind. Seeing Subaru's reaction, Garfiel snorted through his nose and waved a hand at him as if shooing away a bug.

"So get outta here already. I won't do nothin' to you right now. And long as you keep behavin' yourself from tomorrow on, I won't go bitin' you either. But don't go near the tomb, and don't go stickin' your nose into me or the old hag's business needlessly. Neither of us wants a bad time outta this, right?"

"Mutual non-interference, huh. So that's it, you'll just leave it at that? That's awfully generous of you."

"'Count yourself lucky you stepped on Gringham's tail and lived.' Get lost before I change my mind. If I got a choice in the matter, I don't wanna go gettin' hated by Ram either."

Garfiel invoked the name of the girl he was fond of, using her as leverage while making clear he was prepared to follow through regardless. Subaru could feel that the hostility radiating off him was being held back only by a staggering degree of self-restraint. Subaru still had arguments left, counterpoints, room enough to keep the debate going however he liked, but —

—Pulling back here is the smart move.

Having made that judgment, he let out a breath, dropped his shoulders, and took a step back. With Subaru's retreat making clear he had no intention of pushing forward, Garfiel's posture visibly relaxed. Closing one eye and letting out a long breath through his nose, he plopped himself down right on the single path leading to the tomb and, arms crossed, looked up at Subaru.

"That's how it oughta be. Just don't go tryin' anything stupid. —Startin' today, I'm plantin' myself right here till the Trial's over. Tomorrow, the day after, the day after that — mornin', noon, or night — I ain't lettin' you set one foot past this point. Get that through your thick skull."

"...Take a bath every now and then, so Ram doesn't end up hating you too."

"Before my stink gets worse than yours, go do whatever it takes so Emilia-sama can clear the Trial. —Now get outta here already."

Eyes shut, Garfiel apparently intended, quite seriously, to fall asleep right there on the spot. At a glance, he looked wide open, defenseless. Retreating from here and taking a long detour through the forest might not be entirely impossible as a way to reach the tomb, but —

"Better not."

That, too, was probably just another layer of his vigilance. As long as Subaru stayed in plain sight like this, Garfiel had confined himself to stopping him with words alone. But if Subaru trampled over that consideration, Garfiel wouldn't hold back. Subaru had no vision of victory against an opponent skilled enough to throw a dragon carriage — Patrasche and all — nor any plan for slipping past that beastly sense of smell of his.

"Guess this is the bill comin' due for skippin' that tea party..."

Pressing a hand to his forehead, he belatedly regretted the lost chance he'd had to sit and talk with that Witch. Though, to be fair, he hadn't had any real questions worth asking at the time, so it wasn't something he could really blame himself for.

"At least tonight, there's nothin' I can do about it. Gotta figure out some kind of move..."

Without getting past Garfiel, there was no reaching the tomb. And if Subaru couldn't reach the tomb, that left no path forward except for Emilia to take on the Trial herself. And from Subaru's own experience, there was no way she could clear the Trial in the three days remaining. If he couldn't take some kind of action within those three days —

"The mansion gets attacked by Elsa. And the chance to drive her off gets thrown away, right in front of me."

Even if it amounted to relying on someone else, he'd wanted to talk with Echidna, to search for some way out. But now Garfiel's hand had cut that off. And if Subaru tried to push through the Trial himself without Echidna's input, that too would run straight into Garfiel's obstruction. Thinking it through that far, Subaru realized, with almost embarrassing simplicity, that he was checkmated.

"Hold on, hold on, hold on... isn't this, like, quietly a really bad situation?"

Since he couldn't get past Garfiel through skill, the only way for Subaru to make it to the Trial was to talk his way past him, or use some other method to get him to move from that spot. But doing that was impossible for Subaru alone, which meant —

"Even if I try to round up allies... given the situation, neither Ram nor Otto is on my side."

Both of them, having weighed the Trial against the Royal Selection, had already judged that the best course was for Emilia to push through this on her own. Of course, if they watched her wear herself down over the next two days, they might change their minds, but —

"By then it'll be too late for the attack. Is there any way... to make it work?"

There was a gap in timing between when the people around them would start to realize how difficult Emilia's Trial really was, and the sense of crisis Subaru already carried. If Subaru pushed too insistently for his own involvement in the Trial, it might well be taken as a sign that Subaru lacked faith in Emilia. Just imagining how Emilia would feel about that was enough to send a gouging pain through his chest. It wasn't that he didn't believe in her. If anything, he was certain that, given time, she would surely fulfill the role entrusted to her.

—Can you still say that, even after hearing the karma she carries, far too heavy for anyone to bear?

"————"

At the whisper from within himself — so dark, so low — Subaru found his feet stopping. Every now and then, a whisper like this would reach him. A voice from some deeply shadowed part of himself, sneering from behind at the foolishness of reaching for an ideal.

"The Trial keeps eating away at that girl. And still, for the sake of everyone's expectations and her own wishes, she keeps trying to move forward even as she gets hurt. Isn't that right?"

—Do you really, honestly believe pushing forward heedless of the wounds is what gets you through it?

Enduring the pain, enduring the tears, enduring every complaint, and just walking on and on — do you really think that a path will surely open someday, that the wish will surely come true?

—There are wounds that never needed bearing. Pasts that never needed facing. Pasts that never needed atoning for.

"It's because she feels guilty, because she thinks she has to do something about it, that she's looking back at her past at all — and suffering for it... isn't that so?"

—But is now really the time for it? Isn't it simply the wrong time, nothing more?

Is the past really something one is obligated to face? Is that truly so? Must a sin committed be atoned for? Should atonement be something forced upon someone? A past that she — that Emilia — had refused to let anyone see, had rejected. A past that, if not for some Trial like this, Subaru would surely never have thought to force open in the first place.

Perhaps, eventually, given time, there might have come a chance to swallow it down and overcome it. But is it really now? Is this truly the right time? Driven by the obsessive compulsion that something must be done, can an answer reached in that state really be something worth holding one's head up for?

"At least I think it was good that I faced my own past. However much I know it might just be self-satisfaction, the fact that I'm standing here now, having somehow gotten past it — that's the answer."

—But that's only because you were ready to face your past, isn't it?

There had been a girl who told him she loved him — affirmed him, even the self he'd hated so much. Because she'd been there, because of her, Subaru had been able to bare his pathetic self before his parents, to expose the ugliest parts of himself, and still lift his head and say goodbye.

—Does Emilia have that much readiness right now?

Having touched just a fragment of the weight of the past she carried, how much strength had all the words and actions Subaru had given her up to now actually granted her? With nothing but a shallow outlook on life, a scrap of effort, and unfounded declarations of love, how much could he really have supported her back?

"...What should I even be doing for you, I wonder."

He loved Emilia. He was in love with her. He wanted to go on loving her. He wanted her to like him too. He wanted to be loved. He wanted her to keep on loving him — that too, he wished for.

So he wanted to do things that would make her happy. He wanted to be of help to her. If she was suffering, in pain, sorrowful, he wanted to take that burden in her place. Even if he couldn't do that, even if it wasn't something he was allowed, he wanted at least to be a support for her.

—He wanted to do for Emilia what Rem, the girl who had gotten him back on his feet, had done for him. Just as Rem had loved him with her whole heart and soul, Subaru wanted to support Emilia the same way. Only once he could do that, he thought, would he truly earn the right to make good on what he'd sworn to Rem. So then, what Subaru needed to do right now was —

"Give her the time she needs to stand on her own, the resolve to see something through, everything it takes to make that possible... somehow make all of that happen for her, huh."

Saying it aloud, clenching his fist, Subaru looked once more at what he needed to do, and a breath tinged with a faint smile slipped out of his mouth. What do you know — what he had to do hadn't changed one bit after all.

"Give it everything I've got, for her sake — put into words, that's all this long stretch of agonizing amounts to. Well, guess I needed to go through it, just to really understand what I gotta do."

For now: the problems that had to be solved, and the obstacles that came with them. And the breakthrough — full of surprise and originality — that would clear them. Time to start planning all of that. Time wouldn't wait for him. It was limited. And he couldn't afford to panic and rush to the wrong conclusion, either — there had already been far too many things he'd overlooked that way before.

"Hangin' my head over how bad things are ain't gonna make 'em any better. And the fact that things only get worse the longer time drags on — that's somethin' I've felt way too damn keenly already."

Letting things ride and hoping time alone would smooth it all over — that pattern, at least, was absolutely never going to happen. That was just how brutally unforgiving this world's stance was toward Subaru — which was exactly why there was something worth struggling for, worth thrashing against, worth standing up to.

"The situation's the worst. Time's not gonna wait. And same as ever, it's nothin' but one unknown after another. But —"

that was just how it always was, just how it was for everyone, as a matter of course. And still, simply being given the chance to try again put him miles and miles ahead of where he could have been.

"Believing that Natsuki Subaru's courage will save Emilia——!"

Let's see if we can't somehow, somehow fight our way through this — one more time.
