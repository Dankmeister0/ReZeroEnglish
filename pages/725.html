Arc 9 Chapter 29: "The Lowest Seat"

——A frenzied wind and earth, forces of the utmost extreme clashing against one another — truly, a fresh verse of legend now being spun.

The battle between the "Sword Devil" and the "Divine Dragon," a clash beyond human comprehension — it had begun, and in the span of a single blink, that fleeting instant alone, Heinkel had tumbled from the dragon's back, flung out of the legend.

"——"

During the descent from above the clouds, the Divine Dragon had used the wind to hold him steady, but the moment the clash with the Sword Devil began, that consideration — no, that composure — vanished from the dragon, and he was forced to fend for himself. That effort bore no fruit, and it was Heinkel who was flung from the stage early on. Unable even to simply cling on, he might have pitied himself — miserable, pathetic, rolling across the ground. But even that luxury of self-reproach was not permitted to Heinkel now.

"——"

The battle that had begun at the Schult villa was already far behind them, the stage having expanded into the noble district, and the extraordinary clash between man and dragon continued on, accompanied by a surreal, daydream-like unreality. — Heinkel could not tear his eyes away from it.

"——"

In his heart, he wanted to look away. Not just look away — he wanted to turn his back and flee. If even that were impossible, he wanted to clutch his head, curl into a ball, cover his ears, and reject it all. But he couldn't. He couldn't do that. — With Wilhelm van Astrea wielding his sword before him, how could Heinkel Astrea possibly avert his eyes?

"——"

The aged Sword Devil threw his entire body into motion, challenging the colossal bulk of the Divine Dragon — dozens of times his size — dodging countless fatal strikes, and driving in innumerable sword strokes that scraped at the dragon's scales. His footwork, his leaps, his flashing blade, his parries, his posture, his shifts in weight, the way he drew the eye, his application of technique, his use of the terrain — the ultimate form of a swordsman, one who marshaled every last element to add force to his blade. Without a doubt, the Wilhelm now trading sword strokes with a dragon was the strongest swordsman Heinkel had ever known.

Not for a single instant could he tear his eyes from the Sword Devil's masterful technique. He held his breath, forgot to blink, so captivated by the flash of the blade that even the beat of his own heart felt like an intrusion. It was nothing so elegant or refined as a "sword dance." The Sword Devil's very being — seeking to carve into his opponent's life from any angle — was something far more raw, a passion akin to a beast's instinct. That passion became karma, and karma was sublimated into skill, arriving at a legend that could contend with the Divine Dragon.

"——"

— In Wilhelm's form as he clashed with the Divine Dragon, Heinkel glimpsed countless battlefields.

In Watergate City, crossing swords with a young Theresia van Astrea. On the Lifaus Plains, swinging his blade against the mighty White Whale, the enemy who took his wife's life. Clashing blades with the three-headed Blight Dragon that ruled the skies over the merchant city, and severing its long necks. Also in the merchant city, clashing with Kurgan, the Empire's strongest "Eight Arms," and carving his name into history. At Royal Lugunica Castle, wresting the sword from Theresia van Astrea, who had been the Sword Saint. In the Demi-Human War, cutting down countless foes, blood-soaked as he came to be known as the "Sword Devil."

And in countless other battlefields besides these, the figure of the finest wielder of the sword, one who had led the way to victory by blade alone — that was the figure Heinkel glimpsed within Wilhelm now. And at the same time, he understood. — At this rate, the Divine Dragon would lose.

"No good."

Squeezed out as though wrung from him, the words spilled out, and Heinkel — who had fallen to both knees — rose to his feet. His hand confirmed the presence of the sword at his hip, and that certainty drove him forward. The Divine Dragon would lose. Because his opponent was the Sword Devil, who had resolved to win.

"——"

Sad to say, the upper limits of the Sword Devil's and the Divine Dragon's strength were beyond Heinkel's sight. Just as one standing on the ground cannot confirm the sights above the clouds, even if a scene far beyond imagining spread out there, it was not something he could ever behold. So he had no choice but to summon his own poor imagination and picture it for himself. — And in the meager world of Heinkel Astrea's imagination, the Divine Dragon fell to the Sword Devil's blade.

As a premise, this battle overwhelmingly favored the Divine Dragon. Even to Heinkel's eyes, the Sword Devil's blade could not pierce the Divine Dragon's scales; no matter how many blows he drove home, he could land no decisive strike. Against that, every single one of the Divine Dragon's attacks was lethal — a graze just a few millimeters deep would be enough to take the Sword Devil's life and leave his corpse exposed. Heinkel understood that premise too. — And yet, his conviction that the Sword Devil would win did not waver.

It wasn't a matter of logic. By logic or reason alone, no one could surpass a Sword Saint. Which meant that the strength of the Sword Devil, who had once defeated a Sword Saint, lay on the far side of logic and reason. Because the Sword Devil possessed the strength to make his blade's tip reach that far side—

"—No good."

That unshakable, worst-case imagining was one Heinkel's steps could not accept. If the Sword Devil won here against the Divine Dragon, he would next challenge the Sword Saint. And just as he had defeated the previous Sword Saint, he would win against the current one as well — against Reinhard.

That was no good. That alone, absolutely no good. For that battle to even come to pass there were plenty of obstacles in the way — the presence of the Witch of Envy, whom Aldebaran was using by some means or another, among them — but even setting all of that aside, it was no good.

No good. It was no good. No good, and because it was no good, it was absolutely no good. Why was it no good — there was no need to even explain how no good it was, it was simply no good. No good is no good, nothing but no good. No good. Because it's no good. What was no good, was no good. No good is no good, and no good it is. With that clear, unclouded blade of the Sword Devil's, Louanna Astrea could not be saved. She couldn't be saved — and so—

"—Heinkel."

Before him stood the Sword Devil's back, unmoving. The muffled voice came from beyond that back, blocked by bloody foam, a tone that sounded almost drowning — and yet Heinkel knew, without any mistake, that it had called his name. Knowing this, he gripped the hilt of his sword, restraining the trembling of both arms with every ounce of self-control he had.

"...Your way — it won't bring Louanna back."

Debris from collapsing buildings had struck his forehead, and blood ran down. He had been blown off his feet again and again by shockwaves like blast winds, and once been pinned beneath a falling pillar. Half his body had been scorched by the aftermath of the dragon's breath as it was cleaved apart by a sword-flash, and dragging the agony of burned blood, bone, and flesh behind him, he had still walked on. Filthy from head to toe with dust and blood beyond any further degree, he walked, and walked, and walked — until Heinkel stood behind Wilhelm. — Stood there, and with the drawn "Astrea," ran his own father through from behind.

"——"

Through the blade that pierced him, he felt Wilhelm try to turn around. To stop that, Heinkel pressed force into his hand, making him aware of the depth of the wound, blocking him from turning. He could not look at Wilhelm's face now. If he saw himself reflected in those eyes, then now as much as in the past, Heinkel would be rendered speechless. So he would not let him turn around. Absolutely not.

He must not let him turn, must not let him speak. If he let the Sword Devil say he would do it, that he would accomplish it, then that future would be pried open by the sword. That forced-open future might see every dark cloud cut away, might reveal a long-closed patch of clear sky peeking through at last. — But Louanna would not be there in it.

The Sword Devil's blade could not wake Louanna. And without her, Heinkel — Heinkel and all those with him — could not be saved.

"It's not you... not you, Father. It's me. — This is something I have to do."

He wanted to curse himself for how the words he'd wrung out came out weak, sob-choked. Clench your teeth, you failure. Put strength into your voice, you coward. Do you have time to hang your head, you spineless wretch. Even with this hand, this sword, stained with his father's blood, his teeth chattered, heat welled behind his eyes, his whole gut churned, and he — a good-for-nothing who couldn't fully kill the urge to spill everything out — Boasting that he'd made up his mind — just when would he manage to keep fooling this wavering self of his, you sniveling coward.

"—You utter fool."

Heinkel, spurring himself on backwards with an endless stream of self-abuse. And into that same Heinkel's ears came a reproach that was not his own — yet one he could only agree with. A voice not drowning in blood — at that, his cheeks stiffened, and an instant later Heinkel's eyes went wide. — Before him, he found himself locking eyes with Wilhelm, the very man he had been trying to keep from turning around.

"—Ah."

Looking at his own hands, the sword was still in the same place. And yet Wilhelm had managed to turn — because he had twisted his body and forced the sword piercing his torso out by main strength. Naturally, pulling a sword from a wound makes it bleed. Force it, and the wound widens further. But the Sword Devil pulled the blade free anyway. Because without doing so, he could not have turned around.

"...Heinkel."

Wilhelm called out again to Heinkel, who had been left speechless by that reckless act. Wilhelm's throat worked as he swallowed down the blood rising up, keeping his voice from clouding. But his body, already wounded all over from his battles with Yae and with the Divine Dragon, and now carrying the deep wound Heinkel had dealt him, had already lost blood to a fatal degree. As might be expected, having lost the support of the sword that had been piercing his body, Wilhelm's knees gave way and he collapsed.

"O-old... man...... Fa- Father......"

Reflexively, Heinkel's hand shot out to catch the collapsing body. But instead, that outstretched hand was seized, and he was pulled in, unexpectedly. Heinkel's cheeks twitched at physical strength that seemed impossible for someone bleeding out, someone at death's door — and Wilhelm, with eyes that had lost none of their power, said—

"Wake up... already......"

"——"

At that plea, a dark heat kindled in Heinkel's heart, which had begun to shrink back. Was he going to lecture him not to dream? That waking Louanna was a fairy tale, or that even if she woke, did Heinkel think she would be happy about it? — He didn't need to be told that; he already knew there was no way Louanna would be pleased.

Not a single thing Heinkel had done up to this day would ever be approved of by Louanna. Despised, given up on, her affection twisted into hatred — it was unavoidable that Heinkel's name would be etched into her memory as the most abhorrent man in this world. But that was fine. Anger, hatred, contempt — any of it was fine. If Louanna would wake from her slumber, let the world reflect in those blue eyes of hers, and sing out her life with her own lips — then that alone was enough.

He would not dream a convenient dream. Heinkel would live, and die, in this nightmare-like — and yet not-a-nightmare — reality. And for that—

"—No."

But against the headache that surged back along with his dark resolve, Wilhelm called a halt. At that, Heinkel held his breath, staring hard at Wilhelm, wondering what was happening. Wilhelm, who still would not look away from his failure of a son, moved his blood-soaked lips. Heinkel had seen that expression before. A bad memory, from the past. — The anguished face Wilhelm had worn when he told them that Theresia — his mother — had not returned from the battle with the White Whale.

"Wake up, Heinkel... You......"

It was, as far as Heinkel knew, the face of the Sword Devil Wilhelm at his most vulnerable—

"No matter whose hatred you earn, Reinhard will never abandon the seat of Sword Saint...!"

— Just as he had back then, he loosed the words that would wound Heinkel's heart most deeply.
