Arc 4 Chapter 37: "My First Killing Intent"

The roar of a muddy flood, like a torrent, filled his ears. A violent rush of water. A cascade falling from above to below, obeying gravity, obeying the current, throwing up violent spray. As his brain was rattled by the endless roar echoing at his ears, or perhaps inside his skull, Subaru's consciousness was led from oblivion back into waking. Light came into view, and then—

"—Ah, hff."

There was a choking sensation in his throat, the rhythm of his breathing thrown into disarray, and Subaru gagged. Exhaling and inhaling, the interval that should have been steady and regular grew ambiguous, and his body—having lost track of oxygen—shook as if convulsing. Drooling, Subaru opened his eyes.

"Gafuh, ahaa!"

He lay sprawled on the ground, face-down. Propped on his arms in something like a dogeza, he pressed his right hand to his chest and breathed again and again, trying to soothe his aching lungs. As the pain eased, he spat out the saliva that had had nowhere to go, and as his body settled, oxygen finally soaked into his brain, and he let out a long breath. And then—he remembered.

"Uaah, aaah!?"

He remembered the sensation of a cavity opening in his own chest, the feeling of loss as if everything inside his body had drained out through it. He touched his own chest with the palm still pressed there, confirmed there was no hollow, no cause for that sense of loss, and the stiffness gripping his entire body finally eased. Tasting a sensation like his limbs going numb with shock, Subaru rubbed his forehead against the ground to feel the friction, drawing pain from the abrasion as proof that his flesh truly still existed.

"What… was… at the end……"

There had certainly been a sensation of ending—of blood draining from his body as it lay collapsed on the ground, of his Soul being sucked out through that same hole. But that was not the true source of the loss eating away at Subaru's body. The real terror had come just after that, in the fleeting embers of life remaining between reaching death and being led into it. Even with his consciousness hazy and his memory indistinct, that much he remembered with perfect clarity.

—That something, some unknowable something, had devoured him.

"I've died by slashing, by beating, by freezing, by falling… but being eaten, at the end, by something… that's a first."

Saying it aloud, he became newly conscious of what had happened to his body at the very end, and a chill ran through him. The direct cause of death had, in fact, been blood loss from the hollow in his chest—he had no intention of belittling that "death" for its own sake. But he had genuinely tasted a glimpse of a possibility beyond that "death." Did the sensation of his own body being devoured by something carry that much sense of loss with it? Subaru, who had actual experience losing fingers and a leg, felt a revulsion here that stood apart even from those—

"Fingers……!?"

Thinking that far, Subaru abruptly grew furious at just how poor the circulation of blood to his own head apparently was. There was no longer any room for doubt that a Return by Death had occurred. He had sustained a wound that could not possibly have been survived, tasted the reality of "death," and here he now was. There was probably no one in this world with a deeper understanding of "death" than Natsuki Subaru. He had died, and returned—that much was certain. What was uncertain was which point in time the dead Subaru had returned to.

If, by some chance, the restart point were somewhere where everything had already gone past the point of no return, then where would his resolve, his vows, have anywhere left to go—

"Ah……"

He turned his bloodshot eyes about, frantically trying to confirm his location and the current time. But what stopped him mid-scramble, face pale, was the sensation in his fingers when he roughly wiped away the sweat trickling from his brow with his hand. —The three fingers he should have lost were, without question, there on his right palm.

"Fingers… there, which means……"

He raised his right arm as if to double-check, letting his eyes travel down from fingers to elbow. He examined it minutely, past the wrist, through the elbow, up to the upper arm, and found not so much as a scar, let alone a missing part. Though the pale scar from the Witchbeast incident still remained there even now—but that was a separate matter from this. Having confirmed his arm was intact, Subaru next touched his shoulder and hip—the places Elsa's throw had struck—to check. There was no tautness of skin there either, and with that Subaru at least confirmed the fact that he had returned to before his encounter with Elsa, and very nearly collapsed with relief.

"For… for now, at least……"

By some small mercy amid misfortune, he had at least been spared having despair heaped on top of dying. Sagging with relief at that fact, Subaru lowered his eyes and gave thanks for his own bad-luck-turned-good-luck. Then, glancing sideways, he noticed something.

—In the corner of the dim room, there was Emilia, writhing in what looked like agony.

"Emili…a"

He moved to rush to her side on reflex, and it was only then that Subaru realized the place where he and she were was a musty, dim underground chamber. And thinking back, there was only one instance he could recall of the two of them having spent time alone together in such a place. In other words—

"Restart point—unchanged……!"

The tomb, right after clearing the Trial—that was where Subaru, having met his death this time, had been pulled back to: a do-over scene where he'd gained nothing yet, but likewise lost nothing.

—There was surely still some means left to somehow set everything right.

Having confirmed where he had returned to, the thought that crossed Subaru's mind was a positive one, almost unbelievably so given that he had just been trembling at his own final moment mere seconds ago.

It was currently the night of the second day since arriving at the Sanctuary. Factoring in the information from the first loop and the previous one, he sorted through the conditions and events, searching for a way to solve things. It was practically routine at this point—the same old bleak business, but the part where he first thinks it's hopeless, the part where he clutches his head over how impossibly stuck he is, all of it included—it was, without question, business as usual.

"But this time it's not going to be as simple as it's been before."

For one thing, it seemed he still hadn't grasped the full scope of the loop surrounding him. And against the threat that was so plainly, obviously clear, he still hadn't found so much as a thread leading to an effective solution. At present, the clearest threat, and one against which he had no means of countering with pure combat strength, was Elsa. Given that underhanded countermeasures wouldn't work against her, her troublesomeness might well exceed even Petelgeuse's. Dealing with her assault on Roswaal Manor remained, without question, still the top priority issue. But it seemed the problem wouldn't be resolved by that alone—

"Last time, at the end… why was the Sanctuary empty……"

Being transported to the Sanctuary by Beatrice's own hand was inexplicable enough, but the mystery of the deserted Sanctuary afterward surpassed even that. He had run all through it, calling out as he went, and gotten zero response. And then there was the final calamity that had struck Subaru as he headed to the tomb seeking answers.

Even as a cavity had been opened in his own chest, Subaru had had no idea whatsoever what had inflicted that wound before he'd been forced to die. Even now, recalling the wound so vivid it seemed fresh, all that resurfaced was pain and terror—nothing he could glean from it. What in the world had happened at the Sanctuary in that moment? What had happened to Subaru's body? What had Beatrice been scheming? And Emilia—

"……No way."

Thinking that far, Subaru's face stiffened in shock at the contradiction between his own thoughts and his own actions.

Organizing the situation up to now—important. Drafting future goals and a plan to achieve them—also important, surely. Gathering up scattered information, giving it shape, using it as a stepping stone toward the future he sought—that too was surely something to prioritize.

"――――"

But was that something he ought to be doing while, right in front of him, Emilia fought against her present nightmare, neglected?

"I… I'm……"

Emilia, undergoing the Trial, tormented by her current suffering. Her past assailing her, the cross she bore battering her body, her Soul, mercilessly under its weight. That time of agony would go on and on, and end without her gaining any peace from it whatsoever.

Subaru knew that. He knew how much sorrow it would bring her, how much it would grind her down, how much it would weaken her heart. It was because he couldn't bear to watch that, that he had even resolved to take on the Trial in her place. He had racked his brain trying to root out every obstacle, to smooth the path she would walk.

That was how it was supposed to be—and yet here Subaru was, watching her agony now, feeling relieved. Relieved that the time he'd returned to was one where she was already suffering. And with the callousness that came from already knowing how that agony would end, he had prioritized his own thoughts over her wellbeing.

The instant he understood that about himself, Subaru became aware that he had sunk into something hideously ugly. The foolishness of standing before someone precious to him, knowing she was gasping under unbearable suffering, and yet averting his eyes from that hardship to pursue his own self-interest. To Subaru, that was nothing less than a loathsome, contemptible weakness.

"Anyway……"

There was no time to be tormented by guilt or by the contradictions in his own heart. He had to wake Emilia as soon as possible and get out of this place. He could sort out his thoughts outside just as well. There was no reason to prolong her suffering. And besides—

"Now that it's come to this, there's someone I can't afford not to get answers out of anymore."

His own overly-lukewarm attitude up to now infuriated him. A person deeply involved in the core of everything happening, and yet he'd kept things so vague, let it all slide by. The result of that had been the massacre at the manor, and the unreasonable, senseless death here at the Sanctuary. If the future that awaited him, should Subaru remain small like this, were to take that shape—

"Whatever it takes, I'll repaint it."

Saying that, he reached out to wake Emilia. Not even Subaru himself noticed that his own profile, as he said it, was twisted with an uncontainable fury.

"—What do you know, Roswaal, and how much of it?"

Roswaal, reclining on the bed, narrowed his eyes at the sight of Subaru, who threw open the door and said exactly that the moment he entered. Watching himself reflected in those mismatched eyes, Subaru strode into the room without a shred of restraint, slammed the door shut, and bluntly expressed his current state of mind.

—After tending to Emilia once she'd returned from the Trial, Subaru had carried her out of the tomb and headed to Ryuzu's house to let her rest. There, he'd left Ram in charge of caring for Emilia, declared he'd spend the time until she woke elsewhere, and come here, to the building where Roswaal was recuperating. Garfiel silently glaring at him the whole way as he left the house had been a source of unease, but fortunately the boy hadn't tried anything along the way, and Subaru had made it here safely. Though for that matter, all such wariness had evaporated the instant he laid eyes on Roswaal like this anyway.

"Hmm."

Looking up at the far-from-calm Subaru, Roswaal let out one deeply feeling sigh. Then he pointed a raised finger at Subaru in front of him, wagging its tip as he spoke.

"You're mu-u-uch angrier now than you were a moment ago. A go-o-ood sign."

"Don't clown around. And I don't have the mental room right now to play along with your jokes or your nonsense. I'm not above using force if I have to. That's the resolve I've got."

Speaking almost like he was baring his teeth at Roswaal's determinedly relaxed posture, Subaru came to stand right beside the bed and planted a palm on it. Then, looming over the jester from extremely close range,

"I just got back from taking the Trial. —I've got a mountain of things I want to ask."

"……I see. So you took the Trial. I see. I see, I se-e-ee."

By Subaru's felt sense of time, the experience of the Trial itself, of parting ways with his parents, already felt like it had happened days ago. But in real time, that experience had only been a few dozen minutes prior. And this was the third time Subaru had watched Roswaal react in a somewhat inexplicable way to the topic of that Trial. The first time, there had been a fleeting, unprecedented flash of intense emotion. The second time, in that world, he'd seemed to take it somewhat more calmly. Even so, it was a fact that something like a wistful, melancholy sentiment—out of character for him—had shown through. This third time, what reaction would he show? To Subaru, the fury of the first world would be preferable. Even someone like Roswaal, he figured, would let his tongue slip a little more easily while in the grip of anger. But contrary to what Subaru was scheming for, Roswaal etched the faintest hint of a smile at the corner of his mouth, and said,

"We-e-ell then, allow me to ask a question first."

"Ha? What are you talking about? You? A question? ……If you keep clowning around like this, I'm going to get seriously mad and start yelling, you bastard."

"I understa-a-and that your anger is justified. It's with that understanding that I ask my question. If it happens to suit my interests… then there's no rea-a-ason at all for me to withhold my cooperation."

"If I answer that question… no, wait, hold on."

Roswaal, as if proposing something to Subaru, who was choking down his anger. For a moment, Subaru made as if to go along with it, but immediately rejected that judgment. He'd realized he was about to be swept along again by the atmosphere Roswaal created, just as he had every time before. He'd gone along with it, let himself be carried, and had gotten burned every time so far. If he wanted to change how things unfolded, he needed to start changing things right from this point.

"Answering your question is off the table. I'm the one who wants to talk. I go first."

"……My, how hi-i-igh-handed of you."

"I'm not saying I won't answer your questions, but I've got a really bad feeling that if I hand you the first move, things won't go anywhere good. I'm nipping that possibility in the bud."

At Subaru's unwavering stance, Roswaal closed one eye and let out a small breath. Then he held both palms out toward Subaru, pushing the turn over to him with a "go-o-o ahead," and said,

"Feel free to ask whatever you li-i-ike. Indeed, there's no-o-o rule saying I have to be the one to move the conversation forward."

"Your being so understanding all of a sudden is kind of weird, but… whatever, no point digging into it, I'll take it. Here's my question. —What kind of Contract do you have with Beatrice?"

"――――"

The silence that followed was proof enough that the question had caught Roswaal off guard. It was only the slightest thing, but Subaru saw his cheek stiffen, and privately confirmed to himself that his question had struck something critical.

Tracing the path up to the previous loop, newly uncovered facts and newly arisen mysteries had tangled together, and to deal with them he'd judged he needed to prepare, in turn, answers of his own to new facts and mysteries. Among them, questions concerning her—Beatrice—were something he couldn't press anyone but Roswaal about, whether at the Sanctuary or the manor. Above all, his parting exchange with her, and the "Gospel" she'd held in her hands—that was burned into his mind and wouldn't leave. It was something intimately tied to how he'd interact with her going forward. He couldn't leave it vague; he needed to know before he decided. —How he was going to deal with Beatrice this loop, something he could not avoid.

"I want an answer, Roswaal. Don't give me some lame line like 'I won't answer since you didn't answer my question.' I'm hearing this."

Impatient with Roswaal's continued silence, Subaru pressed his demand for a response again. He could feel the anxiety asserting itself in his chest. It was the flip side of a bad premonition, of his own wish that it be proven wrong. In a state of mind where one second of silence felt like a minute, or even ten, as he waited for the answer, Roswaal at last slowly opened his mouth and said,

"—If that question is coming out here, does that mean you've remembered something, I wonder?"

But that wasn't the answer to the question Subaru had wanted—worse, it was a question thrown back on top of his own. Subaru felt irritation at that and clicked his tongue, then waved a hand at Roswaal, saying "shut up," and continued,

"Why are you answering with a question? Even if I let you get away with asking one, you answer mine first. I have zero intention of giving up my turn."

"I see. Then let's do it in the form of trading questions, in order. Your question was 'the Contract between me and Beatrice,' wasn't it. She and I have no Contract. That's all."

"Wha—!?"

Subaru was left speechless, his pace stolen out from under him by the smooth, quick-fire delivery. Roswaal extended a hand toward Subaru, who'd lost his words on reflex, and added a "we-e-ell then,"

"Now it's your turn to answer my question. —Have you remembered something?"

"……Remembered what. I'll tell you now, you and I aren't close enough for things to get across without me saying it outright. Don't think I'll follow a sentence with no subject."

"That response tells me your answer to my question. ……A shame."

Roswaal, having pulled off the trick, lowered his eyes wearily, and said,

"It seems I can't reach you, after all."

"……Reach me with what."

"It's your turn to ask a question. Next time, ask it be-e-etter, so I can't wriggle out of it."

The question left hanging, and irritation at Roswaal's self-aware evasiveness not fading, Subaru calmed his emotions with a deep breath, pressed a finger to his temple, and worked through his thoughts.

"You said you're not in a Contract with Beatrice. Then why is Beatrice staying at your manor? I don't get the relationship between you two."

"That's two questions, and you've been asking about nothing but Beatrice this whole time—what about Emilia-sama? Or do you have a taste for girls with that young an appearance too?"

"I'm not into younger girls, and I've got zero intention of conquering her in a romantic sense. Though in terms of breaking the current impasse, I do think I need to figure her out somehow."

When Subaru thought of Beatrice, there was certainly something that stirred in his heart. But that stirring had a different origin than the one that arose when he thought of Emilia or Rem, and the meaning of that feeling was something Subaru himself didn't understand well. Only, even now, having seen Beatrice holding that "Gospel," Subaru still thought this:

—That he didn't want to admit that the relationship he and Beatrice had built up until now was a fake, exactly as written in some incomprehensible book.

"That's exactly why I need to understand her. And it looks like you're the only one on the inside who's deeply connected to whatever's going on with her. So you're the only one I can ask."

"If you keep getting so worked up trying to scoop up everything that catches your eye like that, won't it interfere when it comes time to choose what's actually important? I'd think that kind of softness only gets in the way of looking, from your true heart, at what matters most."

"I know full well my hands are already full. So her, I'll just carry along by mouth, on the way, as a little something extra. Got a problem with that?"

"No complaints, none at a-a-all. I do think, 'what a cool thing to say,' but I suppose that's fine. —Because the real answer is what happens when it actually comes to that."

Agreeing with Subaru's statement while leaving behind an ominous mutter at the end—Roswaal. At the sharpening of Subaru's gaze in response to those words, he continued with a "we-e-ell then,"

"The reason Beatrice is staying at the manor, was it. Her being at my manor comes from House Mathers' relationship with her—to put it plainly, several generations back, a head of the house had her manage the manor's forbidden library out of goodwill. I've simply carried that arrangement forward in my generation."

"So she's like a hired caretaker. ……Is that different from a Contract?"

"The question format doesn't match the premise, but… we-e-ell, fine. My questions barely matter anymore anyway. You already know Beatrice's true nature is that of a Spirit, don't you?"

Subaru dipped his chin in affirmation of Roswaal's words. He'd never actually seen Beatrice looking distinctly like a Spirit, but her own claims and her presence backed it up. Having confirmed Subaru's nod, Roswaal raised a finger.

"For a Spirit, a Contract with a human carries extremely significant weight. The relationship between Emilia-sama and the Great Spirit is exactly like tha-a-at."

"……Yeah, Emilia had a rough time with all the troublesome promises that come with it. Lately that Great Spirit hasn't been showing his face much either, though."

Things remained unresolved between Subaru and Puck—having been killed by him three times, and their difference of opinion over the sleeping Rem. Since that little cat had vanished into a state like a spiriting-away before those things could be resolved, Subaru's feelings toward him remained fixed in a difficult place.

"Setting aside the Great Spirit's difficult temperament, Beatrice is no exception either. That child is in something of a cooperative relationship with me, but that's ultimately nothing more than a non-interference pact based on our interests happening to align. It's ha-a-ardly likely she'll lend a hand for my purposes, and the reverse holds too."

"Okay, I get that you and Bea-tan are friendly on the surface but indifferent underneath, but that's not connecting to the content of the Contract."

"Oh, my apologies. The Contract relationship is a separate matter. Being a Spirit, Beatrice places great importance on Contracts. If she were ever to speak of a Contract, that would be another, quite la-a-arge problem in itself. That child, after all, is still bound by a Contract from four hundred years ago."

At the too-important-to-ignore remark, Subaru leaned forward, closing the distance to Roswaal and raising his voice with "That's it!"

"That Contract from four hundred years ago—I want the details."

"There's no Spirit alive who'd blabber about the terms of a Contract like that. There's no way any of the people involved back then are still around, so unless Beatrice herself speaks up, there's no wa-a-ay to know."

"Damn it, useless! If I knew what that four-hundred-year-old contract was……"

Wouldn't that explain why that girl was always alone, shut up in that room, making herself small?

"Only, there is one thing I can say."

"—?"

"Beatrice is bound by a Contract from four hundred years ago. It's ne-e-early impossible that she, already under a Contract, would layer a new Contract on top of it. If you want to pull that child out of that place, you'd have to start by getting her to break that Contract."

"Break… the Contract?"

"Or fulfill it, that works too. Given how likely it is that whoever she made the Contract with is already gone, breaking it would be fa-a-ar the wiser path, don't you think?"

The miracle of Roswaal actually offering constructive follow-up to Subaru's line of thinking. Subaru, who'd been eyeing him with suspicion, gradually took on the look of someone with scales falling from his eyes.

"—Did I say even one word about wanting to get Beatrice out of there?"

Subaru's voice was low, his eyes sharp as he pierced the Roswaal right in front of him. His hand remained on the bed, fingers tapping the sheet as if counting time. Dropping his gaze to that gesture, Roswaal closed one eye, reflecting Subaru in his yellow one.

"You really are—a man who notices exactly the things he's not wa-a-anted to notice."

"What do you—"

"Either way, this is unproductive time for me. Shall we cut the conversation short around he-e-ere?"

"Are you—are you kidding me!?"

Roswaal, a look of disappointment surfacing in his eyes, a complete reversal from moments ago. He let out a sigh with an attitude entirely changed from before, his face unmistakably devoid of motivation.

"I don't think there's anything you could say at this point that would move my heart… but do as you li-i-ike, I suppose."

"What the hell is with that ridiculous attitude of yours!? We're trying to have an important—an important conversation, and this is your attitude!? I still have things I want to ask—"

"Then if you have things you want to ask, just ask them, no-o-o? Though whether I answer properly or not is entirely a matter of my mood at this poi-i-int."

Opposite the increasingly furious Subaru, Roswaal kept losing waves of emotion. Facing Subaru, whose face was reddening, he tilted his head, stroking his own indigo hair.

"Aren't you going to ask a question?"

"—Tch. Fine. I understand that Beatrice is stuck at the manor because she's bound by a Contract. I'm done with the details of that for now. What I want to ask is something else. That black book she was holding……tell me what that was."

"Oh-o-oh, so you saw it. What did you think? What do you suppose it was?"

"Don't answer with a question. —I thought it looked… like the book the Witch Cult's people had."

Subaru's halting words were an expression of the feeling he wanted denied. But listening to it, Roswaal wore the expression of someone choking back a yawn.

"The 'Gospel' the Witch Cult's followers carry. A magic tome that channels the Witch's will and describes the path to the future the holder desires. We-e-ell, setting aside the fact that it's directional, it's a fairly labor-intensive prophecy book, more or less."

"—! You know about it?"

"It's not so much a rarity, you know. Witch Cultists are everywhere, and they're a faction associated with a Witch different from the one their cult worships—for me, managing the Sanctuary, it's not as if we've ha-a-ad zero skirmishes with them."

"D-does it really let you see the future……?"

If knowing the future without having to die were possible, that would be an upgraded version of Subaru's own Return by Death. It wasn't envy, exactly, but if every single Witch Cultist were equipped with something that overpowered, that would be a serious problem. But Roswaal shook his head at the shuddering Subaru.

"It's not so convenient a thing as all that. To begin with, the number of times it can record something varies by believer, and it's not a large number to start with. The content is often ambiguous too, open to a variety of interpretation, apparently. Above all, the 'Gospel' can only be read by its holder—strangely, when someone else reads it, the content simply doesn't register. It only shows an incomplete picture of the future."

"Incomplete……"

He couldn't hide his relief at that piece of information. Though for that matter, if the "Gospel" truly held the power of a prophecy book that genuinely depicted the future, Subaru's victory over Petelgeuse should have been impossible. Thinking of it that way, it made sense that even a Sin Archbishop's "Gospel" hadn't reached that level. But—

"That's a separate matter from this. That book Beatrice was holding……"

"If your question is whether that's the same thing as the 'Gospel' the Witch Cultists hold, the answer is both yes and no."

"Don't dodge the question! This is the important part!"

"I'm not dodging it at a-a-all. Beatrice's is a 'Gospel,' but its roots differ from the Witch Cult's. The Cult's is an incomplete thing, but Beatrice's 'Gospel' is a completed one."

"Completed……?"

"That's right, a completed one. Not a defective product whose content wavers unstably, swayed by an uncertain future."

Subaru, bewildered. But before him, Roswaal's expression had turned somehow radiant. An expression and manner of speaking as if bragging about something. Subaru lost his voice at this transformation too, but the moment he truly lost words for real came right after.

"—!?"

Roswaal brought his right hand, which had been held behind his back, forward—clutched in it was a book with a black binding. At this close range, there was no mistaking it—it was, unmistakably, a "Gospel" itself.

"The only two completed 'Gospels' still in existence. And the ones holding them are me, and Beatrice, just the two of us—that's how it wo-o-orks out."

"――――"

Roswaal, before him, gave a small shake of the book in his hand from side to side. But Subaru had no room to spare for attending to that gesture. That Roswaal held the same kind of book the Witch Cult carried—that alone was, without question, astonishing to Subaru. That the book Beatrice had been holding was a "Gospel," and that, inadvertently, his parting conversation with Beatrice had been confirmed as true—that too was part of what struck him with shock. —But what dominated Subaru's mind right now was neither of those things.

"That's… the 'Gospel' that records the future?"

"Indeed it i-i-is. This is the genuine article, the 'Gospel.'"

"You know the future……? Even this, right now, happening like this… is it in that book too?"

"It's recorded, ye-e-es. Though I don't suppose you can read it."

That didn't matter at all. Whether Subaru could read it or not carried no meaning whatsoever at this moment. Only one thing mattered. Only one thing—that was what he had to ask. It was—

"What happens from here… is it written down?"

"Not everything in the world is recorded, but the holder's future is partially made kno-o-own, yes."

"Did you know… this would happen too?"

"Making things unfold exactly as recorded takes a fair amount of effort, you know? I'd like a little praise for my behind-the-scenes efforts, if you don't mi-i-ind."

He couldn't stop his voice from shaking. The cause of that shaking was an outpouring of emotion too intense to contain. What that emotion was, which direction it was aimed—he understood it immediately. It was—

"If this was going to happen, and you knew what was going to happen up to now, then……"

"—Hm."

"—Did you let Rem die knowing it, is that what you're saying?"

"Who's Rem, I wo-o-onder?"

"—I'll kill you!! Roswaaaaaaal!!"

In that instant, an anger beyond containing was the only thing driving Subaru. He leapt onto the bed, clamped both hands around the throat of the reclining Roswaal, and squeezed. A grip strength beyond that of an ordinary person, one Subaru had never once shown before, creaked the thin throat, etching a look of agony across the jester's pale face.

"You knew everything, and still you—!!"

If he'd known, if he'd been aware, if he could have averted the tragedy—if Rem could have been spared that fate—

"Was it you who made me let Rem die!!"

An anger beyond all reason, regret bursting forth, transformed into murderous intent toward the man before him and drove Subaru on. Impulse erased reason; emotion, affection, converted into force. And Roswaal, unable even to voice a sound, could only wait, silent, for Subaru to snap his neck—

"—'Whether you shapeshift or wear a disguise, you can't erase the smell of an Ulgarm,' now, huh!!"

—Impact.

Struck from the side by something hard and sharp, Subaru felt the right half of his face get smashed in and went flying. Thrown off the bed without any chance to break his fall, he slammed into the wall and dropped headfirst to the floor after that. His thoughts blew apart in a single blow, and his whole body wouldn't so much as twitch. Blood ran from his ears and nose, and his right field of vision went black. His eye might have been crushed.

"—Comin' out of the tomb reeking even worse than before, huh. Figured somethin' was up, kept an eye on you, and turns out I was right, huh, ha!?"

Footsteps. He could feel them, ragged, closing in right beside him. His body wouldn't allow him to crawl. Unable to move forward or back, Subaru was grabbed by the head and hauled up.

"Wonder what a Witch-reekin' bastard like you was tryin' to do—should I ask your body instead, huh. Even a piece of shit like you's still needed in this place. Don't screw around, damn it."

A blond young man. Garfiel. As some string of words, anger and murderous intent mixed together, was hurled at him, Subaru's consciousness began slipping away. Half his head felt—though he couldn't confirm it—crushed. He might die. If this was how he died, well, what an absolutely rotten way to go.

But carrying this murky, tangled mess with him into another Return by Death—could he really keep hoping, could he really still wish to save this place?

"I don't… know, Rem……"

With those words as his last, Subaru's consciousness dropped, snapped off, into the dark.
