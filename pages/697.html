Arc 9 Chapter 3: "Each in Their Own Way, They Mourn You"

"So that's the situation—my little sister is going to become the wife of His Excellency the Emperor-kun. Which means, naturally, we siblings have gone and lurched a great deal closer to the goal we set out to achieve!"

"Goal... is that... the revenge you spoke of, before...?"

"That's right! Revenge upon the world!"

Clapping his hands, eyes shining as he gave his answer—the golden-haired, gentle-faced man that was Flop O'Connell—and across from him, the Shudrak woman Taritta widened her eyes just slightly.

It seemed less the answer itself than the sheer force with which Flop delivered it that had startled her, but her lips soon curved into a smile. That she could laugh like that at a word as ominous-sounding as "revenge" was, perhaps, only to be expected of a formidable woman of the Vollachian Empire's proud People of Shudrak.

And that Flop could take the air surrounding such a formidable woman and dye it his own color—well, he was no slouch himself.

Watching the exchange between the two of them below from the fortress's connecting walkway, chin propped on her hand against the railing, Ram appraised Flop and Taritta each in turn.

It was a scene she'd only happened upon by chance, but what an interesting encounter to stumble into.

—Ever since joining forces with them during the decisive battle for the capital, Ram had held the Shudrak in high regard.

On the whole, the only people in the Vollachian Empire whose parting Ram might actually regret were them, and Serena, whose position—caught up as she was with Roswaal—remained a delicate one.

Beyond that, she was also grateful to Katya, Rem's friend—

"—Ram. What are you doing in a place like this?"

"Oh, Mizelda. I was just up here enjoying a bird's-eye view of your sister's love life."

"Hoh. Fine taste. Let's see."

So saying, the woman who came to lean against the railing beside Ram was the strong-eyed beauty Mizelda.

Taritta's own elder sister, she narrowed her eyes at the sight of her younger sibling below, conversing with Flop. A trace of concern flickered across her profile, and Ram, too, narrowed her pale pink eyes.

"Worried about your sister? That's not like you."

"That's a bold thing to say of me. Besides, how long have you and I even known each other? Only a few days."

"Do you think it takes time? For Ram, or for you?"

"Ha ha ha. Fair enough."

At Ram's question, delivered with the corner of her mouth curling, Mizelda opened her mouth and laughed heartily. Laughing, she then slowly shook her head.

"'Worried' feels like too weak a word. So let's say—concerned."

"I see. But naturally so—you're Taritta's sister, after all. Ram is always worried about Rem, too. Or, to put it your way, always concerned for Rem."

"But there's no need even to discuss it—Subaru's a fine man. Shame about the eyes, though."

"—On that point, it seems Ram and you will need some time to see eye to eye."

At Ram, her voice dropping slightly, Mizelda laughed again on hearing it. Glancing sidelong at that smile, Ram said "In that case," and lowered her gaze back down below.

"Is it his character you doubt, then? That gentle-looking man's?"

"No, Flop's a fine man too. His features are a touch too soft, perhaps, but a feast for the eyes all the same. Taritta must know that much herself."

It was an answer that muddled character and looks together, the sort of phrasing liable to lose sight of the point entirely. Still, Ram felt she had some sense of what troubled Mizelda.

And it was not something Ram, an outsider, could speak up about—nor, for that matter, something even a blood sister could dictate to the people actually involved.

Quite the case of a sister's heart the younger sister knew nothing of, it seemed.

"I like you more and more, Mizelda."

"Is that so. If you were a man, I'd have made you my husband."

Two elder sisters, each with plenty to say about their younger sister's romance.

And so, standing side by side both physically and in spirit, with Ram and Mizelda looking on from above, the exchange between Flop and Taritta continued.

"Listen, Lady Taritta! I may have said this before, but if I have, I'll say it again all the same!"

Flop raised his outstretched hand toward the sky, opening and closing his palm as though trying to grasp the distant white clouds, or the sun pouring down its light.

"If you're going to talk big about seizing your goals and dreams, then when the chance comes around, you'd better throw yourself at it without hesitation. This time, the unexpected bond that sprang up with His Excellency the Emperor-kun was precisely the sort of golden opportunity worth staking a life on!"

"And so... the marriage between His Excellency and Medium? Does Medium... properly agree to it?"

"Of course! ...Is what I'd like to say, but marriage being what it is, there was quite the row over it, I'll admit! The biggest argument in the history of me and my sister, I daresay! But—"

"But?"

"If I prioritized revenge on the world over the happiness of the family right beside me, I'd have things backward. In that sense, I never meant to propose anything Medium would genuinely refuse."

"——"

At Flop, his eyes softening at the corners, Taritta lowered her long, narrow eyes.

The marriage under discussion, between Vincent Vollachia and Medium O'Connell—that Flop had proposed it and the Emperor had accepted—was common knowledge among those involved.

More than that, the rumor had spread quite widely, reaching even the ears of common townsfolk.

It occurred to Ram that spreading word of Vincent taking an empress was likely a strategy on the part of Berstetz or Serena, or someone of that sort. Though there still lingered an afterglow of victory from having driven back the Great Disaster, the present reports coming in from across the empire were, for the most part, grim ones. But people's hearts needed hope.

A cause for celebration was needed for that—ideally, one that touched every citizen of the empire alike.

"What a shrewd man, and what a shrewd nation."

Ram had no way of knowing just how much Flop had thought through when he made the proposal.

But given that he'd made the decision to push forward at the moment it counted, some measure of calculation must have gone into it. And the same held true for Vincent, who accepted, and for the dignitaries who gave their approval after the fact.

The one small mercy, as Flop said, was that Medium—turned into a pawn of statecraft—did not, in fact, dislike Vincent.

Taking all that into account, Flop, in proposing this match, had shown himself to be a shrewd operator indeed. —Ram rather wished a certain minister of the interior, somewhere, who kept smearing on affected wickedness like medicine and getting by on self-deception, would take a page from his book.

So thinking, as she looked on at the exchange, Ram glanced briefly at the profile beside her.

"Come to think of it, don't you have any thoughts of your own about Emperor Vincent?"

"Foolish question, Ram. Abel's fine looks go without saying. But by the law of the People of Shudrak, it's custom to bring the groom back to the forest. Seeing as I can't bring Abel back with me, that man is nothing more to me than a feast for the eyes."

"I see..."

"No, that's not quite right. —To me, Abel's a comrade-in-arms who's also a feast for the eyes."

"I see."

Arms crossed, at Mizelda's firm declaration, Ram nodded quietly.

With her question thus settled, the two onlookers turned their attention back to the conversation below.

Between the two below—both a touch frustrating to watch—Flop had just declared he hadn't forced anything on Medium, and Taritta, eyes still lowered, murmured, "Flop is amazing."

"Your own goals, and the people around you too—you think it all through properly and act on it. When a moment calls for a decision, you don't hesitate. ...Truly, admirable."

"—? That's, well, a rather funny thing to say, Lady Taritta."

"Eh... Did I say something funny?"

"Yes, you did. Because the reason I could scrape together the courage to make a decision like this in the first place was that I was following your example, Lady Taritta!"

"——"

At Flop, pointing sharply at himself, Taritta's eyes went wide. Whatever the extent of her surprise, her thoughts seemed to have ground to a halt.

As proof of it, she took hold of the finger Flop had thrust before her face and, on pure reflex, twisted it up and pinned him against the wall.

"Ow-ow-ow-ow-ow-ow-ow!!"

"Ah, ahh, I'm sorry! I was just so surprised... it was reflex!"

"N-no, no, no, the fault's mine for being so careless. As expected of a Shudrak chief—your training really is on another level. Seems I need to take a page from more than just your mindset."

"Mindset..."

"Yes, your mindset. That's exactly the thing about you that impressed me most."

Shaking out his twisted arm, Flop smiled at Taritta. But Taritta, on the receiving end of that smile, looked utterly at a loss, with no idea what he could mean.

At that reaction of hers, Flop gazed back with a fond, wry smile.

"When misfortune befell Lady Mizelda's leg, you found yourself unexpectedly having to take up the mantle of chief. It must have troubled you. I imagine it was painful! But you made your decision and took it up with strength. Seeing you like that taught me the worth of not running from an important decision."

"Flop..."

"So I wanted to tell you my thanks. That was one of the two reasons I called you out here like this! —And there's one more reason besides."

Flop held up one finger on his left hand, then one finger on his right. At his words, Taritta's eyes widened, and she murmured haltingly, "One more..."

Taking in Taritta's murmur, Flop lowered his raised fingers with a theatrical flourish.

"Today will never come again. Tomorrow isn't guaranteed to come at all. In this war, I came to realize anew something I'd thought I already understood. I'm grateful to the kind soul who lit a flame in these eyes of mine, to the kind soul who made this moment possible. And—"

"——"

"Lady Taritta, I have a great fondness for the way you carry yourself and the way you think. To put it more plainly—I very much want to be by your side! So, won't you become Taritta O'Connell?"

"—Ah—"

"If you want to get what you're after, you mustn't let the chance slip away. This, without exception, is me putting into practice what I learned from you."

Smiling, one eye closed in a wink—there was no mistaking it. It was a proposal.

For Taritta, it must have felt truly like the sky falling. But at the same time, it must also have been a joy fit to make the whole earth bloom with flowers.

As a fellow woman, Ram, too, felt a wish to applaud the blessing that had befallen her.

But—

"—But... I..."

At the sight of Taritta, murmuring so and tormented by indecision, Ram narrowed her pale pink eyes.

What lay at the root of Taritta's reaction had, curiously enough, already been made clear in the exchange with Mizelda just moments before.

By Shudrak custom, the groom had to be brought back to the settlement in the forest.

But Flop, as the elder brother of Medium, who was to become empress, would surely find his duties and obligations in the capital growing enormously from here on. To abandon them all and leave Medium behind in the capital—that would be nothing short of irresponsible.

Taritta understood this too, and so her hesitation had crushed her joy.

The very thing that had, by his own account, given Flop his courage—her succession as chief of the Shudrak, the very thing that had drawn their hearts together—had become the obstacle keeping the two of them apart.

Just as Taritta, with a fierce, unwavering sense of duty, was about to accept that absurdity—

"Don't agonize over it, Taritta!"

"—Ah, sister?"

At the voice, cutting in like an ambush, Taritta stiffened, eyes going wide.

Until that very moment, she hadn't noticed Ram and Mizelda on the walkway above their heads, and now she met eyes with Mizelda, gripping the railing tight.

At her younger sister's expression, Mizelda let out a small breath, then swung up her right leg—the one that was a wooden prosthetic below the knee—and planted it with a thud on the railing.

And then—

"Weighing the duties of chief against a man and agonizing over it—do you think that's someone fit to be chief?! Through all this fighting, even I learned to fight on one leg! If you're going to be in such a sorry state, then give up being chief altogether!"

"Wh—! W-wait, sister! That's—"

"No, I won't wait! A sister with a pathetic face like yours has no place in the Shudrak!"

Declaring this forcefully, Mizelda lowered her leg from the railing and turned her back. She strode off down the walkway just like that, her mismatched footsteps—one hard, one hollow—falling in an uneven rhythm as she went.

At Mizelda's retreating back, Ram sighed.

"Is that how you give your sister a push, as her elder sister?"

"She's not the sort of woman who needs everything spelled out. Ram, this'll stop being someone else's problem for you soon enough, too."

"How dreadful."

At being told something she had no wish to hear, Ram answered with her cheek twisted in distaste.

And then, thinking it would be uncouth to linger, Ram made to follow after Mizelda's back, when—

"Lady Mizelda! My deepest thanks! Is there anything I can do?"

In place of Taritta, who stood rooted to the spot, Flop called out to the retreating back of Mizelda, who had just made a momentous decision. At the question, Mizelda, without turning around or slowing her step, waved a hand and said,

"Obvious enough. Send a strong, sturdy, good-looking man as envoy to the Shudrak settlement. That'll keep the covenant between the capital and us intact."

"—Ha ha! Understood, Lady Mizelda! No, wait—Sister-in-law-kun!"

Between Mizelda and Flop, a decision had been reached, sailing clean over the head of the person it actually concerned.

And Ram could tell that the one whose head had been sailed over would reach her own decision soon enough.

"—!"

—Because the sound of Taritta's footsteps, kicking off the ground and dashing toward Flop, reached clearly even to Ram's ears as she followed after Mizelda's back.

"Is it really all right to decide something like taking back the chieftainship on your own say-so?"

"Heh. If someone's not satisfied, all they need do is take it from me. But..."

Mizelda cut herself off there, turning her strong-eyed gaze straight ahead. Following that gaze, Ram, too, understood why she'd broken off.

At the end of the walkway, waving their hands, were two Shudrak women—Kuna and Holly. They greeted Mizelda, then jerked their chins toward the walkway below.

"So-o? Did Taritta finally get honest with herself?"

"Mm. Troublesome girl, even if she is my own sister."

"But then agai-in, can't be helped, no-o. She's the chief's sister, after all, no-o."

"For the record, till everyone talks it over, chief's still Taritta... aah, guess it don't matter none. I got no objection to Mizelda goin' back to bein' chief. Rest of the lot probably don't either."

From the knowing smiles Kuna and Holly each returned, Ram caught a glimpse of just how much Taritta loved—and was loved by—her own people.

With that understanding, Ram wrapped her arms around herself and said,

"Even if it was by chance, Ram's efforts as an onlooker paid off."

"Hearin' just that makes you sound just awful, no-o."

"If there weren't no chief, the picture woulda been just as awful. Some story that'd be."

At Ram's one remark, Holly and Kuna came at her in waves. As Ram shrugged beside them, Mizelda's strong gaze softened, just slightly, and she turned her eyes up to the sky, gentle now.

Gazing at a sky so clear it was almost infuriating, she said,

"Ever since she lost Mariuli—her soul sister—Taritta had been shut up inside herself. Just how much that means, the two of you would understand better than I."

"...Yeah, no-o." "Figured, yeah."

"Now the time's come for that to change. —I'll call it a blessing that Taritta won for herself."

A word Ram didn't know had come up, understood only among the three Shudrak.

Presumably it named some bond unrelated to blood ties—the sort of relationship, Ram gathered, that existed between Kuna and Holly themselves. Given how strong the bond between those two was, it wasn't hard to imagine just how great a tie Taritta had lost.

And that what was lost could never truly be filled again—that, Ram knew all too well herself.

"——"

The empire had won. But in exchange, far too much had been lost. Even close to Ram herself, the wounds borne by Rem, Emilia, and Subaru were great ones.

Simply staying by their side and continuing to speak to them could never be a cure-all for wounds like those. It was a cruel, unyielding, unreasonable principle—

"—Thanks be to the vow of our forebears, to the pride of our ancestral spirits."

"—Thanks be."

Mizelda, Kuna, and Holly blessed Taritta, who had found ahead of them the one way to resist it, in the manner of the People of Shudrak.

Following their way, Ram, too, closed her eyes and took part in a fragment of that prayer.

And then she thought:

—Of the sheer unreasonableness that there existed no way to save every heart mourning something lost.

"Thanks be."

So she said, to a vow and pride within herself, different from the Shudrak's own.

△▼△▼△▼△

Clatter, clatter—the wheelchair's wheels turned, sending up their sound.

It was a sound Rem had grown thoroughly used to by now, though here and there it caught and stuttered—the leftover debris from that upheaval, or cracks in the passageway not yet fully repaired.

Worried about letting Katya go through such places alone, Rem had volunteered to push her wheelchair from behind.

"So, it seems protecting His Excellency the Emperor earned him quite the recognition. They say my brother's being given a post as an independent 'General,' guarding His Excellency's side directly."

"Is that so. That brother of yours..."

"You look like you've got something to say. ...No, never mind, don't say it. Whatever you're thinking, I'm probably thinking about the same thing."

Without turning around, gripping the braid hanging over her shoulder, Katya said this, her voice trembling slightly.

Rem hadn't said much, but she thought Katya's guess was probably right. So, in keeping with her request, she chose to keep her thoughts about Jamal to herself.

Whatever the case, he was her own blood brother, sent off despite all her anxiety. That he'd come back safely must have been a relief even to Katya, for all that she wasn't one to admit it plainly.

Yes—that he came back safe, that was what mattered more than anything.

"Priscilla..."

Caught off guard, struck by a wave of sentiment that squeezed her chest the moment she let her guard down, Rem let the name slip out.

In the mind of Rem, her pale blue eyes lowered, there rose the image of a beautiful woman wrapped in vivid red, like a living flame—the "Sun Princess" who had kept Rem by her side for only the briefest span of time.

With her unshakeable way of being, spreading the heat she carried and burning away everything in her path, Priscilla had brought down the curtain on herself with a decisiveness that admitted no one else in, every bit her own.

In that final conversation—one she hadn't thought would be the last—Rem couldn't forget a single word they'd exchanged.

Only—

"A core of diamond, you called it—but I don't understand."

Meeting face-to-face after several days apart—in what turned out to be their final exchange—Priscilla had praised the person Rem had become. And back then, Rem had been able to accept it with her head held high.

But now she no longer understood. Because Priscilla was gone.

"Hey, Rem. Is it really all right for you to be here with me?"

"...Huh?"

Startled, Rem found the wheelchair she was pushing suddenly stopped short. Looking down, she saw Katya gripping the wheels tight, forcing them to a halt.

Doing something like that must have hurt her slender hands terribly.

"Katya—"

"It's fine. Never mind my hands right now. This is about you."

"About me..."

"You don't have to be here with me like this. You've got your sister, and people who knew who you were before, and even that noisy man..."

"—You mustn't."

Katya, who had been staring straight at Rem and speaking faster and faster, cut off with a muffled "mmph." Having interrupted her, Rem shook her head, grateful for the consideration behind those words all the same.

Ram, and Frederica and Petra too, would certainly be a place for Rem to belong. But even they couldn't fill the hole left by Priscilla's absence. They didn't know the days and hours Rem had spent with Priscilla.

And as for the last person Katya had been about to name—Subaru—

"I don't want to lean on him, not as he is now."

"...You mean he's not reliable?"

"That's not it. ...I don't want to rely on him. I hate the idea of it—of just settling in as one of the many people he does that for."

"——"

Speaking almost over Katya's question, Rem surprised even herself a little. Katya, too, had her eyes wide at hearing the answer, but that was only natural.

Rem herself hadn't really understood the shape of her own feelings until she'd put it into words.

But having spoken it aloud, she found herself convinced: ah, so that's what it was.

"It seems like you're always the one who sees the worst parts of me, Katya."

"I don't think that's all there is, and anyway, if we're talking about embarrassing parts, I've got plenty of my own too... but wait, don't make me think about that, don't make me remember it! Thinking about the trouble I cause people just by being alive makes me want to di—makes it hard to breathe!"

Considerate of Rem's feelings, Katya had likely stopped herself from saying "makes me want to die."

At Katya's consideration, slightly imperfect as it was, Rem's mouth eased into the faintest of smiles. Not overly careful, not overly gentle—just saying things the way she did—Katya's presence was a help to her.

Even though she was the one who'd just asked if it was really all right for Rem to be with her.

"Being near you puts me at ease, Katya. You're always quick to make a fuss and yell, so you keep pulling my attention back."

"Is that supposed to be a compliment? Are you trying to compliment me? Because if so, you've got no talent for it."

Having meant it as an honest expression of gratitude, Rem found it rather unfair to be glared at with such a flat look. And so, in that manner, Rem and Katya were making their way through the fortress when—

"Well, well," came someone's voice, and Rem looked up. There stood a gray-haired young man, waving at them with a loose, easy grin.

Rem had no idea who he was, but Katya let out an "Ah,"

"Do you know him?"

"Not that I'd say I know him, but he's a slacker."

"A slacker?"

"Now, now, hold on—being remembered like that stings, I'll have you know. Well, back then it wasn't exactly wrong, close enough to the mark, I suppose?"

Not quite flustered, but unhurried all the same, the young man set about correcting Katya's impression of him. Somewhat wary as he approached, Rem tightened her grip on the wheelchair's handles.

At Rem's reaction, the young man raised both hands and stopped where he was.

"No need to worry. I'm not the sort who'd go and harm anyone. I'm just a pitiful fellow who's lost his one redeeming feature and is slinking off with his tail between his legs."

"Slinking off, huh... You leaving somewhere? Skipping out again? At a time like this, when everyone's struggling and propping each other up?"

"Now, now, that's not it at all. Though I'll agree the situation's the situation, sure."

With a wry smile, the young man looked back—at the fortress city, its reconstruction only just begun—and spread his thin arms wide.

"Up till now, I've had my sights set way too high. From here on, I plan to plant my feet firmly on the ground and live looking squarely at what's right in front of me."

"...I don't get it. What's with the declaration?"

"I'm paying my respects, miss. There was no guarantee I'd get to meet you, but maybe this was one last stylish little gift from the Stars."

At the young man's roundabout way of putting things, Katya kept her puzzled expression. Rem, for her part, hadn't let her guard down, but it seemed certain enough that the man held no malice or hostility toward Katya.

If anything, just as he'd said himself, something like genuine respect for Katya could be felt in him.

Whether that had anything to do with Katya calling him a slacker, though, remained unclear.

"Miss, you saved the Vollachian Empire. That dazzling sunrise, the lives of people going about their business you can see from here—all of it, you wrenched away from 'Providence' itself."

"...Are you making fun of me?"

"No, no, no, not at all, not one bit. I've been moving this way and that in accordance with 'Providence,' but being surprised by His Excellency the Emperor, or by First General Chisha—none of that ever really got to me. So you're the only one who has."

"I really don't get what you mean. Feels less like anything deep and more like you're just holding a grudge because I told you off for slacking. But—"

The young man gave a wry smile at Katya's response, and she broke off there for a moment. Then, mumbling a little, she pointed back at Rem.

"The reason I was there was because this Rem here dragged me along. And besides, the whole reason I even met this girl was at the Chancellor's mansion, and that was because Todd—ah, whatever, anyway!"

Sharply, Katya swung the finger that had been pointing at Rem around to jab it at the young man instead.

And then—

"Being praised in some way I can't make heads or tails of doesn't go to my head. Honestly, calling a woman with bad legs 'amazing' or 'special' just sounds fishy! Like I could do anything on my own! It's all thanks to other people!"

"K-Katya, isn't that a bit much..."

"Huh? I'm having you push my wheelchair right now, aren't I? Let one of these wheels sink into a hole in the floor just once. I'll turn into a pile of bones right there!"

At Katya, raising her voice with such boldness, Rem found herself overwhelmed.

Recognizing all the while that this exquisite balance of self-deprecation and trust in the people around her was the very thing that made Katya's way of thinking so good—

"Fu, ha ha, ha-ha-ha-ha-ha-ha!"

Just as Rem thought so, the young man burst out laughing, doubling over and clutching his stomach. At that reaction, Katya's shoulders jumped, and she stammered, "W-what?" in a fluster, but Rem felt she had some vague sense of why he'd laughed.

Caught between Rem's vague understanding and Katya's utter lack of it, the young man scooped away the tears welling at the corners of his eyes with a finger.

"Haah, glad I got to talk with you. If something like that is what pushed back 'Providence,' well, there's nothing left for me to say."

"I don't really get it, you going and satisfying yourself all on your own like that..."

"Sorry, but that's something you'll have to get someone around you to sort out. —Well then, this is goodbye from me."

"Where to?"

So saying, Rem found herself asking the young man, who'd hoisted a small bundle of belongings from the ground onto his shoulder.

He was a stranger whose name she didn't even know, a young man she had no reason to hold back—yet she still didn't want misfortune on someone who thought so highly of Katya.

At Rem's question, the young man pointed toward Mount Gildray, rising behind the fortress.

"For now, out of the empire. Well then—take care of yourselves."

With just that answer, the young man turned and walked away, and neither Rem nor Katya had any words to call him back. All they could do was wish him well on his way, given how clear and unburdened he looked.

That he could walk on like that, back straight, was thanks to Rem, to Katya, to all the countless others besides—and above all, thanks to her, who had carved open the future with her own dazzling way of living.

"It's all thanks to other people..."

"W-what, you got a problem with that? I mean, sure, I guess I went a little overboard with it, even I know that much...!"

"No. I think it's very like you, and I think it's a good thing. I don't want to mistake myself for someone standing on her own, either."

In response to Katya, who kept glancing over at her, Rem ran a hand over her own leg. Watching the gesture, Katya, eyes lowered, said,

"Guess you can walk normally now too, before you even knew it."

"Apparently the cause was that the mana that's supposed to flow through my body wasn't flowing properly... It might help with your legs too, Katya."

"...I'm fine. I'll have my brother, or someone, push my wheelchair. Besides—"

"Besides?"

"—You'll still be pushing it for me a while longer, won't you?"

At Katya, asking so timidly, Rem's eyes widened just slightly, and then, her gaze softening at once, she nodded. "Yes."

Both Rem and, of course, Katya understood perfectly well that "a while longer" wouldn't be very long at all—and it was with that understanding beneath it that the exchange had passed between them.

Surely, the parting from Katya would come before too long—when Rem returned to the place she'd originally come from, along with Ram and the others who'd come to bring her back, and Subaru, she would surely feel something achingly painful.

But the parting from Katya, and from the people of this land, was one that left behind the hope of meeting again. What had taught her the pain and anguish of a parting without that hope was her—that final—

"Was that kindness? Or was it spite, Priscilla?"

'Rem. Follow your own heart. Your heart is roiling, but those ripples are never unsightly.'

As Rem murmured this, the last words Priscilla had thrown at her flickered through her mind.

A roiling heart—that surely meant the weakness that would always, and forever from now on, rise up like foam within Rem, threatening to shake her calm.

But she didn't want to be the kind of person who, clinging desperately, wished to reject that and push it far away, of all things.

Because that Priscilla—the woman like a flame who had lit Rem's way, however briefly—she who never mistook what was real for anything else—had told her that Rem's "ripples" were never unsightly.

—So please, forgive this heart of mine for roiling at the reality that you have vanished. At least until the day those ripples finally settle, and I can think of you without tears.
