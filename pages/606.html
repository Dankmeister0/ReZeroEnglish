Arc 7 Chapter 101: "Love Is a Flame"

—Amid the battlefields that colored the Battle for the Imperial Capital, two locations stood out as scenes unlike any other.

One was the second peak, dyed white to keep the damage from spreading, where the Cloud Dragon that had come from beyond the sky spread its wings in magnificent splendor.
And the other was the first peak, where the sky burned crimson red — transformed by the power of the girl at its center into an environment so harsh that the survival of every living thing there hung in the balance.

"—————"

Arakiya shimmered like a heat haze, her body dancing up into the red sky.
The world, transformed by Arakiya as she unleashed the full measure of her power as a Spirit Eater, had already become a hell that consumed the lives of anyone without power beyond reason.

"I'm going to get the Princess—"

—I will bring her back, no matter what.

That was Arakiya's greatest reason for standing on this battlefield — indeed, her only reason.
In it there was none of the loyalty or patriotism of an Imperial General, none of the pride or dignity of a Nine Divine General — one of the strongest existences in the land — nor any ulterior motive like racial anger or personal ambition for glory.

The reasons and meaning the other Nine Divine Generals found in battle—
Arakiya possessed none of the lofty justifications that underpinned such strength, and yet she still reigned as the single greatest fighting force on the Imperial Capital's side — such was the existence of Arakiya, the "Two."

But given her origins — the fact that she was a Spirit Eater — that was only natural.

To be required to possess strength, and to be forced to use that strength not for oneself but for another — that was the nature of a Spirit Eater, and Arakiya was no exception.
To begin with, a Spirit Eater was an aberrant creature that could only exist as such by nature.

Spirits existed in every stretch of earth, in every reach of sky.
A Spirit Eater devoured those spirits, absorbed their power, and became one with them — an outrage tantamount to taking nature itself into one's own body, and one that inevitably brought effects both great and small.
Absorb a fire spirit, and body temperature would rise. Absorb a wind spirit, and there was a risk of being torn apart from within. Absorb a water spirit, and the circulation of one's blood could be thrown into jeopardy. Absorb an earth spirit, and one risked fusing with the ground itself, even losing one's human body entirely.

Indeed, many who had aspired to become Spirit Eaters ceased to be human because of such side effects.
Born in ancient times to counter a single Witch who had enthralled all things and commanded even the silent Great Spirits, this existence was, like the Oni Tribe who could sense Miasma, a creature made purely for war.
With that Witch long dead, the Spirit Eaters had lost their original purpose in the present age — but a faction within the Vollachia Empire, drawn by their power and usefulness, had set out to revive them.
Arakiya was simply one among that effort's subjects, recognized for a rare talent.

That rare talent consisted of the two qualities required of a Spirit Eater: a body that could endure absorbing spirits, and a mind that could remain human even after doing so.

To begin with, the method by which a Spirit Eater drew power from spirits was itself a forbidden art, and extracting only the power while bypassing every proper precondition carried tremendous risk. That was the great difference between a Spirit Eater and a Spiritual Arts User who merely borrowed a spirit's power, or a Temple Knight of the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko who gained power by subjugating one.
The latter two also had to expend effort building a relationship with their target spirit, but once a Contract was formed, there was no danger in using that power so long as the relationship with the spirit did not break down.

A Spirit Eater, on the other hand, had to continually replenish itself with new spirits merely to maintain its power, and with every replenishment lived in constant fear of having its mind and body's sense of self stripped away.
If body and mind ever lost to the spirit being absorbed, even once, the flesh would lose its original state, and the mind would assimilate with the spirit and be dragged down into a plane no longer human.
And yet, too strong a sense of self would badly hinder compatibility with the spirit, making it impossible to draw out one's power as a Spirit Eater in the first place.

For that reason, a Spirit Eater was required to have a sense of self-awareness and ego so faint as to be almost nonexistent, so as to remain compatible with the power of the spirits it absorbed. And yet, as already noted, too faint an ego easily invited assimilation with the absorbed spirit, greatly raising the risk of ceasing to be human.

To prevent that, a Spirit Eater was given a Pillar — a "core," in place of that faint sense of self and ego, so as not to lose sight of who they were.

The Pillar functioned much like the imprinting by which a hatchling mistakes the first thing it sees for its parent, taking root deep in a Spirit Eater's instincts to form its very foundation.
A Spirit Eater's overwhelming power was wielded for the sake of that Pillar, and a Spirit Eater's undeveloped mind was consumed entirely by thoughts of it.
The Pillar was, quite literally, the very reason a Spirit Eater existed.

—A Spirit Eater who, harboring no ambition of betrayal toward its Pillar, swore itself to selfless service without end.

After a number of sacrifices dismal enough to sap the spirit merely to count, the Spirit Eater had been revived from ancient times into the present age. Even the people of the Empire could not bring themselves to believe that this success was worth the number of lives it had cost.
And so the girl who came to stand as the sole successful example was presented, as a being of invaluable ability, to the reigning Emperor of the time, Drizen Vollachia — and Drizen in turn gave her to one of the daughters carrying his own blood, as a milk sibling.

What impression Emperor Drizen Vollachia formed of the girl presented to him, or for what reason he bestowed her upon his own daughter, remains unknown.
If one thing can be said, it is that the girl functioned exactly as the faction that had sought to revive the Spirit Eater's power had hoped, and fixed upon the daughter Drizen bestowed upon her as her Pillar, firm and unwavering.
And above all else—

—as a Spirit Eater, Arakiya was completed as a being of such overwhelming, unmanageable strength that she surpassed every one of her kind from the ancient age, back when the fight against the Witch had demanded their existence.

The world grew hotter as if mocking common sense — merely breathing seared the lungs, the vessels of blood coursing through the body swelled with heat, and even the tears meant to moisten increasingly parched eyes evaporated before they could fall.

In an environment eating away at life moment by moment, no half-hearted fighter could even close the distance needed to make out the color of Arakiya's eyes. Every form of life, without exception, was left behind by a world being remade faster than it could keep up with, withering away to die of thirst and decay.
For that reason, upon the earth overlooked by the crimson sky, only two figures still stood besides Arakiya, the core of that world's transformation — Yorna and Priscilla.
Yet even they could not escape the sensation of their lives being steadily eaten away.

"—!"

With an audible crack, a fourth hairpin was destroyed, and Yorna's cheeks stiffened with a sense of loss.
Through the effect of the Soul Marriage Technique, the many offerings dedicated to Yorna took the place of her life in her stead. Thanks to that, even while fighting Arakiya — a monster of a fighter in the most literal sense — Yorna suffered no physical damage, nor any impairment to her movement whatsoever.
That said—

"If only it could spare this ache in my chest, I dare say."

Yorna's life being taken in her stead — that itself was proof of the love poured into each gift.
The hairpin was by no means an especially fine or costly design. It was simply an offering made by the city's residents under Yorna's protection, crafted from their own horns, scales, and other parts of their bodies.
There was nothing in it but reverence and trust. And so, no price could ever be placed on it.

"So the hairpin's broken. A moment ago it was a hair ornament — you're being worn down bit by bit, aren't you, Mother."

As Yorna let the shattered fragments slip through her fingers, Priscilla landed beside her, the hem of her red dress flaring out behind her.
With the dazzling Sunlight Sword in hand and a fearless expression set upon her beautiful profile, Priscilla — and at her daughter's words and manner, Yorna narrowed her eyes slightly.

"I'll say this much, I dare say: to have the gifts of my Beloved Children counted like some tally of lives left to spend — that displeases me, I dare say."

"Spoken sweetly, for a mother who's switched lives so many times and lived so very long. Does the fact that I've never once given you a gift make you hesitate to call me one of your Beloved Children?"

"—————"

"Not even willing to humor me? Very well — as you wish, let's talk practicalities instead."

Priscilla shrugged her bare white shoulders and turned her gaze forward. At her posture and words, Yorna tossed back, "Practicalities, I dare say?"
At that, Priscilla gave a small dip of her chin.

"It's a matter of how much longer we can go on trading blows with that thing."

Priscilla's crimson eyes turned skyward. — An instant later, spears of water came pouring down like rain and hail, aimed squarely at Yorna and Priscilla below.

"—!"

Gritting her teeth, Yorna threw herself down to avoid the water spears raining down with furious force.
Each of the water spears being loosed had been compressed down to little more than the thickness of a single finger — but no one should mistake that for the power behind them being as slight as a single finger.
The highly compressed water carried a threat that would mercilessly bore through anything in its path and slice through it without discrimination — a single graze enough to send an arm or leg flying, a lethal blow in every sense.

One moment she was splitting the earth open crudely with an overwhelming volume of water; the next she unleashed delicate, difficult-to-counter techniques without the slightest hesitation. There was no consistency to any of it, save that each was equally dangerous.

"—! Priscilla!"

Throwing herself flat, pouring every ounce of effort into dodging the falling spears of water, Yorna called out Priscilla's name as the latter cut the water flows aside with the Sunlight Sword.
Priscilla flashed her dazzling blade, cutting down the water spears one after another, but the number being loosed at her outstripped those aimed at Yorna by well more than double.
Which of the two, Yorna or Priscilla, Arakiya placed the greater weight on could be gauged from the sheer, overwhelming volume of water alone.

"—————"

Crimson flashes of the blade danced through the air; Priscilla's swordsmanship was so graceful it was mesmerizing to watch, like a dance.
And yet, that did not mean she was completely containing Arakiya's assault.
Just as she herself had remarked on the dwindling count of ornaments on Yorna's person, Priscilla too had had a number of the jewels adorning her own beauty shattered in the course of this battle.

The question of how much longer was not one that applied to Yorna alone.

"Even at the cost of severing my own limbs, is it? — What a hideous hope to be so captivated by."

A piercing blow struck true at the center of Priscilla's ample chest, and the gem on her necklace burst apart.
Sparing not so much as a glance for the fate of the jewel that had taken the blow meant for her life, Priscilla's crimson eyes remained fixed solely on Arakiya's form in the sky.
But catching sight of Priscilla's profile as she gazed upon Arakiya like that, Yorna found herself, in that instant, doubting her own eyes. What had flickered across those crimson eyes for the briefest moment was hard to believe.

It was—

"Do you regret it, Priscilla? — The way things turned out for General Arakiya."

"Nonsense. — This entire world is made to suit my convenience, every part of it."

Priscilla scoffed at Yorna's question and leaned forward into a run.
Leaving her mother behind, she cut straight toward the milk sibling from whom she had parted ways — and in that charge there seemed to lurk some feeling hidden from both of them, kept from ever showing in her eyes.

"Perhaps I'm the one who regrets it too, I dare say."

Yorna did not know what manner of meeting and parting had passed between Priscilla and Arakiya.
She had once stood in a position to know, yet that chance had been let go along with her very life — for Yorna's previous self had died, unable to endure the difficult birth of bringing Priscilla into the world.

Afterward, the Imperial Selection Ceremony had been carried out, and the girl who had been a member of Vollachia's imperial family — Prisca Benedict — had also lost her life, defeated in the struggle.
That should have been the entire span of the girl's life, as far as Sandra Benedict, Yorna's previous self, had known it.
But—

"—————"

By whatever strange chain of causes, time had flowed on, and an opportunity that should never have come around again had come around after all — and so Yorna found herself reunited with Priscilla.
Neither of them as Sandra or Prisca any longer, but as Yorna and Priscilla — a reunion born of a miracle that should never have been possible — mother and child now walked together through a crimson hell.
—Yes. Together.

"Princess."

Muttering that short word, power literally overflowed from every inch of Arakiya's body.
A being of a thousand transformations, wielding the power of fire or water, wind or earth, light or shadow — every force that made up this world — she poured her entire self into Priscilla, who was closing in on her.
And Priscilla, as if declaring through sheer bearing that she would meet it with her own body and blade and no other's, charged forward with a battle cry, as torrents of water and bolts of lightning came pouring down upon her—

"—I love you, I dare say."

In that instant, the water and lightning — the two attacks that should have collided head-on with the Sunlight Sword Priscilla swung up — vanished. — No, not vanished like smoke.
Without question, they had been cut down and swept away by a slash of the Sunlight Sword.
Only, the arc the crimson blade had traced was a flash of godlike speed even more refined than anything before it.

"This is…"

Priscilla let a breath of faint surprise slip into her voice as she knit her finely shaped brows.
Winds of red-tinged flame bore down on her from all four directions in quick succession. But Priscilla, without hesitation, cut through that scorching wind with her blade and swept it aside with ease.
Then, as if to confirm something, she regripped her sword, and her hand gently touched her own face. Her fingertips brushed lightly over her eye — though she likely could not have sensed the change there.

Yorna knew. That this was no flame carrying any physical effect.

—It was the flame of a bond that burned in the eye, the shape taken by Yorna's love for her Beloved Children.

"—————"

Standing tall with the crimson blade in hand, Priscilla's left eye now had a flame lit within it.
It was the mark of Yorna's protection, granted to the residents of the demon city of Chaosflame — a secret art by which, through the bestowal of the Soul Marriage Technique, a portion of Yorna's power was shared with those she meant to protect.
By its nature, it was a secret art of Yorna's meant to be granted to children who possessed neither the strength nor the skill to fight, given to them for their own protection — bound by a restriction that meant it could never be granted to a warrior.

But here, an exception was born.
One who possessed the strength and skill to fight, who wielded a treasured sword permitted to none but Vollachia's imperial family — and who, regardless of the scale of her power, satisfied the qualifications of a "Beloved Child" that Yorna wished to protect.

"Priscilla—"

"So you've finally decided to acknowledge me as your own child, Mother."

"Cheeky child. — I simply cannot bring myself to be so dull a mother as to only see off a back racing ahead of me, I dare say. If you're going, my dear, then I'll walk the same hell alongside you."

She had no intention of letting Priscilla go on ahead alone.
At Yorna's answer, Priscilla scoffed — and allowed Yorna's resolve to stand at her side. As if in proof of it, Yorna felt something strange about her own face and gently raised a hand to it.
The source of the strangeness was around her own eye — and there, most likely, burned the same flame as Priscilla's.

The Soul Marriage Technique, which could not be wielded without qualification — and here were two practitioners of it, each satisfying the other's conditions, each supplementing the other's soul. — A phenomenon that should, by rights, never be possible under any circumstances, was happening all the same.

And having witnessed it firsthand—

"…Why."

A thin voice escaped, and a gaze fell to the ground below as though cursing it.
It was Arakiya, her body poised in the sky, glaring fixedly down at Yorna standing beside Priscilla. She brought a hand to her own face, to the eyepatch covering her left eye.
And then, tearing it away as though ripping it off—

"Why! Why, why, why does she — why does the Princess's — go to you!"

Baring her anger openly, Arakiya revealed the lightless red eye beneath the patch. Reflecting Yorna in that sightless eye, Arakiya screamed at her with both crimson eyes.
At Yorna, who bore a flame in her eye and received the blessing of Priscilla's Soul Marriage Technique.
While Arakiya herself, unable to hold a flame in her own eye, could never receive Priscilla's blessing.

"The Princess is mine—!"

"Don't mistake yourself, Arakiya. Even if, hypothetically, your wish were somehow granted — even granting you every possible concession — it would not mean you belong to me, nor that I belong to you."

"—!"

"And I have never once, to anyone, granted a full hundred concessions."

At Priscilla's decisive glare, Arakiya drew a small, sharp breath.
But into Priscilla's fiercely uncompromising declaration cut a small sound, as if in protest.
It was—

"Mother, why did you just hit me on the head?"

"That's no way to speak, I dare say. I've no memory of raising my child to be so high-handed, I dare say."

"I have no memory of being raised by you in the first place, Mother."

"Then I suppose that chance has finally come around at last, I dare say."

So saying, Yorna lowered the pipe with which she had rapped Priscilla's head, gave a slow shake of her own, and then looked up at Arakiya.
In her eyes remained an unchanged anger, mixed now with a faint unease at what had just happened.

Meeting that gaze steadily, Yorna set the pipe between her lips there in the midst of hell and drew in a breath of violet smoke.
Then, with a smile somewhat too bright and clear for the situation, she who wore a form entirely unsuited to it—

"So you've finally looked my way, General Arakiya, I dare say. — The chance has come at last to atone for my own failing, for not giving the discipline that needed giving — to you, and to Priscilla both, I dare say."

"What are you…"

"To put it plainly—"

Confusion rode on those words, and taking it in stride, Yorna breathed out violet smoke.
Wreathing herself in the smoke that spilled from her lips, her eyes burning with the trust placed in her by her daughters, Yorna Mishigure — the mother who had once been Sandra Benedict —

"—am no parent to go easy on disciplining her children. Best be ready, you little girls, I dare say."

So she declared, resolute and unyielding.
