Arc 5 Chapter 14: "Sword Demon Beneath the Moon"

For a moment, everyone had forgotten they were political rivals, and the whole banquet had risen to it.

When Subaru returned to his room after dinner, he found the futon already laid out by the staff during their absence. Looking at the two futons set side by side, he privately marveled at the inn's efficiency.

Hotels were like this too, but this kind of lodging often took the opportunity, while guests were out of their rooms, to freshly prepare the futons, sheets, and towels all over again.

It struck him, as it always did, as a strangely defenseless custom.

"Subaru. It seems that while Betty and the others were away, some scoundrel snuck into the room — !"

"Yeah. Nicely laid out the futon, and folded back up the towels you messed up, too."

"This is...! Yes, it must be a trap to lure Betty and the others into carelessness, I suppose. Thorough and shrewd, these fellows are."

"How about, just this once, we believe in someone's goodwill for free? Well, it's not exactly free, but."

Soothing Beatrice, who had worked up needless suspicion over a simple show of diligent professionalism, Subaru quickly got the sleepy, eye-rubbing girl into bed.

Incidentally, ever since formally exchanging the Contract, sharing a room had become the default for Subaru and Beatrice. Anastasia had offered to prepare a separate guest room for Beatrice, but since it was obvious she'd end up in Subaru's room by nightfall anyway, he'd politely declined.

This wasn't because Beatrice was some child who couldn't sleep alone — it was purely out of consideration for Subaru's condition, whose Gate had become imperfect, given the large amount of Mana generated from his Od while he slept.

"So it's not because Betty wants to be near Subaru, I suppose. It would be wrong to misunderstand, wouldn't it."

So Beatrice had insisted, back when they'd first exchanged the Contract.

Where her true feelings actually lay didn't matter anymore at this point. Over the past year, Subaru had grown thoroughly accustomed to nights filled with a breathing sound that wasn't his own.

"...This green lump is poison, I suppose. Eating it wouldn't end well, would it..."

Having enjoyed herself a fair amount and tired herself out a fair amount, Beatrice was dragged into dreamland the moment she got under the covers, and was now apparently having a nightmare about the wasabi that had traumatized her at dinner.

Stroking the brow of his frowning little partner, Subaru savored her sleeping face to his heart's content before standing up.

"Well then. Guess I'll go take a bath and rest up properly tonight."

At the head of Subaru's futon lay a spare yukata prepared for him to wear. On the way back to the room, a staff member had mentioned that if he didn't know how to put it on, he should feel free to ask — but despite appearances, Subaru could properly dress himself in a yukata, and even a proper kimono.

As for why he could do it properly — the answer was: too much free time in his old world.

"If Ferris and Anastasia-san weren't around, I could've been in charge of dressing all the ladies myself — quite the perk that would've been."

Of course, his true motive had been wanting to dress Emilia, if he was honest, but every Royal Selection candidate was a beauty of well-arranged features. Whatever minor flaws they had in personality, the pleasure of adorning each of their outfits should have been equally satisfying.

"Ah well, no use complaining. I'll settle for having managed to braid Emilia-tan's hair after dinner."

She might undo it before going to sleep, but the "braid wave!" that activated when a braid was undone was Subaru's secondary objective all along. Anastasia's natural hair wave was beautiful in its own right, but he was certain it would suit Emilia's long silver hair splendidly too.

"Cute with the braid, cute when the braid-wave happens. Emilia-tan really is a sinful girl, honestly. Can't enjoy this with Bea-ko, though."

Strangely enough, Beatrice's hairstyle stayed fixed in ringlets no matter what you tried to do with it. Probably something to do with the circumstances of her creation as an Artificial Spirit. It wasn't impossible to force a different hairstyle on her, but the moment you undid whatever you'd bundled it into, it snapped right back to ringlets. That was a sight worth seeing in its own right, so he'd let himself play with it a few times.

Looking forward to tomorrow morning, Subaru quietly left the room with his yukata in hand.

Careful not to wake Beatrice, he did his best to muffle his footsteps on the wooden floor. It felt a little careless, perhaps, but given the lineup of guests staying at the inn tonight, he found himself almost pitying any villain foolish enough to attempt a break-in.

"I doubt anything will happen, but if anything strange occurs within this district, I'll sense it. Please rest easy tonight."

Such had been Reinhard's reassuring words, spoken as they parted ways in the drawing room.

That he hadn't limited it to "within the inn" but flatly said "district" was so reassuring it was almost frightening in reverse. Knowing his personality, there was even a chance he'd wanted to say "the whole city" and had held back out of modesty.

So, for the time being, Subaru walked through the inn without a shred of caution. His heart was already set on the large communal bath — a shame it wasn't an open-air one, but Subaru figured there were pleasures unique to an inn's bath all the same.

"——"

His feet stopped, and his slack, bath-anticipating expression changed, as he passed the veranda overlooking the garden.

The garden where Reinhard and Garfiel had crossed swords that evening took on an entirely different character by night, one that delighted the eyes all over again.

A round moon hung in the black sky, and the thickness of the clouds crossing it lent the heavens an eerie allure. In the garden where a cool breeze blew, a single figure stood motionless.

"—Wilhelm-san?"

The sturdy back, the hair gone white.

An old man whose yukata suited him even from behind — as far as Subaru knew, there was only one person that could be.

"Subaru-dono. Did I startle you?"

He must have long since noticed the presence of someone passing by. Turning at the call, Wilhelm's expression softened as he looked toward Subaru.

Hands tucked into the sleeves of his yukata, standing there against the backdrop of a Japanese-style garden — why did it suit him so perfectly?

"Were you about to go bathe?"

"Yeah, that was the plan. Took the long way around because I wanted to see the garden at night, is all. Not that I got lost in an unfamiliar inn or anything."

"I would never suspect Subaru-dono of such a thing. Besides, I too was the one who first grew intoxicated by the garden's beauty. I believe I understand the feeling of anticipating this sight."

"...Being lifted up like that is a little itchy, you know."

Faced with Wilhelm's unreserved trust, Subaru scratched his cheek, honestly embarrassed.

Wilhelm was one of the people in this other world Subaru respected most purely. There were many others he wished to stand alongside, to compete with — but Wilhelm might be the only person for whom he felt something closer to admiration, a longing to look up to.

As a person and as a man, Wilhelm was Subaru's ideal.

"My apologies to Subaru-dono. For an outsider such as myself to intrude upon the solemnity of the garden by night — you must be rather disheartened."

"Nah, not at all. If anything, a sword demon and a Japanese garden suit each other so well it's burned itself into the photograph of my heart forever. I'm a sucker for people the moonlight looks good on."

As far as Subaru knew, there was no question that moonlit nights suited Emilia best of all.

Her long silver hair was unlike the brilliance of sunlight. Emilia's beauty was that of moonlight — fleeting, yet something you wanted to stay close to. That was why Subaru wanted to be the star that stayed close to the moon.

And so, the sight of the sword demon standing beneath the moonlit night now struck Subaru as something profoundly evocative of longing.

"...Subaru-dono, you really do lose out in this world. Words that stir the heart so — spoken to a woman instead of an old husk like me, they'd surely draw her in."

"Even with this mug of mine spouting smooth lines, the butterflies wouldn't be drawn in — they'd treat me like bug spray. Besides, right now, this kind of talk doesn't work at all on the one woman's heart I most want to tickle."

"To choose one's words with such care, just to glimpse the smile of a woman not easily swayed... that frustration too is part of the pleasure of love, Subaru-dono."

At Wilhelm's teasing tone, Subaru shrugged playfully in turn.

"Ohh, sounds like we're headed for some rare, meaningful lovesick talk. Did you have a time like that too, Wilhelm-san?"

"Would you like to hear?"

"Absolutely."

Subaru gave a deep, formal bow in proper etiquette, and Wilhelm, with an air of great ceremony, said, "Well, I suppose there's no helping it," a pleased look on his face.

"Even now, but more so in my youth, I was worse with words than I am today — a man of few words. Having no interest in anything but swinging a sword, I had no topics of conversation to offer beyond swordsmanship either. My wife, when we first met, must have found me terribly dull."

"But your wife didn't mind talking with you, even so, did she?"

"My wife was a woman of great depth. Though her heart wavered under the weight of the karma she carried, she never once tried to escape her responsibilities, nor stopped caring for others. That she kept speaking with me in those early days was, I believe, thanks to that innate kindness of hers."

Lowering his eyes into memory, Wilhelm wore a smile that seemed simply nostalgic.

Subaru sat down quietly on the veranda and listened to the old man's tale of the past.

"Since I was so poor with words, whenever I met with my wife, it was almost always she who threw out the topics of conversation. What's more, in the beginning, I had not the slightest awareness that I was drawn to her — I even recall feeling irritated by the constant fluttering in my chest whenever we spoke."

"So even you weren't used to women, one way or another, Wilhelm-san."

"I truly had given myself over entirely to the sword. As if, having forgotten even the reasons I first picked it up, I sustained myself by losing myself in it. —It was my wife who reminded me of that reason, too."

"Was that also when you realized you loved her, by any chance?"

"...Subaru-dono, you saw right through me."

Subaru answered Wilhelm's weakly-spoken words with silence.

Surely Wilhelm had no idea what kind of expression he was wearing right now. That fact — being able to see that face — filled Subaru with a fierce sense of pride.

Wilhelm's eyes, the wrinkles at his cheeks, the tone of his voice, his every gesture — all of it told the story.

Even now, he was in love with his wife as she had been when they first met — Theresia van Astrea.

The old man's face, his bearing, his very existence, sang unstintingly of love for his wife.

Who, seeing that face, could fail to recognize the moment he'd fallen in love?

Gather up every ounce of obtuseness among every living being in this world, and still, anyone would know the depth of that feeling.

That was how plainly, how proudly, Wilhelm's love showed.

"——!"

Without realizing it, Subaru, gazing at Wilhelm's face, found himself nearly moved to tears.

Something welled up that he couldn't hold back, gathering hot behind his eyelids. He didn't know why he felt this way. Why should seeing a man's face in love make his own chest burn like this?

If he started crying here, he'd only trouble Wilhelm.

"Subaru-dono is quite right. It was in that moment that I became conscious of my feelings for my wife."

Bowing his head, pretending to scratch it, Subaru hid the sign of tears from Wilhelm. Did Wilhelm, continuing to speak, notice Subaru's tears?

Whether he was too absorbed in his reminiscence to notice, or was deliberately pretending not to — Subaru couldn't tell which was true, and simply let himself sink into the old man's story.

"Swinging the sword was everything to me. And yet, everything I felt before I swung it, and everything I came to feel through swinging it — all of that shaped who I am. My wife made me realize something so obvious. From then on, every time I gripped a sword, I remembered her."

"Is that still true, even now?"

"—It was the sword that connected my wife and me, both then and now."

At Subaru's question, Wilhelm paused briefly before weaving his words together.

Bearing the moonlight at his back, Wilhelm faced Subaru. The emotions flickering across the old man's eyes were complex, not easily read by Subaru.

There was pride. There was regret. There was hesitation. There was passion. There was shame. There was fierce resolve.

—But through all of it, there was love.

"So long as I grip a sword, I will keep thinking of my wife. And so, even now that death has parted us, I will never forget her. When I die, I wish to die with a sword in hand. To me, that means being with my wife still."

"——"

That was Wilhelm's way of loving — clumsy, straightforward, the only way he knew how.

Subaru caught his breath, then took several deep breaths in a row, forcing down his pounding heart. Once the numbness in his tongue faded and he judged he could speak properly again—

"Don't say stuff like 'when I die,' that's bad luck. You're still way, way, way too young to be young, Wilhelm-san — it's a problem if all you think about is retiring."

"Subaru-dono?"

"Crusch-san and Ferris both rely on you so much. Crusch-san's memory being unreliable is hard on everyone, and Ferris, supporting her — he can't show it on his face, but I bet he's desperate too, and you have to help him! And besides, me too!"

"——"

"There's still so much I want to learn from you, Wilhelm-san. In an enemy camp, no less — you might think, who does this brat think he is, being so needy. But I..."

Subaru liked Wilhelm.

That was why, holding onto the love for his late wife in his heart and having exacted vengeance for her, Subaru respected him as a man. Even if Wilhelm hadn't intended it, even in a master-disciple relationship that, by Subaru's repeated experience, had lasted less than ten days, Subaru had seen in Wilhelm a strength worth honoring.

Hearing words evoking "death" from that very man's mouth was, for that reason, especially frightening.

More than ever before, Subaru had grown sensitive to the "death" of people he knew. Part of it was because of the Covenant with Roswaal; part of it was because Subaru's own stance toward Return by Death had changed.

It was something Emilia and Beatrice, too, had secretly worried about.

"...As ever, I'm no good at choosing my words."

Stubborn, desperate — that was Subaru just now. Hearing his voice, Wilhelm laughed.

The old man watched Subaru, still breathing shallowly, with a gentle gaze, and with his lips still curved in a smile—

"I've caused you great rudeness and worry. Though I spoke as I did earlier, it isn't as though all I think about is dying. It's true that, for a time, I was so consumed by that thought — but it seems I've managed to cross that particular pass safely."

"...ah."

Subaru realized at once he meant the battle with the White Whale.

For Wilhelm, that battle against his enemy must have been one he'd entered with the resolve to trade his very life for it, if need be. In that moment, Wilhelm had gone into the fight prepared even for death. And now, having overcome it—

"I find myself thinking how convenient I am. After pouring so much of myself into that resolve, I ended up surviving after all, my life spared. Now, I don't even find it shameful to go on living in disgrace."

"Wilhelm-san..."

"To be needed — I count myself endlessly fortunate for that. Now as then, this body that knows nothing but swinging a sword still trembles with the blessing of being wanted still. There are people I must support, and I am able to visit my wife's grave. I am, I think, far too blessed."

Was that... really so?

Was it truly so? Wasn't Wilhelm forcing himself?

The heart behind that old man's gentle smile was something a shallow young man like Subaru couldn't see through. He couldn't fully accept his own weakness in wanting to believe there was no lie in that smile.

Wasn't he, in truth, forcing himself? If he wasn't, wasn't he perhaps carrying some suffering he couldn't reveal to Subaru?

But then again, if he truly meant it from the bottom of his heart — wasn't Subaru just being arrogant, wishing Wilhelm carried some unhappiness at all?

"Subaru-dono. —That quality of yours is a virtue. But it is also a weakness."

"............"

Watching Subaru sink into thought, Wilhelm said this in a quiet voice.

There was no hint of a smile in it. But it wasn't spoken with displeasure or reproach, either. It was somehow admonishing, the way an elder speaks to someone younger.

Put more plainly, it was the tone of a grandfather speaking to his grandchild.

"My wife had that same quality. A bad habit of suppressing her own heart, worrying over the hearts of those around her, always putting herself last."

"A bad habit, huh. ...No, actually, I'm not that good a guy to begin with. I've never wished for everyone, all of them, to be happy, nothing like that. It's more like — if just the people around me are happy, that's good enough for me."

"It is a matter of the scope of that 'around you.' My wife never wished for it, but she possessed more power than any one woman ought to have needed. And the range that power could reach extended, whether she wished it or not, far further than she herself imagined."

Wilhelm's wife, Theresia, was the previous Sword Saint.

Her history was a simple one to summarize, but Subaru had come to learn of it over the past year. A hero who, within a few years of inheriting the title of Sword Saint, had suppressed the internal rebellion within the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica known as the Demi-Human War.

What she accomplished with that overwhelming power was, quite literally, an achievement that saved a nation.

There was no way Natsuki Subaru possessed anything comparable to such a heroine.

"I get what you're saying about your wife. But applying that to me is a bit much, no matter how you look at it."

"In ordinary times, my wife was simply a woman who loved to admire flowers. Being a hero, a person of great renown recorded in history, does not mean one is always so in daily life. And Subaru-dono — your name, and the reach of your hand, already extend further than you yourself realize. And that, going forward, will only become more true."

"There's no way..."

"I am certain of this. Subaru-dono, you are a person who will gather together those who cannot achieve things alone, and thereby accomplish what could not be accomplished alone — surely you will."

"——"

Subaru was left speechless.

Speechless at just how much Wilhelm had overestimated him.

Was he really believed capable of something so grand?

Subaru was weak, not particularly smart, even weak of will — a half-finished thing. Because there was nothing he could do alone, he'd talked his way through, dragging others into things, patching over the moment again and again. That was all.

That kind of method was bound to fall apart eventually. It might be holding together now, but when something came along that couldn't be patched over with stopgaps, it would surely collapse.

And when that inevitable moment came, Subaru would only end up disappointing everyone—

"My apologies. Piling this on you like this can only trouble you. A man who has done nothing but accumulate years without gaining such consideration has much to regret."

"Wilhelm-san, I..."

"You may not yet be able to see it in yourself. And there may well be many others who haven't noticed it either, even now. But in time, everyone will come to understand."

"I'm small, and I'm a hopeless good-for-nothing, you know."

"Yes. And it is that small, hopeless good-for-nothing that I am fond of."

After a beat, Wilhelm nodded, satisfied.

"And the number of people who feel that way will only grow, from here on."

At Wilhelm's words, spoken as though something had truly moved him, Subaru fell quietly silent.

There was a part of him that felt overwhelmed, and a part that wanted to laugh it off as an exaggeration. And yet, that he didn't dismiss it out of hand was precisely because it was Wilhelm, of all people, who'd said it.

He wouldn't believe it wholesale.

But neither was it something to unilaterally deny and cast aside.

Subaru decided to carry the feeling this conversation had stirred in him, until he could properly digest it.

Subaru was, to begin with, someone painfully aware of how much he lacked. Whatever he gained — things, feelings, words — he'd resolved to drag along with him as far as he could carry them.

He decided to make Wilhelm's words, too, one of those things.

Watching Subaru, hesitant yet resolved, Wilhelm looked on with something like affection.

Subaru, too absorbed in reflecting on himself, didn't notice that gaze.

"...I've spoken rather too much, it seems. My apologies for keeping your feet planted here so long."

Judging that the conflict within Subaru had reached some resolution, Wilhelm brought it up.

Taking the cue, Subaru judged that this night's little scene was drawing to a close.

"Nah, I'm the one who should apologize, for all sorts of things. I was aiming to hear — to draw out — some lovesick talk about your wife, and here we are."

"Not at all. It's been a while since I've felt so satisfied... well, not quite satisfied, but I'm glad to have been able to speak of my wife. Lately I haven't been given much time by either Crusch-sama or Ferris."

"So on top of not getting your fill of lovesick talk, I ended up hearing about the shaky footing within your own camp!"

"I let myself grow a touch too sentimental. A long-winded old man's story — it must have bored you."

Wilhelm, smiling faintly, let a small flicker of emotion pass through those blue eyes.

Subaru, not missing that fleeting glimpse, turned his attention to how this had all started in the first place.

At first, Wilhelm had been standing alone in the garden.

Subaru had initially assumed, same as himself, that he'd come down to enjoy the atmosphere of the garden. But Wilhelm had said something to him — that he was sorry for letting a foreign object intrude upon the scenery.

For simply enjoying the view of the garden, the best seat in the house was where Subaru stood, on the veranda.

Conversely, standing where Wilhelm stood meant losing much of the pleasure of fully enjoying the moonlit garden's view.

Of course, that might just be overthinking it. But if there was another reason Wilhelm had come to the garden, it would be—

"...That spot — that's where Reinhard was standing, wasn't it."

"——"

The place where Wilhelm stood motionless was the absolute domain Reinhard had defended throughout his bout with Garfiel.

Amid the faintly disturbed gravel, the footprints of the red-haired swordsman, standing in his unmoving stance, stood out clearly.

It was only natural that Wilhelm would have noticed that discrepancy and wanted to confirm it. But the true meaning behind why he'd kept standing there — only Wilhelm himself could know.

"Wilhelm-san. I don't mean to poke my nose into another family's business, and I like to think I've graduated from the kind of nosy curiosity that carelessly asks 'so what happened,' so I don't want to go around saying things I shouldn't, but..."

"Yes, go ahead. I'll listen."

"You and Reinhard — can't you two get along? You're family, right?"

An indelicate step into the complicated relationship between grandfather and grandson within House Astrea.

Subaru brought it up knowing full well it risked damaging the trust he'd built with Wilhelm.

He might not have brought it up, had it not been for this conversation with Wilhelm here in the garden. But given the conversation just now, and the profile of Wilhelm looking down at his grandson's footprints—

Having witnessed that, he couldn't avoid it.

"Speaking with Subaru-dono, I found myself thinking."

"............"

"Why is it that I cannot exchange words with my own grandson, like this?"

It was a statement full of bitter anguish, straight from Wilhelm's heart.

The expression vanished from Wilhelm's profile. Expressionless — but not emotionless. Suppressing his emotion was a hardened shell meant to keep an unsightly lament from showing outward.

What Wilhelm carried within was, unmistakably, regret.

"I am a man of many regrets. But among them, there are three regrets in my life for which I can offer no excuse whatsoever. One of those is the cause of the rift that now lies between my grandson and me."

"But Wilhelm-san, you regret it, don't you?"

"Regretting it changes nothing, earns no forgiveness. That is how heavy the words were that I once hurled at my grandson... at Reinhard. Foolish beyond redemption, beyond forgiveness."

Behind that mask of expressionlessness, there was a hint of flame in Wilhelm that seemed almost enough to burn him alive.

It was a hellfire of anger that had burned, unforgiving, at the depths of Wilhelm's heart for years. Regret was the kindling, and the flame scorched Wilhelm, refusing to let him go.

"Using vengeance for my wife as an excuse, I kept averting my eyes from that regret. And now that I've achieved that vengeance, I understand, truly, that I ought to close the distance."

"But you can't work up the courage."

"I am ashamed to admit it. I think of my grandson still resenting me, and my feet simply won't move."

Wilhelm's voice, lamenting with what sounded like utter disappointment in himself.

Watching the old man, who seemed to have suddenly shrunk before his eyes, Subaru was stunned. And after being stunned, he couldn't help but burst out laughing.

"Subaru-dono?"

"S-sorry. I didn't mean to laugh, but I just couldn't help it."

Wilhelm looked at him as though he couldn't believe it, but Subaru couldn't believe it either.

Honestly, how many times was this old man going to surprise him in a single night?

"Wilhelm-san, it's like you're so worked up you think you don't even have the right to call yourself Reinhard's grandpa anymore, but..."

"Yes, that's exactly it. Facing my own grandson, knowing full well I'm the one in the wrong, and still unable to take that step. I'm thoroughly disgusted with such a cowardly self..."

"That just makes you look like a grandpa who's scared of his grandkid hating him."

"...Hah."

Wilhelm, who'd worn a clouded expression until then, caught his breath and stared at Subaru's face.

Subaru, the urge to laugh still lingering, waved a hand.

"I don't really know the details of why things got awkward between you and Reinhard. So it's possible I'm way off base here. But even from where I'm standing, an outsider, it looks like you want to make up with Reinhard, and it looks like you're seriously trying to apologize. If that's true, then you should definitely apologize."

"But Reinhard likely won't forgive me for it."

"If he doesn't forgive you the first time, keep apologizing until he does. Besides — you don't apologize because you want to be forgiven, you apologize because you want to apologize, right? The apologizer's desire to apologize is that selfish. I mean, the one who wants to apologize is the one who did something wrong in the first place."

"——"

This time, it was Wilhelm's turn to be left speechless by Subaru's extreme argument.

Of course, Subaru understood perfectly well that what he'd just said was an absurdly one-sided theory. Even so, he felt that right now, it was necessary to wield it and press it forward.

To get Wilhelm moving. To make him face Reinhard.

"I mean, sure, if you've been distant for years and suddenly apologize out of nowhere, at first he'll probably think, 'what's with this guy.' But if you keep apologizing over and over, eventually that 'what's with this guy' will change into something else. Whether it turns into 'well, I guess it can't be helped' or 'ugh, this guy's annoying,' I don't know, though."

"That sounds like it's getting worse, if anything."

"But it's changing. Isn't that better than staying stuck at rock bottom, not moving at all?"

Subaru had quite the reputation for starting off on everyone's bad side.

Being surrounded on all sides by the worst possible first impressions was nothing much to Subaru.

"Plus, if you slip him some pocket money after all these years apart, his attitude might soften up pretty quick. You know that thing where someone you've had a bad impression of forever suddenly does one nice thing and you're like, oh, they're actually a good person? Use that principle. Reinhard might be surprisingly easy to win over, too. Personally, getting him to call me a friend was pretty easy."

"Surely Reinhard is not so simple as that..."

"—Reinhard said he wants to hear about what happened with the White Whale."

At Subaru's offhand remark, Wilhelm's guard loosened, just slightly.

There, Subaru relayed to Wilhelm the words Reinhard had asked of him, right after they'd reunited, on the way to the tea room.

Hearing that, Wilhelm's blue eyes went wide, fixed in place.

"I don't know if the White Whale connects to the awkwardness between you two. But if it does, Reinhard was clearly troubled by it. He must know, of course, that it was you, Wilhelm-san, who defeated the White Whale. He knows that you spent over ten years trying to avenge his grandmother."

"——"

"I bet he's hoping too — hoping that now might be the time for whatever's been stuck to finally start moving."

Subaru had no way of truly knowing Reinhard's real feelings.

Reinhard was a man so perfect it was almost excessive, and Subaru had privately assumed he'd never lamented his own helplessness or ignorance the way Subaru himself did.

That was far too presumptuous a way to think. He was human too. Surely he had things he agonized over as well.

Even Wilhelm, who seemed almost superhuman to Subaru, turned out — once you peeled back a layer — to be an ordinary man, an ordinary grandfather, full of ordinary worries and ordinary flaws.

What was so strange about thinking Reinhard might be the same way?

Subaru's words had left Wilhelm astonished, over and over, since a while ago now.

The old man closed his eyes as if weighing each word Subaru had spoken, and now spent the passing moments quietly, letting the breeze wash over him.

And so, after a brief silence passed between the two of them, Wilhelm spoke.

"My grandson... will Reinhard listen to what I have to say?"

"First, try talking to him until you're annoying, and getting brushed off — that's not so bad either. Pretty much every girl except Emilia-tan does that to me most of the time, anyway."

"Honestly, now—"

Hearing Subaru's answer, Wilhelm shook his head.

Then the old man lifted his face, gazing up at the moon floating behind him, and—

"I'm no match for Subaru-dono."

He said, in a tone laced with a smile.
