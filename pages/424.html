Arc 6 Chapter 22: "Asterism of the White Starry Sky"

Shaking off Julius's arm and showing he could stand on his own, Subaru turned back to face the "Exam."

Before Subaru's eyes, centered on the monolith he'd touched, countless duplicate monoliths spread out across the white space. Honestly, there were so many that just counting them was exhausting to contemplate.

"This is the 'Exam'... I take it, Shaula?"

"Sure, why not, ssu? I wanna see Master's good side, ssu!"

"Don't cheer me on like you're heckling me at a drinking party..."

He'd never been to one, so he wouldn't know, but it probably felt something like that.

With Shaula cheering him on so lightheartedly at his back, Subaru surveyed the room. It was the same as ever—a floor wrapped in white that threatened to scramble your sense of perspective and elevation, everything about it.

The most conspicuous change was the scattered monoliths, but even those weren't especially different from the first one. They seemed to vary somewhat in size, larger or smaller, but otherwise they were the same—floating in midair, made of the same mysterious material.

"As for anything else that might be a hint... I guess it's that thing from before, huh."

What came to mind was the voice that had echoed in his head the instant he touched the monolith.

'—Touch the most radiant of the hero destroyed by Shaula.'

That voice hadn't come through his eardrums as sound—it was closer to something whispered directly into his brain, inside his skull. Perhaps because it wasn't sound, the voice he'd heard didn't register as belonging to the concept of "someone's voice."

If anything, it had cut into his mind like a sentence he himself had thought up. His own thoughts had no sound to them, nothing at all. So there was no telling whose voice it belonged to. If he had to say, he'd call it his own.

"So the voice I heard is the exam question, is that the call? In that case..."

"Subaru, sorry to interrupt your thinking, but there are a few things you should be aware of. It wouldn't hurt to hear them before you get started."

Julius, who'd played that little prank on him earlier, said this to Subaru as he tilted his head.

Near the stairway down to the floor below, Emilia and the others had gathered a little way off from the monoliths, all of them putting their heads together over the problem. Julius beckoned from in front of them, and Subaru shrugged, impressed. "Huh."

"Right. While I was asleep, everyone else got a head start tackling this. Guess there'd be some progress."

"It may not be so much progress as you'd hope. Well then. Subaru, try touching one of the tablets nearby—no, the 'monoliths.'"

"You that fond of the name? Well, I don't mind, but..."

Narrowing his eyes at Julius's odd insistence on the name "monolith," Subaru walked over to the one just to his right. It was one of the duplicates, separate from the original. Approaching it like this, he could tell it was a bit smaller than the first one.

"There's no trap where the second I touch it, my arm gets swallowed up or something, right?"

"It's fine, I suppose. If something like that were to happen, Betty would just have to be Subaru's right arm for the rest of his life, I suppose."

"Oh, then I'll be Subaru's left hand. Don't worry."

"In that scenario I've lost both my arms!"

Thanks to Emilia and Beatrice's reassuring guarantees, Subaru worked up the courage to reach out to the monolith. Touching it, in itself, didn't worry him much.

Since his companions weren't stopping him and were even encouraging him, there was no reason to doubt its safety. The only issue was simply the possibility it was hiding some surprising fact, like the monolith duplication had been—

"Oh?"

With that unease still lingering, the moment his fingertips touched the monolith, the black tablet blazed with a light bright enough to sear the eyes. In fact, the glare was enough to catch Subaru's breath, and he reflexively covered his face with his arm.

And when the light cleared, before Subaru's eyes was—

"Huh? Where'd the monolith go?"

"Fufufu, Master, it's behind you, ssu."

"Behind me...?"

After blinking, Subaru confirmed that the monolith that should have been right in front of him was gone. While he was still startled by that fact, a pointlessly triumphant Shaula urged him to look behind him.

When he turned around, there it was—facing the stairs, the spot where the very first monolith had stood. There, a single original monolith. Every other monolith had vanished from the room.

"So... what does this mean?"

"It's returned to its initial state. In other words, I'd judge that means disqualification from the 'Exam.' Of course..."

Passing by a sullen Subaru, Julius approached the first monolith without hesitation. He reached out and touched its surface, and at once that voice rang out in his head again—

'—Touch the most radiant of the hero destroyed by Shaula.'

The moment the question was posed anew, monoliths were duplicated one after another from the first one again, spreading throughout the room with the same force as before, distributing the "Exam."

In other words, it was a "retake."

"I see. So you can rethink it as many times as it takes to reach the answer."

"That's our guess so far, at least. Incidentally, regarding these monoliths, I'll state one thing plainly: touching them at random won't lead you to the answer."

"Ah, so you already tried the 'enough random shots eventually hit something' approach."

Subaru broke down Julius's mild phrasing into something blunter. Hearing that explanation, Emilia and Meili patted their own heads in embarrassment.

"Just touch every single one and see" was, admittedly, exactly the kind of tactic they'd try. Though the fact that it hadn't worked meant—

"Well, it's called an 'Exam' for a reason. Means you actually have to solve the problem and answer it."

"Simply offering up an answer at random apparently doesn't sit well with whoever set the question."

"Ah, yeah, there's always that one teacher who won't give you points on a test unless you show your work, not just the final answer. Sure works wonders against cheating, though."

Reaching an answer without the method behind it could only mean cheating or pure intuition. Up through elementary school, plenty of problems could be solved on gut instinct alone, but the point of arithmetic was to learn the method, not to somehow squeak by on each individual problem as it came.

Back in elementary school, he used to get indignant whenever he lost points for sloppy work shown—

"Looking back now, the teacher was right..."

"Sorry to interrupt while Natsuki-kun's off wallowing in memories again, but Natsuki-kun's turn is really just starting now, isn't it. C'mon, snap out of it, snap out of it."

"Huh, oh, right, sorry. But, my turn?"

Anastasia, hands on her hips, called back Subaru as his gaze had begun to drift far away. When Subaru tilted his head at her request, everyone exchanged glances and said—

Since they all said the same thing in substance, it can be summarized in a single paraphrase.

Namely—

'Finding out about the hero Shaula destroyed—that's Subaru's job, isn't it.'

"Until Subaru woke up, we tried the 'Exam' several times too. But this girl here wouldn't even tell us whether she was really Shaula, which was the crucial part."

"To Betty and the others, Shaula was the 'Sage,' I suppose. We were picturing the person engraved on the silver coin, so we couldn't connect that to this girl being Shaula, I suppose."

Such was the answer from Emilia and the others, who up until now hadn't been able to get anything out of Shaula.

Watching Shaula treat him with a familiarity that went well beyond friendly ever since he'd woken up, Subaru found it hard to believe she'd once kept a stubborn silence—but it made sense that things wouldn't progress if the only person connected to the tower had clammed up.

That said, Subaru had a concern of his own.

Namely, even now that Shaula herself had had the seal on her tongue lifted and could talk—

"Well, let's just ask, for starters. Hey, Shaula. If you've got any idea at all who this hero you destroyed might be, tell me every last one."

"Leave it to me, ssu. Remembering the name of every single guy I killed—that's amateur work, ssu. A pro like me stops remembering after the first hundred, ssu."

"Figures!"

At Shaula's forceful reply, a thumbs-up and a wink included, Subaru slapped his knee.

It was more or less exactly the empty-headed answer he'd feared she'd give.

Given her manner and attitude so far, he'd more or less expected as much. There was the possibility that the very person who ought to know the straightforward answer—the hero Shaula destroyed—simply didn't remember anything at all, from the very start.

"Even so, if we leave it at that, we get nowhere. Miss Shaula, do you truly remember nothing at all? Even the smallest detail would do."

"Even if you say that, ssu. I just Hell's Sniped anyone who came near the tower, one after another, and the Witchbeasts outside cleaned up the bodies, ssu."

"Hmm, but isn't that strange? To begin with, this 'Exam' is supposed to decide whether the tower's knowledge gets shared, right? If the question refers to something that happened after Shaula-san's watch over the tower started, the timeline's off."

Shaula pouted, insisting she had no idea, but Anastasia pointed out the oddness in her statement. Everyone but Shaula nodded in agreement—that made sense.

Indeed, it seemed unlikely that a question inside the tower would concern something that happened after the tower's management began. Which meant that, naturally, the time when "Shaula destroyed the hero" fell before the tower's construction.

"In other words, it's from before you started firing at random, I suppose. Come now, try to remember, I suppose. It's because all your nourishment goes to your chest and backside that your memory's so poor, I suppose."

"Mama picked out this look for me, ssu~. But but, even if you tell me to remember, honestly nothing's coming to me, ssu. Before the tower was built, right, ssu?"

"Yes, that's right. Before the tower was built. Back then, is there anyone you have any idea you might have killed... destroyed?"

Gathered around the stairs, everyone focused on Shaula, desperately trying to jog her memory. But Shaula, bearing the full weight of everyone's expectations, only groaned "ahiiin~" with no sign of any results.

"True or not, you've supposedly been around since four hundred years ago, right? If we just rattle off famous people's names from back then one after another, wouldn't you have killed two or three of them?"

"Master, what do you take me for, ssu? I'm a maiden who nibbles on flowers with her friends, ssu."

"That's not a maiden, that's some kind of caterpillar."

"Subaru, I really think that was harsh just now. If it's something she doesn't want to remember, you don't have to force her to..."

"Emilia-tan's kindness is a total virtue, and a real charm point, but this one's the type that gets worse the more you spoil her! I can tell! Because we're the same kind!"

He declared this with his chest thrown out proudly, but to begin with, Shaula wasn't failing to remember because she didn't want to—she simply had poor recall, plain and simple.

Given that the group carried a number of thorny issues concerning memory, it was a topic they'd generally want to handle delicately—but Shaula's case was another matter entirely.

"Honestly, just throwing out names at random isn't necessarily wrong, is it? Not sure how any of them hitting the right answer would interact with the monolith, though."

"True, Natsuki-kun's got a point. Even once we know the answer, how exactly does that translate into touching the 'most radiant'?"

Given that the monolith he'd touched had vanished and been judged, apparently, a failed "Exam," it stood to reason that the final method of answering would be "touch the correct monolith."

The problem lay in how to find that "correct monolith," and it didn't seem like digging through Shaula's memory alone would reveal that.

"But like, if you all just keep thinking forever, nothing's gonna get done, is it~? The naked big sister's being all cooperative and everything, so I think you should just ask her whatever seems like she'd know~."

So Meili cut in toward the adults stumbling right at the starting line of the problem, still clinging to Shaula's bare shoulder. She toyed with Shaula's scorpion-tail hair with one hand, gazing at the cluster of monoliths with a bored expression.

"There's no witchbeasties around, and the talking's not going anywhere, so it's not much fun here at all~. I wanna hurry up and get back to the mansion~."

"—"

Everyone was left speechless by Meili's words, which rather deflated the mood in their own way. A moment later, Meili turned back around, asking, "What's wrong~?" and Subaru patted her head.

"...Something wrong~?"

"Nah, I just figured you were right. Yeah, you're right. This place is nothing but sand, and on top of that there are scary Witchbeasts prowling around outside. Let's wrap this up quick, solve every problem... wake Rem up, gather every way there is to help the people in trouble, and get out of here fast."

Hesitating out of anxiety before even trying was a waste of precious time.

That, he felt, would play right into the hands of whatever spiteful someone had set up this so-called "Exam."

"Master, Master. Actually, right next to that little squirt there's another head that's real easy to pat, ssu."

"I told you already. You're the same type as me—spoil you and there's no limit to how far you'll slide into ruin. So from here on out, we're doing this Spartan-style. Remember, quick."

"Aww, ssu."

Puffing out her cheeks in discontent, Shaula looked thoroughly sulky. Then again, within a mere ten-odd seconds she'd already forgotten about it and started humming to herself, so she was easy enough to handle.

"Well then, we've had a request from a young lady as well. Let's not begrudge ourselves the effort of trying out the possibility right in front of us."

"Mm, that's probably for the best. Being able to fail as many times as you want—that's a pretty easygoing arrangement. Most of the time, life's a one-shot deal, after all... it's a kind sort of question."

Spurred on by Meili in effect, Julius and Anastasia both came to agreement as well.

So, once more, it was time to try jogging Shaula's memory of "the hero destroyed and forgotten by Shaula."

"Well anyway, as far as names you might recognize... let's see. Oh, what about Reid? The first 'Sword Saint,' that guy—didn't you kill him or something?"

"Hiiiiiiiii!!"

"Kyaa!" "Batting cleanup, playing third!"

The moment he brought up the name so casually, Shaula let out a shrill scream and leapt backward. The force of it was so great that Meili, unable to hold on, went tumbling off—and Subaru dove to catch her.

He set Meili down safely on the ground without incident, and by then Shaula, who'd fled in a panic, had shrunk to a small figure clear on the far side of the room.

"Hey! My bad! I don't really get it, but come back!"

"D-don't say something that scary, ssu. Master, you're really mean. The worst, ssu. A maiden in crisis, ssu. This is a liability issue, ssu."

Even while cracking jokes, Shaula's voice as she walked back was thin and trembling. She was putting on a brave face, but not quite managing to pull it off. What drove that was unmistakable fear, dread.

Naturally, there could only be one person that was directed at.

"What, was the first 'Sword Saint' really that scary of a person?"

"Nonsense. He's the man from whom Reinhard and Wilhelm-sama, House Astrea itself, descend. Beyond his skill with a blade, there's no doubting he was a man of fine character. It's true the anecdotes passed down describe him as bold and free-spirited in ways that don't quite overlap with Reinhard and the others, but... otherwise, the whole history of House Astrea down to this generation would be distorted, wouldn't it."

"Well, but, you know, if you dig into history, plenty of great rulers turn out pretty awful once you look at them from a different angle—happens in Japanese history too. Compared to that, this is a pretty mild suspicion, if anything..."

"Honestly, this is getting us nowhere. Fine. This is something that'll be settled clearly enough if we just let our living witness here speak. Come now, let her tell us."

Julius came at Subaru—who was trying to picture Reid's character from Shaula's reactions—with tremendous force. On top of that, he brushed off Subaru's attempt to hedge with an outright laugh.

For the first time in a while, Subaru caught a glimpse of the sarcastic knight from the very start of the Royal Selection. Subaru understood now that his attitude back then had been him playing the villain—but seeing this, his thought was that some part of it really had been genuine after all.

In any case,

"Your impressions of the first 'Sword Saint,' Reid Astrea. Miss Shaula, I'd like to hear your opinion, frankly and without reservation."

"He was human garbage, ssu."

"I'd like to hear your opinion, frankly and without reservation."

"Don't just pretend that didn't happen!!"

Subaru shoved Julius's shoulder to make him face reality, since he'd tried to let the inconvenient part slide right past. Pointing at Shaula, who now wore a wicked grin, Subaru went on.

"There, listen up. The historical truth you wanted to know is right there. A living witness. Have her tell you all the tales you want about Reid Astrea, that man of fine character and superb swordsmanship."

"...To a greater or lesser degree, anyone with outstanding talent naturally has confidence in themselves. That's not something to be condemned for—if anything, it's something to take pride in. And when you're talking about a swordsman of the very highest rank, one whose name endures in history, that kind of behavior, well, considering the context of the era, is entirely reasonable—"

"First time I've seen you this desperate."

Even Julius himself seemed unsure how convincing any of this actually was, stammering through it.

Setting aside Julius, who seemed to have been somewhat betrayed by the history he'd once admired, Shaula's "Truth About Reid Astrea" kept pouring out without stopping.

"Well, anyway, he was a nasty piece of work, ssu. A personality like a bad kid who just grew up without changing, loved picking on the weak, ssu. I mean, from that garbage's point of view, pretty much everyone counted as weak, so no matter who he fought, it was bullying the weak, ssu. I got wrecked something fierce too, ssu."

"But Shaula, you're that strong, and you still got wrecked one-sidedly? Oh, but four hundred years ago, were you still small?"

"I've been like this since I was born, ssu. So I haven't changed from back then to now, ssu... that guy was just on a whole different level, ssu. Seriously the worst, ssu."

Garbage this, the worst that—this past hero was getting quite the earful.

Whether it was because bitter memories kept flooding back, a dark undertone never left Shaula's manner. It was exactly like someone who'd been bullied recalling the deeds of their bully.

"That garbage ought to remember, ssu. That plain, obvious fact—that the one who did it forgets, but the one it was done to never, ever forgets... ssu."

"Since I've got Reinhard as a real-life example, I'm not that surprised, but getting the better of you takes a real monster too—Reid Astrea, huh."

"He was seriously the absolute worst, ssu. But out of ten fights, I could at least make him use both hands maybe once, ssu. I didn't just take it lying down the whole time, ssu."

"...I see."

There was nothing else to say to that. Not winning once out of ten fights, but making him use both hands once out of ten—was that a high bar or a low one?

Subaru couldn't imagine getting Reinhard to use both hands even once in a hundred tries, so he decided to accept that this counted as putting up more than enough of a fight.

"Well, let's put Reid aside for now. If you didn't destroy him, seems pointless asking about him anyway."

"—. ——. Quite right. There are other, more pressing matters to attend to right now."

"Did that take you a second longer than it should have to decide what's pressing?"

Whether it was scholarly interest or mere personal curiosity was impossible to say, but Subaru judged that Julius would probably be useless for a while.

It was rough on Julius, whose illusions had just been shattered, but unfortunately Subaru currently had no reason to concern himself with Reinhard's ancestor. No matter how impressive the bloodline, Reinhard himself was already more than impressive enough on his own, so it hardly mattered at this point. And besides, at least when it came to the character of one's "father," Subaru was fairly confident he'd come out ahead.

"In that case, let's leave guessing at heroes' names to someone who actually knows their history."

"Understood. I shall take up the task with all due respect."

"I hadn't even said it was you yet, but sure, fine. Go for it. Beako, back him up."

"Understood, I suppose."

It was a fine bit of management—handing the job to someone motivated. And as support, pairing him with Beatrice, who had four hundred years' worth of knowledge if nothing else, made for a perfectly solid assist.

"So, what should we do?"

"I say we take a closer look around."

If Julius and Beatrice were going to be tied up with Shaula, then he'd rather leave the hero business to them and look into questions about the layout instead.

If there was any meaning behind the uneven arrangement of the duplicated monoliths, it wouldn't hurt to search for the pattern in it.

"Anyway, the one facing the stairs is... the original."

"The one that gives us the question, right?"

They weren't packed so closely together that you had to be careful not to bump into one, but Subaru brought along Emilia and Anastasia to look over the monoliths scattered around the room.

Checking again, the scattered monoliths came in a surprisingly wide range of sizes. That said, none of them exceeded the size of the original monolith's set at the largest; beyond that, there were countless monoliths one or two sizes smaller scattered about.

"At a glance... maybe seven or eight are the same size as the original one?"

"Maybe? Yeah, I think so too. The ones way off in the distance are all small, I think. Though touching those would send us back to square one too."

"That sounds like it's speaking from experience... ah, sorry. Never mind."

Emilia gave him such a sorrowful look that Subaru cut his unnecessary remark short. Then, back in front of the original monolith, he put his head together with the two of them to think it over.

"Touch the most radiant of the hero destroyed by Shaula... it kind of feels like it's trying to sound cool."

"It's definitely a vague sort of phrasing. Unfortunately, if the story really does rely on Shaula-san's memory, then it's a bit of an oversight as far as a question goes."

"Well, yeah, that's true."

For something billed as an "Exam," having the method to reach the answer effectively depend on someone else—and on top of that, on the very being placed there as the tower's guardian—struck him as thoroughly unfair.

Subaru's group had made friendly contact—not that they'd intended to—and as a result had gotten inside the tower without becoming enemies with Shaula. But otherwise, it would've turned into a hopeless bloodbath. Even supposing one made it inside the tower successfully, there was every chance Shaula would have had to be defeated.

"In that case, passing the 'Exam' would become forever impossible, wouldn't it."

"If the whole point was never letting anyone actually solve the 'Exam,' then that'd be spot on. As a defense mechanism—place a strong guardian, and on top of that set a nasty gate that says you can't pass if you kill said guardian..."

"But Subaru doesn't think that's it. ...Am I wrong?"

"Well, yeah."

As Subaru touched on the question of security, Emilia turned a gaze full of expectation on him. Faced with that look, Subaru affirmed it with a wry, knowing smile.

It was a look Subaru was weak to. When Emilia or Beatrice gave him that look, Subaru had no defense. Rem did it too, and Garfiel and Otto did it sometimes as well. Petra too—once he started listing them there was no end to it. About the only ones who never gave him that look were Patrasche and Ram.

"Ahh, I think I'm starting to see a pattern here."

"—?"

"Nah, never mind, just talking to myself."

His bluster didn't work on someone who could see straight through him. Of course, having companions who could see him that clearly was itself a kind of salvation.

Setting that aside, bringing the discussion back to the format of the "Exam"—

"Barring exceptions, questions are basically made with the expectation that they'll be solved. If you seriously wanted to hide something, it'd be smarter to leave no chance of it ever being found at all."

"But this place isn't like that... is that the read Natsuki-kun's got on it?"

"Shaula, the guardian of this place, said it herself, right? That this is a great library where you can obtain whatever knowledge you want. I can't imagine Shaula herself came up with a phrase that sounds that smart—she's just reciting words someone taught her, word for word. Which means the 'Flugel' who built this library and put Shaula in charge of it fully intended for it to function as a library."

The more he untangled the possibilities like this, the more the unnatural nature of the current situation stood out.

The creator of this Great Library of Pleiades must have intended for the building to fulfill its purpose. As the sieve conditions for that, they'd left behind the "Exam" and Shaula.

"So from the very start, only people who could get along with Shaula could ever use it?"

"But Shaula was told to take down anyone who came near the tower, wasn't she?"

Yes, that was exactly it.

The order given to Shaula was to "eliminate, without exception, anyone approaching the tower," and Subaru's group had only managed friendly contact with Shaula by pure chance. If, without such luck, one wasn't even qualified to challenge the tower to begin with, that would be a rather crude conclusion to draw, but—

"So what's needed is brute strength, luck, and enough charm to get along with Shaula-san? Seems to me like quite a lineup of ill-suited qualifications for the task."

"...Yeah, huh."

Lose to Shaula, get Shaula killed, fail to win her cooperation—in any of those cases, one would lose the qualification to challenge the Great Library of Pleiades at all.

It was an extreme argument, but lining up the conditions established so far, there was no other conclusion to draw.

Still, settling for that conclusion left Subaru with an inexplicably uneasy feeling.

Just then—

"Mmm. Mmmm."

"Emilia?"

"Somehow, I've got this really foggy feeling. Right around here in my chest, it feels unsettled."

Groaning troubledly, Emilia touched her own chest as she let that slip. Subaru's gaze nearly drifted that way without thinking, but he exercised restraint before staring at that pale skin.

Clearing his throat with an "ahem," he furrowed his brow at Emilia. "Unsettled how?"

"Is there something bothering you?"

"Actually, there is. But it might not even be related, and I thought maybe only Subaru would understand it even if I said it, so..."

"At this point I want to hear absolutely anything, so go ahead and say it. It's not like my thinking is necessarily right anyway, and looking at things from multiple angles is basically always a good thing."

"Really?"

Whatever she'd been hesitant about, Emilia's expression brightened just slightly at his words. Then she continued, "Well then—"

"When you touch a monolith, you hear a voice inside your head, right?"

"That's right. Creepy sort of mechanism, but what is it, I wonder."

"That voice, though... doesn't it resemble the one from the 'Trials of the Tomb'?"

"—"

At Emilia's sudden thought, Subaru and Anastasia both fell silent at the same moment. Though the two of them arrived at that same silence by different routes.

Anastasia's silence came from having no idea what Emilia meant; Subaru's silence, on the other hand, came from being caught off guard and then struck by realization.

The "Trials"—those were the ones at the tomb of the witch Echidna, which had existed in the Sanctuary.

A mysterious phenomenon that forced one to confront the past and a divergent present—Subaru had retired partway through, so he didn't know the details, but according to Echidna at the tomb, there had been a third Trial as well, and Emilia had overcome it.

No, in this case it wasn't the content of that Trial or its various hardships that mattered.

What mattered was the echo of it—the fact that, when facing that "Trial," the contents had likewise been announced in one's "own voice."

"Now that you mention it, that's right. Why did that slip my mind... because it's an unpleasant memory?"

"Subaru really, really doesn't like Echidna, huh."

"—!"

"Go through the experience of finding out your savior was secretly the mastermind, and you end up like me."

Since the Sanctuary, Emilia and Subaru had talked about the "Witches" only once or twice. Even when the subject of the Trials came up, Emilia tended to be vague about it, so he hadn't pressed further.

What the two of them did share was the opinion that "Echidna has an ugly personality." Emilia put it in gentler terms, but that was the gist of it for Subaru's part.

"Wow, hearing Echidna's name out of nowhere really surprised me."

"Oh, that's right. Anastasia's spirit is also named Echidna, isn't she. ...Her way of talking is really similar, too. It's strange."

"Whether 'strange' is really the way to leave that, we can save for later—but I see. So it does resemble the Trial."

From the moment he'd first heard about the "Exam," he'd already thought it resembled the "Trials."

If even the way it began had parts similar to the Trials, then perhaps some part of the system—or maybe even most of it—resembled the tomb.

"Come to think of it, the 'Trials' also technically allowed unlimited attempts, didn't they."

"And here too, the 'Exam' exists on the third, second, and first floors—three of them, right?"

"—"

Could it really be exactly that, after all? Subaru and Emilia exchanged glances, struck anew.

The name "Sage," and four hundred years of time. Looking back on that, naturally it overlapped with the era of those "Witches" as well—was it inevitable that their histories would cross paths somewhere?

"Yeah, but, sorry. Even knowing that, it doesn't actually give us the answer here, does it."

Having thought that far, Emilia cut herself off with a flustered look.

Just as Emilia concluded, even if there was a possibility that this place wasn't unrelated to the tomb, that had nothing to do with the "Exam" here on "Taygeta."

As ever, the name of "the hero destroyed by Shaula" still depended on Shaula alone—

"...Or does it? Is that not it?"

"Subaru?"

"If this place is built with the expectation of being solved, then not being able to somehow deal with Shaula means you can't clear it. Is that premise itself wrong, to begin with?"

This place was the tower of the "Sage"; that place had been the tomb of the "Witch."

Both question-setters shared a certain spitefulness in common, but if that wasn't the only thing they had in common, there was a possibility worth considering.

The "Witch" tested people with her "Trials," but she never gave hardships from which no result could ever be produced.

If the "Sage" was testing people with his "Exam," he too surely wouldn't give a hardship from which no result could be produced.

"The possibility of clearing the tower without Shaula's existence being a factor at all..."

"Natsuki-kun, if you've thought of something..."

"Shh."

Subaru, deep in thought, put a hand to his chin and closed one eye, sinking into contemplation. Anastasia, seeing a glimmer of hope in that, started to speak up, but Emilia held up a hand and quieted her.

With a finger to her lips in a "quiet" gesture, Emilia watched Subaru's mind turning, her violet eyes shining with anticipation.

Unaware of Emilia's anticipation, Subaru's thoughts kept turning.

In this "Exam," whether "Shaula" existed at all shouldn't actually matter. If anything, a challenger to the tower might well end up defeating an attacking Shaula, all without ever even learning her name. If that were the case—

"Even without knowing Shaula as Shaula, if there's still a 'Shaula'—"

"—"

"We mistakenly believed Flugel's achievement was Shaula's. The 'Sage's' great achievement was sealing away the Witch together with the first 'Sword Saint' and the 'Divine Dragon.' But the 'Witch of Envy' is by no means the sort of figure who'd ever be called a hero, and she wasn't destroyed either."

The possibility that the premise itself was wrong ended there.

Perhaps, unbeknownst to Subaru, there was some other heroic tale the "Sage" Flugel had pinned on Shaula—but the fact that neither Julius nor Beatrice had thought of it themselves felt far too unnatural. Which meant one possibility inevitably rose to the surface.

"Even without knowing Shaula as Shaula, if there's still a 'Shaula'—that's the idea."

He'd said the same thing he'd already said once before.

That wasn't his thoughts going in circles. Quite the opposite—it was proof that he'd cut away one possibility and arrived at another. And its content was—

"Beako! Come here a sec!"

Following the possibility that had struck him, Subaru raised his head and called Beatrice over.

Julius was busy talking to Shaula from every angle, struggling to pry open the door to her memories. Beatrice, standing beside him, hopped up at the sound of Subaru's voice, which clearly conveyed he'd gotten hold of something. She came running over, and—

"That expression—that's the Subaru face Betty likes, I suppose."

"You like me all the time, don't you?"

"Especially so, I suppose."

Told that without a trace of shyness, Subaru reached his hand out to the girl standing before him. Beatrice took the offered hand in return, her round blue eyes looking up at Subaru.

Those eyes were asking, "What do you want me to do?" So Subaru nodded, and—

"It's simple. —With Muraku, I want to jump up pretty high."

"...Don't tell me this is you giving up and deciding to smash through the ceiling to get up there, I suppose."

"Don't look at me like I'm hopeless. Course not. I want to look down at these monoliths from above."

"Look down at the monoliths..."

At Subaru's remark, Emilia behind him turned to look back at the monoliths and murmured that.

Not knowing what the result would be, Beatrice didn't ask any further questions. She let out a small breath and pulled the hand she held in closer.

"Muraku, I suppose."

Following Beatrice's chant, a faint, pale purple wave wrapped thinly around Subaru's body.

It was magic that pushed away the influence of gravity, sharpening one's lightness to an extreme. Even a light hop would send him a meter into the air; and if he kicked off the floor with all his strength—

"There we go!"

Still gripping Beatrice's hand, Subaru's body leapt high into the air above the room. It rose some six or seven meters, and yet his body never struck the ceiling it should have collided with.

Within that endlessly white space, the floor seemed to extend as though there were no ceiling at all. And so, from up in the air, Subaru's body could look down over the room's entire layout.

"—Just as I thought."

"Did you achieve your goal, I wonder?"

"Sure did. This place is spiteful in the extreme."

Catching Beatrice's gaze as she picked up on his murmur, cradled in his arms, Subaru nodded with a wry twist of his mouth.

Just like that, the two of them fell straight down toward the ground, but the same lightness that had helped them jump also benefited their landing. He touched down without any trouble at all, and set Beatrice—whom he'd been carrying princess-style—down on the floor.

"I've got the hero's name."

"Really!?"

Subaru said it to Emilia, who'd watched both his musing and his leap, with all the conviction he'd gained. Emilia was startled by the words, and Anastasia's eyes went wide too.

Catching the sound of their voices, Julius and the others, absorbed in their own conversation, gathered over toward Subaru's group as well.

"Big brother, you figured it out~?"

"Solved it. Along with what that spiteful examiner was thinking, more or less."

"As expected of Master, ssu! I'm thrilled, ssu! I admire you, ssu!"

Answering with a thumbs-up, Shaula, with Meili riding on her back, shook her head about theatrically. Glancing at that out of the corner of his eye, Julius turned his gaze to the cluster of monoliths.

"I've no intention of doubting you at this point. Tell me how you arrived at the answer."

"It's nothing that impressive. It's not that you all were bad at this—there just weren't that many people who could ever solve it to begin with."

In that sense, this question was spiteful in the extreme.

Getting past the obstacle that was Shaula, understanding the content of the question, and then—right from the very stage of "having any possibility of knowing the answer to the question"—the pool of eligible challengers was already narrowed down.

"The hero destroyed by Shaula—his name is Orion."

"Orion...?"

At the word Subaru spoke, everyone's faces filled with suspicion and turned to look at Shaula. But Shaula, meeting those looks, shook her head desperately. "I don't know him, ssu!"

"No, no, no, that's someone I don't know, ssu. Even if I did happen to kill him, someone who can't even make it this far in the first place being called a hero is just laughable, ssu. So I don't think I did anything wrong, ssu. How about that logic, huh! I'm smart, ssu!"

"So, I first suspected that this one here, who's exactly as un-smart as she looks, had simply forgotten. But that's not it. To begin with, the 'Shaula' in this question isn't her at all."

"There's only one Shaula, and that's me, ssu! It's the name Master gave me, ssu!"

"And the thing is, even that name Master gave you had a source it was drawn from, to begin with."

He jabbed a finger at the nose of the protesting Shaula, pushing her back as she closed in on him. Then Subaru stepped forward and stood before the original monolith.

"The origin of Shaula's name... is that, by any chance, something only Subaru knows again?"

"Not just me exactly, but not something everyone knows either. —Back where I'm from, there's a star named 'Shaula.' It means 'stinger'—and specifically, the stinger of a 'scorpion.'"

Shaula had insisted rather forcefully that her hairstyle was a "scorpion tail"—had that been a hint in its own way, or was it just Shaula's natural obliviousness? Either way, there were several details pointing toward the association "Shaula" = "scorpion" = "stinger."

"According to the legend, the hero Orion got a little too full of himself, and as punishment a scorpion was sent to sting him to death, and he became a star. And the scorpion that killed Orion became a star too, as a reward for that feat—and even now, in the sky, the story goes that Orion's still scared of the scorpion..."

"When Subaru breaks it down, even a heroic legend ends up sounding kind of pathetic, I suppose."

"Anyway, there's this concept of a constellation, where you liken a group of stars to the shape of a person or an animal. Or you could call it an asterism, same idea. —And when you look down at the monoliths from above, well."

With Beatrice's magic making him light on his feet, he'd leapt up and looked down over the cluster of monoliths.

Black monoliths scattered across a white world—the coloring was the exact reverse of the usual, but it formed a chain of black stars rising up against a white world, a shape he recognized.

Monoliths the same size as the original—eight of them in total.

Both the number and the arrangement matched the principal stars that made up the constellation Orion.

And if the phrase had ended with "touch the most radiant"—

"The original monolith sits dead center. Well, I figured that was around where Alnilam would be, and tracing the shape of the constellation from there... following Orion out—"

"And then?"

"'Most radiant' turns out to be a surprisingly tricky phrase. See, stars actually shine in different ways—some are steadily bright, and some only flare up strong every once in a while. In that sense, Orion has two stars that could both count as 'the most radiant'..."

Looking straight down from above, there were two: Orion's right shoulder, 'Betelgeuse,' positioned to the upper left, and Orion's left knee, 'Rigel,' positioned to the lower right.

'Rigel' was the one that shone consistently bright, while 'Betelgeuse' was a variable star that occasionally flared with intense light.

An answer that could go either way wasn't a particularly elegant one, but—

"If it were me, I think I'd go with 'Rigel.'"

The name "Betelgeuse" carried some unpleasant memories tied to a similar-sounding name, after all.

"—"

Having settled on the answer within himself, Subaru reached out and touched the monolith at Orion's left knee, 'Rigel.'

There was no guarantee this was the answer. But it probably had to be the right one.

And at the same time, he understood something.

The sheer, deep-seated spitefulness of whoever had devised this "Exam"—and the resolve he'd need for the height and severity of whatever obstacles awaited on the second and first floors.

"—"

The white room, blindingly, was wrapped in light.

Leaving both sound and scenery behind, everything went howling past, and then, eventually—

"...Whoa."

When the light cleared, Subaru and the others found themselves standing stock-still in the middle of a room enclosed by countless bookshelves, within a stone-built space—a building that extended on from the tower.
