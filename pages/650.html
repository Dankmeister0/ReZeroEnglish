Arc 8 Chapter 34: "I Won't Forgive You Unless You Explain Yourself"

The morning of departure for the imperial capital of Lupugana had arrived.

At the gates of the fortress city of Garkla, evacuees from the capital and its surrounding areas continued arriving one after another even this morning, their numbers swelling to a point that even a city built to hold large crowds could no longer contain them all.
Everyone was run ragged dealing with that situation, but unfortunately, it wasn't a problem that anyone unconnected to the Empire could lend a hand—or a word—to.
Precisely because of that, they had to see through the task entrusted to them, properly and completely.
Properly, and completely—

"It's terribly, terribly hard, sir... but there's nothing I can do to help, sir."

Watching the small, pink-haired boy hunch his little shoulders in frustration, Emilia could almost feel the pain he was going through as if it were her own, and she clenched her fist.
Emilia understood all too well the anguish of not having the strength to do what you wanted to do.
And this boy—Schult—was still a young child besides. Emilia herself remembered a time, when she was small, when she couldn't be by the side of someone important to her.
So she understood Schult's feelings, painfully so.

"Emilia-sama, please, I beg you, sir. Please help Priscilla-sama, and Al-sama, and Heinkel-sama, sir."

His round eyes brimming with tears, Schult pleaded with Emilia.
For a child this small to hold back his tears and ask someone to save the people he loved most—that took real courage.
It was something Emilia herself had never been able to do. So she respected Schult's courage all the more.

And this time, she would bring Schult back a different ending than the one Emilia herself had once known.

"Of course. Leave it to me. Thank you for asking."

"Emilia-sama..."

"Because now, whatever Priscilla says to try and push me away, I can shoot right back that I was properly asked to come, so there!"

Thumping her own chest, Emilia declared this to put Schult at ease.
Hearing it, Schult's already-round eyes went even rounder, and then his expression brightened at once.

"Yes, sir! Priscilla-sama isn't honest with herself, so please, talk back to her all you like, sir!"

Even if it was forced cheer, some of the strength had returned to Schult's voice. Determined to live up to his words, Emilia nodded back firmly with a "Right!"

"Ehh, so reliable. But also so reckless. Bee-tan should keep a proper eye on her."

"...I intend to, whether you tell me to or not, I suppose."

Beside Emilia and Schult as they spoke, two short girls were exchanging words of their own.
Beatrice, accompanying Emilia, and Utakata, accompanying Schult. The two of them watched Emilia and Schult's exchange with satisfaction, then traded glances with each other.

"On our end, it's probably not the least bit reassuring either, I suppose. Best bet is probably for us to keep our heads down and stay small with that Schult fellow so we don't get spotted."

"If it comeS to it, U will protecT Shu and fighT. Bee, go do yoUr besT with Su and the resT."

"...Honestly, aren't you just so reliable, I suppose."

So, despite the difference in appearance, the two of them—whose inner ages didn't quite match their looks—swore to do their best, each in her own way, exchanging their resolve for the battle that would decide the fate of the Vollachian Empire.

And, a little ways off—

"—Well then, I'm off, big bro! Serena-nee!"

Medium called out, bright and high-spirited, flashing a valiant smile at the two seeing her off.
Medium was joining the group pushing into the capital, and seeing her off were, of course, her older brother Flop, and the nostalgic, great benefactor she'd reunited with on the battlefield—Serena Dracroy.

Serena, who had long looked after Flop and Medium since their childhood, saw Medium off to the battlefield with the same beautiful features she'd always had, white scar and all.
It was just like it had been more than five years ago, when the siblings Flop and Medium had left her care to set out on their own and become independent.

"To think that Medium wouldn't just surpass my height, but my rank as well. If you settle safely into becoming His Excellency's consort, you'd best be properly grateful to me."

"Mou~, I'm trying not to think about that right now! I'm about to go see Bal-nii, I can't be thinking about Abel-chin's stuff right now!"

"Indeed. You were never a girl clever enough to juggle more than one thing at once. Which is exactly why—"

Gently, Serena stepped closer and cupped Medium's cheek with her hand.
Serena was tall for a woman too, but Medium had grown even taller than her. And yet, in front of her like this, being touched, Medium found herself back in the feelings of the girl she used to be.
On that day of departure long ago too, Serena had touched Medium's face just like this. She touched the same spot where her own white scar sat, tracing it gently with her finger.

"I envy you—that you can wish, without hesitation, to speak with that man."

"Serena-nee..."

"With both Balleroy and Miles, I failed to exchange final words. This time too, it seems I won't get the chance to speak with Balleroy either. ...Whether that pains me, or relieves me, even I can't say. It's rare that there's much I fear."

Fearless—that was Medium's impression of Serena.
In fact, she'd heard that when Serena took the family headship from her own father, and he screamed curses at her as he carved a scar that would never fade into her face, Serena hadn't even changed her expression.

"That's a lie. It hurt, and my father's words wounded me as well. Only blood flowed where tears should have."

"Hmm? I wonder about that. When something really hurts, doesn't the blood usually come with tears too? In which case, Count Dracroy wasn't crying at all, which means..."

"Quiet. Be silent."

Flop tilted his head, only to be silenced by her cold voice, and Serena, still touching Medium's face, narrowed her eyes and looked at her as if cherishing her.

"The scar on my face is something I had to gain in order to be reborn. I do not wish the same scar upon your face, but I do wish for you to gain something."

"...Yeah. Serena-nee, is there anything you want me to tell Bal-nii? I'll definitely pass it along."

"—. Let's see."

At Medium's resolve, her readiness to carry a message, Serena fell briefly silent, thinking it over. But being clever, she soon returned an answer to Medium's question.
What she wanted to tell Balleroy was—

"—Hurry up and go to sleep already. Miles has been complaining that it's boring without you."

It was, in every way, a message like Serena—fierce, yet full of love, heartfelt words for someone who was neither quite subordinate nor quite little brother to her.

"—Schwarz, worst case, I won't blame you if you don't come back."

"Oi, oi."

Told this by Idra with a dead serious face, Subaru couldn't help but widen his eyes.
He almost shot back that it wasn't the time for jokes, but Idra's serious expression didn't crack at all, and in the end he missed his chance to say it.
In Subaru's place, it was Hiain who raised his voice: "Don't talk nonsense!"
The lizard human blinked his surprisingly round eyes and glared at Idra.

"Oi, oi, you, what the hell are you saying! What do you mean he doesn't have to come back! Don't tell me you're telling our brother to just go and die!"

"If I don't fear being misunderstood, then yes, that's exactly it."

"Hah!? There's no misunderstanding it, then!? You traitor! We've got a traitor in our own ranks! Brother, this is bad, this is really bad!"

"Wait, wait, wait, calm down, Hiain. It's weird for Idra to suddenly say something like this out of nowhere. No way."

Idra lowered her face against Hiain's onslaught, and while Subaru felt for her given how deadly serious the mood was, he wasn't empty-headed enough to swallow it whole.
More than that, that just wasn't the kind of relationship he had with Idra.

"So, why'd you say something like that? Tell me the reason, the reason."

"...I was granted an audience with His Majesty the Emperor, along with Governor-General Gustav. That's when I heard it. His Majesty asked what he intended to do with Schwarz."

Clenching her fist tight, Idra spoke bitterly, and Subaru went "Ohh." Last night, he'd heard that Gustav was going to speak with Abel, but this was the first he'd heard that Idra had been present for it, or that she'd dropped quite the bombshell there.
Still, if Idra had been present, it was a natural enough question to ask.

Originally, the members of the Pleiades Battalion had been assembled with Subaru at the center, for the express purpose of punching Emperor Vincent Vollachia's face in.
The zombie panic had left that matter hanging in ambiguity, but once the current crisis was resolved, the civil war would have to be settled once and for all, one way or another.
That said, questioning Abel about it directly took no small amount of nerve.

"I-Is that true...? So, what did the Emperor say he'd do with our brother?"

"His Majesty said whether Schwarz lives or dies after the war depends entirely on his performance..."

"W-what a joke...! That's just up to the Emperor's whims, isn't it!"

Hearing what Idra brought back, Hiain's voice shook with anger.
In Subaru's mind, he could picture Abel saying that, and roughly guess his intent, but to those who didn't know Abel's true character, it would surely sound like an excuse to dispose of Subaru after the war, once a pretext could be found.

"So, he told me to die, is that it?"

"Huh? You mean that's basically him betraying our brother and siding with the Emperor—"

"No, I mean he's telling me to fake it and run, right?"

Subaru cut off Hiain, who was taking things too literally, and at his words Idra nodded.
Idra lowered her eyes, looking frustrated.

"The one who united the imperial citizens amid the chaos of the dead rising one after another was His Majesty the Emperor. Once this crisis is put down, the seeds of discontent that led to the civil war will wither away, roots and all. ...If you survive, Emperor Vincent's throne will be secure."

"W-what if our brother pulls off some huge feat!? Like, taking down the enemy's boss himself, something that impressive—"

"Even then it would be difficult. Even if His Majesty acknowledged the achievement, you might be kept alive but sidelined, only to be assassinated somewhere down the line once the matter's died down."

"This is horrifying all over again, the Vollachian Empire..."

The fact that Idra and Hiain's line of thinking wasn't overthinking it at all—that was just the way of this country.
At the very least, Subaru wanted to hope that Abel's methods of rule would soften somewhat going forward, but changing Idra and the others' perception of things drastically wasn't going to happen right now.
Still, sorry as he felt to Idra for worrying about him, and to Hiain for searching for a way out of this—

"No playing dead. And of course, no actually dying either. I'm coming back properly. There's stuff I have to tell everyone, after all."

"Schwarz..."

"Don't make such a serious face. I don't know if everything will wrap up nice and neat... but I do think the worst outcome can be avoided."

Either way, when it came to the "succession dispute" that worried Idra and the others most, the very premise underlying it was about to collapse.
What would happen to his relationship with his battalion comrades after that was a worry for later—for now, all Subaru could do was offer his sincere, heartfelt apology.

"I'm coming back properly. —So, quit hiding in the dragon carriage, Weitz."

"Guh..."

At Subaru's call, a muffled voice answered.
Idra and Hiain looked around in confusion, trying to figure out where it had come from, so Subaru sighed and crouched down.

Behind where Subaru and the others stood, there was a dragon carriage scheduled to carry the assault team out.
Underneath the carriage body, clinging to the shaft of the spinning wheel, was a tattooed man. Weitz, who had a skull tattooed on his body, reluctantly emerged from there.

"No matter how well the 'Divine Protection of Wind Evasion' keeps the shaking down, if you cling to a spot like that for a whole day, you're just going to fall off and die like a normal person."

"I entrusted my life to you already... If I die for your sake, I have no regrets..."

"If you die falling off somewhere without anyone knowing, that's not dying for my sake!"

Weitz, his sense of comradeship firing in entirely the wrong direction, fell silent with a "Nguh..." at Subaru's point.
And then, Hiain and Idra both stepped forward too.

"You, weren't you supposed to obediently guard the place our brother's coming home to!? Don't try to sneak ahead of us, you traitorous bastard!"

"Quiet. Don't lump me together with that unpleasant bearded man..."

"Stop treating me like a traitor! You just found out that wasn't true, didn't you!"

"Alright, alright, calm down! Stop fighting! Get along!"

Scolding the three of them as they fell into their usual bickering, Subaru put his hands on his hips.
Yes—these were people he'd grown so used to that this felt like the normal state of affairs. But he needed them to stay behind in the fortress city and prepare for the coming battle against the horde of zombies.

Strong, sturdy, and famously hard to kill—the Pleiades Battalion was now the single most dependable force in the whole Empire for a fight against zombies, where you couldn't kill and couldn't afford to be killed.

"I'm counting on you all to protect everyone here. I've got a lot of important comrades here too. Unlike you guys, ones who can't fight... well, mostly can't fight."

"Say it with more conviction, damn it!"

In his head, picturing Rem and Ram and the others staying behind in the city, Subaru thought that "can't fight at all" was a lie, but also couldn't say there was nothing to worry about if they fought—so his answer came out wishy-washy.
Hiain's voice rose sharply at that, and Subaru gave a wry laugh.

"Right, it's not about whether they can fight or not—they're people I don't want to have to fight. That's the difference with you guys. Go ahead and fight to your heart's content!"

"...I suppose I can take that as an honest show of trust."

"That's right. Weitz, I hope that settles it for you too."

"————"

Looking to Hiain and Idra, and finally to Weitz, Subaru spoke to each of them in turn. Weitz crossed his arms, closing his fiercely intimidating eyes to match his skull tattoo.
After mulling it over in silence, Weitz slowly uncrossed his arms and—

"Fi... fine, fi-, fine..., fine...!"

"Just say you understand already!"

"Fine, I understand...!"

Practically dripping with the air of a man making an agonizing decision, Weitz too agreed to Subaru's plan.
With that, Weitz slowly extended his hand toward Subaru and placed it on his shoulder. Then, in a voice unusually gentle for him—

"Since you've said it, you'd better come back, brother..."

"Oh."

Slightly caught off guard, Subaru quickly broke into a smile and nodded back.

"Got it, brother."

He wanted to answer not just the offered hand, but the trust behind it.
Hearing that, Weitz smiled with satisfaction. But then—

"Hey, hey, hey, hey, don't go copying how I call him, you thief!"

"You're just calling him that on your own... Schwarz called me back the same way..."

"Don't start some stupid argument now! Didn't we just settle something!?"

"Enough, stop it! Brothers, both of you!!"

Scolding them once more as the ruckus started up again for an entirely different reason, Subaru found himself reluctant to part, if only for a little while, with these troublesome brothers of his.

"You understand the role you must fulfill. Whether that life of yours endures past this war or not depends entirely on your performance—never forget that."

"Auau."

At the girl who nodded with a solemn face—or at least, one she presumably meant to be solemn—Vincent closed one eye.
The Sin Archbishop that Natsuki Subaru had been dragging around, this girl with her outlandish reputation—the very power she possessed was the specific remedy against the endlessly resurging undead.
Even granting that as fact, incorporating the cooperation of a Sin Archbishop into a strategy was something unheard of even in the Vollachian Empire, which other nations already called deranged.
To begin with, a situation in which a Sin Archbishop's cooperation could even be drawn out was itself an impossible premise.

"Your Excellency! The dragon carriages are ready! If all goes smoothly, even without the Coupled Dragon Carriages, it shouldn't take too long to reach the capital!!"

Goz's voice, loud enough to carry over a mountain, reached Vincent as he faced the girl—Spica. Of course, Goz was standing right beside Vincent, not on the other side of a mountain, so the sheer volume of it was excessive in the extreme.
Be that as it may—

"It would be pointless if, by the time you arrive, the 'Stone Chunk's' remaining strength has already run out. See to it strictly on your end as well. Tell them not to needlessly take the lives of the undead."

"A battle where neither dying nor killing is the goal is something entirely unknown to us, but I will see to it that Your Excellency's order reaches every officer and soldier! However..."

Here Goz cut himself off, clenching a fist as large as a child's head. Even without seeing his profile, it was plain as day that his face, covered in old sword scars, was twisting with frustration.
Sure enough, Goz's voice, booming and stern like the rumble of the earth, trembled with frustration as he said—

"Is there truly no way I might accompany you!?"

"Auh!"

Goz's plea, wrung out yet far too forceful for it, made Spica reel back as if she'd felt a gust of wind. Even Vincent felt the sensation of the sound trembling against his skin.
Goz's voice alone, without even his beloved war-hammer in hand, was enough to shake the world.

"But no matter how much you lament it, the decision will not be overturned."

"Your Excellency!"

"Setting you aside, the only ones capable of serving as commander are Count Dracroy, an upper count, or the Second General, Zikr Osman. Either could be entrusted with a single front, but a great war is another matter entirely."

"That is..."

"With that wretch Chisha gone, there is no one but you to whom I can entrust the whole army. Take the weight of that to heart."

At Vincent's declaration, Goz's eyes went wide and his whole body stiffened.
Berstetz was a civil official, and while Serena was capable as an upper count, she was no soldier. Zikr, too, had no experience commanding a force this large—which left no one but Goz capable of overall command. That was Vincent's assessment, made without a shred of favoritism.

"I entrust all the officers and soldiers to you. Consider that the survival of the Empire rests upon those sturdy shoulders of yours."

Vincent's single declaration, his gaze fixed straight on Goz.
Receiving it, Goz shut his eyes tight, and in that one moment of closed eyes, cast off all hesitation.
With a fierceness in his eyes befitting the name "Lion Knight," Goz pressed fist to palm before his own chest and swore his oath to Vincent.

"—. Goz Ralfon shall follow Your Excellency's every command!"

"Well done."

"Hah!!"

With that brief word, Vincent gave a nod of his chin, and Goz bowed deeply in response.
No doubt some part of him wished to accompany Vincent and serve as his guard, wielding his own mighty strength. But Goz swallowed that feeling down, and instead—

"I'm counting on you, Third General Aurélie! Protect His Excellency's person, without fail!"

"Oh, leave it to me, First General Ralfon! Now that I've been chosen for this, I'll see the duty through properly!"

Entrusted with this by Goz, it was Jamal who struck his chest with vigor.
Named as Vincent's guard and given the task by Goz as well, he flared his nostrils and flashed a coarse grin.
House Aurélie was, nominally, of lower count rank, but there wasn't a shred of aristocratic refinement about him.
And yet—

"A fine answer! That is what it means to be a soldier of the Vollachian Empire!!"

As Goz puffed out his mighty chest to see him off like that, this attitude precisely was the Vollachian way.
Thinking of it that way, Subaru and the people of the Kingdom, difficult as they'd been to handle, were people whose views one could understand and even sympathize with, but who were unsuited to actually make use of, one had to admit.

"Once more—do not become a tool that fails to function when it truly matters."

"Uu, auh."

Glancing sidelong at Goz and Jamal as they carried on loudly, Vincent turned to Spica, who had her ears covered, and said this to her.
At Vincent's words, Spica scowled and pursed her lips.
For some reason, it struck Vincent that the expression resembled the one Medium had worn last night, scolding him over his conduct, and he let out a sigh.
And then—

—Three dragon carriages were setting out, a number of personnel that seemed vastly smaller compared to the people streaming into the fortress city by beast-drawn cart and on foot.

And yet, without question, it was an arrow of hope, sent forth to prevent the "Great Disaster" that had struck the Vollachian Empire—no, the "Great Disaster" that was, from here on, poised to destroy the Empire entirely.
Each of them, carrying their own resolve and the hopes of many, was returning to the capital now transformed into a city of the undead, there to strike down the mastermind behind it all, the "Witch" called Sphinx.
And—

"...Big brother being part of that group—it feels like a lie."

Katya murmured this from where she'd wheeled her chair to the window, gazing down at the scene below. Rem, beside her, caught the words and softened her eyes.
Katya's brother, Jamal, was one of the personnel heading to the capital as Abel's guard.
For Rem, her memories of first meeting him weren't especially good ones, but given that he was Katya's brother, she couldn't treat him too coldly either—he was someone she found difficult to deal with.
Still, being chosen as an attendant for the final phase like this meant Abel must trust him, apparently.

"My brother's simple, that's all. Easy to handle, easy to use as a pawn to throw away—that kind of reason."

"Even for Abel-san, surely he wouldn't be that coldhearted... I'd like to think."

"I wonder. At the very least, that's what my brother thinks himself, and... y-yesterday, you heard what he said before he left too, didn't you?"

"Um, that's... yes."

Under Katya's upward glare, Rem, unable to talk her way out of it, nodded.
Last night, Jamal had visited Katya, and after reporting that he'd been chosen as Abel's attendant and would accompany him to the capital, he'd said to her, laughing—

"Don't worry, Katya. I'll do His Excellency proud and go out in a real blaze of glory! That way, even without that Todd bastard around, you won't have to struggle to make ends meet, thanks to the reward money!"

"He didn't seem to mean any harm by it at all."

"E-even without meaning harm, some things are still awful, aren't they... why do both my brother and Todd, treating lives, so carelessly, really, just..."

Presumably, in his own way, Jamal had said it out of concern as an older brother for his sister's living situation, but Rem earnestly wished he'd considered Katya's feelings over her livelihood.
Having already lost her fiancé Todd, if Katya were to lose her own brother Jamal as well, she would truly, in every sense, be left completely alone.
That was something even Rem, standing by her side as a friend, couldn't fill in her place.

"I felt the same way, when I was reunited with my sister... reunited with her."

Since she had no 'memories,' reuniting with Ram had, for the current Rem, felt like meeting her for the very first time.
But even without 'memories,' the fact that her 'soul' remembered Ram's existence had made Rem certain that she wasn't alone in this world.
For Katya, Jamal ought to be that same kind of presence.
And yet the fact that Jamal thought the way he did was, by now, simply a failing of the culture that was the Empire.

"Whatever kind of emperor Abel-san is, I'm not terribly fond of the Empire..."

"...Funny, neither am I. Not that I've got any country or place I'm particularly fond of, either."

"At the moment, that's true for me as well."

According to Subaru and Ram, Rem's homeland was apparently in the neighboring Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica.
Given how often Subaru ran the Empire down, the Kingdom was probably at least somewhat more comfortable to live in by comparison.
Still—

"—once this battle is over."

Would she go there, to that Kingdom, herself?
She didn't want to be parted from Ram, and she didn't doubt that the Kingdom was her homeland. And yet, for the Rem of now, there were more things—and more people—she knew in the Empire than in the Kingdom.
She couldn't quite properly picture herself in an unfamiliar kingdom.

"...Rem, looks like they're leaving."

Suddenly, Katya spoke up to Rem, who had drifted into thought.
Called back to herself, Rem looked out the window in the same direction, and saw the three dragon carriages bound for the capital just setting off.
Spica was aboard, and so was Abel, and Subaru too.

"Did you see him off properly?"

"We spoke, at breakfast. What about you, Katya-san, with your brother...?"

"I said whatever I wanted to say. Getting angry wouldn't do any good, but I got properly angry anyway."

Turning her face away, Katya recalled the argument she'd had with Jamal and made a bitter face.
Still, in her own way, she must have felt she'd said everything there was to say to her brother, heading off to such a dangerous place. As if to say precisely that, Katya turned only her eyes toward Rem.

"Did you say what you wanted to say, properly?"

"...What I wanted to say?"

"You seem kind of sulky, is all."

Given this rather unwelcome assessment, Rem blinked.
"Sulky" was the kind of word you'd use for a pouting child. In the first place, Rem wasn't sulking the way Katya was implying at all.

"There's no reason for me to be sulking, either..."

"If that's true, then fine."

"————"

Struck by how oddly pointed that was, Rem found her lips sealed shut against Katya's words.
The way she said it made it sound as though Rem were lying. She wasn't sulking, and there was no need for her to say anything more than she already had, surely.

Subaru had Emilia with him, and Beatrice too.
Spica and Abel were accompanying him, and even that incredibly strong wolf human, Halibel, was going along. Surrounded by such dependable people, he was surely completely at ease.
So—

"I—"

"————"

"I'm regretting it."

Still with her face turned away, Katya moved only her eyes toward Rem, her lips moving hesitantly.
There was no need to ask what that meant, or what feelings lay behind it.

Shutting her eyes tight, Rem reached toward the window through which the two of them had been watching the scene below, and threw it wide open with all her strength.
At once, the cool morning breeze poured into the fortress room, brushing against Rem's blue hair.
Mixed into that breeze was a smell that could hardly be called refreshing. Toward the dragon carriages now heading toward the very place that smell originated from—

"—Come back, and explain yourself, no matter what!!"

With that, raising her voice loud, Rem hurled her wish toward the dragon carriages growing distant.
She couldn't bring herself to say she hoped he'd come home safe. She couldn't bring herself to wish him luck in battle, either. And yet, pushed on by Katya, she couldn't choose to say nothing at all.
What came out, straight from her conflicted heart, was that.

Just what was Natsuki Subaru, to Rem, in the end?
Desperate for Rem's sake to that degree, and yet with Emilia and Beatrice at his side, risking his life for the Empire, drawing everyone's glares for Spica's sake—what was he?

"I won't forgive you until you explain that to me."

It wasn't because of the nose-wrinkling, wicked smell drifting on the wind.
It was because she wanted an answer—wanted to know what title she could give, in the newly-formed 'memories' of a Rem without 'memories,' to this person who seemed to exist everywhere within them.

Whether Rem's loud voice was swallowed by the wind, whether it properly reached him at all, she couldn't say.
But from the rearmost of the three departing dragon carriages, through an open window, she saw a small hand—a child's small hand—wave back toward her.

"How silly. Making such a happy face."

—Watching that, Rem thought she heard Katya, beside her, let out a sigh like that, looking at her profile.
