Arc 6 Chapter 21: "The Monolith's Challenge Letter"

Shaula's words of welcome—even knowing they were meant for someone else entirely, Subaru ultimately decided not to deny it, and simply accepted them.

There was also Beatrice's advice to consider. In truth, there was no doubt that letting Shaula go on mistaking Subaru for Flugel worked in their favor. If she turned hostile and things came to blows now, there was no telling whether they wouldn't simply be swept away by her overwhelming strength. Having once been treated with such warmth, Subaru, at the very least, would find his own hand stayed when it came to attacking her. And for someone already outmatched in raw strength, that hesitation alone would be more than enough to seal his defeat.

"You don't need to worry, Subaru. Even if Shaula figures out you're not really Master after all, I'm sure it won't turn into anything terrible."

So said Emilia, who seemed to have been listening in on the conversation behind them. Emilia, her silver hair bound into a long braid, twirled its end between her fingers as she declared this to Subaru with utter confidence.

"I'm glad to have Emilia-tan's seal of approval, but...what's the logic behind that?"

"I don't really get what you mean by that, but, well, Shaula's a good kid, isn't she? She helped both us and you, so we could just become friends the normal way. And then there'd be no need to fight, right?"

"...Yeah, guess so."

It was an opinion perhaps a touch too optimistic, but being too pessimistic was just as much a bad habit in its own right. Even if Shaula learned the truth, that wouldn't instantly make them enemies. Even if the facts came to light, all that was needed was for Shaula herself to decide against attacking. And Emilia's idea—that they should simply grow close enough for her to want that—wasn't wrong.

Not out of some calculated appeal to sentiment, but because Shaula herself didn't want to fight.

"You'd best not put too much stock in Lady Emilia's airy-fairy thinking, Barusu. This is the 'Sage's' watchtower, and we'll still have to face the dunes again on the way back. There's no such thing as being too cautious."

"Honestly, Ram's always saying things like that. It wouldn't hurt to relax a little..."

"It was letting our guard down that got Ram thrown underground with Barusu and Lady Anastasia. For poor, frail Ram, that despair was unbearable...though Lady Emilia may not understand."

"Hmm, is that so? But honestly, the underground around here might not be so bad? Ever since we got to the Augria Sand Dunes, I've been feeling really great, somehow."

To Ram's caustic remarks, Emilia responded with easy good humor. As mistress and servant, Emilia and Ram's relationship had been quite good ever since a year ago. Ram's words and conduct were somewhat lacking in the respect due her mistress, but Emilia, if anything, seemed to delight in being treated that way.

For Emilia, who surely carried trauma from scorn and discrimination, perhaps it was precisely because she could sense that Ram's words held none of that kind of ill will.

"Anyway, what's this about you feeling great, Emilia-tan?"

"I wonder. Maybe the air just agrees with me? Mana can differ a little in character depending on the country or region, so I think it's something like that...the mana around here in particular seems to suit me. Though I'm not sure how happy I should be about that."

"Yeah, I guess. Not when it's a place crawling with Witchbeasts where a Witch is sealed away."

Emilia clenched a fist as if to say her strength was overflowing, but quickly followed it with a rueful, sheepish smile. Subaru sympathized with how she felt, even as he had to admit it was reassuring news.

As already noted, in a region never lacking for danger between Witchbeasts and a Witch, it was honestly a relief that Emilia's power was growing. Still, the memory of her defeat at Shaula's hands was carved into Subaru's mind, so it wasn't quite grounds for optimism.

"But the conditions were different, too. Unlike when she could attack one-sidedly from the tower, if it's at a distance where you can see each other's faces, I'm sure Emilia-tan wouldn't lose either."

"What is it, what is it-ssu?"

"No, I'm just saying you've got insanely good eyes. You hit us with pinpoint accuracy from the tower even though we were crazy far away, right? How does that even work?"

"Ohh, that's just because my needles are linked to my target through mana, so they get drawn straight to it-ssu. Hell's Snipe was Master's own invention, you know-ssu?"

"...I see. You went and taught her something she didn't need, Flugel."

Thanks to that, he'd had a truly awful time of it. He'd probably never get the chance to talk to Flugel, but if some tea-party-like opportunity ever presented itself, he wanted to give the man an earful.

And so, listening to Shaula humming away in high spirits, Subaru let out a sigh.

And then—

"Sorry to interrupt the pleasant chatter, but we've arrived at our destination."

"Oh?"

It was Julius's voice, from the head of the group climbing the spiral stairs leading to the tower's upper floor. Subaru looked up at the words of the knight, who had been escorting Anastasia by the hand, and saw that beyond Julius, standing diagonally above him, a ceiling had somehow come into view.

"No, wait, not a ceiling—the floor of the level above, I guess."

"What's up there would be the fifth level, 'Celaeno,' I suppose. The only way in or out to the outside is here—Betty and the others first entered through here, I suppose."

With the fifth level close at hand, Beatrice beside him offered that explanation. Nodding along with an "ah, I see," Subaru immediately caught himself with a "hm?" of unease.

If Emilia and the others had entered the tower from the fifth level after they'd been separated, then—

"How did Gian and the Dragon Carriage get down to the bottom floor? There's no way this staircase is wide enough for a Dragon Carriage to fit through, right?"

Subaru spread his arms in the spiral staircase he'd just climbed, checking its width. The stairway was decently wide, but even so, not wide enough for two Subarus with arms outstretched to stand side by side. An Earth Dragon's massive body might just barely squeeze through alone, but there was no way the carriage itself could pass.

Of course, making an Earth Dragon walk a staircase this long was, even if technically possible, without question not a route one ought to take.

"Is there secretly an elevator or something? If so, let's use that from now on. Being health-conscious is fine and all, but it's no good to reject the conveniences of civilization too much either."

"Sorry, I don't really get what you mean. ...But the thing about the carriage is simple. Shaula lifted it up and carried it down."

"...Pardon?"

Feeling like he'd just heard something he couldn't let pass, Subaru asked Emilia to repeat herself. She furrowed her brow at his English reply, but seemed to guess he was asking her to say it again, and continued with a "So,"

"Shaula picked up the carriage and the Earth Dragon, just like that, and brought them down easily."

"No, no, no, no, that's not possible. I mean, a carriage that size might weigh over a ton, and then add an Earth Dragon on top of that..."

Subaru's eyes went wide at the terrifying claim, but none of his companions denied it. In fact, Shaula puffed out her ample chest proudly, nostrils flaring.

"Just like she said, I'm the one who carried it down-ssu. Ahh, something that size is a piece of cake-ssu. If you leave it by the entrance, the Miasma flowing in from outside might make the lizard go funny, you know-ssu. Just a little consideration on my part-ssu. You can go ahead and praise me-ssu!"

"I appreciate it, I do, but finding out your skinny arms aren't actually skinny arms is freaking me out. A monster of strength like that...I don't think even Reinhard could pull that off."

In Subaru's book, the single most jaw-dropping human being around was, without question, Reinhard—but even he surely couldn't lift a mass that exceeded human limits.

Splitting the world with sword pressure, walking on water, coming back to life once—sure, maybe. But hoisting a dragon carriage with one hand, surely even he—

"Huh, could he actually do that, though? Now I'm getting a little worried. About whether that guy's even human."

"Mmm, I was the one you were talking about, but Master's thinking about someone else. I'm jealous-ssu."

In any case, he now understood how the carriage had been brought down to the lowest level. Since he hadn't actually witnessed it happen, the shock was somewhat muted, but he filed it away as fresh evidence of the threat posed by a furious Shaula.

Well, having talked things over for a while, the group arrived at the fifth level. As had been pointed out several times now, 'Celaeno' was the level containing the entrance connecting the tower's interior to the outside.

Deep within the tower's cylindrical structure, a single massive door had been set into place.

It was tall enough to have to look up at, its width easily nearing ten meters. It was a needlessly enormous door, and it left Subaru wondering just how much strength it would take to open—

"Could it be that, like the Trial Gates, even just getting it open is difficult?"

"At the very least, it's a fact that it wouldn't budge even when I pushed against it. Because of that, searching for Lady Anastasia and you proved impossible."

Julius nodded to Subaru, who sighed as he looked over the colossal door. It seemed this door blocked challengers' way with exactly the weight its appearance suggested.

Someone like Shaula, who could carry a dragon carriage with one hand as if it were nothing, might manage it, but for an ordinary person, even opening it was out of reach. That, it seemed, was why Emilia and Julius hadn't been able to go out searching for Subaru and the others, and had been left stewing in frustration instead.

"But it does seem to be falling apart in places here and there, I suppose."

"I think it's 'cause the sandy wind's so strong—the sand rides the wind right on in. Take even a little deep breath and your mouth gets all gritty right away, it's the worst."

Anastasia stuck out her tongue and ran a finger along its pink surface to brush off the grit. Just as she said, Subaru too could taste a sandy grittiness in his mouth, and he realized that on the fifth level, connected as it was to the outside, fine sand dust was constantly drifting through the air like powder.

"The sand of the dunes carries Miasma within it. Underestimate it just because it's only a trace amount, and it could eat away at your body from the inside. Unless we have business here, we should pass through quickly."

"Then let's do that. My goal isn't here anyway—it's the fourth level, 'Alcyone.' ...Though more stairs is kind of deflating."

"Still such a weakling-ssu~. But you can relax-ssu. The stairs between the fourth and fifth levels are way shorter than the ones between the sixth and fifth-ssu. If it were too far, coming and going would get tiresome for me too, so I took that into account-ssu~."

"Stop stirring things up, I suppose."

Amid the swirling sand dust, Shaula—running around with her arms spread wide—took a chop from Meili to the back of her head. Glancing sidelong at the scene, the group passed straight through the fifth level and made for the stairs leading to the fourth.

A faint breeze drifted through, and in the dim, yellow-hazed visibility, looking up revealed a ceiling—the floor of the level above—not so very far off; it seemed Shaula's claim of it not being far was indeed true.

And so, trading banter as they went, the group continued on toward the fourth level, 'Alcyone'—until at last they reached their destination floor.

The fourth level, too, wasn't dramatically different in construction from the fifth or sixth. Given the tower's structure, its cylindrical shape didn't waver, nor did its size change dramatically. —And yet, the change in atmosphere was unmistakable.

For one, the size of the floor at the top of the stairs was markedly different from the lower levels. Compared to the levels below, the circular floor here had been made considerably smaller. And no wonder—along the inner edge of the round building, another circular wall had been built to serve as a boundary, making use of the space quite differently from the single open floors of the lower levels.

The spiral staircase from below connected to the center of the fourth level, and the wall encircling it all around had a number of doors scattered along it, showing that multiple rooms existed on this floor.

"This is the fourth level, 'Alcyone,' where I make my home. And the 'Green Room' Master wanted to come to is right over there-ssu."

"The Green Room...?"

As Subaru stood bewildered by the changed atmosphere of the fourth level, Shaula slipped past him onto the floor and, with an exaggerated gesture, indicated the door directly ahead.

The various doors on the fourth level, unlike the fifth level's entrance, were an ordinary sort of size. They didn't look like they'd require any special strength to open, but the doors themselves carried a strange sense of wrongness.

True to its nickname, "the Green Room," the door was covered in countless green vines, giving it the look of some hidden jungle sanctuary left untouched for centuries.

"If I'm the one who wanted to come here, does that mean Rem and Patrasche are inside?"

"I don't know their names, but there's a girl and a lizard in there-ssu."

"Rest easy. There's no mistaking that it's Rem and that Earth Dragon inside."

To Subaru, who hesitated, came Shaula's testimony—of somewhat questionable reliability. Snorting as if to scoff at it all, Ram was the one who took the lead toward the Green Room.

She reached out to the vine-covered door and, without hesitation, pushed against what looked to be solid stone. Even under her slender arms, the door slid open easily, and the Green Room lay open before them.

"Aren't you coming, Barusu?"

"...I'm coming, yeah."

At Ram's testing question, Subaru kicked his unease aside and stepped forward. Following Ram, who entered the room first, he strode boldly into the Green Room after her. But the moment Subaru and Ram entered the Green Room, the door swung shut right behind them. Startled, Subaru turned to look back.

"Hey, we just got cut off."

"No need to get spooked over everything. —Seems this room limits how many people can enter at once. The room's owner doesn't seem to like it otherwise."

Saying that of the strange occurrence behind them, Ram simply pressed on ahead. Scratching his head at her explanation, Subaru quickly followed after her small back.

The Green Room lived up to the strangeness of its door—the interior, too, was thoroughly painted over in a great mass of green. Writhing plants crisscrossed the walls, floor, and ceiling in every direction, and every so often a hanging vine blocked the way, forcing him to duck beneath it.

And then, deep within that space ruled by green—

"Rem...and Patrasche."

In the recesses of the Green Room was a small space free of the tangled undergrowth. There, thick green grasses lay layered over one another, forming a bed dotted here and there with small blossoms.

On that bed adorned with green and flowers, Rem lay with her sleeping face unchanged.

No color touched her pale cheeks, and her sleeping face showed no change whatsoever. Her chest rose and fell with faint breaths, but aside from the warmth to the touch, that quiet activity of life was the only proof she still lived.

And yet, the relief he felt was enough to sap the strength right out of him.

"She's really...okay..."

"That's what Ram told you, didn't she. Or did you think Ram would lie about something to do with Rem? What possible reason would there be for that?"

"I'm not saying that, but it's only natural I couldn't relax until I saw it with my own eyes. ...Patrasche, I'm glad you're okay too."

Subaru gave a faint, wry smile at Ram's words, then walked further past Rem, sleeping on her bed of grass, to where Patrasche sat with her legs folded beneath her.

She too lay upon a bed of green, much the same as Rem's, not so different from how she'd lie on the rush matting of her stable. What was clearly different, though, was—

"...This grass—is it different from ordinary grass?"

"—"

As Subaru came close to confirm she was unharmed, Patrasche nuzzled her nose against his palm. Gently accepting his beloved partner's response, Subaru tilted his head, sensing a faint, warm pulse emanating from the green bed she lay upon.

"It's said to have the effect of speeding up healing, through a Spirit's power."

"A Spirit...is there one here? Where?"

"You call yourself a Spirit Arte user and you can't tell? This room itself is the Spirit."

"—"

Stroking Patrasche, Subaru hurriedly looked around the room at Ram's words. The overgrown vines, the moss on the walls—no Spirit's presence showed itself to his eyes, but only now, prompted by Ram, did Subaru notice the overwhelming density of mana filling the space.

It felt as if he were breathing highly concentrated oxygen—his breath and body both settling into ease.

Perhaps healing mana had a similar effect. If he calmed himself and took a deep breath, it felt as though even Subaru, unwounded as he was, could receive some of its benefit.

"I sort of get it now. This really is a Spirit. ...Can't it talk?"

"The Spirit here is an unusual sort—though really, there's no such thing as a Spirit that isn't unusual. Same goes for Lady Emilia's Great Spirit, and Lady Beatrice too...but this one in particular doesn't seem to have anything you could call a will of its own, apparently. It simply tries to heal the wounds and ailments of any creature that enters."

"Heh. Yeah."

"Something wrong?"

"Nothing much. Just thought of a Witch I know who was kind of like that."

Perhaps taking it as nonsense upon hearing the word "Witch," Ram snorted and walked over to Rem's side. Then, as if considering the elder sister watching over her younger one, vines began to stretch out right behind Ram, quickly winding and weaving themselves together into a green chair that rose to support her body.

Ram bowed her head with a "My thanks" for the Spirit's consideration and settled her weight onto the chair.

"That's kind of impressive."

"At the very least, it's certainly the most gentlemanly Spirit Ram's ever known. You could stand to learn a thing or two, Barusu—from this Spirit, and from how Sir Julius the knight carries himself."

"Either way, that doesn't sit right with me."

Chasing both examples he'd been told to emulate from his mind, Subaru stroked Patrasche. He rubbed her head, tickled the nape of her neck with his palm, and let her raised head slowly settle back down.

"Get some rest. You saved me again, and you're working way too hard. Taking a paid vacation every now and then isn't going to get you struck by lightning, you know."

"—"

At Subaru's gentle words, Patrasche curled her body up, closed her yellow eyes, and drifted off to sleep. She'd carried him all the way to the tower, and now they'd confirmed each other's safety.

That alone was enough. Patrasche had more than done her job.

"Any change in Rem? If this is a room whose raison d'être is healing, could it do something for Rem in her current state..."

"Sorry to say, but the odds are slim. Neither good nor bad...there's been no progress in her treatment. It can't heal something that's neither a wound nor an illness. That seems to be its judgment."

"...I see."

Even so, the Green Room's Spirit didn't seem to spare any effort in caring for the still-sleeping Rem. And toward Ram, who kept watch over her sleeping sister, it was quite devoted as well.

"So in the end, nothing's going to change if things stay like this."

"...If you want to change it, you'll have to see through the reason we came to the tower, won't you."

"The Great Library Pleiades, and its trials, huh."

Their original goal had been to make contact with Shaula, the "Sage" of the Pleiades Watchtower, to borrow the omniscient wisdom she was said to possess, and thereby save Rem and the remaining victims of "Gluttony" and "Lust" back in Priestella.

But the Pleiades Watchtower's "Sage" Shaula turned out to be a Sage in name only—what actually awaited them was a single strange girl who mistook Subaru for the "Sage" Flugel. The Pleiades Watchtower was, in truth, the Great Library Pleiades, and the omniscient knowledge attributed to Shaula supposedly lay dormant within the countless books stored in that library instead.

Sure enough, their goal had twisted considerably, its destination shifted in a direction no one could have predicted.

"Touch the knowledge, and get Rem back. —That goal hasn't wavered."

"...I see. Then that's fine."

Ram lowered her eyes at Subaru as he rolled his shoulders and stretched lightly. After that, she gently took hold of the sleeping Rem's hand and didn't spare Subaru so much as a glance.

Closing one eye at Ram's attitude, Subaru pointed at the ceiling.

"I'm going to go take on the trial up above. What about you?"

"Without someone watching over Rem, wouldn't that be worrying no matter how you look at it? So Ram will take on that role. The whole reason Ram pushed Lord Roswaal into letting her come along in the first place was to watch over Rem."

"That's...well, true, I guess. All right, I'll leave Rem to you, then."

"Though watching over her is about all Ram can do."

"There's meaning in the fact that you're the one watching over her."

Saying that to Ram, who was uncharacteristically putting herself down, Subaru looked once more at Rem's sleeping face before leaving the room. She existed within a dream where her expression had vanished—neither peaceful nor pained.

He reached a hand toward her forehead where her bangs fell, and touched it gently, as if to tickle her.

"Well, I'm off."

"—"

Of course, there was no reply. Ram, too, understood that she hadn't been the one addressed, and so didn't crudely interject. Satisfied by that, Subaru made for the room's exit.

On some impulse, he reached a hand toward the wall, and touching the vines, didn't forget to ask the Spirit, "Please take care of Rem and Patrasche."

And then, just as he was about to leave the room, he suddenly stopped.

"Come to think of it, you said you couldn't leave Rem alone, but you left her empty just now, right? Why'd you bother coming all the way down here?"

"—"

"Don't tell me you rushed down here to check on me the moment you heard I woke up. If something happened, I'd appreciate it if you'd let me know..."

"Just get going already."

"Huh? No, but, if there's something on your mind, you've gotta tell me..."

"Go. Now."

Overwhelmed by the fierce, demonic aura suddenly surging off her, Subaru couldn't get another word out, and had no choice but to slink out of the Green Room in defeat.

"I can never really tell what's going through Ram's head, but lately it's especially hard to figure out."

"Hmm, I don't think that's really true. Ram's surprisingly honest, for all that. And the way she tries to hide that honest side—I think it's cute."

"That's a rare grown-up-sounding thing to say...though I guess you actually are older."

"That's right, I'm the big sister here. Older than anyone else in this room...oh, wait, not quite."

"Hmph, it's simply a fact that Betty is the big sister here, I suppose. An unshakable truth no one can rewrite, I suppose. You may adore me all you like, I suppose."

Having left the Green Room and rejoined the remaining members of the group, this exchange arose. Having had the seat of eldest team member snatched away, Emilia looked frustrated, while Beatrice puffed out her slight chest with satisfaction—though honestly, neither of them was behaving like an actual big sister.

Besides, the question of who was actually the oldest among the present company was, in fact, an extremely delicate one.

"Hmm? What is it, Natsuki-kun? What, you got something to say to me?"

"Nothing really. Just thinking there's a lot of people here whose looks don't match their actual age."

"Really? I get told all the time that I look younger than my actual age, too. Kinda hard to say if I should be happy about that or not, but being underestimated has its uses too, you know."

Anastasia laughed with a shrewdly businesslike face, though there was no telling what she truly thought.

Anastasia herself was in her twenties, while her outward appearance put her at around fourteen or fifteen. But what Subaru had really meant to point out wasn't a matter of appearances at all—it was the matter of what lay inside Anastasia. At present, her insides remained hijacked by Eridna, still.

If Eridna's origins were the same as Beatrice's, entering the race for eldest wouldn't be hard at all. Though, given she'd hidden the truth this long over such a trivial point of pride, there was no way she'd reveal it now—so Subaru kept that particular observation to himself as well.

Besides, limited to the present circumstances, there was another strong candidate for the eldest-member race.

"Oh-ho, what's up, Master? Aha, I bet you got sick of that grassy smell in the Green Room, huh-ssu? I get it-ssu. I'm not too fond of that room myself, can't stand it-ssu~."

"Didn't you say I smelled pretty rank too, though? Am I fine, then?"

"Master's smell is the kind you get addicted to-ssu. Really-ssu. No lie this time-ssu."

"Shut up."

Pushing away Shaula, who sniffed and nuzzled up close, Subaru glared up toward the level above. Left on the fourth floor, or challenging the trial above—the remaining lineup consisted of everyone save Rem and Ram—

"What about you? You coming along?"

"Me-ssu? Well, there's no real need for me to tag along, but since I've got the chance, I'd love to join you-ssu. Being allowed to talk but having no one to talk to is lonely-ssu, you know. I kept quiet for hundreds of years, so I've got tons I want to say-ssu~."

Said with such a friendly smile and yet such a heartrending thing, Subaru couldn't very well be cold about it. Emilia and Beatrice, too, likely knew something of that loneliness themselves. The feelings they directed at Shaula carried a strong tinge of sympathy, and neither seemed inclined to actively push her away.

"I like this big sister, sooo, I think it'd be good to have her along."

"If we're going to take on a trial, it's only natural to have the examiner present. To keep watch for any impropriety as well, it's only right that she accompany us."

There, in an unexpected turn, came covering fire from Julius and Meili as well. With that, a majority now stood in favor of Shaula joining them.

To settle the matter once and for all, Subaru looked to Anastasia, who hadn't yet weighed in.

At his gaze, Anastasia tilted her head with a "Me?"

"I don't think it'd mean much if I stood alone against it here, and honestly there's no real reason for me to object anyway, so isn't it fine either way? And besides, well..."

"Besides?"

"That kid seems super attached to Natsuki-kun, so if we bring her along, she might let something slip about the trial. That kind of carelessness would work in our favor."

"Sorry, but I don't know a thing about the trial's contents, so that'll betray your hopes-ssu."

"Well, that's what she says, anyway."

Anastasia gave a rueful little laugh, while Shaula wore a smug, proud look as though she'd just been complimented.

Whether Anastasia's calculations would pan out well or badly remained to be seen, but for now, it seemed no one objected to bringing Shaula along.

It couldn't be denied that it felt a bit careless, but then again, letting her wander off on her own was worrying in its own right, so it really just came down to whether they kept the hazardous material close at hand or far away.

"All right. Let's bring her along. She might not be much to rely on, but I'm counting on her commentary."

"You heard the man, shortie. I'll do my best-ssu."

"He was talking to you, I suppose! What were you even listening to!"

Wearing the face of someone handed a topic that had no business being handed to them, Shaula pursed her lips. Listening to Beatrice's furious shout, the group opened the door to the room facing the Green Room, and peering inside, came upon a staircase leading straight up.

"So we're done with the spiral stairs?"

"The fourth level, 'Alcyone,' connects straight to the third, 'Taygeta'-ssu. Though I don't go up unless I've got a reason, so I hardly ever do-ssu."

"What kind of reason?"

"A maiden's secret-ssu."

Giving up on Shaula, who'd already proven her uselessness right off the bat, Subaru turned to look at Julius instead. While Subaru had been asleep, the others should already have challenged the third level.

Hoping to hear something useful, Subaru waited, and the knight gave a leisurely shrug.

"I hate to disappoint your expectations, but there's very little we understand ourselves either. The moment you climb to the third level, you come upon a room referred to as the trial, but..."

"But?"

"What awaits there is an inscrutable riddle. We couldn't even find a clue toward solving it, and were left utterly at a loss—that's the honest truth of it."

"An inscrutable riddle...?"

Unable to quite grasp what Julius meant, Subaru turned his eyes up the staircase. The floor above was dark, impossible to make out from below. Yet its stillness carried an air all the more unsettling for it.

"But it doesn't seem like anything happens just from going in. Even when we go in and out, nothing changes at all...it's just that we keep getting turned away at the door."

"To think we're not even being taken seriously is infuriating, I suppose."

Emilia and Beatrice, too, seemed to share Julius's sentiment.

In truth, for all it was called a "trial," not a single member of the group who'd taken it on had come away missing anything. It was only that the similar-sounding term "Trials"—of the Tomb—had planted a seed of wariness in them.

"Can't be helped. Nothing ventured, nothing gained. —Let's go."

"Right, that's the spirit." "That's the spirit, I suppose." "That's the spirit-ssu."

Receiving three different flavors of agreement from the three of them, Subaru took the lead and set foot on the stairs. Climbing one step at a time toward the floor above—unlike the spiral staircases so far, the number of steps leading up to the third level was about what you'd expect from an ordinary building's height.

So, contrary to how braced he was for it, Subaru found himself stepping into the third level, "Taygeta," almost anticlimactically soon.

"This is..."

The moment he entered, what Subaru felt was a total, overwhelming sense of wrongness. A lump of wrongness, or perhaps better put, a space where wrongness was the only thing that existed.

—It was a white, white place.

It should without question have still been an extension of the cylindrical tower he'd known so far, and yet what greeted Subaru at the top of the stairs was a strange space dyed white in every direction.

The spatial breadth should have been about the same as the tower's floors so far, but in this excessively white space, no wall, no edge, could be seen. Looking up, he couldn't even tell where the ceiling was; looking down, only the spot where the stairs stood gaped open in black, while the rest of the floor was so blindingly white it was frightening to walk on.

He couldn't quite register the floor as a floor, and was struck by the illusion that he might just keep falling forever if he stepped onto it. The ceiling and walls were the same—in a place like this, Subaru felt that if he ever lost sight of the stairs leading back down, he might well go mad.

And then, at the front of that white space—directly before the stairs—hung a strange, floating object.

"A stone tablet...?"

Face to face with the object, that was the impression that escaped Subaru's lips. It really was an object that could only be described that way.

A single flat, square, black slab, made of a material with an unnervingly smooth texture. If it weren't stone, it couldn't really be called a stone tablet, and yet it wasn't metal either—there was simply no other way to describe it. If he were to reach for some other, more pretentious name for it, "Monolith" would probably fit.

The silent Monolith, held aloft by some mysterious buoyancy, floated a few dozen centimeters above the floor.

It was floating—probably. The whiteness of the floor threw off his eyes' ability to judge differences in height, so even that wasn't entirely clear. But the Monolith, suspended in midair, sat there with an air of unshakable calm regardless.

Its size was about as tall as Subaru himself, and its width roughly enough for two people to stand shoulder to shoulder—if he had to compare it to something familiar, it gave the impression of a tatami mat floating in midair.

"So what exactly is this strange material?"

"That, one might say, is a device meant to present us with a riddle."

Standing beside Subaru, whose attention was captured by the bizarre sight, Julius glared at the Monolith. Perhaps having already tasted bitter defeat at this Monolith's hands more than once, Julius's profile looked somewhat harder than usual. All the others had climbed up to the third level as well, huddling close together within the white space, as though enduring its uncertain atmosphere.

"Not a room I want to linger in."

"I agree. Stay too long and you risk losing your sense of balance. Making a sudden dash for the stairs and slipping only takes years off the life of whoever's watching, after all."

"Hey now, Julius. Don't go saying things you don't need to."

At Julius's flippant remark, Anastasia puffed out her cheeks in displeasure. Judging by that reaction, it seemed she was the one who'd slipped on the way back.

But it didn't feel right to laugh at that mistake. In truth, this room had clearly been built for the purpose of throwing off human senses. It was a room that seemed like the very embodiment of its creator's rotten personality.

"So, about this all-important riddle—what do we do?"

"Um, you just have to touch the tablet. That should get it started, I suppose."

"Just touch the Monolith, huh."

"Monolith, is it. A strangely fitting name. Let's call it that from here on."

Leaving Julius to be impressed by odd little details on his own, Subaru stepped forward as their representative. Since no one stopped him, he went right up to the Monolith's side.

Up close, the Monolith didn't exactly radiate some strange sense of pressure—aside from floating, it was an entirely ordinary slab in every other respect. Thinking of it as just a floating tatami mat seemed to take the edge off the awe he'd first felt, at least a little.

"Anyway, I'm touching it. Want to count down?"

"Oh, then, I want to do it. Three, two, one..."

"Too fast, too fast! I get it, though!"

At Subaru's call, Emilia, who'd raised her hand to volunteer, began her countdown. In time with it, Subaru hurriedly turned back to face the Monolith.

And then,

"Zero—!"

In time with the count, Subaru touched the Monolith—and in the next instant, the black slab's surface lit up from within, and all at once the scene before Subaru's eyes blurred.

No—it hadn't blurred. The Monolith Subaru had touched had begun to multiply, glowing black as it did. Its surface aglow, the Monolith launched copies of itself one after another from behind it. They shot around the room at tremendous speed, scattering and hovering in place at irregular positions throughout.

Countless Monoliths arranged themselves throughout the white space, and Subaru was left dumbstruck by the change. Then, passing straight through his stunned eardrums, a voice rang out directly inside his brain.

—The hero destroyed by Shaula: touch upon that one's most glorious moment.

"—!?"

Startled by the voice that suddenly rang out, Subaru reflexively jerked his hand back from the Monolith. Staggering backward, he felt someone catch his back from behind.

He turned around. There was Julius's face. Supporting Subaru with his right hand, Julius touched his own forelocks with his left, and said,

"So, how was it? Did you come to share in our initial shock?"

"Don't go pulling nasty little stunts like that—!!"

Subaru's protest echoed through the room, only adding fuel to Julius's wry, faint smile.

In any case, the "trial" had now begun in earnest.

The Great Library Pleiades, Trial of the Third Level, "Taygeta." Time Limit: Unlimited. Number of Attempts: Unlimited. Challengers: Unlimited.

—Trial, commence.
