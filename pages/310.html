One Day II Part 3: "A Beautiful Girl, a Beautiful Little Girl, and a Beautiful Old Woman"

"So, in short, that's the whole tale of how I've come hurrying to seek your counsel, Ryuzu-san, seeing as you're likely the foremost authority on those two — or so I humbly submit."

"That's quite the roundabout way of going about it. Not that I mind being relied on myself... but when it comes to those two, my tongue does tend to grow a bit sluggish."

"Meaning?"

"It means I feel much the same as Ram and Kuu-bou do. Those two's problem is those two's problem. It's not something I much want outsiders reaching in and stirring up."

At Subaru's proposal, Ryuzu tipped her teacup with a reluctant look on her face. Even so, it was an undeniable fact that she was the key figure in this matter. Subaru wasn't sticking his nose in half for the fun of it — not to the point where he'd back off that easily.

"I get that those two's situation is complicated and a pain to deal with. I've been tangled up in it myself, if only around the edges."

"..........."

"But that doesn't mean it's something we should just leave alone, either. Sure, maybe time'll sort it out eventually... but they've probably both got it in mind to close the gap, and if it's just not working out, that's got to be frustrating — not only for us watching, but for the two of them too, for sure. If there's a way to help from outside, I think we should."

"Spoken like a true busybody."

"I've got a reputation for being oblivious and shameless, you know."

It wasn't exactly praise, but Subaru puffed out his chest proudly all the same, and Ryuzu gave a wry smile.

In the corner of the large room assigned to her, the two of them sat facing each other across a table, raising their teacups to their lips at nearly the same moment, wetting throat and silence alike with hot tea. Just then—

"Hey, got a second?"

The voice rose from a little ways off from the two of them.

Narrowing her round, violet-blue eyes, she fixed them on the two profiles as if glaring. A faint note of complaint bled into her voice — Emilia, who was in no position to be joining their conversation.

"What's up, Emilia-tan? Your angry face is cute and all, but you're gonna get wrinkles between your brows."

"If you think so, you could at least lend a hand! Honestly! Subaru, you meanie! You dunderhead!"

"Nobody says 'dunderhead' these days..."

Subaru laughed at Emilia's adorable command of outdated slang and set down his teacup. Then, looking her over anew, he nodded at the situation she found herself in.

"It's quite the spectacle, really. Like a dream collaboration between a beautiful girl and a beautiful little girl."

"Put that way, it's a bit embarrassing."

"And if Ryuzu-san joins in over there, I guess it becomes a collaboration of a beautiful girl, a beautiful little girl, and a beautiful old woman."

"Put that way, it's a bit embarrassing."

"You're fine with that!?"

He'd expected to get scolded, but instead found her fully prepared to accept it, and Subaru was taken aback. Her cheeks faintly red, Ryuzu was looking at the same thing he was.

At the end of their gazes spread a scene of countless Ryuzus — sharing the same face as the blushing Ryuzu beside him, but without the blush — surrounding Emilia.

—The twenty-six duplicates of Ryuzu, brought out from the Sanctuary.

Apart from the representative Ryuzu herself, the duplicates possessed no self-awareness, and simply waited on instructions. Though they had no assigned role, they obviously couldn't just be left alone, and how to handle them was one more headache weighing on the Emilia Camp.

Standing out as a particularly large problem was—

"Stop just watching and help me out, Subaru."

"Believe me, I'd love to, but those girls don't listen to orders from me. Only you and Garfiel can command them, right? So, y'know, work your smooth-talking magic."

"Easy for you to say... Last time I asked them to 'step back a little,' it turned into a whole disaster. Did you forget already, Subaru?"

"Like I could forget the mess where we had to put together a search party to go clear over the mountains, just three days ago."

In response to Emilia's troubled expression, Subaru pictured the chase from three days prior.

The crystals within the Sanctuary that governed the right to command the duplicates. Set up in two places — the graveyard and the research facility — they had each recognized Emilia and Garfiel as holders of command authority over the duplicates, a role the two of them still carried.

As a result, the twenty-six duplicates remained in a doll-like state with no budding self-awareness, and without instructions from Emilia and Garfiel, they barely functioned at all. Not only would they turn a deaf ear to anyone else's orders, but according to Garfiel, if left with no role to fulfill, they would apparently curl up and simply vanish — literally waste away until death.

Watching that happen just to make the headache disappear was, needless to say, far too bitter a choice.

The chase three days ago had begun, amid that very conclusion, when Emilia — not yet grasping the full extent of her command authority — let slip an offhand remark, sending the duplicates scattering from the mansion in every direction as they each tried to carry out her order to "step back a little."

The troublesome part was that the duplicates seemed to have individual differences after all, subtly varying in how they took instructions. Some took "step back a little" exactly as Emilia had meant it and simply backed off; some stepped outside the mansion; and some left the mansion entirely and ran off far into the distance.

Without Garfiel's nose and legs, they might never have managed to bring everyone back. Adorable, doll-like girls wandering about unguarded wasn't something that could be left alone, and having the duplicates spotted by someone was a fair problem in its own right, too.

"Twins or triplets might fly, but nobody's going to buy twenty-six-tuplets..."

Subaru couldn't recall the exact Guinness record from his old world, but he was fairly sure it never got as high as decuplets. It went without saying that this was well beyond anything that could be passed off as normal.

And when the conversation spread further, to the question of why they even needed to cover it up in the first place—

"Well, it goes without saying that we were made using techniques designated as Forbidden Arts. Frankly, if our true nature got out, it wouldn't end quietly."

"Yeah, figured."

"Taking a single individual as the base, and constructing something with a similar nature through a Pseudo-Od — it's practically equivalent to generating an infinite fighting force. Those who'd want it would want it badly."

Practicality aside, it was a technique that, depending on the research, could prove genuinely useful. In a sense, they were beings that could just as well be called an infinite labor force. On top of that, any possibility of rebellion was sealed off by the right of command, and when they vanished, they left no corpse behind, dissolving into particles of Mana — nothing messy left over, either.

"That's some serious bullshit, though."

"If Sū-bou feels that way, then we can rest easy."

Watching Ryuzu give a faint smile, Subaru felt an indescribable sensation.

Revulsion at someone he knew being exploited, and an ethical aversion as a matter of principle. That was the reason he felt such rejection toward the whole idea.

And yet, if he stripped all that away and considered the technique as something invisible, impersonal — just how well could its sheer convenience be resisted?

People, himself included, drifted toward whatever was easiest. Being weak really sucks, he thought.

"Anyway! So what do you actually think I should do?"

Emilia, who'd been more or less left behind by the conversation, spoke up, looking about at her limit.

The Ryuzu duplicates surrounding her weren't doing anything in particular, but they kept up a silent pressure on her that wasn't doing her mental health any favors.

Well then, what to do — Subaru crossed his arms.

"For starters, how about giving them an instruction that can't be misread, to keep them quiet?"

"An instruction that can't be misread — like what? Even just saying 'step back a little' sent them running off, so what could I even say..."

"For starters, if you just order 'sit,' I don't think there's any way to misread that — they'd just sit, right?"

"...Subaru, you're a genius."

Setting aside Subaru's private opinion that it really wasn't that big a deal, when Emilia asked, "Please, everyone, sit down," the duplicates dropped to the ground in a row.

Seeing Emilia surrounded by little girls sitting hugging their knees, Subaru couldn't help picturing something like a kindergarten teacher for a moment, though the reality of the situation was a bit more dire than that.

This too would need some clever handling eventually. There were a few proposals he wanted to make once Roswaal got back, so for now they were waiting on his return.

"There's exactly twenty-six of them, so if we gave them names based on the letters of the alphabet or something, we could get both memorability and individuality out of it, huh."

"There's that scheming face of yours again, Sū-bou."

"'Scheming' makes it sound so bad. I'm just doing my best to rack my brain so that everyone I know ends up with a happy resolution."

When he grinned, lips curling up, Ryuzu let out a sigh with an exasperated look.

Whether that meant he was trusted or not, Subaru chose to take it optimistically as meaning his smile just didn't inspire much confidence. Then, as Emilia broke free of the duplicates' encirclement and came over, he held out the teacup that had been prepared for her.

"Good work, Emilia-tan. Thanks for putting up with this every time."

"Thanks. But compared to Garfiel, what I go through is nothing. He comes to give everyone instructions at every single meal — he's really impressive..."

Taking a sip from her teacup, Emilia let out a relieved breath as she looked over the duplicates.

They remained sitting right where Emilia had told them to, but the ones who usually looked after them day to day were the representative Ryuzu, and Garfiel, who held the same command authority as Emilia.

Garfiel in particular apparently looked after them devotedly, all while grumbling about it, and it was largely thanks to him that none of the duplicates had ever starved or gotten lost.

He'd been dealing with these duplicates since back in the Sanctuary, so his experience was on another level entirely.

Though of course, that wasn't much comfort to Emilia, struggling to manage them well herself.

"Well, guess there's nothing to do but take it slow. Garfiel's amazing in his own way, but I think it'd be fine to have a slightly more advanced answer too."

"A more advanced answer?"

"Once Roswaal's back, I'll fill you in too, Emilia-tan. Until then, I need you to keep flailing around surrounded by little girls a bit longer — just to put my mind at ease."

"Ugh, you're such a meanie."

Puffing out her cheeks in indignation, the gesture was thoroughly adorable.

In any case, the plan he was drafting wasn't ready to be brought out into the open just yet. He wanted to nail down the details a bit more before announcing it and basking in all the praise himself.

"Anyway — I'd like to set the duplicate problem aside for now and get back to the topic at hand?"

"My answer's the same as before. As for me reaching out to those two myself... well, not much intention of that. Both of them seem determined not to worry this old bag of bones. In front of me, they put on a decent show of getting along fine."

"So those two have been pulling that sly little trick, huh..."

"Nobody says 'sly little trick' these days..."

Dodging Emilia's glare with a sidelong glance, Subaru mulled over the cunning of the siblings in question, based on Ryuzu's testimony.

At the very least, they shared a common awareness of not wanting to worry Ryuzu. And to that end, they were in enough sync to align their stories without even needing to discuss it.

Understanding each other that well, and yet unable to close that last step. As for where the decisive reason for that lay—

"It really is their mother, isn't it — the reason those two split up in the first place."

"..........."

"Those two's mother... you mean the one who left the two of them behind in the Sanctuary when they were still little?"

"I only know what I've heard secondhand, and I haven't even heard what she was like as a person. Well — from what Frederica told me, she was someone with bad luck, but that's about all. You obviously know her properly, don't you, Ryuzu-san?"

Ryuzu put her teacup to her lips, putting off the moment she'd have to speak. But that wasn't going to let her dodge the two pairs of eyes fixed steadily on her forever.

Soon enough she let out a deep sigh, and without turning her gaze toward them, said—

"Those two's mother — Rishia Tinzel — isn't really someone I care to make a topic of conversation."

"Someone you'd rather not remember?"

"It's not that I disliked her. Quite the opposite, really. That girl had a strangely likable nature about her... Her circumstances were unfortunate, true, but the fact that it didn't end there in misfortune is proof enough of that. Her family fell into ruin and she was sold to a slave trader; then, on top of that, her caravan was wiped out in a bandit raid. She was carried off as spoils by the bandits and bore a child... A path of misfortune straight out of a storybook, you might say."

"――――"

It was a story he'd heard once before from Frederica's own lips, but a terrible tale all the same. Beside him, Emilia was at a loss for words at the sheer pity of it. Though there was a chance that, at that last part, she'd simply gone past the limits of what she could fully grasp.

"But Rishia didn't stay unfortunate forever. She won favor within the band of bandits, and survived along with the child she bore. Though that band, too, was eventually crushed by another band of bandits, and once again she was made a plaything there."

"After all that, most people wouldn't be able to bounce back, right?"

"But she did. The bandits fell apart, and on the road she met and came under the protection of Roz-bou. She entrusted Frederica and Gar-bou to his care, and Rishia left the Sanctuary to search for Gar-bou's father."

"—Is that... so. Garfiel told me he thought his mother had abandoned them."

"...If that's what he thought, then that was Gar-bou's weakness talking, I suppose. Being abandoned — there's hope in that."

At the word "hope," so unlike her, Subaru caught his breath.

Where in that story, and how, was he supposed to find any hope in it? Before Subaru could work out an answer, Emilia, lowering her eyes, spoke.

"If she abandoned them, then that's why she never came back... and that way, there's still a chance their mother's alive somewhere — is that the kind of hope you mean?"

"..........."

"Because if she left with some purpose, promising to come back someday, and still never returned... then you'd have to start thinking about something frightening."

"That would be about the size of it, I suppose."

At the conclusion Emilia had reached, Ryuzu shook her head too, her face full of something too heavy to bear.

The true reason the siblings' mother had left the Sanctuary, as Ryuzu had told it. And the reason that promise still hadn't been kept — that led, inevitably, to the worst possible imaginings.

The scene from the past that Garfiel had apparently witnessed — the moment his mother was lost.

It lined up with that story all too clearly.

"Do the two of them actually know all this, I wonder."

"Frederica was already old enough to remember things by the time she and Rishia parted. She won't have forgotten. Gar-bou, though... who can say."

"I think Garfiel remembers too... no, I think he's been remembering. Otherwise, I don't think he could have looked at Subaru with such a clear, bright face like that."

There was no guarantee the scene Emilia pictured was the same one Subaru had in mind, but Subaru too believed that within Garfiel, the past event — his parting from his mother — had already been settled.

The problem was that this resolution hadn't been shared between Frederica and Garfiel. Or perhaps it was Frederica who carried something more deeply rooted where this matter was concerned.

In fact, it seemed more likely that Frederica was the one avoiding Garfiel. Recalling the exchanges between the two of them, that was the impression Subaru had formed.

"By the way, do you know anything about what happened to Rishia-san afterward, Ryuzu-san?"

"...I haven't heard a thing. That much isn't a lie, at least. Even I have things I'd rather not know the truth of."

Averting her gaze, Ryuzu turned her eyes away from a truth she'd likely already worked out for herself. And Subaru couldn't bring himself to be cruel enough to call that weakness.

In the teacup set down on the table, the little tea remaining rippled.

Watching the ripples slowly fade, nothing but a somehow forlorn silence settled over them.

"Still, at this rate I feel like we're gonna end up looking like a bunch of nosy gossips just digging through their business for the sake of it — what do you think about that, Emilia-tan?"

"Ugh, mm... th-that's definitely something I really, really want to avoid."

Having taken their leave of Ryuzu's room, the two of them walked down the hallway, sorting through what they'd learned so far while trembling at the thought of what would happen if none of it led anywhere.

They wanted to avoid ending up as mere gossips who'd dug up rumors for no reason. Of course, their foremost wish was to mend the relationship between the siblings Frederica and Garfiel, but such was life — all sorts of problems tended to crop up while you were busy taking action.

"But if talking to Ram and Ryuzu-san doesn't get us anywhere... aren't we basically out of options? And Roswaal still isn't back yet either."

"If we're trying not to fall back on the passive method of just letting time solve it, then the angle really does seem to be family. But this time, it's probably not about their mother's current situation — what happened to her after she left and all that isn't really the problem. The real issue is how they each felt when they parted, how they feel now that time's passed, and what each of them claimed back when they split up, right?"

"Somehow, doesn't it feel like it'd just be easier to lock the two of them in a room together?"

"I can't hide how shocked I am that you'd come up with such a violent plan, Emilia-tan."

Subaru looked startled, but Emilia, pressing a finger to her lips with a serious expression, prefaced her point with "Well, isn't it though?" before continuing—

"I think what those two need isn't so much time as a chance to start talking. They must have each spent ten years thinking through all sorts of things... so if they could just get the chance to talk it over, I feel like things would work themselves out somehow."

"Ugh, but that still feels kind of passive too. In the end, that's not so different from everyone's opinion that time will solve it. I mean, 'time will solve it' basically just means the right moment to talk will come along naturally, eventually."

"That's why I think we should just artificially create that moment ourselves, right? What I said before was extreme, but... still, I think that's basically what Ram and Ryuzu-san were getting at too. That we should leave the two of them alone together and let them handle it themselves."

Taking her finger from her lips, Emilia waggled it, still raised, as she spoke. Listening to her words, Subaru crossed his arms and furrowed his brow in anguish.

Was that really going to be enough?

It wasn't that he couldn't understand Emilia's reasoning, or her own interpretation of what Ram and the others had said. If anything, it made sense. What troubled Subaru was simply that he was worried.

Would that little bit of involvement really be enough? Wouldn't they need to set things up more carefully on their end for things to actually go well? Wasn't there a risk of it all twisting further and further, spiraling off into something even harder to fix?

"Subaru."

"Nn."

Just then, Emilia poked the forehead he'd been furrowing in thought, lightly, with her raised finger.

As reality pulled Subaru back, Emilia looked up at him from close by, eyes turned upward.

"I know you're the worrying type, Subaru, and that you work really hard for everyone, but—"

"Say it like that and I'm gonna get embarrassed..."

"But I worry about you, Subaru, just about as much as you worry that way about everyone else. I want you to believe it'll be okay, without carrying every single thing on your own shoulders like that. Because I'm sure those two will be okay too."

"...You think so?"

Reduced to the simple label of "worrier," Subaru felt as though whatever had been weighing on his chest had finally dropped away.

Whatever it was he'd been clutching to his chest, felt like he was carrying on his back — it was, in the end, that kind of shapeless burden. One he'd taken it upon himself to shoulder, uninvited, at that.

"I'd be happy if, once in a while, you'd just trust what I say and go along with it."

At Emilia's proposal — or rather, her assertion — Subaru let the tension drop from his shoulders and breathed out.

It might end up settling into a resolution somewhat different from what he'd hoped for, but as things stood, that was probably the outcome that best suited both their opinions and his own goals.

"All right. Okay then, let's figure out how to make that work..."

"—Oh, if it isn't Natsuki-san and Emilia-sama. What are you two up to?"

No sooner had he decided to adopt Emilia's plan than the interruption came.

Coming toward them from up ahead was a gray-haired young man, his arms loaded down with a mountain of documents. Recognizing him, Subaru put a hand to his chin as if in thought.

Now that he thought of it, there was one more key figure in the mansion — irrespective of anyone being away — whose thoughts they hadn't asked for yet.

Thinking that, and then imagining just how useful he might be in breaking through their current impasse, Subaru nodded.

"All right. Okay then, let's figure out how to make that work, and put it into action."

"Mind telling me why you just resumed that conversation like I'm not even standing here!?"

A scream familiar enough by now to be tiresome rang out loudly through the Miload mansion.
