Arc 9 Chapter 55: "The Perpetrator of the Domain"

—Aldebaran's "Domain" is an Authority tied to a Witch Factor.

But Aldebaran is not a Witch Factor's owner.

The "Witch" called this an exception, a special case. But Aldebaran thought it deserved no such tidy phrase—it should rightly be called theft, or a missing number.

As a phenomenon, there was precedent for it. The Witches of Sin, for instance, who had once synced with a Witch Factor and gained an Authority, retained that same power even after death, once they were nothing but souls.

On this point, the "Witch" theorized that a Witch Factor was a key that opened a door granting the right to interfere with the world, and that once opened, a door might remain open even after the key itself was lost.

That explained the Witches of Sin well enough, but it didn't explain Aldebaran, or the "Witch" herself. For that, she favored a theory of inheritance.

Perhaps a deep enough connection to a being who had synced with a Witch Factor could leave someone born with the door to that right—the right to interfere with the world through an Authority—already ajar.

That said—

"The way something with wings would struggle to explain the feel of moving them to something without any, that's how hard it is to explain an Authority like ours—one that wasn't tacked on later. I suppose you could say we were born with the door already half-open."

The "Witch" wore a smug look as she said it, clearly proud of what she considered her finest joke. Unfortunately, Aldebaran couldn't laugh even a little, though he could nod along with the substance of it.

Certainly, if the door had been open all the way, the air would have flowed through more freely.

Instead, with poor ventilation, he was left peering through a half-open door, reaching an arm through the gap. Which was exactly why he kept getting blocked by the door, or catching his hand in it—unintended accidents, every time.

Maybe it would have been better if he could resign himself, like the "Witch," to simply not opening the door. But Aldebaran was too ordinary, too powerless, to manage that.

An Aldebaran who left the door shut had no chance of fulfilling the role the "Witch" expected of him.

That being so, half-open or not, leaving the door closed was never an option for him.

Which is why Aldebaran's Authority, that half-open door, remained opaque in so many respects.

Between the feel of using it, rules of thumb built from experience, and the "Witch's" own guesses layered on top, he had perhaps eighty percent of the whole picture. Even so, that was no obstacle to using the Authority, and Aldebaran never hesitated to use it.

It might sound reckless, but it's no different from not understanding how a cell phone works and using one anyway without any risk to life and limb. Though, granted, Aldebaran's Authority was intimately bound up with life and death, so the comparison to a cell phone might not quite hold.

In any case, this was an Authority he'd lived with for what felt, subjectively, like a dozen-odd lifetimes—and still, the "Domain" held plenty of black boxes. The bugs that cropped up now and then were the foremost example.

The bug within the "Domain" was a phenomenon in which control over a set Matrix shifted—not to Aldebaran, but to some other target placed within that same Matrix.

Normally, Aldebaran created the "Domain" by his own will, and was permitted to make endless attempts, but only within that fixed span of time and space. Throughout, it was always Aldebaran who retained the memories, running a different test with each attempt in search of a breakthrough—that was the standard arrangement.

But if something went wrong somewhere and a bug occurred, the right to redo the "Domain" was transferred to someone else inside the Matrix, and that person would go on repeating the endless, unresolved Matrix over and over, until the "Domain" was finally released.

This bug never showed itself back when he was still under the "Witch," and Aldebaran suspected it first appeared because his defeat in his first real battle had shaken his faith in the Authority.

In fact, without fail, the bug occurred whenever Aldebaran doubted his own Authority.

Long ago, on the Sword Slave Island, it happened when he was matched in a death-duel against an enemy he had no chance—not one in ten thousand—of beating. Later, it occurred again during his clash with Leip Barielle, when Aldebaran lost his own footing, and in the fight against Yae, the assassin sent by Priscilla.

More than anything—

"—How interesting. I've never heard of an Authority ceding its initiative to an opponent."

It happened when he challenged the "Witch of Greed" who had received him in the castle of dreams.

"Quite the rare experience. So that's the world you see, is it. Certainly, without any idea what was causing it, it's the sort of thing that would drive a person mad."

He'd cut into the utterly transformed "Witch of Greed," demanding she give back the "Witch" he knew.

He'd attacked from every conceivable angle, and when all that trial and error, all that ingenuity, proved utterly useless, the suspicion toward his own Authority that welled up inside Aldebaran proved itself true—the bug occurred.

"Doubt in the absoluteness of an Authority, and the change born from it… that's a power derived from weakness. The pity is, it has no effect on a being already lost to madness. She's proof of that, and so am I."

To a bewildered Aldebaran, the "Witch of Greed" related every detail of the hell she'd tasted.

It was, without question, the same "Domain" Aldebaran knew—and yet, at the same time, decisively different. He couldn't fathom how the "Witch of Greed" had endured it.

Perhaps, as the "Witch of Greed" herself had said, she'd endured it only because she wasn't sane to begin with.

"When you invoke your Authority, more often than not, you're the weaker party. You cling to the 'Domain' to find the faintest sliver of a chance at victory, to pry it open, and finally seize it—a victim, so to speak. And if the one who forces the 'Domain' upon you is the perpetrator, then this is an exchange of initiative between victim and perpetrator."

Watching the "Witch of Greed" gleefully lay bare the true nature of that hell, Aldebaran felt the blood in his whole body run cold, and understood that this nightmare was none other than one he himself had made.

Turning the castle of dreams into a nightmare, the bug that had arisen in the "Domain"—both had their cause in Aldebaran's own weakness. It was a punishment born of the unforgivable sin of being weak.

"The plan still lives. The first objective fell through, but the second is still very much alive. Just to scrape together whatever best remains… that's why you and I are both here, doing this now, isn't it?"

That cross of sin—heavy, the kind a person alone might be tempted to throw down rather than bear—the "Witch of Greed" smiled at him, as if to say she would drag it alongside him.

At that smile—one he'd wanted to see and never wanted to see—Aldebaran made up his mind.

"I will—I will kill you myself, without fail."

—Even the bug in the "Domain," even this power derived from his own weakness, even the brand of his defeat—he would use every last one of them, and fulfill his role as the "Star That Follows."

"—Why is it, I wonder, that love diminishes?"

That whispered murmur from the "Witch of Greed"—Aldebaran doesn't know the answer.

He lost the "Witch" who craved love, lost the "Sun Princess" he loved, and even now, still doesn't know.

—Nor does he want to.

Thought accelerates.

Inside a mind pressed down—compressed, probably—by whatever Authority Petra possessed, Aldebaran couldn't rank the endless jumble of thoughts pouring in, and his head was fit to burst.

"—Damn it."

It was a terrifying Authority.

Unlike Divine Protections, whose effectiveness is a matter of hit or miss, most Authorities manifest power that's abnormal, outside all standards—and Petra's "Compression" was no exception. An Authority often comes down to how flexible its Witch Factor holder's mind is, and on that count, hers was applied with terrifying versatility.

Either an abnormally powerful conviction, or a flexibility born of weakness—the talent for drawing out the full strength of a Witch Factor's power had a great deal to do with that kind of temperament. The Witches of Sin and the Sin Archbishops would fall into the former camp; Aldebaran and Petra, one could say, into the latter.

And whether it was someone with a Divine Protection, someone who'd synced with a Witch Factor, or someone with no power at all, the ones Aldebaran always found formidable were, without fail, the latter breed.

In fact, the attack of compressing his thoughts worked on Aldebaran exactly as his opponent intended. For a man who wanted to avoid mental exhaustion, it was maliciously effective at grinding down his reserves.

Setting his Authority aside, Aldebaran too was among the weak. In a battle demanding instant, split-second judgment, he understood all too well the value of a power that let one always search out the best possible move. But by nature, no matter how one struggled, perfection was impossible in the thick of a fight. Somewhere along the line, a decision had to be made, thought had to be cut off, and action taken. Petra's "Compression" forced him to fill in that margin of thought he would ordinarily have discarded, exacting more than twice the exhaustion just to reach the same conclusion he would have anyway.

And so, at the cost of a tremendous waste of mental strength, Aldebaran kept producing optimal moves against a fully-motivated Aldebusters, answers so precise they threaded the eye of a needle.

And in the midst of that exhaustion—

"—Old Man Rom!!"

The instant he'd fought his way through the Aldebusters' latest exchange of blows, what he heard was a voice like a scream.

Felt, who had cried it out, stared wide-eyed at the aged, massive body collapsing in the distance. In that instant, it wasn't only Felt—every other member of the Aldebusters felt their hearts shaken with the same unease.

Without anyone understanding what had happened, Valga Cromwell had fallen.

"—"

Naturally, even without knowing the method, there was only one candidate for who'd done it. As a result, apart from those who rushed to fallen Valga's side, every other trace of hostility converged on Aldebaran. Exposed to that hostility, Aldebaran—behind the helmet the Aldebusters couldn't see into—had his eyes wide with the very same shock they wore.

"—So I've become the 'Domain's' perpetrator."

He hadn't meant to cause it. But he understood well enough that he was the cause, and what had happened.

The bug in the "Domain" had triggered, and Valga—one of the perpetrators—had been made to hold the initiative. And the old giant had, in all likelihood, been made to repeat some brief span of time again and again, enough times to crush his spirit.

"Tch."

A chance to snatch victory from the jaws of defeat, a once-in-a-thousand-years opportunity—Aldebaran didn't welcome its arrival.

As mentioned, the bug's occurrence had nothing to do with Aldebaran's will—at least, it wasn't something he'd triggered on purpose. And Aldebaran couldn't be glad the bug had triggered.

Because, in most cases, the bug destroyed the mind of whoever it caught up in it.

"On the Sword Slave Island, with old man Leip, with that Yae woman too…"

Everyone who had faced Aldebaran, been swallowed by the bug in the "Domain," and dragged into an inescapable loop of "death," had come away with their minds broken, every last one of them.

The sword slaves whose minds had shattered were beheaded by Aldebaran's own hand; Leip Barielle, having lost his sanity, forgot the ambition he'd nursed for years; and Yae Tenzen, broken by fear, became a faithful servant.

For Aldebaran, who had imposed on himself the condition of no killing in carrying out his plan, the bug's power to twist a person's very state of mind brought about a result that was, in nearly every sense, synonymous with "death."

Which is why Aldebaran couldn't welcome this outcome. —Was that really true?

"—No one can beat the you I made."

He hears it—the voice, the expectation, the ideal, of the "Witch" he had once betrayed.

Fearing his opponent's tenacity, their strategy, their unity—dreading a one-in-ten-thousand chance, a one-in-a-hundred-million chance, a one-in-a-trillion chance, and recoiling from it—the "Domain" had met the conditions for the bug to trigger. Which was to say, nothing more or less than Aldebaran doubting his Authority, doubting himself, doubting the "Witch."

"—No one can beat the you I made."

Once again, he heard the "Witch's" voice.

—He saw the next perpetrator's eyes roll back white as they collapsed.

It was fine to be startled by the unexpected. No one takes a blow from outside their own awareness without flinching. But Petra, the "Witch of Melancholy," had something she needed to do before she could afford to be surprised.

Without the old man, who's gonna—" "Damn it! Ram! It's gotta be Ram!" "—Can't be helped. The queen's piece is out of commission. Take that to heart." "If we don't move now, we're no better than pigs waiting to be fed. Let's strike first." "Damn it all! We haven't come this far to lose now!" "'Obviously!!'"

In an instant, Petra "compressed" the unease that had spread through the group, fulfilling her duty as the "Witch of Melancholy."

The cause hadn't been pinned down. But a response was settled on at once, and everyone, united as one, began searching for the best possible move. —Petra was no exception, of course.

Leaving the field to Ram, who had taken over the role of commander in fallen Old Man Rom's place, Petra activated her "Melancholy" Authority, "compressing" the distance between herself and Old Man Rom.

"Old Mr. Rom…!"

Old Man Rom's huge body, fallen face-down, was far too much for Petra's slender arms to lift. But peering into the old giant's face, turned to one side, she felt her throat catch at the plain wrongness of it—the fallen Old Man Rom's face had no life left in it, carrying an exhaustion that made him look as though he'd aged a hundred years in an instant.

"He never exactly had a face that screamed bursting vitality to begin with, but…"

"He definitely wasn't like this just a minute ago. Besides, if Old Mr. Rom ever thought something was wrong with his body, he'd always make sure to report it, y'know."

Old Man Rom understood well enough that carelessly hiding a change in his condition would, if anything, work to the enemy's advantage.

If the "Melancholy" Authority had been placing too great a strain on him, or if there had been any sign he might collapse like this, he would absolutely have reported it himself.

"What about the not-remotely-funny theory that using the Authority too many times in a row just burned through the rest of Old Man Rom's lifespan?"

"Rejected, because that's really not funny… No, I don't think it's my Authority. Al-san did something to Old Mr. Rom."

"But did what, exactly?"

Caught in the same dead-end question, Petra and "Subaru" alike were at a loss for an answer.

All the while, the Aldebusters, under Ram's direction, kept up the fight to corner Al, and Petra couldn't afford to spend all her thoughts on Old Man Rom's collapse.

Thinking so, Petra moved to get back up—

"—You idiot! That's the priority right now!"

"—"

So said Felt, her back turned to Petra, correcting her sense of priority flat out.

To a wide-eyed Petra, Felt, star-staff in hand, flashed her sharp double tooth and said,

"If we can't figure out what just happened, we're all gonna get taken down the same way."

Felt gritted her teeth in frustration, and as if to prove her own words, the Aldebusters' numbers—the ones meant to be surrounding and cornering Al—were dwindling. Looking around, several people, left behind by the raging battle unfolding, lay collapsed on the ground.

From the way Felt put it, the reason they'd fallen was the same as Old Man Rom's—

"Old Man Rom wouldn't go down without a fight! Search around! There's gotta be something!"

After spending a mere few seconds on that exchange with Felt, Petra turned back to look at Old Man Rom's huge body.

She thought Old Man Rom was too sharp not to live up to Felt's expectations. So if Felt believed there was something, then surely there was something.

"Yeah, but if he got blindsided by an Authority out of nowhere, there's no way he'd have had the chance to leave anything behind…!"

"…You think it was an Authority?"

"It'd be weird if it wasn't! If it were Magic, Sister would've noticed!"

"Yeah, you're right."

Felt's insistence, multiplied by faith in Ram's sharp eye.

Together, they backed up the idea that it was Al's Authority that had brought Old Man Rom down. And if Old Man Rom really had left something behind—

"—! This."

Searching every inch of Old Man Rom's limp body, Petra noticed the old giant's huge finger was smeared with dirt. Freshly smeared—the mark of a finger scratching at the ground.

Throwing her whole body against the huge frame that had shifted slightly with the impact of the fall, Petra shoved it aside, and in the midst of nearly toppling over herself, she found the message Old Man Rom had left in the dirt.

It was—

"A symbol? Kanji… no, a map symbol…"

"Not that—it's way simpler!"

Petra cut off, in a raised voice, the same puzzling over the same thing that "Subaru" had fallen into.

The mark Old Man Rom had scratched into the ground—a thick horizontal line, crossed by four short vertical strokes layered over it. Not kanji, not a map symbol, but the "tally mark method," where you count five to a group.

In other words, in the instant just before his consciousness was taken from him, Old Man Rom had been—

"—counting something?"

Getting tangled up in such things and losing sight of the goal would be putting the cart before the horse; refusing to face real, living emotion head-on was Aldebaran's specialty—no, it was a kind of survival skill he'd picked up over the eighteen years since he'd been freed from that black sphere.

"The stars were against me, that's all."

As he said it, Al's hand left his helmet and reached out toward Petra, who could only stand frozen in place, as he closed the distance between them.

She needed a countermeasure, a response, a plan, right now—

At that look, Al opened and closed the hand that had failed to catch her, and heaved a long sigh. Then, still holding his distance from Petra, he settled into a straight, unwavering stare-down—

"—Play ball."
