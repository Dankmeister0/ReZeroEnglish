Arc 9 Chapter 52: "Aldebaran I"

"—No one can beat the you that I made."

Aldebaran can recall being told that by the 'Witch' as if it were yesterday.

In real time, it was nearly twenty years ago; in subjective time, it wasn't even in the same league. He remembers how many times he redid any single moment, but not the sum total of every attempt — and he doesn't even want to think about the sheer, unfathomable scope of every second he's spent. Still, it's an absolute certainty that he's spent far, far longer than a single ordinary lifetime lived and died once through.

That said, has Aldebaran's spirit, having spent all that time, reached the same plane as, say, an immortal elf who's lived for centuries? No, it hasn't.

If a human spirit matures from immaturity, and eventually grows old enough to reach enlightenment, that's because the time spent lets one taste both the bitter and the sweet, giving the spirit room to grow. But the time Aldebaran spent outside of real time was, so to speak, an unbroken series of stalled instants, each one meant to break through whatever hardship lay right in front of him. In practice, no matter how many times you repeat a span of a few seconds — a few minutes at most — you can't expect a person to grow from it.

That's why Aldebaran believed that the time accumulated through repetition via his Authority did nothing but wear his heart down — a wasted, unproductive time that brought no growth at all.

But even wasted time like that, repeated a hundred times, ten thousand times, a hundred million times, will naturally get sorted by the brain, which tries to strip Aldebaran of his genuine memories. And yet there are memories that can't fade even so — the kind carved not into the brain but into the soul, so to speak — and the words at the start of this account are exactly that.

Though, if Aldebaran ever said he didn't remember all that time, and didn't even want to remember it, he can easily picture the 'Witch' — that embodiment of curiosity who wants to know everything — murmuring, "I see. That's a shame," under her breath.

The 'Witch' claims she can't understand emotions, yet she's remarkably skilled at the performance of bending others to her will. Knowing that full well, Aldebaran still can't defy the 'Witch's' words or bearing. Put it that way, and it might rival what little Aldebaran heard from Yae about the sheer inhumanity of the Shinobi Village, in terms of having been trained and remade to be that way.

In this case, whether it's the Shinobi Village that's insane for being lumped in with the 'Witch', or the 'Witch' who's too terrifying for standing alone against the whole history of the Shinobi Village — it's a toss-up either way.

—In any case, the Aldebaran of now is a Thing made by the 'Witch'.

"Even so, you have the right to live your own life. That much, not even I as your creator am permitted to violate unilaterally. So if you ever come to feel, in your heart, that you can't follow me — you may leave the plan at any time. That is your freedom."

Every so often, the 'Witch' would say that, as if testing Aldebaran's resolve.

It was a rare show of consideration for the 'Witch'. As a rule, once she'd secured someone's consent, her standard tactic was to maneuver so that the pledge she'd extracted could never be taken back. In fact, among the lessons and assignments the 'Witch' set for Aldebaran, there were more than a few nightmarish contents that required getting him to agree before he could hesitate about them — a "commit first, think later" arrangement. Whenever she made him do such things, once she'd gotten a single nod out of him, she never once asked afterward if he wanted to quit.

And yet, about that root, that foundational, that very first resolve — she asked him, again and again.

Aldebaran interprets that as some form of the 'Witch's' own guilt or inner conflict, though he isn't certain.

The 'Witch', who claims she can't understand emotion, surely wasn't entirely without anger, sorrow, or joy even so. If she could express such basic human feelings, couldn't she also have felt guilt, or fear of the future? At the very least, in all the time Aldebaran spent with her, he sometimes thought of the 'Witch' as someone who didn't understand the human heart — but he never once thought of her as someone who had none.

Between "having none" and "not understanding," an entirely different landscape unfolds.

Perhaps that was what showed itself in the form of the 'Witch's' questions. If so, had the 'Witch' hesitated, once for every question she asked? Had there been some inner conflict — grieving over the brutal path she was about to make Aldebaran walk, wanting to respect his own will and let him choose that life for himself?

If — if, by some chance, by some infinitesimal possibility — the 'Witch' really did feel that way, then, ironically, that alone becomes the reason Aldebaran never goes back on his resolve.

So—

"—I see."

To Aldebaran's answer, always the same, the 'Witch's' reaction was always the same too.

Maybe he's being deceived. Maybe, from the moment Aldebaran was born until that day came, the 'Witch' spent ample time brainwashing him. Maybe the 'Witch' has none of the guilt or hesitation Aldebaran hopes she has.

Maybe she really is that kind of wicked 'Witch'.

But even when he confronted her head-on with that question, the 'Witch' would only wear that beautiful black-and-white smile of hers and say—

"Of course. —I'm a wicked mage, after all."

And with an answer that could be sincere or could be a lie — there's no telling which — she toys with Aldebaran all the same.

Bathed in a roaring wind, Aldebaran is in the middle of a high-speed glide.

Spreading the stone wings he's formed on his back, catching the wind with them as he flies — he imagines his own figure must look terribly ridiculous, but there's no room to worry about appearances now. While Yae's holding the line, he needs to put as much distance as possible and erase the difference in altitude.

"Can't believe he'd target my 'Territory' on purpose."

Aldebaran mutters that, but most of his attention is consumed by forming and controlling the stone wings on his back.

Shaping stone wings demands a delicate balance — fine adjustments to size and hardness both. Too big, and the wind buffets them too hard; too small, and they can't serve their purpose at all. The balance of hardness and density also ties into weight — shave too much or too little, and a crash is unavoidable.

"Gotta respect Icarus for nailing it on the first try...!"

Speaking of flying on man-made wings brings to mind that great forerunner Icarus and his wax wings — never mind how that story ended, Aldebaran genuinely marvels that the man didn't just glide on artificial wings but actually flapped his way close to the sun.

As for Aldebaran, he's lost count of how many times his ill-formed wings sent him crashing down. — Realizing that was exactly what his opponent wanted, only after countless retries did he finally manage the stone wings he has now.

Even that is a feat of brinksmanship that only holds together with extreme concentration and the utmost care, but—

"Couldn't keep falling like that forever."

That fact, and the sense of urgency behind it, is the biggest reason Aldebaran frantically leveled up from Stone Wings Level 1 to a level where he could, at least for now, actually fly. Aldebaran prides himself on wielding an unbeatable Authority, but that doesn't mean his 'Territory' has no weaknesses at all.

This time, the enemy's surprise attack happened to strike two of those weaknesses at once, without meaning to. Strictly speaking, maybe it's more accurate to say it attacked the same weakness from two different angles.

Aldebaran's Authority, 'Territory', has a range of effect. The teleportation triggered by the ambush knocked Aldebaran out of the area he'd set as his 'Territory'. On top of that, the destination for that teleport involved a fall from an extreme altitude, which became the second blow against his 'Territory', striking Aldebaran from both sides at once.

As a result, Aldebaran, falling at tremendous speed, repeated killing himself before flying out of his 'Territory', then restarting mid-fall, over and over. Through those repetitions, he judged it impossible to turn the situation around while falling from an uncontrollable extreme altitude, and after trying various other approaches, arrived at the attempt to escape using stone wings.

At the very least, this makes the change in altitude gentler, and keeping his 'Territory' up becomes relatively easier. Just as Aldebaran managed to untangle one knot in this tangled mess—

"—Al!!"

A voice like a silver bell sharply called out to Aldebaran, who was desperately trying to stabilize himself.

Glancing back over his shoulder, from an altitude considerably lower than Aldebaran's but heading in the same direction, he clearly sees an angel flying through the air — no, it's Emilia. She carries wings made of ice on her slender back, using the same method as Aldebaran, but with an overwhelming edge in gracefulness, and she's rapidly closing the distance.

"Unbelievable."

He hopes it's understandable that a stunned, almost admiring mutter slips out of him.

As mentioned, Aldebaran struggled enormously just to finish these clumsy stone wings of his. It was precisely because of that hard-won effort that he felt respect for Icarus and his wax wings. But watching Emilia — who, like the mythical Icarus, nailed a set of man-made wings on her very first try and achieved the same result Aldebaran only reached after countless failures — he feels something more decisive than respect, even a sense of defeat.

"Rather than the kid being a genius, I'd almost rather it be that I, who's crashed over six hundred times, am just hopeless — and even that's no comfort."

The strength of Aldebaran's 'Territory' lies in an endurance battle: with infinite attempts, he can grind away until even a single sliver of a winning chance comes along. His greatest advantage is being able to keep flipping through his hand of cards until the moment his opponent slips up and the odds turn in his favor.

But — as was the case in his fight with Reinhard — the world rarely has beings who consistently draw the correct answer. What counts as "correct" varies wildly by individual ability and win condition, but what they share, unlike Aldebaran, is being beloved by fate.

—Aldebaran is hated by fate.

Whether Aldebaran came to hate fate first, or fate came to hate Aldebaran first, is something he can no longer say for certain — but that much, at least, is an unshakeable fact. Whatever else loves or despises him, fate was Aldebaran's absolute enemy.

So he has to kill it. — Even if it means turning the whole world into his enemy, he'll kill fate.

Not wanting that to happen, fate keeps sending its beloved favorites in one after another as assassins. Here and now, that meant Emilia and Rem.

"—. What a twist of fate."

Whose idea was it, in the first place, to recommend choosing Rem as the one to spring the ambush?

Whoever proposed it, in terms of creating a blank space inside Aldebaran, she was the perfect choice among perfect choices. Back when she was the victim of the 'Gluttony' Authority, Aldebaran, under its influence at the time, hadn't been able to notice the strangeness of her existence, and had unwittingly built a perfectly ordinary relationship with her.

That's precisely why, at the moment of that flash-grenade of memory — when Priscilla died, and it became clear that Rem too had been freed from the yoke of 'Gluttony' — he was hit with a blank space bigger than anyone else's.

—Because he'd come face to face with a presence that shouldn't be here, that by rights should be dead.

"————"

There's no room to debate by what method that was brought about.

Without question, he pulled it off. Natsuki Subaru defied fate, and beat it. Natsuki Subaru has that power. Rem's very existence is the proof.

And yet, even so — Priscilla Barielle was not saved.

"—If that's how you're going to play it, I won't be holding back either."

The tears he shed, the fist he battered himself with, the consideration he tried to offer to stay close to her — none of it means anything. What he wanted wasn't tears, or wounds, or comfort. It was, simply, one single life.

"—Ngh."

Behind his helmet, biting down hard enough to taste blood, Aldebaran turns to look back. At the same moment, he reshapes the angle and thickness of his wings, keeping the drop in speed and control to a minimum, and in midair, his eyes meet Emilia's as she closes in on him.

Beautiful as her ice wings look on her, no matter how angelically lovely she is, she shouldn't be able to gain speed or altitude by flapping man-made wings. For her initial burst, she got momentum from the recoil of Rem striking an ice slab, but the acceleration since then has come by another method—

"Yah! Ta! Tei!"

Emilia lets out the same breathless-sounding shouts she uses when attacking, but with every shout, her speed and altitude climb, another notch and then another. The method is simple and clear — she's using magic to conjure ice pillars in midair as footholds, kicking off each one to keep gaining speed and momentum.

Each conjured pillar, after a brief pause, gets kicked down toward the ground by the force of her legs, but it quickly dissolves back into mana and returns to the atmosphere, reborn as a new foothold in turn. Kick it again, gain more momentum. Through that repetition, she's been closing in on Aldebaran.

"That's insane..."

It's a brute-force solution made possible only by extraordinary magical talent and the ability to burn through vast quantities of mana. Thanks to his link with 'Aldebaran', Aldebaran can borrow the power of an external mana tank in the form of the 'Divine Dragon' — but even so, there's no way he could do what Emilia's doing.

It's not a matter of mana capacity — the gap in talent and physical ability is simply too wide. Talent can be closed given infinite attempts, but a gap in physical ability cannot.

The canyon stretches on, long and vast, and the rock walls flanking the two of them as they fly continue on just as far. As long as they're both heading the same direction, Emilia's pursuit won't let up.

Seeing that, Emilia kicks off another new ice pillar, and despite the distance between them, matches Aldebaran's altitude. Then, balancing herself with her ice wings—

"—Al! Please, let's talk! I'll listen properly, I promise!"

"That's rich, coming from someone about to freeze me solid without so much as a warning. If I'd kept falling all the way to the bottom, you were gonna freeze the whole river and call it a job well done, weren't you?"

"But! That plan already failed!"

"A little too honest for your own good, aren't you, Royal Selection candidate!"

Sitting on his hands and falling all the way to the canyon floor would've been walking straight into his opponent's trap. To escape that, he'd taken a gamble and veered off the ice slab's falling trajectory — though maybe he should count it a win just to have split Emilia and Rem apart, even if not both at once.

The answer to that is—

"Here it comes now. —My attitude hasn't changed one bit since the royal capital!"

Raising his hand, aiming to shake off the pursuing Emilia, Aldebaran conjures chunks of rock in midair and sends her charging straight into a barrage of flying stone shrapnel.

Unexpectedly, the scene that unfolds looks strikingly like an actual shooting game, and Emilia, greeted by that hail of shrapnel, lets her ice wings gleam as she—

"I'll say it as many times as I have to. I don't want to cry ever again over anyone, thinking there was nothing to be done about them disappearing!!"

Crying that out at the top of her voice, Emilia dives into the barrage, and the shattering of ice and the shriek of freezing air rang out high and sharp across Agzadd Canyon.

—Aldebaran has never much agonized over the meaning of his own existence as a 'Following Star'.

They say everyone, at least once during adolescence, wonders why they were born, or where life comes from and where it goes when it ends, or feels some baseless urge to rebel against their parents — but for Aldebaran, all of those were, more or less, fairly distant propositions.

In Aldebaran's case, it was hard to even settle on who to designate as the parent he should rebel against, and given the nature of his Authority, he never really got a felt sense that a lost life actually disappears. He'd been given a clear answer as to what he was born for, and as for why he was born — he supposes he could answer that with "love," more or less.

So Aldebaran never had to confront the questions one's supposed to wrestle with in adolescence.

He doesn't think of that as a failure, or as something he missed out on. There's apparently a school of thought that says hardship in your youth is worth seeking out on purpose, but there's no value in an experience that just makes you cringe in embarrassment once you're an adult — the "dark history" kind. Experiences you don't need to have, you don't need to have. — Just as everyone only needs to die once, at the very end of their life.

"I see, so I can't expect you to have the emotions or impulses proper to adolescence. That's a bit of a shame. I'd been thinking through all sorts of ways to handle whatever form your rebellious phase might take."

So laments the 'Witch' — not that Aldebaran thinks she's heartless, but she does make heartless comments now and then — regarding Aldebaran, who grew up smoothly with hardly any trouble.

To Aldebaran, the 'Witch' is the one he's spent the longest stretch of time with since being born, but she has a bad habit of wanting to sample every single human choice, every emotion, every one of countless possibilities. And more often than not, once that appetite takes hold, the 'Witch' has no brakes at all.

Aldebaran has met other 'Witches' besides her, and no matter how much they varied in degree, the fact that not one of them could resist that particular nature struck him as deeply blasphemous.

So the 'Witch's' attitude, waiting for Aldebaran's adolescence to arrive as though somewhat unsatisfied, is — if you consider it the product of an irresistible nature — understandable, in its way. That said, it makes no sense to perform an adolescence just to please the 'Witch'. — Though, in a sense, that very defiance might itself have been an adolescent impulse.

"I suppose it's just too convenient to expect someone who isn't even a parent to trace the outline of a parental experience. ...If I'd made Beatrice, could she have experienced it, I wonder?"

To the 'Witch' asking herself that, almost like a muttered aside, the loneliness Aldebaran felt in response was, he thinks, something else entirely — unrelated to adolescence. But what exactly it was is hard to pin down in concrete terms, and besides, the relationship between them is too complicated to make that demand.

So, as the 'Witch' had decreed, the relationship between Aldebaran and her was—

"—Sensei."

At that call, the 'Witch', who'd been enjoying her exploration of the labyrinth of her own thoughts, looks up. Every time those black eyes reflect Aldebaran, and she tilts her head, saying "What is it?", her pure white hair spilling over her shoulder, he can't help thinking what an unfair 'Witch' she is.

Always absorbed in her own affairs, always honest about her own desires, never once trying to consider anyone else's feelings — and yet, whenever Aldebaran calls out to her, she always meets his eyes.

Of course a rebellious streak proper to adolescence never rises up against a 'Witch' like that. To begin with, the motive is thin, and mind and body have long since submitted to the futility of defying the 'Witch'. Under the pretext of performance testing to master his Authority, the 'Witch' has put Aldebaran through over a million 'deaths' — the pecking order was settled long ago.

Though, however much he brings that up, the 'Witch' herself remains unmoved: "None of that has ever once remained in my memory, so it's a bit much to ask me to feel guilty about it."

Still, he understands why the 'Witch' goes this far. After all, at this point, of the 'Witches' still alive, aside from the one in front of him, there's only the one who's the direct or indirect cause of death of all the others. What contact Aldebaran had with the other 'Witches' wasn't with them while they were alive, either.

He can only meet them within the limited space the 'Witch' prepares. The women he meets there are already dead — nothing but collected souls. And Aldebaran, under the 'Witch's' tutelage, was to make one of them—

"—has to be killed."

That was the reason for existence given to Aldebaran, the 'Following Star', before he'd even reached adolescence — the guiding star of a life he had to fulfill even if it took a million deaths.

To make Aldebaran into this, the 'Witch' was condemned by the other 'Witches', and even parted ways with her own companions — that alone shows how absolute her resolve was.

"—No one can beat the you that I made."

Being told that was, for Aldebaran, both a point of pride and the very grounds of his existence. Honestly, he thinks the 'Witch's' personality is the worst, and there was plenty about her methods of upbringing and how she laid out her policies that he wanted to protest — but even so, he never hesitated to live up to the 'Witch's' expectations.

He believed he could do it. Back then, when Aldebaran still had his left arm, he knew 'death', but he didn't yet know true defeat — which is exactly why he could believe that.

He never had an adolescence — but without question, it was a foolish, indelible dark chapter of his life.

"Tch—!"

At Emilia diving headfirst into the falling barrage of shrapnel, Aldebaran crushed the hesitation in his heart and wrung out every ounce of firepower without holding back.

Just as it had been in the clash at the royal capital, Aldebaran is instinctively weak to Emilia. This is, by now, a kind of fundamental weakness woven into the roots of his very life, and it's also why, even in the royal capital, he couldn't bring himself to take the same drastic measures against her that he'd have used on any other opponent.

Back then, he'd borrowed 'Aldebaran's' power to drop a massive boulder, halting Emilia's pursuit in exchange for making her protect the capital — but here, there's nothing around he can use as collateral damage.

In that sense too, dropping her into this vast canyon reflects a lesson properly learned from that previous battle.

"I don't have the luxury to be grateful for that growth!"

Unfortunately, Aldebaran's heart isn't flush enough to take joy in his own growth, or his enemy's. For a moment, he felt resistance to defining Emilia as an enemy, but he squeezed his eyes shut against that.

That resolve, in fact, is one he made long ago — long before he ever met Priscilla. Strictly speaking, the opponent Aldebaran set that resolve against isn't, precisely, the Emilia before him now, but—

"Even a recycled resolve is good enough to prop up a facade."

Raising that resolve — one he knows full well is just a facade — he moves to cut off Emilia's pursuit.

Straight on, Emilia dove right into the storm of fist-sized rock shards, her ice wings shining proudly. It was an attack meant to at least delay her, hoping she'd take a posture of dodging or deflecting — but true to his bad feeling, Emilia went for the shortest, most direct path: deploying ice pillars as footholds off her back wings, firing ice stakes to counter the barrage, and even swatting away the shrapnel that slipped through her interception with a pair of ice swords gripped in both hands.

The 'Witch' once taught him that simultaneous casting of spells is difficult even for a seasoned mage. Even with the same element, it requires the skill to pull off something as absurd as holding a pen in each hand and writing a sentence with the right while drawing an illustration with the left. Once you're talking about different elements, it's apparently the kind of feat that requires essentially adding extra brain matter just for the purpose — Aldebaran once watched the 'Witch' handle five elements at once. Honestly, he half-seriously wondered whether her head, smaller than an average person's, somehow contained five separate tiny brains.

Setting aside such an outlandish case, even limiting it to spells of the same fire — ah, wait, same element — casting four kinds of magic at once through wings, ice pillars, ice stakes, and twin swords was plenty of a feat in its own right.

On top of that, she's employing an absurd pursuit technique that calls for athletic performance beyond an Olympic athlete's — he feels like he's used up his whole day's supply of astonishment already.

"So here, have a welcome with a little less shock value!"

Against the storm of shrapnel, Emilia chose to push through with courage and raw strength. But courage aside — how about a threat that raw strength alone can't push through?

"————"

Faced with the next development laid out for her, Emilia's cheeks stiffen as she pushes forward, trying to close the distance. Spread out in front of her, all at once, with nowhere to run, is an encircling net of sand and gravel — smaller than the shrapnel and easy to overlook the danger of. It's the equivalent of diving into a downpour; without some absurd ability to dodge rain in midair, taking zero hits would be impossible.

Naturally, scattering the sand wasn't just to obstruct her vision—

"—Ngh, it's heavy!?"

Emilia lets out a cry of surprise as she swings her arm to brush off the clinging sand.

The grains of sand sticking to her limbs and white clothes bind together with the sand around them, hindering her movement with their combined weight and hardness. It's the same debuff attack he used against Reinhard in the sand sea — Reinhard, breaking the sound barrier, tore free of it through sheer momentum, but that method is beyond even Emilia.

And furthermore—

"My wings—"

The sand and gravel's primary target is interfering with the ice wings supporting Emilia's flight.

The ice wings, plunged into the dust cloud, get eaten into by the sand and can't display their proper wind-catching performance. Emilia's balance breaks, and she plunges straight down toward the ground — to avoid that fate, she immediately discards her now-useless wings, reverting from an angel back into an angelic-looking girl, narrowly escaping disaster.

"—Purged them, huh!"

But making her go that far is exactly Aldebaran's real aim.

Even after being forced to abandon her ice wings once, Emilia could redeploy the same thing right away, restarting without losing her upward and forward momentum. But no matter how extraordinary her physical abilities are, she can't catch up to a makeshift hang glider moving faster than a hundred kilometers an hour. That's precisely why Aldebaran keeps pressing, to stop Emilia from redeploying her wings.

With a roar, giant stone arms surge out from the rock walls, meaning to seize Emilia sideways as she flies through the canyon sky. If those kill her momentum, then even if Emilia gains a second pair of wings, she won't be able to catch up to Aldebaran, whose speed never dies.

And what's more, the giant arms barring Emilia's way aren't just one or two. — The stone arms thrust out from the rock walls as their material number in the hundreds.

"More like a haunted house's backstage than a thousand-armed Kannon tribute..."

The visual is closer to that haunted-house staple, hands shooting out all at once from behind a paper screen. Except unlike that gimmick, meant only to startle, these giant arms are genuinely coming to seize Emilia. If even one grabs her leg, that ends this chase completely, and Aldebaran's escape succeeds — a real moment of truth—

"Let's see you cross this—" "—I'll manage!!"

The words he meant to finish get painted over by her sheer force of will.

He doesn't think this is something you can just muscle through with willpower — but the terrifying thing about Emilia is that she just might manage it through willpower anyway.

She demonstrates that terror without holding back — wingless now, she spins the ice sword in her hand, slices off the fingers of the first giant arm, and uses the now-harmless limb as a foothold to leap high into the air.

Her momentum isn't dead. She's regaining a good deal of altitude too.

But — even if the giant arms don't quite number a thousand hands, there are still over a hundred more waiting—

"—Soldiers, lend me your strength!"

Answering Emilia's call, those unnervingly designed ice soldiers appear once more.

The ice troops that should have been helplessly smashed apart amid the falling ice slab bravely stand against the earthen giant arms swarming toward Emilia, using them as footholds — some bodily blocking them, some punching them off course, some getting crushed mid-pose after losing the exchange, showing a wide variety of roles.

What irritates Aldebaran most of all is the one that takes Emilia's hand, helps her escape the giant arms' clutches, and tosses her forward to aid her advance.

A total of seven ice Natsuki Subarus fulfill their roles and shatter, only to be born again and support Emilia once more, shattered again and resurrected again, never breaking the rule of a maximum of seven at once, protecting Emilia from the hundreds of approaching stone hands and continuing to help her.

—Magic, the 'Witch' once said, is a way of giving shape to feelings and wishes.

He'd thought it a surprisingly romantic thing for that 'Witch' to say, but considering how wanting to cut an opponent to pieces turns wind into a blade, and how rage gives birth to crimson flame meant to burn everything to ash, you could argue that counts as feelings and wishes taking form too.

If so, then what wish, exactly, is being given shape by the sight Aldebaran is watching right now — ice soldiers shaped like Natsuki Subaru, helping Emilia?

His stomach churns. Not in some figurative sense — literally, genuinely.

Aldebaran channeled that rising negative emotion into his next attack.

Which was—

"—Sorry for the rerun, but here comes the trump card again."

So says Aldebaran, and above the heads of Emilia and the others — filling the entire width of the gap in Agzadd Canyon — a colossal, overwhelming mass of rock comes plummeting straight down.

Inescapable, crushing death by sheer mass — a reprise of the trap he'd sprung in the royal capital.

What he wanted — no, what he needed — was just one victory, once in his entire life.

It wouldn't be an exaggeration to say Aldebaran's whole existence had been built around that single victory. Everything given to him, poured into him, blessed upon him since birth was a signpost prepared so he could obtain that victory, seize it, win it.

Aldebaran believed he understood the meaning and the weight of that responsibility fully — or so he thought.

But when the moment finally came to put that meaning to the test, all of it — the resolve that should have been in place, the plan that should have been readied, the soul that should have been honed sharp — amounted to nothing at all.

"—No one can beat the you that I made."

Those words, given to him by the 'Witch', were the guiding star of Aldebaran's life. As long as he could believe them, no obstacle, however great, could stop him. And the possibility that he himself might stop believing in the 'Witch' — he'd never say this to her face, and never wanted her to hear it — but he'd thought that could never happen.

So, just as the 'Witch's' words promised, he'd firmly believed that he could never lose to anyone.

—When that faith was shattered, Aldebaran surely died, once, for real.

As for 'death', he's experienced it to the point that counting it feels ridiculous. Even thinking it ridiculous to count, he counted anyway because he'd been told to, and even recording the actual number didn't shave off one bit of that ridiculousness. But he thinks the 'death' in the truest sense of the word is the one he tasted on that day, in that moment.

It was the 'Witch's' cherished wish, the thing for which she'd gone so far as to create Aldebaran, and she'd arranged for him a single, absolute, must-not-lose battle, the only one of his life.

And yet—

"—In the one place you must not lose, you lost."

Seeing the 'Witch' in the familiar meadow, savoring the scent of black tea at the white table of a tea party, Aldebaran was crushed under the despair of realizing just how grave a sin he'd committed. The 'Witch', a peak of beauty expressible in only two colors, black and white — the fact that her infuriatingly beautiful form bore not a single wound was proof that this place was a world of dream.

Because the real 'Witch', to shield Aldebaran, who'd failed the battle he was meant to win, had lost every one of her limbs, reduced to a wretched shape with nothing left to do but wait for death.

Watching that figure blankly, Aldebaran found himself dully thinking of the horribly obvious fact that this 'Witch' too had red blood flowing in her veins.

That heat of blood, that smell, that texture — there was no way those could be a lie. Which meant that this world, where all of that had been lost and the reality that should exist had been painted over with fiction, was the one that was, plainly and unmistakably, a lump of falsehood.

"I'm not overly fond of the word 'fiction.' Even knowing that, strictly speaking, it's a different word, it always brings to mind a certain unpleasant someone."

Whether that was some kind of joke on the 'Witch's' part, or she genuinely meant to say something out of place, Aldebaran never did learn her true feelings, right to the end. He only felt that he was hated. Or perhaps she'd been disappointed in him, and had given up on him completely.

And yet—

"There's no getting your arm back, it seems."

The 'Witch's' gaze had turned to Aldebaran's left arm, taken by the shadow from the shoulder down.

There's no pain. That too is proof that this is the 'Witch's' castle of dream — back in reality, an unimaginable pain would surely be searing his brain right now. A real pain, one that even 'death' couldn't erase, one that would never fade.

"You lost an arm, and lost the battle you must not lose. It seems my plan will end in failure. I can just picture the earful I'll get from my friends. — In the end, it falls to those who mean to carry on Flugel's will. How vexing."

That his own defeat would rob the 'Witch' of everything was almost unbearably sad. She'd sought no one's understanding, parted ways with her fellow 'Witches' and the companions she'd traveled with, all to fulfill her wish — and Aldebaran had ruined it.

And most pathetic of all was that, even knowing this, the resolve and fire he'd had before the defeat refused to return to Aldebaran's hollowed-out soul.

Having lost the battle he must not lose, Aldebaran's heart broke. His first battle, the only one in his life where defeat was not allowed, shattered his heart to pieces.

And watching the inevitable death of the 'Witch' — the being closest to him — would deal Aldebaran a wound truly beyond recovery, in the fullest sense.

"—No one can beat the you that I made."

So when she said it to him once more, Aldebaran couldn't understand what it meant.

"Well, there's no point worrying about appearances now. Let's change the victory condition. We'll leave her to the others over there... and we'll focus on preventing secondary damage."

The 'Witch' is clever, and self-serving. Leaving Aldebaran aside — bewildered, his mind unable to function properly under the crushing guilt of blaming himself — she reaches her own conclusion and tries to push the conversation forward without letting him object.

A changed victory condition, a newly presented goal of preventing secondary damage — but even that doesn't seem achievable to him. After all, Aldebaran had already failed.

And yet—

"—No one can beat the you that I made."

Aldebaran doesn't know what could make the 'Witch' say that.

Without offering more explanation to the blankly standing Aldebaran, the 'Witch' rises and walks slowly toward him, coming to stand directly, squarely, face to face with him. Her face — the one he's seen the most, for the longest, in his entire life — wore, for the first time, an expression he'd never seen before.

"—The stars were just unkind."

And he understood — that meant it wasn't Aldebaran's fault.

Before he could reach any conclusion about the emotion welling up inside him, the castle of dream abruptly comes to an end. Like the violent tearing of the very stage of a picture-story show, and beyond it appears a merciless reality. In an instant, pain and loss seize control of his brain, and noise and fresh blood erode his comprehension. Sky and earth are stained pitch-black, so much that even the sense of time is lost, and Aldebaran's anguished cry is painted over by the shrieking of a world coming apart — not even reaching himself.

But even so, in that world where everything had been thrown into chaos, amid noise so loud he couldn't even hear his own voice, one thing still reached him clearly, faint and hoarse, barely more than a dying breath.

The 'Witch's' final incantation, the one he didn't want to miss—

"—Ol Shamak."

The next time Aldebaran's consciousness returned, it was after everything had ended — no, more than ended: begun again, and that beginning ended, and it began and ended over and over, seemingly many times over — a world with no trace of the 'Witch' left in it.

Whether it was because the preconditions were different, or because it came from a 'Witch' who wielded overwhelming power, the magic's effect differed from what he was used to, leaving him dazed for a moment — but only a moment—

"—Where'd you come from? Who are you?"

The question came from a voice belonging to someone he'd never met.

The man, dressed in tattered, shabby clothes that put him on par with — if not below — Aldebaran's own battered state, looked at Aldebaran with eyes mixed with fear and wariness.

He understands the words. He has little experience dealing with people, but in a sense, that wasn't a problem here either. What mattered was where here was, and when now was.

"Ginunhive... the Isle of Swordsmen's Exile. 'Witch'? That's talk from centuries ago, isn't it?"

At the man's words, suspicious at being asked such a strange question, Aldebaran let out a breath. Seeing that, the man's expression shifts from puzzlement to outright alarm.

That's because the breath he let out turned into a sob, and Aldebaran, collapsing to the ground, wept torrents of tears.

'—No one can beat the you that I made.'

The 'Witch's' words, left behind centuries ago, cling to his soul and won't let go.

For the sin of having made that person into a liar, Aldebaran wanted to die.

He couldn't die.
