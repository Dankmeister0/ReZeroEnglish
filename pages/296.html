Arc 4 Chapter 126: "The Forest's Jet-Black King, Guiltirau's Assault!"

—As if in a chain of women's screams, steel crossed steel, scattering sparks.

"GRAAAAAAH—!"

"Ahahaha! Wonderful, wonderful, wonderful!"

Whirling like a dance, the curved blade lashed out from every direction—up, down, left, right—indiscriminately hunting for vital points. Whatever physical prowess made it possible, each seemingly careless strike carried enough power and precision to be fatal, bearing down to carve into Garfiel's body.

The tip of the blade, bent into a sharp dogleg like the shape of the kana "ku," split the wind and broke the sound barrier, swung through at what was literally godspeed. Garfiel met this by sliding the shields equipped on both his hands into its path, deflecting along the surface rather than blocking it head-on, devoting himself entirely to defense. It was a fine technique—deflecting only the trajectory of the swing without killing its momentum—that sent the woman's body sweeping wide off balance. Threading through that opening, Garfiel snapped his toe up toward her now wide-open torso.

Garfiel's straight kick carried the force of a cannon shell, capable of shattering even a stone wall with ease. Landed directly without the impact being deadened, it would punch clean through the soft defenses of human flesh and unleash more than enough force to obliterate the organs within. In fact, every blow of Garfiel's that had connected fully had pulverized the woman's body down to the bone, giving him the certain feel of a killing blow again and again. And yet—

"I already saw that one."

"Don't screw with me!!"

As his toe snapped upward, the woman arched her back and pulled her exposed flank out of its path. His kick, striking only air, brushed past her back as if stroking it, and his foot became entangled in the jet-black cloak she wore. An instant—but for both the woman and Garfiel, a stall that could be called fatal.

"Sh—"

Releasing a short breath, the woman swept her arm behind her, wrapping Garfiel's entangled foot even further in the cloak's fabric. Her other hand swung her blade in a half-rotating arc, up from behind. The blade's trajectory would sever his right thigh. Before he could think, Garfiel hopped lightly off his remaining left foot on the ground, thrusting the sole of his left shoe up beneath his entangled right foot.

Garfiel's left foot slammed into the flat of the blade as it swept up from directly below. The sound of buckling steel and a snapping wrist rang out together, and the woman, letting out a lewd cry of pain, dropped her knife. She fell back at once, but Garfiel—still tangled by the foot and toppling to the floor—couldn't press the attack either. Using the force of his kick and both hands braced against the ground, he rolled backward, put some distance between them, and only then freed his foot from the cloak.

"Took your wrist and your knife off you, there."

"It's fine. I've got more knives where that came from, and my left hand will be moving again soon enough. As for the cloak... it was only getting in the way of fighting you anyhow."

"Don't go spouting off tough talk."

"Whether it's tough talk or not, why don't you find out with your own bowels."

Garfiel wiped away his sweat with the stolen cloak and tossed it to the edge of the hallway. Elsa didn't so much as glance at the discarded cloak, and, lightly rubbing her grotesquely twisted left hand with her right, called out to the massive shadow waiting behind her.

"Meili. Don't just stand there watching—hand me some replacement knives."

"Ugh, Elsa's so selfish. I'm not your porter or your knife girl, you know. And you keep picking fights Rock-Piggy can't even join in on."

The shadow addressed—a girl riding the back of a huge Witchbeast—puffed out her cheeks at Elsa's call and tossed something over. It was a holder holding the curved blades Elsa used. Drawing two fresh blades from it, Elsa checked their grip one-handed while looking up at the girl.

"Isn't it your own fault, bringing along such a huge Witchbeast just to keep yourself safe? Though honestly, I'm rather glad to get to dance with him without any interruptions."

"And if you get so caught up in that, that the actual targets get away, there's no point at all, is there? If Mama finds out about this, you're definitely getting an earful. I'll tell her properly it's all Elsa's fault, too."

"If I were afraid of being tattled on, I could never sneak off for a little something on the side. Leave being the good girl to Meili and the others. I don't mind being treated as the troublesome one."

As she spoke, Elsa tossed the blades in her hand into the air, juggling the two with one hand. Given the width of the blades, a single slip in that high-speed spinning juggle could have cost her an arm, but Elsa turned back without the slightest concern, catching the knives in her right hand and left.

"Sorry to keep you waiting. My left hand seems fine, at least for just holding something."

"Don't worry about it. Stalling for time's what I'm after too, and I ain't rude enough to butt into a sisters' conversation. Family talk's important stuff."

"My, my. What makes you think this girl of mine is my sister?"

"You're both callin' the same person your mother, ain't you. Don't matter that your hair and eyes and faces don't match. Family's somethin' you can talk about even without blood."

Hearing Garfiel's own theory of things, Elsa's eyes went wide for a moment, her face blank with surprise. Then she put a hand to her mouth and burst out laughing, thoroughly delighted.

"Ah?"

"Fufu... oh, no, forgive me. I just heard something so unexpected that I couldn't help myself. ...My, you really do seem like a very good boy."

"Don't go treatin' me like a kid. I'm a full-grown man."

"Is that so? To me you look like you haven't quite managed to become either a man or an adult."

Her cheeks still loosened with mirth, Elsa answered the sullen Garfiel. At his words, Garfiel knit his brow suspiciously, which only made Elsa's smile burst all the more merrily.

"Elsa, Elsa. This scary-faced guy—could he actually be kind of cute?"

"I agree, Meili. I was just starting to think the same thing. It's been a while since I've met a boy I'd want to keep alive even after dragging his bowels out."

"Quit yammerin' whatever you like. The both of you are gonna get knocked flat by my fists, together."

Garfiel finished with that, cracking his wrist round and round. He didn't quite grasp the true meaning behind Elsa and the girl—Meili's—exchange, but there was no doubting that it made light of his intentions. That much understanding was enough; Garfiel had no kind words to offer in return.

If they wouldn't weep, apologize, and beg forgiveness, then he would beat them down until they couldn't move and mete out the punishment they deserved. That was Garfiel's role.

"C'mon already, get to it. Sure, buyin' time for the boss and the others to get away is the main thing, but I wanna rack up a clean win while I'm at it too. I'll beat you down till there's nothin' left and teach you good—that I'm the strongest shield, in the 'Sanctuary' or out of it!"

Delivering that declaration, Garfiel clashed the shields on both arms together. A high, ringing sound echoed through the hallway, and in the moonlit corridor Garfiel let his fighting spirit blaze at the two who opposed him.

"—Pfft! Elsa, did you hear that? The strongest shield, he says! The strongest shield...! Pfft—! He really is a cute one, isn't he!"

But hearing his declaration, Meili, of all things, burst out laughing, and Elsa's smile deepened as well—neither of them showed the slightest sign of feeling threatened.

"The hell are you two laughin' at, huh?"

"Ah, it's just too funny. When something's funny, I laugh. You, going on and on thinking you're so strong, that's funny enough, but the way those boys ran off is even funnier."

"The boss and the others—funny?"

"Yeah, that's right. I mean, think about it. My pets have the whole mansion surrounded, and if there's any way to escape from it, there's only one spot for it. That was actually supposed to be Elsa's post, but Elsa went and wandered off on her own, so I left a replacement there instead."

—

At Meili's accusing glance, Elsa showed not the slightest contrition and didn't even meet her eyes. Her eyes, steeped in killing intent, stayed fixed on watching Garfiel's every move, making it more difficult than ever for him to act. And Meili's composure wasn't something he could afford to ignore either. Before Garfiel, whose gaze had sharpened, Meili slapped the back of the Witchbeast carrying her.

"Besides this Rock-Piggy here, I brought along one more special one today. I've got him blocking that way out. So really, your stalling for time is kind of backfiring, isn't it?"

"..."

"If you could take down me and Elsa in a snap and go chase after those boys, you could still save them, but that's not the case, is it? So watching you work so hard at stalling without even noticing that—it's just funny, is all."

With a face unable to hold back its laughter, Meili innocently mocked the absurdity of it all. At that childlike malice, Garfiel let out a long, deep breath.

It was true that a number of uncertain factors were stacked against him. As Meili said, there was no mistaking that he was facing a situation beyond his own reckoning. But—

"Hah. That's stupid."

"...Eh?"

"You're the one who don't get it. There's still another monster out there? I'm the one pinned down here? You think somethin' that convenient's gonna work on me or the boss?"

Taking satisfaction in watching Meili's smile vanish, Garfiel took a step forward. Watching Elsa react to it, tilting her body slightly, he went on—

"That's the boss and the others who flattened me. —You think somethin' like your little obstacle's gonna do anything but get laughed off and blown away!"

"No no no no no no no no, seriously no way, what the hell is even happening anymore...!"

Gasping for breath, Subaru had slumped to the floor, whimpering complaints. On the third floor of the main building of Roswaal Manor, at the stairway landing just before reaching the top floor, Subaru's group—Subaru, Otto, and Frederica, Petra, and Rem, five in all—held their breath, frozen in place.

Every one of them, slumped onto the floor, showed exhaustion plainly, their bodies gathering more than a few shallow wounds here and there. Especially—

"Frederica, you okay?"

"...Yes, this much is nothing to worry about. If anything, I'm the one who should apologize to you, Subaru-sama, for showing you such an unreliable side of myself."

"Nothing about this works without you. If anyone should feel pathetic, it's the two of us men, me and Otto. Sorry for being so weak."

"T-this time, at least... I've got no room to argue with Natsuki-san's flippant remark."

Otto sighed with frustration, while Subaru spat out a clot of blood from his mouth. Ignoring his whole body creaking and screaming in protest, Subaru shifted Rem's weight on his back to a better hold. —Taking over the duty of carrying Rem from Frederica was Subaru's job for now. Otto held Petra's hand, Subaru carried Rem, and Frederica, their sole real fighting strength, stood at the front to clear the way—this was the five of them's best plan for now.

Right after parting from Garfiel, Subaru and the others had been attacked by two Hyena-like Witchbeasts. The fight with the hyenas had barely been driven off thanks to Otto's magic stones and Frederica's hard fighting, but after that they'd repeatedly found themselves at the mercy of the Witchbeasts stationed throughout the mansion.

Bat-like Black-Winged Rats lurking in great numbers in the corridor connecting the main building to the annex. Mottled King Dogs, hyena-like beasts, roaming the mansion and lunging in the moment they saw an opening. Pouch-Rats casting nets for Subaru's group when they fled into a guest room, waiting for the instant their guard dropped. And a Two-Headed Serpent, its body as thick as Subaru's arm, that had given them a particularly hard time to deal with.

Smoking out the Black-Winged Rats to drive them away, Frederica cutting down the Mottled King Dog with her claws, fleeing while the Pouch-Rat gnawed on their backsides, Otto desperately negotiating to stall the Two-Headed Serpent while Subaru gritted his teeth and grappled its body, and Frederica lopping off both its heads—and so they'd arrived at this landing.

"Man, this is just—getting split up from Garfiel was a mistake, wasn't it..."

"Don't go saying weak things like that. Garfiel might be off somewhere right now boasting confidently that we'll be fine, so let's live up to as much of that expectation as he's placed on us."

"Your sense of loyalty really doesn't seem cut out for a merchant..."

Of everyone here, Otto seemed the least physically spent. Answering his words with a wry smile, Subaru steeled himself and got to his feet. Honestly, the body of Rem on his back was light enough to be sad about. He'd heard that carrying an unconscious, sleeping person was supposed to be heavy, but that wasn't true in Rem's case at all. He could hardly feel her warmth or her weight. Her presence itself was faint. Only the faint beat of her heart and her breathing stood as proof that she still lived, and Subaru made a point of shifting her onto his back all the more securely. As though he feared, absurd as it was, that he might jostle her loose without even noticing.

"Frederica-neesama..."

"It's all right, Petra. You don't need to look so worried... we're almost at the room we're aiming for."

To Petra, who watched her with such concern, Frederica gave a brave smile. But Frederica's condition wasn't nearly as good as her words made it sound. In the fight with the hyena, her arm had been seized, and her bleeding left arm wouldn't lift; her movements lacked their usual sharpness. She couldn't be counted on as full fighting strength anymore—what she needed was urgent treatment and somewhere she could rest.

"Still, the destination really is just a little further, huh."

Gazing up the stairs toward the top floor, Subaru murmured that. What Subaru's group was making for was Roswaal's study. Needless to say, it held an escape route leading outside—and it was also the ill-fated route that, in previous loops, had let Elsa in. At first, when he'd lost any other way of fleeing outside, Subaru had nearly crossed this route off as an option entirely—but a conversation with Frederica had changed his mind.

This had happened right after parting from Garfiel, after driving off the two hyenas.

"There's a hidden passage in the master's study that leads outside. From there we could escape the mansion grounds—to a cabin in the forest. If we use that route—"

"Sorry, Frederica, but this isn't going to be so simple. That hidden passage—they already know about it. Because that's exactly the route that black-clad woman used to get in."

"—"

Subaru told her this knowing full well it would push things toward despair, as Frederica fell silent. In past loops, Subaru had run into Elsa right at the moment he tried to check the hidden passage. Whether or not that lunatic came in through that route every single time was another matter—but at the very least, its existence was known to her.

"From what I heard just now, between Elsa and that other girl... it sounds like they've got more allies besides themselves. Whether or not whoever they were calling 'Mama' is actually the mother of those two who don't look anything alike is beside the point... but if they've got reinforcements, then of course that passage—"

—was blocked too. That was the natural conclusion. The mansion surrounded by Witchbeasts, and now the escape route to the outside also in enemy hands. In a scene that was truly boxed in on every side, Subaru drove his thoughts white-hot.

An utterly desperate situation. With the escape route rendered useless, it was pathetic not to be able to borrow Beatrice's power here. Had Subaru managed to persuade Beatrice, this wouldn't even be something to agonize over. With her 'Door Crossing', escaping from here would be so easy it wouldn't even need thinking about.

"...I'm being way too selfish."

Knowing full well Beatrice's anguish and its cause, and still he wanted to cling to her power. Bringing up her power now, as a reason he regretted not having gotten her to come along, was precisely proof that he wasn't seeing Beatrice herself at all. Being disliked by her, being cast out—that could only be called a natural result.

"Natsuki-san." "Subaru."

Whatever they thought seeing Subaru's face so wrapped up in self-reproach, a hand clapped his shoulder and another tugged at his arm. Looking over, it was Otto on his right who'd clapped his shoulder, and Petra on his left who'd tugged his arm. Each of them, in their own way, had pulled Subaru's consciousness back to reality, and upon noticing they'd both done the same thing, they made matching faces. Seeing the two of them like that, Subaru let out a breath, feeling saved.

"Subaru-sama. This is only my own thinking, but I still believe we should choose that route."

And to Subaru as he breathed out, Frederica made that assertion. As Subaru looked up, Frederica raised a finger and went on.

"As you say, Subaru-sama, at a glance our situation seems boxed in on all sides. The mansion surrounded by ferocious Witchbeasts, and our only escape route already known to the enemy. Thinking normally, being slowly wiped out this way seems unavoidable..."

"Yeah, that's right. I figured as much too, so I was trying to rack my brain over whether we could find some weak point in the Witchbeasts' encirclement at least."

"By the way, Subaru-sama. That black-clad woman who attacked us—have you crossed paths with her somewhere before?"

Cut off by that quiet question, Subaru held his breath silently. Unable to read the intent behind the question, Subaru nodded and said, "Yeah."

"Before, back in the royal capital, she went after Emilia too. That time we got out of it fine thanks to the Sword Saint who happened to be there. Though hoping for that pretty boy to come crashing in again this time would be way too convenient, obviously."

"I see. So the current Sword Saint, last time. No, in any case, it doesn't matter. What I wanted to know wasn't how she was driven off before, but her personality."

"Her personality?"

Subaru tilted his head at Frederica's words, unable to grasp her point.

"Personality, well—it's exactly what it looks like, a deranged fetish. She loves slitting open other people's bellies and inspecting the contents, the so-called 'Bowel Hunter.' As far as danger goes, she's about as bad as they come."

"And, as you can tell from the way she was so happily crossing blades with Garf, she has a particular fixation on doing it with her own hands... is that right?"

"Not like I'm close with her, but yeah, that's the type she is. ...I'm not seeing where this is going."

"It's simple, Subaru-sama. —Something outside their own plan is happening on their side, in this attack."

It was a firm, decisive declaration. At Frederica speaking so definitively, Subaru's eyes went wide in surprise.

"Right now, the Witchbeasts surrounding the mansion—that girl who was with her is likely the one controlling them, a Witchbeast Tamer, we might call her. As for the original script the enemy intended, I'd think it correct that the Witchbeasts were meant to close the encirclement, assault the mansion, and attack all of us inside."

"Why do you think that?"

"—Because the timing of the attack was off between the Witchbeast Tamer and the Bowel Hunter."

At Frederica's certain words, Subaru knit his brow for a moment, deep in thought. But almost immediately he realized what she was getting at and clapped his hands.

"That's it, that's exactly it! Damn, why didn't I catch that. Yeah, if that's it, Frederica's totally right! Given that lunatic's personality, that's exactly what would happen!"

"W-what does that mean? I'm not following the connection."

Subaru kicked at the floor in excitement and frustration. Otto, unable to keep up with the conversation, wore an anxious face, but Subaru nodded to him.

"It's simple, Otto. The Witchbeast attack was really supposed to corner everyone inside the building. And once we were cornered, unable to escape normally—we'd think, let's head for the hidden passage. That's the natural flow. Right?"

"And that's exactly the flow we're in right now, isn't it? But wasn't the whole point that we can't use the hidden passage because the enemy already knows about it?"

"Right. If the attack had gone according to the correct flow, we'd get cornered, decide to use the hidden passage, flee into that escape route, and get taken down cleanly by Elsa waiting there. That was their script. ...But right now that's been thrown off. Right now, Elsa isn't at the hidden passage."

"—"

Why was that? Given Elsa's nature, the answer was obvious.

"That Elsa hated the thought of losing prey, so she went off and acted on her own. That's why her timing's off from the Witchbeast Tamer's. On top of that, she left her post, the one she was originally supposed to be guarding. —So right now, nobody's at the hidden passage!"

"Originally, that black-clad woman was meant to be lying in wait there. So given this unplanned situation, the chances of reinforcements having moved in there instead are extremely slim. Of course, given time, the enemy will also notice things aren't going as planned. The chance of another person being sent to the hidden passage will keep rising, little by little."

"Then we have to hurry and get out through there before anyone shows up!"

Picking up on Subaru and Frederica's conclusion, Petra practically bounced as she gave the answer. Subaru put a hand to Petra's bright brown hair, ruffling it roughly, and laughed, "Perfect score."

"Given the information we've got, this is the most likely possibility. Either way, it's a more hopeful story than trying to break through the Witchbeasts' encirclement outside. Even in the worst case, just checking the study has some point to it... let's do it. This is the only way to get everyone out safely!"

—And so, spurred by that resolve, the group had now arrived right in front of the study.

Every single one of them was exhausted, in body and in spirit. Even so, the hope of having the goal right before their eyes gave their battered bodies one last burst of vitality to keep moving. And that flickering, clung-to flame of hope—

"...Come on, you've gotta be kidding."

Having reached the top floor, Subaru murmured that without meaning to as he peered down the corridor from around the bend in the stairs. Otto, poking his head out from above in the same way, and Petra, peeking her face out from below, seemed to see the same thing and feel the same way—both were speechless.

"What... is it? The master's study, is it..."

Alone, sitting partway down the steps, Frederica pressed the three scouts for their findings. Even so, she could probably tell from their reactions alone just how bad the situation was. At her somewhat anxious voice, Subaru and the others turned back, muffling their footsteps.

"There's something seriously nasty-looking camped out right in front of the room."

—To Subaru, it looked like the kind of monster they'd call a "Chimera."

It had the head of a fierce feline beast like a lion, and a body with the slender, supple silhouette of a horse or goat. Its long tail whipped sharply about like a writhing serpent, and though its bulk fell short of the hippo-like Witchbeast the tamer had ridden, it was still absurdly huge—big enough to block the mansion's broad corridor. A grotesque presence that looked as though it had leapt straight out of myth—its combat strength could easily be guessed at from that alone.

"That's... the Witchbeast 'Guiltirau.' It l-lives deep in forests thick with Miasma, kind of like the king of ferocious beasts... it shouldn't be the sort of Witchbeast you could bring anywhere near human settlements... let alone inside a mansion..."

"Any chance it's all bark, all show and no substance? Might be surprisingly mild-mannered underneath and satisfied with a bit of dried bonito to chew on..."

"I don't even know what dried bonito is, but should we go up and try feeding it? It'd probably end with about half your body gone in one bite."

At Otto's words, Subaru pictured the size of Guiltirau's head. Yeah. With a mouth that size, someone Subaru's size could probably be finished off in two bites.

"No, but wait, Garfiel when he Beastifies is bigger than that. Okay, let's go get him and have a size-off. If we're bigger, it'll slink away with its tail between its legs, right?"

"And if we go traipsing back to fetch him, we'll just get sliced up by that black-clad woman instead this time. That's not funny, Natsuki-san. ...Did you actually think of something?"

Otto, who'd been trading quips with him, turned toward Subaru with what looked like eyes full of expectation. It was a look as if he'd been hoping the whole time that Subaru would come up with something during their banter. That's an awfully unreasonable expectation to place on me, Subaru thought, turning to Frederica and Petra—

"Subaru." "Subaru-sama."

—only to find the two women looking at him with much the same expectant eyes.

"—Come on, come on, what kind of expectations are you all pinning on me?"

Letting out a deep breath, Subaru shuddered at the sheer weight of the expectations placed on him. Then he shifted Rem's weight on his back once more and closed his eyes.

Their current situation. The fighting strength available to them. Frederica was wounded, and Otto had almost no magic left. Petra and Subaru had no real combat ability, and they were here, on the third floor of the mansion's main building. There was no way to call Garfiel over here, and even considering borrowing Beatrice's power would be presumptuous. That being the case, Subaru had nothing left but his usual style—using everything he had to fight back.

Everyone's abilities, what they could do, what materials they had, the enemy's condition, every last condition—he thought it through, thought it through, thought until he was at his wit's end—and Subaru let out a breath.

"If neither brute force nor magic is going to reach it... then it's finally time to bet on my unrivaled modern-day knowledge."

At first, what drew the Witchbeast Guiltirau's attention was a small sound.

"—"

A light, hard tapping sound rang out again and again, striking the floor, and Guiltirau lifted its snout.

The Silent King of the Forest—that was what Guiltirau was sometimes called in certain regions—unlike other Witchbeasts, disliked making needless roars or noise. Despite its huge, grotesque frame, it bounded lightly across wastelands, approaching prey soundlessly to gouge out a vital point in a single strike and finish it off. It was in that kind of ambush, assassination-like hunting that it excelled the most.

So even though these were orders from 'Master', being made to stay in one spot lying in ambush like this was, to Guiltirau, nothing but a foolish waste of its skills. Of course, it hadn't the slightest intention of being so ungrateful as to defy the orders of 'Master'. After all, it was only because its 'horn' had been broken that Guiltirau had been able to escape its curse in the first place.

"—"

Turning its nose about, Guiltirau probed for the direction of the sound while turning over the orders of 'Master' in its mind. Take up position before this door and hunt down any hostile who approached—that was the duty Guiltirau had been given, and the wish of 'Master'.

That tap, tap, continuing on so carelessly, was clearly footsteps. Like 'Master', many creatures that walked on two legs made this kind of footstep sound as they walked. Some among the strong made no footstep sound at all, but whoever owned these footsteps was not among them. Defenseless, careless, thoughtless, unreserved—not a shred of grace to it. To Guiltirau, this was prey so weak it was practically an annoyance even to tear apart and eat.

"—"

Without a sound, Guiltirau slid away from its post before the door. The footsteps were coming from the direction of the west staircase—the same direction from which, just moments before, sounds like repeated clashes of combat had been echoing.

Guiltirau knew 'Master' had brought many other Witchbeasts besides itself. Most of the ones weaker in strength and smaller in size had surrounded the building, and 'Master', leaving it in charge of defending the door, had gone off to hunt prey riding the back of one particularly large, sluggish Witchbeast. There were plenty of things Guiltirau resented about the choice to bring that bulky-but-that's-it Witchbeast along on the hunt while leaving it behind as backup. Even so, at the very least, if the enemy who appeared here proved to be strong, the reason it had been brought along at all, and its own pride, would be vindicated. That was precisely why, no matter what Witchbeast might be under attack, Guiltirau had committed no such folly as leaving its post to join in, not until an enemy reached its own station.

A weakling who couldn't even make it as far as its post wasn't worth fighting. A weakling who'd be cut down by Witchbeasts weaker than itself wasn't worth hunting either. But this prey had overcome the interference of the other Witchbeasts and made it all the way here. When it sensed that presence, Guiltirau had even felt a secret thrill of excitement stir within it.

—And this was the long-awaited result?

It didn't even know enough to hide its footsteps, and the fighting spirit it gave off was weak and brittle. A fleeting, inferior existence that would scatter to nothing with a single swipe of claw or fang.

"—"

The emotion welling up was anger. Nothing but anger. Tear the prey apart with these fangs, not let a single scrap of blood or flesh settle in its stomach, scatter it all instead. There was no other way left to sublimate this burning feeling, so like humiliation.

Chasing after the footsteps, Guiltirau moved without casting a shadow that wavered in the moonlight. The sight of its huge body gliding along in total silence would have looked, to any onlooker, exactly like something out of a nightmare. The jet-black assassin closed in near the carefree footsteps, and just as its prey seemed to stop right after rounding a corner, it slashed it clean in two from behind with its claws.

"—!"

Without so much as disturbing its breath, Guiltirau stretched its neck and lunged at its prey's back—but—

"—?"

Having closed the distance to within claw's reach, where the presence of prey had just been—there was no sign of it anywhere. Losing the spot to bring down its raised paw, Guiltirau stopped in its tracks at that instant's flash of wrongness. Trembling the tip of its nose, it swung its head around. Wondering where that foolish, brittle, weak prey had gotten to.

"—!"

Once again, the sound of footsteps struck Guiltirau's ears. Lowering its head to look toward the sound, it seemed to have come echoing up from a floor below. The footsteps of prey descending the stepped stairs and fleeing at a run to the lower floor. It seemed the prey had noticed its presence and evaded slightly by picking up its pace, but once Guiltirau realized that, it had no intention of letting its prey escape.

Raising its head, it glanced back at the door it had been ordered by 'Master' to guard. It would mean leaving its post, but this prey surely had to be the very prey 'Master' had ordered it to deal with. Taking its head would be as good as fulfilling the command of 'Master'.

Judging so, Guiltirau chased after the prey fleeing so unbecomingly. To begin with, the moment it had turned its back despite being well within attack range, it had all but declared it had no means to resist. For Guiltirau, back when it had run wild across field and mountain, reigning as king of the forest, hunting down prey that fled about like this for its own amusement had been an everyday occurrence.

Only prey with truly great strength was worth taking into its own body. Prey that turned its back, its resisting fangs already drawn, existed only to keep its own claws and fangs from forgetting the feel of blood and flesh—and that, too, was something they ought to learn.

It descended the stairs, chasing the footsteps. Kicking off the landing wall, sending its huge frame dancing through the air down to the floor below. Chasing the prey fleeing to the second floor, then further to the first, Guiltirau touched down at the very bottom level of the building.

Far off, from some distant part of the building, the presence of combat still reached it. The scent of 'Master', and the reeking stench of that infuriating Master's sluggish Witchbeast it had brought along. And beyond that, the almost tangible scent of blood and steel, the aroma of the strong.

"—"

If it could, Guiltirau too wanted to make its way there and take part in that battle. To swing its claws and fangs as it pleased before 'Master', tear the body of a strong opponent apart and sink it in a sea of blood, and drink in the taste of that victory—how it wished it could. But that wasn't what was wanted of it right now. An order had been handed down.

—If it hunted down this prey quickly enough and caught up in time, would it be allowed to join in after all?

"—!"

Feeling its fangs ache all the more, Guiltirau's body shivered. Following the footsteps as they sounded again, it heard the sound of a door closing, and saw, at the far end of a corridor lost in the dark of night, a door that had just been shut. Swift-footed and silent, it stood before the door, and Guiltirau used its long tail to skillfully pry it open.

This was hardly the first time it had invaded the dwellings of two-legged creatures like this and bared its fangs there. Understanding the structure of what was called a "door," Guiltirau slithered its huge frame into the room. It had braced itself for the prey to be lying in wait, but no sign of the prey could be seen inside the room, and Guiltirau found itself let down yet again. But this time, the disappointment didn't last long.

"—"

Turning its head, Guiltirau's gaze fell on a corner of the room—a wardrobe. From the gap between the wardrobe's twin doors, a bit of the fabric the prey wore was poking out, caught in the door. It must have dived in hastily and caught its clothing in the process. The prey's shallowness, thinking itself fully hidden from Guiltirau without even noticing that, was almost comical.

Guiltirau silenced its footsteps and crept toward the wardrobe. Raising its tail and sharpening its tip, it hesitated not a moment longer.

"—!"

Like the tip of a spear, the loosed point of its tail pierced clean through the wardrobe's doors with ease. Round holes as if punched by an awl—still, coin-sized holes appeared one after another across the wardrobe, skewering through the body of the pitiful prey hidden inside.

Once the number of holes passed twenty, Guiltirau stopped its tail's assault. Then, stretching out a foreleg, it pulled the wardrobe's door down to take in the pitiful sight of what was surely a dead prey by now. The door, riddled with holes, gave way easily, and the prey inside was—

"—Rrgh!?"

The very instant it tried to confirm the prey's corpse, Guiltirau reeled back from a shock that lanced straight through its nasal passages. A savagely pungent stench tore through its sense of smell, a sensation sharp enough to draw a pained groan. Snapping its gaze back at once, it saw, inside the opened wardrobe, a clear glass bottle rigged there had shattered, spilling out a colorless liquid. That was the source of the reeking smell. And what's more, there was no sign of the prey inside the wardrobe at all. The poking-out fabric had just been clothing left sticking out there on purpose.

"—!"

Once again, the sound of footsteps echoed from the corridor outside the room, and Guiltirau spun around. Its nose was useless now, but its ears and eyes were still fine. It saw a shadow dash down the hallway, and though it writhed with humiliation at having its sense of smell sealed off, Guiltirau chased after the shadow anyway.

Never in its entire life had Guiltirau experienced being made such a fool of. Guiltirau, which had until now overwhelmed every enemy it had ever faced, had never once encountered such a wretched creature—one that neither confronted it head-on fair and square, nor fled about helplessly to fall easy prey to its fangs, but instead clung to life so shamelessly, struggling on and on. It would kill it, without fail. Finish it off. Chew it up, scatter it across the earth, and trample it underfoot.

"—"

No longer even remembering to silence its steps, Guiltirau sent the door of the room the footsteps had fled into flying with the force of its huge body. The double doors were blown off effortlessly, and what welcomed Guiltirau's massive frame was a room far, far more spacious than any before it.

A huge table sat in the middle of the room, and at its farthest end stood a fireplace. On the table draped in a white cloth stood a row of lit candlesticks, their flames casting an eerie glow across a room that had until then been lit only by moonlight.

"—"

Fire's presence was a hateful thing to Guiltirau. While it couldn't extinguish that round white orb of fire that hung in the daytime sky, its aversion to flame existing this close at hand ran strong. After all, the forest Guiltirau had once lived in had been consumed by fire, its safe haven lost. And it was in that very moment, with its horn broken, that it had come to serve 'Master'—so for Guiltirau, fire was something that called to mind two contradictory memories at once: liberation, and disgrace.

"—"

There were no footsteps. But Guiltirau didn't miss the sound of something stirring. On the wall opposite the door it had entered through, in this spacious room, was another door. From beyond that door, which likely didn't lead into much depth of space, it could sense a presence.

Guiltirau snorted, but its sense of smell still hadn't returned. It couldn't catch the scent of the prey's fear, of it wetting itself. It was a shame that, in this state, even tearing the prey apart wouldn't let it properly savor the smell of blood or the taste as it drank it in. That sensation, at least, could be put off for later, so long as it managed to tear the prey apart. For now, at any rate, wiping away the humiliation burning in its chest, wringing a death scream from the very prey that had made such a fool of it—that alone was Guiltirau's only salvation.

"—"

Guiltirau stepped forward with its massive frame, heading straight for the room. Then it blew the room's door apart with its tail, sharply extended. Kicking down the door, now riddled with holes just like the wardrobe before, with a foreleg, Guiltirau sucked in a breath and plunged straight into the room.

"—OOOOH!!"

It burst into the room, letting loose a roar. Threatening the prey with its bellow, it would make the flesh of the cowering weakling pay in fang and claw, in blood and meat. Swinging its tail, destruction ran rampant through the room, and clouds of dust billowed up from the sacks and boxes torn open in the cabinets. Its slamming forelegs shattered the floor, ripped through the cloth spread across the ground, and from there too white clouds of dust burst upward—no, clouds thick enough to blot out its whole field of vision kept gushing out, one after another.

"—!?"

Its vision turned white, and the instant it drew breath, Guiltirau's airway was assaulted, sending it into a fit of coughing. Some enormous quantity of powder was swirling through the air. Powder thick enough to strip away its sight, even to steal the very breath it needed to roar.

"It's hooked!"

Someone's voice, someone's, could be heard. And that voice was coming not from this room, but from the room just before it.

"Eat this—the very essence of science! A dust explosion!!"

With a clatter, something was thrown into the white, smoke-hazed room. The brightly flickering thing was one of the candlesticks that had been lined up on the table in the room before. The candlestick struck the wall, its wavering flame dropping to the floor and, for an instant, flaring larger.

"—"

"Uh, huh...?"

But that was all. The candlestick lay there on the floor, showing no further reaction. The voice that had thrown it let out a sound as if something had gone wrong somewhere, and it was clear whoever it was had frozen in place outside the room.

"—!"

By instinct, Guiltirau sensed this as a once-in-a-lifetime chance. Something had gone wrong for its opponent. And had that unexpected turn not happened, danger would have been closing in on Guiltirau itself. Understanding that, Guiltirau spun around and chose to break free of the small room.

Once out in the spacious room, in a space where it could swing its massive limbs and tail to its heart's content, it wouldn't matter what the prey was scheming. It would crush them under an overwhelming difference in strength and wrench out victory. Nothing more than that was needed anymore—

"Ugh, I told you, didn't I—forget the confusing stuff!"

"Doing it the normal way is faster!"

Just as it tried to burst out, Guiltirau heard two more voices belonging to prey. A low voice and a high one. The instant it realized these were the voices of prey of different sexes, Guiltirau sensed a cabinet behind it toppling down onto it.

A cord stretching from the entrance had been tied to the cabinet's leg. Yanked hard from outside, the cabinet toppled and came down on Guiltirau's back. But the cabinet was, at most, only big enough to cover the rear of Guiltirau's massive body. It struck with a bit of momentum behind it, but the damage to Guiltirau was next to nothing.

Taking the impact in stride, Guiltirau sliced through the cord with its claws. This time it truly tried to leap out of the room—

"—?"

A cabinet swung open, and liquid came pouring out from it, splashing generously across half its body. A slick sensation, unlike water. Doused in the slightly yellow-tinged liquid, Guiltirau felt displeasure at its own proud black fur growing wet. But that displeasure of Guiltirau's vanished immediately.

"—!?"

"Courtesy of Otto Suwen's own private stock—commercial-grade oil, every last coin I sank into it—have it all!"

The prey's voice came from outside the room. But by then, Guiltirau had no room left to spare for that weak prey's voice.

—The oil doused over its whole body had caught the candlestick's flame, and that accursed fire was now burning it whole.

"—!!"

The beast king, who had come down from the wilds and clung to its empty throne of the forest to the very end, was swallowed by flames the same color as the humiliation searing through it, never once understanding what had truly defeated it.

Guiltirau: gone before he even got going.
