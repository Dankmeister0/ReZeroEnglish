Arc 4 Chapter 128: "Love Even the Flesh, Blood, and Entrails"

He sank his fangs into the green tail flashing past him, tearing it open in a mindless frenzy.

Purple bodily fluid sprayed out, and a torrent of fresh blood soaked his face, but he paid it no mind. His left eye had long since been sealed shut by a spray of venom.

He masked the burning pain with a roar, slamming his arm down to finish off the double-headed serpent. Kicking the corpse aside, he backed away while keeping watch ahead — then, reacting to a chill that shot up his spine, he snapped backward at once.

A blade of ominous shape swept past, grazing the tip of his chin.

Every witchbeast body caught along the blade's diagonal path fell prey to that fang-like edge. Flesh tore open, fresh blood sprayed out, and he charged straight through the scattering curtain of entrails, driving both drawn-back arms into the torso of the woman who had opened up that carnage.

"——!"

He felt his right shield punch into the woman's chest and his left into her side, each accompanied by the crunch of shattering bone and crushed flesh.

At his ears, before his eyes, above, below, left and right — beast cries, screams, his own roars, impact sounds, the grinding shriek of steel on steel, all tangled together so thoroughly that sound alone could no longer tell him the shape of the world.

It didn't matter. Only what lay before him — the world reflected in his right eye — was real.

The woman's full breast twisted and caved inward, and the force that wrung her innards brought a great gush of blood from her mouth. Even as her already blood-red lips were stained a still darker black-red, and even amid agony that threatened her very life, the pleasure never left her expression.

Perhaps more than her combat strength, more than her endurance in battle, it was that mentality of hers that was the most troublesome thing of all.

"——Hssh!"

"Gaaaahh!!"

A short exhale, and a roar meeting it in kind.

The woman's left arm swung from back to front, and a high, ringing sound chained together behind him — a slash that bounced off the wall, ricocheted off the ceiling, struck the floor, and came for the back of his head.

"——"

The option of turning his attention behind him to dodge it vanished from his mind.

Before his eyes, the woman who had swung her left hand was drawing back her right arm, readying to unleash a black blade fitted with a beast's fang. If he dodged the blade behind him, the lethal strike from the right would slice open his head, or his throat.

He tilted his body at an angle, forcibly wrenching his head out of the path of the blade driving toward the back of his skull.

A dull sound echoed from around his right shoulder blade, and he clicked his tongue as he felt the blade's tip catch and bite into the gap between his bones — the blade had lodged in the joint, and his right arm's movement stopped for an instant.

"R-riiiii—!"

"Shaaa—!"

With a violence savage enough to drown out even sound itself, the drawn-back blade was unleashed.

The blow, thrown without an ounce of restraint, was less a slash than a terrifyingly sharp strike shaped like a blade.

Force enough that a direct hit would blow his head apart, leaving nothing recognizable behind — he threw his left arm up on reflex to cut into its path, but his stance was poor and he hadn't fully absorbed the damage to his right shoulder.

For an instant, metal shrieked as if bitten by a beast, and the arm he'd raised was knocked aside with ease.

With barely any loss of speed, the black blade pressed on toward his head once more. Force enough to blow away half his skull arrived in the space of a blink.

That—

"——!?"

—he kicked up the corpse of a slain witchbeast and wedged it between the blade and the side of his head.

The alien sensation was like pressing a hard-skinned vegetable to his cheek, and the poisonous fluid it carried scalded whatever skin it touched like a burn. Even risking exposure to that, avoiding a fatal blow was worth the trade.

The blade bit into the witchbeast's corpse, and the force of the blow still punched through it into the side of his face.

From left to right, the impact of the blow to his face spun his body, and as he spun he stamped down on the floor once, twice, willing it with all his strength, and leapt backward.

His Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits triggered, and the floor he stamped burst apart as if exploding, obeying his intent.

His body was blown backward by that explosive force, and with the woman now turned away from him, he bore down on her in that same posture — the white blade she'd swung still lodged in his right shoulder.

"—!"

The instant the blade made contact, the woman flinched back slightly.

Even knowing it was the blade's spine, not its edge, that touched her, his sudden movement had thrown off her judgment.

Keeping his right shoulder pressed against her body, he widened his stance and dropped his center of gravity.

The instant the woman stepped one foot back in response, trying to open distance, a left arm shot up from a low trajectory and seized her face in a vice-like grip.

"—Partial Beastification!"

The moment he shouted it, the left arm gripping the woman's face began to change.

Muscle swelled, and the left arm exploded into growth — golden fur sprouted thick before his eyes, transforming it into a beast's arm as thick as a log.

Naturally, at the end of that arm were the beast's blade-like claws, and—

"K-kyaaaah—!"

—thick claw tips gouged into the woman's face, scattering blood and snapping her head back.

It was a wound and pain equivalent to being carved by five blade-fingers, from the outside of her skull to the inside. Even that woman clutched her face and staggered back, screaming up at the ceiling.

"Rrraaah!!"

A straight kick drove into her torso and sent her flying backward.

The force, catching her dead center, was more than enough to further rupture and churn up her already-shattered breastbone and the organs beneath.

The woman collapsed, dropping her weapon, coughing up bright red blood as she let out a broken, halting laugh.

It grated horribly on his ears, and he moved to close in and cut it short, but—

"Tch! One after another, huh!"

As Garfiel moved to press the follow-up attack, witchbeasts came flooding at him through the gaps in the fighting.

Black-winged rats, pouch rats swelling in size in proportion to their fury, mottled king dogs gathered from all over the mansion, and — resurrected once more — the massive bulk of a boulder hog, all came surging forward.

He shredded the swarming black-winged rats with his claws, closed the distance and blew apart a swelling pouch rat with a single strike, kicked and snapped the neck of a mottled king dog lunging to bite him, and turned to face the charging boulder hog head-on.

"Get squashed flat!!"

"Like hell I will, you damn idiot!!"

Several tons of mass came bearing down on him with explosive charging force.

It wasn't so much the impact of a beast as it was a mass-driven projectile, equivalent to a building falling from the sky.

Even Garfiel, whoever he was, wouldn't come away unscathed taking that head-on. He wouldn't even manage to hold for an instant — at best he'd be blown away and crushed flat.

And yet—

"—that's exactly why it's fun!!"

He planted both feet and unleashed his Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits to its fullest.

The earth's blessing surged up through the soles of his feet, and every muscle in his body swelled with a vital, pulsing energy.

His golden eyes drenched in a belligerent light, Garfiel bared his fangs and grinned viciously, letting the blood lurking within him explode outward.

"—Ooooohhh!!"

The wrenching roar wasn't a call outward, but a call turned inward, toward himself.

Coursing through his whole body was a lineage somewhere within him he had never quite been able to accept, never come to love. He called out to it, normally kept hidden away, and as gooseflesh crept over his skin, his soul trembled.

Just like the left arm that had torn open the woman's face, his right arm now swelled explosively.

Starting from both arms, his shoulders, torso, neck, and head followed, his skeleton audibly transforming, and Garfiel's face shifted from that of a human into that of a great feline predator — a giant tiger.

As his torso, waist, and legs swelled larger, his clothes could no longer withstand the pressure from within and burst apart. What remained of him was little more than scraps of cloth caught on his frame, and the shields fitted to both arms now clung barely at the wrists of his swollen limbs like a pair of bracelets — in raw physical size alone, a beast that gave nothing away to the charging boulder hog had manifested right there on the spot.

"——!!"

The floor groaned, caving in deeply beneath his braced feet.

The mansion, sturdily built as it was, couldn't hold up against the confrontation of the two enormous beasts. Bodies too vast to fit within the corridor shattered the walls, and ornaments on the ceiling, scraped by their backs, clattered down.

"—Wagpig!!"

At Garfiel's transformation, the girl riding the witchbeast's back let out a shrill cry.

The cry seemed to be calling the witchbeast's name. Answering its rider's call, the boulder hog let out a bellow like grinding stone and opened a maw lined with millstone-like teeth as it lunged.

The witchbeast bore its weight onto its hind legs, lifting its forelegs, closing in to stomp him flat.

The great tiger, its golden eyes blazing bright, exploded off its hind legs in response, cutting in just before the massive stomp could land, and drove its claws into the thick, rocklike hide.

A sound rang out like a blade driven into bedrock, and the tiger's claws were torn away at the root. The rocky hide let no blade through, and with the charge's momentum unchecked, the giant beast's foreleg slammed into the tiger whose preemptive strike had come up empty.

From directly above, a stomp came down as if to crush both the tiger's shoulders. Pinned upper body pressed against the floor, the tiger's throat let out a scream at the merciless impact of that mass.

"Wagpig, don't you dare stop!!"

Even hearing the sound of bone shattering and flesh grinding apart, the witchbeast's mistress showed no letup.

Hearing her almost tearful voice, the boulder hog roared and reared its forelegs up again, moving for a second stomp to crush the great tiger's head.

But—

"——!!"

If claws couldn't reach and were turned aside, the great tiger had only one weapon left to choose.

Raising its head, the tiger — both shoulders crushed — used its back muscles to haul itself up, and bared its fangs at the boulder hog whose belly lay exposed with its forelegs lifted.

Even a witchbeast whose whole body was armored in rocklike hide couldn't be equally hard everywhere. Compared to its limbs and back, the spots known as vital points were unmistakably less protected.

And so the great tiger sank its sharp fangs into the boulder hog's wide-open belly, left bare as it raised its forelegs.

"Boulder Hoggy!?"

"—Grrrrrr!!"

The great tiger's jaws were large enough to swallow a full-grown human whole, and even the boulder hog's broad belly filled nearly half its open mouth.

For an instant, the boulder hog's hide resisted, refusing to let the tiger's fangs through, but the moment the sharp tips broke the thin skin, like a blade's point splitting open a piece of fruit, they punched through with ease.

The boulder hog's scream tangled together with the momentum of the tiger, still latched on, kicking off the floor to roll sideways.

Spinning its body while keeping its fangs sunk in to tear the prey's flesh apart — this was the predatory behavior of a subspecies of Lesser Dragon known as a Water Dragon, one that dwells along riverbanks.

Had Natsuki Subaru been watching, he likely would have recognized it as something close to the "death roll" behavior of a creature called a crocodile — a creature that didn't exist in this world.

Striking the floor with its hind legs, building momentum from both the sideways roll and its forward motion, it tore the boulder hog's torso apart with its bite.

Beneath its thick hide, the witchbeast's massive body held a vast quantity of organs and blood, equal to its own mass, and all of it came pouring mercilessly out through the fang wounds into the mansion's corridor.

"——Oh."

Rolling its eyes back white, the boulder hog let out a feeble death cry and collapsed.

The great tiger spat out the mouthful of flesh and blood it had torn from the boulder hog, then kicked the massive body over onto its side with a hind leg. The girl, who had dismounted from the witchbeast's back the instant of the collision, seemed at a loss for words, having witnessed her own witchbeast's gruesome death firsthand.

"N-no way... I can't believe it..."

Stepping back, the girl turned to glance behind her at what remained of her forces.

Answering her whistle, numerous witchbeasts had been streaming into the area one after another. But they were only a mass of small and mid-sized ones — the large ones, like the boulder hog, were spent.

"Ugh! This is the worst! Elsa! Elsa! Do something about this!"

"...My, aren't you a demanding one."

Sensing she was at a disadvantage, the girl cursed the unfairness of it all and called out her companion's name. In answer, a woman clad in jet black slowly crawled out of the darkness.

Her gouged face had already regenerated, and she toyed idly, almost annoyed, with a braid soaked in fresh blood.

"Gouging a woman's face without a shred of hesitation — my, you really are wonderful."

"—Aooh! Ooh! Ooh!"

At the woman smiling with a face slicked in blood, the great tiger, both shoulders shattered, gave an excited growl.

Its enormous body trembling, it struck its head against the fallen boulder hog's body and retched.

The tiger kept up its pained growling as it gradually lost its massive form, its swollen body beginning to take back its human shape. A few seconds later, shaking off the golden fur that had fallen away, a half-naked boy stood there in its place.

"Ahh... damn it, I'm back. Head's killin' me..."

"I see... so you're a half-beast. I did think your eyes looked a bit too fierce for a human."

"If that logic holds, then our boss ain't human either, I guess."

Shaking his head, Garfiel checked the feel of his body, now returned to human form.

In the process of his skeleton reverting to human shape, his shattered shoulders had knit back together enough to move. Still, a grinding pain shot through him with every motion, and he could feel his thoughts whiting out with it.

He wouldn't be able to move at full strength for long.

But by now, that had to be just as true for his opponent.

"In your place, I went and spilled your precious witchbeast's guts all over the floor for you. Feel free to go for a swim in that sea of blood, if it makes you happy."

"I'll pass. A beast's entrails are no substitute, not unless I'm truly starving. Entrails are beautiful because they come from cutting open a person's belly."

"Can't make heads or tails of that aesthetic of yours."

He stuck his pinky in his ear, scratching roughly as he let out a sigh of exasperation.

Before Garfiel's eyes, Elsa, despite being in an overwhelmingly disadvantageous position, showed no sign of dropping her composure.

—Elsa's undying constitution: five times, at the most generous estimate, before it would finally give out.

Since making that judgment, Garfiel had already dealt her four fatal wounds. Counting the strike that had just gouged her face, that made five. By now, her regenerative power should be showing signs of hitting its limit.

In other words, Elsa's stock of lives was as good as exhausted. Garfiel himself was wounded too, but he had no intention of falling behind in the fight even so.

With no more help to be expected from Meili's witchbeasts, the two of them were as good as holding blades to each other's throats — and yet, what was with that composed, unbothered manner of hers?

"There's no special reason for it, really. You don't need to be so frightened."

Elsa said it the way one might soothe a small child, looking at Garfiel as he knit his brows suspiciously.

At her words, Garfiel wrinkled his nose and let out a beast's growl.

As if to cover for the fact that some small unease that had risen in his heart had just been seen through.

"Don't screw with me. Don't go talkin' like you can see right through me."

"You can tell just by watching. Cutting open someone's belly means facing someone before their belly's been cut open. And your face — it's one I know well."

"——"

"The face of someone who can't understand a monster."

With the illusion of his throat closing up, Garfiel lost his words, and Elsa laughed behind a hand held to her mouth. Smiling faintly, she tilted her head and said,

"It's fine, really. I've never wanted anyone to understand me. My happiness is mine alone, won by trampling someone else's life underfoot. To live is to trample on death."

"...Listenin' to you seriously would drive a guy crazy, I get that much now."

He raised both arms and clashed his shields together, abandoning any attempt to understand her.

He didn't have the luxury of sparing thought for her circumstances. And any whim of a reason to spare his attention on her had, with those last words, all but vanished.

"I'll ask, just so it's said... if you swear off doin' bad shit for good and run, I might just let you off."

"You really are a sweet boy."

Her laughing off the last shred of mercy he'd shown was the signal for the clash to begin.

His lunge exploded forward, and Garfiel's body shot in a straight line. The white blade rose to meet him, swung vertically, striking the ceiling, striking the floor, the spinning blade closing in on Garfiel as it ricocheted.

The thick-bladed white sword Elsa held was made of many blades layered and linked together, joined end to end in alternating angles — an undulating blade like a serpent's spine, bounding wildly back and forth across the corridor.

High or low — the blade, moving far too fast to track by eye, flew about as a streak of white light, and Garfiel gave up on dodging, raising both shields to cover his head instead. The blade, rebounding from above, sank into his upper left arm, and even tasting the pain of bone shattering beneath the blow, he pressed forward.

"Gusteko, the northern land I was born in, was a terribly, terribly cold place."

On a battlefield where blows were traded in split seconds, the woman's singsong voice somehow slipped smoothly into Garfiel's eardrums.

There was no way he should be hearing it. In the white-hot instant of trading blows that carried death, there shouldn't have been any gap for a voice like that to slip into.

And yet, despite that, the woman's voice slipped smoothly through Garfiel's awareness and crept in regardless.

"It was a country with a brutal gap between rich and poor, and among the poor, abandoned children were nothing rare. I was one of those children too — by the time I had any sense of the world, I had no parents, and I lived slurping muddy water to survive."

"—Raaah!!"

"I got by day to day however I could — stealing things, threatening people — and the faces around me changed all the time. What was I living for, what did happiness even mean... those were days with no time to spare for questions like that."

He swung a fist through, aiming to blow Elsa's face apart.

But the broad swing was dodged with a lean of her body, and the black blade, swung up from below, gouged a shallow diagonal cut across Garfiel's torso.

His flesh torn away by the beast's fang, Elsa licked her lips as she was bathed in the spray of fresh blood.

"That day, in particular, was a cold one."

"Shut it! I ain't listenin'!!"

"The wind blowing down off the high mountains was strong and cold, and the whole town had frozen over. In that bitter cold, cold enough to freeze the breath from my own lips, I was caught stealing by a shopkeeper."

Elsa let out a breath heavy with heat, speaking with a rapturous look on her face.

The momentum of the swinging blade of death only grew, and cut after cut began piling up on Garfiel's body as his left-side guard failed to keep pace.

"It was the kind of situation where I couldn't have complained if he'd killed me, but I was a girl. I can still remember that man's face, laughing coarsely as he tried to strip my clothes off."

"Gah...!"

"With the cold wind blowing, he stripped off my coat, took my underclothes too... and just as I thought I'd freeze to death before he could even do whatever he meant to do, I happened to pick up a shard of glass."

As a long leg lashed up aiming for the side of his head, Garfiel deliberately shot it down with a headbutt. The impact rang through his skull hard enough to snap his head back, but Elsa's instep should have shattered too.

Elsa drew her leg back and retreated. But her expression never wavered from that rapturous look.

"I didn't think it through or anything. I just took the glass shard I'd picked up and drove it into the belly pressing down on me, moved it, and cut him open."

"——"

"I didn't feel anything — not his screams, not any weight to having taken a life. I just, there in the cold wind, thought to myself—"

Before Garfiel, who had gone still, holding his breath, Elsa smiled, entranced.

"Blood and entrails — how warm they are."

The blade rose, closing in to crack open Garfiel's skull. He slid his body sideways, kicked off the wall to circle behind Elsa, and threw a kick meant to shatter her spine — but as she spun, the flat of her blade struck his shin and knocked the kick off course.

The wall, kicked to pieces, sent up a cloud of dust, and Garfiel clicked his tongue as he leapt back.

"If there's such a thing as happiness in this world, it's exactly that — a warmth and beauty that makes you forget the cold. It's the first certain shape of happiness I ever found, I who was born into nothing at all. You can't understand it, can you?"

"Don't want to, either."

"That's just fine. I've never wanted anyone's sympathy anyway."

"Then why the hell tell me all that? Makes me sick."

"Now, why is that, I wonder?"

Elsa tilted her head, looking genuinely puzzled at Garfiel, hostility plain in his eyes.

Then, narrowing her eyes with a lascivious glint, she traced her lips with a red tongue in a sultry motion, and—

"It must be because I truly, truly adore you."

"...Sorry, but I got a woman I'm in love with. Ain't got time to waste on some crazy bitch."

"How cold. But that's fine. What I want is just what's inside you, anyway."

Their conversation seemed to connect, and yet at its root, it didn't connect at all.

Through this whole exchange with her, Garfiel finally understood as much.

He felt no interest, no sympathy, nothing at all for Elsa's life story.

Someone with the groundwork for it had gone through experiences like that and simply become a monster like this.

Garfiel's shields had long since chosen what they were meant to protect.

"—I'm gonna kill you, Elsa Granhiert."

"Only after you kill me will I finally love you, Garfiel Tinzel."

Calling out each other's names, once exchanged, the half-beast and the murderer raised violence high.

The white blade cut through the corridor's darkness as a streak of white light, and the drawn-back black blade was loosed, meaning to tear Garfiel's body open lengthwise.

At the edge of his vision, the blade bounded about with no regard for up, down, left, or right. Against an attack with no way to block it, Garfiel once again discarded the option of dodging. But failing to fully absorb it and having his charge's momentum killed would just be repeating the same folly twice over.

"——"

The blade tore through sound, leaping across the corridor.

If its tip couldn't be seen, there was nothing for it but to aim for the point it was launched from.

Thrusting out his left arm, Garfiel hurled the shield he'd loosened its binding on.

Loosened during their earlier clash, it flew and struck Elsa's left hand square on, her eyes going wide — there was a shattering sound, and he saw her fingers crumple as she dropped the white blade.

The bounding blade, having lost its wielder, lodged itself in the ceiling and went still.

A black smile deepened, and a roar surged upward. The blade carrying death came down, killing the very air as it fell, striking against Garfiel's body as he charged straight ahead.

He put his right arm directly above his head and took the full force of the black blade coming down in a vertical split.

The shockwave punched through the shield and shook his head violently. His vision spun, and he barely caught himself with a planted foot as he nearly pitched forward.

He'd weathered it — and the very next instant, the woman's knee snapped up and crushed the face of Garfiel, bent forward as he was.

"You mustn't let your guard down just because you think you've blocked it."

Her voice, laced with suppressed laughter, reached him as Elsa swung her leg up at Garfiel, reeling backward.

Raising high the knee that had just crushed his nose, she brought her heel down — a dull gleam of a blade concealed within it — its tip driving toward Garfiel's throat, and—

"—it's you who's overlookin' my weapon—"

—his open mouth swallowed the descending heel, blade and all, and bit clean through that slender leg.

With the blade and the bone down to her ankle bitten clean through, Elsa's eyes went round.

"Oh my."

Letting out a startled sound, Elsa staggered and lost her balance, sinking down onto the floor.

Her right foot, shattered below the ankle, was useless, and both her arms, crushed by the recoil of her own attacks, were of no use either. Bracing herself on her left leg, Elsa gazed at Garfiel with the only part of her still whole — her head — and,

"—Ahh."

She drew in a breath, and her cheeks flushed like a girl in love.

Her breath grew heated enough to color the air, and her moist eyes filled with a fervor no amount of wiping could clear away.

—Before Elsa's eyes, Garfiel hoisted up the boulder hog's massive body and hurled it.

Knowing full well the future in which that arcing mass of a projectile would crush her flat, Elsa never took her eyes off Garfiel, not until the moment the shadow swallowed her.

To the golden-haired boy baring his fangs, breathing hard, she offered her love—

"It sends shivers through me."

A tremendous weight crushed the murderer, the vampire, the Bowel Hunter, utterly and completely flat.

The sound of flesh crumpling, fresh blood spilling out mixed with the witchbeast's bodily fluids.

Catching the scent of death, Garfiel let out a roar.

His battle cry rang out high, high above, echoing through the burning, collapsing mansion.

—Here, at last, the battle between Garfiel Tinzel, Shield of the Sanctuary, and Elsa Granhiert, the Bowel Hunter, reached its end.
