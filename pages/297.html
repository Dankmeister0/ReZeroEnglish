Arc 4 Chapter 127: "The Last Day of Roswaal Manor"

"Otto. You mentioned this before, didn't you—something about applying wind and water magic to throw the sound of footsteps somewhere far off."

"...Now that you mention it, I suppose I did say something like that. I'm surprised you remembered. Certainly, a spell that simple I could manage even with my mana tanks running on empty like now... but how would you use it? This only really works for making an opponent turn their back on you for a moment, you know?"

"The way to use it is exactly what you just said. Make footsteps, draw its attention, get it to come down to where we've set the trap. —After that, I'll blow it sky-high with the very essence of science."

"You sound awfully confident, but what exactly is this 'essence of science'...?"

"An easy, ultimate, guaranteed finisher: a dust explosion. The method and materials are simple as magic—wonder of wonders. Just fire, and a little flour, and boom, you've got it. Going by what I know of its power, it should be more than enough to blow one monster sky-high, with change to spare."

"You went and said something like that, and I trusted you and went along with it, and look what happened!"

"Shut up! Sacrifice is inseparable from the progress of science! Why'd it fail, huh? Not enough flour? Fire too small?... Or maybe—don't tell me the physical laws in this world are subtly different? Is that why the dust explosion—"

"Enough! Never mind that, just beat it out properly! Ah, no! No good!"

Subaru and Otto shouted back and forth at each other, while Petra cut in between them, yelling something of her own.

The three of them were currently in the dining hall on the manor's first floor, lit up brilliantly by flames. The reason for that was simple:

"You used too much oil! How are we supposed to put this out!? It's spreading right before our eyes!"

"There's no way I could hold back when we're hunting down something as huge as that Witchbeast! Besides, if we can't take it with us anyway, it doesn't matter whether we burn it or leave it behind—same result! I'm absolutely making sure you pay me back for this, understand!"

"Both of you, quiet! This isn't the time for that! We can't put it out anymore! Let's just run!"

"That sounds like something a middle schooler would say after botching some fireworks and losing control of the situation..."

Subaru said this with a look of exasperation, then noticed the tablecloth he was holding had also caught fire. No matter how he beat at it, the flames showed no sign of going out, so he gave up and tossed it into the blaze.

The fire that had started in the storeroom was now spreading rapidly, and the flames had begun to reach toward the dining hall's kitchen as well. Before long, it looked like it might catch the cooking mana ore and set off an explosion.

"The sacrifice we paid is way too big..."

Subaru's mouth twisted as he looked down at the charred corpse of the Witchbeast lying near the boundary between the storeroom and the dining hall. It was the same creature that had been blocking the entrance to the third-floor study with the hidden passage—lured down to this floor by Otto's sneak-thief magic, and burned to death after being doused in oil from the storeroom. That was the situation now.

It had a musclehead brain to match its massive frame, falling for every trap without a hint of suspicion—a stroke of luck, honestly. Fortunately, it also seemed to have a weakness to fire; once the flames caught it, it thrashed about in what looked like a panic, right on the spot, until it burned to nothing.

When the dust explosion he'd prepared ended in a dud, Subaru had wondered what on earth they'd do—but the backup plan, the oil Otto and Petra had stockpiled just in case, had paid off.

No matter how much Subaru had explained the terrifying power of a dust explosion, the two of them had never quite understood it—and this one time, their lack of comprehension, which had led them to set up a fallback plan, had saved them.

If there was a problem, though, it was that the flames used to burn the Witchbeast to death showed no sign of going out even after they'd achieved their goal of defeating it.

The fire burned the manor's walls, burned the food stores in the storeroom, and the flames were reaching the legs of the dining hall's great table.

Black smoke, and the smell of a world catastrophically charring away. Their vision was starting to haze over, and without a fire truck, there simply weren't enough water-magic users on hand to fight the blaze.

"Honestly, with Garfiel and Elsa fighting and Witchbeasts wandering around, I knew renovations were going to be necessary... but I didn't think it'd get to the level of a full rebuild."

"This isn't the time to be saying that, Natsuki-san. We should follow Frederica-san and the others and get out too. Quickly, before the stairs up to the third floor are gone."

"Hurry! Hurry!"

As the familiar scenery around them was swallowed in flame, the sight felt unreal to Subaru, and the other two tugged at his sleeve.

Aside from Subaru, only Otto and Petra were present here. Frederica, who had been with them, and Rem, whom she'd been carrying on her back, had split off once the operation to trap the Witchbeast began—timing it for when the creature moved away from the door, they'd been entrusted with securing the hidden passage in the study.

There had been some anxiety about leaving Rem in the care of an injured Frederica, but purely in terms of combat strength, it was the reasonable choice. Even with one arm out of commission, Frederica could likely still hold her own better than Subaru and Otto could.

In any case, they had succeeded in driving off the Witchbeast.

Praying that, as they'd predicted, there were no other enemies in the study or the hidden passage, Subaru and the others dashed out of the dining hall and raced up the stairs toward the top floor once more.

"What are we gonna do if Garfiel ends up burning to death because of this!"

"Garfiel isn't that stupid, I'm sure he's fine! Besides, if it's just him alone, he could easily scatter the Witchbeasts outside and get away!"

Subaru worried over the unexpected way things had turned out—setting a fire that ended up cutting into the battlefield itself. Otto's shout hit the mark, but how things would actually turn out was another matter—

"Frederica-neesama!"

Even as he mulled things over, the three of them reached the top floor.

Out of breath, Petra spotted Frederica standing in front of the study and waved. Seeing Petra's state, Frederica seemed to read the success of the operation from it, and her face relaxed with relief.

"Thank goodness, you're all safe. I'm relieved no one's missing."

"Excuse me, why are you staring straight at my face while you say that? Are you implying I looked like I might be the one missing? Honestly, even I'm going to start crying pretty soon!"

"Yeah, yeah, calm down, calm down. Improving your treatment is a task for another day—right now, solving the immediate problem takes priority. Frederica, the hidden passage?"

"It worked without a problem. As for the path, I confirmed it's safe at least as far as the small chamber... By the way, is it just my imagination, or does something smell burnt?"

Frederica narrowed her long, slender eyes, asking after the source of the smoky smell tickling her nose. Subaru grimaced and looked to Otto and Petra, but the two of them just shook their heads in unison.

"Ah, well, actually there was a bit of a mishap—the fire we used to take down the Witchbeast got a little too big. As a result..."

"...The manor has begun to burn. I wasn't expecting the building to come out of this unscathed, but it seems it's going to burn to the ground entirely. ...Well, it can't be helped—not when it's a matter of life."

"Oh, you get it. Yeah, that's right. There are sacrifices you just can't avoid."

"I myself have little attachment to the main house. Ram, on the other hand, is quite attached to it, so do brace yourself to be blamed for this later."

"Uweeh..."

Imagining being torn into mercilessly and endlessly, Subaru's spirits already sank at the thought of the reunion to come.

Still, being able to worry about the future like that was a good sign. Frederica gave a wry smile at Subaru's reaction, and a sense of relief spread through the group.

"Now then, since we've gone and set ourselves a whole new time limit on top of everything, let's get moving on the escape already. Frederica takes the lead, then Petra and Otto after her. The moment you clear the escape route, get to safe ground—though in this case it's hard to say which side of the barrier counts as the right one, so just follow Frederica's instructions. Best case, you link up with Patrasche and the villagers we sent ahead."

His joking done, Subaru quickly laid out the plan going forward.

Frederica and Otto's expressions tightened, and they nodded firmly at the plan. But it was Petra who furrowed her brow at the explanation.

Petra raised a small hand and called his name—"Subaru?"—then said,

"Th-that's kind of a weird thing to say, isn't it? The way you put it, it sounds like Subaru's not coming with us..."

"—Ah, yeah. Sorry, but I can't escape together with you from here. I'm splitting off."

"Why!?"

Petra let out a cry of shock as Subaru confirmed her fear.

Her outstretched hand grabbed at his hem, her fingertips trembling as if to keep Subaru from getting away.

"Let's just run away already! The manor's burning down, and there are scary people and Witchbeasts everywhere! Even if Subaru fights, you can't win, right? Let's run, okay?"

"Well, yeah, that's actually true, so I can't really argue with it, but I'm not going to fight. Though in a way, maybe it is a kind of fight."

Even as it warmed him that Petra was trying to hold him back, Subaru gently pried her fingers loose one by one. Watching grief spread across her big, round eyes ached his heart.

From behind, Otto patted Petra's shoulder, careful not to provoke her further.

"Petra-chan. Natsuki-san has something he has to do. Until he's accomplished it, he can't leave the manor."

"But! Subaru's weak, isn't he! It's dangerous! Why don't you stay instead, Otto-san!"

"That's not exactly a statement born of faith in my strength, is it!?"

Petra shook her head at Otto's protest and looked up at Subaru with tears welling in her eyes. Subaru knelt down to meet her gaze and stroked her head.

"Sorry, Petra. I'm going to get you, and Rem, and Frederica all safely out of the manor. But that alone doesn't cover the whole reason I came back. There's one more person I have to bring out."

"B-Beatrice-sama?"

"That's right. Have you met her?"

Petra shook her head.

It had been a little over ten days since Petra started working at the manor. That shut-in girl hadn't shown herself even once to the live-in staff girl. She was hardcore about it.

And here Subaru had at least left the room to use the bathroom, if nothing else.

"D-does someone like that really exist? Isn't it just... you overthinking things, some kind of delusion...?"

She probably didn't mean it that way, but at this point Petra was even doubting Beatrice's very existence.

It was the "surely not, but is this person perhaps someone who exists only inside your own head" kind of doubt.

"She's a pain, lonely but nosy about looking after people, the type to shoulder everything alone, come up with her own answers, and suffer over them—and since she can't put an end to it herself, she wants someone else to do it for her."

"........."

"I'd never dream up someone like that as an ideal. If I were making her up as some fantasy character, I'd have made her way sweeter on me, a maxed-affection helper character."

The girl who actually existed never went along with what he wanted, not even once; she'd lost track of both her own wishes and everyone else's, was trying to stop thinking altogether, and was an absolute nuisance.

So, someone had to go and tell her.

"Beatrice, you know, Petra—she's about the same age as you, roughly. In a lot of ways, being kind of grown-up for her age—Petra, you might resemble her very first friend."

"Her... first friend?"

Subaru recalled the story of the past that Ryuzu—Theta—had told him.

A girl named Ryuzu Meyer, who had once formed a friendship with Beatrice, and who had gone on to remain as a wound in her heart that never healed.

Between her and Beatrice, even if neither of them had been fully conscious of it, there had certainly existed a friendship plain for anyone looking from outside to see.

"Petra. Once I bring Beatrice back, I want you to be her friend, okay? I bet you'll like her. She's the kind of person who's seriously fun to tease."

"More than Otto-san?"

"Yeah. To the point I won't even need Otto anymore."

Otto looked like he had something to say to that, but Subaru deliberately ignored it.

Then Subaru took his hand off Petra's head and stood up.

"Like I said. I'm going to go find Beatrice. I'll do my best not to burn to death, but if I do, make sure it's recorded properly that the cause of death was a fire started by Otto's oil."

"I'd rather that didn't happen, so if you don't come back safe, I really will beat you senseless."

Otto answered with a look of irritation, pulling Petra's shoulder toward himself.

As if drawing a line between Subaru, who was splitting off, and the other four.

"Frederica. I'm counting on you for the rest."

"I shall spare nothing of myself and will surely carve out a path to safety."

"Don't spare nothing. If you end up in a state we can't get back, there was no point in coming here."

Frederica's eyes went wide.

Seeing her surprised like that felt like witnessing something rare, and it gave Subaru a small, satisfying thrill.

Last, Subaru glanced at Rem on Frederica's back. The sleeping princess, unchanged, showed no sign of seeing off Subaru's resolve.

That was fine. Rem wasn't in the position of seeing him off. Subaru was the one in the position of going to meet her.

"Subaru, be careful, okay!"

Turning his back on the four of them, Subaru broke into a run.

As he left, Petra's voice washed over his back. But Subaru didn't look back anymore. And Petra wasn't asking him to, either.

Little by little, the flames were beginning to engulf the manor.

—Would these flames reach the Forbidden Archive? As he laid a hand on an untouched door, Subaru couldn't help but wonder.

A shield caught the blade swung down at it, deflecting the strike amid the grinding shriek of steel and a shower of sparks.

Threading the gap that opened between them, a kick wrapped in a fierce gust swept toward her torso; Elsa took the blow while spinning her body to disperse the impact, and using the momentum of that spin, swung the blade in her other hand at Garfiel's temple, closing in on cleaving his head in two—but,

"Too soft!"

"Oh my, how harsh."

His jaws opened wide and bit down on the blade—the fourth time that day he'd caught a white blade between his fangs.

In an instant, the force of his jaw crushed the knife to pieces, and Elsa pulled her hand back and leapt away behind her. Her smile deepened at having her weapon taken from her.

"One misstep and your head would've gone flying—you really do have some nerve."

"I've gotcha the knack of it now. And I'm 'bout ready to get sick of your fightin' style, too."

"How cold. Acting like you've figured out everything about a woman after such a short while."

"...Quit talkin' like that, gettin' under my skin every damn time, oi. —Huh."

Garfiel stuck a finger in his ear, grimaced, and then, noticing something, gave a sniff.

Then he turned his gaze to the far side of the corridor, and the corner of his mouth curled up.

Elsa and the others didn't seem to have noticed yet, but Garfiel's nose had caught a strange smell. It was the scent of burning wood and stone—in other words, the smell of fire.

"—Ah, geez, unbelievable! What a useless thing!"

Right after Garfiel smiled, the girl watching the fight from atop her Witchbeast puffed out her cheeks.

Elsa glanced sideways at her, wondering what was wrong, and Meili, still pouting, said,

"Somehow, the shadow lion that was supposed to be holding those brothers back seems to have died. It never listened anyway, and it lost its temper so easily, so it was a hard one to handle to begin with... but why would it go and die on a job that just meant taking a nap in front of a door?"

"I think the real problem is why you brought such a useless Witchbeast along in the first place."

"Right now, this one and the shadow lion were the only ones that weren't in breeding season or hibernation. And still it went and died—unbelievable."

Speaking as though lamenting it, Meili tossed yet another knife over to Elsa. Catching it and testing its feel, Elsa seemed largely indifferent to the report.

Meili, too, didn't seem to feel much over the dead Witchbeast. A pitiable creature.

In any case, Garfiel wore a fierce grin at the news he'd overheard.

"Ohh, scary. Scary-faced cute brother's making an even nastier face."

"Can't beat the boss's ugly mug, though. And, just like I said, didn't I? Any sly little scheme of yours is a piece of cake once the boss and his merry band get their hands on it."

"Certainly, it seems this has exceeded our expectations... but so what? Even if a single useless Witchbeast gets slaughtered, we still have plenty more in reserve. And you, the crucial piece of the combat force, remain pinned down here... the crisis those children are in shouldn't change much at all."

"Guess that's true."

At Elsa's words, the two knives dangling in her hands, Garfiel crossed his arms.

His nose twitched, and he recalled the exchange of blows with Elsa up to this point—then made his decision.

"'Bout time I got things movin', I reckon."

"What are you—"

"Elsa!"

Elsa knit her brow at Garfiel's manner. But before her question could take shape as words, it was Meili who let out a sharp cry.

Looking over, the boulder pig Meili was riding had changed the color of its eyes, its huge body stamping in place in a state of agitation. Meili's calls gradually brought its excitement back under control, but the Witchbeast, too, had apparently finally noticed the same trace of fire that Garfiel had.

Soothing the boulder pig's agitation with a hand, Meili then turned to Elsa with a stern expression.

"Elsa, the building's on fire. Seems like someone set it."

"........."

"'Someone,' huh, that's beatin' around the bush. —Gotta be the boss. Makes sense, real straightforward, I like it. A Witchbeast's still a beast. Fastest way to clear 'em out and make a path is to spook 'em with fire."

"No way... he came to save the people inside, and now he's burning the manor down and running away with them?"

Meili was left speechless at the decisiveness of the act. Elsa, too, seemed to be struggling to reconcile the information with her existing image of Subaru.

But against their shock, Garfiel's heart was strangely calm.

He hadn't, of course, been told beforehand that Subaru would go this far. He'd known the man was capable of bold action, but even Garfiel hadn't expected him to go so far as to burn the manor down. And precisely because of that, the sheer nature of the man Garfiel had decided to follow felt all the more exhilarating.

More than anything, the fact that this situation had been set in motion lit a literal fire under Garfiel.

"A burnin' manor. Outside, a pack of Witchbeasts."

"—?"

"People that gotta be saved, and a tough enemy that's gotta be held back. The only one who can fight is me, and the boss left sayin', 'I'm leavin' this to you.'"

"What are you even saying, brother..."

"Ain't it obvious?"

Elsa tilted her head, and Meili looked at him as though at something eerie.

Against the doubts of the two of them, Garfiel clacked his fangs together, his heart bright and clear.

His body felt light. There was nothing left to fear.

"With conditions lined up this perfect, where in this world's there a man who wouldn't burn?! Ahh, I'm doin' this. 'The Sword Saint Reid draws his blade before the Dragon and laughs'—that's how it goes!"

"That's a proverb that means 'a lunatic out of his mind,' you know?"

"I know that. So? With me and you both here, what's wrong with that?"

At Garfiel affirming his own foolishness with a downright refreshing momentum, Elsa's face went blank with surprise for just an instant. But that lasted only a few seconds.

She quickly broke into a grin, licking her lips with a red tongue and letting the corners of her eyes soften into a bewitching smile.

"That's right. Truly, it is. Just as you say."

Affirming it, Elsa leveled the curved blades in both her hands at Garfiel standing before her.

Crossing the blades, letting her long black hair dance, she tilted her head and said,

"But what will changing your mindset actually do, I wonder? It's not as though you'll suddenly grow stronger, and you already know my constitution well enough from all our clashes, don't you? Calling it back-and-forth would be generous given how many more wounds are on my side, but it's true this exchange has been getting rather fruitless."

"Guess so."

It had been over ten minutes since the fight between Garfiel and Elsa began.

They had already clashed steel well over a hundred times, grinding each other down blow by blow.

In terms of raw combat ability, Garfiel held a slight edge. He surpassed Elsa marginally in strength, speed, and technique alike, and had never once ceded the initiative.

But Elsa possessed an inhuman regenerative ability that healed her wounds within seconds, and on top of that, her own nature of taking pain not as pain but as pleasure meant she showed no hesitation in either attack or defense.

If she charged in willing to take a wound, even Garfiel would end up injured. And while Garfiel needed time to heal his wounds, Elsa needed none.

Since he was at a disadvantage against her in terms of stamina, if the fight settled into a stalemate, it would be Garfiel, not Elsa, who'd be caught by the blade before long.

But—

"Five times... no, six? That's how many times I've punched clean through you."

"Was it, now? And what of it?"

Landing a direct kick, slamming her into the wall with a fist behind his shield, or grabbing her leg and throwing her headfirst into the floor—Garfiel had, several times over, dealt Elsa what should have been fatal wounds.

Each time, though, she recovered at once, so it never amounted to a real sense of progress—

"Four or five more, at most—that's my read on it, anyway."

"........."

"Vampire or not, you ain't some immortal monster. Pile up enough wounds that'd kill anyone else, and... your life'll run out too, eventually. Just gotta make that happen before the manor burns down, is all."

Widening his stance, Garfiel bared his fangs in a savage grin.

At that, the smile vanished from Elsa's face, which had listened to Garfiel's words in silence. She toyed with the tip of her long braid between her fingers and let out a small breath.

"Meili. —Hand that over, and you go on ahead."

"Elsa... are you serious?"

"If a reason to do so has arisen, then not doing so would be rude to my opponent, wouldn't it? It's only a shame that I might not be able to pull the bowels out cleanly."

Meili added no further words to Elsa's answer, given with closed eyes.

The girl dropped the holder she'd been using to supply replacement knives onto the floor, and instead pulled out a different holder—one holding only two knives—and tossed it to Elsa.

"Heh..."

Watching Elsa draw the blades from the holder she'd caught, Garfiel gave a pleased growl in his throat.

That was how much the pair of blades Elsa had just taken up radiated a sense of menace unlike anything before.

The blade Elsa held in her left hand was colored jet black from grip to edge entirely. At a glance, its form looked the same as her previous curved blades, but the blade itself was warped, fitted with countless serrations like a beast's fangs—a weapon specialized not for slicing but for gouging and tearing.

And the blade in her other hand, in contrast to the jet-black form, was a stark white with a thick, heavy width. This one, too, shared the curved-blade shape, but its heavy width seemed built to smash through and sever even bone, and combined with the black blade, it clearly amplified the violent impression.

"That yer trump card, then?"

"I only allow myself to use these when killing my opponent takes priority over viewing their bowels. You're only the third person I've used them on, not counting my mother."

"That's a real unpleasant honor, and a real un-enviable home life, oi."

Garfiel grimaced at the unpleasant confession.

Before Garfiel's eyes, Meili, having handed the knives to Elsa, commanded her Witchbeast and wheeled around. The lumbering creature planted its four feet on the floor, cracking the wall as it began to move toward the main building to chase after Subaru and the others. —However,

"Well, since you went and showed me your trump card, wouldn't it be unfair if I didn't show you mine?"

Stamping down on the ground as though driving it into the earth, Garfiel declared this.

An instant later, a vibration ran through the floor from the sole of Garfiel's foot, tore down the corridor, passed beneath Elsa's feet, and reached directly under the Witchbeast behind her—where the shock burst forth.

"—!?"

"Boulder-pig-chan!?"

The ground beneath it collapsed as though scooped out, and the boulder pig, losing its balance, crashed sideways into the wall. The impact shook the manor, and Meili, unable to keep her footing atop the beast, landed on the corridor floor. Stroking the fallen boar's rump, she looked at the floor, saw the unnaturally sunken ground, and turned back around.

"Don't tell me... this is your doing, brother?"

"Yeah, more or less. Just think of it as me makin' my point—I ain't lettin' you go. Long as your feet are on the ground, you can't get away from anyplace my eyes can reach."

Invoking the power of the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits, Garfiel could freely raise or collapse any place within his sight that could be judged as "ground." Of course, the scale and effective range varied with size and distance, but it was more than enough to make a convincing bluff.

Garfiel had learned from Subaru that the times you're hiding something that would be trouble if it got found out are exactly the times to smile the most shamelessly.

"Meili. Above, the bare minimum will do. Call the other children and get that Witchbeast back on its feet."

"...Mama's gonna scold me."

"Failing to eliminate a threat would earn you a far bigger scolding. Besides, it seems we don't have the luxury of worrying about what comes after, anyway."

"Now you're gettin' it."

At Elsa's words, Meili nodded, all composure gone from her face, and blew a whistle through her fingers.

She quietly watched the thin sound carry far, echoing throughout the manor. If their words held true, the Witchbeasts they'd unleashed would come surging in before long.

An ever more blazing situation.

"I'll tear off your arms and legs, lighten you up, and carry you home. It doesn't seem worth it unless I get to enjoy this for a good long while."

"Ain't givin' up on the bowels an option?"

"I'd sooner stop breathing."

Cracking his neck at her ridiculous reply, Garfiel leaned into a forward stance to meet his enemy.

Elsa swayed her upper body loosely, then drew the white blade in her right hand back slightly and put strength into her arm—

"—I'm certain that of anyone, I could love you down to the very marrow of your flesh and blood."

A ghastly, lascivious smile, and a chain of grinding blade-on-blade sounds.

And then—

"—Gaaah!?"

The white blade had driven diagonally into Garfiel's left shoulder, splitting the bone.

—The battle between the Bowel Hunter and the Shield of the Sanctuary was entering its final phase.

His bruised shoulder, hip, and head ached.

He'd tumbled again and again, battering his whole body each time, biting back a groan of pain every time.

He ran, and ran, and ran, and even as his breath gave out, his knees wobbled, and his vision blurred, he kept running.

Breathing was hard. He kept his head low, trying not to inhale the smoke. Inside a building where the flames had already spread through most of it, he dashed on toward doors he hadn't yet tried.

"—!"

Finding a door that hadn't been opened yet, Subaru threw himself at the handle without a thought and shoved it open.

Inside spread an unremarkable guest room, a sight that would, before long, be swallowed up in a sea of flame as well.

Leaving sentiment behind, Subaru left the door thrown wide and moved to the next room. Next door, and the one after that, and the one after that again—he threw open every door he came across, one after another—

"—There it is!"

Freed from the smell of smoke and char, out flowed the scent of old books and bundled paper.

Sniffing at the familiar musty smell, Subaru barged roughly into the room.

Then he raised his head and called out to the figure at the back of the room.

"Hey! Enough sulking already, let's just talk—"

"Get out, I wonder!"

A shockwave tore through the room, and Subaru's body nearly went flying under some unseen force. But he barely managed to stop it by hooking his fingers into the carpet.

Amid the pressure threatening to drag his whole body backward, he twisted his cheek into a grin and said,

"Ha! Don't underestimate me too much. Like I'd let myself get thrown out over and over with the same trick in such a short span of time—"

"I shan't say it twice, I suppose!"

"Bugerah!?"

Catching a thick physical attack of a book riding on the wind square in the forehead, Subaru's body spun with the shock, tumbled backward, went flying, and was ejected from the room.

He was thrown out into the hallway and slammed into the wall. As Subaru shook his head, the door slammed shut before his eyes, and though he scrambled to lunge for it, it was already too late. The room before him had lost its connection to the Forbidden Archive once more.

"What a nasty streak...! Who'd she take after, that loli...!"

Kicking the door open roughly and leaving it that way, Subaru took off running again in search of yet another door.

This time it led to the second floor of the east wing—the fact that it hadn't sent him flying toward Garfiel's battlefield was, perhaps, a small shred of Beatrice's kindness.

"Then at least hear me out, damn it, you idiot!!"

No doubt Beatrice, inside the Forbidden Archive, was thinking the very same thing.

Chewing on that fact, Subaru kept running through the manor, rejected again and again, still searching for the door that led to the Forbidden Archive.

The battle in the west wing was reaching its final phase, and the flames that had started in the main building were spreading through the entire manor—it was only a matter of time before Roswaal Manor burned to the ground.
