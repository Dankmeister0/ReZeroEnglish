Arc 4 Chapter 5: "The Emilia Faction"

Just as Frederica had declared, departure for the "Sanctuary" ended up being two days later.

Alongside her work at the mansion, Frederica spent time in the stables teaching Patrasche the way to the "Sanctuary." Since she already shouldered the bulk of the mansion's workload as it was, Subaru proposed that she teach the location to him instead, to lighten her burden even a little. But—

"I'm terribly sorry, but the location of the 'Sanctuary' is something far too significant even for you, my lord. It is not something I could reveal on my own authority as a mere servant, carelessly. Even having it drilled into the earth dragon like this is, properly speaking, something I would rather avoid."

—and so he was gently refused. Though it left him unconvinced, there was no way to reach the "Sanctuary" to lodge a complaint with Roswaal about this excess of caution. Since the order of events was backwards, Subaru had no choice but to defer to Frederica's judgment. So it was that Subaru spent his two idle days in Arlam Village, mingling with the villagers, and back at the mansion doing chores in his role as a servant.

Emilia, likewise with time on her hands, pouted over Puck's continued refusal to come out, but she made the effort to close the distance with the villagers, timidly accompanying Subaru down to the village, and also spent the time poring over documents and texts of a kind Subaru couldn't make heads or tails of, building up her knowledge.

While each of them passed their two days this way, a few changes had taken place. For instance, one of them was this:

"Aaaargh! Why does he just pile things up in such disorder like this?! Documents to keep, documents you only need to skim, documents not even worth glancing at—if he doesn't sort them out, it ends up like this...!"

Scratching furiously at his head and grumbling all the while, Otto sorted through the documents at hand at a tremendous pace. He'd glance at a paper, grasp its general content, toss it onto one of the roughly-divided piles on the desk, then move to the next, repeating the same triage over and over. Watching Otto's eyes and hands move at a dizzying speed, his mind seemingly spinning fast enough to smoke, Subaru propped his chin on his hand across the desk and marveled.

"Man, that's impressive. Me, I look at a stack of printouts and I can't even tell what they're about."

"I'm not even getting into the content myself. I'm purely sorting into what can be processed as ledger-related, what can be processed as petition-related, and everything else, just for efficiency's sake. Though honestly, it'd be better if things were sorted like this the moment each document came in... no, well, given how they were arranged, there was probably some system only the person handling them understood."

Otto, oblivious to Subaru's admiration, looked thoroughly fed up as he eyed the mountain of documents. Picturing the owner of those documents—Roswaal's face—in light of Otto's remark, it did seem the theory Otto had spat out in the midst of his griping was probably closest to the mark. Frighteningly enough, competent as Roswaal apparently was, these documents had indeed been organized according to some method of his own. The problem was that this method contributed to an efficiency incomprehensible to anyone else, and with the person responsible absent, there was nothing to do but start over from scratch.

"All right, I've finished the rough sort. Now I just need to rearrange them chronologically... though maybe I should sub-divide into processed and pending first..."

"Meticulous, or maybe neurotic—Otto, you're definitely a Type A."

"What's this 'Type A' business? That doesn't sound like it means anything good."

Subaru waved his hand wordlessly at Otto, who was eyeing him suspiciously, refusing to explain. He didn't actually believe in blood-type personality theory, but it made for decent small talk. Incidentally, Subaru was Type B—or rather, the whole Natsuki family was Type B, through and through. Not that it made him feel particularly good, since everyone he told just said "figures."

"Actually, wait, now that I think about it—"

"What, don't stop your hands. You've got a good pace going, keep at it."

"As someone devoted to efficiency, I'd love to, but isn't this whole situation strange to begin with? Why is a mere itinerant merchant like me sweating away, diligently sorting documents in the March Lord's own study? Isn't my standing here a bit off?"

"You're just noticing that now?"

At Otto, who seemed to have only just realized this perfectly reasonable point, Subaru tilted his head and grinned meanly. Behind Otto's engagement in these chores—and not just mansion chores, but the sorting of documents tied to the March Lord's official duties—lay a scheme of Subaru's own devising. Namely, securing personnel for the Emilia faction within Roswaal's camp.

As things stood—from Subaru's perspective, at least—Emilia's position in the Royal Selection could hardly be called favorable. Subaru, recognized by those involved as her knight, had been of some use, achieving the feats of repelling the Witch Cult and subjugating the White Whale. But given how poor the circumstances surrounding her were otherwise, it was doubtful how much effect that truly had. Compared to the other camps, Emilia was already behind from the very starting line. What made her position even more difficult was the utter opacity of her backer Roswaal's true intentions. Despite ostensibly being her open supporter, Roswaal's conduct up to now had been, in a word, failing as far as support went.

He'd had no countermeasures whatsoever against a Witch Cult attack that should have been anticipated as a possibility, and even now, having weathered the damage from that attack, he remained out of contact. At this point he was troublesome enough that Subaru wished he'd just make it clear whether he was friend or foe. And yet, however much Roswaal's associates knew of his circumstances, an unusual number of them kept their mouths shut and refused to touch on his true intentions. Ram, being Roswaal's ultimate devotee, was one thing, but even Frederica, who held herself to a high standard as a servant, wouldn't say a word, and Puck and Beatrice both maintained their silence toward both Subaru and Emilia alike.

In other words, Emilia currently had no one she could safely discuss things with. Of course, Subaru wanted to be that person for her, and in fact believed he was trying to be. But the reach of Subaru's hands was somewhat shorter than the average person's. For a man all too aware that he was the sort who couldn't quite scratch the itch that needed scratching, being unable to completely wipe away Emilia's anxieties was, in a word, maddening.

And so the man Subaru had set his sights on was Otto—who shuttled back and forth between Arlam Village and the mansion, savored meals and after-dinner tea, and wore a slack, contented smile as he remarked, "Ahh, staying put in one place with nothing to do like this really does rot a man, doesn't it?"

"In other words, Operation: If There Are No Allies Around You, Just Grow Some."

"I feel like I just heard something ominous, but that has nothing to do with me, right?!"

"Who knows~ Maybe~ Oh, Otto-san. You haven't finished sorting these documents over here yet."

"Ah, sorry. Let's see... the mining sites for demon ore and their stock quantities are all in this one and—wait, isn't this completely off-limits to outsiders?!"

"Ahh, you saw it, huh. Yeah, you saw it. Ahh, well, whatever. Don't worry, I'll smooth things over with Roswaal for you."

"The fact that there's not a shred of room to feel reassured is somehow all the more impressive!"

As Otto fussed, holding the handed-over document at arm's length, Subaru maintained his role as a smirking bystander to the last. Otto, aghast at Subaru's attitude, trembled his lips.

"You're not seriously trying to erase me by showing an outsider a document I shouldn't have seen, with no defense whatsoever—planning to just stiff me on all the compensation for the negotiation, are you?"

"No way. I'm fully intending to pay you for your cargo and every single condition of the favor, all of it, in full. On top of that, I'm gonna drag you in even deeper, so you can't run away."

"That makes it worse, if anything?! Why would you try to load that kind of burden onto a mere fledgling traveling merchant?! Please, stop it!"

Otto protested with the look of a man facing something far above his station. Hearing this, Subaru, thinking he might have gotten a little too carried away, lightly smacked his own head, wiped the expression from his face, and murmured, "My bad."

"I guess I got a little too impatient and got ahead of myself there. Things have gotten pretty tangled up lately with no sign of the knots coming loose, and it's had me on edge. Sorry for not asking what worked for you."

"Ah—no, it's just, when you suddenly get that calm about it, it makes me nervous in a different way. ...Um, can I ask? Why do you have such high hopes pinned on me?"

Otto, somewhat bewildered by Subaru's sudden shift to a normal seat at the negotiating table, nonetheless took it in stride and voiced his question. Understandably so, from his point of view—his acquaintance with Subaru was brief, not nearly long enough to have built up much trust. And for that matter, Subaru's own relationship with him wasn't especially strong or long either. Having had several encounters with him over the previous loop, Subaru simply held a somewhat better impression of him than the current Otto's actual track record would warrant. So then, why was Subaru so fixated on Otto?

"To put it bluntly, there isn't much of a reason for me to be fixated on you specifically. It's not that I'm rating you personally all that highly—it's mostly just that your conditions happened to line up, so I called out to you."

"Blunt, aren't you. ...Though I did suspect as much. Conditions, huh."

"When it comes to the Royal Selection, being unaffiliated—no strings attached to anyone. Being sharp about profit and loss, with an obvious eye for negotiation as a means of winning allies over. And, above all, not looking down on the half-elf Emilia with prejudice. That's about it."

"——"

Hearing the three conditions Subaru listed, Otto stared at him in silence. Each condition, given the circumstances surrounding them just described, was not one to take lightly. And Subaru had judged that this man, Otto Suwen, cleared each of these baseline conditions based on his dealings with him so far.

Otto remained silent, waiting for Subaru's next words. His eyes, unlike a moment ago when they'd been trading light banter, now held a cool, calculating gleam, wavering as if trying to gauge what lay beneath Subaru's surface. Subaru sensed he was being appraised. Otto wasn't even hiding it. Fair enough—Subaru had appraised him first, so it was only natural to be appraised in turn.

"And to add my own personal read on things—"

"—Let's hear it."

"I had a feeling you and I could get along well. Honestly, the more allies for Emilia-tan, the better, no question, but if the person happens to also click with me, that's even better still. Oh, and also—no romantic feelings toward Emilia-tan, got it? If it ever came to that, even if you became my bosom buddy from way back, I'd have to cut you down...!"

"Being a rival in love alone makes me a target for annihilation?!"

"If there's a rival around, I've got zero confidence I'd win! Don't underestimate how little confidence I have in myself, you hear? In my whole life so far, the people who've actually affirmed me could be counted on one hand."

The breakdown being: his parents, Rem, Wilhelm, Emilia, and, conditionally, people like Julius or Reinhard. Thinking about it that way, somewhere along the line he'd apparently gone past one hand's worth. Getting that kind of recognition after coming to another world—did that mean he was making some kind of legitimate progress himself? Even though nothing seemed to have visibly changed. Anyway—

"Sheesh, you're a man who doesn't hide what's in his heart, to an almost refreshing degree. Not even a bit of poker-facing with a merchant—you're an easy mark, ripe to be fleeced at the negotiating table, you know?"

"If this were a negotiating table, maybe I'd have hustled a bit more, but the people sitting here aren't a merchant and a mark, they're just me and you, right? If you thought this was that kind of seat, I can swap out my gears and attitude, though."

"You praise the merchant side of me and then turn around and say something like that. Talk about not letting the words get cold before contradicting them... what am I going to do with you."

Still wearing a troubled expression, but with the earlier taut wariness gone from his face, Otto let out a sigh. He closed one eye at the unflappable Subaru.

"Not that this is really the place to bring it up, Natsuki-san, but I do have my own goals too... nothing grand enough to tell people about, but, well, I have a dream, of sorts."

"A man's dream is one of two things—either so grandiose it's ridiculous to say out loud, or so ridiculous you have to keep it locked in your chest. Which is it? Let's hear it."

"The fact that I can agree with that is what bugs me. ...See, I'm the second son of a moderately successful merchant house. I grew up fairly comfortably as a kid, but when it came time to think about becoming independent, my family wasn't about to do anything special for me."

Subaru had no idea how much this paralleled sibling dynamics back in his original world, but it seemed that in this world too, family businesses and noble titles were generally passed down hereditarily—and, moreover, typically to the eldest son. Which meant that, much like the medieval fantasy Subaru pictured, Otto, born the second son, was left with the role of either assisting the elder brother who'd inherit the house, or striking out on his own.

"I helped with my brother's business, learned the basics of trade, and once I'd scraped together enough seed money on my own, I went independent. Got hold of the earth dragon Frufoo and a dragon carriage through connections, and things went reasonably... well, yes, I suppose having a 'Divine Protection' from birth helped too, so it went fairly smoothly."

"Divine Protection—that'd be the one for 'Speech of Spirit,' right? If you've really got a Protection that lets you talk to all kinds of animals, seems like depending how you use it, you could rake it in."

"It's not as convenient as it sounds. I imagine there are more headaches involved with it than you'd guess, Natsuki-san. Anyway, point being, I've been plugging away steadily for a few years now, and somewhere along the way, a dream took root in me."

Continuing from the story of how he'd gone independent, Otto brought up the word "dream" he'd mentioned earlier. Faced with that word, for no particular reason, Subaru straightened up to listen properly. Otto, taking note of this, smiled warmly, prefacing with, "It's a common enough story," before continuing—

"It's the wish every merchant who does this traveling trade dreams of. To have a shop of my own in one place, a castle to call mine, and to keep doing business while staying rooted there. If I could do that in some big town somewhere, that would be the greatest joy imaginable for a merchant."

"That's Otto's dream?"

"It's a boring wish, really. When you trace it all the way back, in the end, what I'm looking for turns out to be the same kind of place I was born and raised in. But well, I suppose that just goes to show that environment was, for me, a symbol of good fortune."

Scratching his cheek with a finger, looking bashful, Otto spoke quickly as if to hide his embarrassment. Subaru, nodding along in understanding at his answer, nonetheless leaned deep back into his chair, weighing whether he'd get a favorable response. But, unbeknownst to Subaru's worry, Otto continued with, "So then—"

"Missing out on a chance to possibly curry favor with someone as significant as a March Lord isn't something I can overlook—not as the second son of a merchant house, and not as a traveling merchant with an eye toward becoming a great merchant someday. Especially when the favor I'd be selling might end up owed by a future king—that's an opportunity too huge to come along even if I lived my life over again."

"Great, so that means you're on board to help out, right? Thanks, I'm glad. I figured you'd say that, Otto. Now keep sorting those documents."

"Wait, huh?! I just said something pretty decent there, didn't I? Isn't that a way too flat reaction?!"

"Felt like if I reacted with genuine, heartfelt appreciation I'd somehow be losing... well, either way, the moment you touched documents an outsider's not supposed to see, there was no letting you off without something happening regardless. Heh heh heh."

"Even coming full circle, this guy's still awful!"

The long, roundabout exchange ended up right back where it started, leaving Otto's renewed resolve rather unrewarded. Even as he needled Otto like this, inwardly Subaru couldn't help but feel a deep sense of gratitude toward him. Though he'd never say so out loud.

"Just so you know, I'm not like you, Natsuki-san, unconditionally on Emilia-sama's side. Right now I'm only keeping company with you all conditionally. If it ever comes down to a factional struggle between the March Lord and Emilia-sama, I'll be choosing whichever side makes sense by the profit-and-loss calculus of that moment. Don't go mistaking me for a full-fledged ally."

"Just the fact that you'd weigh Emilia-tan and Roswaal on the scale and agonize over it is more than enough to have you eating out of my hand. I'll teach you all about how great Emilia-tan is, bit by bit, so don't worry. —You heard all that just now, right?"

Placating Otto as he went on making excuse after excuse, Subaru turned his face in a different direction from Otto at the very end of his words, tossing the question elsewhere. At this move of Subaru's, Otto blinked in bewilderment, and following Subaru's gaze, turned to look in the same direction. There—

"Mm... I mean, yes. I heard everything, clearly, Subaru-sama."

—stood a young maid with chestnut hair swaying, smiling with an adorable face. This was the second change to the mansion over these two days.

Realizing the physical impossibility of maintaining the mansion with Frederica alone (Subaru helped too, but his ability as a servant was still only half-fledged, and he hadn't fully recovered his health besides), she herself had gone down to Arlam Village to recruit help—and the one who'd leapt at the chance with both hands raised was none other than she—Petra Leyte.

A villager of Arlam and one of the evacuees who'd gone to the royal capital, she had safely returned to the village, and should have been spending anxious days in a village where more than half the residents had yet to return. And yet, the moment she heard news of Frederica seeking a new maid for the mansion, she'd jumped at it immediately, and thanks to her enthusiasm and the lack of other candidates, she'd been taken on at the mansion as a provisional hire for now.

"Still so little, and yet leaving your parents to become a maid—that's pretty admirable of you, Petra."

"I'm already twelve, that makes me a fine, capable adult... I mean, capable. Subaru, please treat me properly as an adult too."

"I'll think about it once you master polite speech and get your full license from Frederica so you're not a provisional maid anymore. Until then, you're gonna keep being treated as cute by me forever."

When Subaru roughly ruffled the head of the girl scurrying busily about, she yelped "kyaa!" at having her carefully arranged hair mussed and clung to him. Odd, that wasn't quite the reaction he expected, but it sure beat being cursed at and spat on. Anyway, precocious and eager to seem grown-up as she was for her age, Petra's aptitude as a maid, given her surprisingly solid character and upbringing, was quite high. She still had plenty of shortcomings, but when it came to cleaning and meal preparation, with Frederica's guidance factored in, it was probably fair to say she already surpassed Subaru. Subaru, seriously useless.

And it was this girl who'd been eavesdropping on the conversation inside from outside the study door. Of course, this had been on Subaru's instructions—a scheme to bait Otto's careless remark and seal off his escape route. Realizing this, Otto reddened and glared at Subaru.

"You—you set me up—?!"

"Nothing to get that worked up about. Just because a third party happened to be present, your statement from a moment ago is now recorded as official, in both name and substance. Consider it desperate maneuvering to secure the jury's favorable impression, if you will."

"What's tearjerking about a person who openly admits to 'maneuvering' like that?!"

Clutching his head, Otto, finally understanding there was no escape left, shouted half in tears—but it was already too late. Smirking at Otto, Subaru then turned and gave a thumbs-up to Petra standing by the door.

"Good work, Petra. But won't Frederica be mad if you spend your time on this kind of thing?"

"Right now is my time for cleaning the hallway. Even if I take longer than usual cleaning the hallway in front of the lord's study, I won't get scolded."

"Sharp little thing, aren't you. Guess even little girls are still girls..."

Petra, catching only the last part of Subaru's rather wistful remark, brightened, her cheeks loosening happily. There was, in fact, something reassuring in her unchanging reaction and attitude.

Petra, too, like Otto, was a supporter outside Roswaal's sphere of influence. Compared to Otto, there was less Petra could do as a supporter for Emilia, and her presence's influence might be even less than Subaru's own. And yet, she wasn't afraid of Emilia. On the road fleeing to the royal capital, Subaru hadn't forgotten how she'd approached Emilia head-on, without hesitation. And surely, in all likelihood, Emilia felt the same way about her. By her presence alone, there was surely something that saved Emilia, too.

"More allies is always better than fewer. What you're able to do isn't really the issue. What matters is what you want to do for that person, and what you become able to do along the way. Besides, if we're counting up what each of us can actually do, I'd come out looking pretty bad myself."

Weighing merits against demerits, Subaru himself was well aware the demerits would win out. And yet, wanting to be her ally regardless, there was nothing to do but make do with what they had and long for what they didn't, and muddle through. Call it stubbornness if you like—it was, undeniably, a satisfying bit of wishful arithmetic all the same.

"Still way, way too small a group, but let's build from here. This makes us the founding members of the Emilia Faction."

Making a fist and thrusting it out, Subaru declared it. At that, Otto and Petra, left out of the loop, exchanged glances with each other.

"I never said a single word about joining that faction, you know? Don't go getting the wrong idea, all right?"

"I do want to be on Big Sis's side, but at the important moments, I really don't want to lose to Big Sis, you know..."

Otto scratched his head, looking exasperated, while Petra clasped her hands behind her back, ducked her head, and murmured, fidgeting, each in their own way.

Still, that in the end they both went along with bumping fists anyway left no room to doubt just how good-natured they truly were.

—And so, over the two days leading up to their departure for the "Sanctuary," time passed, leaving Subaru with the sense of only the smallest of steps forward—but a certain, tangible one all the same.
