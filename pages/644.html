Arc 8 Chapter 28: "The Empire's Brother and Sister"

A premonition had been simmering inside him.

When Ubilk the "Stargazer" named her as the light that would save the Empire, he'd wracked his none-too-sharp head, turning the thought over and over, again and again.

Just what was it about her, he wondered, that could make her the mortal enemy of the resurrected dead?

What, exactly, set her apart from everyone else?

That she was kind. That she gave her all. That she could stand firm for someone else's sake. All of it was wonderful — but plenty of others fit that description too.

No, it wasn't that. There was a specialty she alone possessed, that no one else had—

"—the Authority of 'Gluttony.'"

Even if she wished to part ways with her past as an Archbishop of Sin, that cross would never let her escape.

Subaru had sworn to her that he would never let her run from that cross.

She had sworn to Subaru that she would never run from it.

—And that was precisely where hope lay.

"I hold the Authorities of 'Sloth' and 'Greed.'"

The Witch Cult, an object of hatred. The Archbishops of Sin, worthy of contempt.

Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti and Regulus Corneas — the Authorities they once wielded had, through some twisting path, come at last to rest deep within Subaru.

Why had it turned out that way? It felt grotesque, as if the baton had been passed to him by those men, and Subaru had never faced that thought head-on. But he couldn't avoid it any longer.

For her sake, and for the sake of the Subaru who had chosen her, he had to face it.

And then—

"—If you choose to live as Spica instead of Rui Arneb, then just like I've been doing, you should be able to change how you use your Authority too."

Everything had two sides, and any tool was only as good as the hand that wielded it. —No, not just tools.

Whether a person became good or was stained by evil — that final line was drawn by oneself. There were circumstances that made it impossible to draw. But she had been given the chance to draw it.

—Lye Batenkaitos, of 'Gourmandizing.'
—Roy Alphard, of 'Foul Appetite.'
—Rui Arneb, of 'Satiation.'

Those who wielded the power given to them and became sinners the world could never forgive.

And one who, holding that same power, walked instead the path of an atoner, forever making amends to the world.

"—'Star-Eating.'"

Siblings bound together, who had set a wicked precedent.

Those precedents — the deeds of the sinners who bore the names of stars — she, as an atoner, would devour anew.

Newborn, the girl who devoured stars — Spica of 'Star-Eating.'

"—Iaa, iau."

Calling out the 'Name' of the thing resurrected in a Vessel of earth — its 'Memory' sealed away by the Insects — the girl given the name of the Pearl Star activates her 'Star-Eating' through the hand she extends, the fingertips that make contact.

She knows a white, empty place where nothing holds any meaning.

A place where every soul, the beautiful and the filthy alike, is scraped clean and born anew.

She devours the role of those dragged down from that place.

So that never again will that soul be made into someone else's meal, she devours the karma bound to it.

—Devouring the deeds of the Archbishop of Sin who bore a star's name, 'Star-Eating' comes to pass.

"—Oi, ou, aa, ei, a."

In that instant, all the Lamias were struck by wind at once, and their movements stopped.

Goz Ralfon, his whole body bared with burning wounds, didn't miss that single moment.

"—Got you."

In the midst of battle, showered with the threat of the 'Sun Swords' pressing in from every direction, Goz had shed his flame-engulfed armor and endured.

Parrying the attacks without flinching, Goz — his will to fight undiminished — closed his eyes and gave himself over to sound.

Thanks to his Divine Protection of Wind Evasion, he could focus on the sound of the Lamias before him. More than twenty Lamias, and every one of them sounded identical — Goz understood then that he truly faced a fearsome opponent.

But for a Lamia who was entirely identical in every instance, facing Goz was the worst possible matchup.

For some reason he couldn't fathom, every single Lamia had lost her footing at once.

It had to be Vincent. The Emperor of Vollachia, to whom Goz had sworn his loyalty, had prepared a way to break through even a situation like this. It nearly moved him to tears of gratitude.

Holding it back, Goz raised the mace in his hand, twisted the middle of the long haft to split it apart, dividing it into striking head and shaft, then struck the head hard with the shaft.

In that instant, the shockwave of sound that spread out slammed into only the Lamias who had lost their balance.

"————"

The golden mace Goz wielded combined a spear-like long haft with a spherical striking head at its tip, a masterwork forged solely for him.

Weighing as much as ten grown men with a single swing, it combined with Goz's tremendous strength and his extraordinary ears to achieve a technique no one else could manage — the 'Howl,' which struck the unique vibration inherent to each living thing with a matching vibration cast as sound.

Goz Ralfon, for all his bold and boisterous appearance, possessed ears keen enough to distinguish the very timbre of the wind, and the genius touch to play any instrument with delicate skill.

That deadly 'Howl' was precisely the reason Goz was called the "Lion Knight."

Goz's 'Howl,' having caught the unique vibration of Lamia in the midst of battle — no, of all the Lamias — struck them down, and the cracks spreading across every one of them widened until the undead princesses shattered into pieces.

—The same thing happened, each in its own way, aboard the carriages far from Goz's battlefield.

Roswaal too, who had judged that flame was ill-suited against the 'Sun Sword' and had been fighting the Lamias and the 'Pruning Corps' with blades of wind and avalanches of earth.

Garfiel too, who with the monstrous strength of his Beastification tore through Lamia and 'Pruning Corps' and dragon carriage alike with his savage claws, making no distinction between them.

Emilia too, who reinforced the dragon carriages being destroyed one after another by freezing them, and fought the Lamias while weaving through the crowd of ice statues she'd made of the 'Pruning Corps.'

Everyone aboard the Coupled Dragon Carriages did not miss that unforeseen instant, however it had been made.

Even not knowing who would create it, they had believed someone would — and they did not let that instant slip by.

The Lamia Godwins, each wielding a gleaming 'Sun Sword,' were shattered one after another—

Staggering as she walked, she was already as good as dead — it looked as though a single thrust from anyone's blade would shatter her to pieces. In fact, Jamal made a move to strike her down with his melted twin swords, but Abel stopped him, reaching out an arm.

His whole body ached as if creaking apart, and his burned, blistered legs wailed shamelessly with pain. But neither that pain nor those scorched wounds were anything the old man had wished for.

Pain, wounds — none of it mattered. —Why, once again, had he failed to die?

To the old man lying on his back on the ground, cursing his own unsightliness over and over, she spoke.

She did not wear a human shape. Yet her supple, powerful beast's form was, in itself, a complete embodiment of what the people of the Vollachia Empire praised as excellent, as beautiful.

Moving with the unit left behind at the rear of the dragon carriages, she had been running to catch up with the Coupled Dragon Carriages to deliver a message, and had witnessed the old man thrown outside amid the flames of a 'Meteor.'

Shaking her head slowly from side to side, the leopard with the beautiful golden coat spoke to the old man.

That man might simply have found the old man clinging to him bothersome and shoved him away — for he was someone whose true intentions could never be grasped, not even a fragment of them.

'—My, I didn't know you could make such a frustrated face.'

A sheep, or a goat, that had disguised itself as a sword-wolf, false even down to the color of its own coat — both, and neither, the old man wrung the words out of himself.

That, it seemed, had been his own true feeling, hidden behind eyes narrowed thin as thread.

The old man — the Empire's chancellor, Berstetz Fondalfon — bit down on the shame of still being alive, and forced the words out.

Her raised arms were bound with special chains, forced upright in the pose of a prisoner. And yet neither in Priscilla's eyes nor in her bearing was there the faintest trace of weakness.

Wherever she was made to stand, however she was bound, none of it gave her the slightest reason to bend.

Held in the dungeon beneath the Crystal Palace of the imperial capital Lupugana, Priscilla eyed with suspicion the one who had come all this way to see her — Lamia Godwin, now among the undead.

At the same age she'd been when she died nine years ago, she bore the skin and eyes peculiar to the undead, yet the bewitching presence that had earned her the name "Poison Princess" remained alive and well.

But now, in her tattered dress with half her body crumbling away, there was hardly a trace of it left.

If one were undead, a broken part could be repaired. Failing that, one could simply be swapped into a new Vessel of earth. Priscilla, having observed the undead and reasoned this much, understood something else in that moment.

The reason Lamia, who despised unsightliness and disgrace above all else, had come to stand before her looking like this.

Lamia laughed, raising to her lips the hand that hadn't yet crumbled, though it looked ready to at any moment.

Whether that was something to boast of was a matter of personal taste, but Priscilla merely gave a quiet snort. At that reaction, Lamia narrowed her golden eyes,

"Honestly, it's been so dull. You and dear brother Vincent are about the only ones who can hold a proper conversation with me."

"You and I have exchanged proper words only a handful of times, if that."

"It's not about the number of times, silly little sister."

As Lamia murmured this, the hand she'd held to her lips crumbled away and vanished.

Slowly, Lamia was losing her form. Standing right before her as she was, that sight forced its way into Priscilla's eyes whether she wanted it or not.

But Priscilla did not close her eyes. She fixed Lamia with her crimson gaze.

And as she watched,

"If your role is finished, then make your exit swiftly, Lamia. —I'll see you off again, just as before."

At Priscilla's words, Lamia raised her eyebrows just slightly.

Then she laughed, sweet as poison,

"What an unlovable little sister you are."

As far as Priscilla's memory served, it was the very same words, the very same smile, as at the moment of Lamia's death in life.

That manner of speech, and the way he stepped forward out of the darkness—

And Priscilla had once known him, back when she'd lived in the Empire — when she'd been the wife of her first husband, a man who held the rank of a mid-tier count in the Empire.

Not that the living and the dead were about to rekindle old acquaintance over it.

"Of course, watching over Lady Lamia's end was part of the job, but... I've brought a meal for you, milady."

"I don't want it."

Grinning with his lifeless-hued skin, Balleroy lifted the cover off the meal.

Warm steam and aroma spread out, and seeing the dish before her, Priscilla closed one eye.

For something made by an undead, it looked, at least, like a proper meal.

"Unfortunately, I can't taste anything, so I haven't sampled it myself, but I'm used to cooking, so it shouldn't be bad. Ah, do forgive me for serving common fare, though."

"————"

To the silent Priscilla, Balleroy took up the fork on the tray, speared a bite of food with it, and brought it to her lips.

Priscilla's arms were chained to the ceiling. She understood there was no other way to do it, but—

"I'll take your head with my own hands for this."

So saying quietly, Priscilla took the offered food into her mouth.

It wasn't bad. But it was cheap. Even using ingredients found within the castle, if the cook's skill and imagination were cheap, the result couldn't be called fine.

Under Priscilla's silent pressure, Balleroy gave a wry, undead smile. And in that wry smile, something not meant for Priscilla alone flickered through,

"No, it's just, come to think of it, there was a time long ago I used to bring meals like this to a little girl locked up as punishment for some mischief she'd pulled."

Sensing the change in the air, Balleroy revealed the truth behind his wry smile like that.

Feeling a flash of displeasure at that answer, Priscilla told him, "Wipe it," and had him clean her mouth,

"Why do you all mean to destroy the Empire? Having risen again, what is it you want?"

"—The first isn't mine to answer. But the second's simple enough."

Answering her question, Balleroy let both the wry smile and the light air he'd carried a moment ago fall away.

Priscilla too caught the shift in the air around him keenly — the unmistakable anger within him. No, the same anger that had been in Lamia too.

True to that impression of Priscilla's—

"What the undead want, in any age, is always one and the same — to settle old grudges."

So saying, revealing the true wish of the undead, Balleroy Temeglyph let out a lifeless laugh.

"Ah!?"

The dao he'd been about to draw across his throat — its blade, lying on the ground — was pinned down from directly above. Looking up, he saw it was a small foot, followed by a slender waist, and a wildly grinning face.

It was the reckless act of a child in wasou, standing on the dao with a single foot.

"A child does not meet my required conditions either, dazo."

At the child's appearance, Vivas, without the slightest hesitation, spun the knives in both hands and tried to drive the same attack that had gouged into his side into the child's vitals.

Disable the hands and feet first, then move on to checking the internal organs — his worst possible routine.

Watching the blade close in on him, the child raised an eyebrow,

"Ho-ho! Magnificent, the way you don't so much as flinch at a sudden, stylish interloper! But—"

The next instant, Vivas's eyes flew wide as his head was severed from his torso and sent flying.

Whether this encounter would prove fortunate or ill, either way—

He dismissed the three clones he'd made of himself as well, and let out a long, weary breath. Taking on something on the scale of a Dragon was, sure enough, a real chore.

If possible, he'd like this to be the last of the monster-slaying, thank you very much.

"But then again, Ana and the others were saying something about more of the same turning into zombies, weren't they."

If that was true, then the reappearance of the very "Three-Headed" dragon he'd just felled was more than possible.

Probably, Halibel could win again next time too, but whether he'd have an environment where he could avoid dragging in the people around him, the way he had this time, was doubtful. And having his own hands tied up in the meantime was hardly reassuring, hired hand that he was. Julius, at Anastasia's side, was certainly skilled, but—

"He's probably not quite on my level, though."

Cracking his neck, he lit the pipe he hadn't been able to smoke during the fight, and savored the smoke.

Then, having watched the "Three-Headed" dragon's body vanish completely, Halibel turned and searched far, far into the distance for the Coupled Dragon Carriages that had gone racing off, leaving him behind.

And then—

"Man, if anyone deserves a candy for all that hard work, it's me. ...Ah, right, I'd die if I ate candy."

Muttering things like that, he set off running, following the carriage ruts stretching away into the distance.
