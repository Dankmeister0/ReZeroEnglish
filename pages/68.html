Arc 2 Chapter 44: "How to Make Use of Despair"

"—That was quite the cool line you spat out back there."

Fifteen minutes after declaring war right in front of the Witchbeast forest, Ram, walking alongside Subaru, prefaced her remark with that, then continued,

"When you think about it calmly, there's nothing in the terms that says Barusu has to fight on the front lines, is there."

"Whoa, hold on now. Sure, that's technically true, but look at this overflowing motivation of mine. A time-honored one-handed sword, handed down from the village's young men's association—right now, I am living the fantasy!"

"And that sword—if Beatrice-sama hadn't stopped you from going in unarmed, you wouldn't even have gotten it. Bare-handed, you'd have been nothing but dead weight, straight up."

"That, I honestly can't deny."

Readjusting his grip on a hilt that still didn't feel like his own, Subaru let his shoulders sag with a sigh. A black sheath, a bronze-colored hilt, and a blade roughly the length of Subaru's arm—the one-handed sword felt unexpectedly light, and that lightness left him unable to hide his unease. He'd gotten hold of it by following Beatrice's advice, after she'd hastily stopped him from trying to charge into the Witchbeast forest with nothing in his hands at all.

The severity of the situation only deepened as time passed. That sense of urgency was exactly why he'd almost chosen to charge in immediately—but looking back on it calmly now, he could honestly admit that would have been suicide. Beneath that standoffish, hard-to-approach exterior, somehow always getting the job done anyway—that was the vexing thing about that ringlet-haired girl.

"By the way, can Barusu actually use a sword?"

"Hard to say, honestly. I did get to second dan in kendo back in middle school, but I don't think real swordsmanship and school kendo can really be treated the same."

Swinging the sword at his waist back and forth, feeling its weight, Subaru answered. Three years of middle school, and after that, idle sword-swinging for no particular reason whenever he had time. Compared to a total amateur who'd never touched one, he figured he could manage at least a little—but he doubted that put him much past the "dabbled in it a bit" level.

The ill-fitting one-handed sword was a blade he'd been given by one of the young men in the village's youth association. He couldn't very well tell them the honest truth—"I'm about to go into the Witchbeast forest"—so after a fairly painful string of excuses, he'd managed to get it handed over to him. Just how useful it would actually be remained, for now, an open question. And that wasn't the only thing he'd been entrusted with in the village.

"Let's see, in my pocket... candy, a pretty stone, and—whoa-oh-oh! There's a bug in here!"

Rummaging through a pocket stuffed with various things, Subaru let out a shriek at the unpleasant sensation. The winged insect, freed from its cramped confinement, left Subaru's hand and vanished straight into the depths of the forest. Watching it go, Subaru mimed wiping his brow and said,

"What outrageous brats, shoving stuff in during all the confusion. I'm gonna lecture 'em later."

"Proof that they adore you, isn't it. ...What do they even see in a man like this."

"To a child's pure eyes, the true nature of a man like me shines right through."

To Ram, who looked ready to snort derisively at any moment with her flat stare, Subaru shook his head side to side and pointed a finger, saying "a-a-and."

"This time it's not just me—some of it's thanks for Rem too. Actually, I'm more like the delivery guy for Rem's share. See, watch."

"...Sure."

At the hesitant note of agreement in Ram's voice, Subaru nodded repeatedly, looking satisfied. The exchange before they'd left the village had genuinely struck a chord in Subaru's heart. Ram, who had watched the same scene, found herself hoping it really would turn out that way too.

—They'd left the village and entered the Witchbeast forest. The ones who'd sensed Subaru and the others trying to slip into the forest without the villagers noticing were the children, still not fully recovered from having their mana drained. Wanting to thank Subaru and Rem directly, the children, upon spotting them, had crammed all manner of things into Subaru's pockets, calling it a token of gratitude.

The candy, the pretty stone, even that winged insect—to the children, each was a tangible shape given to their feelings of thanks. There was no way he could treat any of it carelessly. Well, the bug got away, but still.

And so, to a Subaru left rather flustered by having these largely unnecessary forms of gratitude thrust upon him, the children had said with beaming smiles—

"'We want to thank Rem-rin too, so bring her by later, okay?'... huh."

Just how dangerous a place she was fighting in right now, and what she was risking her life for— the children didn't know. And they didn't need to know. Because—

"Don't you worry, you little brats. Any bad kids who'd sneak into a dark forest in the middle of the night are gonna get lectured together with the big sister who went and did something rash without even consulting anybody."

Might as well throw in, for good measure, the reckless idiot boy who'd charged into the forest, gotten bitten all over by a dog, and caused a whole lot of trouble for everyone around him. A full night sitting seiza, getting lectured by the village headman—now wouldn't that be something.

Naturally, the corners of his mouth loosened at the picture of that future he'd conjured up. And as Subaru's cheeks subtly twitched with that grin,

"Even so, you're slow, Barusu."

Ram, glancing back at him from far ahead, said that, looking exasperated. She strode along, effortlessly conquering ground in the forest that should have been treacherous underfoot. Despite wearing her usual maid uniform, her gait was no different from crossing the mansion's garden. Subaru, on the other hand—

"C-c'mon, cut me a little slack. I'm short on blood, and also guts. Oh right, I never even got to say bye to Emilia-tan before I left!"

"You haven't said 'I'm back' yet, so last night's 'take care' is still in effect."

"Th-that's how that works...?"

Struggling with his unsteady body and swimming head, Subaru trudged along, using the one-handed sword like a walking stick. The march could not, by any stretch of flattery, be called fast, and served only to fray the nerves of Ram walking ahead.

Unlike the previous night, this search of the forest was taking place in broad daylight. Visibility was far clearer than after sundown, but even so, the thick canopy of overlapping branches and leaves made it hard to see far. He thought he was walking through much the same area as last night, but he couldn't rule out the possibility that they were heading in a completely wrong direction. Perhaps the forest's depths scrambled even the sense of direction of those who wandered in—Subaru genuinely, half-seriously thought that if he'd been alone, he might not even have been able to find the way out, lost without a clue.

"Barusu, wait a moment again. —Clairvoyance, open."

Holding out a hand to stop him, Ram murmured, and her right eye flushed crimson. Ever since entering the forest, Ram had been periodically using Clairvoyance, searching for Rem, who should be somewhere within. But unfortunately, there had been no results yet. Either she was outside the range of Ram's eye, or else—

"Don't go imagining bad stuff. Lady Fate'll come running to make it happen out of sheer delight..."

He rapped himself on the head, burying the worst-case scenario that had surfaced. It had already been three or four hours since Rem deceived her sister and the villagers and slipped into the forest. Compared to the two of them, chasing after her belatedly, there was no telling how deep into the forest she might have gotten by now. But given the sheer scale of what she was after, even running from one end of the forest to the other wouldn't be enough.

Once again, he found himself dwelling on both what Rem was after and just how difficult his own survival was going to be. Even understanding that, he was thoroughly disgusted with himself for how hard it was to make up his mind, out of sheer self-preservation.

"Ah, pathetic. Focus, focus, me. Just focus on guarding Ram while she's defenseless using Clairvoyance—don't think about anything else."

He drew the one-handed sword from its sheath and gave it a light swing to test the feel. The sound of it cutting the air was quiet, and its weight was somewhat lighter than a wooden practice sword. The extra length of the blade made it easier to handle for that alone, and beyond that, it was purely a matter of whether he had the resolve to wield a real blade.

"In actual combat, they say even keeping a real blade's edge aligned is a struggle."

An amateur's blade wavers, unable to cut straight through anything in the first place. He swung the blade upward at a thin branch ahead of him, a sharp upward cut from below—a branch about as thick as Subaru's wrist was severed cleanly, spinning as it fell to the ground.

Unable to help himself, he praised his own handiwork with an "ooh." Breathing out in admiration at the "lack of resistance" beyond what he'd expected, Subaru continued his little sword dance.

One stroke, two strokes—with each swing, branches and leaves scattered, the ferocity of his cuts increasing. Reconfirming, to his surprise, that his three years of middle school kendo and the idle sword-swinging afterward hadn't been entirely wasted, he got carried away and swung the blade in a big horizontal sweep—

"Ah."

With a high clank, the blade sank halfway into the thick trunk of a tree. Getting in was easy; getting out was hard. The embedded blade wouldn't budge no matter what. Sweating buckets internally, Subaru glanced back at Ram, confirmed she was still sharing her field of view via Clairvoyance, then planted a foot firmly against the trunk and struggled with all his might to pull the sword free.

"Bad, bad, bad, this is seriously uncool! Losing my sword like this is so lame! Come off, come out, worst case even if you snap in half just come out—"

"—Barusu, something's watching us."

Behind Subaru, who was struggling to yank the stuck sword free, Ram—her immediate field of vision lost to Clairvoyance—cried out. At her warning that something was watching them, Subaru felt a chill of dread. If she, who could share the sight of others, was saying that, it meant that presence was—

"Close enough to see us with the naked eye...!"

Letting go of the hilt that wouldn't budge, Subaru frantically looked around. And there, to his left—leaping from branch to branch through the trees, a shadow closing in on them at tremendous speed. Recognizing it as one of those already-too-familiar four-legged beasts, Subaru instantly abandoned his effort to pull the sword free. Crouching, he snatched up a few handy stones,

"No time to draw the holy sword, so eat this instead!!"

He hurled a fist-sized stone with all his might at the shadow leaping through the air. A spinning mass like that carried enough force to instantly incapacitate on a direct hit, but it was, after all, an amateur's throw. And against a moving target no less, whether it would connect was practically down to miracle. His volley of throws proved futile—every stone he threw vanished into the forest beyond. As if mocking Subaru's clumsy resistance, the Witchbeast bent a branch and made a particularly sharp glide—landing on the ground, lowering its body a few steps from Subaru. But—

"Aaand—welcome!!"

As if he'd read the move coming, an upward-swinging full swing was unleashed. Aimed at the landed Witchbeast's face, the blow chose as its weapon the iron-fitted, now-empty sheath. The dead-on timing predicted exactly what it would do once his stone feints missed. With the sheath's thick hilt end, he meant to smash the Witchbeast's muzzle to a pulp—that had been the plan.

"Huh!?"

A tremendous whooshing sound of a total whiff rang out, and Subaru's footing, treading on dry grass, slipped spectacularly. At the moment of impact, the Witchbeast lowered its body even further as it lunged, throwing the point of intersection off slightly, and the sheath managed only to clip a bit of fur off its back. Conversely, Subaru's telegraphed, wide-open swing left his back completely exposed—exposed, in fact, right in front of the Witchbeast's twisting fangs.

Right behind him, gouging the earth as it spun around, the Witchbeast bared its fangs. Feeling a chill run up his neck, he tilted only his head—and those sharp canines were about to tear into Subaru's calf—an instant before,

"Playing around at a critical moment—what are you doing."

A gust of wind caught the leaping Witchbeast, violently disrupting its mid-air trajectory and throwing off its aim. Caught off guard by the unexpected intervention, the Witchbeast failed to land properly. It tumbled across the ground and tried to spring back up immediately, but its four limbs trembled violently, and it collapsed there, unable to move. Subaru, readjusting his grip on the sheath, watched the now-still, heavily breathing Witchbeast with unease. Holding the sheath raised, ready to strike down at any moment, he sidestepped over to Ram.

Ram, palm turned toward the crouching Witchbeast, glanced at Subaru as he came up beside her and gave a short "hmph,"

"In the space of just a moment, your armaments have gotten remarkably shabby."

"Yeah, I'll admit my attack power took a nosedive, my bad too. So, what state is that thing in?"

He pointed at the unmoving Witchbeast, questioning Ram, who was presumably responsible. The Witchbeast's eyes were already closed, not twitching in the slightest, as if it had simply fallen asleep. Confirming this, Ram gently lowered the palm she'd been directing at it,

"It's finished."

"Huh?"

"I said I stopped its breath."

Startled by how casually Ram announced this, Subaru approached the Witchbeast half in disbelief. He poked the seemingly-sleeping body with the tip of the sheath, and getting no reaction, steeled himself and flipped it over. Limbs sprawled limply, tongue lolling from its mouth, there was no sign of life in it. It was, indeed, dead.

"What did you even do to make this happen?"

"I told you, didn't I—that I can use a little wind magic. I just cut the tendons in its limbs with a wind blade, then sealed its throat and choked it out. —I had no intention of making it suffer long."

Ram spoke as if it were nothing at all. Looking closely, sure enough, faint traces of blood dripped from the tips of the Witchbeast's limbs, and its expression showed the agony of something that had gasped for air and found none. Confirming all this, Subaru couldn't suppress a flood of questions welling up about Ram. Things like: what part of that was "a little magic"? Wasn't this awfully lacking in the refreshing feel one expects from magic? And wasn't this basically no different from an assassin?

However, it seemed Subaru wasn't the only one wanting to needle their partner. Before Subaru could sort through the several candidate remarks bubbling up, Ram looked over at the one-handed sword still lodged halfway in the tree trunk and said,

"Useless."

"Nngh."

A blunt expression, needing nothing further, was the one that stung the most. As Subaru clutched his chest and sank to his knees, Ram gave a small shrug and walked over to the trunk where the sword was stuck. Turning her palm toward the tree, she used another wind blade to carve away the surface of the trunk, freeing the sword without difficulty. Subaru, sulking, hurried to catch the one-handed sword as Ram tossed it to him,

"Thanks and all, but could you not throw a bare blade spinning end over end like that!?"

Giving up on the catch, he retrieved the sword from where it had stuck into the ground instead. Wiping off the dirt and sheathing it—truly reunited with its sheath, he thought to himself, cringing internally at the lame pun—when Ram suddenly tilted her head.

"Even so, that Wolgarm just now went for Barusu instead of the defenseless Ram. Same as last night—you really do seem to be popular with them."

"Ahh, yeah. Guess I'm giving off a lot of pheromone-y vibes, huh."

Pressing his hands together briefly over the Witchbeast's corpse, Subaru scratched his head and gave a vague affirmation to Ram's observation. The sense of being singled out as the Witchbeasts' priority target had indeed continued from the previous night. As such results piled up, Subaru's suspicion was edging closer and closer to certainty. In any case,

"So, did that Clairvoyance from before find Rem?"

"No, unfortunately. Perhaps she's deeper into the forest. We should hurry. ...Though the chances of Barusu getting attacked will rise proportionally."

Glancing sideways at Subaru, Ram touched her pale cheek as if lost in thought. Her gaze went back and forth between Subaru and the Witchbeast, and then, apparently arriving at some sort of conclusion, if only vaguely, she nodded.

"Because you seem weak, after all."

"That's the conclusion you agonized over? Rude."

"Well then, because you seem like easy pickings."

"That's the same fundamental point, big sister."

"Talking alone won't improve things. Let's go find Rem."

"...Y-yeah, guess so."

Having a partner who stubbornly kept her own pace no matter what made things a bit awkward for Subaru too. She, feigning ignorance of what was on his mind, headed off deeper into the forest. Chasing after her back, Subaru suddenly voiced something that had been on his mind.

"Can I ask you something about 'Hornless'?"

It was a word Ram herself had used before they entered the forest. Somewhat guessable in meaning, but still, Subaru asked. Ram, without stopping and without turning back, replied,

"Exactly what it sounds like. A derogatory term for an oni who, despite being an oni, has lost their horn."

At the appearance of the word "oni," the sight from the previous night surfaced in Subaru's mind. In the forest, Rem, drenched in the blood of her enemies, laughing wildly. The sharp, white horn extending from her forehead—unforgettable. It was, precisely, the very image of an oni from a fairy tale. And yet, on the forehead of Ram, her supposed twin, there was no trace of any such horn to be seen. Of course, it might have been hidden beneath the white frilled cap essential to a maid's attire—but that possibility had just now been denied by her own mouth. Perhaps sensing, without a word being spoken, Subaru's unconvinced feelings, Ram reached a hand into her pink hair,

"In a bit of a scuffle, I lost the one horn I had. Ever since, I've come to rely on Rem for everything."

"...Ah, did I maybe ask something I shouldn't have?"

"Why?"

Ram, walking ahead, turned back, tilting her head with genuine puzzlement. In response, Subaru scratched his cheek and replied,

"Nah, I mean, I don't know how big a deal a horn is to the oni race, but I'd guess it's a pretty huge issue. And I figured I poked at something kind of rude."

"If so, it's a bit late to say that now, having already dug it up. —Well, don't worry."

After lowering her voice slightly to intimidate Subaru, Ram's expression softened just a touch,

"At the time, perhaps. But now, I've made my peace with it. Losing the horn also meant gaining certain things, and saving certain lives. That's all part of one's fate."

What a big-hearted person, Subaru thought to himself. But after saying that, Ram prefaced her next words with "but," a shadow falling across her expression,

"Rem, though—I doubt she feels the same way. —Which is exactly why we need to find her quickly."

Turning forward again, Ram gestured with her hand for Subaru to stop. It was time for Clairvoyance again. This time, without fail, he kept watch around her.

Her right eye flushed crimson, and her vision leapt from here to somewhere far away. From that leaping vantage, it transferred again to yet another vantage, searching out the one she sought. Subaru neither knew nor asked what borrowing the vision of someone whose wavelength matched showed her of the world, nor how great a toll it took on the caster. Every time she used Clairvoyance, tears of blood trickled down Ram's pale cheeks, her legs trembled in small shudders as if she'd walked a full day straight, and her stubbornly forward-moving body swayed repeatedly as though overcome with dizziness. And still, Ram refused to stop advancing through the forest.

Subaru thought he shouldn't just leave it to Ram, who never said a word about how hard or painful it was. At their core, the two twins, Ram and Rem, were fundamentally alike. The type to push themselves to the limit without hesitation, if it meant sparing someone else. Emilia included, the women of that mansion all had a bit too much of a habit of putting others first.

"You should be more like me and just fret over your own footing instead. —Seeing myself look this uncool just makes me sick of myself."

He kicked at the grass underfoot. Scattered grass flew into his mouth, kicked-up dirt into his eyes—a massive miscalculation. Flailing and spitting "ptoo, ptoo," he felt thoroughly embarrassed at his own lack of composure. But Subaru got the illusion that a fool like that suited him just fine, and something that had been tightly knotted inside him loosened just a little. So,

"Ram, are you worried about Rem? Does she matter that much to you?"

He asked knowing full well it wasn't good to disturb her concentration while she was using Clairvoyance. Ram, eyes bloodshot, her sight not here at all, answered with a beat's delay,

"Of course. That girl is, admittedly, stronger than Ram. But that's no reason not to worry."

"...Yeah."

"No matter what she's better at than Ram, she's still Ram's little sister. That position alone will never waver."

Under a firm resolve, her position as older sister was being exercised. Not until knowing this anew did Subaru admit that he had, until now, greatly underestimated her—thinking she used that position merely to lord it over others. And having admitted that, Subaru too had no choice but to steel his own resolve.

"Honestly, I would've preferred to wait until we'd linked up with Rem for this."

Scratching his head, he said that and began stretching his legs. Perhaps sensing something off in Subaru's attitude, Ram, having gained no results, ended her Clairvoyance and regained her own sight. Wiping her blood-filled right eye with a cloth pulled from her breast pocket, she frowned suspiciously,

"Barusu, what are you planning to do?"

"As things stand, I'm basically just tagging along as dead weight, right? I said it before we entered the forest, didn't I. That I'd actually be useful in helping save Rem."

Though it had been a coin-flip of a possibility, the odds had recovered now to roughly seven-to-three in his favor. Of course, there was the anxiety of real-life luck pulling the remaining three, but—

"Seven-three—it's rare for me to end up on the high-odds side. No way I'm throwing that away. —Ram, are you up for walking a bit of a dangerous tightrope?"

"A starving beast, a young man, and a forest—there's not much a maiden would find more dangerous than that, I'd imagine."

"Look at you, saying stuff like that, sis."

Laughing, Subaru then took a deep breath and opened his eyes wide. If Subaru's reasoning was correct, this would change everything. Even knowing it was necessary, he couldn't stop his heart from quailing with fear.

—There was no stopping the jitters at this point. That he was a small, cowardly man was already an established fact. No complaints if he could still see it through even while chickening out inside.

Striking himself with a scolding of uncertain strength, Subaru put it into words. If Subaru was right, it would come.

"Ram, actually, I—"

—He was about to say "I keep coming back from death."

To utter the very thing he'd been forbidden from telling, to break the taboo—that was the pose Subaru struck. Ram, braced for whatever Subaru was about to say, froze in expression.

No—time itself had come to a halt.

The world lost its color, sound vanished, and the very concept of time was wiped away wholesale. And in that world where a uniform stillness had been imposed on everything alike, the one being exempt from that restriction suddenly appeared before Subaru's eyes.

"—So you've come."

The murmur made no actual sound. But he loaded it with every ounce of venom he had, praying it would reach the thing before him. If even a little of his feeling got through, it would be more than satisfying enough to clear the knot in his chest.

—In a world where time had stopped, the one thing left unaffected: a black haze.

Appearing from nowhere, it began, before the bravado-masking Subaru, to form the outline of an arm. Fingers took shape, a wrist formed, an elbow sprouted, an upper arm followed—up to that point, it was the same transformation of the haze he'd witnessed before. But this third time, there was a further change.

"All the way to the shoulder..."

Past the upper arm, a shape resembling a shoulder was molded. Having formed everything from fingertips to shoulder, it was now, undeniably, worthy of the name "a full arm." Growing more distinct with each occurrence since he'd first seen it—while Subaru dreaded the encroachment of the haze deepening with each repetition, he wasn't given the luxury of time to savor that dread in detail.

Into Subaru's chest, which regarded the black haze's growth with unease, the fingers slid without hesitation. Past the thin flesh of his chest, brushing over his ribs, straight for the heart guarded within his breastbone.

Even knowing it was coming, pain, once it crosses a certain threshold, becomes something unbearable. The pain of having his heart gripped directly was in a realm that could not be described without screaming and thrashing about like a madman.

A long, agonizing, unbearable stretch of pain continued. His heartbeat went haywire, blood surged chaotically through his body, and every part of him screamed in protest. Tears of blood burst forth from the pain, and his clenched back teeth cracked and shattered.

Even as he suffered pain severe enough that such things should have happened, Subaru, frozen in place, couldn't vent it through any outward reaction—he could only endure and keep enduring.

Eventually, the span of agony grew distant, his vision dyed pure white—

"—Barusu?"

Called out to, Subaru realized he'd sunk to his knees. Drool trailed from the corner of his bowed mouth; hurriedly wiping it with his sleeve, he stood back up,

"Whew, close one. Daydreaming."

"You're still recovering too, same as Ram. Don't push yourself—leave it to Ram. Now, how do we go about finding Rem—"

Midsentence, Ram's expression suddenly changed, and she looked around. In the hush that had fallen over the forest, only the faint stir of branches swaying in the wind and leaves rustling against each other could be heard. Straining her ears toward it, Ram looked at Subaru,

"What did you do, Barusu?"

"...Just took a bit of a painful gamble."

That immense agony—no trace of it remained anywhere in his body now. Loathing the method that left wounds thoroughly only on the spirit, he could at least be grateful for one thing: it had left him with enough physical stamina to move. Because—

"The wind's stirring... beast-stink closing in. And a lot of it, too."

Amid the deep green that was starting to lose its stillness with a rustling unease, Ram's face suddenly fixed on a direction to the right. Following her gaze, Subaru saw multiple red points of light approaching from a distance. Five, for a start. Ram turned to face what would likely be an encounter within the next dozen or so seconds. Beside her, Subaru drew the one-handed sword from its sheath, bracing himself for the unavoidable battle as well.

"Rem still hasn't been found, and now this...!"

"Ah, don't worry. I bet we'll be able to link up with her before too long."

"How can you say that so confidently?"

Shrugging at Ram's sharp, intimidating glare, he said,

"Rem's whole goal is to wipe out every Witchbeast in this forest. —As long as I'm here, they're gonna keep coming after me as prey. So sooner or later, Rem's gonna have no choice but to show up here too."

He'd been thinking about it all along. Wondering about it all along. The reason Witchbeasts kept choosing Subaru as their target.

Throughout this repeating loop, every single time Subaru encountered a Witchbeast, he had, without fail, received the Curse. That wasn't so much an unavoidable fate as it was the result of some kind of compulsion at work—that whenever a Witchbeast was encountered, Subaru was, without fail, chosen as the target. Witchbeasts reacted excessively to Subaru's presence. He'd worked out the answer to why from the words of someone else who'd reacted just as excessively. Namely—

"The Witch's Lingering Scent."

Witchbeasts were said to be humanity's external enemies, born of the Witch. And they had always shown excessive reactions to Subaru, who carried the scent of the Witch about him. Him being attacked without exception every single time he entered the forest was, no doubt, for the same reason.

If the faint trace of that scent was what drew Witchbeasts out, then he might as well let it reek gloriously.

Enough that every Witchbeast in the forest—every single one that had ever cursed Subaru—would gather, enough that even Rem, chasing after them, would be drawn to join up with him.

That was Subaru's plan, the best strategy he could think of to both link up with Rem and save himself in the process. —Christened: "Operation Natsuki Subaru Decoy."

As ever, it was a fighting style that kept himself off the front lines, but the scheme had gone off well. It had helped that a certain remark Beatrice had once let slip, back when the haze had appeared during his previous attempt to tell Emilia about Return by Death, had stuck in his mind. Alongside the haze's appearance, the Witch's Lingering Scent surrounding Subaru grew stronger.

—Most likely, that haze had some sort of connection to the Witch.

So every time that haze appeared, the trace of the Witch wafting from Subaru grew stronger, and as a result, it began to function as live bait, drawing the Witchbeasts in.

Anguish he could tell no one about, agony he could never voice to anyone. At the exhilaration of having turned the very fate that had prepared all of that against itself, Subaru's mouth twisted into a grin, even leaving behind, for the moment, the threat closing in before his eyes.

—Ah, I finally landed one on the fate that rigged this whole loop!

Cheering inwardly, he readjusted his grip on the one-handed sword and braced against the approaching Witchbeasts. Then, to Ram beside him, he declared loudly,

"Alright, when it comes to fighting, I'm totally counting on you, so take it away!"

"Look back on what you just said later, objectively, and reconsider it."

A beat behind Ram's sigh-laced voice, a blade of wind slammed into the pack closing in from the front. —The battle lines with the Witchbeasts were about to open once more, with a change of cast.
