Arc 9 Chapter 51: "Yae Tenzen"

—A shinobi must never give their heart to anything.

That is the absolute teaching given, in the Shinobi Village of the Vollachian Empire, to those permitted no way of life but that of the shinobi — an ironclad law that must be upheld even above the blood-and-iron laws rooted in the empire's soil.

Entrust your heart to anything, and the finely honed blade that is a shinobi rusts all too easily. For that reason, all who would be shinobi, without exception, must carve that law into their souls and obey it. And yet—

"Ain't it like this with you, girl? Same as me — bein' able to do just about anythin' half-decently means life's got no spark to it, don't it? Wouldn't hurt to keep just one thing in your heart, would it?"

Saying that, the village elder had opened a mouth with teeth remarkably intact for his age and laughed, and Yae remembered being inexplicably furious — what had become of the shinobi's ironclad law?

—Including the fierce conviction that she herself could never live that way.

△▼△▼△▼△

—"Crimson Cherry" Yae Tenzen is a shinobi genius the likes of which appears but once in an age.

That is an unshakeable fact, one Yae herself, however reluctantly, acknowledges. Those around her praised her as the most gifted talent since the founding of the Shinobi Village, loudly proclaiming that her latent ability might even rival the Kararagi City-States' "Adulator." Frankly, Yae herself fervently wished they'd stop comparing her to such an outrageous individual, but with no regard for her own feelings, the people of the village grew wild with enthusiasm over her natural gifts. In truth, if talent is that which arrogantly lets one shortcut past the many hardships of a given field, then Yae unmistakably possessed an innate gift for the shinobi arts.

But in stark contrast to how others regarded her, Yae despised being treated as "special." It was something like a visceral revulsion, a resistance to the very notion of "special" — what might be called a "special allergy." She didn't mind when others were treated specially, but the instant she herself was, she was seized by an unbearable sensation, an urge to throw everything away. That was the nature of her rejection response.

For Yae, with her intense allergy to being "special," the shinobi's ironclad law — the very one the village elder had personally rejected at the start of this account — suited her extremely well.

To give one's heart to something is to create a "special" within oneself. Since she was allergic to that very "special," Yae could observe the shinobi's ironclad law without the slightest effort. She was, in every sense, a woman born to become a shinobi. Though, of course, that very trait was itself part of why everyone around her treated her as "special" — a truly maddening irony.

—Now then, to begin with, the path of the shinobi is a steep one, not easily attained. The height of that hurdle is such a narrow gate that it's said only one in a thousand apprentices becomes a Lower Ninja, one in a thousand Lower Ninja becomes a Middle Ninja, and one in a thousand Middle Ninja finally becomes an Upper Ninja. Though every time she heard this comparison, Yae thought: as if there could possibly be a billion shinobi aspirants to begin with. Well, it's a good thing that the senior shinobi were self-aware enough to know the road was harsh enough to invite such a ridiculous comparison. After all, it's only human to want any torture inflicted upon you to come with the firm conviction of "there's a reason to focus the attack here," rather than being subjected to it while the other party thinks, "what's even the point of this?"

And in fact, the process of completing a shinobi truly deserves to be called torture in its severity. Shinobi training begins around the age a child first becomes self-aware, so Yae considers it a matter that ought to be called child abuse rather than mere torture — but the sheer awfulness of shinobi-making is endless once you start listing it, so if we trip up at the entrance, the story won't move forward.

First, as for candidates for shinobi apprenticeship — these are, for the most part, children abducted from elsewhere. As mentioned, shinobi training begins in early childhood, but since it imposes inhumane and unreasonable exercises, children die left and right without mercy. So, prioritizing efficiency, rather than infants, they abduct toddlers of the proper age to begin training right away. The way they skip right past the most labor-intensive infant stage really shows the inhumanity of the shinobi — thoroughly unpleasant.

And the first thing an abducted child undergoes, before anything else, is memory processing. The method is said to involve drugs and suggestion, or techniques that directly affect memory, but nothing is certain. What is certain is that through this process, the abducted child forgets their family and hometown completely, cleanly. Yae, too, doesn't remember her parents' faces, and doesn't know her own true name. The name Yae was given to her later; before that, she was called "Red Number Eight," matching her hair color.

Those without aptitude first stumble at this memory processing. Either the memory erasure doesn't take well, or conversely it takes too well and leaves them a useless husk — it's a disaster right from the entrance. And Yae forgot her past cleanly, effortlessly — so much so that even she thought herself coldhearted for it. It slipped away so smoothly, without the slightest resistance, that Fujirou Tenzen, who was in charge of her memory processing, was reportedly dumbfounded — or so she was told by the man himself, afterward. —Incidentally, this Fujirou would later become Yae's namer, the previous Tenzen whose family name she would inherit. Though "namer" makes it sound like a parent, what existed between them was closer to the dutiful, affectionless relationship of an instructor and student than anything resembling a parent and child. To Yae, Fujirou was little more than the person who'd given her a name — someone she happened to interact with often within the village.

In any case, once one has forcibly stripped away the heart's anchor in this way and produced a blank canvas free to be painted at will with the black brush called "shinobi" — a child with a past wiped utterly clean — the true essence of the Shinobi Village, that inhuman training institution, begins: not mere physical conditioning, but full-blown modification of the human body.

Breaking and resetting bones to acquire superhuman flexibility and acrobatics; poison training that acclimates the body from small doses gradually up to lethal amounts; learning the dangers of martial arts and shinobi tools through pain, day in and day out — quotas were even imposed on the amount of broken bone, torn flesh, and spilled blood. Fellow trainees who never lived to see the next morning, gone cold, often lay scattered about, but even they weren't permitted burial — used instead as teaching materials for growing accustomed to death — and a steady stream of trainees had their minds broken and lost their sanity from the sight and stench of corpses rotting day by day.

Yae sailed through all these so-called horrific training regimens without difficulty. —No, calling it "without difficulty" would be a lie. Yae struggled in her own way. But the struggle Yae experienced was, she felt, perhaps a fraction — one ten-thousandth or less — of what her fellow trainees went through.

If that's what talent means, then it truly was talent. For the breaking and resetting of bones, she simply switched off the pain receptors inside herself; even against poison that felt like her melted innards were being churned, the blood in her urine and stool stayed minimal; the moment she touched a shinobi tool she could wield it like a partner she'd used for ten years; and any martial technique she'd tasted once, she thereafter used to beat down opponents bigger than herself, never to be struck by it again. Only the stench of corpses was truly hard on her — but even that she got used to, in the end.

And so, one way or another, the young Yae became the one-in-a-thousand at an unprecedented speed.

"From this day forth, call yourself Yae Tenzen."

That was how Fujirou named her, on the day her qualification as a Lower Ninja was recognized. Unfortunately, she felt no particular emotion about it. After all, even back as "Red Number Eight" she'd already been treated differently from her peers, so having a name or not made no particular impact on Yae's self-awareness. She merely found being treated as "special" itchy and bothersome.

—Why Fujirou named her Yae, she still doesn't know, even now. She missed her chance to ask. By now she's grown fairly fond of it anyway, and Tenzen has a nice ring to it too. Far better than ending up as Yae Dunkelkenn by some mistake. Even that little jab of Yae's, the village elder just laughed off with, "Kah-kah-kah! Ain't you gettin' cheeky!" Come to think of it, there wasn't a single shinobi in the village who shared the elder's family name — was that because the elder was a poor teacher, or because he'd simply abandoned that duty? Yae suspects the former is more likely.

Either way, whether she was called "Red Number Eight" or Yae Tenzen, the feel of the world to Yae, and the way she walked as a shinobi, didn't change. —What changed wasn't Yae, but the environment surrounding her.

—And that is the foremost reason Yae came to despise being "special."

—Now, as already stated, Yae had a "special allergy," but for her, having been promoted to Lower Ninja at the youngest age and in record time, an incident occurred that only accelerated that allergy.

Eventually everyone in the village would come to acknowledge Yae's talent and ability, but even at the point of her Lower Ninja qualification, there was already someone enchanted by her brilliant future. —Fujirou Tenzen. Fujirou, both her namer and the senior instructor who trained her, became utterly enchanted by Yae's talent — how she absorbed the one thing he taught her not merely as ten, but as a hundred, even a thousand.

Which is to say, this was an act in violation of the shinobi's ironclad law that one must never give one's heart to anything.

He must, with his own hands, finish shaping Yae Tenzen — a genius the likes of which appears but once in an age — into an extraordinary shinobi. —Fujirou had become possessed by that blind sense of mission.

"You have talent enough for it. Why can't you see that!?"

Fujirou, spitting those words out with bloodshot eyes, believed fanatically in Yae's talent. Day by day she had felt the way he looked at her growing more fevered, but the damage his eventual outburst brought about far exceeded anything Yae had imagined.

Fujirou, his mind scorched by Yae's "special" quality, prepared the Kodoku — a ritual built on the same mechanism as the Vollachian Empire's Imperial Selection Ceremony that decides its emperor — a foolish rampage that dragged in thirty Lower Ninja who at the time held the same rank as Yae, one that could have threatened the very survival of the village.

Led out to a forest far from the village and ordered to kill one another until only one remained, the Lower Ninja, never questioning their superior's instructions, threw their lives away for nothing. Just as Fujirou, who had orchestrated the ritual, had hoped, the only one left standing at the end was Yae Tenzen. However, he was greatly enraged both by her tactic of never striking first and only counterattacking those who came at her, and by the outcome in which she spared the last peer who begged for their life.

—No, to be precise, it wasn't just him, but them.

Fujirou wasn't the only one who'd fallen for Yae Tenzen's talent and lost his way. Inspired by his words, or having witnessed Yae's growth with their own eyes, many others sought to saddle Yae with their own twisted expectations by carrying out the Kodoku ritual. But toward Yae, who had betrayed the expectations of those who blindly believed in her, their fervor, far from cooling, only twisted further and burned all the hotter.

If they'd simply resolved to eliminate the Yae who'd betrayed their expectations and then take their own lives, that would still have had a certain pathetic charm to it. But there was no cure left for those who had settled on the mistaken resolve to repeat the Kodoku again and again, as many times as it took, until Yae fulfilled the image of a shinobi they desired.

"You are a being who will overturn what it means to be a shinobi...!"

Saying that, right up to the very last moment before he fell, Fujirou died still believing in Yae's potential. Onto the corpses of the namer and his foolish companions — throats gouged out after their grating declarations, collapsed face-down in pools of blood — the petals of the white snow-cherry blossoms, blooming with ironic beauty, fluttered down. Yae remembers those blood-stained petals with an indescribable sense of futility.

"Well, well, now that's somethin'. A little girl still wet behind the ears, and you went and took out every last one of 'em, even the Upper Ninja among 'em?"

The village elder, arriving late to the scene, let slip that carefree remark as he surveyed the corpses of the young men and the Yae surrounded by them. It seemed the covert activities of Fujirou and his companions had already been caught by the village's higher-ups, so regardless of whether the Kodoku had succeeded or failed, they'd had no future ahead of them anyway. She'd rather not entertain the uncharitable suspicion that, having grasped that much already, they'd simply been slow to act because they'd decided to dump the job of disposing of the village's troublemakers onto Yae.

"So, they all end up dead, then?"

Not because of that suspicion, but Yae lied in answer to the elder's question. The cause of Fujirou and the others' displeasure — the peer who had begged for their life and whom Yae had let go. —She hid the fact that she'd let him leave not just the Kodoku, but the Shinobi Village itself.

That peer had proposed, "Ah, well, if that's the case, wanna leave together?" but Yae flatly refused. It wasn't that she wanted to stay in the village — she simply didn't want to stand alongside him. In skill and in talent, Yae was far above him. And yet her instincts screamed at her — if there was any moment fate had marked for Yae's death, it would be the moment she stood at his side.

"—Failure, failure."

From the peer who left with those parting words, she sensed an intent to erase everyone who knew he was still alive, but he was rational enough to abandon that particular ambition, and thanks to that, in this Kodoku, only Yae and that peer survived. Merely by not being the sole survivor, perhaps Yae's "specialness" would be diluted, if only a little — or so she thought, in a way.

"Well then, let's head back. —Crimson Cherry."

The village elder, turning his back, called Yae by an epithet she'd never heard before. Why Fujirou had named her Yae, she never did learn, to the very end. But why the elder called her that, she understood without being told.

—Because on the night too many shinobi had died, the white snow-cherry blossoms in full bloom had been magnificently dyed with blood.

—"Crimson Cherry" Yae Tenzen is a shinobi genius the likes of which appears but once in an age.

By the time she'd been promoted to Upper Ninja at the youngest age and that recognition had become common, unshakeable knowledge, Yae's aggravated "special allergy" had become beyond remedy.

Being treated as "special" by others was, to Yae, a curse, a threat, an abhorrent interference that brought nothing but negative effects to her life. Things like expectation and hope carry such a strongly positive image that far too many people wield them without ever realizing they can become blades that gouge deep into others. Worse still, an unwitting blade is a nasty thing, since neither the wielder nor the wounded easily notices the injury. Having had her life thoroughly jerked around by such things, Yae had grown utterly exhausted.

"Ain't it like this with you, girl? Same as me — bein' able to do just about anythin' half-decently means life's got no spark to it, don't it? Wouldn't hurt to keep just one thing in your heart, would it?"

To Yae, the elder — who was fully aware that such a blade existed and said that to her anyway — was infuriating. Here was the village elder himself, urging her to break the shinobi's ironclad law. It seemed like madness. To begin with, Yae had no desire for any "spark" in her life. That was an unnecessary thing. "Spark" meant, in essence, having expectations for one's own abilities, or holding hope for the future. —Nothing less than believing in one's own "specialness," and unmistakably a landmine for Yae.

So Yae deliberately distanced herself from the image of a shinobi that everyone would expect great things of and pin their hopes on. That was the polar opposite of the image of a shinobi that Fujirou Tenzen and the others had staked their lives on trying to complete — a frivolous manner, elusive words and conduct, an approachable yet never truly familiar bearing. Yae Tenzen's personality was formed as that of someone possessing just such a flippant, frivolous character.

To avoid any misunderstanding, let it be stated plainly: this was the natural consequence for a Yae who abhorred "special," and by no means something modeled on the village elder's own personality. Never getting deeply involved in anyone's life, never facing anyone head-on, never being cherished by anyone. That was the very survival strategy, the life philosophy, that "Crimson Cherry" Yae Tenzen had arrived at—

"—You surely don't imagine such an unsightly way of being to be some gift of yours, do you?"

Spoken to her while the two of them were alone, that remark made Yae catch her breath before that crimson beauty. Yae, whose "special allergy" had grown chronic, disliked being treated as "special" by others, but she also had a keen nose for other people's "special" qualities. On that score, the woman Yae now found herself close to was, inside and out, someone who was practically a lump of "special" made flesh. Though, admittedly, when it came to her specialness, whether or not one's nose was keen made no difference at all — it was blindingly obvious to anyone.

—The "Blood-Stained Bride," Priscilla Barielle.

Her days spent with this woman, whom her subjects also fondly called the "Sun Princess," were vivid and deeply memorable even for Yae, who had carried out all manner of missions as a shinobi.

Originally, it was at the instigation of Chisha Gold, one of the empire's "Nine Divine Generals," that Yae had infiltrated Priscilla's household as a maid. What significance she — off participating in the Royal Selection, far away in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica — held for the empire, Yae was never told, nor did she care to ask. Obeying orders without asking too many questions is the true calling of a shinobi, and Yae is content with that. The only one who openly defies it is the village elder — and Yae, being unlike him, is obedient.

That said, it was an irrational and inscrutable mission. Basically, it amounted to surveillance of the target and reporting on her movements — she'd received no instructions for any particular sabotage or obstruction, nor was it a matter of "go assassinate her quickly." Granted, since it was a mission that specifically requested Yae, it undoubtedly presumed a corresponding level of skill and ability to survive — but once she'd slipped into the mansion, days went by uneventfully as nothing more than a maid. —If she may say so, it seemed like nothing but a waste of the genius shinobi that was "Crimson Cherry."

In fact, the natural talent Yae had been blessed with wasn't limited to shinobi arts — it had a versatility that let her handle most anything else smoothly as well. So for Yae, who could handle maid duties flawlessly too, the mission to infiltrate the Barielle estate was, like all her past missions, an easy job. Except that whenever she interacted with Priscilla, she needed to exercise the utmost caution.

Given the nature of her work, Yae had had various occasions to lay eyes on important figures. The "White Spider" who'd ordered her on this mission was one such figure, and the village elder, the "Vicious Old Man," was technically one of the empire's dignitaries too. She'd even glimpsed His Majesty the Emperor from a distance — though on that occasion, she'd locked eyes with the "Blue Lightning" standing beside the Emperor, who'd even waved at her, and she'd trembled at the thought that there was always someone above the one above. Even to Yae, who had interacted with such formidable figures, Priscilla's powers of insight were unfathomable, and there was no predicting what she might see through.

Impossible as it should have been, there were more than a few times Yae wondered whether Priscilla had seen through her as an assassin sent by the empire. Still, if that suspicion were true, there would be no reason for Yae to have been overlooked, so surely she hadn't been noticed. —Or would she say that for certain? Priscilla had that elusive quality of someone who might notice and let it slide regardless.

One could call it a love of danger so great she didn't even value her own life, or one could dismiss it, as she herself always declared without the slightest hesitation, as the delusion of someone who genuinely believed the whole world would bend to her will.

Either way — Yae pitied the "special" Priscilla, thinking it an unfortunate thing. Priscilla, who carried a bewitching quality in not only her rare beauty but in her every bearing, word, and even the way she breathed, would — whether she wished it or not — touch every person's eyes, lives, and emotions through sheer force of her "specialness." Countless people would come to expect things of her, to hope, and be ruled by ill-conceived wishes and emotions. —Even this pity Yae felt was itself something her "specialness" had brought about.

It was as if seeing straight through Yae's heart that she'd spoken the words at the start of this. In her private chambers, eyes cast down on the book in her hands, not sparing so much as a glance at Yae preparing the tea — and then, without warning, without any sign at all, she'd say something like that. That said, as mentioned, Yae was always on guard when dealing with Priscilla. So even this time, though she felt a small jolt of surprise, she was able to respond with her usual light wit.

Lightly, mixing in a bit of playfulness, behaving as though she had no intention of taking it seriously, she put on a smile. At Yae's unchanging attitude, Priscilla closed one eye—

"—A time will come, eventually, when you too must spend the whole of your being. Mark my words well and take heed. See to it your preparations are 'perfect,' won't you."

Yae was never able to ask the true meaning behind those words. Partly because her shinobi instincts warned her that asking might arouse suspicion. But more than that, perhaps, Yae simply dreaded it. The thought of words she'd rather not hear being cast at her from the "special" Priscilla's own lips.

So Yae doesn't know the true meaning behind Priscilla's words. Nor does she know why Fujirou named her Yae, nor what lay behind the village elder's endless efforts — so persistent she wondered if it was mere harassment — to get her to break the shinobi's ironclad law. None of it.

To avoid being treated as "special" by others, she keeps stopping her ears and turning her eyes away from it.

—For that is Yae Tenzen's life philosophy: to neither desire "special," nor be desired as "special."

In the end, Yae's thinking was proven right. Even Priscilla, who had shone so brilliantly and captivated so many with her flame-like "special" way of being, fell upon the cruel, merciless soil of the empire and scattered her life away. And now, someone who yearned for her lost "specialness," his soul scorched by a violent passion with nowhere left to go, is still burning through his life, wanting nothing more than to repay that "specialness."

Yae Tenzen has no wish to live a life jerked around by "special" in that way. That's why. That is exactly why.

That's why, as swiftly as possible, she will erase from this world the "special" that makes her harbor such an unhealthy obsession. That, and that alone, is the reason Yae cooperates, staking her very life, with the man abandoned by the "special" he so desperately loved—

"—!"

Upon a disc of ice spinning at high speed, careening every which way with no regard for up, down, left, or right, Yae nonetheless used it as her footing to construct a battlefield, meeting the charging oni girl — Rem — head-on. The steel thread launched from her right hand ran vertically, while the steel thread from her left swung the sword it had ensnared in a horizontal sweep — two lines of unavoidable cutting light, pincering Rem from front and back at once.

—Through their coordinated effort, Yae let Emilia slip free of the battlefield. Emilia, ice wings sprouting from her back, caught the momentum and wind of the spinning ice disc as it was struck, and chased after Al, who flew off far into the distant sky, gaining speed rapidly. Her retreating back, moving away from Yae — who could do nothing but fall headfirst — finally slipped beyond the reach of the steel thread. It wasn't impossible, still, to hurl shards of rock or blades on a long throw using the thread—

"No looking away allowed!"

—but, interfered with by Rem, who had just dodged the preceding double attack by blocking the sword with her chain and ducking under the steel thread, that chance too slipped through Yae's fingers. Grinding her teeth at the blunder, Yae, in order to escape the Rem now closing in before her, used a thread she'd cast out as an anchor to swing herself in a wide arc, circling around to the far side of the ice disc, as—

"Isn't it unfair, suddenly getting this tough? What's the reason—"

"—Love!"

"...I shouldn't have asked!"

Chasing after Yae, who had circled to the far side of the ice disc, Rem forcibly shattered through the ice to break past it. In the face of the oni girl's savagery — her horn gleaming, her vitality surging — Yae, aiming to draw the fight back into her preferred midrange, evaded the swung, mighty arm and iron ball with deft footwork, clinging to the spinning platform to circle behind Rem. Fast as it was, the ice disc's rotation around its axis, while random in its vertical and horizontal tumbling, had a fixed rhythm to the rotation speed itself. Once she'd grasped even just that much, there was no risk of losing her footing, and Yae leapt freely across the ice, dancing. In stark contrast to Yae, who conquered the ice disc with such grace, Rem's movements were simple and straightforward—

"I won't let you...!"

Declaring that fiercely, Rem drove her spear-hand fingers, her toes, and the iron ball gripped short into the ice, boring them in like wedges to anchor herself, and clung to Yae's heels with beast-like movements. It was a hunting style far removed from refinement or elegance — rough, ferocious, that of a carnivorous beast — and yet, it was fast. In the blink of an eye, she closed the distance to Yae, who fled while dancing.

"Isn't being this heedless of appearances unbecoming of a maid?"

"Unfortunately, there's only one quality our master demands of her maids — giving it everything you've got!"

Rem, lifting her face while still braced on three limbs, twisted her body and, with minimal motion, hurled in a single blow of the iron ball, harnessing the disc's own rotation. Deflecting the force of it by mobilizing her steel threads and flinging it off to her own side, Yae clicked her tongue inwardly.

"The Best Maid of the Two Camps" contest — Yae has no leeway left to joke about it like that. She surpassed Rem in skill, and could insist her overall ability was a rank above, not merely one but three — yet Yae and Rem simply had bad chemistry as opponents. The morningstar Rem wielded was a weapon of raw mass, and a heavyweight blow meant to kill in a single strike was exactly the kind of weapon steel thread struggled most to defend against. Bundle enough strands together and twist them, and the steel thread's strength was such that there was room to block virtually any attack — but that required the proper distance and a corresponding number of strands. For Yae to block one of Rem's blows safely, she couldn't call it a sure thing unless she used seven fingers across both hands. If she didn't like that, the answer was simply to dodge instead—

"The aim is way too precise...!"

Despite the terrible footing, the iron ball Rem hurled struck at Yae like a great serpent. Every trajectory aimed dead center at Yae, almost too honest a line — failing to block it was unthinkable even with her eyes shut — but it kept breaking up the rhythm of Yae's own attacks, every single time. And on top of all that, as if for good measure, the Oni Tribe's fearsome combat capabilities were added into the mix.

"Aaaaaahhh!!"

Limbs driven into the ice disc, Rem let out a roar as red steam burst from every part of her body. That was a result of the low temperature at the altitude of a fall of several thousand meters, combined with the Oni race's trait of activating the mana surging through the whole body, and — on top of that — the rapid healing of the lacerations the steel thread had inflicted. Up to this point, while dodging Rem's fatal blows, Yae had carved several wounds into her aimed at disabling her. But none of them could outpace the Oni's regenerative power. And more than anything—

"Sh—!"

Kissing her ring, once again the steel thread was engulfed in flame. In that instant, having blanketed Rem's field of vision in fire, Yae aimed at the neck of her enemy beyond the heat haze, sweeping her drawn-back arm in a single flash — a strike unleashing the steel thread at a speed approaching sound, aiming for decapitation. This was an original technique of Yae's own devising, not found in any secret scroll — the fastest strike among all the Steel Thread Techniques she could wield, or what Al might call her "ultimate move." An invisible thread-strike of unknown range, and she'd had every confidence that, as a stroke of certain death, it could cut down any opponent whatsoever. —And yet, Rem dodged it. And not just once, but twice.

"—!"

On its first outing, it had been a greedy strike, aiming to take out both Rem and Emilia together. Having been blocked that time, this time — so as not to let her dodge by simply pulling her head back — she'd meant to slash the chest instead of the neck. It had a whistling sound that gave her the certainty no fluke dodge would be possible — which is why Rem evading it was no miracle, no coincidence. It was inevitable.

Rem had read Yae's Steel Thread Technique. There was no longer any ice mist around them — a different method from how Emilia had sensed the steel thread's presence by the way the ice particles were cut. What principle lay behind it, Yae couldn't say for certain.

"I won't let you."

Giving up the pretense of an expression that had no meaning left to feign, Yae murmured, the corner of her mouth going hard. Won't let her. Won't let her go. Won't let this continue. —Both Rem and Emilia — Yae will stop them.

"——"

Spreading wings of stone, Al had fled into the sky without waiting for this battle's conclusion. Yae doesn't think of herself as left behind, or abandoned. There's a possibility he's given up on her, not none — but Al has a stingy streak. He can't easily throw away a card in hand, or a pawn on the board. So Yae took Al's departure as him entrusting the matter to her. She interpreted it as an order: stop the two who are chasing him, by whatever means necessary.

Therefore, Yae had been entrusted by Al — with a license to kill.

"—I'll stop them, even if it means killing them."

Stop them. Stop them. Stop them, no matter what. To let Al accomplish his goal. To eliminate anything and everything standing in his way. So that Al — struggling after losing Priscilla, who had been his "special" — could be made to vanish from the world as quickly as possible.

For unless she does that, Yae Tenzen can never return to being the self who observes the shinobi's ironclad law, the being who gives her heart to nothing, the self who abhors "special" and keeps it at a distance.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

That was the phrase the monster who had made Yae Tenzen know true terror repeated, again and again, the very same words.

Her days at the Barielle estate, where she'd been ordered to infiltrate and sent, came to an abrupt end. Her everyday maid duties, teasing the cute butler-in-training, trading quips with the clownish knight who was never treated like a knight, while serving the fearsome yet dazzling "Sun Princess" — it hadn't been bad. But once an order comes down, Yae's own feelings are irrelevant. Fulfill the duty, and vanish without a trace.

And so, in order to carry out the assassination order that had suddenly come down, as Yae tried to creep toward Priscilla in her bedchamber, that man stopped her in the dark hallway of the mansion. Faced with an obstruction that needed no explanation, one that felt as if he'd known all along, Yae fought a life-or-death battle against the man — Al. Life-or-death as it was called, the gap in ability was plain to see. Despite being a knight to a Royal Selection candidate, Al's skill was far, far inferior to Yae's, and the outcome should have been decided in an instant—

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

—and yet, the man whose heart she was certain she'd struck a fatal blow tossed those same words at her, in the same tone. Even faced with such an abnormal situation, Yae's body moved without hesitation, driving in the next killing blow. Kill him and he won't die. This wasn't her first encounter with that sort. The world is full of all manner of bizarre, astonishing people, and she'd once fought someone with not two but three hearts. Even that one died properly once its third heart was crushed. In short, all you have to do is keep killing until they die.

She severed his head. She cleaved his torso in two. She burned his entire body. She tore off his limbs. She slashed his throat. She smashed his skull open. She gouged out his eyeballs. She flayed every inch of skin from his back. She shattered every bone in his body. She crushed his internal organs. She forced poison down his throat. She buried him underground. She sank him underwater. She strangled his neck. She strangled everything but his neck. She tried every one of her shinobi techniques, took every life he had to give, and carried out every killing that could possibly be carried out.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

But no matter how many times she killed him, Al rose back up and threw those same words at her. She used every shinobi technique she had, tried every method she could think of, and even, in the end, abandoned thought altogether and gave herself over to instinct — but she couldn't kill him. Couldn't finish him off.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

She tried torture severe enough to make him regret ever being born. Useless. She tried abandoning the mission and fleeing. Useless. She tried dragging others into it. Useless. Finally, deciding this truly was the end, she looped her own thread around her own neck to end it herself.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

Even that was useless.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

The same exchange, repeated over and over — a spiral wandering endlessly between death and death, given to her again and again without end — and for the first time, Yae Tenzen felt true fear.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

She thought she'd do anything to be saved. She swore she'd do anything. She thought she'd do anything to end it. She swore she'd do anything.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

Merely knowing fear wasn't enough — Al, the man, the monster, would not forgive her.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

Even after her heart had been broken down utterly, completely, there was no end to the monster's mercilessness. She hasn't even counted the number. In this world, no one has fought to the death with Yae as much as this monster. Countless times beyond counting, Yae killed him; a number she doesn't want to count, Yae was killed by him.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

There's no other opponent like this. She doesn't want one. Doesn't even want to think about it. Even now, freed from that endless cycle by the monster's whim; even now, having been made to swear obedience and following his purpose with her whole heart and soul — it's carved into the very depths of her soul.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

As soon as possible, the monster, the man, Al, has to disappear. For no matter what the trigger, what the reason, what the connection might be, it must never be that Yae Tenzen's heart is captive to anything at all.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

—For her heart being held captive by anything at all — that must never be.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

As Yae's lips formed those words, an invisible flash of cutting light burst forth, aimed at Rem's slender neck. Sensing it with a feeling that made her skin crawl, Rem reflexively swung the arm gripping her morningstar, inserting the chain into the path of the flash to knock it aside.

The line between life and death — she stands, without question, right on that line. A crucible of battle that never came her way in the Vollachian Empire, where she had lost her memories and her strength to fight; a state Rem had never reached even in the fights against the Witch Cult's Archbishops of Sin or the White Whale, before she lost her memories.

"—Subaru-kun."

Amid a sensation of trembling innards and boiling blood, the thing holding the most heat was the horn on her forehead — and tasting vividly the power, the emotion, the very pulse of the world flowing in from it, Rem rouses her own soul by calling out the name of the beloved boy.

The advice she'd received from the dazzling "Sun Princess," compounding with the surge of feeling for the one she loved, meant that the Oni blood flowing within Rem was finally beginning to display its true capacity. Even so, the gap in ability between Rem and Yae remains vast, and the fact that Rem has been able to hang on this long against a woman who ranks among the strongest fighters she's ever seen isn't due to her own strength alone.

It's the fruit of the many, many efforts that have hung on, again and again, trying to stop Yae — trying to stop Al and the others.

Starting from the Pleiades Watchtower, through the clash with the Felt Camp and the battle for the royal capital, and further, Otto's relentless pursuit, and now this splitting attack — stacking irregularities atop diligence, Al and the others have been bound hand and foot by chains of inescapable attrition. That the gap in ability between Rem and Yae is being temporarily closed is thanks to the weight of those chains. On top of that, a twist of fate that could only be called ironic is serving as a tailwind for Rem. And that is—

"—!"

Yae's fingers, grinding her teeth, danced, and the steel thread she unleashed tore through the air, assailing Rem. The countless cutting flashes pouring down — Rem sensed them keenly through her horn, dodging, dodging, linking each into a perfect evasion, preserving the life that should have been severed, and clinging fiercely to the battlefield. The secret to that god-like — no, oni-like — dodging came down to one thing.

"—Killing intent."

She could see it. Feel it. It came through to her. —The opponent's intent, through the Oni Tribe's horn. Dodging that line of death, which appeared tinged with color to her eyes, tracing along it, striking it back, Rem closed the gap on Yae.

Just before sending Emilia off, right after Al had left the battlefield, a clear killing intent had begun to mix into Yae's attacks. A dance of murderous intent that didn't hesitate to sever Rem's neck or hack off her limbs — and that stimulated the Oni Tribe's instincts fiercely, fiercely, and, ironically, produced Rem's hyper-reactions. If Yae had followed her original policy and faced Rem while holding to a no-kill approach, Rem would probably have been neutralized by her much sooner. —As a result of Yae getting serious and beginning to display her true colors as a shinobi, her attacks stopped landing on Rem. If this isn't to be called an ironic tailwind, what else could it be called?

"——"

Driving her toes into the spinning ice disc to support her own body, Rem, amid a field of vision glittering with scattering ice shards, caught sight of Yae, her smile gone, staring back at her. Yae, her narrow eyes sharpened, her heart disciplined, was beautiful enough to steal the breath — and Rem could tell, as clearly as if she held it in her hand, what was supporting that lithe, well-trained body.

What filled Yae's slender frame was a sense of mission. A wish that had to be fulfilled, a prayer she was determined to see through no matter what — these gave Yae an extraordinary strength, and instilled in her the resolve to make an enemy of the whole world if need be. And Rem knows exactly what that sense of mission springs from.

"Because I too... Rem is the same way."

A horn grown on her forehead, wounds inflicted all over her body, plunging headfirst from a height of five thousand meters — the reason Rem can still stand here like this is, too, a sense of mission. A wish that had to be fulfilled, a prayer she was determined to see through no matter what — these gave Rem an extraordinary strength, and gave her the resolve to throw everything away to save just one person. And Rem has no reason to hold back from saying what that sense of mission springs from. It is—

"—Love!!"

"One-track mind, is what you've got...!"

There's irritation in Yae's voice as she answers the roaring Rem. The shinobi's expression — frozen or feigned, never once revealing her true heart — comes unraveled. Rem doesn't take that as immaturity, or arrogance, or carelessness. Gritting one's teeth is only natural — if you're spending everything you have for someone else's sake.

"——"

At the edge of her vision, in the midst of fierce battle, a line etched into the rock wall brushes past Rem's awareness. Agzadd Canyon. Five thousand meters. The last marker. To the canyon floor, remaining—

"—Ten seconds!!"

"—!"

Standing against the wave-like onslaught of thread-strikes surging toward her, Rem deliberately shouted that. Give her information. Increase her material for judgment. Throw her thoughts into disarray — even making just one or two of her twenty fingers and toes hesitate would be enough. Provoke her, make her bare her feelings, make her hate her. Let malice, hostility, killing intent overflow, and slip through it, slip through it, slip through it it it it— And then—

"Here!"

Just as Rem and Emilia had aimed — at the point of the last ten seconds, then five seconds after that — an impact from directly below tore mercilessly through the ice disc.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

That's what the monster who came to occupy Yae's heart often murmured. Even now, Yae dreams of that man's voice, the one she feels she's heard thousands, tens of thousands of times.

At first, she found that verbal habit of Al's hateful. Even the stars, surely, would want to protest — being blamed for absolutely everything like that, who could stand it? Yae's heart had cracked and shattered not because of the stars, but unmistakably because of the monster — and yet here he was, shifting the blame.

But gradually, she came to realize it was different — that this wasn't the man shifting blame at all.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

When he says those words, Al never once gloats. What lies there isn't contempt or mockery, but probably something like mercy, or sympathy. And as proof of that, the monster, the man, Al, never said it.

He never said the stars were bad for Priscilla Barielle's death. He never said the stars were bad for his own pathetic failure to save her life.

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

The times the monster, the man, Al says it are always when his own unreasonable power crushes another's wish or goal. Woven together with mercy, sympathy, guilt, it always means, in effect, this to the other person:

—It isn't your fault.

Ah, how she hates it. Hates it, hates it. Truly hates it.

She never wanted to realize such a thing. She never wanted to notice it — not in Al's words, not in the man's gaze, not in the monster's heart.

Yae doesn't want to find any reason other than fear in Al. Doesn't want to hold one. Doesn't want to feel one. That's why she wants to end this as quickly as possible.

—She doesn't want to give this "special" any name other than fear.

"It's all... the stars' fault..."

Hearing that sound slip from her own lips, Yae pulled back her consciousness, which had blanked out for just an instant. In that moment, she realized her own body had been blasted into the air, her vision spinning round and round.

An impact. Yes, there had been an impact. The ice disc had been struck from directly below by something, and the shock of the shattering ice had run clean through Yae as well. Wondering what it was, she looked down through the edge of her awareness and saw that what had shattered the ice disc was the peak of a colossal iceberg, risen up from the canyon floor thousands of meters below — a terrifyingly enormous mass of ice, formed by freezing the great river flowing at the bottom of the canyon, lying in wait for the falling disc.

From the force and wind of the fall, and the number of seconds elapsed, the approximate fall distance came to about five thousand meters — matching, geographically, the world's largest canyon, Agzadd Canyon — and at the brute feat of power that had frozen the great river running through its depths, Yae was made to realize just how far beyond ordinary standards Emilia's magic was. But what struck her most keenly of all was—

"...What a tenacious woman."

Amid her spinning field of vision, within the breathtaking sight of the shattered ice disc's fragments glittering into diamond dust, Yae caught sight of Rem, her horn shining bright. At the instant of collision, Rem, having escaped into the air as if fleeing the impact, was accelerating off the scattered chunks of ice as footholds, and was in the midst of leaping toward Yae, still airborne.

With five seconds left until the collision, she'd been misled into thinking there were ten. Losing her composure — a blunder unbecoming of a shinobi. Falling behind — a folly unbecoming of a maid. Betraying the role entrusted to her — unbecoming of the "Crimson Cherry"—

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

Not to the oni girl closing in before her, nor to herself for the disgraceful display she was making — but to the monster who wasn't here, who had flown far, far away, into the distance, Yae speaks. Giving to the monster the very same meaning the monster himself gives when he says those words to others.

"—Ah."

Her whole body creaks from the shock of the blow, and Yae isn't even sure her limbs are still attached. But so what if she's numb — she overcame numbness in poison training. So what about the feeling in her limbs — she's had every bone in her body broken in torture training. So what about the clarity of her consciousness — she got through training that drained huge quantities of blood from her body and made her fight on the verge of death. That all of it is proving useful to her right now makes her furious, as though she can hear the village elder's crafty old laugh.

Furious, she is — but—

"—Ahh."

Thousands, tens of thousands, hundreds of thousands, millions, tens of millions of times — she has repeated the same motion. The steel thread, a technique with no practitioner for centuries, one that demands the delicate skill to move one's fingertips by the millimeter — that, even numb, bleeding, her mind hazy, Yae wields. Even if seventy percent of the life has already spilled out of this body, the fingers reeling in the steel thread do not err.

"—Aaaahh!"

Drawing taut both her outstretched arms and legs, in an instant the block of ice bound by the steel thread went hurtling ferociously through the sky. The fragments of the ice disc, shattered by the impact, were ensnared by the thread and, in time with the rhythm of Yae's entire body, wheeled ferociously through the sky before slamming sideways into the incoming Rem.

"——"

A thunderous roar resounded, and Rem, struck by the block of ice, went flying off with tremendous force. As if to finish off the flying Rem, the steel thread caught and hurled ice blocks in one after another, after another, after another, raining down heavy blows on Rem, who had fallen first onto the iceberg's peak. White snow-smoke billowed up, and blood mixed into the drifting ice mist, turning the iceberg into a grave marker for the oni. —In an instant, that grave marker was pierced, and the iron ball, surging upward, drove straight into Yae.

"—Ugh."

The vicious mass of iron, its attached spikes, gouged into Yae's torso, piercing deep through her side. But her vital organs had been shifted aside. Through the shinobi's inhuman bodily modification, Yae could bias the positions of her internal organs. The spike pierced only empty space, scattering blood from her back. Even so, she'd deliberately taken this hit.

Clenching her muscles, she sealed the spiked iron ball within her own body. Next, bursting out through the billowing white snow-smoke, following the chain of the iron ball lodged in Yae, came the blood-soaked oni, leaping ferociously straight upward. That trajectory was straight, without a twist, without any cleverness, a single line poured out with all her heart — and waiting along that trajectory of the incoming Rem was a net-like web of steel thread.

"——"

Of her own will, she dove into the birdcage of steel thread that would tear her own body to shreds, and countless lacerations bloomed across Rem's white skin, threatening to divide her into hundreds of pieces of flesh. But an instant before that could come to fruition, Rem's lips wove a faint incantation,

"Huma."

The ice-generating spell she had used again and again throughout this battle. Yae, wary with her whole body of where Rem — who had chosen that spell at the eleventh hour — would generate the ice, kept her nerves on alert across a full three-hundred-sixty degrees, bracing to block any surprise attack whatsoever. But it turned out to be unnecessary. There was no surprise attack. The ice formed right before Yae's own eyes.

The resistance in the steel thread, which should have been slicing Rem's body to pieces, stopped. Looking closely, the steel thread biting into her flesh had been halted by red ice — frozen blood. The instant that understanding hit, a kiss lit the steel thread with flame, and the explosive heat melted the frozen blood, trying to resume the lethal thread-strike. But it was too late.

"Yaaaah—!!"

Not missing that instant of slack in the thread, the roaring Rem swung her fist toward Yae. It was a blow imbued with both the will to decide this battle and the full-body effort to make it so — and Yae, from empirical measurement alone, could tell that power resided in that blood-smeared white fist. Therefore, she cannot take it. Must not take it. Emilia is still out there.

To reach that point, she draws her trump card.

"——"

Using all twenty fingers and toes across both hands and feet, the fully mobilized steel thread — and yet her enemy slipped through it. But there's more. There's still one more. Yae Tenzen's trump card in the Steel Thread Technique — a red tongue, stuck out at her opponent as if in provocation—

"Bleh."

From the ring hanging at the tip of that tongue, the last flash of cutting light was unleashed. It struck true, catching the slender neck of the fiercely charging Rem, and severed her life in a single flash.

—"Crimson Cherry" Yae Tenzen is a shinobi genius the likes of which appears but once in an age.

"Vicious Old Man" Olbart Dunkelkenn valued her talent and ability very, very highly. She herself greatly disliked being praised so highly, but the way she hated it stirred up quite the sadistic urge in him, so Olbart never stopped.

In raw ability, he, with all his years of experience, was likely still above her. But her talent far exceeded his own, and once she'd put in the years, an unrivaled, strongest shinobi that no one could touch would be born. So, well, he understood the impulse to pin one's dreams on Yae.

The flash of her trump card — the steel thread extending from the ring hooked to the tip of her tongue shot out, and unerringly, it reaped the slender neck of the charging oni girl, severing the connection between head and body.

She had released herself from the bind of non-lethality, and accomplished the elimination of the obstacle standing in the way of Al's purpose. Thinking of that fact, and of how Al would react upon learning it, Yae felt a creaking sensation deep in her chest—

"—Huh?"

—right before she could bitterly savor it, Yae's eyes went wide, doubting herself at the sight before her. Without question, Yae's steel thread had severed Rem's slender neck. She had felt, unmistakably, the sensation of a life being sliced through, the blood and flesh and bone, and had heard, unmistakably, the whistling sound of a life being cut off. And yet—

"—I cheated too, you know."

Yae's crimson eyes met Rem's pale blue ones. Those eyes had not lost their light. And the red line of life taken, which should certainly have cut into that neck, slowly faded away. —Not blocked, not dodged — the blow that should certainly have taken her life had simply been canceled. That was a miracle that shouldn't exist, one that couldn't be explained by the Oni Tribe's regenerative power alone. Someone capable of healing magic on that scale — as vast as this world may be—

"—Al-sama."

She had wanted to lend her strength to the monster who, even with the whole world turned against him, would surely never lose. By doing so, she'd wanted to erase the "special" that had come to breathe within her own heart. And yet—

"—The stars were just bad, that's all."

When she closes her eyes, she hears again those terrible words that make every hair on her body stand on end. But strangely, just for this one moment, what those words brought to Yae wasn't fear.

"The reason for defeat is—"

"—Love."

Along with the answer, the mighty arm swung out, slamming into the iron ball embedded in Yae's torso. More fatal even than a fall from five thousand meters, the oni's blow detonated — and atop the iceberg of Agzadd Canyon, the cherry blossoms scattered.

—Yae Tenzen, the "Crimson Cherry," was scattered before the blue oni, fleetingly, fiercely.
