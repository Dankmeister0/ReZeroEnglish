Arc 1 Chapter 9: "How to Play a Trump Card"

—The negotiation was settled; Old Man Rom, cell phone in hand, gave his word on it. Except—

"All I can do for you is set up a place for you to negotiate with Felt. Past that, when it comes to trading this magic vessel for the badge, you're on your own, boy."

"I already know I'm gonna get fleeced going in. Even Old Man Rom won't vouch for what this thing's worth. So it oughta be a cakewalk."

At Subaru's easygoing manner, Rom scowled as if he had something to say. Probably some words of caution—don't let your guard down, don't underestimate negotiations, that sort of thing. But maybe he sensed that lecturing Subaru, whose face was already flushing red from the alcohol he'd downed, would be pointless, because—

"Well, in fact, that's about the size of it. If you were up against someone a bit nastier, they might squeeze you for the magic vessel and then some on top... but Felt's not that twisted at heart either."

"S'fiiine, s'fiiine. More importantly, Old Man Rom, you got anything better to eat? This is so bad I feel like I'm eating garbage."

"You go and help yourself to a man's supper, then have the nerve to complain. You're getting more shameless by the minute, you."

Shoveling something that looked vaguely like paste-colored beans off a shabby plate into his mouth, Subaru grumbled. His earlier revulsion toward the alcohol had gone somewhere entirely; now, taking full advantage of Rom refilling his emptied glass, he was sipping away at the liquor with the beans as a snack.

"I mean, seriously, a body that huge and this is all it takes to fill you up? It's not like you live on mist—don't tell me you're one of those types who says as long as there's booze, who needs food?"

"You don't know how efficient us Giants are with our fuel. For all our combat strength, we eat surprisingly little. Back when there was a lot more fighting, we made a name for ourselves in that regard all over."

Rom's expression turned grim in response to Subaru's naive question. He raised the wine bottle he'd been pouring from straight to his own mouth and tipped it back, drinking directly from the bottle.

"Which is exactly why our numbers dwindled so much. Even here in the royal capital, I haven't seen another Giant."

"Strong without eating much, huh, that's pretty impressive. Cool. ...Gonna puke."

"You've got some nerve, reacting like that when I'm baring my heart to you here."

There's nothing more deflating to listen to than someone bragging about their own misfortune. Subaru plugged his ears and cut the conversation short, and Rom, giving up on being heard, gave up and went back to eating his beans. The two of them fell into a silence, tipping back their drinks with the bad beans as their only company.

It was only when the sunlight had tilted well toward evening that a knock came at the door of the Loot House, in some peculiar code. Subaru, who'd begun to nod off, lifted his head as Rom's massive frame moved nimbly toward the door in response to the sound. The door looked absurdly small next to Rom's huge build. He pressed his ear to it and, with a solemn expression, called out through to the other side—

"To the great rats."

"Poison."

"To the skeletons."

"A pitfall."

"To our noble lord dragons."

"Damn them all."

The answers came back without a hair's breadth of hesitation after each short question. That must have been the signal and the password. Satisfied, Rom undid the door's lock. Watching his back, Subaru privately griped that it was a tasteless password to use. And then—

"—Sorry to keep you waiting, Old Man Rom. That mark was stubborn as hell. Took forever to shake them off completely."

Warmly, as if boasting of her own conquest, a girl slipped past Rom and into the storehouse. A girl with shoulder-length golden hair. Eyes red as a rabbit's, a mischievous fang peeking from the corner of her mouth. Her small frame was wrapped in clothes chosen for ease of movement—or, to put it less kindly, rags.

Brief as the encounter had been, there was no mistaking her—the girl he'd run into in the back alley. Recognizing her, Subaru instinctively shot to his feet. At the clatter of the sound, the girl noticed him staring and her smile vanished, replaced by a wary look.

"Ah? Who's this. Hey, Old Man Rom. I told you I was bringing back a big score, so keep everyone else out, didn't I?"

"I understand how you feel, but that lad's here on business with you, Felt. And, well, it's not entirely unrelated to you either."

At Rom's answer, the girl—Felt—scowled with even more suspicion. Something about the way her hand drifted to rest near her chest suggested that was where the stolen prize was kept. Under Subaru's silent stare, Felt turned back to Rom, uneasy.

"What's with that guy? ...Don't tell me you sold me out."

"I wouldn't do something that goes against my own code. Besides, I don't think it's a bad deal for you either."

Closing one eye, Rom sought agreement with a "right?" Having just experienced the rare horror of a winking old man, Subaru gave a small nod of affirmation. Then, choosing his words carefully for a still-wary Felt—

"For now, let's just calm down. I don't mean you any harm... blaaarghhhhhhhh."

—Choosing his words carefully, he lost the battle against a rising wave of nausea and it all came out of his mouth.

"WAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAH—!!"

Both of them screamed at the sudden, unsightly sight of Subaru puking. Subaru himself dropped to his knees on the spot and, wracked with a gut-wrenching agony, emptied the entire contents of his stomach. The amber of the liquor and the color of the paste beans mixed together to produce the worst possible creation.

"What do you mean it's not a bad deal for me! This is the worst!!"

"The worst is on my end! My dinner and my liquor, stolen and eaten, and now this! That liquor wasn't cheap! It was expensive, damn it!"

"Shut up! Don't yell! It's pounding in my head like crazy! I feel awful! I seriously feel—blaaarghhhhhhhh."

"WAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAH—!!"

As a second wave surged forward, Subaru's feeble spirit gave in once more, and the two screams trailed on and on. —The commotion went on tearing through the slum's evening dusk without end, until Subaru had emptied his stomach entirely, until Subaru had cleaned up the filth he'd produced, until the stagnant air Subaru had generated had been fully ventilated, and until Rom finally handed down a decree of "no more drinking" to Subaru. —Night fell.

"—Alright, let's get our heads back in the game and get to negotiating!"

Clapping his hands, Subaru declared, as if to shift the mood away from the awkward air hanging over the room. The setting had moved from the counter side of the Loot House to a small table set up in the back of the storehouse. The counter area still bore the lingering traces (smell and evidence) of the earlier commotion, and it was Felt who had stubbornly refused to negotiate anywhere near it.

At the same table, Felt tipped back a glass of milk Rom had poured her. The wariness hadn't faded from her red eyes—if anything, no small amount of distrust and displeasure had piled on top of it by now. Not the most promising start.

"No need for that scary face, c'mon, smile, smile. It's a waste of a cute face, y'know?"

"Don't get all buddy-buddy with me, mister. And I don't care about anything except whether the deal you're bringing turns into money or not. Skip the extra stuff and get to the point, yeah?"

He'd put a finger to his cheek to project his best attempt at friendliness, but Felt's response was cold and curt. Even in this world, Subaru's social skills were striking out completely. The "power of absolute loneliness," isolating him even across worlds.

Even though I've supposedly left everything behind, this one thing won't let go of me, huh. Heh, what a fate. Don't you think?

"Hey, Old Man Rom. This milk—you didn't water it down, did you? It's terrible."

"Every last one of you, calling something a person kindly served up 'terrible'..."

Ignoring Subaru off in his own world, Felt and Rom carried on, looking reasonably amused. Subaru cleared his throat and pulled both their gazes back onto himself.

"Right, well then—let's get to negotiating. Um, Felt. —You've got the badge?"

"...Yeah, I've got it."

To Subaru's straightforward opening cut, Felt answered just as bluntly in the affirmative. She reached into her clothes and drew something out, setting it quietly on the table.

—The badge Subaru had been searching for all this while. It was a badge whose distinctive design depicted a dragon. Its size was about that of a patch back in his old world. Its material was hard to place, but it looked to be made of some expensive-looking metal. From what Subaru could tell, it was a frontal design of a wyvern, and at the badge's center—the dragon's mouth—it depicted the dragon biting down on a red gem.

At the center of the badge, the red gem glowed faintly, softly, and at that light, Subaru momentarily lost his words. Rom too, appraising the badge, fell silent, only letting out a low, difficult "hrmm."

"Well then—"

It was Felt, gripping the badge, who broke the silence. Having confirmed that the preamble had brought the two of them back to their senses, she pushed the badge on the table toward Subaru.

"Now show me your card. This is how good the badge is, and on top of that, I went to some trouble getting it. It'd make us both happy if your card matches up to that, right?"

"Sorry to spoil that evil little grin you're testing me with, but the only card I've got to show is one. See, I happen to be a magnificently proud pauper!"

Everyone makes the same sour face the moment they hear "penniless," huh, Subaru thought. Setting that inner sentiment aside, he played, as promised, the strongest card he had. Felt's face clouded with confusion, just as he'd predicted, when she saw the cell phone slammed down on the table. Pleased by the reaction—the same as Rom's had been—Subaru activated the phone's camera function.

"NATSUKI FLASH!!"

"Whoa, that's bright!"

With a flash of light and a mechanical shutter click, an image was captured. Felt looked as if she wanted to complain about Subaru's rude, unmannered photography, but before her mouth could open, he thrust the phone's screen in front of her. At the sight of herself displayed there, the girl's red eyes went wide.

"This is..."

"That's right, it's Felt! This is a magic vessel that captures a moment in time and preserves the image. The only card I've got to play is this one. I'm proposing a trade—that badge for this."

Playing his biggest card right from the opening move, Subaru pressed the negotiation his way on momentum alone. The tactic he'd taken was one of the standard opening plays in negotiation—a powerful move that, depending on the situation, could decide the whole course of a negotiation on its own. Of course, it also amounted to an implicit declaration that he had no card more powerful than this—which, in effect, Subaru had just gone and blurted outright—

"I see, that's pretty impressive. So, Old Man Rom. If we sold this magic vessel, what would it go for?"

Peering at the screen, Felt gave an interested "hm-hm" nod, but her reaction after that was remarkably lukewarm. Her eyes didn't light up, nor did she reach to take the phone in hand. Her interest wasn't in what the thing could do—it was aimed solely at how expensive the phone itself might be.

Is a man's romantic dream something only a man can understand, even in another world!? Is that how it is!?

"Don't go squawking, it's unbecoming. Sit tight, you've got confidence in it, don't you? So, how's it look, Old Man Rom?"

Whether you'd call it a brazen attitude or oddly manly, Felt's composure never wavered. Looking at her—someone who seemed two or three years younger than himself—Subaru found himself half-tempted to lean in and call her "boss." Setting aside Subaru's own decidedly un-manly pocketbook, Felt asked Rom, standing beside her, about the phone's worth. Sliding a finger along the phone's edge with an air of utter indifference, she said—

"For me, if this magic vessel thing is worth more than this gem-studded badge, that's great news all around. I'm not doubting your eye for stuff on that count."

"Well, I can't guess exactly what it'd go for in hard numbers. I've never dealt in magic vessels myself. But still—that badge is quite a fine piece, and yet it falls short of the magic vessel. Bottom line, this deal is quite a lot in your favor. That's my conclusion."

"I see, I see. Well then, guess that's fine, huh."

Felt seemed pleased by Rom's stamp of approval. Though the reaction was a bit different from what he'd expected, Subaru was still satisfied that his broad goal seemed achievable. But just as he reached out his hand to seal the deal, Felt stopped him with a "hold on."

"The card-showing's over between us. But my asking price isn't settled yet, y'know?"

"...Hearing you announce you're gonna jack up the price yourself is kinda chilling. And besides, even if you say you'll squeeze more out of me, there's nothing left to squeeze. I mean, I'm the unrivaled champion of penniless."

"I'm not that much of a villain either. The old man's vouched for it this much—I'll admit this is worth more than the badge's value. —But it's a lie that you've got no more cards to play, isn't it?"

Standing, Felt looked down at Subaru still seated in his chair. Her red eyes were wet with something sadistic, and Subaru could do nothing but break out in a cold sweat at having his inner thoughts seen through so easily. As his negotiating card, he'd played the cell phone, which surely held the greatest effect. But Subaru still had a few more assets in reserve, things that probably weren't in circulation in this world. The coins in his wallet, various membership cards—depending on how the negotiation went, some of it could probably be converted into money somehow. If it came down to it, even his tracksuit and sneakers might be convertible in the sense of offering the technology behind them. The cell phone was, first and foremost, his biggest card, but he still had a few more cards up his sleeve. Though those were things that could serve as the initial capital supporting his life in this world going forward, so barring some extreme necessity, they weren't cards he could afford to play.

—Though if she said the cell phone wasn't enough, he'd have no choice but to play them.

"Relax, I told you, didn't I? I'm not planning on squeezing any more out of you than this. I'll be satisfied as long as this turns into money for me."

"W-w-well, whatever? Not nervous? Or anything? And, like, I seriously don't get what you mean? I've played the cards I've got to play, and stuff? No need to be scared or anythinnnng—blaaarghhh."

"Don't put more pressure on yourself than you need to and crack! Don't puke! I'll call the whole thing off!"

Under the pressure he'd put on himself, he nearly threw up again, but he forced the reversal that had already made it partway back up his throat back down into his stomach. Swallowing it down and pumping his fist, Subaru drew a look of pure disgust from Felt as she backed away.

"Lucky it didn't actually come out. If what you'd brought hadn't been a magic vessel, I'd have kicked you straight outside just now, for sure."

"If you'd tried that, I'd have knocked you flat first and been done with it."

The two of them laughed together, gruffly, in a downright manly way. Enduring the burn of stomach acid in his throat, Subaru let out an "urgh" as a preface and asked—

"So, what do you mean it's not settled yet?"

"Hm? Ah, simple thing. You're not my only negotiating partner here."

Subaru's face filled with question marks. As if answering that question, Felt raised a finger and said "see"—

"To begin with, the reason I lifted this badge in the first place was because someone asked me to. The deal was ten holy gold coins in exchange for this one thing."

"A theft request came first!? Ten gold coins—I don't really get the going rate, but..."

Glancing at Rom, he nodded as if reading Subaru's intent.

"For this badge, if I moved it smart, I could get four or five gold coins. Could get hammered down to three, too."

"So simply put, the buyback price is double."

"No, I said holy gold coins, didn't I? Unlike the gold coins circulating in the market, holy gold coins are made of rare holy gold, so in terms of value, it's close to twenty regular gold coins."

"Over four times as much!?"

"What are you so surprised about. That magic vessel you brought—at minimum, that's worth twenty holy gold coins. Depending on the buyer, some enthusiast might pay even more. It's not even a contest."

It was a world where the cost of living was hard to grasp, but having a currency above what he'd thought was the top-tier gold coin suddenly appear, and then twenty of those on top of it, was a shock in a word. He found himself, without meaning to, looking at his own phone—with fewer than ten contacts registered—as something like a divine artifact. It wasn't used much, so it still looked clean too. Good thing I didn't have any friends.

"Never mind comforting yourself over that. If this one's worth more, what are you gonna tell your theft client to turn them down?"

"That's why I said I'm gonna jack up the price."

Felt twisted her already cheeky expression into an even more wicked grin.

"You brought out something ridiculously expensive, mister. If they still want the badge, they might come back with a bigger reward on top, don't you think?"

"...In other words, that? If they come in offering more than twenty holy gold coins—"

"You're gonna have to play the rest of your cards and raise the stakes too, mister, or this deal's not gonna go through."

Correcting "wicked" to full-on villainous, Felt declared it with a bright and breezy tone. Sensing that things were taking a suspicious turn here, Subaru said—

"So, where and when is this client of yours supposed to meet up? I'm getting a seat at that negotiating table too, right?"

"Of course. If it's just you at a one-sided disadvantage, my profit might shrink too, y'know. And don't worry about the meeting place. —It's right here."

Tapping the edge of the table with a finger, Felt leaned back against her chair and looked up at Rom. Given an upside-down glance from below, Rom made a thoroughly displeased face and said—

"You, going and doing whatever you please without so much as asking me again..."

"Well, y'know, with Old Man Rom around, I can pretty much take violence off the table as an option for most people. Nobody's gonna wanna pick a fight with this guy, just looking at him."

Seeking agreement, Felt looked to Subaru too, who, after glancing at Rom, nodded along with an "uh-huh." A nearly seven-foot-tall, muscle-bound, bald old man. In fact, he had a track record of lifting Subaru—who weighed close to seventy kilograms—effortlessly with one hand, so the idea of it being all show seemed pretty unlikely. If it came down to a serious brawl, Subaru concluded, it wouldn't even be a contest.

Rom, meanwhile, didn't seem to mind the two of them sizing him up that way.

"Can't be helped, I suppose. Nothing gets done without me around, hm? Honestly, it's a sorry state of affairs. Want a refill of milk? There's a bit of something sweet too, if you'd like."

He'd turned into something like a foolish old grandfather doting shamelessly on his grandchild. Felt had her milk topped right up to the brim by a thoroughly good-humored Rom. Watching her, Subaru let out an exasperated sigh and said "but still"—

"The fact that you'd already called this person here from the start means you were fully planning on negotiating the price regardless of me, huh."

"'Course I was. You have any idea how much trouble I went through stealing this thing? And besides, what if some weakling like me got stiffed negotiating alone? Couldn't rest easy about that, could I?"

"Weakling, huh..."

Judging from Felt's slight, small frame alone, it wasn't exactly an inaccurate description. But having now tasted the sheer toughness and thick-skinned nerve of her spirit, calling her "weak" felt like quite a stretch. And thinking back further, in the course of the trouble she'd gone through stealing that badge, she'd once abandoned Subaru in a life-or-death crisis. Where exactly is she fragile in spirit, he thought, starting to feel irritated all over again.

"By the way, come to think of it, do you maybe not remember me at all?"

"Huh? Did we meet somewhere? I mean, unless it was a pretty memorable encounter, I'm not free enough to remember it. Besides, mister, you've got a pretty plain look about you to begin with."

"The only things that stand out are your hair color and your clothes," Felt cackled. There was no trace of a lie in her attitude, and combined with being dissed over the sheer blandness of his appearance, Subaru was left stunned. Maybe basic human decency has completely died out in this other world. The way she so breezily forgot the scene of an armed robbery-murder was proof of it.

On the other hand, there was Satella, who'd helped Subaru in a situation with nothing in it for her, and Rom, who hadn't thrown him out even after he'd vomited—so who could say. I guess there's individual variation even in another world too. Judging someone only by their bad points—really not good.

"Well, never mind Felt's sorry memory. So, when's this meeting partner of yours coming?"

"That's a real annoying way to put it. I told 'em I'd finish the job by sundown, and they said after sundown, here... Sun's already down, so it oughta be soon."

Maybe that "think they'll show up?" exchange had jinxed it. A sharp double knock suddenly echoed through the storehouse. The three of them exchanged glances, and Rom asked Felt, "The password?"

"Ah, didn't tell 'em one. It's probably my client, I'll go check."

Springing to her feet, Felt made her way toward the entrance. At her behavior—acting like she owned the place, at someone else's house, no less—Subaru shrugged toward Rom, silently asking, "is this okay?"

"Well, we're not exactly strangers. And it's been a fairly long relationship... guess I'll play a bit of bodyguard, then."

Somehow looking a little pleased, like a grandfather glad to be relied upon, Rom pulled a club out from the back of the storehouse. It was about the length of a kendo sword, made, probably, of wood. But spikes jutted out here and there from its tip, and it looked at a glance like something that would deal a fatal blow if you got hit with it.

"A spiked bat, or whatever you'd call it—guess even in another world, a club's standard equipment, huh..."

Being handed fifty gold and a wooden stick was pretty much the RPG standard. As he was sizing up Rom's equipment sideways, thinking it didn't betray expectations visually either, Felt came back with an oddly ingratiating smile.

"Yep, it was my client after all. This way, wanna sit?"

Gesturing implicitly for Subaru to move aside, she turned her charm toward the person behind her. So this is the negotiating partner, Subaru thought, tensing up somewhat as he sized up the newcomer—and was a little surprised. Because the person Felt had welcomed in was a strikingly beautiful woman.

A tall woman. About the same height as Subaru, and somewhere in her early twenties. Her face had an air of gentle, drooping eyes and beauty, and her sickly pale skin stood out clearly even in the dim storehouse. She wore a black cloak, but it hung open in front, revealing garments of the same color clinging tightly to the skin beneath. Slender, yet with curves in all the right places—a nice body. And, like Subaru, she was a rare possessor of black hair, said to be uncommon in this world. Her long hair, reaching past her back down to her waist, was bound as if braided, and she idly played with the end of it between her fingers.

She had a somewhat bewitching air about her, a grown woman. For Subaru, someone with no connection to this type and precious little experience to draw on besides, she was a rare kind of character. Put simply, he couldn't help but be thoroughly flustered.

Psychologically overwhelmed, Subaru found himself unconsciously ceding his seat to Felt. Felt sat down in the now-empty seat, with Rom, club in hand, standing to her left, and Subaru, unable to hide his nervousness, standing to her right. Despite receiving a rather imposing welcome, the woman didn't seem to mind, tilting her head slightly and saying—

"It does feel like there are rather a lot of outsiders here."

"Can't have you welshing on the deal. It's us weaklings' little wisdom. So, Subaru, drinks."

Ordered around with a gesture, Subaru found himself unable to even muster an objection, swept along by the mood of the moment. He picked out a relatively clean glass, poured milk into it, and set it before the two of them. The woman gave a small "thank you" to Subaru as he served her, then looked him over appraisingly.

"I understand the elderly gentleman over there, but who might this young man be?"

She must have picked up on his lack of ease from his bearing and manner. At the woman's genuinely curious question, Felt, apparently ready to get straight to the point, twisted her mouth into a wicked grin.

"This guy's your rival, mister. He's my other negotiating partner."

—And so, exactly as promised, she began jacking up the price.
