Arc 8 Chapter 52: "Medium O'Connell"

"...What clumsy tactics."

In the command room of the fortress city of Garkla, Serena Dracroy stood by the window, murmuring those words as she gazed at the one gap in the towering walls that protected the city.

There wasn't a soldier in the Vollachian Empire who didn't understand the importance of controlling the skies.

Precisely because of that, in this siege she'd fretted most over the allocation of anti-air forces. The Shudrak, formidable archers to a woman, had found no shortage of chances to prove themselves useful.

She'd set the bulk of them to watching the sky precisely because she was wary of the enemy's aerial forces.

And yet the sporadic attacks by undead Flying Dragons sent against them could hardly be called effective — for the Scorching Duchess, who commanded one of Vollachia's finest Flying Dragon Squadrons, it was almost maddening to watch the enemy fumble so.

"If I were the enemy commander, I'd have brought this city down far more efficiently by now."

"...Could you not say such frightening things in the middle of a decisive battle?"

Serena clicked her tongue at the enemy's poor deployment, and it was Otto — the mild-mannered young man likewise stationed in the command room — who overheard her muttering and cut in.

He was a man with more nerve than his soft looks suggested, and Serena thought highly enough of him — though, unfortunately, he had a habit of not wanting others to take risks.

"Keeping all the danger to himself... Fine trait in a subordinate. Dull trait in a husband."

"Being liked by Countess Dracroy always makes me feel like I'm being told there's something wrong with my character, so I've no objection to that assessment, but..."

"Hmph. I see. So you don't care for women with scarred faces."

"I hate to say this myself, but I don't change my attitude based on someone's looks. Whether we can actually talk to each other matters far more. And scar or no, I think you're beautiful, Senior Countess."

"What are you two even talkin' about? Get a grip, would ya."

Otto had answered Serena, arms crossed by the window, with a look of exasperation. And looking even more exasperated at that exchange was Anastasia, the envoy from Kararagi.

Beyond the exasperated Anastasia, Berstetz, entirely indifferent to all this, was busily exchanging words with messenger soldiers over a spread-out map.

Reports on the battle's progress had been streaming in nonstop, along with instructions barked out like furious shouts. Circumstances shifting by the second, blood and lives bursting apart — the fragrant smell of a battlefield.

"Yes, this is what the Empire ought to be. Under His Excellency's rule, one's nose for battle grows dull. To think even I had nearly forgotten that war lives right next door to everyday life."

"If that's what makes an empire, then the emperor tryin' his best to rule peacefully sure ain't gettin' his due."

"Bah, sour grapes. If I had complaints about His Excellency's rule, I should have taken it up by force. Since I haven't, my words are nothing but idle talk. However much his reign runs counter to imperialism, the only way to make it stick is through imperialism itself... It's almost tragic how thoroughly he embodies the Empire."

He himself would surely want no sympathy, but she couldn't help feeling it for the solitary path he walked.

It was likely true that Vincent disdained war, but if he had to crush the arguments of others to see his will carried out, then even that fell within the grasp of the Bloodlust Code.

Nothing precious is born except through clashing and seizing.

Whether or not Vollachia's Bloodlust Code actually preached that, Serena had interpreted the imperial way in those terms within herself, and she embraced that set of values. She knew loving what could only be born of such clashes was a fleeting, momentary sort of thing — but that was simply her nature.

And so—

"...So you didn't come after all, Balleroy."

Serena murmured it while staring out at the vast, desolate sky, the words carrying one last trace of lingering attachment.

The battle had long since begun, and both attacking and defending forces had already taken no small losses. The first spear and the vanguard had both already played out on this battlefield — meaning the Bullet Marksman, who believed in ending fights with the fewest possible casualties, was not flying this battlefield's sky.

Had he come, Serena's heart would surely have already been shot clean through.

"..."

Serena ran a hand along the vertical scar on the left side of her face and closed her eyes for a few seconds.

What rose behind her eyelids was the grubby little boy she'd taken in back in her younger days. That boy grown up, then losing his life, and finally reviving as one of the Undead to level a spear at her—

"That last part was just my imagination, I suppose."

Pale skin, lifeless golden eyes — Serena had never once actually laid eyes on Balleroy as one of the Undead.

He'd targeted Vincent at the Crystal Palace during the Battle for the Imperial Capital, and had made off with the soul-shattered Lamia at the Coupled Dragon Carriages. But on neither occasion had Serena managed to meet him face to face.

And now, here at this fortress city, Balleroy was absent too. That was reality.

"...You have every right to hate me, you know."

Murmuring that, Serena stood at the window picturing the undead Balleroy who hadn't shown himself. And standing there, she had to admit to a state of mind she hadn't expected of herself.

She had thought she wouldn't mind being killed, so long as it was by Balleroy's hand. That was why she'd deliberately exposed herself at the window, waiting for the Bullet Marksman's shot.

"You've gotten that out of your system by now, haven't ya?"

"..."

Serena looked back at Anastasia, who peered at her from beside her with one eye shut.

Anastasia's round, pale blue-green eyes studied her, and the look in them seemed to see straight through to what was in her heart — Otto included, merchants really were a shrewd lot.

That Flop and Medium had managed to survive in the cutthroat world of commerce must be down to some tremendous stroke of luck.

But the truth Anastasia had put her finger on ran too deep to be papered over with such useless musings.

"If the enemy's gonna go after the commanders first, the folks in this command room are the obvious targets. And you go and deliberately stand in the line of fire so they'll aim at you... Could ya at least run it by us first?"

"My apologies. I judged it would be easier to respond if it was clear who was being targeted, rather than leaving it uncertain which of us might be shot. The clever minds gathered here could surely handle matters calmly even if my head were to suddenly burst, couldn't they?"

"Even supposin' that's true, I think we oughta be lookin' for the path where nobody's head bursts, and neither me nor Ana are confident we could stay that calm about it. We ain't Otto-kun here."

"I'd freeze stiff too if someone suddenly died right in front of me, you know!?"

Otto raised his voice as though he'd been dealt an unfair evaluation, but Anastasia and the Spirit she brought with her — Echidna — exchanged glances and gave a shrug of their slender shoulders.

Serena, too, privately agreed with the one woman and the one Spirit — but in any case, her scheme had been foiled.

"With Balleroy absent and no undead Flying Dragon Squadron making an appearance, there's less reason to keep my own living squadron in reserve. Wherever the front turns unfavorable, I'll throw them in. We'll flip the tide in an instant."

"Fair enough. Feedin' in your forces piecemeal is a fool's play, but sittin' on your hardest-hittin' unit while you're bein' pushed back is just bad gambling. I say use 'em too. Only..."

"—The fact that the enemy seems to have no Flying Dragon Squadron of their own at all is a concern in its own right, isn't it."

Anastasia knit her thin brows in concern, and Otto picked up where her words left off. In response to that concern, Serena let out a small "hm" of a breath.

"The odds of an undead Flying Dragon Rider pairing up with a dead Flying Dragon aren't especially high... And yet, having none at all is unnatural too, no question. In that case—"

"—it's likely they're being put to some exceptional use, rather than deployed as a normal Flying Dragon Squadron would be."

"Exceptional... meaning—"

Just as they were about to press into the heart of the matter — a messenger came bursting into the command room with force enough to nearly break down the door.

Out of breath, his expression urgent, the messenger cried "Excuse me!" in a voice cracking under the strain. At that, Berstetz, who had been studying the map, looked up.

"Precisely, please."

"Sir! A target requiring urgent caution has appeared! Enemy Flying Dragon Squadron confirmed!"

"...So it's come."

Hearing the messenger's report, Serena hardened her expression at the arrival of what was bound to come. She kept her eyes fixed out the window, straining to make out the enemy in the sky.

But no enemy Flying Dragon Squadron was to be found within view. Behind the puzzled Serena, the messenger continued the report he'd brought.

It was this—

"—The enemy Flying Dragon Squadron came in over the great mountain behind the great fortress, crossing straight into the airspace above the fortress! They dropped enemy troops directly, and Undead have infiltrated the city!"

—Medium O'Connell is a bearer of the Divine Protection of Morale.

Put simply, the Divine Protection of Morale means that the more one's spirits and motivation rise, the more the body's physical capabilities leap upward in response.

Medium had no awareness of possessing this Divine Protection, and her older brother Flop had no idea his sister was a bearer of one either. Still, she had the sense that when she thought "I've gotta give it my all!" at the moment it counted, strength welled up in her, and that strength welled up too whenever her brother told her "Give it your all!"

So even without knowing the mechanism behind it, the way the siblings' habit of cheering each other on happened to be exactly the optimal solution was itself the rare secret behind the O'Connell siblings' strength.

The trouble with the Divine Protection of Morale, though, was precisely that one's abilities were tied to the ups and downs of one's mood — a double-edged sword where, just as rising spirits raised one's capabilities, sinking spirits meant those capabilities couldn't be brought to bear either.

Which made it, one could say, the ideal Divine Protection for someone as brightly optimistic as Medium — but here, the single saddest event of her life had come along, and it had backfired badly.

In the past, even Medium — who might as well have been the very embodiment of a positive outlook — had been brought low exactly three times.

The first was when Miles, one of her family, died.

The second was when Balleroy, likewise one of her family, died.

And the third was when she learned that Balleroy, who should have been dead, had become one of the Undead.

Both the first and second times, she'd cried and wailed her heart out, and it had taken her days to recover.

The shock of the third time was no less than either of the previous two, but tragically, there simply wasn't time this go-around to spend days crying it out.

So then, had Medium gone into the operation with her face swollen from tears, still sunk in gloom?

—Absolutely not.

Medium O'Connell had, properly and thoroughly, stopped crying.

It wasn't that the third sad event had hit her any less hard than the first or second. The shock — among the greatest of her entire life — had surely left her chest, her head, and no doubt her heart in tatters.

But Flop hadn't left Medium alone as she huddled with her knees drawn up.

"Little sister, I'm sorry for not telling you sooner. We're in a situation where the dead keep coming back to life one after another. I hadn't ruled out the possibility that Balleroy might return too."

After the Coupled Dragon Carriages arrived at the fortress city, Flop had made his way to the room Medium had shut herself up in and spoke to her of their encounter with the undead Balleroy.

Hearing her brother's words, Medium felt the usual "As expected of Big Bro!" welling up inside her, and yet, unlike always, she couldn't bring herself to say it aloud.

Beneath the urge to blame her brother for not telling her, she found herself hating — in a way she never usually did — her own stupidity, wondering why she hadn't noticed it herself.

Thinking things through had never been her strong suit to begin with. So she'd always left that job entirely to Flop.

And so she'd made her peace with the idea that raising a ruckus in his place was her own job, and that had been fine. And yet, being sad now about her own thick head — that was strange.

No. That wasn't it.

"I love Big Bro. A whole lot."

"Ah, thank you for something so lovely to hear. I love you just as much."

"I love Seri-nee too. And Miles-nii too."

"Of course. I've never once doubted that. Naturally, I love them both just as much myself."

"...I love Bal-nii too. So much."

"Mm."

Pressing her head against her knees and sniffling back her runny nose, Medium spoke haltingly. Flop knelt on one knee, listening with a gentle expression. She was grateful that, when she finally spoke of Balleroy at the end, he only gave a short nod in reply.

Grateful — but frustrating too.

"Even though I love him so much, I made myself not think about him. Someone I love that much."

The possibility that the dead Balleroy might come back as one of the Undead.

Flop had said he couldn't bring himself to voice it, but he had kept it in mind even while staying silent. Medium, on the other hand, hadn't even considered it.

That wasn't because Medium was stupid. It was because she'd looked away from it.

She loved her family so much, loved them so dearly, and yet she'd looked away and so hadn't noticed. —Even though she had already, once before, regretted that to the point of wanting to die.

"When Miles-nii died, and then after that, Bal-nii went and did that and ended up dying too — I was so, so frustrated with myself."

Back then, she thought, she should have stayed by his side no matter how much he hated it or tried to push her away.

She should have kept on making a racket at his side with her brother, loud and annoying enough that Balleroy could never have had the chance to hatch a plan to lunge at Abel.

Because she hadn't done that, she'd let Balleroy die.

Medium should have understood, though.

That after Miles died, Balleroy would drive himself to such extremes of thought.

Because Balleroy was—

"—Medium, I can't go to the imperial capital. My injuries haven't fully healed, and on top of that, I have no strength to fight with. I'd certainly just be a burden. But you're different."

"Big Bro..."

"I think Balleroy is in the imperial capital. With us pushed back this far, our one shot at turning things around is defeating whatever leader they've got there. And if they can predict His Excellency the Emperor will head there too..."

"Yeah. Right."

She didn't need him to say it all outright — she understood what Flop meant.

When it came to fighting, Balleroy always chose whatever method settled things fastest. That was the bedrock of his character, unchanged whether he was a soldier or one of the Nine Divine Generals.

Balleroy would surely go after Abel. Which meant he was in the imperial capital.

Which meant, if she went to the imperial capital, she could see him.

"Looks like you're picking back up a little, doesn't it?"

"...As expected of Big Bro. You get me."

"Ha ha ha, I've been Medium's big brother for a long time now! Now then — since this brother can't make it to the capital himself, let's see what he can still do for his little sister!"

"Do what?"

Rubbing her face vigorously with her hands, Medium tilted her head, eyes still red. At the sight of his sister, Flop laughed, hands on his hips.

"Well, even if you say you want to go, there's no telling whether His Excellency the Emperor will agree. So let's put together a proper plan to talk him into it, shall we? No need to worry. I already know His Excellency's weak point."

"Whoa, amazing! As expected of Big Bro! So what's Abel-chin's weak point?"

"—Simply put: love."

Holding up a single finger, Flop declared it with total confidence.

Had anyone other than Medium heard that, they might not have understood what he meant at all, tilting their heads or pulling odd faces.

But whatever Flop told her, Medium's face would break into a grin as she answered.

"As expected of Big Bro! I can always count on you!"

—And so, Medium O'Connell earned the right to take part in the final battle for the imperial capital.

Looking back on it, the way one coincidence after another had piled up to bring her here felt like nothing short of a miracle.

It had all started at the walled city of Guaral. This strange chain of events had begun the moment she spotted Subaru, Rem, and Spica trying to enter that town.

"Thanks, Subaru-chin. For meeting me and Big Bro back then."

Inside a Dragon Carriage on the way to the imperial capital, Subaru had looked startled when Medium thanked him like that.

Subaru, small as ever, had been showing nothing but a profile of fierce concentration as he thought hard about something — but the moment she said that, his eyes went round as saucers.

But right away, Subaru softened into the same gentle expression he wore for Rem and Spica.

"What are you talking about, Medium-san. If anyone should say thanks, it's me. It gives me chills just thinking about what would've happened if I hadn't run into you and Flop-san back there."

Saying that with a bashful smile, he'd gone and arranged the chance for Medium to have her selfish way.

A reckoning with Balleroy Temeglyph, the Bullet Marksman turned Undead. —And for that, he'd even arranged for an outrageously reliable helper besides.

"Thanks, Roz-chin. For helping me out."

Feeling the wind streaming through her hair, Medium spoke her thanks high up in the sky.

The one she thanked was the figure carrying her cradled in his arms through the air — Roswaal, a companion of Subaru's and Emilia's, and the man now lending his strength to Medium's fight.

At Medium's thanks, he broke into a smile and said, "No-o-o, not at all,"

"If anything, I'm grateful to have been allowed to come alo-o-ong. In truth, the opponent you wish to face is a formidable one indeed. I'd like to hold as many cards in hand as possible."

"—? I'm not a card, I'm a girl, y'know?"

"Ah, my mistake. You're the Lady Emilia ty-y-ype, aren't you."

Roswaal gave a small wry laugh as he said that, and a question mark popped up over Medium's head.

Perhaps he'd meant something like "you're lighter than you look." In truth, Medium, tall for a woman, barely came up short of even the tall Roswaal in height. And yet he was holding her aloft as though she weighed nothing at all.

Though it seemed Roswaal's true talent lay not in brute strength but in his skill with Magic. The fact that he was flying through the sky right now with Medium in his arms proved as much.

While with Serena, Medium had flown through the air many times, but always astride a Flying Dragon. Being carried aloft in someone's arms was an entirely new experience.

But—

"The trick to flying is trusting whoever's carrying you and just leaving everything up to them!"

The lesson she'd once learned from a trusted Flying Dragon Rider was what steadied Medium in this moment.

Whether her partner was a Flying Dragon or Roswaal, the way to gain wings was the same: to trust.

"That nerve and that knack — I'd love to pass them along to Otto-kun and Petra-kun, who still ha-a-aven't gotten used to flying."

"Really? Well then, Roz-chin, why don't you just get along better with them?"

"A-ha ha ha, now that stings a bi-i-it."

Roswaal's smile deepened at the blunt remark, and he shook his head slowly.

Seeing that, Medium sensed Roswaal wasn't laughing because he genuinely found it funny, but because he was trying to laugh it off. He probably just wasn't good at getting close to people.

Medium had recently learned that there were people like that. Abel was the same way.

She didn't understand why anyone would deliberately try to keep their distance from others like that — no, actually, she understood it a little now. It was so that they wouldn't get hurt themselves.

"But if you keep doing that forever, you end up regretting it, y'know."

"..."

"So I'm gonna show both Roz-chin and Abel-chin how it's done."

The moment Medium said that, a tremendous roar echoed out in the distance beyond the two of them as they flew.

It came from the east, where thick clouds were gathering one after another, and there, terrifying enough to make the whole world tremble, was the form of the Dragon.

As arranged beforehand, it was Garfiel who would fight the Dragon.

And while Garfiel held the Dragon in check, the others would move as well.

One such pair was Medium and Roswaal.

"..."

She drew in a deep breath, and let it out again.

Once it began, there'd be no turning back. But that was fine.

"Roz-chin."

"Let's begin. Now, there's no need to be nervous. —After all, I'm the strongest mage in the wo-o-orld."

The instant Roswaal said that, Medium felt gooseflesh rise across her whole body for a split second.

Unbeknownst to her, it was the very moment Roswaal unleashed Magic so far beyond her imagining, hurling flame above the thick clouds blanketing the imperial capital, taking aim at the Crystal Palace from directly overhead.

Even while handling such supremely advanced sorcery, Roswaal was also maintaining the flight Magic that carried Medium in his arms.

It was a feat as convoluted as painting a picture with his right hand while composing music with his left, all while singing verse aloud and using his eyes and mind to decipher some unknown language.

But Medium, unable to grasp the value of any of that, had no framework for praising it as amazing.

And so, rather than praising him with words, Medium showed it through action.

"..."

Staring intently at the distant sky, she watched brilliant red bursts of flame chain together directly above the Crystal Palace deep within the imperial capital — and cutting straight through the crimson fireballs blooming one after another, a Flying Dragon soared up into the high sky.

Far, far away, kilometers distant, small as a bean — and yet to Medium now, it was unmistakably the one she wanted to see, wanted to talk to, wanted to touch.

—Medium O'Connell is a bearer of the Divine Protection of Morale.

The Divine Protection of Morale, whose power rose the higher one's spirits climbed, was the ideal Divine Protection for someone as brightly optimistic as Medium.

And now, before the one she'd longed to see more than anything, that Divine Protection's power was producing the greatest, highest effect it ever had in Medium's entire life.

What she wanted to do and what she had to do had become one and the same. Shattering her past regrets, clutching to her chest what her brother had entrusted to her, she would face that beloved face together with a reliable ally at her side.

And so, from far, far away, Medium spoke to the Balleroy who surely saw her now.

"I'm not letting you run off on your own anymore. Talk to me properly, Bal-nii."

—The next instant, a beat late, she struck down an incoming bolt of light with her machete, drawn in a flash.

"..."

The shock tore through her whole body, yet Medium and Roswaal, holding her in his arms, came through unscathed.

And so, across an extraordinary distance, the fiercest aerial battle in the Empire's history opened its curtain.
