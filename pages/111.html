Arc 3 Chapter 34: "Outside of Madness"

—Waking began with the darkness torn open, with the pain of sunlight searing his eyelids.

"—-chan?"

Sensation that should have vanished from his body — warm blood coursing again through the tips of his fingers and toes, the feel of a lower half that should have been shattered to pieces now standing firm on the ground. The phenomenon of every lost function returning all at once in the space of a single blink drove every scrap of information mercilessly into a brain that had only just switched back on, enough to make his head spin, literally.

Before he knew it, the world that had been ruled only by the ringing in his ears was thrown into disarray by the noise of human life. People came and went along the dusty thoroughfare, weaving smoothly around his body where it stood frozen in place as if he were in the way, tossing him brief, annoyed glances that struck oddly deep into his frayed heart.

"Hey, hey, you! You listening?"

A rough voice, laced with a click of the tongue, came from close by, and he swayed his eyes toward it. Standing right in front of him was a tough-looking man, scowling, a vertical scar running down his face. A head taller than him, the man traced a finger lightly along the white scar,

"C'mon, gimme a break, bro. Don't go spacing out on me."

"Eh, ah?"

"The hell kind of vacant answer is that. Eh, whatever. More importantly, what're you gonna do?"

The man let out a breath at receiving nothing but a hoarse mumble in reply, and pressed for an answer all the same. In his hand sat a small red fruit, perched neatly in his broad palm — a combination hideously mismatched with the man's appearance, something that undermined the very sense of reality. He stared at it blankly, saying nothing. A serious flaw had opened up in his ability to grasp the situation. But the tough-looking man had no reason to make allowances for that,

"Cut the crap already, would you? I'm just asking if you're buying this Appa or not. Answer me straight."

Leaning forward, the man reached across the counter separating them and grabbed him by the collar. Yanked roughly forward, his body pitched into the shelf behind. The force rattled the shelf lined with merchandise, and at the sight of goods threatening to fall, the man panicked,

"Wha— what're you doing! Stand up straight. Going all wobbly on me, legs giving out like that..."

"Leg... leg?"

"You've got two of 'em, standing proud below your waist. You seeing some hallucination where your legs are gone or something?"

The man put a finger to his own head and twirled it, then pointed that same finger down at his lower body. Following the gesture, he lowered his eyes to find his own legs trembling in fine little shudders. Swaying with terrible unsteadiness, they couldn't support his weight, and it was only by leaning his upper body against the counter that he was managing to stay upright at all.

"Look, please just cut the tomfoolery there. I've already got enough on my plate dealing with all sorts of unbelievable stuff on a normal day as it is."

At the sight of him slumped listlessly against the counter, the man shook his head, exasperated. Carrying on like this out in the open street was bound to have a bad effect on the people watching from a distance. But though the man's remark was made with that in mind, his body didn't react to it in the slightest.

He couldn't perceive reality as reality. There was a hazy, blurred sensation, a strange sense of alienation as if a thin film had been stretched over everything, and every piece of information simply passed through his brain and spilled away.

What was he doing. What had happened. He felt like something had happened, but what was it.

What, what, what—

His rambling thoughts lost their exit, circling endlessly through the narrow labyrinth of his skull, grinding his mind down without end. If he had just kept wearing away like that, or worn himself out entirely and vanished, perhaps he'd have found some relief. But that feeling of solitary dissolution—

"—Subaru-kun?"

—was shoved aside in an instant by the sound of a girl's voice suddenly trembling against his eardrums.

"————"

Without a sound, not so much snapping up as slowly, yet rigidly, he raised his head, eyes wide. Beyond the counter, behind the tough man's massive frame blocking the way, stood a figure rearranging onto the shelf the goods that had been knocked askew in the collision.

An apron dress in black as its base, with a white apron and a white brim cap. Moving her small, slender frame busily about, she noticed his gaze and turned, her lovely face carrying no particular expression. Her blue hair, cut to shoulder length, swayed with the motion, only sharpening the cool, composed impression she gave off.

Tears came.

"—Hey?"

"Subaru-kun?"

A sob escaped him, his vision blurring. Terrified that the girl who had certainly been reflected there would grow indistinct, he rubbed at both his eyes, desperately, roughly. And yet the girl only grew more distant, and the murmur of the crowd swelled louder.

Before he knew it, his body had lost the counter's support and collapsed onto the street. No strength or will reached the tips of his feet, and lying on his side in the road, he wept, his breath coming in ragged, convulsive gasps. No — that wasn't breathing.

"Fuheh... hihi, haha. Hehi, hihahaha..."

It was laughter.

The murmuring spread wider, and he could feel the number of eyes turned toward him swelling by the moment. Someone was looking at him. Recognizing him. He wasn't alone. He wasn't isolated. Just knowing that was enough to affirm him, even lying here on the ground like a discarded rag.

"What's wrong, Subaru-kun? Are you all right? Please, hold on..."

Too impatient to go around, the girl vaulted the counter and landed beside him. He felt her move to lift his fallen body up, and—

"Eh?"

he instead threw his arms around her undefended, approaching body with all his strength, pulling her into an embrace. The girl received it, stunned. Her breath brushed close against him in that instant, warm and, to his mind, achingly pleasant, and he held her tighter still, burying his nose against her shoulder.

"Wh— eh, Subaru-kun? Um..."

She was saying something. Every word, every syllable, every single letter spun from those lips — the mere fact of it felt like a gospel delivered to him anew each passing second. He held on tight, refusing to let his arms loosen. The girl, shifting faintly, quietly accepted the embrace as well, and made no move to shake him off.

Feeling that warm body, that pulse of life, the presence of another person as close as could possibly be,

"Hiha... uhiha, hihihihi."

Natsuki Subaru — the madman — just kept quietly laughing.

"Honestly, at this point I think I've just gotta throw up my paws, nya..."

Seated in a leather chair, a finger pressed to his cheek, Ferris declared this with certainty. Twitching his cat ears, his chestnut hair swaying, the beautiful figure looked over at Subaru lying on the bed, then turned a sympathetic gaze toward the girl standing at its side,

"The only thing Feri-chan can actually do something about is injuries to the body, y'know. Whether it's on the outside or the inside, I can manage to fix the body just fine... but there's nothing to be done for the heart, nya."

"...No, thank you for all your efforts."

Rem bowed at the waist and offered thanks to Ferris, who apologized for his lack of power. But her voice, oddly flat, had lost all its emotion. Unlike the composure she normally cultivated with deliberate effort, this blankness arose because the turmoil within her was simply too great.

As though looking at something too painful to bear, Ferris averted his eyes from Rem. Still bowed low, Rem didn't notice his reaction, and gently tilting her head, turned her attention to the person lying on the bed. The one stretched out on the bed, being cared for by the two of them, was the black-haired boy — Subaru.

Even bathed in their gazes, his body showed no response to those directing their attention at him. He wasn't asleep. His eyes were open wide, staring intently, earnestly, straight up at the ceiling above him. Every so often, as though suddenly remembering something, a strained smile would flicker across his face, and once it passed, he might just as suddenly burst into tears. His unstable state showed no sign of ending.

Rem didn't know the cause. Right up until this morning — no, right up through the afternoon, while they'd been wandering the lower district of the royal capital — he had been unchanged. There had been some strain to his manner, but he had made an effort to act as he normally did, and by responding to him just as normally, Rem should have been helping to build up their ordinary day together. And yet his heart had shattered all too suddenly.

It hadn't seemed as though there had been any trigger. At the moment Subaru's manner changed so drastically, Rem regretfully had not been right at his side. Still, while helping with the shop, she should have caught the conversation between him and the shopkeeper. He had been proposing to the shopkeeper that he buy an Appa to bring back to Crusch's mansion, and the shopkeeper, never imagining it would end up brought into the household of a great noble family, had been about to casually agree to the deal. It was in the middle of that negotiation that Subaru's heart was suddenly shattered.

Subaru had collapsed there on Merchant Street, weeping while gazing at her, happily, sorrowfully, letting out a distorted laughter that sounded like his lungs were convulsing. Excusing themselves from the scene, and knowing full well the imposition, Rem had carried him to Crusch's mansion, insisting Ferris examine him on the chance it might be the result of some magical interference. The results were not encouraging. If this was a problem beyond even Ferris's reach, then it was a difficulty that no gathering of even the royal capital's greatest mages could hope to heal.

Magic had nothing to do with his current condition. His heart had simply, suddenly, lost its balance.

"I don't wanna say something like this, nya, but — what are you going to do?"

"Without knowing the cause, there's no way to address it... I'm sorry for the trouble this has caused you, Felix-sama."

"Mm-mn, that part's fine, really. Actually, now that he's stopped kicking up a fuss, it's almost convenient for Feri-chan's treatment, wouldn't you say, nya?"

Looking down at Subaru, who lay there nearly unresponsive, as though bedridden, Ferris went on with a "but, but—"

"I'm just wondering if it's even worth continuing the treatment, nya."

"...What do you mean by that?"

Raising her head, tearing her gaze from Subaru's blank expression, Rem finally looked at Ferris. Meeting her eyes, Ferris prefaced his words with, "I don't want you to get mad, nya, but—"

"Treating Subaru-kyun's Gate is meant to keep him from having trouble in his everyday life from here on, right?"

"Yes."

"But he can't lead any kind of proper everyday life anymore, so what's the point of just fixing his body, nya~?"

"—! Subaru-kun is—!"

"You're gonna say he's not finished? Looking at him like this? Seriously? Sure, something happened, that much's true, nya, but someone whose heart breaks this badly over that much — I don't think they'll pull through no matter how they recover, y'know~"

Faced with Rem on the verge of flaring up, Ferris still refused to drop his skeptical attitude. In the eyes he turned down at Subaru, there was a clear, unmistakable trace of contempt. For a man granted the single character 'Blue,' known as one of Lugunica's foremost water mages, it seemed a far too coldhearted attitude to take.

At the sight of Rem falling silent under his attitude, Ferris gave a wry smile, shook his head, and said, "I don't want you to get the wrong idea, nya, but..."

"It's not like Feri-chan's saying this because I find Subaru-kyun hateful or particularly dislike him, nya."

"But..."

"It's not about Subaru-kyun as a person, y'know? Feri-chan just plain dislikes people who lack the 'will to live,' nya."

Cutting off Rem, who had pursed her lips to object, Ferris pointed at Subaru,

"All Feri-chan's magic is really good for is healing wounds and the like. But even so, I keep these hands of mine plenty busy, using them on all sorts of people. Everyone's desperate to live, so what's wrong with helping out with that, nya? I don't hate being thanked for it, and it lets me build up favors with important people, and helps Crusch-sama too, doesn't it?"

"————"

"But using my power to heal the body of someone who isn't even trying to live — that I don't like. A person like that, even with a healed body, is just going to waste that life afterward anyway, right? So no, it's not over. Mm-mn, it already is."

Ferris said this curtly and turned his face pointedly away. Behind that stubborn attitude, Rem could clearly sense the earnestness Ferris must have developed toward however many lives he had watched. Though his manner of speaking affected a certain flippancy, it was a view of life and death he had built up, established within himself, learned from all the lives and deaths he had stared down before. And against someone who held so firm a conviction, there was no point in an outsider like herself objecting with mere sentiment.

Rem only looked at Subaru, frustrated, as though beaten down by Ferris's words. Subaru, oblivious even to being the center of the conversation, was now letting out faint, broken bursts of laughter — the kind that left scratches on the hearts of anyone listening.

At the sight of him with his heart shattered, Rem felt an unbearable emotion echo within her chest. But she managed, somehow, to restrain herself from showing it. To do so would have wounded Subaru's honor, or perhaps Roswaal's authority, and above all, it would have betrayed the very feelings Rem herself had carried all this while.

"—Ferris's opinion is, perhaps, a touch too harsh."

The voice rang out abruptly, resonant, into the room where an awkward silence had settled. Sunk deep in her thoughts, Rem hadn't noticed the visitor's arrival and jerked her head up as though struck. Ferris, by contrast, who had already noticed the knock announcing the entrance, wore a composed expression. Or rather — the eyes he turned toward the visitor were those of a devoted retainer, quietly burning with warmth.

"Crusch-sama."

"I won't go so far as to say weakness itself is a sin. Though I would agree that accepting weakness as it is, refusing to correct it, and settling comfortably into the status quo — that much is a sin."

Crusch, entering the room, held out a palm toward Rem, who had scrambled to her feet, and moved to the bedside, her long green hair swaying. Then she looked down at Subaru's face, still twisted into that dreadful laugh,

"I see. This is indeed a grave situation. Do we know the cause?"

At Crusch's question, Ferris shrugged with a "No~" and threw up his hands in surrender,

"From what Rem-chan says, he just collapsed all of a sudden, and I've examined every corner of his body, but there's no sign of any strange interference on the mana side either, nya."

"What of the influence of something like the northern... Curse Arts? It's a hard thing to credit, but there's a possibility of interference from the Gusteko side against those connected to the Royal Selection. Or perhaps it's a show of force from another camp."

"Both seem hard to credit, nya~. The timing's all wrong for making a move, and besides, who'd even gain anything from targeting Subaru-kyun? Anyone connected to this already knows well enough what a mess he usually is, and anyway, there's no trace of any magical interference at all, Curse Arts included."

"Or. Per. Haps," Ferris said, drawing the syllables out, tilting his head, then leaned in gently beside Crusch, who stood with arms folded,

"Crusch-sama, are you doubting Feri-chan's abilities?"

"Never. There's no possibility of me doubting your ability, your character, your loyalty. Even if you drove a dagger into me from the front, that would never change."

"Oh stop, Crusch-sama, that's an unbelievable line to drop on me... Honestly, my knees have gone weak."

Setting aside Ferris, who was writhing about bashfully, Crusch turned her gaze back to Rem. Rem, who had watched the exchange between lord and retainer in silence, straightened under that sharp gaze and faced her properly.

"This is what Ferris says. And if Ferris cannot help him, then there's no one else in this house able to treat Natsuki Subaru. I'm sorry we couldn't do more."

"—No, it's I who has no words for how generously we've been treated."

In response to the mistress of the house's apology, Rem answered with a courteous bow. In truth, no amount of words, no amount of gratitude, could fully repay the kindness they had received.

She didn't believe Ferris had held back, nor that Crusch had made an arbitrary judgment against someone connected to Emilia, a political rival. Rem herself had at least some connection to water magic, and she understood well enough both the height of Ferris's skill and the fact that it simply had no bearing on what had happened to Subaru. As for Crusch's character, even from just a few days' acquaintance, there was no doubting she was a sincere and upright person. She had the ability, the sense of responsibility, and the bearing of a great noble to have inherited a duchy so young, and as a woman at that. Having been given the chance to speak with her at length, more than once, made that all the more clear.

In other words, there was not the slightest fault on their part. This present state of affairs was one that had come about as it inevitably must, a situation beyond anyone's help—

"—The Witch."

and Rem alone understood clearly that the cause lay in the stench surrounding Subaru's body, which had only grown stronger still.

The Witch's presence enveloping Subaru's entire body — the Miasma — had grown yet denser still.

Whether this was the direct cause of Subaru's collapse or not, she couldn't say, but it was a fact that the presence had swelled just before he fell.

No one else could notice that stench, which only Rem could perceive. For that reason, she couldn't blame them for concluding there was nothing to be done, and blaming others while she herself, unable to offer any fundamental solution, stood by — that was a conclusion her own nature would never have arrived at anyway.

Being able to sense the Witch's Miasma was one of the vanishingly few points on which Rem surpassed her sister. Even before Ram lost her horn, Rem thought she'd had a nose sharp enough in its own right. She was confident in her ability to distinguish the state of mana filling the air, and even in tracking prey by scent through the mountains, she was a match for her sister. But the ability to catch the Witch's presence by scent — that was something newly acquired, gained on the very night her whole clan had been slaughtered.

She didn't know the cause. Her own guess was that it might have been some mutation her body underwent, placed in that extreme state, so as never to forget what happened that night. And Rem had never told anyone else about this strange sense of smell. She had simply come to pay unusually strong wariness toward anyone whose body gave off that stench.

A physiological revulsion, a preconception, was always at work. Though that prejudiced way of thinking had, in the end, been swept away entirely by the very boy who carried the strongest trace of the Witch's Miasma she had ever encountered.

And yet. And yet, still.

That whatever this Miasma brought with it was never anything good — — that much, the oni understood, clearly, and without doubt.

"—Thank you for everything. On my master's behalf, I offer my gratitude for your kindness up to this day."

Rem bent at the waist, bowing deeply. Standing before her were Crusch and Ferris, the two of them, and the three of them had gathered in the entrance hall of Crusch's mansion — in other words, this was their farewell.

"I'm sorry we couldn't be of help. By rights, it would be presumptuous of us to accept payment for this at all."

"No. It's our own circumstances that are cutting the arrangement short. Crusch-sama, you extended us the utmost consideration. The payment is certainly, rightfully, owed."

To Crusch, who lowered her eyes slightly, Rem answered so, lifting her chin resolutely. At that, Crusch spoke the words "I'm sorry" once more, and said nothing further beyond it. She, too, must have understood that anything more would only turn into empty formality.

"It feels kinda unfinished, nya, but I guess it can't be helped. Take care of yourself, Rem-chan. As for Subaru-kyun... should I say 'get well soon,' nya?"

And so it was Ferris who next spoke up, on his lady's behalf. He raised a finger and closed one eye, gesturing behind Rem — where Subaru stood, leaning his back against the door, slumped in a slack, sloppy posture. His reactions were still as dull as ever, and it was doubtful whether he was even aware of them, but he'd come along now if led by the hand, and had at least managed to stand without collapsing. It was only the sudden fits of laughing and crying that remained, stubbornly, beyond fixing.

"For the rudeness of one of our own household, no amount of apology on our part could ever be enough. For your generous treatment of him, my heartfelt thanks."

"He is a man I share a Contract with, and one with whom I've exchanged no small number of words. There's no way I could treat him carelessly. Though I imagine, for you, the difficult part is only just beginning."

"That is... something I've already resolved myself to."

Glancing sidelong at Subaru, still swaying unsteadily on his feet, that faint smile on his lips, Rem clenched the hem of her apron tight and declared her resolve. She had never forgotten the words he'd once spoken to her. Again and again, dozens of times, hundreds of times, she had replayed that moment over and over in her mind. And so she had to give back to him the same, in equal measure, as what she had once been given.

"It's regrettable that it seems I won't be able to answer your proposal, after all."

That was what Crusch, her eyes closed, said to Rem, who had sunk deep in thought. Though the words carried no subject, Rem understood they referred to the private matter that had passed between her and Crusch. Rem drew her chin in, and then, to Crusch as she lowered her eyes, shook her head,

"It's entirely our own lack of ability. — Though it's come to a regrettable end, I pray for your continued success from here on, Crusch-sama."

"You as well — please tell Emilia for me. Let's each fight a battle we won't be ashamed of, before our Souls."

With just that exchange, Rem knew clearly that her role in this place had come to an end. Subaru's treatment, cut short halfway through, and the mission Roswaal had entrusted her with, too. Neither could go on with Subaru in this state. In a place that was, potentially, enemy territory, she couldn't keep dragging along a defenseless Subaru forever.

"Going back to the mansion's fine and all, but do you have a plan?"

"At the very least, if I could just meet with Emilia-sama..."

Still swallowing her frustration, Rem answered Ferris's question so.

No matter how many times she called out to him, no matter how many times she touched him, no matter how devotedly she tended to him, Subaru never gave Rem back the response so like his usual self. Even in that state, Subaru would sometimes utter words that carried some meaning. Such as—

"Names..."

"Hm~?"

"Every now and then, he speaks names. Rem's name, and Sister's. And also..."

While she felt glad her own name was among them, it was also sorrowful, the fact that he never responded at all to her own efforts. Still, of all the names muttered, the one that came up most often was—

"If he could meet with Emilia-sama, perhaps something would change."

"But didn't they part on pretty bad terms? And it's only been what, three days, nya? Wouldn't it be better to give it some time... ah, but I guess that's not an option, is it."

"I know it's not exactly a commendable approach. But this isn't a state Rem can manage on her own judgment anymore. I need to go back, if only to receive instructions."

By speaking, with all her effort, in terms of concern for her master, Rem disguised her true feelings. Using that as her pretext, she understood full well what her heart truly longed for underneath.

And the fact that her own presence alone wasn't enough for it — that was what frustrated her most.

"—Wilhelm's here."

Crusch, lifting her head, narrowed her eyes. Following her gaze, Rem turned to look behind her as well — and there, at the edge of the estate, beyond the iron gate, a single Dragon Carriage had arrived, a familiar old gentleman seated in the driver's seat.

"That's the last of our long-distance Dragon Carriages. We very nearly sent it out on some other errand."

"That's lucky, nya. Push straight through the Lifaus Highway with this, and you should reach the mansion before the date changes, more or less."

Watching the carriage that had arrived, Rem narrowed her eyes against the brightness of the sun climbing higher overhead. It was nearly noon, and if the carriage ran at full speed from here, it would indeed reach the mansion in about half a day. Once close enough to arrival, her empathic ability might let her convey some measure of her intent to her sister.

"Be well."

"Good luck, nya~"

Seen off, Rem bowed her head deeply one last time, then took Subaru's hand and led him away from the Karsten mansion. At the gate, she took the reins from Wilhelm, exchanged a word or two with him, and climbed up into the driver's seat,

"Subaru-kun, over here."

"...Uh, ah?"

Pulling him by the arm, she sat Subaru down beside her on the driver's seat. With the two of them seated together it felt quite cramped, but pressed close like that, she wrapped one arm around his waist, and with her other hand took a firm grip on the reins.

From here, they would have to keep going for a long, long stretch of time, just like this. She worried about the strain it would put on Subaru, and even after reaching the mansion, she would have to protect him still. Surely Roswaal and the others wouldn't welcome Subaru back with open arms.

For Subaru, who might well end up with no one else on his side, she alone had to remain his ally. Otherwise, he would — otherwise, he would—

—Her resolve settled deep within her, Rem pulled the reins, and the Earth Dragon kicked off the ground and broke into a run.

Slowly, the turning of the wheels quickened by degrees. It felt, somehow, as though it foreshadowed the very state of Rem's heart just then — that was the sensation the reins conveyed to her.
