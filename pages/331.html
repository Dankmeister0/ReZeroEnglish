Arc 5 Chapter 15: "The Noisy Silence"

The next morning, Subaru woke feeling refreshed and found himself standing in a garden bathed in the morning sun.

Feeling the gravel through the soles of his shoes, Subaru drew the crisp morning air into his lungs. "Mmm!" he exclaimed with satisfaction, and beside him Emilia gave a small laugh.

"What? Subaru, you seem really in a good mood this morning. Did something good happen?"

"Well, there's the fact that Emilia-tan's braid-waves are cute first thing in the morning, and something kind of nice happened last night before I went to sleep. Plus I liked how huge the bath was."

"Oh, me too, me too. The bath was so nice, wasn't it? The manor's bath is big too, but having it lined with stone like that felt fresh and different."

Emilia had undone the braid Subaru had put in the night before, and now her silver hair, flowing in the breeze, formed the gentle waves he'd been aiming for. Straight and beautiful was one of the charms of Emilia's silver hair, but drawing out a different charm than usual like this was also a privilege of being a beautiful girl. In the end, the conclusion was simply that a cute girl was cute no matter what she did.

Thinking that to himself, Subaru's mind returned agreement to Emilia's words. The large bath at the inn she was describing hadn't technically been an open-air bath, but it had been brimming with the ambition to recreate one. The bathing area was indoors, but as many boundaries as possible had been removed to bring in a sense of nature, and the tub itself had been lined entirely with stone. The special atmosphere of a rocky bath rim was something the tastelessness of, say, marble could never fully capture.

"It was my first time seeing a bath like that, so Crusch-san and Felt-chan, who were bathing with me, and I all got really excited together."

"What is that, some kind of paradise. If this were a gal game, that would definitely be a CG-unlock scene."

"C... G?"

"Nah, nonsense. I'm just glad Emilia-tan looks so happy. Was it fun?"

"Yeah, a lot."

Emilia had pointed out Subaru's high spirits, but her own good mood was plain to see as well. This trip to Priestella, which had begun steeped in anxiety and suspicion, seemed to have turned out to be quite worthwhile, at least for this master and servant. The problem was—

"So, what happened to you two over there looking all wrung out?"

"...It's nothing, I suppose."

"Please don't mind us. Just... urp, drank a bit too much, urp. My stomach's, urp..."

A sulky-faced girl with ringlet curls turned her face away, and a pale, gagging pretty-boy sat beside her. Needless to say, these were Beatrice and Otto, but for the moment Subaru addressed Otto first, since he seemed closer to a genuine emergency.

"Otto. What were you even doing yesterday, disappearing and not coming back even for dinner?"

"I told you when we split up, didn't I? Since I'd finally, urp, made it all the way to Priestella, I wanted to talk to people who, urp, aren't easy to get in touch with normally, urp—"

"'Urp' isn't a word. You're drunker than the day we first met."

"...? I don't think I was in any state to be drinking when Natsuki-san and I first met..."

"If that's what you think, then I guess that's how it was. In your world, anyway."

Otto looked troubled at being blamed for no reason, but the remark was really more of a boomerang aimed back at Subaru himself. From Subaru's perspective, his first meeting with Otto meant their encounter during the period when Subaru had been down and drowning his sorrows in the royal capital. Due to various circumstances, that loop had failed, which meant that from Otto's perspective, their "first meeting" was actually when Otto had been captured by the Witch Cult. Naturally, there was no point at all in bringing any of that up.

"Just don't go teaching Bea-ko anything too bad. Well, I get that you've been running around doing a lot for the camp's sake."

"It's not like anyone asked me to, urp. I'm doing this on my own initiative. —Though that's not the only reason, either."

Responding weakly to Subaru's words, Otto shook his head miserably. Then he looked around the garden and, prefacing with "More importantly...," said:

"What happened to Garfiel? It's rare for him not to show his face in the morning. He's usually up earlier than anyone, howling from the top of some hill."

"Guess he couldn't find a good hill suited for howling. Also, that's kind of a touchy subject right now, so don't go digging into it thoughtlessly. Be gentle with him when you see him."

"I wish you wouldn't say something that unreasonable without giving me the details! ...Ah, ow, my head hurts just from saying that."

"That's a self-inflicted wound if I've ever seen one."

Subaru gave a wry smile as Otto collapsed unsteadily onto the veranda to rest. Then he turned to Beatrice, who had been quiet for a while now.

"So, what's up with you, Bea-ko? You were so excited yesterday, and now today you're suddenly sulking. Not cute."

"I am not sulking, I suppose. Don't go saying whatever you like. Betty simply has a few thoughts on her mind, that is all, I suppose."

"Thoughts on your mind? If something happened, spit it out. It'd be a problem if it were something dangerous."

At Beatrice's less-than-calm words, Subaru narrowed his eyes and pressed for details. Emilia nodded too, turning to listen attentively, and Beatrice bit her lip. But her hesitation lasted only a few seconds before the girl reluctantly began to spin out her story.

"It was yesterday, after Subaru left Betty behind to go play with Garfiel, I suppose."

"That's a bit of a slanted way to put it, but go on."

"Betty went to Emilia's room to pass the time, I suppose. On the way there, she happened to run into one of the inn's staff and heard something from them, I suppose."

"Bea-ko... talking to someone she's meeting for the first time...!?"

Subaru fell speechless at this shocking improvement in social ability. Glancing at Emilia, he found her nodding back at him with the same thought written on her face.

This was huge. To think that Beatrice, of all people, could talk to a stranger while traveling. Perhaps the liberating feeling of travel had, in a good way, spurred Beatrice's growth.

He would have to update the "Beatrice Growth Journal" kept at the manor the moment they got back. Already on its third volume, this journal meticulously recorded Beatrice's daily growth process. Thanks to this trip, a new page could be added to it.

Unaware that her daily activities were being recorded in such detail, Beatrice, seeing Subaru and Emilia's reaction, got angry crying "That's an exaggeration, I suppose!"—missing the fact that the truth was actually far more exaggerated than that.

"To continue, I suppose. When the inn's staff member spotted Betty, they seemed to feel that this was something only Betty absolutely had to be told, and they lowered their voice and said, I suppose: 'At this inn, once night falls, one sometimes sees things that are not human.'"

"............"

"Honestly, Betty hesitated over whether to reveal this, I suppose. Since it could needlessly cause confusion, she kept it to herself for the time being, I suppose. But she remained on guard, at least, I suppose. And then, last night..."

Leaving the silent Subaru behind, Beatrice's story grew more and more heated. Then, as if revealing her greatest secret, she lowered her voice.

"In the middle of the night, Betty sensed some kind of presence and woke up, I suppose. And so, careful not to let the fool asleep beside her with his idiotic sleeping face notice, she quietly slipped out to investigate, I suppose."

"Don't stare at people's sleeping faces, that's creepy."

"I-I didn't stare, I suppose! I glanced, demurely, in a ladylike manner, I suppose!"

The part about looking wasn't actually being denied, but since it was cute, Subaru let it slide. Beatrice cleared her throat, then once again put on a mood-setting expression.

"Seeking out the presence, Betty walked bravely through the inn, I suppose. And then, at the entrance, she finally found the source of that presence..."

"Found it?"

"She saw a pale face floating in the darkness, I suppose. She knew at a glance it was dangerous, I suppose. The other party seemed wary of Betty too, and so a staring match ensued, I suppose."

Perhaps recalling the tension of the previous night, a faint sheen of sweat appeared on Beatrice's brow. Subaru didn't quite understand the mechanism by which a spirit could sweat, but since it added to the sense of realism, he didn't point it out.

"Eventually, the other party yielded to Betty's pressure, I suppose. The standoff ended without incident, and the other party vanished, I suppose. After confirming they did not return, Betty returned to her room at ease, stepped back over Subaru's foolish sleeping face, and returned to the futon, I suppose."

"So you WERE peeking at my sleeping face, that's indecent."

"I-I was only checking that you were all right, I suppose! I did not pat your head or touch your eyelashes, I suppose!"

This was practically a confession by way of denial, but since it was cute, Subaru let it slide again. For now, it seemed Beatrice's testimony of her terrifying experience had concluded. Subaru put a hand to his chin, nodding sagely, and began verifying the account.

Being a Japanese-style inn, of course it would come with ghost stories. Actually, one thing Subaru had come to understand after a year living in this other world was that, strangely, this world hadn't spread that kind of ghost story very widely. This was probably because invisible beings—Spirits—were already a well-known phenomenon here.

If the existence of invisible beings was already an accepted fact, there wouldn't be much demand for ghost stories. And yet, the very fact that this inn still tried to tell them spoke to a certain tenacity—a determination to carry over even the unnecessary parts of Japanese spirit into this world.

Impressed by all this, Subaru let out a satisfied breath, then said:

"So, what actually happened, Otto?"

"Well, I was aware that Beatrice-chan was staring intently at me while I was about to throw up at the entrance, but I was in too much pain to even open my mouth. In the end, I couldn't hold it in and went and heaved into the bushes out front, and by the time I came back, she was already gone."

"There you have it."

"...That can't be true, I suppose."

The truth behind the ghost turned out to be nothing but withered pampas grass. In this case, the truth behind the ghost was a hopelessly drunk Otto, which made it even less redeeming.

Faced with the blunt denial of what she had actually seen, Beatrice looked shaken enough to collapse. Subaru patted her head comfortingly, concluding that Beatrice, too, must have been half-asleep in an unfamiliar environment. The staff member who'd fed Beatrice the ghost story had probably seen through Beatrice's rare aura of "looks easy to fool, and it'll definitely be cute when she is." Her face was flushed bright red with frustration right now, and since that was adorable, Subaru wanted to send the staff member a hearty "well done."

"Yo, you're all gathered here already. Kinda early, huh."

Just as this heartwarming exchange among the four of them was unfolding, a rough, unrefined girl's voice called out. Looking over, Subaru saw a girl with short blonde hair swaying into view on the veranda—Felt.

She had shed her yukata and was now dressed in light, easy-to-move-in clothes that exposed her slender limbs. Even though she'd been growing more womanly in some respects, at her core, it seemed she was still very much a back-alley girl.

"Morning to you too, but if you dress like that, Reinhard's going to start lecturing you again."

"Hah, spare me the sermon. I'm sick of hearing it from that guy, and lately even old man Rom's gotten naggy about it."

Waving her hand with a look of exasperation, Felt lightly hopped down into the garden. Then, ignoring Otto, who lay sprawled off to one side, she tilted her head at the row of Subaru and the others.

"By the way, something's been bugging me."

"What is it?"

"Nah, why've you guys all been doing that weird dance since earlier?"

Felt pointed, looking puzzled, at the strange dance Subaru and the others had been performing—their daily morning ritual, radio calisthenics.

Whether on a trip or in the middle of a journey, when morning came, they did their radio calisthenics. It was already an ordinary sight at Roswaal Manor, and moreover, it had been steadily spreading as a morning custom throughout the Mathers domain. Emilia, having thoroughly gotten used to it, had a face that showed she didn't even understand what Felt was asking about. While admiring Emilia's blankly puzzled profile, Subaru demonstrated a deep breath for Felt's benefit.

"This, my friend, is the secret to health and longevity. A health regimen beloved across generations, from little kids to the elderly, known as 'Radio Calisthenics.' If Emilia-tan becomes queen, it will be decreed by national law that everyone do this every morning."

"That's right. I think it feels great doing it together."

"Th-that so...? If that's your campaign platform, I think I'd actually be less inclined to make you queen..."

Having watched the full routine of radio calisthenics, Felt muttered this with a sour expression. It was sad to have such a difference of opinion, but surely even those who disliked it at first would come to appreciate the convenience and efficiency of this exercise the more they kept at it. In fact, in the villages where Subaru had gone around preaching its virtues, the adoption rate was pretty impressive.

"Bea-ko and Otto hated it at first too, but now they're so into it they'll join in whether it's a morning they're scared of ghosts or a morning they're hungover."

"Betty was just dragged along by Subaru, I suppose."

"I was sleeping with a headache too, and then someone clapped their hands right by my ear and made me dance..."

"'I hate it, I hate it' is basically 'I love it'—that's how into it they are."

"So into it, I suppose."

Beatrice and Otto's endorsement was somewhat weak, but Subaru and Emilia puffed out their chests with pride anyway. Watching the two extreme reactions, Felt roughly scratched her pale neck.

"Yeah, I have heard that weird events are getting popular around your territory, sis. Weird dances, and some event where you hollow out a pumpkin's insides and make it into a mask, and some event where women give men baked sweets or the other way around..."

"For now it's still treated as some backwater curiosity, but eventually I want to make it a nationwide project. Come to think of it, maybe I should consult with Anastasia-san about it."

It was a well-known fact that Valentine's Day was said to be a conspiracy cooked up by the confectionery industry. Meaning, it was a matter of big money changing hands, which meant Anastasia would probably jump at it. Subaru resolved to consult with her on that front the moment he had a free moment.

"Hey. Is that bro always like that?"

"Yeah, Subaru's pretty much always like that. He looks like he's just goofing around, but actually he's working hard thinking about all sorts of things. And then, while making it look like that, sometimes he really is just goofing around."

"Doesn't that basically mean you can't tell if he's joking or serious until you see the results...?"

Felt looked utterly unnerved by Emilia's answer. This happened occasionally—due to issues with her mental age, Emilia sometimes couldn't tell whether the person she was dealing with was younger or older than her—and that applied to Felt as well. Though it might also be because Felt, having grown up rough in the back alleys, was simply more worldly-wise.

"Anyway, are you here alone, Felt-chan? Won't Reinhard get upset that you're not together?"

"I ain't a kid, y'know—having him hovering around me all the time's just annoying. It's not like my sis or the others are dumb enough to try something in a place like this, right? And, well, it's irritating to admit, but if something did happen to me, that bastard would show up in one second flat."

"I see. Then that's reassuring."

Emilia readily accepted Felt's vaguely threatening remark and laughed. This attitude left Felt looking rather deflated in response, and she roughly scratched at her blonde hair to vent her irritation.

"Felt-chan, your hair is so pretty, you shouldn't treat it so roughly. I've been careful about it myself lately too, since Subaru and Frederica keep telling me to."

"Man, you really throw me off my game, dammit. Don't worry about my hair. And I told you to stop calling me 'chan,' it makes my skin crawl!"

"Even if you say that, somehow it doesn't feel right to call you by just your name. I'll try to be careful, but sorry if I slip up."

"Fgrrh!"

Since Emilia meant no harm, Felt, facing off against her, hissed like a cat to vent her frustration. Cut out of context, it looked like nothing more than a heartwarming exchange between girlfriends.

"Well then. Radio calisthenics is done, so let's scatter and wait for breakfast. A morning bath might be nice too. Otto, you didn't take a bath, did you?"

"I only rinsed off with water... I'm sorry that the smell of alcohol hasn't gone away yet."

"You'd better get rid of it before Garfiel comes back and smacks you for it. Back where I'm from, I've heard soaking in a hot bath multiple times helps with that."

"Somehow that sounds like it'd make things worse, but... I'll take you up on it anyway."

Subaru lent a hand to Otto, who had slumped down, pulling his wobbly body up to stand. Sighing in front of Otto, who feebly patted at himself, Subaru absently looked up at the sky. Clear skies again this morning. Few clouds—it looked like it would be good weather. And it was just as Subaru was thinking that.

"—Good morning, citizens of Priestella. It's a lovely morning."

"Huh?"

Suddenly struck by the strange event of a voice falling from the sky, Subaru let out a startled exclamation. Wondering if it might be an auditory hallucination, Subaru hastily looked around, and found Emilia, Felt, and Beatrice all reacting to the voice with the same surprise as him.

"Hey, hey, what is this? That's a seriously huge voice."

"There's no way that's possible. A voice just being loud wouldn't echo across the whole city like that. But..."

Interjecting at Felt's mutter, Subaru suspected something magical was involved. When it came to magic that delivered a voice to a large group all at once, what came to mind was the mind-linking magic 'Nect' that Julius had used before. But that had only been a telepathy-like means of communication among people within a certain range—it wasn't something that delivered sound directly to the eardrums. And more than that, Subaru had something specific in mind that resembled this. Namely—

"It sounds kind of like a speaker, or a megaphone?"

Felt's expression of "huge voice" wasn't actually all that far off from what Subaru had in mind. Echoing down from the sky, and seemingly audible across the whole city, it bore an uncanny resemblance to the kind of sound produced by a speaker. The problem was that this world showed no sign whatsoever of that kind of scientific and technological development.

"Ah, you didn't know? This is a broadcast using the effect of a Magic Vessel installed at Priestella's city hall."

"Magic Vessel... a Magic Vessel!"

It was Otto who offered an answer to Subaru's question. He responded to Subaru's astonishment with an "Yeah," and continued:

"I heard about it too, yesterday, while drinking with various people—apparently the daily morning broadcast from the Magic Vessel at city hall is a Priestella tradition."

"Why on earth would they have such a strange daily custom?"

"It's convenient for getting announcements out to the general public all at once, I suppose. Also, given Priestella's structure as a city, the evacuation routes in an emergency are limited. So apparently, they use it regularly to get citizens used to it, so there's no confusion when it's actually needed."

"Huh... that's pretty well thought out."

Subaru was impressed by the idea of a Magic Vessel meant to handle emergencies. On the scale of a village, this might not matter much, but on the scale of a city, it was easy to imagine the confusion that would follow if something went wrong. Taking measures in advance to deal with that was never wasted effort. The idea of getting citizens used to something in preparation, well before it was actually needed, struck him as refreshingly novel.

"Sounds like they've got a pretty sharp head running things as city magistrate."

"No, actually. It was apparently Kiritaka-san who instituted this Magic Vessel broadcast habit in the first place, and who supplied the Magic Vessel to begin with. Said something about it being only natural as someone involved in running the city."

"Eh..."

Subaru's admiring train of thought froze, struck by this unexpected blow. In his mind, the brief encounter with Kiritaka from yesterday flashed by like a scene from a fever dream. The cracking voice. The magic ore hurled at him. And the effeminate-looking man calling out Liliana's name while shedding tears—

"No, that's not possible." "It's not possible, I suppose." "That's a bit much, honestly..."

Subaru, Beatrice, and Emilia's opinions aligned perfectly, and Otto gave a wry smile.

"I figured you'd say that, but it's definitely true that this broadcast is being conducted by the person in charge, Kiritaka-san. Listen, doesn't the voice sound familiar?"

"—This is using the power of a voice-transmitting Magic Vessel to deliver my voice to the entire city. My apologies to those hearing this for the first time if it startled you. For a little while longer, I hope you'll lend your ears to this peculiar daily routine here in Priestella."

"Who even is this guy."

Despite Otto's well-meaning attempt to back him up, this didn't match up in Subaru's ears with the Kiritaka he'd encountered yesterday. This guy sounded... normal. Didn't feel like a lolicon at all.

"Well, but Clind-san also looks perfectly normal at first glance... maybe the bigger the lolicon, the better they are at putting up a normal front in daily life? A high-status lolicon is downright scary."

An image rose in Subaru's mind of the blue-haired, thin-faced, all-around-capable butler. Overwhelming talent and skill, combined with unreasonable initiative and a hopeless personal predilection. It might be a stretch to hold up Clind as representative, but he could certainly serve as an example of a high-spec lolicon.

"Well, it's also questionable whether this is really this Kiritaka guy or not. Can't rule out just mishearing something."

"—And to all of you, allow me to offer a small token of my... no, allow me to deliver a magnificent Blessing! This morning as well, I have pleaded with and brought along—please, do lend your ears to the singing voice of the 'Songstress' Liliana-jouou!"

"Ah, yep, this is really him."

His voice cracking partway through as his excitement got the better of him—the broadcast and Subaru's memory finally aligned. Dismayed so early in the morning, Subaru heard a rattling sound through the Magic Vessel and sensed people shifting position. Then came a throat-clear, and a faint laugh, followed by:

"Ummm, morning everyone. This is Liliana, the one who's just been so kindly introduced. Being called a songstress every single morning is honestly kind of a heavy mantle to carry, but I'm gonna sing and play my heart out and try to make everyone happy. So, cheer me on, 'kay?"

Even through the Magic Vessel, Liliana's distinctive way of speaking came through so clearly you could practically picture her mannerisms. Strangely enough, while Kiritaka's voice had sounded somewhat cracked and distorted through the Magic Vessel, Liliana's voice carried none of that imperfection. Whether it was some kind of compatibility with the Magic Vessel, Subaru couldn't say, but it seemed the voice of this girl beloved by the goddess of song was protected by some Divine Protection that let it pass clean through any tasteless interference.

"Now then, I'll sing. Please listen. —Sword Demon Love Song, Act Two."

There came the sound of an instrument being readied, and Liliana drew a small breath. Listening to that sound, Subaru found his ears captured by the ring of the words 'Sword Demon Love Song.' If it was a song whose title matched exactly what Subaru suspected, then it was—

What was played was a sad yet dear love story of a demon who lived by the sword, and a woman.

With the "Songstress's" terror attack finally over, Subaru and the others promptly headed to the dining room and waited for breakfast.

Frankly, the existence of "Sword Demon Love Song" had come as a complete bolt from the blue for Subaru. He should have realized it. In any world, in any era, great events that had been accomplished were passed down to later generations in some form or other. That applied regardless of whether the deed was heroic or villainous, and regardless of the medium—text, painting, anything. It really shouldn't have been any surprise that a story of the hero who brought an end to a civil war, and the sword demon who took that hero as his wife, would be passed down as a "song."

Of course, even if Subaru had realized this possibility beforehand, there wasn't much he could have done about this morning's terrorism. He hadn't even known the Magic Vessel existed, so there was no way he could have warned Liliana off from doing anything reckless.

For now, all he could do was purely and simply curse the "Songstress" for choosing, at the worst possible timing, a song with absolutely no sense of reading the room.

The "Songstress's" reputation had already hit rock bottom, and he couldn't fathom the bizarre eagerness to drive it even further down from there. Stupid Liliana. Idiot Kiritaka.

"Wilhelm-san isn't... here yet, huh."

The first to arrive at the dining room were only Subaru and the others who'd been in the garden. Felt, who'd parted ways with them before they moved, would presumably show up after joining Reinhard. The song from moments ago, and the feelings Wilhelm had shared the night before about his wife and grandson—with the old man's smile still vivid in his mind, Subaru couldn't suppress his restless heart.

Subaru himself didn't know what he wanted to say when he saw Wilhelm, or what he even should say. Still, he believed there was meaning in exchanging words. If he were honest, his sincere wish was that Wilhelm had simply let the song wash over him unheeded, but—

"There's no way that punctual bunch would all oversleep together."

Subaru knew, from the few days he'd spent together with them, that all three members of the Crusch Camp—even Ferris—kept punctual, regular sleeping and waking hours. In an unfamiliar travel destination, their nerves would be all the more heightened, so there was no way they'd have missed that broadcast.

"Subaru, you look scary. Did something odd happen?"

Plopping herself down on a cushion, Subaru sat there impatiently jiggling his leg, and Emilia turned a worried gaze on him. After the broadcast in the garden, it had been Subaru himself who'd called for them to head straight to the dining room. Confirming that no one was there yet, and then acting like this without any explanation—her anxiety was only natural.

Emilia and the others had purely been captivated by the wistful lyrics of "Sword Demon Love Song" and Liliana's singing voice. They didn't know that Wilhelm was in fact the very sword demon being sung about, nor did they know how that sword demon's story ended up. So it was no wonder they couldn't share in the anxious agitation gripping Subaru.

What was frightening, too, was the power of Liliana's singing voice—one that could enthrall even Subaru, who knew all the circumstances. Even Subaru, who upon hearing the title "Sword Demon Love Song" had felt a bad premonition, had been unable to cut the song short and leave the garden. No—the thought hadn't even occurred to him. There was something bewitching in Liliana's singing voice. Precisely because of that, once he came back to his senses, the only target Subaru could find to direct his irritation at was that dim-witted, tan-skinned girl.

"...It's nothing. I'm just a little peckish. See, the food at the inn is great, but it's never quite enough to fill you up, right? And you can't sneak snacks elsewhere, so my growing body's just craving food."

"If Subaru says so, I'll accept that for now. Honestly."

Emilia, who usually let herself be fooled easily, had a way of seeing through Subaru's bluster at the crucial moments. Was he really that easy to read? It was enough to shake his confidence.

"Natsuki-san, I don't know what you're so irritated about either, but I had to cut you off partway through explaining the afternoon plan. You were properly listening, right?"

"The afternoon plan? Oh, the renegotiation with Kiritaka. Um, wasn't it the plan to take Liliana hostage and trade her for the magic ore we're after?"

"How on earth did that turn into a proposal for such a hardline strategy!?"

Otto raised his voice in shock, then winced at the pain his own voice caused him. Watching him, Subaru tilted his head, saying "Huh?" Perhaps the anger toward Liliana still screaming inside his chest had aided and abetted the drafting of such an extreme plan. In fact, it seemed like it would have a fairly high success rate.

"For now, the gist of it is that this afternoon we're going to ask Kiritaka-san to resume negotiations over the magic ore. It'd be nice if his aide from 'White Dragon's Scales' would put in a good word for us."

"White Dragon's Scales, what an impressive name. Was that the person who was in the room yesterday too?"

"Yes, that's right. White Dragon's Scales is a fairly famous mercenary band in certain circles. Well, with the caveat of 'a while back,' but apparently they've recently been revived and hired on as Kiritaka-san's private guard. The one who was there yesterday was probably their representative."

"The person in the room, you mean... ah, the one who seemed like the most reasonable to talk to."

Though he hadn't been able to observe the room for very long, Subaru managed to dredge up his memory from before the shock had swallowed it whole, and nodded. A middle-aged man dressed in white. Compared to Kiritaka, who went berserk whenever Liliana was involved, and to Liliana, who was perpetually berserk, that man seemed like he'd offer far more workable human communication.

"Judging from this morning's broadcast, it doesn't seem like Kiritaka-san's anger has persisted, so I think he'll hear us out. Though it'll probably flare up again if Natsuki-san goes in person."

"I'm aware my face doesn't go over well with people I meet for the first time, but hearing it put that plainly still stings."

"It's fine, Subaru. I don't dislike a sharp gaze. My mother had a really sharp gaze too. But she was kind."

"That's not exactly backing up Subaru's face, I suppose. ...Wait, I mean his gaze."

"Not correcting yourself would've let that slide by more smoothly. Don't make me face reality."

Basking in the gentle-yet-harsh reassurance from the women, Subaru urged Otto to continue. Otto picked up with "So, as I was saying," and continued:

"For today's negotiation, I think it's better if Natsuki-san doesn't come along. What do you think?"

"What do I think, my ass, guess I've got no choice but to accept it. But honestly, if that wraps everything up neatly, what was even the point of me coming here?"

"Let's go with whichever ends up doing less damage—Natsuki-san alone wasting a trip, or all of us wasting a trip. Natsuki-san, you can just go play with Beatrice-chan."

"I feel like Betty's being treated lightly here, I suppose! How infuriating, I suppose!"

Beatrice's indignant protest was ignored, and for the time being the camp's course of action was settled. That said, apart from Otto's sound reasoning, Subaru had his own thoughts on the matter.

"In that case, this afternoon I'll go for a walk with Emilia-tan and Bea-ko in the park or something."

"Huh? Aren't I supposed to go with Otto-kun to Kiritaka-san's place?"

"They'll probably expect a renegotiation request from us, but if you bring Emilia-sama along, it'll turn into an unannounced surprise visit without prior arrangement. Same flow as yesterday. ...I'm fine with Natsuki-san understanding that much, but I do wonder what you're scheming."

Subaru dodged Otto's narrow-eyed glare with a whistle. He'd mentioned running into Liliana after wandering around lost yesterday, but he hadn't mentioned where he'd met her. And surely Kiritaka's side would also anticipate that the Emilia Camp would try to renegotiate again. What would Kiritaka, unwilling to let them meet Liliana, do with her in the meantime? The hint lay in Liliana's own behavior yesterday.

"I found a nice-looking park, see. Thought I'd walk around it with Emilia-tan, with Bea-ko sandwiched between us holding hands."

"Wow, that sounds fun. But is it really okay to take it easy like that? Right, Otto-kun?"

"When you look at me with such hopeful eyes, there's no way I can refuse. Well, since neither Natsuki-san nor Emilia-sama can come along for various reasons, I'll go visit them together with Garfiel instead. Please, just don't cause any strange commotion."

At Otto's insistent reminder, Subaru nodded solemnly along with Emilia. But behind that solemn nod, he was silently sticking his tongue out and apologizing to Otto.

Presumably, Liliana would once again be kept away from the Muse Company today. In that case, the only place Subaru could think of where she might go was the same park as yesterday. If they didn't meet, well, that just meant he wouldn't be able to set up an insurance policy—but if it was at all possible, Subaru wanted to establish a connection with Liliana there.

If Kiritaka was head over heels for Liliana, then the odds weren't low that he'd grant a wish if it came from her. That was his calculation. Of course, it wasn't as if he was only thinking of using Liliana. If Subaru and the others met Liliana's request, that would simply be an ordinary cooperative relationship.

If it were only about exploiting Liliana's goodwill, Emilia would object, and it would weigh on Subaru's conscience too. So he intended to tell her, without holding anything back or sparing any detail, the story of their journey so far, since that was what Liliana wanted to hear. If, as a result, she learned that what Subaru and the others had actually done was far from the heroic epic she hoped for, and was disappointed because of it—well, that would be that. The very thought of being treated as a hero and immortalized in song in the history books gave him goosebumps.

He had no intention of dressing things up in an especially bad light, but simply telling the unvarnished truth ought to be more than enough. At the very least, she was sure to be disillusioned once she learned just how pathetically and shamefully Subaru—one half of the pair she believed to be heroes—had stumbled his way to this point.

"—Mornin'. Wow, everyone's early over here."

Just as the Emilia Camp's public and private courses of action had both been settled, the shoji slid open and Anastasia poked her head in. Wearing her usual fox stole, she was dressed today in a kimono. Seeing Subaru startled at the sight of traditional Japanese dress appearing, and Emilia and the others' eyes sparkling with pure curiosity at the unfamiliar clothing, Anastasia wore a triumphant expression.

"Good, good. Looks like I managed to properly knock everyone's socks off again this mornin', glad to see it."

"Anastasia-san, your outfit is wonderful. Is that, maybe, what you mentioned yesterday?"

"Yep. It's the clothing called a kimono I was talkin' about in the bath yesterday. Kinda similar to a yukata, but it takes a bit more effort to put on."

Anastasia happily turned in place, and the kimono, dyed a deep blue, suited her splendidly. The pattern depicting scattered flower petals was charming too, and Subaru could only marvel at Kararagi's skill in recreating such things. And with this, something he'd already considered nearly certain now became a confirmed conviction.

—That, unmistakably, Kararagi had somehow had Subaru's own knowledge of Japan brought into it.

"Is that kimono something that's been passed down in Kararagi for a long time too, by any chance?"

"Heh, you sure know your stuff, Natsuki-kun. Apparently it's been around bit by bit since Hoshin's era. Though for a while, the way of makin' it was lost, and it's only in the last decade or so that a handful of craftsmen have finally started managin' to reproduce it."

"Hoshin's... era, huh."

Once again, the shadow of that mysterious figure, 'Hoshin of the Wilderness,' loomed before Subaru. At this point, Subaru had no choice but to suspect this Hoshin person of something. Most likely, Hoshin was also someone Summoned, just like Subaru and Al. Except, unlike Subaru and the others, four hundred years in the past.

"Once things here are settled, I might need to properly look into Hoshin sometime..."

Subaru didn't really have much intention of digging into the matter of the Summoning at this late stage. Or rather, he ought to say he understood it at some instinctive level.

Neither the mechanism of the Summoning nor the aims of the person who'd carried it out were known to him, but this Summoning was a one-way pull—there was no convenient method to send someone back. On that matter, he had thoughts as countless as the stars—but no solution. What Subaru wanted to know was what kind of footprints those Summoned before him, like himself, had left in this world, and what kind of end they had met. That was the point.

"Good morning, Subaru. Did you sleep well last night? I appreciated you telling Felt-sama to return to her room this morning. She very nearly slipped away again."

"Quiet, you. Anyway, you cut me off before I even started headin' back to my room and caught me. What the hell's up with your nose, huh?"

Following Anastasia, Reinhard came into the dining room as well, holding Felt by the scruff of her neck. He sat the sulking Felt down on a cushion, and, taking his seat beside her, showed no sign of being bothered by the song from this morning's broadcast. There was no way he didn't know who the motif of "Sword Demon Love Song" was, after all.

"Anastasia-sama. You are even more beautiful this morning than usual. I was worried since you wouldn't even show yourself to me, but it seems my concern was unfounded."

"Ehehe, this here's my secret weapon. See, it only arrived, all finished, right before we came to Priestella. Keepin' it hidden from Julius was a real struggle."

Afterward, Julius joined them as well, and Anastasia showed off the kimono to her own knight too. After basking in a round of compliments, Anastasia tilted her head slightly.

"Huh? Aren't Mimi and the others with you?"

"Ricardo left the city last night on an errand I assigned him and hasn't returned. As for Mimi and the others... it seems they've gone off following after one of Emilia-sama's attendants, Garfiel."

"After Garfiel?"

At the mention of one of her own people's names, Emilia looked up, and Julius nodded, "Yes."

"It seems Mimi spotted Garfiel leaving the inn and immediately bolted after him. So Hetaro then chased after Mimi..."

"And Tivey, stuck cleanin' up after them, went along too, and Joshua's the one who got the message. That about the size of it?"

Anastasia, hand on her hip, confirmed this with Joshua, who had been hiding behind the tall Julius. The thin-faced young man, prompted by his lord's words, stepped forward hesitantly, and bowed apologetically with a haggard look on his face.

"M-my apologies. I... I did try my best to stop them, but neither Mimi nor Hetaro would listen to me. Tivey said to leave it to her."

"Well, if Tivey went along, things probably won't turn into anythin' too weird, so it's fine. Wouldn't look good for the host to be makin' a fuss in front of guests, anyway."

Patting the shoulder of the abashed Joshua in forgiveness, Anastasia turned to face everyone seated around the room. Swaying her soft hair, twirling her stole between her fingers, Anastasia smiled elegantly.

"Just as pathetic as you might've heard, sorry to say, but bear with it, alright? Even my cute vice-captain seems to have gotten swept up in her first love."

That Mimi was head over heels for Garfiel was something, naturally, everyone had already noticed. The only ones who hadn't known were Emilia, who was still tilting her head in confusion, and Joshua, who had just muttered, unable to help himself, "So that's how it was...!"

"Come to think of it, I don't see Felt's bunch of screwballs around either. You guys not putting them up at this inn?"

"You mean Gaston and the others? Well, it's a joke sayin' this fancy inn's wasted on 'em, but I'm not the type to just mooch off everyone the whole time either. I stuck 'em with some work and put 'em up at some cheap inn somewhere in the city. But..."

Answering Subaru's question, Felt then bared her teeth in a grin.

"That 'Screwballs' thing, ain't bad. It's Gaston, Rachins, and Camberley, so it ain't wrong, and it's got a ring to it that suits 'em, I don't hate it."

"I want to give myself a round of applause from a year ago, for coming up with a genuinely great name. I was surprised as hell to hear their real names, like, what kind of miracle is that."

A toast to his past self, who had called the Screwballs "Screwballs" instead of "Dimwits." And since it seemed she'd genuinely taken a liking to it, a toast as well to the future of the Screwballs, who from here on out would likely be called Screwballs primarily by their own boss too.

"—Sorry I'm late. Looks like we're the last ones."

The last to arrive at the dining room was Crusch, her long green hair pulled up today. Her elegant, noble-lady attire remained the same as always, and the flower-adorned hairpin was particularly vivid and lovely today. Following her into the room after a bow was Ferris, and then Wilhelm.

"————"

Dressed today, as every day, in his formal attire, back straight, the sight of the old man made Subaru flinch involuntarily. Holding his breath, he tried to steal a glance at that profile, and found himself meeting Wilhelm's eyes, who happened to be looking right at him. Subaru's breath caught, his gaze locking with Wilhelm's. And then—

"————"

With a faint smile, Wilhelm followed his lord and quietly seated himself in the dining room. Watching this, Subaru interpreted the meaning behind that smile as a message: There's no need to worry. Subaru let out a breath of relief. Then he noticed Ferris, seated beside Crusch, looking his way and giving him a wink and a peace sign.

—We handled things on our end, nya-kay.

Seeing the gesture, Subaru received a similar message from Ferris. Perhaps noticing that Subaru had picked up on it, Ferris casually dropped the gesture and went right back to leaning against Crusch, back to his usual pace.

Subaru finally realized he'd been shouldering unnecessary worry and needless meddling. Of course Crusch and Ferris understood Wilhelm's circumstances, and their connection to the song, far better than Subaru did. Of course it was those two who were sensitive to the wavering of Wilhelm's heart, and who could care for him up close. That Subaru's worry wasn't even needed was, in a way, only natural, given the bonds of comradeship those three shared.

"If anything, we're the ones falling short—we still haven't solved the Garfiel problem."

Not exactly "look at others and correct yourself," but it amounted to the same idea—if he had time to worry over other people's affairs, he should sort out his own camp first. Though, considering that the turmoil churning inside Garfiel was something that could only be resolved depending on Garfiel's own resolve, dealing with it wouldn't be easy either way.

"Well now, seems a few faces are missin', but that's clearly just absence with good cause. Since we've managed to see everyone's faces safe and sound again this mornin', let's get to breakfast, shall we."

Clapping her hands, Anastasia, seated at the head of the table, made the announcement. With fewer faces occupying the corners of the room than the previous night, the turnout was smaller, but it was still a lavish assembly. Given the events of last night, expectations were high for the meal itself as well. What would be served this morning?—Amid that modest anticipation, Anastasia smiled.

"Bring it in."

At Anastasia's instruction, the shoji slid open and the inn's staff appeared. Several of them together carried something in, briskly setting it up on the long table. Spreading across the huge table was a large, black object—unmistakably, an iron griddle.

"Today, I'm treating everyone to Kararagi's national traditional dish—Daisuki-yaki!"

Anastasia briskly tied back her kimono sleeves and raised her voice. Before the stunned assembly, the staff swiftly oiled the griddle, then brought in round containers of batter one after another, wheeling them into the dining room on a cart.

Daisuki-yaki—that name, and the griddle and ingredients before him. Comparing the two, Subaru accurately deduced exactly what this was. Which was to say—

"Wh-what, is that okonomiyaki...?"

It was the arrival of okonomiyaki, passed down in Kararagi under the name Daisuki-yaki.

"Subaru, look! I flipped it perfectly! I'm proud of this one! Try it!"

"It came out reasonably well, I suppose. Subaru, since you went to the trouble, Betty supposes she'll let you eat this Daisuki-yaki she cooked, I suppose."

Emilia, beaming with a full smile, and Beatrice, cheeks slightly flushed, each urged him to try what had failed to become a proper Daisuki-yaki on the griddle in front of them. To Subaru's eyes, it looked like nothing more than a lump of black charcoal, but throwing it away and disregarding the feelings of the two of them would go against his principles.

"Why don't you two try tasting your own first, and then offer it to someone else after that?"

Offering this sensible piece of advice, Subaru watched out of the corner of his eye as the two of them writhed in agony after following it, while also sending a glance toward how the other camps were faring.

"There, done, Felt-sama."

"Ohh, nice, real nice. Keep 'em coming just like that. I really appreciate havin' you around for your cooking and baking skills, y'know."

"If only you could rely on me for other things as well, that would be the true honor of a knight."

Across from him, at the Felt Camp, Reinhard, with an extraordinarily deft hand, was churning out the finest Daisuki-yaki imaginable, and one masterpiece after another disappeared into Felt's stomach. Watching this, Felt was clearly a glutton on a level that made you wonder where in that small body she was storing it all. Well, glutton might not be quite the word—big eater, maybe. Either was fine, really. In any case, Reinhard barely had time to eat any himself, he was working so hard, and it looked like Felt had already polished off nearly ten pieces.

Incidentally, the state of the Emilia Camp was as described earlier—since Emilia and Beatrice were useless at it, the successful examples were, for the most part, entirely the work of Subaru and Otto.

"Here, Emilia-sama, Beatrice-chan. This one's properly cooked. Eat this one... Ah! Emilia-sama! It's fine to reflect on overcooking one, but please don't eat the ones that are still raw! Beatrice-chan, that's way too much sauce!"

Thanks to Otto's mother-hen-like efforts, it looked like a stomachache would be avoided for the time being, thankfully. As for the other camps, the one that stood out most was naturally Anastasia's. After all, Anastasia herself had planned this whole breakfast surprise. Having been so confident, it was only natural that her knowledge of Daisuki-yaki ran deep.

"Sas-sas-sa! Sas-sas-sa! Done! This here's the real Daisuki-yaki!"

Wielding two spatulas with practiced flair, Anastasia shaped a magnificently pitch-black imitation of Daisuki-yaki and tossed it onto Julius's plate. A vivid example proving that enthusiasm alone couldn't solve everything.

"As expected of you, Anastasia-sama. However, my personal preference might lean toward one cooked for a slightly shorter time. It is not my intention to trouble you further, but..."

"That's fine, that's fine. Got it. A bit shorter, then. Julius, you're such a boy, and yet you've got such a delicate palate."

Unlike Subaru, who made the cooks eat their own creations to prompt reflection, Julius finished off his charred Daisuki-yaki completely and, without losing his smile, offered Anastasia a suggestion for improvement. That attitude of upholding one's lord was truly a model of knighthood. Subaru had absolutely no intention of imitating it, nor was he likely to.

Beside them, Joshua clumsily made his own portion and ate a messy, misshapen Daisuki-yaki, and for some reason the sight was almost endearing. He'd forgotten his monocle was fogging up even now, and was desperately trying not to let anyone notice his depth perception had gone askew. Since it would be humiliating for him to know he was being thought of that way by Subaru, whom he also disliked, Subaru refrained from saying anything.

Watching this pair of brothers with their wildly differing levels of elegance, it turned out the camp with the most stability was, in fact, the Crusch Camp sitting across from them.

"Ah, Crusch-sama~, Ferri-chan's turned out beautifully. Look, look."

"My, it really has. But I'm not going to lose either. Fufu, look at this."

The flirtatious, girls'-talk-like exchange came from Crusch and Ferris. Their confidence was properly backed by results—the Daisuki-yaki being cooked on the griddle in front of the two of them had come out in proper shape. Ferris's, in fact, was made round with enough leeway to even add cat-ear decorations to the dough.

"Nnfuu. Alright, I'll feed Crusch-sama some of Ferri-chan's Daisuki-yaki with all my love packed in. Crusch-sama, open your mouth wide, say 'ahh~.'"

"Eh, eh? Um, well... a-ahh."

That this went beyond merely heartwarming into something faintly scandalous was probably because he knew the two of them were, in fact, both women. Or maybe it was the innocent, aristocratic-young-lady air that wafted off the memory-lost Crusch's entire being that made it feel that way.

In any case, that master-and-servant pair seemed to have no problems. That left, sitting right next to that pink-tinted space, Wilhelm, facing his own Daisuki-yaki.

"Hmm..."

Trying to flip his batter, Wilhelm narrowed his eyes and gave a small groan. The Daisuki-yaki before his eyes had apparently been cooked too long and stuck to the griddle, and having been forcibly moved anyway, had ended up falling apart. It felt like witnessing an unexpected bit of clumsiness from Wilhelm.

"Feels like I just saw something I shouldn't have. But, if that's the case..."

Should he lend Wilhelm a hand? Subaru started to rise from his seat. But he immediately reconsidered and sat back down. After a moment of thought, Subaru nodded.

"Wilhelm-san."

"...Subaru-dono?"

Noticing he'd been called, Wilhelm looked up. When Subaru pointed at his not-very-successful handiwork, Wilhelm's brow furrowed as if ashamed of his own inadequacy. Subaru nodded at him, then quietly gestured with his chin toward the seat beside him.

Realizing what he meant, Wilhelm quietly caught his breath.

"————"

What Subaru had indicated with his chin was, positionally, Reinhard, seated right next to Wilhelm. He was still following Felt's instructions, mass-producing Daisuki-yaki with hands skilled enough to put a royal castle chef to shame, and hadn't noticed the exchange between Subaru and Wilhelm at all.

But Wilhelm surely understood what Subaru was trying to say. And Wilhelm, too, first showed surprise in his eyes, and then conflict. Hesitation. Uncertainty. Deliberation. It took him a considerable amount of time to reach a conclusion, and then—

"—Reinhard."

"————"

At Wilhelm's strained, wrung-out voice, Reinhard's hands stopped moving. The hand holding the spatula froze mid-flip, and the Daisuki-yaki that had been sent airborne was neatly caught by Felt, who slid her plate underneath it. It was an unbelievably unrefined gesture, but Reinhard made no move to scold her for it.

The red-haired young man's eyes went wide as he stared at Wilhelm, who had called out to him. Wilhelm, too, met Reinhard's gaze head-on, his breath catching.

"————"

"————"

Silence fell. Not just between the two of them—everyone nearby who had noticed the exchange fell silent too.

Amid the resulting stillness, the sound of Daisuki-yaki sizzling on the griddle continued, almost obnoxiously loud in the quiet. When everyone's breath had caught, and the stagnation of time had become almost palpable on the skin—

"That is... well..."

"Yes, what is it?"

"...I don't seem to be able to cook this well. If you have any tricks to it, might you... teach me?"

In halting, broken words, Wilhelm managed to get that much out. How much willpower it had taken Wilhelm to force those words out—Subaru, who knew, and Crusch and the others, who apparently felt the same, all watched with widened eyes.

More than anything, Wilhelm himself looked utterly drained the moment after he finished speaking, as if all his strength had left him. And hearing those words, Reinhard caught his breath and hesitated over how to respond.

Across the red-haired knight's handsome profile and blue eyes ran a wave of emotion the likes of which Subaru had never seen there before. Something close to inner conflict—Reinhard hid it by closing his eyes and letting out a breath. And then—

"—Yes. Understood. Ojii-sama."

Loosening his lips, narrowing his eyes—what else could you call that expression but a smile? It was a smile unlike the kind Reinhard always wore, the kind that put others at ease. Perhaps it was a smile that the young man named Reinhard could only wear once he had stepped away from his role as the 'Sword Saint.'

Wilhelm's expression went blank, and then, slowly, the words sank in. He lowered his face as it was, closing his eyes as if enduring something.

Perhaps he couldn't fully take it in right away. But even if the realization was delayed, it would surely reach him eventually.

Once it did reach him, all that remained was to acknowledge it and fill in the gap. The long, long rift that had formed between grandfather and grandson—closed over the same span of time it had taken to form.

Subaru pictured that future, and simply clenched his fist, his heart full to bursting. He genuinely wanted, somehow, to put Wilhelm's joy into words and offer his congratulations. And so—

"—No way, old man. Bit too convenient, showing up with that now, ain't it?"

A red-haired figure suddenly slid the shoji open, showing his face. At the malice packed into that ruddy-faced man's words, all Subaru could do was freeze, dumbstruck, forgetting the passage of time entirely.
