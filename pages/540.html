Arc 7 Chapter 42: "Beast of Ambition"

"Went ahead and made myself some tea. Anyone else want some?"

The instant that raspy, aged voice reached his ears, the blood in Subaru's whole body began to run backward.

His goosefleshed skin cried out in pain, and the air he'd drawn into his lungs froze solid. As if something heavy and sharp had lodged itself deep in his chest, Subaru's soul was jolted into wakefulness.

—Return by Death had triggered, hurling him back to the worst possible moment.

"—!"

In that instant, before Subaru could react at all, Taritta already had her bow drawn and aimed.

Her target, naturally, was the uninvited guest who had suddenly appeared in the room—Olbart Dunkelkenn. One of the Nine Divine Generals, and the old man who, a mere ten seconds ago, had slaughtered every last one of them.

Even without knowing that, the far-from-gentlemanly way he'd closed the distance was enough to draw everyone's guard taut.

But Olbart, with an arrow trained on him from point-blank range in that very room, only shrugged, as if mocking their vigilance. "Oi, oi."

"Quit it, quit it. I don't much care for having pointy things aimed at me. Old folks already gotta pee more often as it is—you tryin' to scare me into wettin' myself? Shakin' in my boots here, ain't I."

"Such insolence...! Just who do you think you—"

"—Olbart Dunkelkenn."

Taritta's face reddened with fury at Olbart, who breezily dodged the hostility stabbing at him. But cutting her off, it was the masked man, Abel, who spoke the old man's name and drew his attention.

Unsurprised by his own sudden arrival being named outright, Olbart turned his gaze to the man who hid his face.

And so began an exchange of mutual probing so parched it left an unmistakable impression—

"—"

Catching the conversation between Olbart and Abel at the edge of his awareness, Subaru worked to match the unchanged scene before him against his own memories.

It had all happened barely ten minutes ago. —Somewhere in there, he searched for a breakthrough against fate.

Can you actually do it?

"—"

As he searched his thoughts for a way through, a cold voice cut in like an ambush.

It was a voice born of impatience and frustration—none other than Natsuki Subaru's own weakness.

Can you actually do it? In that sorry state that just got your friends wiped out?

The harder he tried to recall what had just happened, the more mercilessly the voice whispered on.

Of Subaru's oversights. His blunders. His irreversible mistakes.

The second chance granted by Return by Death was not for undoing mistakes.

A mistake once made could not be taken back. All Subaru was permitted was atonement that could never fully repay the debt. That alone was the yield of the Authority Natsuki Subaru possessed—

"—!"

"Whoa!? What's gotten into you all of a sudden!?"

"Waahk! Wh-wh-what!?"

"Uu!? Aa, uu!"

To shake off the string of abuse whispered right in his ear, Subaru slapped both his own cheeks as hard as he could.

A dry crack rang through the room, and his sudden act drew shrieks from Olbart, Medium, and Rui. Al and Taritta didn't cry out, but they were equally startled.

Of course they were. He was sorry. But it had been necessary.

"—"

When he opened his closed eyes, the swimming, unsteady view before him had sharpened into clarity. Amid the group still startled by his bizarre behavior, one gaze quietly held steady, fixed on him.

Only the masked Abel showed no sign of agitation, standing composed and dignified just as he had when Olbart first appeared. —Though what went on in his mind was anyone's guess.

"Sorry for the scare. ...Just psyching myself up."

"Red cheeks and all, that's some spirit for a shrimp like you... Hm? Wait a sec. You're that girl in the red outfit from yesterday, ain'tcha. Had me spooked, wonderin' who you were."

Having seen through the identity behind Subaru's strange act, Olbart raised his thick eyebrows with interest.

Just as before, this was the moment Olbart first connected Subaru to Natsumi. As things stood, it would still be possible to pin Infantilization on Olbart as the culprit and steer things toward negotiation.

But that would mean repeating the same mistake. —Olbart was a dangerous man.

At the very least, revealing Abel's circumstances and trying to win him over as an ally was out of the question.

The moment he learned Abel was the true emperor, Vincent Vollachia, he was the sort to bare his years-long ambition and come for his life. —He could already be called a potential enemy.

Beyond that—

"...That startled me, but nice work, bro. Not even the head of the Shinobi could see through your disguise."

"Ohh, quite the feat, quite the feat! I'd love for the folks back in my village to take a lesson from that transformation. How about it—wanna be an instructor? You'd be welcome."

"—"

"—Though I s'pose a friendly little chat's outta the question now. Seems I got everyone way too on edge. Maybe my entrance was a bit much. My bad, my bad."

Olbart stuck out his tongue, owning up to his own misstep.

But the moment for the mood to warm or soften had long since passed. Subaru felt not the slightest inclination to loosen his guard against this old man.

—A face talks plenty, Olbart had said, right before he'd committed his atrocity.

No doubt this kindly-old-man act and easy, casual manner of speaking, and the silent intrusion into an unguarded room to startle them, were all techniques meant to draw out his targets' unguarded reactions.

If the Shinobi of this world were of the same nature as the "ninja" Subaru understood the word to mean, then everything about Olbart's behavior was nothing but traps, poison, and ninjutsu of one stripe or another.

He would spare no method to accomplish his own ends.

"But get too disrespectful with me, and, well, I'm an old geezer with not much time left. Thinkin' about how little I got left might just make me a touch rough."

"Violence...? You mean, in a situation like this, to fight us?"

"Ka-ha-ha-ha! Well, it's true I might just piss myself with that bow pointed at me. But fightin' ain't the only way to get what you want, now is it?"

"Um, what do you mean? What are you trying to say, Gramps?"

Taritta and Medium pressed Olbart, who hunched his small frame further and muttered with feeling. At the questions from the two whose height difference with him had now reversed, the old man twisted his cheek into a grin.

Then, with that hand, he pointed at Medium, and then at Al, who was moving to cut off his escape route.

"Bet it's a pain, bein' shrunk up here and there. That was me. One of my Shinobi Techniques. Wild and fun, ain't it?"

"Old man...!"

"Don't get sore, don't get sore, one-armed pup. If anything, you oughta thank me. Woulda been a shame if I'd gone and killed you by accident and couldn't hear you out, so I just shrunk you instead."

Al, who had started to close in at the outrageous remark, was held back by Olbart's words.

Al's throat caught, and understanding it to be true, he fell silent.

Yes, Olbart's claim was truth, and fact.

Had he wished it, Olbart could have killed them all in yesterday's battle at Beniruri Castle. Rather than the Infantilization technique, he could just as easily have driven a bare hand through their hearts.

That Subaru and the others had survived, if shrunken, was nothing but the old man's whim.

Or so Subaru had thought—

"Well, truth is, when I went at it pretty seriously tryin' to kill you all, every last attempt got blocked, so shrinkin' you was partly just me improvisin' outta desperation, y'know? Ain't that right, pup?"

"...Couldn't tell you what you're on about."

"Ka-ha-ha-ha! Can't tell me about your little trick, huh? Well, s'pose that's fair enough. If I could, I'd love to hear the details and write it up in my secret scrolls."

"Don't matter how many times you ask. —Couldn't tell you what you're on about. That's all."

Al answered in a muffled voice, his face covered by cloth.

It should have been a disappointing answer, yet Olbart nodded, saying "Is that so, is that so," as if he were enjoying Al's reaction.

"—?"

Subaru, meanwhile, couldn't make sense of what had just passed between the two of them.

In the keep of Beniruri Castle, amid that fierce battle, Al had fought like a man possessed, saving Subaru and Medium's lives. Perhaps Olbart, having faced him there, had seen something shine in his way of fighting.

But even granting that Olbart's words were true, Al's technique, hampered by the effects of Infantilization, would not work against a Shinobi.

Subaru had already seen the result of that with his own eyes, in the loop before this one.

From that, an absolute principle followed.

—A simple one: they must never, under any circumstances, fight Olbart.

"—"

Olbart sat there, leisurely turning his teacup over in both hands. Taritta had her bow drawn and nocked, Al and Medium braced for battle—and still, in the last loop, they'd all been killed without so much as a chance to resist.

An unbridgeable gulf in strength lay between Subaru's group and Olbart.

Fighting Olbart meant annihilation, without question. —No, perhaps if they committed entirely to fleeing, one or two might get away, but even that would be like crossing on a single fine thread.

No matter what, they had to get Olbart to leave here peaceably. And yet there was no time to discuss how with his companions.

"Why..."

He wanted to lament the futility of having been sent back to this exact moment.

He knew it was a horribly arrogant, self-serving thing to think, given he'd been granted a second chance through Return by Death, but if only he'd been sent back to before Olbart's appearance, he could have at least conferred with his companions.

He remembered how before, in the walled city of Guaral, when he'd repeated Return by Death again and again against Todd, he'd likewise had almost no time to prepare a countermeasure, and had suffered for it.

That time too, the restart had come after the threat had already set its sights on him.

This time, though the circumstances differed, the conditions were similar. If he had to put it into words, the restart point was something like being thrown into the same cage as a blood-starved beast.

Return by Death's rewind point had always been inconsistent, but this was a particularly severe case.

Or perhaps some abnormality had begun afflicting the Authority Subaru possessed—

"—That's a problem for later."

He could not afford the folly of cursing his lack of time and wasting what little of it he had.

After all, it was never only Subaru himself who paid the price for his own foolishness.

"Olbart... san. You—"

"—You said your technique shrank these people, didn't you."

"Ah—"

Forcing himself to shift gears, Subaru had tried to speak to Olbart.

To get through this situation, he had to weigh every word and action carefully. He'd wanted time to draw that line—to put it plainly, his words had been meant to stall.

But Abel cut him off, speaking right over him.

Caught off guard, a little put out, Subaru stared at the masked face and grasped his intent. Abel, without so much as a glance his way, was substituting for Subaru's role—that is, drawing Olbart's attention in order to buy Subaru the time he needed.

"Oh, right, right. Nearly forgot I still had to talk that over too. Seems old folks just can't help gettin' sidetracked. Cuts me to the bone, it does."

"You? Spare me the unfunny jokes. If you truly claim to have withered with age, you ought to have relinquished your rank as General long ago. That you have not shows your mind is already made up."

"Heh, quite the mouth on you. That mask must make it hard to see sideways, and yet you claim to see into my mind, do ya."

"Naturally."

At Abel's unhesitating nod, a glint of interest colored Olbart's gaze.

With Abel thus drawing out the exchange, there was no longer any doubt that his true aim was not to expose Olbart's nature, but to buy time.

Why he had realized Subaru needed time, Subaru didn't know—

"No time to be picky about it."

Setting aside his irritation at having his thoughts seen through, Subaru turned his mind to how to handle this.

If Olbart learned Abel's true identity, he would carry out his ambition of regicide then and there. Therefore, they could not reveal their hand to Olbart.

And yet, there was no way Olbart would simply leave with nothing offered in return.

"As a General, holding the rank of Third, you might be called one who stands near the empire's summit. And yet your nature is a greedy one. Even that position falls far short of satisfying you."

"Well, bein' called a greedy geezer sure beats bein' treated like a withered old fossil, but I don't reckon it wins me many friends either. Ka-ha-ha-ha!"

Abel spoke of Olbart's nature as he himself perceived it.

Olbart, laughing heartily, listened as though he found it amusing—but the darkness hidden deep within this Shinobi was something even Abel himself had misjudged.

—No, more accurately, he had failed to fully measure it.

"So? What is it this old grump, bursting with discontent, actually wants?"

"—The Seat of First."

"—"

Abel spoke the words quietly, but in a voice that did not waver.

The instant he heard it, the sound of Olbart's breath, like dry leaves rubbing together, went silent. Beneath his thick, long brows, the old man narrowed his eyes, locking gazes with Abel.

What manner of storm was raging within the Shinobi's heart, no one could say, but it was certain that Abel's remark had stirred something within him.

But that was not—

"One-armed pup, don't try anything funny. And you too, bow girl—your breathing gives you away plain as day."

—not because Olbart's attention had grown scattered, or because any opening had appeared.

Al and Taritta caught their breath at Olbart, who said this without so much as turning his face from Abel.

The instant Olbart's focus had settled on Abel, they must have gone searching for some faint opening. But even that probing gesture did not escape Olbart's seasoned eye.

"—Tch. You got eyes on the back of your head or somethin'?"

"Call it a lack of trainin' on your part. It's all about the years put in, the years. Mind you, from where I'm standin', pretty much everybody looks like a green amateur. That's just an old man's unbeatable logic, that is."

Olbart grinned, baring his teeth at the groaning Al, his gaze never once straying from Abel.

Al and the others' little tricks were something he could handle without even looking. —Though he said nothing of it, the bearing of a being beyond them made that plain enough.

And they held neither the strength nor the means, right now, to overturn that.

"Al."

Shaking his head, Subaru signaled Al not to make a move.

He didn't fully grasp Al's abilities and skill, but the man was probably someone whose technique specialized in defense. His skill at surviving while driven purely onto the defensive by enemy attacks, in the fight against Arakiya and yesterday in the keep, was something even Subaru had to call impressive.

But in his current, Infantilized state, he couldn't wield it to its fullest—he'd be killed.

"Still, hearin' you say I'm after the seat of First about knocked the wind outta me. Whatever made you come up with somethin' so out of the blue, masked pup?"

"It is plain enough. Your ambition is not so shallow as to dry up merely for having grown old. —A Shinobi pushes body and mind to their utmost limits; it is a state only a select few ever reach. And you are their head... Until the day you die, the moment of completion will never come."

"Hm."

Olbart folded his arms, giving a short nod at Abel's words.

It could have been taken as a miss, but the very thinness of his reaction only lent the claim more credibility.

In truth, Subaru knew Abel's guess was wrong.

Olbart's true intent, the Shinobi head's ultimate goal, was regicide—entirely apart from climbing to the top of the Nine Divine Generals.

And yet Olbart's reaction was also proof that Abel's remark hadn't missed the mark entirely.

At its core, what Abel had pointed out was the thirst that Olbart carried within him.

As a means of satisfying that thirst, Abel had guessed at the absolute mark of honor among the Nine Divine Generals whose ranks Olbart shared—seizing the Seat of First. —But it was no wonder he'd guessed that.

How could anyone guess that an old man holding the rank of General, leading subordinates as head of the Shinobi, burdened with all the entanglements that came from having lived so very long, secretly wished to cast aside everything he had gained across his entire life, just to leave his name in history as the Shinobi who slew the emperor?

Arrogant and self-centered, without the slightest trace of consideration for others though Abel was, he nevertheless, as the Vollachian Emperor—as one who held the throne—prioritized the preservation of his own life.

Because he understood that should he be lost, the empire itself would be shaken to its core.

That was an awareness Abel, as the Vollachian Emperor, inevitably carried—and at the same time, something that could be called a sense of responsibility, the kind everyone possessed, however thin it might seem beside his own.

—And that, Olbart Dunkelkenn did not have.

If his own ambition could be fulfilled, he didn't care if he died. And it made no difference to him if the fallout scattered ruin across everyone around him.

That extraordinary, self-destructive mindset was the darkness in Olbart that Abel had failed to fully read.

But—

"—Olbart-san, isn't your real target not the Seat of First, but the emperor himself?"

—Natsuki Subaru already knew the darkness within Olbart Dunkelkenn.

△▼△▼△▼△

—In that instant, the color of the air, its scent, the very feel of it against the skin, changed.

"—"

Olbart, who hadn't so much as glanced at Al and Taritta's hostility, backed as it was by real intent to act, now turned his gaze in reaction to the words of Subaru, now a small child.

Still holding his teacup in one hand, the old man stroked his chin with the other as he turned to look at Subaru.

He could not simply let pass the words Subaru had just spoken.

"Kid, you keep buttin' in outta nowhere. Gave me a start, that did."

"Was it that I spoke up suddenly that startled you? Or..."

"You askin' if I'm startled 'cause you hit the nail on the head? If you're bein' serious about that, then I must be one hell of a scary fella, ka-ha-ha-ha!"

"—"

Olbart opened his mouth, trying to laugh Subaru's words boisterously away. But Subaru was certain his true feelings were nowhere near as calm as they appeared.

After all, Subaru had pointed straight at the years-long ambition Olbart himself had once spoken of.

And moreover, at the darkness that not even Abel's sharp eye had seen through, right up until the moment it was nearly acted upon.

"Targetin' the emperor—you mean killin' him? Bro, that's quite the leap. Even if I did somethin' like that—"

"It'd be meaningless. Yeah, I get it. All it'd earn you is infamy, no upside to it."

"Well, I ain't sayin' I can't understand the idea that infamy beats bein' unknown. But like the one-armed pup says, that's a bit of a leap of a notion."

"But you're not denying it outright either, are you, Olbart-san."

Subaru pressed the point, as Al hardened his voice in disbelief and Olbart voiced his agreement.

Honestly, he understood that pushing further meant leaping into dangerous territory, and still he couldn't stop.

While Abel drew out the dialogue with Olbart, Subaru too had been turning thoughts over in his mind.

Since Olbart's ambition aimed at regicide, they could not reveal Abel's true identity, nor could they tell him the false emperor's identity was Chisha. Inevitably, their negotiable cards had vanished.

With that the case, the method Subaru had hit upon to keep Olbart's hand from tearing off Abel's mask was a tactic diametrically opposed to true negotiation—

—namely, not revealing their own hand, but exposing Olbart's.

"Our goal is exactly what we told you in the keep yesterday. To drag down the man currently sitting the throne, Emperor Vincent Vollachia. That should line up with what you're after too."

"Wait, wait, don't go makin' me out to be some grand traitor to the realm on your own say-so. Not a moment's peace with you, kid—gonna have me runnin' to the privy from nerves at this rate."

"But you're not seriously denying it or telling me to stop. Even though you're one of the Generals, directly under the emperor."

Subaru pressed forward, working to reel in the thread of possible futures snagged at his fingertips.

Of course, the threat Olbart posed hadn't lessened, nor had the danger eased in the slightest. He knew full well that one wrong move here would turn this room into a bloodbath.

But Subaru was convinced this was the kind of conversation that could only happen here, now.

In the previous loop's massacre too, it was only after Abel's true identity had come to light that Olbart decided to strike.

For Olbart, that had been a kind of suicide bombing—killing Abel there would fulfill the purpose of his entire life.

Without that certainty, Olbart wouldn't lose his temper mid-negotiation.

Because even as a beast of ambition without a shred of responsibility or loyalty, he would still play out his role as one of the Nine Divine Generals, faithfully obeying the false emperor's order of "no interference."

"Well, His Excellency's the one who got declared war on right to his face, and he's the one who told me not to interfere. I ain't like Kafma, flyin' off the handle over His Excellency at the drop of a hat. Thinkin' ahead, makin' an enemy of the fox girl in this city ain't too clever a move either."

Sure enough, Olbart, even upon hearing Subaru's borderline-outrageous accusation, showed no sign of resorting to force.

That said, he wasn't exactly pleased with Subaru's persistence either, and his attitude was naturally beginning to make Abel and the others wonder about the truth behind this outlandish notion of the Shinobi's ambition.

And then—

"—So that is the intent within you that I could not fully gauge."

"Yare yare, you lot just don't listen, do ya. At this rate, seems there's nothin' left for me to do but throw up my hands and give up on talkin' further."

Amid the others, still half-convinced, Abel was the first to swallow the unbelievable notion whole.

Pierced by that gaze, Olbart tossed aside his excuses as though it were too much bother. To begin with, Olbart had no reason to work hard at winning Subaru and the others over here.

Naturally, Olbart's attitude only heightened the tension among Al and the other fighters—

"Quit it, quit it, startin' somethin' would only cost you. Let me be clear—the reason I ain't gettin' rough ain't 'cause I'm bein' kind. It's 'cause I was told not to. And even then, it's more like, if you strike first, I'll strike back, that kinda feelin'."

"Grh... That may... be true, but..."

Even a stance of non-aggression would be void the instant they made a move. At Olbart's warning, Taritta's cheeks stiffened, and her narrow eyes swept over Subaru and Medium.

Seeing that, Olbart gave an "ah," nodding as if in understanding, and said,

"So you want your shrunken friends turned back, that's the deal, is it? Ain't that askin' a bit much? I was plannin' on headin' off without doin' a thing, y'know?"

"But you're the one who made us little, aren't you, Gramps? Or is there someone besides you who can turn us back?"

"Never really thought about it, but... say, girl, how old are you really?"

"Me? I think I was twenty?"

"Well then, wait ten years and you'll probably grow back, won't ya? Not that I'd know."

Medium, who had raised her hand to ask, flushed at the blunt, dismissive answer. Olbart's stance was clearly to dodge the question in a cloud of smoke, but an answer like that left Subaru and the others at a loss.

Still, the fact remained they had no means to force an answer out of him, nor any way to make him restore them.

Even so—

"You get that we can't just say 'oh, okay' and let you go, right? Olbart-san, could you humor us with a bit more conversation..."

"Nope. After all, you're the one who went and treated me like some nasty old man. I'm a geezer with not much time left, and I've got the right to choose who I spend that little time talkin' to—"

"—Then I shall give you the chance to kill the emperor."

Right after, Subaru caught his breath at the air changing once again, and looked at Abel, who had spoken.

None other than Abel had placed his own life on the Balance with that declaration, and Olbart scratched his head, saying, "Oi, oi,"

"How many times I gotta tell you your whole premise is off, you lot. Havin' worse memory and forgettin' things faster than me—ain't that pretty bad?"

"If your aim were truly the emperor's life, you would have had opportunities enough before now. And yet you never acted. —Because the flames of the Sunlight Sword protect the emperor."

"Ah—"

"I am willing to reveal how those flames may be overcome."

Olbart's expression underwent a change that drew a clear line against all his prior pretense.

The old man raised his brows, gazing at the masked Abel with unmistakable astonishment. Abel, for his part, had risen from his chair and stood there, imposing.

"The Sunlight Sword's... flames..."

What exactly that meant, Subaru couldn't say for certain.

But it was a fact that, for Olbart, the shock was enough to tear away the facade of emotion he'd worn all along, letting what lay behind it show through.

Given the flow of the conversation, it was some secret that protected the Vollachian Emperor, and it was precisely because of it that Olbart's ambition had never borne fruit until now.

Abel had just offered to reveal the means of removing that shackle.

"—I already figured you for someone who won't show his face and doesn't spare an old man's feelings, but just who in blazes are you. That's not the kind of thing you go spreadin' around, even as a joke."

"You think someone spouting sophistry or jokes here could raise the banner of revolt against this mighty empire? If they could, they would have to be either a madman who has lost his senses, or a figure of truly extraordinary nerve."

"And you're sayin' you're neither of those?"

"Naturally."

Abel answered Olbart's question tersely.

Whether he was serious or merely being sophistic, even Subaru couldn't judge. Or perhaps this man called Abel was, in some sense, all three at once—the real thing, the madman, and the extraordinary figure.

"—"

At Abel's answer, Olbart fell silent for a moment.

Furrowing his thick brows, he brought his now nearly cold tea to his lips. Then, setting the emptied cup gently on the table beside him, he said,

"If I recall, it was the bell at the hour of fire, wasn't it?"

"Eh?"

Caught off guard by the sudden question, Subaru let out a dumbfounded noise.

But it was Medium who quickly nodded, saying "Yup, that's right~." With her shrunken body, she pointed out the window, indicating Beniruri Castle's imposing form, and said,

"Yorna-chan's callin' for us, so it'd be real trouble if we stayed this little."

"Yorna-chan! Ka-ha-ha-ha! You got some nerve, girl."

"—?"

Subaru tilted his head, awed by the sheer nerve it took for Medium to casually slap a -chan onto even Yorna's name.

Olbart, meanwhile, seemed just as taken aback, and as he glanced toward Beniruri Castle where Medium had pointed, he murmured, "Well now."

"Now, whether or not I'm actually after His Excellency's life, that's a separate matter, but honestly, I am curious how to get past the Sunlight Sword that guards the Vollachian Emperor."

"—! Then—"

"Whoa, whoa, gettin' ahead of yourself there. I know I'm the one who got your hopes up, but I got plenty of things I still gotta think through. —So how about we make a wager?"

"...A wager?"

"That's right, a wager. You like those, don'tcha? Bein' a boy and all."

Olbart said this, jabbing a finger right in front of the nose of Subaru, who had started to lean forward.

The motion was too fast for Subaru's eyes to follow at all, and he froze rigid.

Olbart, prefacing it with the word "wager," was about to offer up some kind of condition.

Whether that would take the shape Subaru wanted—one that avoided the massacre and improved this deadlocked situation—he had no certainty—

"And if I said I refuse?"

"Then you won't get a single thing you're after, will ya."

"—"

"And by the way, seein' as you lot are holdin' somethin' I've taken a likin' to, from here on out my Shinobi Techniques'll be comin' after you, day and night, without so much as a wink of sleep."

Olbart declared this coldly, as though it were only natural.

A chill ran through Subaru at the sight of the dark-hued emotion writhing deep within the old man's thin, clouded eyes, even as a smile lingered on his face.

However mild and amiable the conversation appeared on the surface, hidden beneath it lay the pitch-black, merciless way of a Shinobi—one that took other lives without hesitation.

"And what shall the terms of this wager be."

By the point conditions had already been laid out, there was no longer any avoiding a wager with Olbart. Abel, sensing the same, asked this in Subaru's stead, since Subaru's mouth wouldn't open.

At the question, Olbart tilted his head, saying "Let's see," and continued,

"Ain't much time before the fox girl's summons, and I've been told not to lay a hand on you lot anyway, so this is trickier than you'd think... Ah, but here's just the thing."

"That 'just the thing'—good for you, Olbart-san? Or good for us?"

"Both, obviously. Me and you lot both."

Olbart grinned, baring his teeth at Subaru's tense question, and held up both hands.

Then, to Subaru and the others, braced as they were, he said:

"—Tag."

"—"

"I play it with the young ones back in the village all the time. Simple, easy to understand—good, ain't it?"
