Arc 6 Chapter 89: "Shaula"

Crumbling, crumbling, spilling away. Crumbling, crumbling, flaking away. Crumbling, crumbling, fading away.

Crumbling, crumbling, crumbling, and everything grows distant, glimmering.

△▼△▼△▼△

—Outside the tower, the stampede of countless Witchbeasts surging forward, broken through by the Witchbeast Tamer's valiant fight. —On the second floor, the defeat of Reid Astrea, who had stolen Roy Alphard's body. —On the fourth floor, the defeat of that hateful, blasphemous Great Sin Archbishop, Lye Batenkaitos, an old score finally settled. —On the first floor, the yet-unseen, unknown "Trial," broken through by Emilia's valiant struggle.

In conquering the Pleiades Watchtower, the multiple trials laid in wait had all been cleared. This too was the result of their companions banding together as one, trusting each other and combining their strength. To put it the way Emilia would, it could be called the fruit of "everybody getting along."

Thanks to that, they had been able to make it this far. Which meant all that remained was—

"—If we can't win with everyone together, what would even be the point!"

At the very start, when they'd first set out for the Pleiades Watchtower, Emilia had said it. That they weren't going to lose something, but to reclaim what had already been lost. Having agreed with those words of hers, they all had to make it back safely from the sea of sand together. And if the number of people on the way back happened to be more than on the way there, well, that was simply how it would be.

Subaru, Emilia, Beatrice, Ram, Rem, Meili, Anastasia=Echidna, Julius and Patrasche—and it would be fine to add Shaula to that list too. No matter how noisy or how much of a pain the trip back turned out to be, that was fine by him.

"—Here I go!"

Of the two Spirit Knights standing side by side, it was Julius, naturally, who took the first strike. Having briefly stepped away from treatment and called back the Qua he'd entrusted to healing, Julius once more borrowed the strength of his six spirits and wrapped himself in aurora light. Judging from the effect of Kor Leonis, in exchange for that vastly swelling glow of Od, both Julius himself and his spirits must be bearing a considerable burden. He sure knew how to make it look good. But then, none of them had ever planned on dragging this fight out.

"—A short, decisive battle."

Trailing a streak of rainbow light, Julius seemed to fly across the sand. He closed the distance in an instant, and his slash bore down on the Crimson Scorpion. It caught the blow with its ferocious compound eyes and swatted it away with claws and tail stinger raging like a storm. And yet the scorpion's carapace, where it met the rainbow blade, let off white smoke at the briefest touch.

It was melting. Julius's slashes, transformed into rainbow light, kept his aurora-wrapped magic sword Al Clarista in constant effect—it wouldn't even properly allow itself to be blocked as a form of defense. On top of that, the rainbow light wrapped around his entire body wasn't merely for flashy show, either.

"—"

The scorpion's tail stinger, launched at point-blank range—the thrust of "death" that had killed Subaru more than ten times over, and had once been the cause of death for both Emilia and Julius as well—Hell's Snipe. Rather than parry it with his sword, Julius let his aurora's defensive power absorb it. The thin aurora wrapped over his entire body was, in turn, an application of the magical concept behind Al Clauselia, the spell that conjured a wall of rainbow light. In other words, offense and defense fused as one—a transcendent swordsman who deserved the name "Rainbow Spirit Knight" had been born.

"—!!"

With its finishing blow failing to land and even its defenses breached, the Crimson Scorpion was having a wretched time of it. But even a giant Witchbeast that had spent four hundred years as the tower's Star Watch had its pride. It made the glow of its blazing red carapace even more vivid, released the heat building within its claws, and slammed it down on Julius with tremendous force, sending the Rainbow Knight flying.

Its claws took on searing heat, and the air around the Crimson Scorpion began to warp under the sheer ferocity of that heat. Scorching claws—if Subaru were the one naming it, he'd call it the "Jesus Scissors Hellfire Form."

He had to hand it to it for busting out a new move at this stage, going out of its way to hype up the final battle, but from the receiving end, he could really do without that kind of growth potential. An enemy character breaking its shell open to power up mid-fight was not something anyone here wanted right now.

"—Minya!!"

The blazing red claws swept through the air, and as they took aim at Julius, a purple arrow shot in from the side to interfere. To butt into a top-tier clash like the one between Julius and the Crimson Scorpion, Subaru alone lacked the strength. While Beatrice's interjection supported Julius from the flank, Subaru searched for his own opening.

"—"

Careful with his positioning so as not to die from the shockwaves alone, Subaru glanced toward the sand swept clear by whirling dust—toward the pack of Witchbeasts driven back from there. In this environment that was the Augria Sand Dunes, the damage and the constant vigilance the Witchbeasts had brought to their journey had been an especially large factor. The sight of them keeping a fixed distance from the tower, just watching this way, was uncanny.

The clouds over the Watchtower had cleared, and on the first floor, Emilia had presumably rewritten the tower's rules. With that now in effect, had something like a measure to keep the Witchbeasts away kicked in? It seemed the Watchtower had originally been built so Witchbeasts wouldn't approach it in the first place, which meant that earlier stampede had been the unnatural state of affairs. Either way, not having the Witchbeasts butting in was a relief for Subaru and the others, who wanted nothing more than to bring the Crimson Scorpion—Shaula—back to her senses.

"—Subaru!!"

"Ah"

The voice reached Subaru as he cut diagonally across the sea of sand, aiming for a better position. When he looked up wondering what had happened, he saw the Crimson Scorpion, having dodged Julius's thrust, carelessly coming down to land right beside him. At Beatrice's cry, hand still linked with his, Subaru skidded to an abrupt halt. But the Witchbeast's tail swung as if swatting away an insect, and in that single blow Subaru sensed "death."

"Guts—!" "—Muraku!"

Subaru's judgment and Beatrice's judgment overlapped in the same instant. The pull of gravity lightened, and the two of them became as light as cotton candy. At the same moment, the whip Subaru had cracked out wound around the base of the scorpion's tail. The next instant, thrown by the force of the swinging tail, Subaru and Beatrice went spinning through the air at tremendous speed.

"Wah—" "Buyan!?"

Not swung around so much as flung off. The instant Subaru felt his feet leave the ground, he and Beatrice slammed full-body into the sand. Light as cotton candy or not, had the ground beneath been solid, that force and impact would have shattered them outright—fortunately, since it was sand, they only got the wind knocked out of them.

"You'll have to give up on the follow-up strike—!"

"—"

Still, the Crimson Scorpion took aim at Subaru and Beatrice, buried in sand. But rainbow light cut across its path to interfere, and a clash of scorching heat and aurora light unfolded.

Light scattered, and each time it did the sea of sand split open, shockwaves kicking up sandy winds beneath the clear sky. Destructive power, evenly matched. Speed, Julius's advantage. Stamina, the Crimson Scorpion's—with no decisive blow landing, they risked simply running out of time and being overwhelmed.

"—Ptoo, ptoo! Damn it, we've gotta do something!" "—Ptoo, ptoo! We have to find a path to victory, I suppose!"

Pulling themselves out of the sand together, Subaru and Beatrice spat out sand with the same gesture. With tears in both their eyes, they cast about for some way to make Julius's rainbow, and their own voices, reach Shaula.

There were three possibilities worth clinging to— Julius achieving yet another awakening, kicking the Crimson Scorpion aside for an infuriating but decisive victory. Subaru and Beatrice completing some new technique, shutting the Crimson Scorpion down for the team's victory. Or Emilia suddenly dropping out of the sky, her cuteness bringing peace to the world in a blast of love and peace.

"I'd like to bet on the first one, leaning a little toward the third..."

As already established, he didn't have much faith in his own awakening, and he wasn't counting on the convenient timing of Emilia showing up here either. Which left Julius's raw strength spiking as the best bet by far—

"Except he's already pulled that off once, so expecting it back-to-back is asking too much..."

At a speed Subaru's eyes couldn't even follow, the sword-clash between Julius and the Crimson Scorpion continued. Watching a scene of destruction where getting too close risked being scorched by the shockwaves alone, Subaru cast about for his next move.

In the end, there was nothing to do but fight using every card in hand, exhausted to the last. So at the very least, don't overlook a single card you're holding—see Natsuki Subaru through to the end.

"Think, think, think..."

Pushing his mind to full throttle, he reeled in something real—not wishful thinking, not a pipe dream. And as he searched through the cards he had to work with, Subaru realized something. That there was one last card, still unused, still left in his hand.

"—Beako!"

"Have you thought of something, I suppose!"

As if she'd been waiting for exactly this, Beatrice answered Subaru's call. Blessed with a partner who understood him so well, lucky man that he was, Subaru said "Yeah!" and gripped her small hand again, nodding firmly. And then—

"—I'm putting everything this whole journey's given us into this, right here!"

Crumbling, crumbling, spilling away. Crumbling, crumbling, flaking away. Crumbling, crumbling, fading away.

Crumbling, crumbling, crumbling, and everything grows distant, glimmering.

Right before her eyes, rainbow light tore past with tremendous force, and on pure instinct she swung both arms to bat it away. The blazing red claws at the ends of both her hands held enough firepower to burn through absolutely anything—rock or steel, it made no difference, they'd cut through it like butter, guaranteed.

"Well, not that I really know what butter even is, ssu."

Talking based on bits of secondhand knowledge, she closed in on the glimmering target. But the place was the wide-open sea of sand, with nothing in the way to work with, so she couldn't quite finish it off. In a place with no escape, or in a long-range fight, this would've been her stage alone—

"A sniper's always a lonely thing, ssu, you know."

This too was something she'd heard—apparently a sniper, a marksman, was someone who held still and waited until the prey was brought down. So she waited. She too kept on waiting, sniper's pride held close to her chest.

Day after day, she stared into the distance, stared at whoever might come to the tower, and kept on waiting.

There was a rule. A rule that bound her to this tower. There were times she'd found it a bother, but she also thought that without it, forgetful as she was, she'd have forgotten all sorts of things right away.

Walking together, talking together, the days spent and the feelings exchanged—all of it, everything.

"Ahh... that, I don't like, ssu."

Everything, every single thing, had left her behind. If told to wait, she'd wait forever—but if she was going to be made to wait, she wanted them to come back. As long as they came back, she could wait forever. So—

"I was so happy that Master came back, ssu."

Because everyone, everyone had vanished. She'd stopped being able to tell whether she should believe those words, that they'd come back. Whether she'd waited because she believed, or waited out of sheer inertia—she didn't even know the answer to that. Hadn't even thought about it. Didn't need to think about it, either. Because before she could rot away, the promise had been kept.

"I'm a lucky one, ssu, Master."

So please don't go. It'd be enough if you just stayed here with her forever. Since she wasn't alone anymore, she was done being a sniper. And for graduating from being a sniper, she figured she ought to get some kind of reward.

"No more being left behind, ssu, Master. ...I want to be loved too, ssu."

Because everything, every single thing, had left her behind. This time, she wanted to follow—anywhere, forever. So—

"I want you to love me, ssu. —Master."

△▼△▼△▼△

Its body trembling, the glow of the Crimson Scorpion's carapace grew even more vivid. It might have been read as a more pronounced version of an "aggression color," but to Subaru, it looked like something else.

That vivid red looked like the result of Shaula screaming and crying. For four hundred years she had locked her feelings away, kept to this tower in obedience to what she'd been told—and now the being called Shaula was bursting with overflowing feeling, shining bright.

Red was passion. Red was raging emotion. Red was the color of a "love" that could not be held back. If the Crimson Scorpion glowed red, it was surely because she loved someone, and wished to be loved in turn.

"—They say women born under Scorpio run deep with love, you know!!"

Shouting with all his strength, kicking off the sand, Subaru swung his shoulder wide and cracked his whip.

"—"

His target was the fickle Crimson Scorpion, back turned to him as it traded blows with Julius. Sorry to interrupt while she was busy, but he'd insist with his whip that the Master she was so fixated on was over here. When she'd come on strong before, he'd brushed her off as a nuisance—but—

"—But then seeing her go off to another man leaves a guy all bent out of shape—that's just how it is—!!" "Even for you, Subaru, that's a terrible thing to say, I suppose!"

Pushed forward by the sharp words of Beatrice riding on his shoulders, Subaru's whip found its mark—wrapping cleanly around the base of the Crimson Scorpion's tail. Except that changed nothing about the situation from a moment ago, when he'd been swung around and buried in sand. Perhaps because of how one-sided that earlier tug-of-war had been, the Crimson Scorpion kept its focus on Julius, leaving Subaru and the others as an afterthought.

Subaru and his group were weak. —That was the perception he intended to exploit.

"El Vita—!!" "Guoooh!!"

Beatrice cast the spell, and the moment its effect took hold of Subaru's whole body, a tremendous weight buried both his feet in the sand. The opposite of Muraku, which lightened gravity's pull, Vita increased it—bumping his weight class up from a top-division wrestler to a full-blown yokozuna, to stand against the Crimson Scorpion's tail. But of course, even that wasn't enough. However heavy he got, it topped out at a bit over a hundred kilos or so—nowhere near a match for Shaula's monstrous strength, capable of hauling a Dragon Carriage around with ease. So—

"—This is the moment! Do it!!"

Feet buried in sand, arms tensed with all his strength, Subaru shouted that out. The next instant, Subaru's body—on the verge of being yanked off his feet by the power of the Crimson Scorpion's tail—held fast to the ground. The reason was plain: the pulling forces had reached an equilibrium. Between the Crimson Scorpion and Subaru—no, between it and everyone with Subaru.

"——!!"

In front of Subaru, holding steady, the one gripping the taut whip was the Ravenous Horse King, its grotesque, massive frame having burst into the tug-of-war. And the Ravenous Horse King wasn't the only one to join this unprecedented tug-of-war. A bear wreathed head to toe in flowers, a mole with wings, a suspicious-looking serpent—all of them joined in.

Witchbeasts that should have been sworn enemies were lending their strength to Subaru's fight. And the one who could bring that about was—

"...Honestly, you really work a girl to the bone."

The voice belonged to a girl wrapped in a sullen, listless air. The one who'd spoken was sitting on the sand, breathing hard, her face drained of color—Meili. She set her lovely profile with resolve and let out one long, deep breath. Then, just like that, she clapped her hands hard, calling, "There now—"

"Come on, everyone. It'd be such a waste to just stand there watching."

A beat behind Meili's clapped hands, the sea of sand shook. It was the stamping and howling of the Witchbeasts still surging forward, ruled by the one who commanded this monstrous realm known as the Augria Sand Dunes—the "Witchbeast Tamer"—no, that title wasn't fitting anymore. This was no phenomenon on the scale of a mere "Witchbeast Tamer." Using the power of her Divine Protection of Demon Manipulation to bend the packs of Witchbeasts occupying the Augria Sand Dunes entirely to her will—that was a sight that no longer fit under so humble a title as "Witchbeast Tamer."

It was the return of a being who had once ravaged the southern Empire—the reincarnation of the "Mother of Witchbeasts." This was her showing her true colors, repurposing the very stampede to make this battle a proper all-out war.

"—"

Meili's expression was one of pain, but there was a trick behind how she'd recovered consciousness and gotten back on her feet enough to join this final battle. Naturally, it was because Subaru had taken on Meili's damage through Kor Leonis and redistributed it—to the last card Subaru held.

It wasn't Emilia, wasn't Beatrice, wasn't Ram. Not Julius, not Echidna, not Patrasche or Meili, and certainly not the sleeping Rem. The final collaborator in conquering these Augria Sand Dunes—

"—Sorry for dragging you into this, Jaian! Lend a hand!!"

Far away, beneath the Watchtower—on the sixth floor, left behind—the Earth Dragon Jaian's presence had registered within Kor Leonis, and Subaru had gone ahead with redistributing the burden onto him. It was a decision that hurt terribly to make, but what hurt even more was that Jaian had met the conditions for Subaru's Kor Leonis to redistribute the burden onto him at all. Which meant that, just like Beatrice and the rest of his companions, Jaian too had wanted to support Subaru.

Leaning on Jaian's movie-worthy devotion, Subaru had the Earth Dragon shoulder the bulk of Meili's burden through himself. That was the trick behind how Meili had been able to stand back up. And that was also why the Crimson Scorpion had unexpectedly lost the contest of strength. And that meant—

"—That, right there, is what beat you, Shaula."

Having won, literally, an all-out war fought with the borrowed strength of both Witchbeasts and an Earth Dragon, at Subaru's words the Crimson Scorpion's feet lifted off the ground. That instant was one the "Finest Knight" would never let slip by.

The rainbow slash he unleashed cut clean through the base of the Crimson Scorpion's tail, still locked in that contest of strength.

"—"

The severed tail burst apart, spreading destruction like a self-amputation, but the rainbow light didn't let it through. Blocked, its trump card sealed away, the Crimson Scorpion swung its claw down at Julius's back with furious force. But an attack made in shaken desperation—after losing the contest of strength and having its tail cut away—was no match for a knight who had broken through his own wall by overcoming a fight with the first-generation "Sword Saint."

"Hah—!"

An arcing slash cleanly severed the left claw at its brittle joint. Even as the force of that flying limb shook its body, the remaining right claw closed in around Julius. And just as the claw began to close, meaning to cleave his tall frame clean through the torso—

"—Al Clanvel."

An instant before it closed, the aurora light wrapped around Julius's entire body unraveled and expanded all at once. In the moment he shed the rainbow armor he'd worn, the explosively expanding aurora light burst the nearly-closed claw open from the inside, blowing it clean off at the root.

"——!!"

Buffeted by the resulting shockwave, the Crimson Scorpion's massive body was thrown into the air. Rolling wildly across the sand, the Witchbeast had lost its tail and both claws, left in a battered, wretched state. And then, surrounding the overturned Crimson Scorpion, the other Witchbeasts moved in to pin it down.

With all eight of its legs pinned down and its movement sealed, the Crimson Scorpion writhed as if refusing the end closing in on it, its sharp-fanged head still moving. Given the Crimson Scorpion's adaptability, it wouldn't have been strange for it to spawn some new technique from here and grow past even this desperate spot—but—

"—This is as far as it goes, Shaula."

Subaru stood squarely in front of the writhing Crimson Scorpion, so as to be reflected in every one of its many eyes. Weapons lost, legs pinned, driven into a pitiful state—finishing it off would have been easy enough now, but that wasn't what Subaru wanted. Even having pushed it this far, he still didn't know what the right answer was—but—

"Meili."

"...I really do wonder what you boys would do without me around."

Called by name, Meili let out a sigh and turned toward the Crimson Scorpion. Standing beside Subaru, she snapped her fingers along with a breath. And once she'd drawn the focus of those many eyes onto herself—

"Now, who are you? A scary red scorpion? Or maybe..."

"—"

"Or maybe, who could you really be?"

At the question, the movement of the Crimson Scorpion's many eyes slowed. They trembled, gazing quietly at Meili, and then its line of sight shifted over to Subaru. Those hostile red eyes slowly began to change color.

"Shaula."

Along with the blazing red glow of its carapace, everything began to settle. The eyes turned green, the carapace turned black, gradually calming, until at last—

"—Shaula!"

At last—

Crumbling, crumbling, spilling away. Crumbling, crumbling, flaking away. Crumbling, crumbling, fading away.

Crumbling, crumbling, crumbling, and everything grows distant, glimmering. Crumbling, crumbling, crumbling, and everything looks distant, glimmering.

Everything spills, flakes away, fades, and looks distant, so distant, glimmering. Left behind, the memories grow far away, and yet they truly shone. Because those days were everything that mattered, she could throw herself desperately into scooping them back up.

"—"

"Do you remember, Master? You told me you'd definitely come back, told me to wait, and then you were gone, ssu."

Tilting her head slightly, Shaula—sitting hugging her knees—asked him that. At her manner, as if reminiscing over some fond old memory, Subaru shook his head.

"I don't remember. I mean, I keep telling you I don't know anything about it. Don't make me say it over and over."

"Well, can't be helped, ssu. Master's forgetfulness got handed down to me, ssu. You and me, Master, we're two of a kind, ssu."

"That's not something to feel good about! Well, I'll admit our sense of language's pretty close, though."

Even so, since she used up his own world's expressions so thoroughly, that sense of familiarity he couldn't help feeling was, in its own way, understandable. Subaru wasn't nearly as charming as her, wasn't as cute, wasn't as devoted. He couldn't have gone on serving someone who'd left him behind for four hundred years.

"I'm not a patient guy. I'm impatient, I want results fast. If I could at least stay right by someone's side the whole time, maybe I'd feel like putting up with it, but..."

"Ahh, that's no good at all, Master. Listen up, ssu. There's a famous saying, ssu. Love is patience, ssu!"

"Isn't that 'beauty is pain,' not love!? That sounds like the slogan of some woman devoted to showering her man with gifts!?"

"It's all to fulfill these swelling feelings deep in my chest, ssu. Go ahead and laugh at me, call me a fool, a pitiful girl, I don't mind, ssu. That smile of yours is lovely too... ssu."

"No, I can't laugh at that. Look, I'm getting a little teary-eyed here."

"Let's see, let's see, ssu."

Pointing at his own face as he said that, Subaru found Shaula leaning in close. Shaula rose up with a sudden burst of momentum, her face coming near enough that he could feel her breath, and up close he took in the fine-boned shape of her face all over again.

Large, sharply narrow eyes with a striking gaze, a nose bridge running clean and straight. Long lashes, and skin so fine-grained it was hard to believe she'd spent so long in the sea of sand—her ever-shifting expressions made it easy to miss, but the overall shape of her was one where elegant beauty stood out more than mere cuteness.

A being given the name of a star, bearing the fate of waiting here in this place, forever, for someone she loved.

"Huh? Master, that's not just teary eyes, are you actually crying a little, ssu?"

"...Your Master's a real piece of trash. I want to punch him out myself, with these hands."

"That's the kind of thing I have to watch with the most complicated feelings ever, ssu! Master knocking out Master—what even is that situation, ssu!? ...Geez."

Lips trembling, Subaru squeezed his eyes shut. Something hot, welling up hot, was pushed out from behind his eyelids and ran down his cheek. Seeing that clear droplet, Shaula murmured "Geez" once more, quietly—and then—

"—"

Almost like a surprise attack, something damp brushed against Subaru's cheek as his eyes stayed shut. When he opened his eyes, Shaula's face was slowly pulling away. Grinning mischievously, she pressed a finger to her own lips and gave a quick flash of her red tongue.

"...Master's bodily fluids, salty and sweet, ssu."

"...The way you put that..."

"It doesn't matter how I put it, ssu. My feelings are exactly what I'm expressing with my whole body, heart and soul, ssu. —I love you, Master, ssu."

She said "I love you" over and over. No one who knew the weight behind it could ever call those words light.

The reason Shaula said "I love you" at every opportunity was because love overflowed from her. Because the words she'd wanted, for so, so long, to convey kept spilling out from inside her. Because for four hundred years, her wish to love and to be loved had never stopped—

"I love you, ssu, Master."

"...I'm not going to tell you I love you."

"I know, ssu. Master's mean, and a shy, bashful type, ssu. But I like that about you too, ssu. I'm head over heels, ssu. Only you, ssu."

"—"

Time had left her behind, and the role forced upon her had bound her body like chains. When that role threatened to hurt someone she loved, she had cried and begged for "death." Rather than hurt him, she'd rather be lost. To that sobbing girl, what had he said.

He'd told her he wouldn't let her go on crying.

"I'm not, going to tell you, I love you..."

"...That's fine, ssu. As much as Master won't say it, I'll just keep saying it, ssu. Then someday, surely, the day will come when you'll want to throw it right back at me, ssu."

"Someday, huh... that's a long-winded way of putting it. You planning to wait another four hundred years?"

"You think so? Four hundred years went by in no time at all, ssu."

There had been a time she'd cried out in an anguished voice that she'd been waiting the whole time.

Left behind by the passage of time, bound and held hostage by her own affection, she had cried out that she'd been lonely the whole while. The current her had no way of knowing there had been a world where she'd bared that heart of hers like that. So behind that breezy, carefree manner of hers, how fiercely must the feelings raging in her chest have been.

He'd sworn he wouldn't let her cry. Even now, he had no intention of breaking that. So—he'd wanted her to cry. To cry, and to tell him it still wasn't enough.

If she would cry and cry, wail and sob, break down in tears— then Natsuki Subaru, not as any Master at all, would run himself ragged with everything he had to stop those tears. And yet—

"Four hundred years was like the day after tomorrow, ssu."

Without a hint of the Crimson Scorpion's form showing through, she simply smiled as a beautiful girl. Beautiful enough to make him stare without meaning to, fragile enough that she might crumble at the slightest touch, Shaula's pale cheeks colored faintly, and with the face of a girl in love, she went on, "Because—"

The girl in love went on with "Because—"

"—Because I loved even the time I spent waiting, ssu."

"—"

"Hey, Master. So, again, someday—"

Crumbling, crumbling, spilling away. Crumbling, crumbling, flaking away. Crumbling, crumbling, fading away.

Crumbling, crumbling, crumbling, and everything grows distant, glimmering. Crumbling, crumbling, crumbling, and everything looks distant, glimmering.

"—Shaula"

Crumbling, bit by bit, part of the peeling carapace turned to dust on the sand. It didn't stop at just one part—it spread to every piece that flaked away.

The severed tail, the severed claws, the many legs the Witchbeasts had been pinning down, and the head cradled in Natsuki Subaru's arms—all of it, every part—

"...Has she finished playing her part, I suppose."

As Subaru tried to gather up what he held to his chest, its presence growing steadily fainter, Beatrice let that slip in a small voice. The lovely-featured spirit gazed, forlorn, at the Witchbeast crumbling away, strengthless—no, at a kindred spirit like herself, one who had gone on being faithful to a fate-given role to the very end.

Subaru's mind refused to accept the meaning of Beatrice's words. But instinct understood. —This wasn't "death."

This was the unavoidable conclusion that had been waiting all along—from the moment she'd been entrusted with this Pleiades Watchtower as its Star Watch, for whenever this day, bound to come eventually, finally arrived.

"Then... if we..."

If they hadn't come here, could she have gone on staying, here, forever? In this tower of sand, forever, waiting for someone who would never return, right here in this place—

"—Subaru, you should already understand. That assumption is an insult to her."

"—"

"And what you ought to be doing isn't regret."

The knight, sword already sheathed, his blood-and-sand-stained attire set right again, told him that. It was heartless—and yet he was, through and through, right.

Grinding his back teeth, hiding how much he hated that rightness, Subaru let out a breath. And then he held the girl who had been alone for so, so long even tighter.

For her, left behind, who had spent so long a time in a place with no one else. Slowly ceasing to be alone, at the very side of the girl now being sent off.

Subaru. Beatrice. Julius. Meili. And, far off, the faint sense of someone running from the Watchtower—their companions still inside it. For the sake of the girl who had spent so long entirely alone, everyone was gathering.

"But, ssu, if it's just Master, one person, that's enough for me, ssu."

He could picture her saying something so charmless, so plainly, and that image turned straight into tears. Slowly, the Witchbeast's fangs traced gently along the droplet running down Subaru's cheek.

With fangs sharp enough to break absolutely anything, gently, so gently, careful not to break Subaru—who was probably the most fragile person there—as if cherishing him. And then—

"—Ah"

The arms holding her lost their sudden weight, without warning. Crumbling, losing all its mass, the Crimson Scorpion's outer shell came apart and turned to dust. Black dust danced over the sand, and Subaru opened his mouth wide.

"Shaula..."

"Yes, ssu, Master."

"Shaula... Shaula... Shaula..."

"Did you call, ssu? Master."

"Shaula, Shaula..."

"Geez, seriously, being loved this much by Master is gonna be a real problem for me, ssu~!"

When he closed his eyes, her voice answering his call came back to his ears. And yet, she was nowhere to be found anymore.

"—Ah"

Crouched on the sand, Subaru clawed at the sea of sand. A voice reached his ears. He couldn't tell whose it was. With no room to spare to check, he simply lifted his head as if drawn to it, and his eyes went wide.

On the sandy ground where black dust had settled and piled, the earth trembled faintly, and something crawled its way out. It was small, no bigger than the palm of a hand. Raking the sand with a pair of tiny claws, using its tail to skillfully haul its own body free of the sand—a small being with a red carapace—

"—"

It made its way over to where Subaru knelt on the ground, and nestled gently against the hand he'd pressed into the sand. And in that simple gesture of touch, he thought he could still see traces of that same old charm—

△▼△▼△▼△

Crumbling, crumbling, spilling away. Crumbling, crumbling, flaking away. Crumbling, crumbling, fading away.

Crumbling, crumbling, crumbling, and everything grows distant, glimmering. Crumbling, crumbling, crumbling, and everything looks distant, glimmering.

—Because you were there, in every bit of it that glimmered.

"Four hundred years was like the day after tomorrow, ssu."

"Because I loved even the time I spent waiting, ssu."

"Hey, Master. So, again, someday—"

"Someday, I want you to meet me again, ssu."

"This time, it's Master's turn to wait for me, ssu. Better the one who's chased than the one who chases, ssu."

"—Master, this is an important, a truly important promise, ssu."

"This time, please don't forget, ssu."

"—I love you, Master, ssu."

△▼△▼△▼△

"You're an idiot."

As if to say there was no way he could forget, Subaru murmured that in a trembling voice. Then he scooped up the thing tickling ticklishly at the back of his hand and cupped it in both palms. As though it, too, found the touch ticklish, almost bashful, the small, tiny scorpion trembled.

Its carapace was red, vivid enough to catch the eye, a deep, brilliant red.

—It was a color that even four hundred years couldn't fade: the color of "love."
