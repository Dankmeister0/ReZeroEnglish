Arc 7 Chapter 105: "The Tangled Vertices (Part 2)"

Pressing his fist against the ground, he forced his trembling knees to work and hauled himself upright.

The blow had landed heavy, reverberating down to his very core, and his innards screamed as though they'd been churned inside out.
Cuts and bruises he could force closed by drawing on the earth's power through the soles of his feet. But the enemy's inscrutable techniques slipped right past that brute-force ward.

Infuriating in manner, hateful in nature — yet the enemy was still one of the men called the strongest in the Empire.
A technique perfected through training beyond human comprehension, and it toyed with the straightforward Garfiel without mercy.

But he couldn't crouch there using lack of experience or years under his belt as an excuse.
For Garfiel, who couldn't do anything clever, the only thing demanded of him was victory. There was no other answer he could choose, and more than anything else—

"Like hell I'm gonna stay crouched down next to you!"

Grinding his clenched teeth, Garfiel lifted his face and let a growl rumble in his throat.
His gaze stayed fixed ahead, but his attention wasn't on the undeniable enemy before him — it was on the man standing at his side, his own personal "enemy."

"Good grief. Your competitive streak is a virtue, but surely in this situation it ought to be directed not at me, but at the old man over there, don't you think?"

"'Convenient-Amnesia Bastard,' that's what you are. Don't you go lecturing me about doing things right. You're the one who did something out of line, and you did it way before I did."

"That would be a year and a half ago now... though if we set aside what you already know about, I'll admit it goes well beyond that."

Shrugging his thin shoulders, the man who never ran out of glib retorts — Roswaal.
With his makeup wiped away and the ridiculous clown's costume shed, his appearance — even if only to hide his true identity — retained the bearing of a proper nobleman.
No matter how twisted his roots might be, it was a skillful disguise that let none of it show.

Still, it was a fact that without that infuriating Roswaal's advice, his life would have been in danger.
Precisely because he was aware of that, a hot surge of anger welled up from the pit of Garfiel's stomach.
Meanwhile—

"—That bit about havin' fought a Shinobi before ain't somethin' I can just let slide."

The one who murmured that was an old man so small of stature he could be mistaken for one of the Dwarf Tribe.
He gave a wave of his right sleeve — the arm ending at the wrist, the hand long gone — and with a face that all but begged to be called a kindly old grandfather, nothing about him hinted at how terrifying a being he truly was.
Garfiel had learned, painfully, that even that was itself a Shinobi technique.
Every gesture, every word, even his frail appearance — all of it was a tool for bringing death to an opponent. A Shinobi turned the entirety of his own existence into a weapon for taking down his target.

That eerie old man, the head of the Shinobi, glared at Garfiel and the others — no, at Roswaal specifically. It wasn't to rebuke the rudeness of butting into a one-on-one battlefield; his interest lay elsewhere.

—In the boast that he had fought a Shinobi before.

"Basic rule is, ya cross a Shinobi, ya don't come out alive. And if the fella somehow botches the kill, word gets sent back to the village, and they just keep sendin' the next one till the target's dead. So how come you're still breathin', eh?"

"That part's a bit complicated. It seems the Shinobi I encountered had circumstances of their own. I don't know if it's the correct term, but — 'rogue Shinobi,' I believe they're called."

"Rogue Shinobi..."

Olbart murmured under his breath, stroking his long white eyebrows with a finger.
The term Roswaal had used meant nothing to Garfiel either. To begin with, Shinobi were the kind of existence whispered about as though real, yet whose actual existence was doubtful at best.
Naturally, no one truly knew their reality. And "rogue Shinobi" was a phrase he'd never heard before.

"A Shinobi who left the village, eh? We ain't had one o' those in quite a while."

Yet without denying the unfamiliar term, Olbart addressed only its substance, plain and direct.
It was the kind of statement that implied he grasped everything — the village's affairs, the Shinobi's affairs, all of it — and no doubt that was simply the truth. Not out of duty, but as a means of survival, he kept everything within his grasp firmly in hand.
That was the way Olbart Dunkelkenn, Shinobi, made his way through life.

"I don't know how long 'a while' means to you, but it might fall outside that range. After all, I met the Shinobi in question nearly forty years ago."

"Haaah?"

At Roswaal's shrugging reply, Olbart, the "Wicked Elder," let out a sound of confusion.
The same doubt rose in Garfiel too. Or rather, he found himself tipping his hat to the sheer nerve of it — the strong chance that everything said so far had just been a bald-faced bluff.
He'd never bothered to ask Roswaal's age, but the man looked to be somewhere around thirty at most — unlike Emilia or Beatrice. Forty years ago, he wouldn't even have been born.
To pull some ridiculous prank on Olbart at a moment like this, of all things—

"—Shasuke and Raizou, maybe? That rogue Shinobi you're talkin' about."

"Hoh."

"Forty, fifty years back, snuck outta the village — if it's someone from that time who's still kickin', it's about only that pair o' brothers. The rest got dealt with. Ain't no other candidates."

To the story Garfiel had written off as a bad joke, Olbart kept patiently playing along. While Garfiel stared wide-eyed at that, Roswaal closed one eye.
His yellow eye gleaming with an uncanny light, he looked back at Olbart.

"Now then, am I obligated to answer whether you've hit the mark?"

"Nope, ya ain't. Plantin' a little hook in the other fella's mind — that's a valid move too, in a fight where a hair's breadth decides who lives... You might just have a talent for the Shinobi trade, you."

"I appreciate the compliment, but I'll decline. Both the talent I want and the path I wish to walk were decided long, long before even forty years ago."

"Ka-ka-ka-ka! That so, that so. —Well then, can't be helped."

Roswaal shook his head, brushing off Olbart's praise.
Laughing it off as though it hardly mattered, Olbart's form blurred an instant later. Blurred, and in the space of a blink, the distance between them vanished. The eerie old man's leg snapped up, striking for his target's throat.
And that—

"—!"

Garfiel felt a faint breeze against his throat and held his breath.
There, right at the nape of his neck, at a distance that might or might not have grazed his skin, a fatal blow was scattered aside. Olbart's unleashed kick had been stopped in the very nick of time.

"To think that after that conversation, you'd aim for him instead of me."

"Ya thin the herd. And ya start with whoever's easiest to thin. Standard play, ain't it?"

Leg strength and sharpness unimaginable from such a diminutive frame — had Olbart's kick landed true, even Garfiel's trained neck wouldn't have come away unscathed.
That the reckless contest never came to pass was thanks to a distinctively shaped dagger that wedged itself between neck and kicking foot — no, not a blade, but a weapon with a striking head instead of an edge. A sai.
It was that weapon that saved Garfiel's life.
Along with the humiliating fact that its owner was Roswaal, and that this made twice in a row he'd been saved.

"Gr—gruaaaaahhh!"

The instant humiliation scorched through him, Garfiel's right arm shot upward, cutting silently through the air.
His target, naturally, was the eerie old man still hanging in midair, pivoting on the leg that had just been stopped. With no room to escape in open air, Garfiel meant to blast straight through his torso and put him out of the fight.

"Whoop-ee-oop."

But just before that mighty arm connected, Olbart twisted his body with uncannily deft footwork, springing diagonally downward using nothing but the toes hooked on Roswaal's sai.
Slipping beneath the loosed fist as though ducking under it, Olbart put distance between them in a crawling, ground-hugging stance. Garfiel immediately went to step in and press the follow-up—

"Let's calm down for a moment, shall we?"

"Grrgh!"

His body, poised to lunge forward, was yanked back at the waist, snuffing out his momentum before it began.
Looking down, he saw Roswaal had hooked the tip of his sai into the waist of Garfiel's clothes. Just as he was about to snap at him over what he thought he was doing, the reason became clear.
A spinning black blade had grazed right past the tip of Garfiel's nose.

"—!"

"A throwing blade, flung into your blind spot with a backhand toss the instant he broke away. A cheap little trick, just putting a spin on it to make it curve — but Shinobi are a treasure trove of this sort of technique. And on top of that, our opponent is the pinnacle of the craft."

"Bein' top o' such a narrow little field ain't nothin' to strut about — folks'd just point and laugh. Ain't nothin' to brag on, bein' the pinnacle of Shinobi."

Unbothered by his missed attack, Olbart closed one eye, watching them with unbroken vigilance.
At the exchange between the two of them that left him entirely out of it, Garfiel ground his teeth in humiliation once again. Not once, not twice, but three times now, Roswaal had saved his life.

In his fight against Kafma, he'd felt the solid sense of having cleared a wall.
And yet, against a man who trampled all over that fight with Kafma, he couldn't lay a hand on him. At this rate, he wouldn't be able to face either himself, or Kafma, whom he'd defeated—

"—Garfiel, don't mistake the kind of strength at play here."

"Ah...?"

"You're strong. That's exactly why he's trying to avoid fighting you on your own ground. Once you understand that all of this is just craft and trickery meant to do that, most of the weakness you feel should vanish."

Saying this to Garfiel, whose fists were clenched tight, Roswaal drew a second sai.
With a short striking weapon readied in each hand, his figure bore none of the court mage Garfiel knew — instead he seemed intent on holding his own as a mere warrior.
There was a moment's confusion, and then, belatedly, Garfiel understood the intent behind it.

Even in a situation like this, Roswaal had to keep to the restriction of not using magic.
If he used magic, there was a risk it would expose Roswaal's true identity. And if that happened, this might no longer remain an internal Imperial conflict — it could escalate into a war between the Kingdom and the Empire.
In other words—

"All I can do is provide support at most... The key to this fight against an Imperial General is you, Garfiel."

"—"

"It's true the matchup is a bad one for you. You're honest and straightforward, after all. So, to make up for that shortfall, I'll maneuver around it. I am, after all..."

"—Rotten to the core."

Stealing the end of the sentence right out of his mouth, Garfiel finished it that way, and Roswaal gave a wry smile.

"Yes, exactly, because I've got a rotten character. Reassuring, isn't it?"

"Hah! Keep tellin' yourself that!"

Garfiel shot that back at the winking Roswaal, then wiped his mouth with the back of his hand.
He scrubbed away the blood that had been trickling freely from where he'd been struck, and let out a deep breath. From the rumbling sky came the presence of scorching heat and bitter cold, twin threats each as fearsome as the other.
Just imagining that prickling, skin-crawling sensation turned toward his comrades made his whole body go numb.
But—

"Just for now."

If he didn't focus on the enemy right in front of him, there would be no next time, and no time after that.

"...So now it's two against one. Gotten a mite troublesome, this has."

Olbart let out a sigh at the sight of Garfiel taking a deep breath. Garfiel, in turn, furrowed his brow at the old man's words. "Ahh?"
What a strange thing to say. It irked him, but if it was two against one, it had been that way ever since Roswaal showed up.

"Just havin' two folks on one side don't make it two-on-one."

To the question forming in Garfiel's mind, Olbart answered while tilting his head.
The eerie old man pinched his long white eyebrows between his fingers.

"Only when the two of ya start workin' together does it become two-on-one... Up till now it was just a coupla cheeky young'uns standin' around — now it's turned troublesome, that's all."

"Then perhaps, sensing you're at a disadvantage, you'd care to surrender?"

"Ka-ka-ka-ka! Runnin' from the enemy, throwin' down arms — that's a disgrace lower than dyin' like a dog for nothin'. And besides—"

"Besides?"

Flicking the pinched eyebrow with his finger, Olbart bared his teeth.
The diminutive old man, contrary to his smile, let an overwhelming fighting spirit pour from every inch of his body.

"—Two-on-one or not, I ain't got a lick of intention to lose, me."

In that instant, the old man's smile blurred, and his form vanished from sight once more.
Not just left or right — with this "Wicked Elder," even the sky and the ground beneath were options on the table. Garfiel's nerves sharpened at the range of possibilities—

"—Between your legs."

Prompted by the voice he heard, Garfiel pulled his body halfway back.
In that instant, his eyes met the eerie old man rising up from beneath the ground.

"Gr—graaaaahhh!!"

A blow struck with everything he had came down, and Olbart snapped his knee upward.
Garfiel's fist drove in, meaning to blast clean through even the withered-branch knee of the old man.

A shockwave rolled through the earth, cracking it apart, spreading devastation in a fierce chain.
The first true hit had landed — and in the truest sense of the word, the death struggle with the Shinobi had begun.

△▼△▼△▼△

—The hero always arrives late.

It was something of a genre convention, but Subaru hated it.
To be precise, it would be more accurate to say that after being flung into another world and living through all manner of things here, he'd come to think, "screw that."

"Maybe that's fine if it's in a game or a manga, some kind of story, but..."

If it were something happening in a fictional world, that kind of development might be necessary to build excitement.
But for Subaru, actually living through this turbulent world, the sooner a hero, a great figure, someone with power beyond human comprehension who could bring conflicts to a resolution, showed up, the better — the earlier the better.

Best case: the hero appears at maximum speed and yanks the root of the problem out in an instant.
Even if people said that wasn't interesting as a story, that was fine — this wasn't a story. Whether it was fun or not could wait until there was room in one's heart for that kind of debate.

"And yet here we are, arriving dead last — pathetic!"

Swaying on the back of a crimson Galewind Horse, Subaru glared straight ahead, seething with frustration.
The battle for the imperial capital, siege and defense that would decide the fate of the Empire. It wasn't clear who exactly deserved the blame, but for now, Subaru burned with anger at his own slowness.
Even though he'd always hated it, he'd gone and done the very same thing as the hero who shows up late.
And on top of that—

"Getting all fired up about racking up the biggest results — that's exactly what the chronically-late hero does too, isn't it!"

The reason the hero who arrives late ends up being the one who shines brightest is surely because the hero themselves regrets being late more than anyone.
In that time, their comrades, the people they had to protect, someone they didn't want to lose, had suffered through hardship and pain — the hero feels that more than acutely enough. That's why.
It was only now that Subaru finally understood the reason behind the fierce fighting spirit shown afterward by heroes who never made it to the battle in time from the start. — That they, too, had been blaming themselves.

"Let's do this, Beatrice!"
"El Shamak!"

Along with that understanding came the shadow magic unleashed by the girl held against Subaru's chest — black clouds forming one after another in midair, descending over the heads of the imperial soldiers lined up below.
It didn't just rob them of sight and whittle away their fighting strength — no, that undersold it entirely. What the soldiers who had clouds capping their heads lost wasn't their eyesight, but their very ability to fight.

"Ohhhh, ryaaaah——!!"

The line of imperial soldiers, clouds over their heads and movements frozen, was smashed apart by the charging blows of the Pleiades Battalion, plowing in with wild, reckless fighting styles.
Stripping the defenseless soldiers of their weapons, stripping their armor, breaking one limb or another and leaving them where they lay — that was the Battalion's basic tactic, a style of combat reflecting the wishes of their boss, Subaru.

It wasn't that he'd firmly resolved on a no-kill policy.
Still, he chose the method that let as few people die as possible. Doing so was, above all, for the sake of Natsuki Subaru's own peace of mind, and at the same time—

"—I hate the Vollachian Empire."

It was a petty spite against an Empire that forced people to fight, to kill each other, to be warriors.

"Schultz, we're about to reach the wall. We need a decision — push straight into the capital, or swing around to support the other vertices."

Shaking the ground, rampaging with his superhuman bulk — Gustav Morello.
Entrusted as warden of the "Sword Slave Isle," and active as an indispensable brain within the Pleiades Battalion as well, he swung his four thick, mighty arms, keeping approaching imperial soldiers at bay and displaying tremendous combat prowess.

As soldiers, punched by his mighty arms, went sailing lightly overhead, Subaru fixed his eyes on the wall looming ahead — the star-shaped wall encircling the city, specifically its fourth vertex.
Seizing each of the vertices was the condition for winning the upper hand in this siege.

"What do you think, Gustav?! Should we push in, or push in even more?!"

"This one does not make judgment calls. This one merely presents the conceivable merits and demerits. — Entering the capital and reaching the Crystal Palace could hasten the resolution. Swinging around to support the other vertices could, through this one's strength, reduce casualties on both sides. That is all."

"Tough call! Tough call, but..."

Even in the middle of battle, Gustav's unshaken composure was reassuring, but at the same time his refusal to help with decisions like this one felt almost infuriating.
And yet, even while laying out the possibilities, he never forced a particular choice.
It was precisely because Gustav had kept holding that line that the Pleiades Battalion had stayed united without falling apart, up until now.
If there was another reason, alongside Gustav's attitude, for the Battalion's unity—

"Gustav! Take Hiain with the flag and half our people to support the other fight! Weitz! Split the other half in two and hold this position! I'm counting on you!"

It was that Subaru, entrusted with the decision here, exercised it fully and firmly.

"—This one understands and accepts."
"Leave it to me, brother! We're all in the same boat, after all!"
"Of course we're in the same boat, Lizard...! It's your request, so I'll hear it out too..."

Hearing Subaru's decision, the named members answered one after another.
It was the luxurious decision to take both of the options Gustav had presented — though that meant splitting their forces between each battlefield—

"—We can pull it off!"

At Subaru's declaration, he could feel the Battalion's morale rise even further.
That was exactly what made them the comrades who had fought together from that hellish island and made it all the way here.

"Schultz, what do we do?!"

"Obviously! We're marching straight through and making our grand entrance on the other side of that wall!"

That was the question from Idra, gripping the Galewind Horse's reins and riding alongside Subaru.
Answering the obvious question with gusto, Subaru pointed at the wall ahead of them. Then, glaring at the imposing wall, he opened his mouth.

"Do it, Tanza! You're the one we're counting on!"

"—Master Schultz, always so glib."

At Subaru's call, a small, nimble shadow shot out from right beside them.
Lifting the hem of her kimono, kicking off the ground with gallant speed toward the wall, was Tanza. Bound to travel alongside Subaru by strange circumstance, she was an indispensable presence within the Pleiades Battalion too.
Because she was — the Pleiades Battalion's strongest attacker.

"—Haaah!!"

Tanza flew like a bullet, spinning in midair, and her geta-clad foot drove straight into the wall.
A single beat, and immediately after, the sturdy wall was smashed to pieces, Tanza's form punching straight through to the other side. The shockwave spawned cracks, spreading fissures across the entire fourth vertex of the wall.

"Follow follow follow follow her——!!"
"Ohhhhh——!!"

And so, at the point where Tanza had driven in her first blow, the full momentum of the Pleiades Battalion crashed in together, at Subaru's command.
It was no longer the attack of individual Battalion members so much as a single blow delivered by the Pleiades Battalion as one living creature, and even the proudly formidable walls of the imperial capital Lupugana couldn't withstand it.

"—"

With a thunderous roar and a tremendous cloud of dust, the wall was forced open by sheer power.
Witnessing that overwhelming sight firsthand, Subaru pumped his fist, and in his arms, Beatrice's round eyes went wide.

"That wall, brought down so easily... this is just too absurd, I suppose..."

"—That's the Pleiades Battalion for you."

Answering Beatrice's awed murmur, Tanza descended lightly out of the swirling dust.
Brushing the dirt off her soiled kimono, she was a girl whose expression almost never shifted, but just this once, she seemed the faintest bit proud.

Even she, who rarely showed anything like a sense of camaraderie, was still aware of being a member of the Pleiades Battalion. Otherwise, she wouldn't be able to receive the effects of their "unity power."
Still, Beatrice didn't seem at all pleased with Tanza's attitude.

"You've got a cheeky look on your face, I suppose...!"

"Cheeky as you may call it, I was simply born with this face."

"It's not about your face, I suppose! Expressions are something you can control, I suppose!"

"Uu! Ah, uu!"

Looking down at the primly composed Tanza, Beatrice, atop the horse, flushed red. Then, as though taking Beatrice's side, Rui, clinging to her back, started up a fuss as well.
At the girls' uproar, Subaru raised his voice: "Wait, wait, calm down!"

"Don't fight! We're a team! Comrades! One single 'Go'!"

"Go...?"

"Understood, Master Schultz."

"Mmm, hmph, I suppose."

Beatrice tilted her head at the unfamiliar term, while Tanza bowed at a term she knew well.
The gap in reactions came down to knowledge from the Sword Slave Isle, but the way it looked poised to become even more kindling for Beatrice's anger made Subaru twist his lips.
But before Subaru could move to mediate—

"You wretch, prepare yourself——!"

"Ehh?"

Mixed into the dust kicked up by the collapsed wall, an imperial soldier had crept close, and now he raised his voice, sword raised high, taking aim at Subaru atop the horse.
Hard to believe as it was, to an enemy soldier facing them, it was plain that Subaru was the one leading this group. Once you knew a child wasn't to be underestimated, not underestimating them was the Imperial way.
And so the soldier's sword flew true, drawn straight toward Subaru—

"Uau!"

In that instant, the arms holding Subaru from behind tightened, and in a heartbeat, his field of view changed.
What had happened was simple and clear: in the blink of an eye, they'd moved from the spot where the Galewind Horse had just been standing — no, not moved. Teleported.

"Wh-what...? Bluergh..."

Dizzied by the sudden teleport, Idra, gripping the reins, felt her insides churn and gagged despite herself. Subaru felt like he had experience with this himself — it was the strange power possessed by Rui on his back.
And then—

"Shamak."

"Wha-!? Guh... guah!?"

Beatrice, with a short incantation, dropped a cloud over the imperial soldier, and Tanza's water-surface kick swept out the frozen soldier's legs with tremendous force, slamming him to the ground and knocking him out.
A moment's teamwork accomplished, the two of them — one atop the horse, one below — exchanged glances.

"Well done."

"You too, that wasn't half bad a move, I suppose."

With that, the hostile mood from just moments before flipped entirely, and the two of them acknowledged each other.

"Well, it's a good thing when little girls warm up to each other. ...Wait, Rui! Quit doing that out of nowhere, you just turned Idra's insides upside down! Though it did help!"

"Ah, uu!"

"Mm, good answer! Idra, take a deep breath! This probably won't be the first or last time."

"I— I'll try..."

With Rui's teleportation power, at least one unexpected turn among all the unexpected turns could be avoided.
If it was necessary, he intended to have her use it without hesitation, no matter how much it made Idra feel like retching.

"To begin with, I probably couldn't take it two or three times in a row myself either... Gustav! Hiain! Weitz!"

At Subaru's call, pulling himself together, the faces standing before the collapsed wall turned back. Looking each familiar face firmly in the eye, one by one—

"I'm counting on you!"

"This one shall simply carry out his duties. You should do the same."
"Alright! The Pleiades Battalion's making a grand, forceful triumphant return!"
"Schultz, we'll hold this position to the death... Go seize the throne...!"

Leaving their respective posts to Gustav and the others heading for another battlefield, and to Weitz and the others staying behind to defend the collapsed wall to the last, all of them reliable, Subaru nudged the back of his head against Idra's chest.
Idra, riding the Galewind Horse in a way that cradled Subaru, responded to that little headbutt—

"Well then, in we go. It seems we're the first ones in."

Laughing valiantly, Idra spurred the Galewind Horse onward, clearing the rubble of the wall and entering the urban district. Swaying on the same horse, Subaru too passed into the imperial capital of Lupugana.
The cityscape of the capital, hidden within the tall walls and impossible to view fully from outside, turned out to be a meticulous one, its buildings lined up in neat, orderly rows.

"Whoever's in charge of this city must be seriously uptight for it to turn out like this."

If the person in question had been listening, they might have objected that they couldn't be held responsible for the construction of a city built hundreds of years ago — but that, too, was an unreasonable thing to expect.
So, tossing out one-sided impressions, the Pleiades Battalion came pouring into the capital.
Their objective—

"Subaru! What do we do, I suppose?!"
"Master Schultz, what will you do?"
"Uau! Au, aa, uu!"

"Obviously, it's decided! We're headed for the capital's Crystal Palace! I'm gonna march right up in front of His Majesty the Emperor, lording it up at the top, and say hello!"

Asked all at once by the girls, Subaru answered them all together.
While Beatrice, Tanza, and even Rui all nodded back at Subaru's answer, only Idra, who ended up watching it all up close, murmured quietly.

"Four children in tow on a battlefield... I really don't seem to have any talent as a warrior after all."

So went the thought — a rather miller's-son sort of thought.

—And so, at that very same moment when the Pleiades Battalion, led by Natsuki Subaru, broke through the wall and finally achieved the first entry into the imperial capital of Lupugana.

"I never imagined they would hold on this tenaciously."

The Crystal Palace — the destination that every member of the rebel host besieging the capital, plotting to overturn the very foundations of this Empire, had set their sights on, burning their lives to reach it.
The throne room, highest within that castle, the place where its majesty showed strongest. On the wall behind hung the national flag depicting a wolf pierced by a sword, and in that chamber carpeted in blood-red, stood the throne in which all the authority of this Empire was concentrated, and the figure of the Emperor seated upon it.

Young and brilliant, possessed of a cold beauty like a honed blade, the Emperor did not so much as change expression even as a great host of those turning their swords toward his own throat came surging forward.
This despite the fact that, just as he himself had once put it into words, the situation had exceeded all expectations.
Though, at that—

"That remark — which layer of the web you've spun, ten threads deep, twenty threads deep, is it referring to?"

"—"

That was far too insolent a thing to say to an Emperor seated upon his own throne, within his own throne room.
And yet there was no loyal retainer there to rebuke it, no soldier to strike off the head of the insolent one — only the brazen footsteps of an intruder treading the carpet, echoing through the chamber.

In that throne room, missing all that should have been present, there was one more baffling matter.
A fact that, had anyone else been present, would surely have made them frown. — No, perhaps they wouldn't even have been able to frown at all.
Because to even perceive that fact required grounds enough to break through a perception that had been actively obstructed.

A gift once bestowed, long ago, by an Emperor of an older age, upon a tribe with whom friendship had been exchanged.
A mask modeled after the Oni Tribe — created to slay the most terrifying being in this world — that turns the eye away, through sheer dread, from the truth hidden behind it.

And so, no one could easily notice that the voice of the person wearing that oni mask was exactly identical to the Emperor's own.
And so, a figure bearing the very same voice as the Emperor, walking through the throne room with fearless, awe-defying confidence, as though it were entirely their own domain, appeared before the Emperor of Vollachia.
It was—

"It's strange — I feel less moved than I expected. Even looking up, from below, at the throne I was once driven from."

It was the triumphant return of the rightful Emperor, calmly come back at last to the throne he had once had no choice but to leave.
