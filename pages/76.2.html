Side Story: "Emilia's Starry Sky Classroom"

1

"Going outside at this hour, Emilia-sama?"

At the voice that called out from behind her, Emilia, who had been walking down the hallway, stopped in her tracks.

Her long silver hair, falling to her waist, shimmered and swayed as she turned around. Gently pressing a hand to it, Emilia turned her deep violet eyes toward the one who had called to her.

"Yes, I was just going to step out to the garden. This morning was a bit hectic, so I haven't fulfilled my Contract with the minor spirits yet. It's scary what happens if you break a Contract."

So saying, the girl of peerless beauty gave a bashful, embarrassed little laugh. At the gesture, the girl who had called out to Emilia—Rem—faintly knit her brows.

"This morning's hecticness... would that be about Subaru-kun?"

"W-well, um, that's true, but..."

There was a slight edge to her tone. At the boy's name Rem had brought up, Emilia gave a wry smile and nodded. Seeing that, Rem narrowed her pale blue eyes.

"Please don't hesitate—if something's wrong, tell me without reserve. At the very least, it's a fact that Subaru-kun is currently a servant of this mansion."

"It's not that I'm holding back. ...It's just, this morning's mess was because I got caught up talking with Subaru, but I think it was my fault for getting so absorbed in the conversation."

Recalling that morning and looking somewhat contrite, Emilia drew a small sigh out of Rem.

As a Spiritual Arts User who borrowed strength not only from Puck but from a great many minor spirits, keeping her Contracts with them—fulfilling the many promises involved—was an important daily duty for Emilia.

Her Contract with Puck granted him the right to choose Emilia's hairstyle and outfit for the day. Beyond that, there were stretches after her bath, and the care of her hair and nails—the terms ranged widely.

Having been with Puck so long that nearly all of this had become routine, none of it troubled Emilia—but that didn't mean it was something she could afford to neglect.

"If I only favor Puck, the little spirits get sulky. I have to make time to talk with them properly every morning. It'd be a problem if they turned their backs on me at some crucial moment."

"So there are all sorts of restrictions in the Spiritual Arts too. Rem isn't very familiar with them."

"I suppose there really aren't many Spiritual Arts Users around. Rem's Magic—did you learn it from Roswaal, too?"

"Yes. Rem learned the basics from Nee-sama when she was little... and after that, Roswaal-sama taught her directly."

Roswaal, the master of the mansion, was the Chief Court Mage of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica—that is, the man recognized as the finest mage in the entire kingdom. His everyday speech and conduct, his outlandish fashion sense, and his fondness for the bizarre all posed their share of problems, but there was no room to doubt the height of his ability.

Rem, tutored under that Roswaal, was likewise a mage of outstanding skill in Water-attribute Magic. Her older sister Ram specialized in Wind-attribute Magic, making the two sisters together a dependable pair of powerhouses.

"I'd rather not think about situations where I'd have to rely on both of your Magic, but..."

"Rem understands how you feel. But please be prepared—it's unavoidable. What happened in the royal capital was only a small part of it... surely there will be more like it to come."

Rather than coddling Emilia as she lowered her eyes, Rem sternly admonished her.

In any case, this girl had a strictness about her, both toward herself and toward others—no doubt a product of her firmly disciplined nature. Emilia found that quality of Rem's rather likable.

"Mm, I know. I can't afford to be weak. With so many people... with Roswaal, with you and your sister helping me, I can't just hang my head."

"Rem and Nee-sama are only following Roswaal-sama's wishes. ...Still, if Rem can be of some help to you, Emilia-sama, there's nothing more she could ask for."

As Rem bowed quietly, Emilia moved to return the bow in kind—then stopped herself.

Once before, when she'd done the same thing, she'd been scolded for bowing to a servant.

Even in something as small as a gesture, she still lacked what one might call common sense. It left her bewildered at times. But she also understood that she had to learn these things, and grow accustomed to them.

"Well then, I'll be in the garden. I don't plan to be out too late, and I doubt anyone will need me, but—"

"Understood. ...And, just one thing."

Bowing politely, Rem looked up at Emilia with upturned eyes and posed a single question.

"If Subaru-kun comes looking for you, Emilia-sama, what should Rem do?"

By "what should Rem do," she likely meant whether it would be all right to tell him where she was.

Emilia had only just said that Subaru had interrupted her conversation with the spirits that morning. Rem's consideration was only natural. But Emilia shook her head at Rem's thoughtfulness, and said,

"It's fine, don't worry. If Subaru comes looking for me, go ahead and tell him I'm in the garden."

"Are you certain?"

"It's not that I want to treat Subaru like a nuisance. It's just that I need to keep my resolve and honor my Contracts properly, that's all. ...Besides, I'm a little curious about the rest of this morning's conversation."

"Emilia-sama, will you truly be able to keep your Contracts in that state...?"

"I—I'll be fine, I said! Really, don't worry!"

Waving her hands back and forth, Emilia answered in a fluster before Rem's dubious stare.

"Still, whatever were you and Subaru-kun talking about, exactly?"

"Hmm... Rem, do you often gaze up at the night sky?"

"The stars? No, Rem's never really given them much thought. Though on the road at night, she's relied on them to check her direction before..."

"Not like that—I mean at more ordinary times. ...Yeah, I was the same way."

Moving to the edge of the hallway, Emilia rested a hand against the window and gazed out at the scenery beyond.

Night had already fallen over the landscape, and in a world without any light of its own, all that illuminated it was the moonlight pouring down from the blackened night sky, and the glittering of the stars.

"—"

Drawn in, Rem, standing beside her, looked up at the starry sky just as Emilia did. To her profile, Emilia said,

"Sometime, if you get the chance, try asking Subaru to tell you about the stars? I bet you'll end up like me—looking forward, just a little, to gazing up at the night sky."

2

"Well then, Beatrice, I'll be taking Puck back now..."

"...Betty knows, I suppose."

At the sight of the girl—Beatrice—twisting her lips, making no effort to hide her displeasure, Emilia's eyes softened into a wry smile.

Beatrice, radiating open discontent, held in her arms the little cat spirit, who was placidly washing his face.

"Hm? What's wrong, Lia? Dazzled by how cute I am?"

"Yes, yes. That's not it—I was just thinking Beatrice always makes such a sour face at me."

"Naturally, I wonder. As far as Betty's concerned, you're the wicked villain who tears Betty and Nii-cha apart, I suppose. Same as that dim-faced man—if it were up to Betty, she'd rather not even look at your face, I wonder."

Turning her face away, Beatrice said this plainly to Emilia.

Under normal circumstances, such an attitude might have hurt—but there was no more malice in Beatrice's words than what was on their surface. Understanding this, Emilia could only give a wry smile at how endearing it was.

"Sorry, but my Contractor is Lia. Your rendezvous with Betty will have to wait for another time."

"It's a promise, Nii-cha. You'll surely, surely come back to Betty again, I suppose."

After an exchange fit for a parting in this life forever, Puck hopped over onto Emilia's shoulder. Settling into his usual spot, the little cat leaned his body against her silver hair, his return complete.

"As always, you lose track of time in the Forbidden Archive. Looks like it's already night, and you're dressed to go out, Lia. Off to chat with the minor spirits in the garden?"

"Mm, that's right. I didn't get the chance to talk with the little spirits this morning. And besides, right now I could also look at the stars Subaru told me about this morning... oh, that's right! Beatrice, do you have anything else to do?"

"Betty?"

Beatrice, who had been turning moist eyes toward Puck, now turned to Emilia with an openly displeased look.

That she opened her heart to no one but Puck was common knowledge to everyone in the mansion. Still, Emilia felt Beatrice was especially harsh toward her and Subaru in particular.

In Emilia's case, she figured it was because of her connection to Puck; with Subaru, it seemed more like a simple matter of incompatibility.

"If you don't have anything to do, want to take a walk together for once? We're living in the same mansion, after all, and I'd like to get along better with you, Beatrice. Plus, I have a really special story to tell today!"

"Though it's just something Subaru told her, secondhand~"

"Puck, hush for a second! So, Beatrice, how about it? No good?"

Silencing Puck's teasing interjection, Emilia tilted her head at Beatrice. But Beatrice stepped back from Emilia's gaze and turned her face sharply away.

"Too bad for you, Betty happens to be terribly busy, I wonder. That special story of yours? Something about the stars? Betty has hardly any interest in that, I suppose."

"Really, no good? Even Rem was really, really interested."

"Don't think Betty can be reeled in by some story you heard from that man, I wonder. No doubt his star talk is just him carelessly slapping names on stars he doesn't even know and getting all excited about it, I suppose."

"Wow, amazing. How did you know that?"

At Beatrice's sharp remark, Emilia's eyes went wide with surprise.

In fact, it had been Subaru who'd brought up the subject of the stars with Emilia, but it seemed the stars looked different here than they did back in his homeland, and he'd apparently been struggling to reconcile what he knew with what he actually saw.

"Subaru seems to know a lot about stars too, but when the way they look is different, it all turns into complete gibberish, apparently. Um, there was Vega, and Altair, and... Pe-Petelgeuse?"

"...Juice."

"Huh?"

"—. It's nothing, I suppose. Now go on, off you go already, I suppose."

Clicking her tongue at Emilia's shaky memory, Beatrice moved to close the door of the Forbidden Archive. Just before the space-separating Door Crossing activated, Beatrice gave one last wave of her hand, and said,

"Well then, until next time, Nii-cha, I suppose."

With that, Beatrice's figure vanished beyond the door. Pouting her lips at the girl who'd been aloof right to the end, Emilia headed off down the hallway in a sulk, toward the garden.

"I wish I could get along with Beatrice the way you do, Puck."

"Betty and I fought all the time at first too, you know. But now we get along fine. That's thanks to the time we spent together... just like you and me, Lia."

Hearing Puck say, with a swish of his long tail, that impatience was forbidden in all things, Emilia nodded, thinking, That's true, isn't it. The two of them passed through the terrace and stepped into the mansion's garden.

A breeze laden with night air stroked her silver hair, and she gently held it down as it shimmered in the moonlight. Emilia walked across the short-cropped grass, heading toward a corner of the garden.

"Somewhere around here... there we go."

After lightly smoothing the ground beneath her, Emilia settled sideways onto the grass. On a whim, she looked up at the star-filled sky spreading overhead, and let out a breath that sounded somehow satisfied.

Emilia, too, like Rem and Beatrice, had never paid much conscious attention to the stars floating in the night sky. Of course, she'd thought the starry sky beautiful before. But it was hard to keep cherishing, without forgetting, a beauty you simply assumed would always be there. And besides—

"Honestly, Subaru talked about the stars looking so happy."

Even as he said, sounding a little regretful, that the stars looked different than they did in his homeland, the boy's eyes as he gazed up at the night sky had seemed to Emilia to shine no less brightly than the stars themselves.

Remembering that, Emilia pressed her sleeve to her mouth and let out a small laugh.

"You seem awfully happy, Lia."

"It's such a strange feeling, somehow. This starry sky shouldn't be any different than it's always been, and yet, just because my own feelings are different, it looks this beautiful."

Subaru had told her that even his own name, "Subaru," had been taken by his parents from a star. Boasting that this was why he was so knowledgeable about the stars, he'd taught her a number of little anecdotes.

"All those tiny stars lined up together are the Amanogawa... the Milky Way, was it? A pair of star-crossed lovers who can only meet once a year—no matter if it rains or the stars themselves come falling down, they cross the river on sheer passion to love each other."

"Huh. I don't know why, but I feel like that's really butchering the story."

"If Hikoboshi can't cross the river of stars before night's end, he won't be able to save the close friend he left behind on the far shore, right? It's a promise—the King of the Stars only opens the river while his friend is held hostage."

"Hmm, I wonder if that's really how it goes. But it feels weird for me to say anything about it, too..."

Emilia's eyes shone at what struck her as such a dreamy tale, but Puck, apparently unconvinced, just kept tilting his head.

Puck didn't seem entirely won over, but Emilia had taken quite a liking to the star anecdotes. She was looking forward, very much, to whatever other stories Subaru might tell her next.

In any case, the stars weren't her only purpose tonight. She mustn't forget her original goal.

"All right, Puck. I'll call the others now."

At Emilia's call, Puck, floating from her shoulder, drifted a little farther away. It was a consideration so that the other minor spirits wouldn't be intimidated by the presence of Puck, a Great Spirit.

"Sorry I couldn't make time this morning. —Now then, let's talk."

At Emilia's murmur, faint points of light began to bloom, one by one, in the darkness of the night.

Each one was a minor spirit responding to Emilia's call, the strength of its glow expressing its joy. Feeling that come through so clearly, Emilia's lips bloomed into a smile like a flower.

And so, Emilia's night of quiet, gentle conversation with the minor spirits slowly began.

It continued on, peaceful and warm, wrapped for a while in stillness—but then,

"Oh-hoh. F-fancy meeting you here, huh?"

As the boy in butler's attire raised one hand and approached with an ill-fitting, ingratiating smile, the moon-watched "Starry Sky Classroom" began once more.

《End》

  • 扉渡り ("Door Crossing") is my best-guess rendering of Beatrice's spatial-transition ability; not confirmed against an official glossary entry.
  • Emilia stumbles over the star name "Betelgeuse" and says "ペテルギウス" (Petelgeuse) — the same spelling as the Sin Archbishop Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti — prompting Beatrice's odd, deflecting "...Juice" reaction. This looks like deliberate foreshadowing/wordplay by the author; I've kept it literal since the intent (and the "juice" association) is ambiguous to me.
  • 紫紺 (Emilia's eye color) rendered as "deep violet" per her canonical eye color rather than a literal "purple-navy."
