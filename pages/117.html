Arc 3 Chapter 40: "The Other Side of Killing Intent"

When he came to, his consciousness was adrift in a deep, deep darkness.

Amid the dim-spreading black, consciousness cast its gaze about, seeking some change. The pitch black seemed to stretch on forever, as if to the very edge of the world — or else it gave the claustrophobic sense of a miniature garden, a world that extended no further than arm's reach.

Where was this place? Why was he here?

Such questions rose up, but even asking them was strange in itself. He didn't even know who he was, the one thinking such thoughts.

Only consciousness drifted, hollow, and no will reached the flesh that was meant to bear it up. He was standing. His feet were on the ground. And yet what he sensed as "underfoot" was indistinguishable from the darkness that filled his vision, so that literally even his footing was uncertain.

Then — suddenly, a change arose in the world of nothing but darkness.

The shadows began to warp and buckle, and the space before him began to crack. After only an instant of that disturbance, a single human silhouette emerged from that place.

He thought it was the shadow of a woman. And the moment he recognized it, some indescribable feeling swelled up inside his chest — what could he even call it?

As if driven on by that explosively swelling emotion, he wanted to run to that shadow. But his legs, as always, would not obey his will, and there was nothing he could do about the distant figure. He wanted to call out, but he didn't know what to say, didn't know how words were even made. He wanted to weep with regret, but he didn't know how to give shape to feeling.

He didn't know. He didn't know. He knew nothing at all.

But the shadow, as though it understood his thoughts, slowly reached out both arms, closing from its side the distance that would not close from his. Those two hands drew closer, slowly, surely, to a distance where they might embrace—

"—I love you."

Bliss flowed from the fingertips that touched him, and joy wrapped around his whole body—

His consciousness slipped back through time, and the instant it took root in flesh again, Subaru pitched forward spectacularly.

"Whoa!? Wh-what happened, kid!?"

Looking down at Subaru, who had gone sprawling on the street without warning, Kadomon flapped his hands up and down in a fluster from behind the counter. The shock of the headlong fall had knocked the breath from him, and Subaru, now sporting a pointless scrape, winced at the sting.

"No... just, my foot slipped a bit."

"Slipped, my ass — that looked like you lost a whole leg. You sure your walking and standing are okay? If you go and lose your common sense on top of everything else, I'm done dealing with you."

"Don't need your worrying... and what's this 'on top of everything else'? Makes it sound like I'm some lawless idiot with no common sense to begin with."

"That's exactly what I meant. Ah, though considering you're mixed up with some noble, I get the feeling you might be something even harder to handle."

Subaru clicked his tongue in protest at Kadomon's relentless ribbing. Then, suddenly, he felt a tug at his sleeve, and immediately after, a warm pulse spread through his body. He turned, and as he faced that warmth—

"—Are you all right? Subaru-kun?"

There was Rem, saying this as she pressed her palm against the wound on the fallen Subaru.

The water mana flowing from her touching fingers interfered with Subaru's scrape, heightening its healing power to stop the bleeding and mend the wound. Even as he felt the pain fading, something else rose up in Subaru's chest at the same time.

"...Rem..."

"...? Yes, I'm Rem."

At Subaru calling her name in a weak, hoarse voice, Rem tilted her head, puzzled, and then—

"What's wrong? You look like you've seen a dead person. You don't need to worry — Rem is right here. Rem, who belongs to Subaru-kun."

Unusually for her, she smiled with something like a joking lilt to her voice. That was probably how pitiful Subaru's utterly haggard state looked to her. And the words she'd used — "seen a dead person" — weren't the least bit funny. Truly, not even remotely, not at all funny.

"Rem, I..."

"It's difficult, isn't it. I think you look much more like yourself smiling than with a clouded face. So I was hoping to somehow make you laugh."

Rem furrowed her brow faintly, looking troubled. As she did, Subaru's scrape had healed completely. Having confirmed this, Rem said "there," nodded once, and moved to withdraw her touching fingers—

"——"

"Subaru-kun?"

Subaru grabbed those withdrawing fingers from above, unwilling to let the warmth escape. Rem looked up at him, startled by the forcefulness of his gesture. But when she noticed the raw, poignant emotion floating plainly on his face—

"Really, what's wrong? It's... I'm happy when you do this sort of thing yourself, but if it's this sudden, it startles me."

"Thin. Small. ...Warm, aren't you."

"I'm a little sensitive about being called small, so I'd rather you didn't. Though from Subaru-kun it's all right. And of course I'm warm — I'm alive, after all."

At that last remark, Subaru looked up sharply at Rem. Their eyes met head-on, and Rem, still holding tenderness in her gaze, said—

"Did something make you anxious? But Rem is right here. Subaru-kun risked his life to save her. So it's all right."

—No. That's not it. That's not it at all.

Subaru had let Rem die. Killed her. Twice, mercilessly, cruelly. The first time, perhaps, had been fundamentally unrelated to Subaru at its root. But the second time was different. The second time, with no room for excuse whatsoever, Rem had died for Subaru's sake. To protect Subaru, to save Subaru, for Subaru's sake, she had spent her life to its very end, and wrung out even more from that spent life, and died for Subaru.

The Rem before him now knew nothing of that. Did not know how much agony and humiliation had been inflicted on her, or how wretchedly she had died at the end of it.

Only Subaru knew that. And knew, too, that if the same situation came again, Rem would try to protect him in exactly the same way without hesitation, and be violated and have her life scattered in exactly the same way.

"——"

Before he knew it, Subaru had gripped Rem's small hand tight, as if trying to press down something threatening to spill out from inside him, and bowed his head so she wouldn't see his face. At his reaction, Rem's fingers trembled with anxiety, wondering if she'd said something to trouble him. But that was only for an instant. Perhaps thinking that such an attitude would only frighten him more, she said—

"It's all right. It's all right. It's all right."

Gently, as if soothing a child, she patted Subaru's back with her free hand. Stroking it, cherishing it, until he could lift his face again.

Gentle the whole while, tender the whole while, as if he were something precious.

"Sorry to bother you while you're busy, but you two making eyes at each other in front of my shop is bad for business."

That was what Kadomon said as he shooed the pair off, having watched the little scene play out at his storefront. On an ordinary day, Subaru would have snapped back with something like "Whether we're here or not has got nothing to do with it — this place barely counts as a business to begin with!" and set off his temper. But right now, he didn't have the mental room to spare for that. He simply let Rem lead him away by the hand, hurrying to leave the spot.

That the shopkeeper hadn't shoved them off immediately, and had at least refrained from disturbing the moment until Subaru calmed down, spoke to a certain decency in the man — but unfortunately, Subaru didn't have the composure to notice even that. Right now, only a single emotion ruled his chest.

—Kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him, kill him.

For even having passed through death and turned back time, that overwhelming hatred alone had not faded.

Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti. That was the name of the enemy fate had set against Subaru now. A man who had committed an unforgivable, mortal sin — the worst kind of murderer, who had slaughtered Rem and the villagers.

Killing that man was the mission given to Subaru now.

"I'll finish him off, no matter what... with my own hands."

He would twist that hideous face with his own two hands — and as he thought this, a dark joy welled up in Subaru's chest, and at the corner of his mouth appeared a warped kind of smile he had never worn before.

Stab him, tear him apart, crush him, wrench him to pieces, make him beg for his life, and then kill him. Just imagining it made an unbearable urge to laugh rise from deep in his throat.

"...Subaru-kun, could I ask you something?"

Rem, walking half a step behind, called out to Subaru as he steeped himself in that dark pleasure. At her voice, he stopped and turned back to her, saying, "What's up?" She gazed intently at his face, then gave her well-shaped nose a small sniff.

"No... maybe it's just Rem's imagination. It's only that, somehow, that... not-good scent from Subaru-kun seems to have gotten stronger."

"A not-good scent, huh."

At her pointing it out, he rubbed his nose against his arm and tried to sniff himself, but he couldn't tell anything. If Rem said so, then the thick scent wafting off him had to be the Witch's Scent. Now that he thought back on it, several of Petelgeuse's remarks had seemed to hint at something close to that. It was a line of thought he'd considered once before and then forgotten about, but—

"So my Return by Death really is connected to the Witch, huh...?"

Every time he tried to reveal his Return by Death, the presence of the Witch surrounding Subaru grew stronger. In the forest of the Witchbeast he'd even turned that fact to his advantage once, but afterward, caught up in one busy thing after another, he'd left the matter unexamined, neglected.

Perhaps the unconscious avoidance of reaching that conclusion was itself part of the powerful force carried by the very word "Witch."

Before he could sink into a line of thinking he had never once tried before, Subaru smiled at Rem, who was watching his silence with anxious eyes. And then—

"Don't make a weird face, Rem. Ruins your cute looks, and it makes the road ahead look dark."

"I'm sorry. Rem is a worrier, so she can't help it..."

"Relax. Whatever happens, the man that is me doesn't change, and I'm not gonna lose to some scent or whatever. If anything, I'll turn it around and use it myself."

The cards in Subaru's hand were never many to begin with. In this world, Subaru didn't hold even the cards an ordinary person would have. So whatever traits he had that could serve as cards unlike anyone else's, whatever they might be, he had to count them as assets and keep them in hand. So what if it was the Witch's Scent? In the forest of the Witchbeast, he'd outwitted that malice neatly and turned it to his own convenience, hadn't he? He could go on doing the same, by whatever means necessary.

At Subaru's smile, Rem fell silent for a while, her eyes wandering as if unsure what attitude to take. To push her hesitation along, Subaru scratched his head and then held out his hand.

"Here, let's go. If you're worried I might wander off somewhere, just hold onto me."

"Eh?"

"You'd never win against me in a contest of strength, so that probably feels safer, right?"

At the remark, laced with an implicit hiding of embarrassment, Rem looked back and forth between Subaru's face and his hand.

"Yes."

Smiling, she took his hand, and stood not half a step behind but right beside him. And so the two set off walking side by side. Looking down slightly, staring fixedly at the hand she held, Rem kept her mouth firmly shut and said nothing, matching her pace to Subaru's steps.

Leading this endearing, uneasy girl along, Subaru made his way through the lower district of the royal capital toward the noble quarter, savoring the warmth of her palm — all the while letting his killing intent smolder on.

Their hands were joined, and yet their hearts faced in exactly opposite directions.

Natsuki Subaru's heart was, without end, being drawn darker, murkier, deeper and deeper into the depths.

This loop made, for Subaru, three loops in total.

The first had been the loop over the stolen insignia, on the day of his summoning. The second had been the loop set off by the Witchbeast uproar at Roswaal Manor. And this one now, the third, was the loop entangled with the Witch's Cult, whose full shape remained unclear.

In this third loop, Subaru had already died twice. In previous loops, once he had Returned by Death like this, he would organize his understanding of the situation up to that point and use it as fuel for devising some countermeasure.

"It's pathetic, but..."

This time there was a flaw: his memories of both the first and second loops were unclear.

Of the first world, he remembered things, if hazily. He and Rem had headed for the manor by dragon carriage; left behind partway, Subaru had made for the manor together with Otto. Then he and Otto had parted ways, and he'd reached the village — and from there, his memory was wrapped in a thick, indistinct fog. The fragments he could recall were Rem's death, Ram's death, and the one fact that he himself had died somewhere inside the manor.

In the second world, it was even worse. He had no memory at all of returning, and his memories only began to clear when he reached his confrontation with Petelgeuse — and even then, the clock had to be advanced all the way to around the time of Rem's murder before things sharpened. Given the broken state of Subaru's mind up to that point, it was perhaps unavoidable, but the memories before that were left as something even more fractured than fragmentary — mere shards. The one thing he had managed to gather and recall from scraping those shards together was—

"Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti...!"

The name of the hated madman — the wellspring of all of Subaru's drive to act now.

Understanding the wretchedness of his own circumstances, Subaru had to push his thoughts still deeper. That is, from the scattered memories of the first and second loops, he had to pick out what mattered, and break through the loop this third time — kill Petelgeuse, completely, without fail. For that, there was nothing to do but work his mind until his brain felt scorched.

First, he had to consider the actual time limit. The attack on Roswaal Manor, and the attack by the Witch's Cult on the village nearest the manor, would likely occur roughly half a day after Subaru arrived at the village. There shouldn't be any major discrepancy in dates between the first world and the second. Considering that in the first world, Subaru had left Crusch's residence three days after the trouble at Kadomon's shop, and it had then taken roughly two more days to return to the Mathers domain—

"The time limit is five days — no, more like four and a half...?"

There was nothing to do but grit his teeth at how little time there was. Even though the scale of the travel involved was vastly different, the time allotted was almost the same as it had been for the manor last time. Within that limited window, he had to annihilate the Witch's Cult — or at the very least, be certain to take down Petelgeuse.

Once he'd confirmed the time limit, next came confirming what was necessary to break the loop. Avoiding the attack on the manor and the village, and annihilating the Witch's Cult. Killing Petelgeuse for certain. Those were the problems Subaru had to solve this time. And if he pushed every one of them down to its purest essence, they all reduced to the last one alone — if Petelgeuse's murder was accomplished, the other conditions would naturally be resolved along with it. In other words, the condition that this fate had thrust upon Subaru this time was—

"Kill Petelgeuse. Just flat-out kill him."

Kill him — the root of all this evil, this vile murderer — and everything could be saved. It was a brutally simple, easy-to-understand rule this time around. All that remained was to find the answer needed to achieve that simple condition.

The forces led by Petelgeuse — the Witch's Cult — were, at the very least, not a scale he could stand against alone. Even Rem in her demon-transformed state, though she overwhelmed them individually, had ultimately been killed. How much of that was due to Petelgeuse's mysterious ability, "Unseen Hands," was unclear, but in any case, there was no way he could push that entire burden onto her alone to begin with.

What was needed to counter a group was, after all, the strength of a group. Against that kind of violence of numbers, the strength the Mathers domain held was rather low. To begin with, he had never once seen Roswaal, the lord of the domain, leading any private soldiers. Normally, a lord would be expected to keep at least some measure of force under his own banner, but in Roswaal's case, perhaps because his own personal power to annihilate whole groups was simply too great, the extent of any such stockpiled force remained unknown.

At the very least, in the two previous attacks, that unknown force had shown no sign of resisting. He could probably strike that option from his mind altogether.

No, wait — thinking back—

"Where was Roswaal, anyway, during that attack...?"

Neither in the first world nor the second had Subaru laid eyes on him. In the second, in fact, Subaru had been present when the manor itself collapsed, and the safety of the three people and one creature besides Rem and the villagers had been unknown. But at the very least, Roswaal had overlooked the attack on the village that was his own domain, and there was no trace of him having annihilated the Witch's Cult. What could be imagined was—

"Roswaal got hit by an assassination or a surprise attack right off the bat and was taken out of the fight. Or else he was absent from some crucial scene where he needed to be there... huh?"

If the former, all he could do was sigh at the Witch's Cult's thoroughness; if the latter, at the sheer bad timing. In fact, Roswaal was a man with a track record of being away from the manor during the previous incident, absent from the very scene that mattered most. The possibility couldn't be ruled out.

Further, in the process of recalling all this, Subaru found himself reflecting on the very end of the previous loop. Which was—

"That huge, incomprehensible creature that smashed the manor apart."

A four-legged beast of an unbelievable size, one that could be mistaken for the manor itself. The sight of it freezing the world with the cold air it spewed around itself had been overwhelming; he couldn't say for certain, but he was fairly sure that his own death had been freezing to death from that cold. If that thing had been one of the trump cards held by the Witch's Cult—

"Then we're even more short on firepower than I thought."

Even engaging the Witch's Cult alone, Rem by herself was conspicuously insufficient as a fighting force. And if a final boss was lurking behind them on top of that, it was out of the question. So the most urgent matter for Subaru, above all else, was strength — and specifically, strength enough to contend with both the Witch's Cult and some enormous boss-class enemy.

And for that strength, Subaru had someone in mind.

That was—

By the time Subaru brought Rem back to Crusch's residence, the hour had already crept toward evening, and the color of the sun had begun deepening into crimson.

"Welcome back."

The one who greeted the pair at the gate was Wilhelm. After a light bow from a distance, he watched the two of them walking close together and narrowed his eyes.

"Hm. Subaru-dono, a wandering heart is only to be expected in a young man, but personally, I can't say I approve much."

"What are you even saying, Wilhelm-san. Rem's just holding my hand so I don't get lost. Right?"

"Yes, of course that's it. Rem would be very worried if she took her eyes and hands off you and you got lost. I don't think we can let our guard down even inside the manor."

"No, I really think that part's fine, though..."

At Subaru's response to Wilhelm's teasing tone, Rem tightened her grip on his hand with a strange stubbornness. Wincing wryly at that, Subaru turned his attention back to Wilhelm.

"Somebody else here to see Crusch-san again?"

The question was prompted by the dragon carriage parked beside Wilhelm. Pulling the ostentatiously decorated vehicle was — not fur, but a black Earth Dragon whose hard, glossy hide had an almost dignified sheen to it. Whether Earth Dragons had bloodlines the way horses did, he didn't know, but at the very least, this one carried a bearing that set it apart from every Earth Dragon he'd seen before. The driver seated up front, too, wore formal dress crisply pressed, and having given a silent nod of greeting, showed no sign of idle chatter.

Sensing the meaning behind Subaru's question and gaze, Wilhelm dipped his chin.

"Yes, that's right. Ever since Crusch-sama announced her participation in the Royal Selection, there's been no end of people requesting audience with her. Though of course, there are also those Crusch-sama herself invites."

"So basically people trying to butter up someone who might be the future king. Well, I bet that comes with all kinds of headaches."

At the blunt, unvarnished summary of the truth, Wilhelm gave a wry smile. Then, suddenly, he looked into Subaru's eyes and his expression tightened. Subaru, puzzled by the change in his manner, tilted his head—

"Subaru-dono. Did something change in your heart, down there?"

"Eh? Where's that coming from all of a sudden. Do I look different or something?"

At the sharp question, Subaru shrugged it off with a joking air and spun around on the spot. Rem, still holding his hand, was made to spin along with him. Wilhelm closed his eyes at the sight.

"There is a darkness nesting in your eyes. A genuine one — not an imitation."

Subaru's feet stopped dead, and the vacant smile vanished from his face. And rising up from beneath that vague smile was his "real" one.

"That's not nice, Wilhelm-san. Makes it sound like something weird's happened to me."

"I wouldn't call it a small change. To carry that dim light in one's eyes requires no small reason. — I know this myself."

Wilhelm, replying in a low voice, likewise carved a faint smile at the corner of his mouth. Sensing that there was nothing bright in it, only sharpened feeling toward some "something" coated over in pure resolve, Subaru found a certain understanding settle in his chest. Wilhelm, too, was a man with killing intent smoldering toward something unforgivable. That was likely why he could see through Subaru just as Subaru had sensed it in him.

"Are you gonna keep me at a distance?"

From Wilhelm's position, Subaru's present precariousness would surely give him pause about letting him near his lord. But Subaru was half-certain that wouldn't happen. And indeed—

"No. You should do as you see fit, Subaru-dono. Compared to how you were until just a moment ago, refusing to look at anything at all — the you standing here now is, to me, far more agreeable."

To the question Subaru had thrown out, Wilhelm answered by shaking his head. Naturally, the same kind of smile that graced Wilhelm's face now rose on Subaru's own. The two of them, without knowing the deep interior of each other's hearts, were at least acquainted on the surface. And so, as the two exchanged their somewhat dark smiles—

"Subaru-kun. You're making a bad face."

"Heheh— ow, ow, ow, ow! Rem, hey, that's gonna come off!"

His ear was abruptly yanked, cutting their wicked little conference short. As Subaru protested, Rem turned narrowed eyes on him.

"Please don't make Rem anxious."

"Whoa, first time in forever Rem's actually making a demand, and it's this vague? Relax, Rem. I'll handle it all, make it all work out somehow."

With what he had to do already clear, Subaru felt no anxiety now. There was something he had to do, and for that, something he needed to accomplish — the simplicity of knowing that was, for now, a comfort. All he had to do was kill the person who had to be killed. How easy that made things feel.

Subaru smiled, spinning out gentle words. And yet, at hearing them, Rem's expression only grew more devoid of feeling, and she gripped his hand even tighter, as if frightened of something. Then, looking troubled, she seemed about to say something—

"It appears the guest is leaving."

Wilhelm, glancing toward the manor, murmured this, and beyond the gate, from the direction of Crusch's residence where the front door had opened, a figure could be seen walking out.

A tall man with long, dull-gold hair. Dressed in finely tailored formal wear and decked out in ornaments, at first glance he planted in Subaru's mind the image of new money. Somewhere around thirty, probably. A profligate noble with loose purse strings — that was his first impression. Subaru's tentatively christened "new money" had no idea he'd been so labeled, and upon reaching the gate, his eyes fell on the three gathered there.

"Well, well. Quite the unusual gathering of faces."

Touching what was apparently his own fashionably grown goatee, "new money" appraised them with that remark. At the baffling sense that his identity was somehow already known, Subaru frowned, and the man bowed politely at the waist.

"Forgive my rudeness. I am Russell Fellow. Pleased to make your acquaintance. — Natsuki Subaru-dono."

"...Much obliged for the courtesy. Mind if I ask how you know my name, while we're at it? Not to toot my own horn, but I'm supposed to be a personality-free everyman, so having my name out there is kind of embarrassing."

"A little connection here and there, that's all. During the Royal Selection council, the man who claimed to be the knight of the candidate Emilia-sama became rather famous. Though not many people would know that this same man is currently convalescing at Crusch-sama's residence."

Against Subaru's rising wariness, Russell, his tongue as loose as ever, smiled with a face free of malice. But the content of what he said only invited further wariness, and the man's apparently deliberate ill nature came through plainly.

"Russell-dono. Did your discussion with Crusch-sama go well?"

But Wilhelm cut in from the side, snatching away the dangerous glare Subaru was starting to level at the man. Russell shifted his gaze from Subaru to the old man and shrugged, shaking his head. "No," he said.

"Crusch-sama is a fierce woman. Indeed, the eyes she turns on us are sharp, and her will unyielding. Given the history between us, it won't go easily."

"Is that so. A shame. If even you won't bend, getting the others to nod along will be an ordeal indeed."

"With the standing of a duke's house, and with Wilhelm-dono present, the conditions are almost too favorable for a start to the Royal Selection. ...You go by Wilhelm Trias now, is that right?"

The two continued a conversation, calling each other by name, that made no sense to Subaru. Wilhelm dipped his chin at Russell's words and bowed his deeply lined face.

"For me to bear my wife's family name now would be far too presumptuous."

"You too are a fierce one. Dazzling, to those of us who cannot live that way. Still, I'll be rooting for the two of you."

Tossing that surface-level remark at the old man, who lowered his eyes, Russell stepped up into his dragon carriage — his own property, presumably — and, giving instructions to the silent driver, said,

"Should Crusch-sama's aim this time come to pass, it would be a happy thing for us as well. It would be a long-cherished wish fulfilled for you too, Wilhelm-dono. I look forward to it."

The carriage door closed behind him, and once the driver drew the reins taut, the vehicle began to move. True to its handler's silence, the Earth Dragon set off without so much as a whinny, and as they watched the departing carriage pull away from the gate, Subaru stepped over to Wilhelm's side.

"Wilhelm-san. Who was that guy just now?"

"Russell Fellow. The abacus man who runs the royal capital's Commerce Guild. His title is merely head of one merchant house, but he's a shrewd operator involved in the open and hidden movements of wealth throughout the capital. You'd do well to assume he knows more about you, Subaru-dono, than just your name."

"Ugh. Being known by some old guy instead of a cute girl doesn't exactly get my heart racing."

"Hm, I share the sentiment."

Responding solemnly to the quip, Wilhelm straightened his posture and turned to face Subaru. "Now then," he prefaced, then—

"Today's visitors end with Russell-dono just now, I believe. Shall we go inside? ...Though, Subaru-dono, is there something on your mind?"

At Wilhelm's question, Subaru scratched his head and twisted his mouth. Being read that easily wasn't exactly fun, like he'd been anticipated. Still, if it meant the conversation would go quickly, there was nothing bad about it. Thinking so, Subaru straightened his back before Wilhelm and set his face into something serious.

"Sorry, but I'm today's last visitor. I want to talk to Crusch-san. The topic's something like — would you lend me your strength."
