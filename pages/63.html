Arc 2 Chapter 39: "A Promise to Stay Up Late"

With Rem accompanying him in full battle readiness, an iron ball for "self-defense" in hand, the search of the night forest continued.

The moonlight was blocked by the trees, and the darkness pooling black through the forest ran deep. Dodging the trees that blocked the way ahead, pushing through branches and leaves as they advanced, abrasions that welled blood sprang up here and there on Subaru's body.

The small stinging pain of those cuts, and the frustration of groping blindly through the dark, fumbling for answers.

Combined with the lack of time, all of it weighed heavily on Subaru's spirit.

"Honestly, I didn't expect it to be this dark. How are you doing okay with this, Rem-rin?"

"In forests, in mountains, my Divine Protection helps... more importantly, please don't raise your voice so much. Keep your breathing down too—even your heartbeat is loud."

"S-t-o-p-p-i-n-g... is that even possible?"

Objecting in a hushed voice, breaking each syllable apart, Subaru slowly followed the back of Rem, who led the way ahead.

Without her, he'd have lost track of even his own position by now and been stuck standing still. Whether it was her night vision, Rem's steps forward were light and unhesitating.

Through a forest of darkness that Subaru couldn't even make out dimly, she was blazing a trail, clearing the way so that he, following behind, could walk it easily.

Even the moonlight felt distant at the bottom of that darkness, and the only thing he could rely on was Rem's back, walking ahead of him. That small figure before his eyes looked so large that, without thinking, he reached out and pinched the hem of her maid uniform.

"...What are you doing?"

"Overcome by the helplessness of the darkness, I unconsciously clung to you—that's the scene."

"If you're going to hold on so we don't get separated, please hold my shoulder or something properly."

"I don't want to get in the way of that back moving forward without hesitation. But I can't bear the helplessness either. That kind of fragile, wistful maidenly heart just... came out, you know."

"Since when do you have the nerve to say something like that... You were being a little cool just a moment ago."

Rem seemed at a loss for words, exasperated. She stopped, letting Subaru keep hold of her hem, and while looking around in a circle, gave a small sniff.

Pointing at that police-dog-like gesture, Subaru said,

"You've been doing that little bit for a while now, but can you actually track by scent?"

"It's the one thing that can't be faked. Appearance and voice can be disguised, but the scent that seeps out from one's very existence cannot."

Rem's declaration was brimming with confidence. All the more reason to pray her sense of smell would lead them to their goal.

Watching her for a moment, Subaru had the fleeting sense that he'd once had some kind of conversation with Rem about scent before. That time, if he recalled, it had been the "Witch's scent" that was the issue. Rem had said that a foul stench came from him.

Now that he thought of it, he recalled Beatrice saying something similar about him. The situation didn't allow for it now, but if things were settled and he still remembered—

"—! Close, it's the smell of a living creature."

Beside Subaru, deep in thought, Rem turned her face to the right, narrowing her eyes. Subaru turned to follow her gaze, but all that spread before them was the same unchanging darkness.

Frustrated but turning back to Rem anyway, he asked,

"Is it the kids' scent!?"

"I don't know, but it doesn't smell like a beast."

"That's good enough. Let's go!"

"This way."

Crouching low, Rem broke into a run, and Subaru, still gripping the hem of her clothes, chased after her.

Somehow, there seemed to be a lift in Rem's voice too. At the literal glimmer of light seen in the darkness, their steps quickened without either of them meaning it to.

Still, hope and anxiety are two sides of the same coin.

The fact that Rem hadn't declared outright that the scent she'd caught belonged to the children was surely, unconsciously, an admission of that same anxiety.

Which was precisely why the two of them needed to find their answer as quickly as possible.

Leading the way, Rem swung her iron ball roughly, gouging away trees that spread across the full width of the path to clear it. Her frantic method betrayed a strong sense of urgency, and Subaru, following behind, found his own footsteps growing correspondingly forceful, kicking hard against the ground.

His breath grew short, his legs heavy. And yet his mind stayed clear. Gradually his eyes began adjusting to the pitch black, and Subaru's vision began to catch faint glimpses of the forest scene.

Then, suddenly, the view opened up before Subaru's eyes. In the middle of the forest was a space where the trees gaped open like an open mouth. A small hill spread out there, the sky open above it, and the moonlight pouring down lent the place an almost fantastical air.

And on that small green hill, bathed in the dazzling blessing of moonlight—

"The kids!"

There, on the green floor, lay the children, sprawled limply with their limbs thrown out.

Rushing over, Subaru and Rem together checked on the children's safety.

Six were lying there in total. As far as visible skin went, there were no external injuries; none of them were conscious, but none had stopped breathing either.

"They're alive. —They're alive!"

Subaru clenched his fist and let out a cheer of triumph, having made it in time.

But beside him, Rem, examining the condition of a sleeping girl, said "No," as a preface, and continued,

"They're still breathing for now, but their weakness is severe. At this rate..."

"Weakness... a curse!?"

Looking closer, the children's faces were pale, their ragged breaths gasping like fish stranded on a hill. Cold sweat beaded terribly on their foreheads, and their sleeping faces were twisted in pain, as if tormented by nightmares.

Curse Arts—something that had struck at Subaru's own body more than once. Seeing it now from the outside, Subaru bit his lip.

"Isn't there something, anything we can do? Rem, can't you undo the curse?"

"Not with Rem's skill, not nearly enough... If only my sister were here to see this."

Just as urgency was driving Subaru, Rem too bitterly acknowledged her own lack of power. But she shook her head, as if to cast off that weakness at once.

"In any case, I'll cast a healing spell, even if it's just a stopgap measure. We need to bring back at least some of their strength."

"Yeah, please. What should I do?"

"Watch our surroundings. And gather the children together in one place."

Following Rem's instructions, Subaru lined the children up in a row before her. Looking at their labored breathing with a pained expression, Rem used the iron ball's handle to carve a circle into the ground.

Crouching at the edge of the circle that fully enclosed the children, she placed her hand on it.

"Blessing of water's healing, I beseech thee—"

Following the incantation, a faint blue light rose within the crude circle, gently enveloping the sleeping children's bodies, and slowly seeping that light into them.

Even to Subaru, who knew little of magic, that gleam felt like a wave of healing—a gentle, soft shimmer.

As time passed, the light permeated the children's bodies, and gradually their labored breathing began to shift, if only slightly, toward something calmer.

"That's amazing, Rem. With this..."

"I told you, it's just a stopgap. As long as I can keep speaking to the mana within their bodies like this, they might be all right, but once it runs dry, this won't even work as first aid. We need to remove the root cause as soon as possible."

"Breaking the curse, right. That means we have no choice but to go back to the mansion and rely on Beako and Puck. All right, let's head back. Carry the kids and—"

"—Subaru?"

Just as he'd resolved on an immediate retreat, Subaru was startled to suddenly hear his name called. Turning toward the voice, he saw a brown-haired boy with his eyes half-open.

The boy, apparently regaining consciousness, fixed his eyes on Subaru and, gasping painfully, said,

"Subaru, Sub—"

"You're awake, Lucas. Good, that's a good boy, a strong boy. But don't push yourself. We'll get you home right away and say goodbye to whatever's causing you all this pain. Just leave it to me, so rest easy now..."

"Someone... deeper... still... deeper in"

"...Hey, what did you say?"

Lucas tried to convey something, his words breaking off in fragments.

Sensing something in those fragmentary words he couldn't afford to miss, Subaru asked again. But Lucas had already closed his eyes once more, losing consciousness, and the question didn't reach him.

Subaru let out a small curse, "damn it," then turned his gaze toward the depths of the forest.

"If the group that was at that spot during the day are the missing kids, then... ah, damn it, it's true. That girl with the braids is missing."

Six children slept here, all of them children whose names Subaru knew. But missing from the group was the girl who had led Subaru to the puppies that afternoon. Shy, perhaps, she was a timid child who'd had trouble joining Subaru's circle.

That girl was nowhere to be seen. And Lucas, with the fragment of consciousness that had briefly returned to him, hadn't said "help me" or "it hurts"—he'd said "that girl was taken deeper in."

A child not even ten years old yet, even now with his own life under threat, had worried not about himself but about someone else—his friend's safety.

"God damn it."

Cursing once more, Subaru stood up on the spot.

Since he apparently hadn't quite caught the exchange with Lucas, Rem looked at him with a questioning expression. Subaru turned to her, pointing a finger, and asked,

"Question, Rem. If you keep casting the healing spell on the kids continuously, can you extend the time limit?"

"...As things stand, yes, it's possible. But truly, that would only buy time. And Rem would be entirely tied down here."

Unable to grasp Subaru's intent, Rem's answer came out hesitant.

Hearing this, Subaru's mind spun rapidly. What should he do? In this situation, useless as he was, what was the best way for him to act?

There was only one course of action he could think of. It would mean quite a gamble, but—

"Rem. One of the kids is still being taken deeper in. I think that's probably where that Witchbeast thing is."

"Deeper in... but if they were taken one at a time, that would mean—"

Subaru stopped Rem with an outstretched palm as she started to voice the worst-case scenario. With her words cut off, he furrowed his brow.

"I know the situation is bad too. But we managed to save the lives of the kids we thought were in serious danger. So shouldn't we make the effort to save the rest too?"

"You might get greedy and end up losing even the ones you were supposed to be able to save and bring back."

"That's exactly why I have Rem, isn't it?"

Rem, pressing her case, looked taken aback. In front of her, Subaru spread both arms wide.

"With Rem's strength, carrying all six kids and escaping wouldn't be impossible, right? On the flip side, I thought I was decently strong myself, but carrying six kids is out of the question for me. Two at most, maybe three if I really push it. I can't use healing magic either. Right now, here, there's basically nothing I can do. So the piece that's free to move should be put to good use."

"............"

"I'll go check what's deeper in. Rem, take the kids and head back to the village once. —Don't worry, I won't do anything reckless, rash, or foolish, and I can't anyway. Once you've handed the kids off to the villagers, just come flying back."

Rem listened in silence to Subaru's utterly unreasonable logic.

Even as her gaze showed no sign of softening, Subaru's lips kept rattling on with fast talk,

"If the kid deeper in is... well, if it's the worst-case situation, I'll turn back too. But if there's still hope, I can at least buy some time."

"We don't even know what kind of opponent it is, and we don't know how quickly Rem can get back. In the worst case, we might even lose track of each other and fail to reunite..."

Unable to accept Subaru's decision, Rem grabbed his sleeve and pressed her argument.

Was she worried for his safety? Or was it that she simply couldn't swallow such a reckless plan?

Thinking it would be nice if it were the former, Subaru gave a wry smile and pried her fingers from his sleeve. Then, taking her freed fingers in his own,

"Don't worry, I won't lose track of you."

"On what grounds..."

"Oh, I've got grounds, all right."

Laughing, Subaru touched a finger to his own nose, then pointed at her face.

"Your nose—you're confident in it, right? And, well, apparently I actually give off a pretty nose-catching smell. L-rin and V-chan have pointed it out to me more than a few times."

Rem fell silent, her eyes widening.

Doubling down on her astonishment, pointing at her with two fingers, as if to get back at some Rem from another time, a Rem this one didn't know about, Subaru said,

"Even if no one else notices, you're the only one who'll notice my smell. The stench I carry with me, the lingering scent of a sinner—right?"

"Subaru-kun... how much do you... know...?"

"Who knows, there's a whole lot I don't know. So much I don't know, that yesterday and today and tomorrow, no matter how many times I repeat it, I still can't quite reach the answer I'm looking for."

At the abrupt shift in topic, Rem looked as though she'd been left in a fog. While Subaru thought it couldn't be helped that she'd react that way, he was also somewhat astonished at his own state of mind—that he could turn this into something to laugh about.

Well, well. He wanted to praise himself for having developed such thick nerves.

"Just like you probably have a ton of things you want to ask me, I've got a ton of things I want to ask you too. So once everything's settled, let's talk. All night, in each other's rooms, in our pajamas—a late-night girls' talk. It's a promise, okay?"

And with that, he forcibly intertwined his fingers, still holding hers, with his own.

With their little fingers linked together, Rem looked at Subaru with bewildered eyes. In front of her, Subaru shook their linked fingers up and down broadly.

"Pinky swear, sealed."

"W-what was that just now...?"

"It's a ritual for promises, back where I'm from. Break it, and you have to swallow a thousand needles. Each one does the damage of one, times a thousand hits—so in an RPG where HP only goes up to three digits, that's an instant-kill move."

Piled on one after another, Subaru's words and actions were now well beyond Rem's comprehension. To Rem, confused upon confused and at a loss for words, Subaru prefaced with "basically" and continued,

"I trust you, Rem-rin. So I want to act in a way that earns your trust too. Let's make that promise now, for that reason."

"————"

"I told you, right? I'm the type who keeps promises, and makes others keep theirs too. So you don't need to worry about me—I'm totally fine, no worries."

"'No worries'..."

Finally unable to hold it in any longer, Rem let out an exasperated sigh and laughed weakly.

Unable to fully suppress the urge to laugh, Rem kept laughing quietly, and as if drawn in by it, Subaru laughed too, stifling his voice.

After laughing for a while, once it subsided, Rem looked up and said,

"It's a promise. —I really will be asking you all sorts of things, you know."

"I'll tell you the name of my first love, anything you want. Except the name of the girl I currently like."

"I already know that one."

"And it doesn't even reach her!"

Something about this exchange with her felt like something he'd once left behind.

Reproducing it now gave Subaru a sense of satisfaction. Rem, too, with a serious expression, said,

"I'll be right back. Please, absolutely do not do anything reckless. Or Emilia-sama will be furious with me."

"I don't want to get an earful from Emilia-tan either, so I'll be careful. —Besides, it'll be fine. Today's me is pretty much on a demon-possessed streak."

"Demon-possessed...?"

"The demon version of being god-possessed! My new favorite thing lately!"

Crossing both arms, spreading his legs, grasping at empty air—a new pose.

What did Rem think of Subaru's flippant bravado?

She didn't remark on Subaru's attitude, only saying,

"See you soon."

With that single line left behind, she scooped up the children all together. The sight of the small girl, on her small shoulders, carrying six people despite being young herself, looked almost like a circus act, and even in a situation like this, Subaru couldn't help but laugh as he watched her retreating back grow distant.

Left alone on the small hill in the forest, in a world where silence had fallen, Subaru let out a deep breath and slowly turned to face the direction in question.

"Well then, guess it's time to get to it, Subaru old boy."

Saying this as if to rouse himself against the trembling that threatened to take hold, he bit down hard on the little finger that had sealed the promise, and took off running.

—Waiting at his destination: despair, or hope, or something else entirely.

The morning of the fourth day, which should have come so many times before, now felt very far away.
