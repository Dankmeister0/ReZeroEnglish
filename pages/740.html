Arc 9 Chapter 44: "A Secret Pact Beneath the Surface"

—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never speak of this.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—If you're a father too, don't you dare lay a hand on a kid!!"

More than the fist that struck straight through his face, it was those words that struck the man harder. —Watching the scene unfold, old man Rom imagined that must have been the pain Heinkel felt.

Relying on his own resilience to blows, Heinkel had tried a counter-strike, taking the hit first before striking back, attempting to retaliate against Flam and Grassis — but at the last instant it was flawlessly blocked by Gaston, or more precisely, by the arrangement of the girl who had used "Compression" to force Gaston into that spot.

"Well, I'll be. Quite the something, that..."

While confirming that the twin sisters had safely slipped free of their opponent's reach, what caught old man Rom's eye was the girl perched on his shoulder — Petra's profile.

"—"

Petra narrowed her large, round eyes, her lovely features drawn taut, her serious gaze fixed without a moment's carelessness on the two battlefields.

It was the battle between Roy Alphard of "Gluttony" and the swordsman who had betrayed the kingdom—the "Turncoat Blade," Heinkel. Originally, the ringleader Aldebaran and his attendant, the shinobi, had been present as well, along with the "Divine Dragon" that had surrendered to his banner.

Though they'd discussed breaking down that dangerous group and creating a situation where they could defeat them one at a time, it would not be an exaggeration to say that the key to whether that plan succeeded or failed rested in Petra's hands.

The plan had been built around the counsel of Clind, who knew the Authority of "Melancholy" inside and out, and everyone had united to make it succeed — but the greatest contributor was Petra.

Without her, this battle couldn't even have begun. — And that was still true now.

"—"

Petra bit her lip and dug her nails hard into the shoulder of the seated old man Rom. A girl's nails were nothing he'd call painful, but he could hardly imagine the state of mind that made her do it without realizing.

The burden of keeping close at hand the Witch Factor that turned a person into a "Witch" or a Sin Archbishop was something only the one who bore it could truly know. And the role Petra had taken on was one for which old man Rom's own name had also been raised as a candidate.

A position better suited to someone lacking in direct combat strength but strong in quick wit and judgment, standing on the battlefield in that capacity.

Old man Rom and Petra were exactly that sort, and in the end it was the difference in their factions that decided it — but the role Petra bore might just as easily have fallen to Rom.

So old man Rom had made up his mind: in this battle, getting Felt back came first, but second was giving his all for Petra's sake. He would pour every ounce of himself into it, so that her burden might be lightened even a little, and so that no oversight or failure would creep into his reading of the battle or his instructions.

"Flower three, the Queen. Thunder two, the Noble. Flower five, the Hunter."

Steadily surveying the battlefield, old man Rom called out his instructions so that Petra on his shoulder could hear them. Each time she acted on them and invoked her Authority, the marks of her nails on his shoulder grew deeper and more numerous.

Basically, "Compression" had its use in the battle against "Gluttony." Roy seemed to realize that old man Rom's group had a hand in orchestrating the fight, but Ram's maneuvering was outstanding. She skillfully kept drawing his attention, making sure he couldn't turn on those directing the battle.

Even with "Compression" thrown in, the back-and-forth exchange remained razor-thin, thanks to the sheer number of moves and cards "Gluttony" had at his disposal — a stark contrast to the other battlefield, where the overwhelming lack of moves and options told a very different story.

"—gh."

Even his groans of pain were swallowed by the sound of blows as Heinkel was beaten again and again.

With Gaston joining the sisters Flam and Grassis, brute strength added to the twins who churned the fight up with speed, what unfolded was one-sided violence that could only be called a lynching or a punishment. But so long as their opponent refused to drop to his knees, Flam and the others couldn't afford to ease up either.

Both battlefields needed a stone cast into them to set things moving. —And it was the instant after he thought that.

"—Grandpa Rom," Petra called out, and her eyes met old man Rom's.

"—"

Just that crossing of gazes was enough for old man Rom to understand that he and Petra had arrived at the same idea. Begrudging even the time it would take to nod, he kept his eyes sharp for the perfect instant.

And then—

"Sun five, the Queen—!"

On the instruction called out, with the hand resting on old man Rom's shoulder and power gathering in her round eyes, Petra activated "Compression" — and beautifully, the combined attack of Ram and the other two drove home against Roy with brutal force.

"—All right!"

Roy's diminutive frame was caught in the wind, took the combined attack of the "Hog King" and the two Witchbeasts, and was sent flying. Scattering blood into the air as he was knocked skyward, Roy had surely taken a grievous wound.

Just before the finishing blow landed, Roy had tried to unleash "Gluttony's" Authority on Ram, but it had misfired, and the fatal opening that created had been decisive too. — Old man Rom was struck anew with admiration, both for Ram's nerve in laying that trap and actually pulling it off, and for the idea Petra had proposed.

—Ram, now Ram Leyte.

An unnatural adoption that turned the system on its head — applied to every member of the front line who might have been targeted by "Gluttony" — it was an unconventional stratagem meant to betray the Sin Archbishop's scheme.

"Well now, with this..."

The situation had moved. It was exactly the move he'd wanted to break the deadlock, now made real, and old man Rom clenched his big fist tight.

And riding that momentum, he turned his eyes to set the other situation moving as well—

"...Nobody move. Not if you value this brat's— this person's life."

—There too, the situation had changed, and he found himself facing the worst possible crisis: Heinkel, holding Felt in a chokehold with a sword pressed to her.

—Gods, Buddhas, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never tell my favorite story.

"Sorry about everything up to now. In every sense, I've put too much of a burden on you, old man."

Aldebaran offered that apology after he'd mercilessly knocked Heinkel down by force, when Heinkel — mid-conversation with Felt — had nearly done something rash.

Honestly, Heinkel was well aware he'd been about to do something irreversible, and he'd already reflected that he had no right to complain about being struck. There was no option but to accept the apology.

Except — the fact that Felt had caught onto — that one thing, at least, could absolutely not be left as it was.

Heinkel's wife, forever asleep: Louanna Astrea.

That the sleep of Heinkel's most beloved woman had its cause in their son, Reinhard.

"Well, hold on. I won't ask exactly what went on between you and little miss Felt. But I said it, didn't I? Sorry for dumping all the burden on you, old man."

"...I don't see where this is going. You apologize, and then what?"

"Of course, I'm not saying 'I apologized, so keep enduring it.' For now, we'll handle the matter of little miss Felt on our end."

"'Handle,' you say, but..."

"I won't do anything dangerous. Might be something rough, though."

At Aldebaran's shrug and those words, Heinkel's cheeks stiffened.

He was a man with an unfathomable side to him, impossible to read. Still, Heinkel understood that grief over losing Priscilla was what drove him to start turning the whole world into his enemy.

At the root of Aldebaran, there was indeed that fierce love for the "Sun Princess."

But even so, Aldebaran had retained a bare thread of decency that stopped short of saying he'd sacrifice absolutely anything for it. — And now Heinkel felt as if that thread had snapped, the restraint finally cast off.

"What... what are you planning to do?"

"I won't do you wrong, old man. There's just one thing I want to confirm."

Without meaning to, Heinkel's throat clicked as he swallowed, and he felt a fierce thirst. It was that peculiar premonition granted only to cowards — half wanting, half not wanting, to hear what came next.

Feigning ignorance of what was going on inside Heinkel, Aldebaran voiced his so-called question.

It was—

"That royal who was supposedly kidnapped from the castle fifteen years ago — what was their name, again—"

Speaking that name aloud had, for Heinkel — no, within the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica itself — come at some point to be treated as something like a forbidden word.

Fifteen years ago, kidnapped from Royal Lugunica Castle, and ever since, her whereabouts utterly unknown — the daughter of the king's younger brother.

It wasn't as though no royal had ever lived an unfortunate life across the kingdom's long history. But she alone was the one born under a Blessing whose growth, and whose end, no one ever spoke of.

"—Filore Lugunica."

Daughter of Ford Lugunica, younger brother of the forty-first king of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, Randohal Lugunica — the royal Heinkel had been suspected of kidnapping fifteen years ago.

Granted, that kidnapping charge had been a false accusation — but in a roundabout way, Heinkel had ultimately committed an irreversible act of disrespect against the royal family he'd sworn loyalty to, against Ford, to whom he owed such a great debt.

It was an act of betrayal against the royal family and their friendship, more unmistakable even than treason against the kingdom itself.

"—Itadakimasu."

The ritual conducted right before his eyes — sinful and grotesque.

From Aldebaran's question, Heinkel had sensed this coming, and had perhaps even hoped for it. Even knowing that this ritual had been the trigger for so many tragedies — even so.

Because if it succeeded, Heinkel wouldn't have to kill her.

"Gochisousama deshita!!"

At the sight of Roy's small back, hands pressed together, declaring the ritual finished, Heinkel snapped back to himself. So desperate had he been to look away that he'd literally missed the act before his eyes by losing himself in thought.

Even as he piled up conduct unbecoming of a knight, Heinkel found it almost laughable that some part of his heart still creaked with unease — and, stepping forward as Roy, laughing merrily, stepped back, he took his place.

And then—

"—Lady Filore."

Kneeling on one knee, Heinkel addressed her so.

What answer had he actually been hoping for? If she answered that yes, that was her, it would mean the ritual had failed. If she answered that she didn't know what that meant, then that too would surely have left Heinkel in anguish.

And so, the answer given to Heinkel — who himself held no answer of his own — was—

"Whoever might that be? No, in fact..."

"—"

"—Who am I, exactly?"

That reply, meaning the ritual had succeeded, ended up wounding Heinkel and plunging him into anguish all the same.

"Filore. So that's my name... It doesn't quite feel right."

Filore's demeanor, now that her "Memory" had been devoured, was truly like that of an entirely different person.

As if it had all been a lie — the coarseness and rough upbringing that had once shown through in her gestures, mannerisms, even the way she spoke — she now carried herself with a grace and gentleness befitting royalty, and it left Heinkel thoroughly astonished.

Had it not been for the tragedy fifteen years ago, she would surely have been a member of the royal family beloved by all.

"Then again, she might've died of the same sickness as the rest of the royal family instead."

To Heinkel, lost in such sentiment, Aldebaran said something heartless.

But it was true. That mysterious epidemic that seemed to target the royal family specifically — had Filore remained in the castle, she surely wouldn't have escaped it either. — Heinkel began to grasp what that might mean, but he shut his eyes and refused to look at it directly.

Heinkel was already at his limit, body and soul. With the fulfillment of his long-cherished wish right before his eyes, his heart couldn't bear the shocking truths piling on, one after another.

"Is something troubling you, perhaps?"

It was Filore who asked this of Heinkel, as he unconsciously covered his face with his hands.

Without her "Memory," she herself was surely in a far more precarious position — and yet, in the sight of her worrying for someone else regardless, Heinkel truly glimpsed the blood of the Lugunica royal family.

At the same time, he became certain of a cruel truth — one confirmed the moment "Gluttony's" Authority had activated: that she was, without doubt, Filore Lugunica.

And thus—

"...So this is the end of the Royal Selection, then."

"And why's that, old man?"

"What do you mean, 'why'? The whole reason for holding the Royal Selection is to find the 'next' one fit for the throne. To put it bluntly, it's a fallback measure born from the royal line's extinction... If it turns out the line never actually died out, everyone's bound to turn on a dime."

Ironically, it was a truth uncovered only because Priscilla had died, because the rampaging Aldebaran had broken "Gluttony" out, and because she, before losing her "Memory," had gouged straight into Heinkel's weak point.

It was almost as if the world itself had arranged things so that Filore might ascend to the throne.

Of course, Heinkel would never voice something as callous as saying even Priscilla's death had served that end — and ultimately, if Filore were to take the throne, that was exactly what he wished for.

To wake Louanna. If only that alone could be achieved, then everything else settling safely into place was exactly what Heinkel wished for too.

So: the "Witch of Envy" withdraws, and Filore takes the throne. That was exactly what he wanted.

"Alter, this is the way things settle most smoothly. Got a problem with that?"

"...Right at the very edge of what I could compromise on, without hurting the girl. I'd call it a splendid solution, Origin. Only—"

"Only?"

"Is this a scheme Origin thought up all on his own? Because it doesn't seem like you, if you ask me."

"—"

Even as Heinkel talked himself into accepting all this, Aldebaran and the "Divine Dragon" were bickering in that way over how to handle Filore. Still, at least the problem Heinkel had been carrying was set aside for now. Though, of course, this created a new problem — namely, having to bring along a Filore with no "Memory" —

"Oh, that won't be a problem at all~. Right, Al-sama?"

Heinkel caught his breath as Yae's face suddenly poked into view right beside him.

As if she'd seen straight through the anxiety in Heinkel's heart, she shot him a mocking sidelong glance, then coaxed Aldebaran along in that sweet, wheedling way of hers.

There was nothing unusual about Yae's faintly mocking attitude. What felt off was Aldebaran's attitude toward her.

"...Right. I did make a promise to the old man, after all."

"Huh? A promise?"

"That we'd handle things on our end when it came to Felt — no, little miss Filore."

So saying, Aldebaran, in that breezy manner of his — yet with an attitude that carried an unmistakable coldness — carried out that "handling," leaving the bewildered Heinkel behind.

At that decisiveness of his — no, at an attitude that had, for better or worse, swung fully to one extreme — Heinkel was thrown into confusion.

The shift in the relationship between Aldebaran and Yae, the "Divine Dragon's" feelings toward Filore, the expanded range of freedom granted to "Gluttony," the truth about Filore now uncovered. — Theirs was never a tightly-bound relationship to begin with, but Heinkel could feel the strain and the fractures between them steadily growing.

Amid all that, Heinkel resolved that he, at least, would keep his sanity intact.

No matter how suffocating a bind he found himself in, it was Heinkel who would suffer most if this group fell apart before its goal was achieved.

So—

"—If nothing else, let me at least stay sane."

So Heinkel swore, as he took hold of the result of that "handling" — Filore, sealed inside a black sphere about the size of a beanbag.

He had sworn it.

"If you don't want Lady Filore hurt, back off...!"

—And yet he had sworn.

Now, cornered, Heinkel had reversed that resolve entirely — holding the grown Filore in a chokehold, sword pressed to her slender throat.

"Felt...!"

At Heinkel's outrage, the sturdily built man called Filore by a different name, his voice tight with frustration.

Flam and Grassis, who had been raining down endless blows, also stopped moving, facing Heinkel with eyes mixing anger and contempt with no small amount of worry and confusion.

For them, the shock must have come doubled, one atop the other.

Just as they themselves kept vanishing and reappearing, Filore had suddenly appeared in Heinkel's grasp — and on top of that, Heinkel had called her by a different name.

It was a scene where anxiety and confusion showing plainly in their eyes could hardly be helped.

"My apologies, but I'd rather not resort to anything rough. You'll have to come along."

Telling this to Filore as he held her in a chokehold, Heinkel loathed his own hypocrisy.

He had tried. Heinkel had made every effort not to drag Filore into this battle that had erupted so suddenly. But he'd given up. As always, his efforts had borne no fruit.

So he shattered the black sphere entrusted to him by Aldebaran, released the sealed Filore, and used her as a hostage to threaten the enemies who had appeared.

"Don't move. You don't want this person hurt, do you."

That even while staining his hands with such a rotten act, he still hypocritically paid Filore every courtesy, was proof that some fragment of decency remained in Heinkel after all.

As long as he hadn't been certain she was Filore, he'd been able to dismiss her beautiful golden hair and red eyes — the marks of royalty — as mere coincidence. But now that he was certain she was Filore, Heinkel could no longer treat her the way he had before.

If asked whether he changed his manner depending on who he was dealing with, then yes, exactly that. — Heinkel had been raised by his parents to devote his loyalty to those of the Lugunica royal family.

"—!"

Filore, the trump card played too late, let out a faint sound from her throat as she was forced to lift her face.

Only just freed from that black sphere, Filore — whether still hazy of consciousness or simply bewildered by the sudden upheaval in circumstances — offered no resistance to whatever Heinkel did.

It was frustrating in its own way, but the threat was working, and their opponents' movements had grown sluggish. Taking advantage of that, Heinkel — worrying about the vanished Aldebaran and the others — thought first to secure safety together with Roy, who was likewise caught in a trap on this same battlefield—

"—Felt!!"

Unlike the strained cry wrung from the sturdy man earlier, this voice carried real strength.

Joy at being reunited with someone precious, a faint anger that was surely the flip side of affection, worry for her safety, and a fierce determination to bring her home no matter what — all of it tangled together in a call that rang out high and clear across the battlefield.

"—"

At that voice, Filore stirred in Heinkel's arms.

Even without her "Memory," even unable to tell friend from foe, perhaps a powerful emotion directed at her could still get through. Slowly, she blinked those red eyes — themselves a mark of royalty — and met the gaze of the aged Giant who had shouted to her from afar.

Her lips parted, and she spun out words. They were—

"—Old man Rom! Get Gaston and the others back! That thing's coming!!"

It was a cry that had thrown off, in an instant, the ladylike grace of a royal she'd worn just moments before — rough yet full of strength, as if the voice of someone precious to her had wrenched back by force a "Memory" that should have been lost — the bold, valiant roar of the "Golden Lion."

—Of course, the reason Felt was able to cry out her plight to her allies wasn't some convenient, storybook miracle of feeling, the kind Heinkel imagined.

The mercilessness of "Gluttony's" Authority had already been told again and again through the tragedies of not just Felt, but every victim who'd suffered it and everyone around them. There was no way something so foolish as saying that hotbed of countless tragedies could be resolved by mere feelings surrounding Felt.

So the reason Felt was able to return from "Filore" back to "Felt" here was simple and clear — to begin with, Felt had never had her "Memory" eaten by Roy at all.

"So, after this, looks like we're gonna have to eat little miss Felt's 'Memory.' —Hey, Felt-chan, want to work together with us?"

Roy had brought this up right after Aldebaran decided on "handling" Felt, who had become a troublesome liability — having caught onto something she shouldn't have and threatening to crack the group's unity.

The plan, it seemed, was to have Roy devour her "Memory" — the inconvenient fact she'd caught onto and all — and, as a bonus, dispose of that mouthy, always-pushing-back personality of hers in the process.

"As long as we don't take her life" — but if you asked Felt, the moment her "Memory" was gone and she stopped being who she was now, that was as good as being killed. She didn't care for Aldebaran's way of doing things.

"But that don't mean I'm gonna start liking you. First off, what exactly do you want outta working with someone like you?"

"Ha-ha, man, that attitude gives me chills. But no worries, Felt-chan. Careful strategizing and perfect planning were never really our style to begin with. What I want to ask of you, straight up, is to just pretend your 'Memory' got eaten."

"...Keep talking."

Closing one eye, Felt decided there was value in hearing Roy out. At her reaction, Roy laughed, thoroughly delighted, "Now that's what I'm talkin' about!" and continued—

"Playing along with the helmet old-timers isn't so bad as it is, but if we don't do anything, the best we can hope for is getting used up and tossed away right at the crucial moment... I mean, look, we've even had the curse mark carved into us — it's a pretty rough spot for us. So, y'know?"

"So?"

"Felt-chan, while pretending you've lost your 'Memory,' just keep quietly watching for a chance to strike back, same as always. We'll take advantage of whatever opening you might create and try to see if we can slip out of this whole 'captive Sin Archbishop' position."

"—. There's also the thought that if I just tell the helmet bastards all this, I could set you two at each other's throats and get rid of you nice and easy."

"Yeah, yeah, that's the spirit! Go right ahead and pull that on us hard!"

Roy clapped his hands together, delighted, and Felt tried to dig deeper into his true intentions, only to give up on it almost at once. Even if he was hiding his real thoughts, she didn't think she'd be able to see through it, and to her eyes, Roy seemed to be speaking in dead earnest.

And in fact, from Felt's position, it was an offer too good to pass up. — As things stood, Felt's position was one where she'd have her "Memory" stripped away with no way to stop it.

"Though, word is you need my real name to eat me. That's the whole reason your brother wound up puking his guts out, ain't it, on account of not knowin' it."

"Yeah, well, that's true. But somewhere deep down, don't you already know, Felt-chan? That your real name might be the same as that princess famous for the kingdom's tragedy."

"—"

"Ahh, or maybe you want to find out for sure? If the 'Blight' works with that name, we'll end up taking your 'Memory' after all, but hey, at least it'd prove it. What do you say?"

"Hah, what a cheap provocation. —Fine, I'll bite."

Felt snorted at Roy, who let his long tongue loll out as he peered into her face.

Just who she really was. — Such a pointless question was nothing but a matter of indifference to Felt. She knew perfectly well that no small number of people would think it mattered a great deal — but it was the same logic as before: losing her "Memory" was as good as being killed.

Felt had already built up a life as the person called "Felt." Sorry, but whatever her starting point had been, any life that wasn't "Felt's" had ended before it ever began.

She didn't need that other life. So she'd go along even with that irritating scheme of "Gluttony's."

"The only one who's gonna win is me."

"Kuku, ah-hahaha! Just knew you'd say that! I believed in you! But, y'know, we're gonna go ahead and take out maximum insurance too, so you can't just walk off with the win all on your own."

"Insurance? Don't be so lame. Bet it all on one shot."

"That kind of bravado's cool and all, but for us, pride and all that ain't what matters. —Eat, or be eaten. That's all there is to it."

So saying, Roy clacked his teeth together as if to celebrate the sealing of their secret pact.

And after that, true to their agreement, Roy pretended to devour her "Memory" and left Felt just as she was. Felt, for her part, kept her half-doubting pact with Roy, playing the part of someone with no "Memory" — impersonating a royal she knew nothing about, Filore Lugunica.

"Sorry, but the only person I could think of to base an impression on was sis Crusch."

As a victim who'd had her "Memory" devoured by "Gluttony," Crusch was the first person to come to Felt's mind. Using as reference the drastic change in Crusch's manner and way of speaking, the Filore she put on apparently went over well with Aldebaran's group, Heinkel especially.

Fortunately, the chances to play Filore never grew frequent or long, since Felt was soon sealed away by Aldebaran's Magic inside that strange black sphere. Being inside that sphere had felt extremely suffocating, nothing pleasant about it — but there was one saving grace.

With less time spent playing Filore, she was spared from slipping up. If Felt let something slip unexpectedly, Roy's "insurance" would kick in. That was the threat she'd been given.

A literal "insurance policy," designed to force Felt into going along even if she hadn't wanted to accept the proposal in the first place, and to prevent betrayal even after she had.

It was—

"—It's coming out of my shadow!!"

She shouted this while held in a chokehold by Heinkel, sword pressed to her throat.

The hidden card that Roy Alphard, Sin Archbishop of "Gluttony," had prepared to deceive Aldebaran's group and slip free of their grasp — the last of the three great Witchbeasts, ranking alongside the "White Whale" and the "Great Rabbit"—

"—the Black Serpent!!"

—The "Black Serpent," the plague-nest Witchbeast that had killed more living things than any other in this world since its birth, had reacted to the violation of the prescribed secret pact and was summoned onto the battlefield where both sides now fought with everything they had.
