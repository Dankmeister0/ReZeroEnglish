Arc 1 Chapter 10: "The Price of a Slip of the Tongue"

I'm finally starting to catch up on my backlog of chapters. I'm aiming to update daily until Arc 1 wraps up—though who knows if I can actually pull that off.

"I see, I think I understand the situation now."

She tilted her glass, then licked away the white trace left on her thin lips with her tongue.

Every gesture the woman who'd called herself Elsa made carried a sultry undertone, and Subaru found himself flustered again and again by the sidelong glances she kept throwing his way while Felt explained the situation.

"Well, that's the gist of it. So now it's a bidding war. Doesn't matter to me which one of you walks off with the badge—I'll just sell it to whoever pays more."

"What a personality. I don't dislike it. —So, how much did this young man here offer?"

Ten sacred gold coins—that was the amount the other side had offered.

Naturally, since he was competing against that figure, they'd assume he'd offered something higher still.

Deciding that flinching now would only work against him, Subaru activated his phone's camera function for the third time. The flash tore through the storehouse's dimness, capturing Elsa's figure on the screen.

Elsa's brow furrowed faintly at Subaru's action, but she showed no further reaction than that.

He thrust the phone's screen at her.

"What I'm offering is this Magic Vessel. Probably the only one of its kind in the whole world. According to Mr. Muscles over there, it's guaranteed to be worth no less than twenty sacred gold coins."

"A Magic Vessel..."

Gazing at her own image reflected on the screen, Elsa gave a nod of understanding at the phone held before her.

It seemed to get across that Subaru's offer was a trade in kind, and that it wasn't a bluff. She drew a small leather pouch from within her clothes—presumably holding the sacred gold coins meant as payment.

She set it down on the table. Metal scraped against metal, a weighty sound reaching them through the pouch.

Felt's pupils narrowed like a cat's, and Rom-jii scolded her for it. Beside them, Subaru waited for what the woman would do next, while Elsa laced her white fingers together atop the table.

"Actually, I've also been given a certain amount of extra money by my client. In case you proved reluctant to sell, so I could sweeten the offer a little."

"Client... so you mean you're the same as Felt, Elsa-san? Just someone asked to go collect the badge?"

"That's right. It's my client who wants it. ...Are you, by chance, in the same line of work?"

Elsa apparently found it odd that Subaru wanted the badge in exchange for a Magic Vessel.

Even asked whether they were "in the same line of work," Subaru had no idea what Elsa's profession even was to begin with.

Since she'd been asked to come collect something, maybe she was some kind of delivery service. Either way—

"If you're in the same line of work as me, that makes you unemployed too!"

"So, this unemployed guy went and named a jaw-dropping price. How much can your owner cough up, huh?"

At Felt's provocative tone, Elsa silently opened the mouth of the pouch.

Turned upside down, the leather pouch spilled out sacred gold coins gleaming with a dazzling silver-white light.

The clinking of overlapping metal made Felt's eyes light up, and even Rom-jii gave a faint swallow. Subaru, meanwhile, was less focused on the shine than on counting the number of coins.

If he wasn't mistaken, the count was—

"Twenty coins. Exactly."

"That's the full amount of sacred gold coins my employer gave me. It seems they'd sized you up as someone who could be bought off with just that... but it looks like that might fall a little short."

The question was directed past Felt, toward Rom-jii standing behind her.

Rom-jii counted the sacred gold coins, then looked down at Subaru's anxious face and laughed.

"Don't make a face like some frightened child. A man oughtn't look so pathetic. ...Aye, twenty sacred gold coins is an outrageous sum, sure enough. But I said the Vessel was worth no less than twenty—didn't I?"

His huge, gnarled palm roughly ruffled Subaru's short black hair.

Shaken about while still nursing the last of his drunkenness, Subaru's mood was plunging toward the absolute worst—

"By my reckoning, this negotiation tips in Subaru's favor. Sorry to you and your employer both, but you'd best put these coins back in the bag and head on home."

At Rom-jii's words, as his rough hand pushed the sacred gold coins back, Subaru's mood flipped in an instant from rock bottom to the absolute peak.

Felt threw her hands up in a cheer of no objection, and Elsa, too, shrugged without looking especially disappointed—so much so that Subaru's reaction, leaping to his feet and pumping his fist, felt just a touch out of place.

"What?! It's fine, isn't it?! I was happy about it! In a way, this is my first accomplished goal since coming here! I'm allowed a little fist-pump, aren't I?!"

"Nobody said anything, you're just way too pumped, mister. As long as I profit, I'm fine either way."

"My employer shouldn't mind terribly even without it in hand. There's no need for me to press the matter that far."

While Felt teased the blushing Subaru, Elsa, rising from her seat, remained entirely composed.

He hadn't expected some parting shot of sore-loser bitterness from her, but the fact that she didn't seem the least bit bothered despite failing to fulfill her task nagged at him.

"Ah, sorry about that, Elsa-san. I bet you're gonna catch some grief for this."

"It can't be helped. If the fault lay with me, that would be one thing, but in this case, it's my employer's fault for trying to get away with spending less."

"Handing you twenty sacred gold coins and calling that 'less'—that's a bit hard on the poor girl, eh."

"Well, guess my luck's just been on an absolute tear lately! Could this be my time to shine?"

In stark contrast to the men's sympathy for Elsa, Felt's attitude was utterly oblivious to the mood.

In any case, it seemed safe to say the negotiation that had been troubling Subaru had now been settled.

Which was, in turn, one hopeful step toward being able to repay his debt to Satella, the benefactor who had first saved him in this world.

By rights, he probably ought to tell Satella about Felt, who'd stolen the badge from her, and Elsa, who'd been hired to retrieve it—but the only real damages had been Satella's peace of mind and Subaru's phone.

He'd barely spoken with them, but Subaru lacked the iron resolve it would take to see these two thrown in the clink.

If ever there were a picture of fence-sitting opportunism, this was it.

"Well then, the negotiation ended in disappointment, but I'll be taking my leave now."

They all saw Elsa off as she stood to go.

Draining the last of her milk, licking it away once more with that same lascivious motion of her tongue, Elsa suddenly turned her gaze on Subaru as though she'd just remembered something.

Those black eyes coiled around the inside of Subaru's chest as if to squeeze it tight.

"—Come to think of it, once you get your hands on that badge, what do you intend to do with it?"

It was a question pitched somehow low, its emotion frozen solid.

Its tone threatened Subaru's eardrums with a sweetness, reaching him as though forbidding any false answer.

"...Oh, I'm giving it back to its original owner."

The moment it left his mouth, Subaru realized his obvious slip of the tongue.

In front of the girl who'd stolen the thing and the person who'd commissioned that theft, he had just declared he'd return the stolen item to the very person it had been stolen from.

It was a declaration equivalent to a declaration of hostility—

"—Oh. So you're involved with her, then."

—more than enough to set Elsa's cold killing intent into motion.

"Ngh—!?"

A sudden impact from the side.

The force struck him around the waist, sending his body sliding sideways, and Subaru tumbled gracelessly across the ground without even managing to break his fall.

Amid the pain and the impact, his vision spinning, he instinctively slapped the ground and lifted his head—to find Felt clinging to his waist as if she'd tackled him.

"What are you—"

"Are you an idiot?! Dodge, you trying to get yourself killed?!"

The curse he'd meant to finish—"doing"—was drowned out by a shout even louder than his own.

From his low vantage point, wearing an expression of shock, Subaru could see Elsa standing there, facing them.

"Oh my, you dodged."

Elsa tilted her head, looking genuinely puzzled.

In her hand was gripped a weapon strikingly out of place, gleaming with a dull sheen.

—A kukri knife, if Subaru's knowledge served him right, was what that weapon would be called.

A blade nearly thirty centimeters long, its length bent into a dogleg shape—a type of blade commonly called "forward-curved." He'd once heard it described as a weapon that used the weight concentrated at its tip to cleave through prey like an axe.

Brandishing that blade, Elsa wore the same smile she'd worn all along, unchanged.

Judging by her stance, she must have already swung that blade through its full arc once. Which meant it had been Felt—leaping in to shield him—who'd saved Subaru from standing in its path.

The fact that his life had hung on a clash that lasted a single instant, entirely outside his awareness, sent a belated wave of terror racing through Subaru's brain.

Alarm bells rang in his skull, his heart hammering blood through him like a war drum. Listening to a pulse so loud it felt like his entire body had become one giant heart, Subaru couldn't stop the trembling in the arms holding him up.

Heedless of Subaru's unsightly state, events continued to unfold regardless.

"OOOOOOOOOOOH—!!"

It was Rom-jii who raised a battle roar and lunged at Elsa, blade still bared.

He swung up the club he'd never once let go of, even through the whole negotiation, aiming to crack Elsa's skull open with that spiked weapon.

The speed of the club as it came down was nothing short of abnormal.

Swinging a blunt weapon that surely weighed no less than ten kilograms as though it were a twig was, quite literally, a feat made possible only by the raw strength of the Giant race.

The club struck the storehouse floor dead-on, and the shock gave the illusion that the whole building was shaking.

Splintered floorboards and the scattered stolen goods went flying in the aftershock, and right before the eyes of Subaru, who'd slumped to the ground, a battle beyond the bounds of common sense began.

"This is my first time fighting a Giant to the death."

"Big talk, little girl. —I'll grind you into mincemeat and feed you to the rats!"

The horizontal swing of the club carried exactly the force Rom-jii had promised.

With that much speed and power behind such a weapon, any clumsy defense would offer less protection than a paper shield. In a room with so little room to maneuver, having that club swung to cut off every escape route was, by itself, a lethal threat.

And yet, Elsa's own skill in facing it down existed in a realm equally abnormal.

Letting the kukri knife dangle loosely in one hand, her black silhouette slid and wove through that lethal storm.

Facing a weapon that would beat her to death without fail if it so much as clipped her, she dodged by margins that were razor-thin in the truest sense, toying with Rom-jii all the while.

This is bad, Subaru thought instinctively.

Something inside him was ringing a decisive alarm bell: at this rate, it was going to end badly.

"This is bad..."

"It's fine. There's no way Rom-jii's gonna lose! For as long as I can remember, I've never once seen Rom-jii lose a fight!"

To Subaru, who could do nothing but voice his fear through trembling lips, Felt shouted her trust with a force that seemed as much aimed at bolstering her own courage.

Her words carried a trust built up over years, one that could not be overturned.

Their relationship—trading barbed jokes while still respecting one another—must have been forged over no small stretch of time.

But unlike her, shouting out her trust, Subaru felt only pessimism.

This wasn't a "fight" of the kind she'd always seen before. This was a "kill-or-be-killed."

"—Take this!"

Before Subaru's unease could fully take shape, the battle shifted.

Rom-jii let out a battle cry and kicked the table up. The wooden table that had, until moments ago, served as the stage for their negotiation shattered to splinters, scattering wood chips that engulfed the entire space in front of Elsa.

A curtain of broken wood.

Aiming at whatever lay beyond it, Rom-jii unleashed his club with the full force of his body.

The blow, driven down from overhead without a shred of restraint, carried enough force on its own to crush even a passenger car flat. But—

"—Rom-jii!"

An anguished scream tore from Felt's throat.

And Subaru saw exactly what had provoked that scream.

Spinning end over end through the air was Rom-jii's right arm, the club still clenched in its grip.

That thick, powerful arm, severed clean from the shoulder, danced through the air scattering blood before it slammed into the wall.

Both Subaru and Felt were drenched from head to toe in the rain of blood flung across the room. But there was no time to spare a thought for the blood.

"At the very least, let me take her down with me—!"

His right arm severed at the shoulder, blood pouring from him like water from a hose, Rom-jii—

—hurled his massive body forward, not even pressing a hand to his wound, aiming his one remaining arm at Elsa.

The shattered remains of the table fell to the floor, and beyond it, Elsa stood in the follow-through of her completed swing.

Rom-jii's massive frame would crush her slender body before the kukri's blade could reverse its course.

But that fleeting, life-or-death charge of Rom-jii's—

"I forgot to mention it earlier—thank you for the milk, it was delicious."

—was stopped short by a single flash of the broken glass held in Elsa's other hand.
