Arc 4 Chapter 9: "The Graveyard"

"House arrest, huh... that's a rather unsettling word to bring up..."

Facing Roswaal as he lay in bed, Subaru mulled over the words he'd been told, forcing himself to speak. Given the flow of the conversation, under normal circumstances he might have laughed it off as a bad joke, but the current situation carried an unpleasant ring of truth. After all—

"Then don't tell me—Roswaal, did the villagers do that to you?"

Emilia, who seemed to have arrived at the same unsettling conclusion as Subaru, spoke up.

Roswaal, bandaged all over his body, faint bloodstains seeping through in places—his pitiful state was clear proof of the truth behind his words. That was precisely why they couldn't laugh it off.

"If there's someone in this village who can do this much damage to Roswaal, that's a pretty unfunny situation..."

Hand on his chin, picturing the balance of power between the two sides, Subaru felt anxiety kindle in his chest.

To Subaru, Roswaal L. Mathers was, beyond his role as Subaru's guarantor in this other world, someone who left an even stronger impression as a mage of exceptional, rarefied skill.

In fact, as the Kingdom of Lugunica's Head Court Mage, his ability was overwhelming enough that he could wipe out a horde of Juggernauts—foes a hundred Subarus couldn't have beaten—while humming a tune. For someone like that to end up in this state...

"Ah, seems you're misunderstandi-i-ing something, but these wounds of mine weren't inflicted by anyone, you know. No need to get all wary or plot revenge on my behalf—everything's fi-i-ine, okay?"

"Relax. You haven't racked up enough goodwill points for me to risk my life on some reckless crusade for your sake... wait, what's that supposed to mean, anyway? That doesn't match what you said before. You said you were under house arrest..."

"Well, seeing as my injured self is being held here like this, calling it house arrest isn't wro-o-ong, is it. It's not that I was injured for the purpose of being confined—rather, having been injured, I'm now being kept confined. ...Though if I go into detail, even that's not quite ri-i-ight either."

Roswaal's roundabout way of talking sent question marks flying over Subaru's head. Breaking it down, trying to calm himself and piece the context together, it amounted to this:

"So the people of the Sanctuary have nothing to do with your injuries—is that what you're saying?"

"Strictly speaking, it's not that they have nothi-i-ing to do with it, but if you ask whether they're the direct cause of my injuries, the answer is no. That's the gi-i-ist of it."

"So indirectly, they are involved, then."

Roswaal, who'd been tilting his head, blinked as though taken aback by Subaru's point. Then, letting out a small breath, he teased, "It's like watching a child grow up, isn't it."

Judging by his reaction that he'd touched on a thread leading to the heart of the matter, Subaru, determined not to loosen his grip on the pursuit, chose his words carefully to press Roswaal further. But—

"—Barusu, how about you show a little concern for Roswaal-sama?"

The one who cut into the conversation with those words was Ram, who hadn't taken part in the scene until now. The girl, the hem of her apron dress swaying, crossed the room with graceful steps and set out steaming cups of tea from the tray in her hands onto the table.

A fragrant aroma filled the room, and having his sense of smell stirred made Subaru realize just how narrow his field of view had become. Next, he was made to realize the sheer, at-a-glance severity of Roswaal's condition—the very man he'd been about to interrogate.

"Are you satisfied, cornering Roswaal-sama when he's this badly hurt and grilling him for every last detail? Roswaal-sama, in pain and suffering, looking like he might cry—it's pitiful to see."

"Don't say it in a way that kills all the guilt I was starting to feel. Besides, is this guy really the type to cry from pain and suffering? Doesn't suit him at all."

"Ohh, it hurts so-o-o much, it's so-o-o painful. Words so lacking in compassion and consideration sting right into my wounds, they do-o-o."

Subaru answered Ram's complaint with a curse. As if mocking Subaru's own line, Roswaal began a little act of his own from his bed. Just as irritation made Subaru's brow start to twitch, Emilia cleared her throat, restoring some order to the disordered atmosphere—both at the same moment.

Drawing the gazes of the other three, she prefaced with, "Anyway," and continued:

"I can tell just by looking that Roswaal isn't doing well, so let's finish this talk all the sooner. Hasn't anyone cast healing magic on him?"

"Healing magic is outside Ram's area of expertise..."

At Ram's answer—expressionless, yet somehow frustrated—Emilia turned a not-very-hopeful look on Roswaal. In response, Roswaal raised a hand and waved it slowly,

"Same here—I specialize purely in destruction, you see-e-e. Breaking, invading, deceiving, that sort of field, I can handle it all well enough. But when it comes to healing, I'm utterly hopeless."

"That's a rough deal. You should polish up your defensive skills as much as your offensive ones."

That said, if it were a game where Subaru could set his own growth path, he'd be the type to dump everything into offensive skills too—an all-out-attack build. He wasn't really in a position to criticize Roswaal.

While Subaru arrived at this odd sort of understanding, Emilia sighed, "Honestly, there's no helping it," and said,

"I'm not at full strength without Puck, but I'll cast the healing magic myself. I need to concentrate, though, so it'll have to wait until after we've heard you out."

"The Great Spirit, you say?"

Roswaal's brow twitched upward at what Emilia had let slip, and then his eyes narrowed. The expression carried a certain coldness, unusual for a man whose face was normally so slack and easygoing, and Subaru couldn't help but shrug and say, "Hey, hey,"

"A serious face—now that's rare. Is Puck being gone really that shocking? Didn't know you were secretly into fluffy things..."

"Unfortunately, the closest I've ever gotten to touching the Great Spirit-sama was the time I held out mayonnaise for him and said 'say ahh.' He's frightening, after all. ...But, I see-e-e."

Rather than letting Subaru's quip slide past him, Roswaal knit his brow thoughtfully. Then he glanced at Emilia with his yellow eyes and said,

"Emilia-sama, is there anything off about your condition, or anything different from usual-u-u?"

"...? Not particularly, other than Puck not showing his face. And even that's been going on since a little before we came to the Sanctuary... oh, but there is one thing."

Raising a finger at the question, Emilia then swept her gaze around—not the room, but in a gesture more like taking in the whole of the Sanctuary outside—before lowering her voice.

"This Sanctuary... no, maybe ever since we entered the forest. I feel like the spirits' responses have been dull. And earlier, when we were outside, I felt... a strange gaze on me."

"A strange gaze?"

At the unexpected remark, Subaru tilted his head, and Emilia gave a nod of confirmation. She explained that this was the reason her expression had clouded earlier, back where they'd parted ways with Garfiel and the others, and then added,

"Like I was being stared at, unblinking. It felt really-y-y unpleasant... I thought it might just be my imagination, so I couldn't bring myself to tell you, Subaru."

"Neither of those feelings of yours is wro-o-ong, Emilia-sama. This place is uncomfortable for spirits, and what's more, the residents here are, without exception, harboring feelings toward you that would make you uncomfortable-e-e."

Over Emilia's considerate words, Roswaal laid his own careless ones. Seeing her eyes waver painfully at the content of it, Subaru opened his mouth, about to snap at Roswaal on reflex, but—

"—Now, now, leave it there. Ain't right to push a wounded man too far. 'A sprinting piebald beak runs hot,' as they say, wouldn't you say."

"Unfortunately, I'm not sharp enough to agree with whatever that means. Quick question, though—isn't it just between the two of us that the language translation isn't working properly?"

Turning around, Subaru shrugged at Garfiel, who leaned his back against the open door, baring his fangs. Seeing the gesture, Garfiel clicked his teeth and swept his gaze around the room,

"Even the old hag's house, which is supposed to be the biggest and best around, feels cramped as hell with this many people crammed in, huh. Guess it was the right call leaving that noisy brother behind after all."

"Come to think of it, I don't see Otto around... did he go home? Did you eat him?"

At Subaru's question, Emilia looked startled, but Garfiel burst out laughing as though he'd just heard the best joke of his life, slapping his knee.

"Sure, I've got carnivore blood in me, but I ain't gonna eat the guy. Especially not that one—he seems like the type who'd keep yapping even a second before getting eaten. Something about worrying over the dragon carriage and the Earth Dragon or whatever... well, he made up some excuse or another and holed himself up somewhere."

Waving a hand, Garfiel stomped his way across the room, dropped heavily into a chair by the wall, and looked up at Ram, who was watching him out of the corner of her eye.

"Tea."

"Would you mind waiting while Ram goes outside to gather some fallen leaves?"

"I've got a bad feeling I already know, but what exactly do you plan on doing with those fallen leaves once you've gathered them?"

"Ram has no intention of wasting precious tea leaves on someone who won't even bother to appreciate their aroma or flavor. That's Ram's answer."

Declaring this sharply, Ram then, in all seriousness, walked right out of the building. Pointing at her retreating back, Subaru gave Garfiel a look that wordlessly conveyed, "What do you even see in her?" Catching this, Garfiel followed her departing back with his eyes and said,

"A woman with some fight in her is way more worth the challenge, ain't she. And as a male, it ain't strange at all to be drawn to a strong, excellent female."

"Quit going on about 'males' and 'females' like you're sexing chicks or something, cheeping away like that. She's a proper maiden underneath it all, you know, Ram is. And here you're—"

"Ah? What're you talking about. I treat her like a woman better than anybody could. More than that, before all that, we—"

When Subaru voiced his disapproval at such an extreme way of putting it, Garfiel, frowning, suddenly raised his eyebrows as though he'd noticed something. Then his expression quickly soured, and he leveled a sharp, hostile glare at Roswaal lying in the bed.

"You still haven't told 'em, have you. If it was just you gettin' beat to a pulp, I'd have laughed it off as a good story. But if that half-elf there—if Emilia-sama's come here, that's a whole different matter, ain't it."

"—Huh?"

Garfiel spat the words out with irritation on his tongue. Mid-declaration, Emilia was startled to hear her own name come up. But paying her surprise no mind, Garfiel, his expression still seeping with anger, bit right back at Roswaal.

"The moment Emilia-sama set foot in the Sanctuary, this whole mess became something that drags us into it too. And what's this, huh? You haven't even gotten to the important part of the talk yet. Did you all come here just to play around?"

The latter half of his fury was directed not only at Roswaal but at Subaru and the others, who'd fallen silent. The rage held in his gaze as he looked at Emilia in particular was no ordinary thing, and Subaru stepped forward to shield her as she shrank her shoulders slightly, saying,

"Wait a second. I get that you're angry, but we've got no idea what set you off in the first place. Talking to someone who doesn't understand is just gonna keep pissing you off, right?"

"That's exactly what I'm sayin' pisses me off. The people actually involved being like that—"

"The ones treating the people actually involved like they don't matter and just barreling ahead with things are you and Roswaal over there. If you actually want us to get involved, worry about it, and do something about this problem, then fulfill your responsibility to explain it. Expecting us to understand without saying a word is about as shameless as I used to be not long ago."

Facing him, Subaru felt the pressure radiating from Garfiel intensify a notch after those words. Garfiel was shorter than Subaru, and with him sitting down now, the height difference was considerable. And yet, none of that smallness could be felt at all. No—considering the sheer quality of the intimidation he gave off, Garfiel felt to Subaru like nothing short of a boulder.

Given Subaru's inherently timid nature, it would've been only natural for him to avert his eyes and take a step back.

But—

"...Subaru."

Subaru felt slender fingers grip tightly at the hem of his clothes. The uncertain voice calling his name slipped into his ears, giving strength to knees that were on the verge of buckling.

Behind him stood Emilia. Anxious, she was relying on him.

There was no way he could bend his knee in front of her—no way he'd let himself do something so uncool.

"—Tch."

As they silently traded glares, it was Garfiel who looked away first. Clicking his tongue, he let his weight sink back into the chair and roughly raked his fingers through his short blond hair.

"Aaugh, dammit! I get it, alright! That was just me takin' it out on you! I lost my temper, that's all—I said I was sorry, didn't I, hey!"

"No, you didn't actually say that. And more to the point, has anyone ever told you you've got an exhausting personality?"

For someone who got emotional and lost perspective so easily, he was quick to regain his objectivity and admit fault. It struck Subaru as a terribly troublesome personality, and he found himself giving a wry smile before he could even get properly angry.

In response, Garfiel let out an ill-fitting sigh, "Haah,"

"Shut it. I'm gonna keep quiet for a bit, so get on with the talk while I do. Things get complicated and stop movin' forward when I'm mixed up in it."

"That's honestly impressive, being that self-aware and still not changing your ways."

"Flattery's wasted on me, my comprehension's too low for it. Anyway."

Garfiel snorted at Subaru, whose exasperation had given way to genuine admiration. Just then, Ram, having returned from outside, held out a steaming cup of tea to him.

"Nothing but common tea, this."

"Isn't that usually said with a bit more humility when you're serving someone?"

Ram deflected with a composed, "Was it, now," and Garfiel, despite the tea surely being hot, poured it straight down his throat in one go. Carnivorous as he was, it seemed his tongue wasn't sensitive to heat. Watching him empty the cup in a single breath like that, Ram let out a deep sigh,

"A man with absolutely no appreciation for the effort of brewing tea, as always. He doesn't suit Ram at all."

"It just tastes like leaves, don't it. If it wets my throat, water woulda done just as well. Right?"

"I'll agree that tea just tastes like leaves, but I've gotta draw the line at nodding along with an extreme take like that. Ram, why don't you let him have the other cup of tea too?"

At Subaru's remark, Ram held out the cup she'd been holding in her other hand to Garfiel. Even from a distance, Subaru could faintly tell what the liquid was, its rippling color a shade of "dead leaf brown"—but he didn't stop her. If anything, he encouraged it.

"Huh, that's thoughtful of you. Glad you get that one cup ain't enough— gehk! Aah!? Oi, this is just plain leaf juice—!"

"If it wets your throat, water, tea, or leaf juice are all the same, aren't they? Now that you've put it to your lips, drink it to the last drop. Leave any behind, and Ram will twist it right off."

Without saying what exactly, Ram glared pointedly at the area around Garfiel's crotch. That alone made her target abundantly clear, and Subaru felt an involuntary chill shoot through him, drawing his legs in reflexively. Garfiel reluctantly drained the cup, grimacing at the bitterness. Beside them, Roswaal, who had been watching the exchange all this while, suddenly burst out laughing,

"Ahaha-a-a. You two, do you actually intend to worry about my condition and let me rest? Or is your real aim to make me laugh and split my wound open? If so, I'd say that plan's succeeding rather well so far-a-ar."

As he spoke, Roswaal lightly pressed a hand to the bandages around his chest with a pained smile. Indeed, a faint red was visibly beginning to spread across the white cloth there.

In an instant, the relaxed mood shattered completely. Her expression changing, Ram hurried to Roswaal's side and laid her palm gently over his hand where it pressed against the wound.

"I'm terribly sorry, Roswaal-sama. Even with Ram right here at your side..."

"I mean, wasn't it your little prank that pushed him over the edge, though?"

The wisecrack was silenced by a sharp look from Ram that pierced right through him, and Subaru zipped his mouth shut and watched over Roswaal's condition. In any case, though there'd been bleeding, it didn't seem serious. This was probably right around when the bleeding from his fresh wound had finally stopped—in other words, exactly the point at which he most needed to be resting quietly.

"Roswaal, I really think we should treat—"

"No-o-o need for that, Emilia-sama."

Having arrived at the same conclusion as Subaru, Emilia summoned spirits floating around her and made to step toward Roswaal. But she was stopped by Roswaal himself shaking his head. The pale, phosphorescent spirits swayed as though caught up in their mistress's confusion.

Watching Emilia's pale profile, Subaru noticed something.

—That Garfiel was watching Emilia as she summoned her spirits with eyes so frozen of emotion it was unsettling, impossible to read.

"There's something more important right now than a trivial matter like my injury, you see-e-e. This isn't life-threatening, so please, prioritize that other matter instead."

"I can't just do that even if you tell me to. There's someone hurt right in front of me, and to just leave that aside for something else..."

"Even if I told you it's something necessary for you to take the throne?"

Emilia's characteristic outburst had just gone off, and the scene—about to see treatment begin by force if necessary—froze. At the words directed at her, Emilia's cheeks stiffened, her violet eyes going wide. Meeting them head-on, Roswaal's yellow eyes were sharp, gleaming with an eerie light as though peering into the very depths of her.

"To House Mathers, this Sanctuary is nothing more than land passed down through the generations. But for your future, Emilia-sama, it holds great... yes, a gre-e-eat significance. That's why I always intended to invite you here eventually. —Though your visit has come rather earlier than I'd planned, it seems."

"Necessary for me...? Hey, what exactly do you mean by that..."

"The problem this Sanctuary carries is, in essence, closely related to the problem you carry, Emilia-sama. Which is why it's here, in this place, that you might find it—your foothold."

"—!?"

Subaru watched Emilia's expression change, frozen in place. Roswaal, who had prompted that change, seemed to be taking her moment of anguish as exactly what he'd intended, watching her face carefully. Subaru, meanwhile, couldn't read the emotional exchange passing between the two of them, left holding onto a frustrating sense of helplessness.

But before that frustration could become words, Roswaal pointed at the still-silent Garfiel.

"You've been named, Garfiel. Show these two around the Sanctuary—no, guide them to the Graveyard."

"—Heh. You sure about that?"

Hooking a finger through the handle of his emptied cup and rocking the porcelain, Garfiel laughed low in his throat. At the question, Roswaal gave a nod, lightly stroking the pink hair of Ram, who was moving to change the bandages over his wound,

"First and foremost, it's essential that they understand the situation, you see-e-e. Explaining the rest can wait until after the sun sets, but the Graveyard won't wait like that."

"Ah, right, the sun's about to go down, huh. Once that happens, it won't be so simple anymore. Fine, I'll take on guiding 'em there."

Standing up, Garfiel set the cup down on the chair he'd been sitting in and turned back toward Subaru and the others. Looking at the two of them—left behind despite being the ones actually involved—he tilted his head, bared his canines, and said,

"Don't be standin' there makin' dumb faces. Unless you wanna end up lookin' like an idiot sayin' somethin' like 'even a rampaging Hoikoro feels like yesterday's news,' let's get a move on already."

"Wait, wait, wait a second. I can't keep up. To begin with, we haven't settled anything at all with Roswaal yet. At least let us finish that first—"

"His wound has reopened. Changing the bandages and letting him rest quietly takes priority. Barusu, you and the others go on ahead to the Graveyard, just as Roswaal-sama instructed."

He tried to argue back against Garfiel's high-handedness, but that was cut off by Ram's resolute voice. As Subaru turned around, she leveled her usual cold gaze at him, resting a hand on the bedsheet,

"Calm down. We'll talk once it's night. Roswaal-sama isn't going to run off anywhere. The Graveyard, however, will run off if you don't get there before sundown."

"Never heard of a graveyard that active before."

Scratching his head as he answered, Subaru felt Emilia's gaze fixed on his profile. Her eyes wavered with a listless emotion, and it seemed she was leaving the decision of what to do up to him.

Stay here and settle things with Roswaal. Or go along with their wishes and let Garfiel guide them to the Graveyard. —The answer was already decided.

"Fine. We'll go to this Graveyard place. That's what's needed, right? But once we're back, you're telling us everything properly, no excuses."

"So-o-orry about how things turned out. Once it's night, let's have a proper, thorough talk about all sorts of things, shall we-e-e."

At Subaru's decision, Emilia's shoulders relaxed, and Roswaal, hearing it, nodded in satisfaction. Garfiel and Ram, too, seemed satisfied, and the two of them began moving to attend to their own tasks. But before that, Subaru held up a finger and said, "Just one thing,"

"There's something I want to ask before we go to the Graveyard."

"Hmm, sure, go ahead? If it's something easy to answer, ask away, whatever it i-i-is."

"Alright, then, I'll take you up on that. —Does the name 'Rem' ring any bells for you?"

Making it look as though he'd already asked his one question, Subaru slipped in the real one. The first to react to Subaru's question was Ram. Though it wasn't the reaction Subaru had been hoping for.

The instant that name reached her ears, Ram tilted her head as if hearing an unfamiliar word for the first time. As Subaru's spirits sank at this, Roswaal murmured the name quietly under his breath, turning it over,

"...Well? Do you know it?"

"Hmm. Sorry, but I'm afraid it doesn't ring a bell at all. The feel of it is a bit like Ram's name, but you weren't just misspeaking, were you-u-u."

"I... see. No, it's fine. If you don't remember, there's nothing to be done. Nothing... to be done."

Head bowed, Subaru shook his head and accepted the answer.

Ram's answer, and then Roswaal's, easily shattered whatever faint hope Subaru had held. These were the two who had likely known Rem the longest. Even the two whom Rem had adored enough to devote her very life to had forgotten her existence entirely.

That fact settled itself neatly into the hollow space inside Subaru. And it made him realize, with even greater clarity:

—that in this world, he really was the only one who could still remember her.

"Subaru, are you okay?"

In a voice full of concern, Emilia gently touched Subaru's sleeve. Savoring the tender feel of her fingertips, Subaru closed his eyes once so she wouldn't see the gloom on his face, then forced his cheeks upward,

"I'm fine, really. It's not like I was getting my hopes up weirdly or anything, it's just, well, this kind of thing happens. —If anything, it just made my resolve harder: that I've got to do something about this, one way or another."

"Yeah. Let's do something about it. I'll help too."

Emilia nodded at Subaru's resolve, promising she wouldn't hold back her cooperation. Soothed in his frayed heart by her thoughtfulness, Subaru shrugged and said,

"So, if Rem comes back, that means the love I've been pouring wholeheartedly into you, my dear Emilia-tan, is gonna have to split its destination. You gonna get jealous or something?"

"Maybe, if the amount of feeling you have for me actually shrinks because of it. But that's not how it is, right? You said you've got my share and Rem-san's share both properly set aside."

To Subaru's flirtatious quip, Emilia struck back with an unexpected counterattack. As Subaru flinched at her words, unable to find a reply, she smiled triumphantly, cheeks faintly flushed,

"Let's go, Subaru. I want Ram and Rem-san to meet again soon too, after all."

"Ah, yeah, right. Yeah, you're right."

Even if she didn't remember, it would still be a reunion between twin sisters. Something in that meeting might call out to buried memory, might bring a tremor to an affection that had been lost.

If there was any meaning to be found in clinging to even so slender a hope—

"Barusu."

As Emilia left the room, following after Garfiel who had gone ahead, and Subaru made to follow after her, a voice from behind called him to a stop.

Looking back, he found Ram, who had come to stand right beside him. Holding a fresh bandage for Roswaal in her hands, she'd approached Subaru's side, and said,

"What's up? If it's bandage play you're after, you can have your fill with Roswaal after I'm gone..."

"Only Emilia-sama is to enter the Graveyard. Barusu, you are absolutely not to go in."

Cut off mid-quip, even his momentum was crushed by the sharp edge in Ram's voice.

Ram lowered her voice, telling him this in tones too quiet even for Roswaal to hear. To Subaru, who knit his brow, she repeated once more, as if to drive the point home,

"—If you don't want to be seized by the Witch's obsession, you are absolutely not to enter the Graveyard."

So she repeated.

—The air inside the Graveyard was cold and clear, greeting Subaru with a refreshing atmosphere quite unlike what the name might suggest.

With every step he took, the sound of his shoes echoed, forcing him, whether he liked it or not, to assert his own presence in this place. And yet that very sound of footsteps also brought a strange peace to his heart.

—In a darkness where even a few meters ahead couldn't be trusted, in a miasma that made even his own existence feel uncertain, even that much was a kind of salvation.

He didn't even know where he stood; even if he wanted to lean against something, he'd long since lost track of any wall. No matter how far he walked, he never reached the end of the path, and Subaru even felt the illusion that he'd stopped moving entirely.

But the sound of his shoes alone denied that illusion. Only the sound echoing clearly and steadily guaranteed Subaru's existence, the certainty of his own footsteps, and he kept moving forward, relying on nothing else.

He had no way of even guessing how much time had passed in the darkness. Even his thoughts had grown hazy, and his throat, having given up calling out, had frozen solid. Despite walking this much, no fatigue came to him—if anything, even the sensation in his limbs had grown faint and indistinct.

And still he kept walking. He had to keep walking. He must not stop.

Stopping was not allowed. He had to keep walking. Even if he felt on the verge of buckling under the weight of the burden on his back, he had to grit his teeth and keep walking.

If not, then how could he ever face her—

"—I see, so that's the root of you. Quite an interesting thing indeed."

A voice suddenly rang out, and the end came abruptly to an eternity that had seemed to have lost sight of its own ending.

The darkness that had seemed like it would stretch on and on forever cleared in the blink of an eye, and the vast world that should have felt farther away than the edge of the world itself remade itself into a narrow, stone-built passage. The soles of his shoes now trod on dust-laden ground without a sound, and above all, a foul, sickening air hung heavy in the space.

Utterly different from the world of just a moment ago, this was a real, tangible, aged ruin—a sight exactly as it had felt before he'd entered the Graveyard—and Subaru lost his words entirely.

Before him, someone suddenly approached. It was—

"I'm sorry that my welcome came across as though I were mocking you. That wasn't my intent at all, but this body of mine is hopelessly greedy, you see. I simply can't escape the desire to know."

She was a girl who gave the impression of being pure white, like a snowfield blanketed in freshly fallen, untouched snow.

Her hair, long enough to reach her back, was a fragile, pure white as though reflecting snow, and her skin, only lightly exposed, was beautiful to the point of translucence. Only her eyes, alight with an intellectual gleam, and the simple garment she wore, were jet black—she could be described in just two colors, adorned with a beauty so distilled it was almost stark.

A beauty so striking that anyone who laid eyes on her would surely be entranced—and yet, faced with it, Subaru's entire body was gripped by an overwhelming terror unlike any he'd ever known.

Even his first encounter with the White Whale hadn't carried this much pressure.

Before Subaru, who had lost all words, the girl swayed her white hair and narrowed her eyes, then, as though something had suddenly clicked, gave a small nod,

"Ah, forgive me. Here I am, and I haven't even given so much as an introduction. My repeated rudeness, I apologize. It's been so long since I've dealt with a person that I seem to have lost my rhythm."

Contrary to the tone of her voice, the girl shook her head faintly, her expression barely changing at all.

Then, seeing Subaru still silenced by his own dread, she placed a hand over her chest and quietly gave her name.

"My name is Echidna. Though perhaps I'd be better known to you as the Witch of Greed?"
