Arc 9 Chapter 9: "High-Efficiency Magic-Formula Guided Door"

"...It's good to see you've returned safely. Relief."

Being a man of few words, that was how Clind expressed his joy at Subaru's return.

Ever since Subaru had been knighted as Emilia's knight and had thrown himself into striving to live up to the title, Clind had coached him—teaching him how to train his body, how to handle a whip, and any number of other things that now fed into Subaru's confidence. In practical terms, he was the closest thing Subaru had to a master in this world.

Despite that position, Clind normally kept a certain distance even from the people of the household he served, Subaru included—so it left an impression when, in offering his words of reunion, he'd reached out to touch Subaru's shoulder, and his eyes had seemed to soften ever so slightly at the corners.

At the same time, it told Subaru that even Clind, who seemed unshakeable no matter what happened, had been worried for him.

"Well, of course you were. I mean—I'm really sorry. I heard you lent Emilia-tan and the others a huge hand in coming to get me, Clind-san."

"No, I merely did what I could, within what I was able. I've received fitting compensation for it, so it's scarcely a concern. Acceptance."

"Clind-san..."

"Besides, wouldn't apologizing in a situation like this run contrary to the Camp's policy? A question."

"Huh?"

"If it were Emilia-sama—the one Subaru-sama calls his liege, who is growing ever purer and more valiant while still retaining her childlike innocence—what would she say at a time like this? Consideration."

At Clind's words, Subaru drew a small, sharp breath.

The assessment of Emilia he'd voiced betrayed rather too much of his fondness for children brimming with limitless potential, but Subaru understood what he was getting at.

At times like this, when someone did something for her, Emilia wouldn't apologize—she would say—

"Not 'sorry,' but 'thank you.'"

"Yes. Well answered."

That he answered this with a straight face, not a smile, was perhaps the very essence of what made Clind Clind.

At his master's unreadable state of mind, Subaru scratched his cheek with a finger and gave a wry smile. This elusive quality of his was probably the root of the trouble Clind's mistress Annerose went through, as well as the source of the complicated feelings Frederica, who'd known him for years, harbored.

Though, as for the particulars of Frederica's complicated inner feelings, she brought Clind's name up far too often for someone who supposedly disliked him, so it wasn't really Subaru's place, as an outsider, to comment.

"Now that I think of it, the bird mail sent ahead said Frederica would be accompanying you as well, but I don't see her conspicuously tall figure anywhere. Confirmation."

"Hm? Ah, right. Frederica was originally supposed to come too, but the plan changed suddenly, and she went with Roswaal instead."

Subaru answered the question Clind posed while his mind drifted toward the distant sky.

At present, the Emilia Camp—Subaru and the rest included—had its members divided up across various parts of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica: Emilia and Otto had gone to report on the events in the Empire to the royal capital, Ram was looking after Rem as she convalesced from her "memory" condition at the mansion, and Roswaal and Frederica had headed for the southern part of the kingdom—Barielle territory.

With Priscilla dead and her husband, Leip Barielle, already deceased as well, Barielle territory had lost the lord celebrated as the "Sun Princess," and great turmoil was to be expected.

With the aim of keeping that turmoil to a minimum, Roswaal had gone to accompany Schult, who from the Priscilla Camp had chosen to return home directly, and Frederica was going along with them as well.

"Frederica is good with children, and Schult likes her, so it's fine to leave it to her, I suppose. If it were just Roswaal alone, it makes me shudder just thinking about it."

"I get it, but don't be too hard on him. Roswaal's mellowed out a lot too... This time, when Emilia-tan asked him to be a help to Schult, he took it on without a second's hesitation, didn't he?"

Beatrice, as ever, was harsh on Roswaal, which was endearing, but Subaru was deeply grateful for how he'd responded this time. While Subaru had been undergoing his "Sparka," Emilia had apparently talked with Schult too, and she'd seemed relieved that Roswaal had taken it upon himself.

Honestly, Subaru couldn't quite shake the suspicion that behind Roswaal's willingness to cooperate in another camp's affairs without a hint of resistance lay some notion of wanting influence over Barielle territory—but even if that were true, Subaru's conclusion, for the time being, was that Roswaal was more trustworthy than having some other ambitious person handle it.

In any case, when it came to meddling in another camp's affairs, Subaru himself had stuck his beak in far more boldly than Emilia or Roswaal ever had.

"—"

As he thought this, Subaru reflected on another complicated debt of gratitude he owed Roswaal—the fact that, after Subaru had failed to save Priscilla, Roswaal hadn't carried out what he'd once declared he would.

Roswaal, who—without knowing that Subaru's Return by Death was triggered by his own death—was nonetheless aware that Subaru possessed the Authority to start over, had declared plainly, after the battle at the Sanctuary, that he would never allow Natsuki Subaru the path of accepting a loss.

If Subaru ever failed to save someone important to him, Roswaal had said, he would burn everything to ash with his own flame, to force that world to start over.

That hadn't been a threat, and it hadn't been a joke—it had been said in dead earnest.

And yet this time, Roswaal hadn't tried to make that terrible vision of flame a reality, as if to strike Subaru down further for having failed to save Priscilla.

Whether that meant some change in Roswaal, or whether Priscilla simply hadn't counted, in his eyes, as someone whose loss warranted a do-over—Subaru couldn't say. He couldn't say, but he hoped it was the former.

"Anyway, that's the situation—Frederica's with Roswaal. If you wanted to see her face, Clind-san, you could always hop on over there..."

"Unfortunately, my method of travel isn't omnipotent either. A condition. Nor is it that I particularly wished to go out of my way just to see Frederica. A denial. Only—"

"Only?"

"It's simply that the deviation from the original plan concerned me a little. A trifle."

Subaru raised an eyebrow at Clind, who gave a brief shake of his head and added that.

Subaru understood Clind's point, but things not going according to plan happened all the time. He wouldn't go so far as to call for improvisation, but being too rigid left no room to move.

For that matter, if it hadn't been Frederica, it would have been Petra going with Roswaal instead. Given the Camp's internal situation, that would have been far more worrying.

So this pairing had come about as a natural course of events.

"—"

"Clind-san?"

"...I apologize for being unable to speak more plainly than that. Apology. Given my position, it was a presumptuous thing to say. Consideration."

Saying that, Clind bowed his head, and Subaru felt a small pang of loneliness at that line he insisted on drawing.

He'd always been someone who kept careful track of what was his business and what wasn't, but even so—

"I'm not Roswaal, and I'm not Annerose. So I don't think there's any such thing as a 'presumptuous' thing to say to me. You're one of us too, Clind-san."

There was the matter of him having just thanked Clind, after all.

If someone who'd gone that far to lend his strength wasn't a comrade, then what was he?

"Both you and I are on the side of healthy, adorable Emilia-tan, right?"

"...Yes, that's true. Affirmation. However, unlike Subaru-sama, who hopes for her swift growth, I am one who wishes to watch over the healthy budding of her potential, unhurried. A cradling."

"I'm sensing an irreconcilable difference in fan factions here!"

"For the record, Betty's position is that Emilia's growth so far makes big brother proud, I suppose."

Recognizing that there was quite a gulf, even within the Camp, in how each of them rooted for Emilia, Subaru concluded that Beatrice's opinion was the most mature of the three.

In any case, having Clind fly them the long distance in a single leap, Meili—indispensable for crossing the Augria Sand Dunes—in tow, had been a tremendous help.

"What will you do after this, Clind-san?"

"Even though it was by my young lady's order, I haven't been able to devote myself to my duties as steward for some time now. Self-reflection. Once the master and Emilia-sama return, things are likely to grow busy again. So I had intended to leave the rest of you in Frederica's hands and turn back... Deliberation."

Clind narrowed his eyes, a shade of worry peeking through his gaze behind the monocle.

It was plain that at the center of his deliberation stood the existence of his mistress, Annerose.

Young as she was, Annerose already carried a first-rate noble's spirit within her—reliable, yes, but also precarious in places. For Clind, who stayed closest to her side of anyone, there was surely anxiety in staying away from such a mistress for too long.

So—

"It'll be fine, Clind-san. Garfiel's here. And Petra's grown a lot more dependable too. Go on back, and set Annerose's mind at ease."

With that scene behind them, Subaru and the others, Meili now among them, set to work conquering the Augria Sand Dunes, and pulled it off splendidly—but even so—

"...There wasn't a single damn moment for me to step up, for real."

"Well, that's kind of the point—we parted ways with Clind-san saying we'd be fine, so if something had gone wrong it would've been way worse. It's a good thing nothing did."

"I get it, y'know? I get it fine! But c'mon, there's still the matter of how fired up I was for nothin', ain't there?"

Slumping in dejection, Garfiel hung his head in front of the main gate of the Pleiades Watchtower.

With the sand wind blowing against them, Meili—who'd been the single greatest contributor to crossing the sand sea—folded her arms and puffed out her cheeks at the sight of Garfiel hunched over so glumly.

"Whaat? You're acting like it's my fault or something. If you wanna show off your muscles that badly, why don't you go run back and forth across the sand pit all by yourself, Fang Mister?"

"Meili-chan, don't provoke him like that! Garf-san, I'm sorry. Meili-chan doesn't mean any harm, she's just a little... mouthy."

"Petra-chan's being awfully harsh on me..."

Meili, who'd been sulking, reluctantly backed down before Petra's intervention.

It wasn't solely because of what had passed between them at that tavern in Mirula, but for some reason the balance of power between these two overwhelmingly favored Petra, which made for an amusing sort of friendship.

"I guess it really does come down to who feeds who, huh?"

"I doubt that's the whole reason, I suppose. Anyone at the mansion who can go toe-to-toe with Petra is rare to begin with—about the only ones are Emilia and Ram, I suppose."

"Emilia-tan's naturally strong, and Ram's just plain strong."

Nodding along to Beatrice's agreement, Subaru found himself convinced by the observation.

Needless to say, neither Subaru nor Beatrice would have said, even under torture, that they themselves could beat Petra. They both knew their proper place. Admirable.

"Still, coming back here again, something's slowly welling up in me."

In Subaru's chest, as he said this, something ached dully at the sight of the tower's imposing form before him.

It was the place that had triggered his being flung into the Vollachia Empire, and, after everything that had happened, it was also the place that had brought him together with Spica.

But what made Subaru's chest ache most fiercely, at this moment, was thoughts of the girl who had vanished.

"—"

Shaula. The first person Natsuki Subaru, wielder of Return by Death, had failed to save.

With Priscilla now added to that number, Subaru had been made to understand, down in his very soul, a fact he'd already grasped intellectually—that Return by Death was not omnipotent.

Which was exactly why he refused to ever taste that kind of pain, that kind of wound, ever again.

"Even so, I really am relieved, I suppose. I've had quite enough of getting caught up in that kind of chaos just from going to the tower, I suppose."

Whether or not she'd sensed what was in Subaru's heart through their joined hands, Beatrice deliberately refrained from touching on his sentimentality, so Subaru was able to nod back and say, "Yeah."

The Pleiades Watchtower itself could be spotted even from a distance back in Mirula. Whether because the density of the clouds and drifting sand had thinned compared to before, there was hardly any worry now of losing sight of the tower.

Of course, that wasn't the case once the Sand Hour, when the sand winds grew fierce, arrived—but now that they'd conquered it once and understood something of the twist in space within the Sand Hour that led to the tower, they were, if anything, almost looking forward to the Sand Hour's arrival.

"Even in that sand wind, he properly kept watch around the dragon carriage and steered us clear of the rocky outcrops and ravines—that's a real accomplishment, so Garfiel's being too hard on himself, I suppose."

"Averting a crisis before it happens is plenty thanks to him, but yeah—not feeling like he's done his job unless he's flexing muscle is probably something Garfiel needs to work on going forward."

If Subaru had to rank, simply, the contributors to conquering the sand sea, Garfiel properly landed among the top three. One of the other two was, of course, Meili, and the other—

"Sorry for dragging you on trip after trip like this, Patrasche. Every single time, your stamina, your breakthrough power, and your grace save us."

"—Hrrn!"

When Subaru spoke to her like that, Patrasche whinnied back as if to say it was only natural.

Having pulled the dragon carriage in the Vollachia Empire, of course, and now again on this road back to the Pleiades Watchtower, she was undeniably an indispensable member of the team.

With one long journey after another, there'd been no time to settle down, but once they were properly back at the mansion with a chance to rest, he'd have to reward her properly—brushing, a picnic, whatever it took.

And so, while Subaru and the others congratulated each other on having reached the tower safely—

"So this is—"

Getting down from the dragon carriage after the others, Al murmured in a hoarse voice as he quietly looked up at the tower.

At the instant of emotion mixed into Al's voice—one that was terribly, achingly earnest—Subaru nearly let his cheeks stiffen, and hastily pinched them with his fingers to hold it back.

Throughout this journey, Subaru had kept himself on guard, doing his best not to show a grave or worried face in front of Al. Given what the purpose of this trip was, he couldn't treat Al as though he'd forgotten all about it—but even so, he wanted to avoid, as much as possible, fretting over him and treating him like he was made of glass.

At the very least, Subaru thought of how he'd feel if their positions were reversed and someone treated him that way.

"There it is—the Pleiades Watchtower, just like we were aiming for. Might've been a cramped trip, but good work."

With that in mind, Subaru worked the pinched skin of his cheek loose before calling out to Al. At Subaru's words, Al, still keeping his eyes on the tower, said—

"...Wasn't cramped at all. The kids went out of their way looking out for me plenty—had a pretty comfortable trip, for a nuisance like me."

"Nobody thinks of you as a nuisance."

"Ah, sorry, sorry, wasn't meant as sarcasm or anything. I'm no good—can't help wanting to say something needless. Pathetic, a grown man my age."

Al grumbled, shaking his head slowly and pressing a hand to the brow of his helmet. Understanding a fragment of the difficult state of Al's heart, Subaru found he couldn't come up with anything clever to say.

Just as Subaru looked out for Al, Al showed consideration for Subaru and everyone else too.

Only, no matter how much bravado he put up claiming he wasn't down, the reality was that he was down, so the pretense couldn't really take hold properly.

As a result, it came out as strange forced bravado and a compulsion to say things better left unsaid.

"Nobody thinks you're a pathetic man day in and day out, I suppose. Anyone can have a moment where they can't hold their head up, I suppose. What matters is looking back on it afterward, I suppose."

"...Man, oh man. That comfort's seriously sinking in slow. Thanks, Bea-ko-chan."

"...That way of addressing me, I only permit from Subaru, I suppose. Mind yourself next time."

At Beatrice answering Al with a prim, aloof face, Subaru felt a rush of tenderness and stroked her head.

Then he turned toward Garfiel and the others, who were about to open the tower's main gate—

"—Thanks, brother."

Al's voice of gratitude reached Subaru's back, and he nearly stopped for a moment, but in the end didn't—he just waved a hand over his shoulder and, together with Beatrice, walked on toward the tower's entrance.

There'd been plenty of hesitation, in various ways, about coming to the Pleiades Watchtower—about whether seeking out Priscilla's Book of the Dead was really the right thing to do.

Still, setting aside whether the Book of the Dead would actually be found, one thing at least seemed certain: that racking his brain over this for Al's sake hadn't been meaningless.

And then—

"Oh, Subaru! How do you open this huge door?"

"Ah, it's super huge, but it's not as heavy as it looks. If I put my whole body weight into it and shove with everything I've got, it opens up little by little, no problem."

"By brute force!?"

"Huh, is that so. Last time it was the naked lady, and Emilia-oneesan... and the Sword Saint guy who opened it, so I never knew."

Beside Petra, whose eyes had gone round with surprise, Meili, hugging her own elbows, let that slip.

It was a bit surprising to have her mention the Sword Saint—implying Reinhard—but apparently, while Subaru had been away, Meili had visited the tower once already together with Felt and Reinhard, in order to win the Council of Wise Men's understanding.

Which meant this was already Meili's third visit to the tower in a short span of time. She was probably, at this point, the woman who'd visited the Pleiades Watchtower more than anyone else in the world, and a position from which that count would likely keep climbing.

"Yeah, yeah, and so, about the last time we came here..."

"Now, now, hold up a sec. That story's fine and all, but let's just get inside the tower already. If it won't open without brute force, then this is my moment to shine—"

Cutting off Meili, who'd been about to launch into her story from last time, Garfiel cracked his knuckles and stepped forward. Reacting to the words "brute force" and leaning eagerly into what he saw as his moment to shine—it was a genuinely endearing trait of his—and in fact, Garfiel probably was the right one for the job.

Thinking so, Subaru was about to give him a push and say, "It's all yours." —That was when it happened.

"—Ah?"

Garfiel, primed and ready to take on the door. Before his very eyes, it began, slowly, to swing open, grinding stone against stone.

Naturally, Garfiel hadn't even touched the door yet.

Beyond Garfiel, stunned by that fact, from the gap of the opening door—

"—Well, are you surprised? In consideration of how the number of people coming and going from the tower is bound to increase going forward, I constructed a new formula that doubles as purification for the miasma drifting over the Augria Sand Dunes."

What came through was a man's voice, brimming with confidence and self-regard.

The owner of that voice, its tone laced with a smile and a boastful nuance, was slowly, steadily revealed before Subaru and the others' eyes.

Waiting there was a figure in a black robe, his green hair trimmed neatly.

"It's a system that, by channeling a minute but specific amount of mana through a magic circle, works in tandem with the door. By this means, I've corrected, right from the entrance, the inefficient state of a tower that was so troublesome to enter and exit! This mechanism, I have named the High-Efficiency Magic-Formula Guided Door—"

"—Since it's a door that opens automatically, it's an automatic door."

"Nnghah!?"

His voice, rising in volume more and more, was just about to hit its peak when a voice, cold as a splash of ice water, cut in from the side, and his momentum stumbled headlong.

Choking on his words, the robed man looked, aghast, at the person beside him—a figure short as a child—and, trembling, pointed at the one standing next to him.

"Miss Flam! How many times do I have to say it before it sinks in. This may be unassuming, its usefulness hard to grasp at a glance, but it's a new technology that ought, in time, to see valuable use in many facilities. Which is precisely why it needs a name to match its worth..."

"But it's hard to say and hard to remember. Automatic door works fine."

"Such a lazy..."

Beside the man, whose eyes had gone wide with shock, a girl with pink hair slowly shook her head.

Greeted by the two of them, roughly the same height—though "greeted" was a stretch given how they'd been leaving Subaru's party out of the conversation entirely—Subaru called out an "Oi" to those who'd arrived first.

"Sorry to interrupt when you're in the middle of something, but who are you two—"

"Sensei-san and Flam-chan, Mister."

It was Meili who cut in like that, as Subaru tried to ask after the two of them.

From the words she'd used—"Sensei" and the girl's name—and from Meili's manner, treating them as people she already knew, Subaru could guess who they were.

The last time Meili had visited the Pleiades Watchtower together with Felt and the others, these two had apparently stayed behind at the tower as sentries and liaisons—members of the Felt Camp—

"Hm, is that Garfiel-dono there! It's been a while, not since Priestella! I trust you've kept well—I am Ezzo Cadner."

"Ezzo-sama's healthy and noisy as always today."

"'Noisy' is nothing but an insult, Miss Flam!"

Saying so, glaring wide-eyed at the girl he'd called Flam, was—just as he'd introduced himself—Ezzo Cadner, the Felt Camp's reputed brains-of-the-outfit mage.

Addressed so amiably by Ezzo, one might have expected Garfiel to be delighted at reuniting with an acquaintance—but instead—

"M-my moment to shine, gone again... grr..."

He let the arm he'd been reaching out droop limply, looking utterly deflated.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—I see. I hadn't heard the particulars, but so Priscilla Barielle-sama..."

On hearing the news of Priscilla's death, Ezzo offered a quiet moment of silent prayer.

His well-practiced repose in mourning let a clear tint of intellect seep through his appearance—that of a member of the Dwarf Tribe who looked as young as a child.

As a member of the Felt Camp, he would naturally have been aware of Priscilla as a rival camp's leader, yet he did not falter at news of her death. Turning his gaze to Al, he said—

"Al-dono, allow me to express my condolences. I never had the chance to meet Priscilla-sama myself, but I was told by Felt-jou that she was a formidable rival, not to be underestimated. Felt-jou's eye is a sound one. Your liege must truly have been a person of exceptional caliber."

"...Yeah. Much obliged, Ezzo-dono."

To Ezzo, who'd solemnly offered his condolences for Priscilla, Al likewise responded gravely.

An outlander with his face hidden behind an iron helmet, and a figure whose adult bearing belied his childlike appearance—the exchange between the two of them would have looked distinctly odd from the outside, but within it was an earnest sharing of feeling.

"Then, Natsuki-dono and Garfiel-dono came directly to the tower from the Empire?"

"That's right. Coming straight to the tower was closer, distance-wise, than heading back to the mansion first."

"I see. Even so, leaving the side of your liege, Emilia-sama, must have been a bitter decision. Reinhard-dono, too, is loath to leave Felt-jou's side to an almost exasperating degree."

"Those two have their own way of getting along, I guess... Honestly, I suspect Reinhard might actually be enjoying it when Felt makes a sour face at him."

Felt was refreshingly offhand with Reinhard, of all people. That kind of reaction from her might well look novel to Reinhard, who was treated with such careful deference by everyone else.

If that was the reason he stayed by Felt's side, then Reinhard had quite the personality himself.

"Of course, the real reason's probably that she's his precious liege... but yeah, like Ezzo-san said, leaving Emilia-tan's side was rough on me too. Only—"

It had been nearly two months since Emilia and the others had left the kingdom, bound for the Empire.

During that time, none of the various problems tied to the Royal Selection had been able to move forward, and on top of that, the shift in relations with the Empire needed to be reported to the royal capital as soon as possible—including, among that news, the report of Priscilla's death, information that would send shockwaves through the Royal Selection itself.

For that reason, putting off the report to the royal capital simply wasn't an option.

At the same time, leaving Al, sunk in despondency, to languish while binding him from the tower he longed to reach wasn't something they could bring themselves to do either—so, just as Ezzo said, it had become a bitter decision.

Even so—

"There wasn't much friction over this, actually. Honestly, I knew I was being unreasonable about it, but it felt like everyone had already accepted that I'd say something like that... I felt bad about it."

It seemed to Subaru that Emilia and the others, already knowing full well what he'd end up saying, had prepared a way to grant his wishes to the fullest extent possible while still keeping things safe.

Amid circumstances that couldn't be put off, like the report to the royal capital and handling matters in Barielle territory as mentioned before, the utmost that could be managed had brought Subaru and the others here to the Pleiades Watchtower now.

"No friction over it... Hmm. Is that how it is."

"—?"

"Ah, no, never mind, just thinking aloud. Just as you assessed Felt-jou and Reinhard-dono's lord-and-knight relationship—each pair has their own way of doing things. As for what happened in the Empire, I can only imagine it, after all."

Ezzo nodded magnanimously, a finger resting against his slender chin.

There was something in Ezzo's manner of speaking and bearing that put one somehow at ease in conversation, and Subaru could understand why Meili called him "Sensei."

In any case—

"I'd like to say let's rest up now that we've reached the tower... but there's probably someone here who can't settle down with the thing he's after so close at hand, so, sorry to rush it, but—"

"To Taygeta, where the archive is, is it. Naturally, I have no reason to stop you. There is, however, one point I'd like to confirm. —Al-dono."

Cutting off Subaru, who—introductions and circumstances more or less out of the way—was looking up at the spiral staircase leading up from the fifth level at the tower's entrance and about to move things toward their goal on the third level, Ezzo called out Al's name.

At the call, Al turned around, and Ezzo held up a single finger.

"I mentioned earlier that I had no personal acquaintance with Priscilla-sama, but allow me to say this regardless. From what I've heard of her character, wouldn't Priscilla-sama be one who has no wish for you to read her own Book of the Dead? Even so, do you still desire your late liege's book?"

"—"

"Ezzo-san, that's—"

"Silence, please, Natsuki-dono. I am asking Al-dono. This is important."

The instant Ezzo said that quietly, the fingertip he'd raised gave off a faint glow, and Subaru felt his own throat lose the ability to produce sound. He grasped at once that this was Ezzo's magic, and was made to realize just how far this man's skill as a mage exceeded what he'd heard.

With Subaru, forcibly barred from speaking up, standing beside them, Ezzo and Al looked each other in the eye.

"Losing someone close to you is a painful thing. I've experienced it myself, and I'm sure anyone has. But I don't believe that everyone who has gone through such an experience comes to this tower and desires a Book of the Dead. All the more so if the very person described within the book has no wish for it."

What Ezzo stated was an opinion of such sharp good sense it could draw blood.

"—"

Al fell silent, his expression hidden behind his iron helmet, unreadable.

But it was a fact that he was wounded deeply, gravely, and nearly drowning in the pain and bleeding of that wound. And it was equally a fact that Subaru, and everyone else, had wished to do something about it.

So much so that it had been, quite literally, unanimous within the Emilia Camp.

"You're right, Ezzo-dono. Just like you said—there are people who can pull themselves back up on their own even when someone close to them dies. Without borrowing the power of any book."

"I'm glad we're of the same mind. But it sounds like there's more."

"There is. There's one more thing you're right about. The princess—Priscilla—doesn't want her Book of the Dead to be read."

"—"

"But she's dead."

A faint hiss escaped Subaru's voiceless throat.

Hearing, for the first time, Al speak of Priscilla with the word "dead" out of his own mouth, Subaru felt as though he'd been cut by a keenly sharpened blade.

It was less a sensation of pain than one of blood pouring out fast and his body going cold.

And Al, having let Subaru and Ezzo hear those words, went on still further—

"Priscilla's dead. —She can't tell me anything, ever again."

"...I see."

At Al's voice, low, as though his heart had frozen solid, Ezzo lowered his eyes.

Hearing the same answer as Ezzo, Subaru felt his own heart pierced by the wretched pain of the ice age that had descended upon Al's soul—a desolate state of mind that had lost its sun, its flame, in the form of Priscilla.

With a face that seemed to share that very same sensation with Subaru, Ezzo let out a small breath.

"I said something meant to test you, and made you say what you didn't want to say. I apologize."

"...No. It needed saying. For me too—putting it into words."

At Al's hoarse-voiced reply, Ezzo nodded deeply.

At that, Ezzo gave a snap of his fingers.

"My apologies, Natsuki-dono. I wanted to ask that personally, without any interruptions."

"Ah—ah, ah, my voice's back... What was that just now?"

"It looked like Yin magic... but it's actually an application of Yang magic. I didn't silence your voice—I simply raised it to a pitch too high to hear normally. The kind of range bats and the like produce."

"You can do that...? My apologies, I underestimated you. And, um..."

With a hand at his throat, Subaru glanced toward Al. Reading the intent behind Subaru's look, Ezzo prefaced with, "I know," and continued—

"Ultimately, this is a matter for the parties involved. It isn't for an outsider to interfere in. Granted, if I'd sensed hesitation or doubt, I intended to gently talk him out of it—but if he can't move forward without doing this, then someone with resolve has no choice but to press on."

"And that's okay with you?"

"What a strange thing to ask. Haven't all of you, too, swallowed your own form of acceptance about this? Besides, speaking for myself, if I died and someone wished to read my own Book of the Dead, I'd welcome it. I'd want them to know everything about my life, and use it as a foundation for the future."

"—"

"For that matter, not that it's out of any dire wish, but I myself have already thrown myself into perusing quite a fair number of Books of the Dead! Which frankly puts me in no position to talk!"

Gone was the composed, adult face from a moment ago—raising his voice like this, Ezzo had reverted to the self-assured face he'd worn earlier, unveiling the "automatic door" formula for the tower's gate.

Startled by the sudden shift, Subaru nonetheless understood it as a deliberate touch meant to dispel the tense atmosphere he himself had created, and found his opinion of Ezzo's character rising.

Honestly, having just come back from the Empire, where practically everyone he met was potentially a hidden threat, simply being able to interact with someone he could feel at ease with was enough to raise his opinion considerably.

In any case—

"Sorry for making you wait around. I feel a bit bad stealing your thunder, Natsuki-dono, since you already know the way, but I'll guide you myself—probably no one goes in and out of the archive more than I do these days. —To Taygeta, where the Books of the Dead are kept, the true heart of the Great Library Pleiades."

And so, the time had finally come for the purpose behind their visit to the Pleiades Watchtower to be fulfilled.
