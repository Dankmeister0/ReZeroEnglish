Arc 5 Interlude: "Conditions for a Pair"

It felt as though so many things had been cast aside, left dangling in midair. 　Accomplished, unaccomplished—two extreme sentiments warred inside his chest.

By nature, thinking had never been his strong suit. 　It was so much better to think of nothing at all, to just throw himself into motion and run around endlessly. 　Perhaps this was his punishment for abandoning thought for so long.

"What the hell... was so 'magnificent' about it...?"

Still unable to find an answer to those final words bestowed upon him by that hero. 　In the end, was he nothing more than a half-baked failure? The sentiment brought him close to tears.

He lightly kicked off the ground, slipping inside a collapsed building. 　Squeezing his compact frame through to peer within, he saw that the interior had indeed been horribly ransacked. At least in his memory, this place hadn't been connected to any battle that decided the fate of the city. Thus, this ruin was likely the aftermath of squabbles among the panicked citizens themselves.

"Can't say there's any of that... telltale stink left behind, though."

Snorting, he surveyed the scene. 　Everywhere he looked were traces that made him want to avert his eyes—bloodstains, crumbling walls—but neither the lingering reek of frenzy nor the stench of blood remained here anymore.

"—Huh?"

Just as he was about to give up and head back, seeing that no noticeable harvest was to be found, his feet came to a halt. In the corner of the trashed room, he spotted a small doll lying on the floor. 　Whomever had dropped it, it was a humanoid doll. Small enough to fit in the palm of one's hand, it was miraculously neither dirty nor broken despite being in a place like this.

"……"

Picking it up, he lightly brushed off the dust, finding himself glancing around even though its owner couldn't possibly be here. Leaving a single doll behind wouldn't change anything, but—

"Tch."

Clicking his tongue, he shoved the doll into his breast pocket and exited the building. Leaning out from a window missing its glass, he dug his fingers and feet into handholds along the building's facade, nimbly descending to the ground.

"Ah! He's back!" "It's true! Big bro is amazing!"

Noticing him climb down with such agile moves, several people working on the ground rushed over. They all wore expressions mixed with anxiety and hope, and to be honest, it stung that he couldn't satisfy either.

"Big bro, how was it inside?"

"Sorry 'bout that, no harvest. Nobody was left inside, and what you were lookin' for wasn't there either."

"I see..."

Landing and dusting off his knees, he gave his answer to the middle-aged man who had run up to him. For a moment, the man's face clouded over at the response, but he quickly composed himself with a nod.

"Well, if nobody was inside, that means we can put this building on the back burner for now. You saved us a lot of trouble, big bro. With the staircase collapsed, we were stumped since we couldn't carelessly check inside."

"As if I could just stand by and watch you pull a dangerous stunt like that. I respect the grit to wrap a rope around yourself and scale a wall, but try slimmin' down a bit before attemptin' it, would ya?"

"He's got a point there! Wahaha, you dodged a bullet!"

Slapping his shoulder merrily, the middle-aged man raised a hand with a final "Thanks a bunch" before heading off to the next building. Watching the middle-aged man and several helpers leave for the next structure, Garfiel crossed his arms, pondering what to do next. 　When—

"..."

"..."

What caught his eye was a pair of figures looking up at the building he had just stepped out of. 　It was a mother and child duo; a teary-eyed girl was clutching the hand of her plump mother. As the mother tried to convey something to her daughter, the girl looked down and shook her head over and over again. 　He couldn't quite tell what they were discussing, but this was hardly a safe environment for a family quarrel or raising a child. Just as he was about to speak up and send them away, he suddenly realized.

"..."

Snorting, he noticed a certain commonality. 　Fumbling inside his breast pocket, the doll he had picked up inside the building peeked out. Lifting it up to sniff, sure enough, it bore a scent similar to the subtle aroma lingering around them.

"Is this what you were lookin' for, by any chance?"

"!..."

Stepping closer to the mother and child, he gently offered the doll in his hand. At first startled by being addressed, the young girl's eyes went wide as she noticed the doll. 　As her timidly outstretched hand grasped the doll, her teary eyes swelled even more with an emotion entirely different from before.

"Thank you so much. She was looking for this non-stop..."

"No need to worry 'bout it. Yours truly just happened to stumble upon it, is all."

Offered thanks by the mother, he shook his head, insisting it was no big deal. 　In truth, he didn't feel he had accomplished anything grand. Though a joyful moment for the girl searching for her doll, weighed on the balance of human life and morale, it was hardly a matter of consequence. 　What was demanded of him right now was a far greater role—

"...And I still ain't found out what the hell that is."

Absentmindedly stroking the head of the girl hugging her doll, he muttered. 　Then, lifting the palm that had touched the girl, he stared at it intently.

"...?"

"It's nothin'. Don't go losin' it again."

His luck surely wouldn't hold out enough to find it a second time. 　Saying so, Garfiel Tinsel bared his fangs in a weak smile at the girl.

Three days had already passed since the conclusion of the series of incidents in the Water Gate City of Priestella. 　The scars left upon the city by that Witch Cult-instigated crisis were deep and vast.

Left behind as visible, indelible wounds were the damages inflicted by the Authorities of the Sin Archbishops of Lust and Gluttony. The issues regarding the 'mutated citizens' and the 'nameless' victims were particularly severe, and it was said that hopes for resolving these matters rested upon the knowledge of the 'Sage' Shaula.

Having not attended the conference, Garfiel didn't grasp the detailed flow of the discussion. However, the mention of the Sage's name—one of the 'Three Great Heroes' recorded in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica—evoked deep emotion in him.

By nature, Garfiel had a liking for anecdotes about legends, folklore, figures who left their names in history, and heroes. 　Naturally, the Three Great Heroes were among his store of knowledge; his admiration for the 'Divine Dragon,' the 'Sword Saint,' and the 'Sage' was profound, and he was well aware of his particularly intense interest in the Sage.

This was because among the Three Great Heroes, Shaula the 'Sage' was a figure exceptionally starved of spoken information.

The 'Divine Dragon,' revere-honored in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica and passed down through fairy tales. 　The 'Sword Saint,' reigning supreme across generations as the kingdom's strongest, remaining to this day a visible legend. 　Compared to both of them, the words spoken regarding the 'Sage'—both name and anecdotes—were far too scarce.

What did she look like? What was her origin? What were her principles? 　From personality to background and everything that followed, all of it was ambiguous. 　Secluded in the Pleiades Watchtower, watching over the 'Witch's Shrine' while wishing for world peace—or so the public rumor went, but how much of that was fact remained uncertain.

Thus, under normal circumstances, there was no way the usual him wouldn't have volunteered to join the journey to visit the Sage. Meeting a figure etched into history—he would never pass up such an opportunity. 　Of course, considering their purpose, he had enough tact to refrain from wearing his personal admiration on his sleeve. Exercising that much discretion, he still would have offered to come along.

Yet this time, the thought of putting himself forward for that journey hadn't even crossed his mind. 　That was surely because he had far too many lingering regrets attached to this place.

In the end, though various reasons were given, the ultimate truth was that current Garfiel was judged useless, and that was why he'd been left behind.

Subaru was a man who, if he deemed it necessary, would try to take Garfiel along even if Garfiel were battered and severely wounded. And Garfiel trusted Subaru enough that even if he were battered or on the verge of death, if Subaru declared he was needed, Garfiel would lend him his strength. 　That it didn't happen meant precisely that.

"The current me ain't no use. —The Boss is real strict, after all."

To Natsuki Subaru, a veteran of a hundred psychological battles, Garfiel's shallow poker face had been entirely transparent. The fact that he was so slow to realize it meant being seen through and left behind was only natural. 　Only natural, but—

"Then what the hell am I supposed to do... I still ain't found the answer to that."

He was well aware that he was pacing in place. 　And he had a few ideas about what was causing him to hesitate.

Yet he couldn't see how to face those issues, nor what the right answer was. That was precisely why Garfiel's feet remained frozen.

"..."

So at the very least, holding those feelings inside, he lent a hand to the city's reconstruction. 　Both arms, which had sustained severe injuries in his battle against Kurgan, were showing signs of recovery thanks to the Divine Protection of Earth Spirits and his own healing magic, even if they weren't completely restored. 　While fighting at full capacity was out of the question, clearing rubble or using his agility for a brief scouting run like earlier fell well within the realm of what he could manage.

Of course, he knew that this, too, fell under the domain of escape. 　As long as he kept his body moving and worked for someone else's sake, he could avoid being conscious of his own hesitation, and prevent those around him from seeing him standing still. 　Garfiel was aware of his own weakness—that this was why he was doing it.

—Garfiel Tinsel did not know that this was, in fact, quite close to an act Natsuki Subaru had once recognized in himself: a means to disguise his own weakness.

Compared to Natsuki Subaru, who had recognized his weakness yet continued to avert his eyes, Garfiel was trying to face himself in a far nobler manner. 　He did not know that Subaru had departed believing Garfiel would overcome it without a word needing to be said. 　He did not know, but—

"Ohhh! Found Garf! Sooo energetic! You really like high places, huh, Garf!"

Subaru also knew that there were plenty of people around who would help him realize that fact. 　And thus, Garfiel was neither as foolish nor as good-for-nothing as he believed himself to be.
