Arc 4 Interlude: "Each Their Own Step Closer"

"—Alright, it's finished!"

Subaru stuck two branches he'd picked up into the mound of snow before him and wiped the sweat from his brow.

It was an amateur's work, roughly an hour in the making, but he found himself quite taken with how well it had turned out. At the sight of the finished piece, a ripple of admiring "oohs" spread through the watching crowd.

"Yeah, I think I've got a real talent for this kind of thing. If I ever fall on hard times, I'll make it rain snow with Emilia-tan and become a Living National Treasure as a snow-sculpture artist."

"Oh, stop saying silly things. I'm not going to help you make it snow for something like that. ...But it's really, really good."

Sitting on the stone steps watching Subaru work, Emilia let out a white breath.

Reflected in her violet-blue eyes was the snowman Subaru had completed—though calling it simply that might be misleading; "snow sculpture" was probably the more fitting term.

Gathering the snow left over in the Sanctuary, he'd built roughly twenty snow statues of Puck. What had driven Subaru to such lengths, even he himself could only call "romance."

For now, Emilia and the residents of the Sanctuary seemed pleased, so that was good enough.

"You probably didn't mean it this way, but I still think Barusu is an idiot."

So said one voice, offering a rather harsh assessment of Subaru.

It belonged to a girl seated on the stone steps just like Emilia, her head resting on Emilia's lap. She'd shed her trademark maid uniform and was now wrapped in a simple white garment.

Her uniform had been burned to cinders, and she had hovered at the border between life and death. Her complexion looked even paler than her usual fair skin, but it hadn't dulled the edge in her voice or the venom in her tongue in the slightest. Which was for the best.

"You two keep calling me an idiot over and over... In the first place, shouldn't you be a little nicer to me, the guy who deserves the credit for all this? I don't think I'm getting nearly enough appreciation."

"Mm, that's true. I'm really, really grateful to Subaru. But it was me who worked hard while Subaru was away, so I'd rather you appreciated me instead."

"Emilia-tan, you've suddenly started saying stuff like that..."

In truth, it was thanks to Emilia that the Sanctuary had been protected while Subaru and the others were away. Without her instructions, there was no telling whether the residents who took shelter in the tomb could have escaped the Great Rabbit's fangs, and to begin with, without Emilia clearing the Trials, there would have been no shelter to secure at all.

Even if Subaru had been there, it wasn't clear he'd have thought to use the tomb as a shelter. After all, he'd been thinking only about fleeing before the snow started to fall.

"Well, the young men's brigade coming back and lighting a fire under Emilia-tan's motivation was a happy miscalculation. ...Really, you saved us."

It wasn't as though things had been any different before, but this time in particular there had been far too many gambles.

He'd been left with the impression that he alone hadn't been enough, that he'd been saved by the people around him again and again—even though he'd resolved to take on the hardest part himself.

"Well, of course. If you did everything all by yourself, we wouldn't know what we were even here for. You run yourself ragged enough that you could stand to rest a little, you know."

"No, but see, when a guy who's short on both brains and brawn wants to make himself useful, running around like a fool is about the only method available to him."

"But it's not going to be like that anymore, is it?"

As if teasing Subaru for putting himself down, Emilia stroked Ram's head on her lap and said this with a knowing little laugh. Subaru immediately grasped what she meant, and rubbing under his nose with a finger, he answered, "Yeah."

There had been plenty of oversights, and he'd been saved by everyone around him more than once, but he'd more or less managed to pick up everything he needed to pick up. And he was sure he'd never again go on agonizing over it all alone.

Subaru no longer hesitated to rely on the people around him, he wouldn't slack off on doing his own part to make that possible, and there were people who'd kick him in the backside if he did.

——

Lifting his head, Subaru turned his gaze from the plaza toward the tomb.

Passing over Emilia seated on the near steps, his line of sight went to the entrance of the tomb. Right now, two people had stepped inside the tomb where the system of the Trials had vanished.

What were they talking about in there? It was something he couldn't help but wonder about, but—

"Well, even I can read the room enough to let them have some time to themselves."

The two of them had had countless chances to talk, and yet they'd never managed to.

There was surely a mountain of pent-up things to say between them.

With the transparent coffin between them, the tall man and the girl faced each other.

"Mother..."

The girl murmured, looking down into the coffin at the woman lying within the crystal.

It was a floating, unmoored feeling, as though her feet weren't quite touching the ground. Part of it was the lingering exhilaration of battle; part of it was the mixture of loss and liberation from losing the place where she'd spent so much time; and a large part of it came from how unreal the scene before her felt.

She had never imagined that a day would come when she'd see her mother's form again like this.

The woman sleeping in the coffin—the Witch, Echidna—looked exactly as she remembered, without the slightest difference.

Long, beautiful white hair; refined features that conveyed both intellect and gentleness. Rare as it had been, the memory of being smiled at came back vividly.

She could recall the instructions given to her at their final parting, every word of them.

"Betty... couldn't keep her promise to Mother, I suppose. I'm sorry."

Stroking the coffin with a finger, Beatrice began their reunion after four hundred years with an apology.

When they parted, Echidna had impressed upon Beatrice that she was to hand over the archive of her knowledge to "That Person," and sent her off with that charge. The countless books that filled the Forbidden Archives entrusted to her, and the Gospel that foretold the future.

Neither of those remained in Beatrice's hands now.

Because both the Gospel that showed the future her mother had wished for her, and the entirety of the knowledge her mother had spent so long accumulating, had all been swallowed by flames that reduced everything to ash and erased from this world.

"Betty never even got to meet 'That Person'... and the books, she let them burn, I suppose. There are probably far too many things she needs to say sorry for."

A poor excuse for a daughter, Beatrice judged herself to be.

Across four hundred years, she'd been a foolish daughter who couldn't even keep the one instruction her mother had given her. And now, in a moment where she should have been unable to even face the mother she'd failed, where she ought to have been apologizing from the bottom of her heart—

"...And yet, don't you have a rather clear look on your face for someone in that position?"

The tall man standing before her murmured this, laying Beatrice's heart bare with disarming ease.

Glancing up into the gloom, what emerged was the long-haired man with lips loosened in a listless smile—Roswaal. Familiar as his face was, Beatrice couldn't help but feel something was off about him.

Perhaps it was because there was now a hint of hesitation in the eyes of a man who, ever since they'd met, had been ruled entirely by a mad sense of purpose. Or perhaps it was because he'd wiped off his clown's makeup and bared his true face.

"If we're talking about clear looks, I can't compete with you, Roswaal. Standing before Betty without your makeup on—that's as good as breaking the previous one's orders, I suppose."

"The clown's makeup wa-a-as, to me, something like war paint. Wearing it let me face people with the resolve of someone donning a mask. Only, I've noticed something, you se-e-e."

"Noticed something?"

"Makeup or no makeup, am I not already a clown through and through these days? If so, how much meaning is left in whether I wear it or not, I wo-o-onder?"

"I see. That makes sense, I suppose."

Beatrice tipped her chin at Roswaal's shrugging, comical gesture. Fidgeting idly with one of her ringlets, the girl continued, "And so?"

"Surely you're the one who has something to say to Mother. This reunion with her must have been... a long-cherished wish, for you and your entire line, I suppose."

"..........."

"From the first Roswaal, who knew Mother directly, down to you now, you must be close to the tenth generation, I suppose. Every head of House Mathers has been short-lived, so the ones who came to show their faces in the Forbidden Archives kept changing, one after another, I suppose. ...You were different from the start, even as a child, weren't you."

Even though she hadn't been deeply involved in it, the history of House Mathers was, if anything, the flow of time Beatrice had witnessed most closely at hand.

The first Roswaal had been Echidna's one and only disciple. Having lost most of his magical talent in the battle against the Warlock Hector, he had nonetheless never given up on being her disciple.

Even after Echidna's death, he had haunted the Forbidden Archives, chasing after something as though possessed, leaving the dazed and stupefied Beatrice to herself, and had presumably passed that pursuit on to the next generation before he died.

From then on, every successive head of the family who inherited the name Roswaal had, one after another, achieved magical power and discoveries rivaling the first, expanding House Mathers as they went.

And then there was the current Roswaal—that is, the man standing before her now.

This Roswaal L. Mathers had displayed the most outstanding talent of any Roswaal before him, even in childhood—a prodigy so remarkable that he'd secretly made even Beatrice shudder.

His power surpassed even that of the first Roswaal, who had been personally discovered by Echidna, and he had likely earned himself a reputation as one of the strongest mages in the world.

"And even with all that talent, even you couldn't escape the curse of House Mathers, I suppose. A line that walked nothing but a grueling road, dreaming of nothing but reuniting with a dead mother... Betty feels a little sorry for all of you, I suppose."

"Is that so? But how different are we from you, really? You too have been bound for four hundred years by the words of a dead mother, no matter how you look at it. No—unlike us, who passed the torch from generation to generation, no one could ever truly sympathize with the suffering of someone who spent all that time alone. Unlike us, who pressed forward with a task to accomplish, yours was the suffering of someone who could only crouch and wait."

To Beatrice's words, Roswaal answered with words of equal weight.

In the end, she supposed, the two of them were much the same.

The line of Roswaals, passing their feelings down through short lifespans in pursuit of a reunion with a single person.

And Beatrice, who had locked her unending life away in an empty cage, waiting for the day a promise would be fulfilled.

To anyone looking on from outside, both of them had been equally foolish clowns.

——

For a while, the two of them remained silent, their gazes locked.

But that quiet standoff was broken when Roswaal suddenly looked away.

"This is turning into a tiresome argument. Two fools pointing fingers and mocking each other—there's a limit to how pointless and ridiculous that can get, you se-e-e."

"...Well, that's certainly true, I suppose."

"May I ask just one thing?"

Roswaal, having wryly appraised the state of the two of them, held up a single finger. Beatrice silently tilted her chin up, signaling assent by not refusing.

At the girl's manner, Roswaal quietly looked down at Echidna sleeping in the coffin.

"Has young Subaru managed to become your 'That Person,' I wo-o-onder?"

The question came. And at the appearance of the words "That Person," Beatrice caught her breath, just slightly. She had never spoken directly with Roswaal about "That Person." Even so, she'd always thought that a man like him might well know things about her that she herself didn't know he knew.

To begin with, the people who had come and gone from the Forbidden Archives before had, if traced back to their roots, all been brought there by previous Roswaals. Naturally, they could have heard the story from those people and passed it down to their descendants.

To put it plainly, even Subaru could be described as someone the current Roswaal had brought along.

Though if she said that out loud, Subaru himself would surely never admit to it.

"...Why are you laughing?"

"—Ah. Sorry about that, I suppose. It's not that Betty was laughing at you, Roswaal. It's just that picturing it was funny, I suppose."

What had been funny was that she could trace, precisely in her mind, exactly what the black-haired young man would say. Whether that meant he was simple or something more, she didn't much want to think about it further.

Still, Beatrice shook her head at Roswaal's question.

"That man... Subaru isn't fit to be Betty's 'That Person,' I suppose."

"...Oh?"

"To begin with, Subaru is lacking every single qualification needed to inherit Mother's knowledge from the Forbidden Archives, I suppose. He has no learning, no sense of purpose for using knowledge, and no groundwork for it either, I suppose. And on top of that, his face isn't handsome, his strength is hopeless, he can't use magic to speak of, and his legs are short, I suppose. There's absolutely, positively no way he's the 'That Person' Betty has been waiting for."

"My, that's a te-e-erribly harsh assessment, isn't it."

"Is it? Betty is harsh, I suppose. That's why, across four hundred years, she let every single opportunity slip through her fingers, I suppose. ...She dragged 'That Person' around by her own selfishness, I suppose."

Looking back on it now, she felt something like guilt toward the people who had tried to lead Beatrice out of the Forbidden Archives. It wasn't as though they had reached out to her purely out of ambition or self-interest. Among them, there had surely been words spoken out of genuine concern for her.

And yet Beatrice had brushed away every single one of those outstretched hands.

"'That Person' should surely have been someone Betty chose herself, I suppose. She should have faced each and every one of them who spoke to her and given them a proper answer. Betty choosing someone worthy of inheriting the Forbidden Archives, Echidna's knowledge... that's surely how it should have been, I suppose."

"But the young Subaru you chose isn't fit to be 'That Person,' is he?"

"That's right, I suppose. But that's fine, I suppose. The one Betty chose was Subaru. Not 'That Person.' She chose Subaru, I suppose."

At Beatrice's answer, she could see Roswaal hold his breath and widen his eyes.

For a man who adored and devoted himself to Echidna, it might be a hard answer to accept. Having stood in nearly the same position herself until only recently, Beatrice understood Roswaal's feelings painfully well.

Precisely because she understood, she felt she needed to spell it out fully.

"Subaru laughed off the wish for him to become 'That Person,' I suppose. He had the nerve to say he could make Betty happier than some faceless nobody, I suppose."

"That's... an arrogant answer."

"But it was pushy, and Betty didn't hate it, I suppose."

It had been raw and unguarded, more than any well-mannered lecture telling her what she ought to do, or any explanation of how he'd make use of Echidna's knowledge.

"And yet, however you arrange the pieces, you could never be Subaru's first priority, could you? That much is clear just from watching him now... and besides, I happen to know it."

"You seem to be under a misunderstanding, Roswaal."

"A misunderstanding?"

"It's not that Betty left the Forbidden Archives because she became Subaru's first priority, I suppose. She left because she wants to make Subaru her own first priority, I suppose."

Choose me, he had told her.

Without you, I'll be too lonely to go on living, he had told her.

It was a convenient thing to say, she thought. Sweet-sounding nonsense, she thought too.

And yet, Beatrice's heart had wavered. It had resonated within her. A heart that had stayed frozen in the same place for four hundred years had been shaken loose.

And once she'd known the tearful sense of release in the moment she took his hand and stepped out of the Forbidden Archives, her heart wouldn't stop.

"Even if abandoning her duty means Betty loses her qualification as Mother's spirit, she doesn't mind, I suppose. Betty is already the spirit of her Contractor, Natsuki Subaru, I suppose. And she'll never regret it or feel ashamed of it... not anymore, I suppose."

Perhaps, to Roswaal, this was a kind of betrayal.

To a man who, like Beatrice, had been bound by Echidna's curse for four hundred years, declaring that she was stepping out first might well be a betrayal. Not stepping out by fulfilling her duty, but by throwing it away.

To both her mother and to Roswaal, holding her head up now could only look like brazen defiance.

——

Her heart was already made up. Her hand was already held.

From now on, Beatrice would live burning vivid, unfading colors into herself, rather than letting them fade to sepia. She would sear them in so that, at the end of some long, long stretch of time, she would never forget what mattered.

And so she waited in silence to see what Roswaal would say.

"There's no need to brace yourself like that. I'm not exactly the Witch Echidna's spo-o-okesman, after all. Whatever answer you've come to, I have no right to say a word about it. Do as you like."

"Roswaal..."

"Besides, even if you hadn't thrown it away, Echidna's order to you was never going to be fulfilled regardless. I was the one who tried to sacrifice you to put my own wish first. If we're talking about betrayal, that's far more of one, wouldn't you say."

——

As though making a confession, Roswaal admitted his guilt for what had happened at the mansion.

Just as Beatrice had already suspected back in the Forbidden Archives, it was Roswaal who had schemed to take her life at the mansion. She figured it was all something written into Roswaal's Gospel, though she had no idea how the pieces connected.

"Roswaal. What became of your Gospel, I wonder?"

"...It burned. At the hands of a wicked maid who defied her master, you se-e-e. So the future now lies entirely in ashes. Or perhaps everything does, come to think of it."

"Everything's empty, and you can't see the future anymore... and yet, you seem to have a fairly clear look on your face too, I suppose."

"—As for that, I wo-o-onder."

At Beatrice throwing his own words right back at him, Roswaal lowered his head. Then he reached an untouching finger toward Echidna in the coffin.

"Losing sight of the road that was certain to lead to the answer I sought is sad, and frightening. ...But perhaps there's also joy to be found in reading onward through a story you've never read before. It's a feeling I haven't felt since more than four hundred years ago, so I can't say for certain whether it's genuine."

"...?"

At the faintly strange content of his words, Beatrice knit her brow.

Seeing this, Roswaal's lips loosened slightly, and to the puzzled Beatrice he said, self-deprecatingly, "Truly, you and I haven't exchanged nearly enough words, have we."

"'It couldn't be helped' isn't going to cut it, I'm afraid. Unlike back at the start, when we had to remain blind to everything else, we had time. We had even more time than that, living under the same roof. And yet, even though we were looking at the same thing, we kept avoiding any talk of what we were looking at, as if we were afraid of it."

"Roswaal, what are you trying to say?"

"I'm saying that we could have spent these four hundred years together the way we once did... the way we spent our time together in Teacher's study."

"Te—!?"

At Roswaal's gentle words, Beatrice caught a nostalgic ring to them and drew in a breath.

Swallowing hard, understanding what it implied, the girl exhaled a trembling breath and said,

"Don't tell me... you're... Roswaal?"

"I've been Roswaal all along, haven't I?"

"That's not—! That's not what I mean... you must know that!"

"I'm joking. Just as you say, I am—I'm Roswaal, Beatrice."

The moment his self-reference shifted, Roswaal's form seemed to double before Beatrice's eyes.

Over the figure of the tall man with long indigo hair overlapped the figure of a younger man with the same color hair—a brilliant young man who had once adored Echidna and followed at her heels.

"If that's true... Roswaal, then what exactly are you...!?"

"The principle is an application of the Soul Transcription technique, one of the methods Teacher devised in her pursuit of immortality. Of the experiments carried out here in the Sanctuary, I adopted the one with the lowest risk and applied it to myself."

"Soul Transcription... transcribing consciousness and memory into an empty Vessel to achieve a subjective form of immortality... but that experiment was supposed to have ended incomplete, because the soul wouldn't take hold, I suppose!"

"With an empty Vessel, the retention rate for the transcribed soul was too poor, you see. It stalled out once... but I forced my way past that problem. If the affinity between the Vessel and the soul was the issue, then the problem could be overcome simply by bringing that affinity closer together."

The affinity between Vessel and soul—that was the problem that had stalled the research.

It was one result of Echidna's mad thirst for knowledge, born out of the process of making Ryuzu Meyer the core of the Sanctuary, when she wondered whether the crystallized existence of Ryuzu could be diverted to some other experiment as well.

But the replicated bodies of Ryuzu had no aptitude for accepting another soul, and as a result the experiment had failed. And Roswaal claimed he'd overcome that by bringing Vessel and soul closer together.

And chewing over the meaning of that, she finally understood, in the truest sense, what the Roswaal standing before her really was.

The first Roswaal had, all this time, kept the path to fulfilling his wish alive across the generations by transcribing his own soul into the bodies of descendants close enough in nature to accept it.

"Will you call me a monster for it? Beatrice."

"..........."

"Will you call the inhumanity of a man who strings together generations of unknowing descendants as vessels, wanting nothing but to reunite with Teacher—will you call that man a monster?"

Roswaal's words pierced Beatrice.

And yet, in the eerily calm eyes with which Roswaal looked at her, there seemed to lurk something almost like a man waiting to be condemned by her.

Did Roswaal, too, want to be judged? Just as she herself had, when she reported the dissolution of her Contract with Echidna to a Roswaal who longed for Echidna.

Did Roswaal want Beatrice, who had known Echidna, to pass judgment on the rightness of what he'd done—on the single-minded, tiresome-to-everyone-around-him obsession of an unrequited love that had lasted four hundred years?

"...It's not Betty's place to say one way or another about that. This might sound odd, but Betty never had much contact with your descendants, I suppose. Well, thinking about it now, I suppose it was all you the whole time anyway. So Betty doesn't feel any more disgust than the words themselves suggest, about you making your own descendants into your foundation stones, I suppose. Well, she does think 'ugh,' I suppose."

"'Ugh,' is it. Ha-a-arsh."

"But well, that's about the size of it, I suppose. More than that, Betty would rather simply be glad that someone she knew four hundred years ago is still alive, I suppose."

"...Is that... so."

At Beatrice's answer, Roswaal closed his eyes. Perhaps it wasn't the answer he'd been hoping for, but that was none of Beatrice's concern.

Beatrice would honestly convey Beatrice's own feelings. She'd only just resolved to do so, the moment she stepped out of the Forbidden Archives. For the time being—

"Roswaal. Crouch down there for a moment, would you."

"Crouch down? Here?"

Roswaal tilted his head at Beatrice, who pointed at the ground and gave the instruction. Following Beatrice's nod, Roswaal crouched down right where he stood, his eyes wide.

Watching Roswaal crouch, Beatrice pulled off the shoe on her right foot. Fitting it securely onto her right hand, she said,

"Grit your teeth, I suppose."

"—Guh!?"

A shoe-equipped right-handed slap drove into the side of his face, now conveniently at just the right height.

A satisfying sound rang out, and Roswaal's face was knocked sideways. Pressing a hand to his reddened cheek, Roswaal blinked in a daze.

Meanwhile, Beatrice took the shoe off her hand and put it properly back on her foot.

"Betty's generous, so she'll let you off with just that, I suppose. ...In any case, it's all just a matter of results, I suppose. Subaru seems willing to forgive you too, so Betty will forgive you as well, I suppose."

"...I think that's simply a result of the fact that no one died, nothing more."

"That's right, I suppose. And Subaru, who ran himself ragged making sure no one died, is amazing, I suppose. You could stand to take a leaf out of his book, I suppose."

"—Ha, ha ha ha! Is that so-o-o! That I ought to take after him! Ha ha! Well, well... ah, truly, isn't this just ridiculous."

Before Beatrice, who stood with her hands on her hips, Roswaal laughed as though he'd just heard the finest joke of his life. Doubling back with laughter he couldn't hold in, he knocked his head against the wall. He kept knocking the back of his head against it a few more times, then let out a deep breath—"Haa-a-ah."

"Forgive me. —Not that I believe I've done anything wrong, mind you. I'll at least say that much."

"No need, I suppose. If you're going to apologize, do it to everyone but Betty, I suppose."

At Beatrice's curt answer, Roswaal nodded and said, "Yes, that's true."

Then, still seated on the ground, he looked up at the coffin.

And then,

"Beatrice. From here on, this is a matter between just you and me again."

——

Beatrice narrowed her eyes at Roswaal, who had lowered his voice.

Crossing her arms, she jerked her chin, telling him to go ahead and speak. Having watched that gesture, Roswaal set a hand on the coffin and rose to his feet, gazing at Echidna within.

In his mismatched eyes burned a passion fierce enough to seem like madness, as he said,

"—If a true reunion with Teacher could be made possible, would you help me achieve it?"

"They're really taking their time. I get that they've got a lot to catch up on, but isn't this a bit much?"

Growing impatient with the lack of change, Subaru, waiting in the plaza, pouted.

Since then, he'd already added roughly ten more snowmen. The Sanctuary's residents and Emilia were transfixed by the array of some thirty ice statues of Puck, each with a different expression.

Incidentally, Ram, who had until just now been enviably borrowing Emilia's lap, now looked considerably recovered and was leaning her weight comfortably against the stone steps. Still, her gaze kept drifting toward the tomb again and again, so there was no doubt she was concerned about what was happening inside.

Beatrice, newly awakened, and Roswaal, in a state of uneasy calm.

He didn't think anything violent would happen, but he could understand Ram's concern well enough. After all, no one had directly confirmed from Roswaal's own mouth whether he'd truly calmed down from his self-destructive state or not.

It was just that Subaru had decided, on his own, that things would probably be fine.

"Well, that's part of why I left it to Bea-ko, though."

Beatrice's history with Roswaal ran longer than Ram's ever could. Inside the tomb was the body of a woman—Echidna's, Beatrice had told him.

If they were going to talk in front of that, it was probably better left to just the two of them, who knew those bygone days.

As for what to do with Echidna's remains, that was a topic Subaru could weigh in on later.

"Besides, it'll be easier to figure out what to do next once Garfiel and the others rejoin us anyway."

If they'd managed to escape the mansion safely, they should be heading straight for the Sanctuary. He'd asked one of the young men's brigade to carry a message and sent one of the returned dragon carriages racing to Arlam Village. At the latest, they'd probably be able to rejoin by tomorrow night.

Considering the time it would take to deal with the snow covering the Sanctuary and to clean up the damage, that much of a gap was probably for the best. It would be more than enough time to let everyone's feelings settle, too.

Having lost track of time absorbed in making snowmen, Subaru felt he'd calmed down quite a bit himself. He should be able to have a calm, level-headed talk with Roswaal too.

He could do it. He would do it. He fully intended to do it.

"Good work, Subaru... why are you swinging your arms around like that?"

"Ah, no, no reason? I'm not shadowboxing to practice landing one on that hateful jerk's face or anything! I mean, I get the feeling someone's probably already handled that for me."

"Is that so?"

With a face that clearly didn't quite follow his meaning, Emilia, coming up beside him, tilted her head.

Then, standing beside Subaru, she enjoyed the spectacular sight of the snow statues lined up in a row and said,

"You really made a whole mountain of Pucks, didn't you. If he could see this himself, I bet he'd love it."

"You think so? I can pretty easily picture him complaining, 'I think I'm a lot more pretty than this, you know.'"

"Ah, that was a pretty good impression, actually. Puck's... ah, seems he's asleep right now."

Laughing at Subaru's answer, Emilia pulled a blue crystal from inside her clothes and murmured this.

The deep blue-tinged crystal, its shape roughly worked into form, now caught the sunlight reflecting off the snow as it sat in Emilia's hand.

Sealed within that crystal was Puck, whose Contract with Emilia had been dissolved.

"But in that state, you can't summon him like before, can you?"

"Right, that's true. This crystal doesn't seem to be pure enough to seal away a Spirit as powerful as Puck. Right now, he's kept from being active at all so he won't break, but... like this, I don't think I can touch him or talk to him."

"So you need a proper stone. Like the green one you had before."

The crystal stone Emilia had worn around her neck. Shattered to pieces when Puck's Contract was dissolved, it had apparently been an extremely rare crystal.

Originally, it seemed Puck had brought it along himself when he made his Contract with Emilia, and even Emilia had no idea where such a thing could be obtained.

"But someday, I'll definitely find a stone that can match it and get Puck back. And then... I want to talk to him about a lot of things. About the things he kept from me, and about everything I was able to find out because of that. All of it."

With resolve kindled in her violet-blue eyes, Emilia lovingly stroked the surface of the crystal with a finger.

Her profile looked strikingly beautiful, and Subaru caught his breath just slightly. Noticing this, Emilia glanced up at him with a "Hm?" so Subaru rubbed his nose.

"Ah, no, it's just... Emilia-tan, you've changed a bit, huh. Like, I mean, you've always been cute, but you've gotten a little... stronger-feeling?"

"If so, that's thanks to Subaru and everyone. I've received so much from everyone. I want to be able to give things back to everyone soon."

"If we're talking about things we've received, I feel like that's pretty much all I've got too, honestly."

Both Subaru and Emilia were people who had, each in their own way, keenly felt their own powerlessness.

That didn't mean they wanted to commiserate over their wounds together, though. Sensing that in Emilia's current bearing, Subaru found it both reassuring and, in a way, a little lonely.

Just when he'd finally gained a little confidence, one small measure of strength to support her with, the very Emilia he wanted to support was running off on her own, all the way to the point of not even needing it.

Even though he kept running the whole time, he was starting to feel like he could never catch up.

"By the way, Subaru... um, you see..."

"Yeah?"

"The two of them in the tomb are taking a while, aren't they. ...Yeah, a while."

While Subaru was lost in sentiment, Emilia suddenly spoke up, her words catching in her throat. Her gaze kept flicking toward the tomb, still as unchanged as ever.

But contrary to her words, the color on Emilia's face kept changing rapidly. Seeing her profile flush crimson, even her ears—slightly longer than her people's usual—turning bright red, Subaru grew flustered.

"Wh-Emilia-tan!? Your face is turning red at some incredible speed, are you okie!?"

"I-I'm okie. I'm totally fine. More importantly, um, there's something I need to talk to you about."

"O-oh, um, sounds pretty formal."

For some reason, in response to Emilia's sudden formality, Subaru found himself answering formally too.

Emilia checked her surroundings, confirmed there was no one nearby, and then, still red in the face, stared intently at Subaru. More specifically, she was staring at his mouth.

"Um, well... Subaru, you, um, said you... l-like me, didn't you?"

"Huh, ah, yes. I said it. I like you."

"—! That's, um, really, really, really wonderful, but..."

Subaru felt an ominous drift in the trailing end of Emilia's red-faced sentence.

After all, it ended in "but." In Subaru's imagination, there was only one possible thing that could follow.

This was, without question, the "let's just be friends" flow.

"But, like I said before, I'll wait for Emilia-tan to turn her heart toward me, and I'll work hard to make that happen too."

"Th-that... that makes me happy too. But, even so, even when you say things like that, I still don't really understand, deep down, what it means to fall in love with someone."

"........."

"It was the same before, in the Dragon Carriage, and it's the same now with the tomb. Subaru tells me he likes me, and I can't say anything back to you again. I thought that was such a terribly cruel thing to do..."

Listening to her voice grow weaker, Subaru breathed a sigh of relief, one hand pressed to his chest.

In short, Emilia's answer was to keep things as they were. Unchanged from before—that was fine by him.

As long as she hadn't grown sick of his repeated, persistent confessions, he'd be fine. As long as Emilia kept wavering, Subaru could keep holding out his hand as many times as it took.

That slight crossing of wires between Subaru and Emilia over each other's feelings—it was all rendered moot by Emilia's very next word.

"But! Um, I think we really need to have a proper talk about the baby in my belly!"

——

——. ——————. ————————————.

"Par-don?"

"I don't know if it's a boy or a girl yet, but either way, we have to make sure to properly love and cherish it! But I've never learned anything at all about that kind of thing, so I don't know what to do... I figured this was something I needed to talk about with the father."

"Wai—wai, wai-wait, wait wait... h-hold on, hold on..."

Subaru's mind couldn't keep up with Emilia, who kept rattling on with a red face.

Emilia herself was speaking fast, breathing hard, clearly in a state of excitement. There was no way the two of them could have a proper conversation like this.

"Emilia-tan, first, take a deep breath, calm down a little. I'll take a deep breath and calm down a bit myself too, right now. Oh, perfect, there's some snow right here."

Crouching down, he scooped up some snow and pressed it to his face, physically cooling his head. Listening to Emilia take deep breaths, Subaru forced himself to think things through calmly.

A baby in Emilia's belly. And the mother was Emilia, the father was Subaru. It made no sense. Subaru had, without a doubt, never once climbed the stairs to adulthood.

"Emilia-tan. When you say 'baby,' you mean an actual infant, right?"

"Y-yes, that's right. I know something like this in the middle of the Royal Selection is a huge deal, but... but the baby being born hasn't done anything wrong, and I want to make sure it's properly happy! I want this child to be properly loved by the very first person who ought to love it."

Emilia's resolve was noble, and beautiful.

But something wasn't adding up. Subaru had never done that sort of thing with Emilia. So had Emilia done it with someone else? No, he didn't want to think about that either.

"Emilia-tan... babies aren't delivered by storks, and you can't pick them up from a cabbage patch, you know?"

"But if a man and a woman kiss, doesn't that make a baby?"

——

He was rendered speechless.

Speechless at Emilia's utter lack of knowledge about sex, and speechless at how adorable it was that she'd gotten it so wrong.

"Subaru? What's wrong? Come on, Subaru."

With a face that understood nothing at all, Emilia called the name of the now-silent Subaru.

Somewhere in that face, there seemed to be a new strength born of her self-awareness as a mother. Perhaps that was exactly why Emilia had struck him as a bit stronger lately. If that was the case, was it really a good idea to correct this misunderstanding?

No. Absolutely not. This was no time to be entertaining that. If things went on like this, the story would proceed with Emilia in a state of phantom pregnancy. Day by day, a motherly Emilia talking to a belly swelling only in her imagination. That would be cute in its own way, but letting it stand as it was would be a real problem.

"Subaru, are you maybe regretting that we kissed...?"

"Not even a little, and I'd want to do it as many times as possible!?"

"I-I see..."

As the misunderstanding deepened even further, Subaru regretted his reflexive answer.

As far as Emilia's understanding went, what he'd just said was equivalent to declaring he wanted to make babies as many times as possible. He did feel that way, but that was surely a matter for much further down the road, after a few more steps.

So now, as the very first of those steps, he had to give Emilia the correct facts.

But just what kind of situation was it, exactly, that made it Subaru's job to do that?

"Y-you're gonna pay for this, Puck...!"

Subaru muttered his resentment at the cat Spirit who wasn't here, who even now lay deep in slumber inside the crystal stone.

In the back of his mind, he thought he could see the little cat put a paw to his head and stick out his tongue with an impish "oopsie."

—It was in the middle of Emilia pestering him to decide on a name for the child that Subaru, at the end of all his inner turmoil, finally realized he could just leave the detailed explanation to Ram or Frederica.
