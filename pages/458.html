Arc 6 Chapter 53: "—A Voice, Sounded"

The incoming tail spikes shattered the stone corridor, smoke and dust billowed up, and destruction spread unchecked. Amid a scene that felt like it was unfolding in slow motion, Subaru watched a small girl in a dress leap into the very center of the terrible whirlwind of destruction unfolding before his eyes.

Her ringlet curls streaming behind her, the one diving straight into the destruction was Beatrice. Courage—no, reckless bravado. Of course, a small girl's body was fragile, far less durable than the stone tower being smashed to pieces by a single blow from the tail spike. Even if she meant to throw herself in the way as an impromptu shield, her height meant she couldn't possibly block the line of fire from Subaru's chest upward. In other words, it was nothing more than an impulsive act that could only end in a pointless death.

Just like when Subaru, bawling, his face smeared with snot, had grabbed Echidna's sleeve. Just like when he'd called out Julius's name, who would have burned in the karmic flames if he'd done nothing. Just like when he'd tried to shove Ram against the wall and couldn't bring himself to follow the phantom girl's warning.

Beatrice, too, disregarding the "impossible," moved to catch the tail spike head-on——

"Wha—!?" "—It's an 'Incomplete E·M·T,' I suppose!!"

In that instant, Beatrice stepped forward, seized Subaru's hand, and thrust her free hand out in front of her. As the girl cried out, something—some invisible, energy-like thing—was wrenched out of Subaru's body all at once. It drained away, and there, before the eyes of a suddenly light-headed Subaru, in front of Beatrice's outstretched palm, the tail spike's blow was blocked as though a wall of light had unfurled.

"——"

The shockwave, which should have shattered them just as it had the building, was blocked by the defensive wall centered on Beatrice's palm, and the damage spread outward around them, avoiding Subaru and the others entirely. The tail spikes didn't stop at one; they came raining down one after another. But every single one was blocked by the shield that took the form of a young girl named Beatrice, and none of the damage got through to those behind her.

A tremendous shockwave raged, and shards of the shattered corridor grazed Subaru's cheek. Sound grew distant, and his vision seemed to haze over in white, and even as he narrowed his eyes against the oddly sharp sting on his cheek, Subaru found his gaze locked on Beatrice's back.

The giant scorpion he'd glimpsed for only an instant—if this rain-storm bombardment really was the scorpion's tail spikes, then their destructive power was equivalent to mortar fire capable of reducing a human body to dust with ease. And from that, Subaru's life was being spared, shielded by the back of a girl so small.

Just moments before, he had begged the gods for salvation, and with that feeling still fresh, he wondered just what otherworldly hell he had been thrown into. Subaru had no room to spare for cursing his own misfortune, yet at the edge of his awareness, at the corner of his vision, something snagged his attention—something that was neither Beatrice nor the scorpion.

It was Echidna, caught up in this attack and exposed to the same danger. She had once declared she would act alone and tried to part ways with Subaru and the others, but she'd been held back by Subaru just before the attack began, and so found herself dragged into this situation. Fortunately, she too happened to be positioned somewhere Beatrice's shield could cover, so the tail spikes' damage was blocked by the wall of light before it could reach her. But that was as far as it went.

What Beatrice could block was only the direct damage of being struck by the tail spikes. The secondary damage caused by the destruction those spikes wrought was another matter entirely—there was no way to guard against that. In short——

"—Ah, ah!"

A faint cry escaped her as Echidna lost her balance and toppled backward. Her foot stepped back to catch herself—but there was no floor there to receive that foot. Shattered by the tail spike assault, the corridor had half collapsed. As a result, the tower's passage had lost its shape, the floor, ceiling, and walls crumbling away, and Echidna had lost her footing. If she were flung out into some unknown open air like this, a fatal fall was unavoidable. Instinctively sensing that terror, a flash of alarm crossed Echidna's eyes.

"—!"

Subaru lunged forward on reflex and grabbed Echidna's hand, stopping her fatal fall.

"Natsuki-kun...!?"

His right hand was the one Beatrice held; his left was the one that had caught Echidna. Reid's forceful treatment had popped his shoulder back into place, but the pain still ran deep, and overexerting it risked dislocating it again. Unable to burden Beatrice, who was desperately holding off the tail spikes, Subaru gritted his teeth, planted both feet, and had no choice but to pour everything he had into holding back Echidna's weight.

"Ngh, gaaah...!"

Small as she was, Echidna still weighed a full grown person's worth. Lifting a single person was never an easy thing to begin with. Much less with one hand, much less while injured, much less in this emergency with an uncooperative partner—the danger of being dragged down and falling with her was far from negligible. He should have known that without even thinking about it. So why had he taken such a risk?

"...Please don't do things that make it impossible for me to figure you out."

"L-like I know...! It was reflex...!"

"That answer... sounds like it could apply to you too, Natsuki-kun!"

Even as he repeated an endless cycle of self-questioning, Subaru shot back against Echidna's spiteful retort, saved on pure reflex. Awkwardly, she twisted her body, and using Subaru's arm for support, managed to scramble back to the collapsed but still-standing section of floor. Having somehow supported her all the way through, Subaru dropped his gaze to his own arm.

—Letters carved into it as wounds, black mottled patterns covering it without his knowing, visibly painful and gruesome white scars left all over his body.

"It moves... better than the limits of my memory would suggest... huh?"

Unfamiliar calluses on his hands, countless scrapes. A build that seemed to have grown just the slightest bit sturdier had barely managed to keep Echidna from falling to her death. Even so, it was a body far too incomplete to carry out a Book of the Dead scheme that couldn't kill even a single person.

"Somehow, I've clawed my way up...! Beatrice! How are you holding up!?"

"About at my limit, I suppose! Any moment now..."

Setting aside his misplaced musings, Echidna, having clambered up, called out to Beatrice with a grim expression. In answer to that call, Beatrice's lovely cheeks tensed—and in the next instant——

"—!!"

The scorpion that should have still been firing off tail spikes was now clinging to the ceiling, and letting out a soundless cry, it swung its massive pincer down at them.

"—"

Facing the pincer sweeping toward him, Subaru's thoughts slowed to a crawl. Common perception held that scorpions were strongly associated with venomous stingers, but in reality, of the more than a thousand species of scorpion, only a few dozen actually carried venom. If that was so, then what weapon did scorpions that hunted without a venomous stinger rely on? —Naturally, that pincer became the weapon.

Sharpness, or perhaps one should call it crushing force. Compared to a venomous stinger it might look less dangerous, but on a scorpion this enormous, neither weapon's killing power was to be underestimated. That much was obvious from the sight of the tower's walls and floor—which had barely retained their original shape—being sliced apart as easily as a knife through tofu.

"—Muraku."

Against that scene, a very symbol of destruction and slaughter, Beatrice, having flown back to them, evaded it with unbelievable leaping power—no, with an unnatural mode of flight that seemed to exploit the law of inertia itself. Centered on Beatrice, there was a strange sensation, as if gravity's pull had been evaded entirely. Beatrice grabbed Subaru and Echidna by the waists of their clothes and put a great distance between them and the scorpion's onslaught. With the distance thus created, Echidna leveled the five fingers of her right hand at the scorpion's face.

"El Jiwald——!!"

White heat-rays shot from each of her five fingers, five beams of light raking the corridor like a giant's claws, scorching the scorpion's face where it clung to the ceiling, its right pincer, and its carapace. Unable to withstand the force and the damage, the scorpion thrashed its tail spike and left pincer wildly, shattering the corridor and scattering smoke and dust as it retreated backward.

"Jiwald! Jiwald! Jiwaaald!"

"Wait! Calm down, Echidna! It ran! It ran away! It's gone, so—!"

Subaru wrapped Echidna up from behind in a bear hug as she continued her assault toward the smoke into which the scorpion had withdrawn. Even restrained, Echidna kept struggling for a while, glaring desperately into the depths of the smoke, but eventually the strength drained from her body, and she let her weight sag against Subaru.

"Hah, hah, hah... d-did I... get it...?"

"...No, you didn't. It probably got away."

Subaru shook his head at Echidna, who was out of breath and looking like she couldn't believe it. The smoke still hadn't cleared, but it didn't seem likely that the giant scorpion's corpse was lying somewhere in it. If anything, he was on edge, dreading that a counterattack of tail spikes might come flying out of the smoke at any moment.

"But that's not gonna happen... is it? That thing just now..."

"—It's a Witchbeast, I suppose. Some tactless intruder that suddenly showed up on this fourth level. The lower level's tremors, the Witchbeast on the fourth level. And on top of that, the disturbance on the upper level."

"Up, down, in between... you're saying something's happening everywhere?"

At Beatrice's grave report, Subaru turned back, his face changing color. Certainly, even now, Julius was fighting on the lower level, and on the fourth level, the existence of that scorpion—the Witchbeast—had just been made clear. And the disturbance on the upper level made sense too, given that Reid had come down from there.

"...You're one of those problems, but you don't seem to realize it."

"—"

Shattering Subaru's train of thought at its root was Echidna, who had pulled away from his chest to stand on her own. She wiped the sweat beading on her forehead, and with a wariness that wouldn't wipe away so easily, she looked at Subaru. In her pale blue-green eyes were wariness, suspicion, and at the same time anxiety and confusion.

"What are you... what on earth are you? What do you want, where do you stand?"

"I don't know the details. I told you already. I don't know what's happening or what's going on any more than you do. Because I'm..."

"—Amnesiac."

To Echidna's question, Subaru could offer only the same bewilderment as before, murmuring weakly. It was Beatrice, who had been mulling it over with a difficult expression, who finished the thought for him. Amnesia—in this loop, the only two people Subaru had confided that to were Emilia and Ram. Which meant Beatrice and the others already knew about the cage, and about Subaru's excuse.

Knowing that, and yet Beatrice's attitude had been like that. And Echidna, too, was mulling over what words to spend in search of understanding.

"How did you get out of the cage?"

"...By the time I noticed, my shoulder was dislocated and I was lying outside the cage."

He'd answered with complete honesty, but he didn't think she'd believe it. Still, piling lie upon lie was exactly what had driven him into this desperate corner in the first place. Having begged the gods with snot running down his face, come what may, he had no pride or appearances left worth putting on now. With that mindset, Subaru answered without a shred of deception, and Echidna hesitated a moment over her words. But then, almost immediately, she closed her eyes, and without looking at Subaru, went on.

"...Why did you save me, just now?"

"—"

"If you hadn't reached out your hand, I would definitely have died. Along with Ana's body—a wretched, regret-filled death, no doubt."

It was a question asked as if it concerned someone else, with a distance that suggested she'd taken a step back. But her doing so deliberately was for the same reason she wasn't looking at Subaru's face, his eyes: it was a painful expedient, so as not to let sentiment cloud her judgment. Facing that judgment of Echidna's head-on, Subaru thought it over. Why had he reached out his hand to Echidna? —Was it because Julius had entrusted her to him at the end?

Had those words been what made Subaru reach out his hand?

"...It was reflex. I don't know."

But Subaru shook his head, reluctant, denying that thought. Certainly, there had been a moment when he'd remembered Julius's words and moved to stop Echidna from going her own separate way. But when she'd truly been exposed to mortal danger, the reason his body had moved wasn't something he'd thought through with his head. He didn't know. Why he'd moved like that.

"Not everything has a reason, does it? It just happened. All of it just happened. So I..."

Braced for her disappointment, Subaru haltingly poured out the feelings inside him. Hearing this, he expected a cold reaction from Echidna, surely——

"—. That might be the very essence of who you are, as a person."

"Eh...?"

Suddenly, Echidna, who had been tensed up before him, let the strength drop from her shoulders and murmured that. Hearing it, Subaru's eyes went wide, and he stared, dumbfounded, wondering what was going on. At Subaru's stunned gaze, Echidna shrugged, and said:

"There's no point arguing about this here. And it'd be foolish to linger and risk another attack from the Witchbeast. Let's move. I want to link up with Julius."

"Ah, uh..."

"Agreed, I suppose. Let's leave this place, for now."

Leaving Subaru, still stunned, behind, Echidna and Beatrice matter-of-factly decided their course. Echidna checked behind them, and Beatrice gave Subaru's hand a firm squeeze. Feeling that small pressure, he looked toward the girl, and Beatrice gave a deep nod.

"You don't remember getting Betty out of there either, I suppose?"

"...S-sorry. I don't... know what you're talking about."

"—It's fine, I suppose."

Hearing how terribly fleeting and lonely a feeling was mixed into Beatrice's voice, Subaru felt as though he'd committed the most terrible sin in the world. But to Subaru, exposed to an unfathomable dread, Beatrice shook her head. And hiding her own lonely feelings away, she carved a fearless smile onto her lips.

"Even if Subaru's forgotten, it's still here inside Betty. What Subaru carved into Betty won't fade away in here, I suppose. So it's fine, for now."

"Beatrice..."

"Even if Subaru forgets, Betty won't. Betty will remember, always. And Betty will make Subaru remember too, I suppose. Whatever it takes to do that, Betty will do it, without fail."

"—"

That answer was far, far too dazzling for Natsuki Subaru, standing alone in that place. Just how much hardship must one overcome, how thoroughly must one forge one's heart into steel, for a girl this young to possess a will this strong?

"—Ah."

At the sensation of being saved, Subaru held his breath. Unbidden, something welling up behind his eyelids seemed bent on killing him, and he fought desperately to keep that heat from overflowing. Beatrice said nothing about Subaru's struggle, only supported him by holding his hand. Just by holding his hand, she became his support.

"After that Witchbeast's attack, we probably can't retrace the path you came from, Natsuki-kun. The danger's undeniable, but we have no choice but to move through the intact corridor, the way the Witchbeast fled."

"Echidna, you too..."

"Forgive, or not forgive. Doubt, or not doubt. Talk like that doesn't suit the moment. Either way, the suspicion inside me isn't going to clear up. It's simply that the situation won't allow for it. Setting priorities is an essential skill in business. I watched Ana do just that, right at her side."

So, there would be no more back-and-forth about it here. That must have been Echidna's conclusion. Subaru, too, decided to avoid throwing cold water on the judgment Echidna had reached by compromising with her own sense of rejection, and to follow the two girls' decision.

Even now, his urge to run away hadn't changed one bit. But he wanted to believe that a scene worth pleading for—better than crouching alone, clutching his head, and offering prayers to god—was waiting for him.

"—"

Behind them, the corridor Subaru had come running down was in a terrible state. Just as Echidna had said, it had taken a storm of tail-spike attacks and was now little more than a mountain of rubble, impossible to pass through. Beatrice's magical defense had only protected herself, Subaru, and the others' lives. Whether heading toward Julius, still holding out on the fifth level, or toward Emilia and the others acting separately, they would have to advance through the corridor on the side the scorpion had retreated to.

"Ugh..."

Beyond the hole where Echidna had nearly fallen to her death, once the smoke from the corridor scorched by her white heat-ray cleared, there lay the thing that made Subaru groan without meaning to.

—It was the Witchbeast's tail, apparently severed almost right at the root.

"I see, so that's why there was no counterattack. Stripping it of one means of attack is a small piece of good news, I suppose."

"Seems so, I suppose. ...Don't fall through the floor. Let's jump across, I suppose."

Looking at the tail that had fallen along with its stinger, Echidna and Beatrice nodded to each other. Overwhelmed by the grotesqueness of the sight, Subaru nonetheless agreed—this had indeed cost the Witchbeast its means of ranged attack. Of course, the floor below was still overrun with countless flame-wreathed centaurs. Reducing the threat of a single Witchbeast was nowhere near enough to say the danger level of the tower as a whole had dropped.

"—Whoa."

Building up a bit of momentum, Subaru leapt over the hole while carrying Beatrice. Her body was light—too light, even compared to other girls her age. It was a lightness that seemed to have nothing to do with the results of that unremembered year Subaru had just felt in his own body.

"—"

Keeping that at the edge of his awareness, Subaru turned his eyes once more to the scorpion's tail lying on the floor beyond the gap he'd cleared.

The tail that had fired off tail spikes like a storm operated on a mechanism that existed on an entirely different level from any scorpion biology Subaru knew. Obviously, the venomous stinger of any scorpion he knew of was never actually launched, nor did it presumably regrow over and over. More than anything, judging from the state of the corridor's destruction, what had been fired wasn't actually a physical, material spike at all. —What had been unleashed was a force shaped like a spike.

In short, just like Beatrice's wall of light or Echidna's white heat-ray, what the scorpion had unleashed could just as well be called something like a magical needle. Of course, since it had been treated as a tail spike, one would want to think that, having lost its tail, it could no longer be triggered—but——

"—?"

Having thought that far, Subaru felt something was off. He'd noticed it while studying the fallen tail closely. That fallen tail had been severed at what appeared to be almost the very root, but the wound looked strange. If it had been burned off by Echidna's heat-ray, then regardless of how cleanly, the wound should have shown scorch marks. Indeed, the area around the corridor bore corresponding burn marks. But the scorpion's fallen tail showed no such traces—its cross-section was strangely, unnervingly clean——

"—Bea—"

Subaru realized something was wrong. Too late. At the edge of his vision, the tail lying on the floor gave a sudden convulsive twitch.

—An instant later, light burst forth.

—There is a mechanism called autotomy.

It's a phenomenon seen mainly in arthropods and lizards—what's sometimes called a "lizard dropping its tail"—the act of an animal deliberately severing part of its own body in order to escape a predator. It's also seen in things like a crab's claws, and in short, it was likely that the scorpion-shaped Witchbeast had done something similar.

When a lizard sheds its tail, the severed tail keeps moving around on its own for a while, drawing the enemy's attention and letting the body escape. Of course, the tail itself has no will of its own—it's purely a reflexive action—but it's a real-world example of how such a reflex can be built into the act of autotomy. If so, then couldn't something like this be possible too?

—Giving the severed part the role of a kind of "landmine," one that detonates and deals damage the instant prey draws near the severed tail.

"Ngh, guh, uhh...!"

He groaned. Letting a groan spill from his throat, Subaru dragged his leg along, scraping it across the ground. The dragging wasn't from exhaustion. It was a physical problem. His left leg had a large laceration, bleeding from a wound torn to shreds, so this dragging gait was the only way he could move at all besides running. And it wasn't only his own body that Subaru was dragging.

"...That's enough now. Leave me. Go."

So she said, the one being dragged along by the limping Subaru—limp, all the strength gone out of her—was Echidna. Subaru had hooked his arms under her armpits, pulling her from behind, doing anything to get away from the site. If they didn't get away from the blast site, that Witchbeast would come back.

—The scorpion Witchbeast that had left behind a parting gift of an exploding bomb and torn Subaru and the others to shreds.

"—Damn it, damn it, damn it, damn it!"

He'd let his guard down. He'd let it down completely. His head had been soaking in lukewarm water, utterly complacent. There'd been the words Beatrice had spoken to him just before, there'd been Echidna's attitude softening somewhat—and so a gap had opened up in his heart, and he'd fallen right into it, ending up like this.

It was pathetic, so pathetic, he was so utterly pathetic to his very core that tears came.

Why, faced with hardship after hardship like this, did he still fail to grow? Fail to change? Weren't suffering, trials, hard times supposed to be opportunities the gods provided for growth? If all suffering ever brought was being beaten down without end, bleeding, having bones broken, having one's soul shattered and one's life stolen away—if that was all hardship ever amounted to—then just what was the point of a person suffering at all?

"Natsuki... kun... that's... enough, now..."

"It's not enough! Not one single thing is enough!"

"...More than me... it's Beatrice, isn't it?"

Echidna, eyes closed, murmured haltingly. Subaru had shot back at her words almost shouting, but at the words that followed, his breath caught. Echidna's words were sad, but they were correct. If asked which mattered more to him, Beatrice or Echidna, sadly, Subaru would choose Beatrice. The value of a life might be equal, but the value of a person to oneself is not. Relationships have an order of priority. In a moment that demanded a choice, he would follow that order.

—But Beatrice wasn't there. She hadn't been there. She was gone.

The instant the tail left behind by the scorpion Witchbeast exploded, Subaru had felt a chill of dread and pulled Beatrice toward him. If only he could have shielded her with his whole body just like that. But, sadly, his movements had been too slow, and Subaru's wish had gone unfulfilled.

The light had burst, scattering countless needles all around from the Witchbeast's tail. Subaru, too, had taken that blast at close range, but the fact that he'd survived, even gravely wounded, was thanks to Beatrice. The instant Subaru had pulled her close, she had tried to shield his torso, his vital points. And as a result, she had taken the concentrated fury of that light head-on——

"—"

"...I see. That girl really is... someone who always draws the short straw."

What came after the words Subaru couldn't finish seemed to be something even Echidna, eyes closed, could guess. Subaru couldn't bring himself to deny that sigh-like voice of hers. Bleeding, groaning in pain, Subaru hadn't even been able to exchange final words with the girl as she melted away and vanished. —Only her final expression remained in his memory.

Eyes that seemed relieved, that seemed to cherish Subaru. Was that—an expression far too convenient for Subaru's own comfort—really Beatrice's final look?

If so, then a "Natsuki Subaru" who could make a vanishing girl wear a face like that should just disappear from this world without leaving so much as a trace.

And so, by process of elimination now that Beatrice was gone, Subaru was dragging the still-breathing Echidna along in flight. As if in atonement, as if in penance, or as if a criminal were seeking out his own punishment.

With what little breath she had left, Echidna tried to stop Subaru from doing this. That it was pointless—she, who had been so fiercely determined to return her body to Anastasia. And that, too, made perfect sense. —Her body had had both legs blown off at the root.

"—"

By now, there was almost no blood left flowing. Dragging a body even lighter than the one he'd thought so light in Beatrice, with almost no first aid administered—what future could possibly be waiting for her?

"Ugh, ah!"

The instant that thought reared its head, Subaru's foot caught on a piece of rubble and he fell. At once, Echidna, being dragged along, was thrown out onto the corridor floor and flipped over. For a while, only the two of them's groans, groans that seemed to curse the world, echoed through the corridor.

"It hurts... ah, it really hurts. A human body really does... hurt..."

"S-sorry... sorry, sorry... no, wait, that's not it, I..."

"Don't go apologizing so dutifully, Natsuki-kun. Besides... I no longer have any face left to show Ana... but this pain is the one and only thing I can repay her with."

"Re...pay her...?"

Crawling over to Echidna, who lay collapsed on the ground, unable to move, Subaru's eyes wavered in incomprehension at her words. What in the world did she mean by repaying her? At Subaru's questioning look, Echidna loosened the corner of her mouth and said, "Well, isn't it obvious?"

"If I return her body to Ana now... Ana would experience pain that feels like the end of the world, and the terror of death. ...This is hell. It's fine if I'm the one who experiences it."

"Ah, uh..."

"I couldn't return her body for Ana's sake, I couldn't help Julius. ...Falling into hell like this suits me just fine."

It was plain that self-mockery and self-recrimination were burning fiercely through Echidna's heart. Subaru could tell, from the lifeless eyes with which she spoke, that her "death"—slowly drawing nearer—had already begun its countdown.

Unable to do anything, powerless, irresponsible, unable to help herself in any way. Regretting that, Echidna was dying. —Leaving Subaru behind, dying.

"W-wait...!"

"Don't... think about putting me out of my misery, okay? I'm... yeah, this is fine..."

Fearing Echidna's fading life, Subaru had raised his voice, only to be beaten to the punch. And yet, to Subaru who had been able to do nothing in the moment, her words offered a new option.

—Putting her out of her misery. It was almost too late for anything like mercy killing by now, but even so, Subaru could still help shorten her suffering, hasten her "death."

"—"

Glancing sideways at Echidna, who kept repeating shallow, low breaths, Subaru forced his aching body up, picked up a chunk of debris from the collapsed corridor, and gauged its weight. It was about the size that fit in a palm, at most fist-sized. But to take the life of a girl who was practically already dying, even a makeshift weapon like this should be more than enough.

"...Echidna."

"—"

Still holding the rubble, Subaru walked to Echidna's side and called her name. There was no reply, and she didn't open her eyes. But he could tell she wasn't unconscious from the way her cheeks tensed faintly, her lips pressed together.

She probably had no strength left even to stop him. If Subaru raised the rubble and brought it down hard on her head, her life would be crushed out easily enough.

"I've never split a person's skull open with a rock either, you know."

So rang a girl's sweet voice inside the mind of Subaru, rubble in hand. But this act wasn't for the sake of reading the Book of the Dead. Even if that ended up being the result, in this moment, that thought had no place in Subaru's head. This was a mercy stroke. A mercy stroke, to send someone at death's door on their way with as little suffering as possible.

If anyone had the right to take a life, surely it was only this moment, honest and true——. That was the sole opportunity for atonement available to Subaru, who happened to be there, to a Natsuki Subaru who had been able to do nothing. The one and only chance—and yet——

"—"

His hand trembled. Something behind his eyelids ached, and his throat froze, forgetting how to breathe. Raise it, bring it down. An utterly simple motion. And Subaru, right now, couldn't do it. As though he'd forgotten how to move his own body, his body wouldn't move.

"...Ah."

A rasping breath escaped him, and with a clatter, the rubble dropped to the corridor floor. Giving way to that sound and to knees that had lost their strength, Subaru crumpled to the ground right there.

"...Something so..."

Can't you even do something this simple, Natsuki Subaru? You can't even manage the pretense of swinging a weapon to ease the suffering of someone dying in agony. Atonement in words only, guilt that's nothing but a convenient excuse—if it's not that, then what is this?

"...Natsuki-kun."

"I... I'm..."

"To deliver the killing stroke... you couldn't even... pick up a rock..."

Opening her eyes a sliver, faint pale blue-green eyes found the kneeling Subaru and murmured that. That weak, breath-like voice seemed to be condemning Subaru's own weakness, and his breath caught. And yet, to Subaru shrinking back at that, Echidna, out of place, loosened her lips, and said:

"...Sorry, for doubting you."

"—"

"—"

He'd been apologized to, as if letting out a breath. Apologized to, by Echidna. Apologized to, for having doubted Natsuki Subaru.

—And before he could even confirm her true meaning, she died.

To Subaru—who had killed Meili, hidden her body, played along without admitting he'd lost his memory, piled up suspicion after suspicion, escaped the icy cage he'd been trapped in, failed to fulfill the wish entrusted to him, been shielded by a girl who'd tried to save his heart, and in the end couldn't even bloody his own hands for the sake of a dying girl, kneeling now before his own pathetic self—Echidna apologized, and died.

"—"

He wanted to die. He wanted to forget everything that had just happened, forget it all, and simply die.

He wanted every person in the world to point a finger at him and pronounce his death sentence—that Natsuki Subaru deserved to die. That he had committed a sin worthy of it. Natsuki Subaru despaired of himself.

He despaired.

Despair was eating away at Natsuki Subaru's heart.

"—"

He couldn't move. He had no right to move. Even if he moved, nothing good would come of it. He'd proven that. Natsuki Subaru had proven his own incompetence—no, incompetence alone would still have been almost endearing. Not that—he'd proven himself something worse than that: a walking curse upon everyone around him.

"—"

Once despair nests in one's chest, once it kills the heart, the soul is eaten away before life itself is. Once that happens, standing up becomes impossible. Resisting anyone becomes impossible.
