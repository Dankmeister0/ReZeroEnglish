Scorpion Tale Prologue: "The Days Spent Waiting for You"

Sparkling, sparkling, twinkling.
Sparkling, sparkling, shining.
Sparkling, sparkling, dazzling.

Sparkling, sparkling, sparkling — everything vivid, everything aglitter.

"—Mmnn~, no wake-up-call voice from Master today either, huh."

Sitting cross-legged on the hard floor, she stretched her body out with a long nnngh and let the complaint slip out along with a yawn.

The voice of the beloved she never stopped longing for — she was always hoping that if she could just hear it first thing upon waking, that would be the absolute best. But sadly, sadly, that wish went on being betrayed, every single time.

Every day, every morning, the same letdown repeated at every waking — no originality to it at all, but nothing to be done.

"Me, not getting my hopes up about Master? That'd be the impossible thing. Impossible, impossible, super impossible."

Next time. Surely next time. Or maybe on an anniversary, or on some nice round milestone number of times she'd gotten her hopes up, there'd be a big payoff to make up for all the dragging-out he'd done. —Such dreams and expectations of a surprise only swelled larger the more her love for 'him' piled up.

And so—

"Today, same as every day, Master really is just the meanest."

She said it sulkily and hopped nimbly to her feet.

"And-a one, two, three, four"—swaying and stretching that supple body was a peerless beauty, slender limbs paired with a knockout figure. A two-tone bikini-and-mantle getup with a color scheme matched to that beloved person's image colors, long brown hair bound with a great ribbon into a plump, curling scorpion tail — truly a great blossom flowering in the desert.

"The bewitching body that exists for Master's sake — grand super-startup, this morning too."

She jabbed a finger sharply out in front of her and declared it to no one in particular.

A declaration of war with no one to hear it, but as it echoed through the dry stone chamber, she made herself hear it.

Pointing out and confirming the facts and the goals — that was important. Saying what you were thinking out loud worked wonders too. Whoever was it that had told her that with such confidence?

"Well, it definitely wasn't Master, that's for sure. There's no way I'd forget anything Master said to me."

And since it wasn't him, it didn't matter in the slightest who had said it.

"But I do think it's golden advice, so by that reading it doesn't count against me. If anything, since I haven't forgotten what he actually said, the pluses might even win out. Yaaay! My victory! Nobody's allowed to complain!"

She tapped out a cheerful little dance on the spot, and started humming along for good measure.

Her hopes had been dashed as usual on waking and she'd gone seriously blue, but the self-encouragement afterward had worked splendidly and her mood was excellent. Today was going to be a good day— And immediately after, her stomach growled.

"Hrmm, hrmm," she muttered, rubbing the bare midriff with its exposed navel, and cocked her head.

"Come to think of it, when was the last time I ate anything…?"

A constitution that didn't much care about meals, combined with a personality that didn't much care about anything, meant her memories around that were pretty vague — vague and hazy, all hazy-wazy.

Left to her own devices, she could go days, weeks, even months without eating a thing and be perfectly fine. That had once been appraised as an advantage for survival, a merit, but—

"Master hated that, y'know. Said it was rough on him to watch me be the only one not eating while everyone else was having a meal… Guuuh, Master's thoughtfulness really soaks right in!"

So, to be able to join in at mealtimes herself as much as possible, she'd made a point of behaving in deliberately energy-inefficient ways. Squandering the energy-saving performance that would have let her go years just by holding still, bustling around as much as she could — that way, eating every meal stopped being a chore.

And 'he' had been ever so pleased about it, so she was really, truly glad she'd done it.

"Kaka-sama grumbled something about my precious trait going to waste, though."

At any rate, her body seemed to have gotten thoroughly used to the arrangement. Wherever that energy-saving constitution had gone, before she knew it she was getting hungry, hungry, and sleepy. —Honestly, unlike back when she'd been with 'him,' now that she was all alone it was more often just a bother.

But when 'he' kept his promise, she didn't want to have turned back into the sort of person who watched meals like they were somebody else's business. She didn't want to disappoint 'him.'

"After all, it's Master's pet theory that a girl who eats like she's really enjoying it is attractive…! Ah, Master, no! Don't look at me like that when my mouth's stuffed full of meat and beans…"

Pressing both hands to her flushed cheeks and swaying her hips, she conjured up a vision of 'him' in her head, smiling breezily at her as she crammed her mouth full with gusto.

At present it was nothing but a fantasy, but image training was indispensable if she was going to meet the coming day for real.

And so, to keep waiting for that day to arrive, today — just like every day — she had to keep her body good and hungry, burn energy in a flashy, busy, restless fashion, and only then sit down to a meal.

For the time being, to that end—

"—Guess I'll plug away at it like always and get my daily routine out of the way!"

—The accumulation of your days makes you who you are, and that becomes the real image of you reflected in other people's eyes.

Now who was it that had said something so fiddly and difficult? That, too, she simply couldn't recall. Well, it wasn't 'him,' so the usual conclusion applied: no need to remember, no problem.

"Sounds like hair-splitting, so maybe Kaka-sama…? Then again, Kaka-sama's face and voice are all fuzzed out in my head. Like… white? Black? Maybe the color's wrong too."

In exchange for receiving healthy sex appeal, outstanding energy efficiency, and a bewitching, dynamite body that could bowl over even that mean 'him,' the contents of her head were pretty shabby. That was just who she was. Ninety-nine percent of her meager memory capacity was cranked all the way over to her love for 'him,' so everything else was a blur.

"Whoop, whoop, if this wall's about had it, then this room gets sealed up too, huh~."

As she spoke, she surveyed the four sides of the room she'd been frequenting so long for her 'daily routine,' and once she'd finished checking over the 'daily routine' carved into the stone walls, she made the call to close the room off.

Fortunately, there were still plenty of empty rooms. Rendering one or two rooms unusable at this rate caused no inconvenience whatsoever. For starters, she'd been saddled with a management job she was ill-suited for in the first place, so this much abuse of authority ought to be overlooked as a matter of course.

"So then, by the master authority granted to me, this door is hereby close-the-door. My memories sealed away in here are don't-touch-me to any and all eyes."

She shut the stone door with a rumbling gogogo and clapped her hands together, a job well done. She'd need to pick out another room for tomorrow's 'daily routine' and onward, but that could wait until tomorrow.

What can be done tomorrow, you can do tomorrow. She'd forgotten whose words those were too, but they were good words.

"Right then, guess it's about time to go 'hunting'…"

She said it with deliberately heavy implication and grinned, then immediately fell to deep consideration — "Maybe 'the hunt' would've sounded better?" — as her feet carried her toward the hunting ground: the balcony.

Long legs striding down the passage, she ducked through a doorless entrance and steeped herself in the open air, where an azure sky, clear to an infuriating degree, came out to greet her like an embrace.

"—"

Right, left, and straight ahead — everywhere she looked, an unbroken expanse of blue.

Exposing her porcelain skin to the pouring sunlight, her bound hair tossed by the rushing wind, she headed for the railing at the balcony's edge, about waist-high. Slip over that railing carelessly and it was several hundred meters to the ground, a no-safety-line, no-take-backs express course. —And onto that railing she hopped, light as anything.

"Mmm~, wind's a bit strong today, huh~."

The railing she'd stepped onto so casually had been her fixed perch for many long years, the stonework worn down to the shape of her feet so that it fit them perfectly and held her fast. Built so that no matter how strong the wind blew, it couldn't carry off this dynamite body.

"Not that I'm the kind of frail girl who'd get blown away to begin with. For a perfect woman like me, raised under Master's tutelage, it's through fire and water~. Ah, but still — a dry wind howling all alone out here, that one does sink into these old bones a little…"

Role-playing the tough-but-just-a-little-lonesome hard-boiled type, she used the wind-tossed tips of her scorpion tail to check wind strength and pressure, temperature and humidity, and assorted other things all at once. Including the turbidity of the drifting miasma, today was prime sniping weather—

"Forget a hundred hits from a hundred shots — maybe I could pull off a thousand hits from a hundred? Scary, so scary! The sheer potential welling up in me! Save me, Master!"

Hugging her own body tight, she thought of 'him' and stored up the surging heat inside her.

Waiting eagerly for the day she could hurl all that heat at 'him' upon his return, letting her affection seethe and bubble like the planet's mantle — that was her everyday routine.

Yes, that was the constant of her unchanging days: not days out of the ordinary, but ordinary days. —This day, too, should have been one of them.

"Hnn?"

The world she'd been surveying all through her one-sided chatter, with no one to chime in and no one to retort — her eyes narrowed as they took in the blue filling the upper half of her field of vision and the dead-leaf color filling the lower half.

As she stared, those pupils with their distinctive pattern floating in them brought the cause of the anomaly into focus.

What she saw was a sea of dead-leaf-colored sand, and cleaving through the billowing dust, a single dragon carriage running for all it was worth. Drawn by a green earth dragon, it had burst out of the sand wind, set foot on 'this side' of the dunes, and was tearing furiously across the sands.

"Ooh, oooooh! That's rare! Been a while! No idea how long it's been since anyone broke through the sand wind! Who in the world has come to call?!"

She shaded her eyes with a hand and let out a cheer, expectation tickled awake despite herself by the scene below.

There was no end to the reckless challengers who took on the sea of sand, but almost none of them ever made it through to this side. The only ones who could reach this side were those who knew the method for crossing the barrier the sand wind produced — 'Sand Time' — or those who, without knowing, happened to be blessed with rare luck.

Which of the two this one was — her boing-kyu-boing chest swelled with excitement as she strained her eyes, devouring the sight. —Maybe, just maybe, today was the day.

"Master—"

Maybe he'd finally, finally come back to keep his promise.

He'd kept her waiting an awfully long time, but if he really had come home, she supposed she could write off the grudge. No — or rather, shouldn't she take advantage of his guilt and demand twice the payback?

Eyes shining with that expectation, she brought the speeding dragon carriage into focus. Her field of view expanded with a stretching lurch, and she confirmed several figures in black robes riding aboard. Unfortunately she couldn't see inside the cabin, but there was no reaction to her gaze, no sign of anyone giving a signal.

"—Aw, what. Another miss, huh."

In that instant the bubble of expectation popped, and the fluttering in her chest scattered away to nothing.

No waving of hands, no waving of flags, no "look over here" appeal. That was the attitude of people who didn't know of the existence of the administrator of this sea of sand, the transcendent sniper.

And so her expectation flipped neatly over, and in its place came the cold face of the marksman—

"—"

As a marksman, what she ought to do was obvious. But it didn't look like she'd get her turn.

Looking closer, the furiously running dragon carriage was fleeing desperately from something. Clinging to the carriage that had broken through the sand wind, pouring out of that same sand wind, came countless witchbeasts. The swarming witchbeasts were a wildly varied bunch — it looked as though every single species inhabiting the sea of sand had marked them as an enemy.

Most likely that carriage hadn't challenged the sea of sand alone, either. And now, only one had made it past 'Sand Time.' And it would not be reaching the goal.

"Scurry scurry scurry scurry, so desperate~."

She murmured it like it was somebody else's business, her voice drained of expectation and interest.

Even as she did, a witchbeast leapt upon the carriage as its wheels bogged down in the sand, and one of the passengers was thrown clear. The pitiful figure tumbled across the sand, and the starving witchbeasts fell upon it all at once, tearing the poor thing to shreds— And immediately after, a roar went up along with an explosion of flame.

"Wahoooo! Ta-maaa-yaaa!"

At the blast echoing across the azure sky and the crater blown wide open in the sea of sand, the interest she'd lost revived just a little, leaking out of her mouth as a cheer.

Self-destruction. The figure that had been thrown clear and swarmed by witchbeasts had blown itself up, taking everything around it along. The blast pressure struck the pursuing pack of witchbeasts broadside, and the flames licked at their tails. Throwing away one's body, trading a life for the time to protect one's companions — and it didn't stop at one.

Twice more, three times more in quick succession the same sort of explosion went up, and each time the carriage lost one more passenger, ten times as many witchbeasts or more were blown away.

And even so—

"—Gaem Ovre~, is what that is."

For all the gallant struggle, it came to nothing: one of the pursuing witchbeasts got caught in a wheel, and the carriage overturned spectacularly in a spray of blood mist. On the sand, wreathed in dust, the first to be sacrificed was the fallen earth dragon. Its death cry rose as it met the witchbeasts' fangs, and behind it a survivor crawled out of the wrecked carriage. Having escaped by the skin of their teeth, the figure then — whether from panic — began running while shouting, drawing the witchbeasts' attention. The fate awaiting such a pitiful lamb was, needless to say, cruel.

"Oh?"

But behind the sacrifice of the earth dragon and the lamb, two figures were quietly slipping away. It seemed the lamb had deliberately played the fool to let those two escape.

"That's what you call guts worth looking up to. —Well, either way the result's the same."

Perched on the railing with one knee drawn up in her arms, she awarded the challengers' final moments a prize for fighting spirit. Let no one call this bad taste; it was simply what her role as watcher obliged her to do.

Whether those who came to challenge this sea of sand — no, this 'Tower' — went home again or ran out of strength, seeing through what became of them was her duty as watcher.

Now, of the two survivors who had slipped out of the carriage, one threw themselves in harm's way to let the other flee, and a final self-destruction gave birth to a blast of wind. Buffeted by it, the last remaining survivor was knocked sprawling onto the sand. And there, mercilessly, the witchbeasts crept in—

"—Hrmm."

With that end right before her eyes, something happened that made her mouth twist despite herself.

It was the challenger surrounded by witchbeasts — the last one, whose life was about to be placed in complete checkmate — and it was the fact that she could now see that figure clearly that gave rise to her hesitation.

Was it, though? No, really? Wasn't she seeing things? And if she wasn't—

Such endless thoughts circled through her head, circling, circling—

"—Hell's Snipe."

In that instant, the head of the witchbeast about to pounce on the lone survivor vanished.

The head of the bear-like witchbeast was blown clean away, and not a drop of blood spurted from the severed cross-section of its neck. The wound, cauterized by light, let not one drop soak into the sand, and the suddenness of it froze the witchbeasts in place.

In a sea of sand where the logic of survival of the fittest held sway, it was a far too foolish moment of stillness.

"—Bang, bang, bang. Ba-ba-bang, bang."

Her tongue flicked against the air, and the light-hearted sounds she made birthed light in the sky. Contrary to that airy ring, they trampled life underfoot with merciless suppressive power.

The sight of lives shot through by light, shattered, snuffed out with nowhere to run, had no sense of reality to it at all. Indeed, the person witnessing it firsthand was staring wide-eyed, dumbstruck and dazed.

"Well, it's nice and easy for me if you just don't move like that."

"—!"

Perhaps it sounded like a sneak attack; the stupefied figure's slim shoulders jumped. Slumped there on the sand, she turned around fearfully, and their eyes met — she was standing directly behind her.

A woman. Probably. Brown hair cut off level with her shoulders, prominent whites showing all around her irises. A loose black poncho-like robe that hid her figure, a plain face with nothing particularly distinctive about it.

Not that she'd had any interest in the other's appearance to begin with. Except for the one and only reason that had made her leap from the 'Tower' balcony down to the sea of sand and slaughter every last witchbeast to save this life.

"Good grief, I must be losing my edge."

Grumbling, she swung her arm, and an instant later the head of the witchbeast trying to heave its huge body up out of the sand beyond the woman — a sand worm — was blown to smithereens by the light of her magic.

Amid the wet splattering of the giant witchbeast's flesh and blood scattering everywhere, she shrugged.

And then—

"—"

The woman toppled over unconscious, wobbling down onto the now witchbeast-free sand.

Of the woman's companions, none had survived. The pack of witchbeasts that had been hunting them, sensing the tide had turned against them, had scattered and fled. So there was nobody around to carry the woman lying on the sand.

Nobody except the one who had very plainly drawn the short straw.

"Honestly, what a pain, and it's not even Master I'm dealing with."

Heaving a deep, deep sigh, she reluctantly gathered the sprawled woman up in both arms. Emotionally speaking she'd have liked to grab her by the legs and drag her home, but doing that risked making the rescue pointless.

Now, who was it that had told her that? —No; unlike all those other words, this one she remembered perfectly.

It was, without a doubt, one of the things 'he' had said to her. Even if she was a challenger to the 'Tower,' she had to be handled with care.

Because—

"—You treat a pregnant woman gently. Lucky for you, your belly's swollen."

Lips pursed, muttering her complaints, in her arms the limp and unconscious woman's belly was indeed swollen.

This wasn't merely a fat woman. She was pregnant. No wonder the carriage's other passengers had fought so desperately to protect her. They, too, had spent their lives to the last on the same logic as 'his' teaching.

"That said, my kindness stops there. It's not like you've cleared the challenge, so don't go getting cocky about it."

She gave that warning to no one in particular. It would be a real problem if there were any misunderstanding.

After all, for anyone who wasn't her and wasn't one of 'them' to set foot in this 'Tower' — in the Pleiades Watchtower — was the first such occurrence in something like several hundred years.
