Arc 7 Chapter 90: "Cradle of Hell"

"There's no mistake! Directly ahead, the First Vertex — 'Two,' Arakiya!!"

"The Second Vertex is presumed to be Madelyn Eschart, 'Nine'! A swarm of Flying Dragons circling overhead!"

"Third Vertex, guardian unconfirmed!"

"Likewise, the Fourth Vertex's guardian cannot be confirmed! Cannot be confirmed!"

"Fifth Vertex, a flashy attack... not a First General! A Second General! Kafma Irulux!"

The reports of the battle's progress came flying in one after another, voices raised so loud they bordered on roars of rage or screams.

The soldiers assigned to scouting duty had taken up the highest ground they could find, eyes bloodshot as they strained to wring even the smallest scrap of information from the imposing sight of the imperial capital, Lupugana, looming ahead of them.

The decisive battle for the imperial capital had finally begun. If one judged fighting strength by simple numbers, the gap between the capital's regular army and the assembled rebel forces was nearly twofold. If both sides charged out onto the plain all at once and clashed head-on, the outcome would be obvious — but.

"I never wished to imagine myself standing on the side that must besiege and bring down the impregnable imperial capital, Lupugana."

So said Zikr Osman inside the makeshift command post, shaking his distinctively styled head from side to side as he marked the reported battle conditions onto the spread-out map, a furrow deepening between his brows.

As a man who had served as one of the Empire's Generals — a Second General — and who still held a deep loyalty to Vollachia, to him the imperial capital was the armor that guarded the heart that needed protecting.

That he might one day find himself in the position of having to storm that very capital and strike down the one who — however nominally — sat the throne and claimed the title of Emperor was something he had surely never imagined.

"But circumstances will not wait for your whimpering to subside. —No, that goes for more than just you. Time passes equally for anyone, whoever they may be."

"Lord Abel..."

"Return to the matter of the battle. Have the scouts report. Priority goes to the Third and Fourth Vertices... Even trivial details will do. From them, we determine the guardians."

"Yes! Reinforce the scouting on the Third and Fourth! Without doubt, a First General has taken the field there!"

Nodding, Zikr gave the order to the subordinates nearby, and the air around them shifted with dizzying speed.

Tasting that parched air against his skin, Abel narrowed his eyes behind his demon mask, gazing from outside the cloth-walled command post at the distant imperial capital, protected by its star-shaped ramparts.

As noted, in terms of raw numbers, the rebel army overwhelmed the regular forces.

However, the assembled rebels had little notion of coordination — a disorderly mob eager to charge ahead rather than let others claim the glory and honor for themselves.

On top of that, the enemy they needed to bring down was holed up in the innermost depths of a capital that was the very picture of solid, impregnable defense.

It had come up as a topic when they took the walled city of Guaral as well — the notion that, in simple terms, an attacking force needs three times the numbers of the defenders to bring them down.

Given that the capital's defenses far surpassed those of Guaral, that gap would be all the more pronounced.

And above all else—

"—The thing most likely to upend the balance of power is warriors strong enough to be worth a thousand men apiece."

"This is the decisive battle for the capital's defense. Even though hostilities broke out sooner than expected, the question is just how many of the Nine Divine Generals have been called back."

Even a disorderly mob of rebels, if struck on the head and doused with cold water, would eventually be forced to listen. Once that happened, they could be worked into a strategy and shaped into something resembling order.

But that was only true so long as the water thrown on them woke their eyes rather than closed them forever — and if they found themselves face to face with one of the Nine Divine Generals, even that would not come easily.

"At minimum, the participation of Arakiya, Madelyn Eschart, and Moguro Hagane is beyond doubt. In ability alone, Kafma Irulux ranks with a First General as well. There's a chance Olbart Dunkelkenn refuses the summons, citing his injured arm, but the odds are slim."

"You believe First General Goz and First General Groovy will not be taking part, then?"

"Whether Goz Ralfon lives or is dead, he will not be here regardless. Whether Groovy Gumlet has been recalled from the west is another matter, but he's a hard man to move. The opacity surrounding the west's movements isn't something either side has engineered. —Though it does smell of something."

At the very outset of the rebellion, Goz had fought to let the fleeing Abel escape the capital, and nothing had been heard of him since — it was highly likely he was dead.

Goz, who commanded extraordinary support from the troops for his high command ability and character, would have been one of the greatest threats in this war had he turned enemy. However, given that he knew the truth of the impostor on the throne, there was not the slightest chance of Goz siding with the other side — he was merely a dead piece, difficult to make use of.

"If one were to make effective use of a dead piece, the only conceivable method would be to use the death itself... pin the blame for Goz Ralfon's death on the rebels and use it to stoke the troops' morale."

"...I would rather not imagine it. Had I been on that side without knowing the truth, I too might have taken up my sword for First General Goz's sake, even as a soldier resigned to die."

Even Zikr, a General in his own right, mourned the hypothetical death of Goz.

It was a testament to the trust and record Goz Ralfon had built up — and indeed, were such a stratagem carried out, it would be one that forced a complete reassessment of the regular army's fighting strength.

However, if there had been any intention of carrying it out, the moment just before the outbreak of war would have been the perfect opportunity.

"Either that chance was deliberately let pass, or the uncertainty of a morale-driven swing in fighting strength was disliked. Whichever it is, that man would still have strings laid out."

Racing through his thoughts, Abel touched a hand to the edge of his mask, probing at his opponent's intentions.

In a battle of reading intentions, there was no room to lose against most opponents. But this particular opponent fell outside the bounds of "most," and was, on top of that, the enemy who knew the color of his own intentions better than anyone thus far.

He would like to say the conditions were equal on his side as well, but when the conditions for victory differ, so do the available choices. And just as Abel stood there locked in a staring match with the map—

"—Abel-chin, Abel-chin!"

A light, buoyant voice slipped through the entrance of the tent, and a girl came darting in with a quick, nimble motion. She latched onto the desk and turned a pair of blue eyes, swimming with overwhelming emotion, on Abel.

"I really do wanna go too! My bro's in there, y'know?! I can't just sit still!"

"Fool. Do not go adding uncertain variables. To begin with, what proof do you have that your brother is even in the capital?"

"There was a girl named Madelyn! The one who took my bro and Rem-chan away! If she's here, then maybe the two of them are too! Right?"

"That is not proof either. Furthermore, in the current state of this battle, your brother and that girl hold little strategic value. Do not mistake what deserves priority."

"Is there anything more important to me than my bro and my friends?! No way!"

Pounding the desk again and again, the girl — Medium — snapped back at Abel's words.

It was an emotional argument, hardly worth engaging with. Ultimately, as he had said, in the current situation there was no reason to prioritize the abducted Flop and Rem beyond sentiment alone — and so, to Abel, it was a judgment without value.

Though there was, of course, always the option of simply letting Medium do as she pleased.

"—. That would only invite needless backlash, would it."

"—? What?"

Her brows drooping, Medium — utterly indifferent to her own worth — tilted her head. Or perhaps she spoke so boldly precisely because she rated herself fairly. Abel dismissed the thought at once as overthinking.

Either way, there was no leisure to spend much more time on her.

"However much you voice your wish, you surely know it is asking too much, as you stand now. If you shake off the reins and charge onto the battlefield, you will only scatter your life to no purpose."

"Mmh! That's not—"

"Lady Medium, Lord Abel is concerned for your safety. And I share his opinion as well — I cannot simply let you go."

Between the downward gaze and the upward one, in the standoff between Abel and Medium, Zikr cut in with those words, gently admonishing her in a calm tone.

As commander, Zikr too understood how uncertain the situation was. And yet, being made to deal with the headstrong Medium on top of it all must have been galling.

Without letting a trace of that feeling show on his profile, the man known as a "womanizer" met Medium's gaze, and prefacing his words with "How about this," he continued—

"That impregnable wall is made up of five vertices. There is no capturing the capital while ignoring those vertices. At the very least, let us gather information on the enemy and clear away the uncertainties before we revisit this discussion—"

"I toooold you! I'm thinking about this properly too! I really wanna ask that Madelyn girl about my bro, but since that's hard, I figured I'd get in from the empty spot instead—"

"—Wait."

Medium tried to emotionally brush past Zikr, who had attempted a reasonable persuasion. But something in her words caught Abel's attention, and he stopped her.

Grabbing her slender shoulder, Abel turned the wide-eyed Medium to face him.

"You said an empty spot. If that refers to one of the vertices, where did you get that information?"

"Where did I— ah! Oh right! Sorry, Abel-chin!"

Her expression changing at the question, Medium took hold of the hand Abel had placed on her shoulder with both of hers. Gripping it tightly, she let her blue eyes well up with emotion once again, and said—

"I was supposed to tell you this! Um, the Third Vertex is all dolls, and the Fourth Vertex is just black shadows... neither one has anybody like Yorna-chan in it!"

"――――"

"Abel-chin?"

At the message conveyed from Medium's mouth, Abel's eyes widened, if only slightly. But it was erased at once by a blink of each eye in turn, vanishing into a quiet exhale.

There was no need to scrutinize the content of what had been told to him. The meaning was clear. The problem was the route by which that information had arrived—

"Who had you convey this to me?"

"Eh?"

"Who."

With his left hand — the one not held by hers — Abel pressed the back of Medium's head, forcing her to face him.

Seen straight through by that gaze into her blue eyes, Medium caught her breath. Then she moved her pale pink lips and answered Abel's question.

And the answer to that question was—

—Swallowed by a vast, violent whirlpool of information, Otto Suwen closed his eyes.

"――――"

A storm of voices, voices, voices came flooding in.

From the right, from the left, from above, from below, from before, from behind — an unending violence of voices surged in without pause.

It was the return of the cradle of hell he had once spent his earliest years in, back before he could even properly remember — a homecoming, in a sense, to a beloved birthplace.

The Divine Protection of the Soul of Language that Otto possessed was said to be, among the countless Divine Protections that existed in the world, one of the most notorious "duds."

This Protection, which allowed one to understand and exchange words with every living creature, was riddled with far too many flaws to serve as a blade one was born wielding.

To begin with, a newborn child has neither ego nor self-awareness; its own self is vague and undefined.

For a child who cannot yet establish a firm sense of self, the only way to define oneself is through the surrounding presences — but for a holder of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language, that surrounding scope was far too broad.

It wasn't a matter of up, down, left, right, or depth — it was, quite literally, absolutely everything.

Put plainly, a child with the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language couldn't even distinguish between human voices, animal cries, the chirring of insects, the sound of the wind, the patter of rain, and the ringing in their own ears.

A creature with both judgment and attention scattered so thin as that could barely manage to survive at all.

That Otto had lived through that period at all was owed entirely to his environment.

A comfortable life, loving parents, an older and younger brother who never treated their sibling — incapable of even proper communication — as a nuisance to be cast aside: it was the presence of such a devoted family that kept Otto alive.

But even after that first step — establishing himself as a living being — the trials of a holder of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language did not end. If anything, the real ordeal began there.

A holder of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language, in exchange for being able to exchange words with everything around them, carries the risk of isolation from everything around them. The inability to form any sense of belonging is the great cause of it.

If one supposes that speaking, touching, and cultivating bonds form the foundation of living, then a holder of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language could, indeed, build a relationship with any living creature at all.

There was no need to limit the other party to humans.

They could exchange words with any animal, any insect, even fish. A Protection that let them connect with every living thing that drew breath. And that was precisely why they could so easily end up isolated.

In the prison of the question of what oneself even was, one could become eternally, utterly alone.

Hence, every past holder of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language had died young, without exception.

Otto didn't even want to think about how many holders had died without ever getting the chance to truly grasp the power of their Protection, let alone make it known to those around them — dying without even managing to speak their own name.

In the midst of an unceasing downpour, in an unbroken gale, in the helplessness of knowing one would never be understood — what value could be found in living?

In fact, Otto had never once heard of a holder of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language who had gone on to accomplish great things.

And he understood more than well enough why that would be the case.

Because, quite literally, the cradle of hell was a place where one was made to listen, endlessly, to a lullaby that led toward death.

"――――"

In that nostalgic hell, Otto consciously narrowed the focus of the voices he chose to listen to.

It worked the same way as picking out, from the noise of a crowded street or a party hall, only the voices speaking of something relevant or interesting to oneself among the countless others.

It was a domain that most holders of the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language had never managed to reach — the ability to adjust the intensity of their Protection's effect.

For a Protection that was always active, one could soften its impact by adjusting the strength of its effect. Otto, too, normally kept his set to its weakest level, so his mind wouldn't be buried under words.

Now, he raised the volume of that weakened Protection, pushing its intensity to the maximum.

"――――"

Once, when Subaru had pestered him into talking about the Divine Protection of the Soul of Language, he'd called it a "channel." The ring of the word hadn't struck Otto as bad, so — to use it — he opened the channel.

The countless "voices" that came flooding into Otto's ears, into his brain, were all connected to the battlefield centered on this imperial capital. Many were laced with revulsion, hatred, fear, or rage, and plenty of them were useless — but he scrutinized them all evenly, without exception.

After all—

"I did ask an awful lot of Garfiel, after all..."

When Emilia had made the decision to take on the decisive battle for the capital, Otto had concluded that it would be Garfiel who bore the most grueling role in this fight.

Given their strength as combatants, it would be Emilia and Garfiel taking on the fight against the most dangerous enemies. But Emilia could not be allowed to fight until she'd exhausted every last ounce of her strength.

Naturally, even before recovering Subaru and Rem, Emilia's life took top priority.

For that reason, should Emilia find herself in danger, she would be pulled back regardless of anything she might say.

That much had been established as shared understanding across the camp, communicated to everyone except Emilia herself. But there was no option of coming all the way to the Empire and going home empty-handed. Subaru and Rem would be brought back.

To accomplish that goal, the role of taking the worst wounds, of shedding the most blood, fell to Garfiel.

"Whether Garfiel understands that or not, who's to say."

Did Garfiel understand just how reckless, how cruel an order the role entrusted to him really was?

Otto was well aware he had issued a horribly selfish, unreasonable order. Garfiel probably thought he'd been asked to do something reckless — but did he grasp the true weight of it?

Or perhaps Garfiel had simply taken it as a sign of trust.

"...I hate that."

Otto murmured the words under his breath, thinking of Garfiel's innocent, trusting gaze.

Even when given an unreasonable order, Garfiel trusted that Otto had his reasons, and took it as his own role to carry it out. And Otto, knowing full well that Garfiel would take it that way, had given the order anyway, entrusting him with a role only he could fulfill.

At a glance, this looked like an exchange built on mutual trust. But Otto, as the one directly involved, thought that the weight each of them carried was hopelessly out of balance.

However you looked at it, it was Garfiel who bore the danger. Not Otto.

He himself got to sit snug in safety, wallowing in a guilty ache in his chest, and that was all. And once it was over, would Garfiel praise him with a "As expected of you, Otto-nii!"

Not a chance.

"If someone must bear it, then bear it yourself too. Don't run from responsibility, Otto Suwen."

With a hand at his mouth, Otto admonished himself so.

When the camp had finally settled on its policy toward this Imperial upheaval, Otto had warned Emilia that "this will mean walking a road of thorns."

In response to those words, Emilia had squared her shoulders with resolve and chosen the path.

It was probably a ridiculous notion, a foolish road — a way of thinking Otto would never choose if he were alone, one that courted danger while offering little in return.

But among that little that could be gained, there was something precious — and the fact that Emilia had chosen a path he himself could never have chosen put Otto's heart at ease.

He had, in his heart of hearts, been glad of it. Which meant he had no right to remain an outsider.

Of all the possibilities that had existed, it was Otto who had chosen this one.

And if blood needed to be shed for it, he refused to leave the shedding of that blood to someone else.

So—

"—Otto-san, you know, sometimes you turn into a huge idiot."

Amid the flood of meaningless "voices," a clear one suddenly reached him, and Otto blinked.

In that instant, the sound of hell's rain and its gale withdrew into the distance, and as Otto narrowed his channel and turned around, his eyes met Petra's, her brows drooping in exasperation.

She came up in front of him and, saying "Here," held out a white handkerchief.

"You've got a ton of blood coming out of your nose. It's kind of scary."

"My nose... ah, I didn't notice."

"Looks like it. ...Please sit down. You don't have to be standing, right?"

Otto stared blankly at the offered handkerchief. Growing impatient, Petra tugged his hand and sat him down on the grass.

Then she pressed the handkerchief to the face of the unresisting Otto and wiped away the blood.

"Does this happen when you use your Protection a lot?"

"...Yeah. Widen the number of things I'm listening to, and the distance, and this is what happens. It's not a method I much want to use, but these are exceptional circumstances."

"So you won't lose to Garf-san?"

"――――"

"Just as I thought. Otto-san, you're a huge idiot sometimes."

Glared at with narrowed eyes by Petra, Otto scratched his head and said, "You've got me there."

It wasn't quite what he meant to say, but Petra's point was right in the broad sense. Being as sharp as she was, she might well have understood Otto's true feelings and simply rephrased them.

Petra, the camp's most promising rising talent, grew sharper and stronger with each passing day.

"Even if you bleed a ton from your nose, Otto-san, I don't think that makes you the same as Garf-san."

"Well, it's not exactly a contest of who bleeds more. Unlike Garfiel, I'd die of blood loss a lot more easily. Still, I should be prepared to pay something out of my own hide."

"Because you're the one who told everyone to go?"

"There's no besting you, is there, Petra-chan."

With his nose squeezed tight, the pooled blood being pressed out of it, Otto gave a wry smile.

Considering only what had actually happened, it was Emilia who had decided the policy of joining this fight, and Emilia who had declared she would see it through — Petra's point should, strictly speaking, be off the mark.

And yet, in truth, just as Petra said, it was Otto who had made her say it.

—No, to be precise, it was the very thing Otto had wanted her to say.

"Really, I'm not tormenting myself for no reason. This might sound odd of me to say, but my Protection is extremely convenient in a war. There'd be no sense in not using it."

"――――"

"Given the circumstances, it's hard to find anyone willing to cooperate, but there's value just in gathering information. I just now had Medium-san carry a message. If Abel-san makes good use of it, we could turn the situation more in our favor... Petra-chan?"

"Haaah~"

Otto tried to at least save face by explaining the practical usefulness of what he was doing, but Petra's reaction to it was cold, and her exasperation only deepened.

With a sigh, Petra ran a hand through her light brown hair and fixed her round eyes on Otto.

"I don't think I'm as dumb a kid as you think I am, Otto-san."

"No, um, right. I think of you as a very quick-witted girl, Petra-chan—"

"And I think you, Otto-san, aren't as smart as you think you are."

"That's... a hard one to answer."

It wasn't as though he was conceited enough to think of himself as some brilliant strategist, possessed of cunning no one else could match.

By his own assessment, what had gotten Otto this far was a wealth of experience-based rules of thumb, a large repertoire of small tricks, and above-average crisis management skills.

Once you started comparing raw intelligence, he fell far short of the schemers who could see through everything.

But apparently, in this moment, that line of thinking of his was beside the point.

"The kind of smart I'm talking about right now isn't that. Otto-san, I don't think I'm as useless a kid as you think I am."

"—. That's..."

"I really think I can be useful. For example..."

So saying, Petra took the bewildered Otto's hand and squeezed it tight. Her small hand began to glow white, and that glow seeped into him.

At once, the ringing that had been echoing in Otto's head eased, just slightly.

"This is... Yang Magic?"

"It's part of the magic Master's been teaching me. Still just the basics of the basics, though."

"――――"

"Otto-san, I know this is a moment to push myself too. But suffering doesn't make you great just because you suffered."

Otto, who had just forced upon himself the resolve to cut into his own flesh, caught his breath at her words.

Seeing him like that, Petra let out a small sigh.

"This is the kind of thing you'd usually be the one saying, Otto-san. I won't tell everyone else, but you've been on edge this whole time, haven't you."

"Ugh."

"You've been angry this whole time. I get how you feel, but your voice and the way you act have been way too prickly."

At that, Otto had nothing to say for himself and hung his head.

Just as Petra said, he felt as though he'd fallen into a pit of misjudgment and mistaken resolve that the usual Otto would never have stumbled into.

Through her small hand, he felt as though what she wanted to say was reaching him directly.

"It's not bleeding from your nose that makes you great. It's doing what needs doing that makes you great."

"Right. So, Otto-san, what will you do?"

Still holding the hand of the sighing Otto, Petra lifted it up.

"If I'm here, you can push forward a bit more efficiently. And yet, are you going to make me step back, telling me children shouldn't be watching?"

"That's a terrible way to put it!"

"Are you going to make me step back?"

Still smiling brightly, Petra pressed the point on Otto. He yielded to that pressure — and yielded gladly, at that — letting out a long breath.

"――――"

Even now, opening the channel would bring the countless "voices" flooding back in. Amid that violent current threatening to sweep him away, Petra's hand held fast to Otto, keeping him from being carried off.

As if to make him think it might be all right, after all, to listen a little farther out, to a few more voices.

"You're going to get scolded together with me by Frederica-san later, you know."

"Okay! I'd much rather you say 'let's bleed from the nose together' than have you bleed all alone, Otto-san. Though I do hate nosebleeds!"

Otto closed his eyes at the sight of Petra, laughing so brightly, having made up her mind to help him.

The hand gripping his so tightly — trembling just the faintest bit — surely this clever girl was not unaware of it herself.

Facing a war for the first time, a battlefield that had swallowed up people dear to her, with the safety of the companions she'd sent off hanging in doubt — Petra, too, wished to do something herself, just the same as him.

Let him honor that resolve, then. —Use the cards you can play, when you can play them. As befits a merchant.

"Petra-chan, lend me a hand. —This battlefield's channel, I'm going to master it."

"We're going to master it!"

At Petra's spirited reply, Otto gave a wry smile and answered, "All right, then, that," before opening the channel once more, to plunge back into the cradle of hell.

—Otto, returning to his beloved birthplace, could no longer be as alone as he once had been.
