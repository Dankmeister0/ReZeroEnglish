Arc 3 Chapter 15: "A Company That Gets Along Far Too Poorly"

A girl clad in dingy clothes like little more than rags, her dull blonde hair and eyes worn ragged by hardship. A girl who, even in an environment that forced her to walk with head bowed, had survived with nothing but sheer vigor blazing in her crimson eyes — a tough, resilient girl.

That was the entirety of the impression Subaru held of the girl named Felt.

Summoned by Reinhard's announcement, Felt, accompanied by a lady-in-waiting, walked quietly across the throne room. Treading upon the red carpet, mindful not to let the hem of her pale yellow dress drag along the floor, she advanced with her small back held perfectly straight — every bit the figure of a noble young lady.

Subaru had once remarked that she might shine if polished — but the rough gem named Felt, apparently polished by the power of House Astrea, unfortunately didn't quite match his earlier assessment. It wasn't a matter of shining if polished. The truth was that, polished, she positively dazzled.

Still very much unrefined within, her outward appearance, once put in order, was nonetheless beautiful.

Under Subaru's flabbergasted gaze, his mouth hanging open, she moved unhurriedly to Reinhard's side. Once he confirmed Felt had come within arm's reach, Reinhard carved a smile onto his handsome face and nodded.

"Felt-sama, thank you for troubling yourself to come."

"—Reinhard."

Reinhard bowed respectfully. Because of the difference in their height, Felt had to look up at him from below, tilting her chin as she called out to him. Called in her cool, clear voice, Reinhard answered with an equally resonant "Yes." Felt gave a faint smile at his manner. And then—

"—Hey, you went and hid my clothes again, didn't you!?"

Hitching up the hem of her dress, she swung a leg — surprisingly long and slender for her frame — in an arc. It flew true, aimed straight at Reinhard's temple — only for the kicking foot to land snugly in his palm, casually raised to catch it. Left standing on one leg, Felt watched as Reinhard, still holding her foot, let out a small breath.

"I was startled. What's this all of a sudden?"

"Don't act all casual after blocking it like it's nothing! My clothes! You're the one who hid them again, aren't you!? Because of you I'm stuck wearing this annoying frilly getup!"

Balancing on one leg, Felt puffed out her cheeks in displeasure, the hem of what must have been a dress tailored just for her swaying with the motion. Handled so roughly, the once perfectly smooth fabric was beginning to gather unsightly creases. Watching this, Reinhard gently lowered Felt's foot back to the floor.

"It suits you very well, so there's no need to feel embarrassed."

"I'm not embarrassed, I'm saying I hate it! And it's not just about the clothes, it's about you too! Doesn't a knight kidnapping and locking someone up strike you as something to be embarrassed about!?"

"If it serves the kingdom's prosperity."

At Reinhard's unhesitating declaration, Felt put a hand to her forehead as though struck by a headache. Then, belatedly, she seemed to notice she'd drawn the gaze of the entire hall upon herself.

"What, quit staring. I'm not a sideshow act, and if you think I am, you'd better toss me a coin or two — looks like a room full of rich folk, anyway."

Bristling with open hostility toward people who were plainly upper-crust at a glance, all while wearing a dress that would rank among the finest even in this room, Felt groused in a manner every bit as rough as her upbringing suggested.

At her thoroughly disappointing thug-like demeanor — a stark betrayal of her polished appearance — Subaru finally managed to shake off his stupor and let out a sigh of relief.

"Here I thought she'd changed completely, but turns out it's just the looks. Phew, yeah, people don't really change at the core, do they. Present company included!"

Subaru, quite pleased with himself for both defending Felt and justifying his own nature in the same breath. Taking his reaction as a cue, the others who had been watching the exchange between Felt and Reinhard began offering their own responses in turn.

"Making Reinhard actually use his hands — she might be more capable than expected," said Crusch, impressed from an odd angle.

"Ain't that just another tomboy added to the mix? Give me a break, honestly," said Anastasia, shaking her head with a thoroughly weary look.

"Standing out more than I do — unthinkable. Now then, is there something I might use to draw attention myself... Perhaps a public execution of the intruder?" mused Priscilla, drifting into a train of thought no one ought to let pass unchallenged.

"That girl... from back then...!? So that's why Reinhard was so shocked..." And Emilia, having arrived at some conclusion of her own, stood with her eyes wide in astonishment.

Emilia, in particular, was no stranger to Felt, so her shock ran deeper than most. The two of them had once fought over the same insignia, and now, in a twist of fate, Felt too had been chosen by an insignia — meaning they were about to become rivals for the throne.

Once everyone's first impressions had run their course, Felt, in response, looked uncomfortable — or rather, clicked her tongue rudely and turned her face away.

Even accounting for their brief acquaintance two weeks ago, Subaru didn't recall her being quite this twisted in temperament — a great deal must have happened to her in those two weeks. Subaru didn't intend to lose to her in terms of sheer eventfulness, but he could easily imagine that going from a mere street urchin to a royal candidate in one leap had been plenty intense in its own right.

As Subaru nodded along to this string of musings, Felt's gaze, which had been sweeping appraisingly around the hall, locked onto him.

She suddenly furrowed her brow, then closed her eyes as if searching through her memory, and within a few seconds her face brightened as though she'd found what she was looking for.

"Oh! What're you doing here, big bro!"

Giving Reinhard's chest a light shove to push him away, she came stomping over toward Subaru with the same momentum. Her clearly high-end dress was handled so roughly it was almost tragic, and the maids who had no doubt coordinated the outfit with such care covered their faces and looked away.

Leaving such sentiments behind, Subaru faced Felt as she approached, grinning from ear to ear. Honestly, given the mood of the room, he'd rather have avoided a face-to-face with her, but reasoning that she must feel just as friendless and uneasy as he did, he couldn't very well brush her off.

"Hey, long time no see. Looks like you've been doi—guh!"

The instant he raised a hand and offered a breezy greeting, a front kick slammed directly into his gut. The impact folded him double, and Subaru dropped to his knees, coughing.

Subaru groaned, uncomprehending of the sudden violence. Felt, still standing with one leg raised, looked down at his sorry state.

"Judging by that, the gut wound seems fine now. Though it looks like you've picked up a ton of other injuries to make up for it."

"If you knew that, you could've gone easy on me... Why's your idea of checking a full-power kick? What if it had torn back open? There actually was a stretch where that was a real risk, you know."

Though it was now fully healed, a straight white scar remained clearly etched into the bare skin of Subaru's stomach. Not only that, marks from Witchbeast fangs remained scattered across his body — quite the impressive display of battered-and-bruised for just two weeks. With countless cuts and scrapes to his name, this was no time to be worrying about the old saying that a scar on a swordsman's back was a mark of shame.

"Well, guess you had it rough in your own way, big bro. But if we're talking rough, I'm not about to lose to you, either."

"...Yeah, I bet. Never thought I'd end up running into you again in a scene like this. The whole insignia mess just kept spinning around and led right to this bon jov—gah!"

"Obviously you can't just go blabbing about the insignia thing out loud, look at the situation."

"...There were cuter ways to shut someone up, you know? Your legs are just as fast as ever."

And since she'd meant to shut the mouth of someone crouched down, she'd kicked his chin straight up from below. Fortunately, the force had been held back somewhat, and Subaru was starting to get used to being kicked, so he managed not to bite his tongue. In any case, he figured bringing up her prior record would be a bad idea, if only for Reinhard's sake.

Though he supposed there was no denying that Felt's own conduct had already made for quite the disastrous high-society debut.

"Felt-sama. Rekindling old acquaintances is well and good, but if you'd come this way, please."

Interrupting the exchange between the two — which might, from the outside, have looked almost amiable — was Marcos, still unmoved by the mood of the room, calmly pressing the proceedings forward.

Felt looked as though she still wanted to argue with his rock-solid expression, but when Reinhard, standing right beside him, bowed apologetically, she turned forward again with a reluctant look on her face.

"So, what is it you want me to do, anyway?"

"I'd like to say 'behave as a lady' first and foremost, but before that, please take hold of this."

Felt made a face at Reinhard's light jab. Into her open palm, he placed the dragon insignia he'd drawn from within his coat.

For a moment Felt's brow creased at the sight of the insignia's shape, but as soon as she saw the gem begin to glow in her palm, her stiff expression softened.

"Been thinking this since I swiped it, but it's a weird rock. Why's it even glow?"

"Swiped?"

"That is because the Dragon recognized Felt-sama as one who possesses the qualification."

For all that she'd scolded Subaru moments earlier for saying too much, Felt let something slip herself without thinking. Marcos seemed to catch her carelessness, but Reinhard's immediate interjection managed to smooth it over before it became an issue.

Marcos seemed briefly puzzled that his remark had been glossed over, but he apparently decided to prioritize the fact before him over that lingering question.

Turning, he looked up toward the assembled dignitaries of the Council of Wise Men, who had watched the proof of qualification unfold in silence.

"As you can see, the Dragon Insignia has indeed acknowledged Felt-sama as a Maiden. With her participation now confirmed, I believe this marks the true beginning of this Royal Selection."

Marcos placed his palm against the iron plate and bowed his head respectfully. Reinhard followed his captain's lead, as did every member of the royal guard who had taken part in the search.

With the knights thus reporting the completion of their mission, it was thanks to their efforts that five Dragon Maidens — that is, candidates for the future queen of Lugunica — now stood gathered in this place.

"I see, so this is the day history turns, huh."

Subaru, who had ended up placing himself in this momentous scene quite unintentionally, murmured this softly, with deep feeling. Sneaking into the royal castle on such a landmark day — looking back, that had been a reckless, foolish stunt indeed. He should have been dead nine times out of ten. Once again, he found himself keenly aware of just how strong his bad luck really was.

Lost in such wildly misplaced musings, Subaru belatedly noticed a stir beginning to spread through the throne room.

The source of the commotion lay across the center from the saluting knights — from the group of civil officials lined up there, Roswaal among them. The details of the murmuring didn't carry, but it clearly contained confusion, bewilderment, and no small amount of open dissatisfaction.

"Excuse me, might I have a word?"

At last, a middle-aged man stepped forward from among the officials. He looked to be in his forties, with brown hair swept back in a full head of it. Stroking his fine, well-groomed beard with a nervous air, he said,

"For this rite of selecting the king, I find no words adequate to the royal guard's efforts. Without your strength, gentlemen, we could never have brought about a state of affairs matching the Dragon History Stone's prophecy in so short a span."

"You honor us with such words."

To the man's pompous praise of the knights, Marcos demurred in the same grave tone as before. In response, the man looked away as though finding this difficult, and prefacing his next words with "However," continued—

"I hate to say this, but even granting that it matches what the Dragon History Stone foretold, is there not perhaps some problem with the selection of personnel?"

"Meaning?"

"I mean to say that, dazzled as you've been by the qualification of Dragon Maiden, you may have neglected the far more crucial matter of qualification to wear the crown of this kingdom."

The middle-aged man snapped the words out sharply, as though berating a slow-witted listener. He seemed to feel some shame at the sharpness of his own tone and quickly fell silent, but from among the officials came several voices murmuring agreement — "Hear, hear," and the like.

Emboldened somewhat by finding he had supporters, he cleared his throat and went on.

"The Covenant with the Dragon is of paramount importance. So long as Lugunica exists as a nation allied with the Dragon, our friendship with them is essential to our survival. But if, in placing such weight upon the Dragon, we come to neglect the people, is that not putting the cart before the horse?"

"In other words, are you saying this: that we of the royal guard, having poured our very blood into searching for Dragon Maidens, have thereby mistaken who is truly fit to be the monarch to whom our loyalty is owed?"

"There's some discrepancy in phrasing, but yes, that's the gist of it."

Perhaps rattled by how bluntly Marcos had summarized things, the middle-aged man offered an indirect warning about choosing one's words carefully. But that attempt at damage control came a little too late.

For the knights, having moved heaven and earth to produce results only to see their achievement met with anything but generous appraisal, this was not a matter to take calmly.

Standing as he did on the knights' side of things, Subaru found this easy enough to sense for himself.

"Smells like trouble — or, I mean, things are getting pretty ominous..."

"Well, from the knights' side of it, they're basically getting bad-mouthed for no reason. Not that I've got much of an opinion either way, myself. How about you two?"

Catching Subaru's mutter, Al let out a muffled laugh and tossed the question to the other two. Julius and Ferris, thus put on the spot, turned only their eyes this way.

"Ferri-chan doesn't really feel one way or the other-nya. I mean, whatever Beardy over there says, Ferri-chan's already given her loyalty to just one person, after all."

"I won't go so far as to say I'm of the same opinion as Ferris, but I do feel much the same. My sword is already pledged. They, too, will eventually entrust their loyalty to someone. I'm not so narrow-minded as to condemn the wavering of one's heart before that day comes."

"Heh, how admirable. Well, same goes for me, I suppose, when it comes to my princess."

As if in rivalry, Al said this, and the other two's mouths curved into faint smiles. Somehow, Subaru felt left out of the group, and none too pleased about it. All three of them — as knights, or as a mercenary — had placed their utter trust in their respective lords. Comparing his own position to theirs, Subaru was struck by the uncomfortable sense that he was somehow standing a step behind.

That shouldn't be true, he thought, shaking his head to chase off the faint-heartedness. He wanted to help Emilia achieve her goal. In that feeling, at least, he shouldn't be losing to any of the three.

While Subaru was driven by this strange sense of impatience, the exchange in the hall had begun to escalate. Starting with the middle-aged man who'd voiced his opinion, the officials began airing their complaints one after another.

"To be a Maiden and a monarch at once — or one who is to become a monarch — she lacks the self-awareness for it."

"No matter how finely she's dressed up, her true nature shows through in her conduct."

"She lacks dignity. She lacks education. How could anyone call such a person a monarch?"

"Oh, I don't see the harm in it, myself. I rather think it might make for quite a lively, colorful Royal Selection, if you ask me-e."

"You would do well to stay silent!"

It sounded as though a familiar voice was about to split the officials' ranks in two, but setting that aside for now, Subaru turned his attention to Emilia and the others caught in the middle of it all.

The officials were plainly singling out Felt for censure, given how conspicuously poor her conduct had just been, but it couldn't be said the fire hadn't spread to the other candidates as well. In fact, Emilia's profile looked pained, as if enduring some ache, and there was no doubt that simply standing there was becoming a burden to her.

If he could just shout at them all to shut up, he might feel some measure of relief, but weighing the drawbacks of doing so, he felt too uneasy to act on impulse.

So then, what settled the matter was neither Subaru's rashness, nor the anger of the knights whose completed mission had been smeared with mud, nor any reflective good sense on the part of the officials realizing they'd gone too far. It came down to a single word — the old man seated upon the dais murmuring, "Quiet."

Stroking his impossibly long beard, Miklotov narrowed his already half-closed eyes even further, looking at Felt, who glared up at the old man's frame with a defiant expression. A silent stretch of time passed, but then Miklotov suddenly let out a small breath and said,

"Knight Reinhard."

"Yes."

Called by name and beckoned forward, Reinhard strode briskly up to the dais. He knelt before Miklotov, removing his sword from his waist and laying it upon the floor in a gesture of utmost respect. Watching this with satisfaction, the old man drew his white beard through his fingers as though drawing forth a memory along with it.

"Hmm. Might I hear from you the circumstances under which you found her?"

Asked to recount the circumstances of their meeting, cold sweat ran down Subaru's brow even though he wasn't the one being questioned. Naturally, telling the plain truth would require mentioning the theft incident Felt had caused.

But heedless of Subaru's worry, Reinhard gave a deep nod.

"Thirteen days ago, I took her into protective custody in a corner of the royal capital's lower district — commonly called the 'slums.' At that time, for certain reasons, an opportunity arose for her to touch the Dragon Insignia, whereupon it became clear she possessed the qualification of Maiden, and so I have brought her here as you see."

Reinhard reported this with a perfectly straight face, deftly blurring over the problematic parts. To Subaru, who knew the actual circumstances, finding the holes in this account would have been easier than nitpicking over trifles, but it was of course not Subaru who spoke up about Reinhard's remark. And the part that person pointed out turned out to be different from the part that had snagged Subaru's attention.

"A street urchin from the slums...? Are you in your right mind, Knight Reinhard!?"

It was the voice of the middle-aged man, who, having gained the backing of the officials, had begun working himself up somewhat. He gestured grandly toward Felt with exaggerated motions of hand and arm.

"To invite a street urchin, of all people, into this rite meant to select the king who will bear the weight of Lugunica's future is beyond all reason. What exactly do you take the throne to be!?"

"—"

"Falling silent when it's inconvenient for you, is it? If this is what the current heir to the Sword Saint has become, I can only conclude that House Astrea's honor has fallen to the ground."

Still holding his salute toward the dais, Reinhard took in the words hurled at him without protest. His cool profile showed not a trace of negative emotion, and the middle-aged man, pressing his case, found himself met with such an unmoved, wind-through-willows response that he fell silent, clicking his tongue in irritation. Then—

"Miklotov-sama, I beg you to reconsider after all. To grant someone qualification for the throne merely because they were chosen by the Dragon Insignia is going too far. The crown of the king ought to be given only to one who is truly fitting. It is not simply a matter of making any insignia glow at random..."

"Rickert-dono, aren't you gettin' just a liiittle too heated, hm-m?"

The middle-aged man — Rickert — turning his words toward the nation's current pinnacle of authority to challenge its policy. Dousing his fiery tongue with cold water was a familiar, drawling voice.

Rickert turned a gaze laden with unmistakable hostility sideways, toward Roswaal, who stood several people down the line. Against that piercing stare, Roswaal raised both hands.

"Oho-o, scary, scary. Look at me like that and my poor timid heart just starts to ache, you know."

"Enough nonsense... Roswaal. I am no more satisfied with your conduct, either. And not only I — many at court feel the same. Until now we have overlooked it as an unavoidable consequence of these unusual times, but with exceptions piling up like this, it becomes untenable. House Astrea trying to hoist a street urchin onto the throne is bad enough, but your own foolish act of recommending a half-devil as king..."

"—Rickert-dono, you had best retract those words just now."

A frigid voice echoed quietly through the hall, and Rickert's face, flushed red with agitation, drained pale. The cause was the pressure that came bundled with that voice — Roswaal, still wearing his unchanging smile, merely tilting his head as if with gentle concern.

"Calling a Half-Elf a half-devil is a wretched custom, you know. And all the more so when Emilia-sama remains a candidate for the throne — do you understand which of us has failed to know his place?"

"E-even so. I do not believe my argument is wrong. Possessing the qualification of Dragon Maiden and being a person fit to be monarch are not one and the same. Miklotov-sama!"

Cowed though he was by Roswaal's quiet intimidation, Rickert called out Miklotov's name, sweat beading on his face.

"Please, reconsider. To carelessly select candidates for the throne on this occasion — I must call it premature. What meaning is there in merely tracing the form of the Dragon History Stone's words..."

"—Knight Reinhard."

To Rickert's plea for a change of heart, however, the sage responded not at all, instead calling the name of the red-haired knight. To that call, the knight answered without hesitation, turning his sharp, resolute features toward the dais as if awaiting what would come next.

Miklotov touched his long beard, toying with it between his fingers as if once again engaged in the work of dredging up memory.

"Do you mean to say — do you believe her to be that?"

"I have no certainty. The means to confirm it have already been lost. —But I find myself unwilling to call this many coincidences mere chance."

"Then what would you call it?"

"—Fate, I would call it."

At Reinhard's clear, unclouded answer, Miklotov closed his eyes as if genuinely moved. Listening beside them, Subaru had absolutely no idea what the two of them were talking about. He checked the others' expressions to see if they understood, but Al's face was unreadable as ever, and Ferris and Julius seemed to be in much the same boat.

Only Reinhard and Miklotov, the two of them, seemed to understand each other. As though his patience with this state of affairs had finally snapped, Rickert stepped forward, lips trembling.

"This is meaningless wordplay! Knight Reinhard, have you lost even the proper path of a knight? Such fogged eyes as would drag in a street urchin would suit nothing else..."

"If you are so caught up in mere surfaces that you overlook what truly matters, then it is your own eyes that are worthless, sir. One might well suspect that the loyalty you have long professed to the royal house is nothing but a hollow facade."

What struck down Rickert as he worked himself up to denounce Reinhard was a quiet but utterly uncompromising rebuke, delivered with unmistakable clarity.

Unable to grasp the meaning of the words spun from Miklotov's mouth, Rickert wore a blank, stunned expression for a moment. The same held true for the officials who'd supported him, and Subaru — inwardly gloating at seeing Rickert's nose knocked out of joint — was equally at a loss to understand it.

A brief hush naturally fell over the hall, but Rickert gathered himself and somehow managed to lift his face again. Surprisingly resilient, he spoke on despite his pallor.

"H-how strange a thing to say. Miklotov-sama, you are a cruel man. What exactly is wrong with my loyalty, or with my eyes?"

"Hmm. Well then, does nothing occur to you as you look at Felt-sama?"

At Miklotov's testing words, Rickert turned a puzzled look on Felt.

Though she was the very subject of the conversation, she had been kept at quite a remove from any actual part in it, and at this fresh scrutiny her face twisted into open displeasure. The other candidates, quietly watching how things unfolded, felt much the same, though only Emilia — with whom Subaru had, if only slightly, the longest acquaintance — let a faint trace of inner anxiety show through her expression, which was more than enough to stoke Subaru's worrywart tendencies.

While Subaru nursed such misplaced worries, Rickert studied Felt's appearance carefully, from head to toe.

"What am I meant to notice by looking...? She's still terribly young. Rather than ascending the throne, there is far too much she still needs to learn, before—!"

Following his own words, Rickert had been looking at Felt, about to point out where correction was needed. But his expression suddenly stiffened as though something had struck him, and his eyes went rigid, widening in place. Then he turned those wide-flung eyes toward Miklotov.

"G-golden hair, and crimson eyes—!?"

"Hmm. Indeed, a rare combination of eye and hair color. —One that carries an especially great significance, here in Lugunica."

As Rickert voiced the source of his agitation, an equal shock spread among the officials who grasped his meaning. The only one left uncomprehending was Subaru, lacking as he was in this world's common knowledge.

Glancing beside him, he saw that Ferris and Julius wore expressions of dawning understanding too. Al, as ever, was impossible to read, though he didn't seem especially surprised either. So Subaru, too, decided for the time being to adopt the stance of someone who understood despite understanding nothing, if only to avoid the feeling of being left behind.

But soon enough, the explanation for the part he didn't understand was provided by Rickert himself, who — perhaps wanting to share his own astonishment with those around him — pointed a trembling finger at Felt.

"Golden hair and crimson eyes — that is the physical mark of the Lugunican royal bloodline! But! What manner of absurd tale is this! In the incident half a year ago, every last member of the royal family went into hiding! There ought to be no room whatsoever for anyone to slip in among them..."

"—Are you aware, Rickert-sama, of the incident that occurred within the palace fourteen years ago?"

Reinhard quietly cut off Rickert just as he was about to voice a forceful denial. And at the content of what Reinhard had said, Rickert's expression stiffened further, the word "Impossible" escaping him in a voice that cracked.

"Surely, Knight Reinhard, what you mean to say is..."

"Fourteen years ago, bandits infiltrated the castle, and an incident occurred in which the daughter of the previous king's younger brother — Ford-sama — was abducted. The bandits were allowed to escape as they were, and the whereabouts of his daughter remained unknown ever since."

"Hmm. That was the incident that triggered the dissolution and rebuilding of the former royal guard, was it not. If I recall, your own kin were not entirely unconnected to it, either..."

"I know information I ought by rights to have no way of knowing. I would ask that you infer the rest from that."

To Reinhard's terse reply, Miklotov answered only with a nod. But Rickert's confusion showed no sign of settling. He flailed his hands about.

"That's an extreme argument — no, an outrageous one. Are you saying the daughter who went missing fourteen years ago has been living, fallen into destitution, in the royal capital's slums this whole time, and that you happened to find her there by pure chance? And what's more, that she was found to possess the very qualification of Dragon Maiden!?"

Rattling off the information hurled at him one after another, Rickert then laughed.

"Ridiculous! It's far too neat a story. It would be far less of a stretch to say that you found some girl who qualified as Maiden and then simply dyed her hair and used Magic to change her eye color. —Though surely you wouldn't attempt anything so reckless."

"On my sword."

To Rickert, who had regained some measure of composure, Reinhard raised the sword he'd set upon the floor, letting the blade peek just barely from its scabbard before sliding it back home with a click. A vow given.

At this composed, orderly gesture, Rickert scratched roughly at his thinning head.

"...Every last member of the royal bloodline has already died of illness, and no means exists to confirm kinship. Do not imagine that everyone will bow their heads before an identity built on nothing but speculation."

"That much is only natural. But I am convinced that Felt-sama is indeed the one fit to inherit the throne. Even setting the matter of blood entirely aside."

"For one who is meant to be this generation's Sword Saint, you've grown remarkably attached."

At Reinhard's forthright answer, Rickert let out a sigh as though giving up. Then he turned once more to look at Felt, who had been the subject of the conversation all this while without ever taking part in it.

"Setting aside the matter of possessing the qualification of Dragon Maiden — you hail from the slums. —And, further, there is the possibility that you carry a bloodline of royalty thought lost. The weight of hardship you would be exposed to defies imagining. Do you have the resolve for that?"

It was a testing question, stripped of any lighthearted implication of provocation. Given the flow of the conversation thus far, it was likely also a ritual of sorts — one by which Rickert meant to part ways with his own dissatisfaction, using her own answer as the means.

Sometimes people, even while acknowledging another, must resort to such roundabout means all the same. Subaru, watching from the sidelines, breathed a sigh of relief, thinking this fruitless debate would finally be put to rest — but then—

"Huh? What are you even talking about, old man. I never said one word about being a queen. Don't go deciding stuff on your own."

Completely disregarding the entire flow of the conversation up to that point, Felt jutted out her lips in obvious spite and clearly declared her refusal. Naturally, a wave of shock ran through everyone in the hall, Rickert included. Having stirred up such a wave of emotion, Felt pointed a finger at Reinhard.

"I got dragged here from the slums against my will. I keep telling him to send me back and he won't, he hides my clothes and keeps sticking me in these frilly things instead. It's way past 'annoying' — I haven't accepted a single bit of this."

Ranting it all out in one breath, shoulders heaving, Felt tilted her chin up provocatively, looking up at Reinhard's tall frame. Having risen to his feet, the red-haired young man gave a faint, troubled smile.

"Felt-sama, must you still say such things."

"From where I'm standing, it's your refusal to give up that doesn't make any sense. Listen up! I said I don't want this!"

"—Dragging this on and on, what a supremely tedious tale this has become."

At Reinhard, unbending in his stance, Felt shouted in exasperation. Cutting into this exchange between the two of them was a single girl — one of the royal candidates who had, until now, kept steadfastly silent — Priscilla, standing with arms crossed, narrowing her eyes in boredom.

Atop her folded arms, her ample bosom shifted as she spoke.

"In form, at least, the five needed for the opening are now assembled. Once things properly begin, those unfit will naturally be weeded out. In the end, it is I who will remain standing, regardless. Whether the rest of you superfluous lot possess the qualities of a monarch or not matters not one whit."

"Ahh?"

At Priscilla's outrageous, near-abusive pronouncement, Felt — still worked up as she was — reacted. She hunched her small frame down further, glaring up at Priscilla from directly below in classic thug fashion.

"Been thinking since earlier you're dressed like you're at your own wedding or something, but you're picking a fight in clothes you can't even move in? I'm famous for how quick my feet get, you know."

"Your head sits far too high. Just who do you take me for?"

"Ha, like I'd know—hngh!"

To Felt, who'd tossed out a threat dripping with small-time bravado, Priscilla answered with haughty disdain. Just as Felt tried to laugh it off, her expression suddenly clouded with confusion and shock.

Felt ran a hand through her golden hair, murmuring a small "Huh?" as her body swayed in place as though struck by dizziness.

"—Princess, that's—"

The instant Felt began to collapse, Al cried out beside Subaru, who caught his breath in alarm. Al alone had understood that the cause of Felt's sudden turn was his own mistress's doing. And triggered by his cry, movement broke out all at once throughout the hall.

Moving with what was truly windlike speed, Reinhard gently caught Felt's collapsing body. And the one-armed Al had stepped in as if to wedge himself between Reinhard, now in motion, and the imperiously reclining Priscilla. The Felt Camp and the Priscilla Camp now faced off at close quarters. Even outside that particular standoff, several others had begun to move.

Crusch had crouched slightly, resting a hand at her hip — where nothing at all hung — in a stance as though about to draw an invisible sword. Marcos, the knight-captain, standing relatively close to the candidates, held out a hand as if to restrain her.

The one candidate who had put distance between herself and the gathering, clutching her head and crying "Spare me already—," was Anastasia, and judging by that, she apparently had no combat ability to speak of.

Having carefully taken in each and every one of these movements, Subaru finally found the one he cared about most — Emilia. She too had moved almost simultaneously with Al's cry, reaching a hand out into empty air, a faint glow kindling in her palm.

"Excessive interference through Yang Magic — what were you thinking, using it like this!?"

The faint glow enveloped the swaying Felt, sending waves of healing through her body. The target of Emilia's open, blazing anger was Priscilla, whose face showed not a shred of guilt over her own actions. She waved a hand, looking mildly bothered.

"I merely shared a small portion of the Divine Protection I was born with. If that much elicits such a reaction, one naturally learns the measure of that person's Vessel."

"When you've done something wrong, you apologize, don't you? Or do you only understand once you've been scolded?"

To Priscilla's utterly unrepentant answer, Emilia pressed on, her usual composure returning as she spoke. For a moment, Priscilla wore a blank, dumbfounded look at this, and then her face broke into laughter she plainly couldn't contain. Still wearing that amused expression, she said,

"Ah, now this is amusing. That was enjoyable, for the first time in quite a while — I might even praise you for it."

"You're an unpleasant girl about every single thing, aren't you. What are you—"

"'When you've done something wrong, you apologize,' was it. Then in your case, shall you show us an apology along the lines of, 'I'm sorry for having been born'? Silver-haired Half-Elf."

Even Subaru could tell that a shock had run clean through Emilia's entire body. As if she'd taken a physical blow, her shoulders jolted violently. Her resolute expression from moments before was wiped away, her eyes wrenched open, brimming with anguish.

"I... I have nothing to do with the Witch..."

"What meaning could such an excuse possibly hold, for anyone at all? You are the living image of a taboo the world forbids, and people find themselves unbearably frightened merely by the sight of you. That is precisely why you cling so completely to that scrap of cloth, meant only to paper over appearances."

Against Priscilla's relentless barrage of cutting words, Emilia, her face bloodless, could only shake her head weakly. Subaru could roughly guess at the gist of what they were discussing, but he had no deep knowledge of the circumstances behind it. And so there was simply no way for him to gauge the true depth of the shock Emilia was suffering.

But he understood one thing at least — that she was being wounded unjustly. That alone was enough to set Natsuki Subaru into motion. But—

"Princess, could you cut it out there? Piling up more enemies isn't gonna help me any, seriously."

What held back Priscilla's tyranny was the weak-kneed plea of Al, standing before her. From within that helmet whose expression should have been unreadable, his voice was feeble enough that everyone could sense the troubled look surely hiding behind it.

"Picking a fight with the Sword Saint especially is about as jumbo-sized a headache as they come. Let's just apologize honestly, yeah?"

"For one who is my own attendant, that is a pathetic thing to spout. What of the Sword Saint? He is merely the strongest in this country, nothing more. Do something about it."

"I wouldn't last one minute."

Coolly assessing the gap in strength between himself and his opponent, Al hoisted the white flag early, declaring outright non-resistance. Priscilla looked exasperated at his attitude. Meanwhile, Reinhard and Emilia, facing off against Al, wore expressions that couldn't hide their surprise and bewilderment.

But at least the scene had been prevented from immediately igniting into an outright clash. It was a moment that could have used some cue to reset things, but it seemed unlikely matters would tumble straight downhill from here, either.

While everyone was racking their brains over how to break this deadlock — a sharp, high sound, small though it was, struck the eardrums of every person in the hall without fail.

"—Has everyone had their fill, then?"

The one who had flicked a coin into a piece of pottery to draw everyone's attention was the white-haired old man — Miklotov. Without so much as rising from his chair, he swept his gaze over everyone present, then settled it on Felt, who was shaking her head as she received Emilia's treatment.

"Felt-sama, is your body unharmed?"

"...I guess, somehow. Damn it, if my knife hadn't been taken off me, she'd have gotten it bad."

Answering Miklotov's concern, Felt, still in poor humor, spat one last insult in Priscilla's direction. Even that sore-loser remark had a distinctly small-time ring to it, though the mood hardly allowed for anyone to point that out. Reinhard turned a grateful nod toward Emilia, which she received with a nod of her own, though she still looked out of sorts. Crusch relaxed her combat stance, Marcos let the tension drain from his body in relief, and at last even Al rolled his one shoulder, looking relieved himself.

Only Priscilla, the very cause of the whole affair, remained unchanged, her eyes still bored, without so much as a trace of remorse to be found — which sat poorly with everyone — but for the time being, this particular disturbance had come to an end.

Having confirmed as much, Miklotov made his declaration anew.

"Now then — regarding the true matter at hand, the Royal Selection — I hereby propose that we convene the Council of Wise Men, with all candidates in attendance."
