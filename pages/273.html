Arc 4 Chapter 104: "θ, Part 2"

"That's everything — every fragment of the past I saw down in the tomb."

Having finished her tale, θ said this as she wet her tongue with a sip of tea gone cold.

Sitting on the bed, Subaru had been listening with a solemn expression, and at θ's declaration that the telling of the past was over, he held his breath — then let out everything he'd been holding, long and deep.

He wrung his lungs out to their limit, expelling the air along with all the nameless emotion pooled in his chest — and then he raised his head.

"So that's the true origin of the Sanctuary, and Ryuzu Meyer's memories."

"You already know what became of the Ryuzu swallowed by the blue crystal, don't you, Su-bo. She's held there still, deep in the Witch's research facility, unchanged since that day."

"But the role of that crystal doesn't match what I already know. Echidna told me that the Ryuzu Meyer in that crystal wasn't there for anything to do with the Sanctuary's barrier — she said it was for some other reason entirely..."

According to what Echidna had told him in the dream castle, the reason she'd sealed Ryuzu Meyer in the crystal was as the result of an experiment meant to fulfill her own dream of immortality.

Create replicas of the girl sealed within the crystal, and transcribe her own memories onto them, thereby establishing a pseudo-immortality. That plan had failed to bear fruit — whether through Echidna's own death or some technical flaw — leaving only the perpetual mechanism that, even now, went on producing new Ryuzu replicas as time passed. Wasn't that how it was supposed to be?

But in θ's account just now, not a trace of any such ambition toward immortality had come up.

If anything, several pieces of information had come tumbling out that were far too significant to simply let pass.

"The real reason Echidna made the Sanctuary — I mean, that thing she was trying to shut out. What is it?"

"..........."

"In the story you told, it was called 'Melancholy,' but I've never once heard of or seen anything like that until now. This is the first I've ever heard such a thing existed. I was sure that if anything was hunting Echidna, it had to be the Witch of Envy."

The Witch of Envy, who in the past had utterly destroyed all six of the other Witches of Sin.

He'd assumed all along that the being he'd met in the dream castle was the one who'd driven Echidna to that end. And yet what had surfaced here was an entirely different figure, one whose shadow hadn't so much as flickered into view before now.

Subaru's confusion could hardly be helped.

Still, there was something that rang a bell about that name, "Melancholy" — though it was a story he didn't want to believe.

"The Seven Deadly Sins are Pride, Envy, Wrath, Sloth, Greed, Gluttony, and Lust, right? ...But I've heard that a long time ago, they took a different form — that there were other sins that later got folded into these."

The Seven Deadly Sins were the kind of keyword that made a certain breed of person's heart race — and Subaru was, naturally, no exception. He'd brushed against that knowledge before, as a fragment of trivia, and when he mustered every scrap of memory from back then, sure enough, it was there.

"If I remember right... 'Melancholy' and 'Vanity' — those were definitely counted among the sins."

Sins that might be called the old deadly sins, dropped from the current Seven: "Melancholy" and "Vanity." If "Melancholy" existed, it wouldn't be strange for "Vanity" to exist too.

If there was another being missing from the Witches whose names had gone down through history...

"It'd help if you could work out for yourself why I couldn't go blabbing this memory carelessly."

"The reason...?"

At θ's words, Subaru's brow furrowed, and she wore a crafty, worldly-wise smile that didn't suit her appearance at all.

"Just what you said a moment ago, Su-bo. A being bearing the name 'Melancholy' — I've never once heard of it myself, outside of this memory. Ask anyone, and it's the same. In a world where even if the Witches of Sin's names are forgotten, their existence is still passed down in story — a being that held that much power, and not even its existence is known. ...Do you understand how frightening that is?"

"........."

θ lowered her eyes, and her explanation threw her unease into sharp relief.

It was, indeed, abnormal.

The Witch of Envy was passed down throughout the whole world as a byword for terror and hatred. The other Witches, too — though treated as beings swallowed by the Witch of Envy — undeniably remained in history.

And yet "Melancholy" had left neither shadow nor shape of its existence known anywhere. Was it just coincidence, then, that within the range of things Subaru and θ had come into contact with, the subject had simply never come up?

—Could it really be possible that not even a hint of that being's existence had reached Subaru — who had shown his face at the Witches' Tea Party and met every one of the Witches in person?

"...Did the first Roswaal die? If so, what does that make the current Roswaal, his descendant? Some branch-family line, that kind of blood?"

"I heard the first didn't die in battle. From what I saw in the memory, he was half dead, but he managed to hold onto his life after that — though he apparently couldn't hope to recover enough to live a normal life. ...After that, the first Roswaal, who was already said to have mastered magic, apparently sank ever deeper still into the path of it."

There must have been a time when θ herself had tried to dig at the truth. No one wanted to confirm the truth of that memory — "Melancholy" included — more than she did herself.

But judging by the fact that no clear answer had ever come back to her, her efforts hadn't borne fruit.

"So the Sanctuary's original purpose was to keep 'Melancholy' away from Echidna. Ryuzu Meyer went along with Echidna's scheme and offered herself up, in order to protect this place, the Sanctuary. ...And the replicas being born from the crystal — that's some trick tacked on afterward?"

"A mechanism meant only to produce a barrier has no need of a function to birth replicas. Nine times out of ten, that's exactly it. The question is the motive behind tacking on that replica-producing mechanism afterward."

"If it was Echidna who did it... then the motive's probably that immortality thing she talked about, plain and simple. But even so... I don't get what was going through her head the moment she came up with it."

Finding Ryuzu Meyer asleep in the crystal — what conclusion had Echidna reached?

She had to know of "Melancholy's" existence — according to θ's memory, that much was certain. And yet, for someone who knew, her behavior during her conversation with Subaru had grown steadily more unnatural.

Where was the answer to this unresolved sense of wrongness?

"Guess I still don't have enough pieces to reach an answer, huh."

Grinding his teeth, Subaru cursed the missing pieces of the puzzle's picture.

He scratched at his head and, for the moment, called off the work of dragging out an answer here and now. And then there was the final part of θ's account of the past — the part Subaru couldn't let slip by.

"...In the end, Beatrice lost her friend, huh."

"...Aye, that she did."

Ryuzu Meyer, out of a sense of unworthiness. Beatrice, out of stubbornness.

Perhaps Ryuzu Meyer couldn't admit it right up until the very end, and Beatrice couldn't admit it even in that final moment — neither one able to acknowledge the other.

Ryuzu Meyer, dissolving within the crystal. That curse-like expression of affection she'd left behind at the very end — how deep a wound had it carved into Beatrice's heart?

Was the result of living through that parting the figure of a girl who, for four hundred years, went on rejecting everyone else?

"You lost something so painfully that you're just plain scared to want it again. ...Not that I can't understand how you feel."

He remembered Beatrice, refusing his hand, begging him to "let her die."

Was it because the wound ran so deep and so sharp — four hundred years of solitude, and the memory of the parting with Ryuzu Meyer that had first triggered it — that she couldn't bring herself to expect anything from anyone else?

He felt he finally understood why the heartbroken Beatrice had come to depend on the being Echidna called "that person," wearing her heart down bit by bit over that long span of time.

The wound in Beatrice's heart, from losing her only friend, had gone on scabbing over without ever truly healing. A wound that should have been healed by meeting "that person" and fulfilling Echidna's instructions had, across years that passed with far too much cruelty, festered and swollen until it was nearly ready to burst.

It was precisely because of that, that the girl now felt she'd reached her limit.

"...Has Beatrice ever met you Ryuzus?"

"No, never. By the time we replicas were born, Lady Beatrice had already stopped coming anywhere near the Sanctuary, and she's never visited since. The other replicas don't know this memory, but as someone who does, I think it's better that she doesn't meet us."

"........."

On that extreme point, Subaru agreed with Ryuzu θ's thinking.

Ryuzu Meyer, asleep within the crystal. θ, as a replica, was the very image of her, unchanged since that day. And yet within θ, there existed no memory of having known Beatrice as Ryuzu Meyer herself.

With the wound still unhealed, meeting θ or any of the other replicas now could bring Beatrice nothing but pain and irritation.

And yet—

"Still, she's got to meet you, θ, and everyone else eventually. There's no way around it."

Because that was exactly what was needed for that girl — who had witnessed the very moment the Sanctuary was born, and who had inherited Ryuzu Meyer's wish — to finally get her stopped feet moving again.

"So is that everything — the whole reason you went so far as to run and hide, just to keep the past buried?"

"...Aye. That's all of it. This strange Warlock calling itself 'Melancholy,' existing only within the memory of Ryuzu Meyer that I saw. That it existed, and yet no trace of that fact remains anywhere — that's far too strange. Abnormal."

"Yeah, I agree with you there. Including Echidna's attitude, there's got to be some trick behind it."

"And besides that... there's this."

She broke off, lowering her eyes before Subaru, whose brow was furrowed. Then, without turning her gaze toward him,

"If this Sanctuary is a place born of Ryuzu Meyer's wish — the wish of the progenitor we're all founded on — then breaking the barrier she built by offering up her very life, and setting this place free... what meaning would that carry? I couldn't muster the courage to face it."

"........."

"Times have changed. It's been a century's worth of years since Ryuzu Meyer's day. Back when fear of the Witch of Envy was at its height, half-bloods were scorned, shunned — but surely their treatment has improved, at least a little, by now. ...Enough that something's been urging me to open this place up, to turn our eyes to the world outside."

"...Of course, it's still nowhere near good enough. Prejudiced eyes are still out there, all over the place. I bet plenty of ugly stuff would still happen even after leaving here. But—"

He remembered what had happened at the royal palace.

Emilia, in the hall of the Royal Selection, putting her own will and thoughts into words, enduring the malice directed at her.

In a world where her ideals were realized, the people of this Sanctuary would be saved. Ryuzu Meyer's wish would be fulfilled.

"When Emilia pulls that off, the Sanctuary that was brought to an end will start over again. And if everything goes right, the whole world will become a Sanctuary — for anyone."

"........."

At the very least, Emilia would work toward that. Of the other Royal Selection candidates too, while nothing was certain, about half of them seemed like people who'd put in the effort toward that same end.

By rights, there was no nobility or baseness between one person and another that could be measured by race.

A time would surely come when that idea, so obvious to Subaru, was widely embraced.

"A pipe dream. Pretty words, easy on the ear."

"Yeah. Kind of moves you, doesn't it?"

"Sweet-talking an old crone like me — you're a bad man, Su-bo."

θ chuckled low in her throat, and an air of aged dignity drifted over her childish features.

At Subaru's shrug and quip, θ's face took on a look of something almost radiant, and she said,

"Maybe I've gotten so old that I've started wanting to be fooled by pretty words like that."

"Or maybe it's 'cause you're a girl, and you fell for my dangerous charm."

"Heh."

"That's the first time — out of all you Ryuzus — anyone's ever laughed me off like that!"

Subaru threw both hands up toward the ceiling, striking a pose as if to say he'd been beaten. θ shook her head at his antics and set her empty cup down on the table.

Then she looked up at the crossed silver shields hanging on the wall.

"The world outside this Sanctuary — the time when all of it becomes a Sanctuary..."

"That time's definitely coming. And when it does, it'd be a waste to stay shut up in here. Because there's nothing more fun than flipping off all the people who looked like they thought it couldn't be done, and telling 'em you were the very first ones on board."

Value is born in overcoming being told outright that you can't do it.

That's what a challenge is. That's what it means to take one on.

Because you press on toward some endless, dreamlike place, struggling to live on is a wonderful thing.

"...Fine, Su-bo. Do as you and Lady Emilia please."

"θ..."

"I'd decided from the start. If it was you who found me here, Su-bo, I'd tell you the past and leave the judgment to you. And even after hearing my story, you didn't back down from freeing the Sanctuary... and you laughed off my worries besides."

"I wouldn't call it laughing them off, exactly — wasn't like I had to force some fake cheer or anything."

"But that's fine. Mm. That's just fine."

As if shaking something off, θ nodded again and again, affirming Subaru's resolve.

Watching her, Subaru realized that θ was, at last, reaching her hand out from the prison of memory she'd carried alone for so many years, never able to confide it in anyone.

Whether he could take that hand and lead her out — she'd entrusted that judgment to him.

"So now, there's not a single Ryuzu left who's against freeing the Sanctuary."

"That's right. ...Though that only means anything once the Sanctuary's actually freed, once the barrier's actually broken. Whether the Trials get cleared still comes down to Lady Emilia."

θ spoke as if to tighten the resolve of Subaru, who'd been sighing in relief that one pending concern had vanished. Hearing it, Subaru firmed up his face, which had started to slacken.

Finding θ had, admittedly, let some of the tension out of him. But the problems that had already broken out remained unresolved all the same.

θ, he'd found before Garfiel.

But Emilia still hadn't been found.

"You do figure there's a real chance Lady Emilia can clear the Trials, don't you?"

"I put a plan in motion for that... or, well, I meant to have someone else put it in motion. Except the medicine worked a little too well, and right now she's kind of missing. Calls for some urgent action."

"M-missing!? Th-that's gonna be all right, isn't it!? If Lady Emilia herself fumbles it after I've gone and confessed all this about the past, a whole lot of resolve is gonna go to waste!"

"That worry's fair enough, and it's hard for me to say anything for sure... but listening to your story just now, θ, something occurred to me."

Ryuzu Meyer, who had thrown away her own life to protect the Sanctuary.

Her self-sacrifice, putting others first right up to the very end, overlapped painfully with that girl who, endlessly putting others first, only ever lost out for it.

Even if hard things had piled up on her now, even if she'd been made to suffer over and over until her heart had shriveled—

He couldn't believe that girl would ever lose sight of what was asked of her, of what she had to do.

"So I'm sure I've got a good idea where she'd be."

"........."

"And besides, we've already been tearing this narrow Sanctuary apart searching for her. My partner's probably out there right now, rolling around half in tears looking. And if she still can't be found, there's only one kind of place where that'd be true."

At the certainty on Subaru's face, θ, who'd been somewhat agitated, let out a deep breath. She didn't press him to confirm what he had in mind, only continued with, "In that case—"

"If you find Lady Emilia, and she says she'll clear the Trials... then the final barrier, in other words, comes down to Gar-bo."

"From what Σ told me in bits and pieces, his past is about parting with his mother. You went down into the tomb around the same time, θ — you got any idea what that's about?"

"That boy's fiercely unwilling to talk about family, you know. Part of it's him minding his manners with me, I'd guess... and what happened with Frederica's weighing on him plenty too."

He'd heard that the split between Frederica and Garfiel had become final when Garfiel shook off her hand, after she'd decided to set out for the world outside.

The reason Garfiel had stayed behind in the Sanctuary was to protect the residents — who, unlike quarter-bloods like himself, couldn't leave for the outside world — from anything that might threaten them.

That matched what Echidna had said, too — that Garfiel feared the outside world.

"Trauma from parting with his mother, huh... I wonder if he hates the outside world. I want him to help me out there too, though."

"You and Gar-bo, side by side, is it... Well now. Mm, mm, I reckon that'd be nice."

As θ thought of Garfiel, her lips softening, the factor of childishness fell away from her, and what welled up instead was plainly the motherly affection of someone doting on a grandchild.

Perhaps, for these Ryuzu replicas — four of them dividing up the duty of being one person — the feeling they held in common without ever sharing it directly was exactly this fondness for Garfiel.

And no doubt what Garfiel felt toward the Ryuzus was the very same thing, in return.

"...First, I need to meet up with Emilia."

As for winning Garfiel over, preparations were already underway in case things came to the worst.

Given that it was built into the wager's victory conditions as part of his Contract with Roswaal, a conversation with him was one obstacle Subaru couldn't afford to skip.

And Emilia clearing the Trials was likewise a matter Roswaal had flatly declared "impossible" — something that, apart from anything Subaru did, Emilia would have to overcome entirely on her own.

Having lost her bond with Puck, Emilia had lost an emotional pillar. In exchange, she should also have lost the thing standing in the way of clearing the First Trial — the "lid on inconvenient memories."

At least, that was how it should be if things went according to Puck's plan. Once she'd gotten past that and faced the Trial, a different sight than before ought to be waiting for her.

And in the face of that — would Emilia be able to give the same answer she'd give now?

Even if he couldn't be at her side in that moment, doing everything he could so that she'd remain that way — that was the role Natsuki Subaru had set for himself.

"I know you'd rather go lend Garfiel a hand, but could you stay put here a little while longer, θ? If he can't get anywhere near this place easily, then having you stay unfound like this'll help keep his mind occupied."

"And while Gar-bo's distracted like that, you go off scheming, is that it, Su-bo?"

"Everybody just casually calls whatever I do 'scheming' or 'plotting' like it's obvious — do I really look like some scheming, cold-hearted villain to you people?"

θ gave no answer to Subaru's cocked head.

Feeling something unpleasant in that silence, Subaru scratched his head with a sigh.

"Can I keep to the original plan — today's the target, tomorrow's the spare day...? If Emilia's where I think she is, the remaining concerns are Garfiel and Roswaal."

The two of them, who might obstruct the victory condition within the Sanctuary, were the bottleneck.

And he'd already gone over the arrangements meant to keep that at bay carefully with Otto. The problem was timing, preparation time, and certainty.

Timing was a matter of the moment. Preparation time — the more, the better. Certainty rose in proportion to good timing and ample preparation time. It was an obvious conclusion, but that's exactly how it ought to be.

"Come hell or high water, it's got to be settled by the day after tomorrow. The one thing I can't afford to get wrong is when to make my move. ...Right, θ?"

"Can't say much when all you're asking for is agreement. — But if a young man's got the face of someone who's made up his mind, then that's the time, I reckon. I'll be waiting, expecting good things."

Even without following Subaru's reasoning all the way through, θ affirmed his resolve.

That troubled little smile might well have been one Ryuzu Meyer herself wore often, too. Surely, it was an expression Beatrice had seen countless times.

Persuade Garfiel. Support Emilia. Go and bring Beatrice home.

Even to himself, it seemed like there was far too much to do, and far too many obstacles standing in the way of it.

The dark clouds hanging overhead hadn't cleared, and no clear-cut answer had been found yet, even now.

And yet, strangely, Subaru's spirit wasn't hanging its head.

It was because what he had to do and what he wanted to do lined up as one and the same.

However great the difficulty, now that he could actually see the wall he needed to run at, it was far easier to grasp than the time when he'd been lost, not even knowing what he should be running at.

Because at last, he was ready to reach his hand out toward that shapeless thing called fate.

Slapping both his cheeks with his hands, snapping out a dry crack of sound, Subaru fired himself up.

He grinned at θ, whose eyes had gone wide, gave a light wave of his hand, and headed for the door out of the building.

Then, hand on the door at the entrance, he turned back as if suddenly remembering something, and said,

"Oh, right — how come Ryuzu Meyer talked like a normal girl her age, but you replicas all talk like little old grannies? Some kind of character thing?"

"What are you on about. — I'm plenty age-appropriate myself, thank you."

θ snorted indignantly, planting her short hands on her hips and puffing out her flat chest.

Calling that figure "age-appropriate" was, whatever way you sliced it, a stretch too far.

Picturing in his mind's eye the girl in the dress who, no matter how the years passed, hadn't grown so much as an inch, Subaru stepped outside the building and let the wind wash over him.

His spirit somehow bright and clear, he set off to take on the last confrontation.

—Thus begins Natsuki Subaru's struggle: to bring the Sanctuary to its end, and to make the Sanctuary begin anew.
