Arc 9 Chapter 24: "Natural Enemy of Mankind"

——"Return by Death."

The Authority that trades a life for the chance to turn back time and redo a span of time one had already lived through once. Under the interference of that terrible "Witch of Envy," spending his life again and again to remove every agony and obstacle he faced, Natsuki Subaru had repeated "Return by Death" more times than he could count.

And it was nowhere near so cute a number as two or three times.

By his own reckoning alone, more than ten "deaths" had struck Natsuki Subaru down.

Not once had it been an easy way to die. Not once had an ending come without a sense of loss. Not once had he let go of his life thinking, well, this is fine, then.

Not once had Natsuki Subaru ever wished to let go of his life.

And yet, Natsuki Subaru kept losing his life, kept repeating "Return by Death." Unreason, absurdity, despair — these had taken Subaru's life. But none of that unreason, absurdity, or despair had been truly unavoidable, if only he'd chosen to avoid it. So then, why had Natsuki Subaru been unable to avoid "death"? It was because—

"...I'm too kind."

Because there was no one he could bring himself to wish to lose. Because there was no one he could bring himself to pray to let go of. Because there was no one he could hate enough to think it was fine if they disappeared.

Because there was no one Natsuki Subaru could ever bring himself to think it was all right for them to die.

And so Natsuki Subaru kept losing his life, kept repeating "Return by Death." Every obstacle, every malice, every enemy, every Witchbeast, every Archbishop of Sin had stood in the way of that life. Again and again, they killed Natsuki Subaru, dealt him "death," shattered his soul.

But he knew. — He understood it perfectly well.

It was true that many enemies, outrageous obstacles, unavoidable calamities had heartlessly stolen Subaru's life from him, again and again. That was a fact. There was no mistaking it. But he knew. He felt it more keenly than anyone.

More than any enemy, more than any calamity, more than any absurdity. More than fate, that shapeless malice — there was something that had killed Natsuki Subaru.

——It was, none other than, someone who kept leaning on Natsuki Subaru's kindness without end.

"I mean, I don't think you need to beat yourself up over it that much, y'know?"

"...Even if Subaru says that, it doesn't make me feel any better."

At the reins of the running Dragon Carriage, Petra glanced beside her at the "Natsuki Subaru" propping his cheek on his hand, and let out a sigh laced with worry.

The "Subaru" beside Petra now wasn't the real Subaru. So to speak, he was an illusion her own brain was conjuring up of its own accord — an Imaginary Subaru. As proof, he was a little translucent, and compared to the most recent Subaru in Petra's memory, his features seemed to retain a faint touch of youthfulness.

Probably because the "Book of the Dead" Petra had come to relive was from a timeline about a year and a half back — from those turbulent first days when Petra had just become a maid.

"Both me and Subaru... a lot's happened since then..."

Given the time that had passed since then, it was only natural that, for better or worse, they'd both grown. Simply in terms of height and hair length, Petra had grown, and she prided herself on having made remarkable strides as a maid and as a woman besides. Subaru, meanwhile, had drawn a growth curve running a happy three-legged race — getting along well with Beatrice, being noisily needled by Otto and Garfiel, and growing heartwarmingly alongside Emilia — and was well on his way to becoming a truly dashing, rugged sort of man.

So it was only natural that the "Subaru" of that time looked a bit younger by comparison to now.

——And yet, even so, he was still handsome. Wasn't it downright unfair that he could lose some of his current manliness and ruggedness and still have this much cuteness and charm?

"No, no... this isn't the time for that, me."

Pressing a hand to her forehead, Petra shook her head slowly side to side, chiding her own inner thoughts. It was fine — she could control herself properly. After all, this wasn't the first time Petra had conjured Subaru up in her head like this, having him say the things she wanted to hear, strike the poses she liked. She was a veteran of a hundred battles, imagining romantic picture-tales and love poems, all sorts of scenarios, placing herself and Subaru into them as characters, heating up her cheeks and her chest. If she thought of those as rehearsals, then the Imaginary Subaru in front of her now was just... more real, more lifelike, with no shortage of realism.

"That's already way past the range of ordinary imagination, isn't it? Not exactly a level willpower's gonna fix."

"It's fine, that's about how I feel about it. And besides — stop peeking into my head."

"That's asking the impossible! Telling me not to peek — I'm inside Petra's head, y'know!?"

"——"

Startled by that retort from the wide-eyed "Subaru," Petra caught her breath without meaning to. Seeing her reaction, "Subaru" scratched his cheek with a finger, as though reflecting that he'd said too much.

That look in his eyes, the one that softened whenever he was being considerate of someone — so thoroughly, awkwardly Subaru-like — made Petra love him all over again.

——Natsuki Subaru's "Book of the Dead" had invaded Petra Leyte's "heart."

That was how Petra analyzed what had happened to her. Ezzo Cadner, who'd originally been an avid reader of the "Book of the Dead," had said that reading the book was a challenge that placed a tremendous strain on the mind — but what it had brought Petra was a shock beyond even that warning.

Though, the shock Petra had felt from Subaru's "Book of the Dead" might well have been something entirely different from what Ezzo had feared.

"...'Return by Death,' and beyond the Great Waterfall..."

What Natsuki Subaru carried was far, far larger than anything Petra could have imagined. All of it — the feelings Subaru had carried leaving his homeland, meeting Petra and the others alone, what he'd endured to save them, what resolve he'd carried into battle for their sake — no, what Petra had come to know of it was only the smallest fraction.

Because she simply couldn't believe that Subaru, burdened with such a tragic fate, had never once used that Authority since the point recorded in the "Book of the Dead" Petra had picked up. Surely, even after that, Subaru had again and again tasted that sensation — his soul torn away, his heart clawed at, his life trampled underfoot — endured it, endured through it, all the way to today.

"——"

The irreparable scar on the mind that Ezzo had feared had indeed been carved into her. Probably, no doubt, needless to say — her own heart had taken a deep wound, her mental balance had crumbled without a trace, and she was in a precarious state; Petra could see that much, looking at herself from outside.

Exchanging words with a beloved who had died and yet had not died, an imaginary beloved conjured in fantasy, letting his words make her heart soar and sink even in a situation like this. This was, without question, a mind completely diseased.

"But it's fine."

Love is blind, love is a fever. Petra Leyte had been eaten away by her feelings of love, and had lived up to this very day on them. She wasn't going to indulge in the convenient notion that as long as she knew she was being abnormal, she could control it. No matter how confidently one claimed to have sound judgment, it was the same as driving a car with alcohol in one's system. Neither Petra nor Subaru had a driver's license, nor any memory of drunk driving, but it was the same thing.

"Cars, drunk driving, traffic lights, toryanse..."

Drawn along by the words that happened to surface in her mind, knowledge came spilling out one after another in a rush, like out-of-season clothes tumbling out of a crammed closet. All of it — words Petra knew, words she didn't know — drifted lazily back and forth across the border between understanding and incomprehension, brand-new old friends all at once. If she didn't sort through them carefully, one by one, she'd probably find herself, before she knew it, slipping into men's clothes and walking around carrying a Guiltywhip.

"Wait, hold on, is my main weapon a whip? Isn't that a little too edgy? Sounds like I'm being way too clingy with Beatrice too — I can't keep up with the generation gap..."

"By the way, everyone gave the master a good scolding, Garf-san reflected on his chuunibyou phase, Otto-san isn't sleeping, and Emilia-neesama is working really hard and doing wonderfully."

"I see, so Roswaal's the mastermind behind everything... eh? Why'd that guy get off scot-free?"

"That's between you and Emilia-neesama to talk out..."

Now, Petra still hadn't forgiven Roswaal even now, and though she hadn't fully opposed the verdict Subaru and Emilia had reached back then, she didn't approve of it either. Of course, there had been plenty of times, the movements in the Empire chief among them, when having Roswaal around had been a lifesaver. But that was a separate matter from this, and Petra intended never to forgive him for the rest of her life.

But — right now, there was someone else even less forgivable than Roswaal—

"——Petra."

"...Even if you say that, it's still no, me."

A quiet, gentle tone of voice, an earnest gaze — there was no escaping the combined force of "Subaru" that forcibly made her forget her own breathing and heartbeat, except to look away like a child. As if ignoring the shameless pounding demanding attention from deep in her chest, Petra turned her focus forward, toward the sea of sand that seemed to stretch on without end.

——Right now, Petra and the others' Dragon Carriage had left the Pleiades Watchtower behind and was racing full tilt straight through the Augria Sand Dunes.

Somewhere beyond this sea of sand was a back she had to catch up to. Her opponent was using the wings of the "Divine Dragon," moving with mobility beyond even an Earth Dragon under the "Wind-blocking Divine Protection" — he had likely already leapt clean across the sand sea in one go. Catching up would be no ordinary feat. And there was another reason Petra and the others had to hurry, besides.

"——The 'Witch of Envy.'"

Sharing consciousness with the pondering Petra, "Subaru" spoke the reason for their haste. Driving the Dragon Carriage without a care for appearances, having already put a considerable distance between themselves and the Pleiades Watchtower over several hours — and still the pressure refused to let go of Petra and the others' souls.

That was the terrible "Witch," supposedly sealed within her shrine, ceaselessly reaching out to punish Petra for having read the "Book of the Dead" and trespassed into forbidden territory. The great tidal wave of that black shadow's demonic hand, one that threatened to invade and destroy the world, was, right now, being held back by a single hero, all alone.

——The "Sword Saint," Reinhard van Astrea.

His lone, desperate struggle was holding the "Witch of Envy's" rampage back beyond the tower, keeping the world's collapse at bay by the skin of its teeth, maintaining a fragile balance. Even now, though, a roar and tremor fit to overturn heaven and earth, flashes like distant thunder, kept occurring intermittently, with no distinction between day and night, as if the trumpet of the apocalypse were being blown without end. Just how long could this balance hold? Neither Petra nor anyone else could say. If there was one thing that was certain, it was only this—

"If Reinhard goes down, everything's over."

"Yeah. If Reinhard-san... disappears, the 'Witch' will catch up to me. Until then, there's no telling how much damage there'll be."

"Doesn't seem like the type to politely yield the right of way to everyone around her."

At that joke from "Subaru," Petra pictured a kindly "Witch of Envy" giving up her seat to an elderly person on a crowded train, but she couldn't quite bring herself to laugh right now. In truth, if she thought about minimizing the damage, regardless of whether Reinhard won or lost, Petra ought to have stayed at the Pleiades Watchtower and accepted being silenced by the "Witch." But the instant Petra so much as considered that—

"——That's absolutely not happening. I refuse, even if it kills me."

"——"

Told that flatly, head-on, by "Subaru," Petra found her objection sealed off. She'd long been aware that she was precious to Subaru. Of course, there was Emilia, there was Beatrice. Rem too. She'd never been so conceited as to think she was treated more specially than them — but even so, she'd been certain: that she had a proper place on the shelf Natsuki Subaru had made in his heart, for carefully keeping precious things. But she hadn't known, until today, just how much weight lay behind the words "even if it kills me," spoken by this Subaru who so often puffed up his chest as a joke.

Because Natsuki Subaru truly was a person who would protect what mattered to him, "even if it killed him."

"...Mm. I understand that, properly. I don't want to die either. ...Al-san has some way to stop the 'Witch' prepared, right?"

"Yeah. He's not stupid either. He knows exactly how much the world could be destroyed. He's definitely got something ready to settle things before that happens."

"Right, definitely."

"Absolutely."

Fist clenched, "Subaru" declared it with such force that Petra nodded back in agreement. Catch up with Al, who'd left the tower, and pry the details of his plan for restraining the "Witch" further out of him. Organizing the necessary steps made her head spin, but how reassuring "Subaru's" words were. — But sadly, the same thing that had just applied to "Subaru" applied to Petra as well.

To the "Subaru" sharing consciousness with Petra, her thoughts and anxieties were laid bare. And to Petra, sharing consciousness with "Subaru," his were laid just as bare in turn.

"If Al-san... wants to end the world..."

Then the certainty Petra and "Subaru" had just confirmed a moment ago would fall apart. In the worst case, Al might have no plan at all for stopping the "Witch of Envy," and might simply be letting the world be destroyed, dragging his own dying heart down along with it. Having lost Priscilla Barielle — his beloved master, or perhaps not simply beloved but someone for whom he'd held a far deeper love — it would be no wonder if Al hated the world. And yet, the fact that she couldn't help but cling to a thread of hope was also true.

"Me, and Meili-chan too, and Garf-san and everyone — we're all alive."

Al, who had even brought the "Divine Dragon" Volcanica to his side, had held the power of life and death over Petra and the others back at the tower. And he had chosen not to take their lives, but to exercise the right not to take them. Was it overthinking it to believe this was one small fragment of hope still remaining within Al?

"...Maybe it's not overthinking it, but maybe it's not very me, either."

"Eep."

Beside Petra, murmuring with a finger to her lips, "Subaru" gave a deliberately exaggerated flinch of his shoulders. Glancing over, Petra narrowed her eyes at the awkward, ingratiating smile "Subaru" gave her, waving a hand.

She had no idea just how much influence "Subaru" had on her thinking right now. There was no way the influence of the "Book of the Dead" was nothing more than a bonus feature for Petra, letting her merely see an Imaginary Subaru.

"I have to be careful..."

Petra loved Subaru dearly, but she had no wish to become Subaru. And besides, she shouldn't become the sort of person who scattered kindness at anyone and everyone, the way Subaru did. She admired kind people, always wished to be kind herself, but people like Subaru and Emilia — it was enough that Subaru and Emilia themselves existed. Petra wanted to be someone who didn't take the kindness of kind people for granted, who never overlooked it as simply natural. And to do that, she mustn't get too caught up in Natsuki Subaru's own feelings.

"—Petra-chan, time to switch, okay~?"

Just as she reached that thought, the Dragon Carriage's little window opened, and a voice called out to the back of her head. It was Meili, who'd been resting in the carriage. Petra shook her head at the girl who offered to take over as driver, saying, "No, I'm good."

"I'm fine, so Meili-chan should rest. You already carried me while I was sleeping, and you've even been chasing off the Witchbeasts for us."

"Not as much as Flam-chan, carrying around the fangs-guy and the teacher-person~. Besides, with the 'Sword Saint' and the 'Witch' having their little fight, the Witchbeasts don't seem to be in any state to bother us anyway."

Shrugging, Meili's expression bore a heavy trace of fatigue, and it was plain at a glance that even inside the carriage, her mind hadn't been able to rest. In truth, being in a situation where she could sense the "Witch's" presence from afar was like being a frog perpetually eyed by a snake — staying calmly at rest would be the strange thing. And given how surprisingly strong Meili's sense of responsibility was, that would have made it all the harder for her.

"What about Flam-chan? Is she still asleep?"

"Yeah, still out cold~. But we do want to rely on Flam-chan's Divine Protection, so it's for the best that she's like that~."

"This girl... Meili being here at all is such a strange feeling too..." — the muttered thought of the "Subaru" trembling at the girl's presence overlapped with Meili's own wistful answer. Setting aside the latter's reaction, Petra turned her mind to the Flam who'd come up in conversation, and to her Divine Protection.

Flam's "Telepathy Divine Protection" allowed her to convey words to her twin sister once a day. Only a single exchange, but as a means of sending and receiving thoughts regardless of distance, it was, in the current situation, a position too important to give up no matter what was traded for it. The condition for that Divine Protection — "once a day" — seemed to be closely tied to sleeping time, apparently, and the more of an emergency it was, the more they needed her to sleep. Though the action Flam had taken to make that happen was beyond anything Petra could have imagined.

"I can't believe she just... choked herself out on the spot..."

"She must not have been able to just rest normally with the 'Sword Saint' on her mind. But being able to just decide to do that anyway — that really does feel like something someone connected to that 'Sword Saint' would do. Even though she's smaller than me or Petra-chan, that girl is way far from normal."

"I don't think you're one to say that, Meili-chan." "Look who's talking."

Petra's and "Subaru's" retorts overlapped, and Meili twisted her lips into a discontented pout. Only Petra's words had reached her ears, but by deliberately keeping up this out-of-place, everyday sort of exchange, both of them were, in their own way, looking away from the pressure of the "Witch." And it was likely thanks to hearts steadied that way that they'd been able to keep racing on, single-mindedly, all this way.

"——!!"

A sudden, high-pitched cry — it belonged to the one who, more than Petra, more than Meili, more than Flam, had been giving everything with more desperate effort than anyone this whole way — Patrasche. Patrasche, who had shared the urgency of the situation with Petra and the others ever since the events at the Pleiades Watchtower, had cried out to announce their arrival at their destination — the end of the sand sea. There, the town nearest the sea of sand, Mirula, came into view.

"Petra-chaaan!"

"Yeah, I know!"

Answering Meili's call, Petra gripped the reins and leaned forward. Patrasche, who'd spared a single glance back, had already turned her focus forward again, plunging into a final sprint after running without rest the whole way here.

"Love you, Patrasche."

The intangible Imaginary Subaru offered up words of gratitude and love that would never reach her, to Patrasche. Watching an untouchable hand stroke the Earth Dragon's neck, in her small head Petra was already unfolding the roadmap of what needed to be done once they arrived at Mirula.

At the end of that roadmap, the final goal raised up was, of course, stopping the actions of Al, who had betrayed Natsuki Subaru — no, there was something beyond even that. And to reach it, too—

"——!"

The Dragon Carriage tore through the town gate at the entrance and arrived at Mirula. Judging by that that they'd made it out of the Augria Sand Dunes, Petra opened her mouth wide. Whatever they did, there was someone whose help Petra and the others had to borrow first — he was the one who had seen them off from this very spot, saying that he himself could not enter the Augria Sand Dunes. So, in other words, that meant—

"——I'm back, so lend me your strength! You can hear me, can't you, Clind-oniisama!"

Calling out for the wildcard who could close the distance she'd opened up to zero, Petra raised her voice.

At the small shadow that flickered across the edge of her vision, Felt scowled magnificently. Their form, their coloring, their glossy sheen inspired pure physiological revulsion — she'd shared a sleeping place with them in the slums since she was old enough to remember, but she felt not a shred of camaraderie or familiarity with them. Felt, who'd once even slept hugging a giant rat to fend off the cold, still felt reluctant even at the thought of befriending a single Zodda Bug. They truly were, without exaggeration, a loathsome existence deserving the title natural enemy of mankind.

"Seriously, Zodda bugs really do show up anywhere, don't they..."

With their absurd, out-of-this-world vitality, Zodda Bugs secured living space anywhere at all. More often than not you spotted them in unsanitary places, but that wasn't an absolute condition either. In fact, the place Felt had just glimpsed a Zodda Bug out of the corner of her eye this time was inside a building that was, comparatively speaking, well-managed and well-maintained — a mansion in the noble district of the royal capital, Lugunica. Lavish, extravagant, filled with paintings and ornaments whose value Felt couldn't begin to fathom — that place, even now that it had lost its master, remained just as ostentatious as ever.

"Oi, that thread of yours can reach pretty far, right? Can't you just, like, zip it out and exterminate the Zodda real quick?"

"Haaah~, well, you see, Felt-sama. This Steel Thread Technique I use is an ultra-rare, legendary skill that hasn't produced a practitioner in like a century, you know? And it's only with that skill, plus the special steel-thread ring made by the 'Curse Tools craftsman' Groovy Gumlet, that Yae-chan's amazing skills as a shinobi maid can be properly shown off. And you want to grab me for something like Zodda Bugs? Seriously?"

"About half of that was too long for me to keep up with. So, what, in the end?"

"I don't wanna get my beloved thread dirty with something so icky, that's a no from me~."

Saying that, Yae tucked her ringed hands behind her back and turned her face away, and Felt gave a listless "yeah, yeah, whatever" in reply. Glancing back after that shadow from before — right, left — apparently she'd lost sight of it. That, too, left an unpleasant feeling.

"Just 'cause I can't see it anymore doesn't mean it's vanished from this world, y'know... far from vanishing, sometimes those things come flying straight at your face."

"I know what you mean. Once that happens, you're basically bracing for death, right~?"

"I was gonna say 'no you're not,' but yeah, don't brace for it."

Feeling-wise, resignation or despair felt closer to the mark than "bracing for death." Anyway, having found unexpected common ground with Yae over the sight of the Zodda Bug's shadow, Felt looked anew at the interior of the mansion — the Barielle townhouse of the royal capital. Some Lugunica nobles who lived outside the capital kept a secondary residence in the capital's noble district for use during visits. The Astrea Manor, where Felt had first been abducted and held by Reinhard, had been one such place — and this mansion, the one Priscilla Barielle had had built, had been constructed for the same purpose.

"Knowing that princess, she probably had the place kept in perfect order even while she wasn't living here, but it sure doesn't look like it, what with Zodda Bugs wandering around. Or did the maids just lose all motivation the second their mistress died?"

"Ooh, even if it's meant to provoke, you probably shouldn't say things like that where Al-sama can hear~? It'll make you sound like a really nasty person, and I'm not exactly thrilled hearing my lady badmouthed either, you know~?"

"Heh? What, don't wanna piss off the helmet-guy?"

"Nope, nope, Al-sama won't get angry, I'm sure. But I think he'd get hurt. And that, personally, I can't really welcome."

Her tone dropping just slightly, Yae tilted her head at the end of the exchange. Instantly, Felt felt a faint tension at her own throat, sensing with her very life the presence of a thread too fine to see. The steel thread that had hesitated to be used on the Zodda Bug was now wrapped around Felt's neck instead.

"If you hurt me, won't the helmet-guy be pissed at you?"

"Maybe~. But that doesn't mean it's okay to let Felt-sama say whatever she wants either, does it? As the all-powerful, all-purpose maid who removes every difficulty in her master's way?"

"Something about calling him 'master' doesn't sit right. Your relationship with the helmet-guy isn't exactly master and servant, is it. Though — I get that it'd be lame of me to keep running my mouth from a position of safety."

Saying that while raising both hands, Felt showed Yae she had no intention of resisting. After watching her for a moment, Yae shrugged and withdrew the steel thread. Even so, if Felt made any suspicious move, Yae would launch the thread without mercy and, as an example, slice off a finger — or if necessary, a limb — to make her point. — The fact that Felt could gauge that speed and precision so clearly meant the provocation had been worth it.

"Figured as much, but you're way out of my league to deal with..."

Right now, the only two people stationed at this Barielle townhouse were Felt and Yae. If Felt could slip past Yae's watch, or somehow defeat her single-handedly, she could escape this captive-princess situation meant to keep the Felt Camp's movements in check.

"That fighting spirit really does show you're a Royal Selection candidate — makes my servant's soul swoon at such a noble spirit, but please don't go thinking anything reckless, okay~? There are people like rowdy thugs or Heinkel-sama that I wouldn't feel bad about tying up or slicing to pieces with thread, but personally, I don't like doing that to cute, lovely women."

"Is that so. Then remember this too — I don't like being called 'cute' or 'lovely' or 'that looks great on you' either. I'm keeping count of how many times I've been called that."

"Hyaa~, scary. By the way, what are you gonna do once you've counted?"

"When the time comes I can pay it back, I'll kick that many kicks to the butt."

By the way, Yae already had three butt-kicks coming to her in Felt's book. The helmet guy — Aldebaran — and the "Divine Dragon," and Heinkel too, each had their own tally of grudges owed. Felt settled her debts precisely. Debts of gratitude, debts of grudge — she made no distinction.

"Not like I'll get many chances to kick a dragon's butt, let alone the 'Divine Dragon's.' Come to think of it, being a hostage is actually kinda funny when you put it that way, huh."

"Tough as ever~, you're too strong mentally, even Yae-chan's a little taken aback. Even setting that aside, I don't want to let Felt-sama and Bol-sama get too close — I'm scared of what might get sweet-talked into happening."

"Even so, having Reinhard's old man look after the 'Divine Dragon' — if you ask me, that's a pretty bad choice, taste-wise."

"Heinkel-sama's basically got two or three extra hands reaching out for that 'Dragon's Blood,' after all. I just hope he doesn't get impatient and go slashing at Bol-sama on impulse."

Pressing a finger to her lips, Yae smiled mischievously, though there was nothing remotely cute about what she'd just said. In truth, from Yae's perspective, even if that hypothetical scenario actually played out — meaning, even if the reckless Heinkel got himself killed by the "Divine Dragon" — it probably wouldn't be much of a problem to her. Considering her attitude toward Heinkel, who was supposed to be an ally, it really was a spitefully unbalanced way of sorting people out. Of course, the "Divine Dragon" was far too conspicuous to bring anywhere near the royal capital, so given that they didn't want him kept together with Felt, who might sway his heart, the lopsided grouping couldn't really be helped.

"Felt-sama isn't so much a beauty who topples nations, as a beautiful girl who could topple a dragon."

"That's another butt-kick added to the tally. ...Actually, for real — that 'Dragon's Blood' that old man wants so badly, can it even be taken from that dopey 'Divine Dragon'?"

"Who knows~? Ah, but even if it can't, Al-sama intends to keep his promise even if it means recovering the older 'Dragon's Blood' said to be stored somewhere in the castle. So the sleeping Heinkel-sama's wife should end up waking up regardless. Felt-sama, you're not exactly on board with that way of doing things, are you~?"

"Yeah. Honestly, I don't like that way of doing it."

She'd said as much directly to Heinkel too, back when he'd been rambling out excuses. Felt didn't like that method. But she also thought it wasn't her place, as an outsider, to flatly deny what someone involved had thought through, acted on, and decided for themselves. So even if it meant waking Reinhard's mother, Felt wanted to aim for a different path than Heinkel's, one that would still reach the same summit.

"——? What."

Slouched carelessly on the sofa in the parlor, Felt noticed Yae's gaze fixed intently on her, and knit her brows. At Felt's reaction, Yae shook her head, saying "no, no~," and

"I was just a little surprised, that's all. That Felt-sama would be thinking of trying to wake Heinkel-sama's wife... in other words, the 'Sword Saint'-sama's mother."

"...That jerk, y'know, sat down right next to me when we all ate together."

"——?"

At Felt's remark, which sounded, on the face of it, entirely unrelated, Yae tilted her head, puzzled. Ignoring Yae's confusion, Felt went on. In the Felt Camp, at mealtimes Flam and Grassis would come call everyone, and — aside from Felt, whose seat at the head of the table was fixed by convention — it was an unspoken rule that whoever arrived at the dining hall first filled in the seats. Next to Felt, at the head of the table, it was usually Reinhard who ended up sitting. And—

"While we're eating, that guy's ugly mug is right there in the spot I see best. If that mug looks even a little less awful, the food I'm eating tastes better, don't it?"

"...I take back what I said about beautiful girl. Felt-sama, you're quite the ladies' man~."

"No idea what you just said, but I'm adding another butt-kick to the count."

"Ehh~, why~!"

At Yae, who pouted discontentedly, Felt turned her face away and offered nothing more. Being used to being held hostage was, frankly, nothing to be proud of, but the trick to keeping her mental footing steady in that state was not to run her mouth and spill everything. And so, while she toyed a little with the fish she'd hooked, letting her stew—

"Yes, yes, sorry if I put you in a bad mood. Anyway, here~."

Saying that, Yae gently held out a silver tray, on which sat a light snack — a grilled sandwich with fillings baked into bread — and Felt's stomach gave a little growl. At the sound, Yae gave a small laugh, and

"Is there a Zodda Bug in there too?"

"Those things are everywhere, y'know. Probably in my stomach too... wait, don't make me picture something that gross. Just hand it over already."

Glaring at Yae's teasing tone, Felt snatched the silver tray out of her hands. Biting into the faintly warm, fragrant grilled sandwich, the taste was just right — not too strong, not too bland. It suited Felt's tastes, and catching her eye, Yae flashed a smile and gave a thumbs up.

"How is it, the grilled sandwich? It's actually Yae-chan's one and only specialty dish, you know~."

"It's good... but if it's your 'one and only,' that kinda changes things. You're a maid, aren't you, supposedly? Or was that a lie too, and you're actually an assassin or something?"

"Hmm, well, assassination is one of a shinobi's main jobs, but me being a maid is the real deal, honestly. It's just that I'm better at cleaning up than at cooking. Ah, and I lied about the grilled sandwich being my only specialty. I'm also good at layer-wraps."

"That's still just sticking filling in bread, ain't it, in the end."

By the way, Felt's impression was that the layer-wraps had more vegetables among the fillings, more or less. In truth, she didn't really understand the finer differences between a grilled sandwich and a layer-wrap. Anyway, it was a relief that even a captured hostage was being given proper meals. At this rate, she probably wouldn't need to keep suspecting every word out of Aldebaran's mouth as a lie. Still, if that was the case, then the actions of the absent Aldebaran, who wasn't here right now, became a problem in their own right. Aldebaran, off acting separately from Felt and the others — the place he'd headed to was—

"——Is the helmet-guy seriously planning on bringing an Archbishop of Sin out of there?"

If she was going to talk about believing him, that meant believing his declaration too — that he'd gone to free the "Gluttony" Archbishop of Sin held captive in the royal capital's Prison Tower. Currently, the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica had two Archbishops of Sin in custody: "Wrath" and "Gluttony." Felt herself had crossed paths directly with an Archbishop of Sin back in Priestella, and had been deeply impressed by their menace and utter inhumanity. That there were people in this world this far beyond saving.

"In Priestella, I went face-to-face with 'Gluttony'... whoever's the older or younger brother of the one that's caught, and his sister too. And I went along with Reinhard for 'Wrath's' transport as well. So take it from me — what the helmet-guy's trying to do is straight-up insane."

"Well, that's probably true~. Any great undertaking, after all, cannot be accomplished by a sane mind. Isn't everything Al-sama has done up to now completely beyond the pale?"

"It ain't about the number, but it's gotten to a point where you gotta ask how many lines he's crossing. And, y'know, using the 'Witch' to pin Reinhard down, fine, I can still get that. But an Archbishop of Sin?"

"Obviously, that must be necessary for Al-sama's goal too, I'd think~."

"And the details of that, you don't know either, huh."

Hands clasped behind her back, Yae only smiled and gave no answer to Felt's words. Still, that answer was more or less what she'd expected. Felt didn't know Yae's true reason for following Aldebaran, but from her she sensed a fierce will, an obsession — that no matter what shocking circumstances came to light, she had no intention of parting ways with Aldebaran. So from here on, it wasn't an answer Felt should wait for — it was something she had to dig out herself.

"...That 'Gluttony' bastard goes around rudely devouring people's 'names' and 'memories.'"

Like Crusch, whose "memories" had been devoured. Like that Julius, the knight of Anastasia whose "name" had been devoured, as the story went. And like the girl in the Emilia Camp whom Subaru had apparently been searching for a way to wake up. — It was possible, come to think of it, that this last girl wasn't entirely unrelated to the "Sleeping Beauty" Felt and the others knew of. Either way—

"—There's something he wants to feed her. Otherwise, there's no way he'd go through the trouble of dragging that bastard 'Gluttony' out of confinement."

A captive, forced into helplessness, Felt refused to let her spirit be broken. That, surely, was — as much as anyone in this world besides Reinhard — the life philosophy she'd learned from old man Rom, who'd been reliable enough to corner that very Aldebaran: "live fiercely."

"Om."

Heartily filling her stomach with the grilled sandwich, Felt devoted herself to putting that philosophy into practice. As long as she didn't stop thinking, didn't stop moving her feet, room would open up for her hand to reach something. In preparation for that moment, she licked the sandwich sauce off the corner of her mouth with her tongue.

Meanwhile—

"...Whoa, seriously, a Zodda Bug. It's only been a few days since I let the caretaker go, and already, ugh, no thanks~."

Whether it was the same one Felt had glimpsed earlier or a different one, Yae — who seemed to have spotted the natural enemy of mankind — scowled in distaste and let slip a grumble like that.

△▼△▼△▼△

——It was a cramped, dark, suffocating place.

Even the word "solitary cell" felt too gentle for it — a space with no sense that it had been meant to keep whatever was locked inside it alive. But then, perhaps nothing was as violent as summing up that space, and the existence confined within it, with words like "cruel" or "merciless."

After all, what had been held captive there was a blasphemer without peer, guilty of countless sins across the world. Even the manner of its confinement broke sharply from the ordinary — a "black coffin" requiring neither breath nor heartbeat, that isolated and sealed away an existence from the world entirely. As such, there was no need for that space to accommodate any regard for life. Which was precisely why that place was, if anything, ideally suited for the mutual cursing of lives.

"...My bad. Guess I'd built up a fair bit of pent-up frustration myself. Couldn't hold back."

Footsteps ringing on the cold, hard floor, the man leaned his back against the wall of the cramped room. The back of his helmet struck the wall, and the sound of metal against stone shook the frigid air of the stone chamber with a jarring edge. At the feet of the man — Aldebaran — trying to steady his faintly ragged breath, a figure lay collapsed face-down on the floor, barely breathing.

That figure was none other than the blasphemer granted this cell-like stone chamber by the kingdom itself. The pathetic loser who'd just been utterly crushed by Aldebaran and reduced to licking the floor — the Witch Cult's Archbishop of Sin in charge of "Gluttony," Roy Alphard himself.

"——"

Drawing a deep breath, then letting it out in a rush. Having steadied his heaving lungs with a forced deep breath, Aldebaran looked around the stone chamber, quite a wreck after roughly a minute of real time, and shrugged. The walls were cracked, the floor shattered, and it would be fair to say the destruction had raged out of control in this cramped, ill-maintained room until there wasn't a spot left to put a foot down. The place had barely the floor space of a proper café's restroom, and yet somehow, an all-out brawl had unfolded right here. Though, it hadn't been a brawl he'd wanted to have, either.

"...I literally just said 'come along quietly and I won't take your life.' If you're gonna rebel, you'd normally wait for a better chance than that."

"——. Hahha, that's a funny thing to say, helmet mister."

At Aldebaran's somewhat exasperated words, Roy, sprawled spread-eagle on the ground, gave a sticky sort of laugh. Still face-down, he turned his gleaming eyes toward Aldebaran by the wall, and, licking his lips lewdly with that long tongue of his,

"You mean, you want me to learn to sit and wait, when I'm always, always, always, always starving? Isn't that basically just abuse?"

"Unfortunately for you, I'm not under any obligation to raise a delinquent brat like you. Back in the Showa era there was some talk about neighbors pitching in on discipline besides just family, but this is Lugunica, and it ain't the Showa era either. Well, given how thoroughly I just beat you down, I can't exactly deny the 'abuse' part."

Tapping the metal fittings of his helmet, making them click, Aldebaran drew a delighted "hahh" out of Roy. Given such thorough discipline — both arms and legs carefully broken — to still be in this good a mood was, frankly, an unnervingly creepy kid. Even in the corporal-punishment-approved Showa era, this level of "discipline" wouldn't have flown. Still, Roy Alphard was an Archbishop of Sin before he was an unnervingly creepy kid, so this much risk mitigation ought to be permissible.

"Well, I never expected this to go smoothly, anyway."

Muttering that, Aldebaran quietly tallied up a running count of six thousand and twenty-two in his head. That number, which by now needed no explanation, was the number of attempts it had taken Aldebaran to fully discipline the defiant Roy Alphard. If he started counting broken bones and teeth on top of that, the number would easily multiply several times over. That was how much effort it had taken to discipline this delinquent brat.

"Didn't expect I'd end up going through trial and error not unlike what I did with Reinhard..."

Roy Alphard, who went by the name "Gluttony" without shame, had a "Gluttony" Authority — the power of "Corrosion," reproducing the skills and abilities of whoever he devoured. Which meant Roy effectively had countless warriors stocked up inside himself — a fighting style that instantly abandoned any tactic that didn't work and just as instantly pulled out something new, evoking Reinhard, who could sprout whatever Divine Protection the situation called for and respond in kind. Fortunately, Aldebaran already had more than a hundred and thirty thousand experience points' worth of fighting Reinhard under his belt, so he'd managed the feat of handling this similarly off-the-charts opponent in just four digits' worth of attempts.

"Still, given you were captured right after fighting at the tower, and haven't gotten decent treatment since — I guess you put up a pretty good fight, all things considered."

"Eeh? What, all that stuff where every single thing I tried got completely shut down for the first time ever — is that you trying to console me, mister? If that's the case, then you know, the best way to console us would be—"

"Overeating and overdrinking'll come back to bite you the next day. Just hearing about it makes an old man whose stomach's already having trouble with fried food get all queasy."

"Cold, aren't you~."

Laughing with a mouth full of fangs, Roy rolled his beady eyes toward Aldebaran. The color in that gaze — interest, and appetite, and curiosity, and appetite, and cruelty, and appetite, basically an entire full course of things sending a chill down Aldebaran's spine. Probably the pain of his broken limbs, and the hunger that outweighed even that pain, were what caused it, but it was a warped way of being through and through. Sympathy for that was the last thing Aldebaran wanted to feel. Like the "Witches," the Archbishops of Sin chosen by the Witch Factor were, from birth, detestable outcasts who'd been derailed from any normal track.

"Well, well... want me to explain the situation?"

"Heh, you'll tell me? What, what, did breaking a kid's limbs make you feel sorry for him after all? But I guess that makes sense. A proper adult couldn't possibly stay calm after snapping and crushing the limbs of such an innocent child. Well, whether a guy wearing a helmet like that on his head while looking the way you do counts as a 'proper adult' is up for debate, though."

"You sure do talk a lot. ...This place is far, far away from the Pleiades Watchtower where you were caught. This is the royal capital of Lugunica. Your other half—"

"——Ah, he died, right?"

"——"

"Even we can tell that much, you know. Family bonds run thicker and stronger than you might think, mister... just kidding, that's a lie. I just figured that out because I can't feel Lye's Witch Factor anymore. And besides—"

"Besides?"

"You might not get it at all from the outside, but even the side that's sealed up can feel a bit of the outside. Even locked up all rigid like that, it's kind of pathetic how scared everyone got."

Making a lewd licking sound with his lips, Roy spoke of his other half's death with breezy nonchalance. In spite of himself, Aldebaran was taken aback by that attitude. Not just that he'd calmly accepted his brother's death — his other half, Lye Batenkaitos — but the testimony that, even sealed in that same "black coffin"-type confinement as Ol Shamak's, there had still been some margin left for sensing the outside world, was what genuinely startled Aldebaran. And, paying no mind to Aldebaran's shock, Roy gave a "there we go" and flipped his body over, turning onto his back so that his view of Aldebaran was now upside-down.

"Aah, that was stifling. This is a bit better... so? Mister, according to our 'memories,' you were one of the people involved in the Royal Selection, right? And here you are, trying to free us Archbishops of Sin — won't that give your precious princess a bad reputa—"

"You're supposedly absorbing not just fighting styles but ways of thinking too, in all their variety, aren't you? If you don't want to be thought of as a useless idiot who's only got the tools but no idea how to use them, think before you talk."

"Whoa, touched a nerve. Careless of me, huh? Fine, fine, fine, sure, sure, sure it's fine! I won't ask any deeper than that. I was just thinking, maybe you've gone and lost it a little, what with your dear, precious princess dying and all... gigh."

"That so. Glad to hear it. Thanks for your consideration."

The wicked smile on Roy's face twisted magnificently as his broken shoulder was stomped down on hard. Aldebaran ground his heel carefully into the broken bone before lifting his foot.

His opponent was an Archbishop of Sin. He'd known as much already, but from the standpoint of getting things to go according to plan, this was about the worst opponent imaginable. That he'd bitten back the instant his seal was released was something he'd anticipated too, but it certainly wasn't the kind of prediction that felt good to have come true. If the Archbishop of Sin weren't a necessary factor in his plan, Aldebaran would gladly have left him sealed in that coffin and tossed him straight into the Great Waterfall. And yet—

"...Tch, and even so, you need us that badly. Rough life, huh, mister."

"You don't shut up much, do you. I'd like to have a word with your parents about how to fix that."

"Birth parents? Adoptive parents? If it's the former, they died taking my sister down with them when they gave birth to us. If it's the latter, I bet they and you wouldn't exactly get along."

"Either way, sounds like they were no good either way. — Can't be helped, then."

Miraculously, the conversation was at least holding together, but there was nothing to be gained from the exchange. Hollow talkativeness was a hallmark of the Archbishops of Sin, and on that point, "Gluttony" lived up to the title without exaggeration. All it stirred up was discomfort and revulsion, doing nothing for his peace of mind — or, for that matter, his will to keep living comfortably.

"——"

Walking over to where Roy lay on his back, helpless as a fish on the cutting board, Aldebaran crouched down beside him. Meeting Aldebaran's gaze through his helmet as he looked down, the upside-down Roy, sparing barely a thought for the pain of his stomped-on broken bone, let his eyes gleam with curiosity, and

"What, what? What are you gonna do next, mister? Torture, to discipline a kid who won't listen? But you know, we're pretty used to that kind of pain."

"Relax. I'm with you on this one — I don't trust pain as a way to make someone obey. If feelings outweigh the pain, then that won't be enough to break them. So—"

"So~?"

"What binds you isn't pain, and it isn't feeling either. — It's a promise."

Saying that, Aldebaran slipped a thumb in through the jaw-piece of his helmet and bit into the tip. A drop of blood welled up with a sharp sting of pain, and he turned the blood-wet finger toward Roy. Then, pressing that blood-tipped finger to the bare skin of the fallen blasphemer, he said—

"Don't bother resisting. I've had practice enough to draw this in my sleep... but this'll be the first time I'm actually testing whether it works."

"Scribbling on a kid's bare skin with blood — even us Archbishops of Sin would go pale at that level of depravity. Going that far into perversion, what do you want from us? Destroy a nation or something?"

"No such plans, and that one you could pull off on your own if you wanted anyway. I'm going out of my way to free a nasty little brat like you specifically because it's something only you can do."

"Only we can do, huh~?"

As the blood-marked finger traced over his body, Roy, savoring the ticklish sensation, showed his fangs and laughed. That lewd look in his eyes, his tone, the very air he gave off, all seemed to be urging Aldebaran to put into words the part he'd left unsaid. Feeling irritated at giving in to that prompting, as if playing right into his opponent's hand, Aldebaran answered anyway. The reason Aldebaran had freed Roy Alphard was—

"—There's someone I want your Authority to devour. And I won't take no for an answer."

"Hmm? Well, that tracks. You're setting us loose. That's about the only reason there'd be."

Tilting his head where he lay, Roy twisted his cheek at Aldebaran's demand. Whether that smile carried affirmation or denial in it, Aldebaran couldn't tell. But whichever it was, Roy's intentions were irrelevant. The means to force him to swallow it had already been etched into Roy Alphard's flesh, carved into his very soul.

"——An Oath sigil. And a seriously thorough one, too — obsessively so, even. Did you figure this out on your own, mister? Or is this secondhand from some crazy curse arts user?"

"Secondhand. No question the person's crazy, but they weren't a curse arts user. A mage. Without question, the greatest mage this world's ever seen. Personality aside."

"And the terms of the Oath~?"

"Devour anyone without my permission, and your soul burns and you die."

Stating it plainly, Aldebaran hoisted Roy's body onto his shoulder without looking at his face. A blasphemer who'd fed indiscriminately, indulging his "Gluttony" to his heart's content — and yet his body was light. Given that he'd technically snuck into the Prison Tower where Roy had been sealed, the lighter the better, honestly. He'd knocked out the guards along the way, but there was no reason not to be careful on the way back too.

"After this, I'll meet back up with Yae and the Felt kid—"

To handle matters in the royal capital, he'd ordered Yae to keep watch over Felt, stationing her at the Barielle townhouse. That mansion, too, had originally been commissioned by Leip Barielle, but its plans had been drastically altered by Priscilla's meddling, finishing in the form it took now. Traces of Priscilla's preferred aesthetic lingered everywhere in that mansion, and even though he'd needed a base of operations, it wasn't a place Aldebaran had wanted to linger in long. So, he wanted to collect Yae and the others quickly and rejoin the "Aldebarans" waiting outside the royal capital.

"Come to think of it, like I said before—"

As Aldebaran mulled that over internally, the Roy slung over his shoulder suddenly brought that up. Glancing sideways, Aldebaran saw Roy, limbs hanging limp, staring down at the floor. Following that gaze, Aldebaran frowned, wondering what it was. There—

"Before you let us out, mister — even while we were sealed inside that black thing, we could vaguely sense the outside, kinda. But this place here, it's completely cut off from the outside, isn't it. In that state, visits are totally out of the question, not even a mouse, let alone a single bug, could've gotten in. And yet—"

"——ah?"

"A Zodda Bug, showing up out of nowhere like that — now that's a pretty rare thing, isn't it?"

Roy's sticky words matched exactly with the small bug that had just come into Aldebaran's field of view. Crawling along the cold floor, that unsettling, revolting-looking form was a Zodda Bug — the bug universally despised throughout this world. It was the equivalent of a cockroach, found anywhere at all, so there was nothing at all strange about one being in the Prison Tower. — Or so it should have seemed. But it didn't, to Aldebaran.

"——Damn it, this is bad!"

Swallowing down the alarm surging up inside him, Aldebaran forgot all consideration for the fact that he was carrying a Roy with broken limbs and rushed to burst out of the cell, out of the Prison Tower. He had to escape, as fast as possible — his infiltration had already been found out—

"——That's far enough."

The instant Aldebaran stepped outside the Prison Tower, that voice struck his eardrums. At the same time, he felt a numbing drop in temperature against his skin, and heard the outside of his helmet creak from the difference in temperature with the tower's interior. White powdery snow drifted here and there around him, and the arrival of this out-of-season cold wave shook Aldebaran's spirit far more than it would any farmer imagining the damage to their crops.

A familiar voice, like a silver bell. Across the sprawling garden surrounding the Prison Tower, treading over a thin blanket of newly fallen snow, she came striding forward, her long silver hair gleaming against the snowy scene. Her beautiful violet eyes usually held a gentle, soft light. — But not now. Those violet eyes were, unmistakably, burning. It was, without a doubt, her anger itself, given form—

"——Give Subaru and Beatrice back."

A quiet, unwavering declaration of intent — the kind of bearing permitted only to a certain sort of monarch. For just a moment, she was beautiful, radiant, and noble enough there to make him mistake her for exactly that.

"——"

Breathing out a white breath, Aldebaran stood frozen. Facing him, standing on the snow, the "Ice Witch" — Emilia — set her fine brows in a sharp line and declared,

"I'm really, really angry right now, so even if it's you, Al, I'm not going to hold back."
