Arc 10 Chapter 3: "A Sprouting of Light"

—Whenever she set foot in the royal castle, Felt always felt as though she were forcing her way through some thin membrane of resistance.

Before the Royal Selection had even begun, to Felt—who had grown up in the slums of the royal capital—Royal Lugunica Castle had always lingered at the edge of her vision, a symbol of a world utterly severed from her own life. A place that existed, certainly, but one she'd never have any dealings with. Vague and insubstantial, like a mirage whose existence held no value to her—that was the impression it gave.

So maybe that was why, even now, every time she had occasion to enter the castle as a Royal Selection candidate, she still felt that same unreal sensation—as if she were stepping into a dream or an illusion. That feeling, however—

"—Felt-sama?"

Called by name, Felt—who had stopped walking—looked up. With his hand on the door, her knight turned back to look at her, and Felt met his blue eyes with her own red ones, giving a small snort before answering shortly, "It's nothin'." That was right—it was nothing at all. No one could stop them from moving forward.

"Let's go."

With that declaration, Felt steeled both herself and him, breaking through that thin membrane of resistance as she strode forward. When her knight—Reinhard—nodded and she stepped through the door he opened, she found the place, as always, crowded with the presence of a great many people, filled with a strange, palpable tension. It was a space where a jumble of individual wills clashed, exchanged as glances and words—but the instant Felt set foot inside, those scattered wills were, without anyone intending it, unified into one. Namely, into the unabashed will to size Felt up, for the sake of the kingdom's future.

"Well, I don't much mind. When I meet somebody face to face, I've got no small amount of that same feelin' myself."

This had nothing to do with the Royal Selection—it was more a survival skill, a bit of street wisdom Felt had picked up living through the slums. Whether the other person was useful to her or not, what they might really be thinking behind their words—she'd made a habit of always sizing that up. In fact, this ingrained habit had served her well even after becoming a Royal Selection candidate. After all, she was now kept busy meeting far more people than she ever had in her slum days, constantly needing to figure out their true aims. If she'd had to build that sense of judgment up from scratch, there was no telling how much trouble she'd have been in. Though, of course—

"There's folks like Emilia-neechan out there who got nothin' to do with any of that, though."

Even though she was a fellow Royal Selection candidate, Emilia had maintained an almost miraculous degree of defenselessness. Felt figured that behind her being able to remain that way lay the extraordinary efforts of her knight, Natsuki Subaru, and of Otto and the others who'd accompanied her this time as well. At the same time, though, Felt's assessment was that neither the Royal Selection nor the circumstances surrounding it were easy enough for Emilia to have coasted through purely on being protected. For all that, Emilia did have a solid eye for people, an intuition for sensing their true intentions. And yet she carried on with that same attitude regardless—which made things difficult for Felt in more ways than one.

"Guess I ended up her friend anyway."

Looking back on the decision she'd just made, Felt let out a deep sigh. She had no regrets about accepting Emilia's offer, but she also thought it had made the road ahead harder to read. She'd never had the slightest intention of resorting to underhanded methods or schemes, but there was no denying that going at it tooth and nail as rival factions would have made it easier to render judgment without pulling any punches. If the other side simply held one-sided goodwill toward her, then the disadvantage would fall on them alone. Felt guessed that this had likely been the core of Anastasia's strategy all along—the true aim behind inviting the other factions to Priestella. —Unfortunately, that scheme looked like it had just been shattered by the turn of events where she and Emilia became friends after the Royal Selection. Anyway—

"Fine, I don't mind it. Been a while since I had eyes sizin' up just me, myself."

Not as bravado but in earnest, Felt twisted the corner of her mouth and accepted the scrutiny directed at her. The throne room she'd stepped into—the very place where the start of the Royal Selection had been declared—had a great many people connected to Royal Lugunica Castle gathered within it, among them members of the Council of Wise Men, who bore the weight of the kingdom's central governance. They were people she'd already exchanged words with the other day, when she'd reported on the matter of the Augria Sand Dunes and the Pleiades Watchtower—the achievement of Meili Portroute, who'd sold her own worth by claiming she could guide them to the road to the Sage's tower. But the air in the room now was distinctly different from back then.

What she felt directed at her now were the kind of eyes one turned on a heretic, a foreign element—back at the very start, in the place where the Royal Selection began, Felt had picked a fight with everyone present. It was close in shade to the bewilderment and confusion that had been directed at her then, as a homeless waif from the slums. And then—

"Guess I know why, and it looks like it's on account of you two mugs I ain't never seen before, huh?"

Stomping heavily across the red carpet, Felt walked all the way to the center of the throne room. Closing one eye, she glared at the man and woman who'd already been standing there before her.

"—"

The two who turned to face Felt were both young, somewhere around twenty years old. A gentle-looking man with hair the color of young grass, and a beautiful woman whose short-bound purple hair recalled flowers that bloomed in the rainy season. Despite the setting, the two of them stood their ground, unfazed by the pressure of those around them. Indeed, even at Felt's opening gambit meant to seize the initiative, neither of them so much as flinched. The gentle-looking man gave a wry smile, while the woman fluttered her hand softly and said, "Ohh myyy,"

"Ufufu, how sudden. This is the first time we've had the chance to meet like this, and here Felt-chan is, prickly just like the rumors said."

"Rumors, huh. What kinda rumors am I supposed to be?"

"Small and adorable, and yet dignified and imposing, headstrong and never backing down from anyone, tomboyish through and through... The gap in charm is enough to make a person dizzy, or so they say."

"Well, that's a rumor I never asked for..."

At Felt's weary reaction to the answer, the woman gave a sultry little chuckle. Dressed in clothing the deep crimson of ripe fruit, with a hat of the same color perched jauntily atop her lovely features, her attire and air made her look something like a scribe, or perhaps a lady-in-waiting serving some noble personage. But the combination of her presumptuous closing of distance and her languid, sleepy-looking red eyes made her hard to pin down. In short, she gave the impression of someone who wouldn't be easy to handle—

"—Sakura, she's one of the Royal Selection candidates. Quit slipping into your usual way of talking to people like that. We won't have enough lives to spare."

So the gentle-looking man standing beside the woman chided his companion's attitude. Even so, the remark was heavily colored with banter, and hardly guaranteed the man's seriousness or sincerity. In fact, at the man's remark, the woman called Sakura only gave a "Hmm~" while still smiling,

"So even Tiga-chan's going to make me the villain like that? I'm already in a bad enough spot thanks to that girl's selfishness, and now even my one and only ally's saying that to me—how disappointing."

"All the more reason not to go making more enemies, then. First impressions have a way of dragging on long after. Especially now, when we're standing in a spot where we're not exactly welcome... right?"

"Yes yes, Tiga-chan's argument wins, fine."

"Glad you understand."

To Sakura, who stuck out her tongue in a sulky manner, the gentle-looking man gave an exaggeratedly theatrical nod. Then he turned back to face Felt and the others properly, removed the wide-brimmed hat he'd been wearing pulled down low, and pressed it to his chest over the deep-purple cloak he wore, saying,

"So, with that, please forgive my companion's rudeness, Felt-sama. I'm Tiga Rauleon, and this here is Sakura Element. A pleasure to make your acquaintance."

As he spoke, the gentle-looking man—Tiga—gave a stylish bow, closing one of his yellow eyes at Felt; he was a dashing sort whose affected mannerisms suited him well. Taking in the sight of Tiga and Sakura both, Felt understood. Without a doubt, these two—who stood out so completely from the atmosphere of the royal castle—were the reason Reinhard had hurried her out of the royal manor—

"Reinhard, these two are..."

"Yes. —They're the two envoys from the Divine Dragon Church."

Standing alongside Felt, who had come to a stop, Reinhard faced off against the pair in question. Without anyone intending it, at the center of the throne room, Felt's party and the two from the Divine Dragon Church now stood facing one another, drawing the undivided attention of every castle official present. Paying that attention no mind whatsoever, Felt glared at Tiga and crossed her arms.

"From what I know, the Divine Dragon Church doesn't get involved in matters of state. So why are you here?"

"I'm not sure you'll believe me, but honestly, this contact isn't something we wanted either. You're right that the Church has kept its distance from politics. Of course, I'm not saying we feel no sense of belonging to the kingdom."

"Unwanted contact, huh? Is that your opinion even knowin' you might be able to do somethin' about Royal Selection candidate Crusch Karsten's condition?"

"—. That's..."

At Felt's words, Tiga faltered slightly. Beside him, Sakura put a hand to her mouth and chuckled once more. At that laugh, Felt growled an "Ah?" low in her throat, and the woman tilted her head.

"No, no, my, Felt-chan really does have sharp ears. That was supposed to be a secret kept within this castle, you know."

"Heh, don't underestimate the ears on the guy standin' next to me. This one can hear the footsteps of me gettin' outta bed to go to the privy at night."

"I won't deny it, but that's a rather misleading way to put it, Felt-sama."

"Shut up. Quit gettin' all wry about it and just look shameless. That'll scare 'em more."

The source of the information—that the talks with the Divine Dragon Church going on in the castle concerned Crusch—was actually Otto, so out of a certain sense of obligation, Felt kept that under wraps. At this point, it wouldn't hurt Reinhard any if his reputation picked up a few more rumors about having supernatural hearing or a habit of eavesdropping. It was practically true anyway that he could probably pick out the sound of a needle dropping a kilometer away. Anyway—

"—It's the Divine Dragon Church part I don't like."

"Oh myyy, does Felt-chan dislike the Church too? I'd like to think our activities have had a good influence, on the slums for instance."

"Sorry, but I hate bein' given charity. I never once lined up when the Church was handin' out bread. Not that I'm sayin' that kinda thing's pointless or that I hate it. What I'm sayin' right now is about you lot bein' able to save Duke Karsten."

"Does that mean you'd rather leave a suffering rival faction to their fate?"

"You tryin' to make me spell out that it ain't that? Sorry, but I ain't got that much time to waste."

Felt stuck her tongue out at Sakura, who despite her syrupy way of talking, kept poking at her with venom. Crusch was indeed a rival faction, true, but the reason she'd fallen into her current state was what it was. Everyone who'd been present that day carried the weight of that debt, and it was only human to want to do whatever could be done if it meant her recovery. —Except that there was exactly one thing that sat extremely badly: Crusch borrowing the hand of the Divine Dragon Church of all things. After all—

"—Duke Crusch Karsten declared, in the place of the Royal Selection, that she would cast aside the Covenant made with the Dragons and break free of the peace granted under their protection."

"—"

So said the old man with the long beard seated in the row of the Council of Wise Men—Miklotov McMahon—cutting into Felt and the others' conversation. At the words of the sage who had finally spoken up, Felt let out a small sigh, and said,

"...The very same woman who declared it pathetic for the whole country to be relyin' on the Dragons—it don't look too good if she goes and borrows the strength of the Divine Dragon Church that worships the Dragons."

"Mind your words, Felt-sama. This isn't a matter of appearances..."

"No, Felt-sama is quite right. Appearances—that is, how it looks to others—is precisely the issue. On that count, Lady Crusch stands to suffer an irreparable blow. Though, that's assuming she takes the hand extended to her, of course."

One of the officials present had tried to interject in the exchange between Felt and Miklotov, only to be reproved by none other than Miklotov himself. The sage's added remark meant that final say over the Divine Dragon Church's offer rested with Crusch and her people. However—

"—Ferris."

With his eyes lowered slightly, the name Reinhard spoke was that of his friend—the bearer of the title of "Blue," Crusch's First Knight, who was even now in the very midst of suffering. Felt herself had seen, when she'd visited to check on Crusch's condition, just how much he'd been suffering and gasping for breath at her side, his beauty—easily mistaken for that of a lovely woman—worn haggard with exhaustion. She hadn't heard of Crusch's condition improving since then. If that was so, then his state of mind was surely no different.

A beloved liege trapped in hell, burning endlessly in flames of virulent poison. Whatever decision the First Knight made when offered a means to save her, no one could blame him for it.

"Naturally, there was debate among us as well regarding this proposal from the Divine Dragon Church. First, we needed to determine whether the content of the offer was even true. And depending on the outcome of that..."

"Might save the folks in Priestella who got hurt too, right? Considerin' that, whatever we're hung up on is small potatoes."

"Hmm. And are you convinced by—"

"You think I could be?"

She understood the logic, and she understood the reasoning. But that was different from her feelings being convinced. Having entered the Royal Selection, Felt intended to keep moving with victory in mind. But if Priscilla had died in the recent incident in the Empire, and now Crusch dropped out over this business, that would be nothing at all like the way Felt wanted the contest decided. If that was how things could be settled, then how was it any different from Reinhard simply going on a rampage and rendering every other Royal Selection candidate unable to recover?

"It isn't a matter of anyone being at fault. If anything, all the blame lies with the Witch Cult."

"Bit late now, but I shoulda roughed up that Wrath bastard more before he got tossed in prison. Might've settled my stomach a little more."

Of course, doing so wouldn't have solved anything, and it was plain as day that it probably wouldn't have satisfied her either. To begin with, most of the damage that had occurred in Priestella had been brought about by Gluttony and Lust—the aftereffects of Wrath were extremely minor by comparison. Reinhard understood this as well, so no one took the words Felt spat out so hatefully as anything but venting. In any case—

"I get the rough gist of it. The Church brought a treatment, and whether the duch—ah, forget it. Whether Crusch-neechan gets saved comes down to whether her knight accepts that treatment. ...We ain't got any right to butt in there."

Whatever she might feel about it, what to choose was something only the people directly involved could know. Having acknowledged as much, Felt turned to Reinhard beside her with a "So?" directing the question at everyone present all at once.

"I get the flow of things up to here, but why'd you need to rush me over here specifically? I mean, I'm grateful for the explanation and all, but you know it ain't just me in the capital right now—Emilia-neechan's here too. Explainin' it like you just did, it wouldn'ta made any difference doin' it for both of us together."

This was a matter that could greatly affect the Royal Selection. All the more reason the information should have been shared urgently not just with Felt, but with Emilia as well. In a situation like this, there was no chance Reinhard would have singled out Felt to steal a march on Emilia and the others—it was doubtful he even had the concept of trying to outmaneuver someone else in him at all. And yet there had to be a reason he'd brought only Felt to this place.

"If there's a reason, is it you Church folks? But far as I can tell, there ain't a thing I need to talk over with the Church right now. ...Well, I guess I could talk about the Divine Dragon."

Her voice trailed off and dropped in tone toward the end—regarding Volcanica, the Divine Dragon so grandly worshipped by the Divine Dragon Church, Felt's own perception of him had only just recently changed.

"I mean, when I went along with Meili to the Pleiades Watchtower, I ended up watchin' that empty-headed Dragon go at it with Reinhard up close and personal..."

The battle between the Sword Saint and the Divine Dragon surely hadn't taken place in a setting where either could go all out, and yet it had still been a fierce, cataclysmic struggle like the end of the world. Through that battle, Felt had learned that the Divine Dragon was in a state with none of the dignity the legends spoke of—but if asked to explain the details, she'd be at a loss for what to even say. But in response to Felt's concern, Tiga shrugged his shoulders and said,

"No, calling Felt-sama here wasn't our doing. Actually, regarding everything that was just discussed, the Church's position is a bit delicate too."

"Everything just discussed, meanin' the stuff about Crusch-neechan? Delicate how?"

"Well, put simply... the Church hadn't actually reached a clear decision yet on whether to act to save Duke Crusch Karsten, I suppose you could say."

"Ahh...?"

At Tiga's answer, delivered with knitted brows and a troubled expression, Felt couldn't help but let out a low growl herself. It made no sense. She'd understood the sequence of events as the Divine Dragon Church having a means to heal victims of the Witch Cult's damage, and having explicitly declared they'd use it to save Crusch—that being why they'd approached the castle. But Tiga's words just now undermined that very premise.

"But that treatment's supposedly already bein' given to Crusch-neechan, ain't it? If the Church hadn't reached an answer yet, then how come..."

"Well, you see... one of our impatient kids went and ran ahead on her own."

"...Ran ahead?"

"Yes indeed. Which is whyyy we came rushing to the castle in a panic ourselves, and we've been asking everyone here for the details. Quite the predicament, isn't it."

How much of it could be believed as genuine was hard to say—Sakura's words lacked any real gravity. But Tiga, who at least seemed far more capable of speaking sensibly compared to Sakura, didn't stop or deny what she'd just said either, simply putting a hand to his forehead as if this were an embarrassment within the family. If that were true, then the Divine Dragon Church had its share of reckless people too. But even so—

"That still don't explain why I got called here."

"Hmm. On that point, we too have found ourselves in quite a bind. Since Sir Reinhard happened to be present, we asked that Felt-sama come along as well. After all, in the matter we now find ourselves facing, Felt-sama is a party directly concerned."

"—"

A matter concerning herself—told as much, Felt narrowed her red eyes and ran her tongue over her lips. It seemed they'd taken quite the roundabout path, but it looked like they were finally getting to the point. The movements of the Divine Dragon Church, upon which Crusch's fate as a candidate hinged, the fact that this had been unexpected even to the Church itself, and the reason Felt had been called to the place where its envoys were present— It was this—

"Regarding the person who has come up in conversation since a moment ago—the one who approached us alone from the Divine Dragon Church... she is a woman with beautiful golden hair and red eyes. And what's more..."

Felt did not interrupt the words Miklotov delivered to her. She wasn't so slow on the uptake that she couldn't tell what it meant, being told the other party's features in a tone that all but declared their importance. Watching Felt's reaction, Miklotov continued with the rest of his explanation.

"That person gave her own name as Filore—the same name as the daughter of His Highness Ford Lugunica, the king's younger brother, who vanished without a trace fifteen years ago."

△▼△▼△▼△

—Filore Lugunica, daughter of Ford Lugunica, the king's younger brother.

The weight that name and existence carried was significant for the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica as a whole, but for Felt in particular, it held an enormous, enormous significance. After all, behind the circumstances that had led to Felt being made to join the Royal Selection lay a reason equal to, or perhaps even greater than, the fact that she'd been able to make the badge set with a Dragon Jewel—the qualification for Royal Selection candidacy—light up: namely, the suspicion that Felt herself might be that very same missing Filore.

Indeed, it had been fifteen years since the infant Filore had vanished without a trace. The age matched up with Felt's own, and her hair and eye color both matched the traits that appeared in the Lugunica royal line. Of course, Felt had never once thought of herself as a member of the royal family, nor had she ever tried to make use of that possibility. —But she also wasn't so self-centered that she could completely ignore what possibility those around her were hoping to see in her.

Many of the people within the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica saw in Felt the possibility of the vanished royal. Whatever Felt's own self-image, it was human nature that such hope and longing couldn't simply be swept aside. And that wasn't only true in the broad sense of "the kingdom's people"—it had been a sliver of hope harbored even by the officials present in that place where the start of the Royal Selection was declared, even by Reinhard himself.

Without a doubt, it was the very possibility that she might be a surviving member of the royal family that had given Felt, born of the slums, her first sword with which to fight upon the stage of the Royal Selection. And then—

"—So there's a real Filore who disappeared, huh."

The situation settled into her chest all at once, and Felt murmured that much under her breath, barely voicing it. No wonder the castle had been struck with such a shock, she thought. It was even possible that the very fear of Crusch being saved by the Divine Dragon Church and, as a result, dropping out of the Royal Selection, had been treated as a mere trifle in the face of this fact. That was how shocking the existence of a Divine Dragon Church nun claiming to be Filore was.

"—Felt-sama."

Called out to suddenly by Reinhard standing beside her, Felt held her breath ever so slightly. It wasn't as if the call had come out of nowhere, and yet Felt failed to catch whatever emotion had been packed into that voice. —No, let her admit it. It was the unmistakable unease that had sprouted within Felt herself that kept her from picking up on Reinhard's feelings.

"—"

When Reinhard had first drawn Felt out into the Royal Selection, what with being forcibly confined in a mansion after being taken from the Loot House, she'd been able to feel nothing but strong resistance and hostility. In the end, through a chain of events that included old man Rom's intrusion, she'd found herself deciding to take part in the Royal Selection—but the very reason Reinhard had brought her out to this place and put her forward as a candidate in the first place had surely been the suspicion, now shaken to its foundations, that she was a surviving royal.

That had now been denied. —No, not denied; it remained, after all, only a suspicion. What's more, Felt herself had, up until now, mocked that possibility and kicked it aside. And yet, when that possibility was actually denied right in front of her, a distinct unease shook through her chest. It wasn't because she'd wanted to be royalty.

It was disappointment—born of a fault Felt could do nothing about—at betraying the expectations of those who had hoped she was a member of the royal family.

"...Pathetic."

Having seen through the true nature of the unease that had sprouted within her, Felt found herself disgusted with herself. For all that she usually put on airs of being dignified and shameless, unshakable no matter what happened, the moment something unexpected occurred—the moment she realized that something she'd thought she could give up without a second thought might actually be lost—she'd gone and panicked unbecomingly, and she hated herself for it.

Gritting her teeth at such a pathetic version of herself, Felt forced her heavy, stiffened neck to move, managing to turn toward Reinhard standing at her side. Whatever expression Reinhard might be wearing there—

"—"

Braced for it, Felt looked up—and in her sight, the pair of eyes that held the blue sky itself, looking straight back at her, showed not the slightest trace of anxiety or unease. Let alone disappointment or dejection—those were nowhere to be found either. There was only, purely and simply, a trust in Felt so vast it bordered on the magnificent. Nothing more.

"...The hell kinda look is that, you."

"I like to think it's my own way of holding an unshakable loyalty toward Felt-sama."

"—. Heh, shut up."

Whether he meant it as a joke or in earnest, at Reinhard's answer—delivered with the faintest of smiles—Felt responded by jabbing him in the side with her elbow. She couldn't help it. Because, infuriatingly enough, Reinhard's attitude just now had lightened the feeling weighing on her chest all at once.

"No matter what happens, however things go, I'm still me. Ain't nothin' to do but own up to that, is all."

She'd known that much all along, but she'd needed some kind of trigger to clearly own up to it. Reinhard had given her that without meaning to, and though Felt felt a certain resentment about it, she didn't click her tongue. It was a grievance, but not a bad one, as grievances went. Though she'd never tell Reinhard that. It was right after Felt had come to that certain resolution within herself.

"—Sir Marcos Gildark has returned."

So came the report from a guard in the throne room, and all at once the faces of those present drew taut. As Felt eyed that reaction with suspicion, Reinhard leaned in close to her ear and said,

"It seems Captain Marcos has been escorting the woman in question to Lady Crusch, regardless of whether the treatment is accepted or not..."

"So it's a face-to-face meeting, huh. Good thing I already made up my mind."

Felt answered Reinhard's whispered words that way, then took a deep, long breath. And, keeping Reinhard standing beside her, she waited for that moment. And then—

"—So you were all waiting for me with quite the crowd. Given the circumstances, I suppose that's only natural."

Felt heard those words spoken as the figure who had passed through the throne room's door peered inside.

"So that's..."

The Divine Dragon Church nun who had barged into the castle claiming the name Filore, was it. Just as Felt had heard beforehand, she was a nun with long golden hair and strong-willed red eyes. Standing tall with her back straight, she looked uncomfortable at being greeted all at once by the eyes of the whole room. But with a massive armored figure right behind her, she couldn't very well retreat either. Eventually, she shook her head as if resigning herself, and stepped straight into the room—

"—Filore-chan?"

"Ghk."

The moment she took her first step, she caught sight of Sakura waving at her, and her well-formed features stiffened dramatically as her face went pale in an instant. She froze there, her weight still leaning forward on one foot, but after a moment she tried to turn her back and flee.

"Filore, everyone already knows, so just come quietly."

"Ugh, not just Sakura but Tiga too..."

"Of course we'd show up. Honestly, you really went and did as you pleased."

At Tiga's call, delivered with a weary shrug, Filore—half-turned away—looked pained, but seemed to resign herself all the same. Giving up on fleeing backward, she came forward with her shoulders slumped dejectedly, took a deep breath in front of Tiga and Sakura, whom she naturally knew well, and then—

"Devoting one's prayers fully to the people of the kingdom is the true purpose of the Divine Dragon Church—I merely followed its teachings. How's that?"

"How's that, she says..."

"I don't think you're getting out of a scolding and a lecture."

"But I wasn't wrong!"

Right after puffing out her chest and declaring it so boldly, her bravado came crashing down. At the nun—Filore—who let out something like a scream and clutched her head, Felt couldn't help raising an eyebrow. She was a girl whose demeanor differed considerably from her first impression at a glance. And besides—

"You're a good deal taller than me. That the difference from livin' it up as a kid?"

"Wait just a moment, living it up? Did you just say that about me? If so, that's a huge mistake. The Divine Dragon Church's motto is the spirit of giving! Most of the Church's mercy and generosity is directed outward, not inward. Which means..."

"Which means?"

"Which means we're pretty much always starving!"

With a hand pressed to her own stomach, Filore loudly proclaimed the Church's ascetic poverty. At the force and volume of it, Felt scratched her cheek with a finger, and no sooner had Filore finished declaring it than she was smacked on the back of the head. It was Tiga, standing behind her, who'd struck the head of the girl now turning around with an "Ow." He looked at Filore with the eyes of someone regarding a troublesome little sister, and said,

"Don't go announcing our shame at the top of your lungs. That's a secret we've managed to keep hidden from the seat of the kingdom's power this whole time. Don't tell me you're planning to lay everything else bare in the same fashion."

"T-that couldn't possibly be true. Do you two really think I'm that loose-lipped..."

"If that's so, then what about the sacrament?"

"—"

The sound of Filore catching her breath at the accusation rang oddly loud through the throne room. With Tiga and Sakura peering into her face from either side, Filore opened and closed her mouth wordlessly for a while before saying,

"...I—I did clear the room of onlookers first."

"Sighhh..." (both together)

"What a huge sigh! Is this—is this how you treat me after I went and helped someone? Even the scripture says as much! 'Even wickedness done cloaked in the dusk of evening appears to the Dragon's eye as clear as a bonfire at high noon. To grow arrogant thinking one can hide it away is a darkness of delusion wrought by one's own hand!'"

Filore made that excuse, hastily pulling out a thick scripture and holding it aloft with both hands. Felt wasn't especially well-versed in the subject herself, but from the gist of it, she took it to mean something along the lines of "secrets get found out eventually, so don't be too overconfident about hiding them." Even so, the Divine Dragon Church's own doctrine probably wasn't too pleased about being treated like a dictionary of excuses.

"Sir Marcos, how did it go?"

Setting aside the exchange among the Divine Dragon Church's people, Miklotov directed the question at the captain of the Royal Guard who had entered the throne room together with Filore. Called by name and asked the question, Marcos, his stern face unchanging, replied with a "Yes,"

"I accompanied her myself and personally witnessed the power of Miss Filore's sacrament."

"Witnessed it, meaning..."

"—The flame of malevolence that had taken root in Duke Crusch Karsten's body has had its lifeline severed, and lost all influence."

At Marcos's report, some emotion Felt couldn't quite name stiffened her cheeks. Crusch had been saved—that was a joyous thing. But having been saved by the Divine Dragon Church, of all people, wasn't something she could call joyous without reservation. But, just as she'd already braced herself to accept moments before, the choice had belonged to the people directly involved alone.

"—Hmm, I see."

Accepting, through the wisdom of his years, something similar to the difficult feelings Felt herself had just tasted, Miklotov glanced briefly at Filore. At his gaze, Filore looked up, and Miklotov, smoothing his long beard with one hand, continued with a "Now then,"

"There is a great deal we must discuss. Naturally, that includes the sacrament Miss Filore has demonstrated for us, and, more than anything else..."

"—? Was there something else?"

Tilting her head, Filore furrowed her brow as if she had absolutely no idea what Miklotov had left unspoken. Filore's reaction aside, the reason Miklotov hesitated to put the rest into words was, most likely, out of consideration for Felt's presence there. But—

"Don't need that kinda consideration. —Reinhard."

"Yes."

So saying, Felt held out her hand, and Reinhard, as though he'd known all along that she would, gently placed something into it. In that instant, having confirmed its feel in her palm, Felt flicked it forward with her fingers—straight at Filore.

"Wah, wh-whoa, what is—"

Filore caught the thing sent flying at her in both hands, thrust out on reflex. At her blinking in surprise at Felt's sudden action, Felt gave a small snort. Then, to everyone else in the room whose eyes had gone wide at what Felt had done, she gave a shrug—

"Everybody here wanted to see this, right?"

There, where Felt pointed with the thumb she'd raised, stood Filore, cradling the thing she'd caught in both hands—and within those hands, the badge she held was shining, glowing bright.

It was, without a doubt, the proof of a maiden chosen by a Dragon Jewel. —A vivid sprouting of light, a Blessing marking the existence of the sixth maiden acknowledged by a Dragon Jewel.
