Arc 9 Chapter 54: "'Warlock' VS 'Witch'"

"—I'm just as flawed a girl as you are, you know."

Aldebaran would never forget the languid, melancholy look on the Witch's face when she said that.

She had thought—

—that even the Witch, who wore the face of someone who knew every law that governed this world, whose bearing suggested roots of knowledge spread to every corner of the world, who carried herself as though versed in all creation, could have moments where she wanted to lament her own insufficiency.

"Of course I do. If anything, your existence... the very fact that you're like this, is proof enough of how lacking I am."

Hearing that left Aldebaran's feelings complicated.

To Aldebaran, this Witch's presence loomed large — great, though it pained her to admit it. There wasn't much else to compare her against, but even so, she felt certain the Witch would seem immense against any measure.

For that Witch to call herself flawed wasn't something to be glad about.

And yet, without the Witch's self-awareness of that flaw, there would be no relationship between her and Aldebaran now. If the Witch had been in a position where she needn't acknowledge her own inadequacy—

"—then both of us would simply be where we were always meant to be. You're free to dwell on the future that didn't come to pass, but unfortunately, you drew the worst possible fate. I'm sorry to say it, but for me, it was a stroke of good fortune."

In that deliberately unkind phrasing, the Witch's own brand of humor and her own brand of honesty seemed mixed in equal parts. Though in truth, there was no way to know.

After all, the true feelings of a Witch who always claimed, with a straight face, that she couldn't understand the human heart, remained vague and elusive even to Aldebaran, who had known her so long.

But if the Witch's claim were true, it was a strange thing indeed.

After all, though her current circumstances were apparently the worst turn of the stars for Aldebaran, she never once thought her days with the Witch were unhappy.

True, being made to experience being killed a million times over in the name of some "trial," or being called by a name she disliked no matter how much she refused it — it was certainly an environment far removed from ordinary happiness.

"To find value in a life alongside a botched Witch — you really are a pitiable child."

It was a complicated murmur, self-deprecation and mockery of another rolled into one.

But Aldebaran didn't miss the gentle note that slipped through despite the Witch's best efforts to hide it.

However slight, however easily missed, however hard to read — the Witch did have feelings. Hunting down the traces that served as proof for that belief was one of Aldebaran's favorite pastimes.

Whether it was really true, she couldn't say. It might be nothing more than a fancy, a misunderstanding.

What mattered was how Aldebaran felt about it. The Witch's true intentions belonged to the Witch. —The truth, as far as Aldebaran was concerned, belonged to Aldebaran.

"That knack of yours for drawing such a clean line — it's remarkably similar to mine. Influence really isn't something to make light of, is it."

Narrowing her black eyes, toying with her white forelocks between her fingers, the Witch said this.

It was a reminiscence murmured by the Witch as she gazed, through Aldebaran, at someone else — and at her own reflection caught in Aldebaran's eyes.

Influence — of course there was such a thing. In birth, and in upbringing alike.

In Aldebaran. In the Witch, too.

Flawed, insufficient, incomplete. —Was that a shameful past, to the Witch?

To think that it was precisely because she had been that way that these days with the Witch existed — was that a curse?

If there was one thing that could be said for certain—

"—Aldebaran."

—that when, ten years for Aldebaran and four hundred for the other, they were reunited within a scene already known to her, and the figure of the Witch she saw there — no, of the Witch of Greed — was one that denied the very flaw she had spoken of that day, what Aldebaran tasted was an unbearable sense of loss, and despair.

Having thought that far, Aldebaran once again felt anger toward Natsuki Subaru.

Of course, Aldebaran didn't think her own scheme, in making Petra read the Book of the Dead, was unrelated to the process by which Petra had come to take in the Melancholy Witch Factor. But if Aldebaran had been the one to set things in motion, then a large share of the responsibility lay with Natsuki Subaru.

Considering the relationship between Aldebaran and Natsuki Subaru, that much could surely be said without reproach.

The great shadow cast over Petra's life had Natsuki Subaru as its point of origin. She wasn't saying, and didn't think, that he'd acted out of malice, but his influence was, needless to say, immense. And it wasn't limited to Petra alone — it extended to the Aldebusters, no, it could be called a kind of fate-force, one that had made itself felt in many scenes even before this battle.

"—Co—"

Even so, "fate-force" was an ironic thing to call it. Of all people, it was precisely Natsuki Subaru and Aldebaran who could be said to hate fate the most, and yet,

"—m—"

it would hardly be an exaggeration to say that their own actions were what determined fate.

"—pre—"

When she thought about it that way, what flitted through Aldebaran's mind was her — a vivid, burning-red memory of her words. That—

"—ss—"

praise she had voiced in that final moment together was trying to sear itself into Aldebaran's heart. She deliberately looked away, stopped her ears against it.

"—A—"

There was no more time left, at this late stage, for looking back on her own conduct. What sustained Aldebaran now wasn't the woman she'd lost, but the Oath she'd made with her Teacher.

"—go—"

What Aldebaran had been born for, what she'd been made "Aldebaran" in order to do — the sum of all of that was about to be put to the test. And for that, there was no time to stop and think— wait, wait, wait, wait. Wasn't that long? Just a breath, a moment made for a single deep breath, and then the fight resumes.

"—n—"

That's how it should be. The other side didn't want to let her catch her breath. She, in turn, didn't want to give the other side much time either.

"—y—"

That mutual understanding should have been settled between them, so why was Aldebaran allowed to think so leisurely, so—

"——"

Bad. Something's wrong. What? What was said to me? In the distance, she could tell Petra's lips had moved. Sound by sound, she picked up what those lips had spun out. Catching what had been dropped in between her own thoughts, gathering it up. Slow. Maddening. Far, far too slow. Catch the meaning. Catch the significance. Catch the intent. —. ——. ——. No good. She couldn't silence her own thoughts. Fine, think while catching it. "Compress"? To crush, was it? Crush — what? "Agony"? Pain? Suffering? Anguish... anguish? Worry it, think it through, unravel it. Damn it, she'd been had. She'd been Compressed.

—Her thoughts had been Compressed!!

△▼△▼△▼△

—Gods, Buddha, Od Lagna-sama. I swear that for the rest of my life, I will never reveal an important secret.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—'Compress Agony.'"

Compression of thought through the Melancholy Authority — a powerful buff effect that sped up her allies' thinking, cut out the need for discussion, and let everyone strike the single best move worked out collectively. Applying that buff to Al was one of the countermeasures Petra had prepared for him.

"If you've got too much to think about all at once, your head's bound to burst, isn't it?"

Presumptuous as it might be, Petra now took pride in believing herself to be, on the subject of the Time Regression called Return by Death, the third most knowledgeable person in the world, after Subaru and Al.

From that learning of Petra's, and from the experience of having relived, secondhand, the suffering of death that Subaru had tasted, she'd found a way to nullify — no, to overheat — Al's seemingly near-invincible Authority.

That method was mental overload, brought on by accelerating thought.

Once before, back when the Royal Selection had only just begun, Subaru had ended up in a falling-out with Emilia in the royal capital, and his heart had been ground down to nothing under the weight of overwhelming stress and mental strain.

By happy accident, he'd found the chance to recover through Rem's devotion and his own anger at a Sin Archbishop, but the fact remained that, however temporarily, he'd lost the will to fight and become an empty shell. —That was what she meant to reproduce.

By now, Al, his fear of death numbed away, might never have his heart broken no matter how many deaths he piled up.

But even so, even if he'd turned the whole world against himself, Al was still human.

A crack forming in his spirit, reaching its limit — that was unavoidable. And that was what she meant to induce.

For that, whatever it cost her, whatever she had to trade away of herself—

"—Domain Expansion. Matrix Redefinition."

She could tell that Al, even as he was tormented by the acceleration of his own thoughts, had quietly spoken those words.

A heavy load was being placed on his mind, but this state was a double-edged sword. The chance that Al's heart, already worn so fiercely down, would hit its limit, was exactly even with the chance that he, just like the Aldebusters, would make maximum use of that same Compression of thought and turn it around to strike back at Petra and the others.

And so, the battle from here on was, in the truest sense, the moment of truth for the Aldebusters—

"—Everyone! This is where it counts! Don't let your guard down!"

"—Yeah!!"

To Petra's rallying cry came the answer of her comrades, their exchange Compressed into an instant.

Finding that reassuring, she moved to face the reset showdown with Al once more — and noticed something.

Among those who had just answered so vigorously, there was one whose voice hadn't been heard.

It might simply have been someone who'd missed their timing. But feeling a strange unease at that, Petra glanced over that way, searching for the figure who hadn't answered.

And then, she found him.

"—Rom Gramps?"

The great strategist who hadn't answered — the massive old man tasked with surveying the battlefield from above and calling out timely, precise instructions — she found him slowly rolling his eyes back white, and collapsing to the earth.

△▼△▼△▼△

—Time rewinds, just a single instant.

"—Domain Expansion. Matrix Redefinition."

Hearing Aldebaran declare those words, Rom Gramps's massive shoulders trembled.

Eyes wide and staring, Rom Gramps froze up, a shock that had no business being there settling into him at the sight of Aldebaran, standing stock-still, facing off against the mixed army.

Rom Gramps, too, had been told of Petra's plan, using the Melancholy Authority.

It was a strategy to accelerate Aldebaran's thinking, load down her spirit, and wear her out all at once. Rom Gramps himself considered it a necessary step for defeating an Aldebaran who could apparently do Time Regression, and had promised to lend his cooperation.

He'd had his misgivings about speeding up Aldebaran's own thinking, but Compressing the process of thought down to the best move was something their own side did too, and he'd accepted that this was a scene to be closed out through their combined total strength. What weighed heavily in that was his belief that, with that approach, he himself could still lend his own strength to the effort.

And in fact, in the fight against Aldebaran, Rom Gramps's calculations had proven correct.

He'd succeeded in moving his comrades swiftly and cornering Aldebaran. —But it was from that point on that something arose to stun Rom Gramps, and to shake him.

"—Domain Expansion. Matrix Redefinition."

So Aldebaran declared, and in an instant, Rom Gramps's position changed. —No, it wasn't only Rom Gramps who had changed; the positions of Aldebaran, of Felt, of everyone taking part in this battle, had all been reset back to mere seconds ago, and it had all begun again.

And Rom Gramps had already been through that same repetition, five times over—

"—What in blazes is this situation."

Turning his head slowly around, Rom Gramps wrung the words out heavily, in the midst of a scene he'd now seen five times over.

There was no one but Rom Gramps who sensed anything wrong in this situation.

"—Everyone! This is where it counts! Don't let your guard down!"

"—Yeah!!"

As proof of that, everyone but Rom Gramps answered Petra's voice as she gallantly took the helm.

And so, all together, they leapt at Aldebaran once more.

"Rom Gramps! Give us orders!"

Unaware of his shock, Felt, staff in hand, searched for her attack position. Beyond Felt, other comrades who held decisive techniques of their own were running too, wanting Rom Gramps's instructions.

To their backs, Rom Gramps, swallowing down a bitter lump, called out instructions different from before—

"—Domain Expansion. Matrix Redefinition."

—The sixth iteration of the final decisive battle against Aldebaran had begun.
