Arc 7 Chapter 10: "The People of Shudrak"

An insolent, arrogant smile — even veiled behind the mask, Subaru could tell it was aimed at him, and he quietly caught his breath.

This insolent man had to be the same person who, back when he'd gotten separated from Rem on the grasslands, had shown Subaru the way and given him that knife. The mask made it impossible to be certain, but there was something familiar in his voice and bearing. That the man knew Subaru's name was proof enough.

"――――"

Inside a wooden cage, laid out on bare dirt, his limbs — no, his entire body — battered and worn: it seemed his string of luck, against all odds, had yet to run out.

He'd led Todd and the other imperial soldiers into the forest, used his own miasma as bait to draw out a witchbeast, and tried to slip away in the chaos of its attack on them—

"And then, I..."

"From what I hear, you were wandering the forest and got caught in a trap. The village folk were making quite a fuss about a human getting caught in a snare meant for beasts."

"A trap... a village...?"

At the masked man's explanation, Subaru shook his aching head and turned his attention beyond the cage.

The wooden cage holding him was, compared to the iron one he'd seen at the imperial camp, a distinctly crude construction. It looked makeshift — thrown together in a hurry.

Beyond the cage, what he could see in the distance was a cluster of tall trees and a clearing carved out among them — to Subaru's eye, it looked something like the village at the Sanctuary.

The Sanctuary, too, had been a village built deep within a forest — the one called the Lost Forest of Kremaldy. But unlike the Sanctuary, which had proper buildings like houses and a chapel even in the depths of the woods, this village was, charitably, made of log cabins, and less charitably, primitive dwellings through and through.

"Back to nature," Subaru decided, was probably the safest way to put it.

But more important to Subaru than how shabby the village looked was the question of who lived there.

Namely—

"—The People of Shudrak?"

"Oh? So you knew. Well, judging by that pathetic state of yours, you must have piled up quite a load of hardship in just a single day. Did you find the woman you got separated from?"

"...Yeah. Thanks to you, I did."

Catching Subaru's murmured reply, the masked man pressed him with that question, and Subaru let out a long breath.

The masked man never lost his composed air, but he, too, was inside the cage just like Subaru. Unless he was some important figure in this village with the eccentric hobby of locking himself in with prisoners, his position was surely the same as Subaru's.

He'd only just become a prisoner at the imperial camp, and now here he was, a captive once again.

Though there was more to it than that.

"—Hey, did someone treat these wounds? My shoulder, my back?"

Touching his own shoulder and back, he could tell from the tight, constricting sensation that the bleeding had been staunched. The sharp, stinging smell that pricked his nose felt close to that of some kind of disinfectant.

At Subaru's question, the masked man gave a snort—

"If you hadn't been treated, you'd likely have died as you were. Even they must have been at a loss over what to do with you — same as with me, wondering what the correct move was."

"Where does that composure of yours come from..."

"If I had to say, from my soul. And you — how long do you intend to keep making such a spectacle of yourself, Natsuki Subaru?"

"None of your—"

He tried to snap back that it was none of the man's business, but the pain of his wounds made him grit his back teeth instead.

The treatment was only the bare minimum needed to keep him from dying — not enough to close his wounds quickly or take away the pain. The conditions here were worse than what he'd had at the imperial camp.

And thinking of the imperial camp, Subaru realized something.

There was a reason he needed to get back to that camp as soon as possible.

"Damn— how long has it been since they brought me here!?"

"...Well, two hours, I'd say. Mind you, that's more than generous mercy on my part. If I'd had no particular reason to hold back, I'd have woken you sooner—"

"Why didn't you wake me sooner!"

"――――"

The masked man narrowed his eyes at Subaru, who pressed his trembling knees to the ground as he protested.

From his perspective, it was an absurd complaint. He'd let Subaru — carried in covered in wounds, half-dead — sleep for two hours because he'd judged that rest was necessary.

If stepping on Subaru's head afterward had been an act born of losing patience, then he too possessed a distinctly Vollachian streak. But if that were the case—

"Then I wish you'd hit the limit of your patience sooner."

"Now that's an odd thing to say. Do you even understand what you're saying? You're saying you wish I'd stepped on your head sooner."

"Yeah, that's right! What other way is there to— guh, ngh...!"

Even as he spouted the unreasonable logic, red flickered across Subaru's vision.

Every inch of his body ached without exception, but the sharpest pain came from his back — a fresh stab wound near his shoulder blade, the last strike from Todd's knife as Subaru had fled.

Now that he thought of it, the very knife that had stabbed him was the one this masked man before him had given him — and to be reunited with him while still bearing that wound felt like some kind of grim irony.

Regardless—

"I left Rem behind at the imperial camp... If I don't get back before the imperial soldiers I set the witchbeast on return to camp, Rem is going to..."

There wasn't much time before Todd and the others made it out of the forest, back to camp, and finished making their various reports.

When the great serpent shattered their formation, Jamal and the others had, naturally, prioritized dealing with the witchbeast. But among them, Todd alone had prioritized killing Subaru.

Todd had probably realized it was Subaru who'd drawn the witchbeast in. And in that moment, he'd moved to eliminate Subaru on the spot, before he could summon a second one. That instant of judgment and execution wasn't something to underestimate — and Subaru couldn't afford to underestimate it.

He'd made a point of stressing how thin his connection with Rem and the others was, meant to give the impression there was no information to be pried out of him about Subaru — but—

"If they don't have proof, they might just try torturing it out of her anyway."

That was the kind of dread the Vollachian Empire — Todd included — inspired.

To keep that from happening, Subaru had to get back and save Rem.

And yet—

"And here I am, stuck in a place like this...!"

"...I see. So this 'Rem' is the woman you were looking for, I take it. Seems she's been through quite an ordeal since you and I parted ways. The imperial soldiers outside the forest?"

"Yeah, that's right! She was captured! I put on an act to escape, but... I couldn't bring Rem with me. So..."

"So that's the desperation. No wonder you had the look of someone used to being a captive."

"Who's got a 'captive face'! Besides—"

Wasn't the masked man just as much a captive himself?

Despite owing the man a debt of gratitude, Subaru — out of sheer desperation — nearly shouted back at him. But something he suddenly noticed stopped him from that rash outburst.

"――――"

Caught up in trading barbed words with the masked man, Subaru had felt a different kind of gaze prickling against the side of his face.

Turning, he saw two points of light peering in through the gaps in the bars outside the cage. As the shape slowly resolved, he realized they were a pair of green eyes.

The owner of those eyes blinked rapidly the moment Subaru's gaze turned toward them—

"—Ah, he noticed Uu."

"Wha—"

"Must tell Mii."

With that, the owner of the eyes slipped away from the bars. Subaru scrambled to stop her, shouting "Wait!" — but he was too late.

By the time Subaru lunged for the bars, she had already slipped away and taken off running without so much as a backward glance.

"Who was that just now..."

"A girl of the People of Shudrak. Curious little thing. Even when I was alone here, she came by several times to peek in. Pestering me no end to show my face, to take off the mask..."

"――――"

The masked man, arms crossed, grumbled on, apparently displeased with the peeping girl's manner.

Unfortunately, Subaru had no room to spare for agreeing with his complaints. His attention had been seized entirely by the one who'd run off — that young girl.

She'd looked to be about ten years old, a young girl with tan skin.

Wrapped in white cloth, dressed in a manner that bared plenty of skin for ease of movement — likely an outfit suited to this land's subtropical climate.

Her hair, cut close to a bob, was an unusual color — pink only at the tips, likely from dyeing. Near the roots it was black, which matched Todd's explanation that the People of Shudrak had black hair.

But what struck Subaru far harder than the girl's distinctive appearance was the fact that it wasn't the first time he'd seen it.

"—She's the one who—"

—killed me. That's what Subaru's memory insisted.

That very girl was the one who'd used a poisoned arrow to pierce Subaru's back and bring about his death.

The same girl who, after Subaru's careless words led to the jungle being set ablaze, had fled the spreading flames and glared at him with eyes drenched in hatred—

The dots connected in Subaru's mind.

The reason the girl had looked at him with such hatred that time was no mystery at all — it had been vengeance against the one responsible for burning her homeland and her people alive.

"What's wrong? You've gone quiet as if someone doused you with water."

"—Ah."

The man's voice reached Subaru's back as he pressed his forehead to the wooden bars, biting his lip.

The masked man, still sitting in his original spot, watched Subaru's wild swings of emotion. That gaze made him deeply uncomfortable, and Subaru looked away.

"An unreadable man, you are. In any case, you'd best stop your ranting. Silencing you here would be far too much trouble, and you'd waste your strength for nothing. You needn't shout every single time—"

"Not shout...?"

"—They'll come to hear you out on their own. Look there."

Following the man's chin as he gestured, Subaru turned and his eyes went wide.

Slowly, flame lit the dim view — torchlight. Several figures carrying torches had appeared, making their way toward the cage where Subaru and the man were held.

At the front walked a tall woman with a well-trained, powerful build. Her hair, likely black by nature, had been dyed red, and white patterns were painted across her tan-skinned face and body. Her green eyes carried an intensely piercing look.

Peeking out from behind her, trailing along, was the girl from before. The group approaching numbered around ten, and every one of them appeared to be a woman.

"――――"

Even so, the sheer pressure of their presence overwhelmed Subaru despite himself.

This wild, untamed group was unlike the Kingdom of Lugunica's knights or the Vollachian Empire's soldiers he'd seen earlier — there was a beauty to them, like a pack of beasts moving in instinctive unity.

It gave the impression of a group built around instinct at its core, rather than logic.

With that impression of their approach fixed in his mind, Subaru watched as the women — the People of Shudrak — came to stand before the cage that held him, fixing their eyes on the two inside.

And then—

"So you've woken up. —Just who are you two?"

—came the question, lumping Subaru and the masked man together as one.

△▼△▼△▼△

—Who are you?

It's a question you hear often enough in stories, but not one you're often actually asked in real life.

Being suspected of hiding your true identity and pressed on it isn't a situation that comes up much in everyday life. Unless you're in a profession that demands such questions be asked, both the asker and the asked might go their whole lives without ever crossing paths with those words.

In that sense, it wasn't a question Subaru could call familiar, either.

Still, he could vividly recall the very first time in his life he'd been asked it.

Who Subaru was, and what he was after.

The first person in his life to ask him that had been none other than Rem, back at the mansion, suspicious of his true identity.

"Not 'you'... 'you two'?"

Shaking off that memory, Subaru raised a question mark over Mizelda's question.

Even though they'd been thrown into the same cell, Subaru's connection to the masked man was thin. Actually, putting them in the same cell had been entirely the other side's doing — Subaru had had zero say in it.

Treating them as one and the same felt like a rather rough way to go about things.

"Don't get hung up on trivial things. I'm the one who told them you and I are acquainted. That question is merely a result of that."

"You...! 'Acquainted'... it's not like we're that close!?"

"I've told no lie. Both of us, seeing the other, recognize who it is. What more does it take to call someone an acquaintance?"

"T-that logic makes no sense..."

It was an absurdly forceful way of arguing, but Subaru recognized that forcefulness.

There was in fact someone he knew who wielded exactly this kind of logic to browbeat Subaru and others into submission.

Subaru's head spun, wondering if this was just how important people tended to be—

"Hey, what are you two muttering about back there. Answer the question."

"Ah— ah, my name's Natsuki Subaru. As you can see, a pitiful, miserable, beat-up lost soul! And the guy behind me is... um?"

"—Abel."

"Right, Abel! A nasty piece of work who hides his face under a mask and has an arrogant, insolent personality to match, but also the type to hand a knife to some lost stranger — a playboy who's made more than a few girls cry with that gap between the two sides of him. That about covers the introduction!"

"O-oh...? I am Mizelda..."

Swept up in Subaru's relentless momentum, the woman at the front — Mizelda — gave her name.

With that, he found room enough to look the women over properly, and a remarkably fitting word came to mind for the atmosphere Mizelda and the others gave off. —Amazons.

Made up mostly of women, with powerfully trained bodies, body paint matching the image of a tribal minority, some even carrying bows on their backs — it fit the description exactly.

The People of Shudrak were, without a doubt, Amazons as Subaru understood the term.

"Casually finding out that mask-man's name is Abel is a surprising development in itself, but..."

"――――"

"That's a matter for later! Listen, Mizelda-san, and all you Shudrak!"

Setting aside the matter of the masked man — Abel — for later, Subaru raised his voice to the assembled women.

From what he could tell, they didn't seem to intend on killing him without so much as a hearing. That much was clear from the fact they'd treated his wounds, and from the willingness to listen they were showing now.

If so, maybe if he spoke to them sincerely and in earnest, they'd understand.

"You might already know this, but there's a camp of imperial soldiers set up outside this forest. They're holding a girl who matters to me more than anything, and if I don't get back right now, she's in danger! So please, let me go!"

"――――"

"And another thing — those soldiers are after the People of Shudrak. They say they'd rather talk it out, but they're braced for it to come to a fight in the worst case. If it comes to that, maybe I could..."

He started to say he could mediate, arrange a meeting between the two sides, but Subaru fell silent.

If he really could pull that off, a fight between the imperial soldiers and the People of Shudrak might well be avoided — but there was no way Subaru could be the one to arrange it now.

As far as Todd and the others were concerned, Subaru was the very one who'd led them into a witchbeast's trap. There was no way they'd trust him, and expecting otherwise would be far too convenient.

Subaru had clearly weighed Rem against Todd and the others.

And to save Rem, he'd chosen to bring harm to them. There was no escaping the responsibility for that choice.

"Sorry, let me take that back. It's true the soldiers are after the Shudrak. They've got a sizable camp, so even if it came to a fight..."

"—Are you saying we'd lose?"

"Ah..."

Between the difference in numbers and the strategies available, there was no denying the disadvantage.

Cutting off Subaru as he tried to convey that, Mizelda spoke in a quiet voice. Hearing her reaction, Subaru realized he'd chosen his words wrong.

The People of Shudrak were, most likely, a hunting people.

For women who honed their skills for exactly that purpose, constantly sharpening their strength, telling them they'd lose in a fight was a landmine of landmines — an argument that should never be made.

"We know the Vollachian soldiers have come. But there is an old pact between them and us. It will not come to conflict."

"—Wait, hold on! I don't know anything about that pact, but they're serious about—"

"Enough!"

"—!"

Subaru, leaning in to press his case, was knocked backward by an impact through the wooden bars. Mizelda, having driven her fist against the bars, had indignation blazing in her eyes.

This, too, was a case of Subaru choosing his words wrong.

Just like their pride in battle, this old pact was clearly something of great importance to them.

And Subaru, unknowingly, had once again trampled all over it without a shred of tact.

"Vollachian soldiers train, moving their forces outside the forest. They have done so many times before."

"Training by moving their forces... you mean military exercises?"

It seemed to be an unfamiliar term, and Mizelda knit her brow at Subaru's words. But bit by bit, Subaru was starting to see the full shape of the trap Vollachia had set.

It was apparently a common occurrence for Vollachian soldiers to set up camp around the Buddheim Jungle under the pretext of military exercises. The People of Shudrak had grown thoroughly used to it by now.

Taking advantage of that familiarity-turned-carelessness, the Vollachian army meant to encircle the Buddheim Jungle and, in one stroke, wipe out the People of Shudrak.

If there was anything troubling him about it—

"Why would they go this far just to target these people?"

Of course, judging by the palpable presence radiating from Mizelda and the others standing before him now, the People of Shudrak were undeniably a tribe of real strength.

But Subaru couldn't fathom, not in the slightest, why it was necessary to deploy an army and surround the forest just to bring them down.

As was clear even now, they didn't seem to have any intention of leaving the forest. They were simply people living their lives here.

And yet—

"Neither Natsuki Subaru nor Abel speaks the truth. Words like that will not reach us."

"—Wait, don't tell me—"

"――――"

Mizelda slowly shook her head, declaring the conversation over.

None of the other Shudrak voiced any objection to her merciless decision. It seemed Mizelda, at the front, was the chief of this gathering — or perhaps of the whole village.

Following her decision, the People of Shudrak turned their backs on Subaru's plea, resolving to dismiss it.

And so, at the backs of the group moving away with their torches—

"Wait, please! It's not a lie, I'm not lying! Everyone's in danger! The pact... the pact is going to be broken! All of you, and Rem — you're all in danger!"

Subaru cried out desperately after them.

But with the chief's decision already made, the feet of the Shudrak did not stop. Only the young girl kept glancing back at Subaru now and again, though not enough to make her stop walking either.

No one lent an ear to Subaru, his voice going hoarse, spitting up phlegm that tasted of blood as he pleaded.

"Geh— dammit. Why is it always like this...!"

Slumping down where he sat, Subaru knocked his forehead against the bars and let out that lament.

With his right shoulder and his left hand's fingers both out of commission, he couldn't even take his frustration out on the cage. A useless wreck covered head to toe in wounds, and now even his silver tongue had failed him.

If that were so, what value was left in being Natsuki Subaru at all?

"...Just his stubbornness and his cunning, that's what."

Even as he tasted a despair dark enough to blot out everything before his eyes, Subaru resolutely refused to give up, gritting his teeth and resolving to fight on somehow.

The Subaru of before might have gone ahead and decided his own limits for himself.

But having looked back on everything he'd been through and recognized that the road he'd walked hadn't been an easy one, the Subaru of now was just a little different.

He'd become a little more stubborn than before about giving up. That was a certain light illuminating the dark road at night.

"Well, that was quite the graceless negotiation."

"――――"

Abel's contempt, laced with mockery, reached Subaru as he gnawed at the branches forming the cell's bars, scheming to see if he could somehow work open a gap to slip through.

It was infuriating. But he couldn't argue back. In truth, Subaru had stepped on every one of their landmines with perfect precision and blown up the negotiation. If there was ever a case of thoughtlessness taken to its extreme, this was it.

And yet—

"If I was graceless, you were downright nothing. Besides, didn't I already tell you? Not to go into the forest because something dangerous was in there."

"So you did. That advice served as a guide. My thanks for it."

"Doesn't matter, you still got caught. I'm brimming with a sense of helplessness right now. ...Damn it, isn't there somewhere loose on this thing?"

He tried throwing his whole body against it, but he found no hole in the seemingly makeshift wooden cell. Each of the log-like bars was driven deep into the ground, set up so solidly it was as if heavy machinery had been used.

Of course, since this world had no such thing as heavy machinery, human hands must have built it — either a great many of them working together, or someone with strength on par with Emilia or Garfiel.

"In a village full of nothing but women, how'd they manage to build something like this..."

"Do not underestimate the People of Shudrak. They are a matrilineal race in which only women are born, descendants of war gods who have lived in this forest for centuries. They have no need of a man's hand for anything but siring children — and even then, custom holds that the man is snatched from elsewhere."

"They're the real deal Amazons... Hey, don't tell me the reason we're captured is..."

Capturing men and using them as tools to extract seed for children.

That kind of thinking had genuinely existed since ancient times in isolated mountain villages. And all the more so here, in a foreign land within another world where Subaru's common sense held no sway — it was entirely plausible.

But Abel scoffed at Subaru's words,

"Rest easy. Even they are choosy about seed. From the seed of one who spoke falsehoods and tried to deceive them, nothing but filth would be born. Such seed, I imagine, they'd want no part of."

"...Falsehoods."

At Abel's words, Subaru cursed his own clumsy explanation.

That Mizelda and the other Shudrak hadn't believed him was, even accounting for his panic, plainly a result of how poorly he'd put his explanation together.

Persuasion poured out with sincerity and desperation, if it failed to understand the other party's ways and where their pride lay, was no different from a rude, reckless act. And it had made no difference at all.

"But it's not a lie. The imperial soldiers really are after the People of Shudrak. And..."

"And?"

"They'll resort to it as a last resort... no, that's wrong. As their first resort — they'll set the place on fire."

At that statement from Subaru, Abel, for the first time, drew a faint, sharp breath.

Flames unleashed on the forest — that was an event that had occurred once Todd learned of the witchbeast lurking within it. Last time, merely hearing what Subaru had to say had been enough for the imperial army to choose to burn the Buddheim Jungle to the ground.

If they actually confirmed its existence this time, the jungle being burned should be just as unavoidable now.

"...Unless Todd and the others were wiped out entirely, that is."

The moment he'd chosen to set the witchbeast on them, he'd already fully accounted for that possibility.

A plan that lured in an enemy who might die was, in every way, no different from indirect murder. Subaru had known that and carried out the decoy plan regardless — and there might really have been deaths as a result.

Thinking about it, a heavy weight formed deep in his chest, and his heart ached as if it were being squeezed shut.

But while he'd carry that revulsion and guilt over the killing for the rest of his life, what he had to focus on now was something else entirely — the fact that Todd and the others most likely had not been wiped out.

"――――"

About twenty men, and a lack of experience fighting witchbeasts.

But factoring in Todd's sharp judgment and Jamal's surprising aggressiveness, hoping that a single great serpent had annihilated all of them was, however you looked at it, too much to expect.

He had to assume, as a matter of course, that Todd and the others had survived, retreated from the forest, and were heading back to camp.

Once back at camp, Todd and the others would report Subaru's betrayal and the witchbeast's existence. Depending on the circumstances, they might even conclude — while still believing only the one point that Subaru belonged to the Shudrak — that the People of Shudrak had taken hostile action.

If it came to that, naturally, just as before, to keep the army's losses to a minimum, the imperial soldiers would set the forest ablaze without hesitation. —The People of Shudrak would be burned.

"—What is it. Why do you look at me like that?"

"No, it's..."

Just as he thought that, Abel remarked on the way Subaru was looking at him. Subaru instinctively looked away, but what he'd been thinking of was Abel's own fate.

It seemed likely that, in the previous loop too, Abel had been captured by the Shudrak just like this — in which case, if the forest had been burned, he would have burned along with it.

It seemed unlikely that, amid that great fire, the Shudrak would have gone out of their way to ensure a captive man's survival. In the worst case, he could have been left to burn to death, trapped in the cell, too late to flee.

If that were so—

"—Then the People of Shudrak, and Abel too — it's practically like I killed them."

He didn't want to let his own life end, nor Rem's, nor Abel's, nor the Shudrak's.

That was exactly why Subaru had to rise up here and search for a way to break through.

"Do you not understand this is futile? They are not so foolish as to leave room for you to break free — not with your strength, and certainly not in your wounded state. Why go this far for the sake of some mere woman?"

Abel said it with an air of exasperation, watching Subaru continue his desperate struggle, still gnawing at the wood.

But those words lit a fire in Subaru.

"It's because she's not some girl I can just wave off as 'mere' to me. There's no one who can replace her, anywhere. Rem is — Rem is the only Rem there is."

"――――"

"What about you, then — is it fine just sitting there finding fault with everything I do? I don't know why you're here, but is getting caught and giving up really all there is to it for you?"

When Subaru had first seen him, Abel had been wearing that so-called cloak of concealment, showing every sign of having come to the forest with some purpose. Even setting that aside, according to Todd, this was a man who'd handed over — without a second thought — a dagger fine enough to have been a gift from the Emperor himself.

It was hard to imagine, and harder still to believe, that he was here for no reason at all.

"Sitting there on the cold dirt like that, what is it you're trying to do?"

"—I was merely waiting for the moment."

Abel answered Subaru's question in an unusually quiet voice.

It sounded different in character from his earlier, somewhat provocative remarks, the mockery he'd been aiming at Subaru — like words that had spilled out from Abel's true feelings.

"Waiting for the moment? You mean an opportunity, like a chance? A chance for what..."

"I know nothing of this 'chance' of yours, but what I was waiting for was the board being set. Until it was ready, I committed myself to observing — for if I put my own hand in too soon, it would only muddy things with unwanted color. In truth, I judged the real moment would come once those outside the forest made their move, but..."

"――――"

"If they mean to burn the forest, I can hardly go on sitting idle any longer."

With that, Abel unfolded his crossed arms and slowly rose to his feet.

At his slender, upright bearing and his words, Subaru's eyes went wide and he froze.

"What now, with that dazed look on your face? Rather disrespectful, to turn such a face toward me."

"I— I don't know about disrespectful, but... you actually believe what I said? I mean, the Shudrak..."

"They did not believe you. You sullied their pride, and dismissed the old pact they hold so dear as meaningless. A negotiation failure fit to be recorded for posterity, truly."

"Ugh...!"

With no defense he could offer even to himself, Subaru was crushed by Abel's assessment.

To Subaru's grimace, Abel continued, "But—" And as Subaru raised his head, Abel gazed at him through the mask—

"I am not of the People of Shudrak. Their pride, their precious pact — to me, it is as good as dust. What matters is only the truth you have brought, free of falsehood."

"...And what if I was lying?"

"That is obvious enough. —Pay for it with your life."

Those words carried a weight entirely distinct from any offhand talk of "death."

Abel meant it in earnest — that if Subaru's actions proved wrong, he was to atone with "death." This was no game, no idle jest; it was a moment demanding genuine resolve.

Sensing that, Subaru straightened his back without meaning to.

Somewhere along the way he'd stopped wrestling with the cage, and now Subaru faced Abel before him head-on. Fixing his gaze on Subaru's dark eyes, Abel's own stare grew heavier with pressure.

"Answer with care, Natsuki Subaru. —Are you prepared to sacrifice everything for the sake of what you wish to save?"

"――――"

The question was direct, allowing no room for hesitation or falsehood.

If he wove any falsehood into his answer here, he would not live. Abel's voice carried enough force to make Subaru believe exactly that.

Subaru took Abel's question into his heart.

Whether he had the resolve to sacrifice everything else, in order to save the thing he wished to save.

As for his answer to that question—

"—I have no such resolve."

"――――"

"All I can offer up is myself. —If it's just that, I can wager everything I've got."

Not with his stabbed right arm, but with his left hand — its fingers broken — pressed to his chest, Subaru gave his answer.

This was Subaru's honest, unfalsified answer to Abel's question.

Being told to sacrifice everything, absolutely everything — that was something he could never bring himself to accept.

To do that, there were simply too many things precious to him in this world — too many dazzling things he hadn't even seen yet.

So—

"A shrewd, impertinent answer, that. You infuriating fool."

"――――"

"But you spoke no falsehood. Very well — I shall refrain from burning you, then."

Hearing that answer, hearing Abel say those words, Subaru felt in his bones that he'd been spared his life.

Cold sweat suddenly poured out from every inch of his body all at once, and Subaru understood that his life had been in Abel's hands the whole time.

Just as he'd assessed back when they'd met on the grasslands, Abel was by no means a superhumanly powerful fighter.

Compared to the powerful figures Subaru had seen or dealt with so far, his strength didn't step outside the realm of an ordinary person. And yet, Subaru still felt he'd narrowly escaped death.

A power different from mere physical or swordsman's strength dwelled within Abel — that much was certain.

"Then this will go quickly. —You there, girl."

"Eep!?"

Paying no mind to Subaru, still drenched in sweat over his narrow escape, Abel suddenly called out to someone. At once, a small shriek rose from the shadows in response.

Startled, Subaru turned to look, and there, in the direction of Abel's gaze, in the shade of a tree some distance from the cage, was the girl, watching them timidly.

The girl with pink-tipped hair flinched at their gazes and tried to bolt—

"Run, and you'll lose your chance, girl. That's not truly what you want, is it."

"Ngh..."

Caught off guard by Abel's preemptive words, the girl let out a small groan. Then, looking rather sheepish, she trudged reluctantly toward them.

And, lips trembling hesitantly, she said, "Uu, Uu is..."

"Mii says do not listen to men's talk. But Uu is curious. About you. Uu is curious."

"...Me?"

With that, the girl who called herself Uu pointed at Subaru. Caught off guard by this unexpected declaration of curiosity, Subaru's eyes went wide, and the girl nodded at him.

"Before, you tried so hard. Uu's people, in danger. But Mii did not listen."

"Ah..."

"Why did you try so hard? You have nothing to do with Uu's people."

Why interfere, when it had nothing to do with him?

For a moment, Subaru's breath caught, thinking he was being called out for meddling. But the girl's words carried no such intent. She was simply, purely curious.

Why Subaru had fought so desperately not just for himself, but for the People of Shudrak too.

Even Subaru himself didn't know the answer to that, but—

"...I guess I just didn't want you to make that face."

"—?"

"That look, clouded with hatred — having to glare at an enemy like that. I don't want you to have to feel that way."

The girl who'd pierced someone through with a poisoned arrow, watching over their "death" with eyes full of hatred.

In Subaru's eyes, that hatred still lingered even now, along with the guilt over the intense event that had led to it. It churned inside him. It had become a thorn, aching with pain.

That was not something that should happen again. It shouldn't repeat.

Return by Death — better never to have to use it at all.

Even so, if there was a way — in a world redone through the price of Subaru's own death — to guide the people he crossed paths with onto a better road—

"I think that's the reason it's worth trying my hardest."

"...Uu does not understand."

Even hearing Subaru's answer, the girl couldn't grasp its true meaning. Of course not. Talking about something like this to someone who knew nothing of Return by Death could only sound like nonsense.

And Subaru had no intention of making her understand it, either. Even the fact that such a thing existed had no place in this girl's life.

"—Satisfied now? Neither of us has the luxury for a long chat, I'd think — same goes for you."

"...Ah, yeah, sorry."

Abel cut through the conversation between Subaru and the girl without a shred of real interest.

Then, as Abel turned to face the girl, she too seemed to feel the same pressure Subaru had felt earlier, tensing her small body and looking up at him.

"Girl, I have no intention of dragging out conversation with you. That one from before — Mizelda, was it? Bring her here. She is the chief, is she not?"

"Mii? What will you talk with Mii about?"

"Nothing much. I merely have a proposal to make."

"A proposal?"

With Subaru and the girl both tilting their heads at him, Abel gave a deep nod.

Then, forming a smile that, even through the mask, couldn't be seen but was surely there,

"—Tell her I will undergo the Lifeblood Ritual. It is the swiftest way to persuade them."

—so he declared.
