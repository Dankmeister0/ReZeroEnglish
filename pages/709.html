Arc 9 Chapter 14: "The Strongest"

—

Hair red as a blazing, brilliant flame, and a pair of blue eyes that seemed to hold a clear, cloudless sky captive — a being that looked as though it had been shaped, from the crown of his head to the very tips of his toes, out of pure, unadulterated heroism. A chosen one whose very birth had been blessed by the world, who had stood at the opposite pole from Aldebaran since the very beginning. Beneath the gaze of Reinhard, standing tall and dignified at the center of the vast, chill night sea of sand, every fine hair on Aldebaran's body stood on end.

—'Sword Saint' Reinhard van Astrea.

Needless to say, he was a being without equal — not merely within the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, but across the entire world — and one of the two greatest obstacles standing in the way of Aldebaran's plan. The other of those two obstacles, incidentally, was none other than Natsuki Subaru — and along with Beatrice, who had already been a source of concern, containment of him had been successfully achieved via Ol Shamak. Which meant that, among the members whose confrontation was all but certain, the greatest wall still standing was this man.

—Now then, Reinhard van Astrea was the strongest. 'Blue Lightning' Cecilus Segmunt was faster; 'Adorer' Halibel was more technically skilled; 'Mad Prince' Vague Adgard stood further from death; the great spirits counted among the Four Greats surpassed him in sheer volume of mana; the Witch Cult's Sin Archbishops possessed more absurd Authorities; the Witches of Sin had taken more lives between them; run-of-the-mill good people told better lies; run-of-the-mill bad people were more relentlessly persistent.

Broken down by category, there was no shortage of individuals who outmatched Reinhard in one respect or another. And yet, Reinhard van Astrea's seat as the strongest never wavered. — Toppling that fortress of invincibility was the absolute condition Aldebaran had to meet in order to win.

"Even I gotta admit, the position I'm in here is way too rough."

"A little late to complain about how crappy your hand is. Just play me, the joker, well."

"...From the dragon-me's point of view, how's the 'Sword Saint' look?"

"Same opinion as the human-me... not quite, actually. This body probably picks up on how strong he really is better than the human-me could, so if anything, I might think he's even more dangerous."

The one behind him, narrowing golden eyes as it stared past Aldebaran at Reinhard, speaking in a manner utterly lacking the dignity befitting a superorganism, was the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica — no, it was 'Divine Dragon Aldebaran,' the being created when Aldebaran's 'Memory' was installed into the now-empty Dragonhusk.

'Aldebaran,' sharing baseline knowledge, thought processes, and even present objectives to the point of near-identity with him, was a lifeline Aldebaran — otherwise all but isolated and without support — desperately depended on. 'Aldebaran' had even volunteered himself, saying to use him well as the joker. Aldebaran had never intended to hold back to begin with, but he resolved anew to wring every last drop of use out of him.

Just as Aldebaran finished re-confirming his hand of cards—

"—Of the 'Sword Saint's' line, Reinhard van Astrea."

—

A prickling sensation raised gooseflesh across his skin, and Aldebaran narrowed his eyes inside his helmet. Declaring one's name and title to the opponent one faced was the custom of those who fought in this world. Astonishingly, even the Sin Archbishops made a point of observing it — some for the sake of their own pride, some out of respect for their opponent, some to bask in a sense of superiority, some to steel their own cowardly hearts — each imposing their own particular reason upon themselves.

The reason 'Sword Saint' Reinhard adhered to that custom was obvious just from looking at his eyes. Even in this situation, Reinhard was paying Aldebaran respect — despite surely having heard from Flam exactly what Aldebaran had done at the tower.

That was Reinhard's virtue, and at the same time the very cause of the unbridgeable gulf between him and others. Aldebaran had no intention of spelling out in detail which way he took it, but—

"Witch Cult's Sin Archbishop in charge of Pride, Stride Vollachia."

"—!"

"...Wouldn't that be a funny thing to say?"

—He'd resolved, at the very least, to spitefully trample all over the man's respect.

"—BOHAAAH!!"

The instant Reinhard's eyes widened at an answer that stuck its tongue out at the hand extended to him, an ungainly roar burst out, and along with it, white light surged toward the 'Sword Saint' where he stood. It was a horribly clumsy blow, born of unfamiliarity with the newly hijacked Dragonhusk — but the power of the 'Dragon's' breath unleashed from that mighty body was genuine.

If the breath fired at Garfiel and Ezzo on the top floor of the Pleiades Watchtower right after the 'Memory' overwrite had been a mere sneeze, this one was a shot loaded with clear, deliberate intent to destroy. As if to prove the difference in concentrated destructive force, an intangible wave of white light — the kind that looked as though it would remake forever the sand sea empty of anyone but Aldebaran and company — swallowed Reinhard whole.

He didn't think it would actually kill him. But if he didn't go at it meaning to kill, it wouldn't even qualify as a fight. As proof of that—

"—Get back, me!!"

"WAAAUGH!?"

Having spent his breath entirely, leaving white smoke and an enormous crater in the sand sea, 'Aldebaran' snagged Aldebaran on a claw thrust out on reflex and flung him sideways without holding back. Tossed about bodily by the sudden betrayal of his literal other half, Aldebaran flew nearly horizontal, rolling, rolling, rolling across the sand before finally coming to a stop. Then, spitting out sand that had worked its way in through the gaps in his helmet, he raised his head — and saw the fact that 'Aldebaran's' action hadn't been betrayal, but support.

Reinhard, who should have taken the 'Dragon's' breath head-on, leapt at 'Aldebaran' unharmed, and a kaiju showdown of thoroughly mismatched scale had begun.

"Not even a burn on you!? Forget being unlikeable — what's the actual mechanism here!?"

"I borrowed the power of the 'Dragon Sword.' This blade won't be destroyed even on the day the world itself meets its end. It's built that way. So I used it to strike the breath aside."

"That answer just proves it's YOU who's amazing, not the sword! And it doesn't explain the lack of burns either!"

"That would be the effect of the Divine Protection of Sunburn."

Giving that deflating reply, Reinhard flipped in midair and unleashed a kick. It collided head-on with a dragon claw swung with force enough to tear the sky apart, and the two produced a thunderous blast, like fireworks or cannon fire going off at point-blank range. Both blows carried destructive force that even a warrior of unbroken, ceaseless training probably couldn't withstand a single hit of — yet, terrifyingly, for both the 'Sword Saint' and the 'Dragon' alike, this was just an ordinary attack. Which meant the exchange continued without pause, without any wind-up or retreat.

"Damn it— GRAAAH...!"

Boom, boom — unbelievable detonation sounds chained together as the attacks collided again and again, and each shockwave that followed blew the sand of the sand sea into the air, threatening to tear Aldebaran, prone on the ground, loose from where he lay. A clash between the two strongest beings on the ground — even watching from tens of meters away offered no safe zone. But Aldebaran could not choose to flee the battlefield out of concern for his own precious skin. — There were limits to how the Matrix could be updated. If he strayed too far from where the Matrix was currently defined, the domain would collapse, and he'd lose the few advantages he had over Reinhard. That was something he had to avoid at all costs.

"...Strong. But — this is far too different from before."

Meanwhile, leaving Aldebaran clinging to the battlefield aside, Reinhard — who had continued trading blows with the 'Dragon' — fell back a great distance and murmured just that. At the serious expression on the 'Sword Saint,' hand resting on the hilt of the 'Dragon Sword,' not even short of breath, 'Aldebaran' — showing no sign of exhaustion himself either — growled, "Ahh?" In response, Reinhard shook his head and said, "No, it's—"

"When I visited the watchtower the other day with Flam and Ezzo-dono, I clashed with you — with the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica — over a misunderstanding, but both your movements and your intent are far too different from that time."

At Reinhard's observation, 'Aldebaran' fell silent. But his assessment was correct. In truth, the one Reinhard had fought that time had been the real 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica — though it had likely been in the emptied Dragonhusk state, having just birthed a Dragonkin. Comparing that time to now, one could only conclude it was, quite literally, a different person. Having landed on the correct answer and proven his insight, Reinhard went on.

"You're weaker now than you were last time. Why is that?"

"...Click."

"—?"

"No, no no, no no no no — fair enough, you're right to say it. Sure enough, just like you say, I haven't got a handle on this body yet. So if it comes off as 'kinda weak, huh?', there's nothing strange about that, not one bit. Except—"

Faced with Reinhard's guileless doubt, 'Aldebaran' cut himself off there. Then, on a 'Dragon's' profile far too different from a human's, he nonetheless made unmistakable the shape of a grin, and—

"—made me want to show you a thing or two."

The instant that declaration left him, 'Aldebaran' spread both wings and took flight, whipping up wind. The distance between him and Reinhard vanished in an instant, and he swung down both arms, thick as great trees, aiming to crush him flat. That utterly telegraphed attack, Reinhard dodged as though ignoring the poor footing — and in fact, ignoring it thanks to the Divine Protection of Sand Play — leaping into close range to evade and drive home a counter.

"Gu—!"

Splitting his long legs between heaven and earth, Reinhard's kick struck home in what would be, on a human, the 'Dragon's' solar plexus, sending the massive body springing straight upward. Chasing after 'Aldebaran,' launched skyward by force rather than under his own wings, Reinhard too leapt, catching up to the airborne 'Dragon' — no, overtaking him. He then planted the tip of his foot on the back of the oncoming 'Dragon,' forcibly halting its upward momentum.

"I'm sorry, but that's as far as you go."

Reinhard, who appeared to be standing on 'Aldebaran's' back in midair, raised an empty hand-blade — a weapon sharper than any ordinary famed sword. That single flashing strike might well have severed even the 'Divine Dragon's' Dragonhusk—

"...Told you I'd show you a thing or two. Just — bear with me a little longer."

An instant before the hand-blade landed, 'Aldebaran,' having been thoroughly outmaneuvered, muttered just that. At the sound of the 'Dragon's' voice — pained, yet laced with scheming — Reinhard's brow drew together faintly. Watching from a distance, Aldebaran, looking up from the ground at the superbeings in the sky, spoke.

"—Plan A."

"—!"

Given the distance, it seemed unlikely Reinhard could have heard Aldebaran's voice. He wouldn't be surprised if the man had, of course — but there was no point in dwelling on the question.

—Because the light of a star, twinkling in the night sky, was already raining down, aimed squarely at the 'Sword Saint.'

—Al Shario.

It was one of the bona fide Forbidden Arts, a specialty of the 'Witches' of old. It was the realization of an unbelievable miracle: using the property by which mana of the same kind draws together, one could pull down to the earth a star — a colossal mass of mana, shining and endlessly burning far off in the sky.

Naturally, dropping a star on a targeted location demanded exquisitely precise magical control, and an unimaginable quantity of mana to make that control possible. In exchange for that difficulty of execution, the mana contained in a star's light rivaled that of a great spirit — it was said to have once brought down even a pack of 'Dragons.'

Its destructive power easily surpassed even the calamitous breath of a 'Dragon' — a being granted the right to destroy the world merely by roaring — and could probably be called the very pinnacle of destructive acts.

"Magic, you see, is a miraculous discipline that holds within it the potential to turn any and every imagination into reality. That a magic powerful enough to laugh off a 'Dragon's' breath came into being was inevitable, if there existed someone who wished to surpass it... In time, a magic even more powerful than the one that fells stars will surely be born too. Human beings really are quite 'Greedy' creatures — don't you find that wonderful?"

So spoke the beautiful white-haired 'Witch' to Aldebaran, wearing an insufferably smug look on her face. That smug face still irritated him even now, thinking back on it, but he could believe it hadn't been an empty boast or bluff. Because that smug-faced 'Witch' had made him witness the genuine article with his own eyes. In other words, this was destruction surpassing even a 'Dragon's' breath — for the time being, at least, the pinnacle of destruction.

"God damn it."

Every possible "best" left room to be surpassed, by some new idea and the magic that could realize it. Grinding his teeth at the fact that, exactly as the 'Witch' had taught, he'd gone and mentally noted that Al Shario was no exception either, Aldebaran watched hopefully for the results of the falling starlight.

—He hated to admit it, but Aldebaran had studied under the 'Witch' and been granted knowledge of every kind. Among the wisdom he'd received, no small portion was knowledge of magic. However, while he could master the theory and incantations needed to realize magic, Aldebaran lacked the crucial ingredient: talent. Knowing how to boil water didn't mean you could fill a bathtub with a single cup of it.

So then, why had the 'Witch' bestowed upon Aldebaran knowledge of magic he couldn't even use? Had she simply wanted to show off her knowledge and wear that smug face? Or was it to smooth even a little of her student's path, on the reasoning that even unusable magic knowledge could help overcome certain difficult situations? Both were nothing more than secondary effects of the 'Witch's' true intent.

The real reason the 'Witch' had crammed Aldebaran's head full of magical knowledge was—

"—Al Shario."

The incantation of the 'Dragon' launched into the sky brought a star crashing down onto the night sand sea. The 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica — possessed of overwhelming power, yet rendered hollow by the severing of its soul — housed 'Aldebaran,' a 'Memory' lodged within that Dragonhusk, a being who had at last come to satisfy the condition required to make shine the treasure given him by the 'Witch,' one that had long gone to waste.

In other words, this — this was the very reason the 'Witch' had filled Aldebaran with knowledge. Aldebaran's knowledge, combined with 'Aldebaran,' who had obtained the 'Dragon's' Dragonhusk — that combination was the means by which, at the end of a stone-pile vast enough to suggest a nayuta, the greatest wall could finally be overcome.

—

Atop the 'Dragon's' back, looking up at the sky, even Reinhard's eyes went wide. The falling star's light carried a magnitude no less than the one Sphinx had unleashed during that final battle in the Vollachian Empire — the fight that had made the end of the world feel imminent. Trembling at the sheer irregularity of a 'Dragon' pulling off, entirely unaided and under its own power, the very Forbidden Art Sphinx had barely managed to realize by exploiting every possible cheat, Aldebaran kept a sharp eye on Reinhard's movements up in the sky.

He wasn't optimistic enough to think Al Shario simply couldn't be blocked. In fact, 'Blue Lightning,' considered on par with the 'Sword Saint,' had achieved the outlandish feat of cutting through a star dropped during that battle for the capital with a single sword stroke. That star-cleaving had been the result of Cecilus's swordsmanship combined with the power of the 'Dream Blade,' and Aldebaran didn't think Reinhard, unable to draw the 'Dragon Sword,' could pull off the same feat. But while that was cause for a little complacency, it wasn't reason to underestimate him. And so—

"O—OOOOOH—!!"

Before Reinhard could show any response to the light of the star, a roaring 'Aldebaran' spun halfway around in midair and slammed a wing blow into Reinhard, who had been facing away. Reinhard blocked it on reflex with elbow and knee, but the 'Dragon's' aim wasn't to slice him open with the wing — it was to use the impact to blow him away, sending him closer to the star.

"—!!"

As it had been at their first contact, Reinhard had a means of running through the sky — no, the Divine Protection of Clouds, letting him use clouds as footholds. Before he could use it to right himself, 'Aldebaran's' roar swept away the surrounding clouds and knocked Reinhard further out into open sky.

With no foothold and no escape, Reinhard, defenseless in open air, collided with the light of the star. Under conditions far worse than the star-cleaving at the battle for the capital, even Reinhard should have had no way to answer a star's blow—

"—Granted."

In that instant, the toe of the leg Reinhard had desperately extended touched the star, and the explosive energy that ought to have erupted there — swallowing the 'Sword Saint' whole in a blast that spelled the star's end — should have occurred. But the explosion that should have happened didn't, and instead, Reinhard walked along the star's surface.

"Wha—" "—the hell!?"

Having dodged the falling heavens meant to force a choice between destruction and evasion, Aldebaran and 'Aldebaran's' cries of shock rang out across the sand sea. Answering that dual shock, Reinhard, walking along the star's flank and arriving at its very peak, said—

"The Divine Protection of Ball-Balancing."

"Ball... balancing..." "He's treating it like a circus ball?"

At that confirmed answer, Aldebaran was left speechless that a beam of destruction had been likened to a mere "fireball." But that speechlessness was quickly overwritten by a surging sense of danger. If the sound of the name Divine Protection of Ball-Balancing was to be trusted, then a circus performer balancing on a ball didn't merely stand atop it — they made it dance freely under them—

"Son of a—"

Reinhard's foot seemed to give the star's surface a light tap. In the next instant, the trajectory of the star brought down by Al Shario changed, and the starlight came bearing down furiously on 'Aldebaran,' the very caster who had summoned it. Seeing that, the 'Dragon' spat a curse and met it with a breath poured out from the very depths of him.

—

For an instant, white and red light wrapped the sky in dazzling brilliance, creating the illusion that the night had come to its end. The sight he should have seen from slamming Reinhard into the star had instead had its process forcibly twisted, converging into nothing but the result of the night sky itself being scorched. — No, and that still wasn't the end of it. The shattered star broke into countless glittering shards, and the sand sea was buried beneath a carpet-bombing of light.

"Gh— guh...!"

Naturally, Aldebaran, who had been trying to witness it all from the ground, was blown off his feet as well, and lay collapsed on the sand, his entire body covered in severe burns. Suddenly, he caught a rumbling, distinct from the star's fall, coming from somewhat farther off, and when he turned his barely-cooperating neck that way, he saw 'Aldebaran' — one wing broken, the other severed clean off — fallen powerless to the ground. And then—

"I'm sorry it came to this."

Reinhard came walking up beside the fallen Aldebaran. Setting aside the half-dead 'Dragon' and the half-dead Aldebaran, there wasn't the faintest sign that he himself had just been caught up in a cataclysm on the scale of an exploding star's light. He'd heard that the Sin Archbishop of Greed had once managed to kill Reinhard — how, exactly, remained a mystery.

In any case, Reinhard, a look of quiet chagrin in his eyes, was already certain this fight had reached its conclusion — and sadly, Aldebaran had no means left to overturn that. Indeed, just as Reinhard judged. — The 8,466th time, too, was his win. But—

"—Next."

In that instant, behind Reinhard, 'Aldebaran,' fallen gracelessly to the ground, raised his head and let loose a reckless, desperate blast of 'Dragon' breath in their direction. The 'Dragon's' breath, unleashed from a wrecked state, was a shadow of its full self by comparison, but Reinhard had no option to ignore it. — Because Aldebaran was standing right along the line of that white light, and if Reinhard dodged, Aldebaran would be reduced to ash without fail.

"That's not a method I can approve of...!"

Turning, Reinhard drew the 'Dragon Sword,' sheath and all, and cut the breath aside head-on. Reinhard, his voice raised in righteous anger — the fury covering his heart was because 'Aldebaran' had launched an attack that dragged in Aldebaran, who should have been his ally. How very kind of him. Next time, that too would be worked into the plan.

Deciding as much, Aldebaran undid the medicine packet in his mouth and downed the poison.

"Wha—!"

Noticing the change, Reinhard turned back, his expression tensing. Watching that, Aldebaran, convulsing in fits on the ground, sent his consciousness drifting far away, drowning in a froth of blood—

"—That's as far as you go."

A dignified voice sliced through the coldly frozen sky, and light pierced the airborne 'Dragon' at a slant. Even as the impact bore straight through his whole body, Aldebaran — his spinal reflex fully conditioned after several hundred deaths by falling — clung to a protrusion on the 'Dragon's' back, avoiding another parachute-less skydive, and plunged with his literal other half toward the ground, the ground, headlong. And then—

"I recommend you surrender. I don't want to have to cut you down."

"Don't get all high and mighty on me, hero. I'm gonna win regardless. — This time the star just wasn't on my side."

—Aldebaran's 8,467th battle had begun.

—Having left the Pleiades Watchtower behind, the encounter battle against 'Sword Saint' Reinhard van Astrea had begun in the night sky above the Augria Sand Dunes.

This fight, which began with being run through by light and sent plummeting, Aldebaran took simply as the arrival of a moment that had always been coming; he wasn't surprised by Reinhard joining the battle. He couldn't help thinking that moment had come somewhat sooner than expected, but it was a fight he'd always known would happen.

If anything, getting to fight at the moment of least exhaustion was exactly what he wanted. — Or so he could put on a positive front, right up until he'd heard "—that's as far as you go" a hundred times. Once he'd been told "—that's as far as you go" more than eight thousand times since then, even bravado wore thin.

To begin with, after taking that very first "—that's as far as you go," it took over a hundred more of them before he managed a safe soft-catch landing in the sand sea via the 'Dragon's' wing. Up until then, he kept falling, breaking his neck or his spine, only to be sent back to "—that's as far as you go," forced through baffling retry after retry, again and again.

"When I finally figured out this '—that's as far as you go' was Reinhard's '—that's as far as you go'! man, there was some weird kind of emotion to that realization..."

Not knowing just what exactly was killing you was not good for the mind. To Aldebaran, being physically taken down was unpleasant, but not the worst of it. What he feared wasn't so much having his body wounded as having his mind wounded. On that count, learning the true identity behind the repeated "—that's as far as you go"s had felt like escaping a labyrinth. Though that clear, refreshed feeling dissipated soon enough.

"Guess I got lucky it didn't lock me into an unwinnable checkmate save..."

Depending on the circumstances, there was a real chance of setting the Matrix right before an unavoidable end — that was about as close to a single weak point as Aldebaran's Authority had. He'd miraculously never once made that mistake so far, but there was no guarantee that would hold forever. Especially now, preparing for the unforeseen, he'd been repeating the definition and redefinition of the Matrix at fifteen-second intervals. Thanks to that, he could return to just before Reinhard's "—that's as far as you go," but if he'd instead set the Matrix's starting point right in the middle of "—that's as fa—," he might have been stuck restarting from "—r as you go," his mind ground down to nothing without ever being able to do a thing.

Once the Matrix updated, there was no going back to any task before that point. Which meant Aldebaran had no choice but to conquer 'Sword Saint' Reinhard van Astrea using a Matrix that began from "—that's as far as you go."

Toward that end, he had already repeated "—that's as far as you go" more than eight thousand times. It was a dizzyingly hopeless fight, like challenging a mountain of despairing height at a tortoise's pace, but with 'Aldebaran' also in the picture, it wasn't as though he stood no chance at all against Reinhard. Thanks to that, preparations to conquer Reinhard were steadily moving forward.

Not just with Reinhard — conquering any enemy required breaking them down into their component elements. Dismantle the elements that made a thing what it was, and you naturally closed in on your opponent's core. Reinhard, too, wasn't simply the strongest for no reason. — There was a reason behind that strength. Aldebaran broke that reason down into the individual elements of "mind, technique, body," and unraveled them one by one.

Body. — Reinhard's ridiculous physical ability owed nothing to Divine Protections; the cause was that his own Od, performance abnormal in the extreme, naturally handled the mana inside and outside his body without the slightest loss — indeed, in a form that produced an efficient, explosive synergy. There existed a technique called the Flow Method, by which one enhanced the body using mana circulated within it — a technique acquired through outstanding talent and years of training. But Reinhard, born with the Excess Mana Circulation Constitution, naturally handled this from birth, existing in a state of constant enhancement as the norm. That was close in meaning to saying that Reinhard's ordinary breathing had hundreds of times the effect of an average person's deep breath — and still left room for Reinhard himself to take an actual deep breath on top of that.

Technique. — As far as Aldebaran's prior knowledge and eight thousand clashes had revealed, the number of Divine Protections Reinhard possessed stood at two hundred fifty-one. Those Protections ranged widely, starting with his signature Divine Protection of the Sword Saint; through combat-useful ones like the Divine Protection of Foresight and the Divine Protection of Arrow Avoidance; to environment-adapting ones like the Divine Protection of Mist and the Divine Protection of Sand Play; all the way to prank-like ones such as the Divine Protection of Shoelaces and the Divine Protection of Layered Clothing. Reinhard held so many rare, powerful Divine Protections — each one alone enough to make someone a national representative — that all ten fingers weren't enough to count them, and his versatility was so overwhelming that even a trap elaborate enough to snare and kill the world's wisest 'Witch' would be broken through with ease.

Mind. — Lastly, the spirit of Reinhard, who took upon himself the role of 'Sword Saint.' His habit of calling himself merely "of the Sword Saint's line" was probably proof that he believed he hadn't yet reached his own ideal of what a 'Sword Saint' should be — but for someone standing opposed to him, at odds in purpose, Reinhard's self-consciousness on that score was beside the point. No matter what Reinhard himself thought, his actions and his sense of mission were entirely worthy of the name 'Sword Saint,' and he would let no enemy of the kingdom — no, of the world — slip past him. 'Sword Saint' Reinhard van Astrea was a cog built for killing the enemies of the world.

The above was the result of Aldebaran's own breakdown of Reinhard's "mind, technique, body." Which meant that conquering Reinhard hinged on crushing, one by one, each of these elements exposed by that dismantling. And so—

"—That's as far as you go."

And with those words, from the moment the 'Dragon' was run through by light, Aldebaran's next battle began.

—8,888.

"—That's as far as you go."

Something he'd come to realize with certainty amid the endless string of battles: for a constitution like Reinhard's Excess Mana Circulation Constitution — which drew in ambient mana to excess and circulated it through his entire body — the Augria Sand Dunes, saturated with Miasma, mana in its corrupted form, was, as far as he could imagine, the worst possible location. In other words, scanning the whole world over, there was no field — save the Augria Sand Dunes — where Reinhard would be weakened to this degree.

Even when he'd doused him in Al Cram, generating hypergravity, catching him in a gravitational field strong enough that 'Aldebaran' himself was crushed under his own weight, and even when Reinhard had powered his way through it by sheer force — his physical ability was still at its weakest state right here in the Augria Sand Dunes.

"—Next."

—10,212.

"—That's as far as you go."

Using light refraction, he concealed the fact that he'd sealed the surroundings in walls of ice. Filling that sealed ice chamber with fog, he then vaporized all the water within the space at once, causing it to detonate. A pressure collapse within the sealed space from a steam explosion — an attack fusing magic and science, one whose outcome couldn't be predicted and whose cause couldn't be fully identified given this world's level of civilization.

It was a stratagem meant to simultaneously deceive both the Divine Protection of Fire Avoidance and the Divine Protection of Water Avoidance, through a steam explosion that was neither simple fire nor water — but Reinhard was granted the Divine Protection of Playing with Fire, which neutralized the blast, and the Divine Protection of Playing with Water, which averted the water damage, and broke through this one too.

"—Next."

—16,008.

"—That's as far as you go."

He created thunderclouds from the temperature difference between fire and water magic, then, applying wind and earth magic, wrapped the 'Dragon Sword' in electrical charge. Working on the principle of a lightning rod, but with no escape route, lightning strikes rained down on Reinhard. Even Reinhard couldn't escape true lightning speed. Bolts irregular in their strikes, reminiscent of the human-realm-transcending sword speed of Cecilus that he'd seen so much of in the Empire, bore straight through Reinhard.

This, Reinhard neutralized completely with the Divine Protection of Thunderclouds; the lightning strikes failed to catch him, but they did catch the 'Dragon Sword,' which took on powerful magnetism. Turning that against him, Aldebaran advanced to the next plan prepared in advance. — Using that magnetism, he sealed Reinhard's movement with the iron sand lying abundant throughout the sand sea, aiming to drive an attack into the resulting fatal opening.

But that too was evaded via the Divine Protection of Sand Play and the Divine Protection of Mud Escape, and ended in failure.

"—Next."

—64,799.

"—That's as far as you go."

The previous failure had taken its toll, and for one moment he forgot the conditioning he should have had soaked into his very soul. In that instant, forgetting the routine of clinging to the protrusion on the 'Dragon's' back, he found his whole body wrapped in a sense of weightlessness, then lost all sense of up, down, or sideways as he was swallowed into the wind and tossed about. Far off, unable to catch the falling Aldebaran, only the roaring war-cry of the 'Dragon' reached his ears.

Probing with his tongue inside his mouth, he released the potent poison once more — for who knew how many times by now. Falling from that height meant certain death on impact. But if, by some remote chance, this time were the exception, that would be a problem. So, just in case, Aldebaran downed the poison. In the time before he hit the ground, agony like his very blood boiling painted his mind pure white—

"—Next."

—132,044.

"—That's as far as you go."

Sadly enough, in the course of repeating a battle that averaged under two minutes again, and again, and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again and again — over, and over, and over again, without end — Aldebaran's own skill had climbed higher and higher.

"Don't get all high and mighty on me, hero. I'm gonna win regardless. — This time the star just wasn't on my side."

Staring straight ahead at Reinhard, facing him, Aldebaran reached into his coat, drew out a single book, and, opening it, held it high overhead. It was the 'Book of the Dead' he'd carried off from the Pleiades Watchtower — Aldebaran's own book.

The plan of showing its contents to shake Reinhard's mind had already ended in failure, quite some plans ago. Reinhard held the Divine Protection of Nightmares, which countered contamination of the mind, and so he was fiercely resistant to any brainwashing-type attack that directly stirred up the psyche. It probably couldn't nullify an Authority outright, but simply being able to ignore the vast majority of its effect was more than enough.

Which was to say, the one Aldebaran was showing the 'Book of the Dead' to wasn't Reinhard at all. It was the other 'Aldebaran,' standing by behind him.

"—Sync complete."

Sensing his intent, 'Aldebaran' dropped his gaze to the 'Book of the Dead,' not yet even fully dry, had its contents driven straight into his brain, and updated his 'Memory.' The 'Book of the Dead' carried off with him recorded every last one of the one-sidedly brutal battles Aldebaran had experienced against Reinhard. — By showing it to 'Aldebaran,' Aldebaran could bring his partner, otherwise left behind by the domain, up to speed at any time.

Both failures and successes — though as things stood, there had yet to be a single successful plan — but simply by sharing how each of those failed plans had been crushed, and how 'Aldebaran' had moved within them, 'Aldebaran's' own proficiency climbed markedly. For that very purpose, Aldebaran had watched every single battle through to its end.

"—As long as I don't give up, nobody's gonna beat me."

That was the absolute conviction by which Aldebaran kept fighting on against more than a hundred thousand defeats. It would never waver. — Having resolved as much, I was the one who started this.

"—Of the 'Sword Saint's' line, Reinhard van Astrea."

By now, right alongside "—that's as far as you go," it was an introduction he'd heard well over a hundred thousand times. Taking that in, Aldebaran drew a breath, held it, and went on.

"I'm a trailing star."

Answering that phrase, spoken as if on a whim, Reinhard came at him. The distance between them vanished in an instant, and despite conditions being at their worst amid the miasma-filled sand sea, the 'Sword Saint' — ignoring the poor footing entirely thanks to the Divine Protection of Sand Play, granted strength by the night through the Divine Protection of the Night Sky, the unsheathable 'Dragon Sword' at his hip — closed in, straight and true.

"—!!"

Fed the 'Memory' of more than a hundred thousand battles, a roar, its clumsiness gone, became white light of destruction and met the closing Reinhard head-on. The 'Dragon's' breath was no magic at all, nothing more than a torrent of mana, pure and primitive in its purpose, simply and overwhelmingly destroying whatever it touched. A single shot that seemed to carve away the vast sand sea forever, spreading out radially with no room to escape — even Reinhard had no means of dodging it. — So he didn't dodge it.

"Su—"

In that instant, a single flash matched the 'Dragon's' breath, splitting the white light clean in two head-on. What did it was the 'Dragon Sword,' Reid, swung fully from below to above, still sheathed. Its legendary blade had yet to touch open air even now; Reinhard had cut through the 'Dragon's' breath using nothing but the indestructible strength of the sheath itself and the sword-strength of his own body — those two alone.

But then—

"Plan D23!!"

"—Al Goa."

The instant Aldebaran shouted it, without a word of confirmation, the 'Dragon' scorched the world with an explosive blaze. He'd predicted a fight against Reinhard was inevitable. — And so, within Aldebaran, plans for fighting the 'Sword Saint' had already been prepared, and those, along with the 'Memory,' had been shared with 'Aldebaran.' Between Aldebaran and 'Aldebaran,' no war council was needed.

—

The swelling flame, rather than coalescing into a fireball, immediately wrapped an entire stretch of the sand sea in fire. The night sand sea was lit up in a red glow, and air that had held even a chill was remade in an instant into a scorched, smoke-reeking inferno. That flame, forged from the 'Dragon's' off-the-charts mana, was tremendous, and under the sheer, senseless heat of fire licking across the sand sea, the sand melted all at once and turned to glass.

The scene it created called to mind the one from when Al Shario's starlight had utterly destroyed the sand sea. If there was a difference, this time the manner of the sand sea's destruction had a certain atmosphere to it; and if there was a shared point, it was the figure of the 'Sword Saint' emerging from ground zero—

"The Divine Protection of Fire Avoidance."

Through a world of red, blanketed by heat that would reduce any ordinary living thing to ash, or at the very least to a scrap of charcoal, the man crowned with hair red as flame strode forward without a hint of hesitation. Sweeping his arm to clear away the flames as he emerged, Reinhard was cutting away the vast majority of the fire's damage with the Divine Protection of Fire Avoidance — and if pressed further with fire, he'd be granted the Divine Protection of Playing with Fire, unleashing an enhancement to his abilities at any fire scene without reservation.

So neither the flame attack, nullified by the Divine Protection of Fire Avoidance, nor the terrain change, nullified by the Divine Protection of Sand Play, was Aldebaran's real target. His real target was—

"—!"

In that instant, Reinhard, standing amid the cleared flames, furrowed his brow and put a hand to his throat. He must have noticed it. — The critical fact that he couldn't breathe.

"—!!"

Judging that reaction as an opening to press the attack, 'Aldebaran' unleashed his breath. Pinned down by the power and explosive force of that white light, a genuine hardness settled into Reinhard's expression. It wasn't an expression born of regret or struggle of conscience — it was the look of hardship in the midst of battle.

"Finally..."

So you're finally making that face, Aldebaran thought, clenching his fist at a sense of progress he hadn't felt in tens of thousands of tries. The phenomenon tormenting Reinhard was an oxygen-starved space, created by the fire's combustion burning away every last bit of oxygen in the area around him. Through his Divine Protections, no effect of earth, water, fire, or wind could serve as a decisive blow against Reinhard — but surely he couldn't be a being so far removed from the laws of life as to survive with no oxygen at all. Aiming for exactly that, Aldebaran had created the oxygen-starved state. — Pinning the 'Sword Saint' down right there.

"No science class to help you this time — go on and figure out what's happening!"

Neither mere magic nor science at the level of armchair theory could bring Reinhard down. Only by forcing magic and science to bend to his will, through a phenomenon unknown even to Reinhard the 'Sword Saint,' or even to Od Lagna, who so favored him, could Aldebaran finally corner him.

Killing his 'Technique' with a phenomenon his Divine Protections couldn't counter, killing his 'Body' with a field saturated in Miasma — he had now sealed off two of the three elements needed to conquer Reinhard. And so, still pressing on—

"Plan L29—!!"

Aldebaran and 'Aldebaran' agreed as one: this was the moment to press the advantage. While 'Aldebaran' pinned Reinhard down with his breath, Aldebaran drew the needed incantation formula in his place, and the 'Dragon's' vast mana came pouring into it.

In the makeup of their Od, in the size of their Gate, the two of them were, quite literally, the moon and a soft-shelled turtle — worlds apart. Even so, this was a trick that only worked between Aldebaran and 'Aldebaran,' who shared a magical crest that should otherwise be as unique as a fingerprint — laying two tall, vertical arrays in midair, crackling with violet lightning, hissing and popping with pale blue light and radiant heat, they turned their barrels toward Reinhard.

Two magical rails, drawn across the sky. Which was to say—

"—Magia Railgun."

A railgun rigged together out of magical theory, its bullet neither fire, nor ice, nor lead. A scale peeled from the 'Dragon' with a green-dragon blade Aldebaran had forged out of earth magic — that scale became the bullet, launched at ultra-high speed, streaking toward Reinhard, still trapped in the oxygen-starved state.

Even Reinhard couldn't outmatch lightning speed. That was already a fact Aldebaran had learned over the course of all the battles so far.

"—!"

The Divine Protection of First Sight and the Divine Protection of Arrow Avoidance triggered simultaneously — Reinhard's intuition kicked in against an attack he was seeing for the first time, right as the railgun's line of fire wavered ever so slightly. As a result, Reinhard tilted his body just slightly, and the railgun, its aim thrown off by only the barest margin, failed to catch him dead center, grazing past behind him instead, and gouging a semicircle out of the sand sea.

But—

"—Ngh."

A burst of blood sprayed across the sand sea, and a pained groan slipped from Reinhard's throat. The railgun hadn't hit. It had only grazed him. But if even a graze from an attack was enough to kill outright, then even Reinhard couldn't walk away unscathed. Reinhard, blood pouring copiously from his right shoulder — genuine damage, the first he'd shown across more than 130,000 battles. And into that opening—

"Do it!!"

A second Magia Railgun fired, and a scale drove straight into Reinhard's center. An inescapable shockwave threw the sand sea into chaos, and Reinhard's figure vanished. — No, not vanished. He'd been swept away along the railgun's line of fire, carried off together with the scale. That power, that speed — take it head-on properly, and even Reinhard couldn't endure it. If he took it head-on properly.

"—The Divine Protection of the Second Coming."

His blood-spraying right arm hanging limp, gripping the 'Dragon Sword' with his left, Reinhard had once again taken the railgun blow bearing down on his center with the sheath. Unable to withstand the impact entirely, he'd been torn from his footing, but he'd avoided a fatal wound — no, that wasn't quite right. He hadn't been torn away; he'd leapt backward in time with the railgun, scattering what would have been lethal force.

"—Al Cram!"

The instant 'Aldebaran' perceived the same thing Aldebaran did, his incantation was cast, and a black orb of magic, generating hypergravity, was conjured behind Reinhard. It was a reworking of the magic that had failed to work back in the eight-thousands — no, in this extreme state, 'Aldebaran' too had broken through his own limits, evolving the magic one step further.

What arose there was no mere force field imposing extreme weight. What formed was a singularity of destructive mass, close to a black hole, distorting space by concentrating at a single point mana enough to bring down a star. It touched the flying Reinhard, and was about to shatter the 'Sword Saint's' life into pieces—

"—The Divine Protection of Bloodletting."

In that instant, the tip of the 'Dragon Sword' was driven straight into the singularity, and the black orb was destroyed by sheer force. The 'Dragon Sword,' said to be indestructible by anyone or anything. Its strength was proven beyond doubt — but Reinhard's left arm, gripping the 'Dragon Sword' as it churned through the singularity, was destroyed.

The railgun had taken his right arm; the singularity, his left. Reinhard was damaged in both. Even trembling at the fact that this combination of two of his plans' most powerful attacks still hadn't managed to defeat him, Aldebaran noted that Reinhard had taken the greatest damage yet. Just like this—

"—The Divine Protection of Arrow-Hitting."

In the next instant, Reinhard, planting the tip of his foot into the sand torn apart by railgun and singularity alike, both blood-soaked arms hanging useless, flicked the fallen 'Dragon Sword' up with his foot — and kicked it flying.

"—Guh."

Light drove straight into the center of 'Aldebaran's' throat, and the 'Dragon' toppled violently. Swallowed up by the wind and tremor the 'Dragon's' collapse produced, Aldebaran was flipped over with no way to resist. He breathed in the rising haze of sand, choked on it. Coughed. And then—

"—That's as far as you go."

Looking down at Aldebaran, fallen flat on the ground, a blood-soaked Reinhard spoke. The 'Sword Saint,' both arms hanging limp, stood beside the 'Dragon Sword' planted upright at his feet; having confirmed 'Aldebaran,' eyes rolled back, unconscious, he then turned his gaze to Aldebaran.

Driven this far, having done this much, and still — he lost. Just like that time — aiming for the far side of the nayuta, unable to arrive, betraying so many expectations.

Taking both his arms, conquering his 'Body' — that hadn't been enough. Launching an attack his Divine Protections couldn't answer, conquering his 'Technique' — that hadn't been enough either.

'Sword Saint' Reinhard van Astrea was simply too strong. And so Aldebaran let out one long, deep breath, and—

"—My associate has your old man in custody."

He played the trump card meant to conquer Reinhard van Astrea's 'Mind.'
