Arc 9 Chapter 23: "The Cast Is Assembled"

—By the time Old Man Rom opened his eyes, several hours had already passed since the retreat from the siege in the forest.

"In the end, Felt's the only one who stayed in their hands, and the rest of us got left behind. ...We're the ones who picked the fight, and this is how it turned out. Doesn't get much more pathetic than that."

That was the remark Rachins tacked on after finishing his report on the camp's losses to the just-woken Rom. Rachins couldn't hide his shame, and it wasn't hard to imagine the sense of defeat churning inside him. Of course, that same sense of defeat lay deep within Old Man Rom's thick chest as well. Still, the number of lost battles behind Rom and behind Rachins were not the same.

"The tally of my losses is nothing to be proud of, mind you—far from it."

In all of Rom's life, could the wins, the victories, even be counted on the fingers of both hands? Even when he'd managed a local victory here and there, the final tally always came out on the losing side—that was the win-loss record of an old giant who'd done nothing but pile up years. In every one of those defeats, he'd barely scraped by with his life intact, though whether that was something to be proud of, he honestly couldn't say.

Surviving, and living on in shame, was precisely what had let Rom meet the irreplaceable things in his life, you could say. —Except that right now, that irreplaceable thing had been taken from him.

"How's Felt doing? Is there any way to check—"

"Heh, too bad—Manfred's Farsight Divine Protection got crushed. Not just by the helmet bastard, either; that maid attendant of his didn't miss a trick. He should count himself lucky he only lost an eyeball for it."

"So they didn't leave that intact, after all. Guess I was hoping for too much."

The Farsight Divine Protection they were discussing was the kind of blessing that let its bearer pinpoint and observe a target's location from a great distance—immensely useful both strategically and tactically. The one who'd been using it was Manfred Madison, who ran The Balance within Flanders' underworld coalition that had lent Felt's group its support—though the Farsight Divine Protection itself wasn't one he'd been granted himself, but rather one he'd stolen from someone else's Divine Protection.

The Black Silver Coin and the Flower Prison Garden were likewise organizations deeply rooted in Flanders, but unlike them, The Balance was a group with a long and dubious history that extended beyond the city as well. What deserved special mention was the technique The Balance possessed for stealing Divine Protections—Rom guessed that they likely kept the original bearer alive while having a substitute make use of the body part that served as the blessing's point of origin. Put simply, if you extracted and transplanted the eyeball of a Farsight Divine Protection bearer—since that blessing worked through the eyes—you could freely inherit its effects. Of course, it wasn't as simple as just pulling out an eyeball and swapping it in to gain unlimited use of the Divine Protection. There was a secret technique unique to The Balance behind it, a mystery never revealed to outsiders. Whatever the method, though, if the eyeball needed for the Farsight Divine Protection had been destroyed, they had no choice but to abandon the plan of relying on Manfred to keep tracking Felt's whereabouts.

"Still, Manfred losing an eye, and the sorry state the rest of us are in... it's a miracle every one of us is still breathing."

"A miracle, huh. I sure as hell can't chalk it up that neatly."

At Rom's muttered remark, rubbing his chin, Rachins let out a loud, discontented click of the tongue. They'd gone in prepared to risk their lives, and they'd survived. He surely wasn't about to start talking like some Vollachian, lamenting that he hadn't managed to die—but having lost to an opponent who'd deliberately handicapped himself against them, it was understandable that Rachins felt like he'd been shown just how wide the gap in strength really was. But if he could give everything he had and still feel this frustrated, then Rachins still had plenty of future ahead of him. No doubt the same went for Gaston and Camberley too.

"—Lord Rom, are you awake?"

"Mm, Grassis, is it."

Just as the conversation reached a lull, Grassis popped up and came strolling over. The last anyone had seen of her was being bound by the thread-user called Yae at the tail end of the battle, but by the look of things she was in far better shape than Rachins. Despite her childlike appearance, she and her older sister Fram had been trained by House Astrea since the day they were born, placing them among the very best warriors Rom had ever known. Their superb mastery of the Flow Method contributed enormously to recovering from wounds and restoring stamina, so she already looked to be in nearly perfect condition.

"That said, don't go overdoing it. ...By the way, how's your Divine Protection?"

"Not much longer till the next round's loaded. ...Assuming Fram hasn't died."

"Don't go saying something so damn ominous!... Though, I can't exactly say you're wrong. Even if the helmet bastard hasn't laid a hand on them, out past the dunes Reinhard's up against the Witch..."

"Cheer up, Lord Rachins?"

"Why the hell am I the one getting comforted?! You're the one who should be down in the dumps!"

Rachins shouted that at Grassis, sticking out his long tongue, after she'd given his rounded back a light pat. At his shout, Grassis said "Whoa" with a face that showed not the slightest bit of surprise, then scurried around behind Rom and glared at the childish young man with a theatrically flat stare. Just their usual horsing around. But Rom stroked the girl's head with a palm bigger than her whole skull, and said,

"We're all just as worried about the folks at the Tower. Once your Divine Protection's ready to use again, would you let us know right away?"

"...Aye-aye, consider it done-osuke."

"Who the hell is 'Osuke'?!"

Rachins overreacted to Grassis, who'd nodded with few words and accepted the touch of Rom's palm. It was precisely because he rang so readily when struck that Rachins got jerked around by the mischief-loving sisters. But acting normal like this was probably a relief for Grassis's state of mind right now. And it meant Rachins didn't have to keep spinning his head in useless circles of defeat, either.

"Young ones like you should just burn with rebellious spirit. The post-mortem's my job."

Despite gathering as many as five hundred fighters and setting up the most favorable battlefield they could manage, Rom and the others had lost their battle against the helmet bastard, and Felt had been taken hostage. A defeat this crushing was, without exaggeration, their worst blunder since the Demi-Human War. Still, if there was one thing different from how Rom had felt back then, even facing an equally crushing defeat, it was that this loss hadn't come away empty-handed, nor had it been entirely unforeseen.

—It wasn't a method he'd wanted to use, but given the risk that the helmet bastard might call the Divine Dragon back to his side in a crisis, Rom had always kept in mind the fact that Felt's presence could serve as a shackle against that.

And it had already been known beforehand that the Divine Dragon Volcanica was the helmet bastard's main strength. So even if Felt's side couldn't beat the helmet bastard in this fight, they'd set as their bare minimum goal placing a shackle on his main strength. Of course, it was true that outright beating him here would have been the best outcome, but Rom took pride in the fact that they'd met their minimum goal, and had even gotten a good guess at the true nature of the helmet bastard's Authority besides. If outright victory was the top result, then their current situation ranked second from the top—no, with Felt taken hostage, it was probably closer to third place.

"Mind you, now that he's experienced it firsthand once, the same trick won't work to break his Authority a second time. We'll need to get creative about Felt too—about how we spring it."

Naturally, Rom hadn't the slightest intention of giving up because of this. The helmet bastard, for his own reasons, hadn't taken their lives. As a result, most of their fighting strength remained intact, leaving room to come up with a new plan and clash with him again. Rom wouldn't let anyone call it cowardly or contemptible to make the fullest possible use of that opportunity.

"For some reason, the helmet bastard looked like he begrudged even the time it'd take to stop moving. At that rate, even if he takes short breaks here and there, he won't get a proper chance to rest his body anywhere. Wear and tear on the mind lays bare the flaws in an Authority. If Felt's serving as a shackle on the Divine Dragon, then there's a wide opening to exploit. And besides, it wouldn't surprise me if the helmet bastard and his companions aren't quite the simple, united front they—"

Muttering under his breath, he sifted through the information gained from that battle inside a head bigger than any ordinary person's. If someone called him fickle for having just lost and already moving on, he'd have no answer for that, but inside Rom's mind, the self-reflection over their recent defeat was already finished and done, and one plan after another for the next battle had already begun to take shape. Some might curse him as heartless for it. His granddaughter in all but blood had been carried off, her safety far from certain, and here he was devoting his thoughts to the next battle instead of worry and anxiety. But—

"—Goddammit. This just makes me feel more pathetic by the second."

What Rachins directed at the profile of this "heartless" old giant lost in thought was not disgust—nothing but an unrelenting, unshakeable frustration.

"Hang in there, Lord Rachins."

"Shut up! Why are you acting like this has got nothing to do with you?!"

"—Domain deployment. Redefining the matrix."

Aldebaran murmured that under his breath and set a new restart point. Having weathered twenty-nine thousand, two hundred and twenty-one rounds of trial and error to escape that predicament, he judged that he'd cleared a checkpoint, and took a short break. Taking a deep, long breath, Aldebaran exhaled until his lungs were completely empty, adjusting the position of his helmet as he said,

"...Sorry 'bout that. I'm settled now."

"Oh, no no~, please don't worry about it at all. Actually, are you sure such a teeny-tiny break is enough? If you'd like, Yae's thighs are available for lending right now."

"Nah, I'm good without borrowing 'em."

"They're all fluffy~ and squishy-soft~, you know?"

"I'm fine."

Waving a hand dismissively, Aldebaran flatly turned down Yae's tempting offer of concern. Rebuffed like that, Yae pouted with a "Aww~," but she surely wasn't taking it as hard as her reaction suggested. Her habitual little invitations, and Aldebaran turning them down, were both par for the course by now. Even so, having Yae stick around without leaving was a help. In the sense of being a burden, that is.

"The headache's eased up some too, at least..."

Muttering that, Aldebaran breathed a sigh of relief at the easing of the mentally-induced headache. Including how close he'd come to losing outright, the fight against Felt's army had worn down his mind far too much. The result of underestimating how badly he'd been ground down was a match that was essentially a loss, one where he'd only happened to scrape out a win.

"Guess it figures I can't do a mental reset in the middle of a fight..."

In this case, a "mental reset" meant using Aldebaran's Authority: spending as much time as possible zoning out without using his head, then downing poison to restart the matrix. Repeating that process was a brute-force technique meant to recover from frayed nerves, a raw and ragged spirit, and a mind backed into a corner. As long as he had a calm environment and the means to redo things, it was a method that could buy him peace of mind endlessly—but it had the drawback that, even if his mind could be put back in order, it did nothing to shake off plain, simple physical and mental fatigue. In fact, extreme sleep deprivation and accumulated fatigue had already left serious damage on Aldebaran.

"Even joking aside, if I actually borrowed Yae's lap right now, I'd be out cold in an instant."

"So you mean you're head over heels for Yae?"

"No, I mean I'd lose consciousness. Though letting you crack jokes like that does help take my mind off things some."

Shaking his head slowly, Aldebaran managed to push back against the crushing weight bearing down on his body. Right now, Aldebaran and the others had moved west away from prying eyes, off the plain where the fierce battle had taken place, and after a thorough safety check, were spending time on letting Aldebaran rest. Though "spending time" was a relative term—not much real time had actually passed.

"By the way, just for reference, I wanted to ask—if I'm about to collapse, is doping myself up on Dragon's Blood a viable option? Or is that a no-go?"

Fiddling with the metal fittings of his helmet, Aldebaran floated the question to "Aldebaran." "Aldebaran," who along with Yae had also been serving as a lookout, scratched at his nose deftly with a foreleg at Aldebaran's question, saying "Well, let's see," and then—

"Ahh, it's hard to make a reliable comparison between this Dragon's body and my own way-too-flimsy original one, but if you put in undiluted Dragon's Blood, I'd guess you'd go boom."

"Boom... So a different result than the Duke over in Priestella, huh."

"Now that I'm in this body, I get it—Natsuki Subaru, whose insides are already a mess from the Curse, is one thing, but the Duke holding up like that is a straight-up miracle. Guess being close to royal blood counts for something."

"...Though 'diluting' probably doesn't just mean thinning it out with water. By the way, I've got this bias that shinobi know medicine as well as poison—so what about Dragon's Blood?"

"It's a rare opportunity, so I am curious, but if Master Al turns into an even more terrifying monster, Yae's heart won't be able to take it, so no thank you~."

She put emotional concerns front and center, but in truth, unless you were an actual expert, reliably diluting something like Dragon's Blood was a long shot to begin with. It wasn't like you could casually mass-produce a Last Elixir. The world just isn't that easy.

"At least let me cast some healing magic on you. There, drive-by heal."

"We're in the same party, so it's not a drive-by heal—it's an official one."

Even as Aldebaran shot back with that correction, an excessive burst of healing magic was unleashed on him via the Divine Dragon's power. Unfortunately, there weren't many visible wounds to speak of, and of course it wasn't as if that would regrow Aldebaran's lost left arm—but it did offer the small comfort of knowing he could bounce back even from serious injury. Well, if he actually took a serious wound, he'd down poison rather than wait around to heal, so it really was just a comfort in name only.

"By the way, Lord Vol, may I ask you something~?"

"...Oh! 'Lord Vol' means me? Didn't click at all. What's up, what's up?"

"Have your feelings for Lady Felt calmed down at all?"

Yae's question, feigned casual, had a true intent so obvious from the faintly tense air alone that there was no need to even ask—and inside his helmet, Aldebaran narrowed his eyes. The same went for "Aldebaran," who'd sensed the very same thing.

"If 'calming down' means shrinking, then it's the opposite, if anything. The affection's overflowing, it's bad. Distance we can't close just makes the feelings grow."

"We're not exactly separated by some unbridgeable distance, and I can't exactly root for that love."

"Aldebaran" was teasing him with his own particular brand of vocabulary, but since the feelings behind those words were the same ones Aldebaran himself shared, a considerable part of it was surely genuine. That seemed to come across to Yae as well, and she nodded with a "Is that so~," then said,

"Lady Felt really is in the way then, huh~. Is there no good way to remove her?"

"Yae, let me just say..."

"Ahh~, jumping the gun there. I already know you'd react that way, Lord Vol, so I'm not suggesting anything violent—just talking it over~. —Worst case, if Lady Felt would just try to run for it, then me and Master Al and Lord Vol would all be happy. Well, maybe not Lord Vol."

"I wouldn't be happy about it either, for the record."

"Even though your source of worry would disappear?"

"—. You're quietly a source of worry for me too, you know."

"Ouch, backfired on me there."

Yae smacked her own forehead with a light pen and shot back that response in a playful tone. The "happy" method she'd been hoping for was the pattern that would trigger the condition set when Felt became a hostage—that if she tried to escape, they had permission to kill her. Felt herself had promised "Aldebaran" that if that happened and she died for it, she'd hold no grudge. But even if that was a perfectly logical arrangement between Felt and "Aldebaran"—no, the Divine Dragon—it wasn't the outcome Aldebaran himself wanted.

It might seem strange to say so, given the way things and their relationship had turned out, but Aldebaran wanted Felt to make a full, healthy return to the Royal Selection and to compete for the throne right to the end, with a clear conscience. Of course, judging from "Aldebaran's" reactions, the suspicion about Felt that had first come up back at Royal Lugunica Castle had, at this point, been all but confirmed.

"I mean, a dragon getting this head-over-heels to the core is way past laughing it off as a coincidental resemblance. So, realistically, how much chance is there of the original personality poking its face out?"

"This is a vessel—a dragonhusk—and that much doesn't waver. No need to worry about the original personality popping up with a cheerful 'hello there.' It's more like the residual thoughts are asserting themselves a bit strongly, and even that strength comes and goes in waves."

"...By the way, how much does that residual thought actually remember?"

"Likes Farsale. Hates Reid. And a hazy jumble of other stuff besides."

"The first Sword Saint, huh. Don't drop legends on me so casually, it's spooking me."

It was a name Aldebaran had heard from the Witch often enough, but the first Sword Saint had nothing but the worst possible reputation from every single person he'd asked. Now that they'd gained this new testimony from the Divine Dragon's residual thoughts as well, it seemed the "good-for-nothing" label really was accurate. As for the rest of that hazy jumble, it'd be a lie to say it didn't bother him at all, but—

"Oh, right, speaking of which—I've got Yae here and my dragon self here, but where are Little Miss Felt and the old man?"

"The two of them are on standby over there. Lord Heinkel is responsibly keeping a firm watch over Lady Felt."

"That pairing is the worst possible combo!"

"—. Ahh~, oops~, silly Yae, I slipped up there."

"—————"

At Yae's blatantly staged reply, Aldebaran let out a deep sigh and got to his feet. Given the treatment he'd been giving Yae, these little needling jabs were something he had to accept and take on the chin.

"Oh, off to see Farsale—no, wait, I mean Little Miss Felt? I'll come too..."

"As long as you can't fix that slip of the tongue, it's more important for your image not to see her. Also, I want to avoid tipping the scales any further in that direction."

Talking him down like that, Aldebaran made "Aldebaran," who'd been about to tag along, sit and stay, entrusted the discipline of keeping him there to Yae, ignored her teasing "A joint effort, huh~," and set off in the direction where Felt and Heinkel were said to be. And there, out of earshot of the strategy meeting in the shade, on the far side of a large tree where they'd been left waiting, were Felt and Heinkel—

"—Listen up, old man, I'm telling you now, you don't need to make excuses to me."

The moment Aldebaran peeked around, a fairly sharp remark flew right into his ears. Looking over, he found Felt sitting with her back against the great tree, and Heinkel standing across from her, looking down at her. Framed purely in visual terms, it was a scene where Heinkel, looking down from a higher vantage point, could be read as holding the psychological advantage over Felt—

"—! W-what do you mean, excuses? Why the hell would I need to do something like that? Don't talk nonsense, kid!"

"Then quit looking at me all scared and jumpy. Your eyes are way too loud."

"Who's—!"

With Heinkel erupting in fury deep in his throat and choking on his next words, that visual read of the scene seemed to be of little use after all. Despite the gap in age and height between the two of them, it was obvious without needing any numbers which of them was the better performer, and Aldebaran felt a pang of pity for Heinkel, caught in such an unfortunate scene. He briefly considered just holding his breath and slipping away, but—

"Mm." "Aldebaran..."

Both of them noticed him at almost the same instant, and Aldebaran, left with no choice, walked on over. Then, picking his words with an "Uh,"

"Old man, quit hassling Little Miss Felt. No matter what you say, you're not gonna come out ahead."

"Wha—Even you're saying I'd lose an argument to this brat?"

"It's not about who wins or loses an argument—the whole premise is bad to begin with. From Little Miss Felt's point of view, you teaming up with me makes you a full-blown traitor... or rather, basically anyone would see you as a major enemy of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, wouldn't they?"

"—! I—I know that. Even so, I..."

Heinkel muttered that with a bitter expression, lowering his eyes, clenching and unclenching his hands. With a pained expression, standing in a painful position, Heinkel nonetheless understood full well just what kind of spider's thread he'd reached out and grabbed. He understood, too, that this was an act that would tarnish the famous name of Sword Saint borne by his house within the kingdom, leaving an irreparable stain behind. Still, even if he had to bear an infamy that likely wouldn't fade for centuries to come—

"You want this thing called Dragon's Blood. You absolutely need it to wake up your wife... Reinhard's mother, right?"

"Kid, how do you know that—"

"Then! You get it, don't you?! I need it! I need Dragon's Blood! And Aldebaran promised it to me! That's why I'm going along with his plan—"

"That's exactly why I'm telling you not to make excuses to me."

Shot down like that in the middle of his desperate plea, Heinkel choked on his own words with a strangled "Ngh." Felt's response, offering him no foothold whatsoever, cut into Heinkel's small conscience far more sharply and stabbed it to shreds far worse than any clumsy scolding could have. Though it seemed Felt hadn't deliberately picked the method that would wound Heinkel most. As proof of that, she scratched roughly at her own head and said,

"Even I think it'd be a good thing if Reinhard's mom woke up. And I get the idea that you wouldn't be picky about the means to that end. So I'm not gonna say you're wrong, old man."

"—————"

"It's just that it's different from our way of doing things. Not good, not bad. Just different."

At Felt's words, said with one eye closed, it was plain to see how thoroughly crushed Heinkel was. Confronted with the unmistakable gap in caliber between them, what she'd probably meant as a kindness had instead become kicking a man already down. If the two of them kept talking any longer, it would only widen Heinkel's wounds. Feeling bad that Yae's little bit of mischief had borne fruit so neatly, Aldebaran clapped Heinkel on the shoulder with "Old man, that's enough," and relieved him of guard duty over Felt. And so, once a dejected Heinkel had left the spot, slump-shouldered,

"You're merciless, Little Miss Felt. You don't look it, but are you maybe angry?"

"Course I am, you idiot. Even right now I'm holding myself back from kicking all your asses. Only 'cause of this thing on my neck."

Saying that, Felt touched the ultra-fine steel wire looped around her own neck—the same one that had come up in conversation with Yae—and curled her lips into a smirk that showed off her fang. Her defiance was cranked up to the max, but apparently she was at least aware her life was held in someone else's hands. Of course, given that not a single one of her comrades had had their life taken, there was a real chance she was underestimating just how much threat that wire around her neck actually carried—

"—I won't make the same mistake twice."

"—————"

That was less a declaration than an Oath Aldebaran had sworn to himself. The girl in front of him wasn't merely a Royal Selection candidate raised tough in the slums, suspected of being missing royalty—she was also a monster-in-training, raised and molded under the tutelage of none other than Valga Cromwell himself. Or perhaps she'd already emerged fully as a monster, and had become something more dangerous than Aldebaran could even imagine. But even so, even if that were true, Aldebaran would not lose again.

"Even if this turns out to be my last job, there's still meaning in growing from it."

"Aldebaran," who was supposed to be his main strength, had grown unwieldy to handle; Yae's insolent, mischievous attitude had gotten to be more than he could stand; Heinkel kept crumbling on his own, entirely apart from Aldebaran's intentions; and now he had no choice but to keep dragging along Felt, an uncooperative dead weight—that was the journey Aldebaran now found himself on. To put it plainly, it had ended up nothing at all like his original travel plan. But the unplanned, fierce battle and its effectively-a-defeat outcome, in exchange for the toll it took on his mind and body, had stripped Aldebaran of his complacency and handed him a solid lesson in return.

"From here on, I'll push the domain right up to its absolute limit."

He'd believed that responding instantly to pressing situations through short-term matrix updates would cut down on the time spent looping back, more so than it would cut down on the number of attempts, and had reckoned that would work out better for him in the long run. But extreme, frequent matrix updates, when facing a large number of enemies like this time, sent the odds of getting checkmated skyrocketing. Operating the way he had been, even without an opponent as cunning as Valga, sheer bad luck alone could be enough to seal off every path forward.

"Space the intervals out too long, and it turns into a game of chicken between whether my brain fries out first or my mind wears down to nothing... but even if my Soul ends up wrecked, that's still a whole lot better than screwing this up."

To begin with, Aldebaran had no vision at all for what lay beyond seeing this plan through. Reach the Kararagi City-States, arrive at the Grand Mogolade Geyser in that land, accomplish his purpose there, then clean up after everyone and everything he'd used to get that far, and bring it all to an end. He wanted nothing beyond that. In exchange, he intended to make absolutely everything up to that point come true.

"—————"

Felt watched Aldebaran renew his resolve like that, silently, her eyes narrowed. Deliberately ignoring that gaze, Aldebaran found himself suffering through a thoroughly, thoroughly awful discomfort at the fact that, out of everything—personality, features, even build, none of which resembled her in the slightest—the one and only thing Felt shared with Priscilla was the color of her eyes.

"If you could, I'd rather you didn't look at me with those eyes."

"Don't ask the impossible. Sorry, but these are my eyeballs, the same two I've had stuck in my head since the day I was born. I never much liked 'em either, since they make me stand out for the wrong reasons... nah, scratch that—I didn't used to like 'em."

"—————"

"These days, I figure they're not actually so bad. So no, I'm not closing my eyes."

Leaning her back against the great tree, sitting cross-legged on the ground with her arms folded, Felt had a gaze and a strength of will that drew people in—Aldebaran could understand why so many looked up to her, dazzled. Now, though, that dazzle was, for Aldebaran, no different from a poison capsule fitted into his own back tooth. Except that this particular dazzle wouldn't take Aldebaran's life—only torment him.

"So? I'm hungry. Where's the food?"

"That's way too shameless to ask so casually. ...Sorry, but we don't have the luxury of a meal break right now. Moving on takes priority over that."

"Some crappy treatment for a hostage. —That hole you were talking about in Kararagi, whatever it's called?"

"The Grand Mogolade Geyser. That's the final destination, no mistake. Though we're making a little detour first."

At that remark from Aldebaran, Felt frowned, her well-shaped brows knitting, and said, "A detour?" Nodding at her with "Yeah, a detour," Aldebaran turned and pictured a place he still couldn't make out amid the distant scenery, and said— It was—

"—Right now should be when it's the most thinly guarded of all. Going to pick up something the plan can't do without."

Fortunately, the detour along the way to their destination went off without a hitch. Unfortunately, the security in place was far too heavy to call "thin," though given exactly what had been sealed away behind it, the thoroughness of that crisis management was almost reassuring in its own right. Still—

"Heavy security alone isn't enough to stop me in my tracks."

If someone wanted to seriously stop Aldebaran, the best move would be exactly what Felt's group had done—surrounding and crushing him with hundreds of fighters. And to pull that off, they'd need to deploy at least a full order of knights, even from the single most vital location of all: the royal capital. And Aldebaran had already made arrangements beforehand to make sure they couldn't do exactly that.

"Though since they had to pull back right away, it was fifty-fifty whether the diversion would actually pay off."

A deranged Aldebaran, and the Divine Dragon Volcanica cooperating with him. It wasn't hard to imagine how much panic had broken out among those in a position to know, once word came in that the Sword Saint Reinhard, dispatched to stop them, had been pinned down by the Witch of Envy, leaving Aldebaran and the others free to cross the dunes unopposed. They must have thought it was a crisis threatening the very survival of the nation, on the scale of nothing since the Witch of Envy's rampage four hundred years ago. Which was exactly why, against "Aldebaran," who'd drawn every eye flying at such a conspicuously visible altitude, they'd had no choice but to show a posture of resistance mustering the kingdom's full might. Even if that meant leaving behind only the bare minimum of forces, even in the royal capital itself. And then—

"—There it is."

—Four hundred and seven times. After that many rounds of trial and error, Aldebaran found the thing he was after, at the place he was after. From the entrance door to the multiple layers of magical formulae inside the room, it was all sealed up tight, refusing any interference whatsoever. In truth, without any assistance at all, even if Aldebaran had managed to reach this place, he wouldn't have been able to lay a finger on the formula before him, and would have ended up retreating with his tail between his legs. But the Aldebaran of right now had—

"—A Divine Dragon's fang. Wonder how many Holy Gold Coins something like that's even worth."

Just running the numbers in his head was enough that every merchant he knew would have had dollar signs light up in their eyes—using that rare treasure as a catalyst, Aldebaran forcibly tore apart the formidable magical formula. A shattering sound he'd never heard before rang out, one so far beyond normal it might as well have been someone tearing a steel bar apart by brute force, and the invisible wall that had unquestionably stood there vanished. Aldebaran stepped inside.

The room, not especially large, was smaller than the bathroom at the Barielle manor. Aside from the entrance door, the room had not even a single window, and felt suffocating—like a solitary cell. No, not merely "like" one; this was, quite precisely, a place that ought to be called a cell. After all, what had been sealed away in there was, without question, a criminal—a sinner, an unforgivable evil.

"Even just getting this far, I've done enough to deserve the death penalty a hundred times over, but..."

There was still room to pile one more sin on top of it all. As if to prove it, Aldebaran reached a hand out toward the pitch-black thing before him. That, too, was something outside the ordinary laws of the world, but this time he had no need for the Divine Dragon's assistance. What lay within him, different in quality though it was, was the very same thing—the culmination of the Witch's teachings, mastered after training so brutal it had left him drowning in blood he'd coughed up himself—

"—————"

From the Fingers he held out, his will was conveyed, and in the next instant cracks spread across the entire surface, and it shattered. Breaking apart into pieces the way pottery does, it nonetheless never made the sound of shards scattering across the floor—instead, its structure came undone, dissolving into the air until it vanished. Right before Aldebaran's eyes, having watched it all the way through, in place of that vanished black mass, a single human figure now sat slumped on the floor, all but crawling on hands and knees.

"Come along with me quietly. —Do that, and I won't kill you here."

"Ha—"

At Aldebaran's quiet declaration, the figure laughed, as though letting out a breath. —No, not laughed. Sneered. Sneering, sneering, sneering, licking its lips with that mouth full of sharp fangs—

"—Nice, that's nice, sure, that's fine, maybe so, that'll do, surely so, of course it will, and precisely because it will, precisely because my chest trembles with how good it is! Gluttonous drink! Gluttonous feast!"

So it was that within the darkness-colored cell, the being freed from the pitch-black coffin that had sealed it away—the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony, Roy Alphard—cheered aloud at the sensation of both liberation and hunger.

△▼△▼△▼△

—It was an end of the world where heaven and earth lost their fixed places, where the sunlit day and the day of the dead ceased to be distinguished, where the boundary between life and death blurred, and where the difference between self and other became unnecessary.

"Young Master! Young Master!"

Crying that out desperately, at the top of her voice, was Fram—someone whose emotions running this high would once have been unimaginable—and her gaze was fixed beyond the sandstorm, past the Pleiades Watchtower serving as the boundary line, on the great black wave of shadow surging from that far side toward this one, and on the red-haired hero struggling all alone to hold it back, resist it, and beat it away: the Sword Saint, Reinhard van Astrea.

"Young Master——!!"

Even as she believed in his strength more than anyone, the girl who knew his face outside of that of a hero all too well was crying and screaming, her voice cracking, trying to deny the absurd reality that had abruptly come crashing down on him. That scream-like voice of Fram's surely reached Reinhard too, even as he challenged the pitch-black end. There was no way he could fail to hear it. Because Reinhard van Astrea had to be that kind of being—he himself, no one else, had decided as much. And yet, the fact that there was no answer to Fram's anguished voice was because even the strongest Sword Saint on earth was exposed to a threat so fierce that a single instant of disturbed focus could cost him his life.

"—————"

Literally like a surging tide, countless black arms swept in, spreading wide enough to fill the field of vision from one edge to the other, becoming an inescapable devastation bent on crushing its target. Against that, Reinhard bore the Dragon Sword on arms nearly torn to shreds, wielding the unsheathable legendary blade in place of those arms, tearing through and smashing apart the inescapable cage of black shadow closing in from every direction—north, south, east, and west. He kept it up ceaselessly, ten times, twenty times a second, over and over and over, throwing himself so utterly into his own calling that the sweat beading on him and the blood pouring from him turned to steam and lifted off his body.

—That, precisely, was the summit clash between beings who lived out legend themselves: the Sword Saint against the Witch of Envy.

"Ohh! This is unbelievable!"

Clutching the reins on the driver's seat she'd flung herself into, desperately carrying out a role she wasn't used to, was Meili, her frayed, disheveled braid left as it was, gritting her teeth. Patrasche, at the other end of those reins, was clever enough that Meili's handling of them never rose above mere decoration. Even so, the reason Meili stood there was to show the resolve she herself had to see through.

With Garfiel and Ezzo unconscious, Fram unable to tear her attention away from Reinhard, and Petra not yet back from the Book of the Dead, Meili was the only one here in anything resembling a normal state. Reinhard's battle was the given premise, but beyond that, it wasn't an exaggeration to say that the survival of the whole group now hung on Meili's own thin arms.

"It is an exaggeration—no, more than that, it's way too heavy a weight...!"

Meili had found herself, more than once before, in situations where she held sway over whether someone lived or died. But on most of those occasions, Meili had been the one taking life, standing on the side that held the power over life and death. The one time she'd wielded her power to protect rather than take life was, at best, that "Trial" back at the Watchtower before, and beyond that, Meili's abilities in other situations were barely a notch above amateur. With that little experience under her belt, the Witch of Envy was not an opponent she had any business going up against.

"Please, Patrasche~... unlike little Witchbeasts, I have no idea at all what you're saying, but~... everyone's in danger~...!"

"—!!"

To Meili's plea, carried through the reins, Patrasche gave a valiant reply. Racing furiously onward, the jet-black Earth Dragon paid no mind at all to the worst possible footing—sand—pulling the Dragon Carriage that carried everyone, dashing along, running, running, running, running, running, tearing straight through—

"Just like this—"

It was the instant she thought they'd race clean across the sea of sand in one go.

"—Ah."

For an instant, the ground buckled as if stretching and contracting, and under an unbelievable impact Patrasche's legs spun uselessly in midair as the carriage, cabin and all, was flung up into the air.

"—————"

This is bad—the word "pinch" filled every corner of Meili's mind. Pinch. Yes, a pinch. That word "pinch" that Subaru used to say whenever he fell into a dangerous spot—it really was easy to say, and once it got stuck in your mouth, it was all that came to mind afterward too. But having it come to mind was one thing; chanting "pinch, pinch" over and over offered not a single thread toward actually solving anything.

What she did know for certain, with total clarity, was that if Patrasche once went down onto the sand, they'd lose the wind-blocking Divine Protection that let them safely leave the dunes at all. If that vanished, there'd be no way for Meili and the others to escape alive from this space, where the boundary between heaven and earth could no longer even be told apart.

—What do I do, right now, how, me, everyone, please, big brother, help me.

"Somebody help everyone...!"

It was just as that utterly useless, tearful voice spilled out, her vision on the verge of flipping upside down—

"—Leave it to me, Meili."

The instant that adorable yet reliable voice reached her, Meili saw, from the overturning driver's seat, someone use the bench as a springboard, kick off far out into the air, and leap toward Patrasche, whose posture had come badly undone mid-flight. It was a girl with a large ribbon, her bright brown hair vivid to the eye—

"Petraaa!"

Right before the eyes of Meili, who'd cried that out, Petra, having leapt out, threw her arms around Patrasche's neck in midair, then forced herself down onto the Earth Dragon's saddle and clung on as if biting into it. Clinging tight with every bit of her small body's strength, she cried out to Patrasche—

"Patrasche! Calm down, listen to my voice!"

"—!!"

"That's it, good girl... now!!"

Obeying the voice of Petra clinging to her, Patrasche swung her long tail hard, forcibly wrestling her midair posture under control, then came down onto the sand with force enough that her feet seemed to stab into it, and superbly braked the momentum of the carriage as it threatened to slam into the ground, running on from there without ever letting the Divine Protection lapse.

That fatal, split-second instant, she'd pulled through beautifully, without a hitch. It was thanks to Patrasche's unbelievable adaptability, and the skill of the girl who'd drawn it out of her.

"Meili! Are you—are you okay?!"

"I—I'm fine~! What about you, Petra—you read that book, and—"

"—I'm fine. More importantly—"

Cutting herself off, Petra sharpened her round eyes and glared back over her shoulder. At the far end of that gaze lay the Pleiades Watchtower, and the battle where Reinhard, defending it to the death as the line that must never be crossed, held back the Witch of Envy trying to break through that very line. Just thinking the words "the Witch of Envy" alone was reality enough to send a shudder welling up from the very depths of one's heart—a fear even Meili couldn't shake. But to that Meili, Petra, still astride Patrasche's back, said,

"It'll be fine, so leave it to me."

"Petra...?"

"Right now, more than anything, we have to get out of the Augria Sand Dunes. Let's go!"

At Petra's ringing declaration atop the Earth Dragon racing across the sea of sand, Meili, feeling a strange, inexplicable sense of reassurance, answered by nodding her head up and down again and again. Smiling at Meili's reaction, Petra then turned back to face forward and set her cheeks firm. Still clinging tight to Patrasche's neck, leaving the manifestation of the end closing in from behind entirely to Reinhard, she aimed straight for beyond the sea of sand—run, run, run.

And so, tasting the illusion of a wind that couldn't actually touch her, Petra murmured quietly to herself.

"We're the ones who have to make this work out. —Right, Subaru?"

"—Yeah, let's do this. With everything we've got, all of it!!"

Feeling, right up close, the voice and presence of someone precious and dearly loved whom she couldn't reach out and touch, her chest burning hotter and hotter, Petra Leyte raced on with everything she had, desperately, desperately.

—With all her might, so that the wish that had to reach beyond this sea of sand would reach it.
