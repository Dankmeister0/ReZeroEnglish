Arc 6 Chapter 32: "Who Are You"

—Faced with a sight he hadn't expected in the least, all Subaru could do was stare in blank astonishment.

It was a night of one strange thing after another. It had started with an unintended doze, then a mad dash searching for Anastasia = Eridna, who had vanished from the green room, then the sighting of a bird that had no business being inside the tower, and the chase that followed. He'd lost the bird at a dead end, felt along the nearby floor and wall, and found a hidden passage—

"—And when the train came out of the tunnel, there was snow country."

The stray quip did nothing to explain the strange sight before him. What Subaru had crawled through was a wall, not a tunnel, and what spread out before him now wasn't snow country at all—it was the night sea of sand, swept by a cold wind. Glittering stars hung in the black sky, and from the Watchtower's balcony he could see, far into the distance, a vista of dunes like a black ocean. Nothing about it matched his words. Instead—

"—Natsuki-kun?"

Catching Subaru's hoarse mutter, a silhouette with hair trailing in the wind turned to face him. A girl gently pressing down her pale-purple, wavy hair with one hand—her light blue-green eyes round, her skin so white it seemed to float in the darkness, a lovely figure. It was Anastasia, the one he'd been searching for. At her call, Subaru hesitated for just a moment, then—

"...A perfect spot for a scenic night stroll, huh."

Hiding his initial shock behind his tongue, Subaru shrugged at Anastasia. At his gesture and choice of opening, Anastasia gave a small laugh and said, "That's true."

"The view really is somethin', I'll give ya that. But it's a shame the view itself is pitch black. Even seein' a town off in the distance woulda made a difference."

"This has its own charm too, though—kind of like looking out at a night sea. Besides, more than that..."

As he spoke, Subaru pointed not at the sweeping view but overhead. Drawn along, Anastasia looked up—and there was the sky, full of stars.

"The air's cold and clear, so the stars are super visible. Romantic, right?"

"It's true the stars are pretty, and I agree the air's clear too... but don't ya think there's somethin' more worth worryin' about than how the stars look?"

"Something more worth worrying about, like what?"

"The fact that we can see stars at all. Has there been a single night this whole way across the sand sea where we could see stars?"

Anastasia said it with an exasperated air, and Subaru clapped his hands. "Ah." Just as she'd pointed out, seeing a starry sky from the balcony like this was strange, considering the journey across the sand sea up to now. Around the Pleiades Watchtower, you couldn't find a single glimmer of star in the sky. Because—

"The miasma was thick, hanging over the sky like clouds, right?"

"And if it's not here, either this tower alone is somehow free of miasma... or maybe we're higher up than the miasma that was hangin' over everything."

"At this height, well, I can't say that's impossible."

Looking out at the scenery from the balcony, Subaru realized their current altitude—the level corresponding to the tower's fourth floor—wasn't some few dozen meters off the ground, but up in the hundreds of meters. He hadn't quite felt it, but the stairs from the fifth floor to the fourth had covered that much height after all.

"...Even so, question is whether we're even at the tower's midpoint yet, at this level."

Rather than looking outward from the balcony, Subaru looked up along the outer wall of the tower and sighed. If his guess was right, then even though they'd climbed above the miasma, the tower's summit, piercing the clouds, was nowhere in sight. Regardless of the darkness, there was still much further to go. After all, the stairs to the second floor alone had numbered over four hundred steps. Just from that, there was no doubting the tower still had plenty of reserve left in it. In any case—

"——"

Having settled the matter of the starry sky and the tower's height for the moment, Subaru turned his eyes to Anastasia. Her lips curved softly, her hands clasped behind her back—there was nothing suspicious in her movements. No particular wariness, no sign of scheming anything. At the very least, confirming that she wasn't about to bare open hostility all of a sudden—

"—So, what's your excuse for this situation?"

"Excuse?"

"Sneaking out of your bedroom in the dead of night, slipping through a secret passage nobody knows about, and hanging out here in the night wind playing with birds... that's way too suspicious."

Subaru jutted his chin at Anastasia, who looked puzzled, and pressed the point.

Birds—yes, birds. The two of them, Subaru and Anastasia, were the only ones facing each other here, but the balcony was crowded with many more spectators besides the two of them. Motionless, silently watching over the scene, like a flock of ornaments—that's what they were.

"——"

Not one or two birds—nowhere near so modest a number. Perched along the outer rim of the balcony, resting their wings, there were no fewer than fifty birds. Enough to be mistaken for a flock, though calling it a "flock" felt off, since the birds gathered were not of any single species.

White birds, blue birds, black birds, speckled birds, big birds, small birds, thin birds, plump birds—a motley, disunified assortment of birds, gathered together as one. That fact alone was quite bizarre enough, but what struck Subaru as even eerier was the behavior of the birds forming this scene.

—With this many birds present, there wasn't so much as a cry, let alone the rustle of a single wing.

Whether the birds possessed the wit to communicate with one another was unclear. But at the very least, these disunified birds were united, at least, in intent.

"I get why that'd make ya uneasy, Natsuki-kun, but..."

Against Subaru's suspicion, Anastasia put a hand to her cheek.

"'Secret passage' seems a bit much, don't ya think? I mean, you're here too, aren't ya, Natsuki-kun?"

"That's because... the bird led me here, or something like that."

"Same here, then. I was just wanderin' around inside the tower at night. And then there was a bird flyin' around. I thought, what's that about, and followed it here."

"——"

"What, that face like ya don't believe me?"

Anastasia, who had been mimicking a bird with both hands, sending them swimming through the air, narrowed her eyes. Naturally, it wasn't a convincing explanation. He had no grounds to deny it outright, but there was no way such a convenient coincidence could be true. Setting aside his own case, Subaru concluded as much.

"This bird..."

"Wonder what these little guys even are."

"—! That's exactly what I want to ask."

Anastasia's evasive, noncommittal manner was part of it, but the mere presence of the birds—watching this exchange from a distance—was uncomfortable in itself. It was hard to read any emotion in their eyes; if anything, the gap felt wide enough to make him think they truly had none. What were these birds, this balcony, this Anastasia, thinking?

"That you don't know anything either, same as me... asking me to just swallow that is way too convenient."

"I've got somethin' to say about the 'Natsuki-kun doesn't know anything either' part too, but... honestly, I really am troubled. Ever since I got here, I've been thrown off my rhythm the whole time."

Sighing, hand to her forehead, Anastasia put on a show of grief. Watching her warily all the while, Subaru suddenly recalled something about the birds.

—That birds fly across the Augria Sand Dunes, toward the Pleiades Watchtower.

He'd heard that story at a tavern in the town where they'd stopped to prepare for crossing the sand sea toward the tower. If you lose your way in the sand sea, look for a bird, the shopkeeper had said. Fortunately, Subaru and the others hadn't made the kind of mistake that would leave them stranded in the sand sea, so the warning had nearly slipped his mind—

"Seeing this, I guess that wasn't just some idle rumor after all..."

Even at Subaru's mutter, the birds showed no reaction. They simply folded their wings quietly, resting them as if loath to fly through the dusk. Exposed to the cold wind, and yet not huddling together, not sharing warmth. Only turning eyes like finely crafted dolls' toward Subaru and Anastasia.

"These ones have been like this the whole time. I was at my wit's end too."

"Believing that is a bit much, going by my track record."

"Track record?"

"In my experience, when I carelessly stumble into a scene like this, it's usually a promise that my life's in danger."

Subaru's history of near-death experiences from careless actions was fairly extensive. Long ago it began with a nighttime prowl around the mansion, ending in him being beaten to death by Rem. Skipping over the various events since, Subaru's rule of thumb had settled into: careless action = death. Following that rule, the present situation seemed quite dangerous—

"Well then, guess it's time to teach that rule of thumb a new pattern. It won't be like that this time."

"——"

"For a scaredy-cat like you, Natsuki-kun, the worst-case scenario would be that I'm schemin' somethin'. Luring you to this tower like this, cookin' up some outrageous plot, thinkin' of makin' a mess of you and Emilia-san and everyone... right?"

Struck right on the mark, Subaru's cheek stiffened slightly. Seeing that, Anastasia laughed, "So easy to read."

"Relax. I'm not thinkin' anything that roundabout, and I've got no hostility toward you, Natsuki-kun. Not toward anyone else in this tower either... ah, 'cept the examiners, that is."

"Shaula and Reid, huh."

"——"

The moment those names came up, Anastasia fell silent, a bitter look on her face. Seeing that reaction, Subaru let out an "ah."

"You were asleep, so you didn't hear, right? About the guy who showed up new in the second floor's 'Trial'... that's Reid Astrea. The first 'Sword Saint.' Apparently there's some mechanism where he was summoned out of the past, or something like that."

"Hearin' that, this tower's design is somethin' else entirely... What was its creator even thinkin'."

Receiving Subaru's explanation, Anastasia added a low remark at the end of her exasperated comment. Sensing the Karuragi dialect drop out of her tone, Subaru held his breath. Up until now, he'd more or less treated and interacted with her as Anastasia. But even so, the true nature of the one before him now—

"—Right now, it's just the two of us here. Won't you level with me?"

"Hm..."

"Honestly, talking with you while you're wearing someone else's skin gets me nowhere. No matter what you say, there's no way I can trust you down to the roots. So..."

"—Not the me acting as Ana, but you'd rather exchange words with me."

The instant she said it, in response to Subaru's proposal, Anastasia's presence shifted. The atmosphere surrounding her was made entirely new; though her form and features didn't change, the very presence confronting him plainly transformed. The emotion held in her eyes, the expression woven by her thoughts, switched over.

"——"

And so, before Subaru, holding his breath at the change, Anastasia—no, the artificial spirit Echidna, who had been playing the role of Anastasia—slowly moved toward the outer rim of the balcony. There was no railing to prevent a fall, only a low wall of token height at the edge. She settled herself lightly onto that wall, and gently stroked the head of the small white bird perched right beside her. And then—

"—This is indeed the first time we've exchanged words like this, just the two of us."

Accepting Subaru's proposal, she smiled, fragile and fleeting.

"Aren't you coming over?"

"No way, there's no railing, and the safety measures here are way too incomplete for that."

"I'm not going to push you off just because you approach carelessly, you know?"

"That's exactly the kind of thing that makes it impossible to trust you. That part of you is just like the original."

Sitting in a spot of extreme danger, inviting him over with an utterly guileless face, Echidna made Subaru twist his lips. But apparently displeased with his phrasing of refusal, she frowned openly. Then, raising a finger as if to lecture him, she said, "May I?"

"I've said this a few times already, but could you please try not to equate me so much with that original witch of yours? Frankly, being compared to someone I don't know is unpleasant in the extreme. Even if that someone happens to be my own creator."

"That way of putting it too, but... yeah, my bad. I'll try to do better."

"I would appreciate it."

Still in Anastasia's appearance and voice, Echidna made her appeal to Subaru. Honestly, even that complaint sounded exactly like the tone of the ill-natured witch Subaru knew, but her point contained circumstances he ought to nod along to honestly. In fact, if someone told Subaru he was similar to Echidna, he'd want to lodge a strong protest for defamation of character himself.

"Still, your concern is only natural. Seeing me come here alone, night after night, to a place no one else had noticed, being suspected is only to be expected. However, having accepted that suspicion as reasonable, allow me to once again deny your doubts."

"You really touch that bird without a shred of caution, huh."

"...Were you listening to what I just said?"

"I was listening, but."

Right away, their respective points of view failed to align. More than Echidna's argument, though, what Subaru found strange beyond reason was that she could stroke the small bird's head—the head of a bird eerily like some crafted ornament—without any hesitation at all. If this were an afternoon park and the birds showed more lifelike reactions, that'd be one thing.

"Aren't you scared they'll swarm you all at once and peck you to death?"

"That, your imagination there, is far more terrifying. Don't tell me that's another one of your rules of thumb."

"There was, uh, a time I nearly got tackled by a cute-looking rabbit with way too much energy."

Ever since, he'd felt his nerve fail him whenever he saw a large number of animals gathered in one place. Having repeated countless "Return by Death" experiences, he could say with confidence that there had never once been an easy death—but among them, that particular memory ranked among the especially awful ways to go.

"...Given the way your face has actually gone pale, I won't force the matter. I don't feel any particular fondness for these birds myself, either."

Perhaps Subaru's color had turned bad enough, because Echidna quickly withdrew her hand from the small bird. Then, placing both hands on her lap, she said, "Now then," and looked back at Subaru anew.

"You proposed that we level with each other... so, having set aside the pretense of behaving as Ana, what exactly did you want to talk about with me?"

"For starters, this place, and its connection to the birds."

"On that, I can only give you the same answer I gave as Ana. I was led here by the birds, just as you were. Before that, I had no clue whatsoever. Only..."

"Only?"

Just as he was starting to feel let down by an answer no different from before, the trailing hint of hesitation in her follow-up made Subaru's brow lift. Hesitating slightly at his reaction, Echidna continued.

"Honestly, I'd been wanting to ask you the same thing."

"The same thing, of me?"

"Since I meant to be fully immersed in playing Ana, it might have sounded like a joke. I came here as though led. And now, here, I'm speaking with you in this place. ...You, standing before the entrance back into the tower."

"——"

"You were also acquainted with Shaula, this tower's administrator. At least, she was treating you with that full assumption on her end. Taking that into account, I think it's cowardly of me to say this after getting the two of us alone in a place like this, but..."

Swept along by Echidna's words, Subaru couldn't find a place to interject. Pausing once, still wearing Anastasia's face, she posed her question to him. That question was—

"—Natsuki Subaru, who exactly are you?"

"Who I am, that's..."

"Let's go back to before you headed to Priestella. After the ceremony honoring your achievements—the Subjugation of the White Whale and the defeat of 'Sloth'—one year ago. Ana investigated you."

What Echidna had just revealed—was that part of the Anastasia Camp's Royal Selection strategy? Investigating Subaru, who'd received a knighthood as the sworn knight of Emilia, her rival candidate—that would be standard theory in a contest like this. But even Anastasia Hoshin, the great merchant leading the Hoshin Company—

"Your background couldn't be found. Ana grumbled that even digging up the minimum baseline information was quite a challenge in itself. Though I suspect that's less about you and more the result of the people around you doing something about it."

If there was someone in Subaru's camp involved in this kind of information control over him, the first name that came to mind was Roswaal, with Otto and Clind a close second. Whichever it was, it wouldn't be strange for them to be doing it. And they were either not kind enough, or not tactless enough, to bother telling Subaru about it.

"In any case, all we could trace was that, right before the Royal Selection began, you were involved in some small incident said to have occurred in the royal capital. We got testimony that the knight Reinhard saw you when he discovered Felt, one of the candidates. But that's all."

Records from before that shouldn't exist. Which meant Echidna's—in this case Anastasia's—investigation had traced Subaru's movements almost to perfection. Except that, from their perspective, it was an insufficient result.

"——"

Echidna's eyes narrowed, and Subaru found himself at a loss for what to say. Up until now, there had been plenty of experiences where he suffered from being unable to convey information about "Return by Death," unable to share information built on that foundation. But this, this time, was a first. That Natsuki Subaru being someone whose origins were unknown could itself become a shackle.

"I..."

"And so, having laid it all out at such tiresome length—"

"—Huh?"

With a serious expression, as Subaru tried desperately to squeeze out words, Echidna spread both hands wide. Her tone was so unbearably light that Subaru could only stare, dumbfounded. At Subaru's reaction, Echidna nodded, satisfied, with an "mm-hm," and said,

"Both Ana's and my perception of you was that of a person of unknown origin who had piled up an enormous number of achievements as a rookie knight... that, up until Priestella. I won't put into words how that impression was transformed during the fight against the Witch Cult in Priestella... but I'm aware it's shifted again a bit, coming to this tower."

"——"

"Please forgive me if, being alone with you in a space with no one else watching, in the dead of night like this, I felt some anxiety, and grew wary. That much, I hope, is understandable."

Closing her spread arms, Echidna tilted her head with a smile. Having listened to her whole story, taken aback, Subaru worked his dry lips, mumbling, seriously agonizing over how he should take it. But in the middle of that agonizing, he suddenly noticed—

—that Echidna's hand on her lap was gripped tight enough to whiten the fingertips.

"...Are you, by any chance, actually scared right now?"

"—That remark is a little uncalled for. For instance, what is your actual relationship with Shaula?"

To Subaru's question, Echidna answered with another question instead.

"This is the first time I've met Shaula, here. I don't know anything."

"The third floor's 'Trial'—was it just coincidence that you solved it so quickly?"

"...It was coincidence."

"Then what about the fact that you, like this, made your way through a hidden passage disguised so as to be unnoticeable at a glance, and just happened to find me alone, and spoke to me?"

"——"

What continued, needling and persistent, was a grievance directed from Echidna at Subaru. By piling up question after question, she was telling him this:

"Try seeing it from the other side, huh..."

"Even so, weighing various factors, I've assessed that the likelihood of you being a hostile presence is low. Consider my having opened up like this a show of sincerity, meant to demonstrate that."

Placing a hand to her thin chest, Echidna showed with her bearing that she had spoken her true feelings. For his part, Subaru very much wanted to credit that resolve, and, reflecting on the suspiciousness of his own background and actions, wanted to nod along to Echidna's words. He very much wanted to—but—

"—It seems my creator left quite the wound on your heart."

The more the artificial spirit Echidna's conduct resembled that of Echidna, the Witch of Greed, the harder it was to sincerely believe in whatever sincerity she offered, no matter how much of it she extended. This, precisely, could be called the Witch's Lingering Scent.

"Your case, I get it. I'm convinced. Whether I believe it, that's a separate matter..."

"Your conflict really comes through loud and clear."

"Say the two of us meeting here was a coincidence, fine. Then what about this place? What do you think a spot like this balcony is even for?"

As a matter of fact, even if this Echidna were scheming something, there was no evidence here to make her confess it. It wasn't trust—it was more that he'd accepted her argument out of a kind of unavoidable necessity. But that, and the fact that this place was a hidden space, were separate matters. Given the trouble taken to hide it, there had to be some reason for its existence.

"I have a hypothesis about that. Three days ago... do you remember what happened in the sand sea?"

"Three days ago brings to mind the whole chaotic mess before we reached the tower, but..."

"During that uproar, while we were being chased by the courtesan bears, a white light struck us. —That, it seems, was Shaula's doing after all. If so, this place is—"

"—Her lookout post for keeping watch over the sand sea?"

At the destination Echidna's hypothesis was heading toward, Subaru snapped his fingers. Her reasoning made sense. In fact, Shaula had sniped at anything approaching the tower from long range, using what she called "Hell's Snipe." He'd been wondering where in the windowless watchtower, with no means of viewing the outside, she could have been doing that—

"Likely, places like this exist all around the tower's circumference. Judging by the look of it, this space is offset from the direction we tried to approach the tower from."

"What about the birds?"

"The birds remain a mystery. Even touching them like this gets no reaction. Though they apparently do have body heat, so they're probably not actually artificial constructs. I'd like to dissect one if possible, but..."

Looking down at the bird beside her, Echidna narrowed her round eyes with cruel intent. But she withdrew the finger she'd been extending toward it, and stared intently at her own fingertip.

"Unfortunately, I don't want to put any more strain on Ana's body than necessary. If you were to strangle a bird for me, that would make things quick, but..."

"Well, if it's absolutely necessary, I'll do it, but..."

Called to this other world for a little over a year now, Subaru did have experience hunting birds and wild rabbits. Of course, killing to eat and killing to test something felt entirely different, but—

"If it's about killing and then eating them..."

"Right, there's also the matter of remaining food supplies. Then, I'd like about twenty birds, please."

"Are you crazy?! Have you never heard of Legend of Zelda?!"

Casually asked to commit mass avicide, Subaru raised his voice in outrage. Echidna, yelled at over knowledge she had no way of knowing, blinked wide-eyed. Setting that aside, killing as many as twenty birds did weigh on his conscience regardless.

"Besides, if we actually kill some, won't that provoke a reaction from this countless flock?"

"...That's a fear I can't dismiss."

At Subaru's unease, Echidna too put a hand to her mouth, conceding the point. The birds showed no reaction to the two of them continuing their dangerous conversation, but their gazes alone remained fixed, as ever, on Subaru and the others—clear outsiders in this place. The word "sky burial" flitted through Subaru's mind, and he stopped Echidna's snap decision.

"If we're going to do it, let's prepare first. For now, let's put it off."

"No sign of running, then. Fine, that's fine by me. ...Honestly, it seems unlikely we'd gain much from examining these birds anyway."

"Cut it out with that vibe like your remark just now was your curiosity or thirst for knowledge winning out."

"—?"

Echidna, who genuinely didn't seem to know the witch's true character, still seemed to retain behavioral patterns close to the original in a way that made her impossible to let his guard down around. So Subaru ignored that point and threw a question instead at the part of her that wasn't witch-like—at Echidna the spirit.

"I haven't really checked properly, but how's Anastasia-san doing?"

"...No change, still. Ana continues sleeping deep within this body even now. She's never inhabited this body this long before, so it would be a lie to say I don't feel any impatience myself."

"Impatience?"

Not being able to return to her original body, is what he meant with that nuance, but Echidna shook her head. "That's not it."

"Ana and I are, in a sense, in a spirit-and-mage contract relationship. Both incomplete—an incomplete spirit and an incomplete mage... an incomplete relationship, in other words. I believe I told you about Ana's body already."

"Yeah, something about a flaw in her Gate, or not."

"That part is close to your and Beatrice's current relationship. What's different from you two is that Ana's flesh has no reservoir for mana at all. To maintain the contract with me, she has no choice but to spend her Od."

"Uh-huh, uh-huh?"

That explanation, too, seemed roughly no different from what he'd been told before. But Echidna, as if to say Subaru's understanding was shallow, pressed a finger to her own chest.

"This state, of me being here in place of Ana, is essentially equivalent to me continuously manifesting. That's an act that eats away at Ana's Od at an accelerating rate. Even now, moment by moment, Ana's Od is being whittled away."

"Od's, like, that person's Soul itself, isn't it... which means..."

"Ana took my hand knowing full well the danger of contracting with me. From that moment, it was assumed an end would come eventually... but now, it's coming sooner."

—So, Ana's body needed to be returned to her as soon as possible.

Echidna concluded the situation she and Anastasia found themselves in with that. Hearing it, Subaru realized he'd been thinking too lightly of their circumstances.

"With a body like that... in that kind of tattered state... can she even become queen?"

"Is that meaning to say, for the sake of your own master, that you'd like Ana to relinquish her claim?"

"—! Don't screw around! That's not what I meant at all! I..."

"Ana will never back down. She won't give up, either. I know that."

As Subaru pressed forward, about to raise his voice, Echidna cut him off flatly. Overwhelmed by her force, Subaru blinked. Then, his lips trembling hesitantly, he said,

"...Does Anastasia-san want her own kingdom that badly? Even if she gets it, and might have to let it go right away?"

"Her time may be shorter than most, perhaps, but Ana herself makes far better use of that short time than others many times over. Besides, Ana has a reason she can't give up the throne."

To Subaru's weak voice, Echidna spoke with trust in Anastasia woven through her words. And the reason she couldn't give up the throne, once told, was this—

"—Because it was wished for."

At some point, Echidna had risen and walked over, standing now face to face with Subaru at the center of the balcony. Peering straight into his black eyes, the words her light blue-green eyes delivered left Subaru unable to move. A weight different from that of the Pleiades Watchtower's "Trials" bore down on him.

"——"

He couldn't move, couldn't speak. Echidna said nothing more either. And in place of the two of them, frozen in stillness, only the sound of wings cut through the cold night. The rustle of wings passed from behind, joining the flock of birds resting their wings. Yet another bird, newly, onto the balcony—

—from behind.

"——"

Subaru still stood with his back to the outer wall of the Watchtower. If a bird's wings beat from behind him, that could only mean from inside the tower. Echidna, Subaru—both had been led here by the beating of birds' wings. Then, naturally, so too must the beating wings of this third bird.

"—What you just said... what does that mean?"

A voice, somewhat dazed, sounding as though the very foundation of its belief had been shaken, rang out across the balcony. At the voice of the man standing frozen there, the birds all spread their wings at once. And then, raising a roar of wingbeats like a torrential downpour, the birds took flight without hesitation.

Into the night sky, into the sea of dunes wrapped in dusk's darkness.

Subaru and Echidna, feeling as though left stranded on a vast ocean, —and, leaving behind, Julius Juukulius.
