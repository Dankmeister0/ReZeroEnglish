One Day II Special 2: "The Royal Selection Prelude"

"I know it was wrong of me, but I've taken the liberty of looking over the documents."

Looking up at Crusch through the iron bars, his gaze had a strange lack of reality to it, as if he were looking at something far away. The old man sat in the middle of the room, legs folded beneath him, holding himself with correct, formal posture.

In the garden he had folded away the tattered cloak he'd worn over his shoulders, and now, clad only in his light garb, his frame was just as it had first appeared — unmistakably that of a man honed to a fine edge.

And yet his cheeks were beginning to show bone, his jaw unshaven, and his once-abundant hair, its color long since faded, had lost all luster, gathered carelessly at the nape of his neck. There was no denying the air of ill health about him.

"――――"

"You look me over like you're appraising goods. ...Understandable, I suppose. Let me apologize for cutting in without giving my name first."

Closing one eye against the silent stare, Crusch pressed a hand to her chest and straightened her spine.

Even the dusty air of the guardhouse basement seemed to shrink back and scatter before her awe-inspiring bearing. The aura she gave off was, without question, an innate quality that drew every eye, and even the old man's gaze shifted, however slightly.

"I am Crusch Karsten, head of House Karsten. During the exchange in the royal castle gardens earlier, I happened upon the argument between you and Lord Bordeaux, and came to hear the details."

"Karsten... Duke...?"

At the revelation of her identity, the color in the old man's eyes shifted once more. It held both understanding and acceptance, along with a fresh doubt welling up anew.

"House Karsten's head should be Lord Meckart, if I recall."

"Indeed. Meckart Karsten is my father. He ceded headship of the house to me some two years past, and I now bear the title of Duke Karsten. You seem fairly well-versed in the family's affairs... though your knowledge appears to be a few years out of date. That is likely the source of the quarrel as well."

"Is the Kingdom's officialdom so short-handed that a duchess herself must conduct the interrogation?"

"Sharp tongue. In truth, I have no intention of prying into the quarrel between you and Lord Bordeaux. What I said just now was merely a pretext — I needed something to satisfy the guards I sent away."

Crusch gave a wry smile at the old man's sarcasm.

The two now faced each other in the underground cell beneath the guardhouse adjoining the barracks of the Royal Guard, located just beside the royal castle. Hearing that the old man who had caused the disturbance in the garden had been taken here, Crusch had dismissed the guards under the pretense of interrogating him herself, and now met him alone. Upstairs, Ferris was likely buying time with idle chatter to keep the guards occupied.

"You said pretext, did you. Which means you have a true matter at hand. Surely you have not come merely to personally mete out punishment for the disrespect shown to Lord Bordeaux?"

"Naturally not. Nor does Lord Bordeaux himself wish for that. He insists the fault lies with him. Your person will likely be released by tomorrow morning. Though should the same conduct recur after that, I'm afraid even he could not shield you further."

"...I shall commit no such folly. What occurred in the garden was due to my own immaturity. Being placed in this cold cell has, if anything, cooled my head all the more."

His tone carried the trace of a smile, but his expression did not shift in the slightest. Even so, his self-reproach was likely no lie. There was no trace of anger toward Bordeaux — only anger at himself.

But the intensity of that self-directed anger reached Crusch as a wind so thick it nearly choked her.

—Anger. It is anger.

What was rooted at the foundation of this old man, driving him with a heat fit to scorch, was pure fury.

Even as he embodied the manner of a blade honed to such a degree that Crusch found her eyes drawn to it, so composed and still — a passion fierce enough to warp the very shape of steel seethed within his chest.

And the source of that fury was—

"—The White Whale."

"――――"

"Your eyes changed color."

The instant he caught the murmur, a dark, murky emotion swept through the old man's eyes. It vanished in an instant, leaving no trace, but it could not escape Crusch's gaze — she had anticipated the reaction.

Crusch produced the scrap of paper she had held since descending into the cell — one that had scattered in the garden — and continued speaking to the old man through the iron bars.

"As I said at the start, I know it was wrong of me, but I've taken the liberty of looking over the documents. Though most of it Lord Bordeaux collected himself — what remains in my hands is only the one sheet that was overlooked."

"...That man has always had a bit of a blind spot in that regard. I've pointed it out to him time and again — that he grows careless underfoot. It seems, in the end, it never was corrected."

"That too is part of who Lord Bordeaux is. It's difficult even to imagine a flawless Lord Bordeaux."

"No doubt."

For the first time, a shared smile graced both Crusch's and the old man's cheeks. Even the sentiment of a bald old man smiling loses some of its warmth described that way, but Crusch let out a small breath before running her fingers over the page once more.

"What I have is only one sheet, but fortunately it is not an unrelated one. What is recorded here is truly fascinating. If this is accurate, it would be a remarkable discovery."

"――――"

"This compiles information on White Whale sightings confirmed across the continent over the past ten years. Not confined to Lugunica alone — Gusteko, Vollachia, Kararagi... including the smaller nations, the number is immense. The dates it appeared, the dates the mist cleared, the damage inflicted. This is only a fraction of it, but you have gone that far, haven't you?"

"――――"

The old man did not answer. Contrary to his eyes, which grew steadily colder in silence, the tongue with which Crusch spoke gained heat little by little.

Naturally so. How could this not stir astonishment, and with it, heat?

The White Whale. Since the age of the ancient Witch, it was the name of a malevolent Witchbeast that had rampaged across the world. It had brought untold suffering thus far and remained, even now, an unconquered work of malice. In the past, a subjugation force had once been assembled, a great expedition mounted to bring it down — but the result was a historic, crushing defeat, and the Kingdom's spirit was broken by a single beast.

The wounds and losses incurred then had not healed even now. Most people did not even realize a wound existed. That was the most contemptible, unforgivable trait of the Witchbeast known as the White Whale. For its victims to be unable even to recognize their own blood, their own wounds — that was an act beneath contempt, an outrage past all decency.

Not Crusch alone. Everyone who knew of the White Whale felt that same anger.

"But you did not let that anger end as mere anger. Your persistence bore fruit, and you have gathered this much information. If the ferocity of a beast said to appear and vanish without a trace could be laid bare, how many could be saved?"

Merchants and travelers of all kinds along the roads were perpetually exposed to the threat of the White Whale. Against a malice whose appearance could not be predicted, against a being one could not even resist, there was no way to guard one's heart enough to truly prepare.

"The number who fall to the White Whale's fangs would surely be greatly reduced. Your deeds carry that much worth. Be proud. Through your actions, you have saved countless lives."

"――――"

"—Your face says, 'and what of it.'"

"――――!"

For the first time since she had broached the contents of the documents, the old man's expression changed. Color returned to his once-frozen face and eyes, and his focus pierced through to bore into the space between Crusch's brows. Reading in his gaze the question of what she intended to say, Crusch drew her chin in.

"I understand your intent. You have spent long years, worn your soul thin, tracking the whereabouts of that beast. And the use to which you would put information gained at such cost could never be something as passive as merely escaping its threat."

"...Then what, would you say?"

"It's obvious. Your aim is the subjugation of the White Whale. You investigated the timing of its appearances for no purpose other than to strike the beast down with flawless preparation. That fierce sword-spirit of yours — I would guess you are a man of no small renown. Would such a man lay down his sword and seek only an escape route? Impossible."

Considered that way, the substance of the broken-off talks between the old man and Bordeaux came into view.

The old man and Bordeaux had been conspiring on a plan to slay the White Whale. But that plan had collapsed, owing to the royal family's confinement to their sickbeds.

Bordeaux's growing workload as a member of the Council of Wise Men might be cited as one reason, but the more urgent one was surely "the problem on the White Whale's side."

"You had petitioned to be sent as an envoy to Vollachia. Which means, by your reckoning, the next place the White Whale is expected to appear is the Vollachia Empire. And then—"

"If its appearance in the Empire is confirmed, then half a year later it will appear next in the Kingdom of Lugunica. —That is the certainty to be gained. And with that certainty in hand, he said he would commit troops...!"

"That was Lord Bordeaux's reason for hesitating, then."

"Why! Why, after all this time, would he go back on his word now! He spoke at such length of his resentment, his grudge against the White Whale! Did you not... did you not also burn with fury at what was taken from you... have you forgotten Theresia, Bordeaux...!"

A furious shout rang out. It sliced through the sour air of the prison and struck Crusch's ears. The old man let his fist fall against the floor in bitter frustration, biting his lip, his anger laid bare.

It was anger at Bordeaux for breaking his promise, hatred for the White Whale, the hated enemy — and above all, an endless, seething fury at his own inadequacy.

"...There is no time. If we let this next chance pass, the White Whale will not return to Lugunica again for years. And by then, I will have grown weaker. Even now... this withering body of mine has no time left. The more this is postponed, the further vengeance slips away...!"

"――――"

It was a scream. He had not raised his voice. He had not even wept. And yet what was wrung from him, wearing his soul thin, was without question the old man's scream.

And a scream powerful enough to shake one's very being always carves the same anguish, the same wound, into the heart of whoever hears it.

Theresia — the name the old man had spoken was one Crusch recognized as well.

Naturally so. There was no one who wielded a sword who did not know the name of the Sword Saint of the Kingdom of Lugunica. Theresia was the name of the hero who had once quelled the Demi-Human War, a civil war that had engulfed the entire Kingdom. Beautiful, and impossibly strong — a woman beloved by the sword. Her end, so it was said, came while participating in the subjugation force the Kingdom had sent against the White Whale — she had died in that battle.

"—Ah."

The instant Theresia's name and fate flickered across Crusch's mind, a single figure surfaced alongside them. A person connected to Theresia, a person who had reached a pinnacle of swordsmanship so absolute it made Crusch tremble to think of it. Only one such man existed within that memory.

That man, spoken of in legend, had bent even the Sword Saint who ended the war to his blade — a demon who had loved and wed the woman beloved by the sword. A demon who lived by the sword, who loved the woman beloved by the sword. A demon who stole a woman from the god of the sword.

"The Sword Demon — Wilhelm van Astrea."

That was the name of a man who had built legend atop legend. So captivating was the sword-duel between the Sword Saint and Sword Demon that it was still sung and retold, and even Crusch, who had heard the song as a child, had felt her heart quicken like so many others.

If that were so, then the man before her now was that very sword-demon she had once admired.

"That name, I discarded long ago. From the moment I abandoned the duty entrusted to me and forsook my house, neither the Astrea name nor the van sword-title have belonged to me."

"Then what do you call yourself now?"

"Trias. The name of a fallen noble house, once at the edge of the Kingdom, extinguished decades ago. A house whose final head had no power to protect his lands, yet loved his people to the end. Now I am nothing but a remnant — a man who fulfills neither debt nor duty to the house that bore him, wandering lost through the world, a demon born of that name."

"Wilhelm Trias."

"Yes. That is the name now borne by this demon, living out his shame."

Pressing his fist to the cold floor, the old man — Wilhelm — bowed in a gesture of respect even while seated. The formality of his bearing, though it showed a disrespect toward a noble, somehow did not register as such. If anything, Crusch found herself holding her breath, captivated.

"Your pursuit of the White Whale is... vengeance for your wife, after all?"

"There is no other purpose... no, there is no other reason for me to live."

In his utterly clear gaze there was not a trace of wavering. Here, at last, Crusch fully understood the whole of Wilhelm's intent. She understood what he had single-mindedly pursued across more than a decade.

—And that, as things stood, that purpose would go unfulfilled.

"—I understand your aspiration. But your wish will not be fulfilled, not as things stand."

"...Why is that?"

"If you rely on Lord Bordeaux, he will not move. At the very least, he could never act in time for the next expected appearance, half a year from now."

Whatever resolution the illness afflicting the royal bloodline might reach, the Kingdom faced unprecedented chaos and confusion. However things unfolded, it would shake the entire Kingdom, and the people, high and low alike, would be swept up in the change. The first to be drenched by that wave were the upper nobility — and the duty fell to the Council of Wise Men.

"――――"

Wilhelm fell silent. But it was self-evident that his heart was nowhere near as settled as his expression and bearing suggested.

Crusch's eyes could feel it — the overwhelming fury pouring off Wilhelm. Ah, it was nothing but anger, endlessly. Within the sword-demon now there was nothing left but anger alone.

—What a waste.

That was the only feeling Crusch could summon toward Wilhelm. A waste. A regret. Such a shame. Such thoughts rose one after another.

Choosing how to live, and being bound by that choice, seem alike on the surface, but their essence differs.

Crusch held it right to live as one truly was, following where the soul led. She believed that to be the healthiest, most beautiful way for the "wind" of one's own way of living to blow freely through.

Precisely for that reason, she could not help but pity the sword-demon bound so tightly to a single way of living. His heart scorched by an unbearable fury, for ten long years he had clouded a blade once flawless with his own rage, sharpening its former keenness all the while — and here the sword-demon stood, as a result.

Should he crumble away without fulfilling his true purpose, the sword-demon's final years would be nothing but tarnished. Crusch wished with all her heart to do something about that, and yet the same responsibility that stayed Bordeaux's hand would not permit her to act rashly either.

She understood now the meaning behind the anguished expression Bordeaux had worn in the garden. Perhaps, for Bordeaux, the name Theresia carried an even closer weight. Wilhelm, who addressed Bordeaux without honorific, and Bordeaux, who called Wilhelm a comrade-in-arms — what lay between the two of them was something Crusch, in this moment, could not begin to fathom.

"—I am grateful you spared me your valuable time. But it seems there is no further purpose left, for either of us, in continuing this conversation."

She had not meant to let it show on her face, but at times the eyes speak more eloquently than words. Peering into Crusch's amber eyes, Wilhelm had already found his answer. Even without relying on something like the Divine Protection of Wind Reading, the passage of years grants an ordinary person's eyes a power not unlike it.

Crusch did not commit the impropriety of feigning ignorance of what had already been seen through.

"...What will you do from here?"

"The same as ever. If I cannot rely on Lord Bordeaux, then I shall seek out other men of influence. Until the moment this body crumbles to ruin, my life shall be spent on nothing but vengeance."

"――――"

There was a dilemma. Only among the upper nobility could anyone give Wilhelm's demand a fitting answer. But the upper nobility were, at present, entirely consumed with quelling the chaos within the royal court. A lesser noble with time to spare might lend a hand, but such a house lacked the standing to muster force enough to subjugate the White Whale. One might consider turning to someone outside the nobility, but that too carried its own trade-offs. A powerful merchant house — say, that shrewd operator Russell Fellow, with roots deep in the royal capital — would surely see the profit in the White Whale's information not in subjugation, but in evasion, and turn it to trade. And in that case, a Wilhelm liable to speak out of turn might well find himself marked for elimination.

Before his flight, Wilhelm would have had the prestige of House Astrea to shield him. But now Wilhelm called himself Trias. His pride would surely not permit him to borrow the strength of that family name. And should he go about merely as an ordinary swordsman—

"You must not act rashly. The only thing left to protect you now is your own person and your own conduct."

"I am well aware. To act rashly and thereby fail to fulfill my long-cherished wish would be the greatest shame of all. Your concern is more than I deserve."

His words were courteous, but his heart had already drifted away from Crusch. Whatever Crusch might think to propose here, the old man had surely already turned it over, exhausted every possibility, through sleepless nights of his own.

If there was information Wilhelm truly sought, in the fullest sense, it could be nothing other than the reason for the chaos within the royal castle. And that was something that absolutely could not be spoken aloud.

"Lord Trias... no, Master Trias. I apologize for prying open a mouth that did not wish to speak. And though it may be presumptuous of me, I pray your long-cherished wish may yet be fulfilled."

"You are too kind."

Closing his eyes, Wilhelm showed no sign of opening his mouth again. Crusch, too, found she could no longer see the purpose of adding further words here.

In mutual silence, Crusch turned smartly away from Wilhelm. Her trailing green hair sliced through the prison air, as though pushing the beautiful woman's back onward, sending her on her way toward the surface.

"Did the talk go all right...? Judging by that face, it didn't go too well, nyah?"

Climbing the stairs and letting out a sigh, she was met by a light, airy voice. At the guardhouse entrance — the lobby — a chair creaked as Ferris leaned back, peering up at Crusch's expression from below.

He tossed the cards he'd been holding onto the table and smiled at the two guards he'd been playing against.

"Well, since my lady's back, that's it for today. Somebody from the Royal Guard will come collect what you owe another time, so have it ready, down to the last coin, nyah?"

"Just what exactly were you wagering to buy that time?"

"Ummm, that's kind of something Ferri-chan can't really say out loud, y'know~. But but, if my lady really wants to know, I'll swallow my shame and... my lady?"

Ferris, cheeks flushed and shifting his feet, changed his expression and stepped closer to Crusch. His extended fingers brushed her cheek, and their eyes naturally met.

"You don't look well. Something serious must have happened."

"There's no hiding anything from you. ...Much like with His Highness Fourier, another worry has arisen that lies beyond my power. It makes me angry at my own inadequacy."

"But that's never stopped my lady before, nyah?"

Ferris gently wrapped his palm around Crusch's hand, clenched so hard it looked painful. Naturally, the tension eased from her shoulders, and at Ferris's smile Crusch's lips softened.

"Truly, I am no match for you. You seem to know me better than I know myself."

"Well of course, I know every little corner of you. There's hardly a single place on my lady's body — inside or out — that Ferri-chan hasn't touched, nyah."

At Ferris's mischievous remark, the guard behind them somehow perked up with interest. Puzzled by the reaction, Crusch nonetheless nodded and said, "Indeed."

"I'll fall back for today. But tomorrow will be different. And the day after tomorrow, different again. —So let it be."

The days following the encounter with Wilhelm at the guardhouse passed just as Crusch had imagined they would.

The royal family's condition showed no sign of improving, and Wilhelm, once released, wandered the country in search of the next ally, without result. Crusch, too, was buried in work in the royal capital, to the point that even her secluded father back on the estate had to be pressed into service.

Of course, none of it could have been Crusch's burden alone to bear — yet since she had involved herself in every matter, any failure to improve the outcome was, by her own reckoning, her own shortcoming.

That sense of responsibility, arguably arrogant in itself, was the very core of Crusch Karsten's pride.

Those endlessly overcast days, with no end in sight, came to a close with startling abruptness.

—The passing of the King of Lugunica, Randohal Lugunica.

And as though following him, the royal kin who had barely clung to life were, one after another, consumed by their illness — Fourier Lugunica being no exception.

Fourier passed with his hand held in Crusch's, watched over to the very last.

Only Ferris carried in memory the sight of tears tracing down Crusch's cheeks.

"The Dragon History Stone has carved new characters. —Does everyone here understand what this means?"

In the council chamber where the upper nobility had gathered, the old man could not hide his excitement. His name was Count Leip Barielle. He was lord of the Barielle domain, but he had also been entrusted by the royal family with special trust, given a certain particular duty.

That special duty entrusted to him was—

"A change in the Dragon History Stone... a new revelation upon the tablet that foretells the Kingdom's future?"

"Just so. This is the duty bestowed upon me by His late Majesty. I bring you now word of that change in the Dragon History Stone. I ask that all of you lend your ears."

He knelt where he stood, pressing fist to palm in the deepest gesture of respect. Leip's rank did not carry great weight within a council of the upper nobility. And yet that every soul present deferred to his word all the same was proof of just how much power and proven trust the Dragon History Stone commanded.

The Dragon History Stone — it was one of the sacred relics enshrined within the royal castle of the Kingdom of Lugunica, granted as proof of the Covenant between the royal family and the divine Dragon Volcanica. Much like the Dragon's Blood that brought fertility to the land, the Dragon History Stone too was treated with extraordinary importance, and only the royal family and a handful of others entrusted with duties such as Leip's were permitted to enter the chamber where it was enshrined.

The Dragon History Stone took the form of a square stone tablet, but what mineral it was made of remained unexplained, and just as with its composition, nothing about how it functioned had ever been revealed. The one certainty was this: whenever some grave crisis threatened the Kingdom, the tablet would inscribe upon itself a method by which to overcome it. In the past, Lugunica had more than once been delivered from crisis by the Dragon History Stone's inscriptions, and its track record was more than sufficiently proven.

And yet—

"Wait. You speak of the Dragon History Stone's inscription, but... can it be trusted?"

Cutting off Leip as he was about to explain the contents, the one who spoke up was the bald old man — Bordeaux. He fixed Leip with a stern glare, looking down at the smaller man from directly above. Leip creased his heavily lined brow and glared right back.

"'Trusted,' you say — that is not a thing I can let pass unremarked, Lord Bordeaux. Surely you, among the honored elders of the Council of Wise Men, know better than anyone how reliable the Dragon History Stone's inscriptions have proven."

"I grant that it has saved us many times in the past, that much I acknowledge. But this time is different. If the Dragon History Stone truly responded to a crisis that would decide the Kingdom's future, then why did it show no response to the plight of His Majesty and the other royals? If the Dragon History Stone truly cares for this country, then surely the right course would have been to show us a path to save His Majesty and the others!"

"――――"

Bordeaux's voice rose, and many figures around the round table nodded in what looked like agreement with his words. Crusch, seated at the same table, watching the proceedings unfold, felt much the same sentiment. If the Dragon History Stone meant to save the country, it should have shown a way to save the royal family. She recalled the moment the strength had drained from Fourier's hand in hers, the moment his eyes had clouded.

There had been nothing there but regret.

"—Regarding that matter, there is something I must tell you all, from my own mouth."

"...You speak so formally. What is it?"

"Yes. First, that I did not disclose this was entirely my own decision, made on my own authority. I ask that you hear me out with that fully understood."

"Then say it! Speak plainly!"

"The inscription appeared upon the Dragon History Stone before His Majesty's passing."

At Leip's words, Bordeaux's expression went blank with shock. But the reaction was not confined to Bordeaux alone. Every soul seated at the round table, watching how the council would unfold, opened their mouths in open astonishment.

Even Crusch lost her words. She stared at Leip in stunned silence, wondering what he could possibly mean.

"You are all no doubt wondering why I kept silent. So first, I ask that you believe this: my conduct was never, in any way, a betrayal of His Majesty."

"F-fool! Have you forgotten your duty, man? To fail to carry out the duty His Majesty entrusted to you, and then speak so brazenly of it! The guidance carved into the Dragon History Stone, the guidance for this Kingdom...!"

"Even if the content of that guidance itself foretold the extinction of His Majesty and his bloodline?"

"Wh—?!"

At the bombshell dropped upon them in quick succession, Crusch caught her breath. Even Bordeaux, his face flushed red, let his gaze waver at those words. And then—

"...Hmm. Lord Bordeaux, it would seem you had best calm yourself. Lord Leip, might we hear the full account?"

"As you wish."

The one who calmed and mediated Bordeaux was an old man with a long white beard. Miklotov, the representative of the Council of Wise Men, his usual gentle gaze never wavering, pressed a hand to his chest and prompted Leip, who bowed at the waist, to continue.

"First, the divine message upon the Dragon History Stone appeared some ten days ago... while His Majesty still lived, and it was I who discovered it. By rights, I should have reported it to His Majesty at once, or else informed all of you and sought your judgment... but on my own authority, I chose to keep its contents concealed until today."

"Hmm. And why would you do such a thing?"

"The inscription reads as follows: 'When the royal line is severed, the Kingdom shall seek out the five candidates chosen by the emblem, and forge anew the Covenant with a new priestess.' I found myself unable to commit the cruelty of conveying this to His Majesty upon his sickbed."

"...What is this."

Upon hearing what Leip had related, one of the upper nobles let out a sigh and murmured the words. So cruel was the prophecy. None other than the prophecy-tablet itself had foreseen that the royal bloodline, fighting against illness, would be cut off. Its track record, its history, was killing the royal family's hope. Leip's inability to voice this should, perhaps, be understood.

"The Dragon History Stone would have us... seek a new king, not from the royal line? And five, at that — what could that possibly mean...?"

"There is the emblem. The sacred jewel passed down through the royal house of Lugunica, telling of the Covenant with the Dragon. Since His Majesty's passing, with the bloodline severed, it has lost its light... but it will, so the inscription says, shine brilliantly once more in the hands of one newly worthy."

"Five who could make the emblem shine... and we are to seek them out?! Absurd! To select one who would determine the throne by such a method...!"

"Then would you disregard the inscription?! If we cannot select a priestess capable of upholding the Covenant, then Lugunica's history ends here! And so does the bond forged with the Dragon!"

Against those who sought to press further denial, Leip hurled back fierce words of his own. Those who had objected on reflexive common sense found themselves, one after another, falling silent.

Yet even amid all this, one hand rose.

"May I speak?"

"Lord Karsten. By all means — you have an opinion as well?"

"Nothing of great import. It's understandable that objections should dominate, but before anything else, we ought to actually verify the accuracy of the inscription. Has the emblem itself been prepared?"

"It has. Bring it forward."

At Crusch's words, Leip nodded and gestured with his chin, and the chamber doors opened. An attendant entered, wheeling in a cart bearing the emblem tied to House Lugunica. Such items were given, on a priority basis, to those holding positions of strong connection to the royal family — such as the Royal Guard.

It was a black triangular stone carved with a golden dragon motif, a red jewel set at its center. Ordinarily it shone as a mark of the royal family's prestige, but since the King's passing, it had lost its light.

"And you're saying it will shine again?"

"So it goes. Provided the one meant to hold it does."

By the attendant's hand, an emblem was set before each person seated around the round table. Looking down at the emblem before them, some broke into a cold sweat; others held their breath.

Should it shine, by chance, in one's own hand, it would mean the path to the throne had opened. Some swallowed hard under the weight of that responsibility; others, under a quiet ambition.

"And how was it for you, Lord Leip?"

"Were I to conceal the inscription and then have the emblem shine in my own hand, it would invite nothing but unsavory suspicion. The jewel would never choose one who would sow such discord."

Leip shook his head at the question, though how genuine that denial truly was remained unclear. Bordeaux, with a snort, took up the emblem first among them. But upon his firm palm, the emblem remained dark, without light. It seemed the emblem, at least, had not chosen him.

Next, Miklotov and the other members of the Council of Wise Men in turn took it up and shook their heads. The flow continued around the table, everyone seated taking their turn, and faint breaths and quiet sighs spilled out one after another.

But then—

"—What is this."

The one to speak was Miklotov, an unusual flash of astonishment carved into his normally gentle face. But surely those words spoke for everyone gathered in that chamber.

At the emblem's dazzling glow, Crusch looked down at her own palm and drew in her chin.

"—It would seem, unworthy as I am, there is still something I can do for this Kingdom."

Speaking with a calm that startled even herself, Crusch closed her fingers around the glowing emblem in her palm. She lifted her face, and closed her eyes.

Beyond her closed lids, she thought she saw, once more, Fourier's final smile.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

—The chaos that followed within the council, and the confusion of Crusch's own standing thereafter, defied all description.

The one thing that could be said with certainty was this: thanks to the Dragon History Stone's inscription, and the existence of Crusch as proof of it, the substance Leip had brought forward took on a tangible reality.

And Crusch's position, no longer merely that of a ducal head, became that of a provisional candidate for the throne — bringing with it far more frequent visits to the castle than before. And above all—

"As expected of my lady! Ferri-chan always thought — from the very first time we met — that my lady was never someone who'd be too small a vessel for something like a dukedom, nyah!"

Hearing the minutes and the substance of the council, and realizing Crusch's position had clearly changed, Ferris had rejoiced grandly, making Crusch feel the reality of it all — a reality Crusch herself had not yet fully grasped.

"C-Crusch-sama? Wh-why are you patting my head, nyah? I'm happy, I'm happy, but! There, ah, my ears are weak...!"

"You always do precisely what I most need of you. I'll continue to rely on you from here on."

"Y-yes, sir...! Ferri-chan is more head over heels for Crusch-sama than ever, nyah."

Praised by Crusch, Ferris flushed and swayed his tail softly, genuinely bashful. And then, abruptly, Ferris's expression returned to normal.

"So, what position will Crusch-sama hold going forward?"

"Provisional, but I am now a candidate for the throne. Nothing is certain yet. There should be four other candidates to be found besides myself. We must confirm that, and also debate whether it is realistically possible to establish a substitute priestess and forge the Covenant with the Dragon."

"But but, it's the Dragon History Stone's own inscription, nyah? No matter how outlandish it sounds, isn't it a proclamation from the Dragon itself — no need to even check with the Dragon, nyah?"

"So it would seem to be thought. Though the ever-suspicious Lord Bordeaux appears to doubt that Count Leip Barielle might have staged it. It strikes me as a somewhat cynical view, but... I confess I too am curious how Lord Leip will conduct himself from here. Also concerning is how quiet Margrave Mathers has been."

Seated at the edge of the round table, dressed in his usual garb for court attendance — that is, his clown's makeup and outlandish attire — Margrave Roswaal L. Mathers had taken part in the discussion. A man who rarely showed his face in the royal capital, and often skipped even important debates, yet had sat quietly watching the proceedings without stirring up trouble even once — that had struck her as odd.

"In any case, there's no use dwelling on suspicion after suspicion. I too am having my belly poked at over nothing, so I'm hardly one to speak of Lord Leip or Lord Roswaal."

"Mmmph! There are impudent people out there harboring suspicions like that about Crusch-sama?!"

"Even I think it went almost too smoothly, myself. Even Lord Leip likely never expected someone qualified to appear right there on the spot."

"But my lady doesn't think for a second it went 'too smoothly,' nyah."

At Ferris's expression, practically saying "hmph," Crusch couldn't help a wry smile. Truly, her retainer Ferris understood Crusch's own heart better than Crusch herself did.

"—Ah, I don't. I only think that what was meant to come has come."

Around that round table, in that council chamber, Crusch recalled what had been in her own heart. When Leip first raised the matter of the Dragon History Stone, she had felt the same surprise as everyone else. She had nodded at Bordeaux's rebuttal as entirely reasonable, and been struck speechless in turn by Leip's answer to it.

But when she heard that whoever held the emblem would place a finger on the path to the next throne, Crusch had decided, instantly, to test it for herself.

And when she saw the red jewel of the emblem shine brilliant in her palm, there had not been the faintest trace of surprise in Crusch's heart. There had been only the sound of her own heart's wind blowing through.

—An opportunity, I thought, had been given to me.

The chance to strike her own answer against the doubts about the Kingdom's very way of being — doubts she had heard again and again from Fourier's own mouth, doubts about the state of things Fourier had wished to voice.

The chance to keep Fourier's death from being a mere sacrifice, and instead to find, in that death, the time to give an answer.

Crusch Karsten accepted that, calmly, as simple fact.

And it had also offered an answer to one more worry that had continued to gnaw at Crusch's heart.

"Ferris, I have one request."

"—I've already confirmed his whereabouts. Just as my lady instructed."

Closing one eye, Ferris relayed that he understood full well the content of Crusch's request. Hearing that, Crusch once again wore her familiar wry smile, and said—

"Summon him to the estate. —At last, I may have a proper conversation with that sword-demon."

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

"I am most honored to be granted this invitation."

Crusch received Wilhelm, who knelt on one knee in the deepest gesture of respect. The setting was the Karsten annex within the royal capital of Lugunica, the two now facing one another in a receiving room.

"It has been since that one glance. Have you been well?"

"Yes. And it seems Lord Karsten remains unchanged as well — I am glad of it."

Having exchanged the requisite pleasantries, they traded glances without a flicker of expression. Since the incident at the guardhouse, it had already been confirmed that Wilhelm's efforts had borne no fruit. That Wilhelm had accepted this invitation was, of course, not without calculation of his own.

"It seems neither of us intends idle chatter."

"I have been told by your messenger that today you would speak the truth to me. If it may be so, I would be grateful if we could move swiftly to the matter at hand."

Wilhelm's glance flicked briefly toward Ferris, who sat beside Crusch wearing an air of innocent unconcern. Today, his tail extended from beneath a short-hemmed skirt, and he clung to Crusch's arm, deflecting both men's gazes with easy grace. Impossible to pin down, and yet skilled at steering conversation in just the right direction — far more talent for the theatrics demanded of nobility than Crusch herself possessed, she privately judged of him.

Setting that idle thought aside, Crusch continued.

"The truth is the answer to why every proposal for subjugating the White Whale has been flatly rejected. That is also the reason Lord Bordeaux withdrew from the matter."

"Yes. Everyone has closed their mouths, unwilling to speak the truth. What, precisely, has weighed your tongues so heavily? I beg you, tell me."

"Once heard, there is no going back. Knowing that, do you still seek the answer?"

"If it stands as an obstacle blocking the path to my wife's vengeance, then yes — if only to overcome it."

An unbending will of steel. At Wilhelm's answer, Crusch drew a breath, and let it out slowly.

It was the answer she had expected, and the one she had hoped for. And yet, even so, there was a slight resistance in opening her mouth here.

What Crusch was about to speak was information permitted, within the Kingdom, only to a limited few. It was not something to be easily divulged to Wilhelm — a man once counted among the lineage of the Sword Saint, but now a fugitive from his own house.

Breaking that taboo required a single beat of hesitation.

But only a single beat.

"—His Majesty King Randohal has passed."

"――――!"

Wilhelm's eyes flew open, and in the next instant understanding spread across his face. But the shock did not end there.

"And not only that. Every member of the royal bloodline has, one after another, also been taken. The long-continued royal line of Lugunica has been utterly severed."

"Impossible! Such a thing could not happen!"

Wilhelm, who had been kneeling, raised his head, teeth bared, and raised his voice. Content so outlandish brought the old man an anger beyond mere dismissal. And it was only natural that he should react so fiercely — for after all, Wilhelm—

"As one who once led the former Royal Guard, is this not news you cannot let pass unheard?"

"............"

"But it is fact. His Majesty took to his sickbed, and that illness struck at the royal bloodline itself. We spared nothing — the finest healers, the finest care — but it was not enough."

"T-the entire royal family, every one of them, their lives cut short...? Surely, that cannot be..."

Shaking her head, Crusch delivered the news coldly, and Wilhelm crumbled in stunned disbelief. His kneeling posture tilted, and he caught himself with a hand against the ground, wordless.

He understood it now. The reason for Bordeaux's sudden change of heart, the reason everyone had cast aside his pleas.

That the great matter threatening the Kingdom's very survival — the problem of the White Whale — should be dismissed as trivial by comparison was, of course, only natural.

But Wilhelm's fury was not shallow enough to be dismissed as merely "of course." And yet he did not know what to resent, what to rage against, what to direct his fury toward.

So Wilhelm fell silent, pressing his fist against the floor, smothering his anger. His fist, pressed to the floor, trembled, and blood slowly welled up. Closing both eyes in bitter regret — that he did not go so far as to bite through his lip was perhaps the last remnant of his pride.

But it must not be so. It should not be so.

"That is no place for a man to plant his pride — mere posturing such as this."

"—! What are you..."

"Your circumstances remain unchanged. Only the dark clouds have parted, and what was blocking your path beyond them has been revealed. Having heard it, what did you feel? Was it not you yourself who roared that you would cross any obstacle to reach your enemy?"

"There is no cause for me to be humiliated this way. What could you... what could you possibly understand...!"

"I understand nothing at all. I have lost even the will to try. To see a figure I once admired from a story, accepting resignation and coming to a halt — it stirs nothing in me but despondency."

Fury kindled in Wilhelm's eyes. But even so, the flame was weak. It was a flame still adrift, without a place to land, flickering fragile and faint. A smoldering ember, not yet reaching the heat, the precious redness, that Crusch sought and desired.

"I told you already, Wilhelm. That once you heard, there would be no turning back. This is a matter of grave weight to the Kingdom, and you are now nothing more than an aging tree, a swordsman of the common streets in name only."

"—And what more do you intend to disparage in this withered tree?"

"No. You are mistaken, Wilhelm. It is I who am asking. It is your role to answer. From the very beginning, I have been asking you this, without end. In our meeting at the prison, and now again in this reunion — from the very start, I have asked you the same thing, without change."

"――――"

"Wilhelm Trias. —You, will you truly remain as you are?"

He caught his breath. The old man's eyes, which had looked so sunken, went wide. In them was a blue that reflected the sky.

Crusch nodded.

"What blocks your path is a great matter of the Kingdom. Before it, every upper noble who might lend you a hand has vanished. Even should someone who could lend a hand remain, they would either be unable to provide the force you require, or else be schemers hungry only for the White Whale's information you possess."

"And you... what is it you intend to offer me?"

"The question still stands, Wilhelm. I will go on asking. I will go on demanding your answer. Beyond an answer to my question, I have nothing to offer you."

"............"

Wilhelm fell silent, sharpening his gaze as if trying to peer into the very depths of Crusch's heart. But Wilhelm would never be able to lay bare what lay within Crusch. Something not even Crusch herself could grasp fully in words — surely only Ferris could see through to it.

And that very Ferris, aside from the heated exchange between them, continued simply to remain close by Crusch's side. No sign of interjecting, no sign of worry.

That, more than anything, was what emboldened and supported Crusch — this he knew. And Crusch, more than anyone, knew of that consideration of his.

And so, Crusch Karsten was able to go on asking.

"Wilhelm. As things stand, your path will be severed before long. Your relentless pursuit of vengeance against that beast will vanish like dew, and your final years will be nothing but disgraced. And so—"

"...You mean to say that you would grant me the chance to raise my sword?"

"I repeat myself, Wilhelm. I only ask. The answer — that is yours to give."

"――――"

Arms crossed, Crusch hurled the words at him. Hearing them, Wilhelm drew a breath, and closed both eyes.

Then he straightened his posture, took once more the gesture of formal respect where he sat, and opened his eyes.

At the blue flame kindled within them, Crusch felt, unbidden, a shiver run down her spine.

"—I would cut down the White Whale, and lay my wife's regret to rest. I ask for your aid."

"...I too am but one among the upper nobility. My standing is no different from the other lords who rejected your every proposal, is it not? Why seek it from me?"

"Duke Karsten. Can you forgive the White Whale?"

"I cannot. I can never forgive it. Such contemptible conduct, such despicable acts, such an outrage, such heartlessness — who could forgive it? But—"

She paused. Toward Wilhelm, Crusch quietly closed her eyes.

"What I find most unforgivable is that its very existence tarnishes the way of life of so many. That it clouds how they live, clouds their very souls... that, I cannot forgive, no matter what. And so, Wilhelm — I cannot forgive the sight of you, as you are now."

"...A pitifully unworthy sight, I imagine."

"Yes, unworthy. Unbearable to look upon. Such humiliation is not easily borne. And so the fiend, the very cause of it, is equally unforgivable."

"――――"

Wilhelm lifted his head and met Crusch's gaze directly. Against the sharpness of that stare, Crusch did not flinch, meeting him in kind.

Their gazes crossed, and then, at the same moment, both eased — and, as though by unspoken agreement, the two of them smiled.

"What would you ask of this old, withered tree, my liege?"

"It's obvious. —The true form of yourself that would captivate me, a self unashamed before its own soul. It is that very figure — the sword-demon Wilhelm van Astrea — that I seek. There is nothing else I desire."

"The name of the sword-demon, the name Astrea — both have already been cast away by this body. Until the moment I cleave the White Whale in two, I shall not adorn myself with them again."

"Then let me be the one to raise the cry of victory. In that moment, the name Wilhelm shall be reborn."

"—Yes."

Nodding solemnly at Crusch's words, Wilhelm bowed deeply in the gesture of a vassal's fealty. Seeing this, Crusch drew her chin in with satisfaction, took up the sword at her hip, and stepped toward Wilhelm. And then—

"Take it."

The proffered blade, Wilhelm took up naturally, drawing it in one fluid motion. It spun once more in his hand, and was offered back to Crusch.

Receiving it, Crusch laid the slender blade against Wilhelm's left shoulder. She touched the point lightly, then set the edge against his other shoulder.

"There is only one thing I ask of you. Uphold that, and nothing more, Wilhelm."

"My loyalty to you, in this blade. My debt to you, in this body. My oath to you, in this soul."

"—Yes. That will do."

Having received his oath, Crusch drew back the sword and held it before her. Then she offered it once more to Wilhelm, who accepted it, sheathed the blade, and raised it reverently.

"That is yours to keep. A swordsman cuts a poor figure without even a sword of his own."

"But—"

"I wish to see it. To see you standing, sword in hand."

At Crusch's wish, Wilhelm blinked once, and rose to his feet. Then the tall old man bowed once before Crusch, fastened the received sword at his hip, and simply stood, spine straight.

At that alone, Crusch felt her own lingering doubts blow away, satisfyingly complete.

—Here, the vow of lord and vassal was sealed.

She would fulfill Wilhelm's wish, and carry forward Fourier's own. If the heavens had chosen Crusch Karsten, then this, too, must be the heavens' own wish.

She would follow where her soul led, and do what she must do. And in doing so, gather up all whom she could reach, and pray that peace awaited them at journey's end.

—On this day, the sword-demon gained the greatest ally in his quest to slay the White Whale.

It was the true beginning of unity within what would become the Crusch Camp of the Royal Selection.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

"Ahh, now I'm really getting jealous, nyah."

Wilhelm, who had gained a path toward fulfilling his great ambition, and Crusch, who had touched something of her old admiration once more — though their feelings differed, both carried joy in their hearts, and at the sight, Ferris pursed his lips.

"Crusch-sama got so into it she threw the whole pretext out the window and jumped straight to the point, mmph."

Originally, the plan had been to tie the matter to the King's passing, explain the legitimacy of Crusch taking Wilhelm under her wing to challenge the White Whale, and from there invite him into her camp, step by step. And yet partway through, Crusch's own passion had taken hold, and before anyone knew it, the conversation had tumbled forward into this shape.

That Wilhelm had been blinded by his own single-mindedness had worked in her favor, but Ferris couldn't deny it had felt like a tightrope walk, and he privately breathed a sigh of relief.

He didn't want to see Crusch fail at the recruitment and fall into despondency. Especially not when it was so inseparably tangled up with her admiration for the sword-demon.

"Geez, Crusch-sama, making my heart race like that... though that's kind of what makes her wonderful, nyah."

The plan had been to inform Wilhelm of her candidacy in the Royal Selection and win the Kingdom's people over through the subjugation of the White Whale. But throwing the pretext aside and going straight to the point had let slip, unmistakably, Crusch's true wish — to let Wilhelm fulfill his heart's desire, and free the old man from the fog of vengeance clouding him.

Alongside this, Ferris had also begun compiling a list of others who, like Wilhelm, had lost family to the White Whale's ravages and had not forgotten it.

For Ferris, who traveled the country as a healer and was well versed in matters both within and beyond it, even this was no great difficulty. There were, if anything, few households in the royal capital whose weaknesses Ferris did not have properly in hand.

"Truly wonderful, and so defenseless, and I just can't take my eyes off her, my lady — so Ferri-chan really has to keep a close watch, nyah."

Quietly narrowing his yellow eyes, Ferris regarded the old man who had lost one of his worries. A person carrying the weight of Crusch's admiration, bathed fully in her expectations.

Should that admiration, those expectations, ever be betrayed — what then?

"That's not something I even want to think about, y'know."

The young man's murmur, spoken only within his own mouth, faded away quietly, reaching no one's ears.

The Royal Selection — and the White Whale's onslaught — became reality exactly half a year after that night.
