Arc 2 Chapter 33: "The Light I Saw, and a Shadow of Unease"

The negotiation with Beatrice, which had gone more or less as expected, came to a close, and Subaru successfully secured her cooperation. It was a result he couldn't deny had leaned heavily on her indulgence, but lamenting his own lack of ability over that could wait until later. Achieve the goal first, reflect afterward. If he started second-guessing himself in the middle of an if-only, there'd be no end to it.

Suddenly curious about the time, Subaru touched his own chin. The forbidden archive was completely cut off from the outside world, and there was no way from within to know what time it was outside. There wasn't even a crystal stone standing in for a clock, and in that sense, he thought, looking at the girl who guarded the library, she was living a pretty free-and-easy life.

From the feel of the stubble on his chin, it had been roughly seventeen hours since he'd last groomed. Probably somewhere around ten at night. Given that his dead-to-the-world nap on Emilia's lap had started sometime past noon, that was an absolutely outrageous amount of oversleeping. And this from the guy who'd been huffing about not wasting a single second — the more he thought about it, the more hopeless he seemed.

Since self-loathing, once started, was also endless, Subaru got down to business with Beatrice, whose gauge of discontent appeared to be filled clear to the brim. The question was how much he could explain to her without touching on the core of the matter. Keeping his guard up against the black mist, intending to resolve his doubts one at a time—

"...You want to know more about Curse Arts Users?"

"Yeah. Unlike mages or Spirit Arts users, I know there's a type of user who works in that vein, but I don't know anything beyond that."

"You really do like asking for strange things. There's nothing to be gained from knowing about people like that, I suppose."

Just as when she'd only touched on the topic in passing before, Beatrice's expression as she spoke of Curse Arts was not a bright one. She'd flatly declared that she disliked their approach to Mana, but this time he needed to press further into that territory.

"They can basically only be used in ways that inconvenience other people, right? I heard it was popular in some country up north or something like that."

"If you already know that much, I don't see the need, I suppose. — Through a curse, you can plant a sickness in the target, or bind them with a restriction that forbids certain actions, or simply reap their life outright... It's a nasty branch of the craft, I suppose."

"Well, sure, it's all in how you use a thing, they say, but if it's that lopsided, the only use I can see for it is tripping other people up."

That's all Curse Arts were, then. What people called a "curse," in other words. Some supernatural power for degrading others was, he supposed, a vice that could arise in any culture or society. Though the fact that it actually held effective power to affect others — that, surely, was unique to this world.

Things like nailing a straw doll to a shrine tree at the hour of the ox — depending how you looked at it, that fell within the domain of Curse Arts too. Turning that over in his mind, Subaru leaned forward to try to get the tight-lipped Beatrice talking.

"So then, here's what I want to know... how do you actually defend against a curse?"

Intercepting a Curse Arts User was itself difficult unless you could pin down the enemy's identity. But the advantage Subaru held in this situation came down to one thing: he knew an attack was coming. Even without knowing the Curse Arts User's identity, since he knew they'd employ "Curse Arts," devising a countermeasure against that was, naturally enough, a reasonable plan. A fine idea, if he did say so himself. However—

"There isn't one, I suppose."

"—Huh?"

"There's no method of blocking a curse once it's been activated, I suppose. A curse manifests its effect and doesn't disappear until it's fully carried out the curse on its target, I suppose."

"Instant-death immunity nullification, seriously...?"

At Beatrice's matter-of-fact words, Subaru's spirit was already nearly broken. A surprise attack with an instant-death-immunity-nullifying spell — just imagining it, the battle conditions alone seemed like the kind of thing that would draw a mountain of complaints online.

"And on top of that, the chant goes off the instant combat starts. We've got no way to resist — oh come on, this is a dead end. It's dead. This is straight-up a shitty game."

He scratched at his head, freshly reconfirming just how bad his situation was. The faint glimmer of a bright spot that had appeared moments ago shattered into a thousand pieces, and Subaru's brain boiled over searching for some other countermeasure. Half his face pale with despair, the other half red with stubborn defiance — a Baron Ashura kind of state. To that Subaru, Beatrice said,

"However, that's only true of a curse that's already been activated, I suppose."

She said it, loosening her lips as though revealing the trick behind a magic act. Subaru let out a "Huh?" and lifted his face to see Beatrice's smug expression. Her face said, plain as day, gotcha — but Subaru's chest had frozen solid. At the tremendous temperature gap that had opened up between them at this point, Beatrice blinked a few times in mild bewilderment.

"...I don't think you need to get that angry, I suppose."

"I. Am. Dead. Serious."

He managed only that broken reply, suppressing every scrap of his emotion. Inside, his chest was a raging storm of curses and insults aimed at Beatrice, but bringing that to the surface and starting a fight with her was something he wanted to avoid for now. After all, a sign of good news finally seemed to be within reach.

As if to collect herself, Beatrice raised a finger and continued, lecturing:

"As I said, there's no way to stop a curse once it's been activated, I suppose. However, before activation, it can be interfered with, I suppose. Before activation it isn't a curse yet, just a mere incantation formula, so removing it is simple enough for anyone with a decent amount of skill, I suppose."

"Like who, for instance?"

"In this mansion, first there's Betty. Of course, and Onii-cha. Then Roswaal, and... the three little girls probably can't manage it, I suppose. Ah, and you can't either."

"Yeah, I know that one from personal experience..."

He'd been on the receiving end of that, once and then twice, and been very well taken care of. Setting that aside, Subaru pressed in close to Beatrice.

"How do you tell if a spell hasn't activated yet? I mean — does that mean a curse needs preparation before it can be triggered?"

"Obviously, I suppose. The stronger the effect of the formula being handled, the heavier the burden it places on the caster. It's the same with anything, I suppose. Curse Arts especially have a strong tendency that way. Given how it affects a person, that's only natural as a price to pay, I suppose."

"That price for a curse... do you have any idea what it might be?"

Grasping at straws, Subaru asked, and Beatrice closed one eye, thought it over for a moment, then licked her lips.

"What it entails changes depending on what kind of effect the curse produces. But there's a rule that can never be gotten around, I suppose."

"A rule that can't be gotten around..."

Subaru held his breath, urging her to continue past the pause. At the pleading look in his eyes, Beatrice tucked her chin slightly and said,

"—Contact with the target of the curse. That's a mandatory condition, I suppose."

The moment that information settled into his head, Subaru's brain spun into a whirlwind of activity. To cast a curse, the caster needed contact with the target. Which meant that both the first time and the second time, Subaru himself had come face to face with that Curse Arts User. — He searched his memory, trying to recall the person.

"Setting aside the people in this mansion..."

Excluding those who could have been the target of the curse, Subaru cast his mind back over the first eight days. A stretch of time spent living as a servant in the mansion, during which he'd had almost no opportunity to interact with anyone outside the mansion's staff. In that window, the outside presence Subaru might have had contact with was—

"—The village at the foot of the hill."

Both the first and second time around, Subaru had gone to that village. And now that he thought about it, both times he'd gone to the village had been on the daytime of the crucial fourth day. The curse formula placed on him by a Curse Arts User in the village, then heading straight back to the mansion, only for the curse to activate — leading to death. That pattern flashed through his mind.

If a Curse Arts User were staying in the village, it would also explain the case in the fourth loop where Rem became the target of the curse. On that loop, he'd gone to the village, and it just so happened she was the one chosen as the Curse Arts User's target. Depending on circumstances it might have been Ram instead, and if Subaru himself had wandered blithely into the village, surely it would have been him.

It all connects. It all connects. Everything, connects.

"Bea-ko. Do you happen to know how many people live in the village closest to this mansion?"

Suddenly hit with a change of subject, Beatrice knit her brow in confusion. But perhaps because Subaru's expression was so deadly serious, she didn't point that out, and instead shook her head.

"Don't know, I suppose. Sorry, but aside from this forbidden archive, Betty doesn't have much knowledge of even the inside of the mansion, I suppose."

"Right. No, that's fine. That was a bit much to ask for anyway. Besides, that much I can just ask Roswaal, one way or another... after that..."

Was the Curse Arts User among the villagers, or among some visitor? In the latter case, tracking them down wouldn't be too difficult. He could simply ask the villagers directly and smoke the caster out. In the former case, though, pinning it down would be next to impossible. If they'd prepared that thoroughly, he'd be at a loss — but,

"But that possibility, if anything, seems slim."

If he assumed this whole affair was tied up with the Royal Selection, then the plague that had wiped out the royal family was an event from roughly half a year ago. Including the time it took for Emilia's name to come up as a royal candidate, and for the framework of the Royal Selection to be established, the current state of affairs was one that had only come together over the past three or four months — a battlefield still fresh and raw. It seemed unlikely that anyone would have had the foresight to plant a Curse Arts User among the villagers that far in advance. If such a person existed, they could only be a precog, or someone who "knew about the Royal Selection before it happened." Once he thought about it that way—

"The Curse Arts User is an outsider. Which means tracking them down isn't that difficult a task...!"

Saying it aloud, Subaru began probing his own reasoning for holes. There were a few points that needed refining, but the broad strokes of the theory weren't bad. To begin with, from the Curse Arts User's perspective, this was still before they'd taken action. That their existence had already been found out at this stage was something no one but a god or a Demon could possibly know.

He tilted his head back, eyes wandering in search of a clock, but there was none. He clicked his tongue, but then, remembering his earlier stubble-timer measurement, let it sink in that it was still "the night of the second day." Against the fourth day, when things started tilting toward the worst, he still had a full day's grace. Which also meant it opened the possibility of a surprise attack against the Curse Arts User, launched from his side.

"Got you by the tail, you bastard...! I wasn't killed once and nearly killed once for nothing, damn it!"

A light had appeared. The situation was changing. Despair had cracked open, and hope seemed to be pouring in from the other side; without meaning to, Subaru clenched his fist and let his voice shake with joy.

Faced with Subaru celebrating this turn for the better, Beatrice — left behind partway through — looked displeased. She reddened her lovely cheeks, pursed her lips to voice her displeasure, and said,

"Asking someone for their cooperation, and then that's the attitude you show — really, how does that work. If what I just said was of use, isn't there something you ought to say to Betty, I suppose?"

"Oh, right, yeah! Thanks, you're a lifesaver — because of you I can see the light! I love you so much, Bea-ko!"

"Wha—!?"

Diving in, slipping his hands under her arms, Subaru hoisted her light body up, lifted Beatrice overhead, and spun her around and around. Despite being wrapped in an extravagant dress, the girl's body was as light as a feather. Combined with Subaru's own giddy mood, the synergy sent his spinning speed to double, then double again.

"Let g— put me down, I suppose!"

"Hahaha, I feel like I could fly right now! No, why not fly together, Bea-ko!? I can fly! You can fly!"

"Don't get carried away, I suppose——!"

"Jumpthro—!?"

Magic power blasted out point-blank from directly above, and the direct hit sent him slamming into the floor in some incomprehensible position. The impact entered through his head, traveled down his body, and exited straight out his backside. He landed on his rear, his head spinning from the raging torrent of Mana surging through his body. Meanwhile, having been released from Subaru's grip, Beatrice, having wrought this, traced a magnificent parabola through the air with her tiny frame.

But just as it seemed her body would go slamming into wherever it landed, the girl came to an abrupt halt in midair. With a light flip, her skirt hem fluttering, she touched down on the archive floor with an elegant, composed quiet. Looking down at Subaru, who was still shaking his head unbecomingly on the ground, she snorted in her usual manner and said,

"Getting carried away and acting so familiar like that — that's why you end up hurting, I suppose."

"That's not all I saw. — It was white."

"—!? Take this, I suppose!"

"Panties—!?"

A second shot struck Subaru's chin dead-on, sending his body flying easily deeper into the archive. He rolled and rolled, crashing into a stepladder, then got buried under the timber that toppled down onto him. Corners jabbed him everywhere, and, eyes welling with tears from the outrageous pain, he cried out,

"Just a little affection-gauge overflow and I get hit with your ultimate move?! What are you even unhappy about, spit it out!"

"That you hugged me, that you lifted me up like a child, that you swung me around like crazy, that you saw my underwear, that you spouted shallow words of love — all of it, I suppose! At this point I'm just angry at your very existence, I suppose!"

"Don't say you deny my entire existence, that's just sad! It's bringing back memories of my shut-in days, and that wasn't even that long ago!"

Thinking back on it, his shut-in days felt like something from a distant past now. In truth, in terms of distance, he'd come so far it was practically incalculable — and he wondered, are you all doing well back there, I wonder. Anyway—

"Coming full circle, calming down — the situation's gotten a lot better. ...Tonight's obviously too much to handle, but tomorrow or so, head to the village and..."

He'd figure out the Curse Arts User's identity, he thought. Since he probably couldn't go alone, he'd likely bring either Ram or Rem along. Or, considering the possibility of a confrontation with the Curse Arts User right then and there, having one of the two who excelled in combat accompany him seemed like an essential condition. If he was lucky, he'd defeat the Curse Arts User; if he was even luckier, it would turn out that Subaru's relationship with that Curse Arts User was a total blank slate, and the whole affair would wrap up in a happy ending. Mission 2, "Conquer Roswaal Manor," cleared.

"Compared to Mission 1, the difficulty on this one was seriously through the roof."

Mission 1, if he recalled, had been "Defeat the Delinquents." Thinking back on it, yeah, he really had come a long way.

He looked back on his standoff with the delinquents, which now felt like something from ages ago, and then reconsidered his days in the mansion all over again. He knew it was still too early to celebrate, but a situation that until now had been a hopeless dead end finally seemed to be opening up. He'd like to be forgiven this much of a giddy mood.

Was there anything else he still needed to resolve? Subaru made sure not to neglect checking his own footing. Precisely because the situation seemed to be turning for the better, he found himself suspecting there must be a pitfall hidden somewhere in it. It was a small-hearted kind of vigilance, but a certain word caught in that net of caution. That word was—

"The Witch's... lingering scent..."

"What is it?"

"Right. The Witch. Rem too..."

The moment he strung that word together, a string of scenes came flooding back into memory. The word "Witch," which had come up again and again throughout, and its true meaning — that was something Subaru still hadn't obtained as knowledge. The only Witch Subaru knew of in this world was a single brief episode from a fairy tale called "The Jealous Witch."

But now that he thought of it, that name had surfaced a number of times — during the loop on the day he was first summoned, and again throughout the repetitions here at the mansion.

Subaru raised his head and looked at Beatrice, whose brow was furrowed in suspicion. There was a faint anxiety in him about whether she'd even answer. After all, the last time he'd brought this up, Ram had shut him down flat, and with Rem it had even become a motive for attacking him. Even Emilia, he recalled, had shown some kind of strong resistance to it. Carrying that anxiety with him regardless, he asked,

"Bea-ko. — Do you know about the Witch?"

"————"

To that question, no immediate answer came. Beatrice closed her eyes at the word that had struck her eardrums, and fell silent as though confirming it within herself. Subaru, too, could only wait, his own heart stirred up by her reaction.

Had that single remark, once again, struck some chord within her?

"The one who drinks the world dry. The Queen of the Castle of Shadows. — The Witch of Envy."

At the words murmured suddenly, Subaru caught his breath and widened his eyes. Paying no mind to Subaru's reaction, Beatrice let a weary sigh spill from her lips and said,

"In this world, the word 'Witch' points to a single existence, and only that one, I suppose. And it's also an existence so taboo that even speaking of it is forbidden, I suppose."

"Feared by everyone, dreaded by everyone, defied by no one."

"That's right, that's exactly it, I suppose. If anything, the real question is why you'd even ask if you know about her, I suppose. There's no way you shouldn't know, I suppose. In this world, that Witch's name is taught to you right after your parents' names, your family's names, I suppose."

"That's... that's a bit much..."

He tried to laugh it off, but found not a shred of humor in Beatrice's expression and swallowed the words. If this wasn't a joke, then it was, without exaggeration, a symbol the world reviled—

"The Witch of Envy, 'Satella.' — The worst calamity there is, who once devoured all six of the Witches bearing the names of the Deadly Sins, and destroyed half the world, I suppose."

She said it in a voice frozen of emotion, and then fell silent, eyes closed. At that name, one he had heard before, Subaru let out a short breath — "Ha" — and found even shifting his body sealed off under the weight of the name that had just been slammed onto him.

"They say she ruled the night. They say human words meant nothing to her. They say she envied everything in this world. They say no one who looked upon her face survived. They say her body never decays, never withers, never comes to an end. They say that though she was sealed away by the power of a Dragon, a hero, and a Sage, her body could not be destroyed."

Beatrice recited this on and on, giving Subaru no chance to interject. And then, as if to close out the litany of information, she set down one final "they say" and said,

"—Her body, they say, was that of a silver-haired Half-Elf."
