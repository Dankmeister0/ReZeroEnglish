Arc 4 Chapter 119: "Love, Unchanging, Now and in the Past"

Crying tears of blood, gritting his teeth, Jous let out a scream.

Faced with his desperate, ghastly figure, Emilia couldn't stop a shiver from running up her spine. The black "Something" that had, until just moments ago, been trying to consume Jous's flesh had stopped eating away at the outside of his body. Now it writhed as if straining to burst from within.

Beneath his black clerical robes, Jous's body twisted and convulsed grotesquely. Just how terrible the state of his insides must be was made clear by the way blood seeped, damp and dark, through the thick fabric — an unimaginable nightmare unfolding beneath the cloth.

"Jous..."

Just what had Jous taken into his own body? And that blow, the one that had struck down Regulus in a single stroke — what had that even been? Whatever had happened, it had been utterly invisible to the eye, and yet it struck Emilia with a strange sense of déjà vu. As though she had seen it, just very recently—

"A splendid proof of resolve. Bishop Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti."

But Emilia's thoughts were cut short by a girl's cool, clear voice. It was Pandora, looking down at Jous — ragged breath, blood-streaked face — and speaking with serene composure. Even having just watched Regulus sent hurtling into the sky beside her, her effortless beauty did not so much as waver.

"Unqualified as you are, how admirably you have taken in a Witch Factor. In the name of this Pandora, I bestow upon your resolve, upon your unbending will, the seat of 'Sloth.'"

"Do you truly think I want such a seat? What I want now is one thing alone. That the mother and child to whom I offer this body without a shred of hesitation find peace — that is all!"

Fortuna and young Emilia had already fled the battlefield. Jous, with the literal blood of his heart — with his very life — was resolved to see them escape. At his answer, Pandora raised her brows in something like surprise, then flushed faintly and smiled in rapt, dreamy delight.

"Love. How wonderful."

"An emotion you will surely never understand — ever!"

Against Pandora's boundless detachment, Jous stood in a posture of total defiance. Kneeling in agony, he raised a trembling arm, forced open eyes reddened with blood, and screamed.

"Authority of 'Sloth' — Unseen Hand!!"

An instant later, a tremendous pressure burst outward from Jous with overwhelming force. And yet Emilia's eyes could not perceive the true form of that pressure at all. Jous simply stood with his arm outstretched, screaming, and no visible change came over the world. And yet—

"The forest — it's being torn apart...!?"

All around Jous, as though some unseen serpent were writhing through the earth, trails of destruction spread outward. Trees were mowed down, the ground split open, clods of earth and flowers scattered in every direction.

"Ohhh... OHHHHHHH!!"

It tore through its surroundings indiscriminately, but in answer to Jous's cry, it turned its path toward Pandora. Pandora watched the scenery before her trampled as if by some rampaging giant, and yet showed no sign of even moving from where she stood. So the destruction bore straight down upon her small frame, head-on—

"Um, hey."

"————!?"

"I'm right here, I exist, and you're just going ahead with your conversation ignoring me? Don't you think that's a weird way to think? At some point even someone as mild-mannered and unambitious as me is allowed to get angry, don't you think?"

The instant the invisible serpent's blow reached Pandora, a white figure cut into the impact. It was Regulus, his hair — neither long nor short — swaying as he caught the shockwave with a raised hand. Having taken a blow that would have been instantly fatal to any ordinary person, he stood there utterly unaffected. Worse than that — even after being slammed into the ground hard enough to blast a crater into it, the body that should have been buried in dirt bore not so much as a single stain, let alone a wound.

"No way..."

Witnessing that result, Emilia pressed a hand to her mouth, speechless. Him returning unscathed from Fortuna's ambush she could still, somehow, make sense of. If his combat prowess far exceeded Fortuna's, then perhaps he really could have weathered that lethal barrier entirely. But Jous's unseen strike was a different matter. Unlike the attack hidden behind a curtain of white snow, Emilia had seen with her own eyes the moment he was launched into the air and slammed into the ground. He should have hit the ground utterly defenseless.

Him being unhurt, she could still forgive as a fluke, barely. But there was no explaining away the fact that not a speck of dirt clung to his body. There was some mechanism — an attack, no, some means of being unaffected by others entirely — at work in Regulus.

"Regulus Corneas...!"

"How irritating. You weren't even chosen by the Factor, and yet you're forcing your body to hold it in place, ignoring its collapse, aren't you? Isn't that basically an insult to those of us who took our seats through the proper procedure? It hurts my tiny, unshakeable pride, you know."

Regulus's malice-dripping face snapped aside with each swing of Jous's arm. His neck twisted with the force of a real blow, yet when it snapped back, there wasn't so much as a mark on his face. He merely furrowed his brow in irritation, taking the repeated blows utterly unguarded.

"I don't think staying here any longer is going to change anything, right about now."

Jous's desperate fight, and Regulus batting it aside with pitiless ease. As Emilia watched an old acquaintance stake his life on this battle, Echidna spoke to her from behind. Emilia turned and glared at the Witch's profile as she watched the battle with an expressionless face.

"You're telling me to leave? When Jous has gone this far, fighting for his life?"

"Whether the process of effort connects to the desired result is up for debate, but unfortunately, I have no intention of debating that with you. I have no interest in bullying the weak, and hearing even one more word out of you is the height of unpleasantness."

"Then you can just watch quietly. I'm..."

She meant to stay here and see Jous's resolve through to the end. But before she could declare it, it was her own heart, of all things, that held the words back. The hand she reached toward her chest on reflex grasped nothing, and Emilia remembered why she had come here in the first place. Emilia had come to face the Trial — to overcome her past.

What Emilia was seeing now was the true past she had wanted to forget. Jous's desperate fight here had truly happened, and perhaps, in place of the young Emilia who had been carried off to safety by Fortuna, she ought to bear witness to how it ended.

—But that would betray both the feelings of Subaru and the others who had sent her off, and the feelings of Jous, who had tried to make sure she and Fortuna got away.

What became of Fortuna and young Emilia, whom Jous had let escape. She needed to find that still-sleeping, still-sealed piece of the past, and uncover the answer.

"Even that dim head of yours seems to understand which is the wiser choice."

"...You're right. Let's go after Mother and myself. Jous..."

"No need to worry. This is a battle between two Sin Archbishops. It won't tip decisively to one side so easily. If a third joined in to help, that would be another matter... but her lending a hand to the fight is, frankly, all but unthinkable."

Before Emilia's eyes — eyes still full of lingering attachment — the battle between Jous and Regulus grew ever fiercer. Following the bloody tears, blood now spilled from Jous's nostrils and the corners of his mouth. In proportion to the mounting damage ravaging his insides, the precision and force of the unseen destruction he wielded surged higher and higher. But Regulus's abnormal, unbroken composure as he stood against it was just as striking. Taking the destruction full-on and unguarded across his whole body, Regulus looked down on Jous's resistance with a bored expression. It was enough to make one think that if he ever actually went on the offensive, the tide of this battle would tip in an instant.

"Haah...!"

And where Echidna's gaze pierced, Pandora stood with her chest heaving, an excited look on her face. Just as Echidna had said, it didn't seem she intended to join the fight herself. Faced with this abnormal battle, the beautiful girl let slip a breathless, sultry sigh — setting aside how abnormal that reaction itself was.

"Switching the scene. — To where you and your mother fled, in the forest."

"—Ah"

At Emilia's resolve, Echidna raised her hand and snapped her fingers. The instant the light sound rang out, her vision warped and twisted, and the forest scenery changed entirely. At the illusion of the ground beneath her feet suddenly being swapped for a different floor, Emilia stumbled despite herself. She lifted her face. In a forest bearing no trace of destruction — and a place she knew well.

"No! No, Mother! Please, don't leave me behind!"

A high, childish wail reached her ears, and Emilia's head snapped up as if struck. Ahead of her stood a great tree familiar to Emilia — hollowed out inside just large enough to shelter a single child, the one she and her mother had always called the "princess's room."

In front of its entrance, young, sobbing Emilia and Fortuna were exchanging words. Fortuna gripped the shoulders of the daughter clinging desperately to her chest, speaking with all the earnestness she could muster.

"Please, listen to me, Emilia. It'll be all right. I'll finish everything and come right back — yes, right back. So just for that little while, I need you to hide here. Please."

"No! Absolutely not! Fortuna, Mother, you have the same face as Jous! The same as Jous, you're going to do something! You're going to leave me and — do something...!"

Young Emilia clung to her mother with small hands, desperate not to let her go. If Fortuna had wanted to, shaking off a young child's grip would have been simple for her. And yet the fact that Fortuna did not coldly shake off young Emilia's hands was proven, more than anything else, by the violet eyes gazing at her daughter.

Fortuna would not shake off her weeping, clinging daughter's hands — because she was Emilia's mother.

"Don't leave me! Let me stay with you! I won't lie anymore! I won't break my promises! I'll be a good girl, I'll be a good girl, so please — don't, don't leave me...!"

At the voice of young Emilia, offering everything she had so as not to be parted from her mother, Fortuna's face crumpled with overwhelming emotion, and she embraced her daughter. If she didn't press her daughter's face to her chest like this, her daughter would see her own face right now. She couldn't stop the tears welling up, couldn't stop them from wetting her cheeks — and she couldn't let her daughter see them.

"Fortuna, Mother..."

The crying face that the young Emilia of the past had never seen, the Emilia of the present now saw clearly. The mother who, within the girl called Emilia, had always been noble, admirable, strong, someone to respect, someone she had never once doubted didn't have a single weak point — she had never imagined that mother could be hurt like this, struck down by grief she couldn't bear, shedding hot tears.

"......!"

Watching her mother's tears, Emilia, glimpsing the past, felt her own cheeks reach their limit. Even the hand she reflexively raised to stop them wasn't fast enough, and tears began spilling one after another from the corners of her eyes.

Looking at this scene, at her mother's face right now, one thing was unmistakably clear. She had never once doubted it, but in this very moment, she could reaffirm it all over again.

"Fortuna, Mother, was... my real mother, after all..."

Who her birth mother had been meant nothing to Emilia now, not anymore. No matter how many times Fortuna told her not to forget her true mother, insisted that she herself was merely a stand-in — nothing would change that. Even coming from her beloved, respected Fortuna, that alone was something Emilia could never accept.

"Fortuna, Mother... I love you..."

This feeling alone — no one, saying anything, could ever bend it.

"Lady Fortuna—!"

A man's voice called out to Fortuna's back as she struggled to pull herself from young Emilia's grasp. Reacting to the voice, Fortuna wiped her face with her sleeve, roughly hiding her tears, and turned toward the sound. Approaching from where she looked was a lightly-armed elf man. One of the elves living in the settlement — someone Emilia knew well, too.

"Archi, how is the village?"

Fortuna asked the approaching man in her usual voice. The man called Archi seemed to have noticed that Fortuna had been crying, but without touching on it, he shook his head.

"It's the same everywhere. The Bishop's companions and the menfolk are holding them off, but..."

"Not going well, is it."

Fortuna lowered her eyes at Archi's answer, gritting her teeth at the poor state of the fighting. Young Emilia looked up at her mother anxiously, unable to say anything, only gripping the hem of her clothes and trembling. Seeing her like that, Archi said,

"It's all right. You don't need to be so scared, Emilia. Trust the village, trust us grown-ups. And besides, your mother is incredibly strong, and incredibly scary."

"O-okay..."

"Archi, the 'scary' part wasn't necessary. Honestly..."

At Archi's words trying to reassure young Emilia, Fortuna crossed her arms as if indignant. Still, unable to keep looking down forever, she nodded at Archi's roundabout consideration and gazed at the princess's room behind her.

"Well, hiding Emilia here won't fool anyone anymore, will it."

"It galls me, but if we stay in this forest, we'll be found before long. Their objective really is...?"

"The seal, deep in the forest. Wherever they heard of it... even that woman is here...!"

Thinking of Jous, whom they'd left behind, and of the Witch Cult's Regulus and Pandora who had attacked — Pandora's presence in particular seemed to carry some deep grudge, for Fortuna bit her lip bitterly. Then she shook her head firmly.

"Fine, I'll go out there myself. I'm the strongest fighting force in this forest — I can't afford to be standing around dawdling in a place like this."

"No! We'll fight! Lady Fortuna, please, take Emilia out of the forest!"

"What good would running do here? It's not just a matter of losing our haven — it's not even about us losing. The problem is letting them uncover the seal!"

Fortuna cut off Archi, who cried out as if trying to make her reconsider, with an even sharper tone. Then, as if ashamed of having shouted back, she said "I'm sorry," and continued,

"You must resent me, all of you. Truly, there was no reason for any of you to be dragged into something like this. Because I came here, and Emilia... unnecessary trouble..."

"That's not — ! There's no one among us who thinks that way!"

"Archi..."

At the regret bleeding through Fortuna's voice, Archi rebelled fiercely, as if there were one thing he could not allow her to say. His face reddened, his long elven ears sharpening with indignation, he flared up.

"Please, stop treating us as if we're outside the bounds of your problems, forever! To our lifespans, it may have been a blink of an eye... ! But even so, we lived through the same time, saw the same things, spent it together! Have you forgotten?!"

"..........."

"Who among us would ever think of you and yours as a burden?! We owe you, and your brother, and Emilia's mother, such a great debt — are you telling us to become shameless enough to forget it?!"

Archi's emotions burst forth, his plea edged with tears. Still young for an elf, he sank to his knees, breathing hard, sniffling as he looked up at Fortuna. Under that gaze, Fortuna, silent, shut her eyes tightly.

"I'm sorry. I was about to deny my family again — the people I live alongside."

"Lady Fortuna... I-I spoke out of turn..."

"No. It needed saying. Archi, I'm sorry. And thank you."

Thanking the kneeling Archi, Fortuna gently held out her hand. Archi hesitated for only a moment before taking it and quietly rising to his feet. Then Fortuna turned back to young Emilia.

"Emilia. Mother has to go do something very important now, to protect everyone. So, just for a little while, we have to say goodbye."

"N-no... I don't want to, Mother. I, I..."

"Please. It's only for a little while, so listen to me. I want you to go outside the forest with Archi. This forest is going to become... very, very dangerous."

Speaking to young Emilia, who shook her head with a face on the verge of tears, Fortuna turned back to Archi. Pinned by the violet eyes bearing her resolve, Archi's slender frame went rigid.

"F-Fortuna... I..."

"Archi. You're still young, you have a future. Please, take Emilia and... it's a hard world to live in, but there is surely hope."

"Don't... don't say something like that, like it's the end! I'll — I'll stay in this forest to the end, with everyone!"

"Please, take Emilia. She's precious — mine, and my brother's, and my sister-in-law's daughter."

"————!"

Fortuna's voice, in that moment, had none of its strength, none of its dignity — it was simply the voice of a weak woman. At the voice of Fortuna the mother, Fortuna the woman, Archi wept. Choking back sobs, he covered his tear-streaked face with his palm.

"That's unfair...! Saying something like that, knowing full well I can't refuse...! I want to fight too, with everyone...! And yet...!"

"I'm sorry. Forgive us, for pushing everything onto a child."

Placing a hand on the shoulder of the weeping young elf, Fortuna begged his forgiveness. Archi, unable to say anything, nonetheless accepted her request in silence.

And now, the only one left for Fortuna to persuade was young Emilia.

"Emilia."

"No! I want to stay, stay with Mother! Please! Please! Please, let me stay with you! I don't want to be alone... I don't wanna...!"

"You will never be alone. Listen carefully."

Young Emilia, wailing, in no state to listen — covering her ears, trying to shut out every word of her mother's parting words. Watching that gesture, Emilia wanted to slap her past self across the face. Not to reproach her past self for being unreasonable. But because she wanted to make sure that not a single word Fortuna spoke, not one syllable, went unheard.

"Emilia."

Crouching down, Fortuna embraced Emilia. She took hold of the arms of young Emilia, who was desperately covering her ears, and pressed her cheek against her daughter's silver hair as the girl pressed her head against her as if biting down. She touched what she loved more than anything, more than anyone, as though afraid it might break.

"Mother will always be by your side. Close your eyes, and I'll be there in the memories that rise up. Hold your arms, and I'll be there in the warmth in your chest. Raise your voice, and I'll be there under the sky where it echoes. Always, Mother is with you. Forever and ever, no matter what... together."

"Liar. Liar, liar, liar... Mother's a liar...!"

"Emilia. — A promise."

To young Emilia, trying to dismiss her mother's words as empty comfort, Fortuna met her eyes and spoke. At the word "promise," spun from her mother's mouth, young Emilia caught her breath and fell silent. Fortuna held out her palm, and guided by her gaze, young Emilia pressed her small palm against it.

"Mother and Emilia will be together, forever. I promise you that, right now."

"R-really... will you really stay with me...?"

"Yes, truly. Mother loves Emilia... loves Lia, more than anyone else in this whole world, so, so much."

At the gentle voice calling her "Lia," young Emilia — Emilia — felt the dam of her tears burst. Sobbing, crumbling into tears, the present and the past, the two Emilias.

"Fortuna, Mother... I love you too, I love you too... I love you, I love you...!" "I love you. I love Fortuna, Mother. So, so much, I love you, I love you, you're so precious...!"

The feelings of the present and past Emilia overlapped, answering desperately the love directed at them. Wringing every last bit of voice from their throats, pressing their bodies together, as if that were the only way to convey everything held in their hearts, the only way to express all that overflowed and could not be contained.

"Lia, I love you."

On her cheek, her eyelids, her forehead, Fortuna's soft, warm lips pressed down. Though she allowed touch, allowed embraces, when it came to expressing motherly love this way, she had always been reserved, someone who would never do this sort of thing — which was why this was the moment Fortuna, truly, from the very bottom of her heart, first acknowledged herself as Emilia's mother.

"— Archi, please."

"...Yes. Understood."

Having conveyed her deepest love to her beloved daughter, Fortuna rose and called to the young man. Called upon, Archi took the sobbing, tear-drenched young Emilia from Fortuna, holding her securely in his arms, and bowed his head deeply once to Fortuna.

"Please, escape safely."

"Yes... yes! Emilia... I swear, no one will ever be allowed to hurt this child!"

At Archi's absolute vow, Fortuna's cheeks relaxed as if in relief. Then she pointed toward the path leading deeper into the forest.

"Go, please."

"————"

Archi said nothing more, and ran off toward where Fortuna pointed. Held by the young man racing through the forest, young Emilia looked back over his shoulder — at her mother growing more distant — and let out a voiceless cry. At that cry, Fortuna's sharp eyes softened, truly gently, and she said—

"—I love you, Emilia."

Held by Archi, young Emilia stared desperately toward where her mother had vanished from sight. As if believing that if she kept staring, her mother, now out of view, might suddenly reappear. As if hoping her mother would come running after her.

"Emilia...!"

Archi, holding the small body against him, could feel that stubborn resolve in young Emilia's heart. Faced with the young heart that had just experienced separation from Fortuna, Archi's face twisted, uncertain what he should say.

"— That was unexpected."

To Emilia, chasing after the running Archi's back, Echidna, running alongside her, spoke up. Still dragged down by the scene of parting from her mother, unable to fully suppress her sobs, Emilia threw a questioning glance at Echidna without a word. The white-haired Witch shrugged.

"That you didn't stay behind, that you followed your past self without hesitation. I rather expected you to cling pathetically to your own mother's fate the way you did watching Sloth earlier."

"...I already told you, didn't I? I came here to see my own past through to the end...! Mother, and Jous, and everyone — that's what it's all for!"

"Yes, yes. I said something unnecessary."

At Emilia's tearful rebuttal, Echidna shook her head, her expression that of someone who hadn't gotten the answer she wanted. Even Emilia, patient as she was, felt a flicker of irritation at her callous attitude, but before she could say anything about it, ahead of her, young Emilia, held in Archi's arms, covered her face with her hands.

"Wh-why... why...? Why did it turn out... like this...? Be-because I... broke my promise... and left the room..."

"That's not true. That's not true, Emilia. It's not your fault at all! Not Lady Fortuna's fault, not anyone's fault! There's no reason at all for you to blame yourself!"

At the far too sudden separation from Jous and Fortuna, young Emilia's heart was pushed to the very brink of shattering. Searching for the cause of the situation she now found herself in, reflecting on her own conduct, young Emilia sank into a sea of self-denial.

Breaking her promise. Leaving the room she wasn't supposed to leave. Learning of the seal she wasn't supposed to know about. It felt as if everything, all of it, had started because of something she herself had done.

"If I'd... stayed shut up in my room, alone... would that have been better? Would no one have disappeared then... could everyone have... stayed together...?"

"Emilia...!"

"Was I... a bad girl? Is that why everyone hates me, and I'm... going to be alone?"

"No... no, Emilia. No one, no one hates you. The world isn't here to torment you... the world, everyone in it, exists to bless you...!"

To young Emilia, tears spilling once more, Archi spoke with desperate conviction. It was born of a wish to make Emilia stop crying, yes, but even more than that, it seemed like a wish born of his own need to believe it himself.

At Archi's cry, Emilia, watching the past, felt her heart struck. It wasn't only Fortuna, or Jous. Emilia had been protected, loved, by him and by everyone else in the settlement — reached out to, so that she would never be alone. That it had always been this way — until this very moment, she had never truly, in the deepest sense, remembered it.

"You there—!"

Someone sharp-voiced slid into view directly in front of the running Archi. At the black-robed figure bursting out from between the trees, Archi stopped short on reflex, eyes wary. But the figure raised a hand toward that gaze.

"Wait, don't panic! I am one of Bishop Romanée-Conti's Fingers!"

"The Bishop's...!"

At the winded, robed man, Archi's expression eased with relief at the mention of Jous's name. Seeing Archi's wariness fade, the man stepped closer — and noticed young Emilia.

"That girl there is... don't tell me, is Lady Fortuna—?"

"There's no need to worry. I... she entrusted Emilia to me, and stayed behind. Lady Fortuna is the most skilled fighter in our settlement. She'll surely defeat the intruding enemy and..."

"...It pains me to say this, but that will likely be difficult."

To Archi, correcting himself, the man said in a hoarse voice, eyes lowered. As Archi's brows rose at those words, the man let out a heavy sigh, his expression grave.

"The Sin Archbishop of Greed has been confirmed, and the Bishop is engaging her. If that were all, there might still have been a chance to drive them off simply by repelling the other Radical Faction believers, but..."

"Is there some other problem...?"

"— The Witchbeast, the 'Black Serpent,' has been unleashed into the forest."

"————!?"

At the man's words, Archi's face went pale with shock. He shook his head, unable to believe what he'd been told, gesturing toward the forest with his hand.

"That's absurd, impossible! The 'Black Serpent,' one of the Three Great Witchbeasts, should be even less controllable than the White Whale or the Great Rabbit! It's not like the White Whale, under Gluttony's control, or the Great Rabbit, whose course can be guided — the Black Serpent obeys no one, it's nothing but sheer calamity! Calamity upon calamity! How could it—"

"...The Witch Cult's Lady Pandora is accompanying them. Only Lady Pandora, through her Authority, is capable of — not controlling the Black Serpent exactly, but guiding it toward a destination."

"Pandora...? I've never heard that name..."

"She is one kept in secret! Even within the Witch Cult, among the Bishop's Moderate Faction and the Radical Faction alike, a taboo neither side is permitted to speak of. And that person has come here."

At the man's strained, wrung-out voice, Archi could find no words to answer. And yet Archi did not sink into despair — because he could feel, in his own arms, the beat of a life not his own. Because he knew he was not permitted to hang his head here.

"I was ordered by Lady Fortuna to see Emilia to safety. Whatever becomes of the forest, this child alone — hope itself — must be protected, no matter what!"

"...I will accompany you. I don't know how much use this worn-out body of mine can be, but..."

At Archi's unwavering determination to resist, the man's own withered expression steadied itself. Robes flaring behind him, revealing legs still well-trained for his age, he broke into a run to lead the way toward the path out of the forest.

"Let's avoid the Radical Faction believers as we go. In any case, once we're past the great forest, our sight—"

—will reach it, the man was about to say, laying out their plan, when it happened. The man leading the way ahead suddenly had something entangle his leg, and he fell. Archi let out a small cry at the man toppling sideways and rushed toward him. But to Archi rushing to his side, the man snapped sharply,

"DON'T COME NEAR!!"

"—!?"

"I was careless... to think it would come this fast!"

The man, having stopped Archi from approaching, pushed himself up from where he'd fallen. But he only managed to raise his upper body. His outstretched legs, for some reason, showed not the slightest twitch of movement. And beneath his robes, hiked up, the man's bare shin — exposed — was covered densely in what looked like black burn scars.

"The Black Serpent's venomous tongue...! Run!"

"But—!"

"There's no saving me now..."

The face of the man ordering Archi to flee began to change with terrifying speed. The skin of his neck and face, visible above his robes, was gradually, steadily overtaken by reddish-black blotches, and the mild-looking eyes beneath widened as his face sank in on itself, as if his eyeballs might spill out. Clawing at his blotch-covered neck with both hands' fingers, the man foamed at the mouth with a great gush of yellow froth.

"B-bub... ah, bu...!"

Just as he seemed to groan in agony, dark, black blood began spilling out from every opening in his face at once — eye sockets, nostrils, earlobes, mouth — the man's very life wrung out and drained away. At that pitiable death, Archi, of course, and Emilia, forced to witness it directly, could not remain composed. Even Echidna grimaced, her face twisted with something like pain.

"A cauldron of plague... the Witchbeast of pestilence, the Black Serpent...!"

Having witnessed the man's fate, Archi called out, in a voice worn to a rasp, the name of the Witchbeast that had killed him. It probably wasn't in response to the name, but in the quiet forest, ruled only by the breathing of Archi and young Emilia, another sound suddenly began to mix in.

A slithering, as if some enormous creature were licking its lips. Something long and slender crawling across the ground, asserting its presence.

The scale of the sound was too different from anything to match the impression easily, and yet it resembled, uncannily, the sound of a serpent baring its tongue at prey, slithering across the earth.

"— DAMN IT!"

Sensing the true nature of the sound, Archi realized that he and young Emilia were standing in the Black Serpent's hunting ground. Knowing that raising his voice would work against them, he had no choice but to raise it anyway. He could think of no other way to resist.

Not knowing which way to run, Archi took off, simply putting distance between himself and the man's corpse. The thought of heading toward where Fortuna had pointed no longer existed in his mind. Now, above all else, he had to escape this threat. He had to protect what needed protecting.

And that desperate, all-out struggle of the young elf—

"Ah—"

—was mercilessly torn apart, his right ankle, kicking off the ground, entangled by the black venomous tongue.

Across his bare skin, where the venomous tongue had crawled, a reddish-black burn scar was etched. The instant he saw it, Archi pointed his palm at his own right leg.

"...Fura!!"

Without hesitation, a blade of wind sliced away everything below the burned shin. Losing his footing, his collapsing body pressed against a tree trunk, Archi endured the massive spray of blood and the searing agony that threatened to boil his brain, gritting his teeth hard enough to crack them.

"Hyuuma...!"

There came the sound of air splitting, and the wound at Archi's severed right leg began to freeze over. White vapor rose as the wound was forcibly cauterized, and Archi let out a scream. At his harrowing act, Emilia could not speak. His instant judgment, his handling of agonizing pain. And more than that, the strength of heart that kept him from letting go of young Emilia in his arms even after all that.

"Archi...?"

Young Emilia, her face pressed to his chest, had not seen what Archi had just done. And Archi, for his part, had absolutely no intention of letting her see it. To young Emilia, he forced an awkward smile onto his sweat-beaded face.

"It's nothing... I'm fine... it's okay...!"

Halting, broken, but still answering so as not to let young Emilia sense anything, Archi spoke. But cruel fate mocked the noble young man's resolve without mercy, endlessly.

The wound he had sealed with the desperate resolve of severing his own leg and freezing the stump to stop the bleeding — even the unharmed, frozen part of that right leg began to dry out as though drained of moisture, cracking like earth that had had every drop of water sucked from it, the damage spreading. As if the ground itself were withering away, Archi's right leg began to die — and it did not stop there.

"...Emilia. Can you see the white flowers, between those two trees?"

"...Yeah."

Crouched with his back against the great tree, Archi asked. Young Emilia, her feet on the ground, looked where he pointed and found the white flowers just as he'd said, and nodded. Archi wiped the sweat beading on his forehead, hiding his pained expression.

"Can you run to those flowers? Past the flowers, and then... straight, straight ahead..."

"I c-can... I can run. But..."

"Then, run—"

Gazing at the white flowers, his voice catching, Archi told young Emilia. Short words, sending her off. Young Emilia's eyes wavered with confusion, but sensing that something was terribly wrong with him, her violet eyes trembled. At the thought of being alone. Of losing someone from in front of her, yet again.

"It's all right. Emilia, you — you won't be alone."

"Archi..."

"Now, run. No matter what you hear, don't look back... run!"

At Archi's sharp voice, young Emilia's shoulders jumped, and stepping forward, she broke into a run. Holding back the urge to look back — because she'd been told not to. Archi's words, Fortuna's words, Jous's words echoed in her young mind.

As if believing that if she kept every instruction, surely everything would return to the way it was. As if telling herself that doing exactly that was young Emilia's only hope now.

Archi watched her run, leaving him behind, disappearing toward hope. Then he let out a long breath and pulled up the hem of his coat.

The encroaching decay had already covered both legs and his waist, reaching up beneath his chest. His legs could no longer move — worse, they looked as if they'd crumble to pieces at the slightest touch. If the decay reached his chest, reached his heart — what would happen then?

A slithering sound, of a Witchbeast licking its lips before its prey, reached his ears. As though it meant to steal away the fleeing girl, the forest's hope, and the meaning of burning up what little life Archi had left.

"Like hell I'll let you go after her..."

The slithering sound, which had begun to fade into the distance, stopped. As though its fading interest in prey that still had breath in it had suddenly been drawn back.

The sound drew closer. Feeling the end draw closer with it, Archi's cheeks relaxed. Because his own death approaching meant that death had moved further away from that child.

"Lady Fortuna... surely, that child will be all right."

The slithering sound, the sound of the end, drew nearer. Listening to it, exposed to a mortal crisis beyond any other, Archi still smiled, as if proud of having seen it through.

"————"

That smile, even as everything in him withered away completely, remained unwithered to the very last.

—The white flowers Archi had pointed to were already long past.

"Haah... haah... haah"

Breath ragged, stretching her small stride as hard as she could, young Emilia ran through the forest. Toward where Archi had pointed, telling herself that going that way, only that way, was best, so she wouldn't have to think of anything else — thinking, instead, of her mother, and Jous, and Archi, and everyone.

"Uu... uuuh!"

She shook her head. Tears streamed. At the corner of her mouth, she fought desperately to hold back a sob threatening to spill out.

What, exactly, was happening now? Why was this happening? What did everyone know, that she herself did not?

She didn't know what to do, didn't understand anything. Wasn't there something she could do? Who were the people attacking Fortuna, and Jous, and Archi? What would make them go away? What was it they wanted—

"The se... seal..."

That was it. Fortuna and Archi had said something. Jous, too, talking with Fortuna, had said it as though it were something important. If so, then their objective was—

"—Ah"

Lost in thought as she ran, young Emilia's foot suddenly found nothing beneath it, and the girl realized she had lost sight of the ground, stepping into a hollow in the forest floor. She tried to catch herself with her hands, but the sharply carved hollow offered no support for her small body, and young Emilia, still carrying the momentum of her run, tumbled down the slope.

Normally, she might have burst into tears at the sting of a scrape and gotten right back up. But young Emilia, already at the extreme edge of exhaustion in both body and mind, struck her head hard against the ground, and for a brief moment, consciousness slipped away from her.

"I... have to..."

There was something she had to do. She felt like she'd found it, felt like it was right there. With a small flame of duty lit in her chest, telling herself she had to do it — her consciousness went out.

—And the story now leaves the child's side for a time, returning to the site of that fierce battle.

To bear witness to where two fates, at last, would come to rest.
