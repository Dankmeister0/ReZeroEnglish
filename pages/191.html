Arc 4 Chapter 25: "The Frozen Forest"

"Ain't no use makin' that almost-cryin' face at me. I already made up my mind, see. And once I've made up my mind, I don't budge. Like they say — 'Even Don Morakin gets backed up when you push him.'"

Garfiel kept talking, watching Subaru's eyes go wide with shock. The condition he'd just named — hearing it, Subaru's mind could do nothing but spin into a chaotic mess of astonishment and confusion. Because—

"Of all people, it's you who's saying that...?"

"Ah? Surprised I'm against it? Man, how thick are you? Just 'cause we've talked a bit don't mean you get to go actin' like you understand me."

At Subaru's words, Garfiel twisted the corner of his mouth in displeasure — but Subaru wanted just as badly to scowl and grumble a complaint of his own. After all, the idea Garfiel had just shot down was one Subaru had built up in the first place from a proposal Garfiel himself had made.

In the world before the redo, Garfiel — unable to bear watching Emilia wear herself down challenging the Trial — had come to Subaru and suggested he take the Trial in her place. To Subaru, who had been stubbornly fixated on the idea that Emilia herself absolutely had to overcome the Trial, that suggestion had come like a bolt from the blue, and he'd judged it had more than a grain of merit and adopted it. But—

"There's a whole storm of feelings in me right now I can't swallow down... but let's set that aside for now. More importantly, why the hell are you against it? Even for you, raising the odds of getting the Sanctuary freed shouldn't be a bad deal."

"Well, if I'm gonna back the old hag's plan all the way, then yeah, I get that takin' your offer would be the more efficient way to go. ...But hell no, I refuse."

"What kind of childish thing is that to say...!"

Garfiel crossed his arms and turned his face away; there was no logic in his words. And precisely because there was no logic, if he was speaking purely out of emotion, things would only get more complicated. From talking with him so far, it was clear that this Garfiel had a considerable moody streak — or, to put it more precisely, his moods swung heavily according to his own personal values. Since general reasoning couldn't be counted on to sway him, there was no telling what approach would actually work.

"Ryuzu-san..."

Since there was no getting through to a Garfiel unwilling to engage, Subaru called out to the old woman with the appearance of a child who had been silently watching the situation unfold. But when Subaru turned the question to her, Ryuzu waved her oversized sleeves — her hands not even poking out the ends — and said,

"Once Gar-bou gets like this, not even my words'll move him. A shame, but there's no one in the Sanctuary who could force him to listen either. Why don't you try challenging him yourself, Su-bou?"

"I ain't got a death wish bad enough to challenge a guy who throws dragon carriages around. ...Damn it, what the hell."

Ryuzu wasn't exactly endorsing Garfiel's argument, but she wasn't actively rebuking him either — which meant she probably agreed with him at heart. Ryuzu, too, believed that it ought to be Emilia who broke through the Trial. There was no telling how much respect she held for Roswaal, but it was safe to assume that, at the most fundamental level, she saw eye to eye with him on where things should land. Even so, Subaru couldn't help but think — if only Garfiel would come around to their side.

"...Subaru."

It was Emilia who looked up at Subaru, struggling with feelings he couldn't put into words, and called out to him with worry in her voice. Her violet eyes reflected his profile as she said,

"I—I'll do my best, so you don't have to push yourself, Subaru. It was just... yeah, it was just a bit sudden, so I was startled, that's all. If I'd known what was going to happen..."

"No, it's you who doesn't have to push yourself, Emilia-tan. I'll find some way to talk sense into that stubborn bastard. Then the Trial won't even—"

"Subaru—"

Emilia, faint of heart, seemed on the verge of caving to Garfiel's argument. Trying to bolster her flagging resolve, Subaru began twisting logic around to arm himself for debate. But even as he started preparing his case in his head, Emilia said,

"Is it that you... no, that even you, Subaru, can't leave this to me?"

"...Huh?"

"I—I showed you how pathetic I could be, so you think you can't leave the Trial to me... that's why you want to take my place, isn't it?"

"No. That's not it."

"No, I understand why you'd be anxious. You managed to overcome it properly, but I— I couldn't even come close to overcoming it... I couldn't even work up the resolve to f-face it... this thing called the Trial, this 'past'..."

Subaru denied it, but Emilia shook her head and refused to accept it. Negative emotion rose in her eyes, her lips trembled faintly, and what little color remained drained further from her pale cheeks. From the way her words grew unsteady, it was clear that the "past" called the Trial was replaying itself in her mind. —It was something that could make even her, for all her fortitude, falter all too easily.

"You don't have to remember it—!"

"But if I don't face it, I can't get past the Trial! That's right, that's right... unless I overcome the Trial, unless I overcome my past... I can't become king. I can't let the villagers, the people of the Sanctuary, go free either..."

He touched her shoulders and called out to her desperately, but Emilia shook her head and stubbornly refused to hear his words. If anything, the more he tried to stop her, the firmer her resolve became.

"I can't just keep relying on you, Subaru. I can't. Just a little while ago you were covered in wounds like that, and still you worked so hard for my sake... and here I am, about to make you shoulder everything all over again..."

"That's fine, that's how it should be. Might be a bad way to put it, but we lean on each other. Or would you rather I say it's about putting the right person in the right place? When it comes to this Trial, I'm just better suited for it. That's all there is to it, nothing more. I can probably do it, so it's faster and more certain if I'm the one who does. There aren't that many things I'm actually capable of. There'll be plenty more chances down the line for you to be the one who has to do your best as yourself, Emilia-tan."

"Isn't now one of the important ones? If I keep turning my eyes away from what I hate, keep running away... then what happens to me?"

—How much better it would have been if he could just shout that there was nothing wrong with running away. Running from what you hate, averting your eyes from what's painful, turning your back on suffering — if you could live peacefully that way, that was a way of life just as valid as any other. Subaru himself had lived his life doing exactly that, keeping as much distance as he could from hardship after hardship. So he believed — and could have said — that such a way of living, even if it was a weak way of living, was nothing anyone had any right to condemn.

And yet, now — now of all times, it should have been Subaru, who understood her stubborn heart, her stifled weakness, her frailty, who affirmed her—

"—————"

For some reason, he couldn't get the words he thought he was thinking to leave his mouth. Faced with Subaru's silence, Emilia squeezed her eyes shut and looked down. Still touching her shoulders, Subaru had no idea what to do with the warmth radiating from her, hot enough that he could feel it. Watching the two of them,

"Hah. Not that I care if you two go on arguin' back and forth like that, but from where I'm standin', sounds to me like Emilia-sama's got the better argument. Fact is, the Trial's a thing meant for Emilia-sama to face. And here you come, buttin' in from the side—"

"Shut up! You... you don't know anything, so—!"

"Ahh?"

Subaru's fury exploded at Garfiel, who spoke so blithely, oblivious to how he felt. Struck by Subaru's anger, Garfiel let a dangerous air seep out of him. But Subaru, too, glared right back without flinching at Garfiel's menacing bearing.

"You push things on someone like that, and you have any idea what happens as a result? Do you think you could stand there calm as anything, watching her get hurt, watching her suffer, watching her wear away to nothing? ...I was out of my mind."

"...The hell are you even talkin' about all of a sudden."

"I'm saying I was thinking so much about conditions and benefits that I wasn't thinking at all about the one thing that actually matters — Emilia. Sure, the results you get from overcoming the Trial are huge. But the wounds she picks up along the way, the tears she cries getting there — none of that gets counted. ...Neither does what she actually wants."

In the previous world, despite watching Emilia wear herself down from closer than anyone, Subaru had never once spoken a word to stop her as she grew weaker before the Trial. She could do it, Emilia would surely pull through — he'd driven her while she suffered, cornered her, pushed her until she was worn down to nothing, and only realized what he'd done when someone else pointed it out to him. Now there was nowhere for the barren feeling to go, having to fire back like this at the very person who'd made him realize it. The sheer nastiness of the phenomenon called Return by Death was cutting into Subaru brutally right now.

And on top of that, all he had was knowledge of facts that hadn't happened yet — foreknowledge with no context behind it. Speaking words that ran ahead on emotion alone, short on both information and explanation—

"What... do you know, Subaru?"

"Emilia?"

Feeling a tug at his sleeve, he looked up to find Emilia staring at him, her eyes wide. Emotion churned in her violet eyes, drowning beneath the tears welling up in them until it could no longer be seen. Emilia shook her head slightly, murmuring "No, no..." as though refusing something,

"Do you know? Subaru, do you— do you know my... my past?"

"Wait, let's calm down. Let's take a breath. This conversation's been going somewhere bad for a while now. So—"

"No, that's not— that's not it... I didn't mean it like that... I just... just—"

Emilia had slipped back into the same state she'd been in right after finishing the Trial inside the tomb. Flustered and confused, the words spilling from her began to fall apart into incoherence, her damp violet eyes reflecting Subaru right in front of her without actually seeing him. She reached out her fingers, clawing at the hem of Subaru's clothes to pull him closer,

"Everyone, they— that's why Mother, too, she— but no. It's not really like that. It really wasn't like that. Back then, I— really, I—"

"Emilia, what are you—"

Emilia clung to him, blurting out words in a desperate voice. Unable to understand the meaning behind her anguish, all Subaru could do was let some meaningless sincerity spill from his tongue and pray it would reach her. Before Subaru, helpless to do anything, a shadow suddenly moved. It was—

"—Ram."

Without answering the brief call of her name, Ram slid smoothly around behind Emilia. She reached a hand out and gently covered Emilia's mouth from behind — and as the silver-haired girl's eyes widened faintly in surprise, Ram said only, in a low voice, "Pardon me."

"...Ah."

The strength drained out of Emilia's body all at once, and she crumpled where she stood. Subaru reflexively reached out as she fell forward, catching her before she could hit the ground, and let out a quiet sigh of relief. Then he looked up at Ram, who had done this, and said,

"What did you do?"

"Something quick to calm her down. Are you going to be angry, Barusu?"

"I've got a thing or two to say about how forceful that was... but I think it was the best option. Sorry, that was a mess for you to deal with."

"It's strange for Barusu to apologize to me over Emilia-sama's own affairs. Since when did you take over as Emilia-sama's guardian from the Great Spirit-sama?"

"I didn't mean it like—"

He'd meant to say "that," but Subaru realized halfway through that his objection had no persuasive power at all. With Puck not showing himself for whatever reason at the moment, it was a fact that Subaru had been watching over Emilia with more tension than usual. Knowing, as he did now, how she'd worn herself down challenging the Trial only made that feeling stronger still. And on Emilia's side, too, with no reliable Great Spirit at her side, she seemed to genuinely be leaning on Subaru more than ever before. In any case—

"If you gotta play bodyguard, then I guess that's the end of this conversation."

Watching Subaru gently hold Emilia, whom Ram had forcibly put to sleep, Garfiel snorted and spat those words out. Subaru almost raised his voice at that attitude, but at the faint stirring of Emilia in his arms, he unconsciously shut his mouth. Losing his chance to object, there was nothing left to do but watch Garfiel turn his back and leave.

"I'll let today's Trial slide as a one-time exception. Startin' tomorrow, it's Emilia-sama takin' the Trial again. I ain't lettin' you take it — not while I have any say in it."

Baring his teeth as he said it, Garfiel walked out of Ryuzu's house. A small figure followed behind him — Ryuzu as well, saying,

"Sorry, Su-bou. But I feel the same way. The sooner the Sanctuary's freed, the better, sure... but goin' along with what Roz-bouya has in mind works out better for us too."

"Works out better how, exactly..."

"Even once we're freed from the Sanctuary, we'll still be under Roz-bouya's protection as his subjects, same as ever. So we'd rather avoid, far as we can, any exception that might sour his mood. ...Not that I'll go so far as to be selfish enough to ask you not to think ill of us for it."

There was an earnestness to Ryuzu's words, born of being a resident of the Sanctuary herself, and it left Subaru all the more without room to argue. Once Garfiel and Ryuzu, the two representing the Sanctuary's side, had left the room, what remained behind, as it happened, was the Roswaal Manor contingent plus one extra.

"So, what's the 'plus one,' Otto, make of this situation?"

"Could you please stop dragging me into this when I'm trying to just quietly wait it out because the mood and the outlook are both absolutely terrible? ...Still, if I'm being honest about my impression from listening in, I do think what Garfiel-san and the others are saying is the more reasonable argument."

Holding up a finger, Otto — put on the spot — nodded several times as he looked at Subaru kneeling there.

"There's the margrave's own aims to consider, and there's also Emilia-sama's standing as a Royal Selection candidate. Sure, if Natsuki-san takes the Trial in her place, it could still be counted as Emilia-sama's own achievement, in a sense... but setting aside any third party who hears about it later, would you actually get the understanding — that is, the support — of the people who are actually staying here in the Sanctuary right now?"

"...I get that much of the logic myself. However you look at it, having Emilia be the one to free the Sanctuary is a much bigger advantage for the situation. But..."

"You don't think Emilia-sama is capable of overcoming the Trial?"

Ram said it flatly, as though kicking through Subaru's hesitant stammering. Her way of putting it was so bluntly to-the-point it was almost refreshing, and it left Subaru, oddly enough, with a calmer expression as he said,

"From what I've seen, I think it'd be tough for her to produce results in a short amount of time. There's no point talking about it without knowing specifically what's in Emilia's past, but... this isn't a situation where we can afford to spend that much time on it either, right? You two get that much."

"At the very least, I'd like it settled within the three years before the Royal Selection is decided."

"That's way too generous a timeframe on its own."

It was probably meant as a joke, but she'd said it with such a straight face that Subaru made sure to rule out the possibility she was serious. And the one who nodded, catching the meaning behind Subaru's words, was—

"There's the burden on the people who evacuated here, and the Sanctuary's food situation to consider too. Looking at it long-term, I don't think it's realistic to keep supporting this many people."

"Yeah, that's about the size of it. Everyone's already stressed enough from a sudden evacuation like this — throw in food they can't be satisfied with, and people's complaints are gonna blow up fast. And there's no point for the Sanctuary folks either, keeping hostages around at the cost of lowering their own food standards. —At least, not all of them."

"You mean Garf might go so far as to thin out the villagers?"

It was Ram who asked, her tone somewhat sharper than before. Subaru raised his eyebrows, finding something unexpected in her reaction, but perhaps she herself sensed something off about her own attitude, because she smoothed her bangs with a finger as if to cover it up, and said,

"It's not something I want to think about much, but knowing Garf's personality, it's possible. When it comes down to the wire, he won't hesitate to act according to his own priorities."

"He's kind of like you in that regard. I agree with you. ...That's why I want to propose freeing the hostages from the Sanctuary before it comes to that."

Last time, the reason that proposal had gone through was a secretly struck condition — that Subaru would take the Trial. This time, with that no longer functioning, the same proposal could be expected to run into considerably tougher negotiations. Even so, Subaru believed they'd ultimately win the concession they needed. But—

"Anyway, if the Sanctuary folks are going to force the issue and insist on having Emilia take the Trial no matter what, I'd at least like to be allowed to clear away one worry that comes with it."

"...That's surprising. I thought Barusu would object more like an unreasonable child about this."

Ram offered that terse observation about Subaru's stance of trying to remove her worries over Emilia taking the Trial. Nodding along, Subaru prefaced his reply with "Nah," and said,

"It's frustrating, it really pisses me off, but... going along with Roswaal's plan is the most reasonable move. Yeah, that's right. If I could disregard Emilia getting hurt, then obviously it'd be better to just push through as things are."

"Knowing she'll get hurt, and still making her walk that path. You've turned into a demon, haven't you."

"Coming from a demon, that's saying something about how much I've changed myself. Just... no, never mind."

He started to say something, then shut his mouth and shook his head. Ram frowned at his evasive attitude, but had no intention of pressing him further for the rest of it. Subaru lifted Emilia, still in his arms, careful not to wake her. She was light. They say an unconscious person feels heavier than usual, but even so, her body was as light as a feather. Just how much was packed into this slender, small body, he wondered.

"Ram. Once I've laid Emilia down in her room, I want to talk with Roswaal. Can that happen?"

"Roswaal-sama is resting. He said not to let anyone in for a while..."

"I want to talk about the Trial. This is one thing where us lower-ranked folks talking amongst ourselves won't get anywhere. I need the brains' opinion."

Dangle the Trial as bait like this, and Roswaal would have no choice but to take down his "no visitors" sign and face Subaru. Subaru had already learned as much from the previous loop. Ram closed her eyes for a moment, as if weighing his words, then let out a weary sigh and said,

"I'll go ask Roswaal-sama's permission. Barusu, put Emilia-sama in bed... and don't try anything strange."

"The nerve, saying that to me in serious mode. I hadn't even thought of it, but now that you've brought it up I'm suddenly hyper-aware of just how soft Emilia-tan feels directly against me and my knees are starting to shake — what are you gonna do about that?"

"Otto, keep watch."

"Understood!"

Brushing off Subaru's nonsense without a second thought, Ram gave Otto a brief order and left the house. What remained were two men and one beautiful girl. In that situation, Otto, still holding his salute, gave Subaru a sidelong look and said,

"Come now, please put Emilia-sama in bed. I can help if you like."

"Before that — what's with your whole obedient attitude toward Ram..."

"Well, come on, Ram-san answers directly to the margrave, right? Thinking about it that way, currying favor with Ram-san looks a lot more promising for my relationship with the margrave than sticking close to Natsuki-san, who's glued to Emilia-sama heart and soul, heh heh."

"Can you not just voluntarily shift from your early 'capable merchant' impression into a petty little scoundrel? You're gonna get treated as a nuisance and buried in the dark one of these days."

Remarking on Otto's mercenary shift in attitude, Subaru carried Emilia into the bedroom with a sigh. He straightened the rumpled sheets a little, then slowly laid her down on them. Just then—

"Oh..."

Just as he pulled the sheets up to her shoulders and moved to step away, he noticed her fingertips had caught the hem of his clothes. Feeling a rush of tenderness at the fragile touch of those fingers, he gently pried them loose. Then, wrapping the freed fingertips in both his hands, he said,

"Wait for me, Emilia."

"—————"

"I'll sort things out soon. So you won't have to cry, won't have to suffer, not anymore. I promise."

With only that vow, Subaru released Emilia's hand. He stood, turned, and headed for the door of the house. —Toward Roswaal.

There was simply far too much he needed to draw out of that Warlock.

"—So, I see-e-e, I've got a grasp of the situation now."

Roswaal, who had been resting his hand against his chin and listening quietly to Subaru's long explanation, nodded as he said that. They were in a room of the house where Roswaal was recuperating, and the two of them were the only ones inside. With Roswaal lying in bed, only his upper body raised as he looked over at him, Subaru kept a stern gaze fixed on him and said,

"So that's the situation. If we're going to factor in the Sanctuary's side as much as possible, I think that's about the best place for us to land."

"We-ell, certainly, the moment Emilia-sama stepped inside the Sanctuary's barrier, Garfiel and the others' aim was as good as accomplished. Even for Emilia-sama herself to leave the Sanctuary, she needs to overcome the Trial. And if the hostages, kept as insurance, are just going to become a burden wasting food, letting them go makes sense too... is that it, then?"

"I think it's a logically sound argument. It's not that I'm abandoning the Sanctuary's problems or putting them off. It's just, purely, that anything not strictly necessary should be cut."

"What a clever way to put it. Don't you also harbor a concern like this — that if there's no prospect left of freeing the Sanctuary, or if Emilia-sama's heart gives out before she can endure the Trial, the hostages could become material for threatening Emilia-sama with. So let's just get rid of that worry ahead of time... something along those lines?"

Roswaal closed one eye, watching Subaru with his remaining yellow one. Taking in what he'd said, Subaru crossed his arms, nodded, and said,

"No, sorry, I hadn't thought that far. I mean, honestly, the fact that you can come up with a scary idea like that on the spot is a little off-putting."

"O-oh de-ear? Overthought it, or rather, thought too much of it? My apologies. Sti-ill, it's a way of thinking that's possible, isn't it."

Roswaal laughed as if to cover up his own excessive pessimism. Subaru gave that laugh a flat look, but honestly didn't think Garfiel would go so far as all that. He was someone with a stubborn, inflexible streak, someone it was hard to get through to at times — but he wasn't the type to touch anything unreasonable or unethical. That was Subaru's assessment of Garfiel, even after only a few days' acquaintance. In any case—

"So then? What is it you want me to do, Subaru-kun?"

"That whole thing I just went over — I'd like it if you were the one to bring it up, not me, if possible. This time around, it seems like... things would go badly if it comes from me."

"Now why-y would that be?"

"That Garfiel bastard seems to have taken a real dislike to me. Not that I'm giving up on convincing him, but I know it's gonna be tough to settle this in a day or two."

He remembered the sharpness in Garfiel's eyes when they'd parted. A gaze filled with hostility and loathing, as if glaring at the murderer of his own parent — Subaru had no idea what he'd done to deserve it. Had he committed some unforgivable act by Garfiel's own code, or made some slip of the tongue that flew straight in the face of his convictions? Either way—

"With him this worked up, he might just shoot down anything from me flat out, purely because it's my idea. And on top of that, Ryuzu-san seems likely to go along passively if Garfiel starts saying things like that. If I can already see that possibility, I'd like to eliminate it."

"And so-o that's where I come in, is it. Well, fine. I'll go have a word with Granny Ryuzu and Garfiel myself. Though I'm not sure how quickly it'll settle, seeing as Garfiel dislikes me too."

Taking up Subaru's proposal, Roswaal accepted the task with an easygoing air. Subaru let out a sigh of relief at the reassuring answer. For now, at least, one worry was on its way to being resolved. If there was a problem still remaining, it was—

"No-ow then. Is that the end of your business with me?"

"No, not yet. —The important thing, the actually important thing, I haven't even brought up yet."

The most important matter, the one he'd stepped into this room for, hadn't even begun yet. Roswaal tilted his head, brushing his long, flowing hair back over his shoulder with one hand, and closed one eye. Whether out of habit or not, Subaru straightened his spine under the gaze of that yellow eye. And then,

"The Trial in the tomb is showing Emilia her past. Do you have any idea what that 'past' is, the one she's seeing and suffering over?"

Subaru cut straight to the "past" Emilia had been so desperate to hide. At the question, Roswaal closed the yellow eye that had been open, drawing his chin in slightly as if in thought. A brief silence fell over the room, and in that soundless world Subaru waited, listening only to the sound of his own impatience scorching him from within. And then,

"Don't you think it's a cowardly thing to do, asking me this instead of asking Emilia-sama directly?"

"Call me underhanded, call me a coward, go ahead if you want. If I could, I'd want to hear everything about Emilia straight from Emilia's own mouth too. But—"

There was no way Subaru could bring himself to pry the details directly out of her, not when she'd been that close to tears, in that much pain, and still trying to hide it. Still, even as he felt a pang of conscience over exposing a secret she was trying so hard to keep hidden, he did not hesitate.

"Part of it's that I want to know about her, and part of it's that I need to know. If there's anything I can use to get there, I'll grab it, even if it's just a straw."

"I've been called a great many things by the people around me over the years, but being treated as a drowning man's straw — now that's a fir-irst for me. ...No-ow then."

After a small laugh, the expression suddenly vanished from Roswaal's face. He let out a short breath, then held it, reflecting Subaru in his two mismatched eyes. Bathing Subaru in that gaze of differing colors, Roswaal raised a hand to touch his forehead and said,

"That Emilia-sama is a Half-Elf. And that Half-Elves, because of the influence of the Witch of Envy, are held in deep, discriminatory contempt. Even yo-ou already know this much, su-urely?"

"...Yeah. I get that Emilia's been treated unfairly because of it too, from watching how she carries herself at the royal castle. I've met some of the disgusting types responsible too."

The vile image of the Witch Cult rose in his mind. Shaking his head once to drive the thought away, Subaru listened as Roswaal continued, "But sti-ill,"

"Certainly, it was Half-Elves who suffered the worst of the persecution... but it doesn't end there. By the way, Subaru-kun, have you ever happened to see an Elf in the royal capital?"

"An Elf? Not a half? ...No, I don't think I have, actually."

Hand on his chin, Subaru mobilized every memory he had and looked back over the world. But across all the worlds he'd repeated so many times, he had no memory of ever seeing an Elf — that well-known race, beautiful, marked by their slightly elongated ears. To Subaru's answer, Roswaal replied shortly, "Thought so-o," and continued,

"Half-Elves weren't the only ones to suffer harsh persecution across the world. The spearhead was aimed at Elves too, as the other half of what causes a Half-Elf to be born in the first place."

"...! No, come on, that's way too indiscriminate. And by that logic, then..."

"Humans should get wiped out too? Sorry to disappoint, but in this world, humans far outnumber Demi-Humans, and their nations are far bigger too. The result of that gap between Demi-Humans and the human race growing too wide is what leads into the Demi-Human War, but that's not relevant right now."

"So what about the Elves' persecuted past?"

The phrase "Demi-Human War," one he'd never heard before, had come up, and though intrigued, Subaru pulled the conversation back to its original track before it could drift from the main topic. Roswaal drew his chin in, and swaying his head side to side said, "In o-other wo-ords,"

"It's not just that Half-Elves can't show their faces in a place like the royal capital — the parent race, the Elves, also find it hard to show their faces in most places. That's a large part of why you've never seen an Elf's face in the capital, I'd imagine."

"That... makes sense. I get it, but where's this going?"

Subaru's question demanded a conclusion. Roswaal, as if drawing it out for effect, leaned his back against the pillow, tilting his face slightly upward as he felt its softness push back against him, and said,

"As an extension of the discrimination against Half-Elves, Elves too found themselves suffering persecution in various places. And if that's how it is, where exactly are Elves supposed to live, I wonder?"

"Elves, huh... the standard image would be them making settlements in forests or something. Living quietly deep in woods where people never set foot, hunting and all that."

"I don't know where you picked that up, but that's roughly the right idea. Driven out of towns, Elves came to live quietly deep in forests. —Elior Forest was one such home for Elves like that."

Subaru's shoulders trembled as he suddenly noticed the change in the tone of Roswaal's voice. He even felt the illusion of a chill, as if the temperature of the room had suddenly dropped. The reason for it was Roswaal before him — some indescribable power in his words made him feel that way. And the name of the place he'd spoken — Subaru had heard it before.

—Elior Forest. It was a name that had come up repeatedly during the discussions held at the Crusch estate, a stretch of land rich in mana ore, under Roswaal's own administration. And—

"It's frozen solid now, isn't it, so no one can even go near it..."

"The freezing of Elior Forest began, and spread, more than ninety years ago according to the records. A world of absolute zero, freezing everything solid, chilling every living creature to death. —And she lived alone, in that world, all by herself."

As if confirming Subaru's dread, Roswaal declared it with all his usual lightness stripped from his voice. Ninety years. Far too long a span of time. And a person who had been there through it. Given the direction Subaru had been steering the conversation, only one answer came to mind. And to a Subaru who had lost his words, Roswaal stated it plainly.

"—Deep within Elior Forest, the Elves who made their home there remain frozen solid, settlement and all, their time stopped to this very day."

"—————"

"All except for one — the sole Half-Elf girl who committed that transgression... you see."
