Arc 4 Chapter 3: "Reunion and Crossed Paths"

——Slowly, he holds his breath only for the instant he turns the doorknob.

Somehow, he'd had a feeling. When he calmed his mind like this and walked evenly through every corner of the mansion, there were times when, out of nowhere, a single door would snag insistently at his awareness. Leaving Emilia and the others in the living room, Subaru had been given a little time to himself and was wandering the mansion alone. He found it just as he stepped into the second-floor corridor of the main building.

The moment he arrived and touched the doorknob, that snag turned into certainty, and by the time he pushed the door open, there was no doubt left in him at all. Accepting only the fact that it was "simply there," he stepped into the room, and—

"Yo. Long time no see."

The forbidden archive, unchanged from before in every way, spread out before Subaru's eyes. The girl who ruled over that dim room was likewise unchanged in appearance, seated across from Subaru on the step of the stepladder she used as a chair, turning the pages of a book.

"—I thought the mansion seemed noisy. So you'd come back, I suppose."

The girl lifted her gaze briefly, catching Subaru in her eyes—Beatrice—and murmured it in a bored tone. She then dropped her eyes back to the book as if she'd already lost interest.

"If you're back, then Nee-cha ought to be back too, I suppose. That girl, and some other useless bug besides, I can feel are tagging along as well, I suppose."

"Puck's probably off recharging his batteries or something, hasn't shown his face. And I don't appreciate you treating Emilia-tan like an afterthought. I'll let the way you're talking about Otto slide for now, though—seeing as he's a bug and all."

"You're noisy, I suppose."

Beatrice scoffed at Subaru's pointless quip and gave a little motion beneath the skirts of her lavish dress, as if crossing her legs. Watching her do it, Subaru walked slowly across the forbidden archive toward her, choosing his words.

"Been a while since we've seen each other's faces, though. Last time was Pete—no, wait, that one doesn't count... so it was before we left for the royal capital, huh. That's, what, about ten days."

"Is that all it's been, I suppose? As far as Betty's concerned, while she's spending her time inside this room, she doesn't much care how much or little time passes outside, I suppose."

"That's a pretty vague way to put it. Also, look up from the book when you're talking to someone. I get that you're happy to see me again after ten days and you're worried you might blush, but still."

"Shall Betty make you cough blood out of that glib mouth of yours right this instant, and turn your face pale instead, I suppose?"

At the girl's words, her irritation plain, Subaru couldn't help the way his cheeks loosened into a grin. Whenever he traded words with this girl, the keeper of the forbidden archive, he couldn't help wanting to crack that composed, stubborn expression of hers. Crack jokes, tease her with his actions, needle her indignation further, and get thrown out for his trouble. He seemed to enjoy even that back-and-forth. Why it was only with her that he felt this way, he couldn't quite say.

"So you're back—does that mean I can figure the ruckus around here lately has settled down, I suppose?"

"You noticed, huh... well, of course you did. Emilia-tan and Ram both said they went looking for you, by the way. Make sure you apologize to them properly later."

"Betty? Apologize? Betty hasn't the faintest idea who she'd need to apologize to, or why she'd need to do any such thing, I suppose."

Beatrice gave her shapely nose a sniff, closed the book with a snap, and rose from the stepladder. She returned the thick-bound book to the shelf, then immediately stretched to pull out the one right beside it. Watching her struggle a bit with the height, Subaru couldn't help stepping up beside her.

"Here, this one, right?"

"...The one beside that, I suppose. If you're going to meddle needlessly, you ought to see it through properly, I suppose."

"Ungrateful little loli... hey, don't drop it. If this thing lands on the top of your foot, it's no joke how heavy it is."

The book he pulled out turned out to be surprisingly heavy when he tried to hold it one-handed. He passed it over carefully, with an air of nonchalance, and Beatrice took it, cradling it against her chest as she received it. He glanced at the title, but with nothing but the letter "I" repeated across it in some arrangement, he didn't yet have the linguistic skill to make it out.

"You're not going to say thanks, I suppose?"

"That's a classic tsundere or quiet-type line, but honestly, the moment you say something like that, it's basically the same as saying 'thank you' already, you know."

At the very least, the fact that she recognized this as a situation calling for thanks spoke to her decency. At Subaru's remark, Beatrice knit her brows in annoyance and turned her face away. Scratching his head at her stubborn attitude, Subaru went on,

"You don't ever have to thank me for anything, but make sure you properly thank the two of them. They were both worried about leaving you behind in the mansion."

"Worried for Betty's sake... Betty never asked for that..."

"Don't give me that lame 'I never asked for it' line. Most people in the world get born without asking for it, and get worried about without wanting to be. ...Though that second half is less about you and more about the people around you being good people."

Emilia's and Ram's fundamental decency went without saying. Emilia scored perfect marks on her usual conduct, and while Ram's usual conduct left something to be desired, what lay beneath it was another matter. But Beatrice didn't nod along to Subaru's attempt to win her over. If anything, she kept her face averted and bit her lip faintly.

"But in the end, you all left the mansion, didn't you, I suppose. ...Leaving Betty behind."

"What's that about? Don't tell me you didn't want to be left behind. You're the one who shut yourself away behind a door and kept your distance on your own—don't go acting like that's some kind of hassle now."

"It's door-crossing, I suppose. Don't go renaming it something willful and stupid on your own, I suppose. ...And besides, having such things assumed of Betty is an insult in itself, I suppose."

Refusing to accept Subaru's words, Beatrice, still staring off in entirely the wrong direction, held firm to her hardened stance. Sensing something unsteady in her manner, unlike her usual self, Subaru knit his brows, at a loss. With her already this out of sorts before he'd even broached the real subject, he couldn't very well bring it up now. And he had no miracle cure on hand to fix her mood, either.

"Fine, whatever. If you're going to be that stubborn, I'll just tell Emilia-tan that you were crying tears of gratitude and saying thank you over and over."

"Don't fabricate things, I suppose. Betty hasn't shed tears in a very long time now, I suppose."

"What, you one of those types who thinks crying is embarrassing? Keep that attitude up from childhood and you'll grow up not knowing how to express your feelings at all. You're supposed to bawl your eyes out without a second thought while you're still a kid."

"Coming from a man who bawled his eyes out on the lap of the woman he loves, that's rich, I suppose."

"Could you please forget that ever happened!?"

Subaru's dark history, which even Emilia—out of consideration, probably—hadn't dug back up. The time his emotional dam, strained past its limit under a tension so unhinged it was almost idiotic, holding back his true feelings, had finally been caught bursting at the seams. On Emilia's lap, the dam of his tears had broken, and Subaru, ever since being summoned to this other world, let loose everything he'd been holding in and cried and cried.

Remembering that moment made his face burn hot enough to catch fire. Though it was also, in equal measure, a memory that glowed warmly, unmistakably, somewhere deep in his chest.

Scratching his cheek and sealing those memories away, Subaru glanced at Beatrice. Still wearing her usual bored expression, she had settled back onto the stepladder and opened the book he'd handed her, beginning to slowly run her eyes over its contents. It was an attitude that plainly rejected further conversation, but if he backed off now, there'd be no point in his having come here at all.

"Anyway, crying or not crying aside... there's something I want to ask you. That okay?"

"Whether it's okay or not, saying it is your business, I suppose."

Her answer, accompanied by the faint sound of a page turning, implied, without saying so, "whether Betty answers is another matter entirely, I suppose"—hardly cooperative. Not cooperative, but Subaru decided he'd been given at least tentative permission. He gave a single nod of "alright" and tried to bring up the real reason he'd come here—

"—Come to think of it, considering you noticed the commotion outside, you sure had zero reaction to it."

But what came out of Subaru's mouth wasn't what he'd meant to say—it was more like a rehash of the conversation they'd just had. At his words, Beatrice, who had until then kept her eyes on the book, lifted her gaze. Feeling himself reflected in her clear, piercing eyes, Subaru swallowed a small breath.

"I—it was pretty rough out there while you were pretending not to notice, you know? Some shady bunch surrounded the mansion..."

"Stop it, I suppose."

"If I hadn't managed to drag reinforcements back from the royal capital, who knows what would've happened by now. And getting back here wasn't exactly smooth sailing for me either..."

"Stop, I suppose."

"It's a tale of tears to hear and tears to tell, crossing a path of crying faces and killer bees—"

A dry crack rang out, and Subaru's rapid-fire speech was forcibly cut short. Looking over, the source of the sound was in Beatrice's hand—the book she'd had open, slammed shut with all her strength. Reading her intent, Subaru made an awkward face, but the girl only leveled a sharp, merciless glare at him.

"Get to the point already, I suppose. —You coward."

"...Yeah."

No words of denial came to him. Beatrice was right. She'd seen straight through the weakness in him that wanted to run. The weakness that, fearing the answer to the question he needed to ask, tried to take the long way around it.

"Are you..."

He held his breath, closed his eyes, and listened to his own heartbeat. Behind his closed eyelids, he could see her—the beloved girl, smiling.

"Do you remember Rem?"

—The question became sound, and shattered against a reality he could not take back.

In the world after the Subjugation of the White Whale, Subaru had spoken with Beatrice in the forbidden archive exactly once.

He'd exchanged words with her intending to get her to evacuate, away from the Witch Cult, but she'd refused, and in the end, the girl had been left alone in the mansion. That was the exchange they'd had. He didn't remember every detail of what had passed between them clearly. But looking back, there was one fact he'd noticed—one he mustn't overlook.

Back then, Beatrice had, just once, said something showing concern for Rem, who should have been returning with Subaru.

At that point—by the time he'd made it back to the mansion—the letter of appointment had already gone blank. Which meant it had been after the moment Rem was attacked by an Archbishop of Sin. And yet, despite that, Beatrice had spoken of Rem—which meant—

"Answer me. You remember Rem, who was in this mansion, right?"

Because he wanted her to remember, the ending of Subaru's sentence shifted—from "you must remember" to something closer to a demand. He couldn't deny that part of it came from a certainty he'd gained amid resurfacing memories, and part of it was bravado, meant to spur on a heart threatening to sink into weakness.

Beatrice looked at Subaru quietly. No emotion dwelt in those eyes; he couldn't read what she was thinking at all. Normally she was a girl whose emotions were so easy to read, and yet at this one moment, he couldn't read her at all. That helplessness, that sense of time itself grinding to a halt, scorched at Subaru's impatient heart.

"Hey..."

Why wouldn't she say anything? Whether she knew or didn't know, however she answered, it shouldn't have been a difficult question. Of course, there was only one answer he wanted. He was hoping Beatrice knew Rem—that she'd laugh off the absurdity of his question.

Memories devoured, a name swallowed, erased from the world—it was too ridiculous a thing to be true. If only she could taste that same absurdity as Subaru had, and feel the same righteous anger at the unfairness of the world. Or better yet, if she'd think to search together with him for the truth behind whatever it was that only the two of them shared in common.

So, please, tell him she knew. Like Emilia, like Crusch, like Wilhelm, like so many others—please, don't tell him she'd forgotten Rem, forgotten that girl too.

He wanted to hear the answer. He didn't want to hear the answer. His impatient heart, his contradictory feelings, twisted against each other. And then, to Subaru, his heart shaking violently with hesitation, Beatrice said—

"—Betty doesn't want to answer, I suppose."

She turned her gaze, which had been fixed steadily on Subaru, away, and gave an answer that was neither YES nor NO. A breath escaped Subaru as "hah," and for a moment his thoughts stopped. Then he waved his hand in a panic.

"W-wait. What do you mean, you don't want to answer? That question only has one of two possible answers—YES or NO, right?"

"Yes, no—Betty doesn't even understand what those mean, I suppose. And besides, Betty's answer is exactly that, precisely that: doesn't want to answer, I suppose."

"That's not an answer, I'm telling you!"

Swinging his arm down from above, Subaru took a hard step forward, right up to Beatrice. The girl seated on the stepladder didn't even glance at his violent motion, her lips pressed firmly shut. At that stubborn attitude, the heart being scorched by his impatience burst into flame. There was no stopping it now.

"That's not the kind of thing I want to hear from you!"

"Why should Betty have to say the words you want to hear, whatever they may be? ...Betty would rather you not make such a fuss, I suppose. The archive will get disturbed, I suppose."

"You...!"

Surging forward, Subaru closed in on Beatrice. He meant to force her face, still turned resolutely away from him, to look his way. To make her look him in the eye and demand to know why she'd say something so heartless. That was his intent. And yet—

"——"

The instant he reached to touch her, Beatrice looked at Subaru. And the moment he saw the wave of emotion trembling in her eyes, Subaru's hand simply stopped moving. Because it looked, for all the world, as if she were—

"That question of yours right now is one you're asking about someone devoured by 'Gluttony,' I suppose."

"—! So you really are..."

"Anyone who knows the Authority of Gluttony could work that much out, I suppose. Roswaal, Nee-cha, even Shaula—they all know it, I suppose."

"Ros—!?"

An unexpected name burst out, and Subaru's throat caught despite himself. Roswaal knew about the Authority of Gluttony—meaning there was a possibility he, too, remembered Rem. No, before even that—

"How much do you people actually know about the Witch Cult? Roswaal must have known that if it got out that Emilia was a half-elf, the Witch Cult would move. And yet if I hadn't done something, this mansion, the village, none of it would've held. What's going on?"

"............"

"Rem and Crusch-san both said there was no way he hadn't taken any countermeasures. But to me, it sure looks like nothing was done at all. Because if that weren't the case, things wouldn't have gotten so awful..."

"Betty cannot fathom how far Roswaal's thinking went, I suppose. Only... Betty doesn't think it's true that Roswaal did nothing at all, I suppose."

At Beatrice's claim, Subaru knit his brows, trying to find some place, somewhere in the midst of his battle with Petelgeuse, where Roswaal's scheming had made itself felt. But no matter how deeply he searched his memory, no matter how he turned it over, no such moment came to mind.

"That's a misunderstanding. Or you're giving him too much credit. If Roswaal had actually done something, there's no way I'd have had to go through all that..."

"If you don't understand it, then surely no one does, I suppose."

With a note of disappointment in her sigh, Beatrice showed her dismay at Subaru's failure to understand. Feeling a flicker of displeasure at her attitude, Subaru also realized the conversation had begun to drift from where it needed to be.

"Wait, forget that—the Witch Cult. If you say you know about the Witch Cult, then you're going to tell me everything you know, start to finish. The Archbishops of Sin, 'Gluttony,' all of it. I've got a mountain of things I want to ask... including about this."

One question after another surfaced the moment he realized Beatrice was in a position to know. Questions Puck had dodged, that he'd never managed to get answered. And now, hope rose in him that she might be the one to answer them after all. He reached into his coat and pulled out a black-bound book. Its cover, and the inside as well, faintly stained with dried blood—a spoil of war from his battle days earlier against a formidable foe.

"I know this thing is tied up in something deep in the Witch Cult. I can't read what's inside, but you, as the keeper of the forbidden archive, might be able to—"

"—A Gospel."

At the sight of the book in Subaru's hands, Beatrice's eyes went wide. Her pink lips trembled faintly as she stared fixedly at the Gospel Subaru held. Her eyes ran over the illegible script inscribed on its cover, her expression one of disbelief.

"Why, of all people, do you have that..."

"It's not like I really wanted it bad enough to say I 'took' it. I told you already—the Witch Cult surrounded the mansion, causing trouble. I took it off their ringleader. Its owner is... not anywhere in this world anymore."

"Took it off... wait, does that mean..."

Beatrice's voice trembled, and she reached out a hand toward the Gospel in Subaru's grip. Puzzled by how finely her fingertips trembled, Subaru hesitantly handed the Gospel over to her. Taking it, she traced the letters on the cover with her fingertips, confirming them.

"This one's owner... you're saying he died, I suppose?"

"...Yeah. He died. Bitten by the Wheel of Fortune... I killed him."

Looking at the outcome, it wasn't as though Subaru had struck the killing blow against Petelgeuse with his own hands. But the circumstances that led to it, and the resolve that it would come to that, had all been an unavoidable reality, an extension of Subaru's own actions. Subaru had tried to kill Petelgeuse. His soul had understood that unless he took that life, there was absolutely no way to settle things between them, man against man.

So there was no doubting that Subaru had held the "intent" to kill Petelgeuse. He wouldn't claim there'd been no hesitation, nor that there'd been no regret afterward, once his hands were dirtied. He hadn't put up a brave front for anyone else's sake, so he wasn't about to lie to himself about it, at least. That he had killed, and that he'd nearly been killed—he wouldn't forget either. He wasn't going to say anything sentimental, either, like living on carrying the burden of the life that man had taken. Petelgeuse was a being who deserved to die, and Subaru, too, had believed that when he killed him. That was all there was to it.

And yet, to Subaru's word, weighted with everything he felt, there was no reaction from Beatrice. She only murmured a quiet "I see..." and, still with her eyes down on the Gospel,

"Did you leave Betty behind too, Juice...?"

"—? Who now?"

"You don't need to know, I suppose. More importantly—if you're the one who killed 'Sloth,' then what became of the Witch Factor, I suppose?"

"Witch... Factor...?"

At Beatrice's question, Subaru furrowed his brow and tilted his head. At Subaru's manner, Beatrice looked at him dubiously, narrowing her eyes as if trying to dig some emotion out of his expression. But Subaru had no idea why she'd be looking at him like that. Irritation rode along with his click of the tongue.

"Don't go flashing jargon at someone who doesn't know a thing, Ms. Know-It-All. What is it, this Witch Factor thing? Nothing about it sounds good."

"You don't know...? Really, truly? Then what in the world were you killing 'Sloth' for, I suppose? Betty doesn't understand at all, I suppose."

"I was just batting away sparks that landed on me! What are you even trying to say!"

Subaru, at the end of his patience with a conversation that refused to mesh, grew louder, while, in contrast, Beatrice's manner drew closer and closer to silence. She pressed the back of her hand to her lips as if deep in thought, staring fixedly at only the cover of the book she held, not looking inside,

"This, Betty doesn't know. ...It's beyond what Betty is able to judge, I suppose."

"What are you concluding all on your own—whoa!"

Shaking her head, Beatrice abruptly hurled the Gospel toward the bewildered Subaru. He caught the thrown book in a fluster and let out a breath of relief.

"What're you suddenly doing that for! I'm not gonna say it's a dangerous book, but it's still a creepy one. Handle it with more care!"

"—It's right that you hold onto it, I suppose. Whatever the Witch Factor chooses, or doesn't choose. Either way, a choice will be forced upon you eventually, I suppose. If that thing can serve you even a little as material for that judgment when the time comes, then Juice, too, might rest easier, I suppose."

"What's a drink got to do with resting easy! What are you trying to—!"

Subaru wrung out his words, clinging to a conversation that made no sense whatsoever. But before he could finish speaking, he felt something wrong behind him. —It was, he could tell instinctively, the sound of space itself being warped by some supernatural force. Why he could sense that, he had no idea, but—

"Are you trying to throw me out? I haven't even gotten to ask anything yet... you think I'm actually going to back off now!?"

"Why should Betty have to tell you the things you want to hear, in the words you want to hear them in? Betty would rather you stop... being so selfish, so arrogant, I suppose."

"Arro—! —Just tell me what you know! I'm not asking for more than that! So, your—"

"—Betty is..."

Being pulled back by the hair—not in the figurative sense, but by a literal, physical force trying to drag Subaru's body backward. Space warped—and turning only his head to look back, he saw that the door, which should have been closed just a moment ago, now stood open, a pitch-black space threatening to swallow him whole.

There was no wind blowing, nor was anything pulling at his arms or legs. And yet an indescribable pressure bore down on his entire body from the front, while an invisible gravity from behind seemed to wrap around his limbs and drag him away. —The full, merciless force of "door-crossing."

"Bea-ko... Beatrice!"

"It's your own body, your own heart, that's trying to leave, I suppose."

"What are you—"

"A heart that doesn't want to hear the answer, a weakness that wants to look away from reality, a selfishness that doesn't want to face your own sins—those are what's pulling your body away from this archive, I suppose."

He wanted to shout back that that was ridiculous, but no voice came. That he'd been afraid of the answer, that he was scared of a reality that might come back, that he hadn't atoned for the sin of failing to save everyone—all of it was true, all of it he'd known. And yet, still,

"I'm—"

"Betty isn't... some convenient tool for you, I suppose."

"—!?"

"Not something that'll let you hear the things you want to hear, whenever you want to hear them, in the words you want, the way you want... Betty isn't some convenient thing like that, I suppose."

At Beatrice's words, wrung out from somewhere deep inside her, Subaru couldn't find a second thing to say. It wasn't that she'd struck a nerve exactly—it was more the shock of being struck from a direction he hadn't expected at all. And the hollow that opened up in him let his resisting body slip,

"Oh, sh—!"

And just like that, Subaru's body was pulled through the door behind him, as if sucked in. If he went all the way through, he'd be thrown out of the forbidden archive entirely. Grabbing the edge of the door at the very last second, Subaru managed to hold his ground, half-flung as he was. He let out a breath, gritted his teeth, and looked ahead—and there, in front of him, was the girl, her face full of sorrow.

"You should ask Roswaal what you want to know, I suppose. —Neither Nee-cha nor Betty will tell you anything, I suppose."

"...Why do you look like you're about to cry?"

At Subaru's last question, Beatrice offered no answer, only lowering her eyes. And then the fingers she extended found Subaru's, gripping the edge of the door—and pried them loose.

Sucked in. Flung out. Shut out. From the door, from the forbidden archive—from the heart of the girl called Beatrice.

"——"

Stumbling backward, he was spat out into the hallway as if ejected from the door itself. Before his eyes, the door that had thrown him out slammed shut violently. Seeing that, Subaru immediately reached for the door, but it was already too late.

"That drill-loli..."

Beyond the open door was not the forbidden archive, but an unused guest room. He turned his gaze around the mansion, but he couldn't feel even a trace of that sixth sense connecting him to the archive. —He wasn't going to see her again today. That certainty alone settled, with a quiet thud, into the depths of Subaru's chest.

Having asked nothing of what he wanted to ask, learned nothing of what he wanted to know, Subaru had been jerked around by the girl's suggestive words and cast out empty-handed.

"What is with her. If you know something, just tell me already, you stingy brat. Sulking and shutting yourself away like that—since when are you some kind of Natsuki family firstborn son?"

Kicking at the door that, until just moments ago, had connected to the forbidden archive, Subaru let out a long breath. He shook his head, trying to shake off the image floating up in his mind—but the look on Beatrice's face, right at the very end, right as they parted, wouldn't shake loose. Because in that moment, she had, without question,

"Don't go making a face like you're about to cry and shutting yourself away all alone. Idiot."

If he let himself believe that it was he who had put that look on her face, then he'd no longer be able to blame her for all the things he hadn't managed to do right.

  • "ジュース" (Juice) is left as a phonetic rendering — Beatrice appears to be recalling a name/nickname of a former contractor, but the referent is deliberately left ambiguous in the source text itself; not resolved here.
  • The pun on 「涙」/"聞くも涙語るも涙" and "泣きっ面にクインビー" (a play on "泣きっ面に蜂," "a bee to a crying face," i.e. "when it rains it pours," substituting the White Whale-hunt's Queen Bee/Killer Bee) is adapted rather than translated literally — rendered as "a tale of tears to hear and tears to tell, crossing a path of crying faces and killer bees" to preserve the idiom-based joke and its connection to the earlier White Whale arc.
  • 「傲慢」("arrogance/Pride") in Beatrice's line and Subaru's interrupted echo ("Arro—!") is meant to evoke the Sin-name resonance (cf. "Gluttony," "Sloth"); kept literal since no clean pun equivalent exists in English.
