Arc 2 Chapter 16: "The Second Week's Bewilderment"

Let it be declared, as many times as necessary: Natsuki Subaru is a one-woman man, and that woman is Emilia.

Since coming to this other world, he's had the chance to encounter one gorgeous face after another—the kind he'd never have run into back in his original world—but she stands head and shoulders above them all.

Sure, her looks are part of it, but more simply than that, it's the weakness of being in love. Under those conditions, her combat power just keeps climbing and climbing.

Which means that no matter how beautiful the rival, there's no way Subaru's heart would ever stray from Emilia. No way, no how, not a chance.

"So this perfectly made bed doesn't mean anything more than me wanting to sleep comfortably, got it?"

He jabbed a finger sharply at the bed, offering the fired-up excuse to no one in particular.

Fresh out of the bath, Subaru returned to his room to find, laid out before him, a bed made with every minute of time he'd had since getting back.

He'd left the laundry basket sitting untouched to get it done—a real undertaking that had him sweating despite just having bathed. It had been a while since he'd done a job he was this satisfied with.

"No deep meaning. No deep meaning at all. Empty the mind, empty the mind. One Emilia-tan, two Emilia-tans, three Emilia-tans... is this heaven?!"

"You're being noisy, Barusu. It's nighttime already, so keep it down."

"Whoaaaway!!"

He gave a great start and staggered back against the wall. At the room's entrance, he saw Ram standing there, having opened the door without a sound. She stepped inside, then knocked on the door—

"You do this right after I tell you to be quiet? Hopeless."

"What is with your personal rules?! Listening to you makes my sense of common sense wobble, it's nerve-wracking! What exactly do you want from me, and how?!"

Ram gave a small "hmph" through her nose at Subaru's shouting. Struck by contempt too pure to even put into words, Subaru had nothing left to say—hands up in surrender.

Striding briskly right past him, Ram made for the bed—no, not the bed, but the small wooden desk just in front of it.

It was ostensibly provided in every room as a space for writing and the like, but for Subaru, who couldn't read or write in this world, it was a useless fixture. Of course, he could always write in Japanese.

"Worst case, maybe I ought to do that anyway. Even if it doesn't carry over, writing things down would at least make them clearer to remember..."

"What are you muttering about? Barusu, come here."

At her curt tone, like she was training a dog, a vein bulged on Subaru's forehead. But getting swept into her pace here would only work against him. If anything, Subaru's true talent lay in dragging others into his pace.

Whatever surprising remarks might come flying, he steeled himself, forging an unshakable heart of steel.

With the resolve of a man marching to battle, he stepped up before Ram, who stood blocking his path.

"So? What kind of nonsense have you brought me this time?"

"What are you talking about? I'm telling you to hurry up and sit down so I can teach you to read and write."

"First I'm hearing of it?!"

Steel, instantly melted.

He registered his hardened heart crumbling to mush in an instant, then, unable to hide his shock, glanced at the desk—where he found a notebook of blank white pages, a quill pen, and a book with a reddish-brown spine, and drew in a breath.

Not a joke, not a prank, not harassment—she genuinely, sincerely intended to teach him to read and write.

"But why, all of a sudden..."

"Barusu, you can't read or write, can you. I could tell from watching you work today. So I'm going to teach you. If you can't read or write, you can't take notes for shopping, and you can't leave a written message for someone."

To Subaru's bewildered question, Ram gave an entirely reasonable answer.

Subaru's mouth flapped open and shut like a fish before he could stop it. Paying his reaction no mind, Ram indicated the red-spined book.

"First, we'll start with a simple collection of children's fairy tales. From now on, either Ram or Rem will be with you every night, so you will study."

It was a generous offer, to be sure, but Subaru's honest reaction was mostly confusion.

This development was, like the bath earlier, a situation that had never happened in the previous loop. And by Subaru's own reckoning, compared to, say, day four of the previous loop, the distance between him and the twins was still supposed to be wide open.

And yet, despite that, why was this well-meaning study session happening now?

"Isn't it obvious? —It's so I can slack off."

"Man, you really don't waver, do you."

"Of course not. The more Barusu can do, the less work there is for Ram. And if there's less work for Ram, then naturally there's less for Rem too. It's all upside."

"Meanwhile I'm the one getting buried in work instead?!"

"...?"

His retort earned him nothing but a puzzled tilt of the head.

Faced with a reaction like she genuinely couldn't grasp the point of what he'd said, he no longer had the energy to argue back.

Even as he was left utterly exasperated, it was also true that he was happy.

"Okay, all right, got it. Let's do this studying thing, shall we~?"

"In your case, Barusu, your conversational grammar is fine, so it shouldn't be too difficult. There's no fault in the sense you were born with."

"That was an insult dressed up as encouragement, wasn't it?"

Even as he said it, he sat down at the desk and picked up the quill, ready to go. He'd never had a chance to even touch one before, but the quill was light, and when he ran it across the notebook, it drew surprisingly elegant strokes.

It was the momentous first stroke of his life in another world. Facing the notebook's cover, Subaru swept the pen in a bold flourish—

"Natsuki Subaru, present and accounted for... there."

"This is sudden, but we don't have time for you to be doodling pictures like that. We have an early start tomorrow too—your time is limited."

"No, this is actually my native language... Yeah, I guess it doesn't come across, huh."

Given that speech got translated, he'd half hoped writing might work the same way—but he wasn't blessed with such a convenient outcome.

From this side's perspective, just as the letters of this world looked like hieroglyphics to him, Japanese probably just looked like some kind of fussy scrawl to them.

Even Subaru's beloved knowledge of four-character idioms couldn't work its magic here.

"But then again, that means I could write down whatever lewd fantasies I want and nobody would notice. That situation could... come in handy, libido-wise!"

"We'll start with the basic I-script first. Ro-script and Ha-script come after you've mastered I-script."

"There are three whole kinds? My spirit's already breaking just hearing that."

Facing a new language, his heart already felt on the verge of breaking. He could sympathize a little with foreigners trying to learn Japanese. With three systems—hiragana, katakana, and kanji—no wonder they'd despair at the height of the mountain they had to climb.

"We'll move on to the fairy tales once you've got the I-script down. Study time is... one o'clock's the limit, I'd say. There's tomorrow too. And Ram's sleepy as well."

"That way you let your true feelings peek out at the end there—I don't hate it, senpai."

"Ram likes that about herself too."

There was no hesitation in her comeback, making it hard to tell whether it was sincere or a joke. Sensing it was quite likely the former, Subaru's lessons in mastering the written word began.

When learning a new language, the first thing to do is grasp the types of characters used. Just as hiragana has what's called the fifty sounds, every language starts with a set of basic characters, and everything else is spoken through their combinations.

And so, mastering a language begins with copying out the letters, but—

"This is more painful than I imagined... reminds me of when I was studying cursive."

He copied out the basic characters Ram had written for him, filling roughly four hundred characters onto a single page. It was the kind of repetition that threatened to induce a gestalt collapse, but he resolved that this plodding effort was exactly what was needed, and threw himself into it.

Honestly, his eyelids were heavy with fatigue and sleepiness, but with Ram sitting there keeping him company, nodding off was out of the question. In the first place, the fact that she was being this friendly from the very first day of his second time around was precious. He couldn't afford to let the chance slip by.

"I mean, you say it's just so you can slack off, but... I was happy about it anyway."

Fighting down his embarrassment, he conveyed his honest feelings to Ram behind him.

Amid the faint scratch of the quill across the notebook, in between the repeated, repeated task of lining up the same characters over and over, Subaru found himself looking back on the previous loop's four days.

Come to think of it, those had been days where he'd spent every spare moment chasing after Emilia—but the person he'd actually spent the most time with had probably been Ram.

All the chores around the manor—cooking, laundry, cleaning—teaching someone as green as Subaru at all of it must have been a real trial. All the more so since, for her, it hadn't just been teaching—she'd had to keep up her normal duties on top of it.

Naturally, some of that burden must have fallen on Rem too. So, over the previous loop's four days, he hadn't actually spent that much time with her directly. Ram had said that capable Rem had been covering that share of the work, but having placed an indirect burden on her weighed on Subaru all the same.

"Honestly, I didn't think I was all that well-liked."

They were busy enough as it was, and then some useless newbie shows up under them. They had to do their own work while carving out time to teach him all sorts of things on top of it.

For someone with as little experience dealing with people as Subaru, just thinking about it was painful enough. And being thought of that way was a feeling he was all too familiar with.

So the fact that he wasn't being rejected, here in the present, made Subaru happy.

"So, I figure I'll probably keep being a bother going forward, but... thanks for putting up with me."

The chair creaked as he turned just his head back, speaking to Ram, who watched silently.

The honest feeling had welled up from somewhere deep inside him. In response, Ram, quietly—

"Zzzz."

—was fast asleep, breathing softly and adorably, tucked into the beautifully made bed.

Shoes off, apron removed, headdress set neatly beside the bed, blanket pulled up to her shoulders—an impeccably proper sleeping arrangement.

With a snap, the quill pen broke.

Following a sudden urge, Subaru opened his mouth wide in a huge yawn.

The drowsiness he'd failed to stifle welled up as tears at the corners of his eyes; he rubbed them away roughly and stretched his back straight.

The evening sky was dyed orange with the setting sun's parting gift, and the drifting clouds moved lazily along, as if consoling the day for its end.

Seeing them off, he rolled each of his joints in turn, checking on the state of his body. The effects of the hard labor were still there, sure, but compared to before, it was a bit more bearable.

"Still, my body's toughness resets no matter what... So I guess I've picked up a bit of a knack for moving without wearing myself out so much."

Not physical conditioning, but pure familiarity with the work improving his efficiency—that, he guessed, was what was leading to the slight reduction in fatigue.

If there was a chance he'd keep looping from here on, he couldn't count on his body getting physically stronger through repetition. All he could really do was preview events ahead of time and get better at chores.

He'd have to accept that much just to keep going. After all—

"Sorry to keep you waiting, Subaru-kun. —Are you all right?"

"Hm, oh, yeah, I'm fine, fine. You done shopping too, Rem-rin?"

"Yes, without a hitch. You looked like you had quite a time of it, Subaru-kun."

The blue-haired girl offering him that sympathy, tilting her head slightly with a shopping bag in hand, was Rem. In her usual maid's attire, she held down her wind-tossed hair and looked at Subaru, her expression easing just the barest amount.

—looked, that is, at Subaru, whose butler outfit was thoroughly filthy with mud, dust, snot, and tears.

"You were quite popular."

"I've had this thing where kids take to me for some reason ever since I was little. Guess it's probably that, huh—some kind of irrepressible maternal instinct in me that just keeps drawing in the hearts of children."

"Children are like animals in that they rank people by character. They can tell instinctively whether someone's safe to look down on."

"That's not a compliment, is it?!"

Rem said nothing in response to Subaru, who put a hand to his chin to voice his displeasure.

Having been on the receiving end of a fairly biting comment, Subaru privately nodded to himself—yeah, that's the sisters for you.

Blunt Ram, and roundabout Rem. Getting along with either of them required real mental toughness. Of course, without physical toughness too, the work itself would never get done in the first place.

With a wry smile, Subaru rolled his hips and turned his head, looking around.

Behind him, from the square, the children's shrieks of delight still echoed faintly. Just a little while ago, Subaru himself had been part of that circle, locked in a desperate struggle against blows and slobber. Every last one of them merciless, it had been a lone, friendless battle.

Right now, Subaru and Rem were in the village nearest the manor.

For all his eccentricity, Roswaal held the rank of margrave, and was of course a proper noble who held several tracts of land as his domain.

This village nearest the manor was no exception, and the people living there naturally knew the two maids' faces as a matter of course. Especially since the two maids had frequent occasion to come shopping, the odds of being called out to whenever they passed by were considerable.

Meanwhile, it seemed Subaru himself had somehow already become known there, at least in name. Despite this being his first time actually setting foot in the village, being welcomed so warmly left him feeling itchy with embarrassment, but happy all the same.

That said—

"Seriously, what's with those brats and how familiar they get... Can't they tell I've got this hard-boiled-dick vibe going on, the kind that'll burn you if you touch it?"

Hard-boiled and adultic—Hard-boiled-dick, for short.

Muttering the kind of title he'd love to murmur in a low voice while puffing on a cigarette, he grumbled it out loud. Walking ahead of him, Rem shot him a sidelong glance without turning her head—

"First it's maternal instinct, now you're playing at being an adult. You're a busy man, Subaru-kun."

"If I were actually busy I wouldn't have ended up in a mess like this. Damn it, I should've just tagged along on your shopping trip after all, Rem-rin."

Even if he had tagged along to the actual shopping, Subaru—who couldn't tell one ingredient from another, let alone their names—would have been useless. So rather than make her fuss over him, he'd hung back instead, only to be discovered and dragged off by the village children, which he hadn't seen coming at all.

"On the whole I've got no complaints, but... something doesn't sit right."

"About what?"

"Nah, never mind. Honestly, it's a relief I don't have to talk to you while braced for an attack, Rem-rin. With Ram-chi, you never know what angle the contempt's gonna come from."

"Onee-sama is wonderful, isn't she."

The conversation wasn't quite lining up.

Rem seemed a touch smug, proud of her sister, and there was no sign of any hidden edge to it, so he guessed it was sincere. But—

"Honestly, isn't Ram-chi's personality kind of a liability? Seems like it'd cause friction pretty often."

"Not as much of one as you, Subaru-kun—immature as you are, both as a servant and as a person."

"Y-e-a-h, f-i-g-u-r-e-d."

To see another's folly is to correct one's own.

Coming from Subaru, who currently couldn't contribute even half of what Ram did, any criticism of Ram's competence or character was bound to be laughed off. All the more so when the one on the receiving end was an even more refined version of Ram herself.

Being less direct about it only made her irony cut several degrees sharper than Ram's.

Trailing behind Rem as she walked ahead, Subaru scratched his head, troubled.

—In the previous loop, hadn't he gone shopping in the village with Ram instead?

"Oh, that reminds me, Subaru-kun—how's your studying coming along?"

"'Coming along steadily'—that's what I'd like to say, but it's not that easy. Guess anything worth doing needs time to grow slowly. Just like love!"

"Let's hope it doesn't wither partway through."

"That comment right there is exactly the kind that makes love wither, Rem-rin!"

He shouted it out, and Rem's expression eased into the faintest smile; Subaru laughed too.

—It had been four days since Ram first offered her nightly private lessons. The plan had been for them to take turns teaching Subaru his letters, but in all that time, Rem had never once taken a turn as instructor.

Given the circumstances, it was probably that Rem simply hadn't been in a position to leave a gap in her duties—but it seemed to weigh on her all the same.

Faced with her uncharacteristically halting reaction, Subaru kept right on laughing.

"D-o-n-'-t wor-ry a-bout it. It's not like I'm being neglected, and I've got no complaints about Ram-chi as an instructor, y'know? I mean, sure, having her just conk out on the bed mid-lesson definitely kills my motivation, but—"

"Onee-sama is doing that on purpose, to fire up your motivation, Subaru-kun."

"What kind of positive thinking is that, out-positive-ing even me? Your devotion to your sister is no joke—that's seriously demon-tier."

"Demon...tier...?"

Rem tilted her head at this latest addition to Subaru's current pet phrases.

"It's the demon version of 'god-tier.' Demon-tier. Kinda has a ring to it, don't it?"

"Do you like demons?"

"Maybe more than gods. I mean, gods basically never do anything for you, but demons apparently laugh along with you when you talk about your hopes for the future."

Apparently things get especially lively when you talk about next year.

Picturing a scene of himself with his arms slung around a red oni and a blue oni, all of them roaring with laughter together, Subaru suddenly noticed that Rem's expression had settled into an unmistakable smile.

"Oh—"

He'd seen her offer a faint smile a few times before, but this was the first time she'd shown him an expression he could properly call a smile.

He had no idea what had struck a chord in her, but Subaru snapped his fingers and said—

"That smile of yours rivals a million-volt night view."

"I'm telling Emilia-sama."

"That wasn't me hitting on you!"

He straightened up and thrust both hands out, striking a solemn pose of begging forgiveness.

Rem, glancing at Subaru striking that pose, raised her eyebrows slightly.

"What happened to your hand?"

"Hm? Oh, some mutt the brats were playing with went and chomped down on me hard."

There was a clear bite mark on his left hand, with a faint trickle of blood that had already stopped. He recalled going through the same thing last time, so this world was being oddly conscientious about retracing the small details.

Incidentally, the back of his butler's uniform had been thoroughly smeared with snot, though—same as last time—he wouldn't notice until he got back to the manor.

"Should I heal that for you?"

"Huh? What, you can use healing magic too, Rem-rin?"

"Nothing elaborate, but enough for basic first aid. Or would you rather have Emilia-sama do it?"

"Mm, that's an appealing offer, I can't deny it. B-u-t... I'll pass."

Gazing at the canine-tooth marks on the back of his left hand, Subaru turned down the offer.

That was because, in a sense, he'd decided the scar made a convenient marker. After all, the moment this loop had begun, the thing that had told him so most plainly had been the disappearance of his scars.

Whether or not a wound was present made for a useful gauge. If he hadn't happened to get bitten by the dog, he'd have had to cut himself with some knife or quill pen on purpose.

"Heh, I grew out of wanting a sword scar on my cheek back in middle school. Looking back on it now, though, the me who was covered head to toe in scars was kind of..."

He felt a pang of nostalgia for the days when he'd longed for a scar on his face and diligently scratched himself up to get one. One scratch hadn't been enough, so he'd made a bunch more, and on top of that, his bad luck had the wounds getting infected, escalating into the full disaster of him turning into Bandage Man. In his middle school graduation album, he was the lone Mummy Man.

"Ugh, I'm adorable...!"

"They say scars are a man's medals, after all. Come to think of it, Roswaal-sama also boasts about a scar he keeps rather than heals. —Around his chest, I believe."

"That sounds like it's about to turn into a way-too-vivid topic, can I pass on this one?"

Sensing the conversation was on the verge of veering into some suspicious maid-and-master territory before he even realized it, he hastily shut it down.

Whether or not she actually understood why, Rem tilted her head in puzzlement; he pushed her along by the back, saying "it's fine, it's fine," and hurried them along the way home.

He had no confidence he could keep up with that topic if it kept going, and besides, Subaru himself was feeling a fair amount of tension of his own.

—After all, this second time around the world had, as of today, reached exactly its fourth day.

"Whether I make it safely to tomorrow morning—that's the real test. Before that, though—"

Whether he could even make a promise with Emilia in the first place was also an important test.

"...Still, though, I gotta wonder if Ram-chi and Rem-rin's grasp of the birds and the bees is okay. I'm a little worried they're getting hoodwinked by that master of theirs somehow."

The more he came to know than he had in last week's loop, the more that suspicion began to rear its head. Once he'd settled his own affairs, he'd probe around a bit, subtly.

Such was the resolve Subaru quietly settled on.

Natsuki Subaru's second week at Roswaal Manor had, at this juncture, reached its greatest crisis yet.

Given that he wasn't managing to properly retrace the previous loop he'd studied up on, this had hardly been smooth sailing to begin with—but now, on top of that, the greatest crisis of all had arrived.

Namely—

"Thanks to being tied up with studying, I can't even go show my face to Emilia-tan."

In a garden lit by moonlight, the two of them whispering sweet nothings, sealing a promise with a pinky swear.

For Subaru, who'd been harboring that hope for the second night of the week, having his event progress obstructed like this at this stage was the worst possible turn of events.

The lessons themselves were still only on day four, and before dinner he'd casually—well, bluntly—proposed to the twins, "Hey, how about we take a little break today, take it easy for once?"—

"Onee-sama, onee-sama. This spineless creature named Subaru-kun is already begging to quit."

"Rem, Rem. This loser dog, lower than livestock, named Barusu, is barking something in dog-language."

—and with that, he'd been shot down with a level of cold contempt he'd never received before.

At this point, he wanted nothing more than to skip out and dash off to Emilia's side regardless.

"If word gets to Emilia-tan through the Ram-Rem grapevine that I skipped out, knowing her personality, she'd cancel the promise for sure...!"

Even if he managed to make the promise, it would mean nothing if it got broken afterward.

A promised rendezvous. The two of them, passions ablaze in a field of flowers, vanishing off into the night town together. Only once he'd pulled that off could it truly be called a Subaru-style HAPPY END.

"But then again, if I actually buckle down and study properly, I'll end up conked out before I even get to see Emilia-tan."

So there was only one best answer available to Subaru.

Namely—finish whatever assignment was given as fast as humanly possible, and swoop down into the garden before the moon hid behind the clouds. That was it.

"Or, alternatively, I lure Ram into sleep with the ultimate bed-making, sneak out and make my promise with Emilia while she's out, come back, and finish the assignment too... In other words, a pincer attack!"

More accurately, that's called being caught in the middle. A sad man's heart, torn between homework and a promise.

He'd prepared a backup plan too, and had already finished adjusting the bed. The bedding, having soaked up plenty of sunlight, was in peak condition, and the warmth one felt just touching it carried more than enough power to drag a person down into sleep's embrace.

On top of that, Subaru had poured every ounce of the hotelier's skill hidden within him into the effort, and a bed-making for the ages was complete.

"I don't think I could ever achieve a better bed-making than this..."

It was a masterpiece into which he'd literally thrown everything he had.

It was a finish that even he found himself smitten with. Normally, Ram just flopping down onto it on her own would've infuriated him to no end—but right now, he couldn't wait for her to climb in.

"Feels just like waiting for a cockroach to crawl into a brand-new roach trap you just bought—the worst!"

No matter how he tried to dress it up, a fact was a fact, so he wouldn't bother correcting himself.

That said, the fact that he was still dutifully waiting for her regardless could be seen as a sign of the very Japanese inability to commit fully to ruthlessness.

He stood ready at the desk, tapping his fingers against his knee, waiting for her to arrive. But—

"Why's she running late today of all days. If it starts late, obviously it's gonna end late too. Unlike school, there's no bell to mark the end here."

At school, a teacher running late would've been more of a "finally, thought you'd never come!" moment—but as things stood now, it was just dead time that purely extended how long he'd be stuck there.

He wasn't entirely without the thought that wasted time should go toward review, but given his mood tonight, he had to admit that calming his heart enough to tackle the letters was a pretty tall order.

That Subaru couldn't recognize how laughably absurd it was to resolve to finish his assignment quickly while in that state of mind, only went to show.

And as the minutes ticked relentlessly by, his impatience mounted, until the moment he registered a knock at his door, he strode over briskly and yanked it open with a bang—

"You're late! Every single one of these seconds might've been nothing to you, but for me it's been like whittling away at my very soul!"

—he snapped, rounding on her.

"...I'm sorry you waited that long? I haven't really come up to this floor much before."

Hearing that from Emilia, who'd been waiting outside the door, every drop of blood drained from his face at once.
