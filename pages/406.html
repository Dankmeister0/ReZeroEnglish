Arc 6 Chapter 4: "The Reason I'm Taking You With Me"

Conquering the Augria Sand Dunes, that death-laden desert region standing between them and the Pleiades Watchtower. To pass through the sand-maze that also served as a witchbeast den, the "witchbeast tamer" Meili had joined Subaru's group as a reassuring ally.

That said, though it had been decided to bring her along, keeping her confined to a room-turned-cell in the manor for a full year had only been possible with Roswaal's cooperation. Given that she'd once conspired with the "Bowel Hunter" to attack the manor, Subaru had naturally expected some manner of argument would be needed to lift her house arrest — and yet—

"Well, why nought? If Subaru-kun and Emilia-sama have decided to let her out, I don't see the pro-o-oblem with letting her out. Even if that judgment turns out wrong and the girl causes mischief, it'll be you lot who get targeted for it, after a-a-all."

Roswaal granted permission for Meili's release just like that. The content of his answer was, one had to admit, thoroughly Roswaal-like — but permission had been secured, at any rate. And so, Meili found herself, at long last, released after a year's confinement.

"Mmm~! The air outside really is different! Being cooped up in that room the whole time, I felt so stifled I thought I'd suffocate."

"I mean, we did a fair bit to try and keep you from feeling that way. It wasn't that bad a life, was it, when you get down to it?"

"Is that soo? I appreciate the thought, but it's still nothing at all like being free to come and go as I please. Onii-san, if you can't notice little things like that, you'll end up hated by Onee-san and the little miss before long, you know~."

Clutching an armful of stuffed animals, Meili glanced back and pouted her lips. Incidentally, when Meili said "Onee-san" she meant Emilia, and "the little miss" meant Beatrice. For Petra it was "Petra-chan," for Garfiel it was "fang Onii-san," and for Otto, well, it was only natural he'd be "the weak-looking one."

"Is this room okaay?"

"No complaints, as far as I've heard. It's an unused room, but it's still been kept clean, so there shouldn't be anything dirty or inconvenient about it. While we were hauling your stuff, Frederica was probably in here giving it a proper going-over."

"...Hmm."

Meili looked around with open curiosity — this was one of the rooms in the row of servants' private chambers in the west wing of Roswald Manor.

Now freed from the room-cell, Meili's position had become a thoroughly complicated one. Obviously she couldn't just be tossed out, so the manor needed to look after her — but was her role that of a guest, or a servant? She couldn't be kept at arm's length like a guest, but letting her burrow in as deep as a servant wasn't quite right either.

In the end, she settled into the ambiguous, neither-here-nor-there position of "collaborator," and was given a room on the servants' floor, treated as something like a half-member of the household. Into that room they now carried the many personal effects that had accumulated in her room-cell over the past year, intending to dye the featureless space with her own colors.

"Even after all this trouble, I dunno if I'll stick around long, though. Once I've got some idea I can live on my own safely, I might just up and run."

"So long as that 'idea' doesn't involve going crawling back to your scary mother at other people's expense, do what you like. Oh, but you'd better finish this job properly first. After that, you're free to do as you please."

"Once there's no bad blood left, I can die wherever I want, is that it?"

"Long as it doesn't reach my ears, I couldn't care less who dies where. ...That's what the old cynic-wannabe me would've said, but I'm not gonna say that."

Setting the carried-in luggage in the corner of the room, Subaru reached over and patted the head of Meili, who stood by the window. The chestnut-haired girl with braids blinked as his hand stroked her.

"Anyone I've gotten mixed up with, I want them to live and die decently, whatever happens. You're free to leave when you leave, but if you do, at least send a letter once in a while. Just enough to know you're getting by honestly — that's all I ask."

Giving her head one last light pat-pat, he withdrew his hand. Meili put a hand to where she'd been patted and glared at Subaru with narrowed eyes.

"...Onii-san, is that how you tamed the little miss and Petra-chan too? Honestly, a girl can't let her guard down for a second around you."

"That's not really my intention, y'know."

Scratching his head, Subaru finished helping with the move and headed for the door. Once the luggage was unloaded, he had no intention of helping decorate the room. Or rather, there was probably an unbridgeable, despair-inducing gap between the girl's decorating sense and his own. Better to make himself scarce before he interfered too much and got himself hated.

"Well, I'll take Onii-san's words with a grain of salt. Besides, you're about to go throw your life away in the Augria Sand Dunes soon enough, aren't you~."

"Not throwing it away — going to pick something up. And that's gonna depend a fair bit on your skills, so I'm counting on you."

"Yes yes."

Waving her hand to shoo him off, she raised one hand toward him without so much as looking his way. Meili, uninterested, pulled personal items out of the box and arranged them around her temporary room. As Meili herself said, it was a room where it was unclear whether she'd stay long, or even come back to at all. Still—

"Look at her, having the time of her life."

The girl, given a room of her own, walked about it with a step that seemed, if only slightly, to bounce. And in the way she went around placing her favorite stuffed animals, there was more than enough of that bubbling excitement of a girl of a certain age receiving her very first room — a sight that couldn't help but bring a smile.

With Meili's participation confirmed, the roster for the "Thrilling Ask-the-Sage Tour" now stood at seven — the three household members Subaru, Emilia, and Beatrice; the three outside collaborators Julius, Anastasia, and Meili; and one more girl to be brought along, Rem. These seven made up the party heading for the Augria Sand Dunes, located at the eastern edge of the world map.

To reach the Augria Sand Dunes, at the very edge of the world, one traveled due east from the true Roswaal Manor — though that journey was longer than the route to the water-gated city of Priestella, taking nearly twenty days one way, as far as anyone could confirm. Which meant this expedition would take forty days just for the round trip, and factoring in a safe arrival at the Watchtower plus however many days they'd need to stay there, it could easily stretch past two months — a grand expedition indeed. And it was precisely because of that scale that a certain problem had arisen—

"Hey, Petra. Sorry for leaving you hanging, but c'mon, cheer up a little?"

"I'm not, like, anything. I'm not angry or anything. Subaru-sama should just go ahead and do all sorts of dangerous things somewhere far away, like always, shouldn't you?"

Having already been left behind for a month over the whole Priestella business, and now facing another two months on top of that, Petra had worked herself into quite the sulk. The girl swished the maid uniform that suited her small frame, marching briskly down the manor corridor with a haughty, composed expression plastered on. As for Subaru, he trailed behind her, apologizing profusely, pouring out every word he could think of to try to smooth things over.

Now, as to why Subaru found himself so desperately anxious to please Petra — well, it's a truth as old as time and universal across cultures: when a girl gets into a mood like this, the fault, more often than not, lies with the man. And indeed, among this manor's considerable female talent, Petra stood as a leading representative. As for the sorry excuses for men likely to have offended her, the manor offered a grand total of — Subaru, Roswaal, Garfiel, or Otto. Were there... any other candidates? Actually, wasn't the entire male contingent a bunch of sorry excuses for men? And once Puck was revived, no doubt he'd join the ranks of the hopeless men too. Hopeless, all of them.

Anyway,

"I mean, sure, a month cooped up in the manor with just Rozzy would get stifling, but this is technically work too, so you can't really be picky about it—guh!"

"I'm not angry about something like that! I'm not a child, you know! And Frederica-neesama and Ram-neesama are right here too, so don't say weird things."

"A kid who leaps at a man's chest and headbutts him straight in the solar plexus, and isn't a child — what would you call that, then..."

Struck square in a vital point of the human body, Subaru writhed in agony as Petra fumed at him. With her hands on her hips, puffing out her slight chest — one struggled to see anything but "childishness" in that pose, but then, a lady's spirit tends to mature well before her body catches up. In Beatrice's case, since her growth never catches up at all, one gets the sense her spirit's been dragged down along with it.

"But Petra, did your headbutt's aim go up a little from before? I remember there used to be a beat, like a little jump, after you dove into my arms and before it connected."

"...Unbelievable. I told Beatrice-chan the same thing, didn't I? Look! Look properly. I grow in a month too, you know. My height's grown, my legs have grown, and my face has gotten cuter, hasn't it?"

"Ah, yeah, you have gotten a bit taller, huh? Height and hair both. Though your face was already cute to begin with."

Originally, Petra had been the prettiest girl in all of Arlam Village. As the object of every village boy's admiration and the undisputed number-one candidate for "girl I want to marry," there had been quite the uproar when it came time to pull her out to serve as a maid at the manor. Specifically, it had culminated in a full-blown sumo tournament in the middle of the village square, pitted against a crowd of bawling children. In the end even the adults had jumped in, and the whole bizarre rite of passage had concluded with a battered, mysteriously beaten-up Subaru being tossed in the air in celebration.

In any case, Petra was a girl beloved by the whole village, and her being sent off to serve at the manor had been greatly lamented. For the sake of Petra's considerable future prospects, Subaru wanted to do whatever he could to look out for her within the manor. That said, he held next to no actual authority in the household, so in practice, all he could really manage was to make sure she didn't forget what ordinary daily life felt like, day to day.

"You're a girl at the age when you want to play and have fun, and I haven't been able to give you much of that either. Even with sisters you're comfortable around, it's gotta feel stifling when it's all work, work, work — I get that much."

"That's not... really the point, though..."

He spread both hands, as if to comfort the sulking girl with a there-there gesture. Petra hesitated for just a moment, then leapt into Subaru's arms. He held her small body close, patting her head as if to soothe her.

"Subaru... I mean, Subaru-sama. Are you going off to do something dangerous again?"

"It's not decided that it's dangerous, y'know? I mean, yeah, there's talk it might be dangerous, but there's also a real possibility it turns out to be a shockingly cheap, safe little tour."

"How likely is each one?"

"Eighty-twenty, maybe, in favor of not-safe?"

"That sounds pretty dangerous to me."

Once pressed with a direct question, Subaru's bad habit of being unable to lie kicked in. He sensed the Petra in his arms growing more displeased, and sighed, unsure what to do. Then Petra pressed her forehead into his stomach, grinding it in,

"I'm worried. Subaru-sama, you're always like this. Ever since I first met you in the village, ever since you first came to the manor. Even with so much hard stuff happening, you go running around here and there again without even resting... You should learn from the master, who doesn't do anything."

"Nah, I bet even Roswaal's sweating bullets over his paperwork somewhere we can't see, y'know? Though, in terms of being useless in a life-or-death sense — yeah, fair, I agree with you there."

"With the witchbeasts, and the Witch Cult, the earth spider and the goddess statue, and Elior Forest, and even the graveyard cleanup, it's always so hard, and then even in Priestella you clashed with the Witch Cult, and Otto-san almost even di—"

"Otto very nearly got murdered again, yeah."

Otto really did die too often. Impressions-wise, Subaru wholeheartedly agreed, but he really wished the man wouldn't die so easily. If Otto died, Natsuki Subaru couldn't very well go on living either. Not that he intended to go around claiming their friendship transcended even death, or anything like that.

"It doesn't have to be Subaru-sama, does it? Someone else could... someone stronger could take care of it instead. Like the master. He's not busy, is he?"

"Okay, okay. I get it. I get exactly how much frustration you've built up toward Roswaal lazing about all day. Let's not start scheming to send Roswaal off to his death at every opportunity, all right? That's way too much discord within the camp."

If it were just Petra slipping a bit of wrung-out rag water into Roswaal's tea, Subaru could let that slide, but it was bad news once actual hostility and malice started getting this concrete. He hurried to head it off before it went further, but the eyes Petra turned on him were deadly serious. A half-hearted deflection or some stopgap words wouldn't be enough to satisfy the girl. More than anything, Petra was genuinely, sincerely worried for Subaru's safety. To toss out some flippant remark here would be an insult to that feeling.

"Well, I get where you're coming from, Petra. I really do. I mean, yeah, apparently there's witchbeasts before we even reach the Sage's tower, and the desert maze is supposed to be no joke, and on top of that, getting close to the tower means catching the eye of some short-tempered Sage — it's a pretty absurd situation all around... but even so, I just don't feel like leaving it to someone else."

"...Why? Subaru-sama, don't tell me you're under the mistaken impression that you're strong? That kind of embarrassing misunderstanding, Garf-san alone is more than enough of."

"Petra's grading scale is seriously merciless! I can't let Garfiel hear that!"

Petra's grading standards were, by all accounts, harsh and severe enough to make grown men tremble. Calling Garfiel's whole attitude toward his own strength a "misunderstanding" — well, that was certainly one way to put it. Though, to be fair, Garfiel never once slacked in his efforts to turn that "misunderstanding" into "reality." And, troublingly, he was probably actually stronger while under that "misunderstanding," if anything. In that sense too, doubly so, this was not a conversation Garfiel could be allowed to hear.

"Well, setting aside my thoughts on Garfiel's whole outlook... it's not that I think there's nobody better suited for the job than me. I mean, if it's about clearing a desert maze, tossing the whole thing to Reinhard would probably be the safer call in a lot of ways."

"Then why don't you do that?"

"Beats me. I never thought of myself as someone who craves glory, even if I do like being fussed over."

Honestly, he didn't fully understand why he wanted to do this himself. But if he had to put it into words, maybe it came down to this.

"—No, yeah, I guess this really is something I want to do. There might be someone better suited, someone with better odds, and I know that — but I still want to be the one to do it."

"Why?"

"It's kind of embarrassing to admit... but when she opens her eyes, I want the first thing she sees to be me."

"――――"

He didn't say who. He didn't have to — Petra surely understood without being told. The girl still deep in slumber, even now — in the Sage's tower, if a means could be found to wake her from that sleep, he wanted to be the one to grasp it.

There might be someone else better suited for the job, someone whose odds were better than his own — but this, at least, was something Subaru wouldn't yield, didn't want to yield. It was his own ego.

"Setting emotion aside, and just talking logically... I think it wouldn't matter who woke her up. If it's some horrible disease going around where you never wake up, and it's just about saving people from it, then I don't care who develops the vaccine or the cure. As long as she gets saved, I don't care about the process."

"...Yeah."

"But once you throw emotion into it, I want to be the one who does it. I want to be the one who saves her. I want to be the one who wakes her up. I want to save all of her, with all of me."

—And that was why Natsuki Subaru was going.

There were plenty of people stronger than him. Plenty of people more dependable than him. Plenty of people smarter than him. Plenty of people with far better characters than him.

And yet, setting all of that aside for his own selfish ego, Natsuki Subaru was going. Simply to save her, and to be praised by her for it — for that reason alone.

"Sorry for being so selfish, and disappointing you."

"—I really am disappointed. Nothing's changed at all, even after hearing that. Not for me."

"Hmm?"

Bracing himself for being hated after that pathetic confession, Subaru gave Petra's head one last pat. But Petra murmured those words in a voice barely audible, then lifted her face from within his arms. Her round, wide eyes were brimming with large tears, and Subaru found himself startled. And then,

"There!"

"Not the solar plexus again—!?"

She snapped her head sharply downward, and her forehead gouged into Subaru's solar plexus. As his knees buckled from the unguarded strike to his vital point, Petra slipped smoothly free of his arms, leapt back a good distance, and stuck out her tongue in a mocking "bleh."

"Subaru-sama is an idiot! So selfish! Fine, just do whatever you want!"

"Guh, oof...!"

"When you come back in two months, I'll be so beautiful you'll be shocked! You'll regret not getting to watch me grow up close!"

"Mm, that part, I honestly do feel a little sad about."

The changes children go through right at the peak of their growth spurt really are something else. Petra, too, was in the full bloom of thirteen — two months apart really could end up changing her impression quite a bit.

"Not to brag, but I grew nineteen centimeters in the year between my second and third year of middle school..."

"Eh, that seems like kind of a stretch..."

Well, in Subaru's case, that was probably a rare fluke owing to how short he'd started out. Petra nearly wilted under the weight of such expectations, but quickly clenched her fists once more. Then, thrusting both fists out at Subaru, she declared:

"Anyway! Subaru-sama, go do whatever dangerous thing you want, however you want, worrying and troubling a bunch of people along the way, and then just come waltzing back like nothing happened, like always!"

"Put that way, I sound like a pretty inconsiderate guy..."

Looking at it that way, that description wasn't exactly wrong, which was a bit much to take. In any case, Subaru held out both hands too, and bumped his fists against Petra's.

"Well then, sorry to make you go through this every time, Petra, but do me a favor and wait for me to head off somewhere dangerous with a stupid grin on my face like always, mess around with a bunch of stuff, and then casually stroll back home. Being the one to say 'welcome back' to us when we return — that's Petra's privilege alone."

"...You won't let Frederica-neesama or Ram-neesama say it first?"

"Yeah, I promise."

"Not even the master?"

"Don't worry about that one — if the first face I see when I get back to the manor is Roswaal's, I'll be the one throwing the punch."

"...Mm, understood. Then I'll accept that."

Withdrawing her fist, Petra sighed and, at long last, let her fighting spirit deflate. Though looking at how things had gone, it was probably fair to say Petra had been the one giving ground here, mostly.

"Honestly, Subaru-sama is hopeless..."

Somehow, he felt like he kept hearing that from everyone whose face he showed himself to. I really can't hold my head up around any of them, Subaru thought.

It wasn't quite worth calling "unfinished business," but as Subaru went around resolving the various matters left at the manor, the last room he made his way to was this one.

"――――"

Only when stepping into this room did Subaru find himself instinctively holding his breath, muffling his footsteps. Probably, even if he'd walked in singing loudly and tap-dancing, nothing about the room's stillness would have changed. And yet he found himself unconsciously guarding that silence — perhaps because the sight of the girl sleeping in the bed within brought such a fragile ache to the heart of anyone who saw it.

A deep sleep he wished, if it were at all possible, she would wake from this very instant. And yet, disturbing that sleep felt, somehow, like something dreadfully sacrilegious. That was how precious the sight of the girl sunk in dreams seemed to him.

"Though I guess that's a pretty subjective opinion of mine, when you get down to it..."

Muttering in a tone of self-deprecation, Subaru pulled a chair up beside the bed and sat down. And so, in this room he'd visited for the first time in a month, he was reunited with the sleeping face of Rem — unchanged since a year ago, still fast asleep even now, just as before.

"Sorry I took so long to come see you, even though I was back. Had a bunch of stuff to sort out... and then I felt kind of awkward about it, and before I knew it, this ended up being the last stop."

"――――"

Naturally, sleeping Rem gave Subaru no reply. Knowing that full well, Subaru spoke to her anyway, yet his expression was gentle. It was an expression Natsuki Subaru showed only in front of her.

The desperate expression, as if throwing something of himself away, that only Emilia ever saw. The expression of trust, as if entrusting his very life, that only Beatrice ever saw. And then there was the expression of Subaru's hidden weakness, that only Rem ever saw.

"Where should I even start... nah, there's actually plenty to talk about, y'know? While I couldn't come see you, I was in this place called the water-gated city of Priestella, and a ton of stuff happened there too. Way too much to cover in one night. ...But I've got plenty of time to tell you about that."

Reaching under the towel blanket draped up to her chest, Subaru searched out Rem's hand as she lay in bed, took it without much thought, and spoke to her. Slender fingertips, a thin white arm. There was softness there, and the warmth of life — and yet, strangely, there was no sense of blood pulsing through it. No — no sense of life running through it at all.

Some manner of force was at work, sustaining her life. But no force was at work to continue that life, to see it through to fulfillment.

Rem's life, caught in that contradiction, remained frozen in time even now, within a deep, unbroken sleep. And yet—

"I might finally be able to reach you."

"――――"

"There's a chance I get everyone's hopes up and it all comes to nothing, of course. There's a chance the Sage turns out to be a hollow shell not living up to the name, and her knowledge's worthless — sorry about that, in advance."

"――――"

"But."

Beyond defeating "Gluttony," this was, at long last, a clear path toward a solution that had come into view. That the road he'd followed to reach that answer had been shaped for him by others was the height of inadequacy — and yet, it was still, finally, a glimmer of light he seemed within reach of grasping—

"I was too scared to ask Echidna."

In the "Sanctuary," at the Witch's grave site, when he'd been invited to that dream tea party and came face to face with the "Witch of Greed" Echidna, Subaru had escaped her grasp by rejecting the witch outright. That had been, at once, a choice to protect the fragile soul that was Natsuki Subaru — and, equally, a choice to push away someone who "might" have known a means of saving Rem.

Of course, there was no telling how much insight Echidna, a "witch" who knew the past, actually held on the Archbishops of Sin currently running rampant in the present. But even so, had he laid out the circumstances, hearing the witch's guesses might have offered some help toward finding a solution.

Immediately after resolutely rejecting the witch's demand, he'd felt confident that decision had been the right one. But as time passed, and each time he felt the flow of the seasons, the shifting of relationships, leaving Rem behind, he grew uncertain whether that choice back then had truly been correct.

He'd resolved in his heart to save her, and yet he himself, unable to take any concrete action. Only now, at last, could he break free from that stifling deadlock and act for her sake.

In the city of Priestella, so many people suffering the same affliction as Rem — the journey to the Pleiades Watchtower was, in part, to save them. But if he were to speak only the honest truth, what pushed Subaru forward toward the Watchtower was nothing else but Rem's existence, and Rem's alone. He knew it was impure, inappropriate — and knowing that, still, Subaru—

"I'm going to bring you back, Rem. —That's my vow."

Just as she had been there for him in those days, that time, when he was at his very weakest. Now, when Rem needed someone's hand more than ever, he wanted to be the one standing there for her.

"—Ow."

"—Gah!?"

Having put his resolve into words, eyes shut tight, Subaru's head snapped up at the sound of that voice. Startled beyond belief, he looked at Rem, eyes wide — but she still lay there quietly, eyelids closed, her hand simply being held in silence by Subaru. Then that voice just now had been—

"Let go, Barusu. It's painful just watching your hand."

"...Oh, it's just you, Ram."

Turning around, he found Ram standing in the doorway, gazing at him with cold eyes. Relieved by her presence, Subaru then realized his own hand — the one holding Rem's — had been gripping harder than he'd intended, and hastily let go.

"Rem's pale, fish-like fingers, defiled by Barusu's lust — it's unbearable to watch."

"Could you drop that phrasing? It's turning my whole heartfelt resolve from a second ago into something filthy."

As Subaru released the hand he'd been holding, Ram stepped into the room and took Rem's hand herself. The elder sister gently stroked her younger sister's white, slender fingers, then glanced sidelong at Subaru.

"You think your own resolve is something pure and clean? Perhaps you ought to examine yourself a bit more carefully before speaking. ...Being lusted after by someone who looks exactly like her sends a chill down Ram's spine too, you know."

"Relax. You two may look identical on the outside, but the difference in the shine of your inner selves means my finger doesn't so much as twitch for you. I'm not some half-baked man whose heart's swayed by looks alone."

"Good, then that's fine. It would be a shame for Rem's feelings, if a way were found and she woke up only to find Barusu had forgotten her over the course of a year. ...Assuming that nonsense about this child harboring feelings for Barusu isn't just Barusu's own delusion."

"How little trust do you have in me... we've known each other a while now, haven't we?"

"Hah."

Snorting through her nose as usual, Ram gestured for Subaru to get up from his seat. He complied and rose, and she looked around the room, then—

"There are preparations for departure, so Rem's change of clothes need to be arranged too. She doesn't sweat, so there's no real need to change her clothes, but... her body still needs to be wiped down and such."

"――――"

"—Your face is drooping, how filthy."

"I couldn't comment so I didn't, and you still got me even in silence!"

Under Ram's scornful gaze, Subaru very nearly stamped his foot in indignation at the unfairness of it, but remembering where he was, settled for clenching his fists instead.

Looking after Rem's daily needs — Rem, asleep with her "memory" and "name" devoured, was essentially disconnected from any physical response, so wiping her body, changing her clothes, and the like held no practical meaning in any real sense. It was simply a comfort, so that the people around her could feel her existence hadn't been left behind and forgotten.

"――――"

Speaking in the most purely practical terms, neither Subaru talking to her like this, nor Ram tending to her sister's needs, carried any forward-looking meaning in any of it. And yet no one tried to stop what might be called a futile act, because everyone understood, without needing to say it, that not everything one does needs to bear results.

"So, has it started to sink in yet, that Rem's your little sister?"

"――――"

Watching Ram tend to sleeping Rem with such careful hands, with a gentleness quite unlike the girl herself, Subaru found himself suddenly asking that question. Even as Ram devotedly cared for her, no memory of Rem, her other half, remained within her. A girl who looked exactly like herself, and was presumably not unrelated to her. Going only by Ram's own sense of it, that was how thin her connection to Rem really was.

But a year had passed like this, day after day. Even with the memories gone, something else had taken shape in their place. Had that taken root within Ram now, as something with real, tangible form?

"It's not something that just sprouts up so easily. It's one thing to not be in my memories — Ram's never even once come across this child while she was awake. Though I can imagine, taking after Ram, she was probably a capable, composed sort of girl."

"Capable, for sure, but I don't have a lot of memories of her being 'composed.' She's surprisingly scatterbrained, panics more than you'd think, and honestly, her going off the rails has gotten us into trouble plenty of times."

Having half of himself carried off — well, let's set that aside for now. Ram responded to that answer with an indifferent "Is that so," and then—

"Speaking of memories that don't exist, even as a comfort, that's rather too maudlin. Ram doesn't care for that sort of thing, Barusu."

"That so? If you say so, I'll knock it off, then."

"...Once she wakes up, if she remembers, we can talk as much as we like. And even if she doesn't remember, so long as she wakes up, we still can, as much as we like."

Peering down at her sleeping sister's face, Ram, her expression unchanged, tickled the bangs across her forehead with a finger. Rem's hair slid smoothly across her pale forehead, and Ram let out a soft breath. That profile looked, to Subaru, terribly gentle.

Even without memory, even with the past lost, that didn't mean the bond between them vanished too. And even if it had vanished, that didn't mean it couldn't be woven anew. That was what he thought.

"Well, leave that part to me. I'll conquer that Sage's Watchtower without fail and wake Rem up for sure. Then it'll be a proper tearful sisters' reunion."

So, in a voice deliberately raised, foolishly bright, Subaru said just that. A solemn atmosphere didn't suit the space between Subaru and Ram, at least.

But Ram turned back to face that remark of Subaru's with an oddly puzzled look.

"What are you talking about, Barusu?"

"Huh?"

Then, with an expression bordering on contempt, Ram said to him:

"Ram is coming along on this trip too, so that phrasing is far too presumptuous, don't you think? If it's going to be a tearful reunion, Ram will handle that part herself."

"This is news to me!?"

At Subaru's wide-eyed reaction, Ram's look of contempt only deepened further. But no matter how she looked at him, what he didn't know, he didn't know, and what he hadn't been told, he hadn't been told — it was simply unreasonable of her.

"――――"

Pressed by Subaru demanding to know what was going on, Ram covered her ears in a gesture of flat refusal. In the end, without any proper conversation ever taking place, this day of preparations passed by as well.
