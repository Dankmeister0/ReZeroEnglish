Arc 8 Chapter 9: "Deciding to Love"

—The withdrawal battle from the imperial capital, Lupugana.

Even having judged it a necessary decision, it was still the greatest upheaval since the founding of the empire.
Not only had the enemy's advance been permitted all the way to the Crystal Palace, the imperial capital that was the emperor's own doorstep — the emperor himself had to abandon both castle and capital and flee. There was no escaping the collapse of authority that entailed.
That said—

"So long as we don't know how that ominous fellow will move, Your Excellency's judgment is the wisest course."
"Kah kah kah! An emperor abandoning his castle and running — unheard of, ain't it? At least, I ain't never heard of such a thing in all my years."
"Do not laugh, Ichishou Olbart! When I think of His Excellency's feelings... when I think of it... I—! Damn it all! Without fail, under His Excellency's command, I will retake both castle and capital!!"
"Waaah, running away, that's a quick decision, isn't it! I'm not one for a glorious last stand, so I think that's the right call!"

And so, not a single voice was raised against Vincent's decision on the spot.
On this point, it had been worth assigning posts based on practical competence rather than mere loyalty. That policy had, in fact, been the cause of a rebellion born of differing convictions — but ultimately, since even his most trusted confidant had betrayed him, some variance in loyalty amounted to no more than a rounding error.
In any case, the withdrawal battle began.

"Olbart Dunkelkenn. Go buy time. Left alone, Moguro Hagane will be struck down before long. — The reservoir must not be breached yet."

"Well, well, working an old man to the bone, aren't you. Wouldn't Goz do just as well?"

"Lightness of body and quickness are a Shinobi's true forte, are they not. Cease your objections and fulfill your duty."

"...Just so you know, once he's focused on flying, even I might not be able to catch him."

Overhead, the dead Balleroy Temeglyph tore through the sky above the imperial capital in flight. His skill as the finest Flying Dragon Rider the Vollachian Empire had ever known remained intact even in death — Olbart's words carried an implicit warning of just how troublesome an opponent he was. Vincent listened, and still entrusted the task to the old monster.

As Olbart said, aptitude aside, Goz could likely fulfill the same role.
However, speaking of aptitude, there was a role only Goz could perform. This loud-voiced, oppressively hot-blooded "general" commanded an abnormal degree of trust from the troops.
Precisely for that reason—

"Goz Ralfon. Put that absurd set of lungs of yours to use. What we need now, to withdraw from the capital, is manpower. Gather the troops scattered about the vicinity."

"Yes! Leave it to me, Your Excellency! This instant!!"

Nodding without a moment's hesitation, the bare-chested Goz leapt atop a nearby pile of rubble. There, he drew a great breath, and let his voice ring out across a world already thundering with the giant Moguro's battle.
An outrageously massive voice rose in defiance against the trembling air of the imperial capital.

"—Hear me, Sword Wolves of Vollachia!! This is the command of His Excellency Vincent Vollachia, peak of we Sword Wolves! Obey my voice! Obey!!"

At that volume like the report of a Magic Stone Cannon, Goz's command rang out, and Vincent, covering his ears, glanced experimentally toward Olbart. Olbart, likewise covering his ears, shook his head slowly.

"Can't do that one myself. Right man for the right job, I suppose."

Leaving only those words behind, Olbart's form left a shadow and rose skyward.
The old monster ran up the walls of the Crystal Palace, cracking under the aftershocks of battle, using them as footholds, then leapt midway onto Moguro's massive body, intervening directly in the battlefield where "Cloud Dragon" and "Magic Bullet Marksman" fought in tandem, unleashing the skill of the "Vile Elder" to toy with them both.

Olbart had spoken as though he had no means against a flying opponent, but between the Shinobi's unknown secret arts and his tools, he surely possessed countless countermeasures.
With the old monster now joining the battle line, Moguro's disadvantage would likely be somewhat reversed.

"Your Excellency, is there anything you'd like us to do?"

"—. How many of you 'Stargazers' are in the vicinity?"

While Goz was gathering men, Vincent posed the question to the idle Ubilk. To that question, Ubilk raised the fingers of both hands to his cheeks and said, "Let's see..."

"For now, within calling range, twenty-seven of us, I'd say."

"More than expected. Then go door to door through every residence you can. The emperor and his soldiers are abandoning the capital. Tell them staying in the city means throwing their lives away for nothing."

"Understood! This is it, this is it. A total war effort... the whole of the Vollachian Empire standing against the 'Great Disaster' — this is what I wanted to see."

"Be quick about it."

With his true purpose as a "Stargazer" fulfilled, Vincent irritably shooed away the barely-contained excitement of Ubilk, then let out a breath.
Then he turned his gaze to Berstetz, who remained silent so as not to disturb Vincent's thoughts and deliberations.
The mastermind of the internal strife — the man who had thrown in with Chisha Gold and brought chaos to the empire by driving Vincent from the throne.

"You're being remarkably quiet."

"In the present circumstance, having two heads issuing orders can only sow further confusion. Should a Sword Wolf be born with two heads..."

"Cutting off one of them would be your role, then?"

"Should it prove necessary, I would gladly offer up this old neck."

Hands still clasped behind his back, Berstetz stated his resolve flatly.
Here was a man far removed from the martial arts of war, yet burning within him was the pride of a man of the empire. Vincent did not believe Berstetz had risen in rebellion out of lust for power, nor did he think the words just spoken were some convenient excuse for the moment.
A purely straightforward, fervent devotion to the empire — that alone was Berstetz's driving force.
Precisely for that reason, the instant he judged the empire in peril, he would abandon without hesitation whatever policy or plan he had held until then, and stand, as he did now, on the same side as Vincent.

"Once Goz Ralfon and the 'Stargazers' have made their preparations, we begin the withdrawal at once. Berstetz Fondalfon, speak your opinion. Be brief."

"—With respect, Your Excellency, if you are to withdraw, I would ask you to retrace your own thoughts."

"————"

To Vincent's brief request for counsel, Berstetz answered thus.
It sounded like a riddle, but Berstetz had no wish to confound Vincent. After a moment's thought, Vincent, too, grasped the true meaning behind his words.
To retrace his own thoughts meant the thoughts of Vincent Vollachia.

—Which was to say, they were also, until a short while ago, the very thoughts that Chisha Gold had traced.

"If Ichishou Chisha had some design in mind when he took Your Excellency's place and aided in the usurpation of the throne alongside this old servant..."

"—That thing would have surmised the nature of the 'Great Disaster,' and prepared for it accordingly."

"Just so. And that is something His Imperial Majesty would be capable of noticing — and something no one but His Imperial Majesty could notice."

Closing one eye at Berstetz's words, Vincent fell into thought.
He could understand the chancellor's stated opinion. — No, it was only reasonable. It was an idea Vincent would have arrived at himself given time, even without Berstetz's counsel — but this had shortened the path there.

Chisha had staked his own life and remained on the throne in Vincent's form. And if he had left behind more than merely the "future" that should have been cut off for Vincent—
That would be—

"—Your Excellency!! The troops, every last one, have returned to their true faces as soldiers! What are your orders!!"

Just as Vincent's thoughts arrived at that point, Goz came leaping roughly down from the pile of rubble at that very moment.
Looking around, gathering steadily from all sides, rattling their equipped swords and armor, were imperial soldiers — a pack of Sword Wolves. In the face of this unprecedented crisis, they must surely have felt confusion and unease. And yet, having gathered at the call of the "Lion Knight" Goz Ralfon, and learning that the Vollachian Emperor himself awaited them beyond, they steeled their expressions, their very bearing, and stood.

"————"

It was a way of being for the people of the empire that struck one as deeply repulsive, deeply hateful.
And yet, in order to fight the calamity that was to come, the Sword Wolves could not be allowed to forget their hunger. Reflecting that this was precisely what those months and years had been for, Vincent looked up at the sky above.
To build up the nation's strength, to labor tirelessly at training troops and maintaining morale, all to preserve an empire capable of standing against calamity. — That calculation had gone awry, but he had no intention of leaving it astray as it was.

"Moguro Hagane! Do not let those insolent wretches use the Magic Crystal Cannon! Protect only that, and I shall grant whatever it is you desire!!"

『————』

Whether his voice, raised as loud as he could manage, reached Moguro in the very midst of his battle against the "Dragon" — currently the strongest living creature in this world — was impossible to know.
But that valiant "Meteor" listened earnestly to human words. Its appearance gave no clue as to where its ears might even be, but on that point, it could be trusted.

Having thus issued instructions to the gathered "generals," Vincent lowered his gaze and swept his eyes over the faces of the officers and soldiers gathered around him.
Fixing his eyes on those Sword Wolves whose fighting spirit showed no sign of flagging, he continued.

—If Chisha Gold had fulfilled the role of Vincent Vollachia to the very end—

"We are leaving the imperial capital! Our destination is the fortress city of Garkla, to the northwest!!"

He declared, in a ringing voice, that the means to stand against the "Great Disaster" surely awaited them there.

—Both the imperial soldiers and the rebels who made up that battlefield had begun to notice that the fight over the fate of the Vollachian Empire had changed shape.

The degree of confusion spreading across the various fronts, the shift in its color, was pronounced — and the Sword Wolves, enraptured as they were by battle and thus sensitive to its flavor, sensed the graceless intrusion of some outside will.
When orders came flying in from commanders with their complexions changed, the minds that had regained even a shred of composure, along with their fighting instincts, changed the thinking of those who had until then been honestly crossing blades with one another.
As a result, those who only moments before had been locked in fierce combat ceased their hostilities, repainting the labels of "imperial soldier" and "rebel" into "people of the empire," and became a single pack of wolves.

Just then, within the imperial capital, Vincent Vollachia, and at the siege line surrounding the city, Serena Dracroy, had each raised their voices as supreme commander of their respective forces.

And yet, entirely apart from this shift that everyone in the empire had sensed, apart from the circumstances now demanding action — there remained one battlefield still burning red, consuming all who came near.

—The "Spirit Eater" Arakiya, guardian of the First Apex, faced off against the sumptuous, bewitching heroines Priscilla Barielle and Yorna Mishigure.

"—I shall love you."

Amid a world dominated by crimson blazing flame, Yorna, fire lit in one eye, kicked gracefully off the ground.
Her kimono hem swirling elegantly, her legs — long for a woman — striking the earth, and in the next instant the ground itself surged upward, lending speed and force alike to Yorna's charge.

This was not the demon city of Chaosflame, but merely the earth surrounding the imperial capital, the emperor's own doorstep.
Far away, beyond memories now distant both as Yorna Mishigure and as Sandra Benedict, she had once gazed upon that imperial capital, which had seemed impossibly far off.
A place forever beyond her reach, hand in hand with a man of such noble standing — so far above her that to even whisper of loving him felt presumptuous — she had gazed upon that capital.

Yorna's "Soul Marriage Technique" could only be bestowed upon what she herself loved.
—If so, then how could Yorna help but love that man, whose memory never faded no matter how much time passed, and this very earth that he, of all people, had loved?

"—And also had it stolen from me."

The Vollachian Emperor of that age, known as the "Thorned King," and the girl Iris, who had fallen in love with him.
For the sake of the emperor driven from his throne, she had sheltered him, supported him, and resolved to walk the path together with him — and many had praised the "Thorned King" for the girl's courage, wisdom, and earnestness, and blessed the future of the two of them.
But that future was deceived by the treachery of the Mole Humans, manipulated by cunning sweet words from a treacherous vassal, and by the Wolf Humans whose ambition and arrogance had been exploited — and the perfect ending was lost.

The paradise the "Thorned King" was meant to build, the construction of that promised world, was abandoned, and the emperor, consumed by obsession, bound the soul of the girl who should have vanished to the soil of the empire with the blood of the traitors.
Since then, again and again, under different names and different forms, Iris had been born anew, over and over.

She loved someone, was loved by someone, and went on living.
But at the same time, she could never bring herself to love this Vollachian Empire, this land of the empire alone. Perhaps the further she was carried from the dream she had once envisioned, the more hateful it came to seem.
But still — even so — such a thing was trivial compared to what truly mattered.

"—I shall love you."

It was not a matter of whether she could love it, but simply of deciding to love it.
It was never an empire filled only with happy memories. Whether in the days she had been Iris, or through the many lives since she had ceased to be her, happiness and misfortune had always come hand in hand.
It was the same for those Yorna now called her beloved children, living in the demon city — there were times her heart ached with fondness for them, and times their impudence made her furious.

"—All of it, is it not the same fullness?"

Yes — the moment "love" clicked into place with a resounding sound within Yorna, the burning earth roared in acclaim.
The ground, which should only have borne the weight of her planted feet, thrashed about in high spirits, the bounding earth serving as Yorna's foothold, flinging her high into the sky.

Spinning round and round, the heel of Yorna's platform sandal dropped toward Arakiya in midair.
Arakiya tried to phase through it by assimilating with the surrounding air. But Yorna's kick exploded against the brown skin that should have let it pass right through.

"—Ah?"

The shoulder that took the kick burst, and Arakiya gave a small gasp at the impossible impact.
Whether fire, wind, or shadow — whatever Arakiya had assimilated with — her body burst apart from the impact, and her expression, which rarely showed such intense emotion, was colored with pain and shock.

For Arakiya, who had made maximum use of her "Spirit Eater" trait to maintain the advantage in every battle against every enemy, this blow must truly have come like a bolt from the blue.
And yet, Yorna felt neither surprise nor satisfaction at the fact that it had landed. Using the planted heel as a pivot, she rose upright, and swung the pipe gripped in her slender fingers, launching a follow-up strike.

"Eh, ah, wa—"

The sound of something hard striking flesh came in rapid succession, Arakiya's expression twisting again and again.
As though unable to comprehend what was happening to her, a thin voice leaked from Arakiya's throat. It was less a reaction to the pain itself than a refusal of the fact that she was suffering pain at all.
Both of Arakiya's wide eyes — even the one that could not see — seemed to demand of Yorna: why?

"Where there is love, graceless restraint has no meaning."

"Love...?"

"Are you not my daughter's ill-mannered milk sister?"

At Yorna's answer, delivered with a beguiling smile, Arakiya's expression filled with incomprehension.
Even given the answer, unable to understand it, her cheeks stiffened, and Arakiya fled at high speed through the sky she could freely traverse, unleashing a wave of natural-disaster-like attacks one after another.
Suspended in midair, Yorna had no means of avoiding the wood, fire, earth, metal, and water bearing down on her.

"Before I reconciled with the empire, that would have been true."

Weaving words from parted lips, Yorna ran through the air, using the earth that rose up wave after wave as her footholds.
Deep-seated resentment, and no small measure of happy memories besides — having decided to love the Vollachian Empire, warts and all, the earth's fervent partisanship continued in answer to Yorna's devotion.

Dodging the rain of fire, the spears of water, the great waves of wind, the slashes of light, one after another, Yorna closed in on Arakiya once more.
The earth arrived wherever Yorna stepped — Arakiya's advantage of being airborne no longer functioned against Yorna, who inevitably reached the same height regardless.

"—Ah"

Assimilating with the spirits in the air, phasing through the enemy's attacks.
Arakiya, for whom this evasion had become habitual, was slow to respond to the danger of the closing Yorna, and took a kick full-on to her torso, her smooth skin exposed.
The destructive force punched clean through the back of Arakiya's body, and the great wind she had assimilated with kicked up, causing a ripple in the world dyed red with flame.

"—Ngh"

At last, Arakiya's expression, too, was marked with the color of pain, surpassing mere shock.
Certain proof that Yorna's attacks were steadily cornering her. It was a flow that could be boasted of as decisively advantageous thus far, but Yorna herself felt no optimism.
Because—

"You, of all people...!"

Cheeks twisted with pain and fury, Arakiya swung the wooden branch she gripped at Yorna.
Utterly unremarkable, merely something picked up off the ground, it showed no sign of breaking even amid the natural disasters unleashed — and in the hands of a "Spirit Eater," it displayed true worth like some legendary blade or treasured artifact.

The swung branch was caught by Yorna, who raised her arm with the pipe running alongside it to block. The corner of her mouth trembled faintly as the two glared at each other at close range, locked in stalemate.

"Ngh..."

The force of the branch she took, transmitted through the pipe, wrung a groan from Yorna's throat.
Pain and wounds — Yorna's "Soul Marriage Technique" could transfer these onto beloved possessions she wore upon her body. Naturally, this secret art required a corresponding attachment, and the proof of it.
And yet, from Yorna's hairpin to her earrings to her obi clasp, none of the items presented to her by her beloved children, the residents of the demon city, had vanished. And yet Yorna herself had taken the pain.
The answer was simple and clear — because love must never be a one-way street.

"—I shall love you."

Having decided to love the empire, love and hatred intertwined as it was, and having decided to discipline Arakiya as well, even though they now stood as enemies — rather than forcing love upon her one-sidedly, she had to aim for mutual love.
That was the exact opposite of the ordinary use of Yorna Mishigure's "Soul Marriage Technique."

"————"

In midair, Yorna and Arakiya faced off, their fierce exchange of blows unfolding.
Priscilla, fire burning in the eye beloved of that one, holding the dazzlingly radiant "Sunlight Sword," frowned at the sight of her mother and her milk sister clashing without hindrance.

As a fellow wielder of the "Soul Marriage Technique," she understood the logic of what Yorna was doing.
But precisely because she was a fellow wielder of the "Soul Marriage Technique," Priscilla also understood the forbidden nature of what Yorna was doing.

"Focusing solely on the strengths of the technique while giving no thought whatsoever to its weaknesses. Mother does something reckless indeed."

She grasped at a glance the mechanism by which Yorna ignored Arakiya's trait as "Spirit Eater" and made her attacks connect, but doing so required both exquisite mastery of the secret art and the resolve to take wounds herself.
The "Soul Marriage Technique," which both Priscilla and Yorna handled with such nonchalance, was a secret art requiring a talent so rare that, historically, almost no successor had ever appeared, and its practical use demanded a miraculous sense of balance.

To put it plainly, Yorna's attempt was a dangerous gamble that, far from merely failing to land attacks on Arakiya, risked severing even her bonds with the beloved children who had given her those gifts.

"————"

Priscilla was neither foolish nor cold-hearted enough to be at a loss to understand why Yorna would go so far as to take such a dangerous gamble to confront Arakiya.
None other than for Priscilla's sake had Yorna ventured into such a taboo act.

—To prevent a situation in which Priscilla herself would have to strike Arakiya down.

"With my Sunlight Sword, there is no need to court the sort of danger Mother is courting."

To cut what she wishes to cut, to burn what she wishes to burn — that is the true essence of the "Sunlight Sword's" nature.
With the white flame unleashed when its true power is displayed, even against Arakiya, who turns every natural phenomenon to her own advantage, Priscilla's blade can reach her regardless of her trait.
It is possible to land an attack on an opponent while the effect of the "Soul Marriage Technique" remains intact.

Indeed, this was precisely what had happened during the scene of Priscilla and Arakiya's reunion after nearly a decade apart, and under the present circumstances there was no possibility of Priscilla extending the same mercy.
That was precisely why Yorna had struck Priscilla on the head and gone off to fight like that in front of her.

"Striking my head, as though she were..."

My mother, she wanted to retort, but that was exactly what she was.
And reflecting on her own state of mind, Priscilla admitted to herself, uncharacteristically, that she had grown heated before Yorna rapped her head with the pipe.
Having come this far, she had also been thinking that she herself must be the one to cut Arakiya down.
And yet—

"—The karmic burden of the 'Spirit Eater,' is it."

It was inevitable that a manufactured race such as Arakiya would come with a uniquely twisted trait.
To wield great power, a commensurate price must be paid. This went without saying for Priscilla's "Sunlight Sword" and "Soul Marriage Technique," and it was the same for Arakiya's power as "Spirit Eater."

Naturally, in taking Arakiya into her service, Priscilla had been told what sort of existence she had been born as. She had investigated it herself as well. And yet, her consideration had been lacking.
As for the outcome of giving Arakiya, who could not stand without something to lean on, the pillar that was Priscilla.

"Should a sun such as myself hide away, the world would be as good as sealed in darkness, I suppose."

For ten years, Arakiya had gone on living in a world sealed in darkness.
Even if it was impossible for anyone else to fulfill the role Priscilla should have played, was there truly no one who could have cast even a single ray of light into that shadow?
When she had decided she could not bring Arakiya along, she had thought that Vincent, perhaps, might have done it.

"An impossible notion."

Frowning at her own reflection, Priscilla cast the thought aside.
Back then, still young and precocious, Priscilla — the girl who had been Prisca — had misunderstood. She had thought she could entrust it to her brother, but that had been a mistaken idea.
Each time she witnessed how radically the Battle for the Imperial Capital had veered toward such extremity, and learned of the caliber of the "Nine Divine Generals" — aside from Arakiya — that her brother Vincent Vollachia had gathered, she came to realize it. To understand.

Vincent had intended to fight something vast and entirely different from mere rebellion.
And at the end of that battle, he had never intended to survive. When the fight was over, to whom had he intended to entrust the throne he himself would no longer occupy?
For the sake of that someone, he could not have allowed Arakiya's loyalty to be redirected elsewhere.

"How absurd."

Building everything up as a plan on paper, and then constructing the world in fact to match it exactly.
That was Vincent's strength — but no matter how brilliant a strategist he might be, if the completed form he envisioned was mistaken, the distortion in the world thus constructed became undeniable.
He would someday pay the price for that unwitting error. Perhaps he would face it in the midst of this very battle, even—

"Ten years, we were apart. It's about time, Brother, that you let your little sister go."

Casting the dishonor onto a party absent from this place, Priscilla stepped forward.
The sky burned red with flame, and the earth raged wild at the whim of the "Kaleidoscope." It was as though heaven and earth alike were conspiring to reject the intervention of a sun such as Priscilla.
And yet, against such an arrogant world, the girl who was Priscilla Barielle — no, who had been Prisca Benedict — had but one thing to declare.

"—The world is made to suit my convenience."

At that moment, it would have been wrong to describe what Arakiya felt as humiliation or shame.

Humiliation requires a name to be debased by chagrin.
Shame requires a pride to be dishonored by falling behind.

Arakiya had no attachment whatsoever to either a name or pride.

The most honored rank of general in the empire, the "Two" — the highest position among the "Nine Divine Generals," excepting Cecilus, who was said to be a wholly incorrect existence in every sense — and an absolute powerhouse possessing, in "Spirit Eater," a trait found nowhere else, not merely in the empire but across the entire world.
Blessed with the kind of rank and strength that so many would give anything for, and yet none of it had ever been able to serve as the "pillar" supporting Arakiya as an individual.

Arakiya defined herself as no different from a beast roaming the wilds.
A beast that lives by hunting other creatures takes no pride in its own fangs and claws. —No, perhaps that was only how Arakiya herself thought of it; perhaps beasts had their own pride, their own code.
If so, then Arakiya's self-perception fell even further from that of a beast.

Fire, wind, water, earth, light, shadow — phenomena that could not even be touched.
Phenomena in which no will intervened, that merely brought about what was wished for.

Arakiya wanted to be, not a human, not a beast, but a phenomenon.
Just as a phenomenon feels no humiliation, no shame — that was what she wanted to be.
If so, then—

"I—!"

Against the barrage of attacks, like a hail of swords and bullets, unleashed at her, Arakiya tried to respond by becoming wind. But she could not fully manage it. The wind was seized, and the blow left pain and weight inside her.
A cry of pain escaped her, her expression twisting in anguish. In battles as "Spirit Eater," she was rarely made to taste pain. But that did not mean she was weak to it.

Arakiya had already experienced every conceivable kind of agony since she was a small child.
In order to gain this trait as "Spirit Eater," the adults around her had tried and inscribed upon her every conceivable technique, over and over, again and again.
Pain did not frighten her. Remembering the past did not make her legs falter.

Simply, the fact of being reached — that mattered more to Arakiya than pain itself.

Fire does not grieve, does not die, when doused with water and extinguished.
Was Arakiya nothing more than a fire that, when doused with water and extinguished, cried out in pain and died?

Being denied as not-a-phenomenon, having herself negated in that way — that was what Arakiya feared.

"I—"
"—Silence."

Driven by impatience, as she tried to raise her voice, her opponent's open palm struck her face aside.
With enough force to make her assimilate with flame, everything from Arakiya's neck up was blown away; though it quickly returned to its original shape, the pain remained, and so too did the wound dealt to Arakiya's spirit.

Not humiliation, not shame, but something like having her very existence denied — resisting it, Arakiya, as though clinging to herself, raised the earth and turned her palm toward the diving Yorna.
She did not understand the mechanism, but both Prisca and Yorna had been diverting Arakiya's attacks somewhere. The instant Yorna sealed that technique, the opponent's attacks began to reach Arakiya, and at the same time, Arakiya's attacks began to reach Yorna as well.

"—Ngh"

Just once, just one shot, just one blow would do.
Yorna herself was boosting her physical abilities through some unknown technique, art, or magic, but so long as she did not use the technique to divert damage, Arakiya's attacks would land properly.
If only it would connect—

"With the princess and...!"

If she could eliminate the troublesome Yorna, it would become a space of just herself and Prisca alone.
If only that happened, surely, once again, everything would go well.

"『If there's something you want, isn't it more productive to go get it than to wait around?』"

Yes, Todd had said that too.
Todd's words, strangely, had resonated deeply within Arakiya's heart.

It was not because he said convenient things, nor for any reason rooted in human likes and dislikes.
In Todd's words there was no emotion directed at Arakiya. That was what felt comfortable. Just as people do not love fire or water, Todd felt nothing at all toward Arakiya.
Being treated with such emptiness had been Arakiya's salvation.
And so—

"————"

The branch in her hand burst into flame all at once, from base to tip, and turned to black ash.
The branch was merely something she had picked up, possessing no special power of its own. It only served as a clear marker for where Arakiya directed her power when she chose to use it.
The fact that the wooden stick had burned away entirely was proof of a discharge of firepower with no need to fix its target.

The "Spirit Eater" Arakiya, evaluated as among the strongest in the empire — her maximum firepower was something that, in her entire life, had only ever been unleashed once before, in a skirmish with Cecilus Segmunt.
Because her opponent then had been Cecilus, no damage had resulted; but Arakiya's outrage, which had turned the northern district of the imperial capital into a scorched wasteland, earned her a decree from Emperor Vincent that a second occurrence would not be forgiven. Later, on Chisha's proposal, that scorched wasteland was repurposed as the reservoir — but truly, that single blow had carried enough power to redraw the map.

"—The world is made to suit my convenience."

—And that voice, ringing with confidence and composure, unleashed a slash that swallowed it whole.

The flicker of the "Sunlight Sword" consumed the flame that should have scorched the world black.
Prisca, who had leapt to Yorna's side with her hair ornament shattered, let her long, beautiful hair stream in the searing wind, her eyes — one alight with flame, one not — reflecting Arakiya in both.
And then—

"Mother."

The instant her lips moved, Yorna, having circled behind Prisca, unleashed a ferocious kick.
Matching her own foot to the sole of that launched kick, Prisca converted its force into her own forward momentum and launched herself, closing on Arakiya at a speed that could be mistaken for light.
Without pause, Prisca raised the "Sunlight Sword" she held—

"Arakiya."

With that single word, she sealed all of Arakiya's movement, and struck — a single flash.
Arakiya's reflexive defensive action, her evasion by assimilating with the air — all of it was crushed and mangled, and Prisca's single stroke pierced her whole body without mercy.

"————"

Arakiya's consciousness cut off.
Ironically, just as it had when she turned the northern district of the imperial capital into a scorched wasteland, immediately after unleashing her maximum firepower, and just as it had when she took Cecilus's single stroke — it cut off in the same way.
But at the very last, she heard it.

"My Sunlight Sword cuts what it wishes to cut, burns what it wishes to burn. —And strikes what it wishes to strike."

As she spoke, the falling Arakiya never came to realize that Prisca had turned, not the blade, but the hilt of the "Sunlight Sword" she had swung, toward her.

—That boy laughs. At any moment, no matter what, he is laughing.

Even should the color of the sky change, the earth crack violently apart, and natural disasters never before witnessed by those blue eyes assault the world, even should an unprecedented calamity stand in his path — he laughs.

Is he mad? Ah, he is mad.
Is he drunk? Ah, he is drunk.

Not on battle, not on drink, not on passion, not on blood.
Mad, mad, mad, mad, drunk, drunk, drunk — that boy goes on laughing.

If not for that—

"—A boring actor's stage wouldn't catch the eye of such busy people, would it?"

Tears can spill even when there is nothing at all. But can laughter come from nothing at all?
If not, then laughter is the beginning of something. Laughter signifies the beginning of a story.

Whether it is a story already begun, or one beginning here — that remains unknown.
To laugh, that boy has defined, is proof that one's resolve to take the stage has been settled.
And so—

"Why don't you go ahead and laugh? I went to all this trouble getting everything ready. Might as well have a good hearty laugh about it — makes the one who got done in feel better too!"

"Oh, could it be you find it strange that I'm here? If you're thinking one of your comrades betrayed you or blabbed, that's needless worry! I just sort of wandered my way here on a hunch, that's all!"

Clapping his hands together, laughing brightly, he struck a pose, stamping the ground with his zōri sandals.
Behind him stood the cracked retaining wall of the reservoir, water gushing continuously from it, and before the boy standing there — Cecilus Segmunt — stood the figure of his adversary.
And that was—

"What to do with you — troubling. — Careful consideration required."

—A short-statured "Witch," trailing a great multitude of the dead behind her.
