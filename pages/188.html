Arc 4 Chapter 22: "Weakness"

—In the darkness, Subaru woke from sleep and slowly raised himself up.

He pushed aside the thin blanket that had been covering him, took a light stretch while trying not to let any sound escape, and swallowed back a yawn as he turned his neck to look around, hearing the many sounds of sleeping breath around him. He'd been resting in a corner of the group sleeping in a huddle together, but it seemed he was the only one whose consciousness had surfaced into wakefulness. And no wonder—the color of the sky visible through the great cathedral windows was dark, and the sunlight hadn't so much as shown its face yet.

Without the convenience of a clock he couldn't know the exact time, but he could at least tell that it was still the middle of the night, not yet a time suited for human activity. Normally he'd lie back down for one more stretch of sleep until dawn, but—

"Guess I went to sleep too early to stay asleep now... Man, I miss the days when I could wallow in laziness for as long as I wanted."

Scratching roughly at his head, Subaru folded up his blanket and quietly slipped out of bed. Around the bed he was leaving lay the many people sleeping there—the evacuees from Arlam Village, who along with Roswaal were currently confined within the "Sanctuary." Having taken leave of the house assigned to him within the Sanctuary, Subaru had taken to coming here to spend his nights lying alongside them. The reason wasn't especially deep.

Within the great cathedral, the sleeping spot the residents had given Subaru was against the wall at the front of the sanctuary. Being assigned a relatively comfortable spot like that was no doubt out of consideration from everyone. Though he did have one complaint—as if in exchange, he'd been surrounded on all sides by a solid wall of children. Granted, it was partly because the children, who'd taken a liking to Subaru, had whined that they wanted to be by his side, and partly out of consideration from the adults, trying as best they could within their restricted circumstances not to burden the children more than necessary. Once he sensed that kind of subtlety behind it, he couldn't very well complain.

"Man, thinking about stuff like this... not like me at all."

He gave a wry smile at himself for considering things like consideration and thoughtfulness. Thinking about such complicated matters would only make life more cramped.

Stepping carefully so as not to tread on the children breathing hard in their sleep, Subaru made his way between the residents sleeping like the dead, out of the great cathedral—damp wind greeted him as he emerged, warm air that spoke of neither warmth nor cold, only an unpleasant humidity. Looking up, he saw thick clouds lined across the sky, hiding away the starry sky that had delighted his eyes with such brilliance the night before. The clouds riding the wind moved swiftly, but the cloud layer was thicker still. He couldn't say whether the weather would turn, but it didn't look like it would be a day for clear skies.

"Come to think of it, I haven't run into anything like real rain since I got here. From what everyone's said, you'd think there'd be something like the four seasons, though."

He'd caught wind that, in line with the elemental attributes used in magic, the seasons here seemed to be divided into "Red Day, Blue Day, Yellow Day, Green Day." If you likened it to the four seasons of his original world, you could probably call this something like adding a "rainy season" to the mix. Likely this world had something similar. The temperature right now was neither hot nor cold. Going by the feel of the wind, the impression was of right around rainy season.

"Rainy season's a pain 'cause the laundry won't dry. My futon's already a mess on the underside from being left out year-round, so it's a real bummer not to be able to air it out in the sun on my rare day off... well, I guess it was every day off, back then."

Basically, since he'd spent most of his time lounging around on his futon, there was pretty much never a good time to air it out. Occasionally, his mother, at the end of her patience, would roll Subaru off the futon, snatch it away, and forcibly hang it out to dry so it'd get wrapped in that sunbaked smell—that was standard operating procedure in the Natsuki household.

Reminiscing over those nostalgic days, Subaru worked through his stiffened body with radio-calisthenics-style movements, loosening it up joint by joint. The great cathedral was a building with grounds as vast as its name suggested, and even though everyone slept in a huddle, there was ample space for each person to stretch out their limbs. Blankets had been distributed to everyone as well; there was nothing to complain about there. If there was one complaint—and it was a luxurious one to have—it was that sleeping on the hard bare ground, as they were forced to, put a strain on various parts of the body.

"I miss my own flattened-out futon, or the fluffy bed back at the mansion. If I'm like this after four or five days, the others who've been stuck sleeping in a heap this whole time must be even worse off."

They put on a brave face in front of Subaru, but there was no denying that the burden on the residents was growing more pronounced day by day. At mealtimes, if Subaru clowned around, the people around him would show a smile, but the way they fell quiet otherwise was surely a sign of stress. An evacuation none of them wanted, followed by house arrest at the evacuation site. The lord to whom they ought to have directed that dissatisfaction had gotten himself injured trying to fulfill that role, and the reality was that the residents' anxiety about the future outweighed their dissatisfaction with the situation. By rights, Subaru, standing on the lord's side of things, should have been on the receiving end of more blame-tinged emotion from them than this—

"Guess that just means they're not the type to lash out like that. Relying on the good sense of your own subjects like this... honestly, as someone running things, that's a failing grade."

The truth of the matter was that the reason the people of Arlam Village hadn't resorted to taking their frustrations out on Subaru was purely because a strong sense of gratitude toward him still remained within them. Though Subaru himself didn't rate his own accomplishments very highly, and so simply assumed it was because the evacuees' hearts were honest and upright. However—

"Can't keep relying on everyone's good will like that forever, though."

—It was already the "sixth day" since Subaru and the others had arrived at the Sanctuary.

For the evacuees who had entered the Sanctuary before Subaru's group, nearly another week was added on top of that, meaning they'd effectively been tasting close to two weeks of confinement. Since Subaru himself had relayed news of the safety of those who'd evacuated to the royal capital, the anxiety over families being separated wasn't running especially high, but even so, two weeks cooped up with nothing to do was bound to take a mental toll. It was only a matter of time before the effect of the sympathy strategy—won through Roswaal's self-sacrificing, self-destructive gambit—wore off. Once that happened, what awaited was a clash between the Sanctuary and the villagers, a result neither side, wanting to win support from both, desired.

"Man, seriously, this is rough. What do I do..."

"——Don't you go takin' one more step past there."

Twisting his neck, lost in thought, Subaru had the foot he was about to plant halted by a threat. Subaru froze in the awkward half-raised posture, foot lifted off the ground, and slowly turned around, neck still twisted at that angle. In his field of view, all that spread out was the trees of the forest, sunk in dim twilight. He'd wandered a bit from the great cathedral, taking a stroll around the area. To such a Subaru—

"Takin' a stroll this early in the mornin'—dunno if that's good taste or just plain carefree of ya. Feels like a case of 'Mystified by the Red and Blue Berries, Mizzled and Mazed.'"

Mouthing yet another unfamiliar-yet-familiar idiom, a blond young man dropped down from overhead—from the branches of the trees—descending like a bird. Garfiel, his short hair standing up on end, landed on all fours on the grass without a sound, and closing one eye, looked up at Subaru, who was watching him in that same posture, from below.

"Ya ain't hardly surprised at all. Where's the fun in scarin' ya if that's all I get?"

"If I'd run into you with zero warning, sure, I'd be shocked out of my mind, but I had a feeling I might run into you if I wandered around this area. Though I'll admit I wasn't expecting the tree-drop."

"So ya were lookin' for me, huh?"

Garfiel stood up, a face full of suspicion, standing half a head shorter than Subaru right in front of him. Puffing out his chest at him for no particular reason, Subaru answered with an "Yeah," and said,

"I figured it was too early in the morning to expect much, but I'm glad I found you. ...By the way, mind if I ask what that threat just now was about?"

"Ain't nothin' that big a deal. Past here, this side of the forest's my huntin' grounds. Anybody wanders in by mistake, I just bite 'em and snap their neck, that's all."

"That's not 'that's all'! That's a huge deal!"

Subaru's shocked shout rang out at the outrageous remark tossed off so casually. It was enough to slice through the stillness of the forest that hung between the dead of night and early morning, enough to send the sleeping birds and beasts fleeing from their perches—

"...Tch. Oi, thanks to that most of the critters done run off. Whaddaya gonna do about that."

"Cowards, all of 'em. Meat off scrawny weaklings like you lot would just make me weak by association merely from eatin' it—somethin' along those lines, and let it go graciously, how's that?"

"That's only gonna work if you're fine with meat vanishin' clean off your plate startin' today."

"My bad! I didn't mean it like that! I get it! I'll go fishing with Otto at the river today, call it even, all right?!"

Having nothing to do as a merchant these days, Otto had apparently gotten hooked on fishing, spending his days heading to the river to dangle a line. Unfortunately, though, his catch was invariably a big fat zero. The way he only ever lost his bait was very much in character. When Otto's name came up, Garfiel twisted the corner of his mouth, baring a fang.

"Hah, that bro's abandoned by luck to a degree that don't even make sense. Ain't stupid, ain't clumsy either. And yet, look at him. That's a case of 'Denzen So Weak to Rain Even Heaven Weeps.'"

"He's kind of the comic-relief type, or the unique-character type, I guess—the position where every family's got one guy like that, and having him around livens up the conversation. He's saved me in a lot of ways... mostly as a way to change my mood."

It was a merciless assessment of Otto, one that would've had the man himself protesting with tears in his eyes if he'd been there to hear it. Hearing that faint-praise-that-wasn't-really-praise, Garfiel scratched his own ear with a finger and muttered "Change your mood, huh..." before saying,

"So, this here mood-changin' thing—did it actually do somethin'?"

"Meaning?"

"Don't play dumb. It's plain as day the folks in the cathedral are gettin' close to their limit. Ain't somethin' you and that bro can keep coverin' for forever."

"Ouch, right where it hurts—and turns out you're actually paying pretty close attention around here."

A dead-on jab at exactly what was troubling Subaru. Receiving Subaru's praise, mixed with surprise, Garfiel wrinkled his muzzle and said,

"When it comes right down to it, I'm the one who's face to face with the folks in the cathedral more'n anybody, one way or another. Even the hag, and the rest of 'em, barely show their faces there. Only natural, ain't it."

"It's not exactly rationing, but you're the one cooking the meals for them, right? First time I saw you doing that, I thought my eyes were playing tricks on me."

"If ya wanna eat somethin' good, learnin' to make it yourself is the surest way to suit your own tongue. And don't go changin' the subject."

Stepping forward, Garfiel raised a finger and pointed it at Subaru's throat.

"Them hostages, they're gettin' about as close to their limit as it gets. ——How much longer you plannin' on flailin' around uselessly?"

"Flailing around uselessly—I don't know what you mean by that..."

"Hah. Listen to ya. What else would ya call that, that mess, if not flailin' around uselessly? ——Stuck stompin' in the same spot for three whole days now."

Hurling mockery, plain for anyone to see, at Subaru's weakening tone, Garfiel bared that judgment with a click of his teeth. Subaru opened his mouth, meaning to deny his words, but couldn't immediately find anything to say and ended up faltering into silence. Seeing that, disappointment settled into Garfiel's narrowed green eyes.

"Hey now. For real, what d'ya actually think? Let's talk it out straight, guts bared."

"Guts bared, huh—that phrase brings back some bad memories, so I'd rather not hear it... though I guess this isn't really the mood for that joke."

Garfiel's small frame hunched further, back curving. There was no hostility in his manner, but a fierce, barely-concealed anger seeped out from his entire body. There probably wasn't any concern of him turning that violence directly, physically, on Subaru, but—

"First, let me say this clearly. I'm on Emilia's side. I believe she can do it, and I don't doubt her for a second. So even if it takes time, I believe without a doubt that she'll break through the Trial."

"That's exactly where I'm callin' bullshit. That princess raised in a box—the weakling who's been draggin' herself out cryin' her eyes out three days runnin' now—can she really pull it off?"

Subaru's and Garfiel's views clashed head-on. Against the sharpness of Garfiel's gaze, not even bothering to hide his contempt, Subaru sharpened his own sanpaku eyes to their limit in defiance—his feelings for Emilia weren't something he could afford to lose to.

It was the morning of the sixth day since Subaru and the others had come to the Sanctuary. And three days had already passed since the Trials began, since the day Subaru broke through the First Trial. As for what had happened in that time—

"Never figured that if the one travelin' with ya couldn't break through the first one, the second Trial wouldn't even start. Thanks to that, ain't been a lick of progress on the Trial these past three days."

"————"

"Wouldn't it've been better off if it was just you alone? At least then ya wouldn't've had to trip over a stone ya'd already cleared, draggin' a deadweight along with ya."

Garfiel's words, not hiding their fierce edge, precisely described the reality that had befallen Subaru and the others.

The Trials of the Tomb—ever since Subaru broke through the First Trial three days ago, they had shown no progress whatsoever. The reason was simple. Emilia still hadn't broken through the First Trial. That is, her own "past."

"The past everyone's gotta settle is different for each person. Unlike me, who's just been living an easy life, of course she's got a lot weighing on her. I don't think of that as her being a deadweight."

"That so. Real sweet of you, seein' as you're stuck on her. But not everybody can just watch over her patient as you, y'know? Honestly, every minute this drags on, my opinion of the princess just keeps droppin' and droppin'."

"That's..."

"Just admit it already. If it weren't for the princess, at least you could be takin' on the second Trial. That'd be a hell of a lot more realistic a shot at clearin' the condition for freein' this place—anybody lookin' at it can see that plain as day."

Garfiel's tempting proposal—but it was a decision that would trample all over Emilia's resolve. It would betray an important part of the design Roswaal had built this whole situation around, and it would also drag through the mud the nobility of Emilia that Subaru believed in. There was no way he could ever affirm it. But what made Subaru hesitate to clearly shake his head at those words was—

"If we give it time, she'll definitely make it through. Rushing her, pressuring her—that wouldn't accomplish anything. And yet..."

"You know as well as anybody that time's the one thing we ain't got, right? Startin' with me, the short-tempered folks in the Sanctuary are already runnin' outta patience, and the hostages are at their limit too, this confinement draggin' on so long. ——It's only a matter of time before that swelled-up discontent bursts, ain't it?"

——In the end, it all came down to a matter of limited time.

Whatever the "past" Emilia faced looked like, Subaru had no doubt that she would ultimately overcome it. But overcoming something that deep-rooted would take her time. If there were any way Subaru himself could help, he had the resolve to face any hardship for it without hesitation. But that "past," already gone by, existed only within her, and Subaru, who hadn't been there, had no opening to reach out a hand. Just as Subaru had gained, thanks to Rem, the resolve to face his own "past," he couldn't help but wish he could become that same thing for Emilia.

Given time, the Trial would be overcome. But there was no time for that now. As Garfiel had said, the two groups within the Sanctuary were shaving away at their limited time, approaching their limit. No more time could be spent on this. And so, Subaru arrived at one conclusion.

"——I have a proposal."

Touching a hand to his chin, Subaru forced the words out. Seeing the change in Subaru's expression, Garfiel closed one eye and twisted his mouth into a grin.

"Well, let's hear it, then."

"We should both be able to agree that the part we each need to worry about is time. I believe Emilia will break through the Trial, so I think what she needs is time. On your side, meanwhile, it's the time limit before the balance collapses that's got you at your wit's end. No issue with that much, right?"

"Can't say you're wrong. If I'm addin' one thing, though, keep in mind I really am doubtin' whether that princess can pull off the Trial at all."

"...On that point, I think you and I are just going to stay at odds. Anyway, if we agree that time is the sticking point, then you should be willing to consider my proposal."

At Subaru's argument, Garfiel, still touching the white scar on his forehead, said nothing. Sensing that his manner was urging him to continue, Subaru gave a single nod and said,

"Right now, the state of the evacuees under house arrest is at its limit. Before long they'll break, and in the worst case, the group will split apart from within the Sanctuary itself."

"Don't much care if that happens, personally. Even if a hundred or two hundred of them village folks bunch up together, all that means is I flip 'em right back over."

"Forty-two people. ...It's not about how you'd handle it. It's about an unwanted clash happening, and the damage that comes with it. You don't actually want to raise a hand against people you cook meals for every day either, do you?"

"Well... that's true enough."

Garfiel looked away, clicking his tongue as if caught out. Sensing the good flow of things from his manner, Subaru inwardly nodded and continued,

"So, to avoid that clash, I want to request the release of the people under house arrest. Right now, I don't think there's much hostage value left in those people. What do you say?"

"Oi, oi, hold up. That's a different matter entirely, ain't it? What d'ya mean there's no hostage value in 'em, first off?"

"Originally, the reason you all kept those people under house arrest was as a means to lure us—or really, Emilia—out here, right? That plan worked, and we came into the Sanctuary, and we're taking the Trials in accordance with the conditions you wanted. Food, care, and guarding the hostages don't just spring up from nowhere endlessly either. In fact, you going out hunting at an hour like this, when you can't even tell if it's night or morning—that's not unrelated to this, is it?"

As far as Subaru could tell, the Sanctuary, forest included, was an unusually vast stretch of land, but the portion actually in use was a tiny fraction of it—to put it in extreme terms, his estimate was that the number of Halfs living in the Sanctuary wasn't all that different from the number of evacuees from Arlam Village. Which meant, put simply, the Sanctuary's food consumption had doubled from before. Given the nature of the land, the odds of them procuring supplies through traveling merchants or the like seemed low, so their food situation likely relied mainly on hunting and homegrown crops. ——Hence,

"There's no need to keep hanging onto hostages who are just straining your stores. Whether or not the hostages are still here, we can't exactly abandon the Trial partway through anymore anyway."

"Guess that's true. After all, the moment we set foot in the Sanctuary, the Half—the princess—got bound by the land's curse. If the princess wants to leave here, no matter what, she's gotta break through the Trial... ah, so that's the angle."

As he spoke, it seemed the meaning of Subaru's proposal sank in, and Garfiel nodded several times, his cheek twisting with a cold, cruel edge. Seeing that, Subaru understood that Garfiel's head was by no means slow to turn. If his usual manner wasn't exactly a pose, then, given the right conditions, his judgment was—

"So the idea is, if ya free the hostages, ya dodge the inevitable collapse, whether it's over food or an internal split. Real deal is, the hostages ain't got no obstacle to breakin' outta the Sanctuary 'cept us."

"If everything up to this point has gone the way you all planned, you should want to carry it through to the end, shouldn't you? Your goal is to get yourselves freed from the Sanctuary—it's not like you want to go down together with us."

"If I'm respectin' the hag's opinion straight outta the gate that's how it'd go, but... eh, small details, whatever."

Waving a hand, Garfiel took Subaru's opinion in, for the time being. Then he touched his chin for a while, mulling it over, and said,

"So why bring this to me in the first place, huh? The one in charge of this place ain't me, it's the hag. If ya wanted to convince somebody and get this movin', takin' it straight to the hag'd be the surest bet. Not to talk myself down, but comin' to me mighta just made things more complicated."

"That's exactly why I'm bringing it to you first—so it doesn't get complicated. I figured if I explain the merits and demerits properly, Ryuzu-san could be persuaded. But in that case, how you'd react is the hard part to predict."

If it was Ryuzu, who could judge both good sense and practical benefit, she likely wouldn't flatly refuse Subaru's proposal. In fact, Subaru had confidence he could win her agreement if he talked with her. But in the case where he settled things with the top of the chain of command first and then had to face Garfiel afterward—

"No matter how you slice it, winning you over is the hard part. Unfortunately, my own self-assessment is that if you come at me by force, I've got no way to fight back. I figured it'd be better to handle the sticking point first so I don't have to worry about it later."

"Real crafty way of thinkin', ain't it, oi. So basically, that's it, huh? Takin' this proposal to the hag, my existence's just a pain in the ass in the way, and you're sayin' if I got a problem with it, come at me with fists, huh?"

"Weren't we just having a rational conversation a second ago—why's it rolling in that direction now..."

"The time I got for usin' my head to talk is three minutes, tops. Already blown past that limit—nothin' I say now's gonna matter anyway."

"What are you even saying, so confidently..."

Subaru raised both hands in a gesture of surrender at Garfiel, who flashed a clenched fist. Of course, Garfiel wasn't being serious about it either. He let out a bored-sounding sigh and said,

"Hah, dumb stuff this early in the mornin'. Fine, do what ya want. Long as ya can convince the hag, I ain't got no reason to butt in. Either way, they're just a pain to deal with. If ya wanna take 'em out, go ahead and do it."

"Right, then, I'll take you up on that..."

"——'Cept, there's a condition."

Just as Subaru was about to let out a sigh of relief, having cleared the biggest hurdle, cold water was thrown on it. Garfiel raised a finger and pointed it at Subaru, whose brow furrowed.

"I'm takin' your so-called proposal here. Ya gotta take on a bit of what's on my side of things too, or this don't add up."

"...There's benefit for your side too, isn't there. Food, and avoiding an internal conflict."

"Don't much matter to me either way, y'know? If it comes down to seriously not havin' enough food, I could just thin out the hostages. Even if they snap and start rampagin', I'm plenty enough on my own to clean that up. Our positions ain't equal here, y'know?"

"...What's the condition, then?"

As Subaru forced the words out through what looked like gritted teeth, Garfiel clicked his canines together again, saying "Now we're talkin'." He then looked Subaru up and down from head to toe and said,

"My condition—no, the Sanctuary's condition for lettin' 'em go is simple. You take the Trial. That'd settle things a whole lot faster."

"——! Wait, that's not right. If we do that, the whole premise..."

Certainly, that was a thought that had crossed Subaru's mind more than once. But it was strictly a last resort, an option he wanted to avoid if at all possible. More than anything, doing that would mean everything Emilia had struggled for up to now would—

"Seems like you're misunderstandin' somethin', so lemme set ya straight... me and the hag and the rest, we don't care who's the one that does it, so long as we get freed from the Sanctuary."

"————"

"Havin' the princess be the one to overcome it, wantin' her praised by the hostages and the hag and the rest—that's your business. Wantin' her to settle some past, whatever it is, that's been naggin' at her—that's your business too. All of it, every bit, is your problem."

Subaru couldn't argue against Garfiel's words. His argument was entirely reasonable, and Subaru, who hadn't taken their circumstances into account, had no room to interject. Just as he said, having Emilia take the Trial, wanting her to be the one to overcome it—all of it stemmed from their own circumstances and intentions. And further, he said,

"——In the first place, is there really any need to overcome the past at all?"

"Huh?"

"Three days, man, three days. I've been right there with you lot at the tomb, watchin' that princess take the Trial and come out a wreck, every damn time. Honestly, I can't stand watchin' it anymore."

"Can't stand... what do you mean by that..."

"Ain't she puttin' way too much on herself, hurtin' herself way too much? Chargin' forward like she's gotta do it, gotta do it, and then comin' back like that, mopin' and apologizin' for not bein' able to. So why the hell do you lot still want to keep makin' that princess go through the Trial?"

What Garfiel was describing was the state Emilia had been in over these past three days of taking the Trial. On the night after the Trial began, Emilia had challenged it again, but faced with her "past" once more, she hadn't been able to overcome it. And what was more, Subaru, who had tried to face the Trial alongside her that same time, hadn't even been able to begin the Trial at all. At first Subaru, left behind at the tomb, hadn't understood what was going on, but after hearing the words of Emilia—who'd returned partway through the Trial—about the reason someone had told her, within the Trial, that Subaru's Trial hadn't begun, he'd come to understand.

Apparently, the Second Trial took place in the space beyond where the First Trial was cleared.

Within the tomb, in the space where the First Trial took place, there was a sealed door at the back of the square room, and Subaru had assumed that once he cleared all the Trials, he'd be able to go through that door—but in reality, the Second Trial awaited beyond it, and the qualification to go there could only be obtained by first overcoming the First Trial. In other words, Subaru alone could challenge the Second Trial on his own. And yet, knowing that, the reason he hadn't gone on ahead alone up to now was—

"Emilia will definitely overcome the Trial. That's why we..."

"Ain't it 'cause that expectation of yours is too heavy that the princess is sufferin' like that? Forcin' her to face memories that hurt her bad enough to end up in that state—is that what you lot want, is that what the princess herself wants to do? Can't figure it, not with my dumb head."

"Emilia's... will..."

Garfiel's words, spoken while scratching his head—but to Subaru, they landed like a splash of cold water on a face still fresh from sleep. Up until this point, Subaru had respected Emilia's resolve in facing the Trial, and had intended to support her, more devotedly than anyone, through it. No matter how agonizing the road, he'd meant to keep reaching out a hand for as long as she didn't buckle at the knee. All without ever confirming where the will of that woman, who kept rising to her feet again and again, was actually pointed.

Come to think of it, Subaru realized he didn't even know why Emilia was trying to become queen in the first place. The declaration he'd heard from her in the hall of the Royal Selection had only been an expression of her will to stand as an equal among those around her—it had never once been the reason she was trying to become queen. Emilia's upbringing, one that hinted at a past of enduring unjust treatment and unjust judgment over and over—within all that, what had she thought, what had she felt, what had she believed in, to aim for the throne? While fervently wishing to be her support, Subaru had neglected even to ask that most basic question he should have asked first.

To begin with, how had Emilia and Roswaal even met? Why was Roswaal trying to make her, a half-elf, queen? That she had the qualification to be queen—the qualification of being the Dragon's Priestess—was proven by the jewel in her emblem. But how had Roswaal come to have the opportunity to let it touch her hand in the first place? What mutual interests had aligned between Emilia and Roswaal to bring them into a cooperative relationship—Subaru knew none of it. He had come this far without knowing.

"Dunno what's got you so shook, but if there ain't nothin' more to say, I'm headin' out, oi. Middle of a hunt here... 'bout that proposal from before, if you're willin' to take my condition, go ahead and run it by the hag yourself. Rest of it, ain't my problem."

Shrugging at Subaru, who stood rooted in place, Garfiel's figure vanished into the forest lit by the dawn. Before he knew it, the curtain of night had already retreated before the light of morning, and the forest, which had sunk into dense, dark night, had settled into the stillness of morning—that time of day when morning dew settles onto the leaves.

Left behind, Subaru looked up at the sky. Overhead, through the gaps between the trees, the sun peeked out just barely from between the thick clouds—only to be hidden again immediately, dropping a mere instant of light onto the world before vanishing. Narrowing his eyes at that single moment of light, Subaru started walking.

"What I was looking at. I faced my 'past,' settled it, and I think that was for the best. But Emilia..."

Subaru had assumed everyone could put a past behind them and walk on. By bidding farewell to what had already passed, a person could move forward—healed all too much by the warm memories he himself had gained, he'd let himself believe that. Because the people around him had all, without exception, been kinder to him than he deserved, Subaru had decided that the "past" was something one ought to look back on and settle. That way of thinking of his—

"—S-Subaru?"

—just how heavy had it weighed on this silver-haired girl, hunched over, hugging her knees in the corner of the room?

In the house given to her, Emilia had let her body drop beside the bed, sitting on the cold floor, spending the time in nothing but quiet, quiet stillness. There was no surprise in her, someone who was supposed to be weak in the mornings, being awake at this early hour. Her eyes, turned toward him, were bloodshot, and her beautiful, dignified face bore deep traces of exhaustion and tears. ——That she hadn't slept a wink was plainer than fire.

Noticing Subaru had come, she turned her tear-streaked face away so he wouldn't see it, and said,

"Ah, sorr—sorry. I-Is it already time? Is it time? I-It's early, isn't it... but I have to do it. I have to try harder... i-it's time for the Trial, isn't it?"

"Emilia-tan."

"I-I'll be fine. Today for sure, this time for sure, I'll... yeah, I'll definitely manage it this time. I'm starting to understand what happens in the Trial now. See, there's a pattern, right? Like Subaru said, yeah, that, it's, see, I'm figuring it out... yeah, so, I'll be fine, really..."

"Emilia-tan, it's fine. It's not even night yet—last night hasn't even ended. Morning's still ahead. There's still plenty of time before then."

"I-I can tell when you're lying. I mean, look... it's dark outside, isn't it. If it's morning, it should be bright... ah, but, I have plans to talk with the minor spirits today..."

Looking up at Subaru, throwing out words in a rush, Emilia's eyes trembled violently. In them was surprise and anger at herself for failing to keep her promise, and just as it looked like she might resolve that with self-recrimination,

"Emilia."

"Ah..."

Catching her hand as it started to rise, Subaru laced his fingers with hers. Emilia stared blankly at their joined hands, then slowly let his black eyes reflect her own image, and said,

"I... I..."

"Right now, it's just me here. So it's fine to say weak things, however much you need to. There's no need to rush, no need to push yourself. I'm on your side. No matter what."

"Subaruuu..."

Clinging to the hand held out to her, Emilia called Subaru's name in a thin voice, bowing her head. Drawn in, arm and all, into his embrace as she sat, Subaru sat down beside her. Stroking her silver hair slowly with his free hand, the strength gradually drained from her body, and after a while, the sound of relieved, sleeping breath reached him.

She was utterly exhausted. Exhausted enough that she couldn't get through the night alone, exhausted enough to depend entirely on Subaru like this. Glancing beside him at Emilia, breathing softly in her sleep, Subaru brushed a finger lightly against her beloved cheek, tracing the marks of her tears.

——And then, he decided: this really was the limit.
