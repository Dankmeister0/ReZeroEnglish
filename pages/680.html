Arc 8 Chapter 61: "Palladio Manesque"

—Palladio Manesque was one of the imperial princes who contended against Vincent Vollachia, the seventy-seventh Emperor of the Vollachia Empire, in the Imperial Selection Ceremony.

In the seventy-seventh Imperial Selection Ceremony, one of the fiercest in the empire's history, he had allied with Lamia Godwin, a candidate regarded as a frontrunner, and together they had driven Vincent Abelux—not yet Emperor at the time—and Prisca Benedict to within a hair's breadth of defeat.

In the end, Palladio's abilities were deemed too dangerous, and he was forced out of the Imperial Selection Ceremony when Vincent and Prisca joined forces to block him. But the fault for that narrow defeat lay nowhere with Palladio himself.
The cause of his loss was that Lamia, his ally, had been eliminated far too early, fatally shifting the tide of the Ceremony in a manner entirely beyond Palladio's control.

"Truly, she was a hopeless fool of a sister, all glib talk and nothing beneath it."

Lamia, whose existence had already been extinguished, meeting an end as unremarkable as her boasts had been hollow.
When Lamia had launched her attack on the column of dragon carriages fleeing in miserable retreat from the capital, Palladio had lent her his eyes at her request. And yet Lamia had died a pitiful death in defeat. In life and in death alike, she had left nothing to match her big talk—there was no calling her anything but a foolish sister.
Sneering at Lamia's disgraceful showing, Palladio narrowed his three golden eyes—the mark of the Undead, including the third eye upon his brow—and fixed his gaze on the battlefield.

Before him, an immense host of the Undead advanced in ranks, waging a relentless and ghastly assault against their target city, wielding their inexhaustible lives and obsession as weapons in pursuit of its fall.
Commanding this tide of Undead that blanketed the earth, bringing down the city in question—the 'Fortress City' of Garkla—and thereby destroying, once and for all, the misguided Vollachia Empire as it had stood until today: that was Palladio's role.
That was Palladio's mission as one chosen by Heaven.

—Palladio was born of the blood of the Evil Eye Tribe, a race blessed by the world itself.

In ancient times, the Evil Eye Tribe was said to have guided the mediocrity of the human race with their extraordinary powers—a race favored by Heaven, granted as a whole the kind of Divine Protection normally bestowed upon only a rare, chosen few.
And yet the Evil Eye Tribe, too, had seen its people driven from history's center stage, their superior power feared by others—an ill-fated race. Palladio bore the mission of saving his kin, who had fallen into a sea of suffering.
He had to prove it: that the Evil Eye Tribe was the most superior race in this world.
And to that end—

"An empire built by cowardly wretches like you? I will tear it down and remake it myself."

Running his fingers through his wavy green hair, Palladio twisted his Undead features as the images of Vincent and Prisca rose, infuriatingly, in his mind.
That the two of them had conspired to survive together, defying the unwritten law that only one may remain in the Imperial Selection Ceremony, spoke to a baseness of character that, as a fellow scion of the Vollachia imperial line, Palladio could only despise. Given as much, it would come as no surprise if, even during the Ceremony itself, some contemptible scheme had been carried out, trampling underfoot the weight of the empire's long history. —No, rather, it was only natural to assume such a scheme had existed.

"How else could I possibly have fallen behind the likes of you?"

Drawing on the blood of the Heaven-chosen Evil Eye Tribe and wielding his extraordinary gifts, Palladio had cut down every enemy before him and secured his standing as a prince of Vollachia.
That he, who upheld the empire's Iron-Blooded Code above all else, should be inferior to weaklings who did nothing but cling to one another and scheme—that was simply unacceptable.
And so—

"—Vincent, Prisca, I will see the both of you dealt with."

Then Palladio, possessing both the bloodline and the royal Vessel befitting one who would rule, would govern the Vollachia Empire once it had been leveled and restored to its proper form.

As for how he would rule, Palladio intended to model himself after the man he had long regarded as the greatest Emperor in the empire's history—the 'Thorn Emperor,' Eugard Vollachia, who had ruled the Vollachia Empire through pain and terror.
Eugard's rule, in which he was said to have bound the very hearts of his own people with thorns to make the Emperor's authority known to all, was precisely the ideal Palladio aspired to.
Later generations had woven foolish tales claiming the 'Thorn Emperor' lived and died for his love of some common village girl, but once Palladio ascended the throne, every last one of those tales would be burned to ash and buried.

The basis for the confidence behind Palladio's calculations was, of course, his gift as a member of the Evil Eye Tribe—the very core of his exceptional nature—and the extraordinary power that came with it.

"——"

He closed both his eyes and focused his consciousness on the third Evil Eye upon his brow.
At once, Palladio's sight no longer confined itself to the cramped view of the battlefield before him; it became a vast, heavenly eye that grasped the entire surrounding expanse in full.
It was, quite literally, the vantage of the sun—Palladio's third eye fused his own sight with the sun itself, granting him the sweeping, world-surveying perspective of a ruler looking down upon all below.
With this power, Palladio carried out his duty as commander of the Undead host with due majesty.

A lesser man might claim that victory could simply be forced through with the Undead's inexhaustible numbers, but to push through by such a crude, brute-force scheme was no war fit for an Emperor.
Only by bringing one's foe to their knees through both strength and cunning could one truly be called an embodiment of Vollachia's Iron-Blooded Code.

"So, you've prepared skilled hands and ample supplies. It seems fortune has blessed you with capable vassals—but that, Vincent, is the very limit of what you are."

The coordinated attacks between the troops stationed on the walls and the elite soldiers sallying out were admirable indeed; despite their superior numbers, the Undead had yet to break through even the first layer of the fortress city's defenses. And yet, the fact that the defenders had committed some of their finest cards so early on was nothing if not a sign of anxiety over their own strength.
After all, Palladio's Evil Eye had already seen through to the true state of affairs within the city—most of its population were refugees who had fled the capital and other towns and villages, useless burdens with no power to fight.
Holding fast behind the thickest, largest of their walls was the only thread they had left to cling to.

"Let me teach the fools who cannot see that thread for the fragile, fleeting illusion it is: how pitiably narrow the world looks through the eyes of you two-eyed creatures."

Mocking their foolish resistance, Palladio raised a hand and sent his voice flying to a place far distant.
This, too, was a power innate to Palladio as a member of the Evil Eye Tribe—a telepathic voice that could reach anyone from whom he had obtained hair, nails, or some other part of the body, no matter the distance between them.
With it, Palladio unleashed the decisive blow that would bring the fortress city to its end.
It was—

"—Send out the flock of undead Flying Dragons. Let us teach these wretches, so fixated on their walls, what an Emperor's way of war truly looks like."

"—Enemy Flying Dragon Squadron has crossed the great mountain behind the fortress and entered the airspace above it! They've dropped enemy troops directly in—Undead have broken into the city!"

A messenger came bursting into the command room, reporting the shift in the situation between labored breaths.
At the walls, the grinding ordeal of a simple, brute-force numbers assault had continued, but now the enemy had struck from the air—the single greatest gap in Garkla's defenses.
The city's greatest fortress, carved into the flank of a towering mountain, boasted enough sheer resilience that even if the walls encircling the city were breached, it would not easily fall—but against the sky, it stood utterly defenseless.

"So, once things started to drag, they finally moved. This use of troops—no doubt it's just as we expected: Lord Palladio Manesque himself is in command."

"The Vollachia prince who contended against His Majesty in the Imperial Selection Ceremony, is that right?"

Otto murmured this to Serena, who frowned at the report and traced a finger along the white blade-scar on her face.
Among those who had thrown in with the Undead, his was one of the names flagged beforehand as requiring particular caution—a rare wielder of the Evil Eye Tribe's inherited power, sadistic in temperament and cold-blooded to the core.
Striking an unguarded blind spot against a foe who had concentrated their forces in one place was, for the side on the receiving end, unbearable—but it was standard practice in war all the same.

In a siege especially, a single small breach could often bring about total collapse.
On a scale as large as this, having such a hole opened up would be fatal.
And so—

"It's exactly the meta, isn't it."

"—? The meta?"

"No, don't mind it. More importantly..."

Serena tilted her head at the unfamiliar phrase; Otto gave her a light wave before entrusting the task of issuing orders to the messenger to Berstetz, standing at the room's center, with a simple nod.
Taking the cue, Berstetz opened his thread-thin eyes just a fraction, and said—

"Give them the order. Tell them it's time for the 'Special Force.'"

Even a phrase so unfamiliar on his tongue, he managed to wield with perfect precision.

△▼△▼△▼△

—It was an arrow loosed with the intent of delivering the decisive blow in the battle for the 'Fortress City' of Garkla.

The usefulness of Flying Dragon Riders was common knowledge to any citizen of the empire, needing no explanation—but applying that to the conditions of the Undead required both rider and Flying Dragon alike to have been revived.
Those who met that condition and joined the Undead side as undead Flying Dragon Riders were few in absolute number. For that reason, their use had to be cautious, reserved only for the moment that truly mattered.

'—Send out the flock of undead Flying Dragons. Let us teach these wretches, so fixated on their walls, what an Emperor's way of war truly looks like.'

The order came flying in by telepathy from the commander of the Undead host.
Obeying that strange voice resounding directly within their skulls, the handful of undead Flying Dragon Riders led their undead Flying Dragons aloft, beating their beloved mounts' wings to seize the airspace above the fortress city.
Clearing the height of the great mountain that loomed behind the fortress city and also served as the border with another nation was a life-or-death gamble even for a seasoned Flying Dragon Rider. Ignoring that very obstacle—the part where lives were normally at stake—the undead Flying Dragon Riders accomplished their mission objective without hesitation.

"——"

Below the Undead's golden eyes lay the great fortress, guarded by its sturdy walls and stacked stonework.
What would be thrown into that fortress were Dragon Ships borne by nearly a hundred undead Flying Dragons, along with the raging Undead riding within them.
Both the carriers and the payload were Undead—a blasphemous strategy indifferent to the boundary between life and death.

"Go."

At that short command, the neighing undead Flying Dragons let fall the Dragon Ships they carried, right where they hung.
Heedless of falling speed or landing, it was an act of savagery deserving no better name than a discard—tumbling over and over, meant to deliver Vollachia's death blow, as it plunged down into the heart of the city.
And then—

"We are—!!"
"""THE STRONGEST! THE STRONGEST! THE STRONGEST——!!"""

The band that came bursting out of the mountainside like a storm slammed into the flank of the falling Dragon Ship and shattered the empire's death blow to splinters from within.

△▼△▼△▼△

—Appointed to the 'Special Force,' he had spent a stretch of time so frustrating it belied the weight of the responsibility placed upon him.

And yet, frustrating as it was, he could say there had been no fear of it all coming to nothing.
Because Natsuki Schwarz was a man who had already earned Gustav Morello's trust.
Whatever Schwarz decided to do, Gustav could lend his aid unconditionally; whatever Schwarz was certain would happen, Gustav could believe in without needing a reason; and whatever enemy Schwarz declared he would defy, Gustav had resolved to resist with all his strength.

So, ever since the battle for the fortress city had begun, he had been able to endure and wait his turn patiently, all while sensing the fierce fighting the other members of the Pleiades Battalion were waging without him.
Still, being able to wait and waiting without it being painful were two different matters.

"RAAAAGH——!"

Roaring with all his strength, Gustav swung his four arms wildly, rampaging without restraint across the deck of the plummeting Dragon Ship he had boarded.
The Undead aboard the Dragon Ship had driven their own weapons into the deck, anchoring their bodies so as not to be thrown off—and that cost them precious time in responding to Gustav and his men's surprise assault.

Those who had joined the 'Special Force' alongside Gustav were not Schwarz's own honor guard—Hiain, Weitz, and Idra—but rather the old hands who had spent long years as sword slaves on the 'Sword Slave Isle': men Gustav knew well.
As governor of Ginunhive, Gustav bore the responsibility of running the 'Sword Slave Isle' without incident, and he understood the sword slaves' abilities better than anyone.
He'd never imagined that the scope of his duties as governor would prove useful in a moment like this.

"Sending rabbits to kill a wolf, are they."

"We are the wind—no one can cage the wind!"

"Kuhahahaha! A brawl, a brawl! Nothing gets the blood pumping like a fight to the death——!!"

The lone wolf Jawsrough, along with the hot-blooded, skilled fighters Fenmel and Mirzak, swung their weapons alongside Gustav aboard the Dragon Ship, bringing down the Undead one after another.
The mysterious morale-boosting phenomenon enveloping the Pleiades Battalion transformed these battle-hardened sword slaves into veteran warriors of the highest order, letting them display their full skill even under conditions as dire as a falling Dragon Ship. —No, if anything, the more their lives were on the line, the more it drew out power beyond what they should have had.

"That's exactly why it was worth choosing them for the suicide squad... no, the 'Special Force.'"

On this battlefield, the role Gustav and his men had taken on carried a risk to life equal to, if not greater than, that of the ones holding the front line against the horde of Undead.
And yet, entrusted with so great a task, Gustav and his men could not afford to die.
Not a single one of them, they had been sternly ordered by the boss of the Pleiades Battalion, was to die.

"This one does not hold with serving two lords. And so this one will not look to you as a master... but a desperate friend's request is the sort of thing one wants to take on, wherever possible."

So saying, Gustav finally wrenched his weapon free of the deck, seized the heads of the Undead moving to intercept him, and smashed four of them together above his own head all at once, crushing them.
He flung aside the crumbling, clattering remains of earth and reached at once for the next foe.

Gustav despised, deeply, the wild and savage temperament of the Multi-Arm Tribe.
He had lived his life sternly disciplining himself, striving as much as possible to govern his own heart and never surrender himself to rage or the urge to fight.
Even within the Vollachia Empire, even while serving as governor of the 'Sword Slave Isle,' he had held fast to that principle.

"RAAAAAAAGH——!!"

But that principle of his—this once, Gustav let himself forget it.
This was a moment for raging on, letting himself be carried by the very Multi-Arm Tribe temperament he had always found so irksome.
And to that end—

"This one and his men are—!!"
"""INVINCIBLE! INVINCIBLE! INVINCIBLE——!!"""

Gustav and the rest of the 'Special Force' carried out their special mission.
For that surging heat was, more than anything, proof that Schwarz and the others were fighting with everything they had for the empire's victory.

△▼△▼△▼△

Draw all attention to sheer numbers, or a conspicuously powerful force, then throw a trump card at whatever stronghold had been left thin as a result.
The strategy Palladio Manesque had adopted, had it succeeded, could have shattered the defenders' plans to pieces and stood as the decisive move settling the outcome of this siege.
But that was only true if it had succeeded.

"If you're going to deploy Undead troops, the first thing you'd think of is a tactic that makes use of the dead's particular traits. Crossing Mount Gildray without regard for death was inevitable."

"And once y'figure the numbers alone aren't breakin' through the wall, well, with the sky sittin' there wide open, o' course you'd wanna play that card, wouldn't ya."

"Still, I didn't expect things to go quite so exactly as read."

With the 'Special Force' deployed to intercept the Undead's airborne assault force targeting the city from above, they had nipped in the bud what should have been the enemy's guaranteed-victory gambit, and everyone in the command post exchanged nods.
As already noted, it had been an entirely sound strategy as far as deploying Undead troops went. But in terms of exploiting an unconventional situation, it was too straightforward, too textbook an operation.
A textbook approach or standard opening is powerful when it lands, but for the same reason, it's that much easier to counter.

"From what I've heard, the reason he lost his life in the Imperial Selection Ceremony was that he clung too rigidly to textbook methods. For better or worse, it seems an Undead's clock stops right where it stopped, doesn't it."

So Serena remarked of the man whose scheme had been read through to the end—she who had sent out her own standing Flying Dragon Squadron and ordered the destruction of the undead Flying Dragon Riders that had appeared overhead.
Palladio Manesque, taken to be the enemy commander—it was true his name had come up as a target requiring caution. But that was only when he made use of the Evil Eye Tribe's traits to support someone else.
In fact, Lamia Godwin's assault on the Coupled Dragon Carriages had, thanks to Palladio's support, very nearly destroyed the empire.
But as luck would have it, the man remained ignorant of how best to put his own talent to use.

"Lord Palladio seems to be under the impression that if you can see your opponent's hand, you can never lose a bet—doesn't he."

"Normally, seeing your opponent's hand would put you at a pretty significant advantage, though."

"Is that so? I'd be happy to make the same bet with you, under the same conditions. Though if you gamble against me, harm might well come to your family."

"This is exactly why I can't stand the Vollachia way of doing things!"

"Greed for victory is a virtue. —You're cut from the same cloth as me, aren't you?"

At Serena's look—as if she'd sized him up as simply bad-natured—Otto's face soured to its very core.
Regardless, it could be said the command post had outmatched the enemy in quality. With his guaranteed-victory strategy crushed, reputation and all, the enemy had no choice but to devise his next move.
And that was—

"So he's playing his prized trump card. —Feeding your forces in piecemeal is the hallmark of a losing battle."

△▼△▼△▼△

"""——!!"""

With a roar like thunder splitting the sky, the three-headed dragon clad in black scales took flight.
Beating its spread wings, it soared over the Undead host, and though countless arrows struck its body, it didn't so much as flinch—the maddened, jet-black Blight Dragon had manifested once more in the skies of the empire.

The majesty of 'Three-Headed' Valgren, once slain by the hand of 'the Extoller,' was a threat beyond any comparison to the other Undead—without question, the true trump card of the Undead host.
With the surprise operation over Mount Gildray having failed, the commander's judgment that he must regroup at once or risk being overwhelmed was correct.

—But this, too, never stepped beyond the bounds of a textbook approach.

"HAAAAAAH——!!"

With that valiant cry, an enormous mass of thorns surged up from the city's walls into the sky, closing in fiercely on the wide-winged Blight Dragon—Kafma Irulux's attack.
The suppressive power that let Kafma single-handedly hold up a front line thick with Undead as far as the eye could see now coiled around the entirety of Valgren's body—its eyes clouded and abnormal—binding its wings before it could tear free.
Of course, against a dragon's raw strength, it was only a matter of time before the thorns were torn apart.

"But this one second—that's my role to fill!"

Veins standing out across his whole body like a mottled pattern, blood trickling from his nose, Kafma hurled his defiant challenge up at Valgren with a face twisted in ferocious resolve.
At the declaration of so puny a creature, the Blight Dragon writhed its three heads as if in fury, and roared.
A dragon's roar, the kind to make all living things freeze in terror—yet on this battlefield, there wasn't a single coward left to cower at the Blight Dragon's cry.

"—Al Clauselia."

Valgren, bound by thorns and frozen mid-air—a rainbow-hued brilliance streaked diagonally across the sky, aimed at one of the writhing necks of the Blight Dragon.
It struck Valgren's right head cleanly, blasting it away at the base.
It was a graceful, magnificent, dazzling stroke of magic worthy of 'the Peerless.'

"Take aim, pleasE!"

Next, the eyes of one of the Blight Dragon's remaining heads were pierced by an arrow of godly speed.
Against the Blight Dragon's massive body it was far too small a target, yet the chief of the 'People of Shudrak' had struck it true, and now turned, calling out sharply to the two beside her.
Of the two she called to, one judged the aim by eye while the other drew, with her whole body, a bow so massive it would take ten ordinary people to pull.

"Do… it nowE!!"

"Smash 'em goodO!!"

The mighty bow the two of them drew together struck home on the blinded Blight Dragon's head with a boom loud enough to pierce the stars, blasting away everything above its jaw and reducing the three-headed beast—already down to two—to just one.

"""——!!"""

Even so, Valgren, having lost both its left and right heads, threw its remaining head back to the sky, and within its great black body began generating a ferocious heat.
The dragon's signature weapon—its breath of destruction—turned toward the fortress city below. —In that instant, the maddened dragon met the gaze of someone watching it from within the great fortress it meant to bring down.
And whoever it was standing at that window raised a hand toward the Blight Dragon tearing itself free of the thorns in the sky above, and—

"—El Fura."

If the goal could be achieved with the minimum possible effort, there was no need for flashy magic or any strength beyond common sense.
With the kind of precision that could serve as a model for how anything ought to be done, a blade of wind sheared away the Blight Dragon's last remaining head with merciless sharpness.

"——"

Stripped of all three of its heads, one by one, the Blight Dragon fell.
That was the anticlimactic end of 'Three-Headed' Valgren, the trump card the Undead had played.

△▼△▼△▼△

"Impossible, impossible, impossible, impossible, impossible, impossible, impossible, impossible, impossible...!"

Trembling, clawing at his own hair, Palladio found himself unable to accept the defeat of Valgren that he had just witnessed with his own Evil Eyes.

The impossible had happened, one blow after another.
Because of it, Palladio had fallen from a position of overwhelming advantage, his pride as the executor of what should have been a calm, unhurried Emperor's war left wounded, forced to taste humiliation.

"Why?! Why could they read my strategy through to the end?!"

Having the undead Flying Dragon Riders carry the Dragon Ships, the surprise operation over the mountain had been flawless.
Whether they'd anticipated it or simply had reserve strength ready to respond at once wasn't clear, but the surprise operation had been stopped. Still, that was merely unpleasant, not fatal. —Or so it should have been.

"What good was the Blight Dragon, the 'Three-Headed' one...!"

Sent out immediately after as the bearer of despair, 'Three-Headed' Valgren—the revived Blight Dragon once said to have run rampant and been feared in the hateful neighboring Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica.
Had that maddened dragon shown its true worth, dominion through strength and terror would have been complete.
And yet Valgren—of all things—had its three heads struck down in that manner and was subjugated.

"Why are you all so witless?! Why can you not fulfill your duty as my hands and feet?!"

The fallen Blight Dragon, the failed strike force, the horde of Undead still struggling even against the first wall—none of them served as Palladio's strength; all of them were fools dragging him down.
Come to think of it, Vincent, Prisca, and even Lamia had all had capable servants.
In the end, the difference between Palladio and the likes of Vincent came down to nothing more than whether one had been blessed with good servants. That was no measure of one's own ability—merely an ornament tacked on from outside.
That they should flaunt such a thing and think to best Palladio with it—they were shameless wretches unworthy of standing upwind of the imperial line.

"The 'Thorn Emperor's' solitary way of being—that is the true purpose of a Vollachia Emperor. I will not bow, not even to a despicable lot like you, nor to your methods...!"

Even back in the Imperial Selection Ceremony, Vincent and his ilk had dodged the schemes Palladio devised, or snatched away victory through methods that ran counter to every expectation.
The very same thing was happening now. —He could not go on overlooking such an outrage.

"Someone—offer me a strategy! I will raise whoever does so up, here and now!"

Burning with humiliation, Palladio called out loudly across the Undead ranks.
In truth, Palladio had wanted to remain the solitary Emperor who sought no one else's opinion. But his enemy had taken advantage of that very nobility of his, wielding malice like a spear against him.
Then Palladio, too, would show the Emperor's authority through sheer ferocity, and nothing else.

"Is there no one?! Speak up! Or else..."

"—Well then, if I may be so bold."

As Palladio turned his head, surveying the pallid faces around him, one voice rose to answer.
Looking closer, he saw a slender figure stepping slowly out from among the Undead host—and at the air about her, subtly different from the other Undead, Palladio narrowed all three of his eyes.
Her bearing set her apart from the mediocre at a glance. All that remained was whether her ability lived up to that impression.

"What can you do?"

"Let's see... First, allow me to show you."

So saying, the white-haired woman standing before Palladio smiled and raised a hand toward the sky.
And then—

△▼△▼△▼△

"—Ana!!"

When the 'Three-Headed' Blight Dragon, whose appearance had been anticipated, had all three of its heads struck down above the city and came crashing down, its great black body crumbling to dust, relief had welled up within Anastasia's heart.
Anticipated or not, the fact remained that their foe had been a formidable one.
Even with adequate preparations in place, there was always the chance the enemy might exceed them, or circle around from an unexpected angle. She, too, felt the relief of having avoided that and crushed a powerful enemy.
That relief of Anastasia's was cut through by Echidna's voice at her neck.

"——"

Echidna, who normally stayed still, disguised as a scarf around Anastasia's neck, sprang upright on her shoulder—the Spirit she'd known for ten years now—and stared with dark eyes up at the sky above the great fortress.
Following Echidna's gaze in turn, Anastasia's pale blue-green eyes went wide.

"Is that... a star...?"

The murmur, uncertain enough to sound almost helpless, betrayed just how little conviction lay behind it.
At the very least, it was a sight Anastasia had never once seen in her entire life, one she'd never even imagined—that much she had to admit.

Higher than the flock of undead Flying Dragons that had crossed Mount Gildray, higher even than the 'Three-Headed' Blight Dragon that had taken the sky above them—light came pouring down upon the city from higher still. A star was falling.
As Anastasia stared, wide-eyed, at the sight so far beyond ordinary reckoning, and Otto and Berstetz alike fell speechless beside her, Serena, watching the same spectacle, twisted the blade-scar on her face into a grin, and said—

"The air's shifted. —Win or lose, the end's close now."

△▼△▼△▼△

"Ha, hahahahaha! Yes, that's it, this is it! This is the sight I desired!"

Seeing that spectacle unfold across the battlefield sky, Palladio danced in mad delight.
Once the pouring light reached the ground, what would the defenses of a fortress city renowned for its solidity matter? The resistance of its pitiful, foolish masses was as good as dust before the light of a star.

"Well done, coming to my side! I shall welcome you among my vassals! What is your name?!"

His three golden eyes brimming with delight, Palladio put the question to the white-haired woman who had conjured that spectacle. The woman turned at his words, and as Palladio took her in anew, face to face, he caught his breath.
Her beautiful white hair, her features arranged with a precision fine enough to send a shiver down one's spine, and above all, the absence of any outward mark of the Undead in either her face or her eyes—hers was a form one might easily mistake for the living.
But then, why would she have thrown in with this side?

To Palladio's unspoken question, the woman bent deeply at the waist and bowed.
And then she gave her answer. —She named herself, whatever she truly was.

"What name would be correct to give is, I'm afraid, a rather delicate matter for someone in my position. So for now, please simply... call me the 'Witch of Greed.'"
