Arc 8 Chapter 41: "The Whereabouts of Hell"

—Groovy Gumlet had no interest in the "Stargazer."

That wasn't a rejection born of loathing or hostility—it was indifference in the truest sense of the word.
To begin with, if you lived an ordinary life, you'd rarely cross paths with the sort of person who spouted nonsense like the "Stargazer." Given his position as a General, he sometimes ran into Ubilk, who was permitted to come and go from the castle, but the man rarely spoke to anyone unless he had business with them.
As for Groovy, about the only exchange he could recall having with Ubilk was telling him to shut up when he butted into a meeting.

Given that impression of the man, it baffled Groovy that Emperor Vincent and his right hand Chisha paid such close attention to the Stargazer's movements. What Chancellor Berstetz thought of the Stargazer was unclear, but as far as Groovy's nose could tell, the man's loyalty to the Empire was genuine, and the complicated anger he harbored toward Vincent never colored his judgment.

So, with the Empire's leadership each having settled on their own stance, Groovy—who had no occasion to get involved—had no reason at all to take an interest in the Stargazer.
Which was exactly why, if he'd learned that this clash with the Great Disaster—one that put the Empire's very survival on the line—had already been foreseen by the Stargazer, a mountain of his usual curses would have come pouring out of his mouth.

But at least here and now, the reason a familiar string of curses burst from Groovy's mouth had nothing to do with anger at some "Stargazer."

"——Fuck!"

The gray brambles that had surfaced over his left breast—the instant his fingers, trying to tear them off, passed clean through them, a searing pain as if sharp thorns had pierced straight through his heart made Groovy's fur stand on end.
The sensation of something sharp being driven into a vital point deep inside his body, directly connected to his life, was a pain that would twist even a battle-hardened warrior's face.
Infuriatingly, the brambles had no physical form. There was nothing to swat away.
Grinding his back teeth against the one-sided assault of pain, Groovy looked to his side—at the iron-helmed man whose waist he was clinging to.
Naturally, he'd been struck by the same curse as Groovy and was likewise writhing in pain, but—

"Damn it, fall back! This is about to get a whole lot worse!"

"Fall back, he says... guh!"

Groovy punched the slow-reacting Al square in the gut, forcibly shoving him backward.
As the groaning man toppled backward onto the street, Groovy grabbed hold of him and swiftly ducked into the shadow of a nearby building.
It was an indiscriminate area attack. Hiding in the street like this was nothing more than a token comfort.

"Still, we'd rather not be seen carrying on and have a swarm of the Undead come flocking over."

"...Didn't you get hit with the damn brambles too?"

"Oh, I have, indeed. Though if they were going to give me vines, I do think it would've made for a rather more flowering gift if they'd thrown in some blossoms too."

Landing on the same street as Groovy and the others, Cecilus said this while tapping his own left breast.
The same brambles showed faintly through his chest as through Groovy's, and he should have been feeling just as much pain. And yet, that he could grin so breezily through pain that made even Groovy scowl was because—

"The conduct of a leading man is always the focus of the audience's eyes. Now, if my face were twisted in anguish over the loss of a beloved someone or a dear friend, that might stir some real emotion. But to screw up my face simply because I am in pain? That would only cheapen the actor's standing."

"So no matter how much it damn hurts or how damn awful it feels, your face won't budge. That's your idiotic damn philosophy, isn't it."

"Hm? Have I told you this before?"

"You have, damn it."

Cecilus cocked his head, but Groovy had been subjected to his philosophy more times than he cared to hear, whether he wanted to or not.
Amid a majority who turned a deaf ear to Cecilus's nonsense, Groovy was the type to snap back whenever something rubbed him the wrong way, and so he was often the one Cecilus latched onto. That was why his philosophy had become so tediously familiar to him.
Though, now that Cecilus had forgotten ever saying it, the fact that he'd been putting that theory into practice even before he'd grown into his current, fully-limbed self was less awe-inspiring than it was, quite simply, stupid.

As Groovy and Cecilus traded words like this—

"cough... huh? The brambles stopped hurting?"

Propping himself up in the alley he'd been dragged into, Al muttered this while looking down at the brambles on his chest. In their place, he rubbed at the belly Groovy had punched, shooting a resentful look his way.

"My gut hurts a hell of a lot more from getting punched... what's going on?"

"It's your own damn fault for not falling back fast enough! ...As for the brambles, it's probably distance."

"Ho-ho, so you mean the distance between us and whoever's casting the brambles? Indeed, if the pain vanished the moment we fell back, that would explain it! Since the three of us were bunched together, it's all the easier to see—first Groovy-san up front, then Al-san sandwiched in the middle, and finally me in the back. Even within less than a second, there was a difference."

"...But the brambles haven't disappeared."

Al muttered bitterly, his fingers passing through the untouchable brambles with nothing to catch.
Just as he said, the pain had faded, but the brambles themselves—the important part—had not disappeared. Even with the obvious problem of pain gone, the fact that the connection to the brambles remained was troubling. In most cases, a lingering curse of this sort came with the ability to sense the target's location.
As long as these brambles remained, there was a high chance their location was completely exposed to the enemy.

"In that case, even hiding might do us no good if a whole troop comes swarming our way. ——Personally, I don't think it's wise to cut down too many of the enemy, mind you."

"You said that before too. Why?"

"As I said before, it's a hunch."

"...Your damn hunch, huh."

Groovy couldn't laugh off Cecilus's unhesitating reliance on intuition as mere foolish nonsense.
It wasn't a situation he could laugh at, and Cecilus had a track record that kept him from dismissing it as nonsense. Among the Generals, both Cecilus and Arakiya were the purely intuitive type, though each was sharp in their own way and for their own reasons.
Groovy himself didn't believe that whoever had triggered a disaster of this scale would resort to something as monotonous as brute force alone.
If anything, an enemy who relied on brute force alone would be no match for Vincent or Chisha at all—

"—"

For a moment, Groovy glanced at Cecilus's profile and fell silent.
Various thoughts crossed his mind, but to begin with, if the enemy had chosen brute force as their sole approach, then clashing with an Empire that had Cecilus and Arakiya in it would be the wrong call from the start.
An enemy that got a premise like that wrong could never be a true threat to the Vollachian Empire.
Of course, if the enemy had even intended for Cecilus to shrink like this, that would be impressive indeed—but Groovy's own nose told him otherwise.

It was Chisha who had shrunk Cecilus. The lingering scent of mana left no doubt of that.
And there was no possibility of Chisha siding with those out to destroy the Empire. Still, not being able to fathom Chisha's true intentions left Groovy with a nagging, uncomfortable feeling all the same.

"If our location's given away, there's no time to sit around thinking. So what do we do. All of us charge in together and take down whoever's behind this curse?"

"Yes. I'd love to say that's the fastest solution! But by Groovy-san's earlier reasoning, the curse's potency is tied to distance. Which means, since the pain receded as we pulled away, if we go charging straight up close to them..."

"It won't stop at that level of pain this time."

"Quite possibly, yes."

At Cecilus's nodded conjecture, Al glared bitterly down at the brambles on his chest.
Groovy agreed with Cecilus's assessment. Therein lay the terror of an enemy who scattered the bramble curse indiscriminately over everyone within range.
The closer one got to the caster, the more powerfully the bramble curse would take hold. Most people, in all likelihood, would be rendered helpless by the pain before they ever got within reach of the caster.

"If it comes to that, even the biggest damn idiot won't be able to move."

"Looking my way as you say that—am I to take it you think I'm the sort of fool who'd charge in without a thought? But even I won't go charging in here with no plan at all. Though if enduring the agony and cutting the enemy down could settle the matter, that would be one way to have some fun with it——"

Pausing a beat, Cecilus clapped his hands together sharply in front of his chest, making a sound. Then, keeping his fingers pressed together, he peered at Groovy and Al alternately through the gap between them, and said,

"But that's not it, is it? If lopping off the enemy's head would settle things, Groovy-san would surely have sent me off to do just that already."

"...That's the damn troublesome thing about this damn curse."

Before Cecilus's eerie smile, Groovy couldn't help but sigh.
If this were Magic, killing the caster would usually be enough to end its effect. Magic was cast by a practitioner using mana to interfere with the world, so once the caster was gone, that was the end of it.
Curse Arts, however, were something specialized for inflicting harm that led its target to death.
For that reason, many were cast by binding to the target's Od, and even if the caster died, most would not lose their effect so long as the target went on living. These brambles were clearly reliant on the target's Od rather than the caster's, making them a textbook example of exactly that.
Breaking a curse of this sort meant either making the caster release it themselves, or resorting to some backdoor trick.

"We need the damn sword, Murasame. Go fetch it."

At Groovy's instruction, jerking his chin, Cecilus and Al both blinked in bewilderment.
Al not knowing was one thing, but Cecilus knowing and having forgotten was infuriating. Grabbing the infuriating Cecilus by the collar, Groovy bared his teeth wide.

"The Wicked Sword, Murasame! For cutting through shapeless nonsense like curses and Contracts, that damn sword is the quickest way there is! Go find it!"

"Eh!? You say that, but it's a sword I've never even heard of!? If I don't know where it is, I can hardly go looking... or does Groovy-san know its location by scent, perhaps?"

"No use. That damn sword cut away its own scent so I couldn't track it by nose. Doesn't matter if you sink it in a pool of blood year-round—you can't trail it by smell, damn it."

Even setting that aside, Murasame despised Groovy for having melted it down from a sword and reforged it into a katana, and it wouldn't even allow him to pick it up.
A troublesome thing to handle, on par with its owner Cecilus—the Wicked Sword's whereabouts simply couldn't be tracked by Groovy's nose.

"—But if we get it, we can do something about these brambles, right?"

Beside the exchange between Groovy pressing in and Cecilus's blank expression, Al rose to his feet and confirmed this in a low voice.
At the seriousness in his tone, Groovy released Cecilus and nodded.

"That's right. If it's anywhere, it'd be at the damn idiot's house or some secret storeroom, but I can't imagine that damn idiot keeping a secret a secret, so there's probably no such thing."

"Agreed. Where's this house, then?"

"In the garden of the Crystal Palace. He lives with Arakiya in a damn shack."

"Wait, wait, please wait! I've been assuming this 'damn idiot' you keep talking about is me, but now an unfamiliar name has popped up—who is that!"

"Arakiya... you're saying he and that girl are a thing, living in the palace garden? Small world, isn't it..."

"Nah, they're probably not a thing."

At Al's reasonable muttering, Groovy answered with his snout wrinkled.
Even watching from the sidelines, Groovy didn't really understand the relationship between Cecilus and Arakiya. The two of them had apparently known each other for nearly ten years, but what Cecilus thought of anyone was a mystery, and while it was certain Arakiya disliked him, they lived together and ate their meals together too.
If they'd been mating, Groovy would know by scent, but there was no sign of that either. Every so often they'd go at each other in earnest and end up reshaping the Empire's terrain, making them a thoroughly troublesome pair to be around.

"Still, I can't imagine either the Dream Sword or the Wicked Sword being left just lying around after the damn idiot shrank. So what's likely is..."

"—If we could just walk into the palace, we'd have done that from the start."

If the swords had been recovered and were sitting inside the palace, that was exactly the worry troubling Al—and he was right.
If they could storm the Crystal Palace, they wouldn't be resorting to a Peak-conquest strategy that depended on reinforcements. But sealing this caster was also a prerequisite for bringing those reinforcements in in the first place—

"Hmm, I wonder. Would they really do something as wasteful as leaving such a magnificent weapon just sitting in the palace? A weapon introduced with such meaningful fanfare simply has to be put to use."

Cecilus cut in with this, his hands laced behind his head.
With his back turned to Groovy and Al as they talked, Cecilus made a show of glancing their way now and again, humming "hmm, hmm" pointedly.

"What. If you've got something to say, quit stalling and damn well say it."

"Oh, no, no. Why don't the two of you just keep talking amongst yourselves? Whatever I might say doesn't seem to reach your ears anyway, so never mind, never mind."

"Are you sulking over Arakiya, damn it! She's the woman you're always trying to kill each other with! She's probably somewhere around here! That's that, you damn idiot, so spit it out already!"

"Ehh, well, if you want it that badly, I suppose it can't be helped. ——Simply put, from the enemy's standpoint, wouldn't it make more sense to hand such a weapon off to someone than to just leave it sitting in the palace?"

Cecilus's sulky expression broke into a grin as he said this, and Groovy furrowed his brow—then felt a pang of frustration at having been handed an opinion that was far more sound and plausible than he'd expected.
In other words, if Cecilus's guess was correct, there was a strong chance the two powerful weapons—the Dream Sword and the Wicked Sword—had been given to one of the Undead to wield.
And what's more—

"If it's that powerful a weapon, wouldn't they hand it to whoever's entrusted with an important post?"

"Damn it."

Just as Cecilus said, Groovy agreed with a curse that this was indeed the most likely possibility.
That being the case, the smart move was to whittle down the possibilities and head for the right location.

"If Balleroy's holed up at Peak Three, it's not him. His weapon's a spear. He wouldn't make the damn stupid call to switch weapons on purpose."

"Then it's somewhere else. Though it would be a shame if it turned out Al-san used his own father and the others as decoys for nothing."

"No, I agree this one needs to be dealt with first, above all else. ...When the brothers get here, having just this one guy holding them up isn't something I can laugh off."

Al's clenched fist trembled, and Cecilus, closing one eye at the sight, looked at Groovy.
Their course was set. It was a plan that couldn't quite shake the feel of making things up as they went—searching the other Peaks for whoever was likely holding the Wicked Sword—but even so—

"Damn idiot, at least get it right on the first try. Where's most likely?"

"Hmm... somehow, I'd guess Peak Two?"

"If it's southeast, that's still fairly close from here. Alright, we'll use the hide again, the three of us—"

"No."

Just as their destination was decided, Groovy cut Al off.
Shaking his head, Groovy tossed the Werewolf hide he'd been holding to Al, then turned his back on the two of them and headed for the mouth of the alley.
And then—

"From here, we split up. Someone needs to pin this damn enemy down so they can't move. ——It's a shitty job, but I'm the only one who can do it."

"——! I get the idea, but you've got the brambles too! The conditions are the same for you as for me, aren't they!?"

Groovy didn't stop his stride at Al's voice trying to hold him back.
Al moved to chase after him, but Cecilus's small hand tapped his chest, stopping him. Holding Al back like that, Cecilus called out to the walking Groovy.

"You do have a winning strategy, don't you?"

"At the very least, I'll buy the damn idiot the time he needs to do his job. Who do you think I am. The 'Six' of the Nine Divine Generals, Groovy Gumlet-sama himself."

Raising his arm with a flourish, Groovy declared the grounds for his confidence to Cecilus over his shoulder.
That seemed to strike Cecilus in an unusually good way. He let out a pair of pleased little snorts that Groovy could tell were pleased even without looking, and said,

"Then by all means, have at it to your heart's content. Next time, let's meet again with the Wicked Sword in hand!"

With that familiar, leading-man flourish of a send-off, he sent Groovy—kicking off the cobblestones—out toward the battlefield.

△▼△▼△▼△

—Parting ways with Cecilus and Al, Groovy, now alone, leapt across the imperial capital.

Heedless of prying eyes, he bounded over streets, kicked off building walls and rooftops, and leapt with wild abandon.
From here on, unlike the stealth he'd practiced under cover of the Werewolf hide, he needed to move flashily on purpose, to draw the enemy's attention.
At last—at long last—a chance had come for Groovy to show what he was truly made of.

"Making me put up with such a damn cramped act...!"

Loading his tongue with all the pent-up frustration he'd stored away, Groovy spat out a hateful curse.
To begin with, Groovy simply didn't like fleeing about in a panic or skulking around hidden from other people's eyes—that sort of small, fussy behavior. It wasn't a matter of being good or bad at it. He just hated it.
And yet, ever since the appearance of the Undead had forced him off the western front, all the way to the imperial capital, he'd been made to endure it endlessly, and he was well past the limit of his patience.
Of course, that frustration wasn't his reason for proposing to act alone. But now that he'd earned himself a chance to run wild and free, he intended to savor it fully.

"——Here it comes, damn it."

Tucking his knees and spinning head over heels through the air, Groovy felt the bramble vines surfacing on his fur-covered chest writhe—the binding curse reactivating because the distance to the caster had closed.
The searing agony stabbing directly into his heart wasn't something even a warrior accustomed to pain could easily bear. As already stated, that much was the honest truth.
However—

"If it's a damn curse meant to inflict pain, there's a way around it!"

Roaring this, Groovy drew a single dagger from the belt cinched around his torso.
From the belt loaded with several daggers, the one he chose had a purple line running along its blade. Groovy pointed the tip of that dagger at his own throat and pressed the edge into himself.
At once, the poison loaded into the dagger's edge flowed into Groovy's body, riding his bloodstream to rapidly eat away at his small, bearded Dog Human frame. The virulent poison surged through him in a kind of cheering joy at getting to fulfill its true purpose of killing a living thing, moving to fatally sever his vital functions—but a hair's breadth before that, he pulled the blade back.

"——Aah."

Enduring the sensation of his stomach's contents surging back up, Groovy's wide-open eyes turned bloodshot.
The capillaries in his eyes burst, staining both eyes red, but Groovy's body held its ground just short of being killed by the virulent poison, and brought about a different sort of change instead.
Part of his body's senses fell silent under the poison. ——He had killed his own sense of pain.

In that instant, the bramble pain that might have stopped Groovy in his tracks vanished, and the tightening grip around his heart was overpowered by the exhilaration of forcibly surviving the brush with death he'd just courted.

Under normal circumstances, this poison was meant to be administered to a captured enemy, used for torture—letting the numbed victim watch, fully conscious, as their own body was destroyed piece by piece.
But he'd suspected that if he adjusted the dosage, he could do just this—move about with his body still free, only his sense of pain broken. He'd wanted to try it but never had the chance until now; it had worked out on the first live attempt.

"If I'd tried it out on that damn idiot Cecilus and botched it, there'd be no taking it back."

When it came to his own body, he knew it down to the very last strand of fur, without so much as a fang's width of margin for error. That was what let him fine-tune the dose, but trying it on Cecilus or Al would mean eyeballing it with their lives on the line.
Not that he was entirely without the urge to test whether even Cecilus could be killed by poison—but suppressing the curiosity welling steadily up inside him, Groovy set his sights on Peak Four.

The situation was what it was. Right now, saving the Vollachian Empire from the brink of destruction took top priority.
In truth, he'd have loved to make the most of this situation where the Undead were rising again, gathering up materials from already-extinct races and rare peoples of limited numbers one after another, but he had no choice but to swallow his tears and let it pass.
A reason burned fiercely enough within Groovy to make him do just that.

"Damn it all."

The curse, spat out short, came from an irritation that refused to fade.

—From the moment he'd laid eyes on the imperial capital under the Undead's control, Groovy had considered the possibility that either Vincent or Chisha was dead. And if one of them was dead, he suspected it would be Chisha.

There had been signs of a certain flat, matter-of-fact scent of death trailing after Vincent's every step. But then, at some point, it had abruptly vanished—and no sooner had Groovy noticed than this whole affair had unfolded.

"That damn pale-faced damn fool."

He'd always been a man who gave nothing away, flat and unreadable—but in the end, he'd managed to hide even his true feelings from Groovy's nose.
On the eve of the decisive battle for the capital, it had been Vincent's order that sent Groovy away from the battlefield to the west—but that "Vincent" must have been Chisha.
What had become of the real Vincent in the meantime, Groovy had no way of knowing, and factoring in the circumstances behind Cecilus's shrinking too, it looked like a total victory for Chisha alone.
But even that victory would count for nothing if the Empire lost.

"Damn it."

As if he'd let such an idiotic thing happen.
Groovy was a bearded Dog Human born in the Empire, raised in the Empire, and living in the Empire. His race's standing hinged on his achievements, and he'd been told over and over that his duty was to fight bravely and die.
Groovy was well aware he'd been born to carve his name into the Empire's history, and in fact, he'd been discovered by the Emperor and risen swiftly, without a hitch, to become a General of the Empire.
And it was the Nine Divine Generals that this very Groovy Gumlet acknowledged as his equals, or better.

Groovy was a warrior of the Empire, one who upheld the philosophy that "the people of the Empire must be strong."
Precisely because of that, victory and defeat were sacred things. ——The victor had to be honored.

"Damn it, damn it, damn it, damn it, damn it, damn it, damn it...!"

In Vollachia, where battle never ceased, if even that unwritten law were twisted out of shape, this world would become hell.
And hell was something for the enemies of Vollachia to taste—this world could not be allowed to become hell itself.
He had to make the enemy understand exactly where hell resided.

"——Damn, found them."

Spinning through the air, Groovy spotted several moving figures in his whirling field of view and muttered this.
The swarm of Undead below had likewise spotted the airborne Groovy and were shouting something. But their reaction was far too slow. Leaving them be wouldn't cause any harm, but there was no reason to leave them be either.
Above all, amid the scent of blood, letting prey go was never an option for Groovy.

Clacking his fangs together audibly, Groovy drew the chain sickle slung across his back.
It was a tricky weapon—a wide, single-handed sickle linked by chain to a weight. Normally, a chain sickle's chain ran no more than a few meters, meant for close- to mid-range combat, but Groovy's was made of a special material.
Because of that, the chain weight could easily reach a cluster of Undead nearly thirty meters away.

Swinging the arm holding the sickle in a great arc, he sent the trailing chain weight flying wildly down toward the ground, the chain clanking as it went. The weight itself was only fist-sized, but with the momentum behind it, its force was nothing you could measure as a single punch's worth.
Naturally, the Undead leapt aside to avoid taking the hit, evading the weight—but they were far too naive.

"You damn fools!!"

At the target of Groovy's roar, the thrown weight made contact with the ground.
In that instant, the weight flared red from within all at once, triggering a massive explosion that engulfed the surrounding buildings—a tremendous shockwave swallowed the fleeing Undead whole, and an entire street of the capital was leveled.

—Northwest of the Kararagi City-States, near the Great Waterfall, lies the Girall Crimson Dunes.
Appearing as a red desert region, that place is the most dangerous land in the world, made up of fine particles of fire mana-stone as small as grains of sand. A land where merely a gust of wind can trigger abnormal chain explosions, it's said to be earth formed from the tears of blood shed by a great Spirit who failed to become one of the Four Great Spirits.
Inside the weight of Groovy's chain sickle was embedded a fire mana crystal recovered from the Girall Crimson Dunes, and having absorbed the surrounding mana, it unleashed this kind of tremendous destructive force.

Bathed in the aftermath of a blast hot enough to blow away an entire street, Groovy landed in the scorched field now rising with flame and black smoke, and there, straightening his back sharply, he—

"AAAAAAAAHHH——!!"

—let out a roar, giving voice to the destructive impulse swelling up from deep within him.
His heart pounded, throb after throb, protesting the tightening grip of the brambles, but there was no pain. Simply tasting the sensation of scorching heat burning through his own insides, Groovy exhaled a breath that smelled of blood.
Then, clacking his fangs together hard, he reset his grip on the chain sickle, and—

"With a damn curse like that, a surprise attack's a damn impossibility, isn't it!!"

He launched the chain weight upward at the figure falling from the sky above, the hem of a red cloak billowing behind it.
The weight's power had already proven itself, and the figure met it head-on with a sword in hand—immediately after, a blazing explosion swallowed them whole.
But even so—

"Tch!"

The instant he saw the flames that had wrapped around the sky get cleanly split in two, Groovy leapt backward.
That unnatural change in the flames was proof that the figure flying toward him had cut straight through them. And that this was the very one scattering the bramble curse over Groovy and everyone around him was confirmed by the brambles themselves, surging as if cheering at their master's arrival.

Had he not silenced his sense of pain with the poison, he'd have been coughing blood and writhing on the ground by now.
If the enemy were the sort to rely on the brambles alone, Groovy would have loved to say he had the clear upper hand—but unfortunately, this didn't seem to be so easy an opponent.

"A surprise attack, you say—how strange a notion. Why should I have to resort to such underhanded tricks."

Speaking, the figure that had landed in the scorched field turned slowly to look his way.
Cracked, pallid skin, golden irises floating in black eyes. The figure fulfilled every mark of the Undead, just as expected—but there was something there that overturned that expectation by a full step or two.

First, garments of a design permitted only to Vollachian royalty; features that vaguely called to mind Vincent Vollachia's own face; and above all, the thing the figure held in hand.
It was the crimson-blazing light of the Sunlight Sword—a blade permitted only to the Vollachian Emperor to wield—

"Damn it."

If the Undead were rising, then naturally, it stood to reason that Vollachian royalty could be among them too.
So the fact that this figure held the Sunlight Sword, while startling, was something Groovy could accept. What he couldn't accept was that it wasn't only that.

The Undead emperor, the Sunlight Sword lowered in his right hand, carried a different weapon in his left as well.
And it was a weapon Groovy had never imagined this particular Undead would be carrying here—

"——Just how far is that damn sword going to keep defying me!!"

Roaring in pure fury, Groovy saw in his field of view that what the Undead emperor held was the Wicked Sword, Murasame——the Sunlight Sword and the Wicked Sword, two impossible Enchanted Swords, standing in the way of Curse Tools Artisan Groovy Gumlet as his enemy.
