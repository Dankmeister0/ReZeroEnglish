Arc 7 Chapter 51: "The Mistress of the Magic City"

The warm, sunlit breeze brushed softly through Subaru's bangs. Feeling its ticklish touch, Subaru took in the sensation of the girl's hand he held, and the breathing of the tall, beautiful woman standing behind him.

The place was the very top of Crimson Ruri Castle, higher even than the spacious room on its uppermost floor — treading upon the roof tiles of the castle itself, at the point "closest to the sky," from which the whole of Chaosflame could be seen at a glance. It was the place nearest to the "Abyss with a fine view" that Subaru had puzzled out.

"Olbart, I really think you've got a rotten personality."

Not the hand joined with Rui's beside him, but with his other hand, Subaru pointed — at the small, elderly man seated heavily at the edge of the castle roof. Sitting cross-legged, sliding his rear around as he turned back, the strange old man — Olbart — brought the gourd in his hand to his lips and gulped down the liquor inside. And then—

"Say, boy. Ain't you gettin' a bit hungry?"

"...Hu-hungry?"

"Aye, that's right, stomach's the thing. Woke up straight into all this ruckus, didn't even get time to eat, did ya? How your gut's doin' affects both head and body, y'know."

Saying so, Olbart set down the gourd and pulled another wrapped bundle from inside his robe. Before a faintly wary Subaru, Olbart gave a small "kaka" laugh and undid the wrapping. Wrapped inside was a black, round object about the size of a golf ball.

"That's..."

"Hyourougan, we call it — ration pills, the Shinobi's travel food. One of these'll keep your stomach goin' a whole day, and keep the head and body workin' fine too. Only thing is, tastes awful."

Pinching the ration pill between his fingers atop the wrapping, Olbart jiggled it to show it off. It was unmistakably a food with no concern whatsoever for taste. It might well be nutritious, but even Subaru had no desire to eat something that looked like that. Rolling it on his palm, Olbart went on,

"I been a Shinobi since I was young, so naturally, I been keepin' company with these things since I was young too. And every time I ate one out on a job, I'd think — like them high an' mighty old geezers back at the village, I oughta hurry up and retire, live a life where I don't gotta eat this stuff no more."

"...Then why don't you? You said you're the head of the Shinobi Village, right? Aren't you important enough?"

"Aye, that's the truth of it. I'm more important, and stronger, than them geezers who used to boss me around... and yet, here I am, still eatin' this."

Saying that, Olbart tossed the ration pill he'd been rolling in his palm into his mouth. The strange old man chewed the food he himself had called unappetizing, and swallowed it down with a gulp. Then he opened his mouth to show he'd finished it entirely, before continuing,

"In the end, it's a matter of habits you just can't shake. This messy, thrown-together food, taste and looks be damned — for me, it's the finest feast there is. Ain't that somethin' to make ya cry? No? Nothin'? Kakakakka!"

Olbart laughed, teeth clacking, and Subaru felt something unsettling in that reaction. He couldn't quite grasp what this whole exchange had been pointing at — and it didn't feel like something he failed to understand merely because of his current childishness. And yet it didn't feel like something to simply brush off entirely, either. Probably, in Olbart's own way, it had been some kind of praise — a compliment, of sorts.

"—Elder Olbart, this happens to be my castle, does it not?"

Just as the conversation reached a lull, the one who spoke was the empress of the Magic City — Yorna Mishigure, who had come up to the castle roof together with Subaru and the others. Meeting Yorna's blue-eyed gaze, Olbart stopped his "ohh" of laughter.

"'Course I know that, obviously. Sittin' pretty on another man's castle right under his nose — feels pretty good, I gotta say. Doin' what you like's the secret to a long life, after all."

"My my, and here I thought an elder such as yourself would have rather more refined tastes. I confess, such a thing would never occur to me."

"Ohh, that so?"

"Naturally. —Shortening one's own life in the name of some 'secret to longevity' can hardly be called the act of a sane mind."

A heat could be felt from Yorna, who smiled elegantly with her sleeve lightly pressed to her lips. That was the anger contained within her graceful gesture, and its target was, of course, Olbart. Surely Olbart too was aware of Yorna's anger, yet the strange old man remained cross-legged, popping another ration pill from the wrapping open on his knee into his mouth.

"So, why is it you three are gettin' along so well, then? A fox girl and two little runts — don't seem the type or type to be makin' friends."

"Unfortunately, unlike the wicked Elder Olbart, I have neither the hobby of teasing children nor any reason to torment them. When it comes to children, the proper course is to treat them with courtesy. All the more so—"

Here Yorna paused a moment, and with her fingertips gave her golden pipe a twirl. Pointing the twirled pipe's tip — the part trailing smoke — at Olbart, she continued.

"—when I hear that Elder Olbart has been leading a child astray, and has beguiled my own attendant besides... as mistress of this city, I must take whatever measures need taking."

"Ho-ho, is that so."

Stroking his own chin, Olbart's amused eyes turned toward Subaru and the others. Sensing the intent behind that gaze, Subaru gave a deep nod.

"Sorry, but I told on you. It's not against your rules, is it? You're the one who set your people on us in the first place."

"Kakakakka! Quite the mouth on you, boy. But that's the right call. We started it, so bein' paid back and cryin' about it after — that'd be shameful. Still..."

"Still?"

"Your head oughta be strugglin' to keep up, and yet you think things through pretty well. You sure you ain't Vollachian royalty or somethin'? Rotten personalities, the lot of 'em."

"Please don't say something that terrifying..."

Judging by hair color alone, Subaru and the current emperor, Abel, had something in common, but everything else about them differed far too much. The shape of their faces and the length of their legs proved as much. Besides, calling someone as rotten-tempered as Vollachian royalty was an insult beyond insults. For instance—

"I can properly say thank you when someone's nice to me."

"And with a smile, and in a loud voice too, if I may add. That is precisely how a child ought to be. I dare say Tanza could stand to take a lesson from it. In any case—"

Her lips curving as she praised Subaru's boldness, Yorna's voice then lowered in tone. Her eyes narrowed slightly, and the warm, convivial air began drawing toward its close. Because—

"As much as I might wish to scold my devoted attendant, there is little to discuss without the girl herself present. Now then — wherever might Elder Olbart have hidden that child?"

That very question from Yorna was the greatest reason she had accompanied them here.

"————"

Feeling the sharp pressure of Yorna's intimidation, its ripples washing over Olbart, Subaru bit his lip and checked the racing beat of his own heart.

—Downstairs, just after entering the castle, they had been discovered by Yorna, and having touched upon a side of her character he hadn't known the day before, Subaru had once again taken a great gamble.

He had told Yorna, as openly as he could, about the situation they'd been placed in, and about the "hide-and-seek" taking place throughout Chaosflame.

Of course, he hadn't touched on Abel's true identity, nor let slip that he and the others had been turned into children by Olbart's hand. What mattered was that they were playing "hide-and-seek" with Olbart, and that Tanza was cooperating with them in it. Pleading as sincerely as he could, resolved to face her with everything he had in order to patiently dispel her suspicions, Subaru had laid the circumstances bare to Yorna. And having heard Subaru's plea, Yorna had thought it over for a while before—

"—I am not so foolish a woman that I cannot tell truth from lie in the words of a desperate child."

—so she had answered, and had brought Subaru and Rui along with her all the way up to the top of Crimson Ruri Castle.

"Honestly, I didn't think you'd be someone I could talk to this openly..."

"Auau."

"Yeah, I know."

Catching Subaru's murmur, Rui shook their joined hands and pouted her lips. At that reaction, Subaru told himself that this was good fortune arrived at through the overlapping of several coincidences. If any single factor had been missing, surely this situation could never have come about.

That he'd parted ways with the scheming Abel. That they'd managed to reach Crimson Ruri Castle thanks to Ubilk's "advice." That it was Yorna herself, rather than a guard, who had found them directly. That the one with him had been Rui rather than Al or Medium. And that Subaru — the one most strongly affected by the "Infantilization" — had been the one to take the gamble.

The biggest reason Yorna had lent an ear to Subaru's words was that she had judged there to be no lie in them — that she'd accepted it as the earnest plea of a child. Just as there are adults who won't take a child's words seriously and simply laugh them off, there are also adults who, precisely because it comes from a child, take it seriously and try to live up to that trust. Subaru would never have imagined that one of Vollachia's "Nine Divine Generals" — in this brutal empire, no less — would be one such adult.

"Whoever said Yorna's head was screwed on wrong..."

Probably, logically speaking, it was because she didn't at all approve of the "survival of the fittest" mentality considered common sense in Vollachia, that she'd been treated that way. At the very least, having been treated this kindly, Subaru had no choice but to think so.

"Elder Olbart, I have two demands to make of you."

So saying, Yorna gently held up two fingers. Subaru had no room to interject in this conversation between transcendents taking place over his head. All he could do was stay conscious of the feel of Rui's hand, ready to move the moment something happened. If they teleported down below, that alone would let them escape the rooftop battlefield.

"Demands, huh. Go on, let's hear 'em."

"First, you will return Tanza to me."

"Oi oi, that's a rough thing to ask. The girl's got her own thinkin', don't she? Otherwise she wouldn't go schemin' behind your precious back like this."

"—Silence."

Half-admitting that he had some idea of Tanza's whereabouts, Olbart's laughing voice was cut short by a single sharp word from Yorna. That word, laced with quiet intensity, carried enough force to utterly transform the atmosphere. Indeed, even Subaru — not the one the word had been directed at — found himself holding his breath.

Having silenced Olbart's glib tongue, Yorna brought the pipe she'd been toying with in her hand to her lips, and slowly filled her lungs with purple smoke, before—

"Unfortunately, I have no intention of hearing Tanza's pleas or thoughts from any mouth but her own. Especially not after they've been filtered through the tongue of the 'wicked elder' — that, I could hardly bear to listen to."

"Kakaka, ain't that a shame. Hated, am I."

"Surely you have more than enough cause within yourself to know why."

Smiling aggressively, Yorna tilted her head, and her hair ornaments rattled softly. The hairpin thrust into her lavishly styled hair, and even that adorned beauty of hers, seemed to Subaru like one more of Yorna's weapons.

—Those in power wish to be feared. It was Yorna who had taught Subaru that such a way of thinking existed. Perhaps making others think her beautiful carried the same meaning as making them fear her.

"The other is that you cease this game you're playing with these children."

"A game, is it. Well, s'posed to be playful, but it's a serious match all the same, ain't it?"

"I believe I made myself clear yesterday, before His Majesty the Emperor. That no one is to lay a hand on the envoys — that I wished this to be upheld even by His Majesty himself."

—No one is to lay a hand on Subaru and the others. That had been Yorna's decision, conveyed unexpectedly through Tanza's own mouth. And Yorna herself intended to uphold it — and felt genuine anger at having had it broken. In other words—

"Are you prepared to make an enemy of me, in this Magic City?"

"Ohh, scary, scary. What a fearsome girl. ...Ah, well, I'll go ahead and make the excuse that I ain't laid a hand on 'em myself, all the same."

"That is merely Elder Olbart's own convenient interpretation, is it not? —Whose city do you imagine this is? Do you take me for a woman generous enough of heart to laugh such a thing off?"

Subaru felt the air itself seem to simmer and boil, and gooseflesh rose over his entire body. The further into her words Yorna went, the more her emotions could be felt rising. Of course, Yorna was not so undisciplined a person as to let that show plainly in her voice or expression.

But it could still be felt. Because Subaru had seen a great many people who were genuinely angry. People who are truly angry burn with emotion, staking their very lives on it. So when you're near them, you can tell. That there is a wholly pure anger there.

"Uu—..."

Rui let out a small growl, sensing the same dangerous atmosphere as Subaru. Along the way, Rui had been quick to attack any pursuers who came after them, but the two people here — Yorna and Olbart — were on an entirely different level of strength from any pursuer. By pure animal instinct, she probably understood that fighting here would mean no chance of winning. Perhaps she was worried about Subaru getting caught up in a fight—

"—! Olbart!"

Feeling his resolve toward Rui start to soften for just a moment, Subaru shook it off. Riding that same burst of momentum, he called out Olbart's name as the old man stood glaring at Yorna. Still seated atop the roof, Olbart deftly kept one eye focused on Yorna while turning the other toward Subaru.

"H-how are you even doing that...?"

"Kakakakka! Body control's the basics of the basics for a Shinobi. Right hand and left hand, right foot and left foot, gotta be able to do different things with each or you can't cut it as a Shinobi. So? What is it?"

"Huh?"

"You just called my name, didn't ya. I'll have you know, this is one of the handful most dangerous spots I've been in my whole life. I can only spare half an eyeball's worth of attention for ya."

Even while keeping up his easygoing manner, Olbart clearly understood well enough that this was a critical moment. Well, even half an eyeball's worth was appreciated. Subaru kept his hand pressed to his chest, took a few deep breaths, and said,

"C-can we just call it even?"

"...Hm?"

At the proposal Subaru put forward, Olbart's left eye alone went wide. Still holding on to that half-eyeball's worth of attention, Subaru pressed on impatiently, raising his voice with a "So!"

"Even, I mean even! You tried to do something rule-breaking-ish too, and I told on you to Yorna-san. So it's six of one, half a dozen of the other, and so..."

"So we both admit we did somethin' wrong, quiet-like, and call it settled between us, that the idea?"

"Y-yeah, exactly! See, that's not bad, right? I'll properly hand over what Olbart-san wants, and our problem gets solved too!"

What Olbart wanted was the emperor's secret that Abel supposedly held. Subaru didn't know the details, but if that could be handed over, it ought to make it easier for Olbart to back off. To begin with, the "hide-and-seek" had been a sort of test of wits meant to establish trust in that information.

"The promise was for three rounds, and we've only found you twice so far... but cut us some slack there. We did properly make it all the way up to the top of the castle, after all."

"Hmm... by the way, was it you who figured out this was the hidin' spot? Not that other kid, the demon-masked brat?"

"Y-yeah, it was me. 'The Abyss with a fine view' — I just happened to think of that. ...There was one other place I was thinking of too, though."

Perhaps Abel and the others were headed there. Either way, sooner or later, Abel and the others would surely arrive at the answer too, and make their way to Crimson Ruri Castle. Subaru had cheated a bit, using Rui's teleportation and Ubilk's "advice." Still, if he could rack up an achievement here, it might make it easier to persuade Abel and the others about how to treat Rui. And if that still proved difficult, there was also the option of winning Yorna over as an ally. Though, once Yorna learned Rui's true nature, there was no telling how she'd react either.

"Anyway! What do you say, Olbart-san. Would you be willing to hear my — no, our proposal?"

Shaking his head to drive away negative thoughts, Subaru looked at Olbart. The reason he'd said "our" in the plural was that this proposal for reconciliation needed Yorna's cooperation. If Yorna were to declare that she wouldn't forgive Olbart for luring Tanza into his scheme, then the proposal Subaru had worked so hard to come up with would all be for nothing. However—

"————"

With one eye closed and the pipe held in her mouth, Yorna raised no objection. Her not objecting, Subaru thought, meant that she was respecting his opinion. It was so, so appreciated. He wanted to thank her so much. So as not to waste that trust — or was it kindness — that Yorna had entrusted to him—

"Olbart-san."

Once more, Subaru called Olbart's name, hoping for a step toward reconciliation. Receiving Subaru's black-eyed gaze, filled with that hope, Olbart picked up the gourd he'd set down with his fingers. Bringing it to his lips, he drained the liquor inside to the very last drop, then said,

"Say, boy. Whether that's thinkin' unique to how you are right now, or somethin' you'd think even as your ordinary self — I haven't the foggiest idea..."

"————"

"But once I've thrown down a challenge, tossin' it aside halfway through, all half-hearted-like — that ain't somethin' I care to do. Unfortunately for you. —Old geezers, y'see, are stubborn things."

Laughing in that oily way, the strange old man crushed the gourd in his hand, and it shattered to pieces. Fragments scattered with a clatter, and Subaru gasped. Immediately after—

"An old fool who cannot even take the hand a child has offered — regret that stubbornness of yours."

Yorna's long leg shot up and came slashing down, and with a thunderous crash, the main tower of Crimson Ruri Castle split clean in two.

△▼△▼△▼△

The foot in its thick-soled geta rose high, then came down straight and fast. In an instant, the main tower, struck directly by her heel, bent, and a beat later cracked, was smashed to pieces, and burst apart with a roar as though struck by an explosion.

Amid the blasted roof tiles flying up and the thick smoke billowing into the air, the one standing at the epicenter, kimono hem flaring dashingly, having unleashed that single ferocious blow, was Yorna. A single continuous leap and heel-drop — the result of that tremendous destructive power was the castle's collapse into ruin. Quite literally, one strike of Yorna's heel had brought down the main tower, ruining the beautiful scenery of Crimson Ruri Castle.

"Uwaaaaah—!?"

Witnessing that overwhelming destructive power firsthand, Subaru clutched his head and crouched down. He'd only barely avoided being caught up in the castle's blasted destruction, but the tremendous shaking nearly sent him rolling right off the roof. Though, in that regard, Rui's arm had held him back.

"Uau!"

"Damn it! Why does it always turn out like this...!"

Pulled tight by the arm, half-crouched, Subaru shouted with tears in his eyes. The billowing smoke had gotten in his eyes, and that was what had brought the tears. It was absolutely not because his proposal had been rejected by that stubborn old fool Olbart. Well, that was painful too, but—

"Yorna-san!!"

"Stand back, don't get caught up in this. Unfortunately, holding back isn't among my talents. —That said—"

To Subaru's call from behind, Yorna answered without turning around. Pausing there, Yorna pulled her own leg free from where it had driven into the roof, brushed the dirt from the hem of her kimono, then looked up—

"He truly is light on his feet, I'll grant him that."

What Yorna murmured, directly above her, mixed among the veil of billowing smoke and shattered tile fragments — a small figure spinning as it flew through the air — was Olbart. Basking in Yorna's gaze, Olbart bared his teeth in a "Ha!" of laughter.

"Both of us said no hand-liftin', s'posed to be... but that's off the table now, ain't it?"

"I confess I'm surprised you believe you could win against me in this city. If age has robbed you of your wits, I would recommend handing your seat among the 'Nine Divine Generals' to the next generation."

"Kakakakka! No can do. I'm plannin' on bein' the longest-livin' of the whole 'Nine Divine Generals.'"

In midair and atop the roof, the two exchanged glances, traded intent to attack, and squared off against one another. Yorna brought the pipe to her lips, Olbart flipped upside-down in midair — and as purple smoke billowed out, Olbart kicked off against the sky and dove in at arrow-like speed.

"—!"

Olbart's eyes blazing, his foot-blade collided head-on with Yorna's spreading purple smoke. The pipe smoke, which should have had neither mass nor solidity, received Olbart's foot-blade and, bending as though pliant, dispersed the impact. Seeing this, Olbart's grin deepened.

"Oshaaaaaa——!!"

Spinning his short legs at high speed, he unleashed a furious barrage of kicks — ten, twenty in a row. The purple smoke dispersed the impact of the first and second blows, but under the force of the kicks it was gradually scattered away, and once the smoke had finally been scattered entirely, it could no longer serve as shield or armor. Though to begin with, it was strange enough that it had managed to serve that function at all—

"Quite the interesting trick. I'd love to write that one down in a technique manual."

"Unfortunately, it is a secret art that a Shinobi incapable of loving anyone could never replicate."

The instant Olbart broke through the smoke, Yorna's figure appeared right beside him. Having leapt up into the air, Yorna swung the fine pipe held in her hand, aiming for the wide-open back of Olbart's head. A length and weight rather too unreliable to call a blunt weapon — yet Olbart caught it with an arm swung at high speed — no, with a kunai concealed in his sleeve.

With a high, sharp sound, the kunai hidden in Olbart's sleeve shattered. Naturally it should have been made of suitably fine material, yet the kunai snapped in two, while Yorna's pipe suffered no damage whatsoever. Just what on earth was that pipe of Yorna's made of?

"Kakaka, elders oughta be cared for gently, y'know. Treatin' 'em rough like this — quite the outrageous girl you are."

"I do not deny that there exist elders worthy of gentle care. But I can hardly imagine Elder Olbart being one of them. In that case—"

"Guess I'll just have to take care of myself, then."

Trading quips, the two lobbed lethal attacks back and forth at each other with ease. The blows unleashed from Yorna's slender arms and long legs carried, despite their beautiful appearance, tremendous power capable of leveling even castle walls. By comparison, Olbart's attacks lacked flashiness, mostly just deflecting Yorna's strikes while slipping in the occasional jab between them. And yet—

"Don't need power enough to level a castle to kill a person, anyway. In the end, stick one long needle right in the forehead and it's over."

"Then, do try it."

At Yorna's quiet provocation, Olbart's crafty old face seemed to dissolve into the sky. —No, he'd simply begun moving at a speed too fierce for ordinary eyes to follow.

There was once a legendary boxer said to float like a butterfly and sting like a bee — and Olbart's fighting style, staged atop the half-ruined main tower, was exactly that. To put it even more fittingly, perhaps it would be closer to say he flitted about like a fighter jet and fired off blows like a sniper rifle.

"My, my, quite the one for flitting about, aren't you. A bit too much mischief, I'd say."

Perhaps owing to the strength of Olbart's legs, the tiles he stepped on burst apart, and in the next instant his figure would appear from an entirely different spot, delivering a strike toward Yorna. Yorna, drawing on superhuman reflexes, either caught these with her pipe or dodged them. Both were transcendents, and in the fierceness of their clash, Subaru forgot even to breathe.

"Au...!"

Beside Subaru, Rui, watching the same scene, drew her body tightly inward. When the fight had first begun, there had been a worry that Rui, unable to bear simply waiting through this situation, might leap out herself. But the battle between Yorna and Olbart was no longer on a level where that was even a concern.

Whichever side won, tremendous damage would surely result. That was a result far too different from what Subaru had hoped for.

"But what can I even do? There's no way to stop this, no matter what...!"

Given how flashy this fight was, and how spectacularly the main tower had been destroyed, surely anyone anywhere in the city would know by now that something was happening at the castle. If that were the case, there was a chance the townsfolk who adored Yorna might come flooding in en masse, or that the false emperor's entourage, Olbart's allies, might spring into action. If that happened, it would mark the start of a city-wide war.

That was not what Subaru wanted. Even if this had been Abel's way of inviting Yorna into the great battle for his return to the throne, there was no telling what tragedy an ill-prepared war begun like this might bring.

"Shiiiihaaaa!!"

A high cry rang out, and the spinning strange old man's two feet drove into the roof just before Yorna. An instant later, the impact traveled through the roof, and the ground beneath Yorna's feet exploded, sending her body soaring high into the air — the one in the sky and the one with feet on the ground traded places from how they'd started.

"————"

Beneath Yorna, now risen into the air, shuriken thrown by Olbart closed in from all four directions. There was no telling at all when he'd thrown them. Whether he'd thrown them after sending Yorna into the air, or had thrown them beforehand knowing she'd end up there — either way, there was no escape. Up in the sky, the shuriken would carve cruel wounds into Yorna's soft skin — no,

"You understand, do you not? I am the mistress of this city, the ruler of the Magic City."

At Yorna's response, around her, the spinning blades of the shuriken came to a halt, stopped in midair. What accomplished this was not Yorna moving at superhuman speed. She hadn't even needed to move at all. What stopped the shuriken was a single roof tile that had been sent flying into the sky.

It could hardly be called a fearsome stroke of luck. Because more than ten shuriken had closed in from all four directions, and for every single one of them to be caught by a tile — that was a coincidence that no amount of luck, however great, could ever bring about.

Before the wide eyes of Subaru and Olbart watching that sight, Yorna gave her arm a gentle wave. Then, as though matching the motion of her arm, the tiles that had caught the shuriken swam through the air, and came to rest suspended in the sky, forming a spiral staircase around her.

Unhurried, the thick soles of Yorna's geta found footing on the tiles, and she descended slowly toward the main tower. It was a scene straight out of a sudden illusion, or perhaps an exhibition of psychokinesis. Of course, it could also be called magic, but it was a little different from the magic Subaru knew. What she had manipulated was not fire or ice, wind or earth, but the very parts of the building itself.

"Kahh, you really are a troublesome one, aren't you. So this is it, huh. The trick Cecilus and that Araki fella were on about. How are you even doin' it?"

"I believe I already told you. It is a secret art beyond the reach of a Shinobi incapable of loving another... I, whatever I may appear, am a woman who treats things with care."

Smiling, pipe held between her lips, Yorna clapped her two now-free hands together. As though that were a signal, a change occurred in the half-ruined main tower. —No, "change" was too gentle a word for it. It was a sorcerous technique that warped reality itself, as though it were a dream.

"T-this is..."

The ground beneath his feet warping, Subaru gasped and Rui clung tightly to his arm. Supporting her body, Subaru watched, right before his eyes, the strange phenomenon unfolding — the restoration of the half-ruined main tower. Slowly, like footage of wounds healing sped up many times over, the building righted itself.

Combining Abel's hypothesis that Yorna was a practitioner of the "Soul Marriage Technique" with her words just now, a single hypothesis came to Subaru's mind about what was happening. It was an utterly, utterly absurd notion, but — Yorna Mishigure was—

"S-she's using the 'Soul Marriage Technique'... on the castle too...?"

Before the mistress of the Magic City, displaying quite literally unprecedented, off-the-charts power, all Subaru could do was fall speechless.
