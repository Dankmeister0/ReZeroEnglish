Arc 7 Chapter 104: "Chaos at the Summit (Part One)"

――The word "Great Disaster" makes Abel narrow the black eyes behind his demon mask.

The very man who spoke it, Ubilk, wears a smile on his face that lets nothing of his heart's depths show through.
Gentle, easy to get along with, a man who treats everyone without distinction. His bearing and manner haven't changed since the moment he first appeared before Abel. Not from when he showed up in the audience chamber with no consideration for anyone, no allies at his back — nothing has changed.

Claiming the name "Stargazer," Ubilk had called out one calamity after another befalling the Vollachian Empire, and no matter what event he foresaw, his emotions never once wavered. As if—

"—Even standing here, facing me like this — is even this just another event you saw through from long, long ago?"

"Oh no, no. That's giving me far too much credit, however you slice it. I'm not, ahh, that grand a being."

"If all you can offer are jests, I won't hesitate to cut out that tongue of yours."

"Ye-e-esh, scary, scary. My stomach's in knots as always, I tell you."

Shrugging his thin shoulders, Ubilk drawls this out without a trace of tension. At his attitude, Abel scoffs, and beside him Serena murmurs, "I don't get it."

Glancing between Ubilk with his raised hands and Abel's profile, she says,

"I've seen that face. That's the 'Stargazer' who comes and goes from the Crystal Palace... one of His Majesty's pet jesters, isn't he? You're acquainted with him?"

"Regrettably so."

"Hm. ...I hadn't intended to stir things up until this business is settled, but perhaps I should press the question — just who is it hiding behind that demon mask of yours?"

Her narrow, sharp eyes carry an intelligent gleam as Serena voices her doubts about Abel's identity.
From the start, she must have harbored great suspicion here. In this great civil war shaking the Empire, a man who'd slipped his way into becoming commander of the rebel army — including the matter of his hidden face — in the sense that his true intentions couldn't be gauged, he was every bit Ubilk's equal.

But—

"Answering that question is not something to prioritize right now."

"There's also the option of tearing the mask off by force. I've some confidence in my own arm, you know."

"Don't. Consider that a warning."

"Oh? A warning, to me?"

At Abel's words, Serena's eyes narrow, glinting with battle-readiness.
It's that very fierceness that lets Serena Dracroy monopolize the epithet "the Scorching Duchess" — she stands here today because she's burned away every insult and mockery ever hurled at her. To a woman of such convictions, Abel's answer carried more than enough heat to provoke.

"Wait, wait, wa-a-ait a moment! Let's all calm down! What's with this tense atmosphere just because I showed my face like this!"

"If you simply keep quiet and watch, you may yet become the unsung hero of this battle without lifting a finger."

"Ahh, that's not something I'm hoping for, you know. Either one of you two — or both — going down here, and with me as the trigger no less — that's the last thing I want."

"—I don't get it."

Reaching a hand toward the two growing hostile, Ubilk's voice takes on a comparatively urgent edge. At his reaction, Serena circles back to the same question once more. As she does, she toys idly with the hilt of the sword at her hip, one eye closing.

"Awe-inspiring as it may be to be attendant to His Imperial Majesty Vincent Vollachia, surely your proper role would be to attack the two of us — myself, who commands the rebels, and this man here. Left to his own devices, this man would have died. Wouldn't that be something to welcome, for you?"

"Don't go killing me off on your own authority. There's a path where you die instead."

"My apologies, but right now I am speaking with this 'Stargazer.' Don't interrupt the flow of conversation."

"――――"

Faced with Serena, who wants to drive the conversation on her own terms, Abel holds off on interjecting for now. When Serena turns her attention back to Ubilk, he wears a blank, bewildered expression.

"Necessary as it may have been, from the outside that was a scene rich in inte-e-erest and flavor, wasn't it. Ah, ah, please don't make such a scary face. I'm unarmed, you know."

"Being unarmed is no reason for me to let my guard down. Give me a reason, and quickly, why I shouldn't have my hand on this sword's hilt. You know as well as anyone — I don't hesitate to cut down an unresisting opponent."

"—You can't cut me down, you know. Not you."

Serena's tone had grown steadily harsher, ending in what amounted to a threat. Ubilk, who until a moment before had worn a thoroughly cowed expression, denied it with just that single line.

The instant he said it, all color drained from Ubilk's face.
Even without seeing the full shape of that emotion, at the very least every emotion he'd shown any willingness to display drained away, leaving behind only a hollow, empty, colorless face.

"――――"

At a declaration that could be taken as a provocation, Serena did not fly into rage, nor did she draw her sword.
What filled her chest more than anger was likely an inscrutable eeriness toward the being called Ubilk — no, toward the creature called a "Stargazer" — and a thread of unease. Like facing an opponent right in front of her and yet somehow not facing him at all — that hollow lack of resistance.

"...Shouldn't we kill you right here?"

"Ahh, how cruel. I'm terribly hurt, you know. What do you think?"

"I too have thought the same, time and again, just as Serena Dracroy does now. And yet, even so, this man has lived on to this day. That is the fact of it."

Being unchanging toward everyone means never knowing whose reverse scale you might touch.
Ubilk, who knows nothing of adapting to circumstances, has touched many a reverse scale in this Vollachian Empire, provoked the wrath of countless people, and at times had blades turned on him.
And yet the reason Ubilk has survived to this day is—

"The stars' will, you see. Oh, Ubilk... it's still too soon, too-o-o soon, to die."

Pressing his hands together before his chest, Ubilk's breezy manner of speaking makes Serena's cheek stiffen.
Whether that's true or not is meaningless to debate. What's true is that Ubilk appeared here at all — no, that's been so from the very first, from when he first appeared at the Crystal Palace.

Ubilk does not die.
As if to say his life is placed under the protection of, and guaranteed by, the stars he speaks of — the "Observers" who mock this entire world without exception.

"――――"

Feeling the insistence of a heat he'd suppressed within, thinking such thoughts, Abel let out a breath.
Abel's personal feelings toward Ubilk and the other "Stargazers" are a trivial matter. What matters is that Ubilk has appeared before Abel's eyes like this.
This clown who ties every event to "the stars" appearing before Abel is—

"The stage you once stepped down from — 'were made to step down from' might be more accura-a-ate, I think — do you intend to climb it once more?"

Ubilk's gaze, ignoring Serena, turns to Abel.
Not understanding the meaning of those words, Serena furrows her fine brows. But he has no intention of explaining it to her in kind detail. Above all, that question is, in truth, thoroughly infuriating to Abel.

"Let me say this."

"Yes?"

"Not once have I ever stepped down from what you call the stage."

Crossing his arms, he first corrects the misunderstanding behind Ubilk's proposal.
Driven from the throne, banished to the distant east, Abel has been placed in dire straits time and again. And yet his fighting spirit has never once been broken.
Much less has he ever forgotten the role he must fulfill, nor ever thought to let it go.

"You wouldn't understand, would you, 'Stargazer.' You, who have never once climbed onto a stage."

"—Hm. That's a bit of a sore spot to hear, is it not?"

"Even that much, you likely don't understand."

Lining up plausible words and expressions, Ubilk tries to keep pace with Abel's conversation. But in Ubilk's words, unaccompanied by any real emotion, there was no resonance of having grasped Abel's true meaning.
And that, Abel had already concluded, was the limit of what Ubilk was capable of.
Therefore—

"The time draws near. That is why you came here as well, is it not?"

"...That's so, but — did you know I would come?"

"Don't jest. I am not like you. I may guess at what lies a second ahead, but I do not claim certainty. — Blind faith might be the more fitting term for it, though."

"――――"

At Abel's manner of speaking, Ubilk's expression shifts, ever so slightly.
The faint tremor in the movement of his brow stems from a negative emotion he rarely shows. Anger or displeasure — whichever it was, it was without doubt a rare reaction.
Not that any cheap sense of satisfaction rose in Abel from seeing it, unfortunately.

"So then, what will you do? Attendant to His Majesty as you no doubt still are — shall I try sending him your severed head?"

"That would cause the Emperor no pain whatsoever. Perhaps it might cause him some displeasure that he wished to have ordered it done himself, but that's as far as it would go. More than that—"

"More than that?"

Sensing that the exchange between Abel and Ubilk — whose details she couldn't fully follow — was moving toward some conclusion, Serena tilts her head.
Abel glances sidelong at her, her wavy brown hair swaying over her shoulders, and pauses a beat.
And then, he declares:

"I will entrust you with a great task. — A role on this battlefield that none but you can fulfill."

△▼△▼△▼△

Kicking through the piling white snow, a shadow tears across the battlefield in every direction.
Ground thinly sheeted with ice, slick in patches; air pushing past bitter cold into something approaching a killing frost that seems to slice the skin; an enemy so formidable that merely being looked down upon freezes the instincts in fear — kicking all these various circumstances aside entirely, he races through at the speed of lightning.

"You, you, you—"

"Ahahahaha! I told you, it's not 'you' — it's Cecilus Segmunt, remember! Being called out at the top of your lungs carries so much further across a battlefield, so I heartily welcome it..."

"Cecilus Segmu-u-unt—!!"

"That's the one!"

Leaving an afterimage, the smile blurs, and in the next instant the sole of a sandal plants itself into the side of the "Cloud Dragon's" face.

With a bursting crack of sound, the head is flung back violently, and Mezoreia's massive body, aloft in the air, sways hugely. Emilia is astonished that the one who did this is a boy far shorter than herself.

"That kid is amazing..."

The dragon's breath had been about to sweep the battlefield clean — a boy who not only stopped it at the very last moment but then, as if it were nothing, announced his own name outright and began a fight against the dragon without yielding a single step.
Emilia found her eyes captured by the blue-haired child who called himself Cecilus.
As though she were witnessing, right before her, the very "grand stage" that boy had so boldly proclaimed.

"――――"

Within Emilia's field of view, Cecilus's body flickers in and out like something from a dream or an illusion. At tremendous speed he appears and vanishes from one edge of her sight to the other, up, down, over and over.
Too fast to follow with her eyes, Emilia feels her eyeballs might start spinning in circles.
And the same goes for Mezoreia, plagued by Cecilus's constant harrying.

"—Guh! The likes of you! An enemy — an enemy of dragons!"

Wings beating in the air, the "Cloud Dragon's" claws and tail lash out wildly, recklessly.
They're aimed at Cecilus, who bounds about energetically using as footholds the ice wall Emilia made — made so that Mezoreia couldn't escape the battlefield.
But every one of the dragon's attacks lands only on spots Cecilus has long since passed through; not a single one so much as grazes the real target.

More than that, Cecilus leaps onto the very tail striking the wall, and rides it straight up onto Mezoreia's back, all in one motion—

"Tei tei tei tei tei tei!"

With kicks so fast it seemed as though he'd sprouted several extra legs, he sent Mezoreia crashing down to the ground.

"O-oh, he dropped it...!"

Wings thrashing wildly, Mezoreia, flipped over in midair, plummets to the earth.
A tremendous roar and tremor shake the ground; the snow-crusted earth is torn up and scattered, and Emilia, bathed in the cold wind, barely holds back her astonishment at the sight.
Still, she recognizes this as an outrageously lucky opening — one not to be missed.

"If I finish Mezoreia off now while..."

"Whoa now, that won't do. I'll have to call a halt to that."

"Eh?"

Feeling it was a bit of a cheap shot but doing it anyway, Emilia gathers mana around herself, intending to drop a great chunk of ice onto the fallen Mezoreia from above.
But her movement stops in surprise when Cecilus slides in right in front of her, just as she's about to act, and points a finger right at the tip of her nose.
Emilia blinks in surprise, and Cecilus, having given her nose a little poke with his finger, says:

"See here — wasn't that just now the flow where I swooped in at the last possible second to save you from danger? And thus began the one-on-one duel with the dragon... you understand what you're meant to do here, don't you? That's right — believe fervently in my victory and pray for it! That's the role of the princess!"

"Um... but I'm not a princess, you know? Though I'm not unrelated to a king."

"'Princess' is just a figure of speech, you see. More precisely, you're the flower of the story who falls head over heels for the hero — namely, me — who rescued you. Don't you think that's a perfect role for someone as beautiful as you?"

"Ah, sorry. I've already decided who I'm going to fall in love with."

"Ah, is that so? Well, can't be helped then. Do go on."

Even rattled off at tremendous speed, Emilia didn't miss the part she shouldn't miss. And so Cecilus caught on right away and backed off gracefully.
Since he backed off, Emilia, without hesitation, once more raised her hand toward Mezoreia,

"Yah!"

And a giant chunk of ice plummeted straight down from the sky, aimed at Mezoreia sprawled on the ground.
It plunged into the dragon's exposed belly as it lay on its back, and a tremendous roar echoed out. Listening to the dragon's low groan of pain, Emilia readied "one more" for the next strike, but—

"Pardon the intrusion."

Right after, Emilia's braced legs were swept out from under her, and her body, letting out a startled "Ahh!" was lifted lightly and pulled away from the spot with a force like yanking her clean out of it.
The next instant, a fierce wind whipped up where Emilia and Cecilus had just been standing, and the shockwave the dragon sent through the ground gouged out that very spot, earth and all, in a great circular crater.
Had they been even one step slower in clearing out, they would have died.

"As expected, when the opponent's a dragon, it's not so simple — the highlights come by the second. I've been feeling frustrated lately, stuck against nothing but pushover opponents, so this is just right."

"Th-thank you, for saving me."

"Not at all, think nothing of it! Even for a beauty who already has someone she fancies, well then, I'll just change up how I show off. Do invite me to the wedding, without fail!"

Having scooped her up and saved her in the nick of time, Cecilus breaks into a grin at Emilia's thanks.
Strictly speaking it's not quite "someone she fancies" so much as "someone she might come to fancy," but there's no leisure now to get into such fine details. What matters is—

"Use it, Cecilus!"

"Use it? Even if you say that, I'm not sure what to use... oh!"

As Emilia, set down on the ground, places her hand against the earth, Cecilus, who'd begun to tilt his head, lights up. In his field of view, ice swords, spears, and axes of every sort rise up out of the ground.
The Icebrand Arts, which conjure weapons of ice, deploy a row of weapons stretching from right in front of Cecilus all the way to Mezoreia.

"Now this is quite the spectacle! Lovely, how cool! Truth be told, I'd rather hold nothing in my hands but suitably excellent famed swords or Enchanted Swords, but..."

"So, no good?"

"No, it's not a rule I'm imposing on anyone but myself, so let's quietly amend it! On this occasion, against a dragon, swinging weapons about wildly makes for a flashier show anyway!"

As he speaks, Cecilus stretches the arms of his small body to their fullest and yanks out two ice swords from either side of him. The moment he does, Emilia goes "Ah" — realizing her mistake.
Emilia made those ice swords. That's why she doesn't feel them as cold — but for someone who isn't her, they might be far too cold.

"Come to think of it, Priscilla never said anything about them being cold either..."

"No need to worry. That sort of inconvenience can be compensated for with the Flow Method, so both I and the others of the Troupe are just fine! Well, I handle it through sheer natural ability since getting caught up in the Boss's whatnot throws off my rhythm, but it's true that I am something specially special!"

"Speshully special..."

Words she's never heard strung together one after another, and Emilia can't help but repeat it back.
This feeling, this unnaturalness, is just like talking with Subaru — and having thought that far, Emilia realizes, "Could it be—"

"Hey, Cecilus! That way you talk — didn't you learn it from Subaru?"

"Mr. Subaru, is it? No, sorry to say, that's not it. The one who taught me this sort of talk was the Boss, but the Boss's name wasn't Mr. Subaru, you see."

"I see, that's not it... I guess I jumped to conclusions."

Having leaned forward with expectation, Emilia deflates a little at Cecilus's answer. But telling herself she mustn't be discouraged, she quickly slaps her own cheeks to steel herself again.
And then, taking up an ice spear just as Cecilus had,

"Switching gears... let's fight Mezoreia together!"

"That change of heart, excellent indeed. Come to think of it, I still haven't heard your name."

"Me? I'm Emilia... no wait, Emilie! Emilie!"

"I see, there are circumstances at play! But I think it best not to pry into such matters here, so I'll leave it be, Miss Emilie. Now then, might I ask one thing of you right away?"

"A favor? Of me?"

"Yes. — While I'm occupied dealing with that dragon there, would you mind clearing that away?"

Having exchanged names with Emilia, Cecilus lowers his voice to make his request. What he points to is a patch of ground a little way off from where Mezoreia fell.
Looking at what Cecilus pointed to, on the snow-blanketed ground, Emilia's eyes go wide with an "Ah."
And then—

"So I'll leave it to you, Miss Emilie. I'm off to do my job... and it's one only the star player of this world's stage could pull off, flashy as it'll be!"

Leaving her with those words, without waiting for Emilia's answer, Cecilus kicks off the ground.
Snow scatters as Cecilus, gripping his twin ice swords, charges straight at the dragon. Mezoreia's scales instantly sense the danger from the small threat barreling in.

"Getting carried away with yourse-e-elf—!"

A blood-chilling roar rises, and Mezoreia digs its claws into the ice mass trying to crush its own body. In that instant, the ice mass, huge as a small hill, doesn't withstand even a single second — cracks race across its entire surface with tremendous force, and it shatters all at once.
The moment the weight of the ice is gone, Mezoreia instantly rolls over, drops flat to the ground, and moves to unleash an attack on the charging Cecilus—

"Eiyah!!"

Right into Mezoreia's snout, the spear Emilia hurls, wound up and thrown, strikes true.
The sharp ice spear can't bury its tip into Mezoreia's scales even a little, but with considerable force it knocks the dragon's head aside, delaying its attack on Cecilus.
Into that opening, at nearly the same speed as the thrown spear, Cecilus dives in.

"No blinking allowed, don't you dare look away, tips and applause all welcome, anything goes!"

Rattling off words with breezy ease, Cecilus's twin ice swords flash out in streaks of white light.
Struck through the scales with a light, crisp sound, knocked right and left, the "Cloud Dragon" has its eyes stolen by that sword dance, its freedom stolen, its chance at a counterattack stolen.

Small as he still is, Cecilus's skill is simply outrageous.
Emilia had thought herself somewhat accomplished in how she used her body and handled weapons, but this is on a level beyond comparison. Perhaps, just perhaps, he holds within him the potential to grow every bit as wildly, absurdly strong as Reid, whom she encountered at the Pleiades Watchtower.

"But please don't turn out mean like Reid, okay?"

Whatever his skill with a blade, Reid was terribly mean-spirited, so she hopes Cecilus's personality won't turn out that way.
Harboring that wish, Emilia, glancing sidelong at Cecilus drawing Mezoreia's attention, hurries toward a spot a little removed from the fight.
The favor Cecilus had just asked of her draws steadily closer.
And there, at the place Emilia rushed to, was—

"—Madelyn! This is no time to be sleeping! Wake up, and persuade Mezoreia!"

Cradling up the body of Madelyn Eschart, buried in the snow and not moving even a twitch, Emilia called out to her, desperate.
