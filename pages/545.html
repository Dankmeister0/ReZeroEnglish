Arc 7 Chapter 47: "The Unvanishing ■■"

"—!"

With a thin, faint shriek, the small body went spinning, spinning through the air. The girl bounced slowly across the ground, unable to kill her momentum, tumbling again and again until she reached the edge of the street. She slammed into the skeletal frame of a tent stripped of its canvas, and the tilting struts came down with a shrill clatter, burying her small body beneath them.

Subaru watched it happen, stunned speechless.

He couldn't excuse himself by saying it happened too fast to understand. It was true that it happened in an instant, too fast for him to even reach out a hand — but in that instant, his black eyes had seen everything that happened, clear as day.

His mind a jumbled mess, he'd bolted out into the dangerous street without a single thought. And sure enough, the enemy had spotted him, and he'd been put in mortal danger — Al and Medium had both been hurt trying to save him, and then—

"...Rui."

Rui, who had spread her arms and stepped in front of him to shield him, was sent flying like a rubber ball. It was a Sheep Human boy who'd done it — white-haired, with short horns. With an arm no thicker than Subaru's own, he'd sent Subaru flying nearly ten meters straight up as if it were nothing. Rui had taken that same boy's swing full-on and been blown away by it. Subaru had seen the blood spray — there was no way he could believe she was all right.

"Sorry."

Subaru's eyes followed Rui, buried beneath the wreckage of the tent. A small apology trembled against his eardrums, and at the edge of his swaying vision, he saw the boy raising his arm again.

Dodging — that kind of nimble feat was beyond him. To begin with, Subaru's own limbs were still buzzing numb from his fall from that great height; he could barely move at all. So even though Rui had thrown herself in the way to protect him, it would all be for nothing—

"—Subaru-chin!!"

"Ah."

"Get up!!"

A shadow came flying in with tremendous force and drove a slender leg into the boy's shoulder. The boy staggered back, startled, and the one who'd cut in was Medium, her long golden hair streaming out behind her. Her face was flushed red as she called out for Subaru to stand.

To Subaru, who lay sprawled on the ground, too rattled even to properly shake.

"Take care of Rui-chan!"

And with that — no words of encouragement, no comfort — Medium said only that to Subaru and ran off. Gripping the single sword she'd brought with both hands, moving nothing like the graceful, dance-like way she usually fought, she threw everything she had into driving the boy back all the same. The boy's face had gone stiff, too, as if it were her sheer intensity — more than her strength — that had caught him off guard.

"Ngh, guhhh...!"

While Medium fought on like that, Subaru forced his trembling limbs to work and hauled himself to his feet. Then he ran toward the tent that had come down on Rui. He shoved the fallen framework aside piece by piece, digging Rui out from beneath the wreckage.

"Rui, Rui! Are — are you alive! Hey, Rui!"

Calling out desperately, Subaru felt that heat welling up again from behind his eyes. This troublesome heat had been lurking behind his face for a while now, waiting for its moment to break free. He ignored it, hurrying, hurrying, clearing away the debris.

And then—

"...Uu."

"Rui!"

A weak groan sounded, and Subaru found Rui, her body caked with dust. Buried beneath the collapsed tent, Rui had been wedged into a pocket of space formed by the fallen support poles, and had narrowly escaped being crushed.

That much was a relief. But right after the relief came a wave of helplessness.

"There's no way I can move this..."

The fallen support beam was as thick around as Subaru's own torso, and it wouldn't budge no matter how he pushed or pulled. Even if he tried to use leverage, he couldn't find a proper fulcrum.

And as he searched for some way to pull Rui free, he noticed something else.

Little by little, Rui's white clothes were being stained — not with dust, but with a spreading red.

"...Ah."

Struck dumb at the sight, Subaru's limbs went cold and his thoughts froze solid. So this was what it meant for the blood to drain from you. It wasn't even his own blood, yet he had no idea where his own seemed to have gone. Shouldn't it have to go somewhere hot instead? Only the pounding of his heart remained, loud enough that it felt ready to burst.

"Ahh..."

Even now, as she let out that weak groan, the red stain on Rui's clothes kept spreading. His frozen limbs wouldn't move; he couldn't even manage to fake the effort of clearing the wreckage, let alone actually do it, to reassure her.

"Rui, Rui..."

From his trembling lips, he kept calling her name. Even that much he couldn't manage properly, his voice cracking apart. His tongue felt numb — no, it was that some part of Subaru's heart was violently refusing to say her name at all.

Now that he thought of it, Subaru had always kept even the act of saying Rui's name to the bare minimum.

Ever since she and Rem had been flung down together from that great tower, Rui had always been kept close at hand. And the whole time, Subaru had regarded her with wariness, kept her at a distance, treated her carelessly. And yet, not once had Rui done anything dangerous or harmful to Subaru.

He didn't want Rem to hate him for it. He didn't want the people around him to be afraid. Frightened of things like that, Subaru had gone on refusing to truly connect with Rui, all while endlessly putting off the one decision that mattered — what to finally do about her.

Why had he been so afraid of Rui, of all things? His numbed brain, his cold limbs, his drying tongue, his heart on the verge of bursting — none of them would let him understand.

He couldn't properly recall what Rui had done to him. It was as if a fog had settled over his memory; in this instant, he couldn't pry open that drawer of recollection.

But there was one thing that was clear without even opening that drawer. That Rui had bled trying to protect him.

That much, at least, was a fact he could neither forget nor look away from.

"D-don't... die..."

"—"

"Don't die, Rui! You can't — you can't die...! You just can't...!"

Twisting his small body into the gap between the beams, Subaru knelt down beside Rui. Rather than pulling her free, all he could do was raise a voice that helped nothing. He took Rui's hand, gripped it tightly with both of his own, and said, desperate and praying,

"Please don't die...!"

Forgetting even to search for something — anything — he could actually do to keep her from dying, he made his unreasonable plea instead. At the sound of Subaru's trembling voice, Rui's eyes opened faintly, and then—

"—Uah, u."

A faint voice slipped out, and just the barest trace of strength returned to the girl's eyes.

△▼△▼△▼△

"Rgh, yaaaaah—!!"

Swinging the single sword she held in both hands — one half of her usual paired blades — Medium fought back against the boy in front of her. The Sheep Human boy's cheeks were tight with tension, but he blocked Medium's attack easily with a raised arm — quite literally catching the blade on his arm and knocking it aside.

"Waaah, he's so hard! It won't cut at all! That's not fair!"

Medium had been throwing everything she had into blow after blow, but each time, the boy's sturdy body stopped her cold. His skin, his hair, his fingers, his head — every part of him was hard as rock, and she was completely at a loss. And on top of the fact that her attacks weren't getting through—

"—!"

"That was close!!"

The boy's wildly swung arm grazed past Medium and slammed into the ground; she felt the earth shudder from the impact, and a crater far larger than the arm itself opened up in the street. The sheer force behind the boy's blows was dizzying. The only reason Medium hadn't taken a direct hit yet was that his movements were riddled with openings.

"He's super tough and strong, but he's terrible at actually fighting!"

He was just like a baby born with a giant body, Medium thought. She'd never actually seen a baby born with a giant body, so it was just a comparison she came up with off the feeling of it, but it seemed to fit perfectly. For now, all she had to deal with was that one baby-boy, but—

"If I don't hurry, more people are gonna come..."

She licked her lips, tasting dirt, burning with a need not to linger here any longer. They'd happened to end up on a street with few pursuers, but if she kept making a racket like this, it would draw attention fast, and the boy's allies would come running. If someone bigger than the boy, or someone actually skilled in combat, showed up, she might not stand a chance. If only she weren't alone, there'd be some way to manage.

"Al-chin! Come on, Al-chin!"

Dodging the boy's wild swings by hopping and leaping about, Medium called out. At the edge of her vision, the one Medium was desperately calling out to was the hunched-over masked boy — no, Al. A child with one hand, in an even worse position than her to wield a weapon — but when it came to handling dangerous situations, he was better at it than she was, and that was what she wanted to rely on now.

"—"

But he gave no answer to her plea. It wasn't that he'd hit his head and been knocked out when he was sent flying. Al was conscious, and he had tried, once, to properly come to her aid. But now he'd suddenly stopped in his tracks and dropped to his knees. Crouched there like that, Al stared down at his own right hand through his mask—

"...Why...?"

he murmured, as though trembling. As if he'd let go of, or dropped, something he should have been holding.

"Al-chin...!"

Glancing sidelong at Al, unable to rise, a thread of weakness pierced Medium's chest. At once, the strength drained from the hand gripping her weapon, and Medium bit down hard on her back teeth.

—It had always been like this.

Whenever Medium's own spirit faltered, her body faltered right along with it. So she always tried to stay positive, keep her voice loud, keep her back straight. When her heart was bright and full, Medium could give it everything she had. Her brother Flop always cheering her on with a "You can do it!" had always pushed her forward too.

But Flop wasn't here. Neither was anyone else who'd say those words to her. Everyone had their own problems to deal with, and Medium herself had been shrunk down small, so she could only hold one sword at a time, and now she was about to lose to a little boy.

"—There they are!!"

"Ah."

A voice rang out that dragged Medium's flagging spirit down even further. Dodging the boy's swinging arm and shifting her footing, Medium's eyes went wide. In her line of sight, appearing from the far end of the street, were enemy reinforcements, flame burning in one eye each.

Charging forward with fierce momentum, they closed in behind the kneeling Al. Even if she tried to rush over there, she'd have to get past the boy blocking her way first.

—She wouldn't make it in time. They were going to die. Al-chin, and Subaru-chin, and Rui-chan too.

Everyone was going to die.

Nearly dropping the sword in her grip, Medium desperately called out for her brother inside her own head. If only, like always, she could hear her brother's voice, his encouragement, his cheering.

But the fresh enemies closing in on Al were far faster than any illusion of a cheer could ever reach her. Al's small body, about to be torn apart by the hand reaching for him from behind—

"—Still can't move, fool?"

Before those sharp claws could reach Al's back, a leg shot in from the side and kicked the shoulder of the attacker away. Al's body toppled sideways, narrowly escaping the blow meant to gouge into his back. The one who'd carried out that rough intervention was the man with his face hidden behind an oni mask.

"Abel-chin?"

The name slipped out of her open, stunned mouth, and Abel's attention flicked toward her for a moment. Even through the mask, she could feel the heat of their eyes meeting, and Medium's shoulders drew up slightly. Abel, who had left the boy to Medium and the others, had stepped into the battle himself.

Maybe he'd say he couldn't just stand by and watch anymore — maybe he'd scold her.

She hated being scolded. In the old days, she'd been scolded, and hit, often. Small as she was, Medium ate a lot, which only made it worse. Hit, scolded, over and over. So whenever she thought she was about to be scolded, her whole body would shrink in on itself—

"Medium."

Called by name, Medium's body froze, bracing for the rebuke to follow. But Abel paid no mind to her fright and went on with what he had to say. And it was—

"—Do well."

"—"

At the blunt words that shook her eardrums, Medium's eyes went wide, and she clenched down hard on her chattering teeth. She bit down, her cheeks twisting, her golden hair whipping.

"Right! I'll do my best—!!"

Pushed forward by a strength that welled up as if out of nowhere, Medium slugged the boy in front of her. The boy staggered back, startled by the blow; she pressed the advantage, driving the flat of her blade up under his chin from below, kicking into his wide-open torso, stomping down on his over-extended knee, and slamming a roundhouse kick into his neck.

With Medium suddenly and fiercely back in the fight, the boy found himself purely on the defensive. Even as it felt a little like bullying someone weaker, Medium reared back her fist and struck him again and again. As she punched, a voice rose up in her mind.

—How's that feel? Pay back double what was done to you, and it feels pretty good, doesn't it!

Those were the words of the benefactor who had pulled Medium and Flop out of their hard circumstances. Remembering the words of the man who had taught her how to fight, how to live, Medium smiled.

She smiled, and thought, Punching someone this much doesn't actually feel that good, though!

"Rgh, yaaaah!"

Even as she thought it, she put everything into a horizontal swing at the boy's head, and his small body went flying. A blow that would normally have taken a head clean off amounted to nothing more than a solid hit against the boy's sturdy body. Still, she'd managed to take down one of them somehow, and now to go help Al and Abel—

"Damn you, scurrying around like that...!"

"Ow! Oh no!"

She'd been so focused on her fight with the boy that she'd let her guard down against an opponent closing in from behind. Her tied-back hair was seized roughly, and Medium's body was hoisted straight up. Her feet left the ground, and she couldn't react in time.

"Uu~! Ah, come on!"

Kicking her legs, eyes watering from the pain of having her hair yanked, she looked back to see that the one who'd lifted her was a hulking Rhinoceros Human, bigger even than Medium had been before she'd shrunk. His hide and flesh were even thicker than the boy's, and he didn't so much as flinch at Medium's kicks.

"Sorry! I can't get away!"

Struggling, Medium craned her neck and shouted with all her might. Looking over, she saw Al still lying where he'd been knocked down in the street, and Abel — who'd done the knocking — now surrounded by the reinforcements who'd come rushing in, backed against a wall. Even Medium understood there was no way for someone as frail as Abel to take on all of them. So—

"Abel-chin! Grab Al-chin and run!"

Somehow, she just wanted him to find an opening, however he could, and get away. At Medium's desperate cry, the men surrounding Abel closed off the line of retreat as if to block her out of the picture entirely. One of them — a middle-aged Bull Human — stepped up close to Abel.

"Sorry, but we can't let you go. This is where you—"

"—You are a Bull Human. The one before was a Sheep Human."

"What?"

"Those who surrounded the inn were all horned as well... With this much to go on, anyone could work it out. Those hunting us — they are the Horned Peoples."

Surrounded and cornered as he should have been, there was no fear in Abel's voice. If anything, his bearing was so haughty it seemed as though he were the one surrounding them, and it was the men who found themselves rattled by the words that came from him.

—No, it wasn't only Abel's brazenness that had rattled them, but the substance of what he'd said.

"Horned..."

Medium murmured, her eyes shifting to the Rhinoceros Human who held her hair. The huge man's attention, too, was fixed on Abel's words. And there, above his broad nose, she could see one thick horn growing. Now that she thought about it, she remembered that the Sheep Human boy from before, and all the many people Taritta had drawn off at the inn, had all had horns too.

"But so what!"

"Fool. —That is precisely the root of this whole affair."

"Huh?"

Whether he'd heard Medium's shout or not, there came Abel's reply. But Medium couldn't see him from where she was, and even being told that was the reason, she had no idea what he meant. She wished he'd either explain properly so she could understand, or just get on with things without needing her to understand at all. Otherwise, at this rate—

"—Try, and see."

"Huh?"

That wasn't a response to the scream in Medium's heart. The words, spoken quietly and with great weight, were directed by Abel at the men surrounding him — his eyes fixed on the Bull Human standing at their head. The man's eyes widened, and he stared hard back at Abel. At that reaction, Abel pressed on, continuing.

"Try, and see whether you are capable of killing me."

"...What could possibly be hard about snapping that scrawny neck of yours?"

The man ground his heel into the dirt, his left eye burning red. Even without the effects of Yorna's Soul Marriage Technique, the man's thick arms alone would have made snapping Abel's neck a simple enough matter. And the resolve to kill, the resolve needed to break that neck, was something they had already settled within themselves. And yet Abel stood with his arms crossed, unshaken, his black eyes piercing through the mask to skewer the man before him—

"Whether it is easy or not — the answer lies beyond the act itself. So try, and see. If you possess the Vessel for it, the flame will honor you. But—"

"B-but?"

"Should you fall short of the Vessel, the flame will burn even your Soul to nothing. —Now, what will you do?"

"—"

With his arms still crossed, Abel spoke, and the Bull Human fell silent. He found himself daunted by the slender man standing so composedly before him. Of course, if it came down to brute strength, victory would unmistakably belong to him. And yet — what was this unshaken composure? Perhaps the answer to that lay hidden somewhere within the words, however like a sham they sounded, that had just been spoken.

"Try, and see. —Whether you yourself can surpass the flame called Judgment."

"Grh, guh, guhhh...!"

Overwhelmed, the man groaned, his thick throat trembling. Drenched in sweat, every muscle taut, not even his companions could bring themselves to speak to him. The moment he'd met Abel's eyes, everyone else had already been struck from the stage. No one but the man standing right before him now had any right to answer Abel's words. The grinding creak of clenched teeth echoed, and echoed, and echoed—

"Guh, gwaaaaah—!!"

With a crack as his ground teeth split, driven on by the pain, the man raised both arms. This was no mere neck-snap — it was a blow meant to crush the very life out of Abel's whole body. One strike from arms as thick as logs, and Abel's slender frame would be pulped, crushed to ruin.

Medium twisted desperately, trying to break free of her captor's grip, but it wouldn't budge. Al remained collapsed on the ground, not even aware of Abel's peril. And Subaru and Rui, too—

"—Huh?"

Rui, buried under the collapsed tent, and Subaru, who had rushed to save her. He could be seen having crawled into the wreckage, holding Rui's hand. Then Rui, pinned beneath the debris, stirred faintly, and then—

—In the next instant, both their figures vanished from beneath the tent without a trace.

"Uu—!"

In that same instant, the vanished Rui reappeared and drove a blow straight up into the jaw of the Bull Human with his arms still raised.

△▼△▼△▼△

Subaru had no idea at all what had just happened.

He'd clung to Rui, pinned beneath the tent, slowly bleeding. He'd thrown at her the selfish, useless words that comforted and helped nothing — begging her not to die. It was right after he'd thought he saw Rui stir at his words.

That Subaru's field of vision had switched in an instant, and he'd felt the whole world turn upside down.

"Uh, wha—"

It wasn't a matter of tremendous speed, or of time stopping — nothing like that at all. Truly, in the span of a single blink, Subaru had simply been somewhere else. And what's more—

"You..."

So said Abel, his breath catching faintly at Subaru's sudden appearance — right in front of him. Abel, arms crossed, leaning back against the wall with unshaken composure, and beyond him, the presence of the men surrounding them — the sheer confusion of it nearly took the strength out of Subaru's legs.

The tears that had been welling up out of worry for Rui just moments before receded, and his throat clenched tight. And as for Rui, who had nearly slipped from his mind entirely because of this sudden phenomenon—

"Ah, uu!"

"Gogh!?"

Spinning her body round and round, Rui ricocheted between the men with the movement and speed of a pinball, landing a strike on every one of them — throat, torso, knee, every vital point — and knocking them all back. At the speed and force of it, the men's eyes bulged, and they all reeled back at once, eyes wide. And while shock and pain rippled through the men, Rui was already moving on to her next action.

"Uau."

Slipping through the gaps between the men, Rui broke free of the encirclement. She dropped to all fours like an animal, then took hold of the ground with both hands and tore its surface right off. It was exactly like yanking a rug laid across a floor — of course, there was no rug laid across a city street. But the ground shifted precisely as if there were. As a result, every one of the men lost their footing and fell where they stood.

"Wha—!? What is this kid...!"

"Auu! Au-au!"

"Damn it all!"

Among those thrown off balance, a Deer Human youth who'd barely kept his footing clicked his tongue and lunged to grab hold of Rui. Rui hurled the thin sheet of ground she'd just torn up at him. The instant the youth swatted it away with his arm on reflex, the paper-thin sheet of earth snapped back to what it had been. Which is to say, it became a wall of earth with the full weight and substance to match.

"Bgahh!?"

Slammed head-on by the wall of earth, catching it almost like a counter, the youth reeled backward. Following through, Rui burst out through the shattering wall of earth, the sole of her foot catching the youth square in the face; blood sprayed from his nose, and this time he went down for good.

"You little—!"

"Uau!"

Stepping over the fallen youth, the others, slower to rise, closed in on Rui. But the instant their fingers were about to reach her, she vanished again, like a phantom. An instant later, she reappeared clinging to a wall some two meters up, tearing the wall's surface free with both hands just as she had the ground, then letting it snap back. The surface of the wall peeled away, retaining its full weight and hardness, and rained down on the heads of the men below.

"Wraaaaaghh—!!"

Screams and roars echoed through the street as men of formidable strength were left utterly at the mercy of Rui alone.

Subaru watched it all, dazed, still collapsed where he sat.

"R-Rui, that's..."

Raising an arm with a trembling voice, Subaru cowered before the nightmarish sight unfolding in front of him. A nightmare. Yes, it was a nightmare. This was, without a doubt, the very nightmare Subaru had been afraid of.

Freely tearing up ground and walls, blinking across short distances in an instant, unleashing combat prowess that would put even a legendary martial artist to shame, Rui beat down the men one after another. It was plain that this was no innate, transcendent power a young girl had simply been born with — no, Natsuki Subaru knew exactly where that power came from.

That was — that was — that was a power that should never exist, an ■■ that had to disappear—

"I suspected she was no ordinary girl. Now it makes sense."

"Huh...?"

Beside Subaru, who could do nothing but sit there dumbfounded, watching the scene unfold, Abel murmured. Watching the same scene, Subaru's eyes wavered, unable to grasp Abel's true meaning. Keeping Subaru in the corner of his eye, Abel pointed straight ahead, indicating Rui as she went on one-sidedly beating down her pursuers,

"This, then, is the reason you kept that girl by your side."

"T-that's not...!"

He tried, reflexively, to snap back that it wasn't like that. But if he were asked, then, why he had kept Rui with him, Subaru would have nothing to say. As it had always been before, so it was now.

"Stop it! Whatever happens to this girl—gohh!?"

"Au-au!"

Seeing his comrades collapse one after another, the Rhinoceros Human had raised his voice to try to stop it. He'd tried to hold Medium, gripped in his massive hand, hostage — but Rui's blow landed before he could even finish his threat. Rui warped across several meters of distance and clapped both hands against the man's head from either side with all her strength. Struck on the ears by her palms, the man's eyes rolled back white, and he went down like a felled tree.

"Wahyaa...! R-Rui-chan?"

"Ah-uu."

Suddenly released, Medium dropped to the ground, and Rui leapt straight into her arms. Medium stroked Rui's head, looking around, still dumbfounded. In Medium's field of view, every single person who had gathered in the street lay collapsed.

Rui had beaten down more than ten enemies, all on her own.

"So neither you nor the clown showed your true colors after all. No matter."

"I—I don't understand any of this... what is going on!"

"Don't make a scene. You'll draw the rest of them. Go retrieve the clown."

Jerking his chin, with barely a hint of relief at having escaped that predicament, Abel gave the order. Where he indicated lay Al, still crouched motionless on the ground. Fearing he might have suffered some grave injury while no one was watching, Subaru rushed over to him in a panic.

"A-Al! Hey, are you okay? Are you hurt anywhere..."

"...I'm not... okay."

"—! You're hurt!? Where? I'll get you help right away..."

"That's not it!"

"—"

Al shouted back sharply at Subaru, who had rushed to his side and shaken his shoulder. Still looking down, he raised a trembling hand and clenched his right fist tight,

"—I'm useless."

"...T-that's not..."

"I'm useless. The way I am right now, I'm gonna die."

"—? What do you... mean by that?"

Subaru tried to ask what he meant, his voice trembling. But Subaru's question was cut off by an urgent voice from behind.

"Abel-chin! You can't do something so awful!"

"Be silent. There is something that must be asked."

"Even so..."

Medium's emotional voice, and Abel's quiet reply. Turning around, Subaru saw the two of them making for the edge of the street, where the Sheep Human boy lay collapsed — the very boy who had first thrown Subaru flying high into the sky when they'd arrived on this street.

The boy, propping himself up, had unmistakable fear written across his face. His feelings were understandable. He hadn't been shrunk down — he was simply a child his own age, no more. Having watched the adults around him beaten to a pulp, now looked down upon by the very ones who'd done it — and one of them a man in an oni mask, no less — there was no way he wasn't terrified.

To that frightened boy, Abel showed absolutely no mercy. Grabbing the shoulder of Medium, who tried to stop him, and pushing her back, Abel crouched down in front of the boy. Their faces drew close, close enough that the flame burning in the frightened boy's eye seemed to dim before it. And Abel, ignoring the boy's terror entirely, put his question to him. It was a question, simple and plain—

"The one who orchestrated all of this — the leader of the Horned Peoples, the girl who called herself Tanza — where is she hiding? Speak, and speak quickly."
