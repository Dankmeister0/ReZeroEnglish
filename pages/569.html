Arc 7 Chapter 64: "Magic Words"

—The accursed technique left behind by "the Wicked Elder," Olbart Dunkelkenn.

A secret Shinobi art that interferes with another's Od, dramatically reversing the growth of their body and reducing them to infancy.
All living things, in the end, are merely vessels for Od, which turns nothingness into being. Depending on the shape of one's Od, the size and form of the proper vessel differs.
That is why every living creature takes on a different shape and form, existing as a distinct life.

If that logic held true—if the body served only as a vessel for the Od—then changing the shape of the Od would let one freely reshape the vessel.
By compressing the Od, one could shrink the flesh that housed it to match—

"Well, truth is, I still don't rightly know what got into my Shinobi forerunners' heads to come up with somethin' like this. Then again, this whole business of trainin' your body right up to death's door—how the hell do you even tell when you're at death's door? Kinda scary if you ask me."

"Anyhow, that's just how it is bein' a Shinobi. The moment you set out to become one, you're on a road straight to hell, strollin' along, light as you please, right over a mountain of corpses."

"With techniques piled up like a mountain, you can't very well write 'em into the secret scrolls without testin' 'em out on somebody's life, can you? My own trump card... one outta dozens I got, mind you, ain't no exception either."

"But that one, now—funny thing is the result and the effect don't quite match up, ain't that interesting? I figured it'd just shrink 'em down, but turns out their head gets young right along with the body."

"Unfortunately, even I don't rightly know the reason for it. Well, I s'pose it's on account of forcibly messin' with the Od. Maybe it's like—the body shrinks to match the Od, so the heart matches up with it too?"

"Anyhow, it ain't a technique I use much, and I gotta kill whoever I use it on most the time, so I never bothered tryin' to puzzle out why. Truth is, it's easier to finish someone off when their head shrinks along with the body, not just the body—real convenient, ain't it?"

"That said, every now and then you get some brat who's had their mind made up rock-solid since they were knee-high! Can't expect much from shrinkin' the head on one of them. ...Ah, and also—"

"—————"

"...Nah, never mind. Anyway, outta all the Shinobi arts, I'm fond of this one 'cause it's interesting. Enemies are easier to deal with weaker, ain't that right?"

So next time, he wouldn't lose.

"N-next time..."

He'd reach this exact same point faster—that, Subaru swore to himself, firm and unshaken.
Deaf to that vow, the lion pushed through the fence and raised its unharmed forepaw toward Subaru—

"—Pardon my rudeness."

In the next instant, the lion's thick neck was severed in a single stroke.

"Ah?"

Subaru, who'd had death staring him in the face, widened his eyes at a death that wasn't his own.
The severed head hit the ground with a thud, and the lion's body slowly toppled sideways. What had done it was the very greatsword that had been buried deep in its neck.
Like a sudden ambush, a small shadow had dropped from the sky, stepped onto the greatsword, and forced it through.
That was what had sent the lion's head flying.

And the one who had pulled it off—

"...Um, why exactly am I in a place like this?"

—was a girl who wore a hard-to-read face that had, just now, taken on an unmistakably troubled look. Tanza.
Having shed her kimono, wearing only her under-robe, the girl posed her question, and Subaru let out a small breath.

He couldn't answer a thing. But when he turned his head to look, it seemed the other three besides Subaru had also, just barely, escaped death and kept hold of their lives.
In other words—

"—That's enough! Well done surviving the 'Sparka'!! By the authority vested in this office, I shall accept the lot of you as members of the Sword Slave Isle!"

A voice, loud and enormous, rang out high above, and Gustav's declaration announced the end of the battle.
At once, the spectators who'd been watching the fight in silence until then—no, the sword slaves—rose to their feet and raised their voices all together, praising Subaru and the others' victory.

"Well done!"

"That was something else!"

"Not bad at all, kid!!"

As the excited men's voices rained down, Gustav crossed all four of his arms and said nothing.
Listening to the men's cheers, Subaru collapsed flat onto the ground. Tanza approached him gingerly and peered down into his face.

"Excuse me, you are...?"

"Natsuki Schultz."

"Eh?"

Watching Tanza's surprised face, her already-round eyes growing rounder still, Subaru, still lying there, let out one long, long breath, and continued.

"For now, I'm Natsuki Schultz. Nice to meet you. There's a lot I want to talk about, but..."

"But?"

"...I just need a little rest first."

He felt bad for Tanza, who tilted her head in confusion, but Subaru was already at his limit.
The taut thread of tension snapped, and the boiling heat that had seemed to churn in his very soul drained away with it. Of course, something still smoldered, resenting this whole situation.

Still, somehow, he'd survived the "Sparka."

"Please... just let me sleep a little."

And with that, Subaru's consciousness dropped away, easily, abruptly, like falling into a pit trap.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—"

Down below, Gustav narrowed his eyes at the boy who'd lost consciousness sprawled out in the arena.
It had been a ferocious battle, and one could say the result was entirely unexpected.
He'd never imagined that boy possessed talent of that caliber.

"Ahh, that was amazing, wasn't it, Gustav-san? Picking up Bassuu was absolutely the right call—good thing we kept him instead of tossing him out! Wouldn't you say that's a fine achievement on my part?"

"...Segmunt."

As Gustav sank into his thoughts, Cecilus spoke up to him with easy familiarity.
At the words of the boy with his hands laced behind his head, Gustav rubbed his forehead and brow with two of his right arms.

"There was a point to what you argued. Did you foresee this?"

"I did hope for it, sure? I thought, 'wouldn't it be great if it turned out like this,' and it turned out exactly like that. It really does feel like whatever you believe in comes through for you, doesn't it!"

"...You staked your own life on such an uncertain possibility?"

"—? Yeah, I did, why?"

Cecilus tilted his head, looking genuinely puzzled.
His whole manner seemed to ask whether he'd said something strange.
As if he'd never insisted, not once, that if the two he'd pulled in—Schultz and Tanza—turned out to be useless, he'd pay for their lives with his own.

"But in the end, nothing happened, right? From where I stand, it was a generous offer on my part. I'm not the sort to be wasted away in a place like this for nothing, after all!"

"Your reasoning is beyond this office's understanding. And—what did you do, in that final scene?"

Gustav posed the question to Cecilus, who stood laughing with his slight chest puffed out.
In that final scene, in the very midst of the "Sparka" he'd staked his own life on, Cecilus had vanished, just once, for the briefest span of time.
Considering what happened right after, it wasn't hard to guess, more or less—

"You went to wake the girl."

"Aw, come on, if you already know, there's no need to ask, is there? Anyway, you were the one who said, Gustav-san, that a 'Set' is made of five, and that if she didn't wake up, they'd just have to make do shorthanded. That means, the other way around, if she did wake up, she was free to join in anytime, right?"

Cecilus closed one eye and looked up at Gustav, whose height towered over his own, adding "Or,"

"Would you like to take it back now? Declare that this 'Sparka' turned out to involve some foul play?"

"—. It doesn't constitute foul play. His Imperial Majesty, too, will surely be pleased at the birth of such splendid sword slaves."

"Haha, it's a bit much coming from me, but His Imperial Majesty doesn't sound like much of a decent person either!"

"Insulting His Imperial Majesty will not be permitted. There won't be a second time."

Gustav delivered the order in a stern voice, and Cecilus shrugged and backed off, noncommittal. Letting out a breath through his nose at the sight, Gustav turned once more to look at the arena.
Schultz, the greatest contributor of all, and the three men, were being carried out by the guards under Gustav's command. They would likely receive treatment from a healer in the infirmary.
The girl, looking rather at loose ends, seemed to be following along after them for the time being.

"He kept every member of the 'Set' alive—all by himself, did he."

It was an outcome that wouldn't have been reached if any one of them had been missing, but who had brought the greatest force to bear went without saying.
Without overlooking the mechanisms and weapons prepared in the arena, keeping watch over companions in the same plight, and observing the enemy's movements as closely as possible, he had crossed the thin thread to the very end.
As one who had witnessed that conclusion—

"See? Doesn't it feel like some grand story is about to begin?"

To Cecilus, wearing a smug look as he sought agreement, answering felt somehow grating—so Gustav merely kept his stern face, and silently crossed his four thick arms, and left it at that.
