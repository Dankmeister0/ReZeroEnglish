Arc 7 Chapter 100: "Myriad Change at the Vertex"

—The senility of Mezoreia, the "Cloud Dragon." That startling truth, once uncovered, had driven Emilia into an outrageously dire predicament.

"With Volcanica, the goal wasn't to beat him — it was to finish the 'trial,' so I could manage that, but..."

The encounter with Volcanica, the "Divine Dragon," on the topmost level of the Pleiades Watchtower — the First Floor — had been resolved by touching the stone tablet at the very peak above the tower's peak. For her own part, Emilia still wasn't confident that had been the right way to settle Volcanica's "trial," but the dragon, who had lived since ancient times, had given her one of his claws as proof. So, for now, she'd decided to take that as meaning she'd been accepted.

Even so, her battle with Volcanica had been essentially one-sided defense from the start, and since she'd never meant to beat him, it hadn't really been a fight at all. And that same situation held true now, even with her opponent changed from the "Divine Dragon" to the "Cloud Dragon." If anything—

"With no way to win except by beating her, it's Mezoreia who's going to end up at her wits' end—!"

As she said it, Emilia leapt high into the air right where she stood, closing in on the white dragon hovering above. The dragon, her long whiskers swaying, her white eyes betraying nothing of what they reflected, swung an arm mercilessly at the small figure flying toward her, slashing down with her dragon claws. It was a careless, casual blow — but one that, if it landed, would easily send a life bursting apart. The dragon's claws were far sharper than any ordinary blade; they'd cleave Emilia's body clean in two without the slightest effort.

"Soldier!"

But Emilia dodged it by leaping again, midair. Her foothold in the air was one of the ice soldiers that had leapt up alongside her — she used its raised arms as a springboard and jumped. Naturally, the ice soldier, too slow to escape, fell prey to the dragon's blow and was smashed to pieces.

"Hyah — yah!!"

With that sacrifice weighing on her heart, Emilia's long leg kicked into Mezoreia's face. The leg delivering the kick wore an ice fitting, its spikes enlarged into something jagged — a viciously brutal weapon. Just because it was a kick didn't mean she'd hold back — a serious blow, without mercy or restraint. That outrageously vicious strike snapped hard into Mezoreia's unguarded cheek. And yet—

"It's not doing anything at all!"

Mezoreia's massive body didn't so much as flinch, even with her face kicked. She did seem to find it annoying, though. Feeling the hostility prickle against her skin, Emilia swung her upper body hard, flipping in midair, and borrowed the help of another ice soldier to dodge the dragon's thrusting claw. A beat behind the one that had been smashed, another ice soldier — one that had jumped even higher — stretched both feet down from directly above. Emilia matched the sole of her boot to its icy sole and kicked herself back down to the ground. Catching the sound of shattering ice in her ears, Emilia landed with a hand braced against the white ground, and looked up at the imposing form of the cloud-wreathed dragon floating serenely overhead.

"—I am Mezoreia. In obedience to the voice of my beloved child, I shall become the wind from the heavens."

"Ugh... that's all you ever say...!"

Even after having her face kicked and smashing two ice soldiers with her claws, neither Mezoreia's words nor her manner had changed one bit since she'd first descended onto the battlefield.

"——"

She surely did regard Emilia as an enemy. That was surely why she swung her claws at the approaching Emilia, beat her wings, and turned that unfocused white gaze her way. But conversation simply wasn't possible. Finding a resolution like the one with Volcanica was proving difficult. The one thing that might offer a clue—

"—Madelyn! I said, listen to me!"

"Shut it! Don't go talkin' to a dragon like you're all friendly with her!!"

The lifeline she turned toward violently knocked away Emilia's outstretched hand. Even without her Flying Winged Blade, that monstrous arm still swung, and Emilia leapt backward to dodge, silver hair whipping. The small shadow chasing after her — Madelyn Eschart — breathed out white breath, her golden eyes blazing, as she cracked the earth underfoot and raised her arm of destruction.

Dodging it with barely a hair's breadth to spare, Emilia clenched her back teeth. Her strategy of steadily dropping the temperature had done its job of keeping everyone else away from the battlefield where Madelyn and Mezoreia were rampaging, but beyond that, it wasn't accomplishing much.

"Human, human, human...!!"

Madelyn's eyes were dyed with murderous intent, her small body wreathed in white steam. It felt like a match to the cloud-wreathed Mezoreia, but it wasn't the same thing. Madelyn's body temperature had shot up to an outrageous degree, and the falling snow was melting away the instant it touched her — no, even before it touched her. The same thing was happening around Mezoreia, too — her "copying Puck" strategy simply wasn't working on either of them. In that case—

"Icicle Line—!"

Leaving the ice walls dividing the battlefield in place, Emilia changed the direction her cold air was spreading. Not to cut her losses on a strategy that wasn't working and conserve strength — no. If the range was too wide to give Madelyn and Mezoreia the effect she'd hoped for—

"Then I'll throw all of this cold at them at once!"

Drawing invisible white lines around the advancing Madelyn ahead of her, Emilia concentrated all the cold air she'd spread across the entire Second Vertex battlefield into a single point. Not merely cold, but true, absolute cold enveloped Madelyn's small body, plunging the dragonkin's body temperature — hot enough to turn snow to steam — down below freezing all at once.

"—Ngh!?"

Even Madelyn had her whole body frozen stiff by that unexpected, absolute-cold barrier. Watching Madelyn's wide eyes turn white right before her, Emilia nonetheless cast aside any half-hearted mercy and poured everything into encasing her in ice, if only temporarily.

"Please...!"

Even as she poured her power into freezing Madelyn, what Emilia prayed for was Mezoreia's next move. If the cloud-wreathed "Cloud Dragon" joined in and dragged this into a melee, Emilia would have no choice but to break off her attack. But in the corner of her eye, she caught a glimpse of Mezoreia hovering in the sky with a vacant expression, showing no sign of cutting in on Madelyn's crisis.

"It's weird, but—"

In this situation, it was a relief that Mezoreia was staying still. When Mezoreia had appeared in the sky, summoned by Madelyn's voice, Emilia had braced herself to be driven into an even more hopeless situation. But once things actually got underway, Madelyn and Mezoreia hadn't cooperated with each other at all — whenever one attacked, the other rested, and once the rest ended, the first would start resting in turn.

From Emilia's perspective, it amounted to a running series of one-on-one fights, alternating opponents. Of course, even so, her stamina was steadily being worn down, and she couldn't imagine either Madelyn or Mezoreia being an opponent she could easily best one-on-one. Even so—

"Y-you...!"

"I'm sorry, Madelyn. I really think we should talk this through properly. But if you won't listen, then for now I just have to make you behave!"

"Guh—!"

Her whole body creaking violently, Madelyn bared her sharp fangs, eyes burning. But once the inside of her body, down to the very core, had been chilled through, there was no avoiding the loss of freedom — not even for so sturdy and vigorous a dragonkin as she was.

"——"

Freezing white every last bit of blood, flesh, bone, and skin, Emilia encased Madelyn in ice. It was settled a mere instant before the hand thrust out toward her could reach Emilia's chest. Keeping both hands trained on Madelyn, Emilia stared at the now-still girl and let out a long breath.

"That was... close..."

Pressing a hand to her chest in relief, Emilia's brows drooped as she looked at the girl now dyed white. She couldn't bring herself to feel joy or frustration over winning or losing. More than anything, this wasn't over yet.

"Mezoreia! You might not understand what I'm saying, but please stop fighting! If we really can't talk this out, then go home for today!"

She'd considered the possibility that the moment Madelyn went down, Mezoreia might fly into a tremendous rage, but the dragon showed no movement, and Emilia felt a small measure of relief. Still, she couldn't let that relief show on her face, so she glared up at Mezoreia with a sharp, resolute expression. She was putting on an act, as if to say she still had plenty left in reserve.

Truth be told, though, Emilia didn't actually have much left in reserve. She'd succeeded in encasing Madelyn in ice, but to maintain that, she had to keep cooling her continuously. That, too, required an ongoing expenditure of power. So fighting Mezoreia while also keeping Madelyn contained was extremely difficult. If at all possible, it would help enormously if Mezoreia would just turn back here. So—

"If you're still planning to keep going, I've got a plan of my own, you know."

Glaring with sharp, determined eyes, Emilia flung those words at Mezoreia. —It was a lie. She'd said it while glaring at Mezoreia with all the confidence she could muster, but the claim of having some sort of plan was a colossal lie on Emilia's part. She figured that if she insisted hard enough, maybe Mezoreia would decide she was dangerous and back off. It was a bluff, modeled after Subaru and Otto — a rare move for Emilia.

"—I am Mezoreia. In obedience to the voice of my beloved child, I shall become the wind from the heavens."

In response, that low, thick voice returned that same answer from Mezoreia's mouth. Whether or not Mezoreia was even seeing the thing in front of her, her elusive, unreadable demeanor nearly broke Emilia's resolve, but she refused to let up, keeping her gaze just as sharp — she wouldn't lose. Then—

"—I am Mezoreia. To the voice of my beloved child—"

"...Huh?"

"To the voice... of my beloved child... in obedience to the voice..."

Mezoreia's words, which had done nothing but repeat the same thing over and over no matter what was said to her, cut off partway through — and then, haltingly, simply failed to continue any further. That wasn't all. Mezoreia, who had sat serenely enthroned in the snowing sky, began to grimace as if in some kind of pain, and started shaking her huge head side to side.

"What's happening all of a sudden? Does your head hurt?"

Seeing that change come over Mezoreia, Emilia's violet eyes went wide with surprise. If Madelyn being frozen made Mezoreia furious and set her rampaging, that would be natural enough. It would be a huge problem if that happened, but it would be easier to accept than this strange reaction. Above the head of an uneasy Emilia, Mezoreia's movements abruptly stopped.

"——"

The dragon, who had been shaking her head in apparent pain, suddenly looked down below with a quiet expression. At the sight of that white-eyed gaze, on a face framed by long whiskers, Emilia let out a small, breathy "—ah." Because just now, for the first time, she felt she'd truly been "seen" by Mezoreia's eyes. That was, in other words, proof that will and intelligence had come to dwell in Mezoreia's eyes—

"Finally, we can talk—"

"I believe I already told you, human."

"Eh?"

In a low voice, one that sounded like the very sky groaning, the dragon turned her face toward Emilia. Pierced by that gaze, Emilia nonetheless stiffened from a different kind of shock entirely. It wasn't surprise at being looked upon by something so much mightier than herself. It was because the raw passion held in that voice was the spitting image of — no, was exactly the same as — what had been directed at her just moments before.

Yes, as if to confirm the shock that had frozen Emilia in place, the dragon continued.

"We dragons got no words to talk with you, ya hear."

"Made—"

Seeing something she couldn't believe, Emilia found her body freezing in place despite herself. Toward that Emilia, the dragon opened her mouth. And then simply breathed out. — That breath becoming white light and dyeing the world was the true essence of the "Cloud Dragon." The sheer terror of a dragon's power wielded with genuine will behind it — that came pouring down upon Emilia without mercy.

The battalion that had intervened in the battlefield from the west — its presence rippled outward, its influence reaching every corner.

Naturally, the ones most affected by the battalion's influence were the defenders of the Fourth Vertex, who had suddenly taken on damage they'd been spared until then, all at once, and been driven to the brink of collapse. But even if the influence wasn't direct, it could be said that the changes brought about before and after the Pleiades Battalion's entry into the fight were felt more strongly at the Third Vertex.

Originally, the forces sent to the Third Vertex — meant to draw a wasted shot from the Magic Crystal Cannon, one of Imperial Capital Lupugana's trump cards — had been intended as expendable sacrifices. Zikr, briefed on the plan by Abel, had resolved himself to being sacrificed as well. Through his own maneuvering, part of the force — the People of Shudrak — had escaped the Magic Crystal Cannon's line of fire, but even that was only a trivial difference compared to the damage the Third Vertex had been expected to suffer.

Either way, the forces at the Third Vertex had been expected to be lost from the outset. The moment those forces survived instead of being lost, the premise of Abel's original calculations — which had factored in even a painful defeat for the rebels — had been thrown off. But the fact that forces remained could hardly be a bad thing. Correcting his miscalculation at once, Abel cross-referenced his original scheme against the actual state of affairs. As a result—

"—So that's Senior Count Serena Dracroy's Flying Dragon Squadron."

In the middle of a melee against the stone golems, Zikr murmured from atop his beloved horse, Ley, as he swung his saber. His round eyes were fixed on the blue sky, half-claimed by white and red — a clash of wings, a battlefield distinct from the one on the ground, where fangs bared against fangs as they crisscrossed the air.

On one side, a swarm of wild Flying Dragons obeying the orders of "Flying Dragon General" Madelyn Eschart, rampaging with their savage nature laid bare. And opposing them, the unit of Flying Dragons and their Riders, wings beating as they carried their partners on their backs, flying through the sky with the blade of savagery sheathed in trust.

Overwhelmingly superior in numbers were the wild Flying Dragons carrying no riders, but as for which side was demonstrating greater skill in aerial combat, that was clearly the Flying Dragon Riders. The feral Flying Dragons gave themselves over to their overflowing wildness, swinging claws and fangs violently, but the Riders' handling was, by comparison, vivid and refined. With deft movements that made one forget they were even in the air, they evaded fangs, and one after another, the Riders pierced the wild dragons' wings with their weapons and brought them down.

"So this is the difference..."

Being the Empire's Second General, and personally acquainted with the "General" of the Flying Dragon Riders as well, it was, if anything, the swarm of wild dragons under Madelyn's command that fell outside Zikr's common sense. Even so, there was no arguing with the sheer violence of numbers, and being utterly overwhelmed by that mass at the walled city had driven a thorn deep into his heart. Now, that perception was being overturned once more. For better or worse, he was witnessing firsthand the undeniable skill of the Empire's traditional Flying Dragon Handlers.

"If His Excellency's strategy has borne fruit, then I must follow through as well."

With Abel playing his card and Count Dracroy's Flying Dragon Squadron committed to the fight, weakening the aerial dominance of the dragon swarm controlling the skies, Zikr, too, gained the composure to look around him. He'd charged in from the front for the sake of morale, but once it became a true melee, Zikr's abilities as an individual combatant were hardly anything to boast of. Issuing formation orders as needed, he pressed further into the enemy's midst— toward First General Moguro Hagane, fused with the city wall, whose threat had not diminished in the slightest.

"I only hope Lady Beatrice is unharmed..."

And so, occupying part of the mind of Zikr as he raised his saber was that girl with the softly-colored hair done up in lavish curls, in her dress — Beatrice, who was, in all likelihood, the one to whom he owed his life. At the moment the Magic Crystal Cannon had fired, in the field of view of a Zikr who'd resigned himself to death, he had seen the figure of a girl who'd flung herself before the surging light and somehow dealt with it.

Beatrice, already small to begin with, had looked no bigger than a pebble up there high in the sky. Even so, Zikr was certain — beyond any doubt — that it had been her. He never mistook a woman once he'd caught her with these eyes. It was Zikr's special ability. Hence Zikr could say with certainty: it was Beatrice who had somehow dealt with that blow from the Magic Crystal Cannon.

The problem was that he could just as certainly be sure that what she'd done could not possibly have come without some cost.

"Please, let her be safe—!!"

In his heart of hearts, he wanted to declare loudly that Beatrice — who had rewritten a hopeless crisis — deserved the greatest commendation of all, and to insist that her safety must be secured no matter what. But in the midst of battle, Zikr — who had been spared a death he should rightly have met — had a role to fulfill as a General. So all Zikr could do was pray.

—That what little good fortune remained to him might pour down, all of it, upon that girl.

Meanwhile, much as Zikr prayed at the front line for the safety of the one who'd earned such credit, Taritta too had witnessed just who had accomplished the feat of making that Magic Crystal Cannon blast unnaturally vanish.

"We were tolD to fall back, and it was fortunate that let us take in the whole battlefielD..."

The rebels, having taken a heavy blow, had somehow regrouped. Zikr, about to lead them in a charge, had ordered the People of Shudrak to provide support from the rear. Naturally, Mizelda had voiced her displeasure at the decision to leave Shudrak out of the all-out assault, but Taritta had accepted it, trusting that Zikr must have had his reasons. And yet, as it turned out—

"To think he'd try to protect just us alonE. The Shudrak really have been underestimateD."

"Sister..."

Looking back on what had happened, Mizelda's view, colored with that note of anger, was correct. The Crystal Palace in the imperial capital had originally been the castle where Abel lived. It was hard to imagine Abel not knowing about the Magic Crystal Cannon it housed, which made sense of Zikr's otherwise unnatural order. No doubt it had been Zikr's own decision, made unilaterally, to hold the Shudrak back. Zikr, resolved to be a sacrifice, and the Shudrak, kept apart from sharing that fate — underestimated, Mizelda had called it, but that wasn't quite right either.

It had been a decision of Zikr's own making, born out of consideration for them. Even so—

"I feel the same way you do, sisteR. Zikr's thoughtfulness brings me no joY."

Taritta had arrived at the same conclusion as the combative Mizelda. She knew Zikr's disposition — called a "womanizer," but showing women a certain baseline of respect — and it wasn't Taritta's place to say anything about the way he chose to be. But neither did Taritta and the others owe anyone the duty of having their own way of being, as People of Shudrak, called into question. So Taritta gripped her bow firmly and said—

"Let's tell that complaint straight to Zikr's facE."

"Heh, good answeR. It'd gall me to let Zikr and Jamal hog all the glory, after alL. —You heard heR, kin!!"

Planting her prosthetic leg deftly into the earth, Mizelda raised her great cleaver and called out to her comrades. Every last one of the assembled Shudrak answered — with their eyes, their expressions, the tone of their voices — that they felt exactly the same as Mizelda's words and Taritta's resolve. And so, spirits high, the band of Shudrak moved to join the front line of the battle for the Third Vertex that Moguro Hagane guarded — and just then—

"—It seems this band has more than enough fighting spirit."

A cold, dry voice spoke up, and Taritta and Mizelda turned around. Taritta instantly nocked an arrow to her bow, and Mizelda too dropped low into a ready stance. That was how sudden the newcomer's appearance was — the sort to put even the People of Shudrak on high alert. But their wariness melted away the instant they saw who had appeared.

Slowly treading through the grass, approaching from behind the Shudrak band, was a girl with pink hair fluttering in the wind — and a face they knew, at that. —No, that wasn't quite right either. Because the owner of that face as Taritta and the others knew it, and the girl who had appeared now, were two different people.

"—? You're giving me such a strange look. Wondering where I came from? I just jumped down off a Flying Dragon, that's all."

"N-no, that's not why we're surprisеD."

"Then what is it?"

"—Because your face is the very image of a girl we knoW."

At the Shudrak's stares turned her way, the pink-haired girl tilted her head. When Mizelda answered her question, the girl's pale pink eyes widened slightly. Then, breathing out a short "I see," she said—

"So — were you all getting along, with that girl who has the same face?"

"At the very least, we were fond of heR."

To that question from those pale pink eyes, Mizelda answered with a solemn nod. "We," she'd said, speaking for the whole group, but Taritta had no objection to that. And every last Shudrak present had an idea of who this girl standing before them must be. Natsuki Subaru had told them. —That she had a twin older sister.

That twin sister, with the same face as the girl Taritta and the rest knew, murmured "I see" once more, the same as before, and then—

"Then it seems Ram and all of you should get along well enough."

As she spoke, a path opened for her — Ram — to step forward, and the girl came walking up, quite as a matter of course, to stand before Taritta and Mizelda. Gazed upon by those pale pink eyes, Taritta nodded.

"Yes, I would hope so. What's the situatioN?"

"I have a rough idea. Including the fact that there isn't a single faint-hearted woman standing here right now."

Sweeping her gaze over the assembled Shudrak faces, Ram spoke quietly. She had seen straight through to the reason why the People of Shudrak had been placed apart from the battle raging ahead of them for the Third Vertex. Having seen through it, and fully aware that such consideration lay behind it, she said—

"Men will say things like, 'I'm so-o worried about yo-ou,' or 'Won't you plea-ease stay back,' but let's teach them something. —That it's none of their business."

"Agreed, indeeD."

Ram declared this, gripping her staff, and beside her Taritta gave another deep, emphatic nod. Consideration, thoughtfulness, concern — for Shudrak standing on a battlefield, all of it was an unwelcome kindness. If anything, they were the ones who needed to teach others how they themselves chose to be, sparing none of it.

Ram, speaking with what sounded like real feeling, turned to face forward, and Taritta turned the same way. Just then, Mizelda, standing on Ram's other side, broke into a wild, feral grin. And then—

"This is the first we've met, but I'm certaiN now. —You're a warrior too, just like ReM."

There came a heavy shock that resonated straight to the core, as if a weight had been hooked into his very organs.

That abnormal sensation kept repeating — once, twice, three times, four — as though more and more shackles were being added. Handcuffs, leg irons, one obstruction to movement after another raining down on him — he shook them off with violent impulse, trying to push through and past them. But—

"Yer methods are too straightforward, boy. Might've worked fine on that Kafma fella, but I ain't the type to meet a foe head-on, playin' along with his footin'."

"Gah—"

At the tips of Garfiel's outstretched five fingers, the old man's face, sinking into the ground, vanished, leaving mockery behind. The instant his eyes went wide, he sensed a presence behind him and drove in a mighty backfist. The tremendous impact shattered what felt like a spine, but the rebound told him it wasn't the old man at all. Looking, he saw it was a corpse from the battlefield, flung up from the ground the old man had sunk into. By the time he realized he'd been neatly trapped, it was already too late.

"There ya go, fell for it again."

His body, spun around from the backfist, had its shoulder tapped from behind. In other words, the eerie old man — Olbart — hadn't moved from where he'd sunk underground at all; he'd simply leapt straight back up from that same spot. The small old man, light on his feet, who had touched his shoulder, flashed his neatly aligned teeth and retreated back out of reach.

Just touched. Not struck, not cut. Just playing around? Being toyed with? —No. Olbart never did anything without meaning to it. There was always a meaning, a meaning, a meaning, a meaning—

"—Ngyah!?"

The instant his thoughts flared white-hot, a heat exceeding even that erupted in his touched right shoulder. Looking, he saw Olbart's handprint had risen up red and raw on the shoulder that had been touched. Oozing blood, smoking, the handprint was trying to burn through skin, flesh, and bone.

Poison — the instant he judged as much, he didn't hesitate. Garfiel opened his mouth wide, bit down on the shoulder marked with that red handprint, and tore the poison eating away at him off, taking the flesh of his own shoulder with it. The taste of his own poisoned blood and flesh was the worst thing imaginable, and the bone grazed by his fangs sent out a furious jolt of pain. But that pain lasted only an instant. The deeply gouged wound in his shoulder sent up a mist of blood-steam, and flesh surged up with tremendous speed, beginning to knit the wound closed—

"Best move ya had, but that's some reckless business all the same."

As Garfiel let out a great breath, his muzzle took a direct hit from Olbart's kick, straight on. Leg strength unbelievable for so diminutive an old man snapped his head back and broke his nose, sending Garfiel reeling and blasted off his feet. He tumbled head over heels across the ravaged earth before flipping over, sprawled spread-eagle.

Had it not been for Garfiel's sturdy neck, that kick could have torn his head clean off. But his head and torso were still connected. Slowly raising a hand, he set it against his broken nose and, with a single breath, snapped it back into place. A hard crack rang out, and the flow of his nosebleed improved.

"...Damn, bein' hard to kill is a weapon all on its own, ya know. Not talkin' feelings here, mind ya — talkin' about the body. Yer just too much trouble, boy."

Watching that painful sight, Olbart sighed, sounding exasperated. Waving his left hand idly — how many times now had this eerie old man landed a merciless attack on Garfiel? At the very least, more times than the fingers remaining on the one-armed old man's hand could count.

"—Shut yer trap, old man. I ain't done yet."

Blood-steam still pouring hard from his shoulder, Garfiel slowly rose to his feet. Countless wounds marked his bared upper body — the aftereffects of his fierce battle with Kafma Irulux hadn't even faded before he'd clashed with one of the Empire's very strongest, and yet his fighting spirit showed no sign of dimming. Measured even against the Empire's own code, it was a valor that many officers and soldiers would call splendid and admirable — but unfortunately, the eerie old man facing him was utterly and endlessly removed from such warrior values.

With eyes utterly devoid of any admiration, Olbart shrugged.

"Can't keep foolin' around with just you, though. Seems that wall I slipped away from is startin' to smell fishy. Sounds like some troublesome folk have shown up — gotta get back or it's lookin' bad."

"Troublesome folk...?"

"Listen close and ya oughta hear it yerself. Don't tell me yer ears are worse than an old geezer like me?"

Prompted by Olbart, who put a hand to his ear, Garfiel became aware of how narrow his own focus had grown. Irritating as it was, he strained his ears, and understood what Olbart meant. Sure enough, the footfalls of a great many — a great many harboring power beyond the ordinary — were shaking the earth, and he could feel it in his eardrums, in the soles of his feet, as it began to repaint the very air of this battlefield.

"Hah, ya serious? Lettin' yer guard down in front of me — that's braver than it is smart."

In that instant, the explosive charges Olbart had thrown into that opening detonated on either side of Garfiel's head. A roar and red light spread out with searing heat, power enough to easily reduce a human to dust unleashed, and Garfiel's form vanished into the blazing flames.

"That oughta do at least a littl—"

So Olbart said, peering into the burning flames — and it was right after that.

"GAAAAAAAAH—!!"

The instant he'd been swallowed by the blast, Garfiel used the flames themselves as cover for a charge. He'd guessed that showing an opening would draw Olbart into action. He hadn't known exactly what the old man would spring on him, but the gamble had paid off. Garfiel's arms closed in on Olbart, whose own attack had just been turned into a blind—

"Green, ain'tcha, whelp."

His outstretched arms were crushed aside, and Garfiel's chin, eyes bulging, was kicked up from directly below. As his face snapped upward, Olbart's leaping foot came down to plant itself on it. Olbart stood one-footed atop Garfiel's upturned face, and in that very pose, the eerie old man gazed off into the distance as if to mock him.

"If the troops Kafma pulled back come out, that oughta be plenty to hold this place. ...Long as you ain't around, ain't nobody gettin' through anyhow."

That was Olbart declaring he no longer intended to keep up this fight. Eyes bloodshot, Garfiel swung up both arms, broken at the elbows, trying to crush the diminutive frame standing atop his face. But Olbart leapt clear with barely a hair to spare, dodging both arms, and as the eerie old man spun around, his fluttering right sleeve flashed.

"Even you'd die if that head of yers went flyin', wouldn'tcha?"

A cold sentence of death, the sensation of it closing in as a flash of white steel raking up his spine — Garfiel tensed the muscles of his neck and, at the same time, moved to leap clear of the spot—

"—Your chest."

In that instant, guided purely by the voice he heard, he brought both fists together before his chest without a thought.

A faint grunt of "Hnh" from a rasping voice came at the same moment as the shattering crash of steel breaking apart. The fists brought together before his chest had shattered the blade thrust at him — a stab from Olbart, straight at Garfiel's front, driven to pierce his heart: a hidden blade in place of the arm he'd lost.

With the tip of that blade lodged shallow in his chest, Garfiel's body staggered back. Had his reaction been even the slightest bit slower, his heart would have been carved right out, and he'd have died without question. No matter how great his recovery, losing his heart meant there was no coming back. Had he ignored that piece of advice and kept guarding his neck instead, he would have died. Even knowing that—

"...Goddammit."

"My, cursing at me — how unkind. I'd at least hoped to hea-ar a word of thanks."

Garfiel clicked his tongue, and his back was caught, supported by someone standing behind him. Deciding he might as well, he leaned his full weight onto them, and a sense of wry amusement came through from the hands supporting him. That only made it more irritating, and Garfiel's muzzle wrinkled.

Garfiel wasn't, generally speaking, much good at disliking people — but Olbart in front of him fell squarely into the category he disliked. Yet without question, the one he disliked most was the one behind him now. Sandwiched front and back between people he couldn't stand — about the worst possible situation, as far as Garfiel was concerned.

"Once I'm done wreckin' that old man, yer next..."

"That's really taking your temper out on the wrong person, isn't it. But I think you're lucky it was me who came, don't you? Surely you don't want Ram seeing you looking like this?"

"Grrgh...!"

Struck where it hurt, Garfiel's throat let out a feeble groan. At Garfiel's reaction, the one behind him let out a stifled laugh. As they went back and forth like that, Olbart, his hidden blade now shattered, spoke up from a short distance away—

"Just so ya know, I did say anybody who crosses that line on the ground over there forfeits their life?"

"My apologies, good sir. I'm afraid I never heard that particular warning. After all, I came from the sky."

"From the sky, was it."

Glancing briefly up at the sky, Olbart closed one eye. Even that listless-looking posture was nothing more than part of this eerie old man's repertoire of tricks for toying with his opponents. Reining in the urge to carelessly lunge forward, Garfiel let out a breath. Pulling the blade tip from his chest and letting the wound close over it, he said—

"If you're here, that means..."

"Ram is off at another battlefield. She wouldn't listen no matter how much I told her to fall back."

"...'Course she wouldn't. And you, comin' all the way out here yourself, means..."

"Naturally, I do intend to be of at least some use. Fortunately, I'd say."

So he said, stepping up to stand not behind but alongside Garfiel, his gaze fixed together with him on the troublesome "Wicked Old Man" — a man baring an unfamiliar, unpainted face rather than the makeup Garfiel knew so well: Roswaal L. Mathers, who, while keeping his identity concealed, closed one eye, leaving only the blue one open, and—

"—Because I, too, once fought a Shinobi to the death, you know."

He said, deepening his smile into something every bit as wicked as his enemy's.

△▼△▼△▼△

—In the instant that light imbued with will and power was unleashed, Emilia glimpsed a vision of her own "death."

Fiercely stubborn, never one to lose hope no matter the circumstances— for an Emilia bracing herself with that very mindset, it was a shocking thing.

"—Ah"

Somewhere in her mind, a small voice screamed that she had to move, right now. But left or right, which way to move — her body wouldn't respond. Normally she moved without thinking at all, yet now she couldn't. The reason was that something other than her mind — her heart — had instantly sensed there was no escape, not left, not right, not forward, not back.

"Icicle Line."

So Emilia chose not to flee, but to block — no, to deflect. She conjured a thick wall of ice before herself, angling it so the light would slide off across its slanted face. Not a question of whether she could do it — she simply had to.

When the dragon's breath had been unleashed from the skies over Guaral, Emilia had reflexively chosen the same defense. Back then, though, Priscilla had been there, and after Emilia weakened the light even a little, Priscilla had cut the rest away with the red treasured sword she carried, and that was how they'd managed.

This time, Priscilla wasn't here. It was just Emilia, alone. She had no idea if she could do the same thing. But she had to.

"Both me, and everyone behind me—"

It was obvious at a glance that the light being unleashed carried far more power than enough to simply blow Emilia away alone. A similar light had been unleashed far off just a while ago — this was much the same. Surely this light would sweep across the battlefield and ruin everything in its path. So—

"—Come on, me."

Bracing herself, she conjured the ice wall in front of her body, and gripped an ice sword tightly in her hand. She'd made the ice sword because Priscilla had cut through the breath with her treasured sword. This ice blade held none of that same power, but it was a good-luck charm. That stand of hers, into which Emilia had poured everything she had—

"Disappear, human—"

The breath was unleashed, white light pouring down, closing in on Emilia. That the voice had come from Mezoreia's throat, the true nature of the fury held in those eyes turned upon her — all of it, in this instant, she forgot. She forgot, and braced herself. And then, in the instant the breath annihilated the ice wall and threatened to swallow even Emilia herself—

"—Eh?"

Just as she moved to meet the light with her ice sword, Emilia's eyes went wide. The unleashed light — rather than catching Emilia, it veered just the slightest bit to the side. Even so, an enormous shockwave still erupted, nearly tearing her silver hair and clothes to shreds. Bracing herself and enduring it, Emilia looked to Mezoreia to see what had happened.

Mezoreia, who had unleashed the breath, had her head turned up and off to one side. It wasn't that she'd reconsidered at the last moment. Her neck had been forcibly wrenched aside — by the impact of a weapon that had struck her face from the side: the Flying Winged Blade.

"That's... the one I accidentally threw off, isn't it..."

It was Madelyn's beloved weapon, the Flying Winged Blade that Emilia had tried to throw back and had, through sheer clumsiness, sent flying off into the far distance instead. And now it had slammed straight into Mezoreia's face. No way, Emilia thought, her eyes widening.

"Could it be... the one I threw came back around just now?"

"Hahahahaha! Now that's a wonderfully dreamy idea, quite splendid! But sadly, no! It was stuck over there, so I just kicked it back for you!"

"Eek!?"

Emilia, who had been imagining a miraculous coincidence, was thoroughly startled by that breezy, self-assured voice. When she spun around in a fluster, she found the owner of the voice crouched right beside her, gazing intently at the ice sword in her hand.

"This is quite the beautifully-made piece, really splendid. I've been thinking that if I'm going to carry a sword around, it ought to be a fitting masterwork — and just going by looks, I'd be tempted to put this one on the shortlist."

"Um, thank you...?"

"No, no, if we're saying thanks, allow me to be the one thanking you."

Emilia, who hadn't expected to be complimented in a situation like this and had reflexively thanked him for it, was met with a bright laugh from the owner of the voice — a boy with blue hair tied back. He straightened the knee he'd been bending and rose to his feet on the spot, saying—

"White light and red splitting the blue sky apart! Torn over which way to run, I came dashing in to find waiting for me a great dragon and a beautiful lady! Truly, that's me for you! Don't you think my luck's just a little too strong!"

"Um...?"

"Don't you think it's too strong!"

Pressed again with sparkling eyes, Emilia felt compelled to answer, and replied, "I think it's... reeeally strong." At Emilia's answer, the boy's grin deepened, looking thoroughly satisfied, and—

"Right!"

—he stepped forward from Emilia's side, out into the "Cloud Dragon's" line of sight. Emilia moved to stop him, to warn him it was dangerous, but the words caught in her throat. Pressure. But it wasn't coming from the massive dragon — it was coming from the small back right in front of her. It was coming from that boy, cheerful in a way utterly out of place here—

"And now, the grand stage has come around to me! Come one, come all, feast your eyes! Behold the shining moment of Cecilus Segmunt, the 'Blue Lightning'! Don't you dare blink — miss it, and you'll regret it for the rest of your life—!!"

Not retreating a single step even before a dragon, a being beyond the ordinary, the boy — Cecilus Segmunt — declared this with boisterous, majestic bravado.
