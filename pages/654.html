Arc 8 Chapter 38: "Rowan Segmunt"

Rowan Segmunt is a Stargazer.

Not in the sense of the Vollachian Empire's official post — the one Vincent Vollachia had created when he first took Ubilk, one of their number, into his service — but a Stargazer in the original, true sense of the word.
A being granted a heavenly mandate that must take precedence over everything else in their life, one who casts aside all things to accomplish that supreme decree — that is what a Stargazer is.

That Vincent had deliberately crowned Ubilk with the title of "Stargazer" as an official post was, one might surmise, a calculated move — attaching bureaucratic meaning to the name so that the very concept of the "Stargazer" would eventually be hollowed out into a mere formality. But the wise emperor's true intentions can be set aside for now.
What matters here is that Rowan Segmunt, too, was one who had been granted a heavenly mandate.

The compulsion placed upon one who becomes a Stargazer, who receives a heavenly mandate, is powerful indeed.
It could grant a mere male prostitute the standing to come and go from the castle and voice his opinions to the emperor; it could make a man forget the love he bore for the daughter his frail mother had risked her life to bear; it could make someone abandon, without a second thought, a purpose to which he had devoted his entire life — such was the force with which it could reach into and reshape a person's existence.
Most Stargazers have the whole of their prior lives bent and twisted by their mandate, forced into an abrupt change of course. And yet none of them consider it a tragedy.

If anything, having been handed some great ambition that must be fulfilled even at the cost of an entire lifetime, and being able to believe without a shred of doubt that achieving it is the very meaning of one's birth — that, to them, feels like nothing less than happiness.
No matter how abnormal or pitiable it might appear to those looking on from outside, that is simply how it is.

That said, when it comes to the tragedy common to all Stargazers, Rowan's own circumstances were an exception even among his fellow Stargazers.
After all, the heavenly mandate granted to Rowan Segmunt was to attain the Heavenly Sword.

—None other than the very ambition Rowan himself had already harbored before ever receiving his mandate.

"To master the swordsman's path, steel in hand these many years—"

Rowan sang merrily, his face flushed with drink, head swaying loosely from side to side.
There was no particular tune to speak of, but his spirits were sky-high, and he simply couldn't help but sing. His drunken stagger swayed this way and that at just the right rhythm, looking for all the world like the footwork of a dance.

Some time back — a bit before the whole uproar over the "Black-Haired Crown Prince" had begun — the scent of blood, nostalgic and fragrant both, had already begun drifting over the whole of the Empire.
It was a portent of war to come, and given how absurdly peaceful Vincent's reign had been up to that point, Rowan had had a feeling that the backlash, when it came, would throw the world into no small amount of chaos.
That premonition had proven true, and now the Empire had entered an age of calamity so severe that even the line between the living and the dead had grown blurred.

"Ahh, what a world, what a world — just the sort this one fancies."

The further the world drifts from peace, the more chaotic the times, the more steel is honed and refined.
Not that one needs rivals to hone oneself against — an enemy worth cutting down will do just fine. But by and large, the sort of warrior who makes one's eyes widen finds it hard to draw first breath in an age of peace.
Where a soul comes from, this one cannot say, but it seems the resolve of it is settled before it ever comes to rest inside a vessel of flesh.

Those born into an age of turmoil draw their first breath already gifted with the talent to survive it.
Which is why hoping that same kind of soul might dwell in one born outside such an age is a wager with poor odds indeed. Rowan himself had failed more times than he could count, and it wasn't until he had put eight of his own children to the sword that he was blessed with Cecilus.
Of them all, only Cecilus, still in his birth-bath, had looked upon the bared blade and smiled as though he'd found something he could love for the rest of his life.

"And now even that Cecilus has hit a wall — the Heavenly Sword must still be a long way off, then. Ah, ah, truly, truly... this one was born into the wrong age."

Rowan pressed his rough, callused palm to his forehead and let slip a lament he had voiced countless times before.
The more chaotic the world grows, the more violently peace is torn apart, the more the age demands overwhelming strength. If the road to the Heavenly Sword is this distant and this treacherous now, how much better it must have been four hundred years ago, in the age when every living thing feared the Witch.
Had he been born in that age, there would have been no fear of the road to the Heavenly Sword ever running dry.
Let alone what Rowan might have done with Cecilus, if—

"—Oi. You actually mean to go through with this?"

"Hmm?"

From behind, someone called out to the unsteadily swaying back ahead of him, and Rowan turned around with a dubious look.
Planting both feet firmly on the cobblestones, glaring down at him from his considerable height, was a red-haired swordsman — a man who went by the name of Heinkel.
The man who, until just a little while ago, had supposedly been drinking himself senseless right alongside Rowan now wore an expression stone-cold sober, in stark contrast. Strange, that — he ought to look tidier now than when Rowan had first picked him up out in the wilds, and yet he somehow looked even more haggard than before.

"What a gloomy mug you're wearing. Here now, Red, have a drink. The city's chipped and cracked in every corner, but happily, the Undead lot haven't touched the food or the drink."

"Happily...!"

"Ohh, seems that rubbed you the wrong way."

Heinkel's jaw ground, his cheeks stiffening at Rowan, who held up his gourd of drink.
Taking that reaction as a refusal, Rowan had no choice but to pour the unwanted drink down his own throat.
Just as he'd told Heinkel, not a single shop or house in sight showed any sign of life, and though the Undead wandered about searching for the living, what they were after was blood — not food, and not drink.
Which meant one was free to fill one's belly and one's cup as much as one pleased — a rather liberal state of affairs, that.

"And what's wrong with that, pray? If it's only going to rot away untouched otherwise, it's the height of reason for us to help ourselves to it."

"I don't give a damn about the food or the drink! I'm not expecting any... any sense of decency out of you and your son either. Answer me instead — do you actually mean to go through with this?"

"—"

At Heinkel's raised voice, Rowan closed one eye and fell silent.
The red-haired swordsman looked too impatient to wait even those few seconds, and though Rowan tried to guess what exactly it was he found so objectionable, he couldn't quite land on an answer.

Guessing at other people's thoughts and feelings was something Rowan was notably bad at.
The same flaw seemed to have been passed down to Cecilus, though that one simply forced his way past the problem from an entirely different angle. Rowan couldn't manage the same trick.
In any case—

"Is what's eating at you, Red, whether or not to go along with that helmeted fellow's plan?"

"That's right. I came back here through circumstance, but like Aldebaran said, this is a chance to make up for things. I can't afford to botch this."

Nodding, Heinkel pointed a finger toward the far end of the street — no, further still, toward the sturdy ramparts that kept anyone inside the imperial capital from glimpsing what lay beyond.
They were the star-shaped walls that had, even during the decisive battle for the capital, functioned to protect the citizens from the rebel army, and—

"—one of the five points we have to bring down."

In a voice heavy and taut, Heinkel named their operational objective.
It was the "path" that the man in the iron helmet, the one called Al, had spoken of needing to lay down — foreshadowing meant for the "hero" who would follow after Rowan and the others now inside the capital.

At present, the only living souls on this street were Rowan and Heinkel; happily, not a single one of the dead was in sight. Cecilus and Groovy, along with Al, were off acting separately.
All of them were moving to punch a hole through the sturdy defenses guarding the capital.
The method by which Rowan, Heinkel, and Groovy had forced their way into the capital had been a fairly unorthodox one, and it would be hard for others to imitate. Given that, the notion that they'd need to carve out a way through was a reasonable enough proposal.

And so, having parted ways with his son — noisy even shrunk down as he was — Rowan had figured on a relaxed little journey for two middle-aged men. But the intensity radiating off Heinkel here made that plainly impossible. Rowan scratched his cheek, at a loss for what to do about it.
Oblivious to Rowan's feelings on the matter, Heinkel clicked his tongue and said,

"Honestly, I don't get what your son meant by 'foreshadowing'..."

"Well now, best not to fret too much over the way that one talks. Anyhow, it's no mistake that so long as that wall stands, whoever comes after won't be able to follow through."

"If you understand that much, then...!"

"—why is it, you mean to ask, that this one chooses the 'not' side of 'whether or not'?"

Heinkel bared his teeth, about to shout, but Rowan cut him off with a shrug.
The look on Heinkel's face, caught flat-footed, was a masterpiece — the sort of thing he'd have liked to make a drinking snack of, but drinking too much more wouldn't do. Scrounging up more liquor around here would be a chore, and there was no guarantee the man in front of him wouldn't carelessly draw his sword.

"Ah, but then, Red here can't very well draw a sword against this one, can he."

"—!"

"Ah, ah, no need to feel shame over it. At the least, this one doesn't hold with the notion that drawing a blade on just anyone makes a man brave... whether it's fear or not, that this one can't quite say."

He tapped a finger against his own temple, touching on a failing that had nothing to do with drink.
Watching Heinkel's eyes go wide at that, Rowan turned to look at the wall and said,

"The reason this one can't get behind the helmeted fellow's plan is dead simple... his aim is to take the capital back from the dead lot, isn't it? This one's got no particular wish for that."

Which meant there was no reason to lend a hand to any plan to punch through the capital's defenses.
That was Rowan's honest sentiment, but Heinkel still looked utterly bewildered. Rowan tilted his head — he'd thought he'd given a fairly clear answer.

"That means — what, you're siding with the Undead?"

"Now how'd you get there? That's a whole different matter. This one just finds it convenient that the Undead are running wild and the country's in turmoil — that's all. Wouldn't go so far as to side with the Undead."

"...No good. I haven't the faintest idea what's going on in your head. To begin with—"

"Hm?"

"Even if you've got no stake in it, your son — the 'Blue Lightning' — is all in. Are you telling me you'll just let that slide, knowing full well?"

Heinkel put a hand to his mouth, trying to chew through information being forced on him against his will. But the point he raised next missed the mark just as badly, as far as Rowan was concerned.
It was true enough that Cecilus was keen on Al's plan.

"This one is this one, and the boy's the boy — that's all there is to it. And besides—"

"Besides?"

"—the Cecilus we've got now is a far cry from the peak of the Heavenly Sword. Shrunk down like that, his swordsmanship dulls right along with him. He can't keep the promise he made when he cut this one down, not in that state."

"—"

Rowan gave his own chest a light stroke, calling to mind the sword-scar carved there.
Bitterly, he thought of the incident Groovy had spoken of — the "attempted assassination of the emperor." Rowan's own role in it had been to try to get Cecilus to carry it out, which made him something more like an instigator. In any case, the attempt had ended in failure, and the single stroke he'd taken as punishment for it still hadn't let him forget the instant's searing heat.
Having thought that far, Rowan suddenly tilted his head.

"But say, Red, you're awfully quick to sink your teeth into the subject of this one and the boy. Come to think of it, you've reacted more deeply to it than anyone. —Is there something behind that?"

To the fact that the shrunken Cecilus was still Cecilus, and that Rowan was the father of both the small and the large versions of him, Heinkel's reaction had been heavy and dull.
When Rowan asked after the reason for it, an unexpected answer came back.
It was—

"...I'm Heinkel Astrea."

"Astrea... Astrea, Astrea, Astrea... oh, ohhhh!"

Rowan's eyes went wide at Heinkel, who had given his name with maximum gravity.
At first he simply rolled the sound of it over his tongue, and it took several repetitions before conviction soaked into his brain. But once it had clearly sunk in, the meaning of it set his blood boiling all at once.

"Then that means, Red! You hail from the bloodline of the Sword Saint!"

House Astrea of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica — bearers of the title of the strongest in all Lugunica, and rumored, moreover, to be the strongest across the whole of the Four Great Nations, kingdom aside.
Notably, word had it that the current Sword Saint, Reinhard van Astrea, stood a cut above even the many generations House Astrea had produced before him.

"Surely not, surely not, Red! Don't tell me you're the current Sword Saint, going about under the false name Heinkel? So then, a relative... no, wait — your son! Your son is Reinhard! The father of the Sword Saint! Well, well, this catches this one quite off guard! What a strange and curious bond of fate this is!"

Reinhard was of an age with Cecilus — the original Cecilus, that is, or so Rowan had heard.
Given that, and given that Heinkel was roughly of an age with Rowan himself, the nature of his relationship to Reinhard was easy enough to guess. At the same time, it explained Heinkel's sour, as-if-he'd-bitten-a-bug expression too.

Rowan and Heinkel were, the two of them, fathers — of the Blue Lightning, and of the Sword Saint.

"But more to the point — that makes you a descendant of the very line that attained the Heavenly Sword, doesn't it!"

"...Ah?"

"The first Sword Saint, Reid Astrea — that illustrious name matches both memory and ear! If that's so, then as a fellow swordsman, this one holds due respect for one who has reached the very summit this one aims for!"

The title of Sword Saint, proof of House Astrea's exceptional nature — its very first bearer, said to be the first ever to reach the concept known as the Heavenly Sword, the pinnacle and transcendent above all swordsmen.
The instant that thought struck him, Rowan's earlier attitude toward Heinkel suddenly seemed terribly rude, and he felt the urge to apologize. What discourtesy, to have treated the descendant of one who attained the Heavenly Sword so.

"My apologies, Red. This one begs pardon for the discourtesy shown thus far. Never did this one dream that a scion of Reid Astrea, who attained the Heavenly Sword, could have fallen so far."

"—"

"Red?"

Rowan placed a hand on the sword at his hip and bowed deeply, but Heinkel gave no reply.
Suspicious, Rowan glanced up at him, only to find Heinkel covering his face with both hands, shaking his head. Then he let out a long, weary breath, one that spoke of something he simply couldn't bear, and said,

"...Fine. I get it now. You and I — you and I are just wired differently, right down to the root."

"This one hasn't the foggiest what's what, but don't let it get you down too much. This one is this one, the boy's the boy, and Red is Red. No matter how it goes, there's no cutting deep into a man except through steel."

"—Back there, I only made it out alive thanks to you. I'll give you that much credit, at least."

As if refusing to exchange another word, Heinkel turned his back on Rowan.
A small urge to cut into that dutiful-looking back reared its head, but Heinkel wasn't the sort to draw a weapon even with his life on the line, so Rowan let the pointless bloodshed go.
Killing that gained nothing only clouded one's steel.

Heinkel would no doubt head straight on toward his assigned point and busy himself with the work of sabotage.
Whether he'd factored in the chance of being pinned down should a skilled foe appear there, Rowan couldn't say, but if he'd been counting on Rowan for that, well, that was a shame.
But Rowan had his own purpose, and it wasn't one his relationship with Heinkel took precedence over — not in the slightest.
And so, parting ways with a drinking companion was a bit lonely, but this would be goodbye for now, he thought—

"Leaving your son to fend for himself too — what is it you're after, exactly?"

Just as he was about to break into a run, Rowan gave a wry laugh at the voice of Heinkel, still with his back turned.
His face, his look — everything about him lived up to the impression of a man dragging around a streak of unmanly sentimentality. In any case, Rowan answered the question without hesitation, tapping the sword-scar on his chest.

"Why, for the sake of this one's own cherished ambition, of course. —Same as you, Red."

If leaving one's son to fend for himself was the charge, then that much, at least, was the same for both of them.
And with no interest whatsoever in whatever reaction Heinkel might have had to that, Rowan set off at a light run toward his own goal, through an imperial capital utterly devoid of the living.

—Now then, Rowan Segmunt is a Stargazer.

In fairness to him, it must be said: even Rowan had a self, a set of wishes, a life, before he became a Stargazer — before he lost himself utterly in the pursuit of fulfilling his granted mandate.
Of course, as with most Stargazers, the moment Rowan was granted his mandate and became one of their number, he was compelled into a way of life different from what had come before. But to those around him, the change in Rowan hadn't stood out much at all.

That was because, even before receiving his mandate, Rowan Segmunt's cherished ambition had already been to attain the Heavenly Sword, and he had already been throwing himself headlong into everything within his power to accomplish it.

Fellow strongmen who sought to master their craft as Rowan did, fearsome Witchbeasts that attacked villages, villagers nearly wiped out by those Witchbeasts, villains who oppressed others, saints who gave to others — he cut down anything and everything he could get his hands on, so long as it might serve to temper his steel, but it never amounted to much.

He studied under various schools, mastered their techniques, then cut down the heads of those very schools, and tried to fuse together the many methods he'd absorbed within himself — but once he realized it only threw the balance of his own technique out of alignment, he abandoned that path too.
Quite literally, he walked and walked and walked and walked down that bloody road, and still the path to the Heavenly Sword eluded him. He craved it with his whole heart, and had even gone so far as to consider ending his own life — and it was at that moment—

—that was when Rowan received his mandate and became a Stargazer.

Fated now, whatever else he might do, to bring about the attainment of the Heavenly Sword, Rowan found himself driven not to reach it himself, but to trial-and-error his way toward creating a being who could reach it in his stead.

But he had no idea what the right answer was. At bottom, Rowan's approach was always the same.
For the time being, there was nothing to do but try everything within reach. The path of seeking out a talented individual and raising them up to the attainment, he gave up on almost immediately.
He could do it better himself. And if someone couldn't outdo him, then the Heavenly Sword would remain a dream beyond a dream — better to cut down and discard so fleeting a hope.

As he went on this way, he came to a realization.
That he himself had been chosen, marked out to reach the Heavenly Sword — there had to be some meaning in that being him specifically.
With that thought, he tried once more to aim for the Heavenly Sword himself, but after blindly cutting down person after person only to confirm it was a road that couldn't be reached through blind effort, he let that go as well.
That wasn't it. It wasn't Rowan himself. —It only had to be Rowan's seed that reached it.

And so, through Rowan Segmunt's tireless effort, a vessel capable of attaining the Heavenly Sword was born — Cecilus Segmunt — and Rowan fulfilled his role.

Watching his own child, still soaking in the birth-bath, laugh with delight at the bared blade pressed to his throat, Rowan was released, at long, long last, from the fetters that had bound him so tightly.

A Stargazer, once they have witnessed the fulfillment of their heavenly mandate, is freed from the role assigned to them.
What comes to govern the spirit afterward is a total inability to understand the very values one had, until just a moment before, followed without question, without any real reason, twisting even one's own beliefs and convictions along the way.

Naturally, the same thing happened to Rowan.
He had thrown himself desperately into fathering a child gifted with the talent to attain the Heavenly Sword, in order to fulfill his mandate, and having gazed upon the child born at the end of it all, the very instant he thought I've done it, he stopped caring at all.

What was left in the end was a son recognized as having the possibility of reaching the Heavenly Sword, and himself — a man who had lost sight of his own goal of reaching it, having spent his prime years for someone else's sake instead.
Faced with that unbearable truth, Rowan very nearly moved, this time for real, toward the decision to end his own life — the one he had failed to carry out before.
But—

"Ahh—"

When his son — the one born already promised to walk the road to the Heavenly Sword, the one who had laughed at the life-threatening blade — took hold of the finger of a despairing Rowan, that thought was blown away without a trace.
The shock that frail little life delivered to Rowan was beyond measure even for a man who had cut down countless lives before and bathed in enough blood to fill a lake.

He understood, then.
This creature, now too feeble to so much as hold a single chopstick, would one day acquire swordsmanship equal to the task of reaching the Heavenly Sword. If so, then what reason was there to decide that his own future held nothing but decline past his prime?
If an infant could reach the Heavenly Sword, then a road remained open even to an aging swordsman.

And so Rowan cast aside the grief that would have closed off his road, and swore anew to press forward toward his own desire.
Granted his mandate, having fulfilled his mandate, having brought into the world a child capable of reaching the Heavenly Sword, and freed from his role as a Stargazer — that was Rowan Segmunt.

—And even now, he has never given up on the road toward reaching the Heavenly Sword himself, and continues to press onward.

Having parted from Heinkel, Rowan's feet carried him lightly, at a run, toward the northern side of the capital.
Ahead lay the Crystal Palace, the most conspicuous building in the whole capital, and there was no room to doubt that it had, by now, become the Undead's stronghold.
The mastermind behind reviving the dead — the very one Groovy and Al were making such a fuss over — was probably in the castle too, but that gave Rowan no reason to stop his feet.

Let it be stated plainly: he wasn't heading for the Crystal Palace to storm the castle and be the first to take the enemy mastermind's head, or anything of the sort, in order to settle the situation.
Every word he'd said to Heinkel had been the plain truth — Rowan couldn't have cared less whether the Empire was saved or not. If anything, the bigger the chaos and calamity, the better, as far as he was concerned.

That Rowan should now be rushing headlong toward the Crystal Palace had a reason behind it.
Of course, the reason was his own flesh and blood, Cecilus Segmunt. Though to say Cecilus was the reason wasn't to speak of any parental love or affection.

"That blockhead, letting go of the Dream Sword and the Wicked Sword — what an outrageous thing to do."

As he ground his teeth, what came to Rowan's mind were two blades harboring tremendous power — for all the Enchanted Swords and holy swords said to exist in this world, only ten blades held true power.
Two of those ten were Cecilus's beloved swords, the very ones he had let slip from his grasp after shrinking down: the Dream Sword, Masayume, and the Wicked Sword, Murasame.

Under no circumstances could those two swords be allowed to go missing.
To reach the Heavenly Sword, one's own talent and mastery of swordsmanship went without saying, of course — but the steel needed to prove that mastery genuine to the world had to be worthy of the task as well, and that was not something to compromise on.
Masayume and Murasame were precisely the two blades worthy of that role.

"No telling when that boy'll go back to normal, but he'll cut a sorry figure without his swords, won't he."

When the shrunken Cecilus returned to his original size, there would be no story to tell without his swords.
Or perhaps, in this vast, sweeping hour of calamity, Cecilus might reach the Heavenly Sword. And if that happened while he had no sword to hand, it would be an outrage.

—Even should he reach the Heavenly Sword, cutting down a Cecilus who lacked a proper sword would prove nothing about Rowan himself having attained it.
For the sake of that proof too, Cecilus needed his swords back.

"That rascal, shrinking down and forgetting the promise he made his own father — what nerve."

A promise had once been exchanged between father and son, Rowan and Cecilus.
When Rowan, hoping to bring Cecilus to the Heavenly Sword by the quickest route, tried to turn the whole of the Empire against him, Cecilus — of all things — refused, on the grounds that it felt "too much like a sloppy villain," and sided with Vincent, moving to cut Rowan down.
At that time, in order to get Cecilus to let him go, Rowan made him a promise.
That someday, when Cecilus reached the Heavenly Sword, Rowan would without fail come to cut him down.

Cecilus accepted the terms and dropped a badly wounded Rowan into a river to let him escape. Rowan very nearly died, but survived, and had spent the time since honing his skills while he waited for that day.
And now, that day looked to be drawing near at last.

"If the place hasn't changed—"

Cecilus, despite holding the seat of "Ichi," had spent nearly all his rewards on swords.
Because of that, he'd never owned a mansion fitting one of the Nine Generals, instead building himself a shack out in the wilds north of the Crystal Palace and living out of it. If that hadn't changed, then there was a good chance the Dream Sword and Wicked Sword were both stored away in that shack.
To retrieve them and deliver them to Cecilus, Rowan pressed on deeper toward the Crystal Palace—

"—!"

Slipping past the eyes of the Undead, passing straight by the Crystal Palace, just as he made for the base of the shattered floodwall, a sudden presence made Rowan leap hard to the side.
And that proved to be the right call.

—A tremendous impact came crashing down from directly above, its ferocious destructive force caving in the main street in a great circular crater.

The wall surrounding the Crystal Palace's grounds along the street buckled and collapsed, plumes of dust and smoke spreading like an explosion to fill his field of view, and Rowan clicked his tongue as he drew his sword clean from its sheath.
Cutting through the billowing dust with the same technique he used to send a slash flying at distant targets — Cloud Cutting — he could see, beyond it, the one responsible for the impact.

It was a slender, tall woman.
A woman with long white hair, clad in a blue dress, standing with a graceful, flowing posture. Rowan was hopelessly ignorant when it came to matters of beauty, but even by his own standards, hers was a supple frame he could call beautiful.
Her narrow, blue eyes carried a shade of grief in them, and Rowan found that puzzling.

Unlike the Undead, hers weren't black eyes with golden irises floating in them. Blood ran beneath her pale skin, and the woman staring him down was, in all likelihood, one of the living.
And yet this was the castle of the dead, the city of the Undead — which meant, by rights, her actions should mark her as an enemy of the living.

"And you'd be—"

"—Iris."

"...Didn't expect to just get a name handed over like that."

Still holding his sword low at his hip, Rowan wet his lips with his tongue and narrowed his eyes.
That she was a woman gave him no reason to think little of her. More than anything, the air she carried was that of an almost unbelievably powerful fighter. Her expression wasn't one of someone eager to wield that strength, but that, too, was a trivial matter to Rowan.
With an expression that seemed to cling, of all things, to precisely that trivial matter, the woman who had named herself Iris trembled the fox ears buried in her white hair, and said,

"Welcome home. So long as it's within my sight, not a soul need die."

"Well now, is that so. Beg your pardon, then."

Before the woman making such an earnest appeal — Iris — Rowan closed one eye and readjusted his grip on his sword.
Whatever her intentions, if they stood in the way of his own path, there was nothing for it. And besides, retreating before a fighter of this caliber was simply unthinkable for a swordsman.
Which meant—

"—the road to the Heavenly Sword remains steep and treacherous yet. This one will cut you down, and press on ahead."
