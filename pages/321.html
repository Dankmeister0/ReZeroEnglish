Arc 5 Chapter 5: "The Water Gate City of Priestella"

The first thing to greet Subaru and the others upon reaching the city was a towering, mighty outer wall, so imposing it demanded you look up at it.

"Yeah, that's exactly the prison-like impression I got when I first heard the description..."

Having handed the driver's seat over to Otto, Subaru stuck his face out of the carriage window and muttered under his breath. Catching that mutter, Beatrice, who was poking her own head out from below Subaru in the same way, said,

"That Joshua fellow called it scenic and picturesque, but Betty can't see it that way at all, I suppose. Rather than putting one at ease, it looks more like the sort of place that would wear you down, I should think."

"Yeah, same here. Well, the big bridge and the water gates are impressively built, I'll give it that."

Nodding at Beatrice's impression, Subaru answered while lowering his gaze. The dragon carriage carrying Subaru and the others was currently crossing a massive stone bridge called the "Tigracy Grand Bridge," which led to Priestella's main gate. Stretching up a little and looking toward the horizon, one could see the gleam of sunlight reflecting off a water's surface. To Subaru's eyes it looked close to an ocean, but in this world, where seas fundamentally didn't exist, it was apparently nothing more than a great river and a lake.

"Originally, Priestella was a city built atop a lake, I suppose. Inside that outer wall, the elevation drops in a bowl shape the closer you get to the center, I should think. Given that the city itself was built as a trap, it stands to reason that water would naturally collect at the center, I suppose."

"A trap for taking down some nasty Witchbeast, or so the story goes. Emilia-tan mentioned that too—is that actually true?"

"Even Betty has never seen the real thing before, and she hasn't been told exactly what it was meant to catch, I should think. But well, having seen it in person, I don't doubt it was true, I suppose."

Beatrice's bluish eyes gazed at the city's main gate, visible past the far end of the bridge. The inside of the city was still hidden by the outer wall, but perhaps Beatrice's eyes could already picture roughly what lay within. Just how much detail the Forbidden Archive's books contained about the world was a mystery, but Beatrice's depth of knowledge was often a great help.

"...Why is it that Betty's head is being petted, I wonder?"

"Because Bea-ko's right there, I guess. As long as I've got the time, I want to keep petting you."

"I don't quite understand it, but this feels both condescending and completely unnecessary, I suppose!?"

Still, since Beatrice made no move to bat his hand away, Subaru kept petting her head as his gaze drifted past the railing to the lake beyond. The water's surface swayed gently, so clear you could see straight to the bottom, and as far as the eye could see there was no trace of environmental damage—no litter, nothing discarded.

If this wasn't just limited to one part but held true across the whole city, that was a wonderful standard of civic conduct.

"Now that you mention it, same along the highway too—I haven't seen any litter or illegally dumped industrial waste. I guess part of it's just that there isn't much excess stuff lying around, but still, that's impressive."

"Priestella is especially strict about it—maintaining the scenery is mandatory here. There's a simple entry inspection when you pass through the main gate, so please don't get weirdly proactive and refuse to sign the oath document."

From the driver's seat, Otto's warning came flying in, having caught Subaru's mutter. At the word, Subaru cocked his head and asked, "Oath document?"

"In the royal capital, dragon carriages bearing a family crest are usually exempted, but in Priestella there are no exceptions—you have to submit paperwork every time you enter or leave the city."

At Emilia's answer, Subaru nodded, impressed, taking it to be something like a passport check for entering the country. Then, turning the phrase "oath document" back over in his mind, he tilted his head again.

"Don't tell me it's paper with some kind of self-Geas power on it? Like, this oath document interacts with the Od in the body of whoever signs it, and the moment they break it, they lose all Gate function or something…?"

"No way, that's really, really scary… There's no compulsion like that. It's just a document promising you won't do anything bad. But it does mean you're keeping watch over your own conscience."

"...If everyone were as strict with themselves as Emilia-tan, the world would be peaceful, but still."

At Emilia's argument, faintly rooted in a belief in humanity's inherent goodness, Subaru, all too aware of his own less-than-sterling character, gave a wry smile. In any case, he understood that the oath document effectively carried no binding force.

"There are strict rules set by the kingdom's laws that must be followed, but when it comes to running a city, the city's magistrate or lord holds a great deal of authority. There are several things in Priestella that simply differ from the kingdom's laws. The oath document should spell that out properly too, so don't scoff at it—read it through properly."

"What a pain in the ass. Can't Otto-nii-i just read it and chew it up for me later, huh? What's wrong with that?"

"That way you'll never grow, will you. Garfiel, you have a position to think about too—it's about time you learned at least how to skim a document, instead of just filling your head with weird knowledge from all those books you like reading."

"It ain't weird knowledge, it's a man's romance. Right, boss?"

"Yeah, that's right."

Garfiel, up on the roof, stretched his neck down at a precarious angle. At his call, Subaru nodded firmly, at which Otto let out a sigh, Emilia wore a face like she was watching something endearing, and Beatrice sighed and shook her head.

"Honestly, Subaru and Garfiel are both hopeless children, I suppose."

"Truly, chaperoning this lot is no easy job."

Just how much Otto agreed with Beatrice's words was hard to say. The grief and exhaustion in Otto's voice, sadly, was likely something none of the others riding in the carriage could sympathize with.

—Patrasche let out a whinny, and everyone's attention turned forward. The main gate of the Water Gate City of Priestella was drawing close.

The entry inspection itself concluded almost anticlimactically easily.

Just as Emilia and Otto had told them beforehand, the document listed precautions for entering the city along with the city ordinances one was obligated to observe. That said, the contents were all things that wouldn't be violated simply by living normally, so there wasn't much need to brace oneself and think "I must obey this!"

A quick glance-through, a swift scrawl of one's own name, and once the inspector gave approval, that alone was enough to be waved swiftly through the main gate into the city. There was a brief moment where the entry inspector flustered somewhat at Emilia giving her name, but since they had presumably also let Anastasia through, they may well have been privately speculating about just what was going on.

"Well, when two girls holding something like a claim to the royal succession show up around the same time, I guess that alone would stir things up."

"But it seems like Anastasia let them know ahead of time, so it didn't take too much time. Maybe it was Joshua-kun, or maybe Mimi-chan."

"Setting aside how bad his attitude is, I could see Joshua doing that, but I don't think it was Mimi."

Subaru couldn't imagine that cat girl having that kind of consideration in her. Not that she was selfish or anything like that—it was more that he struggled to put into words exactly what he meant.

"She's cute, though."

"That's true, Mimi-chan is cute."

Emilia nodded along with mysterious conviction. Subaru crossed his arms too, thinking there really wasn't any other way to put it. And then, for some reason, Beatrice stepped on his foot for saying it. While Subaru placated a sulking Beatrice to kill time, Garfiel and Otto—who had been held up at the inspection the longest—finally made it through the main gate.

"Good work. Why'd they hold you two up so long?"

"It's Garfiel's fault. That's exactly why I kept telling him, over and over, to practice his handwriting…"

"Garfiel, don't tell me you can't write?"

"Nah, that ain't it. It's just, kinda, from the boss's perspective you'd probably call it avant-garde, or whatever. Ah, whatever, don't matter."

In other words, his handwriting was so terrible it was illegible, and that was what had held him up. If that were the case, the inspector should have offered to write on behalf of someone who couldn't read or write, but Garfiel's pride probably wouldn't allow him to rely on that.

"I'm telling you this for your own good—let this be a lesson and study up. I know you write letters to Ryuzu-san, but I sure hope those are in legible handwriting."

"Hah, don't make me laugh, boss. Me and the old bag go way back. Even if I write with my left hand, granny'll figure it out just fine."

"That just means you have zero intention of putting in the effort at all!"

Poking fun at a proud-looking Garfiel, Subaru let out a breath, feeling drained. As for Garfiel's grandmother Ryuzu, she didn't actually live together with him at Roswaal Manor. At present, Ryuzu had taken on the role of instilling a minimal degree of personality and knowledge into the twenty-four Replicas who shared her same origin, and having them settle respectively in several villages within the Mathers domain.

Command authority over the Replicas still rested with Emilia and Garfiel, the two of them, and instructions from that pair could reach the distant Replicas. The aim was to make use of that to give them a role as a sort of makeshift chain of command for relaying orders.

This had been Subaru's proposal, and the responsibility for it had fallen to Ryuzu. Garfiel had opposed it at first, but by now he seemed to have more or less come around to accepting it. For that reason, Ryuzu and the Replicas were currently staying at Arlam Village. From there, once each one finished her education, the Replicas were to be sent out in turn to the various towns and villages.

"It's basically Mission: Impossible, which sounds bad when you put it that way, but…"

Still, the idea of leaving them to exist with no role or purpose at all had felt distasteful to him. Perhaps that notion had been pushed along by the guilt of a Subaru who had, once, within a loop, used them as expendable pawns.

"Subaru? The luggage inspection for the carriage's finished too, so let's get going."

Called back by Emilia from his brooding thoughts, Subaru hastily looked up. He gave a wry smile at her dubious gaze, then took hold of Patrasche's reins. The clever, jet-black Earth Dragon, as if sensing the knot in her master's heart, nuzzled her muzzle against the side of his neck.

It was strange to Subaru how much comfort that rough, coarse texture could bring.

"...Thanks, as always."

To that wordless show of consideration, Subaru answered by stroking her hard scales. Patrasche responded with nothing but a snort of breath, and this time, for real, he pulled the reins and led them into Priestella. Passing through the main gate, there was a river flowing between the outer wall and the city proper. They crossed a stone bridge laid across it, the inner gate opened, and Priestella was unveiled before them.

"Oh…"

Faced with the sight spreading out before him, Subaru couldn't help but let out a groan of admiration. That reaction wasn't his alone—Emilia and Beatrice, lined up on either side, each showed their own form of the same astonishment.

It was a city of water so striking it made him want to apologize for ever having called it a prison. When Subaru first heard Priestella described, he had pictured something like Venice, the water city of his old world. And as it turned out, he hadn't been wrong.

A circular city, its outer wall built in a matching circle to encase it all the way around. Ignoring differences in scale, the internal structure of the city was perhaps closest to the layout of a stadium built for watching sports. The outer ring sat at the highest elevation, with the ground stepping progressively lower the closer one got to the center. Stone buildings crowded together across the tiered, terraced land at each level, giving Subaru's eyes the strongest impression yet of a Western-style streetscape.

Furthermore, large waterways ran through various parts of the city, and there was one especially massive waterway—no, canal—that divided the circular city into quarters. He could see several ferry-like boats gliding across the water's surface, and the very word "pilot" sent a shiver down Subaru's spine.

The blue city. The water capital. The Water Gate City of Priestella. Its name and reputation held no false advertising. It was, honestly, a sight that inspired nothing but awe.

"Amazing…"

The word of admiration slipped out unbidden, and not a single member of the group contradicted him. As the gatekeeper opened the inner gate and watched their reaction, a look of deep satisfaction crossed his face. Surely everyone who visited this place reacted just as Subaru and his companions had.

And witnessing that very reaction was, without a doubt, the greatest reward the gatekeepers received in carrying out their duty. No doubt about it, surely. Well played, indeed.

"I get it now, this really is a sight to behold. Guess Otto-nii-i wasn't just blowin' smoke after all."

Recovering from the shock a step ahead of the others, Garfiel rubbed the tip of his nose with a finger. Even so, his excitement clearly hadn't fully died down yet, a faint flush still coloring his cheeks. A man's romance—something touching that "great big something," some "amazing place" that resonated with it, had clearly shaken him to the core.

"Since it's a place tied to Hoshin, the god of commerce, I always thought I'd like to visit someday, but this is a feast for the eyes. It was worth coming just for this."

Beside him, Otto too wore a face overcome with emotion, and for some reason had his hands pressed together. The phrase he'd used—a place tied to Hoshin—brought to mind a name Subaru had heard a few times now: "Hoshin of the Wilderness."

"Hoshin, that's the guy, right? The legendary merchant from way back who built himself up from a burnt wasteland."

"There are a few details that are a bit different, but broadly, that's right. Four hundred years ago, Hoshin crossed alone to Kararagi in the west, back when it hadn't even been properly developed yet, and remade the region's entire economic foundation through sheer talent alone, amassing a fortune. A genuine hero, through and through."

Given that he was a merchant to the core, there was real heat in Otto's eyes as he recounted the legend of Hoshin. Now one of the Four Great Nations, the Kararagi City-States—that its very framework had been built up single-handedly made it easy to understand why his great achievements were still passed down.

"That's the story behind why Anastasia took the Hoshin family name too, wasn't it—paying homage to him."

"I think it's audacious, honestly. In terms of showing off her ambition and her goals, I don't think there's a clearer way to do it, but precisely because everyone knows the name, everyone also questions it. So far, though, I do think she's produced results that live up to the name, without falling short of it."

"If she's seriously trying to produce results that don't fall short of that name… well, snatching up the Lugunica throne would be the ultimate proof, wouldn't it. Calmly marching straight toward the goal, huh."

Admiring it all honestly, Subaru finally managed to tear his attention away from the breathtaking view before him. Then he stroked the head of Beatrice, who was still captivated by the water capital, and tugged at Emilia's sleeve to pull her attention back as well.

"So, the place Anastasia and the others are waiting at is called the 'Water Feathered Robe Inn,' right? I don't know where it is, but given her position, it can't be some cheap dive."

"Right, that's true. It sounded like word had already been passed to the inspectors, so I think it's a proper place. It seemed like Otto-kun and the others heard more about it earlier…"

At Emilia's words Otto nodded, and with a light hop, sprang up into the carriage's driver's seat. Then, jerking his chin to indicate the passenger compartment behind him, he said,

"I've got the route in mind, so I'll guide us. In this city, boats take priority over dragon carriages when it comes to transport, so we can't rush. Going slow will still be a bit much for Natsuki-san to handle, I think."

"Look who's talking. If it were just Patrasche and me, I wouldn't even need to say a word—just tremble and stare meaningfully, and she'd get the idea. Right?"

Not to be outdone by Otto, Subaru gazed meaningfully at Patrasche, but Patrasche turned her face away from a winking Subaru. He could have sworn, somehow, that she'd sighed. Deflated by the unexpected reaction, Subaru sagged, and Emilia patted his back consolingly while Beatrice took his hand and pulled him back into the carriage.

"Alright, let's move out."

From atop the carriage roof, which had by now firmly become his own private box seat, Garfiel made the declaration with great pomp. Otto, smiling wryly, worked the reins, and the carriage slowly began to move. Its pace was genuinely leisurely—even accounting for the slope and the need to slow down for it, it was a speed that could only be called a crawl.

"Still, looking out the window, there really aren't many dragon carriages around, huh."

"Right. And the roads give priority to the waterways too, so looking at it this way, the roads wide enough for a dragon carriage don't go straight—they kind of wind and curve instead."

"Ah, true enough."

Just as Emilia pointed out, since the large waterway ran through the center of the city, the roads meant for walking or for dragon carriages naturally formed something like detours. It felt inconvenient, but watching a small boat glide smoothly along the waterway beside the slow-moving carriage, it didn't seem so bad after all.

"Come to think of it, dragon carriages have the 'Divine Protection of Wind Evasion' and all that—do boats have something similar? Like a no-capsizing blessing, or a sea-breeze blessing, something like that?"

"I haven't really heard of anything like that—I don't think there's a Divine Protection granted to the boat itself. Though the rowers might be blessed with things like the 'Divine Protection of Lakes' or a 'Divine Protection of Water Transport,' or something along those lines."

"It isn't very widely known, but Water Dragons do have a corresponding Divine Protection, I suppose. Same as Earth Dragons—basically, they're not much affected by water, or something along those lines, I should think."

"Water Dragons, huh. I'd like to see one at least once."

"Given where we are, I imagine there's one somewhere in the city, I suppose."

Even as she answered Subaru's question, Beatrice, who disliked animals, showed no enthusiasm. Not only did she harbor a strange sense of unease around Patrasche, but apparently she didn't have a very good impression of Water Dragons either.

"If it's something like a Liger, I bet you could hold one without it feeling that bad, right?"

"It's not as though not touching animals will kill someone, I should think. Betty is obviously cuter anyway, I suppose."

"Competing on cuteness with something on the animal spectrum feels kind of pointless, though… if anything, doesn't competing on the same turf just lower your own stock?"

To Subaru's eyes, Patrasche looked cool and cute both. Of course, the vector of that "cute" was different from the cuteness he felt toward Emilia, Rem, or Beatrice. At Subaru's answer, Beatrice gave him a dubious look, but Emilia, catching the word "Liger," gazed at Subaru with keen interest.

"Me too! Do you think if we asked, they'd let us touch one?"

"You should've asked while Mimi was still at the mansion. Emilia-tan, you really do hold back in weird ways like that sometimes."

"Well, he's an important mount, and I didn't want to be presumptuous about it. It's been a while since I've been able to touch Puck too, so I've been missing the feel of fur."

Even Emilia, who felt family-like affection for Puck, apparently still had her heart stolen by that fur. When Subaru promised, "I'll try asking," Emilia looked overjoyed and began humming to herself. Tone-deaf, as ever.

Listening to that humming with a warm, indulgent ear, Subaru propped his chin on his hand and gazed out at the streetscape. Subaru leaned against the window, while Beatrice knelt on the seat to peer out the window as well. Watching their thoroughly childlike postures, Subaru was debating whether he ought to scold them for bad manners, when—

"Ah, Subaru. Perfect timing, I suppose."

"Hm? Oh—oh-h-h!"

Called by Beatrice, he looked and saw, cutting through the waterway in a spray of water, the silhouette of a fish—no, not a fish. It was a creature with a long, serpentine body, but with short, proper limbs attached. A Water Dragon. Its glistening blue hide called a snake to mind, but its head was, sure enough, unmistakably that of a "dragon." Its jaw bristled with sharp fangs, and long whiskers, like a catfish's, hung from it. Where the Earth Dragon brought to mind a lizard dashing about on four legs, the Water Dragon was much closer to the image of an Eastern dragon. The kind of look that made you want to call it a "Shenlong."

"But it's got kind of a proud look to it, or, I dunno, a nasty personality kind of face."

"From a human perspective, I suppose that's how it looks. In truth, compared to Earth Dragons, Water Dragons are overwhelmingly the more troublesome type of creature to tame, I should think. Apparently, only by caring for one meticulously from birth all the way until it becomes a full-grown dragon will it finally acknowledge you as its master, I suppose."

"Guess that takes a lot of time and effort, then. Me and Patrasche just understood each other the moment our eyes met."

"Just why that one's taken such a shine to Subaru is something Betty can't help but find mysterious, I suppose."

That was true for Subaru as well. Patrasche had originally been in service under Crusch, but from the moment she'd been lent out for the Subjugation of the White Whale, she'd already taken quite a liking to Subaru. Looking back on it now, when he'd been told to "choose whichever Earth Dragon you like," choosing the self-assertive Patrasche had clearly been the right call. In truth, there had been more than a few situations they never would have gotten through without Patrasche.

"Hmph. My Patrasche has a far more refined face, I'll have you know."

"Subaru. Why are you suddenly talking like Anne?"

Subaru found himself burning with a strange sense of rivalry toward the Water Dragon dancing its way along the waterway. And then—not that it could have noticed his gaze—the Water Dragon's head suddenly turned this way. It surfaced, and from its mouth came a shrill, piercing cry. For some reason, Subaru felt certain it was saying something like, "Heh, quit gawkin' at me, outsider," and it rubbed him the wrong way.

"That bastard—he just made fun of us, I bet. Well, in that case…"

"——!"

Fired up to get his revenge by doing a vocal impression of the huge black Witchbeast he'd seen at the burning mansion, Subaru geared up. But before he could carry through, a sharp, dignified whinny rang out toward the water's surface, cutting him off. It was Patrasche.

Sensing her master's anger at the Water Dragon's provocative attitude, she'd answered on his behalf. What meaning that cry carried was unclear, but the Water Dragon, apparently frightened by the voice and glare of the Earth Dragon glowering down at it from land, let out a strangled cry and dove beneath the water. The small boat it had been pulling shot forward at a burst of speed, and Subaru caught sight of the boatman flailing in alarm as he watched them go, dumbfounded.

"Wh-what was that just now?"

"—Natsuki-san, please don't let Patrasche-chan get up to anything strange. We've barely arrived and I really do not want any trouble, thank you very much."

Otto's irritated voice came from the driver's seat, and Subaru gave a small wave back, then whistled to Patrasche so she'd hear. It wasn't as though he was skilled enough to actually communicate through that, but he meant it to convey his gratitude.

"Water Dragons are cool and all, but Patrasche's still number one."

"...Well, Betty supposes even she thinks our girl is better than that vulgar Water Dragon, I suppose."

Perhaps because Subaru had said it so happily, Beatrice reluctantly agreed.

The rest of the journey proceeded without any particular trouble. Crossing over stone bridges that spanned the waterways, Subaru idly pictured in his mind the layout of the city he'd glimpsed from the main gate, and said,

"Come to think of it, this town seemed like it was divided into four by the waterways."

"Yeah, that's right. Priestella's divided into districts by the big central canal, and apparently, going clockwise from the main gate, they're called First District, Second District, Third District, and Fourth District."

At Emilia's answer to his question, Subaru gave a snort.

"Not exactly imaginative naming, huh. They could've at least gone with something like East Blue-style names. Don't you think?"

"I think so too, boss."

"Subaru and Garfiel's taste in this is beside the point, I suppose."

While the two of them hit it off, Beatrice's attitude remained cold. Watching the three of them, Emilia's cheeks softened, and, with the air of someone reciting knowledge straight out of a book, she raised a finger.

"Apparently the kinds of shops and workplaces gathered in each numbered district are different, and the residential areas are concentrated mostly in the Second and Third Districts, further from the main gate. The 'Water Feathered Robe Inn' we're headed to is in the First District, since a lot of traveling visitors pass through there."

"Guess we should be arriving pretty soon then… oh, looks like perfect timing."

While they'd passed the time chatting, the slowly-moving carriage showed signs of coming to a stop. It seemed they'd arrived at their destination inn, and Otto, from the driver's seat, came around to the passenger compartment.

"We've made it safely. I'm going to speak to the staff and have them move Frufoo and the others to the stables, so if you'd go ahead… no, actually, please wait by the entrance."

"Why'd you change your mind? Is there some problem if we go into the inn first?"

"There is. If you happened to run into Anastasia-san and I arrived to find you'd already been thoroughly talked circles around, I really couldn't bear it."

At Otto's less-than-flattering words, the group made sour faces, but given their track record, none of them could really argue back. Carrying just their hand luggage, they got down from the carriage, and Otto, led off by a staff member who'd come out to guide them, disappeared around to the back of the establishment.

Watching him go, Subaru stretched, and finally turned his gaze toward the inn—the "Water Feathered Robe Inn." And then—

"So, what kind of inn have they gone and invited us to, I wonder… wait."

Subaru's mouth fell open in astonishment. Beside him, Emilia tilted her head slightly, a finger to her cheek.

"It's such a strange-shaped building somehow. I think this might be my first time seeing one like this."

Emilia's succinct impression was, essentially, the same one Garfiel and Beatrice shared. Subaru alone, however, held a different impression entirely. And no wonder. Because—

"This isn't so much an inn as… it's a ryokan."

A flat wooden structure, its wooden doors sliding panels with glass panes. Add to that a hedge, a gravel path laid from the gate to the entrance, and roof tiles used on the roof—there was no room left for mistaking it for anything else.

Amid a streetscape brimming with exotic, foreign atmosphere, it was an architectural philosophy that went beyond mere incongruity. That day, Natsuki Subaru came face to face with wafū architecture, in the form of the establishment known as the "Water Feathered Robe Inn."

"Oh-ho, seems y'all are surprised. Well then, it was worth the trouble of choosing this place as our inn after all."

To the group, still standing there dumbfounded, that gentle, amused voice suddenly called out. Still in a daze, Subaru slowly turned his gaze toward the source of the voice—toward a figure peering in at them from beyond the hedge.

A white dress made from animal fur, with an especially eye-catching fox fur stole. The cold season had already passed, so the dress itself was a light, summer-dress sort of thing, but that stole alone remained unchanged from the last time he'd seen her, as if it were something precious to her. A petite frame, long, wavy hair of a pale lavender hue. A lovely face wearing a soft, gentle smile, and a pair of pale blue-green eyes holding something fathomless within them.

There was no room for mistaking her—she was the very person who had invited Subaru and the others here. Anastasia Hoshin, come to greet them in person. And then—

"It's been a while, hasn't it. Thank ya kindly for makin' the long trip out here today. Y'all must be tired from the journey, so… why don't we get you settled in your rooms first, and talk after?"

Before Otto could even return, she had, as a matter of course, already seized the initiative. —Every single person there felt it, keenly.
