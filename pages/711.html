Arc 9 Chapter 16: "Accomplices"

—The first thing "it" felt like was a nightmare seen with eyes wide open.

"B-b-b..."

A sensation like the soul—not the mind—refusing to understand.

It was as if the sensation of "a moment ago" had been severed like a taut thread cut clean through—completely disconnected from the self of right now, and yet, even though the body had accepted it, the soul refused to.

The nightmare continued.

Something forced its way in through the mouth opened to scream, devouring the contents of the body from within—an unbearable sense of loss, an impossible sense of ending, an unforgivable sense of desecration.

Who, exactly, could bear the feeling of having one's own existence devoured?

Yellow stomach acid dribbled from the corner of a mouth, a body convulsing on the cold ground.

A dim, half-lit space—a place that felt far too wrong to be the beginning of a life—stood as proof that this torn soul, tormented by a nightmare it could not wake from, had already repeated this same thing over and over again—

—Why.

That question rose up inside the mind.

It was a "why" aimed at injustice, at absurdity, at unexpected misfortune, at some manner of malice, at an unforgivable threat, at an unending vicious cycle.

—Why, why, why, why, why.

Why did it have to suffer like this? Had it truly committed some evil so great? Was its sin severe enough to warrant such retribution?

If the mistake had truly been irreversible, then it should have been allowed to begin atoning before the punishment was piled on and on like this, layer after layer, until it was drowning.

And yet—why?

—Why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why.

To that question which would not fade—

"Let us invite you—to the Witch's Tea Party."

So spoke a voice brimming with delight, one that sounded like both a Blessing and a Curse.

"—Petra-chaaan!"

Petra's body swayed and buckled right in front of her, and Meili reflexively caught her by the shoulder.

It happened a mere five seconds after Petra, resolve fixed, had turned the page of the black-bound book—the Book of the Dead. From the way the air felt, it was clear she had succeeded in reading it.

The Book of the Dead was a grimoire that let one relive the life history of someone who had died.

However, not just anyone could freely read any book—there was a condition to reading one: only someone who shared some point of connection with the book's subject could do so.

It made it impossible to indulge in the poor taste of endlessly peering into the history of some total stranger.

Though whether peering into the history of someone one did know was any more refined a practice was, admittedly, up for debate. In any case, Meili—who had her own experience searching for a Book of the Dead she'd wanted to see—was in no position to pass judgment on the matter.

What mattered was that, judging from Petra's reaction, she had satisfied the condition for reading that particular Book of the Dead—the one with its title scraped away, discovered in Natsuki Subaru's bag.

And then—

"Petra-chan, c'mon, Petra-chan. ...No good, she's not waking up."

Meili gave the pale, closed-eyed cheek of the Petra she held cradled a light tap, but the limp, strengthless girl showed no sign of waking. Fainted, blacked out, knocked unconscious—it was one of the three.

Spurred by Meili's worry, the crimson scorpion atop her head began clicking its pincers busily, as if trying to help wake Petra up. Naturally, it had no effect.

Anyone who read the Book of the Dead had someone else's entire life crammed whole into their head.

The mental toll of that must have been considerable. Flam had mentioned that Ezzo, who'd apparently overdone it and read more than ten of the books, had foamed at the mouth and wet himself before passing out the first time.

Fortunately, Petra didn't seem to be suffering in quite so dignity-shattering a way for a maiden—but even so—

"—!"

The unconscious girl's expression was thick with agony or anguish, and her breathing—labored as though she were feverish and suffering under a high fever—was painful just to witness.

"Petra-chan, whose memories—what kind of memories—did you get shown...?"

Glancing over at the Book of the Dead that had dropped from Petra's hand, Meili stretched out a foot and pushed the book—lying open upside down on the floor—away from them.

It faced spine-up, but it would be a problem if she accidentally caught sight of its contents and ended up collapsing just like Petra. —Though she couldn't really think of anyone the two of them had in common for whom the Book of the Dead would even work.

"Don't tell me it's Elsa, is it?"

The one person that Meili and Petra both knew of, and who had already passed on, was Elsa Granhiert—her face flickered to mind.

Considering the purpose of this journey, it wasn't an implausible guess that Subaru, having found Elsa's Book of the Dead, had hidden it away in his bag so that Meili wouldn't catch even a glimpse of it, just in case.

But, even so—

"...Yeah, I know. If it were Onii-san, he wouldn't scrape off the spine just to hide something from me."

Meili answered the crimson scorpion's protest—its long tail swinging side to side—with a quiet nod.

It was the sure trust Meili held for Natsuki Subaru.

He'd told her it was fine not to forget Elsa Granhiert. —If he really had found Elsa's book, he'd have agonized over it, hesitated, and in the end told her about it.

And even that hesitation wouldn't have been for his own sake, but out of consideration for both Meili and Elsa.

So this had to be the Book of the Dead of someone else entirely—but even so—

"—?"

Meili slowly lifted her face, noticing the sudden stiffening in the movement of the crimson scorpion perched on her head.

The crimson scorpion had turned itself around atop her head, its red compound eyes pointed at empty space—no, at the tower wall—and the moment Meili registered that, a ticklish shiver ran down the back of her neck.

It was a premonition, and not a good one at that. Sensing the faintest change in the air against her skin, both Meili and the crimson scorpion's awareness picked up on an unpleasant presence somewhere beyond the tower, out past the dunes.

"This is... no way!"

The instant a suspicion crossed her mind, Meili—still cradling Petra—slipped the crimson scorpion with one hand into the collar of her clothes. Right then, something came spilling over from beyond the wall like a pot boiled down too thick and sloppily overflowing—no, it wasn't anything so vague as that.

She could tell that a terrifying power—the power of a Witch—was closing in.

That same thing which had once blown Natsuki Subaru and Rem all the way to the empire in the south—it was coming.

"You've—got to be—kidding me...!"

That other time, the Divine Dragon Volcanica, who'd been atop the tower, had met the Witch's power head-on.

But right now, for whatever reason, the Divine Dragon had left the tower together with Al, and there was no one anywhere who could do anything about this. If they collided with the Witch's power without the Divine Dragon, there was no way it would end with something as cute as just getting blasted somewhere far away.

In other words—

"This is... the end, here?"

Meili let out a breath at the premonition of such an anticlimactic ending, the words slipping out of her before she could stop them.

At the sound of her own sigh—one that seemed to admit to a hopeless reality—Meili felt a strange slackening, as if a weight had been lifted from her shoulders. And then she noticed that her arms had pulled Petra in even closer.

Unconsciously, she was trying to shield Petra. Against that—the power of a Witch, of all things.

"Onii-san, you idiot..."

Even Meili herself didn't know why exactly that curse had slipped out.

Still, as if to shut out the sound of her own feeble voice, Meili curled her body over Petra's. A shield of flesh this thin wouldn't do a bit of good. She thought, with a pang of frustration, that she should have eaten a lot more meat—

—And then, an instant before the Witch's power reached the tower, something cut in between.

"—————"

Just as before, what was happening beyond the wall remained unknown.

But light and sound tore straight through the tower's thick walls and blew Meili and Petra clean off their feet inside the room.

"Aaah!"

Meili screamed as her body, still holding Petra, was slammed against the floor and wall.

It was pure chance that she'd been near the wall and already lying flat on the floor, and it paid off. Had she not been, the two of them might have been thrown against the wall and hurt far worse.

Even so, her hair was a mess, her clothes were a mess, and the room was in a dreadful state with belongings scattered everywhere. But miraculously, it seemed neither Meili nor Petra had suffered any serious injury.

"What... just happened...?"

"Petra-sama! Meili-sama! Are you unharmed!?"

Blinking her hazy eyes, Meili pushed herself upright where she lay. Petra, limp in her arms, remained motionless, eyes still closed.

The instant she confirmed as much, Flam came dashing frantically into the room.

Flam, who had been carrying Garfiel and Ezzo down to the lower floor ahead of Meili and the others finding the Book of the Dead, softened her eyes a fraction upon spotting the two of them—then her face turned serious.

"It's the young master. The young master is holding it back."

"Holding back... what exactly...?"

"That would be..."

"—You mean the Witch of Envy?"

At the name spoken aloud, Flam's expression visibly went pale.

Seeing plain fear etched into the eyes of a woman who hadn't been shaken even this badly upon learning that Al had betrayed them and the Divine Dragon had turned against them, Meili offered a quiet "Sorry."

It was a natural reaction. Even Meili herself was only able to stay this composed because this was her second time.

Most people, simply from feeling the Witch of Envy's existence as something real, would find the blood in their veins turning to ice, rendering them unable to move.

"It's just that Emilia-oneesan and the others are way too special..."

Whether it was because their resolve had reached some final extreme, or simply because of the courage and grit they possessed.

Either way, demanding that of everyone was an unreasonable, impossible ask, Meili thought, rising to her feet while still supporting Petra.

"The Sword Saint's fighting out there right now, right?"

"—Yes. The young master is holding it in check. But... if even the young master can't push it back..."

"...If we stay here, we'll just get in the way."

She didn't know what caused the Witch of Envy's tantrums, but as far as Meili was concerned, both times it had thrown a fit, she'd been in the tower. She didn't want to think she herself was the cause of the Witch of Envy's rage—so maybe she should figure it just disliked people lingering too long in the tower?

The instant that thought crossed her mind, the crimson scorpion, having wriggled its way back out of her clothes, gave the nape of her neck a light poke with its pincer.

"Sorry, sorry—you were there both times too, huh."

"Meili-sama! Let's hurry and leave the tower. I'll carry Petra-sama—"

"—No, I'm fine. I'll carry Petra-chan myself, so Flam-chan, could you go get Patrasche and the others ready?"

Shaking her head slowly, Meili turned down Flam's offer, hoisted Petra onto her own back, and rose slowly to her feet.

For a moment Flam's face showed hesitation over the decision, but she quickly nodded her assent and went ahead.

"Down to the spiral staircase! I'll get the Dragon Carriage ready."

"Okaay, thanks."

Once Flam's back, dashing off, had disappeared from sight, Meili too broke into a run after her.

Just before she went, she felt a pull of reluctance toward the belongings scattered across the room—especially the Book of the Dead, the cause of Petra's lost consciousness—but,

"No time for that right now."

Cheered on once more by the clicking sound of the crimson scorpion back atop her head, Meili held Petra tight so as not to drop her, and dashed out of the room.

If it really came down to it, she could ask Petra about the Book of the Dead's contents later—after Petra had safely, fully come to terms with that "someone's" life.

With that thought, Meili ran with all her might. Petra, carried on her back, let a single tear spill from behind her closed eyelids, framed by long lashes.

And then—

"I'm sorry... I'm... so sorry, Subaru..."

A feeble, spilling apology, one that seemed to tear straight through the girl's heart.

The girl's anguished plea, as though begging forgiveness from a nightmare, was swallowed up by the shockwaves of the impossible clash between the Sword Saint and the Witch of Envy echoing from outside the tower, and reached no one's ears.

"—Milk. Cold."

Aldebaran plopped himself down heavily on a counter stool and gave the shop's master that order.

It was the only tavern in Mirula, the town nearest the Augria Sand Dunes. The place had the exact run-down atmosphere of a saloon straight out of a Western, and there wasn't another customer in sight.

That said, jumping to the conclusion that the place simply wasn't popular would be a bit narrow-minded.

After all, Aldebaran had shown up at the hour caught between deep night and early morning—the very time when both morning people and night owls alike are at their sleepiest. It was hardly strange for a countryside tavern to be closed at such an hour.

And yet the owner had opened up shop anyway for a visitor like Aldebaran, showing up at such an hour. Truly a considerate, reasonable man—a model of what a service-industry proprietor ought to be.

"So yeah, I respect the hell out of how you run this place. Ain't no way I'd think about pulling something rotten on a guy like that. I'll pay up proper, too."

Propping his elbows rudely on the counter, Aldebaran shrugged at the bearded proprietor.

The shop seemed to double as living quarters, and the owner, roused in the middle of the night, was still in his nightshirt—not that Aldebaran had any complaints about him standing behind the counter in his sleepwear. What Aldebaran was actually protesting was the man's reluctance to actually get moving and pour the milk he'd ordered.

Though, even about that—

"Hey now, honestly, whatever you're saying just sounds like a threat to me, me."

"Well, yeah, I guess no matter how you slice it, that's fair."

"I mean, it's probably 'cause a one-armed guy hiding his face behind a helmet who suddenly barges in in the middle of the night just doesn't play well with the home crowd, right?"

"Look who's naming the biggest reason of all."

The Divine Dragon—a being revered above all else in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica—was talking to him through the wall like that; well, it couldn't be helped that it had shown up looking less approachable and more like a hooligan.

Of course, there was no way a body the size of "Aldebaran" could fit inside the shop, so the Dragon had been left waiting outside in the street, looking cramped and uncomfortable. Whether that did anything to ease the proprietor's peace of mind, though, was doubtful.

And so, a suspicious visitor in the dead of night plus a visit from the Divine Dragon—that was, roughly speaking, the thoroughly pitiable scene this tavern's owner had found himself caught up in.

"...Here's your milk."

Having taken a good long while to compose himself, the owner finally brought out the milk in a wooden mug. Accepting it with a "Thanks," Al gestured with his chin toward outside the shop.

"Not to push my luck, but could you also bring my buddy outside a barrel of water or milk, whichever? Skip the booze, though. No telling what'd happen."

"Huh, booze would've been fine too, wouldn't it? No way a legendary dragon can't hold his liquor."

"A mythical dragon getting taken down by booze—that's basically the plot. I need you not getting taken down for a while yet. ...Even if you're a useless partner for the time being."

"Tch, come on now, Master, do me a solid."

Clicking his tongue with a noise as loud as a gunshot, "Aldebaran"—who had been peering into the shop through the window—withdrew his head. Watching that happen, the owner hesitated a moment longer before, with an air of having made up his mind, hoisting a barrel onto his shoulder and heading outside.

It was only then that Aldebaran noticed one of the man's legs was a prosthetic, and he reflected that he'd gone and made a troublesome request. He thought he should have just carried the barrel himself.

He thought that, but—

"...Man, I am seriously, unbelievably exhausted."

His joints ached physically too, but compared to the sheer scale of his mental exhaustion, that was practically nothing.

As for just how worn down his mind was, he felt as spent as if he'd fought a powerful enemy some hundred thirty-two thousand and forty-four times in a row. —No, wait, that made it sound too generous. To be precise: he'd lost a hundred thirty-two thousand and forty-three times in a row, and only on the hundred thirty-two thousand and forty-fourth try had he finally managed to bring it to an end.

Roughly speaking, it could be said he'd succeeded—just as planned in advance—in holding down the Sword Saint, Reinhard van Astrea. It could be said, but Aldebaran felt not the slightest sense of accomplishment.

What he had instead was a strong sense of defeat, an overwhelming sense of helplessness, and just the tiniest bit of—

"...Relief, I guess. That I made it through."

Despite having magnificently pinned down each half of the twin greatest obstacles standing in the way of his goal, the fact that all he had in his chest was mere relief made him hopelessly small-minded.

Of course, calling it a magnificent takedown could only be read as irony. —At the very least, Aldebaran had already left everyone at the tower, Reinhard included, torn to shreds. And that number was only going to keep climbing, faster and faster, from here on out.

The most severe of those crises was, even now, still ongoing—

"—the Sword Saint versus the Witch of Envy, the battle of the century."

Aldebaran muttered this while lifting the chin-guard of his helmet, sipping milk from the mug.

At the edge of his vision, past the window from which the Dragon had withdrawn its head—there, filtering in and flickering into view, was the aftermath of a fierce, apocalyptic battle unfolding somewhere out beyond the sea of sand.

It was a battle that flashed white again and again, slicing through the night over the sand sea, spawning sandstorms visible even from this distance while unleashing peal after peal of thunderous roars.

No matter how far removed from combat, no matter how dull one's instincts, anyone could sense on a gut level that the sight before them was identical to what would unfold on the day the world ended.

That was why, despite the hour being some indeterminate stretch between night and dawn, the people of Mirula had gotten out of bed, stepped outside, and stood staring blankly out past the sand sea.

"—————"

That battle wouldn't end even once the sun began to rise and a new day began.

Unless someone drew a line somewhere, people would go on watching that battle endlessly. But it felt like, without any real trigger to make them stop, the townspeople would likely just keep standing there.

It wasn't a matter of lacking any sense of crisis, or feeling like it was somebody else's problem.

It was simply that when a person is bathed in a light too great for their own Vessel to hold, they go blind.

And while still blinded like that, it's too dangerous to start walking anywhere. So more often than not, someone left blind will instead follow after the voice or presence of the very light that blinded them.

Blindly, as if drawn in by the heat of a light strong enough to blind them in the first place.

So there was no reason to blame the people standing frozen in place—

"—Wow, wooow! Up close it's really, truly a Dragon, huh~! Honestly, I couldn't tell for sure until I got this close."

Suddenly, a high-pitched girl's voice sliced through the air, tense and bloodless as if in the middle of a war.

The voice—cheerful and bubbly to a jarringly inappropriate degree—came from outside the shop where Aldebaran sat guzzling his milk.

At that voice, Aldebaran slid his backside around on the stool and turned to face the entrance.

The next moment—

"—Are you in there, Aldebaran!? Get out here, now!"

A red-haired man came bursting through the tavern's entrance as though breaking it down, and the moment he spotted Aldebaran, he came stomping violently forward, right up in front of him.

Without pause, the man drew his sword with tremendous force and leveled its point straight at Aldebaran.

At such an overwhelming show of temper, Aldebaran raised a hand and said, "Whoa, whoa,"

"You're the one who told me to come out, and now you're the one charging in yourself—that's not gonna work, man."

"Don't go nitpicking my words. Forget that—that thing outside! That was, without a doubt, without a doubt...!"

"That thing outside?"

"Don't play dumb with me!"

The moment Aldebaran cocked his head, as if to say this was going nowhere, the man grabbed him by the collar, hauled him bodily to his feet, and shoved him out through the tavern's entrance.

There was the sharp sensation of a blade's point pressed against his back. He tried saying, "You don't gotta do that, I'm not going anywhere," but the man had no intention of listening.

Which, honestly, was fair enough. —From the man's perspective, he was standing before the very thing he'd longed for.

"Oh, Al-sama, you're out. ...Why exactly do you look like a hostage?"

"Guy's got way too much blood rushing to his head, apparently. Don't go provoking him either—I'm the one in danger here."

"Oh come oon~, don't say that. Al-sama, you don't die no matter what happens to you~"

So said the girl waving her hand cheerfully at Aldebaran as he stood there with a sword to his back—a girl in a maid outfit with a Japanese-style flair, done up in shades of red.

The girl, with her cat-like, whimsical air, said it all so lightly. Aldebaran thought the reality of the situation was rather less lighthearted than that, but he didn't say so out loud.

It'd be pointless to say anyway, and she especially was someone who could never be allowed to know.

In any case—

"Man, the tight spots just keep on coming, huh, me."

"Don't act like it's got nothing to do with you, me."

Aldebaran shot that back with a heavy sigh at the Divine Dragon, who looked down on the whole scene from its high perch of a head, laughing all the while like it found the situation entertaining.

Then he turned his whole body around—

"Man, what a reliable bunch I've got."

—"Aldebaran," who had seized the Divine Dragon's Dragonhusk.

—Heinkel Astrea, the kingdom's self-professed (and universally agreed-upon) freeloader.

—Yae Tenzen, the "Crimson Cherry Blossom," the woman shinobi who had once targeted the life of the "Sun Princess."

—Those were the assembled "accomplices" of Aldebaran, who now stood against the entire world.

—Sixteen.

In the campaign to bring down the Sword Saint, Reinhard van Astrea, the existence of his father was, quite literally, a vital weak point—one that could serve as a trump card.

Setting aside the question of feelings—whether Aldebaran actually wanted to use that trump card—failing to prepare it in the first place would have been unforgivable negligence. So he'd gone and won the man over properly.

Heinkel had been at rock bottom of his own despair, for reasons different from Aldebaran's, but as long as one didn't botch how the conversation was framed, he still had enough sense left to actually listen.

Aldebaran and Heinkel, despite belonging to the same camp, had never exactly been on friendly terms—but the man's desire was consistent and easy enough to understand.

A single-minded love, one might call it. —For a man whose very core was built out of something Aldebaran both longed for and deeply loathed at once, the promise of a reward after cooperating had worked wonderfully.

Though, of course—

"—You promised me the Dragon's Blood! And here, right in front of me, is the very Dragon itself! And you're still telling me to wait!?"

Aldebaran had already pictured him bellowing exactly that, spittle flying, eyes bloodshot.

Heinkel closed in with a temper fierce enough to look like he might strike at any second, his sword's point aimed at "Aldebaran"—who lay obediently in the street like a house dog.

To be precise, the blade's point was aimed at the Dragon's chest—nothing less than the heart pulsing within the Dragonhusk, the very organ that stored the Dragon's Blood.

—Dragon's Blood brought fertility to the land, and could heal even the worst of afflictions in an instant.

So the legend passed down since ancient times in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica told it, said to be one of the three great treasures the Divine Dragon had bestowed upon the kingdom when the Three Great Heroes drove back the Witch of Envy long ago.

And it was said to be the single drop of heart's blood from the final beat of a powerful Dragon's heart—a thing now, with most Dragons having departed the earthly world, obtainable through legitimate means only by extremely limited methods.

And now, the possibility of obtaining that Dragon's Blood lay right there before him. —That fact alone was enough to sear the soul of Heinkel Astrea, a man who had chased after a miracle for over ten years, without end.

"At last—at last I can save Louanna...!"

"...That your wife's name?"

"That's right. My wife's... Louanna, who has been sleeping and sleeping without end—the Dragon's Blood should be able to wake her. That's why I cooperated with you!"

"Yeah, I know how much you've contributed, and I give you credit for it. Fact is, having you as an insurance policy is exactly what let me hold your kid down."

"Then—!!"

Calling it a back-and-forth argument would be too generous; it was more accurate to say Heinkel's feelings had run so far ahead of him that no reasonable discussion was possible.

Quietly updating his matrix even here, Aldebaran tried to pick out his next words—what could he say to calm Heinkel down—when—

"Uhh~, sorry to interrupt your little chat. I've got something I wanna ask real quick~"

"What is it, maid! I'm having an important talk with this guy right now—"

"That thing crackling and flashing and booming out there over the dunes—that wouldn't happen to be Heinkel-sama's son, would it?"

"—Ah?"

The girl—Yae—swayed her hair, tied up in a shade halfway between vermilion and pink, and pointed out past the sea of sand, where the world-upending battle was still going, without pause, even now.

Heinkel, whose attention had apparently been consumed entirely by the Divine Dragon he'd dreamed of for years, blinked his blue eyes at the raging battle.

"...Hey, what the hell is that? Whoever's fighting out there—"

"Just what you'd expect—the Sword Saint and the Witch of Envy."

"—! Wh—why... why the hell has it come to that!?"

Eyes wide with shock, Heinkel glared out at the sand sea where his son and the Witch collided. His shoulders trembled violently, and then—

"This isn't what I was told! Use me to stop him—that was the condition... make him stop, now, right now! The Witch of Envy? That's insane, that's...!"

"Sorry, but I can't grant that request. Your reward was the Dragon's Blood, old man. Stopping this here goes against the original terms. Can't give you both."

"Gh, guh...! Then...!"

"Then what?"

"—Uh."

Heinkel's rage flared, but no further words followed, and his eyes darted about.

Aldebaran felt nothing but pity at that weakness of will in Heinkel. —Aldebaran sympathized with Heinkel.

Because he too was a mud doll toyed with by fate, saddled with a family too large for what he could bear.

Unaware of what was going on in Aldebaran's heart, Heinkel's sword point rose and fell, over and over again, and then—

"Heinkel-sama, I absolutely do not want to get dragged into whatever mess you're making, so could you please stop dithering and throwing a tantrum like that?"

Unable to stand watching any longer, Yae ended up hitting him with a single, coldly withering remark.

At those words, Aldebaran put a hand to his forehead, and Heinkel's eyes went wide with fury as he glared at her. Yae took that glare head-on, smiling as she tilted her head.

Then, with eyes that, unlike the smile on her lips, held no trace of amusement at all, she fixed her gaze on Heinkel.

"You agreed to this once already, so wobbling and whining about it afterward is just bad sportsmanship. If the thing you want is in two places at once, obviously you can't reach for the other one while your hand's already stretched toward the first, right? You're not a child."

"—! No matter how old he gets, that brat's still a brat!"

"Ugh, there's just no talking to him~. —Al-sama?"

Yae looked over at Aldebaran, clearly fed up with the composure-less Heinkel. At the meaning behind her narrowed eyes, Aldebaran shook his head.

Yae didn't know the meaning of holding back. She was a woman who had never learned any method besides killing.

So—

"...I'm counting on you, me."

"You got it."

The next instant, "Aldebaran's" forearm swung down and struck Heinkel—still glaring daggers at Yae—straight from above, pounding him flat into the ground.

"Whoaa," Yae exclaimed, eyes going round as she got hit by a tremendous gust of wind, and there in front of her, the finished picture was Heinkel, face-down and half-buried in the ground.

"Sorry, old man, but I still can't give you the Dragon's Blood. Right now, I really can't afford to have the Divine Dragon's heart stopped."

"You're acting all cool about it, but isn't Heinkel-sama dead? That hit just now looked like it'd put even me at death's door."

"He won't die. I didn't... hold back, exactly, but he won't die. I always figured the old man was unusually tough, but huh, so this is why."

"...I haven't really confirmed this properly, but what's the trick behind this Divine Dragon-sama here talking exactly like Al-sama?"

"I'll explain later. Wasting time right now would be a shame."

Answering Yae's request for an explanation with that, Aldebaran crouched down beside the buried Heinkel and reached to sheathe the man's drawn sword. —That was when it happened.

"Oh."

His outstretched wrist was suddenly seized, and Aldebaran held his breath. The one who'd done it was Heinkel—who'd just taken a blow from the Dragon that apparently hadn't been held back at all.

Heinkel's eyes weren't quite focused, but his face bore an expression of desperate, fearsome intensity as he glared at Aldebaran. Sensing the meaning behind that gaze, Aldebaran let out a breath and said,

"I'll keep my promise about the Dragon's Blood. And I won't let Reinhard get swallowed up by the Witch of Envy either. —So, well, just trust me on that."

"—————"

It wasn't as though Heinkel had believed Aldebaran's answer with his whole heart. But seeing him let his head drop limply back down at those words, Aldebaran freed his wrist from the man's grip.

Then, glancing over at Yae, who'd been raising her heel to deliver Heinkel one more blow,

"I'm tired, and I'm down an arm—mind carrying the old man for me?"

"Do I actually get a choice in that? If I do, I'm saying no."

"You don't. It's an order. You carry him."

"Tch~, Al-sama, you demon~. Fiiine, got it."

Sticking out her tongue with a "blegh," Yae crouched down and started digging the buried Heinkel out. While she worked, Aldebaran glanced over at "Aldebaran."

"Did he say anything, about the old man?"

"Ah? Oh, nothing much. Just that he's healthy and tough as hell."

"...Being tough and healthy with that guy's position and personality just means a life packed full of suffering. Poor bastard."

Sorry things had turned out this way, but Aldebaran hoped Heinkel would go on living as strongly as he could manage.

His circumstances were plenty rough, sure, but Heinkel's misfortune wasn't the very worst in the whole world either. Not that Aldebaran expected anyone to actually listen to such a stock, boilerplate consolation.

"Besides, whatever relationship might've let that kind of comfort land probably just ended, anyway."

He had no right to feel sorry about that, nor any claim to wish he could make up for it.

Aware of that much, Aldebaran cut off any further expression of feeling. Instead, he called out to the girl still at work, "Yae,"

"Money."

"That's the worst possible thing to hear out of context. He~re."

Yae rummaged in her robes and tossed him a leather pouch, which Aldebaran caught. Pulling a single gold coin out of it, he flicked it with his finger—

"This covers the trouble too. Keep it."

So saying, he had the tavern's owner—who'd watched their whole exchange in silence—take it.

There was an empty water barrel beside "Aldebaran"—the man had properly gone through with the order after all. He'd probably put the poor guy through a great deal of stress, but at least he'd reunited with his accomplices and gotten a bit of a breather.

"That was my money, though."

"Quit griping. You just got here, but can you move right away?"

"Shinobi training is out of its mind. They make you run without sleep or rest from the imperial capital all the way to a village half a month away, with nothing but a single bamboo tube of water."

"Your crazy shinobi stories never miss."

She probably meant to say that, having gone through hell like that, she wasn't about to buckle from something this small—but what actually got through to Aldebaran was just fresh confirmation of how insane shinobi were, and the exhausted memory of having been thoroughly worked over by the single most insane shinobi in the whole empire.

In any case—

"W-wait, hold on! ...No, please wait."

The owner called out, stopping Aldebaran and the others just as they were about to leave the spot.

Given a motley crew like this, on top of the violent blow the Dragon had just dealt, it must have taken real courage to even speak up—but the owner swallowed hard as Aldebaran and the others turned to face him,

"That thing... the calamity happening at the Pleiades Watchtower—could you please stop it? You're the Divine Dragon Volcanica-sama, aren't you!?"

"Ah..."

At the owner's plea, Aldebaran fiddled with the seam of his helmet's fittings with a finger and sighed.

Looking around, he could see the people of Mirula, keeping their distance, all turning eyes full of desperate hope this way—no, at "Aldebaran."

In their eyes were fear and unease, but at the same time, awe and hope as well.

Seeing the legendary Divine Dragon Volcanica up close, they thought—surely he would put a stop to that cataclysmic battle raging far off in the distance.

But—

"Sorry. Right now, stopping that isn't my job to do."

"—!"

"The Sword Saint will stop it through sheer stubbornness if nothing else, but even so, there's no guarantee the shockwaves won't reach all the way out here. You'd best leave town for a few days."

"A few days... how many, exactly..."

Given an answer different from what he'd hoped for by the Divine Dragon he'd pinned his trust on, the owner still didn't give up, and pressed on with more questions, searching for some reason that might settle the unease stirring in their hearts.

Even granting they should leave town, for how long?

The answer was—

"—Seven days."

"—————"

"In seven days, I'll settle it. —In seven days, I'll remove the cause of the world's ending."

Having declared as much, Aldebaran turned his back on the owner, on the people of Mirula, and started walking.

Yae, carrying the unconscious Heinkel on her back, followed behind him, and "Aldebaran," rising with a heavy, resounding noise, began walking as well.

Aldebaran, who had made an enemy of the entire world, moved forward together with his accomplices.

That those feet must never, under any circumstances, stop.

"I'll keep going. —So that I can be me."
