Side Story: "Ram and the Night Study Session"

"...Barusu, you write pretty characters."

At Ram's remark, the sound of a pen gliding across paper — which had continued unbroken until then — stopped.

The boy sitting at the desk right beside her — Subaru — looked up at Ram with a startled expression.

"What?"

"No, I mean, what, where'd that come from all of a sudden? Not that I want to get scolded or anything, but getting praised out of nowhere by you throws me off way more than I expected."

"It's nothing. I only said what I thought. Setting aside the fact that you still stumble sometimes since you're mid-way through learning, the way you actually form the characters is decent. You write surprisingly pretty characters."

"The second you tacked on 'surprisingly,' the compliment lost half its value!"

In response to what was, in Ram's own way, praise, Subaru ended up looking up at the ceiling with a dissatisfied air. Still, he quickly perked back up with a "heh," laughing it off.

"Never mind the 'surprisingly' part, heh heh, so that's it. Hearing my handwriting's nice, honestly, that makes me happy. Guess it wasn't for nothing that I went to calligraphy class as a kid and quit almost immediately."

"Also... no, never mind. It's nothing."

"What is it?! Stuff you start to say and then swallow is usually something important, so don't hold back, just say it! If it's more praise, I'm accepting compliments all day long."

Subaru pointed a raised thumb at himself, striking an affected pose, and Ram gave a deep nod.

"Right, I'll tell you, then. Praise properly when you produce good results. Scold you when you do something wrong, and be careful about how treats are given. ...I was putting into practice something from a book I read once."

"That sounds an awful lot like how you train a dog, doesn't it!?"

"In the sense that your manners leave something to be desired, I suppose it's much the same, isn't it?"

"I got all happy for nothing—!"

As Subaru reeled backward dramatically, the back of his chair let out a loud creak.

A simple bed and a plain, unadorned desk — this was the servant's private room assigned to Natsuki Subaru. The scarcity of personal belongings owed to how recently he'd started working here.

What repeated night after night in this bare room, entirely unconnected to anything romantic, was "learning to read and write."

"Barusu, you got it wrong again. Honestly, stop making the same mistake over and over."

"Ow—"

With the tip of the wand in her hand, Ram gave a light flick to the hand Subaru held his quill pen with. He dropped the pen, and rubbing the back of his reddened hand, glared reproachfully at Ram.

"H-hey, that's way too spartan! I'm the type who grows more from praise than scolding!"

"If you want to be praised, produce results. Then Ram will grudgingly give you proper praise."

"Don't do it grudgingly! Well, fine, never mind that... but could you go a little easier on the lessons? I still can't even tell the characters apart properly."

"Insufficient effort."

Ram cut off Subaru's excuse-laden words without mercy.

Still, Subaru shrugged and sighed, as though he'd known that would be her answer.

"That's what you say, Nee-sama, but honestly, you don't know how hard it is to encounter a foreign culture until you actually try it yourself. It's not just learning to write — I've got to learn the job too."

"...Fine. Go on."

"What I'm saying is! If you're going to call me under-studied, then surely you, Nee-sama, must be exceptionally gifted in understanding and mastering this sort of thing yourself, right?"

At Subaru rattling this off quickly, Ram furrowed her brow, wondering what he was getting at. Then Subaru smirked, satisfied he'd made his point, and pulled something out of the desk drawer. It was—

"Ta-daa! Behold, the fifty sounds of hiragana!"

"...And this is?"

What Subaru had produced was a large sheet of paper that spread across the whole desk. Densely packed across it were rows upon rows of unfamiliar shapes she'd never seen before.

"It's the script used where I'm from, this thing called 'hiragana.' It's what you'd call the 'I-script' over here."

"You mean the official script of the Kingdom of Barusu?"

"Could you not with a country name that sounds like it only exists inside my own head!? A guy who got so carried away with his worldbuilding he even invented a whole writing system — that's gonna end up as some pathetic embarrassing chapter of my past!"

"There's a fairy tale called 'The Solitude King' among the ones used in our textbook. It's the spitting image of your current state, Barusu. It's quite funny, so look forward to it."

"That's a spoiler I really didn't need!"

As if the title alone let him imagine just how brutal the story's content must be, Subaru's face took on a pained look. Paying him no mind, Ram ran her eyes over the characters Subaru had called hiragana.

There seemed to be some regularity to them, yet also none at all. Each had its own peculiar shape, and to Ram's eyes they looked like nothing so much as a child's mischievous doodle of snakes.

"So? What is it you want Ram to do, showing her the script of this Kingdom of Barusu?"

"Well, I figured you're probably bored while I sit here practicing my writing anyway. So how about, just to kill time, you try encountering a foreign culture too? And while you're at it, I want you to learn how hard it is to learn a script, and cultivate a little sympathy for me."

At Subaru's proposal, whose real motive in the second half was fairly transparent, Ram dropped her shoulders in exasperation.

What an elaborately roundabout way to try to needle her.

"I don't see any reason Ram would need to accept that proposal."

"How about, it'd prove your point's legitimate? If you can pick it up quick, then sure, I'll admit that 'insufficient effort' crack from before was fair. From then on, I won't argue with your teaching methods, I'll just work like a plow-horse and learn obediently. But — if you can't—"

"If I can't?"

"You've gotta be nicer to me! Rem too! That's the deal! This part's important!"

Subaru made his case with his chair groaning under him, and Ram looked at him the way one might regard a troublesome livestock animal. Subaru quailed a little under that gaze, but even as he shrank back, he didn't withdraw his demand.

Not that she was worn down by that look of his, but Ram sighed as if there were nothing else for it, and said,

"Being around you, Barusu, the number of times I sigh goes up day by day."

"I hear that a lot."

"Yes, that's an accurate assessment. ...Hand it over."

From Subaru, who had thrown caution to the wind and puffed out his chest, she snatched away the paper covered in "hiragana." Skimming over it, it did seem compatible in some way with the official I-script.

"Is the order of the characters similar to the I-script?"

"Roughly, yeah. But I'll give you the full rundown anyway. I want this to be a fair fight, after all."

Somewhere along the way this had turned into a contest, but once it was a contest, Ram had no intention of losing. No matter who the opponent, achieving total victory in any contest was Ram's creed.

She listened as Subaru explained the hiragana, pointing at each character, hammering the general layout and order into her head, memorizing shapes and stroke order while guessing at how each form had come to be.

It didn't take all that long before Subaru finished explaining his so-called fifty sounds. Then, twisting his mouth into a grin, he told Ram, "No handicaps now,"

"Your time limit's until today's study session ends. At the end, I'll give you a quick test question, and it comes down to whether Ram can answer it or not."

"Fine, I'll take you up on it. I'll make you study like a plow-horse, and let Ram take it easy for a while."

"Heh heh heh, I'm gonna turn that face so sure of victory into a crying face!"

Making a remark that sounded thoroughly small-time, Subaru threw himself back into concentrating at his desk. Watching him out of the corner of her eye, Ram settled onto the bed and set to work mastering the hiragana.

—It struck her, suddenly, that it had been a long time since she'd spent time on studying like this.

"Barusu, where did you study your letters?"

"Whoa, that's out of nowhere right when I just started concentrating. If you're trying to rattle me, it's not gonna—"

Glancing back at Ram, Subaru cut himself off.

Perhaps the reason he didn't continue with his usual banter was that Ram's expression, lit dimly by the light of the room, carried a faint air of wistful nostalgia.

Falling silent for a moment at that profile of hers, Subaru scratched roughly at his head.

"Where, huh — that's actually a tough one to answer. I think 'my parents taught me at home' is probably the most accurate answer. Honestly I don't really remember studying it in kindergarten."

"Kindergarten?"

"It's a school that only gathers little kids, really little kids. Basically a place where you play, eat, and sleep, but yeah, I guess it's also a place that teaches you the importance of acting as a group while you're still a brat."

The height Subaru indicated with his hand was roughly desk-height or lower. It seemed to be a school for children around four or five years old — and Ram, too, had attended a similar place back in her home village.

"A school, hm. Did you get to attend one too, Barusu?"

"I guess, yeah. I went properly through all of compulsory schooling... but once it became a matter of my own choice, I kind of drifted off it a bit. And while I've just been drifting further and further off ever since, well, here I am now."

Subaru lowered his eyes, showing the air of someone talking about something hard to say. Seeing this, Ram judged that pressing him any further on the subject would be cruel.

Even from what he'd just said, she could glean that Subaru's original standing had been fairly substantial.

Ram's guess was that Subaru hailed from the wealthy class of some small nation outside the Four Great Nations.

The Four Great Nations divided the world among themselves, but there were also, scattered here and there, a number of small nations that belonged to none of the four — and she'd heard that many of these lands had cultivated cultures all their own.

Subaru's unusual appearance, his explanations of a foreign culture Ram had never once heard of — it was all a bit too elaborate to be pure invention. He was probably a runaway from home, or something along those lines.

This "hiragana" now in her hands only lent further weight to Ram's theory.

"So how about you — how long have you been working for Roswaal, anyway?"

"Ram and Rem first met Roswaal-sama when we were still eight years old... so that makes it nine years ago. We've been in his service for nearly ten years now."

"Huh, nine years already... wait, eight plus nine — does that make you seventeen? Same age as me?"

"I wonder just what kind of surprise that's supposed to be."

"I mean it as, 'you look young for your age, Nee-sama.' But seriously, though. If Ram's seventeen, that means Rem is too, right? That's surprising... I thought you two were younger than me."

"Calling someone younger than you Nee-sama — you really are a sinful one, Barusu. Though for that matter, Ram was mistaken too. Given how restless you are, I took you for someone much younger."

This wasn't teasing — it was her honest opinion.

Even his features, setting aside the sharpness of his eyes, often came across as childish. Above all, the flippancy of his words and behavior did nothing to suggest the age of seventeen, an age at which one ought to have settled down somewhat.

Though, when it came to a mismatch between appearance and age, there were plenty of others in this mansion who fit that description just as well.

"Still, eight years old? You've been in his care since real young, huh. Did you get your parents' proper consent for that... or is going into service around that age just normal over here?"

"It's not unusual to leave one's parents from a young age. Though in Ram and Rem's case, it was for a somewhat different, special reason."

"Special, meaning?"

"It's simple, really. Ram threw a fit, insisting she wouldn't go unless it was together with Rem."

"Ahh, is that so."

Though it had only been a short time, Subaru — who had come to know something of the relationship between Ram and Rem — nodded, looking satisfied with the explanation.

Ram was well aware that, across the board of household duties, she placed a burden on Rem. And that the fact the mansion's daily life managed to hold together at all was thanks to Rem making up for what Ram lacked.

So she had no intention of nagging Subaru into correcting his assumption.

After all, she didn't expect anyone to understand the relationship between herself and Rem, aside from Roswaal, a colleague currently away from the mansion, and a handful of other close acquaintances.

Not, at least, until it became clear what kind of presence Subaru would go on to become within the mansion.

"...It's about time we stopped wasting time chatting, I suppose."

Above the room's door, the color of the magic crystal was shifting toward a deep yellow, indicating the hour when the night ran deepest. Today's study session was about to come to an end.

"Yeah. Well then, good work today. Looking forward to tomorrow too, please and than—"

"I could make you regret assuming tomorrow will simply arrive as a matter of course, you know?"

"My bad! It was a joke! Alright, it's showdown time. Here's the question — read what's written here, and then give your answer in 'hiragana,' if you please!"

As he spoke, Subaru drew a sheet from the stack of blank practice paper and presented a line of hiragana labeled "mondai" ("question") in I-script beside it. In exchange, he collected the reference chart from Ram's hands.

"Right, I've taken back the reference chart. Now you're as good as stripped bare and left out in the wilderness. Kuku, go ahead and answer it if you can! And once you get it wrong, starting tomorrow you and your sister are both gonna teach me the I-script real nice and gently—!"

Getting worked up over his own petty logic, Subaru sneered in anticipation of watching Ram struggle.

Ram, meanwhile, paid no mind to Subaru's petty villain act, and simply read over the hiragana question. She observed the writhing cluster of characters for a moment—

"...Done."

Taking up the quill pen, Ram wrote her answer in smooth strokes beside the section marked "kaitou" ("answer") in I-script. Once finished, she thrust the answer sheet right in front of Subaru's face.

"—!"

"The question was 'the names of the sisters who live in the mansion.' The answer is 'Ramu to Remu' — 'Ram and Rem.'"

"No way, seriously!? That's perfect! You did that in that short amount of time, for real!?"

Comparing Ram's answer against the question, Subaru sank to the floor in shock. At his reaction, Ram felt certain her guess had paid off correctly.

Given Subaru's personality, it had been easy to imagine he'd make up a question related to the mansion. From there, narrowing down the characters she needed to memorize to candidates like Emilia, Beatrice, and the two of them made the memorization simple enough.

"In other words, what matters most is being resourceful. Barusu, you haven't forgotten our promise, have you?"

"W-wait... there's gotta be some kind of trick to this. I'm just too dumb to see it, but you must be cheating somehow—guh!"

Flicking a finger against the forehead of Subaru, who was being a sore loser about the whole thing, Ram headed for the door with a yawn. Opening it, she glanced back to see Subaru rubbing his reddened forehead, and said,

"I look forward to tomorrow. Have a nice dream. Ram certainly will."

"You demon..."

Subaru's frustrated face disappeared beyond the door. That last bit of sour-grapes grumbling came with such perfect timing that Ram nearly burst out laughing despite herself.

"Now Barusu should start taking this a little more seriously, at least."

Having ruthlessly outmaneuvered him and, in doing so, spurred on Subaru's motivation for tomorrow onward, Ram was thoroughly pleased with herself.

She made her retreat from the room before any grumbling could reach her ears, looking forward already to the sour face Subaru would make tomorrow.

"Still, though..."

There was, however, one thing that gave her pause. It was about the "hiragana" Subaru had written.

That "hiragana" — she was fairly sure that once before—

"...I feel like I've seen it somewhere before."

That murmur fell away into the hallway, reaching no one, and faded out — slipping, too, from Ram's own memory as something of no real importance.

The End

  • "孤独の王様" rendered per the provided glossary as "The Solitude King."
  • Left "hiragana," "kaitou," and "mondai" in romanized form since the text explicitly treats them as foreign loanwords Subaru is introducing, with in-line glosses provided by Subaru/the narration itself.
