Arc 2 Chapter 23: "A Temporary Contract"

Having driven even Emilia from his room, Subaru's fourth lap began with a bleak, dismal start.

On the morning of the first day, after wounding Emilia with a thoughtless remark, he'd faced a reunion with Roswaal, back from his return to the manor. Roswaal's mocking, unreadable manner hadn't changed, but Subaru felt he was being looked over with an unusually appraising eye, and it left him uneasy. Whether that was unique to this time around, or had been the same in every loop before, Subaru had no way of knowing now.

The words they'd exchanged were few, and little of the content had stuck in his memory. He thought he recalled being told he'd be treated as a guest, though. Something about staying as long as he liked — conveniently generous words, or so he vaguely remembered.

That could be called exactly what Subaru had hoped for, or it could just as easily be called something that no longer mattered at all. If he wandered out of the manor now, he'd certainly be killed to keep him quiet. And yet, if he was simply judged to be dead weight to Roswaal's faction as things stood, a future of being ground into mincemeat before long was just as plainly waiting for him.

Go or stay, it was BAD END either way. It felt like saving his game in a spot where the incoming BAD END couldn't be avoided. And it was an autosave, no less — what an absurd, unreasonable setting.

Cursing under his breath like that, Subaru spent the whole day curled up idly in a corner of his room. Since coming to this other world, this was surely the first day he'd done absolutely nothing. Come to think of it, Subaru was now living the "kept man" existence he'd once longed for. Meals were provided without his having to work, and no one complained even if he shut himself up in his room. Here he was, in another world, and yet living the same shut-in life as back in his own. He should have been looking forward to it, yet he felt not the faintest stirring of the satisfaction he'd once craved.

Unable even to choose the appealing option of drowning himself in sleep, Subaru couldn't manage to sleep on the guest room bed at all, and instead spent his time doing nothing, head in his hands.

In his hand he gripped a quill pen backward, its tip stained again and again with blood that had dried after each dip. Whenever drowsiness threatened to close his eyelids, he stabbed the back of his hand with it, forcing his consciousness back awake through pain.

Though he barely stirred on the bed, Subaru's breath, drawn through his mouth, came rough and fast. His pulse beat quick and shallow under unbroken tension, and his eyes roved restlessly around the room, seized by constant paranoia that something might be wrong.

He had already died three times, and his days at Roswaal Manor had now entered their fourth iteration. By Subaru's experience, the number of deaths within the first day of his summoning had been three. Having broken through that one day on his fourth attempt, a fourth death was now, for Subaru, uncharted territory.

—If he died here, now, there might be no returning next time.

He could find no way to avoid death. And yet, he didn't want to die. But going on living here was unbearably painful, unbearably hard, all the same. All that Subaru, on the verge of losing his mind, could manage was to arrive at a conclusion that could only be called the product of a mind that had already lost itself.

Namely: doubt everything, resist everything, and cling to life with everything he had.

Already into the afternoon of the second day of the fourth week, the time he'd spent doing nothing diverged so wildly from any previous repetition that his memories of earlier loops were no longer reliable at all. He had no idea what might happen, and nothing that happened would be strange. And whatever did happen, it was bound to work only to Subaru's disadvantage.

Disillusioned with reality, despairing of his situation, resigned about the future — Subaru's heart had already broken, shattered. Now, all that remained was its wreckage, clinging on in an unsightly, futile struggle.

Day no longer felt like day, night no longer felt like night, even the passage of time had grown hazy, and the boundary between consciousness and unconsciousness shimmered like a heat mirage. Only the pain of the pen stabbing the back of his hand remained vivid, and the vividness of that sensation brought him a small, twisted gladness, so he gouged the wound open again, and again. Gouge. Gouge.

"—My, what a spineless face you've gone and made, I suppose."

A voice, sudden and out of nowhere, made him snap his head up as if struck. At the end of Subaru's gaze — glinting like some feral animal's — stood a girl leaning in through the doorway, hand on the knob, one he hadn't laid eyes on even once in this particular loop. Beatrice's visit — that was a first, across all four repetitions of this world. And at once Subaru's guard shot up.

An anomaly occurring only this time around — that could only mean, surely, that it would end up threatening his life somehow.

"...This time it's you?"

A low, rasping voice came out, startling even himself. More than two days of unbroken silence, his throat run through with nothing but ragged breath, had left it parched and cracked. More hostility than he'd meant to put into it came through, and the girl who found herself on the receiving end of that ill will head-on—

"In the space of a single day or two, how does one manage to rot this thoroughly? Humans truly are hopelessly foolish creatures, I suppose."

"I'm not in the mood for your lecture. —What'd you come here for?"

Beatrice sneered at Subaru's wretched state. Displeased by his lack of any verbal filter, she knit her brows and said,

"Nii-cha and that little girl asked Betty to come look in on your face, I suppose."

"Puck and... Emilia?"

"Your behavior's been strange ever since you woke up, so she suspects Betty might've done something to you when you first came to. Isn't that rude, I suppose?"

So Emilia — who by all rights should have been hurt by Subaru's thoughtless words — had nonetheless been worrying over his state of mind. She'd apparently reasoned that the cause of Subaru's sudden change might trace back, indirectly, to his contact with Beatrice just before, and had gone and appealed to Beatrice herself. Beatrice, who for some reason couldn't push back hard against Puck, had — with Puck doting hopelessly on his "daughter" and lending his support to the cause — reluctantly come to show her face to Subaru like this.

Emilia's concern — it left just the faintest trace of warmth on Subaru's ravaged heart. But her worry was hopelessly misdirected. The cause of Subaru's sudden transformation had nothing to do with Beatrice, and this, of all things, wasn't a problem anyone could solve no matter what they did.

"Got it. That's enough. I understand what you're saying, so just go now. You came to apologize. That's enough."

"And why exactly should Betty have to apologize? First we need to correct that point before Betty can even think about leaving, I suppose."

Waving a hand, trying to shoo her off carelessly, Subaru found Beatrice clinging on regardless. She pushed further into the room, dragging the hem of her long skirt as she moved—

"—Hm?"

Suddenly she wrinkled her nose and stopped in her tracks. Her profile, lovely enough when silent, turned this way and that, sniffing about the room the way a dog tracks something by scent. At length she fixed Subaru with a glare, thorough displeasure filling her eyes.

"It's not just that your face has gone gloomy and stale — you've gotten considerably more concentrated, I suppose."

"—Huh?"

"I'm talking about the smell. It's an awful stench, right up my nose. It might be wise not to see those twins for a while, I suppose."

Pinching her nose, waving a hand, Beatrice made a show of the foul odor. Not even noticing her gesture, Subaru's mind was racing to place the memory that matched the keyword "smell." A smell — right, someone had said something like that around the end of the third week, hadn't they—

"What is it that smells like, from me?"

Lifting his face, for the first time putting real feeling into his voice, Subaru pressed the question. In answer, Beatrice raised an eyebrow and crossed her arms.

"It's the Witch's smell, I suppose. Enough to make your nose crooked, isn't it?"

The word "Witch," murmured aloud, made a corner of his mind suddenly throb. He knew that word. He'd seen it — recently, hadn't he. It was—

"The Jealous Witch."

"In this world, if someone says 'Witch,' what else could it possibly refer to, I suppose?"

At Beatrice's faintly mocking tone, Subaru found himself leaning forward without thinking. He reached a hand toward her.

"Why do I smell like that, to you?"

"Who knows? Either the Witch has taken a liking to you, or else she's marked you as an enemy. Either way, someone singled out by the Witch for special treatment is nothing but trouble, I suppose."

"Special treatment from someone whose face and name I don't even know — that's a chilling thought."

Beatrice shrugged, her attitude making it plain, without words, that she found even further discussion of the topic distasteful. Faced with her stubborn refusal, Subaru fell silent, and began sinking into a sea of thought as he tried to piece together these scattered fragments of information.

The Witch. The Jealous Witch was a world-famous figure out of a fairy tale, hated and feared across the entire world, someone whose very name everyone shrank from speaking aloud. What knowledge Subaru had of her existence amounted to nothing more than what little was told in a story that barely qualified as a story at all.

Naturally, he had no memory of ever meeting her, nor any recollection of having had contact of any kind with her. For something like the Witch's Lingering Scent to have soaked into him, without his ever having experienced any such thing, was simply unreasonable. —Rem, too, he was fairly sure, had said something like that to him once.

If part of her excessive bloodlust traced back to that, then Subaru had, once again, earned someone's hatred over a fact he had no memory of at all. Since every single one of his supposed crimes was a false charge, his sheer bad luck at repeatedly getting killed was truly unrivaled.

Having confirmed that the hopeless fact was, indeed, hopeless, Subaru let out a long, weary sigh. Watching him do so, and apparently judging that the conversation had nowhere further to go, Beatrice swept a hand through her long hair,

"If there's nothing else, Betty's leaving now, I suppose. Betty will make sure to tell nii-cha that she properly came to see you."

"Wait a second."

He called out to her back as she reached for the doorknob, about to perform her "Door Crossing," stopping her mid-motion. Beatrice turned only her head to look back at him.

"You feel bad about it, right? About me?"

Subaru threw out the question with deliberate spite. It was, to some extent, a gamble — but his heart insisted that doing something was better than nothing, and he followed that instinct. To Beatrice, who was making a face like she found this distasteful, Subaru lightly slapped the sheets and pressed again,

"You. Feel bad. About me. Yes or no. Answer."

"Betty doesn't feel bad, I suppose."

"I'll tell Puck."

"Guh... Maybe, just the teeniest bit, perhaps Betty might feel something like that, I suppose."

Turning her whole body back toward Subaru, Beatrice crossed her arms and looked away with an air of superiority. Subaru looked her small frame up and down, then thought back over all the time he'd spent with her so far — and after some agonizing, he made his decision.

"If you want forgiveness, grant me one favor."

And that was:

"One night... no, until the morning of the day after tomorrow is fine. Will you protect me?"

For someone who looked younger than himself, begging like this was nothing short of shameless.

At the request Subaru put to her, Beatrice fell silent for a moment.

"That's awfully vague, I suppose. Is there some reason you're being targeted?"

A perfectly reasonable question in return. Beatrice, still eyeing Subaru askance, began pacing in a small circle around the room.

"To begin with, Betty wants no part of trouble being dragged into this manor, I suppose. This manor is an irreplaceable place to Betty — is that so hard to understand?"

"...I'm not planning to do anything myself. I just want to deflect whatever sparks come flying at me."

"And you can't even handle that yourself — leaving it to someone else, and still calling it such a fine ambition, I suppose."

"This time, I got no comeback for that."

Beatrice sighed at Subaru's bowed head. For a while after that, silence flowed through the room.

Head still down, Subaru was expecting to hear, before long, the sound of the door closing — the sound of Beatrice discarding his plea and retreating back to her forbidden archive. The moment he heard it would be the moment even the single, faint thread of hope that had barely begun to connect for Subaru snapped for good.

Filled with that kind of resignation, Subaru heard instead—

"Give me your hand, I suppose."

Beatrice had walked up beside the bed, her small hand extended toward him. Subaru, caught utterly off guard, could only stare. She took his right hand irritably, and, looking at the wound-covered back of it, scowled.

"How disgusting. Even a habit of self-harm — are you beyond all hope of saving, I suppose?"

"That line belongs to Roswaal's whole identity. ...Me, I'm just someone whose teenage angst leaked out a little too much. A chuunibyou case."

"I don't understand a word of that, but I understood that it deepens how hopeless you are, I suppose."

Sighing, Beatrice turned Subaru's right palm upward, hiding the wound on the back of his hand, and pressed her own small palm against it. Naturally, as if drawn to it, their fingers intertwined, hands clasped together.

"—Thy wish, I hereby accept. In the name of Beatrice, a contract is sealed here."

Solemnly, she declared this, and Subaru found himself at a loss for words. And then, from their clasped palms, an overwhelming heat surged through him. It traveled up his arm and spread through Subaru's entire body, striking his depleted vigor like a sudden jolt.

Bewildered by his body's sudden warming, Subaru looked up to find Beatrice glaring at him with eyes that seemed to say, from the bottom of her heart, how utterly reluctant she was about all this.

"A contract, even a temporary one, is still a contract. Once bound according to proper ritual, it's absolute, I suppose. Your incomprehensible request — I'll grant it, I suppose."

You may thank me, she added at the end, and though she stood below him, Beatrice looked at Subaru squarely with an unmistakably lofty gaze. Receiving that look, Subaru abruptly felt a wave of inexplicable emotion welling up, and turned his face away in a reflexive attempt to hold it back.

A surge of overwhelming feeling, a sensation beyond words. Not knowing how to handle something extended to him from such an unexpected quarter, Subaru shut his eyes against the warmth of the hand still clasped in his.

"Seriously. ...I almost got made to cry by a little girl."

"Don't call Betty a little girl. —Also, if you tell nii-cha about this, Betty will absolutely never forgive you, I suppose."

"Way too intense. That's some serious demon-mode energy right there."

Met with a gaze that carried nothing but genuine hostility, Subaru answered with a wry laugh. It was the fourth week's first smile — small, but real all the same.
