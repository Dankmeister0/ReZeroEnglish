Arc 9 Chapter 1: "O Prayer, Be Like a Cloud"

—The final stretch of time passed peacefully, astonishingly quiet.

"—My star."

Called to in a voice flat in tone, and yet carrying more love than it could possibly contain, Iris=Yorna's long lashes trembled as she looked up at the man beside her. It was nothing more than an ordinary word, one she felt she'd already been called by hundreds, thousands of times over the course of this miraculous meeting—and yet, in this moment, it felt special. No—to be precise, every one of those calls, taken in isolation, had been special. But the specialness of this one was different from all the others.

Because this call meant the end of this miraculous span of time.

"Yes, Your Excellency."

She met the gaze looking down on her, and from her own lips returned a call of love in kind. Her voice did not waver, and no weak, hesitant feeling made her flinch—she praised herself silently for that. At their parting long ago, Iris=Yorna had been unable to leave him with any words. But every time she looked back on that era after being reincarnated, she had thought, again and again, of what she would say if only she had the chance.

If only she had the chance, she vowed, never—absolutely never—would she leave behind the sniveling complaints of a weak woman.

"It is the reverse of that time, is it not. This time, it is I who go before you."

"'Tis so, I dare say. ...Revenge, is it? So even you have come to know the malice of the human world, Your Excellency."

"That is so. I have learned much. ...No, I was taught it—by you, first of all, and by the many others who walked alongside me."

"—"

Narrowing his long, narrow eyes, he—Eugard Vollachia—looked back on the old days and the people who had once been there, the light in his irises stirring gently, and Yorna felt her heart struck by it. He had always been a man who wore a stern expression in front of everyone but Iris. Being such a man, after Iris was gone, had he ever once been able to loosen the tension in his cheeks, in the furrow of his brow? And now, after he went on ahead to that place between this sky and this earth, would he—

"Do not worry, my star. I have been a man of fortune beyond all hope."

"—Ah."

"I met you, and you let me live. I walked with you, and was given blessing. I parted from you, and even the wrongs I committed, I have now, upon meeting you again, been given the chance to set right."

Eugard's words—the voice of her beloved, counting his blessings one by one—sweetly melted away the unease and fear inside Iris=Yorna, unraveling them as tenderly as if he were cherishing her.

In that ancient age, the loss of his beloved Iris had burned Eugard's body with rage, and the hatred he left behind had become a law of wrongdoing that triggered all too many tragedies. That law—the current Vollachia emperor, Eugard's descendant, known as the "Sage Emperor," had promised to abolish. Breaking the curse that bound the Wolf Humans and Mole Humans—that was Iris=Yorna's fervent wish, and Eugard's last regret in life. At last, this world could be given back to those in this age who had the right to live in it. To that end—

"That, my star, is the same for you."

At the sudden words of Eugard, who held her in his arms, Iris=Yorna caught her breath. She felt as though the inside of her heart had been cut open, precisely, as accurately as it could possibly be.

"Your Excellency... I... I—"

"Even if it was given to you in a form you never wished for, I can easily imagine that in whatever era you found yourself, you have lived weaving your bonds with all your heart. No doubt it is the same for you now, in the form you wear today. —See that through to its fullest."

It was something that gently took from Iris=Yorna—she who, being an unnatural existence unto herself, wandered in search of where she belonged—the choice to retreat into some easily understood way of being.

Eugard was always right. Not because he was wise, nor because he was strong. It was because he was a compassionate emperor, one who could think earnestly of whoever stood before him, revere them, and wish for their happiness. Called the "King of Thorns," feared more than anyone, Iris=Yorna's beloved emperor thought of her, revered her, and wished for her, with a love too vast to hold. That was why Eugard's words were always right, for Iris=Yorna. And that this rightness held true for others besides Iris=Yorna as well—

"—That, I have no choice but to prove with the whole of my remaining life, I dare say."

"Well done."

Eugard answered Iris=Yorna's resolute reply with a smile in his voice. At Eugard's manner of deliberately saying no more than that, Iris=Yorna narrowed her eyes slightly, then reached out and cupped his cheeks in her hands, gently bringing her lips close.

"—"

Their lips met—the man and woman who had each lost their lives in that era, who had achieved a reunion that should never have been possible, and now the distance between them was closer than anywhere else in the world.

"—"

That closest of contacts in all the world went on for a while, and a while, and a while longer, until at last the distance slowly opened again—and Iris=Yorna's eyes met Eugard's. And then—

"—My star, I love you." "As do I, Your Excellency. Forever, for all eternity, without fading, I adore you."

They kissed, exchanged their words of love like spells cast from the soul, and the last thing of all was the smile their lips formed. That was the true final curtain of the tale called "Iris and the King of Thorns"—a story that had begun in an age long past, and kept postponing its ending all the way to this present era.

—One night had passed since the settling of the struggle against the "Great Disaster" that had shaken the entire Vollachia Empire.

The threat of the Undead, who had run rampant across the empire, was driven back through the unity of the empire's subjects, who upheld the Iron-Blooded Code, and at their forefront stood the valiant figure of the Sword Wolf of Vollachia at its very pinnacle—Vincent Vollachia—who had raised the Sunlight Sword and clashed blades head-on with the enemy's leader.

Centered on the Imperial Capital, Lupugana, damage had reached Vollachia's other major cities as well—the Fortress City Garkla, the Iron-Blooded City Glarasia, the Demon City Chaosflame, and the Sea of Clouds City Mezoreia among them—yet none of them fell into utterly catastrophic ruin. And so, though great scars remained, what enveloped the Vollachia Empire now was a mood of victory.

Of course, with the future to consider, the leadership could not go on basking in that victory forever. Reports of damage came pouring in one after another from every region, along with countless matters those in command had to rule on—all of it shared among them, leaving them no time even to sleep. Among those reports, one piece of news had slipped in as if quietly smuggled through.

It was news of the death of a woman who had contributed greatly to the Vollachia Empire's victory in the fight against the "Great Disaster," and who had captivated so many with her blazing way of life—

When Emperor Vincent Vollachia heard that news of her death, in a room of the Fortress City now serving as a makeshift headquarters in place of the ruined imperial capital, he fell into a silence quite unlike himself—him, who always answered every matter instantly after only a moment's thought—and after keeping his face lowered for a while, he is said to have left behind only one word—

"—I see."

That alone, it is said, was all he left unsaid otherwise.

△▼△▼△▼△

With the battle against the "Great Disaster" concluded, people began moving to reclaim their former lives. Vollachia's land had been ravaged by an unprecedented calamity—an army of resurrected Undead—yet the people living upon that earth lifted their heads with resilience, rising one by one, and set to the work of rebuilding. At the sight of the empire's people carrying on like this, Petra Leyte could only feel overwhelmed.

"I don't really like the empire all that much, but..."

Murmuring so, Petra stood rooted in the streets of the Fortress City, her heart a tangle of complicated feelings. Most of the reason Petra couldn't bring herself to like the empire was her anger that it was the place that had forcibly dragged off Natsuki Subaru—someone dear to her like family, and the person she loved. Even setting that aside, when it came to like or dislike, Vollachia still leaned toward dislike for her—but even so, so that this country would not fall, Petra and the others had done their part and struggled with all their might. That the "Great Disaster" had been driven back, and the empire spared destruction, was something Petra, too, was glad of. Still, even so—

"—Subaru."

Speaking the name of the one she called "Subaru-sama" in front of others, honorific and all, Petra worried—in the truest sense, surely more than anyone—about him, who had worked harder for this empire than anyone, even more than the emperor himself. Because he was that kind, deeply compassionate Subaru, Petra couldn't help but worry and worry over just how much his heart must have been worn down in an empire so full of terrible things. And on top of a Subaru who already gave her plenty to worry about, the very last blow had been far too cruel.

"Priscilla Barielle-sama..."

That was the name of the woman who had carved a great, great wound into Subaru's heart—deep, and agonizing. Given her position, Petra had never once exchanged words with her. The chance had been there. Before the "Great Disaster" struck, when they had stood in the same camp heading into the decisive battle for the imperial capital, there had been ample opportunity to speak with her. And yet, Petra had deliberately avoided approaching Priscilla. That was because Priscilla, like Emilia, was one of the candidates in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's Royal Selection—an opposing party—and Petra had thought one ought not to grow overly familiar with a rival more than necessary.

And now Petra regretted it. Because she had never connected with Priscilla, Petra felt she had no right to mourn her—to mourn Priscilla Barielle's death—and so she could not stand beside Subaru and Emilia sharing the same feeling. She couldn't tell the two of them, hurting as badly as they were, that she understood how they felt. And that, to Petra now, was an unbearably frustrating thing—

"—Well, well, Miss Petra. All alone, are you?"

"Ah, Zikr-san."

Petra, who had been pressing her lips tight and holding tension in her cheeks, lifted her face at the call to find Zikr Osman there, his hair styled full and round. As one of the empire's Generals, Zikr had played an important role in the fight against the "Great Disaster." He never put on airs about his standing, and treated Petra as one human being to another—an easy man to like. Given how well he'd performed, Zikr would likely be relied on heavily even after the war, so it was unusual to find him alone in a place like this. As Petra blinked at him in surprise—

"The general reorganization of the troops has finished, so I've finally come down on my own two feet to confirm the damage. Even with reports reaching my ears, there's nothing like seeing it with one's own eyes."

"I see. Reorganization... I don't really understand the details, but thank you for your hard work."

"My thanks for the concern. Though what role I've managed to fulfill hardly bears comparison to those working so energetically—First General Goz, or First General Kafma."

"I don't think it's about who tried harder being greater."

Zikr, already looking somewhat abashed, grew even more so at Petra's remark. For her part, Petra reflected on her own comment—she recalled that just now, worrying over Subaru in her heart, she had compared his efforts to someone else's, to that sharp-eyed emperor's—and she reproached herself for it. It frustrated her that, however much she wanted to see things the way the people she loved did, she couldn't quite manage it.

"But the Generals really have it rough, huh. It hasn't even been that many days, and they already have to go flying off to other cities."

"Indeed. The regional 'Generals' are handling things, but the influence of a General personally showing their face can't be ignored—for the troops' morale, and also in the sense of keeping people in line."

"I get that soldiers would be happy to see someone they admire, but 'keeping people in line'...?"

"Sad to say, even in a situation like this—no, precisely because things are so dire—there is no shortage of people looking to exploit it for immediate gain, or to fulfill their own ambitions."

"—"

Zikr's brows drooped at the corners, and at his answer Petra lowered her round eyes. In truth, at the substance of Zikr's reply, Petra thought to herself, "Just as I figured," and accepted it.

Faced with the appearance of a great enemy, the Vollachia Empire had chosen to unite and stand against it. But once that national crisis—the "Great Disaster"—was gone, problems reverted from the nation's perspective back to the perspective of individuals. People would seize what they lacked, steal it, and in some cases try to force everything through by sheer strength. The Generals whose names Zikr had raised had gone out to serve as a deterrent against exactly that.

"First General Goz departed the moment he confirmed his wife, who had been in the imperial capital, was safe. First General Kafma left just as soon, upon accepting his promotion to First General."

"That's a really big deal, right? Everyone was surprised."

"In the empire, there are only nine posts for First General. In this last battle, several of those seats fell vacant... The emergence of a new First General can be called one of the few pieces of good news. First General Kafma understood that too, which is why he accepted the rank of First General this time, having declined it once before."

"Isn't there any story like that for you, Zikr-san?"

"For me, you mean?"

Watching Zikr praise the conduct of the Generals whose names had come up, Petra tilted her head and asked as much. She hadn't confirmed their fighting firsthand, so Petra didn't know just how outrageously the Generals had rampaged. But in the sense of having witnessed hard work up close, Zikr's own valiant efforts had been considerable too.

In the battle over the Fortress City, there had been those who watched the overall situation from command posts and issued orders to the whole, and there had been those who took up swords and kept clashing with the Undead alongside the troops. And the role Zikr had fulfilled was to connect those two, achieving coordination that was neither too much nor too little. It was unglamorous work, but without it, the whole might well have unraveled and collapsed.

"Since I and the other sisters were in the healers' corps, working behind the scenes, I feel that all the more."

"...That is a most gracious assessment, Miss Petra. From a lady's lips alone, such words make this Zikr Osman feel truly rewarded, to the depths of his heart."

"I'm not saying it lightly, or anything..."

"Of course, I know that. But I mean it in earnest as well."

Feeling as though she'd been brushed off, Petra tried to press further, but Zikr shook his head. Then he turned his round, lively eyes toward the sight of the city, and his cheeks relaxed.

"If it's a reward you mean, this sight, this outcome, is exactly that. When His Excellency the Emperor fled east, I happened, by chance of duty, to be in the walled city. Thanks to that turn of fate, I was granted the honor of attending him, and was able to be present here. I am a man of fortune beyond all expectation."

"...I don't get it. You should be praised more, Zikr-san."

"You don't understand? Then, Miss Petra, would you use that clever head of yours to picture someone dear to you?"

At the sudden suggestion, Petra let out an "Eh?" The one who came hazily to mind was Natsuki Subaru, who had occupied so much of her thoughts until just moments before. At Petra's reaction—having unconsciously done just as he asked—Zikr nodded deeply.

"That someone dear to you smiles, because of what you've done. Don't you think that alone is enough to feel richly rewarded?"

"That's... well, yes."

"It's the same thing. I am a General of the Vollachia Empire—the scope of the someone I picture is likely broader than the one you just thought of, Miss Petra. So this is enough for me."

Closing one eye and placing a hand on his chest, Zikr smiled at Petra as he said it. There was no strain in the way he said it, nor any sense that he meant to deceive her. And so Petra, too, quietly lowered her gaze, at a loss for words—

"—That said, His Excellency Vincent holds firmly to rewarding merit and punishing fault. It hardly does for a man of merit to speak so selflessly that it makes it harder for the troops to speak up for themselves."

So said a voice, cutting in from another direction in place of the now-silent Petra. Petra and Zikr turned toward it, and there, walking slowly toward them, was Serena. Serena Dracroy, a conspicuous white scar on her cheek and yet still not lacking a shred of her dignified beauty, had her acquaintance Roswaal in tow beside her—which made Petra's lips twist. Serena and Roswaal did not miss Petra's blatant reaction.

"As ever—how does a man manage to be so thoroughly hated by someone young enough to be his daughter? Keeping a subordinate who bears you outright rebellion at your side, of all things—you'll have no grounds to complain if you're stabbed in the back."

"I wouldn't go and hire a child so thoughtless as all tha-a-at. Whatever she thinks of me, I keep her because she's capable. The same goes for Petra-kun, and Otto-kun as well."

"That's an illness with you. One day, it'll be the very reason your feet get swept out from under you."

Serena shrugged, trading easy, unguarded banter with Roswaal. Her fierce words criticized Roswaal without mercy, which, as far as Petra was concerned, was rather satisfying to hear. But if she were honest, she couldn't feel much of that satisfaction just now.

"Senior Count Dracroy, shouldn't you be busy...?"

"The very same could be said right back to a General. Don't worry about me. It's true I haven't slept a wink in about three days, but if anything my eyes are wide awake for it. Couldn't drop off even if I tried."

"That doesn't sound like something that should reassure anyone..."

At Serena's fearless smile, Zikr shrank his shoulders as though abashed. And alongside that exchange, Roswaal's gaze turned toward Petra. Petra, who had been deliberately avoiding contact with him, tensed slightly under that gaze.

"Wh-what is it, Master?"

"Now don't be so wary of me. Simply put, I'm just worried whether you're alone, and whether you're overworking yourself. Where's Frederica?"

"...Frederica-neesama has been busy this whole time, so she's resting now. I asked her, over and over, and she finally agreed to rest."

Petra recalled Frederica, sleeping now almost without a breath, as if she'd simply passed out. Frederica had worked alongside Petra as one of the healers' corps, and, whenever needed, had used Beastification to run as a messenger through every corner of the fortress that had become the battlefield. Her sleepless, tireless work deserved nothing but respect—but as her little sister figure, Petra couldn't help worrying all the same. Even her resting now wasn't because she'd listened to Petra's pleas—it was only after being forced into it by Ram and Garfiel that she'd finally given in.

"I couldn't get Frederica-neesama to rest, no matter what I did."

"Frederica has such a strong sense of responsibility, after a-a-all. It's not that she thought little of you, Petra-kun."

"—! I know that without you telling me!"

"Is that so. I suppose it is. Forgive me for saying more than I needed to. —Are you all right?"

"...The Yang magic is working properly on me, so I'm fine."

At Roswaal's subtly deflating response, Petra frowned and answered so. Petra's magical skill, learned from Roswaal and honed further day by day, was said to be suited to the Yang attribute, whose traits mostly involved boosting a target's strength or restoring their vigor. In Petra's case, she still felt uneasy using it on other people, but she could expect enough effect from applying Yang magic to herself to make up for insufficient stamina or lack of sleep. Of course, stamina wasn't something that welled up infinitely, so a limit was bound to burst forth somewhere.

"Right now, I want to push myself, even just a little. Are you going to stop me?"

"—. No, I won't stop you. Anyone, so long as they're alive, will have occasions when they must push past reason and common sense on passion alone—must push themselves. If now is that time for you, Petra-kun, then I have no intention of taking it from you."

"...I really do dislike you, Master."

Petra turned her face away from Roswaal, who closed one eye and regarded her with the blue one. He was a man she could hardly forgive, and yet Roswaal so often affirmed her thinking, just like this. That it wasn't done to flatter Petra, but was truly words spoken from his own convictions, made it all the worse. It was almost as if it meant Petra could understand Roswaal's true nature.

"What a troublesome master and servant. That sort of thing doesn't differ much between empire and kingdom, does it. Don't you agree, Second General Zikr?"

"When a young lady as clever as Miss Petra is, at that age, it's little wonder Margrave Mathers pours such passion into her education. When earnestness faces earnestness, I'd think a clash only natural. Though of course, I believe Senior Count Dracroy's beauty, too, has been polished by her own fierce way of living."

"Well, well—a General of the empire, through and through. I thought him a decent man, and here we are. Don't go showing me a face so much to my taste, and delighting me so."

Alongside Petra and Roswaal's conversation, Zikr bowed respectfully at Serena's remark. Watching out of the corner of her eye as two people of a particularly strange temperament, even by the empire's standards, went on talking, Petra sighed. —Roswaal's true nature, whatever it might be, did not matter. Now that she had ended up face to face with the Roswaal she'd been avoiding, Petra would swallow the poison, plate and all. To that end, Petra firmed her cheeks and looked up at Roswaal.

"Master, what do you intend to do with Subaru?"

"Hm."

"Please answer me."

Petra's question, direct, and yet lacking in specifics. Faced with it, Roswaal let out a small breath. He closed one eye again, and this time looked at her with the yellow one—the light Petra found hard to bear, so thin on humanity.

—The unnaturalness of the relationship between Subaru and Roswaal had left a strong impression on Petra. The outrages Roswaal had committed against Arlam Village, Petra's home, and against the Sanctuary, birthplace of Frederica and Garfiel—Subaru and the others had forgiven nearly all of it, almost entirely. But Petra suspected there was something else at work between Subaru and Roswaal, something different from that.

Looking only at Roswaal's wrongdoing and the pardon granted for it, Subaru and Emilia, along with Frederica, all fell into the same category of members far too kind. Following them were Beatrice and Garfiel, who openly declared themselves "angry"; Ram occupied her own peculiar position; and Petra and Otto belonged to the group that clearly refused to forgive Roswaal. Naturally, Petra and Otto always dealt with Roswaal holding a certain tension. Of course, neither of them wanted to stir up trouble, so that tension was more like a quiet, slow roasting over flame. And the fact that Roswaal never so much as changed expression while being roasted like that was exactly the maddening thing about him.

But the blue flame that Petra and Otto directed at Roswaal, and the heat of the air drifting between Subaru and Roswaal, seemed to Petra like fundamentally different things. It was an ugly way to put it. She didn't even want to admit it. But Petra had become certain of it all the same.

Between Subaru and Roswaal, there was a secret that neither Petra nor anyone else could enter into. That shared secret, she was certain, was the source of the different sort of tension between them. And whatever that secret brought about, surely, in all likelihood, would make Subaru unhappy.

"What do I intend to do with Subaru-kun, is it, hm-m-m."

At Petra's hostile gaze, Roswaal murmured that in a voice faintly hoarse. There seemed to be none of his usual air of evasion or mockery in it. Still, even without that clownish atmosphere, Petra couldn't carelessly trust Roswaal. As if to bear out Petra's misgivings, Roswaal, one eye still closed, said—

"What do you think, Petra-kun, would be the correct thing for me to do-o-o?"

"...Are you making fun of me?"

"No, no, I have no such intention. In truth, I'm at my wit's end over it, tru-u-uly. ...What attitude I ought to take toward Subaru-kun, as he is now, that is."

"—"

As he spoke, Roswaal lightly raised both hands and lit different colored lights at his fingertips. With red glowing at his right hand and blue at his left, he weighed his next move in earnest. Which way that red and blue would tip—which would be the better outcome for Petra, no, for the people dear to Petra—she had no way of knowing. Not knowing as she was, if there was one thing Petra could still do, it was only this.

"If—"

"Hm?"

"If you make Subaru suffer, or make him sad, any more than this, I will absolutely never forgive you, Master. ...No—I'll absolutely make you regret it."

Not forgiving him wouldn't cause Roswaal the slightest twinge. That much Petra had known full well already, having served him a year and a half now. So, calling to mind the full weight of that year and a half, Petra said the thing Roswaal would hate most. Yes, she would make him regret it, whatever it took. And for that—

"No matter what, I'm going to get in the way of whatever you want to do, whatever you're trying to do, Master."

"—"

For a beat, a blank silence opened up between Petra and Roswaal. Petra tensed her cheeks, meaning to make the sternest face in her own cute personal history. Faced with Petra's direct appeal, Roswaal fell silent. Just how much Petra's earnest, stern face contributed to his musing during that silence was unclear, though.

"...That would certainly be a problem, indeed-e-ed. You seem to be loved far more than I am."

"...Loved—by what?"

"By a strange power I decided to defy, and against which I've never once managed to win. Not even once."

Shrugging, Roswaal said something Petra couldn't understand yet again. But whatever passed across his profile as he murmured it kept Petra from taking it as mere idle talk. Certainly, Roswaal was up against something that left him keenly feeling powerless, unable to win. Being told she was loved by such a thing didn't strike any chord in Petra.

Petra simply wanted to be loved by the people she herself loved. And she wanted the people she loved to be smiling and happy, as much as possible. So—

"I just want Emilia-neesama, and Subaru too, to be smiling..."

Pressing a hand tight to her own chest, Petra murmured that like a prayer. Wishing fervently that this entire prayer of hers might hang like a cloud over the people she loved most, and shield them to the last from whatever terrible thing came pouring down on them, like sunlight.

—Beside that exchange between Petra and Roswaal, watching the master and servant pair charged with prickling tension, Serena smiled over at Zikr.

"Well? The kingdom's people have quite a bit to them too, don't they? Thoroughly strange and complicated—just my taste."

"...You're lovely when you smile like that, Senior Count, but I can hardly call it good taste."
