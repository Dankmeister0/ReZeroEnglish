Arc 5 Chapter 69: "The Hideous Banquet"

The hem of her dress swaying in the wind, everyone saw the girl give an arrogant little snort. Under that gaze, the girl — Beatrice — cast her eyes over the battlefield as though surveying it. In the great plaza of the waterway district stood the wounded 'Gluttony,' Lye Batenkaitos, facing off against Otto and the other three. Looking over Otto, Felt, and the rest, Beatrice let out an unconcealed sigh.

"What a pitifully shabby lineup, I suppose."

There wasn't even room in that breathy remark of Beatrice's for a retort, let alone an insult in return. Still, Beatrice's entry into the fight was undeniably a major turning point in a situation that had been growing steadily worse. Something like relief welled up naturally in Otto's chest.

"Bea—!"

Starting to call out that name, Otto clapped a hand over his own mouth on reflex. Batenkaitos was a being that devoured names. Just a moment ago, he'd asked Felt and the others himself, taking pains to make sure his own name wouldn't be learned. He couldn't very well break that rule himself now.

Batenkaitos's attitude toward Otto, who kept his name hidden — there was clear irritation in it, at having his "meal" interrupted. Which meant that for Batenkaitos's name-devouring to work, he needed to already know the target's name. Which meant Beatrice's name must not be learned either. Seal it away, feel out an opening.

But Otto's consideration turned out to be wasted. Because—

"—Beatrice-sama? Why ever are you out and about?"

Batenkaitos cocked his head, gazing at Beatrice as he said this. 'Gluttony' kept his eyes on Beatrice, continuing on, sounding genuinely puzzled.

"..."

"You were so stubbornly against going outside. Except at mealtimes, and when you were with the Great Spirit-sama... ah, though there were exceptions to that too, weren't there?"

To Beatrice's stony silence, Batenkaitos kept piling on words. It would be a bit of a stretch to call it familiar, but it still sounded like the speech of someone who'd held a certain distance and relationship with her for some time.

"It's not as if you two were acquainted before... Beatrice-sa—!"

The relationship between Batenkaitos and Beatrice. The meaning of holding back her name evaporated, and Otto's throat closed mid-call as he tried to press the matter. This time unconsciously, his eyes were on Beatrice's profile.

Beatrice bit her lip, violent emotion swirling in her large, round eyes. It was rare for this spirit girl to bare her anger so openly. Before Otto's astonished eyes, Beatrice let out a long breath and glared at Batenkaitos.

"—So that's what the little trick was for. I see how it is, I suppose."

Beatrice murmured, her voice heavy and low. It wasn't the low voice of intimidation, but the low voice of someone trying to force their emotions down. Struggling to keep the anger from her expression, Beatrice peered into the depths of Batenkaitos's clouded eyes, and whatever she saw there twisted the corner of her mouth in disgust. And then:

"How many people have you crammed inside yourself, you disgusting thing?"

"Who knows? But I think our portions are a lot more modest than Roy's, you know. Roy's got no taste, he'll eat anything — the amount just isn't comparable to us, who pick and choose! We believe quality is everything when it comes to eating, so that's where we and Roy just don't see eye to eye."

Batenkaitos, who called the devouring of 'names' and 'memories' a "meal." There seemed to be some peculiar aesthetic sense behind his self-styled gourmet pretensions and his contemptuous dismissal of some familiar figure as a glutton with no taste — but either way, it was the sort of thing Otto could never hope to fully understand.

"..."

And what Otto couldn't fully understand now was Beatrice's attitude as well. The girl's revulsion didn't feel like it was simply an assessment of the Batenkaitos standing before her. It felt like the result of some other, more deeply rooted negative sentiment entirely.

Or perhaps — and it struck Otto, thinking this far — the same was true from Batenkaitos's side, treating Beatrice as someone he already knew. The possible reason why Batenkaitos, one-sidedly, knew Beatrice.

"...No way."

The way Batenkaitos fought belonged to a realm only reachable through long years of martial refinement. From what Dynas had said, Otto had already formed one hypothesis about that fact.

"I'd guessed that maybe you could inherit not just a person's 'name' and 'memories' when you devour them, but their bodily experience too. It'd be hard to reach the level you have in both unarmed combat and knife-work at your apparent age otherwise. So I figured..."

Able to make even a devoured opponent's skill his own nourishment. If so, it would explain how Batenkaitos, looking to be in his mid-teens, had come to master abilities rivaling a martial master's in field after field. And if that hypothesis was correct — and not only correct, but carried a further implication—

"But if what can be inherited isn't limited to bodily experience alone—"

That was a hypothesis meaning a viciousness entirely different from a mere threat in combat strength. Because Batenkaitos had said it himself.

—That he was searching for the one behind that speech that had echoed across the city.

That the person was weak and fragile, someone who made you anxious if you weren't there beside them, supporting them. Knowing Natsuki Subaru, and having spent a long time with his character, Otto could sympathize with holding such a feeling about him. That boy had something mysterious about him that made others feel that way.

But that impression only came from having spent time close to him, fondly, with affection. Knowing Subaru's fragile strength and weak courage was proof of having stood near him. And if there were someone whose proof of that had now been stolen by 'Gluttony' — there was only one girl who came to mind as the one who fit.

"—!"

Otto arrived, belatedly, at the same revulsion Beatrice had already reached. Seeing whatever it was in Otto's eyes as they changed, Batenkaitos gave an elegant bow to the gazes now fixed on him, and smiled, baring his fangs.

"Head servant to Roswaal L. Mathers, Margrave of the Frontier— no, no, that's wrong."

Shaking his head partway through the introduction, Batenkaitos spread both arms wide. He fondly stroked one of the white scars on his own wound-riddled body. The scar rising on his shoulder looked as agonizing as if he'd been struck by a sharp iron stake.

"Now, just one dearly beloved person. — The attendant of my most beloved, my future hero, Natsuki Subaru. Rem... was it?"

"..."

"Bring her to me, won't you! Our beloved hero! Our hero was supposed to come pass judgment on us, all the way out here!"

Sticking out his tongue, licking at his wound, Batenkaitos sneered. Blood rushed to Otto's head despite himself. His clenched back teeth ground audibly, and he wanted nothing more than to slam a fist into that leering face out of sheer fury.

'Gluttony's' attitude, his tone, his smile — all of it was mocking the feelings of one particular girl.

Without any idea how desperately that girl was being begged to come home safely, she was just being trampled underfoot with scorn and contempt. That was what lit the fire in Otto's heart. This 'Gluttony' — this was an opponent he could never, under any circumstances, forgive.

"Beatrice-san...?"

Otto had been counting, with his fingers, the remaining rounds of magic stone tucked into his sleeve, when Beatrice drifted smoothly in front of him. She raised a hand as if to restrain him, and Otto, unable to read her intent, furrowed his brow. Then—

"Let Beatrice correct that first remark, I suppose. Someone went and summoned Betty here, and quite well done it was."

"..."

"This one... this one alone, Beatrice cannot let meet Subaru. If Subaru meets him, Subaru will be hurt. Irreparably, most likely. So."

"Here, we finish him off ourselves."

Otto finished the tail end of Beatrice's sentence with that flat declaration. Beatrice didn't turn around, but her stance made it plain enough that she agreed. She was, by anyone's reckoning including her own, Subaru's partner. Otto understood all too well, to the point of pain, her refusal to let this vile thing anywhere near him.

"Wait wait wait wait, wait a damn minute!"

Just as the two of them, resolve renewed, turned to face their formidable enemy — it was not Batenkaitos who threw cold water on the moment. It was Felt, who had been standing beside Otto, silently watching the exchange up to now. Readjusting the bundle in her arms, she pointed at Beatrice.

"You strutted out here like you're some big shot, but seriously, what's some shrimp like you gonna do? I heard you're that guy's partner and all, but—"

"Ah, um, right. Well, that part of the explanation's a bit complicated, actually..."

Felt's point was entirely too reasonable, and Otto floundered trying to explain. That Beatrice was a spirit, and Subaru's Contracted partner, was fine to have known publicly. Still, it was also true that there was some anxiety over Beatrice's combat strength without Subaru present—

"Being called a shrimp by a shrimp is downright offensive, I suppose. If you've time to worry over Betty's abilities, you'd do better worrying about that unpromising, meager little body of yours."

"Man, you're a mouthy little brat, aren't you. For the record, my chest and my height have both been growing plenty ever since I started eating proper meals and sleeping regular. You're the one who oughta be worrying about the future."

"Unfortunately, Betty's appearance is fixed by design. So..." — mmph."

The bickering, wildly out of place for the moment, was about to escalate further, but Beatrice cut herself off mid-word. Her eyes had caught on the long, slender bundle Felt was carrying. It was the Magic Vessel Felt called her "trump card," but Beatrice looked at its shape with something like astonishment.

"That — could that possibly be a 'Meteor'?"

"A Meteor?"

"Mother... a staff a great mage made, ages ago, to harass a Dragon with. It was supposed to be lost, no one knew where it'd gone — how ironic, I suppose."

Felt nodded vaguely at Beatrice's explanation, though the crucial part had been rephrased. But to Otto, who knew that the "Mother" Beatrice had substituted in referred to the 'Witch' erased from legend, this was an outrageous statement in every possible sense. There were plenty of things he wanted to press further on, but factoring in that the 'Witch' had used it to harass a Dragon, its reliability as a weapon seemed high indeed.

"I've heard it's a pain to use, but can I expect real power out of it?"

"It's a staff with a legend of having made a Dragon half-cry. Certified authentic, I suppose."

The scale of the comparison was so absurd it was hard to really grasp. But it got across, at least, that this was a preposterous weapon. Otto nodded at Beatrice's words, but Felt still wasn't convinced.

"Whatever this thing is can wait. More important — this shrimp—"

"Your conscience is more or less decent, I suppose. But that's needless worry. Because—"

"Huh?"

"It's already been set in motion, I suppose."

Before Felt, still tilting her head, Beatrice broke into a smile unfittingly seductive for her appearance. Her lightly raised right hand pointed at Batenkaitos, and every throat that turned to look froze solid.

—Crystals glowing purple had blanketed the area all around Batenkaitos.

"Oh my, Beatrice-sama, how merciless."

"There's not a stock of restraint or mercy anywhere in this world set aside for you, I suppose."

El Mynah. One of the rare offensive spells among dark magic bared its fangs.

The instant Batenkaitos muttered his complaint, the purple radiance danced wildly and surged toward the small body standing directly in its line of fire. A sharp, hard sensation struck the slim, unmoving frame head-on — crystal shattered, the flagstones cracked, and smoke billowed upward. The devastation across the plaza was proof enough of the destructive force just unleashed.

"So, how was that, I suppose?"

Showing off that overwhelming magical power, Beatrice turned a triumphant face toward Felt. It was a bit unbecoming for a Great Spirit some four hundred years old, but even Felt, for once, seemed to have nothing to say in return.

"W-well, yeah, guess you're not totally useless."

That she still managed a sore-loser retort was, admittedly, a testament to Felt's nerve.

"Y-your voice is shaking, Felt-ojousama."

"That goes for you too, dumbass! Quit yapping and keep your eyes front!"

To Gaston's trembling attempt at banter, Felt snapped back as if to hide her own unease. But not everything in her words was a bluff. Because at the epicenter of Beatrice's spell, where Batenkaitos should have taken the attack defenseless, there was no sign of him at all.

"He's coming—!"

Dynas's voice rose, laced with dread, and every eye turned toward where he glared. There, hands and feet planted on the ground, 'Gluttony' crawled toward them with a spider-like motion. 'Gluttony' laughed, baring his fangs, eyes bloodshot.

"Ha-ha! Beatrice-sama, as expected! Nice, nice, nice, nice, nice, nice, nice, nice, nice!"

Shaking his head side to side, hair whipping wild, Batenkaitos bounded across the ground and charged back toward them once more.

"—Five shots left."

Even as he responded to the approach, Otto caught Beatrice, licking her lips, leaving behind an ominous murmur.

The instant she activated El Mynah and hurled a warning strike at Batenkaitos, Beatrice had felt a great magic stone shatter in her bosom. That left six magic stones remaining — and factoring in Beatrice's own stamina for activity, only five were usable for offense and defense.

—In the skirmish that had served as prelude to the current battle for the city, at the Time Tower plaza, Beatrice had clashed unexpectedly with the Deadly Sin Archbishops of 'Wrath' and 'Greed.' As a result of that fight, Beatrice had poured everything she had into treating the wounded Subaru and the city's residents, consuming enough mana to compromise her own ability to act.

The spirit Beatrice was an artificial spirit created by the 'Witch' Echidna. Her power was strong enough to leave ordinary spirits far behind, but in exchange she carried a number of troublesome weaknesses. Chief among them was the fact that she had no way to recover mana she'd lost, other than receiving it from her Contractor.

Drawing on ambient mana in the air, or receiving mana offered by someone other than her Contractor — neither was something Beatrice could convert back into her own power. As a result, recovering depleted mana meant receiving mana from Subaru and simply letting time pass — there was no other way. The fact that she was able to move at all right now was because she'd resorted to a kind of forbidden technique.

—At present, Beatrice had been entrusted with seven great magic stones.

Great magic stones were rare items that, over long years, had accumulated vast reserves of colorless mana within themselves. One of them had already shattered; six remained. These were what had been entrusted to her by the pervert — no, by Kiritaka — who had roused Beatrice from her deep sleep and begged for aid here at the plaza.

"I beg you, I humbly beg you, Great Spirit-sama. Please, lend your power to this fight to protect this city. My beloved people are in this city."

Battered and half in tears, pathetically pleading, Kiritaka had shattered a magic stone of incalculable value without a moment's hesitation to wake her. Beatrice, seeing no other choice, had agreed to answer his call.

If she was honest, Beatrice had wanted to rush straight to Subaru's side. The situation across the city had transformed entirely, and Subaru too was surely exposed to the same danger. If she wasn't beside him, Subaru was the type who'd worry himself sick. So now that she was awake, surely she ought to go to Subaru, for his—

"Foolish, I suppose. No — I am being foolish."

Beatrice sharply rebuked herself, for trying to use the excuse of "worry" as an indulgence. If Subaru had chosen to fight, and had still left Beatrice behind regardless, it was because he had some plan for fighting without her presence. Subaru didn't overestimate her. If anything, he tended toward underestimating her. He wouldn't recklessly throw himself against an opponent he couldn't beat, and if he faced an opponent he truly couldn't beat without her, he'd have used any means necessary to wake her.

So, however much it galled her to admit it, she wasn't needed for the fight Subaru was engaged in right now. Rushing to Subaru's side would have to wait until she too had earned results enough to be worth being swept up in his arms, once he returned from ending his own battle.

To Beatrice, standing as reinforcement, Kiritaka had offered up seven great magic stones. Beatrice, who was fundamentally poor at diverting mana from outside sources, used the power of those stones as an emergency measure — an extremely inefficient conductor.

In her dress pocket, the seven magic stones were, by rights, lumps of pure mana potent enough to work near-miracles — and she was squandering that lavishly, forcing it through the activation formula of the simplest spells. Normally, ten units of power would suffice to activate a spell; instead she was pouring in a thousand units to force it through. On top of that, there was no throttling it — no matter how minor the spell, a single casting shattered one stone regardless. She needed to keep one in reserve for her own sustenance, which left five usable.

—In other words, she had to finish this within a mere five moves.

"Looks like my reunion with Nii-cha's gotten further away, I suppose. In exchange, you're going to see hell."

The great magic stones Kiritaka had owned had originally been sought out to serve as an Anchor for Puck. That very object, the whole purpose of this journey, was now being shattered for Beatrice's fight — there was no word for it but irony.

"I'm sick of seeing hell! Everyone we eat gets a taste of it, right at the end, every single one of them!"

Shouting, Batenkaitos lunged at the group before him. His movements looked careless, but his sharp eyes were most wary, above all, of Beatrice.

He had no idea that Beatrice's mana was running dry, that she'd be running on empty after five more castings. To make sure he kept thinking that, she'd traded one magic stone for a powerful area attack — as a piece of misdirection, that was a solid, passing result. Beatrice raised both hands, palms turned toward Batenkaitos hanging in midair.

"Al Mynah!"

"—!"

"A lie, I suppose."

For an instant, Batenkaitos braced himself, anticipating the activation of a top-tier spell. Seeing this, Beatrice stuck out her tongue and leapt backward in a great bound. As Batenkaitos flinched, drawing in on himself, Gaston and Dynas both sprang at him.

"Owoooo!"

"Eat this!"

The two men, roaring battle cries, moved to intercept Batenkaitos with a coordinated assault of twin blades and fists. Heavy, sharp blows crashed against 'Gluttony,' but Batenkaitos evaded them with masterful footwork, and instead wielded his dagger in a counterstrike against the two of them. The flashing tip of steel arced toward Dynas's throat.

"Watch— guh!"

"Sorry!"

Gaston threw himself into the dagger's path, taking the blow to shield Dynas. A hard, ringing sound cut the force of the strike, but as Gaston staggered back, coughing, red blood spilled from the corner of his mouth.

—The limit of the combat technique that handled mana, the so-called 'Flow Method.'

Maintaining a resilient body that resisted blade and blow, Gaston's fighting style was a technique known as the Flow Method — one branch of a technical system exploring uses of mana distinct from Magic. Compared to Magic, it was a discipline less dependent on talent — a field where sheer volume of training was what counted, requiring blood-soaked effort to master well enough to wield in real combat.

"But it seems he's been pushed too far, I suppose."

As far as Beatrice could tell, Gaston's aptitude and talent didn't rise above the ordinary. That he'd managed, even half-formed in his Flow Method, to hold his own against a Deadly Sin Archbishop at all was purely because Batenkaitos had been holding back on him considerably.

"There, ya go!"

"Guh, ah!?"

Down on one knee, coughing blood, Gaston took a kick to the jaw. Blood sprayed from his nose, and the huge man crumpled, unable to move — out of the fight. Their fighting strength had just dropped by one.

"You did so well, Gaston! An award for effort! You tried your very hardest and it still wasn't enough! That's the perfect rating for a guy like you!"

"You... you bastard!"

At the sight of Batenkaitos mocking the fallen Gaston, Felt, blood rushing to her head, lunged in and swung her Meteor at him. Used properly, its power carried the 'Witch's' own seal of quality. But wielded as a mere blunt instrument, it couldn't bring out even a tenth of its true potential.

"Whoa there! Felt-chan, not bad!"

"Sh-shut up! Get away from me, you piece of—!"

Struggling to handle the unwieldy weapon, Felt still drove sharp blow after sharp blow at Batenkaitos, drawing on her raw physical ability. Batenkaitos evaded every single one, dancing away with footwork like something out of a performance. The Meteor's strikes grazed his hair here and there, but never landed with enough force to do damage. The gap in skill was overwhelming. He was toying with her completely.

"Hey! Just get away already, I suppose! The merchant's got the big one recovered, I suppose!"

"Like I can pull off something that fancy!"

The disparity in ability was plain; if he ever went on the offensive, her defeat was certain. While Felt pressed her attack on Batenkaitos, Otto managed, in a frantic scramble, to drag the unconscious Gaston out of the combat zone. Dynas, testing the feel of his twin blades, searched for a chance to break into the fight between Felt and Batenkaitos, but no sure opening presented itself.

The moment anything changed, Batenkaitos would strike precisely through that gap. His attitude of watching and waiting, patient as a predator, for any shift in the situation made it plain, undeniably, that 'Gluttony' — despite being outnumbered — was the one truly in command of the field.

"What, what is it, what, what could it be, I wonder why, I wonder what it is! Wouldn't it be nice if someone came to help her? Poor little Felt-chan, is she just gonna get abandoned?"

"Shut your damn mouth! You're the one who oughta just quietly stand there and get hit—"

"Right. — But, y'know, I'm kind of getting bored of this already."

"Wh— aaagh!?"

As Felt shouted, raising the Meteor overhead, that was the instant Batenkaitos closed in. The distance between them vanished, and 'Gluttony's' palm brushed against Felt's slight chest. An instant later, the impact sent the girl's body flying lightly through the air, and Felt tumbled across the flagstones with a sharp scream. The force was too much for her to even properly break her fall — but that wasn't the real problem.

"No! He got a proper hold of her...!"

Felt, struck hard in the chest, coughed, and Otto cried out at the sight. Beatrice, reading the meaning behind the alarm on his face, understood at once. 'Gluttony's' meal preparations were complete.

"Felt-chaaan. — Itadakimasu."

By whatever principle it worked, Batenkaitos held up the left hand he'd touched Felt with, palm showing off deliberately, and licked it with his long tongue. As though something precious belonging to the girl named 'Felt' rested right there upon it.

He set it upon his tongue as though cherishing it, caressed it with the rough texture, savored it thoroughly as if scraping out every last trace, then let it fall into his stomach and chewed it down without mercy. When that was complete, 'Gluttony's' meal would be finished, and the 'name' would settle inside the blasphemer.

And the traces of the girl named Felt would vanish from the world—

"Bleh— ugh..."

"Huh? What's with you? What's your problem, you rude piece of—"

Shaking her head, Felt looked down at Batenkaitos, who had dropped to his knees, retching. Of course, her existence hadn't vanished — Felt merely tilted her head, looking thoroughly annoyed.

—It was the moment 'Gluttony's' meal failed, pathetically.
