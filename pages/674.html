Arc 8 Chapter 57: "Cecilus Segmunt"

—Cecilus Segmunt is a "Stargazer."
This opening line has already been used, so it's scrapped.

—Cecilus Segmunt is this world's leading man.
This fact keeps proving itself through showing rather than telling, so stating it outright would be gauche.

—Cecilus Segmunt is one of a kind in this world.
This claim isn't limited to Cecilus alone, which makes it a bit like saying "my headache hurts" — technically true, yet somehow beside the point.

—Cecilus Segmunt is Cecilus Segmunt.
This definition fits best, so this is the one we'll go with to open this time.

Very well, then—

△▼△▼△▼△

—Cecilus Segmunt is Cecilus Segmunt.

By heaven and earth and every god watching over them, this fact is Cecilus's premise, one that will never waver.
No matter what has happened to this body of his, even if — as everyone around him kept saying — there really once existed a grown, gorgeous young man version of himself, one with limbs longer and more elegant than now, a handsome face brimming with star quality, that person is nothing more than Before-Cecilus. Here, in this very moment, Cecilus Segmunt exists as this single self and no other.
Therefore, he will prove it.

"Let the Observers on high bear witness — to which the world shall choose."

Cinching his hair back tight with a torn strip of cloth, he finished the job and slapped both cheeks hard with his now-free hands.
Cecilus rarely psyches himself up this way, but a shift in mood plain for anyone to see is dramaturgically essential. Inserting a beat like this lets the audience know that the stage — carried along by the actor's change of heart — has moved on to the next scene.
Having done that, Cecilus cocked his head. "Now that I think of it—"

"You're all surprisingly well-behaved. Feel free to resume your usual chatter as always — I don't mind one bit."

At once, the voices of the audience who'd been forced into silence exploded back to life, and Cecilus nodded inwardly at this return to business as usual, letting it restore his own rhythm.
Ordinarily, having their eyes riveted on him was what kept Cecilus's need for validation fed day in and day out, but making them fall silent like that had been a first, just now. It had surprised even him — they must have been shaken pretty badly to clam up like that.
That fact alone felt wonderful — this was what people meant by a good omen.
After all—

"—I'm about to go show a thing or two to someone who underestimated me."

The instant he said it, a flash of light pierced straight through Cecilus's face.

"Ceci—"

Al cried out at the sight of Cecilus, his grinning face skewered dead center.
He'd been rushing over, and at the sight, for some reason, he tried to tighten his grip on the qinglong blade held to his own throat. But he stopped himself just short.
Because the pierced image of Cecilus wavered, and he realized the one that had died was only an afterimage.
And then—

"You'd best step back a bit."

The instant those words reached Al's eardrums, Cecilus's afterimage burst, and a shockwave spread outward.
The sound, arriving late behind the flash of light, ruptured the surrounding space; it swelled as it chased the spreading destruction, and a street that no longer retained any trace of its original form was blown away in a neat circle spanning a dozen-odd meters.

"Whoaaaaugh?!"

Al's scream trailed off as he got caught in the aftershock of the attack, but Cecilus, facing forward, didn't even catch it at the edge of his vision. Still, he didn't ignore his existence entirely, either.
Picking up on Al's behavior and physical abilities so far, his position just before, and the way his scream had receded into the distance — piecing together such surrounding information — Cecilus, treating it as the natural dramatic effect deduced from the nature of the enemy's attack, pictured Al's unseen form on the theater screen inside his head.
It had, in all likelihood, turned out exactly that way. Because this was the state of affairs that looked best.

—An actor cannot see every corner of the stage, but an actor must know every corner of the stage.

That was an expansion of the senses — no, an expansion of his mental field of imagined vision.
Cecilus fixed his own position as center stage, and from there directed the choreography and stage effects of the cast around him to his own taste — that is, he ran headlong toward realizing the single most visually striking development the world could offer.

"——"

As Cecilus ran the razor's edge between life and death, above his head the heroine floating there continued transforming into something monstrous.
The heroine, who hadn't so much as flinched under Cecilus's full-strength knife-hand strike, had sprouted numerous fine shards of magic crystal along her slender shoulders and arms, transforming into a fantastical figure that looked as if she wore wings of light.
Amid that form, beautiful enough to be called lovely, what drew the eye most were the bands of light hovering around the heroine, gently stroking the sky — bands built from thin, flat plates of magic crystal.

By whatever principle, the demonic bands, reflecting back the red of the burning cityscape, swam through the sky on a trajectory like sheer silk fluttering in the wind, and in an instant cut clean across the world, loosed straight at Cecilus.
The very same thing that had sent Al flying earlier now bore down again on the face of a Cecilus whose eyes were shining bright — closing in, closing in, closing in—

"—Lick. Huh, now that's a strange flavor. How to put it... gemstone-flavored?"

In that instant, with the tip of his tongue stuck out, Cecilus checked the taste of the band he'd dodged a hair's breadth before it skewered the center of his face. The sensation on his tongue was soft yet hard, and the taste, quite literally, was close to that of a gemstone.
It looked like a piece of hard candy, and the taste was identical to the disappointment of rolling an actual gem around in your mouth — in other words, what the heroine wore were bands of gemstone.

"Now that I think of it, she started out flinging light and fire, then partway through switched to pillars of stone and limbs of earth. So the gemstones are an extension of that too, are they... Very elegant, right up my alley! Nice, nice, the climax factor is really climbing!"

Pulling back the tongue that had licked the band, Cecilus let out a booming laugh, riding high on his own excitement.
To think that, at this final crossroads, in this scene of the grand showdown, his opponent would be a heavenly maiden draped in a robe of jewels — now that was something to be humbled by. On top of that, he'd gone ahead and worked out the meaning behind the outcome of the exchange just now, entirely on his own.
The knife-hand strike Cecilus had unleashed at the speed of lightning carried enough force to cleave not just a stone wall but even a castle of iron in two. But then, he'd heard tell that among gemstones there existed things harder than even that.
Which meant that the heroine Cecilus was up against was—

"—A heavenly maiden of diamond!!"

The instant the words that came to him were spoken aloud, a band of diamond shot out from the heavenly maiden's robe.
Flashing, cleaving the world in two as they surged forward, the bands of light numbered twelve in all — minus the one he'd dodged and licked, that left eleven, which came at Cecilus from all directions, eleven directions, as if to enfold and kill him.
It was, in truth, a domain filled wall-to-wall with "Death" — a mountain of corpses, a river of blood, telling him to dodge every raindrop in the storm—

"If that's what's needed! Raindrops, grains of sand, I'll dodge them all!"

Eleven raging lines of death, then one returning to make it twelve lines of death — he wove through them, wove through them, wove through them-em-em-em-em-em-em-em—

To be struck was fatal; to be fatal was ruin; to be ruined was curtain call.
The toe of his planted foot was shaved off, blood spraying from the wound on his chest evaporated in midair, and he wedged his small stunted body through the gaps in the lattice of bands to refuse the final curtain. How about that, how about that, did you see? If this had been his adult body, he wouldn't have been able to squeeze through the gap — so, netting everything out, the win went to the current Cecilus.
Having won, he resolved to keep on winning, and bared his teeth in a gleaming grin to seize that victory—

"Now's the time to let imagination take flight!"

The relentless barrage raining down gave him no room to pause, and he ignored all of it, spraying blood from wounds that were anything but shallow.
The acceleration of his thoughts mid-battle was remarkable — thunderous, ecstatic applause for the grand set piece thus far. But at this rate, he'd slide into a dreary stalemate and get shunned all around, so Cecilus shifted every function in his body into gear, prioritizing stage presence, in order to derive the best possible directorial plan.

—First, he cut the function that distinguished color. The world was dyed in black and white.
The rich hues of a colored world were lost, but a monochrome world had a certain essential goodness to it that only monochrome could express.

—Next, he cut the function of distinguishing sound. The audience's voices, the sound of his own heartbeat, the whistle of the light-bands cutting through air arriving late, the sound of the stage being torn apart — all of it, he left behind.
A soundless world was flavorless, but there was a taste that could only come from the refined art of savoring one's own constraints. It was in this very environment that one could notice such things — the joy of being moved by the skill of an actor's gestures, of the play on his face.

—On top of that, he cut pain and taste and smell and all such functions, bundled together, all at once. His body he devoted entirely to the fight; his mind he concentrated wholly on flashing thought. The curtain rose on the theater of his imagined mental vision.

"—Now then."

With that breath inserted, in the black-and-white world — the space in which he continued dodging the diamond bands' onslaught — Cecilus, quite literally, looked down from above on himself, striving away in fine style.
It was a sensation like stretching out an instant, expressing the acceleration of thought in imaginative terms. Even so, this too was no cure-all. A leading man might be skilled at the art of holding a scene, but if the situation itself failed to move, there was a limit to how long he could keep captivating an audience. A story only shakes the heart when it moves.
That diamond heroine had begged him tearfully, and that had made Cecilus good and angry — but he had to twist the true meaning of that plea to his own convenience and find the propulsive force to move this slow-motion, yet steadily progressing, situation forward.

"——"

Thinking about it normally, she was someone connected to Cecilus. Perhaps she was a hardcore fan who, one-sidedly, knew of a Cecilus who was simply too famous — but even if that were so, it posed no problem for the line of thinking that followed, so he'd leave the question of right or wrong there unasked.
What mattered here was that the Cecilus Segmunt she had in mind wasn't the him of now, but the troublemaking Before-Cecilus who kept coming up in all sorts of talk.
Anyone else hearing this would be exasperated, and in fact Schwartz and Tanza had been exasperated, but the story of that Before-Cecilus held not the slightest interest for Cecilus.

Whatever was said of him, it was someone else's business; whatever that man had done, it was mere coincidental resemblance; whatever he'd left behind, it was borrowed glory — another man's loincloth to wrestle in.
That was how Cecilus felt about it, and so, within him, the division between Before and After had been settled clearly and completely. It seemed the heroine — suspected of being in over her head thanks to eating something she'd picked up off the ground — felt the same way.
The heroine did not equate Before-Cecilus with After-Cecilus.

"That in itself is something to welcome, and it's not as if I could just be told 'grow big' and become that on the spot, so I've got no choice but to proceed with things as they stand!"

"Now what's this about. Even though I'm grateful she's thinking of us as two separate things, I definitely feel put out that the one being needed isn't this me right here."

Beside a Cecilus nodding along in deep thought, another Cecilus popped his head up.
He had just finished declaring Before-Cecilus and After-Cecilus separate entities, and having an Another-Cecilus show up here besides only muddied things further — but it was also true that having someone to talk to helped things along.

"Well, let's just chalk this up to heightened concentration making the acceleration of thought easier to visualize. More to the point, right now we're talking about the heroine."

"I'd say it's less a matter of the heroine and more a matter of my own stance toward her. Of course, watching Boss and Tanza-san, it's not hard to imagine that the relationship between a story's key figure and his heroine can produce a chemistry unlike anything before."

"Which means there's that kind of chemistry between me and her too?"

"Who's to say. In the first place, what was the reasoning behind deciding she'd be the heroine, again?"

"Went with the flow."

"Flow, huh~"

"But but, this thing Boss calls a 'live feeling' isn't something to scoff at. Snap decisions, reflexive calls — in the end, that's inspiration relying on instinct, and haven't I gone by instinct in plenty of situations before?"

"That's true enough. Which means the current situation is also a result of following instinct, so wouldn't following instinct going forward be best?"

"I'm starting to feel that way too. But then there's this — my instinct's thinking something like, 'eh, not thrilled about being relied on as anyone other than the Cecilus Segmunt who is me, right this very moment.'"

"I see. But we said what she's after isn't the current me. What's being begged for is Before-Cecilus, and the reason for that is probably that she thinks there's something Before-Cecilus could do that After-Cecilus can't, isn't it?"

"Ah, well then, if the current me surpasses the former me, that solves it, doesn't it?"

"No objections he-ere!"

The two Cecilus-es nodded to each other, reaching an agreement both found satisfying, and immediately a small squabble broke out over which of them would return to being the Real Cecilus being looked down upon from above.
Here began, and ended, a fruitless battle to decide which of them held the true Cecilus's soul — and immediately after, time for the Real Cecilus began moving at normal speed once more, and he resumed dealing with the twelve lines of death spun off by the diamonds of the heavenly maiden's robe.

"—Since it looked slow, the felt speed is fast, fast, fast!"

The twelve dancing bands of light moved independently, as if each had a will of its own, tracing irregular paths like living things rather than ordinary weapons. When he tried to knock one down with a knife-hand strike, the part that touched it was gouged clean out — painful just to look at. Their cutting edge, he could tell, was far beyond even Onibami, the blade Rowan treasured so dearly. This was quite the hard-mode opponent.
Still, surrounded by that highly lethal, beautiful cluster of gems, Cecilus thought—

"That way she said 'kill me' — the idea being that the Cecilus in her head could do it."

Which was to say, it was proof of her trust that the Cecilus inside her head could easily break through this encirclement of diamonds, reach her, and, in splendid fashion, stop her heart for good.
Naturally, for a Cecilus who intended to surpass the former Cecilus, going one better than that was a mandatory condition—

"Something Before-me, who couldn't be hit or touched, never did — how about this, then?"

For the hurdle he'd set himself to clear in order to surpass himself, Cecilus's thoughts sparked.
Before-Cecilus might well have dodged the incoming attack wholesale, but Perfect-Cecilus — the fusion achieved between After-Cecilus and Another-Cecilus — would create a showstopper that went beyond mere dodging.

A band of light aiming for his head, a glimmer closing on his torso, a sweeping strike less a leg-trip than a leg-shearing attempt — he dodged them by tilting his head, twisting his body, and stepping over them left foot then right foot in turn, then afterward leaned back, crouched, straightened his knees, and leapt to dodge every last shot. The result—

"Behold, cat's cradle of light!"

He kept dodging the twelve independent bands of light by a hair's breadth, guiding them so that they tangled together in midair, and the result — a magnificent net of knots — became the "Tokyo Tower."
He was recreating a string game Schwartz had once shown off as a way to pass the time on the journey from the Isle of Sword Slaves — one that, rare for her, had left Tanza openly moved, and Schwartz genuinely flustered with embarrassment.
Of course, doing this served no real purpose.
The bands of diamond, no doubt formed from an enormous store of mana, had no limit to their length, and any knot he tied could simply be untied, broken down, and reconstructed, returning to its original state as if nothing had happened.
He couldn't go around flaunting the mistaken pride of thinking he'd sealed away his opponent's attack with this.
He had simply gained something. A conviction.

"I can surpass myself."

He hadn't seen the real thing. He hadn't actually compared them side by side.
But the Before-Cecilus Cecilus pictured in his head could not have made this "Tokyo Tower." Because there was no "Tokyo Tower" inside him.
So while Before-Cecilus might also have dodged every one, this Cecilus had succeeded in crafting a showstopper beyond mere dodging — and by that much, the current Cecilus led by one step.

"——"

The instant after that conviction, directly below Cecilus, the structure of the light "Tokyo Tower" came undone.
The flow of it being reduced once to mana and then reconstructed was as expected — what slipped a little outside expectation was the colorless explosion that occurred the instant its structure came undone.
If she'd simply erased it, that would mean she'd been played with and taken it lying down — a declaration of her own defiant spirit that she hated that idea—

"You're quite the sore loser yourself!"

Still colorless, in a world where he had made even his own voice go ignored by his own eardrums, Cecilus, gazing from afar at the heroine who'd set this off, found himself impressed.
Impressed as he was, a colorless pillar of light was surging up from directly below — with enough force that, if it struck home, it would cancel out every band he'd dodged so far. And his own body, aloft in midair, had overwhelmingly less freedom of movement than it would on the ground.
Bad, bad, bad, bad, bad — which meant he was starting to burn with excitement.

—Once more he expanded his field of imagined vision and shifted every function of his body into a new gear.
Color vision, unchanged. Hearing, unchanged. He rebooted pain and touch, hiding the agony that instantly welled up behind a teeth-baring grin, and searched, within a sense of one second divided a hundred ways, for the "key."
The "key" that, if he had it, would let him turn the script to its next page.

"—Found it."

Baring every nerve in his body, committing an act of dimension-defying savagery like exposing raw flesh to the wind, Cecilus's outstretched foot, rising through the air, struck something.
Not some conveniently placed wall or building. What his foot struck was a single pebble — a fragment of rubble kicked up from the cityscape being destroyed, caught up in the battle between Cecilus and the heroine.
He used it as a foothold.

"Hah—"

Using the impossibly faint sensation against the sole of his foot as a foothold, Cecilus's body sprang through the air.
So as not to be caught by the rising colorless destruction, Cecilus went further still, stepping off swirling splinters of wood, shards of broken window glass, and finally even a good-sized flake of ash, to flee higher still.

Why was this possible. Because it was possible, it was possible — that was what he made anyone who witnessed it believe.
Ignoring every manner of logic and concept, Cecilus fleeing skyward was an act of violence against common sense. Anyone who saw it might call it a miracle, but to hear Cecilus tell it, this much was just the opening act — a mere handful of the miracles that visited every sort of ordinary moment, coloring the world.
And if he kept right on making miracles happen—

"Reality gets impatient and bares its fangs."

Before the outcome of his escape — Cecilus fleeing the pillar of light through an unreasonable ascent — could resolve itself, the reconstructed bands of light chewed their way out from inside the colorless pillar and clung after Cecilus in the sky.
The twelve bands, twining together in a spiral, spread their tips radially toward Cecilus and bit down, just as a carnivorous flower catches a small insect within its beautiful petals. With the petals closing shut like a bud, Cecilus, on the verge of being swallowed by the diamond flower, had no means of escape.
That is, probably, surely, if he were a Cecilus other than the current one.

"BOOM."

An explosive report of air, a hundred times louder than the sound of the word spoken aloud, shook the battlefield.
Unfortunately, Cecilus himself, having shut out sound, couldn't hear it, but it did a number on the eardrums of the heroine and Al, both present at the scene. Though the one with the most right to complain was probably the sky itself, kicked and made to explode by Cecilus.

Cecilus, who could outrun sound and run at the speed of lightning, knew that air had a wall to it. Kicking off that wall wasn't easy, but it wouldn't do to be taken for some ham actor who fumbles his showstopper moment. Whether performed cold with no rehearsal or not, only a leading man who dazzles perfectly deserves the name.

As a result, along with the explosive report, Cecilus's body was launched, this time straight downward. Plunging in a single steep dive, faster than the diamond flower's bud could finish closing, he escaped its petals and fell to the ground.
Cecilus, plunging straight into the ground, produced an impact exactly like a bolt of lightning striking down.
As the price, everything below his right knee was left a pitiful wreck, but a dashing figure winning without a scratch and a fierce figure bringing down an opponent while wounded were, in terms of making a good picture, hard to rank one above the other. Griping after the fact about how he should have done this or that would be disrespectful to the audience; feeling as though he'd been showered in thunderous applause, he squared his blood-soaked chest and pressed on, forward, forward—

"—Ten steps."

By eye, he gauged the distance to the heroine hovering in the air, and deliberately planted his blood-soaked leg forward.
Aiming at Cecilus as he broke into a run, the once-closed bud spread open again, and diamond petals rained down as if scattering spores — which he left behind with one great stride.

"—Nine steps."

The space to Cecilus's left and right warped, and from that twisted distortion pillars of stone shot out.
An attack method beyond just the diamond bands of light came back into play here — gritting his teeth at the spiteful timing, he forced recklessness, unreasonableness, and outright rashness onto his planted right leg, accelerated, and slipped through the gaps between the pillars of stone jutting from left and right, sacrificing nothing but a few stray hairs and grinding nothing but the air itself.

"—Eight steps."

Breaking up through the ground, multiple house-sized squares — cube-shaped chunks of rock — rose up, and like spears hurled in a driving rain, they fired attack after attack at him.
Straightening the knee of his raised left leg, he planted his foot on the tip of an oncoming stone spear — one lethal enough to serve as a gravestone if it landed a direct hit — and used it as a foothold to leap straight into the rain.

"—Seven steps, six steps, and so on, abridged!"

Improvising for the unforeseen, he reset his step count, wove through the storm of stone pillars built as if to reject After-Cecilus, and closed a distance that felt infinite toward zero.

The heroine, raging with all her fury, allowing no one near except the Cecilus she herself had recognized as capable of reaching her.
And Cecilus, fired up to prove to that stubborn, unyielding heroine that even the current him could reach her.

It was, quite literally, a battle of stubborn pride dangerous and catastrophic enough to rank among the world's finest, on a level to remain etched in the Empire's history.
Cecilus mastered that battle of wills, and accomplished it.
It wasn't about what would come of accomplishing it. He accomplished it because he wanted to accomplish it.

"—Two steps!!"

With knife-hand strikes swung from both hands, he blew away the pillar of stone blocking his path, gauged by wild guess the distance to the heroine who'd been hidden behind that cover, and there, leaping out, spotted her figure roughly ten meters up in the air — giving himself a standing ovation inside his own head for his own fine instincts — and brought her into range.

"——"

There, having gone through the process of breaking down and reconstructing once more, the twelve bands of diamond light stood in his way again.
Having passed through the storm of stone pillars, the heroine turned her bands of light once more upon Cecilus, who was looking up at her. In that instant, the tips of the bands, spreading wide to take in a broad range, flickered faintly, splitting the already paper-thin bands further — from twelve to twenty-four, twenty-four to forty-eight, forty-eight to ninety-six, ninety-six to one hundred ninety-two, one hundred ninety-two to three hundred eighty-four, three hundred eighty-four to—

"—Nngh!"

The bands of light, having branched so many times that counting them became laughable, fell like a waterfall, making the sky shimmer beautifully.
Left, right, and behind — every direction that could serve as a retreat was buried in stone pillars, and the stage had become an inescapable barrier. The only path lay straight ahead, and the falling droplets of light meant certain death not just on a direct hit but on the barest graze.

—Fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable, fatal, inescapable.

"——"

The world without color or sound was filled wall-to-wall with the possibility of "Death."
Before an unavoidable fate called "Death," he recalled the miracles he had kept stacking up.

That his right leg was still attached below the knee was a miracle; that he could still move with a chest wound so deep it looked like his own heart might be visible was a miracle; that his torn kimono hadn't come apart to expose him in some undignified way was also a miracle; and that his own existence, still brimming with drive at the sight of a heroine who kept right on crying, was a miracle.
And then—

"——"

—The miracle that, at the very best possible timing, a prop reached the hand Cecilus had stretched toward the heavens.

—Twelve thousand two hundred eighty-eight times.
That was the number of times it took Al to grasp his own role on this stage.

"——"

The instant Cecilus, having tied back his own hair, shifted his mindset through logic Al couldn't fathom, the battle accelerated further still, into a realm that scoffed at any intervention from Al.
To begin with, this was a battlefield where he'd known full well there was almost nothing he could do.
Within the domain he'd mapped out, even after close to two hundred rounds of trial and error, he'd only managed to intervene twice. Those two times had certainly mattered — but in a battle that had shifted up yet another gear, not a single chance for Al to intervene came around, not once.

To begin with, he couldn't even withstand the aftershock of the attacks Arakiya aimed at Cecilus.
Unlike before her transformation by the magic crystals had progressed, when she'd merely fired off welling-up mana in a haphazard way, Arakiya's attacks now had become clearly refined. In the process of that unleashed force of destruction — something that ought to be called bands of light — wrecking the whole surrounding area, Al, caught up in the crossfire, had been blown to bits over two thousand times.

In a battle where merely being present was fatal, what exactly was he supposed to do about it.
And yet, he couldn't take the option of abandoning any attempt at intervention and leaving the battlefield either. Because at this rate, Cecilus would be killed by the bands of light before ever reaching Arakiya.

To change an unchanging outcome, Al searched for room to intervene — and racked up crossfire casualties in the process. Over and over, hundreds and thousands of times repeating, he noticed something.
There were issues with positioning, and there were times Al, having carelessly drawn attention to himself, got reduced to cinders by a single band as casually as swatting an insect — but if he could just manage to witness the moment Cecilus reached right in front of Arakiya, he could see Cecilus making an inexplicable move even while exposed to an unavoidable attack.

—Right before the bands of light Arakiya wore branched into a hopeless number and came raining down, Cecilus reached a hand up toward the heavens.

"——"

At first, Al thought Cecilus was about to unleash some full-power knife-hand strike from there. But Cecilus's right hand showed no sign of moving even the moment the rain of light poured down.
It was hundreds of rounds later that Al realized it wasn't a knife-hand at all — the five fingers were spread wide.
Not a knife-hand, not a fist — he was holding his open palm up toward the sky. It made no sense. Al even considered the surprise possibility that Cecilus had suddenly started using magic here of all places, but either way, the result was the same: that magic misfired, Cecilus got swallowed by the glimmering light, and Al got blown up along with him.

"——"

Even after confirming that, with no room to intervene, the count of his attempts kept piling up to no purpose.
The ultra-high-speed battle produced its outcome as fast as the blink of an eye, and even in the time it took to confirm the situation, weigh possibilities, and turn a hunch into form, recoverable defeats kept piling up at an accelerating rate.

There was no impatience in that fact itself, but the fact that he couldn't reach beyond it did stir up a burning frustration.
Was he hesitating to pull back where he should be pulling back, had he crawled into the wrong dead end — eaten away by that outlandish regret, he'd get reduced to cinders, feelings and all.

Even with Al this desperate, the Cecilus in question wouldn't so much as glance back. Arakiya, too, paid Al not the slightest regard. It made him irrationally angry.
He found himself thinking this was just the two of them being told, on and on, thousands of times over, through violent and impulsive means, that this was a fight between the two of them alone, and no one else.

—What if it wasn't like that?

"——"

It was a possibility that suddenly flitted through his mind, and Al immediately shook his head to deny it.
He had witnessed, a few times before, the sprout of some passing thought beginning to develop — but none of them had ever amounted to more, swallowed each time by the light of ruin and vanishing.

But this time, rare as it was, it carried across the ending into the very start of the next resumption.
If it wasn't like that. —If there were room to intervene.

"——"

Thoroughly, Al decided to cling to the possibility that had sprouted.
Cecilus dodging every attack, reaching Arakiya. The glimmering bands of light Arakiya wore branching countless times, raining down, the finale in which light swallowed the world.
He kept challenging the stretch of time leading up to that finale, over and over, again and again, in order to completely grasp what was happening.

Along the way, he was appalled to notice Cecilus licking a band of light.
Along the way, he was appalled to notice Cecilus kicking off empty space to leap.
Along the way, he was even more appalled to realize it wasn't actually empty space at all — he'd been kicking off pebbles and ash.

Advancing his understanding that way, further and further and further, one thought reared its head.
Cecilus — perhaps, possibly, quite conceivably—

—the reason Cecilus was reaching his hand up to the heavens might be to create a showstopper moment.

"——"

What exactly was expected of him — Al, even as he was blown to bits, was struck with dismay.

—There once stood a building called the Loot House.

The story is drifting from the main point, so let's correct course.
To put it plainly, what brought down the building called the Loot House was the fact that an utterly ordinary, dull blade happened to meet a wielder capable of letting it fulfill its true purpose as a sword, and was swung by him.

Adult me, huh. Which would make this Before-me's memory, then?

Hmm, hmm, not bad at all! Grew up in a rather ideal way, didn't he! Kept all the star quality, added some allure, and stepped up his game as a man!

Ten years, given how far the name "Blue Lightning" had spread by now — that felt like a fairly reasonable figure. —In other words, it was quite likely that the past scene Cecilus was watching had taken place, in reality, within just the past year or two.

Taking that in, Cecilus advanced his thinking further, tilting his head as he did.
Naturally, what nagged at him was the reason he was seeing this past, and why he was seeing this particular past — leaving the latter question aside, he could sort of understand the former.

If it's true that I've shrunk, then I'd guess the memories from before I got small have just been sealed away — not erased and gone entirely.

He had no memory of the moment he shrank, so he didn't really understand the mechanism behind it, but surely the ten years Cecilus had lived through couldn't simply be wiped out of existence altogether. And yet, that he couldn't recall those ten years of memory was surely because body and mind influence one another.
Just as Cecilus made real with his body whatever he believed he could do, once the body had demonstrably shrunk, the mind, too, would need to regress to match. —Probably, something along those lines.
So he figured this memory itself had simply been dormant, something that had been inside Cecilus all along. Figuring that, there was no problem in the mere fact of seeing it.

Yes, the reason Cecilus grumbled with a sour look was that he'd hit upon an unpleasant possibility.
Cecilus had never experienced it himself, but apparently, when human beings are on the brink of death, they sometimes go searching back through the memories of their own life for a way to escape the death bearing down on them. Some function, it seems, that searches through the accumulated life lived so far for material to refute "Death."

Which was to say, if this really was, as Cecilus suspected, the memory of when he'd shrunk, then wouldn't the reverse also hold — that this scene existed to reverse the process?

Isn't this basically saying, "watch yourself shrink here, pick up a hint for how to turn back, and break through your current situation"? Isn't that incredibly lame?

He'd declared he would surpass himself, undertaken a death-defying journey to reach a heroine in tears, had a supporting player like Al deliver an assist beyond the call of his role — and yet he himself would fail to see his original resolve through.
With the answer to such a blunder of his own seemingly right there in front of him, Cecilus felt thoroughly disgusted.

No way, no way, no way!

Arakiya — the center of her chest, where a great mass of power sat lodged.
