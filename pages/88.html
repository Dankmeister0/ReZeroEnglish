Arc 3 Chapter 12: "The Illegal Entry into the Castle, and Its Consequences"

"When things go this smoothly, it just makes me nervous, y'know."

Scratching his head, Subaru sighed as he looked around.

The sturdy stone-built passage was roughly two meters wide — narrow enough that four adults standing shoulder to shoulder would be a squeeze, but plenty spacious enough for its actual purpose of servants passing back and forth. It was Roswaal Manor's various corridors that were needlessly huge and a pain to clean, by comparison.

In any case—

"I was expecting way more obstacles along the way... and here I just breezed through in one shot."

Hidden away in a corner of the deserted passage — a stretch of the underground corridors beneath the royal castle — Subaru let out a breath at the sheer sloppiness of the security, unseen by anyone.

—The "Operation Sneak-Into-the-Castle: Thump-Thump Edition, Presented by Kadomon" had been set into motion barely an hour after the three of them had hatched their scheme.

"Listen up, don't you dare bring my name into this. I can get you into the noble quarter — the upper district — with this hand of mine, but finding a way into the castle itself, that's on you. If push comes to shove, go find a guy called Henehehe in the west block of the upper district. He'll get you back down here quietly enough."

Even as he made sure to cover his own tracks, he didn't forget to pass along an escape route for when things went sideways. No matter how you sliced it, the gruff shopkeeper just couldn't help looking out for people. It was that very good nature of his, in fact, that had gotten him roped into this reckless scheme in the first place.

Thanks to Kadomon's suggestion (though he'd deny it was his idea), the plan for smuggling Subaru into the upper district had been settled: stow away among the cargo of a dragon carriage bound from Merchant Street to the upper district.

The lower districts — apparently a catch-all term for the outer ring of the royal capital, encompassing the commercial areas like Merchant Street, the neighborhoods where the commoners lived, and the slums as well. Regular shipments ran from these lower districts up to the upper district, and the checkpoint's oversight of them was said to be fairly lax.

"So that means the merchants are, like, really trusted?"

"It's less that the merchants are trusted, and more that they're seen as not stupid enough to jeopardize such a sweet arrangement by getting on the bad side of the folks up top... I suppose that'd be the more accurate way to put it."

The one who answered Subaru's question, arms crossed, was the muscular giant of a man. Rom Gramps had traded his worn-out rags of just a little while ago for something comparatively presentable and clean, stroking his own bald head as he did. Noticing Subaru's questioning gaze at the change,

"Once you're inside the upper district, you won't draw much suspicion either way, but staying tidy still helps you avoid catching the eye. Not that you need worry about that, boy."

"Well, well, as far as my current position goes, I'm the manservant of a decent enough household right now. First trip to the royal capital, so they've spared no expense on the wardrobe, you know."

Raising a hand against the appraising look, Subaru glanced down at his current outfit and laughed.

It was formal menswear that Rem had prepared for him as part of his life as a servant at Roswaal Manor. He'd been told it was fine to get it dirty, but between the look and feel of the high-quality fabric, Subaru — a small-minded commoner at heart — had been handling this practically-brand-new outfit with more care than strictly necessary.

In the lower district, that formality had, if anything, been a factor that made him stand out badly. But in the upper district, he judged, that same incongruity would be neutralized and come across as fairly mild instead.

Satisfied with that reasoning, Subaru said "More importantly," and looked up at the now-tidied Rom Gramps.

"Still, man. Rom Gramps actually tagging along for real — that's got me pretty worried, honestly."

Bringing up what was currently his single biggest concern, Subaru let that slip out timidly.

When it came to smuggling a person into the upper district, it was Rom Gramps himself who had offered to accompany Subaru. Frankly, as someone who wanted to minimize even the slightest chance of discovery, it was a proposal Subaru would rather have declined, but—

"Hmph, when it comes to getting into the upper district, I've been around the block a fair few times. Where d'you think I've been fencing the goods that pile up in the Loot House, hm?"

"That way of putting it makes you sound like some old man going 'I used to be a delinquent too, y'know' — it's annoying. Wait, so this is a regular habit for you? That's more dangerous than reassuring, you old geezer."

Seeking backup, Subaru glanced toward Kadomon, but the gruff man had stepped away from their conversation and was deep in discussion with the driver in charge of the dragon carriage. Judging by the fact that he hadn't offered a single comment about Rom Gramps climbing aboard, this apparently wasn't the old man's first or second time pulling this trick.

The fact that the driver hadn't reacted much when Kadomon mentioned smuggling someone up to the upper district only reinforced that impression.

"In this case, should I be lamenting how slack the checkpoint guards are, or giving a thumbs-up to the merchants tough enough to exploit that laxity for a little extra coin...?"

"If trouble breaks out, it comes back on Kadomon and his people too. You can't pull off something this reckless without a fair amount of mutual trust. It's because I'm along that you get to hitch this ride, boy — go on, be grateful."

"Dragging a upstanding, promising young man like me into a life of crime with such glee — that's the textbook definition of a bad influence. I swear I'll never turn into an old geezer like you, but sure, I'll offer up some surface-level gratitude. As expected of you, Rom Gramps! Yo, your head's shining bright today too!"

"Wahaha, you mouthy little brat. Should I kick you off the cargo bed right in front of the checkpoint?"

They traded smiles with each other, then let out matching sighs at the sheer pointlessness of the exchange.

Then Subaru shrugged and steered the conversation back to where it started.

"So, seriously, why are you actually coming along? Thanks to you I've got a free pass on the dragon carriage, sure, but there's no real reason for you to be walking this dangerous a line, is there?"

This wasn't something that would have happened just because Subaru knew Kadomon. He understood full well it had only come together thanks to Rom Gramps's cooperation — which was exactly why he couldn't pin down a clear reason for the old man endorsing his recklessness like this.

At Subaru's question, Rom Gramps furrowed his brow, his expression turning grim.

"By your account, boy, that Felt girl was taken off by the 'Sword Saint,' wasn't she. If that's the case, then asking around up top seemed to me like it'd give better odds."

Rom Gramps's face soured as he said "Sword Saint." Hearing that word, Subaru recalled the image of the red-haired young man, and remembered that his residence would be up in the upper district too.

There was no doubt he came from a decent enough family — a natural consequence of being called the "Sword Saint," no less. Still, in a world where he couldn't imagine grand nameplates hanging outside front gates, tracking down the man's mansion seemed like it'd be quite the challenge.

"We don't even know which house is Reinhard's, and even if we did, it's not like we can just ring the bell and have a face-to-face chat, right? Though that pretty boy seems like the type who'd welcome even an unannounced drop-in — what do you think?"

"Only you would pull a fool stunt like that, boy. What I'll be doing is strictly reconnaissance — laying the groundwork for later."

"For... later?"

Tilting his head at Subaru, Rom Gramps gave a slight nod. "Aye."

"A stepping stone toward bringing Felt back, is what. Once we know where the mansion is, with enough money and connections, we should be able to look into the details of the place too."

"Feels like you're aiming for something just as reckless as me, just from a different angle. I mean, me too, but it feels like you're also lighting a fuse you really shouldn't be lighting, old man."

Subaru chided Rom Gramps for his extreme proposal, but objectively speaking, the two of their goals were about equally terroristic. One was scheming to sneak into the royal castle; the other was drafting an invasion plan against a noble's estate — charges serious enough that summary execution on the spot wasn't out of the question.

"As far as Felt goes, though, if it's really Reinhard, I don't think there's anything to worry about. Honestly, isn't it more likely she's living better than she was here? Could even be — like, just like me — she got picked up as a servant or something and is living it up."

He hadn't been able to tell in her grimy slum clothes, but now that Subaru thought about it, once cleaned up, Felt had struck him as having a pretty decent face. Wasn't it possible someone like Reinhard would notice that underlying prettiness, take pity on her struggling in poverty, and take her in?

Shaking his head, Subaru internally denied his own idea — that didn't seem like the kind of person who'd act out of that sort of condescending sympathy, matching against the image of Reinhard in his head. In any case, it was true that it was hard to imagine her being treated badly. But at Subaru's carefree line of thinking, Rom Gramps pressed his lips into a grim line.

"No, that won't do. Who the other party is isn't the issue here. That girl's whereabouts being up in the upper district — that alone could sooner or later bring about a bad situation."

There was something in Rom Gramps's stubborn — no, desperate — bearing that made Subaru uneasy. Part of it, sure, was concern for her safety. But it felt like there was some stronger emotion bound up in it too, something more personal.

But before Subaru could open his mouth to press for the old man's true meaning—

"Oi, we're ready. If you're serious about this, get aboard soon. Third cargo bed, if you would. Gramps knows the usual arrangement, so let him fill you in, kid."

Kadomon, having finished his talk with the driver, came back to report that preparations were complete. Rom Gramps raised a hand in acknowledgment, then fixed Subaru — who clearly had more to say — with a quiet gaze.

"Whatever the case, first we've got to get past the checkpoint, or nothing else matters. I'm saying this especially for your sake — don't go doing anything unnecessary. Lax as they are, they're still the royal capital's guards. Get caught, and it'll cost you dearly."

"Getting cut down on the spot isn't the worry, it's the fact that it can apparently be settled just by paying up that makes me sense a certain rot at the core here... I'll be careful."

Nodding despite a lingering sense of dissatisfaction, Subaru fell in behind Rom Gramps as the old man started walking.

There were four carriages bound for the upper district, sizable enough to occupy nearly half the width of Merchant Street's broad thoroughfare. The earth dragons pulling the cargo were also a different breed from any Subaru had seen before — unlike the slender, lizard-like ones, these had thick, short legs that gave Subaru the impression of a sluggish tortoise.

Four earth dragons in all, pulling four carts. Subaru and Rom Gramps were to board the third of the four dragon carriages, whose cargo bed was mainly loaded with—

"Man, this reeks — don't tell me this is a fish shipment?"

"Reeks enough to make your nose curl, which is exactly why the guards don't bother poking around in it. Convenient for us too, since it'll mask our smell. Here, don't you dare forget the deodorizing spice."

Rom Gramps handed over a small green pouch he'd been holding to Subaru, who was already grimacing at the fishy stink pouring into his nostrils. The pouch fit neatly in his palm; when Subaru sniffed it from the outside, he nearly keeled over.

A pungent smell that overpowered even the ambient stench stabbed at his nose, a throbbing pain sharp enough to make him think he might start bleeding. Subaru dropped the pouch reflexively; Rom Gramps crouched down and retrieved it.

"That there is our lifeline, don't go treating it so carelessly, you heathen."

"I feel like I just took out an advance on divine punishment — what is this stuff!? Forget 'making my nose curl,' I thought my nose was gonna fall clean off, no exaggeration!"

"Karnago Powder has a strong stink at first, sure, but rub it on your body and within a few minutes it'll wipe out that intense smell along with your own body odor. Walk around the upper district still reeking of fish, and no matter how nicely you're dressed, you'll draw ugly attention in no time. Consider it a necessary suffering and just deal with it."

Rom Gramps pressed the pouch to Subaru's chest with a spiteful grin. Subaru accepted it sullenly and, wanting to keep it as far from his nose as possible, tucked it into his trouser pocket.

Kadomon gestured impatiently at the two of them going back and forth like this. Realizing they couldn't afford to waste any more time, Subaru steeled himself and climbed onto the cargo bed.

Just as expected — no, worse than expected — the reek assaulted Subaru, and even before departure his resolve was already nearly broken.

Some sort of cooling measure was apparently at work to keep the raw fish chilled; the inside of the cart carried a chill well beyond mere brisk air. Watching the white puffs of his own breath, Subaru cast his gaze about, searching for somewhere to settle.

"Don't go looking around like that. The hiding spot's a bit past the entrance — there, behind that wooden crate. I'll be hiding too, so make yourself small."

"What did I do to deserve twenty-four hours pressed up against a muscle-bound old man... The floor's not soaked in fish juice or something, is it? This outfit's brand new."

"Given your stinginess, better that than the chance of it getting soaked in blood instead, no? The crates are full of salted fish, so it should smell a bit less bad over there too."

Rom Gramps pushed him along, telling him to hurry up. The moment his palm shoved Subaru's back coincided with a light jolt as the carriage began to move. Before retreating deeper inside, Subaru poked just his face out of the cart's entrance and gave Kadomon a final wave farewell.

Kadomon returned the gesture with a perfunctory wave of his own, then, as if signaling he didn't want to look at them anymore, turned his back and walked off in the opposite direction down Merchant Street.

Having seen that through, Subaru finally settled himself in the depths of the cargo bed — in the corner behind the designated wooden crate — and focused on keeping his racing heart in check for his first-ever smuggling run.

—Grimacing at the ache of his pounding heart, lightly regretting the recklessness of his own actions along the transport route — that was how the journey went. That Subaru's worries had turned out to be no great problem at all was proven when the driver up front secretly passed word back that they'd safely cleared the checkpoint.

It had taken barely twenty minutes since departing Merchant Street.

The whole thing went so simply it was almost anticlimactic — in fact, Subaru turned an expression of astonishment toward Rom Gramps, as if he'd just been told a joke.

"Is getting past the checkpoint really that easy? They didn't even check what's inside!"

At worst, he'd half-expected something like what he'd read in manga — spears jabbing repeatedly into the cargo bed, some violent inspection scenario — and had even prepared to lie flat on the floor, so this felt like a real letdown.

Rom Gramps snorted at Subaru's needless worry.

"Didn't I tell you, it's an unspoken arrangement. In practice, only the first three carriages actually get brought up as food supplies from Merchant Street to the upper district. That fourth carriage behind us is loaded with several items the checkpoint guards have no choice but to turn a blind eye to."

The way Rom Gramps said this, with a face full of mischief, made it plain he'd known the details of this checkpoint's lax surveillance from the very start.

Hearing that, Subaru pursed his lips, showing his displeasure at the old man's slyness.

"So it was rigged from the start, huh — I worried for nothing. Wait, going by how you're talking, does that mean what's loaded in back is bribes?"

"To put it plainly, aye, that's about the size of it. There's plenty up top who'd find it real inconvenient if this dragon carriage got stopped partway. It'd take more than a little something to hold it up."

He muttered that with his arms crossed, then turned a serious gaze on Subaru.

"That said, getting on the bad side of that lot is forbidden. Being found out and suffering for it isn't what they want either. No chance to explain yourself — just cut down without a word, and the truth buried in the dark along with you. A poor sod gone to meet his maker, understand?"

Rom Gramps was implicitly warning Subaru against reckless behavior. Even Subaru couldn't manage a wisecrack in the face of that gravity, and nodded solemnly in agreement.

The swaying dragon carriage had begun climbing a slope; the sound of the wheels biting into the ground changed to that of paved road, telling them they'd passed through the upper district entrance — cleared the noble quarter.

"We'll get off the carriage a little further on. After that, it's just me making the rounds of my contacts, gathering information in the noble quarter. ...Boy, you ought to do the same."

"...That won't accomplish what I'm after, though."

"I helped you get this far into the upper district because you pushed so hard on wanting to come. But sneaking any further up than this — as things stand, it's simply not possible. You'd just be throwing your life away for nothing. I understand you've got things pressing on you, but you're not prepared."

Struck by that plain truth, Subaru fell silent. Rom Gramps roughly patted Subaru's head with a hard palm, then, prefacing with "And another thing,"

"Charging into something without preparing — there's a way of looking at it as 'preparing to fail.' Rushing won't get you the result you want. So at the very least... you understand, don't you?"

"Getting lectured like that from the grown-up's point of view makes it hard to argue back."

Forcibly brushing the hand off his head, Subaru turned his back to the old man, sitting cross-legged in the cargo bed. Rom Gramps gave a wry smile at the sulky attitude, but peered outside through the gap in the cart's canvas cover, and judging it was about time for their planned drop-off point,

"There, time to get off. Stopping the dragon carriage on purpose would look unnatural. It's moving slow enough that I don't think you'll get hurt, but you'll need to run alongside it as you get down, matching its speed."

He slipped his arm through the gap in the canvas, clearing space for himself to slide out. Sunlight poured into the cargo bed that had been shut off from light, and Subaru, wincing at the sting to his eyes, looked outside.

Just as he'd imagined from the sensation of paved road, the noble quarter of the upper district was fundamentally, categorically different from the towns of the lower districts. Unlike Merchant Street or the slums, packed tight with simply-built buildings, each and every building in the noble quarter was vastly larger, its grounds far more expansive. Wide open space that, from Subaru's perspective, seemed nothing but wasteful sat idle, with a building settled at its center.

It resembled the atmosphere of a grand mansion out of a story. He didn't spot any building that quite rivaled the size of Roswaal Manor, but then again, that manor was precisely the sort of self-indulgent creation Roswaal had built out of pointlessly using up huge amounts of space. One more piece of frivolity that would fail to be maintained at all, if not for the excellence of the Ram and Rem sisters.

I see — witnessing all this, Subaru felt in his very skin, in a real, concrete sense, the difference between the upper and lower districts. This, surely, was proof that the sort of gap between nobility and commoners you'd see in a story was rampant even here, in the royal capital of Lugunica.

It wasn't a great impression, what with the bribes in the carriage behind them included, but the fact remained he was riding along on the coattails of that wrongdoing himself. He wasn't exactly a paragon of integrity and purity to begin with. Easily settling that internal conflict, Subaru focused his attention on getting down from the carriage.

"Well, think you can manage it?"

"I've got prior convictions when it comes to failed attempts at falling off carriages. ...I'm a little scared, so if you'd show me an example, I'd be grateful enough to send you a cash reward."

"Not putting on a false front — that's a good trait of yours. Fine, watch closely."

Nodding at Subaru's honest confession of his own cowardice, Rom Gramps went down first.

Keeping one hand hooked on the edge of the cargo bed, he leaned his body out; the old body moved with practiced ease, sending itself entirely outside. After that, he gripped the cargo bed with both arms, timed it, and set his feet on the ground. Left as is, one would expect him to be dragged along by the carriage and further caught up in the wheels for a gruesome mincemeat disaster—

"No public execution parade after all... could it be?"

"The Divine Protection's at work, y'see. Long as you don't let go, you won't get thrown off so easily. And—"

Just like the carriage they'd ridden on the way to the royal capital, this dragon carriage too apparently had the Divine Protection of wind at work. Without showing any strain from having his body outside, Rom Gramps broke into an easy run, matching the dragon carriage's pace.

The carriage's speed was roughly equivalent to someone pedaling a bicycle slowly. Faster than a light jog, but considerably slower than a short-term full sprint. Even for Rom Gramps, whose stride was considerably larger than an average person's, matching the pace wasn't a difficult task.

He let go, and Rom Gramps's body fully left the dragon carriage's protection. At once, the natural flow of inertia took hold of his massive frame, but for Rom Gramps, who'd anticipated the effect, its force was negligible. Without so much as a stumble in his stance, he kept up with the carriage at a run.

Watching that dazzling display of skill, Subaru couldn't help an admiring exhale. As Subaru expressed his praise for Rom Gramps like that, the old man, running alongside the carriage, looked up and clapped his hands.

"There, follow my example and come on down. If you like, I'll catch you nice and gently."

Words meant, perhaps, to needle Subaru's competitive streak into action. But Subaru, on the receiving end of that, only looked down at Rom Gramps through the gap in the canvas cover — smiling.

He showed no sign of following Rom Gramps's example, of hooking a hand on the edge and swinging his body out.

Was he secretly quailing inside at having to pull off some unexpectedly acrobatic feat? Such a suspicion creased Rom Gramps's brow into a frown, but by the time the old man's jogging pursuit of the carriage had covered over three hundred meters, he caught on to what Subaru was actually planning.

A vein bulged on his furrowed brow, his face reddening, spittle flying as he shouted,

"S-so that's it, boy! You're planning to ride this all the way into the royal castle without getting off, aren't you!?"

Seeing through Subaru's true intentions, Rom Gramps bellowed, his running face full of alarm.

Letting the words wash over him, Subaru neither confirmed nor denied them. But his wordless attempt to close the canvas flap made plain enough that it was, in fact, true.

—The dragon carriage was carrying its cargo into the royal castle.

He might have caught himself afterward and tried to walk it back several times over, as if realizing his slip, but Subaru's ears hadn't forgotten the fact Kadomon had let slip at the very start. This carriage's destination was the royal castle, and it was all but certain the cargo being transported would be brought into the castle's food stores. If that turned out wrong, well, so be it — that plan was still surely far higher-probability than getting off with Rom Gramps in the noble quarter and searching from there for a way into the castle.

Rom Gramps, having caught on to Subaru's scheme, kept up the chase, but once the location shifted from the noble quarter toward the very center of the city, he had no choice but to give up pursuit.

Just as Subaru had a purpose he couldn't give up on, Rom Gramps had reasons of his own that took priority. Causing a conspicuous scene here wouldn't serve either of their interests.

Understanding that full well, Subaru — watching that broad back peel off the road with a bitter expression and slip its massive frame into a corner of a side street — felt a pang of guilt.

In the end, this meant trampling on both Kadomon's good will and Rom Gramps's consideration. Given the maximum help the two of them had offered, it would be no surprise if this were judged as a betrayal in response. And yet, Subaru had used their kindness anyway, and had no intention of making excuses for it now.

—Getting to Emilia's side.

That, and that alone, was the single priority for Subaru right now. Every problem lying in his own path, for that purpose, was nothing more than a clause to be disregarded. The royal capital was a distorted land where danger ran rampant. Leaving Emilia exposed, unguarded, in a place like that — that wasn't something Subaru could stomach, emotionally.

If Subaru could at least be by her side, maybe there was something he could do.

Or perhaps — Subaru unconsciously thought — "Even if it doesn't work out, there's a chance I can somehow make it work."

—Never noticing just how arrogant, how inhuman a thought that was.

The dragon carriage, oblivious to the trivial dispute that had just occurred among its cargo, rolled leisurely along the smooth paved road toward the royal castle. Carrying enough gunpowder to make the discovery of a mere bribe seem downright adorable by comparison, slowly, ever so slowly.

Having overcome such a sorrowful parting with the benefactors who'd helped him, Subaru now had to face his trial alone.

Countless dangers and hardships stood in his way; amid them, friendships and love would be born. And lives, ambitions, dreams would be extinguished — trampling past all that, forging ahead while bleeding—

"—was the kind of development I'd been hoping for, ideally, but is this actually really bad?"

Having slipped out of the castle's food stores with embarrassing ease, walking through the castle interior with its sieve-like security, all Subaru could do was express his exasperation with a sigh.

The journey by dragon carriage had continued about thirty minutes after leaving Rom Gramps behind, and the carriage, carrying Subaru along with it, had waltzed straight into the royal castle grounds without a hitch.

Along the way, only the fourth carriage had peeled off the route en route to the castle — Subaru judged that must have been off delivering its load of bribes by some other route. In that sense, it was fortunate the first three carriages' destination hadn't been anywhere other than the castle — Subaru himself had had his life spared, so to speak.

"Guess cohabiting with salted fish was worth it after all. Getting salt all over my body was a miscalculation, but I handled it with the deodorizer."

Feeling like there was still white powder clinging to various parts of his body, Subaru nervously brushed it off while praising his own quick thinking.

Naturally, once inside the royal castle, they couldn't avoid opening the cargo bed when unloading the goods. Facing the possibility of being caught red-handed right then and there, Subaru had escaped that danger by climbing inside a crate of salted fish. In truth, they had checked the contents of the crate right next to Subaru's, and it had been nothing short of a miraculous stroke of luck that they hadn't checked the crate he was hiding in.

After that, the movement of the cargo itself apparently made use of applied wind magic, carried into the food stores without any direct human handling. Thanks to that, no one noticed the oddity of an unnaturally heavy crate, and this hybrid atrocity of recklessness layered on recklessness had somehow, against all odds, succeeded.

For Subaru, this was a stroke of luck, but the castle's utter lack of vigilance immediately tied into worry over the safety of Emilia, who was presumably somewhere inside — not something he could celebrate unreservedly. Factoring all that in, Subaru slipped out of the food stores and, choosing areas with few people around, made his way through the underground passages like this.

"Well, now that I've snuck in, though... I've got absolutely no plan."

Frankly, having actually made it here, what to do next was a complete blank. If he could just watch over Emilia from the shadows, that would be the best option — but he didn't imagine he could reach the heart of the royal castle, where she presumably was, without being spotted at all.

At worst, Subaru harbored the rather shameless calculation that even if he were discovered partway, so long as he could somehow reunite with Emilia in the confusion, she wouldn't just have him tossed aside.

Once he'd made it this far, surely even Emilia, however angry she might be, wouldn't try to send him packing. He could apologize with all sincerity and rely on her mercy. Having come this far, Subaru's thinking veered optimistic. There wasn't a trace of remorse visible in it, and looking at him now, whether that was fortunate or not, no one could say how others might judge him.

And then—

"Crap, someone's coming...!"

Sensing a presence up ahead, Subaru hastily cast his gaze around. The place was an underground corridor, presumably a plain space meant only for servants' use. Amid a row of shabbily-built doors, Subaru instinctively slipped into a nearby room.

Fortunately, the room he'd ducked into was empty. The cramped space looked like some sort of changing room used by servants — several closets stood in a row, and a sweet fragrance hinted, with a faint sense of transgression, that it was a women's room. But he had no leisure now to savor that sweet sensation. Subaru hurriedly opened a closet at the back, confirmed there was nothing inside, and quietly hid himself within.

All that was left was to hold his breath and wait for the presence that had appeared in the corridor to pass by. Even while thinking optimistically that he'd manage somehow if found, he'd put himself in a situation where being found wouldn't end lightly at all. His resolve was wavering for lack of any solid plan to fall back on, but that was a trivial concern to him right now.

For now, he'd just get through this situation, and worry about coming forward or taking some other action after. But such short-sighted thinking on Subaru's part—

"—This room reeks."

—was easily brought to a halt by a roughly-opened door and a woman's voice.

A sensation like his heart might leap right out of his mouth from the tension. Feeling the blood drain away with an almost audible rush, Subaru clenched his teeth hard enough to nearly rattle against the despair welling up in him.

Unlike changing room lockers, this closet had no convenient peephole to let him peek outside from within. Which meant Subaru, in the darkness, had no means of imagining the situation outside beyond the other party's actions.

His throat threatening to close up with tension, Subaru once again regretted his own actions. He'd been careless, he'd been arrogant, he'd let it go to his head. He'd gotten carried away.

Now that he was actually cornered like this, the sheer stupidity of his own rash actions became abundantly clear. Why had he calmly gone through with something this reckless? He couldn't fathom the reasoning behind any of his judgments from just a little while ago.

And yet, even while reaching that conclusion through cold reason amid his confusion, Subaru couldn't bring himself to choose the option of coming forward to the person outside.

He still couldn't let go of the hope — thinner even than a spider's thread — that maybe, just maybe, his presence hadn't been noticed yet.

Tormented by that desperate, unbearable feeling, Subaru held his breath, and then—

"—I'll only wait five seconds. My princess has even less patience than I do."

Through the closed closet door, a muffled voice came, delivering that pronouncement.

The tone had a somewhat lazy quality to it, but the sound of a blade being drawn from its sheath, ringing out in the silence, proved there was no bluff in the intent behind it.

He didn't have the nerve to write this off as a bluff and gamble on staying silent, hoping the situation would blow over on a thread of hope.

"Five, four, skip the rest—"

"Wait, stop, hold on, I can explain! —I'm coming out, coming out right now, just wait!"

Panicked at the voice hurriedly moving to swing the blade, Subaru fearfully pushed the door open. Slowly, the closet creaked, letting a cool breeze flow into the space thick with stale odor, gently caressing that despairing feeling for just an instant.

Of course, what followed next would be an interrogation that admitted no excuses. Given what Subaru had gone and done, there was no way this would be settled with a light charge. He hadn't failed to anticipate this, but it was also true he'd been optimistically thinking he could somehow muddle through. Before he could be struck down without a chance to argue, he ought to play whatever card he could — with that thought forming in Subaru's mind—

"See, Al? Just as I said, was it not?"

A haughty voice he recognized rang out, and as Subaru couldn't help but look up, before him stood an orange-haired girl, smiling with insolent confidence, pointing some fan-like accessory in her hand right at him.

"That we'd meet this clown again before long. It came sooner than I expected, I'll admit, to my surprise."

"Ah, well, yeah. As expected, princess, you've got a good eye. And you — you've got rotten luck."

The middle-aged man standing at her side — clad, as before, in his bizarre black helmet and light armor, Al — voiced agreement with the girl in the first half, and in the second half let his tone carry a note of sympathy toward Subaru.

Maybe he'd at least managed to avoid being cut down without a chance to explain. That sense of relief washed over Subaru's entire body, and before he knew it, he'd sunk right down where he stood, letting out a long breath as a dry laugh escaped him.
