Arc 9 Chapter 19: "Outwitting One Another"

"—I love you."

At that word of love — a true kotodama, not merely skin-deep — the world made a sound as though it were being repainted.

"——"

In truth, no actual "sound" was ringing out. The sound of something cracking. The sound of something smashed to pieces. The sound of something torn apart. The sound of something ripped open. The sound of something slashed through. The sound of something crushed flat. The sound of something pulverized. The sound of something split asunder. The sound of something bored through and gouged out. The sound of something hurled down and shattered. The sound of something—

"—I love you I love you I love you."

In truth, no actual "sound" was ringing out. It was only that the affection offered up from that black shadow was an invasion of the soul at the very center of this world — an invasion so total that it created the illusion of hearing the "sound" of everything being lost.

A world in which everything, absolutely everything, was being swallowed whole. A world in which everything fell, and kept falling, into a darkness whose nature and depth were beyond knowing. There, in that deep, deep darkness that seemed to have no bottom at all, the soul was made to understand — that everything, the precious things and the worthless things alike, had melted away into it. And yet—

Was it weakness, not wanting to admit it? —No. It was kindness. Was it weakness, not being able to give up? —No. It was kindness.

Was it weakness, not being able to refuse love? —No. It was kindness.

"—I love you I love you I love you I love you."

Against that love, whispered without end, a voice rose in refusal, with all its strength. But it knew. It couldn't help but know. That even that refusal, given with all its strength, had failed to refuse completely.

"I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you I love you."

And in it, a glimpse of possibility, however unwanted. Even if that person's heart already had its first, second, and even third place filled, the glimpse came all the same. A cowardly, shameless heart that would try to take advantage of that — and it wanted no part of sympathizing with it.

Where such self-serving feeling always, always led—

"I love you. I love you. —Love me."

—See? There it was.

"Love me. Love me. Love me. Love me. Love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me love me——love me"

It had known this was how it would end. It was as plain as if you could hold it in your hand. Clearer than staring into fire. Like twisting a baby's arm. Easier than crushing a tometo, easier than opening closed eyelids, easier than breathing in and out.

It had known this was how it would end. Because that wasn't for the other person's sake. Because that was for its own sake. If it didn't have to consider the other person at all, if it could fill both its head and its heart with nothing but itself — that was how it would end.

Even though that was, above all else, embarrassing enough to set your face on fire.

"—I hate you."

—Its heart felt as if it would freeze solid.

"I will never, ever love you."

—As if those words had been spoken to no one else but itself, it felt as if it would die.

But even that fear, that felt-like-dying terror, was — compared to what waited just beyond it — so much sweeter, sweeter, sweeter, so much, much, much, much sweeter. Because—

"Petra's... handkerchief...?"

—Because it knew, with the certainty of a rope being fastened around its neck, that the moment had come.

—A welcoming party of roughly five hundred men.

That was the troublesome formation Felt had prepared to intercept Aldebaran. Naturally, the moment Flam had been left alive, it was a given that word of Aldebaran's betrayal would reach Reinhard and bring him rushing over. And it was just as obvious that, in the course of things, the same information would reach Felt and the Council of Wise Men.

To eliminate that foreseeable, formidable enemy, Aldebaran had gone so far as to use his greatest asset — "Aldebaran" himself — as a decoy card, meaning to clear his own path forward. And that plan was being blown apart by the unexpected initiative of a girl he'd underestimated.

"Damn, but the world's got way too many star players I can't hold a candle to."

"This ain't the time for jokes, damn it!"

When Aldebaran shrugged and grumbled that, Hainkel bellowed at him — no, actually, the one Hainkel was shouting at wasn't him, but Yae. His furious outburst at Yae's proposed human-sacrifice tactic had, by sheer coincidence, landed right on top of Aldebaran's grumble. Anyway—

"Like the old man says, this isn't the time for jokes. Let's deal with it."

Tightening his cheeks inside his helmet, Aldebaran set aside his lament over the unreasonableness of the world, called out to his two companions, and opened a war council. A pale-faced Hainkel and a Yae wearing an unreadable, faint smile — these two, plus the talentless Aldebaran, were the hand left to them after using their main force as a decoy card.

"A little thin, sure, but their side isn't exactly fielding its best lineup either."

"Deal with it, you say — how? To begin with, the enemy is—"

"Roughly five hundred strong. Doesn't look like knights or guards, but they're rough types, used to violence. And there's no doubt it's little missy Felt on the other side."

"Five hundred, huh~. That's quite something."

Yae's admiration, finger pressed to her lips, was probably at how well they'd managed to gather that many people in such a short time. Aldebaran was in complete agreement — without executing something close to the best possible move at every turn, there was no way anyone could have assembled a situation like this.

"Wait, wait, wait! Five hundred!? And not even soldiers?! What the hell's going on. And how'd you even get that information in the first place—"

"Stop right there, old man. Doesn't matter how I know the enemy's numbers. What matters is it's the truth. Besides, we made a promise at the start, didn't we? If you want the Dragon's Blood, you don't go against my policy."

"Ngh...!"

"A man who can't keep his promises gets hated, y'know?"

Pressed once more, Hainkel fell silent, though his discontent was plain to see. Hainkel's state was that of a horse running after a carrot dangled in front of its face. The difference from a horse was that Hainkel's craving could be satisfied by nothing but that specific carrot. Which was why Hainkel couldn't defy Aldebaran, the one holding the carrot's string.

"So, now that Hainkel-sama's gone quiet, what do we do? Five hundred people is pretty rough, you know?"

"You're supposed to be Vollachia's masterpiece of a Shinobi, aren't you. Can't you do something about it?"

"Masterpiece or not, I did fail my assassination~? Besides, mowing down a hundred or a thousand people on a battlefield isn't a Shinobi's job. I'm not the 'Blue Lightning,' after all."

"Well, true enough."

The "Blue Lightning" that Yae, pouting, had brought up — Cecilus — was someone Aldebaran had, if only briefly, spent time alongside. His strength was, just as Yae described it — half joke, half exaggeration — that of someone in the same incomprehensible, off-the-scale category as Reinhard. With power like his, one could probably punch straight through even a thousand enemies head-on, but unfortunately, Aldebaran had let go of such lofty ambitions back in his teenage years. Which was why—

"Realistically speaking, waltzing out to where five hundred people are lying in wait would be idiotic. We've got a forest to work with — we'll take a route around, so we're not spotted."

Given the tone of the voice that had called out, playing dead was probably a lost cause. But if they'd had Aldebaran's group's exact position pinned down, they'd surely have sprung an ambush back at Grassis. Since that hadn't happened, it meant they knew Aldebaran's group was somewhere in the forest, but not more than that. In that case, depending on how they maneuvered, they might even be able to split up the five hundred further—

"Ahh~, Al-sama, this might be a bit of a problem."

Just as he'd settled on a policy and was about to move, Yae gave his sleeve a couple of tugs. Aldebaran turned to see what it was, and she, gazing steadily toward the direction where Felt's group lay in wait, her eyes tinged with wariness, told him. It was—

"Looks like they're moving pretty fast over there."

Where Yae's gaze pointed, Aldebaran saw the same thing she did. —Firewood trailing white smoke, being hurled one after another into the forest.

△▼△▼△▼△

"Well, wasn't exactly expecting them to just come out anyway."

So Felt grumbled, standing in the middle of the group deployed on the plain, glaring at the forest where the enemy lurked. Whether it was really right to flat-out call helmet-guy the "enemy" was something she still had some hesitation about, even now. But Al wasn't a friend or comrade who'd gone off the rails, nor was he an acquaintance she had any deep relationship with. More than anything, calling him the enemy was simply more convenient for raising the morale of the people she'd gathered.

So Felt called helmet-guy — Al — the enemy, plainly and without hesitation. And that enemy was inside the forest Felt's group had surrounded.

"So, this is for sure, right? Otherwise I'm just some embarrassing idiot yellin' greetings at an empty forest."

"Ha ha ha, stand tall now, 'Golden Lion.' True enough, it works through one eye alone. Still, narrow the range this far for me, and it comes into view, wanted or not."

The one who answered Felt, mixing in a somewhat distinctive accent, was a man named Manfred Madison, head of "The Balance." "The Balance" apparently had a custom of tattooing a balance-scale somewhere on the body as proof of loyalty to the organization, but Manfred had displayed his proof of loyalty to a wildly excessive degree. His shaved head, his face, his neck, even his eyeballs were tattooed — and presumably everything under his clothes was the same. And it wasn't only his appearance that was bizarre. —So was the very way he existed.

"—'The Divine Protection of Farsight.'"

Manfred's left eye rolled with a bulging motion — the one eyeball, so far as could be seen, without any tattoo on it — and glared steadily at the forest. Not long before, that same left eye of his had held a tattooed eyeball. The reason it didn't now was simple — the eyeball fitted in it now was a different one. The eyeball Manfred had in now was the left eye of a man who used to be his subordinate—

"As expected — another's Divine Protection does not settle in so easily, no. But see it, I did."

"That's handy, but super gross. How d'you even do that?"

"Blunt words, I do not dislike. —Only, how one steals a Divine Protection remains a secret of 'The Balance.'"

Without touching on the method itself, Manfred confirmed the enemy's presence in the forest. As someone who'd once suffered at the hands of that "Divine Protection of Farsight," Felt had no room to doubt the information it provided.

"If they're not coming out..."

"There's nothing for it but to make them want to."

Cracking his thick neck, Old Man Rom picked up where Felt's words left off. The great strategist who'd built a plan around getting maximum use out of Manfred's incomplete "Divine Protection of Farsight" had already prepared the next move against the enemy holed up in the forest. And it was—

"Green pyro-tree wood is hard to burn all the way through, and it smokes something fierce. Douse it with water and it won't stop putting out smoke easily. Perfect for this, I'd say."

The plan Old Man Rom put into words like that was simple and clear: smoke Al out. Led mainly by the muscle-proud Gaston and his band of rough types, lit pyro-tree logs were hurled one after another into the forest. Just as Old Man Rom said, the logs did nothing but pour out smoke without the fire spreading to their surroundings, sending up thick, billowing clouds of white smoke.

"Smoke's effect doesn't stop at robbing you of your sight. There's a mental toll too, but the worst of it's the suffering when you breathe it in."

"Coughing your lungs out, that's rough as hell, yeah."

"Aye, agony straight out of hell, I'd wager. And on top of that—"

As he spoke, Old Man Rom narrowed his eyes. There, in the direction he was looking, Camberley and his girlfriend, Toto — mistress of the Flower Prison Garden — were giving orders to their subordinates, sending a magically conjured wind, raised all at once, into the forest. The wind they created didn't mow down the trees; it drifted softly through the gaps between them, carving out a path for itself — that is, a path for the smoke — so the white smoke spread throughout the whole forest.

"As far as I know, there's no small number of folk who can shrug off poison or a deep wound. But in all my needlessly long life, I've never once known anyone who could calmly endure the suffering of being smoked out."

Seeing the aim and progress of his own proposed strategy playing out, Old Man Rom said this in a low voice. Felt listened to it with satisfaction, while at the same time realizing all over again just how outrageously excellent her foster father was at dangerous knowledge.

Felt didn't know what Old Man Rom used to do, back in the day. But she understood that it wasn't the kind of thing he could speak of with his chest puffed out in pride, and that Old Man Rom himself looked back on that past with bitterness. So, until Old Man Rom chose to speak of it himself, Felt wouldn't ask. Without asking, all she'd do was say this:

"See? —My Old Man Rom's a tough old bastard, ain't he."

"—Smoke."

Watching the thrown logs — the ones putting up white smoke — slowly creep through the forest, Aldebaran was struck by grudging admiration for the enemy's nasty adaptability. Being surrounded by a huge number of people was bad enough, but this was one more piece of bad news piled right on top.

"Th-those bastards, they set a fire! They're gonna burn us alive!"

"Please calm down. A fire attack would make it impossible to confirm the bodies, and they'd risk getting caught up in it themselves. That's a foolish plan on top of a foolish plan — a poor strategy, in other words."

"Maybe the enemy went and used that poor strategy anyway! She's some kid from the slums, isn't she!? People from there don't think about consequences!"

"Geeez, Al-sama!"

"I know! Old man, just be quiet for a second!"

With the drifting smoke smoking out what little calm Hainkel had left, Aldebaran forcibly berated him into silence, panicked as he was. Hainkel couldn't defy Aldebaran out of his craving for the Dragon's Blood. Still, panicking in a truly desperate, do-or-die crisis probably couldn't be treated the same as mere griping or objection.

"—I agree with Yae. This has to be a smoke attack, not a fire attack, in aim. Though the bad news is there's no way to endure a smoke attack. Yae, how about you?"

"Whether I can endure smoke, you mean? No way, no way. I've done poison-resistance training and torture countermeasures and all that, but getting teary-eyed and coughing from breathing in smoke isn't something training fixes, y'know?"

"Yeah, figured. So, nothing for it but to run from the smoke."

Given that they had to avoid being smoked to death, there was no other conclusion to reach. The problem, though, was that this was exactly what the enemy wanted. —Naturally enough, whether it's a fire attack or a smoke attack, fleeing from the danger right in front of you is simple human instinct. And the unnatural wind that had come barreling in alongside the smoke was an untouchable hunter, meant to drive Aldebaran's group along with the smoke—

"Figured even five hundred people couldn't surround the whole forest...!"

Smoke to fumigate them, and wind to guide that smoke. With just those two things, you could drive even a small band like Aldebaran's, or a decent-sized force for that matter, toward whatever position you were aiming for. Felt had, in this way, completed a siege net around the forest — one that even five hundred people alone couldn't have pulled off.

"Or rather — it's the brain behind the problem, isn't it. Damn it, they really pulled it off."

Clicking his tongue in bitter frustration, Aldebaran pictured a map of the world in his head. The destination lay far, far to the west from here — he'd already spent a full day on rest and travel, and his limit had dropped from seven days to six. He couldn't afford to get his feet caught here by Felt more than necessary, and settle into a drawn-out fight.

"Al-sama!"

"—Yae, give me a read on the wind's flow! Old man, follow Yae's instructions and charge straight ahead in the lead! You can cut down any trees in your way!"

"G-got it."

"That answer was too quiet~!"

"I said got it!!"

Overwhelmed by Aldebaran's sharp orders, Hainkel shouted back half out of desperation. Just like that, following Yae's instructions, Hainkel drew his sword and literally carved a path open with it. Chasing after that sturdy back, Aldebaran thought things through.

Since they were about to make a big move, resetting the region was necessary — but updating the matrix meant losing the path back to before this smoke tactic had been carried out. At the very least, in the branch where Aldebaran left the forest on his own initiative, Felt hadn't carried out the smoking tactic. Of course, in that case, it would've meant a head-on collision with five hundred people.

"Which one's the lesser evil?"

—In the end, Aldebaran's victory was a guaranteed thing. There was no one, anywhere on this earth, who could beat an Aldebaran who had sealed away even the Sword Saint Reinhard van Astrea and rendered even Natsuki Subaru powerless. So Aldebaran's victory was absolute. —All that remained was what, beyond mere victory, he wanted to pursue. Which path offered the higher probability of accomplishing that goal, the one beyond mere victory?

"Al-sama, if you let him go alone, Hainkel-sama's gonna die, y'know?"

"—...True enough. Let's go!"

His deliberation lasted only an instant; Aldebaran nodded at Yae's call and broke into a run. At the same moment, in that instant, he updated the matrix, redefined it — fixing the next starting point — and casually discarded any lingering attachment to the possibility that had existed just before.

Life is a chain of choices. That's true for anyone, not only Aldebaran. And most choices are made in an instant, with the option not chosen never given a second thought. But that's fine. Aldebaran, too, made a point of doing the same, as much as he possibly could.

What to have for breakfast tomorrow. Whether to put on the right sock or the left one first. He treated choices like that — casual, everyday things — the same as choices made in a fight where his own life was on the line. There was no point clinging to the option he hadn't chosen, the option that hadn't been chosen.

Both Aldebaran and Natsuki Subaru simply happened to have more chances than most people to cling to their options. —Unless he accepted that and nothing more, he'd end up stuck treading water forever.

"Hainkel-sama, the footing's getting bad, so go right. That big tree too, if you would."

"Don't make it sound so easy! Damn it!"

"Oooh~, amazing! Just as expected, you're strong against anything that isn't alive!"

Even though he'd cut down the big tree exactly as instructed, Hainkel found himself, for some reason, being taunted, and gnashed his teeth. Chasing after that profile, Aldebaran noticed the smoke gradually growing denser — the wind had begun closing the smoke in around them. In other words—

"—An ambush is close."

—Rachins, leading the ambush unit, deployed at the forest's exit.

Honestly, he was full of the urge to scream that he'd been handed a job way too heavy for him. To begin with, the very fact that his life had come to intersect with things like the "Sword Saint," the "Divine Dragon," or the "Witch of Envy" felt, to Rachins, like a nightmare.

"Damn it all... where the hell did my life get so twisted up?"

Scratching roughly at his head, letting his complaints spill out endlessly until they soaked even the ground beneath his feet, Rachins irritably trampled the weeds under his boot soles. It was petty venting, plain and simple. But infuriatingly, this weed, even ground under his boot again and again, only bent its stem softly without ever getting crushed or torn. If anything, it pushed right back against the sole of his boot, insisting on its own vitality.

A shameless way of existing that could only be called weed-like, through and through — and in it, Rachins felt he could line up any number of familiar faces, and sighed.

Before he knew it, he'd walked, somewhere along the way, quite far off. But no matter where the wind scattered its seeds and no matter where it put down roots, a weed was a weed. Its leaves might change a little in color, its stem in thickness, but a weed never turned into a tree or a flower.

"The hell, so no matter where I end up, I can only live my life the way I live it, huh?"

No one in particular had said it, and yet it felt like everyone had, so Rachins clicked his tongue. Except that click of the tongue held none of the gloomy, sullen color it had held just before. All that was left was the considerable hassle that had been thrust on him, and the resolve to see it through. And so, having settled into a kind of defiant acceptance—

"—Hey, there's movement."

The one who called that out was one of the rough men deployed on the plain alongside Rachins. A man of the Flanders underworld, the same breed as Gaston, Camberley, and Rachins — no, if anything, purer, more thoroughgoing villains than any of them. Strange company to be standing shoulder to shoulder with, but men so well-versed in violence were a reliable presence here.

Drawn along by their reaction, Rachins turned his eyes to the forest too. If the plan went as intended, helmet-guy and his companions, driven out by the smoke, would come bursting out from there. Crushing them with sheer numbers was Rachins's group's—

"—Oh—"

In the next instant, what came flying out of the forest wasn't helmet-guy. —It was a cannonball. —No, that wasn't right either. To be precise, it was a single great tree, hurled with the force of a cannon shot, spinning end over end as it bore down on Rachins's line.

"——"

Its trunk was thicker than Gaston's own body, its length more than ten meters — an utterly unlovable cannonball of a tree, driving straight for the very center of the line. Seeing it bearing down on them, the rough men cried out and scrambled to flee. But—

"—El Goa!"

A booming explosion rang out through the sky, and a crimson flower of fire blossomed against the blue. It shattered the incoming tree to splinters, transforming what should have blasted a gaping hole in their line into nothing worse than a scattering of fragments that would merely sting on impact. And the one who'd done it was Rachins, finger pointed up at the sky.

"Don't you dare go chicken on me! In times like this, whoever flinches first loses! You lot get told that all the damn time too, don't you!?"

Barking that at the men who'd gone timid from what just happened, Rachins drew the knife at his waist. Pointing its blade toward the forest, he turned his head, scanning the men around him. "You ready?"

Honestly, partly because of his own inner turmoil, the rough men's attitude toward Rachins — the one entrusted with running the show here — had been openly dismissive. Now was when he'd wipe that away.

"You saw that just now, so you get it — the good draw landed in our hands! Helmet-guy's about to come crawling out any second now. There's no time to slack off—"

—was what he was a breath away from finishing with, "there's none."

"Hey!!" someone shouted, and the rough men's eyes slid off him again. Fuming about it, Rachins narrowed his sanpaku eyes in anger and turned to face forward.

One after another, tree-trunk cannonballs just as big as before — or bigger — came flying in.

"Goddammit all!!"

Right after the curse left his mouth, several flowers of fire bloomed in the sky — and the ones he missed blew the line apart. Catching sight, out of the corner of his eye, of several rough men flung about by the impact, Rachins clicked his tongue and pointed his knife at the forest again. The situation was changing dizzyingly fast. If the enemy kept scavenging cannon fodder like this on the spot, it would be Rachins's side getting run over at will.

"—Let's go, don't fall behind!!"

Roaring with a force that threatened to crack his own voice, Rachins let out a battle cry and charged forward. He did it to stoke their will to fight. —Not his own, but the fighting spirit of the men around him.

It's a common enough thing, but in any brawl between rough men, whoever gets hit first is in trouble. Strange as it is, the instant even the most violent man gets struck, his strength drops. The idea that the more wounded you get, the more you bleed, the stronger you become — that's just a matter of image. In reality, people grow weaker in exact proportion to how many hits they've taken. The same held true on the level of morale. Right now, Rachins's group had already taken the first hit, in a way none of them had expected. That weakness from being struck first had to be overwritten with sheer momentum. And so—

"WAAAARGH!! FOLLOW MEEEE!!"

The hot-blooded types were simple creatures, and Rachins's emotional shout was quick to spark them into action. Fiercely, they chased after Rachins running in the lead, weapons in hand, closing in, closing in, closing in on the forest. Feeling them at his back, Rachins backed it up with even more showmanship—

"—Al Goa!!"

Raising his hand to the heavens, Rachins chanted that in a voice that sounded like it was tearing blood from his throat. In that instant, what was born above Rachins's head was a massive, massive fireball — a spell of the Goa family of fire magic, whose power rises from the base form, through El and Ul, with Al at the very peak. It was the sort of knowledge anyone with even a little familiarity with magic would know — which was exactly why the feat of Rachins actually chanting it and producing that massive fire was such a considerable achievement.

The rough men's morale visibly surged all at once, and to further stoke it, Rachins let the fireball hovering overhead rise, deliberately sending it soaring high into the sky. Naturally, aiming right for it, a fresh volley of felled trees came flying out from deep in the forest, targeting that fireball. No matter how many trees it took, they meant to knock that fireball down—

"Idiots."

Sticking out his tongue, its tip decorated with a colored ring, Rachins jeered at the enemy's overeagerness. Tree-cannonballs came shooting out of the forest with the force of an endless barrage, but most of them ended up striking nothing. Because with the very first shot, "Al Goa" — a mere papier-mâché fake, a flattened, stretched-out Goa made to merely look the part — was already shattered, and the remaining shots did nothing but pointlessly gouge up the plain.

All of it was to make the enemy waste their cannon fodder shooting down the fake Al Goa, minimizing to the utmost degree the damage raining down on Rachins and his rough men. The scheme worked perfectly, and Rachins and his rough men plunged straight into the forest in one rush.

"We took some losses in that first wild volley, but...!"

Even so, thanks in part to Rachins's covering fire, the fifty-man band of rough men made it in almost entirely unscathed. And just like that—

"—Aah, geez, you all came charging in just because Hainkel-sama panicked, didn't you~."

The instant that sweet, languid woman's voice reached his ears, Rachins's feet had already stopped. It wasn't that a bad feeling had seized his whole body and made him stop in his tracks without meaning to. It was true that a bad feeling had seized his whole body — but he hadn't stopped of his own will.

His whole body had simply, abruptly, lost all freedom of movement. And not just Rachins, either. The fifty rough men who'd charged into the forest alongside him — all of them, all at once. And the one who'd done it was—

"No matter how much better I am at many-against-one, there's such a thing as a limit, seriously."

So said the red-haired woman, looking down through the gaps between the trees at the now-frozen Rachins and his men. A slender woman in a maid outfit with a somewhat wafu-style design, watching the men — sealed in place, voices rising in confusion and fury. —Hovering in midair.

The woman's figure was up at the height of the trees. But she wasn't standing on any trunk or branch as a foothold. She stood upright, brazenly, in empty space, watching the immobilized Rachins and his men with a smile. Confirming this by barely managing to move his neck and his eyes, Rachins understood.

"I can only bind up about a hundred people at once, roughly speaking, y'know?"

—that a woman whose outrageous combat skill was undeniable, but whose personality was even worse, was traveling right alongside the helmet-guy they were supposed to bring down.

△▼△▼△▼△

An attack that might best be called a "fallen-tree cannon," making use of the trees Hainkel had cut down.

Pulling that off without the off-the-charts mana of the Divine Dragon required improvisational skill on a level nothing like just throwing together fried rice from whatever's in the fridge.

"Honestly, Al-sama really works me hard. Combine 'harsh' and 'work' and you get 'hard work' — and I do mean hard."

With the cooperation of Yae, who couldn't seem to help slipping in a complaint at every turn, they floated the many great trees Hainkel had felled into the air, preparing a launch platform. And for the actual launch, they mobilized every bit of scientific knowledge Aldebaran had—

"Old man, pull your arm back as hard as you possibly can when I give the signal. As hard as you can."

"It's not just this woman — I've got a thing or two to say about your attitude too, Aldebaran...!"

"C'mon now, 'Dragon's Blood,' 'Dragon's Blood.'"

"—Tch! Just give me the damn signal already!"

In the end, applying scientific knowledge required capital investment as an absolute prerequisite, and so Aldebaran relied on the raw arm strength of a Hainkel with veins bulging in anger at how he was being treated. Driven along by the smoke, they'd moved through the forest, exactly falling into the enemy's design. —Naturally, elite forces would be lying in wait outside, ready to clash with Aldebaran's group. That had to be thinned out, at least somewhat.

"Old man!"

On the signal, Hainkel clenched his back teeth and put all his strength into his arm. The muscles of both his arms bulged, and though he lamented being a pickled drunk with no talent, he reached a level of raw brute force Aldebaran himself could never attain — and with tremendous force, the fallen-tree cannon fired. First, a single trial shot, then a second and a third slammed home one after another — and if that made the enemy retreat, all the better. If not—

"Draw them in properly—"

—that was the plan: to smash straight into the faces of those brave men. But—

"Honestly, Hainkel-sama, getting scared of a fireball like that... are you an animal or something~?"

The instant a fireball big enough to be visible even from outside the forest rose into the sky, a violently rattled Hainkel launched the fallen-tree cannon without waiting for Aldebaran's signal. As a result, they failed to wipe out the bulk of the enemy, and ended up letting them charge deep into the forest.

For that, Hainkel was getting needled, again and again, by Yae. Which was probably fair enough. —After all, she was the one who'd had to clean up every bit of the mess that mistake left behind.

The roughly fifty enemy men who'd charged in were held in place by Yae's skill alone. —By a weapon strung out across the entire surrounding area, extending from the five fingers of each of Yae's hands. That was—

"—Threads? You're kidding me."

Breathing hard in evident distress, the man who'd realized the true nature of whatever had sealed his movements — a thug he recognized. Rachins, if he remembered right, one of the former hoodlums attached to Felt. At those words from Rachins, Yae, floating in midair, let out an admiring "Ohh~."

"Well guessed. Of course, it's not just plain weaving thread~, you know? What you'd call a Shinobi Technique. Though I've never actually seen anyone else who can use it besides me."

Saying that, Yae looked down at the men from high up in the air. She looked like she was floating with nothing to support her at all, but in truth, there was a proper foothold. —That very thing was the thread strung throughout the forest, and the Steel Thread Technique that used it was Yae's specialty.

"A so-called 'thread-wielder'... out of all the fanciful, romantic weapon-types out there, that's one I rank pretty high on my list of favorites."

"Whoa, how rare~. Did Al-sama just compliment me? Should I expect spears to fall from the sky tomorrow?"

"Don't worry, I was about to finish with, 'though I like the weapon, the wielder's another story.'"

Yae stuck her tongue out at that reply; without remarking on her attitude, Aldebaran, too — borrowing the strength of Yae's threads, up on a tree he'd climbed — looked down at the enemy below. Yae's steel threads were nearly impossible to make out even squinting hard. Strung so densely, they'd ensnared Rachins and his men just like a spider's web, binding them completely.

Binding anything and everything freely, handling it all with dexterity, was Yae's true forte. Her use of the steel threads wasn't limited to just sealing the enemy's movements — as with the fallen-tree cannon earlier, she could also bind felled trees, lift them into the air, and rig them so that pulling the thread would send them spinning and flying. Wrapping many threads around Hainkel's arm and making him pull like a cart horse was, likewise, all part of that same setup.

In any case, the fallen-tree cannon dealt relatively little damage compared to its visual impact. In the end, most of the enemy had simply gotten past it — but honestly, if that many men had come charging in freely to attack, without Yae, it would've been a pretty rough situation for Aldebaran.

"Any fight that isn't one-on-one, and the difficulty for me shoots straight through the roof."

Just as he had at the Pleiades Watchtower, Aldebaran could take on the tag team of Garfiel and Ezzo in straightforward combat — but if there'd been even one more person there, the story would have changed completely. Even so, victory itself wouldn't have wavered, but the number of attempts it would've taken could easily have jumped by an order of magnitude. In that fight too, he'd provoked Garfiel to avoid a two-against-one as much as possible, steering things toward a one-on-one within that confined space.

If it's one-on-one, Aldebaran can find a path to victory even against Reinhard. But the more the number of opponents increases, the more finding that path to victory comes to require an astronomical number of attempts. Which was why having Yae around, someone skilled at multi-enemy combat in Aldebaran's stead, was such a blessing. And there was another way Yae's skill was a blessing, too.

"Hey, what are we doing with these guys. ...Are we killing all of them?"

Before that blessing could even be explained, it was Hainkel who voiced that question. Unlike Aldebaran and Yae, who'd escaped into the air relying on the threads, Hainkel, left behind on the ground, looked over at Rachins and the others caught in the steel threads with a bitter expression. Hainkel's blade tends to falter against strong opponents, but the reason for the bitter look on his face here was likely something other than that same cowardice.

In response to that timidity, Aldebaran shook his head and said,

"No, there's no need to kill them. If anything, it's smarter to leave it at injuries. A dead man doesn't need anything done for him, but a living one needs treatment. That's the better way to tie up the enemy's hands."

"I... see. Right. Guess that's right. I was thinking the same thing, actually."

"Oh my, look at the two old men understanding each other so well. Little Yae's feeling left out."

Aldebaran could tell from his answer that Hainkel had visibly relaxed with relief. That said, choosing not to remark on it was, perhaps, Yae's own particular version of mercy. In truth, whittling down the enemy's fighting strength was an essential task for Aldebaran's group. To that end, they had to act more cleverly than simply taking lives at random. So—

"Heh."

"Ah? What is it? Relieved to hear you're gonna live, punk?"

"Which one of us is relieved, drunkard. ...Seriously, it's not just the hair, the look in your eyes, that kind of stuff — the rest of you resembles that bastard too."

"——"

Grinning, baring his teeth, the thug — no, Rachins — glared at Hainkel. It wasn't so much the ferocity in his eyes as the content of what he'd said that struck Hainkel silent. Judging from that reaction that the conversation shouldn't be allowed to go any further, Aldebaran leaned out from the thread wrapped around the trees, meaning to cut in and interrupt—

"And you too, helmet-guy!"

"Oh?"

"Both this drunk and you, you're both slacking off. Go choke down some more hardship, idiot."

Cursed at like that, Aldebaran couldn't help being taken aback at how thin and off the mark the content actually was. But almost immediately, he realized that Rachins's eyes weren't the eyes of a man doing something meaningless. The instant he realized it, he was made to understand that the target had been that very moment of Aldebaran's being caught off guard.

And it was too late. The price for that lateness was paid, immediately.

"—What's this, slow as a pig, huh."

That single voice reaching him, and the "Pig King's" cannon-like fist slamming home, striking through the blank instant in his consciousness and crumpling Aldebaran — happened in the very same moment.
