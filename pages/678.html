Arc 8 Chapter 59: "Sphinx"

—Sphinx was a failed product of the Witch of Greed's research into immortality.

A duplicate built on the same principle as a Spirit's Mana Body, based on the flesh of the girl who became the core for creating the garden known as the "Sanctuary" and offered up her own body for it—Ryuzu Meyer.
It was theorized that if an already-existing soul could be fused to that duplicate's hollow life, it would achieve a pseudo-immortality—a duplication of the soul itself.
However, this scheme collapsed easily in the face of a simple fact: that the vessel of life and the soul each differ in size and shape from individual to individual.

The Witch of Greed's soul did not fit within Ryuzu Meyer's duplicate.

Born from that thoroughly reasonable failure was the Witch of Greed's botched creation—a being who would go on to leave a deep and lasting stain of misfortune upon the world: Sphinx.

Given the name "Sphinx"—the name of a mongrel monster—this being was meant to be disposed of as unfit for the purpose it had been created for, but survived through a cruel twist of fate.
And, as with many created lives, Sphinx too began to act in pursuit of the purpose for which it had been made.

To fulfill the created "Witch's" purpose—to become complete as the Witch of Greed—Sphinx would go on to pile up disaster after disaster.

"—Fool."

Blood spilling from the mouth that spat that curse, Jamal collapsed on the spot.
The white light that had been fired was aimed at the heads of Subaru and the others. Because Jamal had shielded them, what he took was aimed at a child's head height—still, it was undeniably a grave wound, piercing clean through one side of his abdomen.

"Jamal—!"

"The intended target has avoided harm, it seems. Still, this falls within an acceptable margin."

Subaru, shoved aside and shielded on reflex. Ignoring his reaction, Sphinx—the very one who had done this—showed no concern at all for the attack's failure.
At the quiet words of the Undead ringleader—words one could flatly call cold—Subaru gritted his back teeth.
For the fallen Jamal, continuing the fight was out of the question—worse, if he wasn't treated immediately, his life was in danger.

"Subaru! Focus on what's in front of you, I suppose!"

"—!"

At once, Beatrice, pulling at Subaru's arm, called out to him in a sharp voice.
At her call, Subaru tore his gaze from Jamal and looked at Sphinx. Likewise, Beatrice, Spica, and Abel all had their eyes fixed on Sphinx as well.
The three of them, now on full alert, had their attention concentrated entirely on their enemy, Sphinx.

Tending to Jamal would have to wait.
That was correct. Absolutely, correct.
And yet—

"I judged you to be the foreign element that would derail my plan. Was I mistaken?"

Sphinx tilted her head and said this to Subaru, who—though he'd torn his eyes away—couldn't tear his mind from Jamal. Peered into by those golden Undead eyes, Subaru's heart grew only more disturbed.

"—! Match me, Spica, I suppose!"
"Ahh-u!"

In Subaru's place, Beatrice and Spica moved as one.
At Beatrice's call, Spica—her long golden hair dancing—leapt at Sphinx. Timing it with Spica's leap, Beatrice deployed violet crystal arrows to the left, right, and rear of the still-standing Sphinx, sealing off her escape routes in support.
With the two working in tandem to close off all sides, Sphinx should have been left with no recourse—but that was not the case.

"El Ziwald."

Contrary to the modesty of the incantation, the destruction that followed was immense.
Against the closing threat, Sphinx spread open her empty hands and, just as she had attacked Jamal a moment before, this time radiated white heat-beams from all ten fingers of both hands.
This time, however, it wasn't a momentary beam like a ray gun—it was a continuously projected sword of light.

Ten fingers, ten blades of light—longer in reach than any middling greatsword—swung about, Sphinx seized control of the space around her with an attack that shredded every direction, herself at the center.
Bathed in that white light, every one of the violet arrows Beatrice had deployed was cut down, and that same ferocity bore down on Spica, who had leapt in from the front.

"—Uh!"

An instant before it could cleave Spica's body in two, she vanished from everyone's sight.
A teleport. Triggering a short-range teleport, Spica escaped Sphinx's line of fire and reappeared atop the ruins of a building off the street.
And Subaru and Beatrice, who had been caught in the white light's line of fire as if incidental to Spica—

"Fools—did the sight of the enemy's ringleader addle your wits?"

The one who never failed to fire off a sarcastic remark even in that instant was Abel, who had shoved down Subaru's and Beatrice's heads, forcing them to crouch on the spot.

"There's no hope of Jamal Aurélie standing back up. Your efforts are what matter now."

"I—I know that without you telling me, I suppose! I'll repay your quick thinking just now with what I do next, so watch closely, I suppose!"

Beatrice, roughly saved, puffed out her cheeks, shoved Abel aside, and got to her feet.
Snorting at Beatrice's spirited retort, Abel rose likewise and looked down at Subaru, who remained fallen.

"What's the matter? Your partner's spirit is admirable enough, but you—"

Abel had likely meant to hurl a provocation—aren't you going to get up?
But he cut himself off almost at once, as if sensing something was wrong, his well-shaped brows knitting together.

"Subaru?"

Beside him, Beatrice blinked in the same bewilderment as Abel.

"—"

At the feet of the two of them, Beatrice and Abel, Subaru remained face-down on the ground. Spica, teleported to a spot some distance away, noticed the same and her eyes filled with confusion.
In that instant, none of them could have understood what had happened.
And so, in place of Beatrice and the others, whose understanding could not keep pace with the events before them, the first to grasp what had happened to Subaru was, ironically, their enemy, Sphinx.
On her pallid Undead face, whose capacity for emotional expression was markedly diminished, Sphinx nonetheless let recognizable confusion and doubt surface in her eyes as she said—

"...Why ingest poison? Explanation required."

"—! You—!"

Right after Sphinx's question, Abel's expression changed and he grabbed Subaru by the collar, hauling him up. But there was no complaint from Subaru at this rough treatment.

—Because Subaru had already bitten through the paper packet of poison hidden in his back teeth, and was, at this very moment, tasting the hellish agony that led to "death."

"Nooooo! Subaru!? Subaru!?"
"Uh-au—!?"

At the sight of Subaru, convulsing and foaming blood at the mouth, Beatrice and Spica screamed.
Beatrice tried to cling to the trembling Subaru, but Abel shoved the girl aside, thrust his fingers into Subaru's mouth, and pulled out the medicine packet, his face twisting into fury.

"What do you think you're doing!? Have you lost your senses!?"

"Bu-bu-bu, bubububu...!"

"You talk big about fighting fate, and this is what you do!?"

Amid the sensation of his whole body dissolving into ruin, Subaru heard it all—Abel's furious voice, Beatrice's sobbing voice, Spica's anguished voice as she desperately flew back toward them.

To that voice of anger, that crying voice, that voice of despair, he could give no answer in words.
And yet, it did have meaning, properly. It was a necessary thing. —This was the best move.
The best, the very best best best best best, way to reach a future where no one had to die.

"Bubu, bu."

"Explanation required."

Without being able to convey it to anyone, without the "Witch's" question ever being resolved, his breath ran out.
Natsuki Subaru died. —Again, just as he had already done many times over in this final battle.

—For the first several years, Sphinx's journey—one that could be called a quest—was fraught with extreme hardship.

The body of Ryuzu Meyer, from which the duplicate had originated, lacked any aptitude for surviving a harsh world—easily threatened by extreme weather, drastic shifts in temperature, and at times Witchbeasts or malicious humans, with scant means to counter any of it. There were simply too many flaws already present at the stage of selecting the vessel.

Since concepts like growth or training held no meaning for a Mana Body, there was no hope of improvement, either.
The acquisition of knowledge and skills not dependent on the physical body was possible, but the path to learning them often put her life at risk, and her days were spent constantly alongside danger.
On top of that, Ryuzu Meyer, the base body, was a Half-Elf, and since Sphinx had inherited those outward characteristics as well, she was frequently subjected to persecution and stares of strangeness.

However, she hesitated to drastically alter her outward features from the original appearance.
These were things given to Sphinx from the very start, and there was a risk that drastically changing the base conditions would render her unable to fulfill her own created purpose.
Sphinx therefore refused to disguise herself, and instead sought other means of survival.

So long as her life wasn't taken, she had at times reduced herself to a sideshow attraction or a slave. Since Ryuzu Meyer had a well-arranged appearance, she had also become a servant to a generous master.
Sphinx, voracious for knowing and learning, found her abilities prized wherever she went.
Once her quickness to learn was recognized, value as a useful tool arose in all manner of situations. In the course of this, Sphinx realized that this, too, was a means of protecting herself.

To make her way across the world in a body so frail and lacking in vitality, the best course was to create her own utility, offer it to those who sought it, and place herself under their protection.
In that way, until she could protect herself on her own, Sphinx chose the means of surviving by placing herself beneath the umbrella of others.

Such days continued for a full hundred and fifty years or so.

—Since being summoned to another world, what has been Natsuki Subaru's most common cause of death?

If asked that question, Subaru could answer with confidence: "suicide by poison."
At the Crimson Lapis Castle in the "Demon City" of Chaosflame, he'd also tasted no small number of "deaths" in that hellish game of tag with Olbart, but unless one simply labeled the cause of death "Olbart," the manners of dying there had been varied.
If one excepted those despair-inducing eleven seconds, then poison undoubtedly held the record for most deaths.
It was a deeply inglorious thing to say, but one might even claim that the person who had killed Subaru the most in this other world was old man Null, who had mixed that poison.

In any case, the poison used so frequently in his lone struggle to escape the "Sword Slave Island" Ginunhive without a single casualty—in forming the "Pleiades Battalion"—was, even now, still tucked away inside Subaru's mouth.

"—I'm bringing everyone back safe."

That was the absolute, non-negotiable condition Subaru had set for himself in taking on this final battle for the capital.
Originally, he'd held this stance and mindset even before his body had shrunk, even before he'd been flung all the way to the Empire, but through his days here in the Empire so far, his resolve had only grown stronger.
A large part of the reason for this was Subaru's own rebellious streak against the Vollachian Empire, which kept trying to force the conclusion of "death" upon him by every trick available.

—If the Empire was going to force death upon him, then he would throw it off no matter what it took.

He would do everything within his power to that end.
Even if it meant tasting hellish agony dozens, hundreds of times over.

"—Ga, gu-!"

His field of vision opened, flickering crimson red, and Subaru's throat screamed at an agony as though every drop of blood, every scrap of flesh and bone, every single blood vessel, down to each fragment of every cell in his body, had all been thrown together into a blender.
But the lethal agony he'd truly been tasting only a moment before, and the furious shouting and crying voices that had caused him even greater suffering than that pain—both vanished into the distance. —Return by Death.

Suicide by poison had triggered Return by Death, and Natsuki Subaru had gone back through time.
In that instant, Subaru tried to switch his awareness over, thinking he must have returned to the scene right after washing his face at the imperial soldiers' camp—the last place he'd stopped before storming the capital—

"—Fool."
"—"

The sensation of being shoved in the chest, and the sound of that strained, cursing voice—Subaru was shown, plain as day, that his Return by Death restart point had been updated.

"The intended target has avoided harm, it seems. Still, this falls within an acceptable margin."

Spilling blood along with his curse, Jamal—the blade of the sword in his hand gouged into a round hollow—collapsed. Out of the corner of her eye at this sight, Sphinx, the perpetrator, analyzed it flatly in her cold voice.
Both this sight and those words were things he had seen and heard only some tens of seconds before.

"Subaru! Focus on what's in front of you, I suppose!"

"—!"

Beatrice called out sharply to Subaru, whose thoughts were thrown into disarray as he tried to comprehend and grasp reality.
Her voice shattered the contents of his head, and he let out a full breath to release the shock of the poison's agony just before and the shift in his restart point. Adapt to the new situation in front of him.
There was no time to stand dazed.
Too much might be lost in the meantime.

—The updating of the Return by Death point.

What that meant was that, in the final battle for the capital which had already begun, he could no longer swap around the assignments of the "Save-from-Ruin Squad" he'd distributed to each vertex.

"—"

He believed, as things stood, that he'd placed the best personnel in the best battlefields.
But it galled him that he hadn't been able to fully confirm the conditions with the least damage. In particular, not having verified the outcome of pairing Emilia with Tanza was, psychologically, a heavy blow.
Of course, there was no end to his anxiety and worry over the safety of the other members as well, but—

"I judged you to be the foreign element that would derail my plan. Was I mistaken?"

At Sphinx's question, head tilted, Subaru looked at the Undead before him.
The blocks he'd built up in his mind up to this point came crashing down with a clatter.
The blocks of trial-and-error thought he'd shaped had been aimed, viewed from every angle, at reaching a better final form. And now they crumbled away without a trace. —No, not crumbled: he was tearing them down.

Beside the blocks built up in his head, the Natsuki Subaru inside his own mind appeared and smashed them apart roughly, utterly demolishing them.
Until the blocks' original shape was unrecognizable, until worrying over them was pointless, he tore them down, tore them down, tore them down, tore them down over and over.
In that way, he demolished them until there was nothing left to be done about it, and set the matter of the blocks aside, once, to be forgotten.
Only then could Natsuki Subaru finally set to work on a new round of trial and error.

"—No, you're not mistaken."

After a beat that felt long, Subaru, both hands gripped by Beatrice and Spica, answered while fixing his gaze on Sphinx, just as Abel—narrowing his black eyes—was doing.
He still hadn't found a way to break this situation open. He didn't know the fate of his companions. Jamal was, in this very moment, still bleeding.
His resolve to deal with all of it hadn't changed one bit.

Defeat the "Witch" Sphinx, and end this "Great Disaster."
To that end—

"I'm the natural enemy of disaster who's going to derail your plan."

—Sphinx may be called a "Witch" now, but for the first several hundred years after her birth, she possessed nothing like the power that title implied.

This, too, was something that only became clear once a being like Sphinx came into existence: there is a correlation between "soul" and "vessel," and any mismatch between them stands as a major flaw obstructing one's ability to live.
By nature, a vessel is optimized to fit its soul.
To put it simply, the skills and talents of the "Witch of Greed" could not be handled properly except in the "Witch of Greed's" own body. Sphinx, a being into whom the "Witch of Greed's" soul had been imperfectly poured into Ryuzu Meyer's vessel, found her created purpose obstructed from the very moment of her birth, yet again.

As a result, it took Sphinx a hundred and fifty years to gain power befitting the name "Witch."

Burdened with the flaw of a mismatched vessel and soul, Sphinx needed an effort equivalent to relearning everything anew—down to how to walk, how to breathe, even how her heart was supposed to beat.
This took nearly a hundred times as long as it would for an ordinarily talented person to become a mage.
Moreover, in this era, far too many factors stood in the way as obstacles to Sphinx's activity toward fulfilling her created purpose.

Being treated as a Half-Elf because of her physical characteristics, and made into an outlet for the bitter resentment directed at the "Witch of Envy," that byword for all "Witches."
Being persistently hunted by a person who claimed to be a disciple of the "Witch of Greed," one who suspected the existence of Sphinx as a product of the "Witch of Greed's" research.

There were other reasons too, but mainly because of these two, she could never remain in one place, and Sphinx's quest to fulfill her created purpose was forced to stall again and again.
At the end of such days, when she succeeded for the first time in casting magic entirely on her own, Sphinx remembered feeling great disappointment at the sight of the small flame lit at her fingertip.

—Was this really all the magic that a thing meant to succeed the "Witch of Greed" could manage?

"—Fool."

Spilling blood from the mouth that spat that curse, Jamal collapsed once again.
Shoved by that Jamal, in the instant he steadied himself alongside Beatrice and Spica—whose hands he held—Subaru confirmed that Return by Death had triggered again.

"The intended target has avoided harm, it seems. Still, this falls within an acceptable margin."

Even having missed her aim, Sphinx showed no fixation on a result that differed from her expectation.
The "Witch's" objective was to wipe out Subaru and the others present here. In that sense, her judgment was that felling Jamal—counted as a legitimate fighting force—with the first strike was a sufficient result.
But—

"Subaru! Focus on what's in front—"

"Beatrice! Heal Jamal!"

"—! Got it, I suppose!"

Beating Beatrice to it as she started to call out, Subaru instructed her to treat Jamal. Hearing this, Beatrice's eyes widened, and she rushed straight to Jamal's side.
At Beatrice's swift response, Subaru shot a glance to Spica, whose hand he held with his other one.

"Spica, I'm counting on you!"

"Ahh, u!"

With great force, bouncing like a rubber ball, Spica sprang at Sphinx.
To meet Spica head-on, Sphinx fired ten beams of light from the fingers of both hands. —So, before that, Subaru scooped up the sword Jamal had dropped and hurled it.

"El Ziwald."

Jamal's sword, spinning end over end, was cut down by the swing of Sphinx's heat-beams.
Traced by the swords of light, the blade was split into six pieces midair, but it succeeded in buying time for Spica's teleport. Without this cover, even Spica's teleport wouldn't have made it in time.

"—U!"

With a short bark, Spica's form teleported atop the rubble beside the street.
And the heat-beam that had originally targeted Spica along with Subaru behind her—an arm reaching from behind roughly shoved him down, letting him dodge it.
Abel's hand, lunging in, pressed down on the back of his head and forcibly crushed him down.

"Fools—did you underestimate the enemy's ringleader?"

"No matter the pattern, this emperor never misses a chance to needle me...!"

Bracing his hands against the ground to avoid being flattened with a splat, Subaru quickly got back up and complained at Abel's hateful remark.
Saying nothing to Subaru's retort, Abel, wary of Sphinx, said—

"There's no hope of Jamal Aurélie standing back up. He ought to be abandoned."

"I don't think so. He's got a sister waiting for him to come home."

"His bereaved family will be given ample compensation."

"A hole in the heart from a family member dying doesn't get filled by that."

Beatrice held her hand over the wounded Jamal, casting healing magic on his injury.
Frustratingly, Abel was right. Even with clever Beatrice's healing magic performing at maximum effect, the chance of Jamal returning to this fight was close to zero.
But if he could save Jamal from the brink of death here, one of Subaru's worries would be erased.
That carried enormous, enormous psychological significance in pressing forward with the fight against Sphinx.
So—

"Sphinx! I'm the one who can kill you!"

"—"

Sphinx, who had been sweeping her gaze around to confirm the situation, looked at Subaru at this blunt declaration.
If her attention left Beatrice and the others and turned toward Subaru, that was success.

"You're Undead and you might think you've conquered 'death,' but that's a total lie, a huge mistake. Nobody lives forever. There are no exceptions."

"...That's persuasive. As a matter of fact, you have overwritten my 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' and interfered with the souls of the dead. It's unlikely that I alone would be an exception. However—"

"However?"

"—No. There's no need to state it deliberately. Verification required."

At Subaru's deliberate provocation, Sphinx shook her head slowly from side to side. But at Sphinx's response, Subaru's cheek stiffened faintly.
It wasn't for any reason like the content of Sphinx's reply being inconvenient.
It was because Sphinx, responding this way, had loosened her lips faintly—she had smiled.

"...Ryuzu-san wasn't exactly expressive either, but—"

Sphinx's smile—she who, on top of being revived as an Undead, gave an impression of even poorer emotional expression than that same Ryuzu—sent a chill through Subaru that felt as though it seeped up from the depths.
That chill felt like it had a reason bigger and more fundamental than simple revulsion at seeing an adorable-looking being turned Undead.
And that became clearer with Sphinx's next remark.

"There is one thing confirmation is required of. —Are you, over there, indeed Emperor Vincent Vollachia, without error?"

Next, the point of Sphinx's words turned to Abel at his side.
Unexpectedly, Subaru raised his brows at the conversation's crosshairs landing on Abel. Given his position, it was only natural for Abel to draw attention, but Subaru hadn't expected Sphinx to concern herself with him here.
Abel, who had no combat ability and, given the situation, should have been the one with the least reason to warrant caution.

"Well? Response required."

"I have no intention of disguising my name or station for a lowly brigand such as you. —Correct: I am Vincent Vollachia, emperor of this realm."

"Thank you for your answer. Further confirmation is required."

Undaunted by Abel's sharp glare, Sphinx fearlessly continued speaking.
Perhaps because she was self-aware as the "Great Disaster" that would destroy the Empire, Sphinx confronted Abel, the Vollachian emperor, and Subaru found himself at a loss over how to respond next.
If the conversation dragged on even a little, the more time Beatrice would have to use in treating Jamal.
Judging that he should make good use of that advantage here, Subaru didn't interject in the exchange between the two.
Into that—

"I make no promise of an answer. But you may go ahead and speak."

"—Priscilla Barielle. Or, Priska Benedict."

"...Eh?"

Subaru was thrown off by Priscilla's name coming up so suddenly, so unexpectedly, as a topic.
Priscilla, who had taken part in the battle for the capital, had gone missing during the retreat that began on account of the "Great Disaster's" appearance. Confirming the safety of her, whose fate was unknown, had—together with Yorna, likewise missing—been something they'd verified, oddly enough, through the continued operation of the "Soul Marriage Technique" she was able to use.
That the "Soul Marriage Technique" Yorna had cast on Tanza and the people of Chaosflame, and that Priscilla had cast on Schult, served as proof that the two women were alive.

Yorna, one of the "Nine Divine Generals," was one thing—of course—but this was that Priscilla they were talking about.
Given the circumstances of Al staying behind in the capital for her rescue, Subaru had naturally assumed she'd stubbornly survive somehow—but for her name to come up here, from Sphinx's own mouth, and in the form of a question directed at Abel no less, was unexpected.

Certainly, Priscilla had been involved in the fighting ever since the walled city of Guaral, and had hinted at some connection to both the Vollachian Empire and to Abel himself. Given the needlessly meaningful atmosphere, he'd once guessed it might be some entanglement related to her past husbands, hadn't he.
But that—

"—"

Glancing over at Abel, whose relationship with Priscilla—the one Subaru had never confirmed—had just been raised, Subaru caught sight from the corner of his eye of Abel narrowing his black eyes.
Subaru knew that tiny reaction was, in fact, proof of no small amount of unsettlement in Abel.
Whether she'd seen through it was unclear, but Sphinx, addressing the silent emperor—

"—Are you her elder brother, without error? Certainty required."

Continued on with that bombshell statement, one that betrayed all of Subaru's expectations.

—Even after becoming able to use magic herself, Sphinx's way of life did not change drastically.

Having once succeeded in invoking magic, her use of the Gate within Ryuzu Meyer's Mana Body stabilized, and she succeeded in reproducing most of the magic the "Witch of Greed's" soul had grasped.
However, this did nothing to change the dangerous position Sphinx found herself in, and her days of hiding from sight and never staying in one place to avoid threats continued unchanged.
Still, compared to before, it became easier to hide from prying eyes and escape threats.
Thanks to that, a hundred and fifty years after her birth, Sphinx was finally able to begin research into her created purpose: the reproduction of the "Witch of Greed."

—The first hundred years were spent coming to know the "Witch of Greed."

Despite originating from the very same soul as the "Witch of Greed," Sphinx had let slip too much of what came from that soul, and the final goal she aimed for was far too blurred.
And so, to learn of the "Witch of Greed," long since lost, she began a journey tracing legends and documents across many lands—but the Witch Cult, which sought to erase the "Witches" from history (though that didn't seem to have been their original founding purpose), interfered with her activities as well, and progress came slowly.
As a result, after a hundred years with no visible progress, Sphinx changed her approach and sought a different method.

—The next hundred years were aimed at making up for the deficiency of the "Witch of Greed" within herself.

From the fact that a hundred years spent traveling the world had produced almost no results, Sphinx concluded that the greatest clue to knowing the "Witch of Greed" was herself, who possessed the very same soul.
Sphinx, born as a lacking, botched failure of an attempt to reproduce the "Witch of Greed."
If she could fill in what was lacking in that soul and restore its original form, wouldn't that fulfill her created purpose of reproducing the "Witch of Greed"?

—However, this attempt too, after a hundred years, was revealed to be untenable, and stalled out.

Even after changing her approach, she had not abandoned the search for materials to learn about the "Witch of Greed," and there had been developments there—and it was this that caused Sphinx to lose sight of the mountain peak she should be aiming for.

It seemed the "Witch of Greed" had possessed a deeply complex human nature.
According to the materials she found, the "Witch of Greed" exchanged words with many beings, granting them the knowledge and wisdom they desired, and was said to have repeatedly interfered with history, for better or worse.
On the other hand, within Sphinx's own hazy memories, there was also an image of a "Witch of Greed" who avoided contact with people as much as possible, one who would not, except under rare circumstances, involve herself in the lives of others.

These contradictory images of the "Witch of Greed" bred confusion in Sphinx's research.
By this point, more than three hundred years had already passed since Sphinx's birth, and with the desire to press her research toward fulfilling her created purpose forward, Sphinx was once again forced to make a decision.

And so Sphinx decided. —That she would drastically change her methods from what they had been until now.

△▼△▼△▼△

—Abel and Priscilla, siblings, the two of them.

This shocking fact, brought to light by Sphinx, connected however with a certain sense of understanding within Subaru, matching up with and untangling the numerous questions he'd had up until now.
Priscilla's intervention in the Empire's civil strife; her having friendships within the Empire, starting with Serena. The way she'd occasionally exchange conversation with people connected to the Empire hinting at some meaningful past, and had repeatedly shown signs of some extraordinary relationship with Abel as well.
And, just like Abel, that abnormally arrogant, high-and-mighty personality.

Abel being high-and-mighty could be explained by his being emperor.
So then, why was Priscilla so high-and-mighty? That answer, too, was solved: because she was of the imperial family.
—No. More precisely: because she had been of the imperial family.

"Wasn't the way you decide an emperor some crazy rule where siblings kill each other?"

"I have no intention of debating the merits of the 'Imperial Selection Ceremony' system with you."

"Forget debate—the fact that you're emperor and Priscilla's alive already tells me pretty much what you two siblings think of that rule."

The more he heard, the more Abel and Priscilla's blood relation made total sense—but the more he learned, the more he understood that the present situation was something the Empire ought to consider impossible.
By rule, in the Vollachian Empire, those holding the right of imperial succession are meant to kill one another, and the emperor isn't decided until only one remains. —Abel was in violation of this.
In other words, Abel and Priscilla were in a position of having conspired together and defied the "Imperial Selection Ceremony."

"There was no conspiracy. I decided, and I carried it out. Priska died, and Priscilla remained. That's all there is to it."

"That's all... ah!? Wait, doesn't that mean Priscilla was in a position where she absolutely could not come back to the Empire!?"

"Can you even imagine how much anguish I suffered at the walled city? For that matter, if we're bringing that up, it's been that way ever since I first heard about the Kingdom's Royal Selection and its candidates."

"For once, I actually feel sorry for you..."

It was beyond Subaru's imagination as an outsider, but to let his sister Priscilla escape and survive, Abel must have crossed some fairly dangerous bridges. Surely, even now, this was a bombshell dangerous enough that if this information got out, it would jeopardize Abel's standing as emperor.

"—Reflection required."

Following a dull creaking sound came a painful, splintering crack of bone breaking. It was not Spica—it was the result of Subaru's right elbow being bent backward and snapped.
Searing pain stabbed his brain, and for an instant every other bruise and scrape was blasted away. Blood-flecked foam spilled from the corner of his clenched mouth, and unbearable tears streamed down in fat drops.

At that example made of him, neither the Sphinx who had broken Subaru's arm, nor the one who had come within a hair of breaking Spica's, nor the one who had ordered it, showed the faintest flicker of emotion.
Only the gaze of an observer, checking whether the demonstration was having the desired effect toward fulfilling her wish.
However—

"—Enough of this."

Abel's response, even as Subaru's arm was broken, yielded not a shred of emotion.
At this, Sphinx finally let out a small breath, and—

"If possible, I had wished to bring you along while keeping you alive."

"Do not jest. If you absolutely must accomplish that, then turn me into an Undead and take me along."

"That the conclusion, given the deliberation materials so far, is that this would be difficult."

At Sphinx's reply, Abel closed one eye as if in thought. But before any progress could be seen in that thought, the Sphinxes acted.
The Sphinxes pinning down Subaru and Spica each raised a single finger. —Pressing that raised finger against the backs of the heads of Subaru and Spica as they were hauled upright.

"A-Abel..."

Beaded with cold sweat from the pain of his broken arm, Subaru called out to Abel, breath ragged. Spica, thrashing beside him, was in the same situation, and Sphinx's aim was clear.
But it was meaningless. The demonstration hadn't worked, after all.

"Hostages mean nothing either. Or does a 'Witch' fail to grasp even that much?"

"Is that so?"

"What?"

"Things like protective instinct and affection are difficult things to handle. Nor is that any exception for you, the pinnacle of the Empire. —Exertion required."

So said the one Sphinx still free, telling Abel this, and slowly raising her hand.
And raising a single finger on that lifted hand, she aimed it at Abel's chest, and—

"Please do not move. If you value their lives."

"—Vile wretch."

Abel's short, teeth-gnashing words brought a twisted smile to Sphinx's lips.
At that instant, light lit at Sphinx's fingertip. —Not only the one aimed at Abel, but also the fingers pressed against the backs of Subaru's and Spica's heads.

—He had been waiting for that very "Witch's" vicious choice.

"Jump, Spica!!"

"Eh?"

An instant before the lethal light was fired, Subaru forgot his pain and shouted this. In response, one of the confused Sphinxes vanished from the spot. —Dragged along by Spica's teleport.
And with Spica's leap as the signal, there was movement on Subaru's side too. That was not Subaru himself moving—

"Even so, isn't this pushing it way too far, I suppose!"

The one who raised her voice in anger like that was Beatrice, who had lain there waiting intently for Subaru's signal.
Having patiently endured, she poured the urge to leap out—which she'd held back even in the instant Subaru's arm was broken—into a violet crystal arrow, and slammed it into the Sphinx who had done it.

"—"

The situation shifted, changing places dizzyingly fast.
The attacks of the Sphinxes restraining Subaru and Spica both ended in failure.
Though, on Subaru's side, it was Beatrice who stopped it, while on Spica's side, it was none other than Sphinx herself who stopped it.

—The teleported Spica had cut into the line of fire of the Sphinx that had launched magic at Abel, causing that beam of light to shoot straight through the Sphinx restraining her.

"This is—"

Having provoked that unwanted friendly fire, the one remaining Sphinx's eyes went wide.
One had been struck by her own magic and vanished; the other had been bathed in the magic arrow and turned into a violet statue.
Even so, this was as far as a surprise attack could manage.

"It seems, at least, that even the very last one won't be standing back up."

Subaru with his broken arm, Spica with her movement sealed, and even Beatrice, who had seemed unconscious, had all attempted counterattacks. But sadly, Jamal, who had been thoroughly beaten down, would not be getting back up.
Having confirmed this, the last remaining Sphinx swiftly raised both hands, generating ten swords of light from the five fingers of each, meaning this time to truly cut Subaru and the others to pieces.
And because of that, she never even noticed she had taken her eyes off the one opponent she should never have taken her eyes off, of all people.

"—Sword drawn: the 'Sunlight Sword' Vollachia."

A quiet, solemn voice struck Subaru's eardrums, dependable to an infuriating degree.
No matter the din, no matter how crowded with people the place, no matter how brutal the battle unfolding on the field, that man's voice reached exactly the person he wished it to reach.
That was, in other words, the sovereign's vessel—the bearing of one who stood above others, burdened with the fate of leading them.
And, as if to prove it, what dazzled the eyes of everyone present, illuminating them in red—

"—You were not supposed to possess that."

"Fool. —Did I ever once say that I had let go of the 'Sunlight Sword'?"

Drawn from an empty sheath was that crimson treasured sword, one that had not been drawn no matter how much hardship stood in the way, solely to draw out the chance of victory in this one single moment.
And then—

"The battle of deception—we win."
"You underestimated us. —The scheming of my two strategists."

Enduring the pain, Subaru gloated in triumph, while Abel gloated in the skill of the strategist not present here, who had laid a plot to make even the "Witch" believe he had lost his trump card.

—In the next instant, Abel, sword in hand, kicked off the ground and leapt.

It was a dash beyond any comparison to the Abel of before, one possessed of genuine power. The "Sunlight Sword" was raising its own chosen owner up to a dimension worthy of it.
Truly, as if the world itself were being lit by sunlight, Abel powerfully swung the treasured sword up—

"—The 'Sunlight Sword' cuts what I decree it shall cut, and burns what I decree it shall burn."

"—"

A single crimson flash ran diagonally, burning away without the slightest resistance the ten beams of white light from Sphinx, who had tried on reflex to intercept Abel.
There was, in the "Sunlight Sword's" radiance, a supernatural power that made the absurdity of light itself catching fire feel like an inevitability—and for that very reason, the next outcome was likewise inevitable.

"—Ah"

With that, letting out a rasping breath, Sphinx, having leapt far back, looked down at herself.
The "Witch," who had retreated with swift movement, had nonetheless had one lock of her pink hair severed by the "Sunlight Sword's" flash—and in that instant, Sphinx's entire body burst into flame.
And that fire spread not only to the Sphinx who had been cut, but also to the Sphinx crumbling to dust from her own attack, and the Sphinx that had turned into a violet crystal.

—The "Sunlight Sword" Vollachia was fated to burn the "Witch" Sphinx to ash.
That result was the sight of the Sphinxes burning like that.

"—"

Wrapped in flame, Sphinx—wary that she might make some desperate last struggle before burning out entirely—but—

"No need. To be burned by the 'Sunlight Sword's' flame is precisely that kind of thing."

"...Is that so. Honestly, I have to question your nerve, keeping that hidden all the way to the last moment."

Abel, the "Sunlight Sword" still hanging from one hand, pointed out that Subaru's caution was needless worry. Returning something like a curse to Abel for it, Subaru got no answer.
He simply fixed his gaze on Sphinx, consumed by the rising flames, within a fire that reached even to her soul.
Gazing at her, Abel—no, Vincent Vollachia—said:

"This scheme, this time. —Well done, Chisha Gold."
