Arc 5 Chapter 21: "The Optimal Solution"

—Having brought his second Return by Death to a close, Subaru found himself pummeled by an exhaustion unlike any he'd felt before.

Confirming, twice over, that he had gone mad was a heavy weight on the spirit.

Especially the second time, when even he himself, in the instant before death, could clearly feel everything—his thoughts, everything—collapsing. A wave of terror that made the very core of his body shake without end had swept away the individual known as Natsuki Subaru all at once, shattering his fragile mind, and with it his soul, into pieces.

The probable cause of death this time was a heart attack brought on by the madness of terror. Or perhaps it was Sirius showing mercy to Subaru, who by then had become nothing more than a machine leaking bodily fluids. He had gone in alone to try to save Lusbel, and paid an absurd price for it.

And yet, though Subaru had died twice in a mere thirty minutes, even a death so helpless as that had not been without its harvest. As if offering a parting gift to the dying Subaru, Sirius had gone out of his way to explain, kindly and in detail, just what had happened to Subaru and Lusbel. Which was to say—

"Fear building up between the two of us... some kind of resonance?"

Lusbel's fear passed to Subaru, and Subaru's fear at feeling that fear passed back to Lusbel again, amplified. This unending cycle of fear overwrote itself again and again, growing at last into an absolute terror so total it led to death. It was safe to assume that was the flow that had led to this death.

The first death, and now this second one. The sequence of events leading up to them, and Sirius's own words. Factoring in that the man was the Sin Archbishop of Wrath, Subaru could form a rough picture of the nature of his Authority. Mistaking a scene that should provoke anger or disgust for one of joy and delight. Mistaking the fear the boy felt as if it were his own, and having that same effect act upon the other party in turn.

—Sirius of Wrath was a deviant who maliciously manipulated the emotions of others.

Likely, just like Petelgeuse's Unseen Hand, this was a special, aberrant power belonging to none of this world's established forms of Magic. An Authority held by a Sin Archbishop of the Witch Cult. Fitting for Wrath, it was an ability intimately tied to emotion.

It could only be called an Authority "fitting" of a Sin Archbishop's wickedness. But even having died twice to arrive at that much of a guess, the most Subaru could state with any certainty amounted to about that much information. The real problem was the conditions that triggered that Authority—in other words, how to break through it.

Petelgeuse's Authority of Sloth had, in a sense, been simple to counter once its trick was exposed. In essence, his Authority had been two things—the Unseen Hand, and the "Sloth" that canceled others' actions—and Subaru happened to have resistance to both. Even now, what exactly had been the root cause of that resistance in either case, Subaru still didn't know. Even now, having somehow picked up his own version of the Unseen Hand—Invisible Providence—he still didn't know.

The fact that the Witch's Scent tied to Return by Death, and the White Whale's fog of forgetting, had had no effect on him had, in fact, made Subaru somewhat optimistic—he'd started thinking things like maybe I'm just immune to the Witch Cult's cheat abilities!?—but given that he'd been thoroughly killed by Wrath's Authority, it seemed that hope had been misplaced.

Considering the two encounters with "Wrath" so far, in the worst case, the trigger condition for his Authority might even be simple "contact with Sirius." Considering the possibility that one fell under its spell the moment one heard Sirius's voice, or the moment one laid eyes on him, the path to victory was unusually severe.

To put it in the most extreme terms possible, probably the fastest and surest method would be to blow the Time Tower where Sirius resided to bits with Magic from outside, without ever seeing him. The only moment, as things stood, when it was even possible to pinpoint Sirius's location and land a preemptive strike without ever laying eyes on him, was this instant right after a Return by Death. But that would require turning a blind eye to the sacrifice of one brave boy, and Subaru had no intention of choosing that option until things had truly, truly reached their absolute limit.

Who had the right, with what mouth, to speak so grandly of a "necessary sacrifice"? Even a single life sacrificed to save the many was, from that single life's own perspective, the same as losing the entire world. How could Subaru, who could not even accept the sacrifice of his own life, so selfishly count another's life as a mere number? That settled his policy. He would save Lusbel. He also refused to let anyone die for nothing. Having to do both at once—that was the hard part of being Natsuki Subaru.

"I've gotta save Lusbel... and then, there's no choice but to take down Sirius."

Once Lusbel was rescued, it was reasonable to assume the same situation as before would recur. No matter how he struggled, facing Sirius alone would be nothing short of suicide. What was truly frightening was Sirius's combat ability on his own—a fighting style apparently using the chains on both arms—but the fact that he'd blocked Subaru's surprise whip attack showed he was a considerable talent.

Even setting aside the fact that it had been an unskilled strike from Subaru, the speed of a whip's strike was not something an amateur could track with the eye. How much extraordinary skill would it take to meet that strike—coming from an unexpected direction—with the tip of a thin chain? Subaru had no intention of underestimating his opponent to a degree beyond imagining.

In other words, what Subaru needed now was the existence of a "Sirius-killer"—someone who, as pure combat strength, could overwhelm Sirius; who would believe Subaru's words and act alongside him; and who, on top of all that, possessed resistance to Sirius's Authority of Wrath.

"There's no way somebody that convenient exists..."

Subaru sighed at himself for thinking something so shamelessly convenient. And yet, the idea of a "Sirius-killer" immune to the Authority of Wrath didn't feel, oddly enough, entirely off the mark.

Just as Subaru had been immune to both of Petelgeuse's Authorities of Sloth, wasn't it possible that there existed, by chance, people whose constitution made them immune to Sirius's Authority of Wrath as well? If so, Subaru just so happened to have been, by sheer coincidence, a "Petelgeuse-killer"—and thanks to that same constitution, a ray of light might now be visible. For now, since everyone in the square had been swallowed up by Sirius's speech the first time around, it could be assumed there had been no "Sirius-killer" present in that crowd.

Of those present who might have stood a chance against Sirius, there were four, including Subaru, whom Sirius had named first—and it was only upon reaching that point in his thoughts that Subaru finally realized.

The significance of Rachins having been there in that square.

"—Reinhard!!"

Subaru, who had just chided himself for entertaining something as shamelessly convenient as wishful thinking, had finally managed to recall that a man who truly was the embodiment of shameless convenience had been there all along.

Subaru himself had no intention of making excuses for himself, but it had to be said that the reason it took him so long to connect Rachins's presence to Reinhard's was, ultimately, the appallingly short interval between his Returns by Death.

Right after death, right before and after death, and then the fifteen minutes right before and after his descent into madness. If someone wanted to insist that, under such conditions, with an imminent crisis staring him in the face, it was only natural to calmly explore every possible option and select the optimal solution—then Subaru wanted to say, loudly, that such a person should go ahead and Return by Death in his place.

It wasn't as though Subaru particularly wanted to Return by Death. If it were permitted, his honest wish was to hole up in the mansion with Emilia, Rem, and Beatrice, and live like some kind of plant. But a daily life spent lazily nudging stamens against pistils was not something allowed to Subaru. For that simple reason alone, he struggled desperately through each day.

So even now, in this very moment, Subaru was desperately trying to persuade the man before him.

"I finally found you, and I'm not letting you go! Please, call Reinhard here right now! This is an emergency!"

"Shut the hell up! Why the hell would I go out of my way to call that redheaded bastard over here just to get an earful I might not even deserve? Screw off!"

The two of them, hurling angry shouts at each other in the street, drew the gawking stares of onlookers. The atmosphere on the verge of turning into a brawl, wrapped in the jeering voices of people enjoying the spectacle, was infuriating in its own way.

Having recovered from the shock of Return by Death and arrived at his conclusion, Subaru had wasted no time putting it into action. Just as before, he'd left Beatrice behind to guard Emilia, using the excuse of buying sweets to slip away from the park. From there he'd made straight for the square in question, tracked down Rachins—useful as a go-between to reach Reinhard—and had only just now begun negotiations.

Part of the reason things were on the verge of turning into a shouting match was that, having had trouble finding Rachins at first, Subaru had grabbed his shoulder roughly the moment he finally found him. That said, having been shoved in the chest in retaliation, the truth was that Subaru's own state of mind was far from calm either.

Regardless, there was no undoing the fact that it had turned into an argument. Growing impatient, Subaru spoke faster and faster, pointing at Rachins, who glared back at him.

"Listen, okay? Just calm down and hear me out. I'm not messing around here. If you don't want to die, call Reinhard right now."

"Ha? Some scrawny little punk talking about people dying, acting all high and mighty? I don't need that Reinhard bastard. Keep talking and I'll beat you to death, you piece of shit."

"Aaahh, that's not what I—!"

Taking Subaru's words as a provocation, Rachins's anger showed no sign of cooling. To begin with, there was no foundation of friendship between Subaru and Rachins. On top of that, it seemed Rachins didn't hold his supposed ally Reinhard in particularly high regard either. Between his reluctance to call the man and his dislike of Subaru, two factors stubbornly held him back from complying with the request.

"You stubborn idiot..."

Holding his head, Subaru ground his back teeth at Rachins's attitude. Of course, Subaru bore some self-reproach for having botched the approach. But being stonewalled so flatly like this made him want to voice a complaint or two of his own. From Rachins's position, there was no reason he should obediently follow whatever Subaru said—but Subaru, for his part, also had reasons he couldn't reveal everything.

—But at this point, there was no helping it.

Suppressing his instinctive fear, Subaru put a hand to his chest and said,

"Rachins. This isn't a joke. The reason I want you to call Reinhard is because there are people coming who we can't handle on our own."

"People you can't handle? Don't just make shit up."

Rachins snorted derisively. At that expression, Subaru lowered his eyes, took a single deep breath, and then opened his mouth. Praying, don't come, don't come—

"—The Witch Cult. They might be coming."

"——!"

The instant Subaru managed to say it, his eyes widening, was almost the same instant Rachins's expression changed at the ominous words. Right after saying it, Subaru glanced down at his own chest, but there was no change. Even revealing part of what he'd learned through Return by Death had incurred no penalty. At that, first and foremost, Subaru let his shoulders drop in relief.

—For Subaru, Return by Death itself had been something he hadn't experienced in a year, but the various penalties tied to it were, even now, very much alive and well.

Especially the time he'd resolved to tell Beatrice everything—that had been true, unprecedented hellish agony, in every sense of the word. It was a brutality that seemed to forget entirely how, at their parting at the Witch's tea party in the Sanctuary, the Witch of Envy—likely the true culprit behind that black hand of madness—had seen him off with such grace. Because of that, Subaru still, to this day, had never managed to confess to Beatrice, or to anyone else, the truth of Return by Death itself.

Of course, within the bounds that didn't trigger the penalty, he believed he'd already shared a full set of relevant knowledge with his partner Beatrice. The details of that would take too long to go into, so he'd omit them here.

Regardless, what mattered was that, without triggering the penalty, Subaru had managed to convey to Rachins a legitimate reason to call Reinhard. In fact, even Rachins seemed to find something worth mulling over in the mere sound of "Witch Cult"—he narrowed his slightly upturned eyes in contemplation.

"Oi, kid."

"It's Natsuki Subaru. Quit calling me kid already, Rachins."

"It's Mr. Rachins to you. Damn Subaru. How much of that story can I even trust? Even scumbags don't bluff with the Witch Cult's name."

Lowering his voice, Rachins turned a gaze brimming with hostility on Subaru. In this world, the names of the Witch of Envy and the Witch Cult carried a weight beyond measure. That was proof of just how deeply the word "Witch" had taken root as the very embodiment of Absolute Evil. Even Rachins was asking the question with a seriousness he never otherwise showed—which left Subaru no choice but to respond in kind.

"It's not a joke, or a moment of confusion, or a lie. The Witch Cult is coming here. A lot of people are going to be in danger."

"Where'd you even hear— ah, damn it. Right. You two took down the Witch Cult's 'Sloth.' Shit, does that give this any credibility...!?"

Before Subaru could even offer his grounds, Rachins had already dug up his own justification from what information he had. In truth, it was a wide-of-the-mark guess, but its result ended up helping Rachins reconcile the story's consistency in his own head far more than expected.

"'Here' meaning the city? Or you saying they're coming to this square?"

"You're actually gonna believe me?"

"You're the one who said this ain't a joke, damn Subaru. Listen up. I don't want that Reinhard bastard's nagging, but I sure as hell don't want to end up dead either. If whether I can pull this off comes down to my mood, then you'd better watch your mouth too."

While leaving room for doubt, he was hammering out the details in preparation for the worst. Surprised at how unexpectedly rational a judgment Rachins had made, Subaru, feeling he'd gotten through, went on to fill in the missing pieces.

"Alright, my bad. The Witch Cult's 'Wrath' is going to attack this square. Location's the Time Tower, and the target is everyone in this square. Not any one individual."

"Sounds about right for the Witch Cult's lack of standards. Damn it. Time?"

"Probably less than five minutes. So please, call him right now."

"Five minutes!? The hell!? Why didn't you say that sooner!?"

"Because I've been asking you for five minutes already!"

Rachins raised his voice at the lack of time, but Subaru had already been cursing that same fact more than enough. If it were at all possible, Subaru himself didn't want to rely on such a tightrope of a method. Running through the whole town shouting Reinhard's name to track him down would have been far more reliable. But time wouldn't allow Subaru that luxury.

"Anyway, if you can call him, no joke, do it right now. The method you mentioned yesterday was something like a signal flare, right?"

"What, signal flare? ...The signal is firing Magic up into the sky where that redheaded bastard can see it."

"...Simple enough, I guess, but what happens if by some coincidence someone else fires Magic into the sky at the same time?"

"No need to worry about that. That redheaded bastard can apparently tell whose Magic it is just by looking at it once it's up there."

Even without the details, Subaru could tell just how extraordinary that was. It also made him realize, plainly, that over this past year, Rachins too had improved himself to the point of being able to use simple Magic. It was hard to imagine, from their earlier exchanges, that either of them could use Magic back when they'd first met.

"Oi, this ain't a show! Get the hell outta here, you gawking bastards!"

It seemed Rachins had taken Subaru's persuasion to heart and decided to call Reinhard. Spitting at the onlookers who'd been eagerly waiting for the brawl to start, Rachins gathered his mana and thrust his fist upward.

Watching red mana—Goa, the fire Magic—gather there, Subaru felt the illusion that he could hear, as sound, the situation shifting.

This should change things dramatically. Even though he'd been the one pushing for it, Subaru was glad that Rachins had listened to his words and taken this action. There had been coincidences along the way—the true motive behind his action being self-preservation, or the reasoning that had moved him being off the mark—but even so. Having defeated Petelgeuse, everything Subaru had shown over this past year, and the fact that Rachins himself had changed, if only a little—it felt like all of that had drawn this result into being.

If neither Subaru nor Rachins had changed since a year ago, this wouldn't have happened. So the feeling that this result would lead to the right answer was, itself, a solid, tangible sense of certainty.

Reinhard would come to this place. Once that happened, dealing with "Wrath" would be easy. However skilled the man might be, that skill paled utterly before the Sword Saint. Subaru had some inexplicable confidence that even Wrath's Authority itself would have no effect on Reinhard.

"Goa!"

Rachins wove the word of power, and red flame shot up from the tip of his raised arm. It rose straight up, and burst with an oddly dry crack at the center of the blue sky.

It was a rather shabby effect to call either fireworks or Magic, but even an effect that modest ought to be more than enough for that hero.

Was it because of that vague sense of relief? That something so obvious, so utterly obvious, hadn't occurred to him? Was it because he'd stopped being able to see anything else, convinced that calling Reinhard would turn the whole situation around for the better?

"—Oh my. A single fireball, off in the distant sky. Such a lovely glow."

If a commotion arose outside the Time Tower, it was only natural that the enemy would sense it too.

Sirius, now visible atop the white tower, still had his face wrapped in bandages, his expression unreadable as ever. And yet it was easy to tell that expression was a smile, because such a light, buoyant feeling—as if enjoying birdsong on a balmy spring day—came through plainly in the tone of his voice.

Sirius shaded his eyes with one hand and narrowed them at the glow of the rising Goa. With a gesture exactly like someone watching fireworks, he enjoyed the red flame, then clapped his hands.

"All right! Well then, everyone, sorry about this! Good morning, everybody!"

An unnaturally loud, resounding clap of applause rang out, and the crowd, whose attention had been stolen by the Goa, reflexively turned their gazes toward Sirius instead. Even Subaru was no exception. So he couldn't blame them for their reaction.

"Don't! Don't look!!"

Catching sight of that villainous, bandaged smile at the edge of his vision, Subaru immediately cried out a warning. But not a single person heeded that warning and looked away. Of course not. Subaru himself, at their first contact, must have felt the exact same pull.

Even with his face turned away, the left cheek angled toward Sirius could feel it. The wave of life-threatening malice that existed there.

How could one look away from a carnivorous beast lunging with its jaws wide open? Turning away from those sharp fangs was something one did only after giving up on life, after one's spirit had already been broken. For those who still refused death, being unable to look away from Sirius was an instinct unavoidable simply by virtue of being human.

"Oh my. That was much faster than I expected, everyone settling down so quietly. It might be thanks to the two of you, who were kind enough to keep everyone's attention before I even showed my face. Thank you. Let's give those two a round of applause."

Sirius said "clap clap" with his mouth while gesturing with both chain-draped hands toward the two of them: Rachins, arm still raised overhead, and Subaru, desperately trying to turn his face away. Feeling a chill race up his spine, Subaru gritted his teeth desperately, trying not to react to Sirius's attention. It was already hopeless to try to pull back the consciousness of Rachins, who had lost his words and was caught in Sirius's gaze. Nothing Subaru said to him now would reach him—and even before that, Subaru himself had confidence that if he tried any special action from here, he'd be caught by Sirius too.

In fact, Subaru couldn't cover his ears right now. He had predicted that the moment he perceived Sirius's presence, he would surely become unable to resist. So he'd intended, from the start, to look away first, cover his ears, and take countermeasures against the mental manipulation even while exposing himself defenselessly. But that countermeasure, thought out in advance, hadn't survived even past the point of looking away.

What was covering his ears even for? Obviously, so as not to have to hear Sirius's voice. But why did he have to avoid hearing Sirius's voice, when hearing that voice felt this pleasant?

"——"

Before he knew it, Subaru had turned his face back—the face he was supposed to have turned away—and was looking at Sirius. Seeing Subaru do so, Sirius swayed his body side to side happily, clasping both hands together. The chains rattled and scraped against the floor, and that metallic sound felt, somehow, like the sound of the solid defenses of Subaru's heart being torn down.

"All right! It took nineteen seconds for everyone to turn and look my way. Sorry about that. But I'm delighted. And it seems like some of you love me even more than I expected, which is also delightful. Now then, I suppose I should introduce myself."

Amid the whirlpool of wordless gazes looking up at him, Sirius bowed politely. Then he raised his head, and, surveying the surroundings with those bulging eyes, said,

"I am the Witch Cult's Sin Archbishop in charge of Wrath. My name is Sirius Romanée-Conti."

A dreadful self-introduction had been made. A name that ought, by rights, to be spoken as a symbol of loathing and terror—shunned and eliminated. And yet the crowd below received that declaration as though hearing the name of a familiar neighbor.

"Fufu, thank you. Sorry for taking up everyone's time like this. But I'll finish up quickly, so please, don't worry."

"—Is that so. Then it seems I'd best finish things quickly myself."

And that voice, deeper and gentler than Sirius's counterfeit affection, soaked into Subaru and the others in the square, and struck their hearts.

"——"

Sirius's gaze dropped, and all of their gazes turned to the side in unison. Everyone's eyes turned toward the canal flowing behind the square. That canal, where water normally flowed gently, now had water thrown up by something racing against the current at tremendous speed.

It burned red, like flame. It had eyes as clear as the blue sky. Its form and figure were so perfectly composed that any existence would be enchanted by the sight.

It was the very embodiment of what every human being pictures in their heart when they think of the word "hero."

"It took me some time to find a shortcut. My apologies for the delay in arriving."

Not five seconds, but thirty—for that delay, the hero apologized. Calling it a shortcut, the Sword Saint had performed the feat of racing across a roadless path—no, across the very surface of the water—to reach the scene. He swept his eyes over the people gathered there, and then, noticing "Wrath" perched above their heads, let out a breath.

"I see. I understand now why I was called. Your judgment was correct, Rachins. Or was it you who called me, Subaru?"

Stepping from the water onto land, Reinhard entered the square, offered a word of comfort to the still-frozen Rachins, then clapped a hand on Subaru's shoulder. At the tangible sense of presence that traveled through that palm, Subaru gasped and trembled all over.

"R-Reinhard...?"

"Yes, it's me. That this was an emergency is obvious just from looking at this. The one up there is... a Sin Archbishop, isn't it."

Reinhard stood there in his now-focused field of vision, nodding firmly. He furrowed his handsome brow, and made a conscious effort not to turn his face toward Sirius. It seemed even Reinhard had grasped, at a glance, just how dangerous Sirius was.

"He's got some kind of brainwashing power. Right now it's a bit better, but... the longer you hear his voice, or look him in the eye—"

"I understand. It's not just his voice or appearance, either. It seems merely feeling his presence on your skin has an effect. I doubt even I can keep my composure for very long."

"No way, you're—!?"

At words from Reinhard that could be taken as weak-willed, Subaru fell silent, a sense of despair washing over him. Without any real basis, he'd simply believed that Reinhard would be fine—but what Reinhard himself had just told him suggested the possibility that even he might not be able to handle Sirius. If that were true, it was hard even to imagine how one could possibly deal with that evil at all.

"Sorry if I'm wrong. Could you, by any chance, be that famous Sword Saint? If so... what a wonderful day this is!"

"As you say. I have been granted the title of Sword Saint—Reinhard van Astrea. Unfortunately, I'm well aware that title is still more than I deserve."

"Not at all! But that's fine! I'm delighted you came! Because you're the one person in this country who carries the greatest trust and highest expectations as a knight, on your shoulders alone. Everyone loves you, and you love everyone in turn. You're the embodiment of my hope, the very manifestation of 'Love' itself!"

"Is that so?"

Sirius carried on in what could only be described as a fit of ecstatic, dancing joy. Reinhard, for his part, kept his face turned away, yet still managed to hold a conversation with the rambling Sirius. If mere communication with him led straight to madness, then Reinhard's actions here were nothing but a noose tightening around his own neck. Subaru, panicking, said,

"H-hey, Reinhard... you shouldn't keep talking to him like that. That's bad. Should be bad. I think it was bad. Though I'm not really sure why."

"...It does seem that way. It's not just a problem for me personally. Considering the effect on everyone around us, I shouldn't let this drag on too long."

Subaru, who was supposed to have been panicking, found he could no longer remember what he'd even been panicking about. Reinhard sighed at Subaru tilting his head in confusion, then took one step forward. And then—

"Reinhard?"

"I can't hold out much longer myself. —So let's settle this right away."

The instant he finished speaking, Reinhard bent his knees slightly and leapt. It looked like nothing more than a small hop over a puddle in front of him—and yet it produced an effect like a blast of wind, a shockwave rippling across the ground that stole the breath from everyone nearby.

Leaving that astonishment behind, Reinhard converted the force of that explosive leap directly into upward momentum, and—

"Ufufufufu! Ah, how utterly extraordinary—!"

The Sword Saint's kick, driving upward from below, sent Sirius's body—crossing his arms to block the blow—soaring effortlessly, far above the white tower and into the sky.
