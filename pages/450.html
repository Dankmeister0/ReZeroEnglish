Arc 6 Chapter 45: "A Sinner's Embrace"

—Down the right arm, striped with its black mottled pattern, painful red droplets dripped.

Fortunately, it seemed, even for a right arm that had undergone such a bold change of image, its durability was no different from that of an ordinary human body. Dig in a fingernail and pain shot through it; press down with force and the skin split, and blood came out.

He had the feeling—just a feeling—that the bleeding from that wound stopped a little faster than it should. But it really was a negligible difference.

"——"

He wiped away the blood roughly with his palm, and pulled his sleeve down, leaving a smeared trace of blood on his right arm. Even though it was the result of his own doing, it wasn't something he wanted to keep looking at for long. Of course, he also hid the words carved into his left arm—Natsuki Subaru, present—by pulling the sleeve down over them.

"Anyway, I can't stay here long..."

He'd already wasted a considerable amount of time in this room. In the center of the room, unchanged, lay Meili's corpse, breathless and unmoving—but there was nothing more Subaru could do for her now. He had no wish to perform some particular rite for her, nor to defile her in death.

—No, wait. What he was about to do might well count as defiling her in death.

"——"

Her body was light. Slipping his arms under the girl's armpits, Subaru dragged Meili's corpse and shifted its position to a corner of the room. There, he found what might once have been furniture in this abandoned room. Perhaps originally a stone table, or a stone bed. In any case, there was a roughly cut, square block of stone sitting there, and he gently hid Meili's body behind it.

—He apologized to Meili from the bottom of his heart for the wrong of it, but Subaru had decided to hide her death.

To begin with, if he confessed everything without concealment, who exactly could defend Subaru in this situation? Subaru himself couldn't fathom the true intentions of "Natsuki Subaru"—so who else possibly could?

"There's no defending it. Whoever killed Meili... even if it wasn't me, the weapon that killed her was these two hands of mine."

He had no way of guessing what level of criminal investigation existed in this world, but if a jury looked at the marks on Meili's hand and the wounds on Subaru's wrist, they would return a unanimous guilty verdict against him. And Subaru couldn't find within himself any grounds to object, to appeal.

That being the case, what Subaru could do was abandon the trial—no, hide the crime. He didn't think this was the right judgment, or the best choice. But Subaru had no other means available to him. Who to trust, who to suspect—he no longer knew.

If there was one thing he could say for certain, it was—

"Next time I see you, Meili... you're the only one who isn't a suspect."

Total annihilation, and strangulation—Subaru had now seen Meili's corpse with his own eyes twice. At this point, he could at least conclude that Meili was less suspicious than anyone else. The problem was that even granting that, there was no longer any way to hear her side of the story.

It was the standard convention of closed-circle mysteries: only the dead are excluded from the list of suspects. But the detective in this story, Natsuki Subaru, broke the rules by possessing the power of Return by Death—rewinding time by dying. With that, he could handle any tragedy and still be a great detective, incompetence notwithstanding.

"Though 'the detective himself is the culprit' is also a mystery-novel convention."

Unfortunately, however excellent Return by Death might be, so long as "Natsuki Subaru" remained the strong suspect as culprit, the incident would keep repeating no matter how many times it played out, and stopping it looked difficult. The detective's own alternate personality as the culprit—that pattern, too, was well-worn in mysteries. In that case, would the case be closed once the detective realized the truth and threw himself off a cliff?

Combine Return by Death with "the detective himself is the culprit," and the case becomes eternally unsolved. An inescapable, spiraling labyrinth, more or less.

"Is this really the time to be saying that...! Anyway, for now, I just need to buy time."

It wasn't a problem time would solve, but time always brought something. Believing that, Subaru wished to break free from this dead-end situation and rose to his feet where he stood.

"——"

As he made to leave, Subaru stroked Meili's forehead once—she who no longer moved. Surely, she would never have wanted to be touched by him again. As proof of that, the forehead he touched was cold enough to make him sad, rejecting him.

And so, however unwillingly, having concealed the scene of Meili's murder, Subaru left the room. Then, checking left and right down the corridor, he tried to slowly withdraw from the scene—

"—Ah, Subaru! Good, you're here."

"—Ah!?"

Startled by the sudden call, his shoulders jumped, and turning around, Subaru found Emilia coming toward him. Trotting up to him, she tilted her head at Subaru's stiffened cheeks.

"Sorry, did I scare you?"

"—I, I was startled. I was startled, but that's, well, uh, that. It's just that it was sudden, I'm totally fine. What about you, Emilia-chan?"

"—? I was just wandering around the tower, wondering if there was anything I could do before lunch. More importantly, Subaru."

"Hm?"

"Just now—was that another one of your jokes?"

At Emilia's puzzled question, Subaru held his breath. Her violet eyes stared straight at him, but Subaru couldn't grasp what she meant by her question. It was true there'd been a waver in his voice, but the content of what he'd said shouldn't have stood out as anything unusual.

"——"

Under Emilia's gaze—not just steady but downright piercing—Subaru felt an uncomfortable sensation and swallowed an oddly bitter mouthful of saliva. Right behind him, in the room, Meili's corpse still lay sleeping on. He'd hidden it well enough that a casual glance into the room wouldn't reveal it, but it was only a childish concealment that would be found out easily with a slightly more thorough search.

In any case, getting away from this spot was the priority right now.

"Sorry, gotta use the bathroom."

"Oh, okay, got it."

Leaving behind words that were, by any measure, a flimsy excuse, Subaru quickly moved away from in front of the room. Walking fast, putting distance between himself and Emilia, he had to get somewhere he could kill time alone as soon as possible. There, he'd deal with the wound on his right arm—

"Wait, Emilia-chan? I said I'm going to the bathroom. Why are you following me?"

"Huh? Well, in a little while everyone's going to gather for lunch, so don't you think it'd be better to stay together? Don't worry, I'll wait right outside the door."

"I'm not worried about you hearing me pee or anything..."

Emilia fell into step beside Subaru as he tried to put distance between them, peering into his face. It was a reasonable point, reasonable as such things go—but generally, being on the receiving end of a reasonable point is a painful experience. This case was no exception; Subaru, on the receiving end, struggled to come up with a reasonable point of his own.

Yet even as he struggled outwardly, inwardly Subaru carefully tried to gauge what was in Emilia's heart.

—What did Emilia intend, wanting to accompany Subaru like this?

"——"

"—?"

Emilia blinked in bewilderment at Subaru, who kept sidelong glances fixed intently on her. It was an adorable face that looked as though it were scheming nothing at all—but in reality, she was a powerful suspect.

During the last total annihilation, Subaru hadn't seen the corpses of Emilia and Beatrice. It was true that, going by the wound on his arm, "Natsuki Subaru's" true intentions remained unknown—but even granting that "Natsuki Subaru" had been secretly at work, that situation couldn't have been created by one person alone.

Shaula, Echidna, Ram. Julius, Meili—killed, and finally the tower brought down by the power of shadow, and at the end, someone had laughed as they took Subaru's own head. Someone whose existence Subaru still didn't know of had been inside that tower.

"Subaru, are you okay? Maybe you still haven't fully recovered?"

"—Gh, I—I'm fine. I was just lost in thought a little."

"Really? But I think you look pale."

Gently, Emilia reached her hand toward Subaru. Unable to bring himself to brush it away in time, he let her do as she pleased, fingers touching his cheek. At that ticklish sensation, Subaru furrowed his brow.

"...You're really watching closely."

"Mm, that's right, I am. Lately, I keep noticing my eyes following you around without realizing it... I don't really know why, it's strange."

"—Yeah, it's strange, all right."

Carefully, he kept watch over Emilia's every move. Her smiling hand was touching Subaru's face. In the blink of an eye, there was no guarantee that hand wouldn't slash open his throat. Or perhaps there'd be no need for that at all—with Emilia's strength, she could just as easily snap his neck outright.

And yet, he couldn't sense any malice or hostility in the way her hand moved, and Subaru was at a loss. In the end, Subaru's sense of these things—Subaru who only knew a peaceful Japan, a student walking home from the convenience store—couldn't be relied on. He had almost no experience touching real human malice or ill will.

What Subaru was familiar with was the atmosphere of being kept at a wary distance as a nuisance, and a silence of indifference that couldn't even be called cold contempt. —Neither of those, though they left Natsuki Subaru isolated, ever hurt him.

So he didn't know whether the emotion floating in the violet eyes of the girl before him now was real or false. And if it was true that he didn't know anything at all—

"——"

What kind of face would she make, if he threw it all out there? That beautiful face of hers, which looked as though she'd been raised seeing nothing but pretty things, as though she had not a single worry in the world—how satisfying would it be to crumple that face up?

—To tell her: the Natsuki Subaru you're worrying over doesn't exist anywhere anymore. —No, that's wrong. To tell her: that guy is a cruel, deranged murderer.

"—Come to think of it, about Meili—"

"—Gh"

His throat made a sharp hitch, and Subaru's eyes went wide. It was a moment of unguarded shock he couldn't hide at all—why bring up Meili's name here, of all places? If Emilia's aim had been to see exactly that reaction, then Subaru had walked completely into her trap.

But Emilia's eyes had drifted away from Subaru, down to the floor. It wasn't the corridor she seemed to be looking at, but something larger—as if she were looking at the tower as a whole. In that posture, after a moment of faint hesitation, she said,

"Would you get mad at me for jumping ahead of myself? Once we get back safely from this tower, I want to talk about what to do about Meili."

"——"

"Of course, what that girl did a year ago was wrong, and I understand Otto-kun's point that we shouldn't trust her so easily... but we made it across the sand dunes thanks to Meili, and if she still meant to do something bad, I think she could have done plenty before we even got to the tower."

Pinching the hem of her own clothes, Emilia laid out, bit by bit, what she'd been thinking. It didn't feel like idle chatter—among the group here in the tower, Meili's position was a peculiar one; apparently, she was originally an assassin sent after Emilia and Beatrice's lives. Having failed that mission and ended up a captive, she'd nonetheless made use of her innate special ability to accompany them on this journey, contributing to the very goals of her former targets.

"An amnesty, huh."

"I don't think everyone would agree to just setting her free. But something else—like, letting her live outside the座敷牢... outside the confinement room, at least, right?"

"——"

"Of course, that's on the condition that I actually talk to Meili herself, and she wants it too. It'd be terrible if I got ahead of myself and she ended up hating me for it."

Waving her hands hastily, Emilia insisted it was just one opinion of hers. Likely, this was something she'd thought through carefully in her own way, and had chosen her moment to share. Here and there, you could glimpse traces of it having been revised in anticipation of objections.

—Was this Emilia's true feeling?

"——"

Was it all right to believe it? At the very least, up to this point, there hadn't been a single moment where Emilia's manner had shown hostility toward Subaru. —No, if he was going to say that, then to begin with, no one had shown hostility toward Subaru at all. Not Beatrice, not Echidna, not Ram, not Julius, not Meili—not a single one of them had shown him hostility.

The only certainties were: the culprit who'd pushed Subaru to his death, the culprit who'd wiped out everyone connected to the tower, the culprit who'd taken Subaru's own head. —And "Natsuki Subaru," who had killed Meili. Only that these were despicable evils to be reviled.

So then, was it all right to believe in Emilia? In her—innocent, unstained, like freshly fallen snow?

—Emilia, speaking with such warmth, so worried over Meili—the very girl these hands had killed—and even so, would she still say she believed in him?

"...How stupid."

"Huh?"

"I said it's pointless. You must have realized it yourself. Jumping ahead of yourself? You're right about that. In a situation like... like this, how can you possibly talk about future prospects?"

Hemmed in on every side, with no room whatsoever to spare concern for anyone else. Especially not when it concerned the future of a girl who was already dead. Even setting aside the fact that she didn't know about that "death," what a carefree head she must have.

"——"

Having lashed out emotionally, Subaru immediately bit his lip at his own words. What a stupid thing to say. Something pointless to say, something that would only arouse suspicion, hurled at Emilia on nothing but the momentum and emotion of the moment. There was no justification for this act at all. Just venting, a child's tantrum. And then—

"Subaru!"

"Bfh"

"What's gotten into you all of a sudden? Even if you're in a bad mood, you shouldn't talk like that."

"——"

Startled, stunned, and expecting her to bow her head, hurt, and dissolve into tears and sobs—Subaru had braced for that reaction. But scattering that expectation, Emilia caught Subaru's cheeks firmly between both hands and looked straight into his black eyes.

"—Ah"

"If you're sulking, if you're in pain, then talk about it properly! Me, or Beatrice, either is fine. If Subaru's in trouble, then I'll be in trouble right along with you. But don't shoulder it all alone, and get frustrated all alone, and end it all alone. That's just like Roswaal used to be, back when he was awful. Don't copy him."

Earnestly, with all her might, Emilia pressed the words at Subaru. Then, releasing her hands from his dumbfounded face, she pulled his head toward her in one firm tug. And she drew Subaru's head into her chest, stroking it gently.

"Do you understand? My heart isn't angry at all. I won't be disillusioned either, so talk to me."

"——"

Pressed against that soft warmth, beyond its heat, he could feel a rhythm carving out life. It was gentle, as though it were a lullaby sung to a newborn child, and Subaru held his breath. In that instant, what welled up in Subaru's heart was an intense feeling of shame.

She'd done this much for him, and he'd said something so awful, and still, even now, she was kind. To doubt someone like that, to hate her blindly and hurt her—what was the point of any of it?

—Was it really true? Was there really someone out there scheming to kill Subaru? —Wasn't the fall that killed him actually just an accident? —Not something anyone did on purpose, just someone tripping by chance, bumping into him.

Inside this tower, maybe there was no one bad at all. Maybe the one with the filthiest, ugliest, most dangerous heart was Natsuki Subaru and "Natsuki Subaru" alone—an intrusive foreign element that shouldn't have existed here in the first place.

"Emilia, I..."

"—Mm."

"I..."

He didn't know where to begin. He only knew that he wanted to tell her, to confess it, to lay it all bare.

The lost memories, Meili, the fact that he went back in time every time he died. She might not believe any of it. But she might believe it. And if she believed him, maybe they could find a way to break through this. If he could just find that, then Subaru—

"—Emilia-sama! Barusu!"

Just as he was trying, somehow, to wring out the jumbled contents of his mind, a sharp, urgent voice cut in from the side, painting over Subaru's anguish. Still held in Emilia's embrace, Subaru couldn't immediately look toward the voice. From above his head, he heard Emilia, having confirmed who it was, murmur, "Ram."

"What's wrong? Right now Subaru and I are having a really important talk..."

"That much Ram can see for herself, but... please stop. This is an urgent matter."

"O-okay..."

As the sound of footsteps arrived right beside them, Emilia hesitantly released Subaru. Feeling the warmth pull away, and the heat at the corners of his eyes, Subaru forcibly wiped his face with his sleeve to chase it off. Then, together with Emilia, he turned to face Ram.

Having been caught looking so pathetic, or at the worst possible timing—swelling with emotion, he wanted to lash out at Ram over it. But even having just reflected on doing exactly that, the fact that he couldn't recover himself right away was genuinely infuriating.

But Ram's manner showed no room to tease the two of them for embracing, nor to spare any consideration for Subaru, at a loss over where to direct his emotions after being seen like that.

"Ram, what's wrong?"

"...It's an urgent matter. Please come to the third floor library at once. Beatrice-sama has found something terrible."

"Beatrice did?"

At Emilia's startled question, Ram gave a short nod. "Yes." Then, turning her back on the two of them, she said,

"Anastasia-sama... no, Echidna, perhaps. In any case, Ram will go find her and Julius. Barusu, please accompany Emilia-sama."

"Ah, yeah, got it..."

At her tone that brooked no argument, Subaru found himself nodding before he could object. Without waiting to see his answer, Ram kicked off smartly and dashed away from the two of them. Startled by this, Subaru turned back to Emilia.

"Um, Ram said that, but..."

"—Let's hurry. Ram was that serious. Something terrible must have happened."

"——"

"Subaru, I haven't forgotten what we were just talking about."

"...Yeah."

At Emilia's pointed reminder, Subaru nodded weakly and followed along. Between his guilt over Meili and the urgency of the situation, Subaru's plan to be alone had now been completely derailed.

And so, together with Emilia, Subaru hurried toward the third floor, Taygeta. Racing up the long staircase, what greeted them was the library of the "dead," boasting its vast collection of books.

"—So you've come."

Standing before the stairs, with the shelves holding countless Books of the Dead at her back, Beatrice greeted them. Arms crossed, her short frame still, the girl with the distinctive patterned eyes lowered her gaze and let out an unusually tired sigh.

"Beatrice, Ram called us here. She said you found something terrible."

"It's certainly not good news, in one sense, I suppose. If anything, it might be better called an ill omen, I suppose."

So saying, Beatrice slowly shook her head at Emilia's question. Then she turned her blue eyes toward Subaru.

"Betty's been examining this Taygeta library since morning. Not just because Subaru collapsed here, but because as a structure, this room is close to yet distant from the Forbidden Library, so Betty was trying to analyze it, I suppose."

"N-never mind the preamble. What happened? Tell me."

Having raced up the stairs, breathing slightly ragged, Subaru rushed toward the conclusion. At his words, Beatrice closed her eyes for a moment. Then, slowly, she pointed toward one of the bookshelves off to the side, behind her.

"Third shelf from the top, the book all the way to the right, I suppose."

"—Third from the top." "All the way to the right."

Following Beatrice's indication, Subaru and Emilia repeated the words to themselves as they moved toward the shelf. The shelves were packed tight with books, and the otherworldly script on the spines was entirely unreadable to Subaru. As ever, it looked like nothing more than pictographs to him.

So he had no idea, either, what the title was of the book Beatrice wanted to show them. At the very least, given that it was here in this library, it was certainly a Book of the Dead—

"No way..."

Emilia, right beside him, let the words slip out. Glancing over at her, Subaru's cheeks stiffened at her stunned reaction.

Such shock, and grief following swiftly on its heels. What in the world had struck her heart so hard, with such force and no mercy? Beside Emilia, who was clearly in shock, Subaru felt a righteous anger rise in him—and then, her lips trembled.

And then, Emilia said:

"—Meili Portroute."
