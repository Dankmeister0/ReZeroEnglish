Arc 6 Chapter 6: "The Juukulius Name"

The journey had begun on a morning full of unease, but though the roughly month-long trip was long in distance, no particular incident occurred along the way. Pressing straight eastward down the highway, a journey where nothing happened was, frankly, boring.

"It was the same on the round trip to Priestella, and the same on the Lifaus Plains too... but the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica really keeps its roads safe, huh."

"Road maintenance and patrols to preserve public order are indispensable to keeping the peace of the kingdom. Compared to other nations, Lugunica is especially thorough about that. The incidence of harm from bandits or packs of Witchbeasts is kept quite low, as a rule."

"So other countries aren't like that?"

"In the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko, buried under eternal snow, road maintenance itself is barely feasible to begin with. No sooner is a road cleared than it's buried in snow again. In the Vollachia Empire and the Kararagi City-States, so many different peoples mingle that there are many with differing customs. Where customs differ, conflicts arise. In that sense, it might be hard to call their public order stable."

"Huh."

This was the conversation between Subaru and Julius, riding their two Earth Dragons side by side, cutting through the wind at their leisure. Ahead of the two Earth Dragons pulling the large Dragon Carriage, Subaru's Patrasche and Julius's blue mount led the way. If everyone stayed inside the carriage, reactions would be too slow in an emergency — hence this formation. But as the boredom-killing chatter made plain, the journey was peace itself.

"Fwaah..."

"Subaru."

Too much boredom, plus scenery that never changed, brought on yawns. Subaru, hand at his mouth as drowsiness swelled unbidden, drew a reproachful look from Julius. Leaning his weight against his beloved mount, Julius swept back the bangs that, protected by his 'Divine Protection of Wind Evasion,' never stirred in the wind.

"I understand the feeling of not being able to sustain tension indefinitely, but the moment when tautened nerves go slack is said to be the most dangerous of all. I won't say never let your guard down, but slacking off in a way visible to anyone's eyes is not something I can approve of."

"One yawn and you go off like that. You yawn too, don't you? Come on, you do."

"Of course, such a physiological phenomenon occurs in me as well. However, if one has the self-awareness of a knight, isn't it possible to suppress it enough not to show it in public? That's something you still lack."

"Yeah, yeah."

Prodded along with a pointed, needling persistence, but Subaru's way of deflecting it had by now become something of an art. He and Julius had already been traveling together for nearly twenty days. From Priestella to the mansion, and now on this leg toward the Augria Sand Dunes, riding their Earth Dragons side by side the whole way — one picks up a few tricks for handling this sort of thing.

"I believe it's proper etiquette to look at the person you're speaking with, when the conversation is a serious one, don't you think?"

"If you're looking away during a serious conversation, that just means it's not the moment for a serious conversation. You could stand to loosen up a little yourself. You're too tense."

"If neither you nor I stay vigilant, then how—"

"You don't have to be so on edge, they're not gonna jump us just like that. If those guys were gonna make a move, they wouldn't do it out in the open like this."

"——"

Cutting him off, Subaru cracked his neck as he said it. As if caught off guard by Subaru's words, Julius blinked. Then, almost at once, the handsome man let out a small breath.

"Am I that transparent, even to your eyes, when I'm anxious?"

"It's kind of a guess, honestly. But I think everyone thinks you're being too uptight. Well, I guess 'as usual' could just mean this is your usual..."

"You're the only one now who would know what my 'usual' is."

"...Yeah, huh."

As the tone of Julius's voice dropped, Subaru's own voice naturally lowered too. Behind them, the women's conversation inside the carriage couldn't be heard, and likewise, this conversation surely couldn't reach them either. Man to man — and while their positions were complicated in various ways, they were also, for now, allies working together. Perhaps this was a moment to speak a little more openly about the things that needed saying.

"I mentioned it to Roswaal too, but — how are the quasi-spirits doing?"

"Just as I said back there. The buds gather around me, but they won't rest their wings on the perch of my outstretched arm. My words don't seem to reach them either."

At Subaru's question, Julius raised his arm and made the quasi-spirits visible. The six faintly glowing quasi-spirits, in their six colors, still floated around Julius even as he rode. But they wavered as if bewildered by the arm held out to them, and instead tried to draw away. So indeed, the bond between Julius and the quasi-spirits did seem to be lost.

"Can't you re-form the Contract? Since they're staying close, that means your Divine Protection — the thing that draws spirits, whatchamacallit — is still working, right?"

"My 'Divine Protection of Gathering Spirits' does still seem to be intact. If anything, that only adds to their confusion — as though they're struggling to handle some great, incomprehensible feeling within themselves."

"This might sound bad, but — what about settling for a different spirit?"

"If I were a spirit user like Emilia-sama, able to borrow strength from the minor spirits wherever I go, that option might be worth considering... but that method wouldn't let me draw out a spirit's power to its fullest. It took several years just to forge the bond with the quasi-spirit buds in the first place."

"...Well, even Emilia-tan can't pull Puck-level power out of some other spirit. Guess the spirit who becomes your partner really is something special."

"Emilia-sama and the Great Spirit-sama. As with you and Beatrice-sama."

A troublesome thing. As a fellow spirit user himself, there was no way Subaru could say something like "just go find your next spirit" so easily. It would amount to the same thing as asking whether, if his bond with Beatrice were severed, he could just let her go. Naturally, that was a proposal he'd answer with a raised middle finger. For Julius, his relationship with the six quasi-spirits posed the exact same problem.

"And so, for now, I can only fulfill my duties as a knight through swordsmanship. Of course, I don't think the sword is inferior to the Spiritual Arts, but it's a fact that my own strength is lacking."

"This trip's partly about getting that back, and besides, coming from a swordsman as good as you, that's just being snide. And having me be the one you say it to makes it even more snide."

The deficiency Julius spoke of — his strength lacking without the Spiritual Arts. A year ago, it was precisely that level of strength that had left Subaru a wrung-out rag on the training grounds of the royal capital. Compared to back then, Subaru himself had gotten somewhat better, but he didn't think the gap had closed. From Julius's perspective, the Subaru of back then was on the level of twisting a baby's arm. The Subaru of now was more like dealing with a five-year-old — that was probably about the size of the difference.

"You and Reinhard both have this bad habit of underselling yourselves, you know that? Modesty taken too far turns into poison! I think that's a saying that holds up anywhere."

"I'd like to return that exact sentiment to you, but let's see. — Setting you and me aside, what Reinhard has isn't modesty, nor underestimation of himself, I think."

"Not modesty or underestimation...?"

The same red-haired hero came to both their minds, yet Subaru cocked his head at the difference in how they perceived him. Reinhard was, by anyone's estimation, a superhuman, the strongest, peerless and complete — Subaru hadn't imagined their evaluations of him could differ. To Subaru's puzzlement, Julius shook his head, saying "No."

"As for Reinhard's actual strength, you and I hold him in the same high regard. Rather, everyone who knows him surely shares the same feeling — that he is humanity's pinnacle, or perhaps, a being who has transcended it."

"It's amazing that saying that isn't even an exaggeration."

"It's not just his strength — even his self-awareness of his own way of being is complete. I first met him when he wasn't yet ten years old... and he hasn't changed since."

"Being like that since he was ten, seriously?"

Gripping Patrasche's reins, Subaru was stunned by Julius's recollection. Just when, exactly, had Reinhard become the Reinhard he was now? It seemed a rather philosophical question, but apparently he'd already been complete nearly ten years ago. If so, what a fiercely dramatic fate that boy must have carried, even then.

"Was Reinhard already the 'Sword Saint' back then?"

"He inherited the 'Divine Protection of Sword Saint' when he was only five. The previous 'Sword Saint' was Theresia van Astrea-sama... Reinhard's grandmother. She passed away during the Great Conquest fifteen years ago, and the Divine Protection was transferred to him."

"Right, that's... right."

The previous 'Sword Saint,' who died in battle against the White Whale, and whose remains were even then subjected to disgrace after death. The soul of the woman who had been Reinhard's grandmother and Wilhelm's wife had, at last, truly returned to the sky in Priestella. But the Divine Protection had already been passed on into Reinhard, fifteen years before that.

"I wonder what that feels like."

"Hm?"

"From age five, inheriting your grandma's Divine Protection, and on top of that being born into a family carrying the blood of a legendary hero, right? I can't even imagine what that must feel like to live with."

— The weight of having a parent's expectations riding on you — Subaru thought he understood that much, at least a little.

The pain of being crushed under the weight of others' expectations — that, Subaru understood. Of course, what Subaru and Reinhard each carried, and the responsibility involved, were far too different in weight for the comparison itself not to feel disrespectful.

"Honestly, I'm weak, you know. It's nothing but feeling frustrated over and over from being weak and not having enough strength — there's hardly a night I don't fantasize about being so strong it'd be almost too much to handle."

"That sounds like a fairly hollow thing to fantasize about, if I'm honest."

"Shut up."

Interrupted with a jab in the middle of something important, Subaru clicked his tongue.

"Anyway, that's basically the personal opposite of the kind of worry I have. I don't think Reinhard was already Reinhard at five, but I do wonder what that felt like for him."

"Even I can't say for certain what he was feeling back then. Only—"

Here Julius paused a moment, then lifted his face. Gripping the reins, gazing straight ahead, Julius narrowed his eyes as though dazzled by the sunlight pouring down.

"—The moment I caught sight of Reinhard, back then, was a great turning point for me."

There was something almost proud in the way he said it. Julius narrowed his eyes against the sunlight, but perhaps it wasn't only the brightness of the sun that made him do so. Back then, seeing that Reinhard for the first time — surely his eyes must have looked just the same as they did now.

"So that means you and Reinhard go back ten years? I'd say that just barely clears the bar for 'childhood friends,' wouldn't you say?"

"No, not quite. That was when I became conscious of Reinhard, but he only came to know of my existence much later... after I was appointed to the Royal Guard. That was when I was sixteen, so I've only been on close terms with him for about six years now."

"...That's a pretty big gap in between, huh?"

"Back then, I lacked the qualifications to stand alongside him — someone who had already inherited the title of 'Sword Saint.' Made to realize that, painfully, by someone younger than myself — that was where my beginning was."

Julius's quiet words, carrying a heat of feeling that ran counter to his tone. Glancing sidelong at the distant look in those yellow eyes, Subaru nodded at the word "beginning."

Surely, more vividly than his understated words let on, that day remained clear within Julius. If it was Julius's beginning, then of course it would remain vivid in memory. Subaru too carried the memory of his own "beginning," burning warm in his chest, seared into his soul.

Being saved by Emilia in another world, being held up by Rem when his crumbling back needed support, taking Beatrice's hand amid the flames — every one of these was, for Subaru, a "beginning."

"Still, though — if you're saying your beginning starts from there, does that mean you were surprisingly a bit of a delinquent as a kid? Like, some dissolute noble, a wastrel son type?"

Fighting against a flow of feeling that felt anything but someone else's business, Subaru spoke in a deliberately teasing voice. He doubted that could possibly be true of Julius, of all people, but it was a fact that when they'd first met, Subaru had thought of him as an obnoxious noble youth. And in stories, it was practically a given that such a noble youth would have led some obnoxious, arrogant sort of life as a boy.

With that feeling folded into his words, meaning to steer the conversation elsewhere, Subaru tossed out the topic — and Julius, loosening his lips into a faint smile, said,

"I'd appreciate a bit more care in how you phrase that... but there are parts I can't deny."

"Seriously!? So you really were the obnoxious noble type after all!? Did you kick commoners around from horseback, or torment the servants until you drove them out!?"

"I can't fathom why that's making you so gleeful."

Julius shot the increasingly excited Subaru a cold look, then glanced back over his shoulder. Probably checking on the carriage — and by extension, the women inside it. Naturally, there was no need to confirm it, since the conversation couldn't possibly reach them, but it was a matter of feeling.

"How much do you know about me — about House Juukulius?"

"Nothing at all."

"That's a satisfyingly immediate answer."

In truth, his prior knowledge was zero. Julius gave a wry smile at Subaru, who puffed out his chest with impressive skill even atop his mount, then touched the knight's sword at his waist. Something passed down in his family, perhaps. From his profile, it was clear he was hesitating over something — whether he even had the right, as he was now, to carry it.

"— I was not, originally, the legitimate heir of House Juukulius."

"——"

"To be more precise, I should say I am not the formal, legitimate heir. The current head of House Juukulius is Alviero Juukulius. His younger brother, Klein Juukulius, was my father. He has already passed away, and was adopted into the head's line."

At words far beyond what he'd expected, Subaru found himself, for a moment, unable to say anything. How well the workings of this world's noble society matched up with the social knowledge Subaru knew from his own was hard to say, but Julius's position was, indeed, complicated. And in that case, what did that make the position of his younger brother, Joshua Juukulius — that young man who had looked up to Julius as his elder brother, and who was now lost from the world's memory?

"I don't know what my actual relationship with Joshua, the one left behind in Priestella, really is. Simply because it doesn't remain within me — whether he is my true younger brother, carrying the blood of my birth father Klein Juukulius. Or whether he is the true, legitimate heir, carrying the blood of my adoptive father Alviero Juukulius."

"...And if it's the latter, then what happens?"

"Then 'brothers' would only be a figure of speech, and our true relationship would be that of cousins, I suppose. In that case, the headship of House Juukulius ought to belong to Joshua Juukulius."

"——"

Julius's manner of speaking was flat, and precisely because of that, it struck home as fact. The complicated part of this account was that even Julius himself couldn't grasp his own actual standing. If Julius and Joshua were true brothers, then Julius would be the one to inherit House Juukulius. But if, hypothetically, they were not true brothers, then it would rightly be Joshua who inherited the Juukulius name. And the means of confirming which was true was something Julius — robbed by the world of Joshua's very existence, having lost both the memory of his brother and his own place — had no way of knowing.

"It's a moot point, but thinking of it that way, my position is exceedingly complicated. Not even knowing my own tomorrow is not a pleasant thing to contemplate. Though, at present, I ought to prioritize the unsteadiness of my own footing over the fate of the Juukulius headship."

"——"

Perhaps because Subaru had shown more shock than expected, Julius said it as though making light of it. Mouth gone dry, stomach twisting, Subaru gritted his teeth at himself. Making Julius, of all people, worry about his feelings right now — that was foolish beyond measure.

"Given an upbringing like that, I was somewhat adrift back then. My adoptive father took me up to the royal castle, and I was on my way back after demonstrating the clumsy manners I'd been taught. That's where I saw Reinhard, still only eight years old at the time. — That's all there is to it."

Somewhat forcibly, Julius prioritized bringing the conversation to a close. Subaru had neither the courage to press further nor anything clever to say in reply. Watching Julius turn his face forward with a lonely air, Subaru unclenched his back teeth. And what came out of him, unbidden, was a question.

"Why'd you suddenly feel like telling me all that?"

"I believe it was you who asked — or is that my mistaken impression?"

"No, that's — I mean, that's on me, but that's not it. It's just — weird for you to tell me something like this, isn't it?"

"Not particularly. Among Reinhard, Ferris, and the Royal Guard, it's common knowledge. Naturally, Anastasia-sama knows as well. It's nothing special."

"——"

Not quite convinced, Subaru twisted his mouth into a frown. Being told "everyone already knows" didn't mean it was something one went around volunteering to just anyone. It still struck him as an unnatural thing to say. Perhaps Subaru's dissatisfaction showed through in his twisted expression, and reached Julius. He drew the reins, urging his beloved mount to quicken its pace just slightly, and said,

"Yes, it's something everyone already knew. — Which is precisely why I may have wanted you, of all people, to know it now. You, who in this present world remember me best, aside from myself."

The blue Earth Dragon accelerated, tail swishing, and Julius's expression slipped from view. Even so, it wasn't a speed meant to leave Subaru behind. It was, as ever, a somewhat affected way of simply declaring the conversation over.

"——"

"Yeah, I'm fine. Thanks for looking out for me, Patrasche."

The running jet-black Earth Dragon lifted her head, glancing back at Subaru. In a gesture that seemed to say "shall I pull up alongside the other dragon?", Subaru stroked the lady's neck in gratitude.

"...Not like me."

Small, sulky words slipped from his mouth.

Was he getting cold feet, or was he playing at being some tragic hero? Either way, there was nothing for it but to be irritated with himself.

"Ah, damn it. What an idiot I am. No, I am an idiot..."

Scratching at his head, Subaru spat out his irritation, silent, inside his own mouth. In the end, no fitting reply to Julius's words that day ever came to him.

And it didn't come to him until the Augria Sand Dunes lay just ahead. It still hadn't come, even once the group arrived at Mirula, the town nearest the dunes.
