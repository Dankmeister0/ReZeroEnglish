Arc 3 Chapter 11: "Three Heads Are Better Than One... at Scheming"

Inch by inch, the standoff dragged on, and Subaru caught his breath at the feel of sweat trickling down his forehead.

It was a spacious room. Two beds sat side by side at the far end, while near the door there was enough open space to swallow Subaru's entire bedroom back in his old world. The luggage brought from the mansion was arranged neatly in that open space, but it took up only a small fraction of the whole. None of it would pose the slightest obstacle to the grand showdown Subaru, standing frozen in the middle of the room, was about to perform.

"So you really don't intend to let me through, no matter what?"

Feeling his lips go dry with tension, Subaru forced the question out past the sting in his throat. His voice was low, hiding its tremor. In answer, the obstacle before him—the one blocking Subaru's path—spread both arms wide, hiding the door from view with an attitude of firm resolve. Knowing full well the posture meant it was hopeless, Subaru pinned a thread of hope on a pleading look. For an instant, he thought he saw a flicker of hesitation rise in the eyes watching him—but it dissolved without a trace before her professional resolve.

"Even if it's a request from you, Subaru-kun, I can't let you pass here."

The one blocking his path—Rem—looked straight at Subaru and declared it plainly. Sighing at her stubborn attitude, Subaru gave a playful shrug.

"Come on, think about it calmly, Rem. I'm the one who got left behind here, and it's not like I've got some big connection to lean on or anything. In other words, even if I leave this room, there's nothing I can actually do. You get that, right?"

Speaking quickly, Subaru pressed on, trying to talk down Rem's guard. At his words she smiled prettily and said, "Yes."

"Ordinarily speaking, simply leaving this room certainly wouldn't grant Subaru-kun his wish. It's not a place so easily reached, and even if you did manage to get there, I doubt there's much you could actually do once you arrived."

"Right?"

"But that's only true if we're talking about it in terms of Rem's common sense."

Cutting him off flatly, Rem regarded him without letting her smile slip. Her gaze swept him from head to toe, and Subaru straightened under it. Then she went on.

"In Subaru-kun's case, there's no telling what outlandish idea you might come up with to accomplish your goal. You might well find some way to manage it, somewhere beyond Rem's imagination."

"That feeling of yours toward me—somewhere between trust and distrust—is downright uncanny. I'm not nearly as good at leaping outside the bounds of common sense as you're giving me credit for, you know?"

"If that's what Subaru-kun thinks, then I suppose it's so. Inside Subaru-kun's own head, anyway."

He tried gently to talk her out of her thinking, but it had no effect whatsoever on Rem, whose image of Subaru had solidified beyond all reasoning. In any case, talking her into stepping aside was proving to be an extremely difficult task. Which meant his only option was to outwit her somehow and seize the way through.

"That's a tall order in its own right. If it's like this right out of the gate, I dread to think how the rest will go..."

Scratching his head with a sigh, Subaru narrowed his eyes thoughtfully at Rem. No—his gaze was fixed on what lay beyond her: the far side of the inn's door, through which Emilia and Roswaal had gone, leaving Subaru behind.

"...Wait, I'm staying behind?!"

Breakfast had ended, and no sooner had they returned to their room to catch their breath than Subaru was informed of the day's plans—and let out a cry of shock.

Before the astonished Subaru stood two people in the room: Emilia and Rem. And Emilia, the one responsible for wringing that initial cry out of him, planted her hands on her hips and looked down at Subaru. "Well, of course," she said.

"You haven't forgotten why you came to the royal capital with us, have you? To check on the safety of the people you know here, and to get your body treated. That was the deal."

"Yeah, but, you know, we could interpret that a little more broadly and work things out nicely so that—"

"Absolutely not! This isn't a game, so I can't bring in outsiders. I can't even take Rem along."

With Emilia laying down the law with unusual force, Subaru—who'd had a slip-up the day before—found his tongue too clumsy to talk back. He turned a pleading look toward Rem, standing in the corner of the room, but the blue-haired maid shook her head at his silent plea.

"Even Rem can't take Subaru-kun's side here, I'm afraid. It's also Roswaal-sama's wish, and Emilia-sama is in the right. Please be understanding."

"Damn it, no allies?! But when I remember yesterday's screwup, I can't say a word—so frustrating!"

Even Rem, who was fundamentally partial to Subaru, kept her priorities firmly in order. Yesterday's blunder—in short, the incident where he'd broken the rules and gotten himself lost—had resulted in Subaru's activities in the royal capital being restricted rather severely. On this point, even Subaru had no excuse to offer and admitted it was his own fault, so now that he'd already managed to reunite with old man Rom and the fruit shop owner, it wasn't that big a deal. Looking back, though, it was a shock in itself that every single person he'd wanted to see again turned out to be some grizzled, unkempt man.

"No, wait, I wanted to see Felt again too, so it should be fine. Thinking back on everyone connected to the royal capital, three out of four are men... though if you throw the oddball in, it's six out of seven!"

Needless to say, Elsa wasn't on the list of people he wanted to see again, so there was nothing to bump up the female ratio. Here he was in an isekai summoning fantasy, and all he was racking up were flags with male characters—what was the point?

"It's fine, though. I've got the main heroine flag standing tall..."

"There you go muttering to yourself again. I really don't think that's very goood, you know."

Being given a flat, unimpressed look by Emilia—the very main heroine in question—left Subaru with nothing to say, and he could only fall silent. Once she'd confirmed there would be no rebuttal or excuse-making from him, Emilia nodded, satisfied.

"It won't take that long, I'd like to say... but honestly, I'm not sure how long today will take. So go ahead and have your meals with Rem. If you wait around for us, I'm sure we'll end up keeping you waiting way too long."

"Hmph. Fine, if Emilia-tan wants to be mean like that, I've got ideas of my own too. Hey, Rem. Let's you and me have ourselves a lavish feast today!"

"No. Today's menu is whole-roasted appa, appa salad, and appa pie made with plenty of appa jam. And for after the meal, I've also prepared some fresh-squeezed appa juice."

"An entire meal of nothing but appa?! You bastard, that Scarface!!"

Conveniently ignoring the fact that he himself had brought home a net total of nine appa, Subaru pictured the tough-looking fruit shop owner floating in the empty air before him. He raised a finger in defiant farewell to the vision flashing him a thumbs-up with a great big grin, then, resigned to his fate, bared his teeth in a half-desperate laugh.

"Fine, whatever! Appa's my favorite fruit anyway! Seriously, it's Appa Night Fever! Let's the two of us dig in and finish off every last bit!"

"Oh, no, not at all. Rem is a maid who attends to every little need, so I certainly wouldn't dream of interfering with the pleasure of Subaru-kun, who loves appa so much. It's all yours, every bit of it."

"You act like you're on my side, but every now and then you cut me loose without a shred of mercy!"

Subaru shouted at Rem's attitude—less a matter of knowing her place than of knowing exactly how to use it to her advantage. Emilia watched their comedy-routine exchange with a thoughtful expression, but soon shook her head as if brushing something off.

"Anyway, I'm leaving everything to Rem. I think Roswaal already told you this too, but—stay put, alright? ...I mean it. Stay put."

"Pausing for effect before saying it twice—man, Emilia-tan must think about me a whole lot!"

Subaru gave a thumbs-up in response to Emilia's insistence. By now used to the gesture, Emilia laid her palm over his outstretched hand. Subaru caught his breath at the sudden contact. He'd built up a fair amount of resistance to conversing with her at close range like this by now, but he was still hopelessly weak against the occasional surprise attack. Emilia, for her part, seemed not to notice the change in him.

"Subaru. I won't ask for much."

"Uh, yeah...?"

"Please—just let me believe in you, okay?"

Said quietly, almost pleadingly, with her eyes closed, the words brought Subaru's thoughts to a momentary halt. He chewed over what she'd said in his mind, ground it down, swallowed it whole, and turned his imagination carefully toward its meaning and intent before answering.

"Ah, yeah! Totally, I'm on it! I'll live up to Emilia-tan's expectations! I basically live for that!"

Still not entirely sure what she was actually talking about, he affirmed her words wholesale out of sheer reflex. He'd accept it for now and figure out how to act on it later. To Subaru's answer, which admittedly carried a whiff of making-it-up-as-he-went, Emilia's violet eyes clouded with something like worry.

"Right. ...I believe in you."

That's what she quietly murmured as she said it.

And so the story circles back to where it began.

With Emilia and Roswaal off toward the royal castle, Subaru, left behind at the inn under Rem's watch, spent his brief span of free time on writing practice.

For reference, having all but conquered the I-script by now, his next goal was mastering the Ro-script. If the I-script corresponded to Japanese hiragana, the Ro-script apparently corresponded to katakana; aside from a slightly different shape, the pattern was much the same as the I-script, so he expected this one to take less time to learn than the last.

About an hour and a half into his Ro-script practice—"bored" wasn't quite the right word for Subaru, who was unusually enjoying his studies, but in the sense that he could no longer sit still, nothing could have been more accurate. For Subaru, learning the script carried the not-unpleasant sense of letting this other world soak into him. That alone gave him enough motivation to throw himself into his daily assignments without complaint.

But if the thing sitting on the opposite side of that scale from his motivation was Emilia, then there was simply no contest.

Subaru rose to his feet and casually made for the door, only for Rem to quietly block his way. The all-purpose maid, who'd been simultaneously handling the room's cleaning, sorting the luggage, and playing backseat driver and teacher to his studies, had read straight through the excuse of "just going to pick some flowers" and moved to obstruct him.

—Namely, his scheme to slip out of the inn and head for the castle.

He felt no small amount of guilt at the thought of breaking his promise to Emilia. What might be called Emilia's anxiety had, in fact, hit the mark dead-on; her decision to have Rem keep watch over his movements showed just how well she'd read the kind of person Subaru was. And yet, even understanding all of that, Subaru simply couldn't bring himself to sit quietly and wait at the inn.

"I mean, I get that she's got nothing but bad memories of the royal capital, but isn't she being a little too jumpy about it..."

What kind of hardship Emilia had faced the last time she'd visited the royal capital should already be clear enough. Even though she'd presumably come to the capital in secret, enemies after her insignia had nearly stripped her of her qualification—and of her very life. Had Subaru not been there, it was plain that her fate would have run out that very day.

—Whenever he thought back on the day they'd met, Subaru couldn't help but become acutely aware of his own position.

Summoned to another world without warning, and handed not a single thing that could be called a purpose. On the surface he played the carefree optimist, but as for what lay underneath—well, he tended to be fairly rash and reckless, prone to just letting circumstances carry him along. In short, his outside and his inside were more or less the same, which meant there was no denying that the chain of events bringing him to this point had largely been a matter of getting swept along by the current of the moment. No denying it, but—

"Even a guy who tends to drift along has, at least in his own mind, been choosing which current to swim in."

He wasn't denying he'd drifted to this point, but Subaru could have chosen not to wash up here. The fact that he was here now, all the same, was because he'd chosen it, in his own way.

Subaru, dropped into another world with no purpose to speak of. Precisely because he found himself in that position, he'd turned the emptiness of his situation on its head and settled on this thought instead:

If no purpose had been given to him, then he'd just have to decide on one himself.

"I'll help Emilia."

That wasn't the only reason—that she'd once saved him. Wanting to be of use to Emilia was a reason welling up from the very bottom of Subaru's heart.

"In other words, yeah—love power."

"What did you just say?"

"Ah, sorry, never mind—I kind of got serious there, and saying it again is a little embarrassing."

Rem tilted her head and asked him to repeat himself, and Subaru reflected ruefully on the muttering he'd just let slip. He cleared his throat, shifted gears, and set his mind spinning full-tilt on how to get past the obstacle in front of him.

Not that he had the faintest idea how he'd actually get into the castle even after escaping this room. But whatever the case, nothing would happen unless he got out of here first. That was Subaru's way—not just letting himself drift, but letting momentum carry him too.

"Still, the situation itself was as bad as ever."

Getting past Rem, standing there with her arms spread wide like a guardian statue—plainly speaking, it was an unwinnable game. The gap in physical ability was obvious, and Subaru's condition wasn't exactly in peak form either. Most schemes to outwit her would simply be swallowed up by the overwhelming difference in raw ability, and he had no special power that could overturn that gap. He briefly considered just unleashing Shamak and making a break for it under cover of the smoke, but without any Bokko Fruit on hand right now, the whole thing would end with him coughing up smoke and passing out. And using Bokko Fruit at all had already been strictly, strictly forbidden by everyone at the mansion anyway.

So there Subaru was, boxed in on every side. For someone whose wits weren't exactly cheat-level sharp or fast, there was only one more method he could think of.

And that was—

"Ugh... don't tell me, is this...!"

"Subaru-kun?"

Subaru's face suddenly went pale, his hand flying to his forehead as his body swayed violently. His knees trembling, his breath ragged, Rem reached out in a panic at the sudden change in him and gently supported his sagging shoulders.

"What's wrong, Subaru-kun? Don't tell me you've despaired over the sheer recklessness of your own idea...!?"

"Just how low is your rating of me if you put a number on it?! N-no, not now... ugh, it hurts..."

Held up by her support, Subaru clutched his chest and finally sank to his knees, gasping in pain. Rem knelt down beside him in turn, drawing close.

"Not your head this time, but your chest? Are you alright?"

"Huh, was it my head? No, it's my chest that hurts... this is, uh, that thing..."

"You're giving off a slightly shallow vibe there, but do you actually know the cause?"

To Subaru's shallow display of agony came Rem's rather pointed brand of concern. Hearing it, Subaru clutched at her collar as if clinging on for dear life, bringing his face close to hers, lips trembling.

"...M—"

"M?"

"...Mayonnaise deficiency."

Couldn't you think of any other method, and also, seriously, the same gag twice?—he mentally jabbed at himself. But once the words were out of his mouth, he'd see it through to the end. That was Natsuki Subaru's unbending policy. A petty, pointless kind of manly pride.

"It's the withdrawal symptom that hits a mayo-lover who's running low on mayonnaise... Thanks to you, it had been kept in check, Rem, but after just one day away from the mansion... I can't believe it's progressed this far already."

"That's terrible... whatever should Rem do...?"

"Make me some mayonnaise... if I can just get a little mayo-smooch in, my life will be saved... ugh, it hurts..."

Finally unable to bear even sitting up any longer, Subaru clung to Rem and toppled sideways onto the floor. With Subaru's head resting on her lap, Rem stroked his aching brow while murmuring softly, "Mayonnaise..."

"I'll need to gather eggs, oil, and various other things... but—"

"It hurts... I'm gonna die. Please, Rem. You're... you're the only one I can rely on..."

"...Rem alone is who you rely on?"

She couldn't stand to watch Subaru suffer. But there was no telling what he'd do the moment she took her eyes off him. Rem had been wavering, torn between her own feelings and her orders, but Subaru's final words seemed to have tipped the scale. She clenched her fist and muttered forcefully, "Only Rem can do this!"

"Understood! Leave it to Rem—to me!"

"Ow?! And—oof!"

Rem shot to her feet all at once. As a result, Subaru's head slipped off her knee and cracked against the floor, and then, on top of that, her determined step forward drove her heel straight into his ribs. Fired up to the max, Rem seemed entirely unaware of her own act of violence. Without pause, she quickly straightened herself up and said,

"Right, then Rem will go gather the ingredients right away and get started on the mayonnaise! Please don't lie around on the floor, Subaru-kun—rest properly in bed."

"This agony... has nothing... to do with mayonnaise...!"

"Huh? Yes, I know. Rem will do her very best for Subaru-kun, don't worry. And once it's done... fufu, I'm looking forward to it."

Taking Subaru's agonized words entirely on her own terms, Rem left behind an unnervingly adorable smile and dashed out of the room. Clutching his aching chest, Subaru watched her back recede and confirmed that he'd achieved his goal. Confirmed it, yes, but for a while afterward he found himself unable to move, thanks to the little accident along the way.

Once the pain finally began to fade, Subaru got to his feet, rubbing at the lump that had quietly risen on the back of his head, brushed the dust off the body that had been lying on the floor, and turned his head around.

"E-even I'll admit that was a dumb plan, but it worked. I'm a little worried about Rem's future now, but let's just say I was saved this time by how easy she is to fool."

As for the ribs and the back of his head that had been sacrificed in exchange, well, those were necessary sacrifices. If the path had opened up in exchange for a little pain, he ought to call this one a win for now.

"Now all that was left was to leave Rem a quick note and make his escape. Going out the door and running smack into her on the way back would be a disaster..."

Leaving a hastily scrawled note on the table, Subaru threw the window open and looked down. The room was on the second floor, so climbing down by holding onto the window frame and such wouldn't be all that dangerous a drop. The alley below the window was fairly deserted, too, so the risk of being seen climbing down and mistaken for an underwear thief was low.

"—Well then, guess I'll get going. It's not for nothing that I used to love pulling prank home-invasions on my parents via the balcony back when I was a kid."

Climbing up and down houses along drainpipes and the like was right in his wheelhouse. Squeezing his rubbery body neatly through barred windows was another skill he'd picked up out of sheer necessity—a set of skills he'd unilaterally dubbed "the Rubber Men," plural, for no particular reason.

Digging up those old memories, Subaru set his hands on the window frame and, with fairly little hesitation, let his body slide down into the alleys of the royal capital.

—It happened just moments after Subaru had quietly slipped out of the room.

"...Still, it's a little bit of a shock to Rem, being thought of as someone who'd fall for a trick like that."

In the middle of the now-empty room, Rem, having returned, murmured quietly to herself while touching the table. Rem picked up the note from the table she'd just touched. On it, in barely legible, hastily scrawled I-script, were only the words: dad's critical, rushing home. Going by Subaru's character, she judged it to be some sort of sophisticated joke—the kind that wasn't easy to parse at a glance.

"He didn't have to go and do something as dangerous as climbing down from the window—Rem wasn't going to stop him from simply leaving the room."

This was the result of her considerate plan to hide herself at the end of the hallway and make sure Subaru came out through the door properly. Sensing that all presence of him had vanished from the room, she'd come back to find the window flung wide open and a note left behind—it was obvious he'd deliberately chosen the more dangerous route out.

"...Honestly, Subaru-kun really is hopeless."

Yet the expression on Rem's face as she murmured this was, contrary to her words, somehow pleased. Whatever its actual content, the note was still, without question, addressed to her, and she folded it neatly before carefully tucking it away in the inner pocket of her maid uniform. For a moment, Rem pressed a hand over the chest where she'd tucked the letter, closing her eyes and sinking into some private thought—savoring it a while, then, letting out a satisfied breath, she opened her eyes and said,

"—But whatever could Roswaal-sama be thinking?"

Tilting her head and resting a hand against her cheek, Rem mulled over her master's order and gave voice to her doubts. What she'd been ordered that very morning, by her newly-awakened master, went something like this.

Namely: "Do not interfere with Natsuki Subaru's actions. No matter what Emilia might order you to do."

When she'd asked what it might mean, her question had been quietly turned away. Given that, as a servant, Rem had no choice but to comply in silence. She felt some guilt at going against Emilia's own orders, but for her, there was no question that Roswaal's word outranked Emilia's—with Subaru, in some respects, slotting in as an exception all his own.

In any case,

"—Please come back safely, Subaru-kun."

She didn't think he'd gone dashing out with absolutely no plan at all, but he was still the kind of boy who wouldn't hesitate to put his own safety on the back burner and run for someone else's sake. All Rem could do was pray that his wish would come true, and hope that, along the way to fulfilling it, he wouldn't be hurt any more than necessary.

—Except the former was Rem giving him far too much credit, and the latter was Rem romanticizing things far too much.

Rem, whose overall assessment of Subaru ran altogether too high, closed the window through which he'd left, turned back into the room, and shook her head as if to shake off the conflict she'd been wrestling with, throwing herself into her duties as a maid.

And so it was that Natsuki Subaru, without any clear sense of whose palm he was dancing in, found himself set loose upon the royal capital for a second time.

"So there he was, having taken off running with nothing to guide him—one Natsuki Subaru-kun."

Having landed safely in the royal capital, Subaru—also known as Rubber Man—was, to put it bluntly, in a state more or less equivalent to not thinking anything through at all.

There was, of course, no method for heading to the castle alone, and to begin with, there was no chance Subaru by himself could get past even the checkpoint on the way to the noble quarter, let alone the castle itself. He considered relying on the trusted, proven Shamak to break through the checkpoint, but kept arriving at the same conclusion—that if he tried it, he'd just end up coughing smoke and collapsing by the roadside—and rejected the idea over and over. Pushing the theory that Shamak could solve everything was a problem in itself.

"Shamak feels pretty all-purpose and all, but the plain truth is I just don't have the chops for it. If I could at least control how much mana I use when I cast, things would be different..."

During his days at the mansion after the witchbeast incident had wrapped up, he'd received a little instruction from Roswaal on how magic worked. If his assessment as a court mage could be trusted, it would take Subaru around two years to learn to handle mana the way a competent mage could. Training him to the very limit of his talent, it would take twenty years to complete him as a thoroughly second-rate mage. And to begin with, it would take more than half a year just for his overworked body to return to its previous condition.

"There was no way he could've made it through without using magic, so it's not like he regretted it or anything."

According to Roswaal, there was no telling what would happen to Subaru's body if he overworked his Gate again. Which meant that, for the time being, Shamak simply had to be struck from the list of options. Since it was an option he hadn't really had to begin with, losing it wasn't all that painful.

"Still, saying goodbye to the romance of being able to use magic was lonely—or rather, a real shame, honestly."

In any case, that summed up Subaru's feelings on the matter. He didn't place nearly as much weight on the whole situation as Ram and Rem, or Roswaal, who felt responsible for it. For Subaru, who'd never had much of a connection to mana or magic to begin with, losing something he'd never really had was a loss hard to feel in any concrete way. Whatever consequences that might carry, at least for now, they hadn't caught up with him yet.

"So anyway, that's the situation—I've gotta somehow get up to the castle without relying on any magical pow-wa. Feel free to lend me anything you've got, brains, connections, whatever. Please!"

"...And what exactly am I supposed to do, hearing all that? Honestly."

"Before that, don't go having dangerous conversations in the corner of another man's shop. I'm trying to run a decent, above-board business that stays off the authorities' radar here—what am I supposed to do if some weird rumor starts going around?"

"Above-board business, my ass, when the shopkeeper's face doesn't look the least bit legit to begin with. And the moment you're hooked up with some back-alley old geezer like this, it's already a lost cause on the rumor front, don't you think?"

"You never run out of lip, do you, kid..."

The one scowling at Subaru's thoroughly rude remarks was Scarface, with the vertical white scar running from forehead to chin. And beside him, a bald old man wedging his brawny, muscle-bound frame into the cramped shop. Feeling the faint illusion that the whole place—himself included—had grown that much more sweaty and rank, Subaru let out a breath. "Still, though," he said.

"To think the shop old man Rom's connected to would turn out to be yours, mister. Small world, or maybe small royal capital... or should I say, birds of a feather?"

"Don't lump us together."

"Don't object in unison like that. Unlike the beautiful maid sisters I know, a bald guy and a scarred guy teaming up is way too niche a demographic, don't you think?"

The two of them exchanged glances, trading identically sour expressions. For what it's worth, the shop was still very much open for business, though—quite apart from Subaru and Rom's presence—the rate of customers actually stopping in was low. Despite having a decent enough location right on Merchant Street, the shopkeeper had gone and cranked the difficulty up to hard mode all on his own, purely with his own face. A recommended self-imposed challenge run—a state of affairs Subaru couldn't help feeling a certain kinship with.

"What. Hey. Why's your hand on my shoulder."

"Ah, well, I was just feeling a little sympathy for a fellow bird of a feather, thinking we'd both like to live life on a slightly easier mode, you know? The gods sure don't play fair."

"Don't drag me into your pessimism. Unlike you, I've got a wife and a daughter. I'm a winner."

"No way, boom-boom-BOOM!"

Betrayed out of nowhere by his fellow bird of a feather, Subaru's words and actions went haywire with shock. Staggering back, Subaru turned to Rom to demand the truth of that statement. The bald old man gave a firm nod in answer to Subaru's trembling black eyes.

"It's true. And what's more, a beautiful wife and an adorable daughter."

"Damn it all! This is the first time I've been treated this badly since coming to this world, you hear me?!"

His most furious outburst since being summoned to another world. Giving himself over to the dark, roiling emotion welling up from the pit of his stomach, Subaru lunged at the shopkeeper—only to be instantly struck back down by a mighty forearm to the forehead.

"I'm... I'm powerless...!"

"If the scene called for it, maybe, but saying that here and now is nothing but sour grapes."

Looking down at Subaru, sent sprawling by a counterattack after taking his frustrations out on the wrong target, Rom stroked his own chin with a thoughtful expression. When he'd first been called out, Rom had come along overjoyed, fully expecting it to be about Felt. Given that it had actually turned out to be a request for help with one of Subaru's little schemes, he had every right to be angry—yet the fact that he set that aside and racked his brains along with him spoke volumes about the old man's good nature.

Subaru wasn't entirely without guilt at leaning on that kindness and milking it for all it was worth, but shelving that sentiment and pushing straight through on pure self-interest was one of his strengths.

And so,

"Let me ask again, then. How do I go about sneaking peacefully into the castle?"

"Keep thinking like that and commit some major felony, and you'll probably end up locked away in the castle dungeon on your own accord—goal achieved, technically. Except then your head and your body would end up saying their goodbyes."

"Give me a more constructive suggestion. That's exactly why this shop doesn't get any customers."

"Now, now, don't say that, boy. It's precisely because Kadomon's shop doesn't get much business that we're able to have a private chat like this. Look at it that way, and it's not all bad. Right, Kadomon?"

"Do you two have some kind of grudge against my shop or something?"

At the shopkeeper's flat, unimpressed glare—Kadomon's, that is—Subaru and Rom exchanged glances, shrugged, and both denied it in unison. "No way." Watching the two of them make that infuriating gesture, Kadomon traced the scar on his face with an irritated finger.

"Quit talking about doing something as terrifying as sneaking into the castle. With the king absent, the castle isn't as heavily guarded a priority as it might otherwise be, but that doesn't mean you'd get off scot-free if you were caught breaking in."

"Well, the risk is about what you'd expect, I suppose. Boy, get caught and you'll die."

Rom nodded his agreement to Kadomon's words. The conclusion the two of them had reached was advice for Subaru to simply give up. Understandable enough, since neither of them had any reason of their own to push things that far.

"Well, put that way, I don't really have a clear-cut reason to push myself that hard either..."

Unlike the times before this, there was, right now, no reason compelling enough for Subaru to stake his life on it. Marching straight back to the inn, sulking off to bed, and waiting for Emilia and the others to return while smooching down some of Rem's homemade mayonnaise—that was an option too. In fact, doing exactly that would, without a doubt, make Emilia and the others happier. No doubt about it, but—

"Why is it that I have to go this hard, I wonder."

Subaru had half convinced himself, as though it were some kind of calling, that he had to be there at Emilia's side. He had no idea how much help that actually was to her. He didn't know, but he thought that if he were there, he might be able to seize the moment she needed help, and make the most of it. If he wasn't by her side, he wouldn't even get the chance to make use of it. And if that was the case—

"Even if it's self-centered of me, I still want to be with her. That's just how I feel..."

Even being apart from her for just a little while, a fear searing enough to scorch him alive gnawed away at Subaru. It was reason enough, weighed even against his own life, to still push his feet forward.

"Well, it's also true that this whole place's ominous jinx of a reputation is part of what's driving me."

In a place where dark premonitions tied to the Royal Selection never seemed to let up, Subaru's unease showed no sign of running dry. The possibility of Emilia coming to harm somewhere he wasn't—if this were a place where he could deny that possibility just a little more easily, he suspected he wouldn't have had this much reason to feel so anxious.

"I don't need to be right next to her—I just want to be somewhere I can at least see her face. Isn't there some way to manage that?"

"It's not likely to be that simple. Getting past the checkpoint alone might work if you throw around a bit of a bribe, but making it all the way to the castle, well..."

It probably wasn't that he was moved by Subaru's attitude, exactly, but he'd at least picked up on the fact that Subaru wasn't going to give up. Perhaps unwilling to stomach the bad aftertaste of leaving him alone, letting him run wild, and having it all end with him captured and executed, Rom began earnestly lending a hand to the plan to infiltrate the castle. Compared to Rom, who—without quite realizing it—was cementing his position as an accomplice more and more firmly, Kadomon's relationship with Subaru wasn't nearly so deep. He watched the two of them earnestly trading ideas out of the corner of his eye, an exasperated look on his face.

"Either way, just make sure it doesn't come back to bite my shop."

He said it dismissively. He couldn't really ask more of him, given Kadomon had no real reason to throw himself in seriously, but Subaru pouted at that cold profile all the same, jutting out his lips.

"Don't say that. Here you are, running your shop with all your grit on Merchant Street even though customers never come. Surely a guy living life with that kind of spirit's got at least a hint to spare."

"Let me just say, today's just a bit of a slow day—my shop does a fair bit better than this normally, alright? ...Anyway, instead of pulling some baffling little trick, why not just go explain the situation at the checkpoint? That girl you're traveling with is at the castle, isn't she. ...Huh, what did she look like again?"

Having offered up that perfectly reasonable suggestion, Kadomon then knit his brow as though rifling through his memory. It seemed that the image of that beautiful silver-haired girl simply wouldn't surface properly in his memory. It was the result of witnessing firsthand the power of a hood woven with a perception-obstructing formula. In any case, unfortunately, following the perfectly reasonable suggestion he'd offered wasn't an option Subaru could take.

"That method's guaranteed to get me busted by Emilia-tan! The ideal is to secretly watch over her, in shadow and in sunlight both, all while playing it off like I never did a thing. Codename: Operation 'Super-Strength Me, Under the Floorboards.'"

"What kind of name is that, sounding like you're hiding while also loudly announcing yourself?"

"What a pain. Well then, how about slipping in among the supplies being carried into the castle? As it happens, the merchant guild's got a food shipment scheduled to go to the castle today, so if you're lucky..."

Kadomon offered the suggestion with a laugh, more as a passing bit of small talk than anything else. But his scarred face immediately stiffened. Because he'd seen, the very instant that information hit Subaru's ears, the way his eyes had lit up. He clapped a hand over his own mouth as though ashamed of his slip of the tongue, then thrust out his palm, trying to say "Forget I said that!"—

"That idea—YES, let's do it!"

—but Subaru flashing his teeth with a thumbs-up and a wink, all with a great big grin, beat him to it by a long, long mile.

Rom looked up at the sky as if lamenting such recklessness. The sunlight pouring down made his bald head gleam a faint, pale white.
