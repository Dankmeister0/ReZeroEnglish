Arc 10 Interlude: "—Why?"

—When I found the still-unbloomed bud of a flower in that garden, I stopped in my tracks without meaning to.

It was a garden ablaze with flowers of every color, blooming in wild profusion. Unfortunately, I wasn't well versed in the names or varieties of flowers, but I'd heard that these were precious blooms, grown from seeds gathered from every corner of the kingdom and coaxed into bud.

A flower garden this large and this magnificent — the gardener entrusted with its care must have found ample opportunity to demonstrate his skill. The garden's splendid result was proof enough of that.

Honestly, when I'd first come to the garden, I hadn't expected much from this riot of color. I'd never had much sensibility for appreciating flowers to begin with, and I'd never taken particular interest in the garden back home, either. Even presuming on my understanding father's indulgence to be allowed here — the royal castle of Lugunica, the royal capital — I didn't rate myself so highly as to think I could join the responsible adults in their discussions.

So, with nothing better to do, I'd wandered to the garden to kill time until my father's business was finished, and there, quite unexpectedly, I found myself captivated by that bud.

"——"

At the end of my amber gaze was a large bud, its head bowed low. Its petals, tinged faintly with red, were closed still, and I found myself drawn to that not-yet-blooming thing — perhaps because I saw something of myself in it, an immature being burdened with a frustration that had nowhere to go. Though I suppose that notion is a touch too poetic, a little too self-indulgent.

Still, it was true that amid all those flowers in full, magnificent bloom — flowers that seemed to already know every last corner of their own charm — I felt a kinship with the one bud that had yet to reveal what it truly was.

—Just what was I capable of, I wondered, and what was expected of me?

"I..."

I had the pride of knowing I'd been blessed — blessed in birth, blessed in lineage, blessed in talent. And as one who had been granted such birth, lineage, and talent, I also carried something like a sense of duty — that I had to answer for it with results equal to what I'd been given. And I was also aware that this pride and sense of duty were fatally incompatible with what I actually wished for.

"——"

If I had to put it into words, I was the one seed among all those planted in this garden that harbored doubts about how it ought to bloom — an immature seed that remained, stubbornly, a bud. The other flowers, without doubt or hesitation, understood their own place and fulfilled it completely. And yet, I—

"...Just what kind of flower am I meant to bloom into?"

I asked the bud that question, and held my breath through the silence that of course offered no answer. Wrapped in the sweet fragrance of the flowers, I couldn't even come to an understanding with the bud I'd felt such kinship with.

Because what this impudent self of mine actually wanted wasn't to bloom into some beautiful, vivid, magnificent flower — it was to become a thick, sturdy great tree that could shelter those flowers from wind and rain. Yes, and so I began to feel that I, harboring such an impossible wish, was a hollow and ridiculous thing—

"Fgoh?!"

—In that instant, along with a terribly unbecoming shriek, something came falling from above into the bud-strewn garden.

"—!"

Startled by the sudden event, my eyes went wide, and I found myself unable to move, as though my feet had been sewn to the spot. In my mind, blanked white with my thoughts stopped cold, there belatedly rang out alarm bells warning of danger, along with all the lessons and preparations I'd been taught for just such a situation. But before I could pull any of that up from the back of my mind in time, the figure that had fallen practically headfirst into the flowerbed before me sprang back up, and—

"Bwuh! Bwaugh! Ptoo, ptoo! Wh—what is this, dirt? Is this dirt?!"

So he said, tossing his beautiful golden hair into disarray, blinking his jewel-red eyes, as a boy caked head to toe in dirt crawled his way out of the flowerbed, looking utterly wretched. And that was—

"Ohh, as befits me... Just when I thought it was all over, falling headfirst onto the flagstones, it seems even dire straits can be brushed aside by my innate good fortune..."

—That was my first memory of the last "Lion King."

△▼△▼△▼△

"—Ah"

A hoarse breath escaped me, and consciousness stirred awake. My five senses began to wake, and all manner of information came flooding into my brain one after another. —In that instant, the thing that most forcefully assailed my mind and body was an inescapable, ferocious thirst.

"——"

Thirst — and not merely thirst of the throat. Not enough. Not enough. Not enough. Everywhere in my body, in every last place, there wasn't enough water. I craved moisture; I starved for wetness. My nostrils and throat, the passages for air, were parched; the walls of my stomach, meant to take in food, were parched; my eyeballs were parched even as I blinked; the vessels that were supposed to carry blood through my whole body were parched; even my very Soul, crying out its deficiency, was parched.

Thirst. It was thirst. Thirst was eating away at that life.

"—Ah, eh, ah"

My mouth was parched, and my numbed tongue couldn't produce a proper sound. Wanting to do something about this thirst, I tried to call for someone. It wouldn't become a voice. Faster than calling out, I thought, to move myself. —No, that's not right; I didn't even have that thought. Like a drowning person who clings to their rescuer and drags them under too, my mind was parched and blank.

I appealed to my utterly parched body, from the crown of my head to the tips of my toes, scraping together what little strength remained anywhere in me, and somehow tried to rise. I rise. I try to stand. I stand. I try to start walking. I start walking. I try to search. I search.

"—Oh"

Desperate. I was desperate. With my mind ruled by thirst, I warned myself that this was my last chance. If my feet stopped, if I carelessly fell, if even one thing went wrong, I wouldn't be able to do the same again. I couldn't search. I couldn't start walking. I couldn't stand. I couldn't rise. I would sink into this immense thirst, and never again, never again, never again—

"——"

With all the strength of my feeble body, I somehow shoved the heavy door open and made it outside. Cursing the thirst in my body that made me feel I might lose even to the faint breeze blowing past, I swept my gaze around. My vision was clouded, hard to make out. Narrow. I realized at once it was because one eye was closed shut. But before I could secure my sight, carried on that breeze, a faint sweet fragrance drifted to me.

Drawn by it, I tilted my head and found it — a vase, arranged with yellow and pink flowers.

"—!"

There wasn't even time to think. I lunged at the vase as if collapsing onto it. Just like that, I tore out the flowers arranged in it and tilted the vase cradled in both hands. Then I drank the water meant for the flowers as though bathing in it — drank, drank, drank. The spilled water ran down the corners of my mouth and my cheeks, soaked my neck, drenched even my black nightclothes. It didn't matter. Coughing, I flung the vase away, and the sound of shattering ceramic rang out.

"Ehh-hgh, ehh-hgh..."

I wiped my mouth roughly with the back of my hand and turned around. Vases stood at even intervals down the hallway. On legs that had regained a bit more strength than before, I approached, threw away the flowers, and gulped down the water. I discarded the vase. On to the next one. I repeated that twice, three times, drenching my parched body in water, reclaiming my own existence from the thirst, and moved on to yet another vase—

"—You mustn't!"

A sharp voice called out as my wrist was seized, and I spun around. The one gripping my wrist was a white-haired old butler, his eyes wide with a rigid stare, holding me back — before I could pull up from the depths of my thirst-dulled memory just who that was, a turbid, violent emotion came surging out of me.

"Let go of me!"

Even as my right arm was held fast, I swung my free left hand and struck the man's body with all my strength. I held nothing back, quite literally. It was a blow meant to shatter bone or tear flesh, but the old butler parried it effortlessly with his own free hand, and both of us came away unharmed. I understood that every possible reason lay behind it — my condition, his skill, his experience. —But I couldn't stop.

"Let go! Let go! Let me go...!"

With each word, a blow; with each swing of my arm, I remembered how to swing it, and my strikes grew sharper. But no matter how sharp my fingertips cutting the air became, the old butler blocked them with ease. Gradually, the chill of the thirst crept closer again, seeming to crawl up from the soles of my feet.

"Please, just let go! My throat is parched... it's so parched... parched, parched, parched, parched, parched, I can't bear it...!"

"Please calm yourself. I will prepare water for you at once. Also, you mustn't walk about barefoot. You need attending to, as well."

"Water... really? There's... water...?"

"There is. This very instant, if you wish. But before that, allow me to see to your injuries."

The desperate force with which I'd been twisting away gradually weakened at the old butler's appeal. The thirst was still there, even now. A thirst that had grown just a little better than the moment I'd woken. If there was water to soothe that thirst, I was glad. Grateful. Thankful.

"Attending to..."

As the strength gradually drained from my body, that out-of-place word made me lower my gaze. Looking down, I saw scattered red traces on the carpet laid along the hallway, beneath the old butler's feet and mine. They began partway down the corridor and ended at the soles of my own feet. —Blood. I hadn't noticed, but it seemed I'd stepped on shards of the broken vases and painted the hallway with blood. It wasn't only shards I'd trampled underfoot, but also the flowers I'd flung from the vases. The petals, crushed and scattered, mixed with the flowing blood to draw out a horribly filthy mottled pattern.

A horrible, filthy, mottled pattern. —In that instant, an ominous black mottled sigil flashed across my mind.

"—Ah, aah, aaaaah!!"

My thin throat let out a scream, and I writhed. He must have loosened his grip because I'd seemed to be settling down. At my reaction, the old butler's hold slipped, and I swung my now-free arm, tearing my soaked nightclothes to look at my own body. Bandages wound around me, and gaunt, pale skin that had lost all color — and beneath those bandages, hidden from view, a ghastly black sigil covered my whole body, cursing it with a venomous malice.

"Nooo, Ferris! Ferris!! Where are you?! Where?!"

My own body itself felt like some hopelessly defiled thing, and a scream escaped me. I didn't want to tear off the bandages and look beneath them. I didn't want to carry that ugly black sigil hidden beneath the bandages in this body for even one more second.

I didn't want to see it. I didn't want to touch it. I didn't want to be in contact with it. I didn't want to be defiled by it.

I'd rather just die—

"—Crusch-sama!"

That body of mine, which should have been so ugly and dreadful, was caught from behind and held by a pair of slender arms. It wasn't a restraining hold. It wasn't some technique meant to stop me by force of martial skill. It was simply, forcefully, impulsively — arms wrapped around me from behind and holding me close, nothing more. I could have shaken it off if I'd wanted to. But not a single fragment of such a wish rose in me.

Instead, I simply let my body be hazily wrapped in and entrusted to a faint, welling blue light.

"It's all right. I'm here. I'm right here beside you, Crusch-sama, I promise..."

"Feri...s..."

"Yes, yes. That's right. It's Ferris."

The arms wrapped around my body tightened, but I felt no pain, no fear. By the time I noticed, I had crumpled to the spot, sitting flat on the floor. Naturally, the one embracing me — Ferris — had sunk down onto the hallway floor along with me. When I tilted my head to glance at him, his lovely face was close enough that I could feel his breath. His round eyes trembled, quivering with tears, and the sight of him desperately trying not to let his expression crumble was so brave, so dear. Naturally, the sense of rejection toward everything that had been surging through my whole body receded.

"...But I'm scared. I'm still scared. My body — that black mottled sigil is still there, even now—"

"Crusch-sama, if it's about that, it's already—"

"—Vil-jii."

Ferris cut off the old butler, who had begun to say something, with that name. At Ferris's glance, Wilhelm — yes, Wilhelm. It was Wilhelm. The old butler was Wilhelm, the "Sword Devil," a peerless swordsman, a man I could rely on.

With Wilhelm before me and Ferris behind, I sat there, sandwiched between them.

"I..., am..."

"Please listen, Crusch-sama. There's no need to worry about your body. —Excuse me."

"—Ah"

Holding my body as it shook and trembled, soothing me as my gaze wavered uncertainly, Ferris's hands slowly began to undo the wound bandages. I held my breath at that gentle yet terrifying touch, able only to watch as the bandages were unwound, little by little. From my neck down to my chest, where I'd torn my own nightclothes, the skin peeking through the frayed gap was laid bare — and there, the sigil I had so feared wasn't there. My eyes went wide.

"Eh..."

"It's not just here. Your arms, your shoulders, your legs too... the affliction has been removed."

As he spoke, Ferris undid the bandages at my shoulder too, and I dazedly lifted my arm. What was there, though it looked parched and roughened, was an arm from which every trace of that black mottled sigil had vanished. Only then did I finally realize — that agony, the one that had felt like my body was burning, like scalding water was being poured through my entire frame, had, at some point, stopped.

The thirst was still there. Even now, its remnants remained. But that was a thirst craving life itself — the thirst of a body from which that agony had been removed.

"Ferris... did you remove that Curse? From my body...?"

"——"

With a moment's respite born in my heart to look back on myself, I finally arrived at that thought. Ferris had stayed devotedly at my side through that unrelenting agony, through every morning and night, every day I was eaten away by it, striving with all his might, so bravely, so desperately, to heal it. That wish had borne fruit, and he had rescued me from that prison of suffering—

"—No. That's not it. I couldn't do anything."

But that question and that hope were denied by none other than Ferris himself. Shaking his head, his yellow eyes dyed with disappointment — a deep disappointment and despair aimed at himself — he gently touched my arm, from which the sigil had vanished, as I held my breath, and his lips trembled several times over.

He hesitated over what he had to say, resolved himself, hesitated again, repeated that back and forth many times, and finally, having made up his mind, Ferris put it into words. And it was—

"To heal your body, Crusch-sama, I sought the cooperation of the 'Divine Dragon Church.'"

"...Eh"

"A girl from the Church who calls herself the 'Saintess' lent us her power. I wasn't able to save you, Crusch-sama, myself. ...I'm truly, terribly sorry."

At Ferris, his trembling eyes brimming full of tears, his voice shaking as he said this, I lost my own voice. Slowly, the meaning of the words he'd spoken sank in, as my mind — released from pain and now gradually being freed even from that fierce thirst — came to understand.

The "Divine Dragon Church" — it was an organization that deeply, deeply revered the existence and grace of the "Divine Dragon" that had long protected the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, and that devoted itself to maintaining the peace and tranquility of the kingdom's people. To keep its activities from wielding too much influence, the Divine Dragon Church forbade itself from involvement in the affairs of state — a principle I could admit was admirable and understandable. And yet ours was a relationship in which I couldn't so readily accept it — because the existence the Divine Dragon Church prized above all else, and the way we ourselves lived, were fundamentally, irreconcilably at odds.

"—The Dragon."

The Divine Dragon Church revered and gave thanks for the Covenant forged between the kingdom and the "Divine Dragon." —Which was to say, it shared the same reasoning as the kingdom's own: that upholding the Covenant took precedence above all else for the kingdom's prosperity, and that the royals lost to a far too merciless illness were merely cogs sacrificed for the sake of that Covenant.

—The same set of values that had digested the death of Fourier Lugunica as nothing more than an incidental event.

"—!"

A formless emotion came crawling up, welling within me, striking my chest, and I felt a wave of nausea. The thoughts, images, and feelings that surfaced in my mind — all of them stimulated my five senses in complete disarray: light struck my hearing, scent struck my sight, pain struck my taste, voice struck my touch, taste struck my smell — things that should never connect were ganging up to batter me senseless.

—Why had that name come to me? —Why had that smile risen before me? —Why could I hear his voice? —Why could I feel that remembered face? —Why did I remember the taste of the blood and tears shed in grief over that death?

—Why was it that Crusch Karsten could remember Fourier Lugunica at all?

"—Ah"

There were sensations coming back to life within me. A sense of loss and a sense of duty, tenderness and sorrow, anger and joy, warmth and cold. —The fond memories and the painful attachments toward that person, forever gone now, mingling and melting together.

But there was one thing I understood, fatally and completely.

"—The Royal Selection."

At the sound that spilled from my trembling lips, Ferris's shoulders shook violently, and I could tell that Wilhelm — whose face I couldn't see — had likewise gone rigid.

We could not coexist with the "Divine Dragon Church." Never could we coexist. That we couldn't match our pace with theirs, couldn't look upon the same things together — that was something we ourselves, no one else, had chosen, decided, and declared. And yet, if it was true that I had been saved by the very party whose help we could absolutely never accept—

"Ferris—"

"—Yes."

When I called his name, there was no tremor in Ferris's voice. With a short nod, his gaze was steady and direct; though his expression stiffened, he didn't look away. Whatever might be said to him, those yellow eyes told me, he had chosen this already prepared for it.

"——"

I have to say it. I understood the reason. Why he'd had to make that choice. I understood that it had been, for no other reason, to save me. How many days of anguish had he spent, at the side of me being eaten away every morning and night, every single day? How keenly he must have felt the powerlessness of his healing hands, unable to save the one he wished to save — I had watched it from up close, closer than anyone.

So I have to say it.

I'm sorry for making you worry. I understand how you feel.

"——"

So I have to say it.

I made you face a painful decision. But the responsibility for that is mine.

"——"

So I have to say it.

Please don't grieve. Because of you, I'm able to be here like this at all.

"——"

I have to say it. I have to say it. I have to say it. I have to say it. I have to say it. I have to say it. I have to say it, have to say it, have to say it, have to say it, have to say it, have to say it, have to say it, have to say it, have to say it, say it, say it, say it, say it, say it, say it, say it, say it, say it, say it—

"—Why?"

It spilled out, tumbling free. That was it. Something utterly different in warmth, in texture, from the words I'd thought I had to say.

"You knew already, didn't you?"

Stop, stop right now. Close your mouth, shut your eyes, paint over your own consciousness. Don't look at what's in front of you, at what happened, at the choice that was made — forget it all, turn your back on it all.

"Ferris, you of all people — you were supposed to be the same as me."

I mustn't say it. I mustn't let him hear it. I mustn't let it be known. Because he had only tried to save me. Because he had only offered up his prayers. Because he had only wished to take away the suffering of someone dear to him.

So I mustn't say it.

"—Why?"

I mustn't.

"Why did you betray your vow to His Highness Fourier?"

—That she would rather have died outright than betray that person.

  • フェリス (Ferisu) romanizes as "Ferris" (a nickname for Felix Argyle), not "Felix" — used consistently here per standard convention, though the character's given name proper is Felix.
  • 「ヴィル爺」("Vil-jii") is a diminutive/affectionate nickname Ferris uses for Wilhelm ("Old Vil"); rendered as "Vil-jii" to preserve the Japanese address term rather than translating it as "old man Wil" or similar.
  • 「余」("yo") is an archaic first-person pronoun associated with royalty/high nobility; rendered here through tone/diction ("as befits me") rather than a literal "We," since the speaker (implied to be young Fourier Lugunica) is not yet formally addressing others in a royal capacity in this scene.
  • The unnamed narrator of the flashback and the "渇き" (thirst) sequence is understood from context to be Crusch Karsten (confirmed by later dialogue), though her name is never stated in the flashback itself.
