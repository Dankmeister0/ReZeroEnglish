Arc 4 Chapter 18: "Parent and Child"

—I feel my mind clearing.

The chorus of agony that had been screaming fit to crack his skull only a moment ago fell silent, and now Subaru's mind held only a single thought — only the resolve to face the father standing before him.

"I've got someone I love. Me too."

He repeated his answer to the question once more. By speaking it again, Subaru became aware that his heart had begun to walk forward. Before him, Kenichi, hearing that confession, blinked several times, aware that it had strayed from the flow of their conversation until now, and yet—

"...That so."

In a quiet voice, he lent his ear to Subaru's words. That attitude was a salvation. Even though Subaru had always known his father was the kind of person who would listen like that, he'd kept his mouth shut all this while. So now, he meant to put an end to it.

—Because there had been someone who pushed him to do just that.

"Yeah. I'm not just some brat curled up small in his room anymore."

He didn't even know how much he'd changed. Even putting on a brave face and saying he wasn't a brat, he was well aware, himself, that he was one. The courage to lift his head, the resolve to face his own weakness, the decisiveness to run from what he hated — he'd made himself not think about any of it. Having been less than a brat, he'd only now finally admitted to being one. And even that much, he hadn't been able to admit entirely on his own.

The silver afterimage floating in the back of his mind tickled sweetly at Subaru's chest. It was the radiance that had brought warmth to a Subaru who'd been stalled, unable to move, growing cold.

Silver — a color that should by rights feel cold — now poured endless heat into Subaru, becoming the strength to take a step forward. As if feverish with that heat—

"What I was so scared of, why I curled up so small — I remember all of it now. No — I knew all of it. I knew, and I pretended not to see... While I pretended not to know the weakness only I had noticed in myself, I wanted someone to—"

He couldn't fudge it by saying "someone." He knew exactly who that someone was.

"I wanted my mom and dad to slam it into me."

"――――"

"I wanted the two of you to slam it into me — that I was hopelessly small, an inadequate fool, a self-righteous piece of garbage... I wanted you to make me give up."

Kenichi's eyes, silently fixed on Subaru, didn't waver. Subaru saw himself reflected in those irises that held the same color as his own. The sharp corners of eyes so keen that anyone might mistake them for perpetually irritated now drooped in a way that was hopelessly weak.

—Pathetic, he thought.

"I've always been kind of a jack-of-all-trades — able to get by at anything without much effort. Running, studying, I was decent enough at both... And since I could do things right away that the friends around me struggled with, I actually wondered why everyone else couldn't."

Call it the arrogance of childhood, or maybe just an innocent sense of omnipotence. As a small child, Subaru had been quick to improve at both sports and studies, better than average. As a matter of course he ran faster than those around him, as a matter of course he was cleverer than kids his age, and as if it went without saying, he was the center of everyone around him—.

"Just like I'd expect from that man's kid."

The adults and neighbors around him said that of Subaru, again and again. Knowing that "that man" meant his father, and having those around him acknowledge him as that father's son — being told those words was, to young Subaru, a source of pride.

His father — Natsuki Kenichi, Subaru's father — was, even in his son's eyes, a magnetic person. He laughed often, talked often, cried often, got angry often, moved often, worked hard. His father never hesitated to declare aloud that he loved his son and wife, and there were always people around him who adored him for it; his father always stood at the center of a great many people's smiles.

Such a father was, to Subaru, the greatest thing he had to boast of, and the fact that this father cherished, above all, his family — himself and his mother — gave Subaru a sense of superiority verging on arrogance.

—I want to be like Dad. I want to be like Dad.

To young Subaru, the breadth of his father's back was the breadth of the world itself, and the world was nothing more than what could be seen from atop that back. So day after day, Subaru had been able to spend his life amid happiness, in pursuit of happiness. But—

"I don't remember when it started... but I think I lost a footrace to someone. From around then, the things I'd always been best at stopped being 'best.' Kids faster than me at running started showing up, kids quicker at solving problems started showing up. I could tell my 'number one' was shrinking, little by little, but for real, and I started thinking something was wrong."

The more he tried to force it, the further the stars that had shone across the sky of Subaru's heart receded. No matter how he reached for them, no matter how he ran beneath the open sky, the stars that had once blazed so brightly all around him disappeared, and the darkness and stillness of night enveloping Subaru only deepened. Even amid that shapeless anxiety—

"Just like I'd expect from that man's kid."

Those words alone were Subaru's salvation, the hope he clung to. Even losing at footraces, even no longer being first in cleverness, those words kept propping up young Subaru's pride.

Rather than practice running, rather than diligently working through homework, he began to take the lead in doing stupid things.

Sneaking into the school at night with friends, drawing white lines all over town, chasing off the stray dog everyone knew as dangerous from the neighborhood hangout — by keeping everyone entertained like that, Subaru tried to protect the stars that had begun to dwindle around him.

"Working hard at studying, how stupid. What's there to boast about in just being kind of fast? Me doing stuff like this, making everyone laugh — that's way more impressive, way stronger."

To protect that misguided pride, all he could do was keep running. Taking the lead in whatever everyone else feared, volunteering for whatever everyone else hated — carefully, carefully guarding his place so it wouldn't be lost.

"But once you start trying to protect yourself like that, next time you have to do something even bigger. You can't do anything smaller than what you did before. If you did, and people thought you were small-time, that'd be a disaster."

So Subaru's behavior had no choice but to escalate more and more. Ask him why he did such things, and the answer was: because he was Natsuki Subaru.

—Yes, it had to be Natsuki Subaru who did it. Natsuki Subaru had to keep being braver than anyone, freer than anyone, wilder than anyone — the kind of existence everyone admired.

And so he strained, and kept straining, and hid the fact that he was straining, without even noticing it in himself, deceiving himself and everyone around him that he could do more, that he still had more in him. Because he was, after all, Natsuki Subaru, the son of Natsuki Kenichi.

"I believed I could do anything. I made myself believe I'd do anything. And doing that, everything I did got stupider and stupider, until all I did was make noise without thinking about anything..."

And so, like a moth drawn to flame, he sought heat without even noticing he was being burned. Had he really been an insect, Subaru would have ended there, entranced by the fire until consumed. But Subaru wasn't a moth, and the friends around him were far more human than he was.

—There wasn't, I think, any particular trigger.

Gathered around whatever recklessness Subaru proposed were companions who wore the same delinquent face. The number of those gathered companions began to shrink, like teeth falling out of a comb.

"I thought they were idiots. I thought, 'You won't get to feel this fun unless you're by my side.' Let them regret it or whatever, let them waste their boring little time however they liked. I was going to look higher."

By continuing to search for the location of the stars like that, he could keep from losing sight of the ones above his own head. The stars that had once filled the sky disappeared, until all that was left to Subaru was that one twinkling star, and he kept running, staring only at it — until, on a whim, he lowered his eyes from the starry sky to the earth—

"There was no one left around me but me."

It was only natural. Subaru, who never looked back, who chased endlessly after a star no one else could see. Even the friends who'd found it amusing at first couldn't keep up with a recklessness that kept escalating with no landing point in sight. Not noticing this, he sneered at those who left as fools, while those who remained grew anxious and suspicious of his way of thinking.

And one by one, his friends vanished from his side, until, before he knew it, he alone was left behind beneath the starry sky. Sulking, angry at that, he looked up at the sky as if he'd forgotten—

"Even the stars that were supposed to be shining above me — I couldn't find them anywhere anymore."

Losing sight of the stars' light, losing the friends who'd been around him, left utterly alone in the darkness of night — it was only then that Subaru finally realized.

—He himself was nothing special at all.

"Just like I'd expect from that man's kid."

The magic words that had once given young Subaru such pride, that had once brought such vitality to his heart. Somewhere along the way, they had become, for Subaru, words of a curse.

"When I go outside and wander around town, I understand. Wherever I go, whatever I look at, my dad's traces are everywhere. ...Which makes sense, right?"

Subaru's narrow world was, precisely, the same scenery he'd once seen from atop his father's back. For a Subaru who'd sought his father's same height wherever he went, there was nowhere in that narrow world he could look without sensing his father's lingering scent.

Gradually, the world became something terrifying to Subaru. And along with that, what ate away at Subaru's heart was the mediocrity he'd noticed in himself, and the shame of not wanting his parents — or the people who knew his parents — to find out about that mediocrity.

Loved by everyone, relied upon by everyone, smiled at by everyone. Natsuki Subaru, the son of that Natsuki Kenichi, could not be seen as some coward, hunched over and fretful about others' eyes, cowering with his head in his hands at the vastness of the world.

His own bad reputation would be an insult to a father who declared his love for him outright, and would eventually lead to disappointment from that great father of his. That, to Subaru, was more terrifying than anything.

Through elementary and middle school, Subaru had thoroughly made sure to avoid standing out at all costs. Classmates who'd known him in the lower grades sometimes tilted their heads at the sight of this now-quiet Subaru — but children in their impressionable years never noticed a shred of the darkness in a classmate's heart, and by living their days healthily, forgot such trivial things soon enough. And in the midst of that buried time, what made Subaru clever was that while he played the shadow entirely among his classmates, he kept up, without fail, his act as the same mischievous son at home.

Subaru, who'd grown quiet as a weed in the shade at school, would return home and, as if a different person, recover all his old wildness. The many heroic tales he told after coming home from school were the kind that made his mother, busy with housework, break into a smile, and brought a grin to his father, worn out from work.

—Had his parents realized that all of it had been nothing but tall tales spilling from Subaru's mouth? Even now, Subaru didn't know.

And so, through elementary and middle school — the time that would consume most of his life — Subaru built a false image of himself, a human being made entirely of painted-over lies. Everyone forgot the string of bad deeds the old Subaru had committed, and came to see this unremarkable classmate as someone whose name they merely knew. Even feeling a trace of loneliness at such a thin relationship, what covered Subaru's heart was a fear greater still — an awe of a certain power carried by his own surname, Natsuki.

"Looking back on it now, that's a pretty dark way to live. But somehow, I made it through elementary and middle school like that. And then I got through it, and became a high schooler... probably because of test scores, even though it was the local school, most of my classmates didn't go on to the same one..."

Even Subaru, who'd formed the habit of thinking everything through negatively, had a sliver of courage left — enough to see the drastic change in environment as an opportunity, in a positive light. Wringing out that last dregs of courage, Subaru gritted his teeth and lifted his head.

Starting high school, a new place. An entirely unknown set of relationships to build with unfamiliar faces. There, surely, no one would look at Subaru and see "Natsuki Kenichi's son," even if they knew his name was Natsuki Subaru. And in that place — maybe he could see again the glimmer of the starry sky he'd once let slip away.

Where he chose to spend that courage was what decisively knocked Subaru off the path.

"Even I have to admit, it was a spectacular failure of a high school debut. Well, of course it was. A guy who'd never properly built relationships with people through elementary and middle school, in a place full of unfamiliar faces, getting all worked up and pushing his tension way past the limit, pulling reckless stunts... even an idiot could guess how that'd turn out."

The fact that he hadn't understood something even an idiot could understand made him realize, looking back, that he hadn't even been an idiot — he'd been something less.

He didn't want to go into detail. The result could be guessed. For Subaru, who had no model for relating to people beyond his father, if he was going to try to build relationships with someone in a new environment, his father was the only reference he had. —Behavior that, as a small child, might have been a charming source of teasing, was nothing but poison to classmates his age, going through puberty and undergoing changes of their own psyche.

"Poison doesn't even cover it. Deadly poison. The kind like a poisonous mushroom with white flesh and red spots on the cap, the kind that screams at a glance, 'deadly, eat this and you'll die in agony.'"

How could someone like that possibly get along with others? The foot Subaru had tried to step forward with in his new environment slipped from the very first step, and he plunged into the abyss. And so, having established himself as the guy who couldn't read the room, the incomprehensible one, and spent his time isolated, one morning it simply occurred to him—

—Ah, I don't want to go to school today.

"I think it was a morning when both my mom and dad happened to be out on errands. Thinking it was such a hassle, I just stayed lying down past the time I usually got up... and when I panicked and finally sat up, it was already almost noon, and just as I was about to stand up to get changed—"

Subaru noticed how strangely calm his own heart and body had become. Going to school, sitting alone by the window, spending the time silently, pretending to sleep — Subaru's heart had always been tormented by anxiety and fear. He didn't want to be in a place like that; from the moment he arrived at school, all he thought about was going home. No — from the moment he woke, all he thought about, all through the day, was going home from school.

He hadn't been bullied. He hadn't been ignored, either. It was only that Subaru himself had built the wall. He was afraid of being touched by someone's kindness, of holding on to hope. The thought of seeing the glimmer of the stars again terrified him.

A single day spent without all that misery. Enchanted by the sense of liberation, of relief, of the strength draining out of him, Subaru's feet gradually, gradually drew further away from school.

"Skipping once a week became once every three days, and before long once every two days... it didn't even take two months before I'd stopped going to school entirely."

There's no need to describe the days that followed. Subaru's heart, once he stopped going to school, was filled with a sense of relief. Part of that was the liberation of getting away from a school he'd had to endure such painful hours in, but what dominated Subaru's heart even more was a certain resignation and self-acceptance.

Subaru, who without much of a reason had stubbornly clung to his own self-righteousness and become a truant. No one would think "Just like I'd expect from that man's kid" looking at a Subaru like that, and more than anything — if his parents were disappointed enough by such a pathetic Subaru, surely his father and mother would stop "loving" him.

A worthless opinion of a son they didn't love would mean nothing at all to the two of them. If someone insulted their beloved son as worthless, they'd surely get angry. They'd be sad. And seeing them like that, people around them would pity them, and belittle them all the more. The thinner the relationship between Subaru and his parents grew, the less they'd need to worry about all that.

So, Natsuki Subaru—.

"I don't love you. I hate you. You're not... my kid. I wanted them to say that, to throw me away like that. I wanted them to make me give up."

Hoping for the existence of stars that couldn't possibly exist, looking up at the sky for a fleeting hope. He wanted to be freed from being such a spineless, pathetic person, such a foolish existence unworthy of being Natsuki Kenichi's son.

—That was exactly the truth of his heart that even Subaru himself hadn't realized.

Having laid his heart bare face to face, it was only in speaking it aloud that Subaru truly grasped the ugliness of his own heart. Unwilling to admit weakness, unwilling to admit foolishness, he'd averted his eyes, and even the smallness of trying to push the cleanup onto someone else made him nearly sick. And still, the reason Subaru hadn't given up on himself, hadn't abandoned himself, was that he'd had support.

"Rem loves you, Subaru-kun."

Over the silver afterimage flickering behind his eyelids, a faint, fleeting blue glow now overlapped. It blew a gentle wind into Subaru's heart, slipping warmth into limbs that had begun to go cold, healing him.

"Let's start over from here. From one... no, from zero!"

So she had said, and pushed at the back of a Subaru who should have been finished. She'd lifted the face of a Subaru who'd been looking down, thinking he could no longer walk, taken his hand, embraced his back, kissed his forehead, and given him courage.

Entranced by the silver glow, given heat by it; pushed forward by the blue warmth at his back, set walking by it — Natsuki Subaru, who should have ended, had begun again from zero. Because he'd realized that, because he'd remembered that, because he'd decided to walk forward from zero — he had to settle things with the past, with the time before zero, the time that had been negative.

Having finished listening to Subaru's long confession, Kenichi closed his eyes, falling silent as if in thought. Before that father's figure, Subaru desperately choked back the shame at his own weakness threatening to spill from his throat.

Given this reprieve to look back, it was precisely because of his current state of mind — one he could believe had changed, if only a little — that he could see the ugliness of his own true feelings. In the end, Subaru had, both now and in the past, pushed the cleanup of his own actions onto others. Lacking the courage to give up on himself, not wanting to become the worst villain in his own world, wanting to remain the protagonist of a tragedy, he'd silently waited, without ever putting it into words, for someone to volunteer to play the villain.

Not going to school, spending his days idle in his room, remaining that foolish self — he'd thought that someday Kenichi would break down the door and put an end to Subaru's world. Deep in the unconscious depths of his heart, he'd expected exactly that kind of resolution from those idle days. And it was with that deadlocked state of mind that he'd arrived in another world. Even there, Subaru had indulged his self-righteousness, until finally—

"—Subaru."

Kenichi's eyes, which had been closed in thought, opened, and called Subaru's name. Subaru, sunk in a sea of contemplation, had his awareness pulled back by that call, and returned suddenly from the past he'd been recalling to the present — right before his eyes, his father's face. And then—

"Father Head!"

"Gwaah!?"

Struck on the forehead by a tremendous impact, Subaru screamed, seeing stars. Pressing a hand to his forehead against the sharp pain and backing away, he saw Kenichi, risen from the bench, looking down at him.

"You see that, Subaru? That was my Father Head, delivered with love — a strike of righteous anger."

"You said 'head' but that was a heel drop! Leaning your face in like that as a feint — way too much finesse for that move!"

"Only worked because you were sitting and I was standing. Man, my body's gotten stiff. Nothing like the old days. Guess I've been skipping my post-bath stretches."

Kenichi, looking dissatisfied, began a round of stretches. Before this father, Subaru, half in tears, rubbed his throbbing head. Given such an unexpected reaction, he had no idea how to respond. What Subaru had expected was something else entirely, something more—

"But man, Subaru. You're — how do I put it... pretty dumb, huh."

"Ughp."

Cut clean in half by that blunt remark, an involuntary groan escaped his throat. Looking down at Subaru, Kenichi crossed his arms and huffed through his nose.

"Fretting and moping over and over like that... which of me or your mom did you inherit that kind of twisted thinking from? That's totally your mom's little brother's genes, that is. You know, the short, bald, fat one with the face that always looks like he's brooding over something."

"That's harsh... though yeah, I guess my life goals of not going bald and not getting fat do trace back to Uncle."

Father and son, both showing no mercy to their relatives, in a scenario where an unrelated uncle got burned. Somewhere far off, under the same sky, unaware he was being trashed by family, Kenichi went on, "Anyway," with irritated eyes,

"There's a lot of stuff that bugs me, but the biggest one's this: you're trying to get me to hate you, but you're doing it so passively, and that pisses me off. Holed up, not going to school, catching some listlessness syndrome, all so eventually your old man would snap and yell at you, huh? ...Are you an idiot. Wanting to be scolded — that's some starved-for-affection, deluded-girl nonsense. After all that wrestling around with me since this morning, still not enough for you?"

"There's so much wrong with the way you're phrasing that, but the gist is right so I can't even argue..."

"Also, if you wanted me to give up on you, you should've gone about it way more actively. Who's gonna give up on their own kid just 'cause he shut himself in his shell? If you wanted me to hate you, you should've, like, gone and massacred half of humanity for no reason. Then I'd hate you."

"No villain in a shonen manga even goes that far! What kind of insane standard is that!"

"—What you said just now is that kind of insane, to me."

Cut off flat like that, Subaru lost his words for a moment. Kenichi bent at the waist to meet his eyes, saying "You listening?" and continued,

"Even if you were slow as a snail, too dumb to solve times tables, and cutting yourself and bragging about it on a blog just to get attention..."

"I'm not that slow or that dumb or that much of an idiot..."

"Even if you were that slow and dumb and idiotic, I still wouldn't hate you or give up on you. Obviously, right? I'm your old man, and you're my son."

Saying so with an exasperated sigh, Kenichi stretched his back with a grunt. Subaru looked up at his standing father. Basking in that gaze, Kenichi went on,

"Anyway, how much of a superhuman do you think I am? The way you were talking earlier, it was like I was some hi-tech, ultra-skilled, perfect-perfect super-superhuman or something."

"Way too much overlap in that description."

"You just don't know about it, but I've had plenty of worries, regrets, failures, cried, screamed, gotten dumped too... nah, that last one not so much. Good looks, you know. You didn't take after me there, though."

"Overconfident much."

"Point is, I was immature too at your age. Sure, I was decently well-known, but I wasn't anything special. Maybe I could stop time a little, that's about it."

"Should've used that when you got hit by a car last year."

A triple punchline. Pleased at how well the joke-and-comeback routine had landed, Kenichi held out his hand, and Subaru, having no choice, met it with a high-five. As soon as their hands met, Kenichi gripped it firmly.

"I could still twist this wrist, hurl this dumb, idiotic, annoying son of mine around, and beat some sense into that character of yours..."

"Ow! Ow, ow, ow! Wait, my wrist, it's locked... ow, it hurts!"

"...But looks like you've already been broken down further than that needed, and come back up again."

Released from the wrist lock, Subaru stood up shaking his aching hand. Watching him with one eye closed, Kenichi gave a small snort.

"Thought so this morning, but you changed again all of a sudden just now. What's with that face?"

"...I told you. I've got someone I love."

The silver glow had taken Natsuki Subaru's hand and led him forward.

"And there's someone who says she loves someone like me, too."

The blue, warm light had gently pushed at Natsuki Subaru's back.

"Those girls don't know I'm Natsuki Kenichi's son. In front of them, I'm just Natsuki Subaru. ...No."

Shaking his head, he looked his father steadily in the eye, standing right before him.

"In front of anyone, I was always just Natsuki Subaru. I just decided on my own that I was carrying some weird signboard, and crushed myself under a weight that never even existed. I finally get that now."

"Way too slow. I'm the head of this household, you know. You haven't even been handed the family headship, and you're going around acting like you're carrying a signboard on your own. I'll smack you."

"You just did something way worse than smacking me!"

Stamping his foot at the excessiveness of the earlier attack, Kenichi laughed it off with an offhand "My bad, my bad." Then, narrowing his eyes, Kenichi said, "More importantly—"

"You said you've got someone you love, and then said there's a girl who says she loves someone like you too — what, does that mean you're two-timing? You, of all people?"

"Don't say 'of all people'! I know it's more than I deserve, okay?! But so what! What's wrong with having two brightest stars!"

It wasn't defiance so much as it was Subaru's honest feeling right now. He loved Emilia. He loved Rem too. The two of them had made him stand, made him walk, given him the strength not to run even now, facing Kenichi, facing his own past.

The night sky that had once covered Subaru, full of stars — the shining stars he'd once looked up at. A light no weaker than any of those was, now, blazing above Subaru's head from the two of them. And around that brightest star, the stars that should have vanished shone again, with a different light.

That was the starry sky Subaru had earned outside the room he'd shut himself away in, desperately struggling in a world he'd been unexpectedly invited into, suffering, grieving, crying out, screaming, getting angry, laughing, running, rejoicing, pressing forward.

"Well, whatever. Do as you like. As long as it stays within the law and works out fine in the end, I'm not gonna object that much. Seems like you've got some kind of talent for charming people, anyway."

"If I had that, I wouldn't have crashed and burned on my high school debut and ended up isolated. I can't pull it off smooth like you, Dad."

"I don't think that's true, you know? You're my son, after all. And also, I think you're mistaken about a lot of stuff — that's actually the worst part."

"That part?"

Wiggling his fingers on his crossed arms, Kenichi nodded "Yeah" at Subaru's tilted head.

"I'm all loose and wild in front of you and your mom, but your old man's got proper TPO sense, you know? Might be hard to tell since I'm always like this around you, but if you try acting like your old man with just anybody, of course they're gonna back away, dude."

"Wait, wait, wait..."

"Obviously, right? If some guy came at you at this energy level on a first meeting, you'd back off a little too. Keep it buttoned up till you're close with someone. Only undo the buttons once it's warmed up more. If you start in April, hold off till about the end of June."

A shocking truth. Turns out Dad was actually a sensible person who properly adjusted his attitude depending on who he was with. Not knowing that, and using his usual father as a reference, thinking that if he just acted like his dad he'd surely become popular with everyone! — what shallow thinking on his own part.

"What was all that stalled time of mine even for..."

"Well, I wouldn't call it wasted. I mean, that's exactly what got you to where you are now, right? Are those stars you found not worth that time?"

Words for a Subaru holding his head in regret. Hearing them, he lifted his face, and could answer immediately. Because that much, at least, he could say for certain wasn't true.

"—No, that's not it. I think no matter how many chances I got, I would've ended up wanting the starry sky I have now, and running for it. So, I'm glad I'm who I am now."

"That so. ...Well then, guess it worked out, huh?"

Kenichi smiled, relieved, at a Subaru who had settled things in his own heart like that. Seeing that smile head-on, the heavy knot that had been solidified in Subaru's chest fell away with a sound. The darkness he'd been carrying cleared, and the gloomy feelings inside him were washed clean.

It was a terribly selfish, self-righteous sort of emotion, but Subaru, right now, felt saved. To be able to face the past like this, to be able to break away from his old self, to be able to walk forward as his present self while still carrying that old self within him — he could feel pride in that.

So—.

"Sorry, for keeping quiet about a lot of stuff. I had all these feelings I couldn't even sort out myself, and that's why I stopped going to school and caused you trouble. I'm really sorry. Really."

"Don't sweat it. It's my mistake for not noticing you looked up to your amazingly great old man that much. Sorry for being such a huge presence to you, huh!"

"It's true and I still really don't want to admit it!"

"Ha ha ha, don't be so shy about it. You're my son, with my blood in you. I bet you'll grow up to be about half as cool as me."

"Only half? Usually it should get more refined with each generation."

"Well, the other half of you's made of your mom, and factoring that into the mix with my coolness, I feel like the Naoko component's got a pretty strong negative modifier."

"Mom, I'm sorry, I can't argue with that!"

Unable to defend his mother, who wasn't there, Subaru pressed his hands together and apologized to the empty air. Watching him do so with amusement, Kenichi shook his head with a sigh.

"But still, this took a load off my shoulders a bit. Can't help that you stalled out for a while, but it's from here, right, from here."

"Yeah. Um, sorry for the trouble..."

"If you're gonna feel sorry about it, just take your time and pay me back properly, okay? Take good care of your mom and me in the future, eldest son."

—And so, when those words were said to him, Subaru found himself unable to move.

"――――"

He'd had the resolve to apologize for everything so far, and the determination to confess his present feelings. Having carried that through properly, Subaru had thought he'd finally melted away his long-held knot, and could face his father and mother with a clear heart. Having spoken everything about himself up until now—.

"—Ugh."

So — the instant he was spoken to about "from here on"— what welled up through Subaru's whole body was—

"...S-sorry."

"Subaru?"

"Sorry... sor— sorry... I— I'm sorry, sorry..."

Kenichi's bewildered voice came from right in front of him. But he couldn't see that face. Tears, overflowing in torrents, blocked Subaru's vision, blurring the shape of the world. He covered his face with his hands, desperately wiping away the streaming tears. But no matter how much he wiped, the tears kept coming, one after another, without stopping. They wouldn't stop. They wouldn't stop for him.

"I'm sorry... I— I'm... I can't ever... sorry, I'm sorry, I'm... I'm sorry..."

—He'd known. Somewhere in his heart, Subaru had realized it long ago.

From the moment he'd been invited into another world, basked in its sunlight, narrowing his eyes at that brightness — as if it had been revealed to him then, Subaru had known. —Surely, he could never return to his original world again.

Having conveyed his own heart to his father like this, having confessed the dark feelings he'd bottled up inside, having received forgiveness even so, having been given support for his resolve to walk forward, having been given all of that, having been raised enough to be able to do all this.

"And still, I... I can never give anything back... I probably... won't ever see you again... sorry. Sor— sorry. ...I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I'm sorry."

The tears wouldn't stop. A surge of emotion so intense he might collapse right there on the spot. And yet Subaru could still stand, could still keep from crumbling, because there was a body holding him, embracing him from the front as he sobbed.

It was a hard, big palm, holding firmly onto a son who had grown to nearly the same height as him now, and yet, patting his back as it heaved with childlike sobs, as if soothing him—

"—No matter how much time passes, you're still such a handful of a son. Honestly."

Saying so, he held the sobbing Subaru, forever and forever, soothing him, loving him, embracing him without end.

"You calm down?"

"—Yeah. Sorry. Really, I've just been causing you trouble."

"You really have. Look at my shirt. It's all crusty around the chest from your snot and tears. I'm too embarrassed to even walk around the neighborhood properly now."

Flicking Subaru's forehead with a finger now that he'd stopped crying, Kenichi laughed crudely, uproariously. Subaru, his face swollen from crying, watched that smile. Kenichi sighed at the gaze mixed with sadness and guilt,

"I don't know why you were bawling like that, but it must be embarrassing, so I'll keep it a secret. At least be grateful to me for that much."

"...Yeah. I'm grateful. Really, from the bottom of my heart, more than anyone else in this world."

"Say it that much and even I get embarrassed."

Scratching his cheek with an embarrassed laugh, Kenichi. Subaru couldn't keep looking straight at that face for long, and looked away. Kenichi shrugged at him, waving a hand as if shooing away a bug,

"There, there, crybaby, go on home already. Your old man's in the mood to keep walking a bit longer, so I'll take the long way back. Walking next to you all teary-eyed would start some weird rumors."

"...Grown man and his grown kid, what are they even doing — that kind of thing, huh."

"Exactly. Walking home with you looking like that now, and having my friends gossip about it, would be embarrassing."

"That's a line that could be fatal depending who hears it, so watch where you use it."

Unable to help himself, he shot back at his father's remark, and a nostalgic sort of ache pierced Subaru's heart. Forcing it down with his back teeth, turning his face away, Subaru managed to force out the words, "Well then—"

"I'll head back first. Try not to get stopped by the cops or something."

"Sorry to disappoint, but every officer around here knows my face. Even if you're setting me up for a bit, I can't deliver."

"That wasn't a setup."

Kenichi's unchanging attitude. Feeling saved by it once again, Subaru grew disgusted with his own unchanging weakness. How long was he going to keep being protected, relying on others? It was hopeless.

He didn't want to expose any more weakness in front of Kenichi. Subaru let out one sharp breath, then, as if resolved, turned his back and stepped forward. Walking a little fast, trying to disappear from here as quickly as he could.

"—Hey, Subaru."

At his back, Kenichi's voice called out, and his feet stopped without meaning to.

"You've got all kinds of stuff going on too, I bet. So there's only one thing I'll say."

"――――"

"Do your best. I'm counting on you, son."

Being counted on, being disappointed in — that had terrified him. The fear that he might be betraying his father's expectations had gripped Subaru and never let go. So his father's expectations had been, to Subaru, a symbol of terror—.

"—Yeah, leave it to me. Dad."

Still facing away, Subaru pointed a finger up at the sky.

"My name's Natsuki Subaru. Natsuki Kenichi's son. —So, I can do anything, and I'll do anything. Your son's amazing, you know."

"Yeah, I know that. I mean, half of you's made of me!"

Kenichi's voice, laughing uproariously as he declared that, showered down on Subaru's back. Listening to it, a smile rose unbidden to Subaru's own lips.

Still facing forward, he began to walk. His knees didn't shake. His heart wasn't shaken either. He just walked, looking straight ahead, steady.

—From the back he'd always watched, he would now walk forward, watched from behind in turn.

Just that alone could give him this much strength, he thought. And Subaru kept walking, without stopping.
