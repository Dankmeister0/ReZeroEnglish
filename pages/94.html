Arc 3 Chapter 18: "The Greedy Merchant"

Anastasia, her pale purple, wavy hair flowing down her back. Standing at her side was the knight Julius, his deep purple hair combed neatly, giving him a dandyish look.

The two of them stood out for how thoroughly upper-crust both master and retainer were, but after such a string of distinctive personalities, Subaru couldn't help but feel a creeping unease that yet another oddball was about to appear. His concern seemed to be shared by everyone else in the hall — the eyes turned toward the two as they stepped forward carried a heavy mix of anxiety and tension from the very start, and that feeling hung thick in the air. Amid that unpleasant, taut atmosphere in the hall, Anastasia, drawing every eye to herself, began first with,

"If y'all are expectin' someone with as strong a personality as the last two, I'm afraid I might disappoint ya. Bein' too strong a character doesn't exactly go over well for me — bein' plain is more my selling point."

She clapped her hands together twice, loudly, shaking everyone's attention, and then, seeing the surprise on every face, offered a gentle smile for all to see. At that smile from Anastasia, the strange unease that had gripped the hall loosened, just slightly. A few coughs could be heard here and there, and in the gallery one could spot signs of people feeling embarrassed at having still been dragging along the mood from before.

Anastasia surveyed those reactions with satisfaction, nodded once, and continued.

"Well then, allow me — Anastasia Hoshin — to say a few words. I'm an outsider here, so please forgive me if I'm a bit uncouth, yeah?"

She pressed her clasped hands together, tilted her head slightly, and flashed a bit of charm. Just like her opening line, it was a witchy little gesture that had the crowd's reaction wrapped around her finger in a single motion. And then,

"I am Julius Juukulius, Lady Anastasia's foremost knight. Please leave supporting her to me. I shall see to it gracefully."

Julius brushed back his forelocks lightly, his every motion needlessly refined, asserting his own presence. So — it was already clear that the line about "being plain is my selling point" had been false advertising.

Setting aside Julius's practiced, gorgeous flourish, what really caught Subaru's attention was Anastasia's manner of speech, which kept setting off little bursts of "this feels out of place in another world" in his head. Being a native of the Kanto region himself, it wasn't a dialect Subaru was especially familiar with, but even so, it was the kind of accent you'd be hard-pressed to go a full day without hearing at least once on TV. The way the intonation rose and fell — Subaru still wasn't quite sure how capable this world's translation function actually was, but he had to admit, it was doing a pretty impressive job here.

Subaru nodded in silent praise of this invisible function. Meanwhile, the proceedings marched solemnly on. Having finished her introduction, Anastasia now faced Miklotov, who stroked his long beard from the dais and said,

"That distinctive way of speaking — are you from Kararagi?"

"That's right. I'm from the lowest tier of the Free Trade Metropolitan Area, in Kararagi."

"Hmm, the lowest tier — then what brings you to Lugunica?"

As Anastasia spoke of her origins, Miklotov's eyes narrowed slightly.

If the meaning of "lower tier" was the same as it was in Lugunica, then Anastasia's standing would be that of a commoner. Or, going by the word "lowest," perhaps something even further down than that. If so, was her current, lavish outfit something Julius had bought and given her? And yet, she wore it with a practiced ease, and more than that, her sheer nerve — standing unfazed before the assembled dignitaries of the nation, Council of Wise Men included — didn't quite add up with that theory.

While Subaru turned that question mark over in his mind, Anastasia met the sharp gaze directed at her with an unruffled expression, gave a small shrug, and said,

"I'm from the lowest tier, sure, but these days I've got myself a proper estate built in the capital. I've also set up shops in several cities besides. I've come to Lugunica on that very business."

"The Hoshin Company is the trading house of which she serves as president — one that has begun growing at a remarkable rate. Alongside its expansion within Kararagi, talk arose of opening a branch here in Lugunica as well. That, in fact, was the very thing that first brought Lady Anastasia and me into contact."

Julius supplemented Anastasia's words. Hearing this, Miklotov gave a nod of understanding. "I see."

"So then — born into the lowest tier, yet a young merchant who rose through her own commercial talent, is that it?"

"The nice thing about Kararagi is, even a li'l gal like me gets a chance. Funny thing is, seems I've got a nose for sniffin' out the smell of money."

Anastasia touched her own well-shaped nose, looking rather pleased with herself. At those words, Subaru could see unease ripple through the crowd — especially among the officials. Subaru himself had taken it in a rather vague sense, something like "the result of some straw-millionaire-style alchemy of getting rich," but apparently, to these people, it wasn't nearly so simple a story.

Subaru wasn't well versed in just how high the wall stood between commoner and noble, but judging by the reaction, the wall Anastasia had so casually hopped over with a laugh must have been an extraordinary thing indeed.

In the murmuring throne room, Anastasia continued to recount her own achievements, modestly and quietly. To bolster her further, Julius stepped forward to press the point home to the still-rippling officials.

"Here in Lugunica as well, Lady Anastasia's Hoshin Company is expanding with the cooperation of House Juukulius. What's truly remarkable is the fact that this company's rise — spanning two nations — was built up by Lady Anastasia in the span of mere years."

Judging by appearances, Anastasia's age wasn't far off from Subaru's own — likely somewhere just shy of twenty. Subaru didn't know at what age she'd first realized her gift for commerce, but weighing that fact against everything else, it was easy enough to see that she was a monster in the world of economics.

"Lady Anastasia is a genius of commerce... no, I might even say a prodigy of the rarest kind. Her commercial talent, like her beauty — forgive me, I mean her gifts — are not bound by age. Her eye for opportunity, her talent for moving people — both leave someone as unremarkable as myself green with envy."

"My my. For the finest of knights to sing such high praise — that is quite something indeed."

At Julius's heaped-on modesty, Miklotov nodded magnanimously. But Subaru, listening off to the side, couldn't quite swallow that last bit. Something in what Miklotov had just said caught in Subaru's mind, and he tilted his head.

"Did I mishear that, or did he just call him the finest of knights?"

With each knight currently standing beside their respective candidate, the only one who could answer Subaru's question while still standing in the same row was Reinhard. He turned only his eyes toward Subaru and answered,

"You heard right. In the Royal Guard of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, aside from Commander Marcos, Julius ranks highest. There is technically a vice-commander as well, but that's a purely honorary post — you can consider it essentially vacant."

Reinhard glanced toward Marcos standing at the center, then turned his gaze to Julius's back as he conducted himself gracefully under the crowd's attention.

"Skill with a blade, mastery of Mana, lineage, and accomplishments — Julius lacks nothing as a knight. He's rightly and without question deserving of the title Finest of Knights."

"But in the lower town of the capital, when people say 'Knight among Knights,' it sure sounded like they meant you. Even the dimmest folks knew the name, and you never denied it."

"There's quite a bit of difference between that title and my actual qualities. Though it's true that, when it comes purely to swordsmanship, I likely surpass Julius. I've never met anyone stronger than myself."

Subaru blanched a little at how casually that claim of being the strongest had come out, but Reinhard, having just dropped that bombshell, went on with an unbothered, composed expression, without a trace of boastfulness.

"But the world isn't so simple that skill with a sword alone makes everything turn out fine. Taken as a whole, I fall well short of Julius... and in that regard, I probably don't measure up to Ferris either."

"I can't tell if that's you rating yourself too high or too low..."

"I don't believe I'm over- or under-rating myself. It's simply that my particular gifts allow me to do only certain things, and I hold in high respect those who can reach what's beyond my grasp. That's all it is. And of course, that includes you as well."

"Leaving aside the question of how you rate yourself, you definitely overrate other people way too much."

At the very least, Subaru didn't think he'd shown accomplishments worthy of Reinhard's earnest support so far. Subaru was well aware that he himself was an unremarkable, middling talent, and he judged that everything he'd done up to this point had simply been the mediocre result of mediocrity doing mediocre things, as mediocrely as it could. Which was precisely why a conversation with Reinhard — who would look him dead in the eye and declare, with complete sincerity, "you're seriously amazing" — was so unbearably itchy to sit through. Like some invisible organ in his body, in some spot he couldn't reach no matter how hard he scratched, was itching unbearably.

"And yet you go and say it to someone's face, all bold as brass, you pretty boy. I'm gonna end up on the 'route to me' at this rate — watch yourself."

"There are a lot of words in there I don't quite follow, but — if it's with you, I suppose that's fine too."

"I've got no interest in delighting the fujoshi crowd, thanks."

Subaru tossed out a phrase that probably wouldn't translate well if said out loud, and curtly cut the conversation with Reinhard short. Meanwhile, the exchange between the Finest of Knights and the head of the Council of Wise Men continued.

"I understand now that House Juukulius and Lady Anastasia enjoy a good relationship. Hmm. Then I should like to ask Lady Anastasia herself a question."

"Finally, my turn. With Julius takin' up so much of the spotlight, I was startin' to feel like a shrinkin' violet over here. Ask away, I'll answer anything."

Anastasia, who had left the floor to the eloquent Julius, smiled as she said this, and Miklotov returned the gesture with the benevolent smile of a kindly old man, nodding. Then, suddenly, a sharper glint entered his eyes.

"Then allow me to ask — Lady Anastasia, as a citizen of Kararagi, with what purpose do you seek the throne here in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica?"

"Ahh, figured that'd come up eventually. My background, I mean."

Anastasia fidgeted with the ends of her purple hair, looking troubled. Belatedly, Subaru caught the intent behind Miklotov's question. In short, he was raising the point that crowning Anastasia — whose legal citizenship lay elsewhere — as queen of this nation was bound to raise all sorts of problems.

Obvious as it might sound, so long as nations existed, this world too had divisions of country and people. Subaru had no idea how high that particular wall stood, but even in a state of emergency, it seemed unthinkable that a country would simply hand its throne over to a visitor from abroad. Even if she claimed to have the right, it would be as unrealistic as a Japanese citizen becoming President of the United States — long before the question of "rights" even entered into it, the citizens who made up the nation simply wouldn't accept it.

Her qualification had been confirmed, as had her background. So the next question was why she sought the throne at all — just as Crusch and Priscilla had each been made to answer, Anastasia now faced the same demand. The whole hall held its breath, waiting for her words to begin. Against that mounting tension, Anastasia gave a faint smile.

"Bein' expected like that just makes me nervous. Sorry to disappoint, but I don't have some grand philosophy like Crusch-san, and I sure don't have the kind of sweeping confidence Priscilla-san has, believin' she was chosen to become queen."

"Then surely you won't tell me it was simply that the Dragon's Seal reacted to you, and you're going along with it as it comes?"

"Ahaha, if that were the case, I'd never have had the nerve to show my face in a place like this. 'Course, I've got my own reasons for bein' here."

She laughed wryly at Miklotov's words, then, after a breath, said —

"— Truth is, I'm a bit greedier than most folks."

She said it as easily as sticking out her tongue. The remark, so different in tone from what everyone had been expecting, left most of the hall looking as if they doubted their own ears. Anastasia, pleased by the reaction, nodded and continued.

"Given where I came from, I s'pose I had an unusually strong greed for things, ever since I was little. And here I am now, one of the—"

Born in the lowest tier — that's what Anastasia had said. If her words were to be believed, then her childhood, like Felt's, must have been one where simply getting through each day took everything she had. Just what sort of character gets forged in a place like that — Subaru could guess well enough, comparing it to his own common sense, by picturing Felt's own scrappy, none-too-scrupulous toughness.

Felt, standing rather aimlessly and sullenly in the row of candidates, seemed to sense Subaru's overly warm gaze on her and turned to look his way, so Subaru shuffled a little to duck behind Reinhard. Felt, whose eyes then met Reinhard's as she followed Subaru's retreat, made an openly disgusted face, while Reinhard, in return, beamed back at her with an almost aggressively refreshing smile.

"Greed is a scary thing — once you start lookin' upward, there's no end to it. First it's today's meal money, then tomorrow's, then a whole week's worth. Wantin' to sleep somewhere safe with a roof over my head, wantin' a bed, wantin' an even softer bed."

Anastasia counted off her wishes on her fingers, each demand rising, little by little, higher than the last.

"Started out as an errand girl at a small trading house, and when I stuck my nose into how the shopkeeper did things, turned out I had a knack for it. Kept it up a while, and before long I was bein' trusted with the big deals, and life got easy enough that I nearly forgot I'd ever lived in the lowest tier. But even though life got easier, I didn't get more free. No — if anything, I got less free."

"...Hmm. And why is that?"

"That's the terrifyin' part of greed, right there. Basically, the more things came within reach of my eyes and hands, the more things there were I wanted to grab hold of. Wanted this, wanted that, that wasn't enough, none of it was enough — and before I knew it, here I am."

Anastasia smiled brightly and pointed down at her own feet. It was plain that she wasn't simply indicating the floor, but the royal castle itself. She then gestured with her hand toward the other candidates.

"I'm greedy, so I want everything. But I've never once been satisfied. I don't know what real fulfillment feels like. That's why — I want my own country."

"You would weigh a kingdom on the scales of material desire?"

"If my scale breaks under the weight of it, then I want it to break. If I can't fit it all inside myself, well, that just means I've finally been satisfied."

To Miklotov's words, tinged as they were with reproach, Anastasia answered boldly, returning his gaze with a smile. She had declared, plainly, that her reason for seeking the throne was her own "greed" — and having said as much, she went on,

"But if, even after gettin' my hands on a whole kingdom, I still wasn't satisfied... well, I s'pose I'd just have to aim even higher, kingdom and all included."

"And if what you gained turned out, to you, to be worthless — what then?"

Even placed on the scales, what would become of anything that still failed to match her passion? Even the kingdom itself, perhaps — that was the implication behind Miklotov's question. Anastasia waved a hand and said, "Ah, no worries there, none at all," and continued,

"Told ya already, didn't I? I'm greedy. So whatever happens to somethin' once it's mine, it stays mine. And anything that's mine, I put to work satisfyin' my greed even further. So my life in Kararagi, the Hoshin Company, every employee workin' there — all of it's part of my passion, all of it exists to satisfy me. There's no way I'd throw any of it away."

So, she said, catching her breath, sweeping her gaze across every face in the hall,

"— So you can all rest easy, becomin' mine, yeah?"

She smiled, warm and gentle, in the very same manner as when they'd all first laid eyes on her in this hall — all while, beneath that gentle surface, an almost maddening hunger still smoldered.

The idea behind it was vulgar enough, but for that very reason, her argument was simple. She desired the throne purely out of her own greed, and she pledged that, once she had it in hand, she would devote everything to the prosperity of the kingdom. Once something was hers, she wouldn't abandon it — and once it was hers, her very nature wouldn't let her rest until she'd pushed it to greater heights. That much, she had made clear to everyone through her words just now. And then —

"Yes, I understand. Lady Anastasia's argument is sufficient. Now then — Sir Knight Julius, do you have anything to add?"

Once the master's speech concluded, all that remained was the customary time for the retainer to make their case. Where the previous two knights had spoken purely of the unshakable conviction of their respective masters' spirits, Julius said, "Of course," fiddling with his forelocks as he stepped forward.

"Lady Anastasia phrased it in vulgar terms as 'greed,' but turn it around, and it is in fact proof of both deep ambition and deep compassion. At the same time, from the standpoint of a manager, she is also capable of choosing not to be swayed by sentiment. For a ruler, that quality is indispensable."

"Hmm. Yes, quite so."

"And I should add, as I mentioned before — Lady Anastasia's talent for commerce, her prodigious gift, is something this kingdom needs desperately right now. Due to the wars and extravagance of the two previous kings, the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's financial troubles have grown severe."

Julius's sudden touching on the nation's shameful secret sent a stir through the hall. One of the elders of the Council of Wise Men frowned, looking down at the young knight.

"That is hardly the sort of matter one should speak of so carelessly in a public setting, Sir Julius."

"That fiscal reconstruction has been a matter of national urgency for decades now is common knowledge. I feel no need to conceal it before the people gathered in this hall. With even state projects currently stalled, do you not think the very act of looking away from this is what has led to our current financial straits?"

"For a mere knight to meddle in matters of state so far outside his purview..."

"Furthermore—"

Cutting off the elder, whose temple had begun to bulge with veins as he prepared a retort, Julius raised a finger and continued, giving it a slight shake.

"My own House Juukulius has not been greatly affected. Had I continued looking away and glossing over the matter, it is a problem I might never have had to face in my own generation. But even if my house remains unharmed, I cannot simply overlook it if the royal family I serve is in dire straits."

Which is why, Julius said, turning his attention toward Anastasia standing beside him,

"I sought contact with the Hoshin Company at the very height of its prosperity in Kararagi, hoping to bring a new wind into Lugunica. And in the course of that, I came to see in Lady Anastasia the very qualities of a ruler. What else should I call this, if not fate?"

As if catching fire, Julius's tone rose higher, his delivery growing more resonant and rapid. Adding gestures with his hands, he grew almost theatrical, like an actor upon a stage.

"If it is Heaven's will that has chosen someone, then that someone can be none other than Lady Anastasia. I swear upon my loyalty to my royal house, upon my devotion to this kingdom — I declare without hesitation that Lady Anastasia alone is fit to be queen. —Thank you all for your attention."

Sensing that the audience had been thoroughly absorbed by his speech, he delivered that closing line as a signal of its end, and bowed. The hall, which had seemed frozen in time until that moment, began to stir again, and everyone, looking as if they'd just come back to themselves, once more took in the argument of the third pair of master and retainer. Even amid all this, only Commander Marcos, leader of the Knights, remained unmoved in expression, as he said flatly,

"Sir Knight Julius, I believe that will be quite sufficient."

He accepted and deflected that impassioned oratory with complete composure. Julius, apparently well used to his superior's curt reactions, replied gracefully, "Yes, thank you very much," and returned to Anastasia's side.

"Splendidly done, Lady Anastasia. Truly, you are a flower that blooms most beautifully in settings such as this."

"Feels like you went and stole my thunder again, Julius. Though you praised me so much I can't even get properly mad about it."

Laughing together, master and retainer returned side by side to the row of candidates. And so the third camp's argument came to its end, and next in order came —

"Now then, the next candidate — Lady Emilia."

After a brief silence, the name of the silver-haired girl, who had until now kept perfectly still, was called.

The only one among the row of candidates without a knight at her side. At the sound of her name, she raised her face, her beautiful, pale profile touched with both anxiety and a firm, unwavering resolve, and said,

"Yes."

Emilia stepped forward. Her Royal Selection was about to begin.

—And at that moment, what was Natsuki Subaru doing?
