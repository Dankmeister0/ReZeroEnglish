Re:Zero EX: "Life in a World Mistaken from Zero II"

An annual April Fools' joke taken a bit too far. Please consider this a non-canonical side story and enjoy it with an open mind.

"The pillow feels different..."

Subaru muttered as she checked the soft, pillowy sensation against the back of her head. It wasn't just the pillow that felt different from her usual awakening. Her body—just a hair taller than the average Japanese high school girl—the bed supporting it, and the silky sheets were all entirely unlike what she was used to.

"So in short... alternate world life continues..."

Springing up with her usual morning-person reflexes, Subaru felt the annoying brush of her hair spreading across the back of her neck as she confirmed the fact. Subaru had awakened in a bed of absurd proportions—a far cry from her middle-class upbringing—inside a massive room with space to spare even with such a bed. Given that it was assigned to an uninvited stranger like Subaru, it was likely a guest room, and she deduced there was more than just one or two rooms of this scale.

"Stomach wound, completely gone. No stitches in sight either. Man, doctors in another world sure are top-notch. If I ended up damaged goods, I'd never be able to get married."

Cracking such jokes as she stepped off the bed, Subaru flipped up her top to inspect the fair skin of her stomach. Tilting her head and nodding while annoyed at the inconvenience of not having a hair tie, she realized... This meant—

"—Looks like this time I managed to avoid 'Return by Death'."

With a sigh of relief, she accepted the fact that she had somehow survived the massive blood loss at the end of the clash in the Loot House.

Natsuki Subaru, a high school girl in the bloom of youth, suddenly summoned to an alternate world. Cast into a parallel realm without any particular special skills, she nonetheless possessed a special Authority bestowed upon her, a staple of portal fantasy since time immemorial. Its name: "Return by Death"—a super-powered ability that sent her back in time upon dying, allowing her to trial-and-error her way out of tragedies and forge a path toward her desired future.

"Well, triggering it causes mind-numbing agony, so it's a cheat skill I'd rather never use again if I can help it, though..."

"Muttering to yourself in someone else's sanctuary—you've got nerve, girl."

"Whoops, the first villager speaks."

A voice called out from behind Subaru as she lamented her abysmal luck, wishing she could have chosen her power when being summoned. When she turned around, a small figure sitting atop a step ladder in the middle of a vast archive was staring at her with half-lidded eyes.

—It was a shota boy around ten years old, clad in a reddish-purple tailcoat with short, tightly curled cream-colored hair.

Distinctive patterns floated in his round, bluish eyes, and a slightly cheeky expression sat on his adorable, neat features. A large book lay open across the knees of his short-pants-clad legs, making it clear he was deeply annoyed at having his reading interrupted. Be that as it may—

"Glaring at me with such a cute face isn't gonna work on a high school girl who writes off pretty much every mammal smaller than herself as 'sooo cuuuteee!'"

"...Not only did you ruin Vitcha's masterfully crafted labyrinth, but you keep spewing incomprehensible nonsense. You severely lack respect, girl."

"Labyrinth? You mean that corridor that looked like it'd loop forever? Sorry, sorry for hitting the jackpot on my first try. See, it's not just my speech—my intuition can't read the room either. Forgive me~"

Winking with one hand raised, Subaru brought up the corridor she had passed through after exiting the room where she first woke up, where she'd encountered a rather bizarre phenomenon. After repeatedly passing decorative paintings, vases, a continuous carpet, and doors hanging the exact same nameplate, Subaru had judged it to be an endlessly repeating hallway. Without hesitation, she had chosen the door to the very first bedroom she'd come out of.

Opening it had, as expected, brought her not to her initial room, but to the curly-haired shota who had presumably created the corridor in the first place.

"What do you wanna do? Should I step back out and do a grand, dramatic double-take? If that'll satisfy your ego, I'll put on the performance of a lifetime for ya."

"...Good grief. Vitcha's patience has reached its limit."

"Oh?"

Closing the book, the child climbed down the ladder and walked toward Subaru. Intimidated by his oddly pressuring aura, Subaru's legs froze, leaving her standing stock-still as he closed the distance. Then, an outstretched hand gently touched Subaru's stomach.

"Eek."

"If those are your last words, you truly are a hopelessly foolish girl."

"Ghyah—?!"

Instantly, a shockwave shot through Subaru's whole body, originating from the child's palm pressed against her belly. Before she could even think to brace herself, Subaru collapsed helplessly on the spot. Despite being the culprit, the boy didn't bother to catch her. As a result, she bumped her head on the floor. The carpet was plush, though, so it didn't hurt.

"Not very gentlemanly... for a boy... in two ways, here..."

"I am surprised you can still speak. Nor is that assessment wrong. —Do not measure a noble being like Vitcha by your pathetic human standards, human."

With that cold, dry declaration as the last thing she heard, Subaru's consciousness faded. Honestly, she was passing out far too often since being summoned, and she caught herself worrying whether sleeping on the bare floor so much would make a delicate maiden catch a cold—before blacking out completely.

However, despite Subaru's glowing assessment, their reaction was less than favorable. "Brother, she is a guest, but could something be wrong with her?" "Reu, it goes without saying—what's wrong is her head, and it's far too late."

"If anything, Brother's wording is seriously brutal!"

"In that regard, I bet Emilio-kyun would treat me way gentler..."

"Oh."

"I'm so glad, Subaru. You woke up safely."

"EMILIO-KYUN IS HERE—!!"

"What's that '-kyun' thing? Where did it come from?"

He asked such an innocent yet oddly difficult-to-answer question.

"So you've already hit it off with Victor. And here I thought he was giving me the silent treatment lately—has his shyness been cured, I wonder-r-r?"
