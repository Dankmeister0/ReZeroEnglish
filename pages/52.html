Arc 2 Chapter 28: "Resolve, and Confession"

"—So you've finally come to, I suppose."

When Subaru opened his eyes, the sunlight had already tilted far to the west.

Partway down the cliff—the spot Subaru had reached after his first fall—the forest broke off, opening into a gaping mouth of empty air.

He'd had no room to notice it in the chaos of the fight with the beast, but now, with the sky visible above him, an inexplicable sense of relief settled over Subaru.

No longer hemmed in by trees, it was probably the illusion of having escaped that suffocating feeling that made him feel this way—enough that even his dead-end situation, which hadn't actually changed in the slightest, stirred up a self-serving hope that maybe, just maybe, something had shifted.

"Well? Say something, I suppose."

"...Something."

"That's a dull, stale joke, and you look positively pathetic making it, I suppose."

Beatrice spat the words out bitingly, then smoothed down her long, sumptuous ringlets.

Perched atop the sheer rock face of the cliff, her usual dress looked utterly out of place—like someone had cut her figure out and pasted it onto a landscape painting after the fact.

"...Can't picture you doing the outdoors thing, dressed like that."

"Betty didn't want to go tromping through some musty mountain either, I suppose. If you hadn't run off to a place like this, there'd have been no need for her to come all this way, I suppose."

Beatrice said this irritably, brushing off her hem.

Subaru knit his brow at her words in puzzlement—and then, belatedly, realized why.

All those deep wounds he'd suffered had, aside from the traces left behind—bloodstains, torn clothing—mostly closed up.

His left wrist, which had been nearly severed, the gash on his right arm deep enough to show bone, his right leg which he'd been sure had stopped working entirely—all of it had been restored.

"There were too many for Betty to erase the scars entirely, I suppose. The least you can do is accept that much as the price of your own carelessness, I suppose."

Looking closely, he could make out the remnants of the fang wounds here and there. But they'd faded so much that they'd only be visible in bright light, and besides, he wasn't vain enough to fuss over scars.

Purely and simply, having his wounds healed like this was the greatest relief of all. Only—

"Why..."

"What is it, I suppose."

"Why did you save me? I..."

—Even to Beatrice, who had formed a Contract with him, he couldn't confide anything.

At Subaru's faltering, Beatrice snorted, wearing her familiar look of exasperation. She shrugged and went on,

"Protecting your safety is the Contract Betty made, I suppose. If the person she made it with went and died a pathetic, bloody death, it would reflect on Betty's dignity, I suppose."

"I thought the terms were... only until this morning."

"If you've got room to nitpick, Betty could always open the same wounds right back up again, I suppose. How convenient—there are even scars already marked out as targets, I suppose."

"I'm sorry thank you very much please forgive me."

He groveled, pressing his head against the rock face, to deal with Beatrice's quiet fury.

In any case, though she'd teased him—

"You're still keeping your Contract with me..."

"A Contract's a Contract. It stays in effect until one of us falls, I suppose. ...Now that Betty thinks about it, not setting a proper time limit might have been a mistake, I suppose."

Whether she meant to genuinely erase her earlier nitpicking or not, Beatrice behaved as though she'd forgotten all about the time limit Subaru had mentioned.

A foul-mouthed girl he didn't get along with—that had been his strong impression of her. So this show of mercy from Beatrice struck him somewhere in the chest, and he pressed a hand there.

Overwhelmed by welling emotion, Subaru lost his words.

Beatrice watched him fall silent like that, and without remarking on the change in his expression, pointed a finger at him.

"Come to think of it, there was something Betty needed to ask, I suppose."

"...What is it?"

He tensed up despite himself, wary that depending on what she asked, he might be forced into another encounter with the black mist.

But what came out of her mouth had nothing to do with his groundless worry.

"About the wounds Betty healed for you... did you finish off whatever gave them to you? Or did it get away, I suppose?"

"...? You mean the wounds from that black wolf-looking thing? Then..."

He walked slowly to the edge of the cliff and pointed down below, showing Beatrice.

At the tip of his finger lay the beast's corpse, still sprawled in the same spot as before. Beatrice stepped up beside him, confirmed it, and nodded.

"Was that the only one, I suppose?"

"If there'd been two or more, I'd have just gotten chewed to death twice as fast."

Even managing to bury that one had been pure chance.

If he'd been attacked by a whole pack, it would've been like some manga panel—a cloud of dust kicked up, and once it cleared, nothing left but Subaru's picked-clean skull.

At Subaru's answer, Beatrice touched her chin and closed her eyes for a moment. Then, as though satisfied, she gave a small shake of her head.

"Betty doesn't sense anything else nearby, so it was probably a stray, I suppose. If you just happened to run into the one that slipped through the barrier, your bad luck really is something else, I suppose."

"Yeah, I'm starting to suspect I'm basically a walking bad-luck charm myself."

It felt like every stroke of misfortune his whole life could hold had decided to pile on at once.

Even if he were guaranteed that nothing bad would ever happen again once he got through this, he'd still rather someone dial it back and dole the misery out in smaller doses.

Ever since coming to this other world, the memories that surfaced most vividly were, without exception, ones steeped in pain.

And yet there should have been just as many happy ones.

Feeling something ache weakly at the back of his memory, Subaru lowered his eyes to mask his expression. But Beatrice paid his sentimentality no mind.

"If not that, then... well, thinking about it is probably pointless anyway, I suppose."

"What is it? It bugs me when you go and convince yourself of something all on your own like that. Whatever it is you noticed, I want to see it, hear it, touch it too."

"It would just be one more thing piled on top that you can do nothing about, I suppose. More importantly, you should be racking your brain over what you're going to do with yourself from here, I suppose."

Beatrice pushed away Subaru as he stepped closer, once again cutting off clean any avenue of retreat he'd been searching for.

As if to drive the point home to the now-silent Subaru, Beatrice crossed her arms and said,

"Let Betty tell you now, this is as far as she can help, I suppose. She can't arrange a chance for you to go back to the mansion and explain yourself to the elder of those sisters, I suppose. If that chance ever existed, you're the one who threw it away, I suppose."

"I...!"

If he could have said it, he wanted to scream that he would have.

If not for that restriction—the one that felt like his heart being crushed in a fist—Subaru would have spilled everything and begged for forgiveness.

That none of it would offer any clue to healing Ram's heart. That Subaru, too, was just another victim swept along by that impossible situation.

Because even that much wasn't permitted to him, he'd fled all the way out here, gotten attacked by a wild animal, and ended up crawling for his life.

"Am I an idiot? ...No, I am an idiot."

Yet again, he'd used his situation as an excuse to pretend not to see what was right in front of him.

Repeating pretexts, excuses, arguments, self-preservation over and over, Subaru had ended up here at the cliff's edge. That was proof, in both a literal and a spiritual sense, that he'd been driven into a place with nowhere left to run.

He'd run and run and run and kept on running—which way were his feet even facing anymore?

"Why would you... for someone like me, who can't go back?"

"Call it a whim, I suppose. The least you could do is die somewhere out of Betty's sight, or she'll be troubled by bad dreams, I suppose. ...Though the way you're still hesitating at this point, even that seems like a long shot, I suppose."

Her words, stripped of all kindness and honed to nothing but severity, cut into him.

Beatrice's gaze, as though looking at something utterly worthless, was bone dry, and without realizing it Subaru made a strangled sound in his throat.

"If I choose to run away..."

"Betty will at least help you avoid being found by the people at the mansion, I suppose. Where you disappear to after that is your own business, I suppose."

It was a curt way of putting it, but it was undoubtedly her genuine feeling.

Beatrice never went back on what she said. That much was clear from the fact that she'd come all the way out into the mountains looking for Subaru, and hadn't abandoned him even now, crouched here so pathetically.

No doubt, if Subaru chose to run, she would affirm that choice.

Not out of consideration for him, but out of an indifference weighed against the terms of their Contract.

But if, hypothetically—

"If I said I wanted to talk to Ram, what would you do?"

"...It would be pointless, I suppose. The elder of the sisters no longer has room to spare for listening to your words, I suppose. Whatever you say, she won't listen, and she won't believe it, I suppose."

At Subaru presenting this other option, Beatrice, uncharacteristically, lowered her eyes as she answered.

Perhaps what rose in her mind was the image of Ram, raising her voice in resentment, whom Subaru had left behind at the mansion.

What state had she been in after Subaru fled? Seeing Beatrice's manner just now, one could tell it was a situation too fraught even to ask about.

Watching Beatrice fall quiet, Subaru tipped his face to the sky and let the taste of his own foolishness sink in.

That moment, that place, had been the watershed.

Subaru shouldn't have run from that place. Even without the resolve to endure the pain, he should have faced Ram.

He'd missed his chance, and the opportunity to bind their hearts together was lost. And once something like that had slipped through his fingers, there was no possibility of it ever coming back.

The rift that had opened between the two of them was that deep, that vast.

"The elder of the sisters has endured everything for her younger sister's sake. And the younger sister lives for that elder sister's sake. Lose either one, and those sisters are no longer whole, I suppose."

Cutting into the silence, the girl's languid voice drifted out.

Before Subaru, who had lowered his gaze, Beatrice toyed with her own sumptuous hair.

"Lose either one, and the function is lost. Given how deranged she's become, that elder sister probably doesn't have much longer either, I suppose."

"What... what's that supposed to mean? What do you know...?"

It felt like she'd just muttered something outrageous.

Subaru pressed in on Beatrice with enough force that he nearly grabbed her, trying to press her for the true meaning of what she'd just said. But—

"Getting awfully full of yourself, aren't you—too pushy, with wandering hands and a nasty look in your eye, human."

His hand was caught, and Beatrice's body spun like a dancer's. Caught up in her whirling motion, his feet tangled, and before he knew it he'd hit the ground flat on his back. The impact forced the air out of his lungs, and Subaru groaned, eyes flying wide.

"It won't ever go back to how it was. Thinking about that, Betty supposes she has her own feelings on the matter, I suppose. Though perhaps Betty has no right to say so, I suppose."

"Acting like you've got it all figured out, all by yourself... quit playing so high and mighty. Talking all vague like you're some important character—give me a break."

Was she enjoying doling out information in dribs and drabs, savoring the way it made him swing between hope and despair? Subaru cursed her bad taste for it. But Beatrice shook her head.

"What is it you think you could possibly know, after only four days—and with someone you barely even properly met, at that? And what would you do even if you did know, I suppose? Don't go shoving your self-satisfaction onto others. You have no more business with that mansion, I suppose."

"That I don't know anything..."

He started to say it, to protest that he did know something—but the words died in Subaru's mouth.

Natsuki Subaru, as a person, was—since waking in the mansion—someone who'd shut himself in his room the whole time, a suspicious character who hadn't had a proper conversation with anyone until Rem's death that very morning.

At least, in Beatrice's eyes, that was all Subaru amounted to.

But even if he told her about the ten days he'd actually lived through, what good would it do?

Looking back over those ten days, what could Subaru really claim to know about them?

Had Subaru gained anything from those two sisters substantial enough to argue back at Beatrice, who was looking down at him with such a harsh gaze right now?

"So in the end, I was just—making a pathetic scene all on my own, without knowing anything, without understanding anything..."

—You know nothing.

There was no mistake in what Beatrice said.

Subaru knew nothing. He understood nothing. He'd squandered every chance, and washed up here with nothing but his own body to show for it.

Still sprawled spread-eagle on the ground, Subaru covered his face with his palms and stared into the darkness.

In that pitch-black field of vision, what came back to him were the handful of peaceful days he'd spent at the mansion. Those days had shattered to pieces, and Subaru's heart had shattered right along with them.

"Trace along the boundary so you don't touch the barrier, and cut through the forest that way. Once you reach the road on the far side of the mountain, it shouldn't be hard to walk the rest of the way to a town, I suppose."

Unspoken in her words was that this was as far as Beatrice intended to look after him.

In the truest sense, her Contract was nothing more than "Subaru's safety within the reach of her hand." If there was no factor there connected to her own convictions, the fact that Subaru was just a stone by the roadside to her didn't change.

Beatrice began walking around as if searching for a safe spot to descend the cliff, and Subaru, in a daze, got to his feet to follow after her back.

Leave the mansion. Forget everything. Flee somewhere far away. Whether he could actually survive, he didn't know, but this was a life he'd fought so hard to keep hold of. He'd treasure it. He'd treasure it.

"—So I'm supposed to pretend I never heard all that crying, too."

At the words that slipped from his mouth, Subaru clutched at his own chest and clenched his teeth.

He'd heard a voice full of hatred. A furious roar of resentment had chased after him. A shriek soaked in curses—that she'd kill him—had been hurled at his back.

But before all that—hadn't that girl been crying?

Something that should have already shattered was screaming inside Subaru's heart.

"Hey. I'm thinking something stupid here..."

The back of his throat trembled. His stomach clenched tight. A shiver that raised gooseflesh spread through his whole body, and Subaru, standing rooted in place, began breathing in short, ragged gasps.

He himself knew he was thinking something stupid.

It was a ridiculous idea. It made no sense whatsoever. Nothing added up, and above all, weighing the benefits against the costs, the answer was obvious.

"Even an idiot could see it without thinking. Right? Obviously."

—Are you really okay with that?

"Course I'm okay with it. Three cheers, hooray—you only live once, and all's right with the world."

He tried to shake off his hesitation and step forward.

But his feet wouldn't obey his brain's command. They stood rooted in place, forcing a dialogue on him.

Someone's question. His own question, from within.

—Are you really okay with that?

"I said, I'm okay with it, obviously..."

—What exactly is supposed to be "okay" about that?

Subaru couldn't find words to answer his own inner question. All the while, the voice kept piling on more questions, demanding an answer.

—Are you okay with never being able to have anything to do with that place again?

—Are you okay with never being able to touch those people's lives again?

—Are you okay with just leaving behind the people you left in that place?

—Are you okay with shaking off that crying girl and leaving it at that?

—Can you really give up on the future that should have been waiting there?

"—Screw that."

The odds of any payoff, weighed against the risk, were far too low.

High risk, high reward—that had to be about the least likely choice for someone living Subaru's kind of life.

To begin with, the moment a situation called for betting anything at all, you could call that defeat already, by the standards Subaru lived his life by.

Never getting deeply involved with anything, never choosing anything, just drifting along wherever the current took him.

That was how he'd lived, and that was how he'd been fine living—so why not keep on living that way, even in this world?

"After all that... yeah, after all that struggle—this is a life I only just barely managed to hang on to."

To pretend not to see the bonds he'd earned, to betray a warmth that had undeniably been real, to swat away even the mercy of heaven that had come to him in the very place he'd fled to—and to arrive, after all that, at a conclusion like this? Wouldn't that just make him too much of a clown this time around?

"No—since it doesn't even make for a funny story, a clown would be a lot better off than that."

A buffoon whose ridiculous antics couldn't even make himself laugh, let alone anyone else—what value could something like that possibly have?

Subaru didn't know, and he didn't want to know.

"That's right. This is a life I only just barely managed to hang on to. ...So."

He caught his breath, and lifted his face.

"—I'm the one who decides how to use it."

The parts of his heart that had been pulling against each other fell into alignment, and Subaru's will became one.

Just then, a cool breeze blew through, stirring his bangs, and Subaru narrowed his eyes at the ticklish sensation. Far off in the sky, the sunset was sinking, dyeing the world in shades of orange.

The sun of the fifth day, which he'd wanted so badly to see, was fading away toward its end.

Watching it go, Subaru finally remembered.

Just what it was he'd been so desperately looking forward to that sun for.

"—Get back, I suppose."

Beatrice muttered abruptly in a low voice, circling around toward Subaru.

She raised a hand to hold him back, her lovely features etched deep with wariness, eyes darting across their surroundings.

"Bea—"

"Quiet, I suppose."

His question was cut off instantly, and Subaru too let his gaze wander, searching for whatever had put her on guard.

He had no idea what Beatrice sensed, or what exactly she was wary of.

So the fact that Subaru spotted it first was purely a gift of chance.

"—Ram."

The pink-haired girl his murmur pointed to had found Subaru, and was smiling at him with a bewitching curve of her lips.

Her figure was high up in the air, at the very top of a towering great tree. Ram stood braced on a thick branch, one hand resting on the trunk to steady herself.

Her gaze locked with Subaru's, and in the instant he found himself catching his breath, something gouged into the earth, throwing up a cloud of dust around them.

"Whoa!"

Pelted by the swirling grit, Subaru instinctively threw up his arms to cover his face. When he cautiously lowered them a few seconds later, what remained before him was a rock face gouged out in a neat semicircle.

A chill of horror shot up Subaru's spine at the sheer destructive force of it—not from imagining what would happen to him if he'd been hit by it.

—It was from remembering himself actually having been hit by it that he shuddered.

"That was..."

Last time, fleeing frantically through the mountains with Rem in pursuit, there had been a blow that blew off his right leg, and finally gouged out his throat.

An invisible slash—he'd never once given thought to the true identity of whoever had unleashed it. But—

"So it was the two of them together..."

There was a sensation of something knotted up inside his chest dropping away all at once.

He knew it was a ridiculous thing to feel, but what Subaru felt in that moment was a lonely ache at knowing she, too, had given up on him—and, alongside it, an inexplicable sense of relief.

Subaru tilted his head at himself, puzzled that he'd felt relief at all.

That, of all things—the out-of-place sentiment that those two really were only whole together.

"We've been found, I suppose. —That girl's clairvoyance is a nuisance, I suppose."

"Clairvoyance, huh..."

An ability with a grandiose name like that was right up Subaru's alley.

Just as Beatrice's grave tone had suggested, what followed was swift. Ram crouched down, then slid smoothly down the trunk of the great tree.

Landing with considerable force, she carried that momentum straight into a sprint without losing any of it, and closed the distance to the ledge partway down the cliff where Subaru and Beatrice stood in a single bound—arrival.

"Finally found you. Though I didn't expect Beatrice-sama to still be with you."

Ram declared this with her eyes gleaming, as though standing before a hated enemy.

Seeing her young features twist with hatred, a wave of anguish swept through Subaru's chest.

She still wore her usual maid uniform, but where it was normally worn with meticulous care, it was now disheveled beyond recognition.

Her skirt, snagged here and there, was dotted with holes; the white headdress she usually wore had vanished somewhere; and her pink hair, which should have been neatly kept, had come loose and wild, losing all its elegance.

—Dressing each other, doing each other's hair—the two of them had always done that for one another.

Even Subaru knew that. He remembered them mentioning it once, at some point.

There were plenty of other things he knew, too.

"Get back, I suppose. By the Contract, Betty protects this human. Even if the one facing her is you, she won't hold back, I suppose."

"Please step aside yourself, Beatrice-sama. If anything, Ram can't afford to hold back, not when it's Beatrice-sama standing in the way."

"What an amusing joke, I suppose. Did you just say 'hold back,' against Betty, I suppose?"

"You seem to have forgotten, Beatrice-sama, that we are not inside the mansion right now. Far from the Forbidden Archives, and in the middle of a forest—under these conditions, are you confident you can protect that gentleman from Ram?"

Before the silent Subaru, the two girls' fierce standoff continued.

Beatrice's reaction—narrowing her eyes in evident chagrin—made it clear Ram's hard-line stance was no bluff.

Beatrice's strength had its limits, and it was likely true that Ram held the advantage under these conditions. Even so, Beatrice refused to move from in front of Subaru.

Subaru, still standing behind her,

"Booiiing—"

grabbed two of her sumptuous ringlets, one in each hand, and stretched them out as far as they'd go.

He let go. Her hair sprang back with all its considerable volume, bouncing. Bouncing, bouncing.

"Yeah, that's a pretty solid feeling."

"Wh-wh-wh-what..."

Eyes wide, lips trembling, Beatrice whirled around, shaking.

Subaru tilted his head at her with a "Hm?" And then—

"Wh-what do you think you're doing, I suppose!? Do you want to die, in a situation like this, I suppose!?"

"Don't be stupid, I don't want to die one bit. Seriously, dying is something you should only do once, at the very end of your life. I mean that."

As he said this, he pulled Beatrice back by the shoulder and stepped out in front of her as she stumbled.

Standing before him was Ram, staring back at him with a stunned expression. She raised her guard even higher against Subaru stepping forward, biting down on her lip as though to steel herself.

"You've got some nerve. Does that mean you've finally resigned yourself to it?"

"Resigned isn't quite it. More like... I'd say I've made up my mind."

"—To what."

Not understanding Subaru's intent, Ram scowled.

Subaru pressed his palms together and bowed deeply to her.

"I'm sorry. Because I chickened out, I made you grieve a whole lot."

"—! So you do know something about Rem, after all!"

"No, sorry, but I genuinely don't know anything about that. Honestly, there's a whole lot I don't understand. But—"

He cut himself off, took a single breath, and then said,

"I decided I want to start learning about all the stuff I don't understand."

"Now, of all times!? What are you talking about!"

At Subaru's declaration of resolve, Ram, unable to see it as anything but nonsense, snarled. She stamped her foot down as though in a fit of frustration.

"Rem is already dead! There's no taking that back now! Even if you understand something now, of all times, what could you possibly do about it!?"

"I can't say anything cool like 'I can do something.' This mess right here is the result of me not being able to do anything. I know better than anyone that I've got zero credibility."

It wasn't that he was being defiant about it. Even now, nothing but regret stabbed at his chest.

He was sick of his own stupidity, and if a person could die of shame alone, he might already be dead.

And yet, behaving pathetically, struggling to survive pathetically, exposing every hopeless bit of his own disgrace—this was the place he'd arrived at.

And this was the conclusion he'd reached.

"What could you possibly understand about Ram, and about Rem!?"

"I don't understand anything, because I never even tried to. I still don't know a single thing about the parts that actually matter. But—"

For ten days, Subaru had walked alongside them.

They didn't know that, and even if he told them, they wouldn't be able to understand it.

But Subaru remembered those ten days, without question.

Even if they themselves had forgotten, Subaru's Soul remembered the things he'd seen with them, the times he'd laughed with them, the time he'd spent with them.

It wasn't that he knew nothing. Subaru knew them.

And those two—

"There's something you two don't know either."

"What..."

"That I—love you two—! That's what I mean!"

The elder sister, blunt as she was, who couldn't help meddling and fussing over him.

The younger sister, polite to the point of being insolent, and sarcastic underneath it all.

In the days he'd spent with them, Subaru had come to treasure every one of those hours.

Even with the memory of having been killed by their own hands still lingering, both were memories he couldn't bear to forget.

So much so that, if it meant he could share that time again, he found himself thinking he wouldn't mind choosing "that" all over again.

At Subaru's outcry, Ram simply froze, eyes wide with shock.

Of course she did. From her perspective, Subaru's declaration was nothing but incomprehensible ravings—just one more piece of nonsense among tens of thousands spouted by a crazed lunatic.

So the decision to cut him down was made in an instant.

Her thoughts halted, and the moment the stiffness left her body, Ram's hostility was set into motion.

But even a single instant of stillness was still a delay.

"—!"

Subaru broke into a run a fraction of a second before her instant decision could be carried out.

Turning his back on Ram, passing by Beatrice's side, Subaru's body carried at full speed—toward the cliff.

"Wait—!"

Behind him, a girl's voice rang out, high-pitched like a scream.

Which of the two girls it belonged to didn't register in Subaru's mind as he ran.

His thoughts were in chaos. He'd thought he'd made up his mind.

But his heartbeat betrayed his resolve, sending pain shooting through his whole body, and his hands and feet felt as heavy as if they'd been stuffed with lead.

Even though he was running at full speed, the world seemed to move in endless slow motion, as though trying to buy even one more second to make Subaru change his mind.

It was ridiculous. Even now, he was hesitating just like that.

If he thought about it, it made no sense. He'd struggled so hard, clung to life so desperately.

Even when he'd thought he wanted to die, in the end he'd never been able to resist his own survival instinct.

And yet here he was now, doing this.

"...Never got the chance to thank Beatrice."

He said aloud the one lingering regret that occurred to him—and with that, left everything else behind.

The cliff loomed closer. How many steps left—counting was too terrifying. His resolve nearly broke. It felt like it already had. This wasn't sane. This wasn't rational. An urge to burst out laughing welled up—and yet he couldn't laugh at all. There was no way he could laugh.

Even if he'd lived on as he was, he'd only have gone on living like the dead.

If he gave up on the future waiting in that place, that was, to Subaru, the same as dying.

If he was only going to live this reclaimed life like a dead man anyway, what was wrong with spending it however he pleased?

The cliff was right in front of him now. The drop was more than enough. Down below, an earlier guest was waiting impatiently for Subaru. He'd kept her waiting a good long while. And sorry, but he wasn't going to end up in the same place as her.

"Guess a good guy like me's bound for heaven when he dies."

His feet left the ground. They clawed at empty air. They touched nothing, reached nothing.

His body swayed. His posture collapsed. Up or down, he couldn't keep his balance.

It was fast. The wind was fierce. His eyes stung. His head hurt. The ringing in his ears sounded far away. It felt like he'd left his heart behind somewhere. He couldn't hear it pounding—only an ominous bell tolling.

If dying was how it ended, then that was that.

It wouldn't be any different from living like the dead anyway.

But if—if, hypothetically, he could go back—

—Because she had screamed, "I will absolutely kill you."

Then he would—

"—I will absolutely save you."

The instant he voiced that resolve, his head slammed into the hard ground.

A tremendous shattering sound rang out—and then, nothing more could be heard.

Not even the voice of resentment could catch up to him now. Nothing more—
