Arc 6 Chapter 27: "The Wall of Electra"

—A woman. There was a woman.

She was a woman like any other—an ordinary village girl, unremarkable in birth and upbringing. Loved by her father and mother, loved by her siblings, and loving her family in turn, she was an utterly ordinary woman. She had a betrothed chosen by her family, and like any of the countless village girls before her, she was meant to live out a life already decided, tucked away in a corner of some cold, poor village. That was the life laid out for her—a paved road with no room for doubt.

That ordinary life was shattered by a vile man of power who came to her village. Whether it was something about her that drew him, or merely the vice of coveting what belonged to others, she never knew—but the man of power wanted her, and forced his want upon her.

Even in a poor village of a small country, the gulf between commoner and the powerful could not be erased. She had no way to refuse him. Against the injustice of her fate, all she could do was submit.

But the woman was loved—by her family, by her betrothed, by the people of the villages around.

Unable to bear the tyrant's outrage any longer, the people's anger caught fire, and in time it birthed the flames of war. The blaze spread; the villages became an army; and at last the man of power was burned to ash along with his manor.

In the span of a single night, the woman's place in the world changed utterly. From an ordinary village girl to the betrothed of the man who now led an army raised in her name.

Still the flames raged on unspent, and those around them, wary of the danger, sent troops against them one after another. Every single one was turned back, routed by people who rose up, fired by love for the woman. The fires of war spread in the blink of an eye, consuming the small nation, then the nations around it, and at last a great power besides.

Word spread of the woman said to be the spark of it all, and people began to whisper of her as a heavenly beauty. Fantasy piled upon fantasy, and beneath the swelling weight of attention and expectation, her slender body seemed ready to be crushed.

No one noticed. Not her family, not her betrothed, not the people—no one was truly looking at the woman herself. If she waved, cheers rose for her. If she walked the road, crowds gathered for her. If she spoke, tears of joy fell for her.

"This... this is wrong. It's... a mistake..."

She covered her face and denied it. It couldn't be. It shouldn't be. She was only a village girl—not a heavenly beauty, not some ruinous temptress who topples nations, just a common village girl. Where was there any such worth in her?

The people were drunk on a dream. Led astray by fantasy, dancing to the tune of an ideal.

In time, unchecked, the flames that had begun with her consumed even the great power itself. Amid the ruin of that fallen power, atop the castle they had seized, her betrothed knelt before her. And he spoke:

"—I love you. This victory, the smiles of the people, all of it—you gave us all of it."

The capital burned. The dead piled high. The people trembled with a mad ecstasy. The villagers she had once lived among, the family who had loved her, the betrothed who had wished for her happiness— —none of them were there anymore. Not one. Nowhere.

—The woman left without answering her betrothed. She abandoned her family, her fellow villagers, everything.

She had never given any of it. Had never wanted any of it. The woman had simply lost everything, that was all. Having lost the days of being loved that had surely once been real, the woman walked on alone across a scorched wasteland.

And still, the dream, the fantasy, the ideal would not let her go. Everywhere she went, everyone loved her, devoted themselves to her, desired her—and everything was destroyed. Everyone loved the woman. Like a curse. Without ever knowing the true love that lay within her.

The Witch of Lust, seeking the love she had lost, would go on being loved by a love that only ever met with ruin.

—The blade-intent unleashed without restraint stabs into Subaru and the others challenging "Electra."

The man wore a crimson kimono, both sleeves shrugged off his shoulders, baring an upper body wrapped in sarashi. The Stick-Swinger, his eyes lit with a maddened gleam, hurled a declaration of despair their way.

The second layer's Trial—its condition being to win the Stick-Swinger's, the proctor's, recognition of their qualification to advance—had already claimed two casualties, felled by the man's absurd, common-sense-defying skill. Even under such conditions, Emilia had somehow managed to wrest out a victory, wielding her natural boldness, a bit of pleading, and a combat style built on sheer overwhelming volume—but even so—

"—Wasn't the condition supposed to be that just one of us clearing it was enough!?"

"Ahn? Who the hell said that, you? Don't go making shit up, you. Why the hell would one of you clearing it mean the rest of you clear it too? Use your damn head, your head! Or is your brain made of fish fry, you?"

"T-to get lectured on common sense by the last person in the world who should be talking about it...!"

The man clacked the chopsticks he held one in each hand together, crossing them idly; at his infuriatingly valid point, Subaru groaned.

In truth, it was they who had jumped to conclusions about the clear conditions of the Trial. The third layer's Trial had been a puzzle, and the moment it was solved, the very structure of the room had changed—which had only spurred that misunderstanding along. They'd naturally assumed that the second layer's Trial worked the same way: that once one person cleared it, a new archive would open up. With that premise collapsed, and each of them now apparently required to clear it individually, breaking through this Trial looked all but hopeless.

They had just witnessed, firsthand, how brutally difficult the condition Emilia had drawn out of him—"make the man move even a single step"—had turned out to be, despite how simple it sounded.

To put it plainly, Emilia currently ranked alongside Julius as the strongest fighting force among those challenging the Pleiades Watchtower—and with Julius's contract with his quasi-spirit severed, she was, without argument, the strongest of them all. And it was that Emilia they had sent to break through in the very first match, when the man's guard was at its most careless.

In other words, they had thrown their strongest vanguard at their enemy in his weakest, most weakened state. If a single victory had been all that was needed, that would be one thing—but if this was a survival gauntlet requiring individual wins, then from here on they'd have to throw weaker fighters at a man who would no longer have any openings to exploit.

Making the man move one step—the victory Emilia had barely clawed out after such an onslaught of attacks and no small amount of luck—could the rest of them possibly seize that same win?

"—Hold on, I suppose. There's a fatal flaw in your argument, I'd say."

"A flaw?"

Subaru, shaken, was groping for some thread of victory within their disadvantageous situation. Right beside him, Beatrice, gripping his hand hard as if to tell him to calm down, threw those words at the man.

At that, the man scowled, fixing Beatrice in his one good eye.

"The hell, you. Ten years too early for you, you. At least five more years, you. Come back once your arms and legs and height've stretched out proper and your chest and ass have filled in nice, you."

"...I never intended to indulge your nonsense to begin with, but now I've lost every last trace of the inclination, I suppose. So I'll just come straight at you, I think."

"Straight at me with what?"

"Isn't it obvious, I suppose. Emilia told you that if you moved even a single step, it would be 'our' victory, I believe. Which means Emilia's win was a win for all of Betty and the others too!"

"—!"

At Beatrice's point, Subaru caught his breath and looked over at Emilia without thinking. Could that bold negotiation of hers really have concealed such an intention all along? He marveled at the hidden femme fatale lurking within Emilia. But under his gaze, Emilia only pressed a hand to her mouth and said, "Ah." No—it wasn't that at all. It was just her being herself. It was E.M.T.

"Oh, right, now that you mention it, I did say that! I said 'we'! So? Doesn't that mean all of us together got past your Trial?"

"That's just a manner of speaking, you. Doesn't count."

"I see... all right. Subaru, Beatrice, I'm sorry. He says it's no good..."

"You're giving up far too quickly, I suppose!!"

Beatrice shouted at Emilia, who slumped her shoulders and backed down with a solemn face. Still, thinking it over calmly, Subaru had to admit it had been a rather strained bit of wordplay to begin with. Though that was also a measure of just how hopeless the height of this wall had made them, that they'd wanted to cling to such a slim possibility at all.

"Well, I get where the brat's coming from, more or less. Way it sounds, at first it was just 'however many of you it takes, come at me and get past me.' —But playing along with what I'm told's boring, so I forced myself awake for it."

"Forced yourself awake... you mean back at the start, that was you breaking the system!?"

"Beats me, you. Use words I can actually understand, you. Quit spouting stuff that sounds like some early-gray-haired scholar, you."

The man's mood flipped between good humor and foul in the blink of an eye. Nodding along noncommittally to his rambling, Subaru pieced together a theory about the man's true nature—the system underlying this Trial.

The third and second layer Trials, the man's seemingly deranged words right after he'd appeared, and everything he'd said and done since. Piecing together the wrongness glimpsed in the fragments of all that led to a single understanding. In short: there was a fatal discrepancy between the Trial as it was meant to be and the Trial as it currently stood.

"So really, this was supposed to be a Trial where everyone works together to meet the condition—but because you 'woke up,' now each of us has to meet it individually?"

"Hah! What d'you know, you get it after all, you. But which way'd have been easier, who's to say? Whether it's easier to kill me with everybody piling on at once, or to let me grope a tit and then land one punch, ain't like anyone can kno—whoa!"

"—Watch your damn mouth, you harassing bastard. I'm quietly losing it over here."

That careless remark was met by a whip-strike laden with fury. Subaru drew the whip from behind his waist and snapped it forward with nothing but a flick of his forearm, prioritizing speed. But the man caught the whip-strike—one that should have approached the speed of sound—effortlessly between his chopsticks, and gave it a little shake. The odds of landing it had been low to begin with, but the whip's surprise attack had failed regardless—

"Hah! Didn't figure you for the stick-swinging type, but a whip, of all things, you? What kind of taste is that, you? Save the whip-lashing for your enemies and your own woman, you."

"You are the enemy, aren't you!? And besides, I plan on taking things properly step by step with Emilia, and there's no version of that future where a whip's on the table!"

"Subaru, Subaru, calm down, I suppose. You're saying things that don't make any sense, I think. And besides, you're getting swept up in his pace!"

"That's right, Subaru! Don't get so angry! He only touched my chest, that's all—nothing strange happened."

"That IS the strange thing, Emilia-tan!" "Normally that's the part where you get mad, Hot Stuff."

Subaru and the man both jumped in at the exact same moment to correct Emilia's attempt to calm things down. At their perfectly synchronized retort, Emilia blinked in surprise, and Beatrice let out a long, deep sigh.

And then—

"—Um, is now okay?"

Amid an exchange between the four of them that seemed to have had all the venom drained out of it, the one person who had kept silent through it all finally let their voice tremble out, timed to perfection.

"...I don't want to say this, buuut, I think we should turn back."

The one who spoke up, raising a small hand, was a girl with deep blue hair worn in braids—Meili. With the unconscious Shaula's head still resting on her lap, she slowly shook her head from side to side. Her yellow-green eyes, reflecting the man standing further off, held a clear and unmistakable fear.

"I don't get how you can even talk normally with that man, mister. ...The knight mister got beaten, and so did the scarf lady, and even the naked lady..."

"Shaula's out cold for an entirely different reason, but... yeah, it is strange."

Meili's faint-hearted opinion was, when weighed against their current fighting strength, an entirely reasonable judgment. If anything, it was Subaru—still wanting to stay and press on with the challenge—who hadn't managed to stay fully calm. He was completely intoxicated by the Stick-Swinger's blade-intent. How much of that owed to the sexual harassment done to Emilia, or to Julius and Anastasia both being struck down, he couldn't judge objectively—but even so—

"—Hypothetically, if we pulled back and came at this again later, would you allow that?"

"——"

The man had shown a certain fair-mindedness in recognizing Emilia's clear—though it could just as well be that he'd simply painted himself into a corner—but having shown that much didn't mean he'd be reasonable about anything else. Even to act on Meili's sound judgment, if the man didn't agree to it, combat would be unavoidable. Until now, he'd been holding back, sparing their lives. But now that he'd behaved as if shifting gears entirely, there was no guarantee their lives would be spared on any next attempt either. If it came to that, challenging him wouldn't even be an option. They'd need everything they had just to retreat.

Without exaggeration, the Stick-Swinger's strength had reached a catastrophic level. He'd defeated Julius with nothing but two sticks, traded blows with Emilia, and still carried himself with room to spare. Without exaggeration, that strength was on par with Reinhard's. Just what was the administrator of this tower thinking, putting a monster like this here? Between that and the constellation-themed puzzle on the third layer, the whole thing seemed designed with no intention of ever letting anyone clear it.

"So? Which is it?"

Inch by inch, Subaru shifted his heels back toward the fallen Julius and Anastasia. Noticing his movement, Beatrice and Emilia subtly repositioned themselves too. If the Stick-Swinger were to bare his fangs, their only option would be for Emilia to unleash Icicle Line again the instant it happened, while Beatrice cast Muraku over the three fallen and they made a run for it. In the worst case, they were even weighing whether to unleash the still-unfinished third stage of Subaru and Beatrice's trump card—

"—Forget it."

"Huh?"

"FOR-GET-IT! Forget it forget it forget it forget it, forget forget forget! I'm not into it anymore!"

The man flung those words abruptly at Subaru and the others, who had already braced body and soul for a full-force retreat. At his childish outburst, they stared wide-eyed as he slid his arm back through his loosened left sleeve, pulling his kimono back into its original state. Then, scowling, he strode off—away from Subaru and the others, still on guard—toward the Trial Sword, pitifully kicked off to one side. He stepped on the fallen blade, flicked it up, and caught it with ease. Then, with a sharp thrust, he drove its tip back into the white floor, restoring it to exactly the state it had been in before the Trial began. And then—

"We're closing up shop. Go home, you lot. I'm bored of this. Not doing it anymore."

He plopped himself down right where he stood, one knee raised, and spat the words out.

"——W-wait, wait, wait! That's way too damn arbitrary, what is that!? You're just deciding everything about the Trial based on your own mood!?"

"Shut it, you. The say over this place's been left up to me from the start, you. And if I say I ain't doin' it, then I ain't doin' it."

At such outrageous, high-handed words, Subaru found himself at a loss. At his shock, the man went on, "And another thing—"

"—When I've got no motivation, I don't play around. You up for that, you?"

"——"

A chill swept over Subaru's whole body, like a gust of wind out of nowhere. The man, having tucked even his chopsticks away into his robe and cast off every last weapon, twisted the corner of his mouth. It was unmistakably a smile—but of a wholly different quality than any he'd shown before. It wasn't the ferocious yet sunny grin from before. It was the smile of a monstrous beast, wrapped in a pitch-black, blood-reeking, murderous intent.

"...Ah."

A small, strangled sound reached his ears. It hadn't come from Subaru—he looked over to find it had come from Emilia, beside him. She pressed a hand to her own pale throat, her jewel-like eyes wide with shock. Her knees gave out and she slumped to the floor—as if only in that very moment realizing she could no longer stand, that she had forgotten to breathe—

"H—"

And it was the same for Subaru, who only "remembered" to breathe himself thanks to Emilia's reaction. Before he knew it, he was on his knees, choking for air, his whole body drenched in a torrent of sweat. Just like Emilia—no, even more than her—he had been overwhelmed by the man's blade-intent. He'd all but forgotten the beat of his own heart. It would be no exaggeration to say he'd very nearly been killed.

Had there been no one nearby to remind him, he'd have died right then and there, killed by nothing but a glare.

"Better rack your brains and find yourself a way to win, you. Same trick Hot Stuff pulled ain't gonna work again—not unless you've got another sleeping horndog of a woman lying around somewhere. Get lost, I'm sleeping."

With no playfulness left in that low voice, he said only that much, and his head dropped. Wait a while, and gradually what could be heard was the man's snoring—loud even in his sleep. In a sense, it was a snore that betrayed his character not one bit—but not one person left in that room had the composure to laugh at it.

"Let's go baaack, quick."

To leave this place even a moment sooner. Meili gave voice to that instinct without resisting it, and her words became the trigger. Slowly, carrying their wounded, Subaru and the others found themselves with no choice but to retreat from the Trial.

"—I see. So that's the reason you all came crawling back from the second Trial with your tails between your legs."

"...Harsh, big sis."

"Stop that, that way of talking. You'll start making the theory that you're that girl's master over there sound plausible."

"Yeah, that'd be bad. I'll watch it."

At the less-than-comforting warning, he shrugged weakly, and Ram let out a small sigh in response. The setting was the fourth layer, a room adjoining the green room. Facing each other there were Subaru and Ram, joined by Emilia, Beatrice, and Meili—which, counting Shaula lying sprawled off to the side, made six in all.

—Having descended the long, long staircase and fled back from the second layer, the group had first carried their wounded into the green room and entrusted them to the spirit's care. By their assessment along the way, Julius had simply been knocked unconscious, and Anastasia's condition was down to exhaustion from overusing magic—neither, they were told, was in any danger of dying. So they'd left them in bed, and—both to respect the room's occupancy limit and to explain the situation—had brought Ram along and moved to another room. But—

"From what I hear, there was some absurd proctor waiting for you... but Lady Emilia alone met his approval, did she not? Couldn't she have gone to see the archive by herself, at least?"

"That's..."

"Oh, huh, I didn't even think of that. Right, if it's just me, maybe I could've gone up to the next layer... should I go ask Mr. Stick-Swinger if that's possible...?"

"...No, let's not. I don't want to poke the bear while he's in a bad mood and stir up trouble we don't need, and even if he said only Emilia-tan could go up—that's, y'know, dangerous."

"I'll be reeeally careful, though?"

"Dangerous." "Dangerous, indeed." "Dangerous, I suppose."

Subaru, Ram, and Beatrice all threw cold water on Emilia's resolve at once. Emilia backed down, thoroughly deflated by the sheer weight of their worry—but it wasn't out of mere overprotectiveness that they'd crushed her enthusiasm.

As things stood, none of them wanted to carelessly provoke the Stick-Swinger. That much was an undeniable fact. Truth be told, Subaru had plenty he wanted to say about that man's self-indulgent behavior, but setting aside when the Trial would even resume, challenging him while he was in a foul mood was nothing short of suicide. In fact, none of them had even seen the man hold a sword yet.

And even if Emilia alone could slip past the man—

"I'm honestly suspicious that the second layer's Trial is really just the one thing. It went way too easy... well, maybe it only looks that way because Emilia-tan blew through it so smoothly, but I've got a bad feeling that can't really be all there is to it."

"I think that's overthinking it, honestly. That man... he's rough, and some of what he says is weird, but he doesn't seem like he'd lie. No, I mean—he seemed like the kind of person who couldn't lie."

"Whether that inability to lie comes from principle or just plain lack of brains changes how I'd rate him for it..."

In any case, he had no grounds to dismiss Emilia's point. If anything, when it came to sizing up the Stick-Swinger's character, Subaru mostly agreed with her. The Trial that looked so easily cleared could, put another way, be read as something only their strongest fighter, Emilia, had managed to pull off at all. After all, Julius had been struck down—

"Hope he doesn't get too torn up about it..."

"Are you worried about Julius?"

"I dunno. Worried, yeah, I guess I'm worried... but it's not that simple, either."

The image of Julius, utterly defeated and struck down in a head-on clash with the Stick-Swinger, was burned into Subaru's memory. Recalling the look on Julius's face in that final moment, Subaru's unease couldn't be called overthinking.

His swordsmanship hadn't reached the man at all; he'd been toyed with like a child at play, and in the end even his knight's sword had been snapped—

"There's a spare sword back at the dragon carriage, but that's not really the issue, is it."

"A sword can just be reforged. Betty doesn't understand that kind of attachment, I suppose."

"C'mon, Beako, you treasure the handkerchiefs and mittens and aprons I made you, right? It's like that, but if they got torn up—a deluxe version of that."

"...Sorry for saying something so thickheaded, I suppose."

Beatrice reflected honestly on her careless words, and Subaru stroked her head and let out a breath. He couldn't imagine how Julius would react once he woke in the green room. Whether he'd sink into an uncharacteristic depression, or put on his usual composed front regardless, Subaru had no idea what to even say to him either way, and the thought weighed heavy.

And if there was something else nagging at him, it wasn't limited to Julius alone.

"Anastasia... Echidna's, that desperation of hers—what was that, exactly..."

What clung most stubbornly to the back of Subaru's mind was the decision made by Anastasia—Eridna—who had cut into the one-on-one duel between Julius and the Stick-Swinger to lend her support. There was nothing strange, in that moment, about a hand reaching out to support Julius. Watching him fall into such a losing position, wanting somehow to lend him aid—that must have been how everyone present felt, Subaru included.

But if it came down to it, Echidna—who occupied a bystander's position, acting only by borrowing Anastasia's body—doing what she did went beyond surprising. It deserved outright astonishment.

Throughout their journey up to this point, and even after arriving at the Watchtower, Echidna had never once forgotten to conduct herself as Anastasia, and had declared she would act with the utmost consideration for that body. At least as far as Subaru had been able to see, she hadn't broken that word. So what meaning could there be behind her suddenly, at this point, taking action like that—

"That girl's exhaustion is a different order of serious than simply overusing a bit of magic, I suppose."

"—How do you mean?"

To Subaru, lost in thought, Beatrice—the only one privy to Anastasia and Echidna's current state—leaned in and murmured quietly. To Subaru's question, Beatrice continued with, "In other words—"

"Puck, Betty, and that scarf spirit too—we're all special, even among spirits. Each of us is stronger than your average spirit, but in exchange, there's a bit of a restriction attached, I suppose. Betty shouldn't need to explain her own case to you at this point."

"Right. You're the possessive type, so now that I've contracted with you, I can't contract with any other spirit. I'm all yours, Beako. You can rest easy."

"I—I only find that a little bit pleasing, I suppose. Anyway, moving on—that scarf spirit. It's true she's borrowing that girl's body, but... traveling with them this far, I've come to understand something, I think. That girl has no magic she can draw on besides Od."

"Nothing besides Od?"

"Same as you, Subaru, there's a flaw in her body's Gate, I suppose. The valve's broken, and the function that draws in mana from outside is dead. So unless she carves away at her own life, she can't use magic at all, I think."

"That's..."

Isn't that fatal? Subaru caught his breath. The problem harbored within Anastasia's body left Subaru astonished at the sheer fact of it. In this world, it was an extraordinarily heavy handicap to bear. He remembered Anastasia—Eridna—saying that her trump card would cost her, carved from her very self. That had been literal. Truly, word-for-word literal. And now that the truth of it had come to light, it only left Subaru more bewildered still.

If, as a matter of fact, Echidna working Anastasia's body to the bone amounted to nothing less than shortening the life of her host—

"—then why would she go that far to try and save Julius?"

It hadn't looked like some calculating move, some scheme with an angle to it. What lay beneath that desperation had unmistakably been the earnestness of someone worried for Julius's safety. Had Echidna truly come to feel that for Julius? Was that, too, the work of his Divine Protection of Gathering Spirits?

"—I'm worried about Lady Anastasia and Sir Julius too, but there's another matter we ought to press further into first."

"The Stick-Swinger, you mean."

"Yes. It may sound heartless, but to Ram, what becomes of the Trial matters more. —If we can't get past it, we'll never reach the means to bring Rem back."

It was Ram who cut into Subaru's train of thought, voicing an opinion that was, in a sense, coldly pragmatic. Her words, by her own admission, lacked a bit of tact. But Subaru couldn't bring himself to blame her for it.

"——"

Behind Ram's stiff expression—one that didn't look it—he could sense a faint, simmering impatience. He could guess at it: the frustration of having her fingers on the very edge of a chance to bring back her lost sister, and yet unable to quite reach it.

"A loose kimono, one eye, red hair, blue eyes... quite the assertive build and getup, though."

"Does anyone come to mind? Might be hard to picture without seeing him in person, but he's strong like a straight-up monster, no exaggeration. Could be Reinhard-tier."

"A nightmare, then."

"But Subaru's not lying about it. I've never seen Reinhard fight seriously, but... mm, I think he really was that strong."

Ram's tone had carried a note of disbelief, and Emilia backed Subaru up. Coming from Emilia, who had actually exchanged blows with the man herself, saying so much meant something. There was no reason for her to lie about it, either. Reaching that conclusion, Ram put a weary hand to her brow.

"If we're to believe Barusu and Lady Emilia's words, then this is an enemy on par with Sir Reinhard... and those said to stand shoulder to shoulder with the strongest on this earth number, at most, one per nation in this day and age."

"So Reinhard's the strongest in the kingdom, and each of the other three nations has its own strongest, huh."

"The Vollachian Empire's First General, the 'Azure Thunderclap' Cecilus Segmunt; the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko's 'Mad Prince'; and the Kararagi City-States' 'Admirer' Halibel. None of their descriptions match, though."

"None of them with long red hair?"

"We just don't know for the Holy Kingdom's 'Mad Prince'—no details on his appearance are known."

"Prince, huh, a prince... he didn't really seem like one, did he?"

Granted, if you went by the "mad" part alone, Subaru couldn't say for certain it wasn't him—but though the man had had a beautiful face, there'd been nothing of royal refinement about him. That had been a beauty of the wilds—the kind of art piece permitted only because it belonged out in the wasteland.

"Then he'd have to be some martial artist unknown to the world at large..."

"His clothes were Kararagi's traditional dress, I suppose. And he handled those chopsticks like a master too, I think."

"That's so far from their normal use, I'm not sure 'mastered' is even the right word..."

Besides, no matter how hard he tried, Subaru couldn't reconcile that man with the image of some "martial artist unknown to the world." The man's age looked a bit above Subaru's own—mid-twenties, probably—but that someone of such strength, and such an overwhelmingly vivid personality, could go unknown all the way to that age struck him first and foremost as simply wrong.

And there was another thing. The question of whether a man built into the strange workings of the Pleiades Watchtower could really be an ordinary human at all had been nagging at him this whole time—

"Oh, hey, misters, got a sec?"

"Hm?"

"The naked lady looks like she's about to wake up, ya knooow?"

In the corner of the room, Meili, who had been devotedly lending her lap to Shaula as a pillow, raised her hand. Just as she'd said, Shaula, still resting her head on Meili's lap, began writhing about with an oddly sultry wriggle, groaning things like "Mmmnn, ahhnn." And then, under everyone's watchful eyes, her eyelids slowly slid open—

"Master... don't leave me alone... I don't wanna be lonely anymore-ssu..."

"Don't say something that tugs at the heartstrings right out of the gate! You're actually awake, aren't you!?"

"Aw, maaan-ssu. Thought if I said something pitiful, Master would get all soft on me, but you're a meanie-ssu. But hey, I love even that about you, Master-ssu."

"So much for worrying..."

With a swing of her long legs up and back down, Shaula sprang lightly to her feet. Swaying her long, tied-back hair—what she called her Scorpion Tail—she glanced around the room, then tilted her head. "Huh?"

"Huh? Why're we here-ssu? I'm pretty sure we cleared the Trial thanks to Master's dramatic flash of insight, and then we headed up..."

"Ah, that part wasn't a dream. That really happened."

"And then Master hugged me and smiled and said he'd never let go again..."

"That part WAS a dream! You fainted the second the second Trial started!"

Subaru shouted down Shaula as she recounted her horrifying dream, trying to jog her memory of what happened right before she fainted. But Shaula only snorted derisively at the word. "Faaainted?"

"There's no way I'd do something so unseemly as fainting-ssu. Even reuniting with Master after hundreds of years apart, I didn't go and faint-ssu. Me, fainting? That's so ridiculous my belly-button's boiling tea-ssu!"

"No, I get wanting to doubt it, but you really did faint. Subaru and Meili were both reeeally worried. Believe her, okay?"

"Wha—! Master, worried about me!? Heheheh, okay, I believe it-ssu!"

"Cheap..." "Me being treated like an afterthought feels reeeally unfair somehow."

At Shaula's slack-jawed face and doting about-face, Subaru and Meili wore matching complicated expressions. Still, once she'd accepted the fact, Shaula tilted her head the other way this time. "Wait, huh?"

"But what happened that made me faint-ssu? Me going down like that isn't normal-ssu. In a situation like that, everyone but Master should've been wiped out, no question-ssu..."

"I understand you place excessive faith in Barusu—no, in your Master—but that's how it happened, apparently. Try to remember slowly. ...There's a long, long staircase before you."

"A long, long staircase..."

Meaning to guide the memory back to the surface, Ram spoke on in a quiet voice, almost like hypnotherapy. There should have been the awkward problem that Ram herself hadn't witnessed any of it firsthand—but she unraveled Shaula's sealed memories slowly, as if she'd seen it all with her own eyes.

"What greets you is a white room, a steel sword driven into the floor. The instant you take it in hand, a strange voice echoes through the heart of everyone present—"

"Thump-thump..."

Ram's oddly emotive storytelling had Shaula and Emilia both thoroughly swept up in it. Shaula was one thing, but Emilia surely already knew exactly what had happened down to the smallest detail—yet Subaru, afraid of breaking the flow, kept quiet about it. And then the tale drew close to Shaula's sealed memory—

"At that moment, a figure appears at the back of the room. A man with long red hair and blue eyes, dressed in the garb of a foreign land..."

"Hyaaaaaaah!!"

The instant the story reached the crucial scene, Shaula screamed and leapt backward. She came flying straight at Subaru as if to tackle him, but he'd braced for it this time, dropping his weight and catching her whole body head-on, solid. He wouldn't go toppling over this time. Instead, he found himself clamped in place by Shaula's soft skin as if in a vise.

"Ow ow ow ow! D-did you remember!? You remembered, didn't you!?"

"W-w-w-why is that guy here-ssu!? You told me he was dead, Master! But he's alive-ssu! I knew it, he's one of those guys who won't die even if you kill him-ssu!"

"Huh!? What are you even..."

Eyes watering from the pain, Subaru tried to ask what on earth Shaula was talking about—and then, he realized. The meaning behind Shaula's words. Matching exactly what she'd described, there was only one person, among everyone they'd encountered since coming to this tower, who fit. It was—

"The Stick-Swinger! The Stick-Swinger, Reid-ssu! That fiend! That devil! He came back to life just to grope my chest again-ssuuuu!"

—Reid Astrea.

It was the name of a swordsman who had left his mark on legend. A great swordsman said to have cut down Witchbeasts, cut down master swordsmen, cut down a Dragon, and at last, cut down a Witch herself.

He was the first ever to be granted the title of Sword Saint, and one of the Three Great Heroes who had saved the world. He was the origin of House Astrea's glory, the lineage of Sword Saints that included Reinhard van Astrea, and remained, even now, the ultimate object of longing for all who lived by the sword.

It was hard to believe. That was the name of a life that should have been lost four hundred years ago. Had this not been a tower that had existed for centuries, built by the hand of one connected to a Witch, such a possibility would have been laughed off outright.

But here, there was a living witness who knew that era four hundred years past. And here, too, was a tower built by a Sage who had lived through those same four hundred years.

Given how ill-natured she'd been, wasn't it exactly the sort of thing she'd say—setting the very first Sword Saint as the "strongest guardian" and daring anyone to surpass him?

With that fact as their harvest, Subaru and the others hurried back to the green room. Once they knew their opponent was Reid Astrea, they would need to plan around it. Fortunately, that Sword Saint was said to be a man whose legends were never in short supply. And fortunately, this party happened to include someone well-versed in the great figures of the past.

Of course, Subaru could easily imagine the defeat still weighing on him. But once he knew the true identity of his opponent, surely that shame could be washed away. After all, he'd simply drawn a terrible opponent. After all, the man was a Sword Saint—one who bore the same family name as Reinhard, and who was, in fact, the very founder who had built that name up from nothing.

Thinking of it that way, surely even that defeat could be swallowed down and made sense of—

"—That damn idiot."

Carrying those words of comfort with him, Subaru returned to the green room—and let slip a strangled, choked-down voice. In the back of the room stood the beds of woven grass the spirit had fashioned to lay the wounded upon—four beds in all, holding Rem, Anastasia, and, furthest back, Patrasche. The one bed between Anastasia's and Patrasche's sat empty. Only the snapped knight's sword lay atop that vine-woven bed.

—At the sound of footsteps striking the stairs, and a blade-intent that pricked at the skin, the man slowly opened his eyes.

He felt no anger at having his sleep disturbed. To begin with, life itself was an ever-present battlefield. If a man resolved that his own body always stood upon the line between life and death, then whatever happened there, his heart could remain undisturbed. How much playfulness he brought to it, though, was another matter.

"——"

Climbing the stairs, the figure gradually came into view. The blade-intent was familiar. The footsteps, the footwork, familiar too. It had been only just now. There was no way to forget it. But that should have been just as true for his opponent, and that struck him as strange. He'd taken him for a slightly wiser man than this—

"——"

"Hah!"

That impression vanished the moment he saw the eyes of the man climbing toward him. What made his throat rumble instead was an urge welling up from within. He let it roll grandly over his tongue, then raked a hand roughly through his red hair. And then—

"This time, it ain't gonna end as playin' around, you."

"——"

He didn't think it would mean much, but he tossed the words out anyway, as a bare formality. At that, his opponent closed his eyes once, then immediately cast aside every last emotion. Then, without hesitation, he reached out and drew the sword standing in the floor free, leveling it into a stance.

"Of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, serving in the Royal Guard—Julius Juukulius."

"——"

Having given his name, the knight broke into a furious charge the instant the man's eyes narrowed. At that, the man—Reid Astrea—twisted his cheek into a cruel, merciless smile.

"Long as you're spoutin' off some boring title like that, you ain't even good enough to be my plaything, you."

Probably, when they retreated from the room, Subaru carried Julius on his back, Emilia carried Anastasia princess-style, and Beako and Meili carried Shaula between them like a stretcher, holding her arms and legs. Shaula was making herself light, but Beako and Meili wore out partway and dropped her, and Emilia-tan scrambled to catch her as she went rolling off. In the end, it's believed Emilia-tan ended up carrying both Anastasia and Shaula, one over each shoulder, while cheering Beako and Meili on as they made their way down the stairs.
