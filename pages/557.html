Arc 7 Chapter 58: "Those Without a Place to Belong"

——It was three years ago that Taritta first began to be tormented by nightmares concerning her "Divine Revelation."

Until then, Taritta had lived a life with nothing to do with "Divine Revelation."
Naturally, she'd also been utterly ignorant of the existence of those called "Stargazers."

——Speak of "Divine Revelation," and what might come to mind?

Fate, destiny, the unavoidable trials and obstacles one encounters while walking the path of one's life.
If that's what comes to mind, then Divine Revelation is clearly something different. Those things are like rain falling while walking a road with no shelter—things there is no way to guard against.
Even the People of Shudrak understand such unavoidable happenings.

Put plainly, "death" is surely the most unavoidable of fates.
Old age, sickness, hunger, and wounds—all things bound up with "death"—are unavoidable, the curtain of a fate that always falls in the end.
No one, whoever they are, can defy fate. Nor, she believes, should they try to.

When kin die, they pray and sing, that their souls might be welcomed into peace.
Singing, holding a feast, and sending them off—that is the Shudrak way, and the values Taritta believes in.
Her older sister Mizelda is a Shudrak among Shudrak, outstanding even by their standards. Taritta, while not her sister's equal, likewise carries the blood of a line that has passed down the chieftainship for generations.
Being Shudrak was, to Taritta, a truth beyond question.

Following the teachings of the Shudrak was simply natural, leaving no room for doubt.
Taritta was never one for deep thinking to begin with. She had no intention of going out of her way to fret and pile worries onto herself.
She believed her life would simply continue on behind her sister, alongside the kin who shared her path and the blood flowing through her as one of the "People of Shudrak," from beginning to end.
That was her fate, her destiny—or so she thought.

"—Hey, Taritta. I was chosen to be a 'Stargazer,' and given my Divine Revelation."

—Until, that is, her closest "sister of the soul" confided this to her.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—So this is something other than the 'Great Disaster' that destroys the Empire."

Before Taritta, her resolve shaken and her footing unsteady, the man in the oni mask stared straight ahead.
The writhing jet-black mass visible in the distance—something one's instincts recognized at a glance as the "Great Disaster"—yet the man, Abel, sneered that it was nothing to fear.

"Then there's no reason to stumble along the way. —Off the board with you, you presumptuous thing."

Declaring flatly to that towering violence that it hadn't been invited, Abel stepped forward.
At once, small Medium raised her voice beside him. "Wait, wait!" Grabbing the hem of Abel's clothes, she used her whole body to hold him back.

"You're suddenly all fired up!? But it's dangerous for you, Abel-chin!"

"Fool. What must be done was already decided. If that were the true 'Great Disaster,' I'd lament how woefully unprepared we are. But if it isn't..."

"If it isn't?"

"Then that's nothing more than a diversion before the main event. I have no time to indulge in diversions. In which case, I'll bring the curtain down on it quickly. —Even you should have noticed that, watching from afar."

Jerking his chin toward the "Great Disaster" to indicate it, Abel put the question to Medium that way. But hearing this, Medium frowned, unable to grasp his meaning.
She looked back and forth between Abel and the "Great Disaster,"

"I don't get it at all! Being all mysterious is a bad habit of yours, Abel-chin!"

"Are you blaming your own slow wits on me?"

"Like I said! If I'm not convinced, I'll get in the way! You're the one getting in your own way here, Abel-chin! If you're so smart, you should understand that!"

"——"

Stamping her feet and puffing out her cheeks, Medium refused to let go of Abel's hem.
Medium's remark was brazen if anything, but perhaps thinking even the time to argue the point was too precious to spare, Abel let out a small breath and said,

"That girl, along with Yorna Mishigure, Kafma Irulux, and the residents of the Magic City are fighting desperately to stop that 'Great Disaster'—have you not noticed?"

"Hmm~, that bad habit again?"

"—. That thing has a will. A plain, obvious, human will."

Abel said so, and Medium tilted her head. "A will?"
At Medium's question, Taritta, standing stock-still behind them, furrowed her brow as well. That "Great Disaster," which looked like the end of the world made manifest, having a will?
What's more, Abel had said a "human" will. Where in that thing could such a thing possibly reside?
To begin with, the very idea of regarding that thing as a living creature hadn't even occurred to her.

Its bloated, writhing whole body scraping away the world as it moved—that was nothing but the end itself given form.
That such a thing had a will—just what had Abel seen to judge that?
Just as Taritta and Medium arrived at the same question—

"That, just now, reached its hand out toward the two of you."

"Huh?"
"—Ah."

At Abel's somewhat blindsiding words, the two let out foolish-sounding noises.
Medium had no idea what he meant, while Taritta felt a faint sense of wrongness. —When she'd carried the two of them away from the falling shadow, Taritta too had felt it.
Something like a faint obsession, from that dreadful jet-black thing.

"Most likely, the will of whatever is struggling within its belly is being reflected. It keeps reaching out, one after another, seeking salvation. Look."

Before Taritta, who caught her breath, Abel this time extended his own hand to point at the "Great Disaster."
Drawn by the gesture, Taritta and the others looked and widened their eyes, understanding what Abel meant.

At the center of the Magic City, sitting in place of the collapsed Crimson Lapis Castle, spreading merciless destruction and finality all around it as it expanded—the "Great Disaster," extending its jet-black shadow, sweeping things aside, trying to draw everything into the ending that was itself—it was aiming, invariably, not at buildings or the earth, but at people.
What's more—

"—Is it only targeting Rui-chan and Yorna-chan?"

Medium's hoarse murmur—Taritta had gotten the same impression.
Watching the fierce back-and-forth with the "Great Disaster" from a distance, it was immediately clear the shadow's targeting was skewed.
Those chiefly targeted were the two of them—Yorna, putting on a great display of swordplay left and right, and Rui, dodging the shadows she drew in with instantaneous movements.

Taritta's eyes widened at Rui's tremendous movement as well, but Yorna's offense and defense, wielding destructive power, was likewise proof of her being an extraordinary talent among the "Nine Divine Generals."
And yet the thorn-wielder, the man of the "Insect Cage Tribe," who exhibited suppressive power to rival even Yorna and repeatedly kept the damage from the "Great Disaster" to a minimum, was surely no less skilled. Despite that, including the residents of the Magic City hurling buildings, the attacks flying their way were nothing more than stray arrows, so to speak.

"If Rui-chan's being targeted too, then it's not going in order from strongest, huh."

"If it were purely a matter of strength, Kafma Irulux is by no means far inferior. But viewed from above, the logic is self-evident. That thing has a will. If so, then my moving is only reasonable."

"H-how... can you say something like that...?"

"Obviously. There's no way that thing thinks favorably of me. If it's reaching out its hand to cling to someone, I wouldn't even make the list of candidates."

At Abel's matter-of-fact way of speaking, Taritta felt something inexplicable.
She understood Abel's idea of defining that "Great Disaster" as something possessing a will. But even taking that into account, it was strange that he could see through the "Great Disaster's" criteria for judgment.
It was as though Abel already knew exactly whose will lay behind that "Great Disaster."

"—Natsuki Subaru's."

"—!?"
"Eh!?"

As though pushing from behind at both Taritta's suspicion and Medium's incomprehension, Abel, eyes fixed on the "Great Disaster," declared it flatly to the two of them.
Watching the two of them stare with eyes wide in shock, Abel drew his chin in deeply and said,

"That thing overflowed from within Natsuki Subaru. If so, it's no wonder his will has a hand in it. I always thought him a man with more than his share of secrets, but I hadn't expected it to be to this degree."

"W-wait, Abel-chin... are you saying that thing, that thing is Subaru-chin!?"

"Strictly speaking, it is something that permits the reflection of Natsuki Subaru's will. I'm not saying that thing is him, nor that he commands it. —Either way, the result is the same."

"Why do you seem so calm about it, Abel-chin!?"

Her round eyes wide open, Medium protested in a voice on the verge of tears.
Looking down at her small figure, Abel narrowed his black eyes behind the oni mask, precisely as one scorning a child troubling an adult with emotional selfishness.

"Do I look calm? Because he let that overflow from within himself, my plans have collapsed. I need to reconsider, but there's no time for that now. And yet you think I look calm?"

"No! No, that's not it, I said! That's not what I mean... Subaru-chin is saying help me, isn't he? So I'm asking why you're so calm about it!"

"—Will standing beside that thing's suffering resolve the situation in front of us? Unfortunately, reality is neither so accommodating nor so brimming with brotherly love."

Medium's earnest words, however, could not shake Abel's steel heart.
That was because he was cold, someone who didn't understand the human heart—no, someone who didn't place importance on the human heart—and at the same time, a person capable of discerning what should be prioritized.
A quality that those who stand above others, rulers and leaders, must possess.
The same, surely, was demanded of Taritta, who must take the seat of chieftain.
But—

"I... can't..."

She could hold neither Abel's way of thinking, nor his decisiveness.
Much less, like Medium, could she raise her voice head-on and say she hated what she hated.

"I can't spare the time to argue here either. I'm going. I must speak with Yorna Mishigure."

"...With Yorna-chan? But how?"

Deciding he could no longer indulge further back-and-forth, Abel tried to step forward. But Medium, cutting into his words, her gaze spoke eloquently of just how difficult that advance would be.
Where Abel declared he was headed—if he meant to speak with Yorna, what awaited him was the very front line against that rampaging "Great Disaster."

A battlefield where even for transcendent beings, a single instant's judgment split life from death.
For Abel to blithely stride into that would visibly do nothing but endanger his life. Without even managing a move that might change the tide of the battle—

"——"

At the thought of a move that could change the tide of battle, Taritta's whole body froze.
Perhaps she alone possessed that as an option. That dreadful "Great Disaster" scattering its finality—only Taritta had the means to quell it.
Only Taritta, who knew of the Divine Revelation granted to drive back the "Great Disaster"—

"—I'll step up too."

"What?"

Taritta trembled, faced with a momentous decision.
Beside Taritta, Abel's voice mixed with suspicion at Medium, who had declared this so flatly. His black eyes wavered as if peering into the heart of the young girl holding him back,

"What are you thinking? Were you even listening to what I said? The basis for my going forward is that thing doesn't think favorably of me. If you come along there..."

"I get it, I said! That's why I'm not going with you! Where I'm going is where Rui-chan and Yorna-chan are fighting! I'm going to make Subaru-chin find me."

"——"

"If Abel-chin's idea is right, then Subaru-chin will reach out for me too, right? Then Rui-chan and the others' burden will lighten up a little too! And maybe you won't be targeted either, Abel-chin."

"My chances of being targeted were low to begin with."

"Then they'll be even lower! Right!?"

Stepping firmly forward, Medium pressed her own case.
For a moment, it was clear Abel was seriously considering Medium's proposal. Just as she said, if the "Great Disaster's" targeting reflected Subaru's will, the chance of Medium being targeted was high.
But what remained worrying was, after all, Medium herself, now smaller and slower in her movements.

Even if she volunteered for the role of drawing the "Great Disaster's" attention, there was a real possibility she'd be swallowed by the shadow before she could accomplish much of the role at all. If that happened, it would rattle everyone around her as well.
Taritta herself, of all people, couldn't imagine remaining unshaken if Medium were lost.
Bright and lively, unafraid in how she treated others—Medium was one of the people Taritta held dear. She wouldn't be able to face her brother, Flop, either.

—Then again, could she, as she was now, truly face anyone at all?

"Rejected."

"Abel-chin!"

"I considered it for a moment, but in your current state you couldn't fulfill even the role of decoy satisfactorily. The impact your death would have on those around you is itself liable to become a concern. I can't take that kind of gamble."

Beside Taritta, who stood frozen in place, Abel rejected Medium's proposal out of that same concern.
But Medium's eyes, as she gritted her teeth, weren't convinced. Left as it was, she might well ignore Abel's opinion, dash out onto the battlefield, and shout at the "Great Disaster" anyway.
Into such a precarious moment—

"—Then I'll protect Miss Medium myself. That oughta settle it, no complaints, right?"

Treading over the rubble, the voice of a newly arrived figure cut in.
At that familiar voice, the three of them turned around, each in their own way, to look at the newcomer.

A suspicious-looking figure, a thick right arm hanging a qinglong blade, his face wrapped in a shabby, ill-fitting rag. She'd never particularly sensed him as a man of great strength, and yet somehow, in this moment, in this place, he stood there brimming with fighting spirit striking enough to catch the eye—the man, Al.

"Al-chin!? You got big again... you were able to change back!?"

"Ran right into the old geezer comin' back, as it happens. Didn't have time to swing by the inn and grab my helmet, so bear with me lookin' rough for a bit."

"You look plenty cool! But if the old man's here, then..."

Al, back to his original state aside from the rag wrapped around his face. Medium's voice brightened at his words, and she looked all around.
Naturally, Medium, in the same state herself, would surely want the option to change back too.
But at Medium's reaction, Al added a single word—"Sorry"—

"Sorry, but I couldn't bring the old man along. Says he's playin' it smart."

"Uu~, I see. Okay, got it! But I'm glad at least you got to change back, Al-chin! Has the scary feeling settled down now?"

"...The scary feeling's probably not something that's ever gonna settle down, my whole life."

Her hope taken from her, Medium lowered her eyes for just a moment before immediately shifting gears. Receiving her words, Al lowered the tone of his voice and shook his head.
His tone carried a fear and unease he couldn't fully hide. Faced with that "Great Disaster," anyone would feel instinctive terror. No one could blame him for it.
And yet, even carrying that unfeigned fear, Al said—

"Even so, it's gotta be done. ...Fate, huh. Bring it on."

"Al-chin..."

At Al's resolve, raising his gripped qinglong blade firmly, Medium looked visibly moved.
Into that exchange between the two cut a small "hmph" of a snort—

"Quite the big talk, coming from the shameful sight of one shrinking and trembling. That Olbart Dunkelkenn shouldn't have any Noh for inspiring others."

"That's no lie. All that geezer did was say stuff that pissed me off. It's not like I stood up outta spite over that, either."

"Then what made you stand? How do you intend to convince me it's fine to include you, who became unable to move like that, within the plans going forward?"

"——"

Still held in place with his hem gripped by Medium, Abel put the question to Al.
His icy eyes behind the oni mask were surely still turning over thoughts of the victory to be seized, even now. To join in that, how would Al persuade Abel?
Taritta, who found even breathing difficult under Abel's gaze, couldn't begin to imagine.
Under the watching eyes of the three of them—Taritta, Abel, and Medium—Al said—

"Sorry, but I'm not gonna waste words tryin' to convince you, Abel-chan."

"Oh?"

"My master's that one lewd-cute princess, and that's it. The reason I tagged along like this is to help out a brother-in-arms... if that's the case, then I'll do just that. As for my moves, you can go ahead and factor 'em into your equations or whatever, however you like, Abel-chan."

Declaring this with a brazenly defiant attitude, Al shouldered his qinglong blade. His refreshingly clean-cut resolve made Abel's expression, hidden behind the oni mask, tense faintly.
But even if that answer didn't sit well with him, Abel had neither the power to drive Al off, nor a relationship with him substantial enough to hold him back. Exactly as Al had intended.
Whatever the case, Abel would have to build his strategy around Al's presence as a given.

"The more you come back to your senses, the more troublesome a clown you become. How do you mean to take this on?"

"Trade secret. —Well, I won't let Miss Medium die. And I'll protect you too while I'm at it, Abel-chan, so don't you worry none."

"Al-chin..."

Even with the "Great Disaster" rampaging right before them, Al's answer was almost too powerful, too full of strength.
Medium's eyes widening at those words was an expression of both surprise and respect, at how Al had recovered from the "shameful sight" Abel had described.
So Medium, with her whole small body, gave a great nod, and said—

"I'll do my best with Al-chin! Is that okay, Abel-chin?"

"—. Your existence wasn't part of the plan to begin with anyway."

"Much obliged. Just the words 'unaccounted-for fighting force' make me feel like I'm getting special treatment, and it's putting me in a good mood."

Shrugging at Abel's answer, Al stepped forward, and Medium fell in beside him. Only then did Medium finally release Abel's sleeve.
Shaking out his freed arm, Abel let out a small breath and turned back to face the "Great Disaster."
And then—

"—Taritta."

"Ah..."

"If you mean to aim for my back, do as you like. But mark this. —Whether to obey your Divine Revelation or not, in the end, there's nothing for it but to choose for yourself."

Without turning to face Taritta, letting her hear only that much over his shoulder, Abel's feet advanced toward the battlefield.
Medium and Al, facing the same direction as him, looked ready to accompany that advance as well.

"—!"

Only Taritta, unable to move either forward or back, could do nothing but watch the three backs go.
The words Abel had just spoken kept echoing in her head, again and again, like a warning to herself. Nock an arrow to the bow in her hand and shoot Abel in the back—that would make things simple.
But if she could just leap into something so simple, Taritta wouldn't be agonizing like this in the first place.

"Obey my Divine Revelation, or defy it... I... I..."

Biting her lip hard, Taritta lowered her head, holding back a heat that welled up slowly within her.
She could summon neither the resolve to raise the arrow gripped in her hand, nor the courage to voice the feelings knotted in her chest.
With only violent tremors and thunderous roars dominating, amid the Magic City breaking apart, Taritta hung her head.

—Just as before, only to be swept along, only to be mocked by the stars.

Fiercely warding off the surging jet-black darkness, a storm raged within Yorna's heart.

"——"

Wielding her pipe, she made a wall of the violet smoke it produced, blocking the "Great Disaster's" attacks and holding back its expansion.
In between, she fired parts of the city itself like cannon shot, dealing even the smallest wound to the "Great Disaster."
Repeating this again and again, dealing with the reaching shadows that could cost a life with even a graze, she poured her heart and body fully into every possible countermeasure.
But this sort of slow, losing battle couldn't last.

"—Tch, what am I going weak-kneed for?"

At the faintheartedness creeping into her own chest, Yorna twisted her beautiful lips in objection.
Whether or not it would have any effect, she had no intention of taking back the bold words she'd flung at Abel. This city was hers, and those living in it were under her protection.
The last bastion for those with nowhere else to go—she couldn't lose that—

"Uuaaauuu!!"

"Child...!"

Suddenly, a massive black filth poured down aimed at Yorna. Before Yorna could respond, a small shadow leapt in sideways and threw itself against her waist, and the world changed the instant she blinked.
For a moment there was a sense of her head reeling, but right after, seeing the ground several meters to the side scraped away by the raging violence, Yorna understood she had been protected.

The one who had pulled that off was the blue-eyed girl with golden hair tied up in one—Rui.
Her slender shoulders heaving roughly, Rui, her lovely face touched with a grim cast, stared fixedly at the "Great Disaster," her round eyes opened wide in anguish.
Only, what she was looking at wasn't the "Great Disaster" itself, but whatever was inside it.

"You're worried for that child, aren't you."

"Uauu...!"

Answering Yorna's words shortly, Rui's figure bounced again like a rubber ball, heading toward the "Great Disaster."
And Rui bounded around the upper portion of the "Great Disaster"—the bloated, shapeless "Great Disaster" had no head as such, but if it did, the part that would correspond to one—drawing its attention.
The arms of shadow launched one after another aiming at Rui's small body numbered close to a hundred, and Rui's contribution in dodging every one of them, preventing the damage from spreading, was immeasurable.

If she hadn't been there, Yorna could hardly imagine that she and the residents of the Magic City alone could have held the "Great Disaster" in check. That thought sent a chill through her.

"Use it! Girl!"

"Au!"

Helping along Rui's breakthrough power was Kafma, who employed the thorns extending from both his arms.
He offered up his thorns, which boasted such tremendous suppressive power, as footholds, deftly aiding Rui's evasive maneuvers. Of course, whatever thorns of his were too slow to escape were swallowed by the "Great Disaster," but this seemed to have no effect on Kafma himself, and true to his earnest appearance, his understated, seasoned performance continued.

Hidden behind such offense and defense from Rui, Kafma, and Yorna, the residents of the Magic City continued to resist the "Great Disaster," however slightly. Those with flame lit in one eye were under the influence of Yorna's "Soul Marriage Technique," their power boosted by it.
But the boosting of power had individual differences and limits.
Furthermore, Yorna's "Soul Marriage Technique" was, by nature, weighted in favor of the weaker party.

It could let a weak thing face down a strong one, but it wasn't the sort of thing that let a strong thing contend against even stronger ones.
This much came down to differences in the quality of souls, something beyond Yorna's control.
Hence—

"—Still only a few minutes."

It felt as if an outrageous, endless stretch of time had passed, and yet only a few minutes had elapsed since that "Great Disaster" had swallowed the Crimson Lapis Castle and Yorna and the others' all-out battle had begun.
Despite that, the exhaustion Yorna and the others had accumulated was not something that could be dismissed as negligible.

That was simply what an environment amid mortal danger was like, where one's nerves were constantly strung taut against a fatal blow.
That, surely, was the difference between those considered true warriors, and a woman like Yorna Mishigure, who had merely happened to be granted power.

She wasn't so blessed a being that she could challenge, without any forethought, a "Great Disaster" that true warriors might be able to whittle down, and wrest away victory.
That very self-awareness was itself a strength of Yorna Mishigure, ruler of the Magic City.
And then—

"Uoryaaaa——!!"

Into a battlefield that seemed to be a losing war of attrition, new fighting strength was thrown in along with a rousing battle cry.
With a momentum for which the word "thrown in" was truly fitting, what came leaping in was a small girl charging forward, a machete gripped in her hand.

The girl's intrusion, her long braid dancing, just as lively as her bearing suggested, led the awareness of everyone on the battlefield, Yorna included, straight into confusion.
Naturally so. What could possibly come of one girl joining the battlefield? It looked like nothing more than a girl who'd simply thrown herself into reckless bravery, her courage about to backfire on her.
What's more, the strange intruder wasn't her alone.

"Here we go, Miss Medium! Boldly now, this is our entrance into the fight!"

So roared the masked man, bearing a qinglong blade, who landed behind the girl.
He was one of those who had come the day before as an envoy to the castle keep, but unlike then, he now hid his face with a mask instead of a helmet, and moreover, the way he held his qinglong blade was bizarre.
For some reason, he was running out with the blade of his qinglong sword held right up against his own neck, in a dangerously precarious posture.
At this rate, some mistake might have him accidentally slicing off his own head.

In any case, at this pair's intrusion, which invited only confusion, the air on the battlefield froze slightly.
But that freeze only affected the defenders whose attention was split by this chaotic melee. —The "Great Disaster," possessing nothing resembling a will of its own, felt no surprise at the intruders.
Even so, perhaps the "Great Disaster" had some sense of annoyance, for the fact that the majority of the shadows it unleashed turned not toward Yorna or Rui, but toward the girl who'd charged out, was an anomaly.
Thinking she had to protect the girl, Yorna too reflexively moved to act—but—

"Right! Step off that footing and jump! Hook your toes on the rubble, then up!"

"Unrya!"

The masked man shouted, and in time with it the girl let out a bouncing voice.
Following the man's word, the girl leapt right, then stepped off a fragment of rubble and leapt forward. When her foot reached the rubble ahead, she used the strength in her toes to spring high upward.

The shadows swept, scraped, and enveloped, one after another, the positions the girl had just occupied.
But the girl narrowly dodged every one of them and won through unharmed.

"That is..."

It was a sight she had witnessed once before—yesterday at the castle keep, when the envoys who'd taken up Yorna's proposed contest were targeted and attacked by a group commanded by the Emperor—no, by one impersonating the Emperor.
That masked man had, even against Kafma's thorns and Olbart's attacks, instructed his allies on countermeasures and escaped the crisis. And that from a man who otherwise seemed to possess no particularly outstanding ability.
Was it a frighteningly good instinct, or was there some further reason?
She suspected the latter, but had no idea what the true nature of that reason could be.
Even so, the situation had changed.

"Al-chin! What next!?"

"Don't rush me, this takes concentration! Oi, you there, tattoo guy, help out! I'll give the instructions! Get in the way of that thing's movements!"

"I refuse! Why should I, at the word of some unknown character like you—"

"Above! Spread your thorns out wide!"

"Mn!?"

Prompted by the girl's cry, the masked man—the one called Al—called out to Kafma.
Naturally, Kafma had no intention of obeying someone of unknown ability, but the jet-black shadow surging in response to Al's shout proved this was no time for arguing.
Reflexively crouching and raising his hand overhead, thorns spread from Kafma's arm, and the great wave of shadow changed course as though flowing over the thorns.

Of course, the thorns couldn't withstand a shadow that scraped away everything, but the one second bought was enough for Kafma to save his life.
Leaping into the gap and escaping disaster, Kafma glared at Al. "You...!"

"Can you read the movements of that 'Great Disaster'?"

"Got a bit of history with it, is all. You in the mood to listen now?"

"—. If it's for reducing the damage, there's no helping it. But if this is a lie, you won't be forgiven!"

"More flexible than I figured. Appreciate it."

As Kafma honestly acknowledged the advantage, Al, with a wry smile, stepped forward.
And once again giving directions to the girl and Kafma, with the qinglong blade still held against his own neck, he began commanding the front line against the "Great Disaster."
Alongside that battle—

"—Yorna Mishigure."

"You are..."

With Al and the girl unexpectedly turning into fighting strength, Yorna, about to join them herself, turned her attention instead to the oni mask—Abel—who appeared stepping over the rubble.
Only a few minutes had passed since their earlier parting, but he wasn't a man endearing enough to have changed his mind in that time.
To begin with, the rift lying between Yorna and him might not be something so easily wiped away by the mere passage of time—that much unease seemed to lie beneath the surface.
As if to push that thought of Yorna's along further—

"Has your head cooled enough to consider the proposal—the abandonment of the Magic City?"

Flatly, coldly, Abel put to Yorna once more the proposal she thought she'd already thrown back at him.
Naturally, "cooled" wasn't a word that suited a head still burning with fighting spirit. As long as the situation and the relationship hadn't changed, Yorna's answer wouldn't change either.
Deeming it meaningless to even argue, Yorna turned her back on that cold-blooded Emperor.

"You said this was the final land for those who've lost anywhere else to go, anyone to rely on, didn't you."

"——"

"My view that this is nothing but worthless sentiment hasn't changed. But I'll correct your mistake."

"My... mistake?"

At a remark too significant to let pass, Yorna's feet, about to return to the battlefield, stopped.
When it came to this Magic City of Chaosflame, there was no way Yorna was mistaken about anything. This was Yorna's city, and its residents were all her Beloved Children.
As for that, any mistaken perception on Yorna's part—

"Just what is it you say I've gotten wrong?"

"What those without anywhere to belong cling to, what they rely on, isn't this city. It's you."

"——"

"To begin with, the Magic City is a city built atop devastated land and the wreckage of war. What was raised as its symbol was the Crimson Lapis Castle, but the true symbol has always resided in the castle keep. That is to say, you."

Once again, Abel's gaze fixed on Yorna as he declared this.
Struck in the eardrum by the strength and sincerity of those words, Yorna's cheeks stiffened.

She understood the logic. But it was a pretty sentiment, nothing more.
However much Yorna might be the spiritual pillar of the Magic City's residents, in fact, when rain fell they needed shelter, and when hungry they needed something to eat.
The city could provide those things, but Yorna alone could not cover all of it.

"Or do you mean to say that as long as I'm there, my children would endure hunger and not mind being soaked by rain?"

"—They would say exactly that."

"Eh..."

"Do you think of all those standing beneath your banner as nursing infants? Babies who fuss and wait to be soothed, to be given milk? —That is not my view of them."

At the sight of Abel answering so, Yorna quietly caught her breath.
Right after, a violent roar rose up behind them, and fragments of rubble caught in the "Great Disaster's" rampage came flying toward Yorna and Abel. One of those fragments struck the ground right beside Abel, who stood his ground; the impact battered his slender frame and tore the oni mask from his face.

"—."

His pale face now bared, blood trickling down a brow the fragment had faintly grazed, yet the fine figure of a man didn't so much as let his expression waver; he picked up the oni mask that had fallen beside him and gripped it in his hand.
And then, with his bare face still exposed, Abel—no, Vincent Vollachia—glared at Yorna, and said,

"The common folk are foolish. Without pain, they forget to resist; without an enemy, they won't even arm themselves. They don't know how to unite absent calamity, and out of sheer fear of death they avert their eyes from it entirely."

"——"

"But it's precisely that weak, short-sighted foolishness that makes them what they are. The Empire binds its people with the law of blood and iron; in the Magic City, it's been your very way of being that has disciplined how its residents live. And so—"

There he cut off his words, and turned his gaze into the distance. Drawn to look that way as well, there she saw the residents, Yorna's love kindled in their eyes, resisting the "Great Disaster."
The sight of them fighting on valiantly, striving to protect this city.
No—

"For your sake, they would endure hunger and rain alike. And they would choose to hope, together with you, for the day when the sun rises again and their bellies are filled."

And so—

"Abandon the Magic City. Where they're headed is the same place as you. And what they rely on is you yourself."

"—Ah."

"Or will you lament that you cannot? Over a wish that, struggle however you like, will never align with your own desire?"

As he spoke, Abel wiped away the blood trickling from his brow with his sleeve, and put the oni mask back over his face.
His expression once again hid behind its perception-obstructing veil, but the words spoken just before had struck deep, driven into the depths of Yorna's chest, lodged there as a thorn that wouldn't come loose.

She'd known, from the moment she'd read his letter, that Abel was aware of Yorna's wish.
But to bring it up here, now, of all places—that was far too heartless. And that very heartlessness was itself the quality demanded of the present Vollachia Emperor.
Precisely because of that, the countermeasure she could take was—

"And what then, abandoning the Magic City and fleeing that 'Great Disaster'? Even after it drinks this city dry, it might well not stop, mightn't it."

"Testing me, even at this stage? You must already understand as much yourself."

The true meaning behind the words about abandoning the Magic City and letting the "Great Disaster" do as it pleased.
At Yorna's question, Abel jerked his chin, answering with displeasure. What his gesture indicated was the "Great Disaster" itself—no, the ground beneath the raging "Great Disaster."
That was to say, the ruined site of the Crimson Lapis Castle where the "Great Disaster" had appeared—

"Do in the Magic City what was done when the castle was swallowed. —Even if the swallowed castle wasn't enough to blow it apart, the Magic City you love may well be a different matter."

△▼△▼△▼△

"——"

Far off, his robes' hem fluttering in the wind, sharp black eyes watched the "Great Disaster" destroying the city.
Yorna's strategy of holding back the spread of damage as much as possible seemed to be showing effect, thanks to the contributions of Kafma, the city's residents, and the unplanned reinforcements.
But even that wouldn't last.

That was how overwhelming the threat of that "Great Disaster," darkness itself, truly was.
Perhaps in ancient times, people who witnessed such things firsthand, resolved to the world's destruction, left behind legends so that survivors could pass down the terror in story.
The foremost of these was the "Witch of Envy," and that "Great Disaster" too was something linked to her line.
Only—

"—A calamity distinct, then, from the 'Great Disaster' that destroys the Empire."

"Yes indeed, yes indeed. Ahh, this has really got me. I never expected things to go this far, and honestly, it's got me forgetting my place and wanting to lodge a complaint with the stars, it does."

Beside the man standing with folded arms on the high ground—Vincent—crouched a "Stargazer" in a blue robe, watching the same sight.
This man, who had joined them without any warning along the way, had insisted over and over, in the face of that raging "Great Disaster," "In-a-a-any case, this one's outside my jurisdiction!"—flatly shirking responsibility.
But not lying about what he'd actually seen was one of this "Stargazer's" few virtues.
Given that such a "Stargazer" declared it so boldly, that calamity and the fall of the Empire the "Stargazer" had foreseen were surely, after all, separate matters.
In other words—

"Oh, there he is, there he is. Geez, had me pretty worried, y'know."

"Oh-ho, if it isn't old man Olbart."

Behind Vincent, whose eyes had narrowed in thought, an old man appeared with a light presence. Called by name first by the "Stargazer," the old man—Olbart—nodded, "Yeah, yeah."
Glancing sidelong at Olbart as he came to stand alongside him, Vincent frowned.

"Where did you lose your right arm?"

"Sharp eyes as ever, Your Excellency. If I said my right hand's tucked away in your breast pocket right now... well, that'd be a good laugh, wouldn't it. Truth is, that big ol' shadow swallowed it up."

"I se-e-e. That sounds like it must have been quite the painful experience."

"Oh, painful don't even begin to cover it. At my age, I about bawled and hollered like some sniveling brat. Shameful, shameful."

Fluttering the stump of his right hand, missing everything past the wrist, Olbart blustered on.
Setting aside the farce between Olbart and the "Stargazer," Vincent looked back at the battlefield once more.

The "Great Disaster" writhing on the ruined site of the Crimson Lapis Castle, and the city's forces holding it back.
By now, the plan for driving back that "Great Disaster" had surely already been conveyed to Yorna.
All that remained—

"Still, Your Excellency, you sure it's all right takin' it easy in a place like this?"

"It's no matter."

To Olbart's words, tilting his head, Vincent answered quietly.
Conveying the plan to Yorna and persuading her was the other party's role. In exchange, Vincent, for his part, understood full well the role he himself had to fulfill.
And so—

"The move has been made. —The move that had to be made, as Vincent Vollachia, Emperor of Vollachia."
