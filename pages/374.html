Arc 5 Chapter 57: "The Heart's Whereabouts"

――Subaru's desperate stalling for time was already closing in on ten-odd minutes.

"Oh, come on already! Why don't you learn your place and just die quietly like a good boy!?"

"Gwaaaah!"

The kick that slammed home gouged into the building with a beauty that was almost obscene, and the structure, stripped of its supports, collapsed helplessly.

Billowing smoke and dust blanketed the area, and Subaru heard Regulus click his tongue in irritation at having his view blocked—all while darting across a battlefield where his makeshift traps had already run dry.

"Scurrying around like a little rat... don't you have it in you to fight me head-on like a man? I've got no interest anymore in what your relationship is with that harlot, but for someone calling himself a knight or whatever, don't you think that's a little pathetic!?"

"Say whatever the hell you want, as much as you want!"

"That's what you focus on!?"

When Subaru talked back to Regulus's endless, rambling malice, the reply that came back was a scattershot of sand that would scrape the life right out of him.

The instant he put distance between himself and the murderous grains of sand hurled his way, the gap in the rubble he'd been hiding in until a moment before was blown to nothing.

A graze meant a mortal wound; a direct hit meant instant death.

By some miracle, Regulus's attacks hadn't connected with Subaru yet. But the moment one did, it would be over, so being grateful for this "luck" was beside the point.

"Focus! Focus! Focus—!"

Gasping for breath, wiping away sweat, he poured every last nerve into evasion.

His face grimed with the swirling dust, Subaru spat out the muddy-tasting saliva pooling in his mouth.

His parkour training was paying off.

Unlike the days he'd spent conditioning his body with no particular goal, the clear sense of purpose and drive to improve had made a real difference in what Subaru could do.

All those days spent training near the new Roswaal Manor's forest until he wanted to throw up hadn't been for nothing.

"Not that it's much—hah—more than barely managing, hah, to keep some amateur-footed bastard on his toes...!"

Still, this was holding down one of the Witch Cult's Sin Archbishops.

Given the current battle and what Subaru alone brought to the table, it was without question performance worthy of a medal of honor.

Once Priestella safely escaped the Witch Cult's clutches, he was absolutely going to demand a grand round of praise for this—

"So..."!

"You think just stalling for time is going to let a guy like you actually do something about me? Don't go getting the wrong idea just because you cracked a little bit of the secret to my Authority of Greed and think you can stand against me!"

Without giving him a moment to breathe, Regulus's tantrum-like destruction only grew wider. He no longer hesitated over how much damage he caused so long as it meant finishing off Subaru.

Priestella, once known for its picturesque scenery, was losing its shape piece by piece under the Witch Cult's worst atrocity.

Stone bridges spanning the canals were smashed, shops adorned with glasswork crushed flat. Feeling an oddly out-of-place beauty in the glittering shards of scattering glass, Subaru trusted his right leg and ran through the gaps in that raging collapse.

Ironically, there was power welling up from that very right leg.

The right leg, being eaten away by something dark and unknowable, was Subaru's lifeline right now.

He shot out his whip, wrapping its tip around a gutter on the building right beside him. Trusting the grip of the line, he kicked off the ground and ran along a wall to traverse the streets—slipping right past Regulus, and, to make sure the wide-eyed madman saw it, sticking out his tongue in provocation.

"You—you don't know your damn place!!"

The shards of stone flung from Regulus's swung-out arm went flying wide of the mark, missing Subaru as he pulled away on the whip's momentum.

He wasn't thinking about attacking—only about escaping, completely.

Everyone Greed had destroyed until now had been destroyed because they were brave. If only they'd been weak, fragile, steeped to the bone in cowardly retreat—surely then they wouldn't have lost to a man like this.

So he would settle their regret here.

For that—

"Not yet, Emilia? —Where the hell is this guy's heart!"

"Is there... really no one who has any idea?"

Before the brides who had offered their cooperation, Emilia bit her lip at the unexpected answer she'd received.

All fifty-three brides exchanged glances at Emilia's question, and, just as before, shook their heads without conviction.

"I'm sorry. There's no lie in wanting to break free of that man's control and help you. But..."

The one who bowed her head in frustration was the de facto representative of the brides—the blonde woman who'd given her name as Sylphy.

Sylphy, having prefaced that she knew the circumstances better than any of Regulus's current brides, went on:

"I can't believe that man would entrust something so important to us. He called us his wives, his brides—but he never once actually treated us that way. Not a single time."

"I can tell there's soooo much wrong with Regulus, but that can't be it. It has to be—Regulus's 'Lion's Heart' has to be entrusted to one of you."

Sylphy sagged in disappointment at having no idea, but Emilia wasn't about to give up there.

Sylphy and the other brides had, of their own will, finally steeled themselves to escape Regulus's control. She couldn't allow the very first step of that resolve to stumble like this.

Besides, Emilia believed in Subaru without a shred of doubt.

Subaru was amazing. He knew so many things she didn't, and his knowledge and effort had carried them through every hardship. That was precisely why Emilia didn't doubt his conclusion that Regulus's Authority was the "Lion's Heart."

That didn't mean giving up on thinking for herself, or blindly depending on him.

It wasn't a matter of blindly following along just because "Subaru said so, so it's fine." Subaru could be wrong too, and sometimes he would fail.

But being able to correct that—or to pull him back, or to be the strength that helps him—that was what Emilia's trust in him meant.

"Subaru thought the 'Lion's Heart' was surely entrusted to his wives..."

Touching her chin as if in thought, Emilia laid bare the mystery of the Authority of Greed as Subaru had explained it to her.

—An Authority that stopped the time of an object, sealing away all change.

When she'd first heard it, she'd thought it unbelievable, but there had been far too much in Subaru's subsequent explanation that made sense.

Whether it could actually be carried out in reality was the one unclear point, if she had to pick something—

"There's some soooo mysterious power at work there."

It was probably a mystery close to a Divine Protection, something beyond ordinary reason.

Unfortunately, Emilia herself wasn't blessed with a Divine Protection, so she couldn't understand the peculiar sense of omnipotence it granted those who had one.

But she felt something close to that from Regulus. Perhaps an Authority was a Divine Protection made uglier, made viler.

"A heart, a heart..."

The worst-case scenario for Emilia would be if a true partner, one whose heart genuinely belonged with Regulus's, were hiding among the brides.

In that case, the real bride would try to hide the heart entrusted to her from Emilia, for the sake of Regulus's victory.

"—"

While Emilia agonized over this, Sylphy and the other brides were searching their memories aloud, each in her own words.

Emilia watched them intently with her violet eyes as they exchanged words with serious faces.

She'd ordered the minor spirits nearby to attach themselves to every bride's body and check for anything unusual. The minor spirits weren't especially sharp when it came to detecting changes in humans, and testing the women who had come forward to help her didn't sit well with her at all.

But they were long past the point where she could leave her suspicions unaddressed just because she "didn't want to suspect" anyone.

"Mm..."

The minor spirits gave their answer.

It wasn't quite certainty, but none of the brides showed any notable reaction. As far as Emilia could probe, it was safe to assume none of them were openly conspiring with Regulus.

Then the possibility left to consider was—

"Huh?"

A sudden sensation, like her hair being tugged, made Emilia look up on reflex.

Right in front of her, drifting across her field of vision, was a minor spirit glowing a pale blue. The spirit that should have been attached to one of the brides swayed as if it had something urgent to tell her.

Tracing its path with her eyes, she saw the spirit head toward Sylphy, who was still desperately leading the other brides who'd raised their voices in defiance of Regulus.

The minor spirit spun round and round near her back, bobbing up and down as if pleading something.

"Hey, can I have a moment?"

At Emilia's call, Sylphy turned with a puzzled look. Emilia walked right up to her and stared at the minor spirit insisting on its presence at the height of Sylphy's chest.

"What's wrong?"

"Sorry, just a moment."

The minor spirit, not manifested physically, was invisible to everyone here but Emilia. So its desperate appeal was something only Emilia could understand.

Which was why Sylphy's eyes went wide with shock when Emilia suddenly reached a hand toward her chest.

"Huh? Wh-wh-what?"

"Wait, just be still for a moment. I'm checking something."

"Checking—checking what...?"

Sylphy's face flushed as she asked, unable to hide her surprise.

Emilia answered her with a serious expression.

"Your heartbeat."

"—!"

"I'm a Spirit Arts user. My proper Contract partner is taking a bit of a break right now, but I can still talk with minor spirits. I had one check everyone's body, and it says only your heartbeat is strange."

"My... heartbeat...?"

Sylphy caught her breath.

Hers was the face of someone told something unbelievable. Her shock was only natural—right after hearing an overview of the "Lion's Heart," she'd been told there was something abnormal about her own heart.

And with matters having come this far, there was only one reason for it—

"How awful... Regulus has been fusing his own heart with the heart of whichever woman he decides to call his bride...!"

"To begin with, it was real impressive of you to lecture me so high and mighty, but I have absolutely no idea where you got the hope that your stalling would ever bear fruit. I don't know what kind of head lets you figure out my Authority in the first place, but if you did figure it out, why in the world would you still feel like fighting?"

Looking down at the blood-soaked Subaru, Regulus's cheeks pulled upward in a triumphant sneer.

Collapsed on the ground, leaning against fallen debris, Subaru gasped in pain, half his face stained crimson with fresh blood.

"Ah—guh..."

"You had yourself quite the field day running around however you pleased, but when you finally go down, it's really anticlimactic, isn't it. Well, of course. It has to be that way. Considering the gap between you and me, this is just the proper outcome settling into its proper place, that's all. Now I can finally stop wasting my attention on this unreasonable business."

Regulus walked over to the fallen Subaru, grinding an inconvenient stone underfoot with his heel—as if flaunting that his Authority remained intact.

"Really, don't you think you're being a bit presumptuous? Plenty of people have come at me before, all fired up to take me down just like you. But not one of them ever laid a hand on me. That's what happens when you desire more than your own Vessel can hold—it's just the natural order of things. You understand, don't you?"

The Sin Archbishop of Greed, speaking of selflessness, declared his contempt for those who let excessive desire consume them.

To desire bred pointless conflict; to desire bred endless hunger; to desire bred the ultimate cruelty.

That was exactly why selflessness alone was noble.

To wish to live in honest poverty, to impose virtue upon oneself—that was the highest good.

"You could just be satisfied with what you have now, but no, you go and want things beyond your own worth and ruin yourselves. Every single one of you, and not one of you ever learns a thing. You people really are hopeless creatures."

As if lamenting it, Regulus ran a hand through his white hair, shaking his head as though drunk on tragedy.

And yet, there was no falsehood in the sorrow packed into his voice. Regulus, at least, was genuinely grieving and lamenting the foolishness of Subaru and everyone else who wasn't himself.

Never once realizing that this was the self-righteous voice of a lonely, self-appointed omnipotent being.

"Before... I die... your power..."

"Huh? Oh, you mean the classic 'one last souvenir for the road to the afterlife'? Fancy you knowing such an old-fashioned phrase. Is that the kind of useless accumulated knowledge that let you reach me?"

Regulus laughed at Subaru, who, gasping his last breaths, only wished to confirm the truth about the Authority. Now that he'd lost the strength to keep scurrying about, he was nothing more than a pitiful thing waiting to be crushed.

"Well, since you're asking so nicely. I suppose I'll give you one last reward for trying so hard, little man. I'll tell you that everything you desperately stalled for was completely pointless."

"Point... less...?"

"It's simple. My heart, the one you and that girl are searching for, is indeed being kept by one of my brides. —But neither I nor any of them are aware of which bride is holding it. Rights are equal, love is divided evenly, and the burden of duty is fair to all."

At Subaru's stunned expression, Regulus shrugged. "Well, isn't that only right?"

"When you take multiple wives, treating them all equally is only natural. It's precisely because I can carry that out properly—down to every clever little detail—that I'm entitled to exercise that right. Which means I'm constantly proving my love for all of them, at the risk of my own life, no less."

"What do you mean, the bride isn't even aware she's holding your heart?"

"It's not a difficult story. —There's no such thing as a person who's constantly, endlessly aware of their own heartbeat day in and day out, is there?"

At the way Regulus's mouth split into a grin, Subaru understood.

He understood the vile method by which Regulus hid his heart.

Simple, effective, and above all, impossible to guard against.

"You fused your own heart... into your brides' hearts...!"

"Managing a husband's assets is a wife's duty. But you see, I'm a selfless person. I don't possess any of the excess, worthless assets that people like you tend to carry around in bulk. So what I entrust to my wives is my very existence itself... isn't that just beautiful? The very embodiment of marital love."

—Grotesque.

Regulus's Authority was knowingly monstrous. And yet he carried it out with no malice, no remorse, believing it a perfectly natural act.

When he'd sent Emilia off toward the church, Subaru had given her several hypotheses about how the "Lion's Heart" might be hidden. But even he hadn't accounted for this particular pattern.

Though, that's not to say there was no way to break it—

"In theory, that's... but there's no way Emilia could ever do it."

If everything Regulus had just smugly lectured about was the whole of the "Lion's Heart" Authority, then there was a way to break it. If he could just get word to Emilia, her strength would let her carry it out easily enough.

The problem wasn't whether it was possible, but whether it should be done.

—It meant choosing which lives to keep and which to discard.

"Huh? Hey, you..."

Regulus made a baffled face at Subaru, who had risen with a look of pure loathing and was now glaring at him.

A moment ago Subaru had been swept up in the city-leveling attack and had looked at death's door—yet now that he'd stood up, he was ostentatiously brushing the dirt off his knees. At Regulus's suspicion, Subaru raised his eyebrows as if just remembering something.

"Playing dead—ah, no, wrong. Playing about to die. A flying rock cut my forehead, so I figured I'd try it out a little."

Smear some blood on your face, act like you're in a bit of pain, and this is what you get.

Subaru smiled at Regulus, who had fallen for it completely.

"I believed in you. I knew that the moment you found an enemy on the edge of death, you'd absolutely gloat and prattle on high and mighty like an idiot."

"—!!!"

"Whoa there!"

Regulus kicked off, his body charging straight in.

The speed was fierce, a burst of movement worlds apart from his otherwise amateurish footwork. In this rare, split-second moment, Regulus possessed an acceleration that had even shocked Reinhard.

But the trick behind it had only remained invisible up until the Authority itself had been revealed.

"Ngh—!"

He unleashed the power he'd stored in his right leg, launching his body left without hesitation, exactly as he'd already decided. Regulus's aim to blow Subaru's body away was thrown off by the weakness of only being able to "fly in a straight line."

In essence, Regulus's momentary superhuman burst was an application of time-stopping to his own body. His Authority was to cut his body's time off from the world so it received no outside interference. Taken to its logical extreme, that meant freedom from gravity, from air resistance, from the very laws of inertia.

The reason he didn't do it constantly was that some kind of limitation existed—

"—Ngh, you bastaaard!"

"I'm the one who wants to scream, way more than you...! I have to get word to Emilia somehow..."

He had to convey the answer—the whereabouts of Regulus's heart.

Convey it, and then—would she choose? That method—

To save the city, he had to tell Emilia.

"E-mili...a..."

Facing off against the enraged Regulus, Subaru looked toward the direction of the church where Emilia should be.

Amid the collapsed cityscape, the half-ruined church stood on a street that had, just barely, escaped the reach of Regulus's aftershocks.

—And in the next instant, that place was enveloped in a pale blue light.

The fusion of a bride's heart with Regulus's own.

At the conclusion Emilia had reached, a wave of unease swept through the brides all at once. Sylphy's shock, in particular, was greater than the rest—she was the one whose heartbeat Emilia had just confirmed, the one who'd just been told she was, at this very moment, sharing Regulus's pulse.

"M-my heart, fused with..."

When Emilia let go, Sylphy staggered backward, her face pale. The woman standing behind her caught her back with worried hands—

"Don't touch me!"

In the next instant, Sylphy's voice cracked as she shook the arm off.

She held out a hand as if warding off the other brides, then turned her eyes to Emilia.

"Are you certain?"

"...The minor spirit says it's unnatural. I felt something off about your heartbeat too. It sounds... doubled. Like two overlapping."

"—"

Sylphy pressed a hand to her own chest and closed her eyes, as if confirming Emilia's words for herself. She swallowed hard at the speed, weight, and rhythm of that beat, then let out a long, deep breath.

"So that's how it is... just how far does that man have to trample on people's hearts before he's satisfied...!"

"Wait, what are you doing!?"

With a bitterly dry smile, Sylphy went deeper into the church. In the building half-destroyed by Reinhard's blade, shards of the ornamental glass that had once been fitted into the skylight lay scattered everywhere.

Sylphy picked up one of the glass shards and turned back around.

"You understand, don't you? That man's whole scheme. He's forcing his own weakness onto other people, meaning to drive them into an impossible decision."

"A decision... don't tell me..."

"If you want to stop his heart, the only way is to stop mine, the one entrusted with it. In that man's own words, I suppose it'd be something like, 'not even death can part husband and wife'."

Sylphy said this in a listless voice, toying with the glass shard. Emilia understood the meaning at once, and in the same instant understood everything else—Sylphy's resolve, and the full extent of Regulus's malice.

"Wait, no! There has to be something—there has to be some other way..."

"Do you really think a convenient method like that exists? With our hearts fused into one, there's no way anywhere to stop only his beat without stopping mine too... Besides—"

"Don't you dare give up on your own like this! If I let something like that happen, then what was it all for... what did I even leave the forest for...!"

Another sacrifice, about to happen.

Because of Emilia's helplessness and ignorance, another sacrifice was about to be born right in front of her. Just like everyone in the forest. Just like Fortuna, like Joose. Everyone but Emilia spending their lives to fill in what her own reach couldn't cover.

"Being dragged off by that man and called his bride... they were painful days."

Desperately, Emilia searched for some other way.

Meanwhile, Sylphy's heart only grew calmer, steadily and quietly hardening her resolve toward the ending she believed inevitable.

"I was just desperate not to touch that man's temper. I overlooked whatever atrocities he committed, telling myself I'd somehow protect the new brides he brought in, the girls in the same position as me. Protect them the way I was protected, the way the ones before me did for me..."

The true reason Sylphy always took the lead, stepping forward for everything as if she were the brides' representative, was revealed at last.

Someone had come before her too—someone who stood at the very front for the sake of the women being taken by Regulus's short temper. And that will had been passed down to Sylphy, and from it, today's brides existed.

"Even if that man defiled our hearts, he never laid a hand on our bodies... I'm sure if both had been defiled, we couldn't have endured it. So up until today, I endured, endured, endured, endured his every word, his every sound, his every act... and yet!"

Having said that much, Sylphy bit her lip and raised her head.

In her eyes were great fat tears, and an anger so hot it seemed it might scorch even those tears away.

"That man's hands had already defiled even my body! Even the one thing I thought I'd at least managed to protect—I hadn't protected it at all! We were nothing but that man's slaves, this whole time!"

She screamed through her tears, blood trickling down her hand. The glass shard, gripped too tightly, had cut into her palm; she winced at the pain, and yet her lips loosened into something almost fond.

"A woman with a scar is out of the question, so that man kills us the instant we get even a single scratch. ...This wound—this is my freedom."

"—"

"None of this is your fault. I'm grateful to you. There's surely no better way to take revenge on that man, for all these days, than this."

Sylphy smiled at Emilia, then looked at the other brides—the women who felt like her other half.

She held the glass shard in her right hand, her left pressed against her own chest.

"I'm sure that when I die, the heart will just move to someone else. It has to. There's no way that man would show favoritism to just me. There's no way he wouldn't be obsessing over some incomprehensible fixation like that."

"...You're probably right."

Someone—one of the other brides—murmured it quietly.

As if agreeing with Sylphy's words, the bride who'd answered stepped out from the group. The woman with long brown hair also bent to pick up a glass shard that had fallen near Sylphy's feet.

"I've thought about dying, more times than I can count. Living like this, you can't even call it living. If that's how it is, wouldn't it be better to go to the world after death, where my whole family is..."

"But I could never do it, because I didn't want to die. Even if death would free me from the suffering, I couldn't feel anything good about it."

"But if dying... if this life of mine can land one blow against that man... if dying isn't for nothing..."

One after another, the brides stepped forward and picked up shards of glass.

They looked at the sharp points as if their hope itself lay there. As if, prompted by Emilia's words, the brides had finally found their own hope—found the place to spend their lives.

"Thank you. I'm grateful to you. —I can guarantee, with absolute certainty, that man has no other brides besides the ones here. So please."

"—"

"Please, make sure it reaches him. Make sure our anger reaches that man. —This is something I can only ask of you, the one he wanted and yet who was still able to refuse him."

Sylphy's plea was spun out in a gentle voice.

Every one of the brides, holding shards of the stained glass—the shattered symbol of the church where marriages were sealed—chose their own ending.

They turned the sharp points toward their own pale throats, ready to drive them home in one motion and end their lives—

"Wait."

That desperate act was stopped by a single word from Emilia.

Emilia, who had kept silent this whole time, spoke a word with weight behind it—both in the spiritual sense and the literal, physical one.

Hands of ice rose from the ground and seized the brides' arms, halting the motion of the glass shards and stopping them from taking their own lives.

At what Emilia had done, Sylphy's eyes went wide, and her lips trembled.

"Please, understand! Your—your feelings make me happy. But this is the only way..."

Nothing but death would be repaid in kind.

Nothing but death could land a blow on that man, on Regulus.

That was Sylphy's conclusion—the brides' conclusion.

Emilia understood their anguished answer too—to stop their own hearts. And she had thought and thought and thought of some way to refute it.

So—

"I'm sorry. That's not it."

"Huh...?"

"If it were Subaru, maybe he'd have thought of something. ...But I'm not smart, so no matter how much—how soooo much I thought about it, I couldn't come up with anything... so..."

A pale blue radiance danced around the murmuring Emilia.

Drawing on Mana, the flickering light was minor spirits entering a visible state. A vast, overwhelming number of minor spirits manifesting, enough to fill the ruined building—it was a sight fantastical enough to seem almost divine, and Sylphy and the others held their breath.

"I'll stop your heartbeats myself. —Because stabbing your throat with something like that won't let you slip away peacefully, not easily."

When Emilia raised her hand, the minor spirits began to glow in answer, and blue snow began to fall within the church.

The snow settled thinly around the brides, and where it touched their pale skin, it turned into white crystals.

The gentlest, cruelest magic Emilia was capable of.

"—I'm sorry. This is the only way I have."

"Don't apologize."

Grasping Emilia's intent, Sylphy let out a breath.

The brides' hearts were one. They gazed at Emilia, her voice tight with resolve, and spoke together with a single voice.

"Thank you."

"—!"

And then, a pale blue aurora enveloped the church—

Pillars of ice, thrusting skyward as if to pierce the heavens, froze the church solid.

The air let out a groaning shriek, and even the sky itself withered white. Just how much sorrow did those rising ice pillars carry within them?

Surely, only the creator of this scene could know.

"...Emilia."

A peerless spectacle of ice swirling with overwhelming Mana—nothing other than the scene Emilia herself had created.

The one certain way to defeat the "Lion's Heart"—stopping the heartbeat, taking with it the life of the bride whose heart had been fused with his. Subaru had thought of that method too. He hadn't been able to think of any other.

And yet, it was a method he couldn't bring himself to carry out.

Subaru had even resigned himself to the fact that Emilia wouldn't be able to reach that conclusion at all.

But looking at this scene, the answer was plain.

—Emilia had chosen. That was the answer.

"Hey, hey, now that's a bit..."

Regulus, looking in the same direction as Subaru, twitched his cheek as if imagining the result those ice pillars had produced. He, too, surely understood that his own brides had been standing right where those pillars now rose—and understood the meaning of that sight.

And so—

"You! This is what you wanted to do!? Is this something a human being does!? Ripping away, on a selfish whim, the very thing a person could never stop loving! Just... just how heartless do you have to be to do something like this—!?"

Stamping his foot, Regulus screamed at the blood-soaked Subaru.

The cobblestones shattered beneath his shoe soles, the ground warping so violently it created the illusion that the whole city itself was tilting. Stepping forward with all his weight, Regulus jabbed a finger at Subaru.

"Are you satisfied? Are you satisfied now!? Taking the lives of innocent wives who did nothing wrong, just to kill me, and then celebrating over it—what kind of person—"

Regulus's body, mid-vile-tirade, venting his grief over what had been taken from him, was blasted off his feet.

The cause was an ice lance, launched by the girl who had just emerged from the church entrance down the street.

The ice spear, gaining tremendous speed and spin, struck Regulus's motionless body again and again, sending him flying like a rag doll, while more lances still chased after him mid-flight.

The momentum never let up, and Regulus's body was driven straight into the canal, which froze over with a violent crack, forming an ice statue around him.

"—Take that as a letter of severance, from your brides."

Treading over frost-dusted streets, silver hair dancing, Emilia returned to the battlefield.

She turned her eyes to the ruined streets and to Subaru running toward her, narrowing her violet eyes at how battered he looked.

"Subaru, that wound—!"

"I'm fine on my end! It's just a wide cut, that's all, made it look flashier than it is. More importantly, at the church... how are the brides?"

"...They all wanted to take Regulus down. So..."

Emilia lowered her eyes, her attention flicking briefly toward the frozen church.

That reaction alone was enough to convey that the answer Emilia had chosen wasn't hers alone to bear. And the weight of that choice was something Subaru would have to carry too.

"But—but this should mean the 'Lion's Heart' effect is broken now, right? His invincibility trick's run out of tricks..."

"No. It doesn't seem like it's that simple."

"What?"

As Subaru started to reason through what should have been gained in exchange for the sacrifice born of that choice, Emilia gave a faint shake of her head.

Right after that surprising reaction, the ice statue in the canal behind them cracked.

The fissures spread rapidly, reaching even into the canal whose water had been dammed up. The collapse linked canal and street, and a surging flood of water swept in, soaking the ground beneath Subaru and Emilia's feet.

"Truly, laughably arrogant, hopelessly vulgar, appallingly incompetent, unbelievably shameless, hopelessly inferior...!"

Splitting the inrushing current, the madman touched down—drenched in water and yet not wet at all.

Not a single stain marred his white tuxedo, his white hair wasn't even mussed by the wind, and his pale face bore not so much as a bead of sweat, let alone a wound. His very existence was a daydream—or rather, a waking nightmare, through and through.

"So, what are you going to do about it? How exactly do you two plan to take responsibility? You went and acted so high and mighty, saying whatever you pleased, and it all ended in a completely misguided, colossal failure, leaving nothing behind but a pile of sacrifices—how are you going to make up for a situation like this!?"

Even in his fury, the "Lion's Heart's" effect remained fully in force on Regulus, unchanged.

There wasn't the slightest sign that Emilia's fierce assault, as intense as it had been, had dealt him any damage at all.

"No way that's right! You went and blabbed all about the 'Lion's Heart' Authority yourself... there's no way you've got the brains to bluff your way through something like that!"

"Do you really think I'm generous enough to let something that unforgivable just slide? I'll have you know, not violating another person's heart is the bare minimum manners any human being should have without ever being taught it!? No one has the right to look down on anyone else, so how can you do something so brainlessly stupid? Are you missing not just a heart, but a brain too?"

At Subaru's unintentionally provocative words, Regulus made a mocking face and tapped a finger against his own white-haired head.

"I bet that easy little harlot just miscounted, didn't she? Not even remembering how many lives she's taken—that's exactly the mindset of a slaughterer. Something's seriously wrong with her."

"You've got some nerve saying that, of all people..."

"Don't try to shift the argument. Whatever I may or may not have done up till now has nothing to do with the fact that woman is a monster. Don't run from the crimes you've committed. Don't look away. Shelving your own sins and blaming the other person instead—aren't you ashamed of yourself as a human being?"

On and on, endlessly, Regulus burned with misdirected fury, condemning others.

Regulus Corneas existed without ever once questioning the twisted nature of his own being.

Just how much contradiction could he pack into a single continuous stream of speech before he was satisfied?

Conversation with Regulus wore down the nerves. It was enough to make one wonder, facing a Sin Archbishop, whether staying sane was itself the wrong approach.

"But... damn it, this is a miscalculation."

Even after eliminating every candidate who could have been the heart's substitute, Regulus's invincibility hadn't been undone.

It shouldn't have been wrong in theory. And through this life-or-death game of provocation, Subaru had also confirmed that Regulus wasn't clever enough to deceive him skillfully through words and behavior.

Regulus had no ability to deceive others, no talent for smooth-talking anyone.

He was completely devoid of empathy. In his world, there was only himself. His marriages were mere imitation, his statements pure platitude, his combat that of an amateur, his very being pure evil—truly, a "petty king."

"Fifty-three people..."

Beside the shuddering Subaru, Emilia suddenly murmured.

Up until now, she'd shown no reaction at all to the outrageous claims, the abuse, the unbearable resentment pouring from Regulus's mouth. She'd only said that one line.

"Huh? What? What did you just say?"

"Fifty-three. That's the number of women you forced to your side. Miscount? I would never do such a thing. I don't miscount the number of lives."

"Hmm. And? So? What exactly are you trying to say?"

Regulus brushed off Emilia's quiet correction with open mockery, sticking a finger in his ear in a blatant show of contempt.

It was a gesture so infuriating that even Subaru, the foremost expert in provocation, wanted to applaud out of sheer spite. But in response, Emilia turned to face him instead. To the breathless Subaru, she shook her head.

"It's all right, Subaru. I already understand everything."

"Understand, what do you..."

"Besides, I'm soooo angry right now... I'm not going to forgive him. Not anymore."

Subaru, taken aback, watched.

Emilia, quietly wiping the expression from her soft features, lowering her voice. And in pretending to freeze her emotions like that, she was more furious than he had ever seen her before.

With a cold flame smoldering behind her frozen eyes, Emilia touched her own chest.

And then, she said:

"Regulus's heart is right here—right now, inside my own chest."
