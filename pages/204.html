Arc 4 Chapter 38: "Caterpillar"

—The first thing to snag at the edge of his consciousness was the steady sound of dripping water.

Droplets fell at even intervals, tapping out a steady rhythm, and with every pulse they sent into the quiet room, his consciousness—sunk so deep—was drawn little by little back toward waking.

His sleeping brain resumed its work, and nerves barely back online felt the blood begin to circulate through his whole body. He twisted, groaned, and tried to sit up—and couldn't.

His limbs, trying to brace against the ground, refused to obey him, and the only action left to him was to press his cheek against the cold floor. At the same time, he tried to rely on his returning senses to feel out his surroundings, only to find his entire field of vision painted over in darkness.

—Had his eyes been crushed!?

He nearly leapt to that hasty conclusion given his condition, but before the horror of it could set in, he noticed the pressure binding tightly around his face, his eyes, and abandoned the thought. He quickly judged that he must be blindfolded instead—and only belatedly registered how abnormal that state itself was.

His eyes hadn't been crushed—but they had been covered instead. And likewise, the reason he couldn't move was the same: his arms and legs were bound tight.

He could feel the texture of something like thin rope at his wrists and ankles, and with his hands bound behind his back, even struggling to work them loose was difficult.

"Wh—what is..!?"

Fortunately, he hadn't been gagged, so his voice came out fine. But being able to run his mouth while literally unable to lift a hand or foot didn't amount to much more than talk. And it was equally true that whoever had put him in this state seemed unlikely to be friendly enough to be satisfied with mere conversation.

The bewildering situation he found himself in, and the fear of being unable to gather any information about his surroundings—holding both of those tangled together, Subaru held his breath and set his mind racing.

He organized what he knew of his current state. Both eyes: covered. Limbs: bound, no sign of coming loose. Voice: usable. Shout for help? At best, whoever had tied him up would just come running. Anything nearby he could use to free himself? Searching around was next to impossible when he could barely crawl. A throbbing pain at his right temple—the moment he became aware of it, it began insisting on its presence with a dull, pulsing ache.

"My head... it hurts..."

Becoming conscious of the pain at his temple brought back to Subaru what had happened to him right before he'd lost consciousness.

He had Returned by Death, left the tomb, and gone to confront Roswaal about the newly uncovered facts and his own suspicions. There, driven into a fury by one unforgivable remark, he'd lunged at the man—only to be beaten down by Garfiel, who'd been keeping watch over him.

No—"beaten down" wasn't even strong enough a word for it. He'd been crushed by an overwhelming, one-sided force. A single restraining blow from Garfiel had smashed his head in, and Subaru had thought he'd simply sunk straight into death.

"If I died, then this would have to be a Return by Death, but..."

If that were true, Subaru's restart point should be the tomb, right after the Trial. Applying the rules that had held so far, his Return by Death should have pulled his soul back to that same room he'd left barely an hour before, right before he'd headed to Roswaal's bedroom.

At the very least, as far as he could remember, the only time he'd ever been left lying around abducted and confined like this was when he'd flipped over the low table in imitation of some TV anime, sent his father into a real rage, and gotten tossed into the storage closet as punishment.

But that memory was from around the time he was barely old enough for elementary school, so it wasn't remotely plausible that he'd Returned by Death that far back. And if the restart point hadn't changed, then the only conclusion was that he'd been tied up like this immediately after Returning—which made no sense at all. Which left only one conclusion.

"I... failed to die."

Between the lingering pain in his head and his current circumstances, that was where his reasoning had to land.

He'd done about as violent a thing to Roswaal as could be done. Taking that into account, being treated this way was only morally fair. Not that his feelings had made peace with it.

"—Ain't it somethin', how quick you got a grip on your situation."

Just as Subaru finished piecing together an understanding of his situation, as though timed to it, a voice dropped down from above. He lifted his face and turned it toward where the voice seemed to have come from, blind though he was. And from the tone of it, he knew exactly who it belonged to.

"...Garfiel."

"Got that right too. Glad to see your head's workin' fine, more or less. Went and hit ya a bit harder than I meant to, my bad, my bad."

Called by name, Garfiel offered his apology to the blindfolded Subaru. Though his tone carried none of the regret the words implied, and he went on, "Didn't think—"

"—that a little pat like that would nearly do you in, is all. Heard you were Emilia-sama's knight and figured you'd be able to take a bit more than that. Guess I overestimated ya."

"Sorry to disappoint. I'm not the brawn type, I sell myself on brains around here. ...Where the hell am I?"

He answered Garfiel's mocking with a quip of his own, then cut to the point. Hearing it, Garfiel let out a short breath through his nose.

"Relax, you're still inside the Sanctuary, no doubt about that. Just not the cathedral, the tomb, or any of the houses we lend out to guests, though."

"So you've got a room set aside just for holding people prisoner. I get better safe than sorry, but having a facility like this prepped... honestly, that's a little much."

"Take it up with whoever built the damn thing, bad taste and all. And you, of all people, could probably go tell 'em to their face."

Garfiel expressed his displeasure, sounding rather genuinely irritated. Something in his words snagged at Subaru, and he frowned.

"What do you mean, to their face...?"

"Don't play dumb, reekin' of Witch's stink like that. You must've met her in the tomb. Otherwise, why would that smell of yours have gone and spiked so sudden?"

"Met her in the tomb...?"

Something in Garfiel's words snagged at him.

The tomb. The Trial. The person he'd met there. There was a blank space. He'd parted with his parents in the Trial, then finally made his way to an empty schoolhouse, and there—

"The Witch...!"

—He had encountered Echidna. The Witch of Greed.

Tracing that sense of wrongness back to what he'd lost, Subaru brought Echidna's existence back into his mind once more. The sensation was one he'd had in the previous loop too—an anomaly so strange that, now that he'd remembered, he found it almost funny that he'd forgotten her again in the first place.

It was probably the result of some "condition" imposed on him during that first encounter, he guessed, but the fact that it couldn't be overcome even by Returning by Death was still startling.

Even a Return by Death that carried memories across couldn't land an effective blow against a trick that interfered directly with memory itself. In other words, every time Subaru Returned by Death, he would forget Echidna and have to start over from remembering her all over again.

"Wait—is that what Roswaal meant, when he said I was forgetting something...?"

With the Witch's existence resurfacing in his mind, he became conscious of that possibility, but he couldn't help thinking it was a bit too hasty a conclusion. After all, though he'd remembered Echidna, he couldn't find anything in their conversation that might break open his current situation.

Judging by the way Roswaal had spoken, the "something" he wanted Subaru to remember had to be something that, on its own, would reveal even Roswaal's own unreadable true intentions.

Though that was assuming the complete edition of the Gospel he possessed was accurate in the first place.

"You gone quiet on me—that mean you got somethin' to hide, or somethin' comin' to mind?"

"I'm not some high school girl who dies if she stops talking, y'know. I can go quiet and think a thing or two over. Though right now, a thing or two isn't nearly enough thinking to cover it."

There was simply too much to deal with—Subaru's brain alone barely had enough cells to spare.

Emilia. Rem. Beatrice.—Right about the point where a line of women's names started stacking up, he felt the urge to call out his own situation for how it looked. And beyond that, the Sanctuary, Elsa, Roswaal's true intentions, the Gospel. And then—

"Garfiel, too, huh."

Persuading him and winning his cooperation was an indispensable piece of the blueprint Subaru had drawn up for saving the mansion. When it came to driving off Elsa, Subaru probably couldn't muster fighting strength beyond his. He'd tasted with his own eyes—and felt it in his own skull—just how reliable Garfiel's strength would be against her.

"...I'm pretty sure my head got smashed in by you punching or kicking me. What happened with that, exactly?"

"Ha. Took ya a while to finally bring that up. Wasn't smashed in, just caved in a bit. Didn't need you up and dyin' on me if I just left it, so I fixed it up some."

"Fixed it—who did?"

"Who else could've pulled somethin' like that off right then and there, 'sides me?"

There was a note of pride showing off in his tone, and Subaru found himself at a loss for words.

He'd never once imagined that Garfiel—rough and brutish as he so often seemed—had mastered healing magic. Subaru twisted his still-bound body.

"My head's not, like, square now or pointy somewhere it shouldn't be, is it?"

"Next time I cave it in, I'll make sure to hold it in shape while I fix it up."

An exasperated sigh—probably paired with a shrug, judging by the reply—and with that, Subaru at least confirmed that his body had escaped a state of near-death.

That settled, the next issue was what Garfiel's actual motive was in doing so.

"Feels kinda weird to thank you for fixing me up when you're the one who caved it in, but... what's your angle here, exactly?"

"Beats me, what d'you think my angle is?"

"Given the situation, from where you were standing, knockin' me flat back there was probably the right call, so I've got no complaint about that. I flew off the handle and went too far, and there's a whole mountain of ways I could say I don't not think it wasn't a good thing you stopped me."

"Man, that's one hell of a complicated mood you're in. I get wantin' to smash that smirkin' face of his in, but it's the old hags who'd be in trouble if that happened."

It seemed Garfiel harbored no small amount of ill will toward Roswaal himself. Still, for both Subaru and Garfiel alike, they relied on Roswaal's existence too much to simply cut him loose on feeling alone.

For Subaru, Roswaal was his backing in this world; for Garfiel and the others, he was the Sanctuary's administrator—both bound by necessity.

Still, the fact that the first thing Garfiel brought up when discussing it was Ryuzu—and by extension, the livelihoods of the Sanctuary's residents—felt oddly out of character for him.

At the same time, it made him nod along with the assessment of Garfiel that Frederica, his own relative, had offered in the previous loop. In fact, he'd heard that Garfiel had chosen not to leave the Sanctuary with his sister precisely out of consideration for the feelings of the residents who'd be left behind.

"Ryuzu-san and the rest matter to you, so Roswaal's existence is indispensable to protecting their way of life. Without a guy people call a 'Demi-Human fetishist,' the Sanctuary—where the residents can't even leave—wouldn't be able to survive. Is that it?"

"Don't go layin' out embarrassin' guesses with that know-it-all face. Who said anythin' about some sentimental reason like that. I'm just here 'cause I can't go outside, is all—"

"Frederica, your own blood relative, could go outside just fine—but you can't, Garf?"

It was information he'd only just picked up in the previous loop. Playing that card, Subaru tried to gauge Garfiel's reaction. But the response that came back was more violent than anything he'd imagined.

"—!"

Wind howled, and in the next instant a tremendous cracking boom sounded right beside Subaru's head where he lay. The sound of exploding air rang out, and before his brain could even process that it was Garfiel closing the distance in an instant, the ground beneath that stomping foot shattered, and the shape of the room itself changed.

The floor bucked as though it were bending, and Subaru, letting out a small shriek, was sent flying by the shockwave. Unable even to brace himself, he tumbled across the hard ground, and before the momentum of his roll could die out, he slammed into a wall and was forced to a stop.

His brain rattled, the air was wrung out of his lungs by the impact against his back, and the back of his head, which had struck the wall, screamed with pain. Subaru coughed, drool spilling from the corner of his mouth. At the sight of him—

"Who the hell told you somethin' like that, you little shit. Was it Frederica runnin' her mouth... no, there's no way she'd say somethin' like that. We cut ties clean, brother and sister, right when she left."

"That's just a figure of speech, it's not like the actual blood in your body gets cut off—"

"I'm sayin' there's somethin' off about you bringin' that up now, in this exact moment. If you were gonna use it, there were plenty of other chances to bring it up."

Even as Subaru groaned, he talked back, and Garfiel's intuition sharpened unpleasantly. It was as though he suspected Subaru had picked up some fact he shouldn't have known, the moment his eyes were off him.

Which, in fact, wasn't off the mark at all—it was a dead-on guess—but the line of reasoning that had led him there was far too straight and narrow.

As if it were either an abnormally sharp instinct, or else something he had "reason to suspect" himself.

"Don't tell me... you too... are you?"

—The instant that possibility occurred to him, Subaru's voice could no longer hide its trembling.

"—————"

To Subaru's question, its subject left unstated, the only reply was an unsettling silence.

In real time it was probably no more than a few seconds, but to Subaru right now, that silence felt like an eternity.

No answer came. Why wasn't he speaking? Subaru's question had been far too vague to begin with. It would have been better if Garfiel had just plainly said he didn't understand, or driven a kick into him. If there'd been that kind of blunt, simple reaction, Subaru could still have clung to it.

And yet—

"Me too... huh."

A sharp footstep. Garfiel's footfalls striking the flagstone floor drew closer, and Subaru sensed him crouch down right beside him. Subaru lifted just his neck off the floor, and Garfiel, likely bringing his face in close, bared his sharp fangs as he said—

"What made you think that, huh? Oi."

"Quit talkin' in a way that stirs up such a bad feeling. I'm not even makin' sense, right? You can just flat out deny it, easy... y'know?"

"Don't say that in a voice like you're about to cry, man."

Letting Subaru's near-begging wash over him, Garfiel said it in a deliberately evasive tone. Subaru, frustrated by his non-answer, was already a mess on the inside.

He'd wanted a denial from a Garfiel too sharp for his own good. And yet, all that came back were meaningfully loaded words that only confirmed the premonition in Subaru's chest.

Beatrice, Roswaal—one after another, people he'd counted among his allies had turned out to be holding Gospels of their own. For Subaru right now, a third person turning up like this wouldn't be strange in the least.

"So...! I'm saying, maybe you know too...!"

"—Ah, that's what you mean. Wonder where I gave it away."

"—!?"

His throat closed with shock, and Subaru pictured Garfiel in his blocked-out vision.

The tone of his voice, the languid sigh—everything about it was so far removed from the Garfiel Subaru knew. Yet, without moving away, he left more suggestive words hanging.

"You look surprised, but there ain't nothin' strange about it. I'm a resident who's been in the Sanctuary forever, been dealin' with all this for a long, long time. Plenty of chances came my way—way more than once or twice."

"B-but... you, you're supposed to hate the Witch, aren't you? Enough to overreact the way you do... and yet—"

"Ah, yeah, that's right. I hate the Witch, I'm suspicious of you too, reekin' of Witch's stink, and I don't look kindly on Emilia-sama bein' half-witch either. But that don't mean I think what that thing says is wrong. At the very least, it's true that I learned somethin' I wanted to know."

"Something you wanted to know...?"

"—Ain't got a reason to tell you that. Why don't you go ask her yourself. Though a chance like that might never come round again for you."

He spat the words out, and Subaru sensed Garfiel rise to his feet. Then he moved away from Subaru's side, and, it seemed, put a hand on the door—the exit of this holding room.

There came the creak of a wooden door, and Subaru called out "Hey!" to Garfiel as he moved away.

"Wait! ...Wh-what's gonna happen to me. I mean, what's happening to me right now?"

"Considerin' that bastard Roswaal nearly got himself killed, this is soft as hell already. For now, till there's a result, you're gettin' kept tied up and held under house arrest."

House arrest—a word he'd only just heard the other day. And from Roswaal's own mouth, no less. That Subaru should end up in the very state Roswaal had supposedly been in, and precisely because he'd assaulted him, was almost too ironic to bear.

Garfiel snorted at Subaru, left with nothing to say.

"I'll have food brought to you mornin' and night. Just don't try anythin' funny. I'll be keepin' a close eye on the one takin' care of you, too."

"I'm not even worried about... not right now, anyway! More importantly, a result? You said result, right? What result? What am I waiting for...?"

"Result—ain't it obvious?"

This time Garfiel answered Subaru's question with outright mockery, tossing the words out.

"—The result of Emilia-sama's Trial, that's what. Once she heard what you went and did, she got real fired up about makin' up for it or whatever."

—With Garfiel gone from the room, Subaru, left alone in his cell, sank into a sea of thought.

The words Garfiel had left behind just before leaving wouldn't leave his head.

To clear Subaru's name, Emilia had apparently roused herself and was throwing herself at the Trial. If the Sanctuary could be freed, the thinking seemed to go, that achievement could paper over the scandal Subaru had caused.

It was such an Emilia way of thinking—one that didn't doubt Subaru in the slightest.

"But, in that moment, I..."

However much a fit of rage it had been, Subaru had, at the very least, seriously choked Roswaal's throat.

With these two hands, he had firmly gripped a human throat, closed off the windpipe with a trained grip strength, and put his full force into either breaking that neck or choking the life from it.

Bound behind his back and unable to move freely, his arms were still—he could feel his own fingers trembling in small, fine tremors.

Once the rage of that moment faded, all that remained in his hands was the lingering heat of the dark impulse to threaten another person's life. Nausea welled up from his empty stomach.

All the more so given that the edge of that murderous intent had been aimed at someone close to him.

"I don't... understand anything anymore..."

He had no idea anymore who to trust, what to think, or how he should act.

What was Beatrice's position, really? What about the Gospel she held? What about all the days they'd spent together?

What was Roswaal thinking? What was this complete Book of Gospel he held? What was it he wanted Subaru to remember? What was the true meaning behind his utterly incomprehensible position?

How could he get Emilia through the Trial? No—was it even right to send her toward the Trial in the first place? He no longer knew if even the starting point was correct.

What was Garfiel's game? Did he have a Gospel of his own too? Without his cooperation, driving off Elsa would be impossible. With their relationship worsening every time he Returned by Death, how could he possibly get Garfiel all the way out to the mansion?

What to do about Elsa's attack—fight her off, or flee? Why had the day of the attack shifted between the first and second loops? How had that murderer known about an escape route she had no business knowing? Who was Elsa's employer? What would it take to drive her off? He could never forgive that woman.

Beyond that, there was the Sanctuary's founding and its purpose. The nature of the remaining Trials, and why the Trial itself even existed. Echidna's purpose, sleeping there in the tomb, and how to meet her again. What had happened in the emptied Sanctuary at the end of the previous loop.

And in that final moment, what exactly had it been that killed and devoured Subaru?

"Not a single... answer comes..."

Round and round and round, unanswerable questions kept circling endlessly through his head.

His eyelids, bound painfully tight, kept his sight sealed away, and as long as he couldn't perceive the world, a person could find nowhere to direct their questions except inward, into themselves.

And since that inner self was packed with nothing but mysteries and doubts, it amounted to a dead end.

And it wasn't only the unanswerable doubts that tormented Subaru—it was also the mounting anxiety over time slipping steadily away, even as he sank into these unanswerable musings.

With his eyes covered he couldn't say anything for certain, but by Subaru's own bodily sense, it seemed highly likely that at least a full day had already passed since the night he'd choked Roswaal.

He guessed he was being held somewhere dark—probably some hideaway building in the middle of the woods—but even accounting for the fact that no light source reached him, the chill in the air was too pronounced to ignore.

Compared to the daytime temperatures he'd experienced so far, this had to be a sudden drop in warmth. And rather than dwelling on that, it would be more productive to simply assume it was nighttime, after sunset. Following that reasoning through to the conclusion that it was currently night made clear that, at minimum, a full day had passed.

Natsuki Subaru had suffered no end of injuries, gashes, and grievous wounds ever since being summoned to this other world. He'd learned, through this very body, what treatment for wounds of every grade—low, medium, high—actually felt like.

And from Subaru's own experience, having half his head caved in or cracked open was, frankly speaking, a fatal wound—and surviving it without Ferris around was, honestly, something he had to call a miracle. It probably just meant Garfiel's skill had been that good.

Basically, in this world, as long as you weren't dead, most wounds could be healed given a skilled enough healer. That said, naturally, the more severe the injury, the greater the burden of treating it.

Physical exhaustion, stamina spent on recovery—taking all that into account, Subaru's injury this time wasn't the sort he could feel healed from within a few hours of treatment starting, that is, within the same night.

Nine times out of ten, at least one night had passed. And more than anything, what backed up all this reasoning was—

"I'm... hungry..."

—the way his empty stomach, having taken in nothing this whole time he'd been asleep, kept growling painfully, insisting on its existence.

The passage of time—indistinct, unmarked ticks of it—wore away at Subaru's spirit.

How much time had passed since then, he couldn't say. Subaru's bindings hadn't changed one bit, and the time kept slipping by as he was left, on and on, abandoned in this holding room.

"—————"

With so much blank time on his hands, he tried counting the seconds himself to measure time's passage, but within an hour or so of counting his sense of it began to warp, and eventually his spirit broke, giving way to resignation.

Even if he knew the time, what good would it do him now? After all—

"It's already... hopeless, isn't it..."

Meals had already been brought to Subaru six times. If that twice-daily morning-and-evening delivery had been kept on schedule, then three full days had already passed. Three days since Subaru had woken up—which meant he'd now entered at least the fifth day since arriving at the Sanctuary.

Leaving the Sanctuary this very morning and making it to the mansion would already be cutting it razor-close to the projected time of Elsa's attack. And the instant he passed that point, the deadline would already have slipped by unseen.

To begin with, in this particular loop, Subaru had gotten off on the wrong foot from the very first button.

No amount of regret could undo the fact that he'd lunged at Roswaal, unable to hold himself back. If he hadn't given in to emotion right then, he could have drawn far more out of Roswaal about the heart of the matter—and, above all, he wouldn't have soured his relationship with Garfiel to the point of ending up locked away like this.

The result of surrendering himself to the boiling heat of his emotions was this: his present state as a helpless caterpillar.

Unable to accomplish a single thing he needed to, unable even to see the faces of the people he wanted to protect, he lay here exposing his own pathetic state, trembling as the deadline drew near.

So Subaru had already given up on this loop.

"—Look at me now, huh."

Because of the blunder Subaru had made, any means of stopping the massacre bound to happen at the mansion was gone. That, in turn, meant the survival of the four people at the mansion could now only be viewed as hopeless.

Rem. Petra. Frederica. Beatrice. Subaru would let them die knowing exactly what was coming—after denouncing Roswaal so loudly for doing precisely that.

"...I'm garbage. Just go and die already."

He wanted to die. If the restart point hadn't changed, and he could try again, Subaru could return to that night and take another crack at it. Granted, he'd still be groping in the dark, with no clue what to tackle first—but that had to be far better than this pathetic display. He could do far better. He had to.

"Otherwise, what would even be the point..."

Had he really accepted giving up, decided to bite through his own lip and watch this to its end?

Having judged that saving the mansion was impossible and that this loop's death could not be avoided, Subaru did not—immediately kill himself and Return by Death.

The situation was, indeed, as bad as it could get, and merely surviving on like this promised nothing but a hollow, meaningless future waiting for him. He ought to gamble on Returning by Death, rewind the world, and strive to win the best possible future available. But—

"If I go back without understanding anything, it'll just end up right back where it started."

At the very least, he needed to know what would happen to the Sanctuary after Subaru was gone.

What would happen to the Sanctuary once it passed the sixth day—that much, at least, he had to confirm. That was why Subaru had screamed until his throat gave out, clenched his back teeth until they nearly cracked, swallowed down the lingering attachment to a mansion still beyond his reach, and given up on this loop.

If today was the fifth day, then something should happen tomorrow.

For these three days, truly no one but Garfiel and a single caretaker had visited this holding room. The caretaker was taciturn, silently following Garfiel's instructions, and gave nothing away of their nature. Still, from the way they'd wiped down Subaru's immobile body and fed him meals, he suspected it had been a woman.

In an environment where his every move was watched, he'd been given no chance to pry any further. So he had no idea who this apparent collaborator of Garfiel's actually was.

But it was, at least, a place Emilia—rousing herself to save Subaru—couldn't find.

It was probably, in all seriousness, a secret place known only to Garfiel and his people—somewhere it would be difficult even for Subaru to send out any kind of distress signal, let alone be discovered.

Though to begin with, since the matter had already been settled between Garfiel and Roswaal that Subaru would be kept under house arrest, escaping wouldn't accomplish anything anyway.

"If Emilia can clear the Trial in order to save me, that'd be great and all, but..."

If their positions were reversed, Subaru could confidently say he'd clear the Trial for Emilia's sake. But he simply couldn't picture Emilia clearing the Trial for his sake. He couldn't believe his own existence would motivate her that much—which was, in itself, an excessively harsh underestimation of himself.

In fact, the very absence of good news over these three days probably meant that Emilia, just like in every previous loop, kept challenging the "past" again and again without ever managing to overcome it.

In other words, the situation—for the mansion, for the Sanctuary, for Subaru, for Emilia—was stalled solid, hopelessly stuck. Exactly the thing Garfiel had once shouted at Subaru in anger.

"In the end, it's me who..."

—has to do something about it.

Emilia, the mansion, the Sanctuary—every single problem that arose, he would overcome with these two hands, with the one and only weapon given to this body.

A quiet resolve. Nothing but his sheer stubborn refusal to give up kept Subaru going.

A conclusion he had reached again and again, over long, long hours of thought. Nodding to a heart he had already stared down more times than the fingers on both his hands could count, Subaru simply waited for time to pass.

—The situation finally moved when he felt the sensation of his sleeping body being shaken.

"—Mn."

Someone gripped his shoulder and shook him awake, and Subaru surfaced from a shallow sleep back into reality.

He sensed drool trailing from the corner of his mouth. Since he couldn't use his hands, he'd started to grow accustomed to the rather awkward, upper-body-straining motion of wiping it off with his shoulder, and as he wiped his wet mouth—

"Who...is it..."

His voice came out raspy, partly from just waking up, and partly because he'd wrecked his throat from too much screaming.

Ruining his throat from screaming had practically become routine by now, and he'd gotten to where he could half-ignore the blood-spitting pain of it—which was nothing to be happy about.

In answer to Subaru's call, the person who'd woken him let out a short breath. Then—

"Sorry to interrupt your little nap, but can you move, Natsuki-san?"

"Huh?"

The voice he heard belonged to someone so utterly unexpected that Subaru couldn't stop himself from blurting out a dumbfounded sound.

The other party, apparently mistaking Subaru's shock for grogginess, said "This won't do" in a quiet voice and gave his cheek a light slap.

"I'm out here crossing some dangerous bridges to come save you, you know, so get your act together. Neither of us wants this to be the end, right?"

As he spoke, he cut through the shackles binding Subaru's wrists and ankles with a blade. Testing the feel of his limbs, free for the first time in ages, Subaru roughly tore off his blindfold.

"Whoa, your hands, your feet, even your eyes look like they hurt."

In his dim, blurred, distorted field of vision stood a man, his shape indistinct, wearing an expression of clear distaste.

It was the last person he'd expect to find here—the shocking appearance of Otto Suwen.
