Arc 6 Chapter 42: "The Tower of the Dead"

The stairs, the stairs leading upward — Natsuki Subaru climbed them, slowly, one firm step at a time. Knife in one hand, hatred in his chest, madness swelling in his eyes, he walked upward.

"Kill, kill, I'll kill them. I'll definitely kill them, I'll kill them...!"

The grinding whisper was an unending litany of curses. If words held power, then the countless curses spilling from his mouth were driving Subaru's actions forward.

Every time he spoke the word "kill" with clear intent, the power dwelling in the knife seemed to grow.

"Kill, kill, kill, kill, kill, kill..."

As he muttered, his advancing vision blurred now and then. It was probably the mental strain, and the exhaustion of crawling through the desert underground for hours. There were moments his head felt strangely heavy, and Subaru shook it side to side again and again.

Now was not the time to collapse in a place like this. This place was, for Subaru, an environment crawling with dangerous individuals. An environment where he didn't know who was friend and who was foe. That made it as good as an enemy's den.

To protect himself, he had to kill. If he didn't, they would come to kill him instead.

"Kill, kill, kill, kill, kill..."

It wasn't that he wanted to kill — he had to kill. If, hypothetically, kotodama required words to be used correctly, then the word "kill" that Subaru spoke was probably wrong. If it were to truly reflect Subaru's state of mind, the word suited to this situation was not "kill."

I don't want to die — that would be more accurate.

And so, resolved in his heart to kill the very first one who appeared before him. Natsuki Subaru reached the fourth floor. And then, he saw it.

"—Ha."

Breath leaked out of him. With a shrill clatter, the knife in his hand fell to the hard floor. His fingers had stiffened, refusing to move. He merely shook his head, slack and slow, and staggered backward.

The overflowing stench of blood, and the traces of a battle so fierce it froze the very body. The stone walls and floor had been smashed, cracked open — a space with the lingering air of destruction that could only mean some overwhelming force had rampaged through it. Subaru stood frozen in place. And frozen there, he saw it.

—Shaula lay collapsed on the floor, her head crushed, her form a wretched, pitiful sight.

Shaula's corpse was in a state gruesome enough to make one want to look away.

Her long black hair, once tied up, had come loose and spread across the floor. Her limbs were flung out limply, but both arms had been severed — one at the elbow, one at the wrist — their ends nowhere to be seen. Her fair, healthy skin was carved with countless lacerations, and a tremendous amount of blood had been sprayed all around. The trail of blood continued beyond this spot — the place where her life had ended — further down the corridor, proof that the fight had been long, and had moved through several locations.

And then there was the wound to her head — surely the final, fatal blow. To call it merely a "wound" would be far too gentle a word; it was a fatal hole that had trampled the very life out of her.

As if someone had brought down a massive hammer on her head as she lay there. Nothing else could account for such a savage method — crushing the skull, scattering it apart, spilling its contents around. Something, some horrifyingly immense force, had crushed that head.

Shaula's head, crushed, obliterated. Brief as it had been, Subaru remembered how she had closed the distance without hesitation, flashing him that unguarded smile—

"—Bl—"

Dazed, appalled, despairing, Subaru had been staring blankly at the corpse when he fell to his knees on the spot. And then, unable to hold back the nausea, he vomited the contents of his stomach onto the floor all at once. The contents, churned together with stomach acid into a viscous mess, spilled out of his body along with a sour stench.

It splattered onto Shaula's crushed remains on the floor as well, adding, however unwillingly, one more indignity to her already horrific death.

"Uegh! Oreghh, goehh, ub..."

But Subaru, doubled over and retching, had no room left to spare a thought for such desecration of the dead. With his stomach clenching in pain and the nausea rising without end, he could only focus on opening his throat as wide as possible and expelling the searing sensation welling up inside him.

At last, once he had emptied his stomach completely, Subaru let his body collapse onto the floor. Spreading his arms and legs out in a great X, he glared up at the ceiling, then covered his face.

—He had witnessed a human death for the first time.

"——"

Coming face to face with a human corpse was, for Subaru, a first. In most cases, the first corpse a person encounters in life is thought to be that of an elderly relative. But Subaru's grandparents, on both his mother's and father's side, were all still alive, and he had never attended a funeral. Nor, in any other circumstance, had he ever once come across a person's death.

So this first encounter with another's death was a shock to Subaru. And all the more so, given how plainly deranged the manner of death had been.

That a person could have their life taken from them this cruelly.

"Me too, huh."

Subaru, who had been covering his face with his arm, muttered that and pushed himself upright. He wiped the vomit clinging to his mouth with his sleeve, shook his ringing head, and slowly stood, leaning on the wall for support.

Subaru, whose back had been shoved, who had been sent plummeting far down to the floors below. His own corpse, too, must surely have become a mangled lump of flesh too gruesome to look at twice. He felt a faint relief that he'd been spared the experience of seeing his own body like that.

If he had ever had to see his own death with his own eyes, he doubted he could have stayed sane.

Just understanding the fact that he had died had been enough to shatter his heart, striking it with an impact so violent it felt as if it would burst, breaking it into pieces.

"Any, way..."

Cutting off his train of thought, Subaru — taking care to keep Shaula's corpse, lying beside him, out of his line of sight — drew a certainty from the fact of her death.

The certainty that some terrible internal threat was indeed lurking within this tower. And at the same time, he now understood clearly that this threat's target was not Subaru alone, but extended to others within the tower as well.

"——"

He was sorry for Shaula, but this, for Subaru now, could even be called good news. Not knowing who had killed him, Subaru could find no peace of mind unless every suspect was eliminated. But with her death, the suspect pool had dropped from seven to one fewer. At the same time, he now had confirmation that whoever had killed Subaru was also in conflict with someone other than Subaru — or was a dangerous individual trying to eliminate everyone in the tower.

In other words, there was a chance that someone besides Subaru would kill the person who had killed Subaru. All that remained was for Subaru to kill everyone else who survived. Then, he could feel at ease.

Kill the culprit who was trying to kill him, kill the candidates who might try to kill him, and then Subaru — alone, remaining in the tower — should be able to indulge in peace.

"In that sense... the troublesome ones are Ram, and Echidna. It'd be convenient if that Julius fellow's already dead too..."

Beatrice and Meili, being children, weren't anything to think hard about in terms of ease of killing. Emilia, and Shaula too, even though she was already dead, hadn't been wary of Subaru — meaning it would have been easy to catch them off guard and kill them. But Ram, hostile toward Subaru, and the crafty Echidna, were troublesome existences. Even in catching them off guard, those two gave the impression of being the hardest targets.

As for Julius, it was hard to say, but being the only man besides Subaru, he warranted genuine wariness. Cheap as it looked, the fact that he wore a sword at his hip was also a problem. But conversely, if that sword were taken from him, he might be driven into a one-sided corner. Subaru had done kendo, so taking the sword would surely give him the advantage. And then there was—

"The one up top. That bastard."

His thoughts turned to the red-haired man occupying the upper floor of the tower under the pretext of being an examiner — and eliminating him. Subaru immediately shook his head. Eliminating that one was impossible. That was a being living by logic outside the norm, a transcendent existence not to be touched. By Natsuki Subaru's common sense, defeating that one was utterly, decisively impossible.

There were things — beings — he could not kill. The one saving grace, if any, was that it seemed unlikely that one was the being that had shoved Subaru off the edge. That one wouldn't try to kill with such a trifling method — that was a negative sort of trust he held.

"——"

Picking up the fallen knife, Subaru stepped over Shaula's corpse and headed deeper in. For a moment he hesitated over whether to search her body, but it seemed unlikely her scant clothing held anything useful, and at the same time, his conscience balked at further disgracing the dead.

She was dead. The dead were no longer enemies to Subaru. She had simply been unlucky. That was all there was to it.

He didn't do anything so admirable as put his hands together in prayer. Subaru left Shaula there and moved on, slowly, deeper into the tower, tracing the trail of destruction, creeping through the fourth floor's corridor.

The tower was so silent that not a single sound could be heard, and the silence itself felt almost deafeningly loud.

A shrill ringing scraped at his brain, and he thought he could hear the sound of blood circulating through his own body. And yet, strangely, his pulse had slowed, calm now as if the initial excitement had been a lie.

On his way up to the fourth floor, the pitch-black, simmering hatred still clung to his chest like a stain that wouldn't come off. Even now, his resolve to carry out slaughter for the sake of his own survival hadn't wavered. To stab, to gouge, to take the life of whoever he first laid eyes on — he had that resolve. And yet—

—Rounding a corner, he found Echidna's body, cut diagonally in two, and Subaru no longer knew how much use his resolve would be, in this hell.

Echidna's corpse had collapsed, bearing a wound as if some large blade had cleaved it in one stroke, from the right shoulder down to the left side.

"——"

Imagining "a large blade," the first thing that came to mind was the sword Julius had carried. It had looked cheap, very much mass-produced, but it would surely have functioned well enough to cut through a body as slight as Echidna's. The problem was that he didn't know whether Julius had any reason to cut down Echidna.

From what he'd been told, Echidna and Julius were in a master-and-retainer relationship — there was supposedly some more complicated history behind it, but at any rate, that was the closest description. Of course, if Julius were a homicidal maniac targeting the lives of everyone in the tower, none of that history would mean anything at all.

"U-ub... haa, haa..."

Leaning against the wall, defeated once again by his stomach's protest, Subaru raised his head. The state of this corpse was somewhat less gruesome than Shaula's had been. Still, it was a sight that required courage to look at directly, but the manner of death gave a starkly different impression than Shaula's had.

Whereas the fallen Shaula gave every impression of having been killed and left to lie, this corpse of Echidna's retained a measure of respect for the dead. To put it plainly, her body had been covered with a white cloth, and her eyelids had been closed.

"——"

Given the difference in how they'd been killed, there was also a large difference in how the dead had been mourned. Which of the two was the more "normal" reaction, in this case — just thinking about it was enough to feel he might lose his mind.

"Echidna's gone too..."

His breath trembling, his voice hoarse, unable to stop his limbs from twitching in small spasms, Subaru committed only the bare facts before him to memory, and pressed onward, deeper into the tower, seeking a survivor to kill. Or perhaps by now even he was unsure whether what he sought was the living or the dead, or even why he was seeking it at all — and still, Subaru walked on.

—Deep in the corridor, he found Ram, her torso blown apart from behind.

Her torso had a great hole gouged from just below the chest to the waist, an atrocious state, nearly torn in two. The wound looked, to Subaru, just like the one he'd inflicted killing that earthworm in the desert. She had probably been shot in the back while running down the corridor, trying to reach somewhere. Biting her lip, her dying face full of unfulfilled regret, there was something like a vengeful curse in it.

He vomited.

—In the hall where they had held the morning briefing and eaten together, there were the corpses of Julius and Meili.

Julius's manner of death was the most horrific of all so far. All over his body were traces of blows, and lacerations dealt with overwhelming sharpness. The wound where his left arm had been blown off at the elbow had been crudely bound with a torn strip of his cloak, as if treated. There was no single obvious cause of death. If all those wounds together were the cause, then it was likely blood loss that had killed him.

And had he fought so desperately to the end for the sake of the girl collapsed behind him?

He vomited.

Leaning her back against the wall, a hand pressed to her stomach, Meili was the only one who had died with a peaceful expression. On the abdomen her small palm touched, there was indeed a wound soaked in bright red blood. Her cause of death, too, was likely blood loss. Wounded, she must have waited here for help until her life slipped away.

And yet, the fact that her corpse's expression seemed so serene refused all understanding.

He vomited.

"——"

Corpse, corpse, corpse, corpse, corpses. There were corpses. Nothing but corpses. Nothing but corpses lying scattered about.

He couldn't grasp what had happened. He couldn't get a hold on what was real.

Ideally, everyone but Subaru dying would have been the condition for his peace of mind. But even so, that everyone was dead without Subaru's involvement made no sense at all.

Shaula, wretched, her head crushed, her whole body cut to ribbons. Echidna, felled by a single, gruesome blow cleaving her from shoulder to side. Ram, her torso blown apart from behind, dead with regret and curses left unspoken. Julius, bearing traces of a fierce battle waged across his whole body, who had finally lost his life. And Meili, who had simply lost blood from a wound to her stomach, and died accepting a slow death in peace.

What could have happened, to bring all this about? Who could the culprit be, for this situation to be something he could accept?

"Emilia, and Beatrice..."

Five corpses found, two suspects unaccounted for. Had one of those two — or the two of them together in some conspiracy — brought about this state of affairs? Had the two who, at the very start, had shown concern for Subaru when he woke with no memory, harbored murderous intent and madness behind that kindness, and carried out this atrocity?

Five corpses, four white cloths — each draped over one of the four besides Shaula. The four bodies other than Shaula's had been laid carefully on the floor, their eyelids closed. There was respect for the dead in that. Only Shaula's body lacked it. To begin with, at what point had this slaughter even taken place?

"The blood..."

Dry, he thought. Wandering unsteadily, searching through the tower for Emilia and Beatrice, whom he had not yet found, Subaru recalled the state of each body and thought it over.

The choking stench of blood and the state of the corpses came back to him vividly. It stabbed into his skull, and his stomach writhed with pain, but there was nothing left in it to bring up. Even the saliva needed to spit out the last residue of vomit in his mouth wouldn't come, he was so parched — not even a drop of cold sweat.

Subaru's body was utterly dried out. Likewise, their blood, too, had dried.

Even granting that blood dries less easily than water, there had been that much of it. The several hours, perhaps over ten hours, needed for it to dry meant that much time had already passed since the massacre. Subaru himself had no idea how long he'd been wandering the desert underground, and he had no confidence whether that much time had passed or not.

There was confusion, and chaos. The constraints on his faltering consciousness screamed at him to do something about this unmanageable present. He had to buy peace, had to find it — and for that, he had to reduce the number of suspects.

If either of the remaining two, the ones responsible for this situation, died, he would be freed.

"——"

—He entered a green room. A black lizard, spotting Subaru, gave a cry.

It was the first time since returning to this tower that he had found a living being.

"Ha. What good's a lizard."

Seeing the lizard's survival, Subaru let out a dry, cracked laugh. He would have preferred, if possible, to come across a corpse rather than someone still alive — but coming across a survivor at this point, and having it be a lizard of all things, what good was that supposed to do him? Clicking his tongue, he immediately turned his back and left the room. A room with nothing but a lizard in it — there was no business for him there. And yet—

"Don't follow me!"

"——"

Behind Subaru as he left the room, the black lizard, its massive body drawn in, tried to follow. When it stood, the lizard was surprisingly large — genuinely the size of a horse. It lumbered along, moving its sharp-clawed feet, following behind Subaru. Unable to bear the pressure of its presence, Subaru swung his arm and spat, snarling at it in warning.

"I don't have time to play around with you! I've got to kill whoever's still alive in this tower! If you're gonna get in my way..."

Brandishing the knife in his hand, Subaru glared at the lizard head-on. The sharp-faced lizard looked at the large knife in Subaru's hand — but then immediately shifted its gaze away from the knife, back to Subaru himself.

"Gh..."

Ignoring the knife's existence, its posture fixed steadily on him, made Subaru's throat tremble. It was as if his murderous intent meant nothing to it at all. That was intensely unpleasant, uncomfortable, and it stoked the hostility smoldering in the depths of his chest—

"—You bastard, don't screw with me!!"

Shouting, Subaru swung the knife back and brought it down on the lizard facing him. The tip of the blade plunged into the jet-black scales. At first there was slight resistance, but it broke through easily, and with an unpleasant, squelching give, it sank deep into the lizard's body. The lizard, which hadn't moved a muscle, had the knife buried in its left side. The blade had sunk in to the hilt inside the lizard's body, and bright blood spilled from the clearly deep wound.

"With this..."

Subaru tried to continue with something like "how do you like that," but his throat let out only a thin, cracking breath instead. For the first time, he had tried to kill a living thing, had carried out the act meant to kill it. The excitement of that, and the violent pounding of his heart, had brought that on — but there was another reason his words wouldn't continue.

"——"

"Ah, gh..."

The lizard, the knife driven into it, didn't so much as twitch, and simply looked at Subaru. There was no reaction at all to having the blade plunged in deep. No response to pain, no surprise, nothing. Only the lizard's sharp eyes watched what Natsuki Subaru had done. Those unreadable eyes accused Natsuki Subaru.

"Damn it... damn it, damn it, damn it! What is this, what the hell is this!"

Clawing at his head, Subaru finally broke, unable to bear it any longer. Shrieking, Subaru forgot even to retrieve the knife he'd driven into the lizard's body, and stepped back. —No, he didn't have the courage to touch that knife. The lizard's eyes were too frightening.

"You, and everyone besides you... corpses! The living too! Whoever it is I can't even tell if they're alive or dead! What the hell are you all thinking, what do you all want!?"

Knowing full well it was pointless, Subaru unloaded everything on the lizard before him. Wandering the tower, wandering through underground darkness where nothing could be seen, suffering from the fact of being thrown into a world where he understood neither left nor right — all the pent-up emotion he'd bottled up came pouring out.

"Anyone who tries to kill me, I'll kill every last one of them! Anyone who tries to lean on me, I'll cast every last one of them off! Don't get the wrong idea! Don't get cocky! Acting so damn familiar with me on your own... give me a break!"

"——"

"I don't know a single one of you! I don't care one bit what any of you are thinking! Every single one of you, shoving your own circumstances onto me...! If you're all so busy dealing with your own problems, well, I'm busy dealing with my own too!"

Shouting, screaming, at some point Subaru found himself in tears, on his knees on the spot. Before him, the lizard said nothing, only watched Subaru, his shoulders shaking, breathing raggedly. Unable to look the lizard in the face, Subaru curled up, pressing his forehead to the floor.

"Just... leave me alone. Abandon me, all by myself..."

Squeezed out from his throat, Subaru's tearful voice echoed emptily through the silent corridor. How much time passed like that? Ten-some seconds, several tens of seconds, a few minutes. Subaru, unable to move, sprawled on the floor, suddenly noticed something.

A faint, truly faint tremor, closing in toward him through the floor—

"Ah."

"——!!"

In that instant, Subaru raised his head and saw, right before him, the lizard's mouth gaping wide open. The lizard's mouth, lined with sharp fangs, closed in on him. He watched it, thinking with a strangely detached feeling that he was about to have his head crushed and die—

"Gh—!?"

The lizard's great mouth closed around Subaru's left shoulder, and then, immediately, took off running. His body lifted off the ground, wrenched upward forcibly. The lizard's sharp fangs sank into his shoulder, and the pain of flesh being gouged tore an involuntary scream from him.

"Gi, agh! Hi, hiii!"

He had tried to kill it. He'd had some notion that he might be killed for it. But now, with fangs actually sunk into his shoulder, the fear born of real pain easily painted over his cheap resolve. Would he be eaten alive — shoulder, then whole body, chewed up, and die? To die eaten by a living creature was about as close to the worst way to die as he could imagine. The instant that premonition sent a shudder of dread through his whole body—

"——"

The corridor where Subaru had been standing just moments before was blown apart from directly below by a massive amount of black mist. It raised a violent, thunderous roar, scattering dust, trampling the corridor's floor, walls, and ceiling, and, still seeking prey, turned its point toward Subaru and the lizard. —To Subaru, it looked like a black, black arm formed of shadow.

"An arm... of shadow..."

The image of the woman who had tormented him on the stairs flashed through his mind. The shadow-woman with her face hidden behind a black veil had thoroughly tormented Subaru's heart, carving fear into him. The shadow overflowing before his eyes now closely resembled the shadow that woman had worn.

That shadow, rampaging through the tower's corridors at will, was chasing after Subaru — and the fleeing lizard.

"You...!"

The lizard didn't answer, and, still gripping Subaru in its jaws, tore through the corridor at ferocious speed. Forced to face backward against the direction of travel, Subaru couldn't see where the lizard was so desperately fleeing to. Instead, all he could do was stare straight at the pursuing shadow's fury, and his blood ran cold from head to toe.

To be swallowed by that shadow, that thing, might mean an end more terrible even than death.

Intuitively grasping this, Subaru pressed the lizard's fangs deeper into his shoulder. His throat trembled with the pain, but if he was shaken loose, that would be the end. At this point, whatever revulsion he had toward the lizard was nothing but useless clinging.

"——!!"

Ahead, the corridor exploded with a sound, and shadow blocked their path. In that instant, with split-second judgment, the lizard cut its course, diving into a side passage a hair's breadth from being swallowed by the shadow, sprinting to shake off the shadow surging like a raging torrent. But as it ran and ran, trying to escape the shadow, the space it burst out into was—

"—! The spiral staircase...!"

Subaru gasped, speechless, bursting into the space of the enormous spiral staircase connecting the fourth and fifth floors. Of course, there was the sheer horror of seeing, from the fourth floor's height, all the way down to the far-below fifth floor — the very sight he'd once fallen to his death overlooking — but that wasn't all.

Below, the staircase rising from the fifth floor to the fourth was being swallowed by black shadow, sinking away. In other words, the entire lower half of the tower was being enveloped, and sunk, by a vast quantity of shadow.

"Can't escape downward... but even so, behind me too..."

The lower half of the tower was already in the shadow's grip, and the black torrent was closing in from deep within the corridor they'd fled through as well. It was hopeless — a completely cornered situation. All that remained was the unreasonable choice between being swallowed by the shadow, or dying some other way.

"——"

For an instant, the possibility of taking his own life crossed his mind. Wasn't it better to choose death by his own hand than to be swallowed by that shadow? And besides — perhaps, even in death, some possibility would remain for Subaru.

"A, hi—"

The moment he consciously thought of suicide, his whole body began to shake violently. What if he chose to end it himself, and that really was the end? He vaguely assumed that his current self was the result of a do-over, but was there any guarantee that was actually true? Or, as he'd first considered, how could he be sure this wasn't a prophetic dream after all? If the previous times had been painful prophetic dreams, and this were the final one, then that truly would be the end.

To begin with, why did he have to die at all? He hadn't done anything wrong. Why, in this situation, should it be him who paid with his life?

"No... I don't want to die!!"

Shamelessly, with no thought for appearances, Subaru wailed. There was no one in the tower to hear it. Only the dead and the missing. So the only one who heard it was not a person at all — only the jet-black lizard.

"——!"

Still holding Subaru in its fangs, the lizard let out an explosive roar from deep in its throat. Immediately after, gaining tremendous acceleration, it hurled itself into the empty space of the spiral staircase, as if fleeing the surging torrent closing in from behind.

"——"

Naturally, no matter how forceful the leap, momentum would eventually be lost, gravity would take hold, and it would fall freely, swallowed by the shadow below. But the lizard broke through that hopeless situation with a startling, situation-changing method.

"——"

The lizard's two hind legs, their sharp tips, dug into the tower's wall. Of course, even clinging to the wall, a fall would eventually be unavoidable — if that wall had been vertical.

Rallying its throat, the lizard tore straight up the wall. Being swallowed by shadow from its base, the massive tower had begun tilting slightly. The wall, nearly vertical, now leaning at an angle — the lizard forcibly stomped across it, breaking through.

"No... way..."

Being flung about, Subaru couldn't grasp the full picture of what was happening. But it came through clearly that this black lizard was pouring out every ounce of strength it had, pressing forward, for the sake of survival.

"You—"

Immediately after, the black torrent engulfed the very spot the lizard had just kicked off from. The torrent, searching for the prey it should have swallowed, sensed it clinging to and running along the wall, and surged toward that spot. In that instant, the lizard twisted its body, narrowly dodging the surging torrent's attack. A violent impact and a whirlwind erupted, and a great hole opened in the section of the tower wall the lizard had dodged from.

"——!"

Without a moment's hesitation, the lizard slipped its body into the great hole opened in the wall. Violently shaken left and right, Subaru's inner ear lost all sense of balance, and he could no longer properly grasp the world around him. But even amid that, he understood that the lizard's forceful movements were made with the consideration of keeping the shadow's attacks off Subaru — and with absolutely no consideration at all for its own body being shredded by the shadow.

They broke through the great hole. Immediately after, greeted by a sand-laden wind, black scorched Subaru's vision. He was in open air. Had they burst out of the tower entirely? Still, the lizard raced up the tilting tower wall, desperately, desperately trying to escape the shadow—

"—Gh, ah!?"

The lizard's slender neck bent sharply, and the next instant, he was struck full-force by the wind. The lizard's fangs, which had been sunk into his left shoulder, were pulled free, and the pain of flesh being torn away burst white sparks behind his eyes. But before that pain and the sensation of wind could even register, his whole body slammed into something hard.

Struck, tumbling, thrown, he let out his breath. He opened his eyes.

Through the flickering, flashing view ahead, he glimpsed a black night sky.

"Ah, eh...?"

At that sight, so far outside anything he'd imagined, Subaru scrambled to sit up. He looked around. It was a space made of the same material as the tower, and yet unmistakably an outside space — something like a balcony, a space attached to the tower's outer wall, it seemed.

Through the hole in the wall, up along the wall, and into this space — the lizard had thrown Subaru here, and then—

"Lizard...!"

Struck with dread, Subaru scrambled toward the direction he'd come tumbling from, and looked down below. And there, he witnessed the fate of the lizard that had thrown him to this place.

—The falling lizard, swallowed by shadow blacker even than its scales, vanished.

Stabbed by a knife driven in out of unreasonable rage, disregarding its own pain and fear, it had thrown Subaru all the way to this balcony, and then, just like that, the lizard had been — Swallowed by the shadow, into a fate more terrible than death.

"What... what is this."

What was this. What was this. What was it. Subaru no longer understood anything at all.

"——"

Subaru gazed from the balcony at the lower portion of the tower being swallowed by shadow. Then, suddenly, beside him, a single white bird alighted on the balcony's outer edge. With an emotionless gaze, the white bird — a large bird — watched Subaru, and Subaru let out a breath that was almost a laugh.

Dead suspects, suspects whose whereabouts were unknown, a lizard that had risked its life to save him, a white bird suddenly appearing in this situation — little by little, little by little, the tower being swallowed by shadow, vanishing.

"——"

Tasting the sensation of an ending closing in, Subaru went limp and sank down where he sat. He understood how desperately the lizard had tried to save him. He understood, but that effort would come to nothing. — Only a little more time before death, granted, and nothing more.

"——"

Slumped there, Subaru suddenly raised his head. Behind him, there was a sudden presence. Not a bird, not a lizard, not shadow.

The presence of someone, alive, had appeared, standing.

"...What are you."

Without even the strength left to turn around, Subaru asked in a weak voice. At that voice, whoever stood behind him gave a faint tremor of the throat, and laughed. In a voice he had never heard before.

"—Guess next time, hero."

In that instant, there was the sound of wind, and Subaru's vision leapt high into the air, spinning round and round. His body felt strangely light. Rising into the sky like a bird, he realized.

Someone, from behind, had severed Subaru's head—

"—Subaru! Hey, Subaru, are you all right?"

The reconnection of the head that should have been severed, and the switch of consciousness, happened in an instant. On a bed of soft, pliant vines, the one who greeted Subaru as he opened his eyes was the one whose voice, like a silver bell, and whose owner, he had searched for so desperately and never once found.

"Emi...ria..."

"Ah, Subaru, thank goodness. You're awake. I was so, so worried."

Faintly, Subaru opened his eyes to find before him the relieved expression of a girl — Emilia. Seeing Subaru awake, she let out a breath of relief, a smile forming on her lips.

"——"

Looking at Emilia, her slender, beautiful neck seemed dazzlingly bright to his eyes. With some strangely parched sensation, Subaru quietly reached both hands toward Emilia's slender neck. That slender neck fit easily within the grasp of his two hands.

"Subaru? What's wrong?"

Blinking, Emilia, her neck held in Subaru's grip, widened her eyes in surprise. She was startled by Subaru's gesture, but made no move to resist it. If he wanted to, with just a bit of full force, this neck would surely snap easily. Even with her life in his grip, Emilia's response was strangely dull, and it made him feel, if anything—

"Emilia, it seems Subaru's still half-asleep, doesn't it, I suppose. After all the worry he caused Betty and the rest, taking it awfully easy, I suppose."

"—!"

At once, at the voice called from right beside him, Subaru released his hands from Emilia's neck. Looking over, he met the eyes of Beatrice, standing by the bed with her short arms crossed, sniffing in exasperation. At her words, Emilia gave a wry smile and said, "That's true,"

"But honestly, being half-asleep is totally fine. I was so scared it might've been something worse... when we found Subaru collapsed, even Beatrice looked like she was about to cry."

"You didn't need to say that part, I suppose!"

Beatrice's face reddened, and she fumed indignantly at Emilia's guileless remark. The two of them, exchanging this back-and-forth, had no understanding at all of what impulse Subaru had just been gripped by moments ago. Before that even — they had no awareness of how dangerous this situation was. That was plainly evident even in how they treated Subaru—

"—So then, this is..."

Once again, right after "Natsuki Subaru" had lost his memory — in other words, the moment Natsuki Subaru had become able to recognize that he'd been summoned to another world — he had been pulled right back to that place. And that meant, at the same time—

"——!"

"—! You...!"

At a faint cry and the sound of breathing, Subaru turned around in a rush, taking in the sight. In the corner of the green room, sitting well-behaved, was a massive black form — the lizard that had run itself ragged for Subaru's sake, right up to the very edge of being swallowed by the shadow, now sitting there calmly.

"...Somehow this doesn't sit right with me, I suppose. Finding Subaru should've been Betty's and Emilia's achievement, I suppose."

"Fufu, don't sulk. It's fine, isn't it? Subaru and Patrasche get along so-o well."

Behind him, he heard that exchange between Emilia and Beatrice continue. But Subaru couldn't even react to their conversation. He only threw his arms around the lizard's great body before him, grateful that its presence was here.

—Grateful to the one being, in that place, who had never once hurt him.
