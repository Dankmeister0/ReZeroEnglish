Arc 7 Chapter 28: "Victory Condition"

—The report that the captured Arakiya had been stolen away sent a violent shock through the meeting hall.

"No excuse for it. Even Holly's bow couldn't catch her, and we let her get away." "Yeah, no..."

The two who bowed their heads in apology, bow in hand, were Holly and Kuna. Bringing Holly along with her bad news, Kuna had immediately gone up to the top floor to chase after the fleeing Arakiya. And just as they had when they'd shot Todd from outside the city, they'd combined Holly's arm strength and Kuna's eyesight in a bow-snipe aimed at the escapee. But the fugitive had dealt with the two-person long-range snipe, thoroughly outmaneuvering them, and had gotten away with Arakiya in tow.

"One of 'em stayed behind as a decoy, and the other one hauled the Nine Divine Generals off. Can't just let it end like this, so if we go after 'em right now—"

"—It's pointless." "—It's pointless."

Kuna's words, spoken with a wrinkled nose and a frustrated air, were cut off by two voices speaking as one. The ones who'd spoken the same words at the same time were Abel and Priscilla, both still seated at the round table. The two exchanged a brief glance, and then Priscilla closed her eyes. A gesture of yielding the floor to Abel, it seemed.

"...This is the sort that infiltrates enemy territory with a small force, accomplishes its objective, and withdraws. Given how a pair like that would split roles, the division of labor is obvious. Both the nerve to decide on this course and the judgment to make good their escape are worth acknowledging. Send pursuers after them, and they still won't be caught."

"—Tch, but still—"

"Within half a day, Arakiya too will likely wake. Once that happens, our pursuers stand no chance. And should that change her mind and bring her back to the city, we'll be finished in an instant. I'll say this now: there won't be a second stroke of luck like before. At the very least, I must call it reckless to commit our current strength to this."

"Ngh...!"

"Kuna... Abel's right, is what I'm sayin'."

Stringing his words together, Abel worked to break down Kuna's resolve as she refused to back down. Standing beside her, Holly gently stroked her shoulder as if to console her. For Kuna, sitting at the round table as acting chieftain, this must have stung bitterly. But setting aside the poor delivery, Subaru found himself nodding along with the substance of what Abel said. The imperial soldiers who'd infiltrated wouldn't have attempted something so reckless without confidence in their chances. Under these circumstances, they'd set an achievable objective, found a path to success, and carried it out. Naturally, they wouldn't have been so careless as to plan a one-way trip—their method of escape surely had an equally solid plan behind it.

"However one dresses it up in words, that one's fate wasn't sealed here. Which means, presumably, some role still remains for her to play."

"Priscilla..."

Into the silence that had fallen, Priscilla tossed out those words. For Subaru, who up until moments ago had been discussing what should be done with Arakiya, his feelings about her not being executed, and about her being carried off before anyone could question her, made for a complicated mix. Still, Priscilla—who had shown no particular concern one way or the other for whether Arakiya lived or died—what did she make of the woman's escape? That much couldn't be read from her crimson eyes.

"So, in the end, what're we doing about the Arakiya girl? Leaving it be, then?"

"...If she's escaped outside, there's nothing else to be done."

To Al's tactless question, Subaru answered with a bitter expression. Given that the fugitive had dealt with a two-person sniping attempt from Kuna and Holly, they couldn't send anyone of half-baked ability after her. That narrowed the candidates for pursuit considerably, but every one of their capable fighters was in a position where they couldn't be spared from Guaral right now. He racked his brain over various angles, but Subaru's strengths fundamentally relied on surprise, and he was weak whenever the enemy struck first. That weakness had just been laid bare.

"By the way—probably not, but is there a chance they're just faking an escape and the other one's still hiding somewhere in the city?"

"Unlikely. It's said to be a sturdy fortress city, but there are escape routes besides the two main gates. We've spent these past several days carefully sealing every one of them."

"Carefully..."

"Yes. I judged they'd try to infiltrate the city through a hidden passage, so I hardly imagined they'd break through from the front with some outlandish scheme."

Zikr, though he'd clearly had the rug pulled out from under him, answered calmly without so much as a sour expression. For the commander of a fortress city, guarding against hidden passages was a natural precaution. But Zikr's orders, given while he reigned as defender, had ended up giving the wind at the back of the soldier who fled with Arakiya.

"Most likely, the soldier who carried off one of the Generals was part of the team searching for hidden passages. As Lord Abel said, this isn't the sort of thing one attempts without confidence in success. If so, it was precisely because he knew of a usable route that he was able to act."

"Could you find where those sealed passages are on a map or somethin'?"

"—A diagram could be drawn up, but not right away. The city has fallen, and the soldiers' unrest runs deep. This confusion won't settle easily."

"...Guess that's fair, since it's not a video game."

If this were a scene from a video game, defeating an enemy commander and recruiting him would bring his subordinate units over to your side, whole and intact. But reality isn't a game. Even the soldiers represented as mere numbers in a game have, each of them, a life of their own. And having thought that far—

"Holly-san, are you hurt?"

"Hm? Worried about me? Natsumi sure is sweet."

At Subaru's question, Holly raised both arms, beaming. Flexing to show off her biceps, she made a show of her own good health, and—

"Got smacked around a bit, but nothin' serious. Heal up quick, no problem."

"I see. That's good... but."

Trailing off, Subaru lowered his gaze, and Holly's face filled with a question mark. Noticing what was on Subaru's mind, Kuna scratched her head with an "ah." And then—

"A few of the lookouts posted inside the buildings got taken out. All dead. Nothin' to be done."

"—Not 'a few'—do you know exactly how many?"

"...Seven, I think."

Pressed for the precise number of the fallen, Kuna answered, hesitant but forthcoming. That number—seven dead—struck deep into Subaru's chest. On the top floor, among the Shudrak and imperial soldiers wounded in Arakiya's attack, Rem was working tirelessly to treat them, and there'd been no report of deaths among them. No report there—but even so—

"...Bloodless surrender, my ass."

Pushing back his long black wig, Subaru let out a sigh of pure self-disgust. The bloodless surrender he'd promised so confidently, declared he would pull off—the scheme meant to bring about Guaral's fall—had, contrary to his bold words, produced no small number of dead. His verbal pledge had been broken, and he had no excuse to offer even if called a shameless liar. Even if no one else blamed Subaru as a fraud, Subaru himself condemned himself. What a monstrous liar he'd turned out to be—

"—Bloodless surrender, you say?"

Catching Subaru's muttering, a voice repeated it back as if turning it over in thought. It was Priscilla, resting her chin on her hand at the round table. Raising her well-shaped brows slightly, she gazed at Subaru with an unusual expression. Keeping that same expression, Priscilla prefaced her words with a single "You," and continued:

"That's a remarkably reckless thing to hear. Don't tell me you meant to take the city without spilling blood? In the midst of this war, without overwhelming force to speak of?"

"...That's right, got a problem with it? No, guess you would. In the end, it failed, so..."

"I was merely stunned anew at the foolishness of the notion. What astonishes me further is that you actually tried to carry out such a pipe dream. —Abel, you. Are you in your right mind?"

"—. The scheme itself was sheer madness, granted."

When the question's edge turned to Abel, he answered quietly, arms crossed. The plan Subaru had proposed had been madness—that much, anyone would think upon hearing the outline, and rightly so. And yet Abel had approved carrying it out. That, it seemed, was something Priscilla—who apparently was an old acquaintance of his—could not understand.

"Looking down upon the Empire from the throne, how could you loosen your grip so far? There is no achievement without sacrifice. No honor kept without spilling blood. That is the Empire's way, is it not?"

"I have no intention of defying the Code of the Sword Wolf either. I called it madness, but there was a chance of success. In fact, had Arakiya's presence not intervened, the bloodless surrender would have succeeded."

"—————"

"What went wrong was not the scheme. It was my own judgment. Priscilla, even you I will not permit to mock my strategist's proposal. The sanction was mine. The responsibility is mine."

"Oh…"

With words far more unexpected than anyone anticipated, Abel stood in direct opposition to Priscilla. "Strategist" was a title unsettling to hear assigned to him, but Abel's words were plainly meant to shield Subaru. Caught between the two's dangerous glaring match, no one could get a word in.

"Well now, brother. You've really won yourself some favor, haven't you. Quite the promotion."

"...Being Emilia-tan's knight and Bea-ko's guardian is more than enough for me. I don't want any titles I never asked for on top of that."

Pushing away Al as he leaned in close, Subaru also pushed away the responsibility trying to press down on him uninvited. Still, thanks to Abel's having stood up for him, Priscilla's attention seemed to have shifted elsewhere. That didn't mean the wound in Subaru's heart had healed, though. The responsibility for the lives lost surely lay with Subaru, who had devised the plan and carried it out.

"Can't save everybody, brother."

Fiddling with the fittings of his helmet, Al murmured this at Subaru's profile. Glancing over, Subaru saw him looking up at the ceiling rather than at him, and—

"Everyone you get involved with—can't save 'em all. Not that there's anything wrong with tryin', but keep at it and your heart'll break before you know it. Wouldn't recommend it, myself."

"Al..."

"Everybody lives how they please and dies how they please. Like you said, brother, it ain't a game. So the buck for a person's own life stops with that person."

It was a remark born of the cynical stance Al occasionally showed. Half advice, half something said to soothe a stubborn child, it urged Subaru to accept the tragic events that had occurred, exactly as they were.

That was, in some sense, probably the correct view. In fact, Al was right. You couldn't save everyone, and once you decided to try to save everyone, there'd be no end to it. That was why Subaru had never, up to now, tried to save absolutely everyone. But war was a different scale entirely. —Could he really say that choice was right?

Weren't there far more than ten or a hundred lives that could be saved by a single action of Subaru's?

"What a farce this all is."

Meanwhile, off to the side of Subaru and Al's conversation, the standoff between Priscilla and Abel continued. To Abel, who had declared the responsibility his own, Priscilla drew a fan from between her breasts and swept its tip around to indicate the meeting hall—no, the entire city.

"Even granting that the city was successfully taken by your strategist's proposal, this is the state of things. That miracle of driving off Arakiya won't repeat itself twice. That method—can it even be used again?"

"No, there won't be a second time."

At Priscilla's question, Abel nodded without hesitation. Hearing it called "the method that drove off Arakiya," Subaru, brow furrowed, also arrived at the memory—during Al and Arakiya's clash of blades, there had been a moment when something had gone strange with Arakiya.

"That wasn't something Al did, was it..."

"Hm? Wasn't me. For one thing, if it'd been me, I'd have handled what came after a lot smoother. I got knocked back and dropped, remember?"

"True enough."

Now that it was mentioned, whatever had happened afterward was too clumsy to have been Al's doing. Following that exchange between the two, every gaze in the meeting hall gathered on Abel. If anyone could have set something up in that moment, it was him alone, and under the weight of those gathered stares, Abel gave an irritated snort.

"Arakiya is a 'Spirit Eater.' She has the property of devouring spirits in the atmosphere and absorbing their power."

"Spirit... Eater...?"

At a term so utterly unfamiliar and yet so ominous-sounding, Subaru couldn't help widening his eyes. He'd already sensed that the Vollachian Empire operated on rules and a foundation different from the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, but "Spirit Eater" was a particularly outlandish rarity even by that standard. Subaru himself was, however marginally, a Spiritual Arts user who borrowed the power of spirits, but this sounded like something altogether different in nature, and to put it bluntly—

"Absolutely cannot let Bea-ko meet her... is that 'Spirit Eater' thing common?"

"Not at all. 'Spirit Eating' was originally one secret art passed down only among tribes dwelling in Vollachia's frontier. But due to its potent nature, those tribes were wiped out, and even the method of housing it was lost."

"At least as far as I know, no 'Spirit Eater' besides Arakiya has been confirmed to exist. Had there been another, I would have taken pains to protect them. That one is the Observer's... no, it's irrelevant now."

Disliking the tangent, Abel shook his head and returned to the track with "Let's get back to it." Subaru too, relieved to hear that "Spirit Eating" wasn't common—no more worrying about Beatrice getting gnawed on—asked "So?" in return.

"How did you throw off Arakiya, the 'Spirit Eater'?"

"Not 'throw off.' —I merely induced Mana Poisoning."

"Mana Poisoning... ahh, I see, I see. That's quite the deft trick you pulled off."

Nodding as if satisfied by Abel's answer, Al stroked his own chin, looking impressed. But hearing "Mana Poisoning" alone didn't quite click for Subaru.

"If I remember right, it's something like—if someone with a body sensitive to Mana goes somewhere with really high Mana concentration, they tend to get sick..."

"Due to the nature of being a 'Spirit Eater,' Arakiya is highly susceptible to that sort of effect. That said, incorporating spirits requires, as a precondition, a resistance well above the norm. Exceed that threshold in trying to intoxicate Arakiya with Mana, and..."

"Right, he had to use the treasure. Counting what was spent on that, my supply on hand is exhausted."

Saying "that," Abel gestured with his chin, indicating Subaru. At the word, Subaru furrowed his brow with a "Treasure?" and tilted his head. Called upon as having used some treasure, Subaru had absolutely no memory of it.

"What treasure? When did I ever do anything of yours—"

"During the 'Lifeblood Ritual,' you broke a ring to snap the Witchbeast's horn, did you not? That's what I mean."

"Ah..."

It had slipped his mind entirely, but now the memory resurfaced—of borrowing a magic-capable ring. In the chaos of that battle, out of sheer necessity, he'd used the ring to smash Elginu's horn along with his own fist, breaking the Witchbeast's horn clean off, but in exchange, Subaru's arm and the ring alike had been left in tatters. And now that something, spoken of in the same breath, had been used in the fight against Arakiya, apparently.

"Timed with bringing down the balcony, I crushed underfoot a ring I'd kept hidden. It took time for the Mana it held to spill out and saturate the surroundings, but..."

"And the Arakiya girl went and got herself Mana-poisoned right on cue, that's the picture. Well, guess that means I'm the guy who got taken down easy by a girl in that state."

"So that's what kind of trick you were pulling in that moment..."

Everyone there had been battered and wounded, and yet even while being pushed to the brink by one of the Empire's strongest, Abel had still been searching for a path to victory—his refusal to give up left Subaru genuinely impressed. Subaru liked to think his own stubbornness was fairly considerable too, but the gap in raw cleverness between him and Abel was so vast that he couldn't shake the difference between mere desperate flailing and a calculated, do-or-die counteroffensive. In any case—

"That was originally a scheme prepared for use against Arakiya, was it not? Small-hearted of you, to have such a contingency ready not knowing when she might turn on you."

"The closer one keeps someone, the more one should prepare countermeasures for when they turn hostile. ...Particularly with Arakiya, there was never any telling when she might bare her fangs at me."

"—. Still, a surprise tactic only works once. Preparing enough Magic Crystal Stone to induce Mana Poisoning won't be easy a second time. Securing proper military strength before the next opportunity will be essential. But—"

Having said that much, Priscilla cut her words off meaningfully. Hiding her mouth behind her opened fan, she narrowed her crimson eyes and looked down upon Abel. With an expression somewhere between probing and testing, she let out a small breath, and—

"Bringing the 'People of Shudrak' under your banner, as expected... but adopting the scheme of a starry-eyed strategist who champions a bloodless surrender, and storming a fortress city with it—that foolishness is beyond excuse."

"—————"

"With this, I can hardly recommend to my allies that you deserve their support."

"Allies!?"

At Priscilla's utterly casual, matter-of-fact declaration, Subaru was stunned. And it wasn't only Subaru—everyone in the meeting hall save Abel and Al was equally startled. Al, being Priscilla's companion, made sense not to react, but Abel's lack of surprise felt downright unreasonable. Subaru didn't press that point, though, and instead—

"Wait wait wait, that's a huge leap! Allies... to start with, I don't even get where you stand. I heard you came to help Abel, but..."

"I've come to help no one. Don't take this fool's idle chatter at face value."

"Fine! Whether you came to help Abel or not, I don't care about that anymore. That's not what matters. What I want to know is your big-picture goal."

As a minor, immediate goal, it had already been explained that talking with Abel was the objective. But what Subaru wanted to know was why Priscilla—she, Al, and presumably the other retinue she'd apparently brought along—were in the Vollachian Empire in the first place. And on top of that, it would be best if he could learn whether or not she had any intention of siding with Abel.

"Answer me. Honestly, neither I nor anyone else here can figure out what to make of you."

"Hah, what insufferable conceit. It matters not one whit to me what a pack of fools thinks of me. No matter what you all think, I shall do as I please. After all..."

"—'The world is built to suit my convenience,' right?"

At Subaru tracing back her familiar philosophy, one he knew well by ear, Priscilla snorted. Then, sensing the number and sharpness of the gazes turning on her had increased, she closed one eye.

"My aim is to return the emperor, driven from his throne, back to his seat. Otherwise, tiresome visitors shall keep calling on me without end."

"Needless to say, I imagine, but those assassins were not sent on my orders."

"I never doubted it. Which is precisely why I troubled myself to come all the way here. More precisely, to have myself carried here—by wing, one might more accurately say."

Still with one eye closed, Priscilla, having answered Abel, lifted her gaze upward. What that indicated was not the ceiling, but the night sky beyond it. Or rather, not merely the sky, but a being that claimed the sky as its own domain—

"Don't tell me you and Al flew here from the sky?"

"Ahh, kind of like flying-dragon express mail, yeah. Honestly, when the princess jumped down all by herself, I thought it was the end of the world. Can't do the same thing myself, and I had a hell of a time gettin' down even after they lowered the altitude for me."

"A flying dragon... the water dragon at Priestella scared me plenty too."

The water dragon, a fantastical creature beyond mere earth dragons, and now, on top of that, the existence of a flying dragon surfaced. From what he'd heard, flying dragons were extremely ferocious, and taming one required specialized skill. Because there were so few with that skill, being able to ride a flying dragon at all was apparently a rare thing.

"So then, someone capable of sending flying-dragon express mail is Priscilla's ally?"

"Do not engage in such base conduct, snatching at the tail end of my words. In your case, baseness outweighs cleverness by far. Polish your charm a little. Come back after fixing your makeup, and you might yet be presentable."

"If I fix my makeup here and now, I'd be a lunatic..."

If it came to that, the fact that he hadn't even undone his cross-dressing disguise in the first place would probably become an issue too, but everyone present could read the room, so no one brought it up. In any case, the conversation had nearly gone far off track again, but—

"The goal being to return Abel to the throne—can I take that to mean we can cooperate?"

"And that I cannot simply nod along to, honestly speaking. —You've not forgotten my earlier words, I trust. If this one lacks the qualities of an emperor, there's no meaning in restoring him."

"—————"

Sharpening his gaze, Abel glared at Priscilla, who had directly questioned his qualifications as emperor. What Priscilla took issue with wasn't that the "bloodless surrender"—which Subaru had proposed and Abel had approved—had failed to be carried through, but rather the fundamental problem with the very conception of it. Measured by Vollachian standards, softness was a fatal flaw. In fact, it was precisely because of Subaru's carelessness that lives had been lost. Her suspicion couldn't be denied. And yet—

"The throne will be reclaimed. Whatever anyone says, that is absolute. —Priscilla, even should it be you who says otherwise, that will not change."

In Subaru's stead, as he'd been on the verge of falling silent, Abel declared this firmly. It was a proclamation carrying the same heat—or perhaps even more—as when he had first revealed his true identity to Subaru and declared he would retake this nation.

"—————"

Hearing that resolve of Abel's, the expressions of everyone in the meeting hall shifted. Kuna and Holly, as members of the "People of Shudrak," kindled a fighting spirit of loyal service; Zikr bowed his head as if facing something divine. Al, mindful of Priscilla's reaction, turned to look at her, and Priscilla, on the receiving end of that unflinching declaration, narrowed her eyes and said:

"Your spirit has not waned, but reality does not match it. In fact, are you not, even now, driven from your throne?"

"—————"

"The circumstances are already known to me. The question is—who began it? Whose scheme is it?"

"—The one who drew up the design was, surely, Chancellor Berstetz."

To Priscilla's question, Abel answered with hostility kindled in his black eyes. A chancellor was the office that in practice stood at the summit of state governance, close confidant to king or emperor. If the pinnacle of the military were the generals or the order of knights, the chancellor might be called the pinnacle of the civil officials. In any case, it was precisely the number-two position most conducive to committing betrayal.

"That old husk of a tree. Making use of Lamia's legacy, of all things—how bold of him."

"Naturally, I foresaw his treasonous intent and prepared accordingly. But..."

Abel cut himself off there and let out a quiet breath. It was a reaction unlike him, the first hint of something close to weakness he'd shown. As emperor, he who had not wavered even as he was driven from his throne—here, for the first time, showed a faint fragility. The cause of that fragility was not the treacherous chancellor, but—

"The one I had watching him—Chisha Gold, the 'Fourth' of the Nine Divine Generals—his treasonous intent, I failed to see through."

"—Absurd. General Chisha?"

At Abel spitting out his mortification, it was Zikr who involuntarily raised his voice. For Zikr, one of the Empire's Two Generals, the names of the "Nine Divine Generals"—unknown to Subaru—were evidently close and familiar. Drawing every gaze at the round table, he stroked his own rich head of hair and said:

"General Chisha is a figure of unusual note even among the Nine Divine Generals. He himself made his name less through martial renown than through strategic acumen, and during the 'Imperial Selection Ceremony,' it's said he supported Lord Vincent most ably of all..."

"That mean he was like, your number one right hand? So you got betrayed by your political right hand, the chancellor, and your longtime right hand, the general, both?"

"Do not repeat yourself. My right arm is attached to my right shoulder."

"Comin' from you right now, that just sounds like bluster..."

Hearing Zikr's explanation, Subaru found himself shocked at how unexpectedly thin Abel's base of loyal support was. Though, listening to the extreme ideology of the Vollachian Empire, it seemed hardly unusual—an emperor judged lacking in strength would be betrayed instantly, it seemed.

"Does this happen a lot in this country—the emperor being chased around?"

"Since I took the throne, I've been driven off, if only in form, exactly twice."

"But that's already a repeat offense!"

"Fool. The ones who dragged me around were the Kingdom's Royal Guard. Complain to them, if you have complaints."

Given a look of genuine indignation, Subaru grew too tired to argue further and fell silent. Come to think of it, he vaguely recalled Julius once mentioning having gone to the Empire as an envoy, but he preferred to believe that had nothing to do with this. If it did, the world would be far too small.

"So, His Majesty the Emperor's been driven from his throne by his own right-hand man and closest aide, and the Arakiya girl's turned enemy too. ...Ain't that pretty bad? Got any allies at all?"

"Goz Ralfon, another of the Nine Divine Generals... he exerted himself to let me escape. Without his holding the line, I could not have triggered the transference device."

"Ooh, as expected of General Goz...!"

"However, if the other Divine Generals besides Chisha and Arakiya have also turned traitor, there's no telling how far Goz alone could have held out. It's quite likely he died in that fight."

The existence of a "Divine General" who had protected Abel at the cost of his life was a ray of hope, but apparently a rather fragile one. Even so, Zikr shook his head with an "No—"

"With respect, no word of General Goz's death has reached me. Whether in battle or by illness, the death of a man of General Goz's stature could not stay hidden for long."

"Then perhaps there's hope he's a captive."

"Most likely. No—certainly! A warrior of General Goz's caliber surely would be!"

At Abel's words, Zikr bowed reverently. For a man to have earned such respect from Zikr, this "Goz" figure must be quite the remarkable person. Or perhaps, contrary to the fearsome name, a woman.

"—With the chancellor and a Divine General turned enemy, the Empire is as good as a death trap."

Before any pointless questions could be inserted, Priscilla, having finished listening, murmured this. Agreeing with her sentiment, Subaru raised a hand with a "Hey" and said:

"Bit late to ask, but what about Abel just coming out and declaring himself emperor? That way, the ones running the government back in the capital like nothing's wrong would be exposed as the treasonous rebels, and..."

"Sorry, brother, but hoping the outraged populace or soldiers would go string up the coup government for you—that ain't happening."

"I wasn't thinking anything that violent, but why not?"

"—Because this is the Vollachian Empire, which reveres those with power."

Subaru's proposal was rejected, and Al's words were supplemented by Abel. The emperor driven from his throne crossed his arms, furrowing his well-shaped brows slightly, and said:

"Were I to come forward and declare my intent to retake the capital myself, I would be welcomed. But that would not translate into support for me. What is taken must be taken back. That is the way of things here."

"Not just objects or land, but the throne too..."

"There is no exception. —Which is why what I must do is already decided."

Just as Subaru was about to clutch his head at the sense of being boxed in on all sides, Abel's next words caught him off guard. He'd thought they were at an impasse, but Abel's answer was, if anything, the opposite. Rising to his feet where he stood, he slowly placed his hands on the round table. And then—

"General Zikr, the map."

"Yes! At once!"

At the word, Zikr issued instructions to the imperial soldier standing by in the corner of the room. The soldier promptly took down the map hanging on the meeting hall's wall and spread it out across the round table. Roughly unfurled, it was a world map showing the entire world, Vollachia included.

"Where we stand is the eastern territory—the fortress city of Guaral, here. And the imperial capital of Lupugana, which must be retaken, lies roughly at the Empire's center."

"...Vollachia's pretty huge, huh."

Indicated on the map, Subaru was newly struck by the scale of the country's territory, something he'd barely been conscious of until now. This world was governed by four great nations, each holding roughly a quarter of it, but the Vollachian Empire's territory, occupying the southern portion of the world map, was by far the largest compared to the others. Even the Buddheim Jungle, which Subaru's group had struggled so hard to cross, occupied only a modest sliver of geography when viewed against the whole of Vollachia.

"Each city of the Empire is managed by its own city lord or governor. No exception for Guaral—every city maintains its own autonomous military force, and won't hesitate to fight when the need arises. —We shall bring these under our banner, one by one, and secure the strength needed to retake the capital."

"...I get that this is the textbook pattern for a nation-conquest sim."

"You seem displeased."

"Course I am. Guaral alone was this much of an ordeal. I can't imagine this all going that smoothly."

Pointing at the map, Abel explained, and Subaru managed, somehow, to keep pace. He could follow it, but only as a matter of logic—the matter of feeling was another thing entirely. As he'd said before, this wasn't a game. A method that might hold up in some simulation RPG surely wouldn't translate so easily into reality. Abel's outlook struck him as awfully optimistic. But—

"There is a means to dispel your misgivings. Indeed, a means essential to accomplishing what I must do."

"An essential condition..."

"—Securing the 'Nine Divine Generals.'"

Hearing that answer, Subaru's eyes went wide. Of course it would come to that. After all, hadn't Abel just finished explaining that it was precisely because of betrayal by those very "Nine Divine Generals" that he'd been driven from the throne? The favorably-disposed "Divine General" was of unknown fate; of the two Divine Generals whose allegiance was known, both were already enemies. Which left—

"And the rest of the Divine Generals...?"

"There it is."

Raising a finger at the question that had suddenly arisen, Abel nodded toward the unsettled Subaru. Then he swept his gaze across everyone present besides Subaru, and said:

"Let the people of the Empire be strong. The 'Nine Divine Generals' are the very embodiment of that creed. Which is to say, for one who would be sovereign of the Vollachian Empire, uniting the 'Nine Divine Generals' is unavoidable."

"So that means..."

Following Abel's words to their conclusion, Subaru's eyes widened at what lay ahead. At Subaru's reaction, Abel's cheek twisted into a combative smile. And then—

"—The 'Blue Lightning' Cecilus Segmunt. The 'Spirit Eater' Arakiya. The 'Wicked Old Man' Olbart Dunkelkenn. The 'White Spider' Chisha Gold. The 'Lion Knight' Goz Ralfon.
