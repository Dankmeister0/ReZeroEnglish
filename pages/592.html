Arc 7 Chapter 87: "Summit Showdown"

The siege of the imperial capital Lupugana by the rebel army — it did not begin with insurgents gathered from every corner of the land acting in concert, united as one, to strike down Emperor Vincent Vollachia.

Certainly, at present, a great many insurgents have gathered around the imperial capital of Lupugana.
Having converged from every region of the Empire, they surrounded Lupugana, the heart of the Vollachia Empire, and mustered a force that overwhelmed the number of imperial soldiers the capital possessed.
In most cases, battles are decided by numbers.
That principle becomes only more decisive the larger the scale of the conflict, and with a simple ratio of forces putting the rebel army at more than double the imperial defenders, one could say they had secured an overwhelming advantage in the assault on the capital.
And yet—

"—That's only true if the forces gathered function as a coherent body."

In the deepest recesses of the imperial capital Lupugana, seated upon the throne in the audience chamber of the Crystal Palace, Vincent narrowed his black eyes as he received reports of the full-scale clash with the insurgents now underway.
The capital surrounded by the rebel army, under assault by an overwhelming disparity in numbers — placed in a predicament without precedent even in the Empire's history, yet there was no trace of fear or impatience upon that cold, demonic countenance.
That was in large part due to the very analysis he had just voiced.

"The insurgents gathered may all agree on wanting my head, but there is no room for compromise in the methods by which they mean to take it. To begin with, these are men who rose up each for himself the moment the opportunity presented itself."

"The more desperately each strives not to be outdone by the others, the less likely they are to think of coordinating their steps with anyone else. It brings to mind the Imperial Selection Ceremony, does it not?"

"—"

"That blood-soaked rite in which the siblings of the Vollachia imperial family fought one another for the throne, to determine the next Emperor... Indeed, on that occasion too, it was those who refused to work with others who were removed from the board first. Although—"

So said Berstetz, standing in attendance beside the throne, before he cut himself off and let a beat pass. Behind those thread-thin eyes, the old man's emotions could not be read, but one could make a fair guess.
For Berstetz too was one who had lived through the bloodiness of the Imperial Selection Ceremony.

"On that occasion, His Excellency Barthroy, who built the largest faction, was viewed with wariness for his very character, and was likewise removed quite early on."

Heedless of whatever was passing through Vincent's mind, Berstetz touched the mustache beneath his nose and spoke the rest of what he had held back.
The detached, as-if-it-were-someone-else's-affair manner of his telling rang utterly hollow.

"You speak so freely. It was none other than you and Lamia who removed that brother of mine."

"To my shame, this lowly servant merely followed Her Excellency Lamia's judgment. What is more, I failed to grasp His Excellency Barthroy's true intent, and so continue to live in disgrace to this very day."

"Brother Barthroy's true intent, is it."

"Yes. None other than the scheme forged between His Excellency Vincent and His Excellency Barthroy."

Berstetz answered with a deep dip of his chin, his expression unchanged.
Given the events in question, one might expect at least a word of bitterness mixed in, but he was a man untouched by such messy human sentiment. Still less would he waste words on Vincent, understanding full well the futility of it.
That deed had been the result of Vincent Vollachia — no, of Vincent Abelux, before he had yet become Emperor, setting things in motion—

"Seeing as the real Excellency is out beyond the wall right about now, I think I understand how His Excellency the Chancellor must feel, not knowing where to aim his little barbs."

"—"

"Hmm? Both of you giving me such stern looks at once... did I go and say something unnecessary?"

So saying, the mild-mannered man who shrugged his shoulders with an air of breezy indifference, even under the gaze of Vincent and Berstetz — the two highest figures of the Empire — was a heterodox presence permitted entry to the Crystal Palace regardless of rank: Ubilk, the Stargazer.
The perpetually smirking Ubilk had not suddenly cut into the conversation. He had been present from the start of Vincent and the others' talk — he had simply held his tongue until now.
That said—

"Even with no one else present, that is hardly a matter to speak of so lightly."

"Ah, tacking on 'the real' was unnecessary of me. Small wonder His Excellency the Emperor and His Excellency the Chancellor are cross. Still, if I don't vent a little now and then, I'm liable to blurt something out by accident. —And that would hardly be fair to the soldiers risking their lives to defend the capital at this very moment, would it?"

"—"

"If it slipped out carelessly that the Emperor Vincent everyone is so desperately protecting is, in fact, an impostor—"

Ubilk raised a hand to his mouth, brazenly voicing the very thing he had just been chided for.
He was a man valued for his ability, yet his conduct was frivolous, the very picture of a jester. Still, it would not do to let him think the role of jester was simply license for mischief.
However excellent his ability as a Stargazer might be—

"it does not cancel out the disrespect you have piled up. Once everything up to now has borne fruit, I shall strike off your head with my own hand."

"Of course, I am well aware. But Your Excellency... no, you are a careful man. I do believe my life is safe right up until the very, very last moment."

Even pierced by Vincent's stare from the throne, Ubilk received it with a smile. He went on, saying, "More to the point," and turned his gaze toward Berstetz,

"it's His Excellency the Chancellor I find frightening. I get the sense you're hiding a whole hell inside you somewhere."

"It hardly becomes a Stargazer to fear a lowly thing such as myself. Why not consult the Stars on just how unworthy of fear I am?"

"My apologies, Your Excellency the Chancellor. The Stars look down upon the earth from high in the sky — unless something asserts itself with a dazzling brilliance, they simply won't take notice of it."

Things of no consequence did not reflect into the futures a Stargazer saw.
Whether that was mere pretense or his true feeling was impossible to read from Ubilk's words and manner. But setting aside his usual idle chatter, when Ubilk spoke in fulfillment of his role as Stargazer, his words could not be ignored.
Nine years had passed since Ubilk began serving the Vollachia Empire as its Stargazer — and it might be said that his record of achievement had all been building toward this very moment.

"—Oh my."

All at once, into the audience chamber where silence had fallen, a distant roar reached down from the sky far above.
A faint tremor traveled up through the floor, and even deep within the Crystal Palace, the atmosphere of fierce battle could be sensed. That said, most of whatever had caused this roar was likely the capital's own doing.

"And yet in raw numbers, the rebel army surrounding the capital should have the advantage..."

"They are, in the end, nothing but a rabble scraped together... no, they aren't even united among themselves — a pack of outlaws who might well trip each other up depending on circumstance. Such a lot could never hope to stand against a pack of blade-wolves bound by the iron-and-blood code."

"I see. But however hot-blooded the people of this Empire may be, surely they would not march on the capital without some prospect of victory. Would each tribe not throw in its own hero, precisely for that reason?"

"Have you forgotten what you said before me just a moment ago?"

Vincent said this to Ubilk, who had tilted his head, his attention drawn by the tremor to the battle outside. At those words Ubilk closed one eye, and Vincent, propping his cheek on one hand, went on,

"that the Stars take notice of any dazzling light upon the earth. If that is so, it would hardly be strange for them to have already sung to you of the glittering stars capable of moving this battle's course."

"Ah~"

"Was there such a thing? Stargazer."

Narrowing his black eyes, Vincent asked quietly. At the question Ubilk scratched his cheek with a finger, wearing a wry smile,

"Regrettably, no, I haven't heard anything of the sort."

"Then, in keeping with your own role, that is presumably exactly what it means."

So Berstetz concluded, capping off Ubilk's wry smile.
Ubilk fell silent with an awkward look, though Berstetz felt no sense of having bested him. To begin with, whether or not Ubilk's Stargazing had spoken of it, the matter was already plain enough.

Certainly, every one of the insurgents was coming to take the head of Vincent seated on this throne. To that end, each tribe would surely send forth its own strongest warrior, seeking to claim supremacy.
But that very idea was mistaken from the outset — the great mass of them had misjudged the matter from the very start.
Naturally so. —Just what did they think the Nine Divine Generals were, anyway?

"To measure them merely as 'heroes of each tribe' with so small a ruler is to err from the start. What, precisely, do you imagine were the standards by which I selected each of my Generals, my Nine Divine Generals?"

What Vincent Vollachia had gathered were the very strongest beings in the Vollachia Empire.
Heroes scattered unaffiliated throughout the Empire were simply no comparison. Had there been so much as a sleeping lion lying dormant somewhere in this land, Vincent would never have overlooked it.
Whatever it took, he brought such beings under his own hand, granting them a station befitting their strength, preparing for the future.
The very moment a hero failed to catch Vincent's eye, that hero was already inferior to a monster.

—The Emperor of the Sacred Vollachia Empire had assembled "monsters" that no hero could hope to match.

The impregnable city of the imperial capital Lupugana — a land that, since the Empire's founding, had never once allowed an outside enemy to breach it — was guarded by a star-shaped rampart, and at each of its five points, a "monster" stood in waiting.
Those transcendent beings, none inferior to the others, would surely crush without mercy the arrogant heroes who had gathered. Witnessing that carnage, even those whose blood had rushed to their heads would surely come to realize—
that they themselves had been turning their eyes from reality, dreaming a dream of frenzied rampage.

"However, it would be difficult to demand good manners of every last insurgent."

"So it would seem. What of it, then?"

"With the principal Generals already having taken their places upon the battlefield, I am well aware there is no stage left for a lowly thing such as myself... and yet simply waiting is hard to endure."

"Ho-ho, my, my — for a man of His Excellency the Chancellor's standing to be so hot-blooded."

Ubilk gave a little jab at Berstetz, who bent at the waist to bow to Vincent. But the old chancellor took the remark in stride, unruffled,

"In the end, I too am a person of Vollachia, that is all."

So he said, showing not even a smile, before walking out of the audience chamber. Making no move to call him back, Vincent watched in silence as the old statesman departed.
Once the door had closed and Berstetz had gone, Ubilk shrugged his shoulders and said, "Is that all right?"

"Just as he himself said, I don't imagine there's anything His Excellency the Chancellor can actually do to affect this battle."

"That one has endured with more patience than one might expect. He is, after all, the sort who has long sealed himself away for the sake of an ideal. To use your own turn of phrase — I would rather avoid a blowout."

"I see... By the way, does His Excellency the Chancellor know?"

Ubilk asked, hooking a thumb into the front of his own robe while wiggling the rest of his fingers idly. Vincent, who understood the meaning behind that unhelpfully vague question, closed one eye.
Taking that silence as his answer, Ubilk nodded and said, "Just as I thought,"

"I had a feeling it might be so, which is why I very nearly held my tongue against a careless remark... though whether I'm glad or sad my hunch was right, I find myself in a rather complicated mood."

"Sad? Can a thing like you even comprehend so human a feeling?"

"What a terrible thing to say. It's almost as if you're telling me I'm not human — you wound me. Why, I'd say I'm a good deal more sound of mind than 'One' or 'Two,' if I do say so myself."

"If I take that fool Cecilus as an example, he comes off worse in most respects. Arakiya, too, is a girl who lives more by a beast's logic than a human's, but seeing as she has settled on an owner, she can at least be managed. But—"

There Vincent cut himself off, piercing Ubilk with those black eyes — regarding that existence which would not waver even under the cruelest of gazes, that manner of being wholly unconcerned with its own life.

"You are merely something shaped like a person, made of human flesh—"

"...Now that wounds me."

At Vincent's words, Ubilk lowered the corners of his brows and wore a bitter smile.
Doing so made him look as though he were sad, as though he were pained. It was an imitation of emotion, as if he had been taught to do so by someone — or so, at least, it appeared to Vincent's eyes.
With his expression still true to that impression, Ubilk placed a hand on his own chest,

"By your logic, then, are all the others who share my role... every last Stargazer who has ever lent an ear to Heaven's Mandate, to be treated as mere dolls?"

"I know of no Stargazer besides you. There is no answering that."

"Come now, there's no need for such a lie. I don't flatter myself I'm trusted that much. Naturally, you must have already sought out every Stargazer in the land and put them to torture."

"—"

"I do believe you're not so thoughtless a man as to decide what a Stargazer is by looking at me alone."

Vincent's expression did not stir at Ubilk's probing feint.
He said nothing as to whether the man's guess was true or false. He had only come to a certainty that every last Stargazer granted Heaven's Mandate, without exception, carried some derangement of the mind.
Though he had no intention of troubling himself to explain how he had come by that certainty.

"Were that not so, one could hardly commit so savage an act as crushing one's own Evil Eye for the sole purpose of proving one's own innocence."

"Ah, that hurt terribly. The sense of loss was nothing ordinary either. But it did put you in the mood to actually listen to me, didn't it?"

As he spoke, Ubilk pulled the hand at his collar further open, baring his own bare skin. In the middle of his thin chest was a large, painfully carved burn scar.
It was the mark left from when Ubilk had once pressed a branding iron to his own chest, searing away the Evil Eye that had resided there.
Though a member of the Evil Eye Tribe, he had burned away the very Evil Eye that was his reason for being. —It had been an act meant to prove he himself posed no danger, and had no one stopped him, he might well have gone on to crush his own limbs as well.
And so—

"I do not expect a proper answer from you. Only answer with due care."

"Anything you like."

"There has been no new whisper of Heaven's Mandate in your ear. Is that correct?"

"—Yes. I don't tell lies. No new Mandate has come down upon me. Although, to begin with, there has never once been an instance of a new Mandate descending before the time of the previous one had been fulfilled..."

Slowly shaking his head from side to side, Ubilk smiled.
With neither joy nor humility, wearing a truly inorganic smile, he went on.

"Heaven's design will not overturn the board you have built. —Every last person in this Empire, without exception, rests upon the palm of your hand."

"—"

"Is something the matter?"

With his declarative words failing to produce the effect he'd hoped for, Ubilk looked puzzled. At that question, Vincent shook his head and said, "No."
He did not think there had been any falsehood in Ubilk's remark just now. The man valued whatever unfathomable whisper of the Stars he heard over even his fealty to the Emperor, but his lack of self-interest in the matter was genuine.
And so there was likely no scheme of Ubilk's own hidden in those words either.
Only—

"—Every person in the Empire rests upon my palm, is it."

That flourish, needless though it was, left a small snag lodged in the chest of the one playing at Emperor.

—And that snag from the throne would prove to be exactly right.

"O-o-oohhhhh—!!"

At one of the points of the star-shaped fortress, a fierce roar went up, and a single tiger charged forward with tremendous force.
Both arms sheathed in silver-gleaming gauntlets, he closed the distance at a speed that put lightning itself to shame, driving in toward his foe's very bosom — reaching for the one who had shattered a horde of Centaur People with a frightening flurry of blows.
One step, two steps, at a speed too fast for the eye to follow, Garfiel Tinzel charged forward with a battle cry.
But—

"Fighting many against one is indeed a specialty of mine, but—"

"—"

"even in a one-on-one duel, my technique loses none of its edge!!"

Meeting the valiant war cry with a counterattack, Kafma Irulux, standing opposite him, readied both arms.
His tattoo-covered tan skin swelled from within, and in the next instant, an enormous mass of thorned brambles shot forth, filling the field of vision of Garfiel as he charged straight in.
The speed of those countless thorn-studded brambles called to mind a carnivorous beast pouncing on its prey. In other words, it was the fastest possible attack for reaping an opponent's life.

"Don't be so damn troublesome!!"

Atop the rampart, the thick wall built to protect the city offered a fairly broad footing, but compared to an open battlefield it still forced upon them a cramped, throat-squeezed sort of confinement.
With his escape routes sealed off by the brambles spreading sideways and surging in like a great wave, Garfiel chose, rather than some half-hearted dodge, to raise his gauntlets and dive straight into them.
Trusting in the strength of his gauntlets and the power of his legs, he shot in a straight line into the storm of thorns—

"Your resolve is admirable, but your judgment is a mistake."

Kafma murmured this at the very instant Garfiel made contact with the leading edge of the surging brambles, but in the next moment Garfiel clenched his back teeth against an impact far beyond what he'd expected his gauntlets to absorb.

"—Ngh."

However bizarre the manner of their release, brambles were brambles, and from the size of the vines and thorns he had braced for a certain degree of force. But the actual power of the brambles far exceeded his expectations, and Garfiel's forward momentum slowed.
It was, without exaggeration, a pressure like an entire forest slamming into him bodily.
He revised his understanding: he was being struck by a forest a single human being had produced, a great swath of nature overgrown with thorns.

"Then how about this!!"

"What!?"

Grinding his teeth until they creaked, Garfiel, taking the full brunt of the wave of brambles, brought his foot down hard. Through the sole of the boot he slammed down, a section of the rampart serving as his footing surged upward, and on the far side of the brambles Kafma's own footing tilted, throwing the wide-eyed General off balance.
It was the effect of Garfiel's Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits. Ordinarily a blessing that grants power drawn from the earth, it could also, conversely, be used to interfere with the earth — though just what counted as "earth" depended heavily on the bearer's own interpretation.
In Garfiel's case, any footing his own feet were planted firmly upon counted as earth.

"Long as you ain't flyin' through the damn sky!"

Something like the famed Dragon Ships of the Vollachia Empire, borne aloft by Flying Dragons, would be a different matter — but short of that, Garfiel could convince himself that any given surface was solid ground.
The tilting beneath Kafma's feet did not stop at once — the ground kept surging a second time, a third, chasing after him, and the male of the Insect Cage Tribe dealt with it while leaping backward. In that interval, Garfiel batted aside the brambles whose accuracy had been thrown into disarray by the assault and pressed forward, driving on even as thorns tore at the cheek and shoulder he failed to block in time.
Bright red blood scattered, sharp pain carved through his entire body, but the distance closed — closed.
And so, with the distance between them narrowed down to a mere few meters—

"Then I'll close the distance myself!"

Abandoning his mid-range bramble assault, Kafma, who had been on the verge of retreating, struck back instead.
For an instant Garfiel braced against the possibility of the shockwave that had swept away the Centaur People, but Kafma did not open his own chest — instead he severed the brambles from his swinging arms and set the wings on his back thrumming.
In that instant, as those wings beat at a speed almost impossible to perceive, Kafma, kicking off the rampart, appeared right at Garfiel's side. It was not raw speed — it was the shift between slow and fast.
With that technique, he had circled around behind the edge of Garfiel's awareness.
But—

"Like hell I'll let you!"

Wrenching himself around to face the direction his awareness had been flanked from, Garfiel roared.
The backfist he unleashed roared through the air and slammed toward the face of Kafma, who had appeared at his side. Kafma blocked it with both arms snapped upward in defense — and those arms were now sheathed in black carapace.
Like Garfiel's own gauntlets, it was a defense meant to protect himself. But brambles, wings, ribs, and now carapace — already a fourth distinct ability. Just how many "Insects" did this man have inside him?

"Ha—!"

The blow was blocked, but the exchange between the two did not stop there.
Extreme close range was Garfiel's own domain. Using the momentum of the turn from where his backfist had been stopped, he swung his left fist through, and drove a headbutt into his opponent as he tried to deflect it.
Impact and groans of pain burst between the two of them, and from there a furious exchange of fists aimed at vital points followed.

"—"

Gauntlet and carapace, both sides traded fists hardened for the fight. But there had been no lie in what he'd claimed a moment before. Any distance within arm's reach was Garfiel's domain — to be pushed back here would make a mockery of that claim.

"Gyaaaaaaahhh!!"

He caught the incoming fist with a rolling parry and, in return, drove his own fist into his opponent's chest and torso. As the jaw recoiled downward, the opposite fist caught it from below in an uppercut, and he slammed a knee into the bent-back torso. With a groan of pain, Kafma beat his wings and fell back a great distance.

"Think I'll let you get away!"

"—Ngh."

He seized the leg of the man leaping back and forcibly slammed him down onto the rampart, back-first. Pressing Kafma's body against the ground just as it was, he charged forward with tremendous force, meaning to tear the wings from his back.
Atop the wall, Garfiel ran on, kicking up clouds of dust and smoke, as Kafma's back sprayed blood and his wings were torn away, one, then two. Carrying that same momentum, all at once—

—In that instant, forcibly beating the wings on his back to lift his body, Kafma's chest cracked open. Beyond the jutting ribs, his eyes met a golden "Insect" nestled within his chest.

"—"

Obeying the outcry of his whole body's survival instinct, he abandoned his hold on Kafma's body and leapt sideways. It was the right call. The exact spot Garfiel had occupied a moment before — everything along the golden Insect's line of fire — was gouged out entirely, carving a round crater into the top of the rampart. Even Garfiel lost the fine hairs off his skin to it, his spine going cold.

"—I'll admit to my own lack of insight."

"Ha?"

Just after tasting the sensation of blood draining from his face, Garfiel was pulled back by that voice.
Looking over, he saw Kafma, down on one knee atop the wall, touching his torn wings, turning eyes filled with admiration and respect toward Garfiel.
Wiping away the blood trickling from the corner of his mouth, he rose to his feet where he stood,

"I had no knowledge that a warrior such as yourself existed. I am ashamed of my own lack of perception."

"Heh, I ain't blamin' you for not knowin'. Bein' known'd be a lot damn scarier."

"—? What do you mean by that?"

Kafma knitted his handsome brow, questioning the true meaning behind Garfiel's words.
From his perspective, it likely had not even occurred to him that a person from another nation — and a person of the Kingdom, no less — could be involved in this great crisis of the Empire.

'If it becomes known just who and where we're from, it could well develop into a serious diplomatic problem. So please, don't carelessly rise to any provocation and say more than you should.'

So Otto, remaining behind at the main camp, had said to Garfiel until he was blue in the face, before Garfiel had plunged into the battlefield like this.
Since Kafma had given his own name, Garfiel had already given his in return, but if he revealed any more information than that, there was every chance that the evidently sharp Kafma would piece the whole thing together.
He could hardly, of all things, turn the Empire's internal strife into a full-blown conflict dragging in the Kingdom as well, right here.

"Sorry, but I ain't tellin' you nothin' beyond my name. Even that was pushin' it. Call it 'a Kururukiak you didn't recognize,' that's the story."

"—. Whether or not you hold your tongue, there is no doubt that you are a man of superb skill. All the more reason I regret this."

"...Regret?"

Garfiel furrowed his brow at Kafma, who shook his head slowly, his voice dropping in tone. There was no falsehood in Kafma's voice, only a regret seeping through with no intent to provoke.
But that regret did not stem from Kafma himself—

"Were the circumstances otherwise, I would have wished to cross blades with you head-on, and to talk with you afterward."

"What are you—"

—talking about, Garfiel meant to demand, questioning the meaning of those words, when it happened.
The strength drained from the knee he'd meant to step forward on, and Garfiel dropped to his knees right there. A rasping breath of "Ah?" escaped him, and pressing a hand to his own chest, Garfiel's eyes went bloodshot.
His heart throbbed, thudding heavily, the beats growing large, dire. —Hearing that tremendous alarm bell of instinct, that outcry warning of mortal danger, Garfiel realized.

—Something was crawling around inside his own body.

"Gagh...!"

"If you meant to trade blows with me at close range, you ought to have avoided taking a hit."

Kafma said this to Garfiel, whose teeth chattered and who wept tears of blood, and thrust out his own hand. At the tips of Kafma's five bent fingers, what could be seen was a white, writhing tube of "Insect."
Through the wound dealt by the brambles, the "Insect" had implanted an egg via that tube. It had since hatched inside Garfiel's body, and was now crawling around within him.

"Guh, agh, ohhh..."

The moment he realized it, Garfiel wrapped his arms around his own body and triggered healing magic.
A powerful wave of healing wrapped Garfiel's body in a faint light, forcibly mending the wounds he had taken. Witnessing this firsthand, Kafma raised his brows slightly, openly showing his surprise.

"Healing magic — so you are a healer as well. Such versatility is a marvel to behold. But—"

"—Nngh, ah, ohh."

"the purpose of the 'Insect' circulating through your body is not to inflict a wound, but to make your body into its own nursery bed. —You may heal a wound, but you cannot heal a transformation."

Kafma's cruel pronouncement was proven true by the affliction that refused to heal.
Garfiel, letting out gasps of agony, cursed the result of having clawed at his own chest — that he had only gone and sealed off the escape route of the "Insect" inside him.
His breathing grew labored, his vision dyed a deep red. Unable to bear watching Garfiel writhe in such agony any longer, Kafma stepped forward with a face full of grief,

"Your skill and your strength — I, Kafma Irulux, shall carve them into my heart, without fail. —Sleep bravely now."

Sheathing his right arm once more in black carapace, he drove his fist into the immobilized Garfiel. Blood sprayed from the impact that shattered his skull, and his body tumbled violently, rolling end over end.
Rolling, rolling, he bounced off the edge of the rampart just as he was, swallowed by a sense of weightlessness, and fell.

"—Ah."

Far too faint a sound to be called a death cry slipped from his throat, and helpless to do anything about it, Garfiel's body fell, utterly defenseless, down past the rampart's edge.

'Listen carefully, Garfiel, and remember this well.'

'If it becomes known just who and where we're from, it could well develop into a serious diplomatic problem. So please, don't carelessly rise to any provocation and say more than you should.'

'That said, knowing you, I don't expect too much patience out of you. A sharp word invites a sharp word back — and as a merchant, I can hardly tell you not to trade in words. So—'

'So, be sure to remember just this one thing, at least.'

'If you do end up trading words with someone—'

—In a battle against the Insect Cage Tribe, it is impossible to avoid every single unforeseeable killing blow.

The creatures known as "Insects" that they took into their own bodies had achieved a form of evolution and growth that surpassed, on the whole, the framework of human imagination, and the abilities that manifested from them could be called endlessly varied.
Add to that the fact that one could resonate with the "Insect" grown within one's own body and, by honing one's technique, spur it toward yet further change, and no two identical warriors were ever born even among the Insect Cage Tribe themselves.

And Kafma Irulux, moreover, was a singular genius born of the Insect Cage Tribe.
Kafma, who had refused the offered promotion to the Nine Divine Generals for reasons of his own belief and philosophy — his strength was one that even Emperor Vincent had acknowledged as being on par with any of his Generals.
As a warrior of Vollachia, he was a towering figure who had been permitted to stand alongside those at the very summit.

Naturally, having been born into the Insect Cage Tribe, he felt neither hesitation nor guilt in borrowing the power of an "Insect" and wielding its ability as his own technique.
Including the power of the "Insect," that was the strength of the warrior known as Kafma Irulux.
And yet, even so—

"It is not that I feel nothing about it."

Required to guarantee victory and to annihilate as many enemies as possible, Kafma had no leeway to spend locked in a duel with just one lone warrior.
And so, he had used a forbidden technique to fell his opponent by the fastest means possible, and struck the enemy down.

"—"

The rite of taking an "Insect" into one's body was, even for the Insect Cage Tribe, something undertaken only after painstaking preparation.
From the time one was a newborn infant, one's body was remade in anticipation of one day taking in an "Insect," and the rite itself was forbidden until one reached the age of twelve. In other words, taking an "Insect" into one's body required, at minimum, twelve years of preparation.
If that process were skipped and an "Insect" implanted regardless, the subject's body could not endure it.
And so—

"To settle matters with you in such a fashion as this is a source of deep regret."

Expressing his regret, Kafma closed his eyes and offered a moment of silent prayer for the opponent he had struck down. But the time he spent steeped in sentiment was brief, and Kafma at once turned back, looking out once more over the far side of the rampart.
He had beaten back the Centaur People's first assault, but a second wave would surely come charging in soon. They could throw themselves at him as many times as they liked — he would beat back every last one of them, each time. It was right after he had thought as much.

"—Ngh, what!?"

Standing with legs planted wide, just as Kafma was about to call up the bramble "Insect" circulating through his body, his eyes went wide at a roar that erupted from close at hand — a tremendous impact like an explosion.
For an instant he suspected the rebel army had brought out some weapon to bring down the rampart, but Kafma at once understood that this suspicion was mistaken.
But that understanding did not lead him to any relief.
For—

"—Garfiel Tinzel."

In the field of vision of a Kafma gone rigid with dread, Garfiel stood — having crawled back up the very rampart he had fallen from.
There he stood: the swollen form of a ferocious beast, a berserker with his entire body wrapped in blazing flame.

△▼△▼△▼△

That the place he had fallen to had been open grassland was the one small mercy amid misfortune.
Had he landed back-first on the stone of the rampart, he might never have wrested up the strength to stand again. Thanks to falling onto earth, the ground itself had sided with Garfiel.

Even so, the damage his body had suffered was severe, and the sensation of his cracked skull and something stirring around inside him was, minute by minute, drawing Garfiel's life toward death.
His cracked skull, healing magic could mend. But to invoke healing magic, the "Insect" was in the way. And because that "Insect" wasn't an attack, healing magic couldn't remove it. In the first place there wasn't even time to use healing magic, the "Insect" was in the way, his skull was cracked, and blood, blood, blood, blood—

"Ga, oh."

Clenching his back teeth, holding his precarious life together by a thread, he stopped trying to think.
It was a cracked head to begin with. There was no reason to rely on it. What he needed was the instinct to live — to obey that, to force his body to move, and then—

And then, from a Magic Stone Cannon that had been set up atop the wall, readied to intercept enemies coming from beyond the rampart, he swallowed the magic stone loaded within it, and crushed it inside his own belly.
In that instant, the searing mana stored within swelled up inside Garfiel, searing the "Insect." Once the "Insect" was burned away, all that remained to Garfiel was a horrific wound alone.

—No. A wound, and an enemy. That alone.

'Listen carefully, Garfiel, and remember this well.'

'If it becomes known just who and where we're from, it could well develop into a serious diplomatic problem. So please, don't carelessly rise to any provocation and say more than you should.'

'That said, knowing you, I don't expect too much patience out of you. A sharp word invites a sharp word back — and as a merchant, I can hardly tell you not to trade in words. So—'

'So, be sure to remember just this one thing, at least.'

'If you do end up trading words with someone—'

"If I did, then—"

His tongue tangled, his reason growing thin under the reflexive Beastification triggered to heal his grievous wounds, and still echoing endlessly through Garfiel's mind were the words of the older-brother figure he could rely on no matter the situation.
Wavering, wreathed in searing flame, he turned to find, facing him, a fearsome enemy.

That enemy was what he needed.
To carry out what his older-brother figure had told him, he needed an enemy.
After all, he'd said it. —Otto Suwen had said to Garfiel:

'—Whoever that person turns out to be, you make absolutely sure to beat them down until they can't stand anymore!'

"I'll smash you to bits——!!"

A beast's roar rose up once more, and Garfiel, transformed into a ferocious beast, shattered the rampart underfoot as he leapt. Witnessing that momentum firsthand, the enemy before him — Kafma — widened his eyes, and smiled.
Smiling, Kafma too raised both arms and hurled himself forward.

"Come, Garfiel Tinzel!!"

"AAAAAAAHHHHHH——!!"

The fang-bared onslaught of the golden tiger diving in, and the wave-like purple brambles filling the field of vision — the two collided head-on, and a point of the star cracked.
It was, quite precisely, as if bearing out the very snag the Emperor upon his throne had felt—

"—"

—It was the first crack of an immovable fall from heaven, one that would carve itself into the Empire's history.
