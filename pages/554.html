Arc 7 Chapter 56: "The Great Disaster"

—Abandon the city and shift to a fighting retreat.

At the declaration from the masked man, Yorna's cheeks stiffened, her gaze sharpening.

Only moments ago, she'd resolved to respect the opinion of the man before her, believing it to be the best course for protecting Chaosflame, the city she ruled.

But contrary to that resolve, what the man had voiced was the abandonment of the city—

"Such an opinion — I could never possibly agree to, not for anything."

"Oh? And why is that."

"Why, you ask? This is the demon city of Chaosflame — the final refuge for those driven from the Empire, who have nowhere left to go and no one left to turn to... And you would have me cast it aside?"

"Not something you can accept, is it. ...Worthless sentiment."

"—! Vincent Vollachia...!"

At the cold words of the arms-folded man, the Empire's ruler, Yorna ground her teeth, calling out his name with a fierce glare still fixed on him. In answer to that address, the man touched a hand to his own oni mask.

And then, with black eyes visible even through the mask, he fixed them on Yorna as he spoke.

"For one going by the name Abel at present, that form of address is inappropriate. And besides, if you think to beg mercy from an Emperor, that would be shallow thinking indeed."

"...So it's 'let the Empire's people be strong,' is it."

"That's right."

The Emperor — no, the man who called himself Abel — declared that this was simply the Empire's way.

The Emperor who stood at the summit of the Vollachia Empire had to be the embodiment of the iron-blooded creed that its people believed in and revered. Whether or not it was true, the Emperor had to declare it so without hesitation.

At Abel's declaration, Yorna chided herself for having pleaded her case the wrong way.

To begin with — did Abel know the truth of that shadow, the identity of the vast darkness swallowing Koururi Castle?

Was that precisely why he had arrived at the merciless decision to abandon the city?

"Do you judge it so dangerous a thing that we must turn and flee at once? Just what, pray, is that thing?"

"—The Great Disaster."

"The... Great Disaster...?"

Abel's answer to Yorna's question was quiet.

But contrary to the quiet of his voice, the weight of the words he'd woven together was more than enough to leave Yorna bewildered — at that phrase, "a great calamity."

"What, exactly, is meant by the Great Disaster?"

"A thing that endangers the Empire's very survival, and brings ruin so complete that not even sunlight reaches it. ...So the Stargazer put it, at least. When I first heard it, I took it for empty bluster."

"—"

"Seeing that, one can tell there was not a shred of exaggeration in the description."

Jerking his chin, Abel named the manifested darkness — the Great Disaster — ruin itself.

At the sound of "Stargazer" spoken from his lips, Yorna couldn't help but feel something bitter within her. The existence of the Stargazers was one of the Vollachia Empire's uglier customs.

At least, to Yorna, it was something she could describe no other way.

After all, the origin of the wish Yorna so fiercely yearned for was by no means unconnected to the Stargazers.

In any case—

"Then does my lord intend to swallow the Stargazer's words whole, and turn tail and run? If so, that would be the very picture of weakness. Hardly the embodiment of the Empire's way at all... or so I find myself thinking, at least."

"If you think cheap provocation will move me, you'd best correct that misunderstanding at once. In the first place, it wasn't I who swallowed the Stargazer's words whole and acted rashly on them."

"...It seems my lord has some feelings of his own about the Stargazers."

"What person of sound judgment could possibly think well of them? But cut one down as unnecessary, and the next arrives at once. That's the troublesome part of them. ...Hm."

"Uー!"

Cutting Abel off mid-sentence was Rui's earnest, insistent growl.

She seemed to be scolding the two of them, Yorna and Abel, for their conversation having nothing directly to do with a means of quelling the Great Disaster.

That was only natural. Because within that Great Disaster—

"Uau!"

"...Yorna Mishigure. There's one thing I want to confirm."

"...What might that be?"

Abel narrowed his eyes at Rui, who pointed at the vastly writhing Great Disaster and appealed to them urgently. As he turned the point of his question toward her, Yorna narrowed her own long, sharp eyes as well.

She could more or less guess what he was about to ask.

It was—

"If this girl is with you, then there should be another — a small black-haired child. Where has he vanished to?"

"...If you mean that boy..."

Yorna's glance, flicking briefly aside, told the answer more eloquently than words.

Where Yorna's gaze went was toward the very thing Rui was so desperately pointing at — the Great Disaster. The darkness that had swallowed Koururi Castle; that boy had been present at the very spot where it first came pouring out.

—No; if anything, to Yorna's eyes, it looked as though that Great Disaster had come welling up from within the boy himself.

"So it is, after all."

Receiving Yorna's wordless reply, Abel settled the final piece into place within his own mind.

It sounded like an ultimatum, and Yorna looked at Abel, murmuring, "My lord..." But in answer to that address, Abel shook his head slowly, side to side.

"My plans are thrown off, but it can't be helped. There's no point clinging to a method if it yields no result."

"And that means?"

"It means the policy doesn't change. We abandon the demon city and fight a retreat. —Even so, losses will occur. Not all of them can be wiped away."

"—! That answer—"

It was the very moment Yorna moved to press him, unable to accept it.

"Aー, u!"

"Gh...!"

Letting out a groan of pain, Abel pressed a hand to his stomach and sank to his knees.

What had done it was Rui — darting into his guard faster than Yorna, who'd been about to close in, and driving a palm strike home with her short arm.

She glared at Abel for having handed down his merciless decision, her breath ragged, a growl rumbling in her throat, and—

"Uau... uau!"

With that cry, Rui spun around, and in the blink of an eye her figure vanished from sight.

It was the instant teleportation she'd shown during the fight against Olbart at the castle keep. Repeating that ability, which couldn't carry her far at a time, Rui made her way back once more to the battlefield ruled by the Great Disaster.

Far off, it was the spot where Kafma was using the insects within his own body to hold the Great Disaster in check.

Yorna couldn't believe that Rui held any decisive blow against that Great Disaster.

If she did have one, she would have tried to save that boy on her own, without relying on Abel. So she had no trump card.

If there was a trump card to be had—

"—Could not the flames of the Sunlight Sword stand against it?"

"—"

"My lord!"

Abel, still clutching his stomach, knelt atop the rooftop.

Even as she thought Rui's blow no more than his due, Yorna reflected that the key to breaking open this situation still lay with none other than Abel.

The Emperor of the Vollachia Empire — and the true Enchanted Sword he possessed, one blade counted among the Divine Swords.

If it was the flames of the Sunlight Sword, then even against that thing called the Great Disaster—

"—I have no intention of drawing it."

But to Yorna's appeal, Abel's answer was nothing she could have wished for.

Nor could Yorna believe it the answer of sound judgment. Not when he himself had judged that Great Disaster dangerous enough, a catastrophe grave enough, that he would not even balk at abandoning the demon city.

"And even so you will not draw the Sunlight Sword... How could I possibly accept that?"

"—"

"Answer me, Vincent Vollachia! If my lord... if you are this Empire's Emperor, then there is a role you must fulfill! If you are the Emperor...!"

Her vision going red, Yorna seized the kneeling Abel by the collar and forced him to meet her gaze.

Even as this went on, Chaosflame, the city Yorna ruled, was being driven ever deeper into crisis. How long could Kafma and Rui hold out against the Great Disaster? How much had the shock of detonating the swallowed Koururi Castle actually managed to whittle down the Great Disaster's power?

None of it was clear. All that was clear was that things could not go on like this.

At this rate, Yorna's vow, her wish, would go unfulfilled.

And for that, she needed — desperately, desperately needed — the cooperation of the man before her.

"If you are the 'Emperor' of the Vollachia Empire..."

"...I will not use the Sunlight Sword."

"—! You—"

Yorna bared her sharp canines to intimidate Abel, who still stubbornly refused to change his stance.

It was a rare thing indeed for her to bare her fangs inside her mouth in so unseemly a manner. And yet, should she wish it, those fangs could easily tear through the slender neck of the man before her.

But such a threat was nothing but an everyday affair for Abel, for the man before her.

A being who stood, day in and day out, on the receiving end of ceaseless murderous intent and hostility from all around him — that was the Emperor of the Vollachia Empire. And precisely because of that, that person had risen to such a station—

"You are the Vollachia Empire's...!"

"—When will you understand, Yorna Mishigure."

"—!"

"Vincent Vollachia, the Emperor of this generation, and the Emperor you picture in your mind are two different things. There is no reason I must act according to your wishes or ideals."

At that plainly declared stance of severance, Yorna let out a small breath.

Then she released her grip on the man's collar and slowly stepped back. Stepping back, she fixed her eyes on Abel, who held a hand to his throat, and ground her teeth.

She understood it even without being told.

Mask or no mask, the man before her bore no resemblance whatsoever to the man Yorna pictured in her mind. Even if the blood of the one Yorna loved flowed through that body.

"...I cannot accept your words."

"If we do not abandon the demon city, you will lose everything else besides."

"This demon city is all of me, now!"

Answering so with both arms spread wide, Yorna drew the pipe tucked at her breast and touched flame to its bowl. An instant later, she drew deep on the pipe and blew the smoke skyward.

The spreading violet smoke became an enormous, enormous cloud, drifting beyond the battlefield where the Great Disaster writhed — toward the heads of the people of the demon city, who must have been trembling before that threat.

The cloud made of violet smoke slowly scattered apart, raining down upon each of the residents — her children, every one — until the violet smoke settled into each person's hands.

"—Love me."

That murmur of Yorna's was so faint that only Abel, standing there beside her, could hear it.

And yet Yorna's intent was understood by every single person who now held the falling violet smoke in their hands. So even without her voice reaching them, they all alike tossed the violet smoke into their open mouths.

"—Be loved by me."

The words Yorna went on murmuring brought nothing to anyone in particular.

If anything, those words were surely an indulgence Yorna granted herself — a ritual invocation, one she needed in order to push herself forward.

Those the violet smoke had reached, those who had taken it into themselves, slowly, slowly raised their faces. —In their eyes, each of them kindled a flame in one eye.

Those loved by Yorna, and who loved Yorna in turn, looked up at the Great Disaster and began to burn their very souls.

Truly, those bound together by soul — the power of the Soul Marriage Technique — spread to cover the entire demon city.

"This city — Chaosflame — I will not let it be lost. —My children and I shall see to it that our ill-mannered guest takes their leave."

Yorna raised her pipe before her, swaying it as she took her stance.

All around, a rumbling sound rose from across the whole city. Fierce and dreadful, it was the sound of countless footsteps, countless stamping feet, countless breaths, countless wills to fight.

It was the advance of the beloved ones, gathering in answer to the loving call of the demon city's mistress.

Watching them, Yorna bent her knees lightly and leapt high into the air.

At the far end of that leap waited the shadow of calamity, brimming with vast darkness.

As the one who would drive back, utterly, the thing that threatened this demon city—

"—The 'Kaleidoscope' Yorna Mishigure shall be your opponent."

A resounding rumble of the earth, and a furious roar as though the entire city itself were screaming.

With the world ruled by those sounds, Abel lowered the hand he'd held to his throat and gazed out at the battlefield.

"—"

Yorna, having charged in recklessly, was swinging around the shattered remains of Koururi Castle and the wreckage of the destroyed cityscape, driving fierce blows into the Great Disaster.

It was an attack so far beyond the ordinary it amounted to striking with entire buildings, but against the Great Disaster — a jet-black darkness of uncertain substance, whose total mass kept right on expanding — how effective it truly was remained unclear.

Was it doing at least some damage, or was it entirely meaningless?

"—It can hardly be entirely meaningless."

It wasn't that he'd been moved by Yorna's reckless assault, but the fact that it wasn't some absurd opponent against whom every attack simply failed to register could be read from the decline in its momentum.

Koururi Castle, swallowed the instant the Great Disaster appeared — Yorna's attachment to the castle she'd long used as her residence, converted directly into destructive force — had certainly shaved down the Great Disaster's momentum.

Had that not happened, the Great Disaster would have gone on expanding at its initial pace, swallowing this demon city whole in a single stroke, and extending its abyss across the entire Empire without pause.

—Even so, if left as it currently stood, the Empire's collapse, merely postponed, was unavoidable.

"That too is one of the infuriating things about him."

Grinding his back teeth, Abel recalled the narrow face of the man who had foreseen the Great Disaster.

A conveniently self-serving being who foresaw the coming threat, yet refused to be asked for anything more than that. Or perhaps he should be called a pawn of the Observers instead.

In any case, resentment toward that man served no purpose here. If anything, there might be some value in turning his thoughts to another being in a similar position.

"—Natsuki Subaru. You—"

Fixing his eyes on the writhing Great Disaster, Abel spoke the name he had been avoiding.

It was just then.

"Abel-chin! We're back!"

"—"

Amid a din so great that every sound risked being swallowed by the earth's rumble and the roaring cry, that high voice nonetheless reached Abel's ears clearly.

Turning, he found the voice had come falling from the sky behind him. Landing with a light sound were a girl waving her hand, and the tan-skinned woman carrying her.

"You're back, are you. Medium, Taritta."

"Somehow! But this isn't quite the situation you told us about, is it, Abel-chin? Where's Yorna-chan?"

"...Over there."

Freed from Taritta's arms and set down on the rooftop, Medium asked her question, and Abel jerked his chin in answer. In that direction was Yorna, flying freely through the sky against the Great Disaster, repeating attacks of mass and momentum using buildings in every direction.

The only two challenging the Great Disaster head-on were Yorna, and Kafma, who likewise possessed a means of long-range attack.

That said, since Kafma's thorns were also the Insects he cultivated within his own body, they weren't something that could go on being endlessly swallowed by the Great Disaster.

Beyond that, the gathered residents of Chaosflame were each dismantling the buildings around them, several people working together to hurl the wreckage at the Great Disaster like cannon shot.

It was the same fearsome group tactic — the city itself turned into a weapon — that had once tormented the imperial soldiers sent to subjugate the city, back when Yorna had raised her flag in revolt against the capital.

But whether that tactic, which had proven effective against a mass of soldiers, held any clear effect against an opponent of such unfathomable strength as the Great Disaster remained uncertain.

To begin with, watching it from a distance like this made it plain enough—

"The Great Disaster... it's getting bigger...?"

"We can't just leave that alone, right!? Abel-chin, what do we do!"

Watching the Great Disaster as it went on gradually expanding, Medium and Taritta pressed Abel for his decision.

Originally, the aim had been to secure Yorna's cooperation and shift into a fighting retreat that abandoned the demon city, but that had gone unrealized because of Yorna's attachment to it.

In that case, the situation would change depending on how Vincent acted. —No—

"—Or perhaps, before that, you'll be the one to change the situation, Taritta."

"Eh..."

Named directly by Abel, Taritta's long, sharp eyes went wide, and she fell speechless.

But it was less the surprise of being told something with which she had no connection at all, and more the sort of reaction that came from not expecting to be told it here.

In other words, she did have some inkling of what Abel had just pointed out.

"S-something about the way you just said that was weird? What do you mean?"

"There's no 'what I mean' about it. Taritta — what did you just call that thing?"

"W-what did I call it, you ask..."

"—You called it 'the Great Disaster.' Where did you learn that name?"

At Abel's pressing words, Taritta caught her breath.

With no idea what was going on between the two of them, Medium blinked in confusion, rolling the unfamiliar word "Great Disaster...?" around on her tongue.

But it was Medium's reaction that was the natural one.

Seeing so unfathomable an entity, no one ignorant of it could call it "the Great Disaster." Only someone who already knew of its existence could name it so correctly.

Which meant—

"Were you taught by the stars a means of averting ruin?"

"—! W-wait, please, I...!"

"—"

"I... am..."

Taking a step forward, Taritta pressed a hand to her own chest and choked on the words that should have followed.

At Taritta's abnormal state — her face gone pale, her eyes darting about — Medium hurried to her side, calling "Taritta-chan!" and supporting her body.

But Taritta had no room left to respond to Medium's concern.

Likely, she had believed she'd kept it hidden all this while. Perhaps she had thought she could go on staying silent about it forever.

"Fool."

There is no such thing as a secret that stays kept, or a truth that never comes to light.

At least, if one makes no effort whatsoever to conceal that secret or truth, the day it all comes out is bound to arrive. All one can do is push that day further off.

Or perhaps, if it could be put off until after one's death, then within the world as that person perceived it, one might say the secret had been kept to the very end.

"Taritta. If you know that Great Disaster, then — do you regret it?"

"Regret? Regret what... Abel-chin! What are you talking about!? What did Taritta-chan..."

"Isn't it obvious. —Failing to finish me off with that arrow of hers, in the forest."

"—Ah."

Struck with shock, the color drained fully from Taritta's face this time, the light in her eyes trembling weakly.

Staring straight into those eyes, Abel let out a breath and went on.

"Or will you answer your heavenly mandate even now, huntress of the deep jungle. No — perhaps I should call you this instead."

"—"

"The new Stargazer, who inherited the heavenly mandate to ward off the Great Disaster."

The violent rumble of the earth, the thundering roar — it was as if they were proclaiming the end of the world.

"Guh, ngh, nnngh...!"

He clutched his head, trying to cover his ears, but he couldn't.

That was the drawback of having only one arm. He couldn't cover both ears with both hands and shut his mind off completely from this end of the world.

He pressed his right ear against a raised shoulder, jammed the fingers of his outstretched hand into his left ear, and tried to make do with makeshift, incomplete earplugs. It was hopeless.

The ground shook. The air itself cowered. The world was dying.

All of it was a symbol of terror, eating away at Al, draining the strength from his entire body.

"Why... why, of all places, here...!"

His voice cracking, he cursed, and cursed to exhaustion, the hopeless turn events had taken in this very moment.

Of course, such cursing meant nothing at all. Because this wasn't even a curse, not really. It was nothing more than sour grapes — the kind of after-the-fact "I should've done this, I should've done that" that was nothing but a hollow display for himself, a claim that he hadn't really lost.

Hadn't he told himself he was prepared for this to happen?

—No. He hadn't. He'd never actually steeled himself for it. Even when the possibility crossed his mind, he'd only looked away, pretending to think optimistically that it surely wouldn't happen.

Acting alongside him — alongside Natsuki Subaru — the risk of it coming to this had been more than enough. More than enough wasn't the word; it had been more than plenty.

If anything, acting alongside anyone other than Natsuki Subaru never would have led to a situation like this.

And yet it couldn't be helped. Because he couldn't just leave him be. He mustn't leave him be. Natsuki Subaru must not have broken, not there.

Because that was necessary — and so he had—

"—Oho, and here I thought, who's bawling their eyes out — turns out it's you."

"—!?"

"Ka-ka-ka-ka! What was that jump just now! Looked just like a caterpillar, that did — priceless, priceless!"

At the voice that suddenly sounded within the room, he startled upright and spun around.

Whether it was because the sight of him had been so pathetic, the one who saw it clapped his hands — no, stamped his feet in reaction instead — laughing uproariously.

Unfortunately, it seemed the man wouldn't be able to clap his hands again, not anymore.

Because—

"Honestly, my own right hand, my companion for over ninety years, went and left me first. What a bother. Now how in blazes am I supposed to make my ration pills from here on out? Ka-ka-ka-ka!"

So saying, the eerie old man — Olbart Dunkelkenn — laughed with delight, waving the right hand whose wrist and everything beyond it had vanished.
