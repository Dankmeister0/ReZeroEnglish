Arc 3 Chapter 17: "An Arrogant, Insolent Speech"

The remark landed in the tension-charged hall like a bomb tossed into the room. It was tantamount to playing with fire in a powder magazine, and everyone present held their breath and watched to see what would happen. Who had spoken the offending words no longer mattered. What mattered was what consequences it would bring.

Breath held, all eyes turned ever more intently toward Priscilla at the center of the hall. Bathed in those stares tinged with awe, Priscilla merely glanced toward the direction the voice had come from and said,

"Tedious, and worse, artless abuse. The envy of the vulgar, jealous of me — I've heard so much of it, it wouldn't even serve as a lullaby."

Priscilla split the stagnant silence threatening to take hold of the hall with a voice utterly, sincerely bored, and without the slightest hesitation. There was not a trace in her tone of any concern for the unease around her, no consideration paid to it whatsoever. It was plain to everyone that this arrogant remark, through and through, was the honest entirety of her inner thoughts.

Faced with such a bracingly shameless display of arrogance, Subaru found himself keenly aware that she, too — in a sense quite different from Crusch — stood on a plane set apart from ordinary people.

Judging by the reaction around her, that earlier remark had clearly been treated as something close to an insult or a slur. "The Blood-Colored Bride" was hardly a gentle title, and though it went undenied, before anyone could press further as to what it meant—

"Well then, shall we begin again — might I ask for your name, and that of your attendant as well?"

That Miklotov threw out the main topic before the mood in the room could sour any further spoke to the seasoned experience of a man who had weathered many such scenes. So too did his ability to show — without it ever feeling like mere formality — genuine respect toward someone far younger than himself.

For someone occupying the country's highest seat, he seemed the type to spend more thought on looking after those around him than on pulling everyone forward by force. A rather unenviable position, in its way.

It wasn't as though Priscilla failed to grasp the meaning behind Miklotov's mild manner. She too nodded readily, as if the rude remark from before had already been forgotten.

"Very well. It would be cruel to further shorten what little time remains to an old man's bones. Be grateful for my generosity of spirit."

Adding an unnecessary remark, she thrust out her ample chest all the more haughtily.

"I shall now give my name — carve it into memory and do not forget it until the end of this life of yours. My name is Priscilla — Priscilla. Ahh, Priscilla... now, what was it again?"

Having charged out of the gate with great vigor, she then stumbled like some doddering old woman losing her train of thought. Subaru very nearly lurched forward with a reflexive retort, but barely managed to restrain himself out of respect for the seriousness of the surrounding mood. Even so, there was no doubt that Priscilla's remark had thawed the atmosphere in the room into something oddly lukewarm.

She tilted her head a few times, then looked up at Al standing beside her and jabbed him in the side.

"Now then, Al. I am troubled. Lend me your aid, and quickly."

"Nah, I mean, I never expected you to trip right out of the starting gate, so I'm surprised too. What about the pounding heart I had, all excited waiting for my own introduction turn — who's gonna make up for that?"

"I care nothing for the pounding of your heart. My will comes first — now, out with it."

"Talk about self-centered! Anyway, don't tell me — you're not seriously saying you forgot the family name Barielle, are you?"

"—Ah, that was it."

Al, having offered his hand tentatively, had it seized in a firm grip, and he tilted his head skyward, palm to forehead, lamenting his lord's regrettable state of mind. Setting aside the attendant who made no attempt to hide his pose of grief, Priscilla turned forward once more and smiled boldly up at the Council of Wise Men on the dais.

"I am Priscilla Barielle. The next queen. Bow before me."

Terse, and — Subaru felt — about as complete a self-introduction as one could give.

At the very least, it was not the sort of speech that came from the mouth of someone who seriously wished to become queen. What's more, unlike Crusch, she seemed to have nothing further to add — as if that single line just now had said everything there was to say, and she looked entirely satisfied with it.

Surely it couldn't end there, Subaru thought, on edge now for reasons quite different from before, when—

"Barielle... would that be, House of Lord Leip Barielle?"

It was Miklotov who interjected the question. The old man twirled his white beard between his fingers, narrowing his eyes as if searching his memory.

"Hmm. Now that I think of it, I don't see Lord Leip's face among us. Might I ask—?"

"That lecherous old fool suffered a grievous accident half a year ago, never regained consciousness, and just recently kicked the bucket."

"Goodness, Lord Leip has... Hmm. In that case, what was the relationship between Lord Leip and Lady Priscilla...?"

Miklotov's face took on a pained expression at learning the man in question had passed. Priscilla snorted at the old man's question.

"To me, he'd be called my late husband, I suppose. Since he never so much as touched my fingertips, ours was a relationship in name only, in the truest sense."

A hush fell over the hall once more. Subaru felt a faraway ringing reach his ears as he turned Priscilla's words over in his mind. In other words, she had just spat out news of her spouse's death as though it bored her.

"Princess, isn't that a bit too pitiless a way to put it, even so?"

Perhaps he'd finally had enough of it, or perhaps he meant to smooth over the poor reaction from those around them — Al leaned in and murmured to Priscilla. But she brushed aside even that small consideration without a second thought.

"What need is there to dress up a fact when stating it as fact? That old fool took me as his wife, burned with ambitions far beyond his station, then got himself caught up in an accident that had nothing to do with him, and burned out on his own before the spark could ever grow into a real fire. If that isn't a joke, what is? Well — for someone who staked his very life on it, it's not much of a laughing matter, and in the end he was a thoroughly worthless old bag of bones."

If a single clean cut would have been merciful, Priscilla instead cut the already-slain deceased down again and again with the blade of her words. Even Subaru, who had never so much as seen the man's face, felt a stirring of sympathy for him, while Miklotov, who apparently had known him, wore an expression of grief.

Whether she noticed the change in Miklotov's expression or not, Priscilla, her manner unchanged, swept back her orange hair.

"If that old bag of bones had any value at all, it was this — he handed over everything he'd hoarded, all of it, to me. Since he and I were husband and wife, and he had no heir... well, in short, House Barielle now belongs to me."

Having reached this reckless conclusion, Priscilla swept her gaze over those around her, as if daring anyone to object. Under that gaze, a swell of discontent could be felt, but no one gave it concrete shape. One might have hoped for something from Rickert, who had argued so fiercely with Crusch, but even he, discontent plain on his face, kept his mouth shut. Whether there was some reason for it, or whether he already knew from the start that he would not be taken seriously, her outrageous behavior seemed poised to go unchallenged. And then—

"Hmm, I understand the situation. Given our long acquaintance, the news of Lord Leip's passing came as something of a shock, but... Lady Priscilla's account holds together. That the head of House Barielle is now yourself is certain, indeed."

"Naturally."

"I would like to hear further details in time, but — what of your knight there?"

To Priscilla nodding along serenely, Miklotov now turned his attention to the attendant standing beside her. Al, suddenly handed the center of attention, said—

"Ahhf... ah, me?"

—responding in a voice that was clearly stifling a yawn, proving himself, alongside his lord, an incarnation of taking things at his own pace. The two of them, master and servant, seemed to compete at lowering the temperature of the room. Given that Subaru had at least some passing acquaintance with them compared to the other candidates, it was, in a sense, a development worse for his nerves than for the parties actually involved.

"Yes, you. An unusual getup — a face I don't recognize from the Royal Guard... a helmet, is it?"

"Oh, you noticed? This helmet's Vollachian Empire make, from down south — a real pain to smuggle out. Sturdy, long-lasting, and looks cool besides, so I make good use of it."

"The Vollachian Empire's... then you are not affiliated with the Royal Guard?"

"Being called something as fancy as 'knight' makes me itch, honestly. I'm more like, y'know, a wandering mercenary type. A drifter, that sort of thing."

Gesturing to himself with his single arm, Al openly and proudly revealed his identity in that muffled voice of his. As he laughed it off with a shake of his shoulders, his helmet clinked with a metallic rattle, which only further provoked those around him. Sure enough, veins bulging, voices rose from the Royal Guard camp at his manner.

"What insolence! Do you not understand where you stand?!" "It would be cruel to demand courtly conduct of a crude barbarian, but that does not mean this behavior can go unremarked." "And furthermore, what is the meaning of hiding your face before the Council of Wise Men? An Imperial helmet, no less... this is disrespect beyond measure!"

"Whoa, hey now, don't get so worked up. My delicate heart's gonna get shredded under all this pressure, y'know?"

Against the knights' angry voices, Al remained utterly unruffled. But as he spoke, he traced a finger along the surface of his helmet.

"Well, I don't think you're wrong, exactly. Hiding my face in front of important folks like this — yeah, getting treated like a suspicious character is fair enough."

"Had you not looked the very picture of a suspicious character, you would never have caught my eye, Al."

"Not exactly a comforting thought. Anyway, I'd appreciate you forgiving the rudeness of hiding my face. See, the thing is—"

Looking up at the Council of Wise Men, Al slipped his fingers into the gap of his helmet and lifted it just the barest amount, revealing part of the hidden face beneath. The area below his chin rose, exposing his jaw and the area beneath his nose to the open air—

"Ugh——"

Someone let out a faint groan at the sight of the wretched scarring on his face. Al then turned in place, model-like, displaying the same sight to the entire hall. Naturally, the knights' fury subsided at once.

Small wonder. Even from the portion visible alone, Al's face bore the accumulated marks of countless battles — burns, gashes, scar upon scar. Subaru too carried his share of conspicuous scars, but these were like his own, multiplied tenfold without exaggeration.

Whether the reaction from those around him went as he'd intended, Al, removing his finger and settling his helmet back into place, laughed.

"So yeah, that's the unsightly mug I've got under here, and I hope you'll all forgive the rudeness of hiding it while I face you like this."

"No, the rudeness was ours — my subordinates behaved poorly."

To Al's deliberately vague deflection, Marcos bowed his head in apology. Then, his stony expression hardening further, he said,

"I apologize again for pressing further, but — an Imperial background, and those scars... could it be you have experience as a sword slave?"

"Heh, well look at you, Captain. Didn't expect you to know so much about the shady corners of that secretive Empire. Yeah, I've got sword-slave experience. A veteran of about a decade or two."

A murmur rippled through the hall once more, several of the knights muttering the term "sword slave" under their breath. It wasn't a word Subaru recognized, but from the characters alone, it seemed to mean something like "a slave who wields a sword." From that, one could probably guess at the sort of position it implied.

"Meaning you were somewhere that turned fighting into a spectacle for entertainment?"

"That's the one, bro. Well, that's the story behind my skills and my scars — hope that clears things up. Oh, right, I never gave my name, did I."

At Subaru's muttering, Al pointed at him in confirmation, then rapped his own helmet lightly with that same finger.

"Cut ties with Vollachia too, so these days I'm just a wandering drifter — call me Al. Got myself taken on by the princess here, and I've been plugging along ever since."

Al grinned at Marcos with the same easygoing, deadpan air as ever. His demeanor, much like his lord's, showed not the slightest concern at having been glowered at by those around him. If anything, it was the knights who had been condemning him moments before who now found themselves at a loss for words, struck dumb by the sheer severity of the scars carved into his body. Along with the missing arm, it had become plainly, unmistakably clear that the road he had walked had been anything but smooth. And—

"No way..."

That was a fact that struck Subaru too, having witnessed the same sight, with no small measure of shock.

Until now, Subaru had avoided directly addressing Al's condition. Partly this was to avoid the thoughtlessness of bringing up the topic of missing limbs to a one-armed man; partly, it was because he had unconsciously refused to confront it as a reality that might, someday, become his own.

If Al truly came from the same world as Subaru, then what had happened to him was not, by extension, something Subaru could treat as somebody else's problem. Losing an arm, bearing scars on his face too terrible to show others — for Subaru, whose body already bore countless gashes, Al was quite literally one possible future.

A chill, bone-deep, crept up along Subaru's spine. Heedless of Subaru's private turmoil, the proceedings of the Royal Selection continued on.

"Hmm. If you hail from the Vollachian Empire, then how did you come to be connected with Lady Priscilla? That country is a sort of world unto itself, keeping not only information but people and goods from leaving its borders."

"There's always a back way, a side road, a detour, no matter where you are. Picked my moment and made my escape from the Empire. As for why I'm with the princess here now..."

"It is nothing complicated. The result of my little amusement."

Priscilla, silent until now, apparently unable to stomach not having her own turn to speak, cut in. Interrupting Miklotov's question to answer it herself, she toyed with the colorful rings adorning her fingers.

"That I should become queen is all but the will of heaven. Which being so, any attendant would do just as well as any other. Therefore I chose, for my attendant, whatever pleased my own eye. The result stands there before you."

"I see, quite so. And the method of this selection?"

Rather than push back, it was faster to simply accept and move the conversation along. Without touching on the arrogant core of Priscilla's remarks, Miklotov flattered her pride while urging her onward. Pleased by this consideration, she blew lightly on her nails, her expression content.

"Nothing much to it, really. — On condition that whoever caught my eye would be added as my attendant, I gathered braggarts of arm and blade from across my domain and had them compete. It made for an amusement diverting enough, in its way."

Answering Miklotov thus, Priscilla cast a meaningful sidelong glance at Al. If her words were to be believed, then Al, in the end, was the one who had caught her eye. It made the extent of his true ability, one-armed as he was, all the harder to imagine. At Priscilla's answer, Miklotov and the other members of the Council of Wise Men nodded, apparently satisfied.

"So then, the winner of this tournament was he...?"

"Nah, didn't win, actually."

But to that satisfaction came an unexpected interruption. Al watched the old men's surprised faces, shoulders shaking with amusement.

"Life's not sweet enough that a one-armed guy just breezes past a crowd of braggart swordsmen. Making it into the final four in a knockout bracket was already my luck running hotter than it had any right to."

"Th-then why did Lady Priscilla choose him as her attendant...?"

"I already told you. I chose whoever pleased me."

At the question, Priscilla thrust out her chest and gave the back of the man beside her a hard, forceful slap. A dry, cracking sound rang out, and as Al let out a yelp of "Ow-ee!" she went on,

"To begin with, gathering under so brainless a banner as 'braggarts of arm and blade,' overconfident enough to answer such a call, and yet even under all those curious stares, never once denying this strange existence of his. And above all — of all who had stayed in the Vollachian Empire, not one other claimed to hail from beyond the 'Great Waterfall' with so grand a tall tale."

Priscilla's smile deepened, her crimson eyes beginning to gleam with delight. Her words picked up pace, and she stamped her foot on the floor with a loud crack, as if to draw every eye in the room.

"Therefore, I chose Al as my attendant. That I should be the one to choose Al, and that I should walk the path to becoming queen — both, without exception, are the will of heaven meant to make me shine."

Priscilla declared, without the least shame, that the world itself had blessed her. In it there was not a shred of hesitation nor doubt — only a confidence so absolute it was almost dreadful. Setting aside the sheer outlandishness of what she said, her bearing, poised to stand at the very summit, was precisely the sort of charisma possessed only by those born to command others. A pity, then, that with such fine qualities wasted on so unappealing a substance beneath, it all amounted to nothing.

"Hmm, I understand the relationship between the two of you. But that leaves me with a question — by what means was it learned that Lady Priscilla is the Dragon's Priestess? If it was not a knight who found her..."

Unlike those left speechless by Priscilla's declarations, Miklotov had the capacity to take it in stride. Having accepted her words, he moved on to the next point of doubt — and, indeed, what the old man raised was precisely the sort of question worth asking.

Looking at each of the camps so far, it seemed that for each candidate, the Royal Guard had entrusted its members with the emblem and sent them out to search, and the knight who found the candidate in question had then attached himself as that candidate's attendant. Ferris for Crusch. Reinhard for Felt. And presumably the same would prove true for Anastasia and Julius. That being so, the question that naturally arose was about Priscilla's group, and—

"In Emilia-tan's case..."

Presumably it would turn out that Roswaal was her sponsor, in some equivalent role. Roswaal was the one supporting Emilia in the capacity of patron. And as for the knight-like figure who was supposed to have discovered her — Subaru had yet to lay eyes on any such person. Not only had there been no such figure on Roswaal's domain, but if there was no one here in the royal capital either, then presumably no such person existed at all. Even as Emilia looked to be growing isolated by the mood of the room, Subaru felt, deep down, a certain relief that no such knight had shown his face.

Thinking that far, Subaru suddenly realized he did not even know why Emilia had come to participate in the Royal Selection in the first place. What purpose had brought Emilia here? And for what reason had she come to aim for the throne? Belatedly, Subaru realized he had never so much as touched on even that much.

"Members of the Council of Wise Men, the answer to that question has been confirmed on the Knights' side."

Stepping forward with an "if I may," it was Marcos who addressed Miklotov's question. His stony expression furrowed faintly as he spoke.

"In truth, when the previous king and his kin passed away, it was the late Lord Leip who was quickest to notice the clause within the Dragon History Stone, and who moved swiftly to secure a candidate. Originally, the management of the Dragon History Stone had fallen to Lord Leip's responsibility, and... there are reports suggesting several days passed before the contents of the clause were even reported."

Marcos spoke, plainly reluctant, of the suspicious conduct of the deceased. At his words, something apparently clicked for Miklotov, and he touched a finger to his long, drooping brows, nearly hiding his eyes.

"I see — he was, after all, a man of no small ambition. It follows that he would put considerable effort into the search for the Dragon's Priestess."

Guardian to the next queen — and in this case, having taken Priscilla as his wife besides — the man must have harbored ambitions for power on a still grander scale. Considering that, he really had been quite the extraordinary old man.

"And yet, how ironic, that having found the very priestess he sought, misfortune should befall the man himself."

"He was an old bag of bones whose stories never once stirred the heart. That in his final, final moment he should scrape together some humor is about the shape of it. Truly, a death fit only for a bitter laugh."

Without so much as a smirk, Priscilla handed down her verdict, stamping her late husband's life with a failing grade. Miklotov, at the harshness of the assessment given to someone he had known, could manage no reaction beyond a wry smile. In any case—

"There is no need to speak further of some dead relic of the old guard. I stand on my own, and mine alone. All other reasons are trivialities not even worth touching upon. Freely, without reservation, you shall revere me as your queen."

Brimming with confidence, Priscilla announced the conclusion she had reached, several times over, in this short span of time. While no one in the hall could find words in response to her manner, only the jet-black helmet standing beside her turned to look at her steadily.

"So, princess, what's the payoff for doing all that? What do we get out of it?"

"A simple matter. — Come to me, and you shall thereby gain the right to be counted among the victors."

Laughing, Priscilla drew a breath and continued.

"Given that the Royal Selection is, at its heart, a contest, the ultimate aim must surely be victory. Therefore, choosing me is, in itself, the answer. Therefore, following me is the righteous path for all of you."

"That heaven itself has chosen you, you mean..."

"Naturally. For in this world, nothing happens except what is convenient for me."

And thus—

"I am the one fit to be queen. No — none but I could ever fulfill that role. There is nothing more to be said. All you need do is stand before me and let your eyes shine at my splendor."

Sweeping back her orange hair and casting it boldly into the air, Priscilla turned away with serene composure. Her bearing made clear that she had said all there was to say, and she began walking toward the center of the room, her back turned fully to the Council of Wise Men on the dais. Following after that retreating back, the jet-black helmet cast one last glance up at the dais.

"Might sound a bit much, put that way, but everything our princess just said is a hundred percent fact. Come over to the princess's side, and so long as it doesn't run against her wishes, you're guaranteed to be rewarded — heaven's chosen the princess. Chosen Priscilla."

Having said his piece, Al swept his gaze once around the entire hall. Everyone caught in that gaze held their breath, and having confirmed as much, the one-armed man gave a light wave of his single arm.

"Well, whenever you decide to fall in under the princess is up to you. Personally, though, I figure you're better off jumping on the winning horse sooner rather than later."

Master and servant alike, brimming with confidence to a degree that suggested they'd left humility behind somewhere in the womb. As the two of them returned to the row of candidates, the tension that had gripped the hall naturally eased somewhat, and an atmosphere suggesting everyone might finally catch their breath began to spread.

Subaru too, the time having passed during which he'd had to mind even his own breathing, felt some relief, and worked to loosen the unnecessary tension in his body. At least the speech of the one he was acquainted with had concluded — hardly what one could call a proper speech, but it was for the best that it hadn't spiraled into real chaos.

"Why the hell do I have to be this on edge..."

Muttering with a misplaced flash of anger, Subaru glared at the backs of the two as they returned to the line. Just then, Priscilla turned back unexpectedly, and their eyes met at an unforeseen moment. Catching Subaru's gaze, she smiled meaningfully, then, as if to spread a bit of charm, gave him a wink. The gesture was excessively seductive, and Subaru, with no resistance built up against such things, was thrown into an instant fluster.

No surprise, really — she was practically his own age, and already a married woman — or rather, a widow. A young noblewoman widow, her voluptuous body left with nowhere to go — just putting it in those terms was material enough to leave one's chest feeling rather full.

"A girlish boy, a woman in men's clothing. And now a rich widow — the genres just keep piling up."

The one regrettable thing was that, widow though she was, there wasn't the faintest shadow of that fitting about her. Though perhaps it was cruel to expect more from someone who had merely lost a husband in name only, on paper.

"————Ah."

While Subaru's thoughts wandered down such pointless paths, his eyes met Emilia's — who had apparently been following Priscilla's gaze as well.

She seemed a little surprised to find Subaru at the end of Priscilla's line of sight, and then, lowering her eyes, looked away — from Subaru's perspective, with an air of sad betrayal. Struck by a shock like a blow to the gut, Subaru staggered, mentally scrambling for a wildly out-of-place excuse: No, that wasn't emotional infidelity, I swear!

Even amid all this, the Royal Selection showed signs of proceeding steadily onward.

"Next, then — Lady Anastasia. And her knight, Julius Juukulius! Step forward!"

"Right, then." "Our turn."

The purple-haired girl responded with an easy grace, and Julius raised one hand serenely toward the sky, then, sweeping it downward, made the sleeve of his uniform crack loudly through the air. The dry, sharp sound rang out, wiping the previous mood clean whether anyone wished it or not, and Anastasia, smiling with an "much obliged," stepped forward at the cue. Julius fell into step beside her, with not the slightest sign of strain.

—And so, the pair who, of all in this Royal Selection, most embodied the ideal of lord and knight, finally stood revealed together.

Facing the next candidates, Subaru too steadied his focus once more and turned to face forward.
