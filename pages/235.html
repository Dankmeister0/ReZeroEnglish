Arc 4 Chapter 69: "Liar"

The hard ground's touch, cold as ever, dragged Subaru's consciousness back to wakefulness.

Eyes opening face-down, Subaru pushed himself up and spat out the dirt and gravel that had gotten into his mouth. Spitting again at the taste of earth, he looked around—his vision uncertain in the faint darkness.

—The Trial chamber, in the tomb.

Subaru's world beginning again in the same place it had ended. Even as he felt relief at having made it back, a sense of confinement tried to ensnare him at the thought of facing a world that might once again become hell. Sensing the dead end that thought was leading toward, Subaru shook his head in rejection. Then he stood, brushing the dirt from his body, and swept his gaze around the room—and found Emilia, lying collapsed in a corner.

"..........."

Calling out to her, Subaru moved to jog over, then hesitated for a moment. What crossed his mind was the instant just before he'd been pulled back—Emilia, holding a fading Subaru in her lap, sharing a kiss with him without ever noticing he was disappearing.

Without meaning to, Subaru touched his own lips with his fingers, frowning at their chapped feel. In that final moment, he'd been coughing up blood; his face must have been thoroughly soiled. He had no way of knowing what Emilia had felt in kissing him like that, but it surely hadn't been anything that stayed as a good memory.

It was the same for Subaru, who had been at death's door—he could recall the event itself, but he hadn't carried back the sensation or the feeling of it. His first kiss ever, and his first with Emilia, had been barred by the impermanence of death and left him with no lingering emotion at all.

"――――"

And yet Subaru's hesitation wasn't regret over that. Recalling the kiss wasn't about looking back fondly—it was the sense of crisis over the change in the Emilia who had given it. The Emilia who had come to depend on Subaru, who had fled from reality.

With Puck nowhere to be seen, unable to bear the pressure from the villagers and the people of the Sanctuary, and having lost even Subaru as her support, Emilia's heart had cracked and shattered. If that was the result, then what had Emilia been like in the loops before this one?

"..........."

Subaru had left the Sanctuary to return to the mansion four times. Of those, only once—the last time—had he made it back and been reunited with her. So what had become of Emilia the other three times? In every one of those other cases, the Sanctuary had already suffered the Great Rabbit's assault by the time he returned. Even if Emilia's mind had held its balance, it seemed reasonable to guess she couldn't have withstood the witchbeast's rampage. But what had become of her heart?

"What had become of her, hell—there's nothing to guess. If it ends up like that when I'm not there, then I have to be there. There's no other way..."

There was no room to view the situation optimistically. No point deceiving himself by leaning on some hoped-for future, glossing over what he might overlook. To seize the best possible future, he had to keep assuming the worst, always.

The world prepared the cruelest, most unreasonable fate imaginable for Subaru. Then of course Emilia, Beatrice, Elsa, Roswaal—every circumstance surrounding them would naturally be arranged in the form most difficult for Subaru to face.

"What I have to do is..."

Save Emilia's heart. Save everyone connected to the Sanctuary from the threat of the Great Rabbit. Save his friends at the mansion from Elsa's rampage. —An impossibly high, impossibly steep road.

—Can I do it? Somewhere inside him, his weaker self asked the question, laying out an escape route, an excuse, a hedge.

—It's not about whether I can. I just have to. Baring his teeth at that weakness in himself, Subaru spoke his resolve not to go back on his vow.

Pick out the problems, the obstacles. Make the clear conditions plain. Build the timeline. Judge how best to use the time available. And try again, as many times as it takes. Even if Subaru's heart wore down with every failure, if there was a future to be seized at the end of it, that was fine by him—no matter how much of what he didn't want to see he ended up seeing. And so—

"—Emilia. Are you all right?"

He reached out and shook the shoulder of the beloved girl lying there. At the touch of Subaru's fingers, her closed eyelids trembled, and Emilia's consciousness was pulled back from the world of the Trial—from the past, into the present.

Her eyes opened, violet radiance reflecting Subaru's face, and within mere seconds tears welled up as Emilia, denying the past, clung to him. And so, holding her back as she sought support, Subaru swore in words that he would be her support—while in his heart, he swore an even firmer vow.

—He would protect Emilia through it all, and save everyone, every single one of them. No one else. It had to be Natsuki Subaru who did it.

At the end of the previous loop, in the midst of confusion, Subaru hadn't been able to fully organize his thoughts. Now he set about sorting through them.

Above all, the most important was Roswaal L Mathers. Roswaal, who had fallen prey to the Great Rabbit's fangs before Subaru's eyes and scattered his life away.

Roswaal knew about Subaru's Return by Death. He hadn't reached the trigger being death itself, but he knew Subaru was "doing it over." Whether he'd learned that after coming to the Sanctuary, or from even earlier, Subaru didn't know—but even that fact had probably been recorded in the Gospel Roswaal carried.

In the previous loop, Subaru hadn't been able to recover Roswaal's Gospel. If it had been tucked inside his robes, it was now inside the Great Rabbit's belly along with Roswaal's body. Even if it had been left in the temporary lodging where he'd been recuperating, Subaru hadn't had the presence of mind to dash inside and check. So there was no way to touch on its contents.

—What, ultimately, was Roswaal's goal?

If he was acting according to what the Gospel prescribed, where did the reason for throwing away his life at the end lie? —That answer, too, was surely in the Gospel's contents. Roswaal was probably staking his life on obeying the Gospel's writing.

Subaru didn't know in what form Roswaal's Gospel's descriptions took, but the idea that acting in accordance with the text was a signpost leading to a desired future was probably the same as with Petelgeuse's Gospel and the others. As Subaru recalled, when the Witch Cult's Gospels diverged from what was written, Petelgeuse would insert his own interpretation, allowing a certain latitude in the text by his own judgment, ultimately trying to make things add up in the end. Roswaal was different. Roswaal, who could act with "doing it over" in mind, would—when a future arrived that diverged from what was written—pay with his own life to erase that divergent stretch of time, making it as though it had never happened.

Petelgeuse, adapting flexibly when things diverged from the text. Roswaal, refusing to allow divergence, resolutely forcing the text to be upheld.

Both were troublesome opponents who possessed a Gospel; both shared the motivation of wanting to make the Gospel's contents a reality—but their ways of facing it were utterly different. Comparing Petelgeuse's manner of dependence to Roswaal's, Subaru couldn't help feeling that Roswaal's nature was the worse of the two.

—The problem was the actual contents of Roswaal's Gospel.

If it recorded even the outcome of this incident befalling the Sanctuary and the mansion, then tragedy would repeat itself as many times as it took to reach the conclusion Roswaal desired. Even the snowscape that had covered the Sanctuary last time was likely something Roswaal had acted to bring about, in order to make the Gospel's text a reality. Meaning that snowfall was a phenomenon meant to occur every single time. The reason Subaru hadn't encountered it before now was simply that he'd never once managed to make it back to the Sanctuary after leaving it for the mansion.

Roswaal's causing snow to fall on the Sanctuary was a means of isolating Emilia.

What possible meaning could there be in that? The unbearable pressure from those around her should have been more than enough to eat away at Emilia without resorting to such a roundabout method. Strongly conscientious as she was, Emilia was keenly sensitive to the expectations of those around her, gritting her teeth through anxiety and helplessness to keep pushing forward with the challenge— to the point that, without Subaru there to let her lean on him, she'd lose her footing entirely and fall into self-destructive despair.

Was driving Emilia to that self-destructive despair Roswaal's aim? But if Emilia stopped acting "for someone else's sake" like that, the Sanctuary would never be freed. And if it wasn't freed, that meant losing the option to "flee" when the Great Rabbit came. Roswaal's actions and his attitude toward Emilia gave rise to no end of contradictions.

And above all, there were the words Roswaal had flung out at the very end, just before he was devoured.

—Strip away everything except what truly matters to you.

That was what Roswaal had let slip. He'd also said that doing so would let one become like him.

Setting aside whether Subaru wanted to become like Roswaal or not, if he probed the meaning behind those words, it meant that Roswaal had stripped away everything except what mattered to him, and that was what had left him standing there, at the very end. In fact, resolve enough to throw away even his own life—there was no room to doubt that.

If he forced the text to be upheld, drove Emilia into isolation, and brought the situation to a close in the form he desired—would Roswaal then obtain that single, solitary "important thing" he'd named? Or, what had been Roswaal's true intent in telling Subaru all that?

Either way—

"There's no way in hell I'm letting go of anything."

Emilia mattered to him. But of course, there were so many other things Subaru wanted to protect, wanted to stay beside—too many to count.

Subaru's narrow world would turn desolate the moment even one piece of it went missing. Greedy, self-centered as he was, there was simply no way he could stand for that. So he couldn't heed what Roswaal said.

"Roswaal. I'm—not going to become like you."

Somehow managing to calm the sobbing Emilia, Subaru carried her, now asleep, out of the tomb.

The faces waiting outside were shocked at the Trial's failure and at Emilia's state—same as always, the first order of business was moving to Ryuzu's house so Emilia could rest. Along the way, there was a moment where Garfiel, seemingly mindful of the mood, forced an overly cheerful air that struck Subaru as almost painful to watch instead, and another where he noticed Ryuzu sending him a meaningful glance—but Subaru made no mention of either.

The former he deliberately let pass, meaning to confirm what he wanted to confirm later. The latter, because Subaru already had a rough idea of what that look meant.

"C'mere a sec. Got somethin' to say to your face."

With the sleeping Emilia left to Ram and the night's gathering breaking up, Garfiel called Subaru aside. Having half-expected the summons, Subaru answered with a plain "yeah" and let the short figure lead him ahead, out toward the woods at the edge of the Sanctuary. He couldn't tell if this was the same spot as the last time Garfiel had called him out, but he could tell that the look on Garfiel's face was the same as before.

Eyes blazing, Garfiel glared straight at Subaru. It was a world apart from the mood right after leaving the tomb—unmistakably hostile. Naturally, the first question out of his mouth was—

"Oi, you..." "You saw something, inside the tomb... didn't you?"

Cut off before he could get started, a vein bulged on Garfiel's forehead—only for his eyes to go wide when he heard his own intended words spoken right back at him. Oddly enough, seeing him caught off guard like that dispelled the usual thorny impression, revealing something almost childlike underneath.

At Subaru's one-eyed squint, Garfiel quickly shook his head, snapping his fangs as if to shake off the surprise.

"Damn creepy, that. But if you already get it, this'll go quick. Spill it, straight up. Unless you wanna hurt."

"Right. I've got some things I want to confirm too, and I'm a busy man. —I don't mind answering your questions, but how about you answer some of mine in exchange?"

"You think you're in a position to be layin' out trade terms? I'm the one who gets to feed on you, and you're the one offerin' up flesh that ain't your own so you don't get eaten. Like the old saying—'Mージェー offered up his younger brother after his older one.'"

"That's the worst-sounding idiom you've come out with yet, out of all of them."

Shrugging in reply, Subaru lowered his eyes for a moment and chose silence. Garfiel looked impatient, but even so, he didn't press Subaru to hurry. Subaru let out a long breath and decided on his answer.

"I went through the Trial in there. I saw the past."

"—! So you did have the qualification after all...! And? The result was...?"

"Failure. It's not something you can just casually deny or accept the past over, is it. ...It should be the same for Emilia, too."

Truth in the first half, a lie in the second—Subaru watched Garfiel's reaction to each. Garfiel's face had changed color hearing that Subaru had undergone the Trial, but Subaru could see his shoulders drop in relief on hearing it hadn't ended in a breakthrough.

"You look awfully relieved there."

"The hell're you—"

"I thought—you're taking Emilia's failure, the Sanctuary staying unopened, and looking pretty happy about it."

At Subaru's words, Garfiel—brow furrowed—gave a short snort as if catching on. He lowered his stance slightly, glaring up at Subaru from below.

"You... what'd you hear about, in the past—no, in the Trial...?"

"How this Sanctuary came to be. The lighter background details. And—you and Ms. Ryuzu, I guess."

"—! No... way... you...my—"

Anticipating what Garfiel's attitude would likely lead to next—asking whether Subaru had learned his past—Subaru shook his head first. He hadn't gone that far into the content of Garfiel's Trial, the part Garfiel was worried about. Only—

"I don't know what you saw in your past, not that much. But I've got a pretty good guess as to why you're keeping quiet about having gone through the Trial yourself."

"...You know even that much?"

"Some of it's guesswork. Feel free to get mad at me for reading too much into it."

Subaru's acquaintance with Garfiel, in this world, was still barely a day old. Most of the information Subaru would come to learn through his dealings with Garfiel from here on wasn't, by rights, something he should be told yet at this stage. The same, of course, went for Ryuzu Meyer herself, sleeping in the testing grounds.

So here too, Subaru meant to bluff his way through by dangling the existence of the Trial and Echidna—a convenient chance to gain this kind of knowledge inside the tomb. As things stood, Subaru couldn't think of any new information he'd gain, or any new move he could make going forward, by talking with Garfiel. Right now, he just wanted to wrap up this conversation quickly. But—

"—Hey. Why don't you try the Trial again?"

"――――"

To Garfiel, who'd gone silent, Subaru posed that question on top of it all. Hearing it, Garfiel dropped his head, refusing to let his expression show. His arms hung limp; the wariness had gone slack, drained of force. That was why Subaru judged there'd be no immediate attack.

"I just—I can't help feeling like there's no consistency to what you're doing. You push Emilia toward freeing the Sanctuary, but then you look relieved when she fails. And if you're actually serious about stopping the Sanctuary's release, your methods are way too half-assed."

If he truly didn't care about the consequences, Garfiel could simply beastify and take Subaru's and Emilia's lives right there. Of course, that would turn the refugees' and Roswaal's feelings against him in the worst way—but if Garfiel's true goal was to seriously obstruct the Sanctuary's release, that would be the surest, quickest way. And yet Garfiel never went that far, not until the very last possible moment—not until, say, Subaru actually crossed the line of trying to get the refugees out of the Sanctuary.

—There was some boundary inside Garfiel that Subaru still didn't know about.

If he could just make that clear—

"I'm thinking I'd like to borrow your strength too."

"—Don't... don't talk outta your ass."

At Subaru's words, Garfiel's voice caught for a moment before he shot back. Lifting his head, Garfiel—his usual intensity entirely gone from his expression—shook his head.

"Like you said yourself, my interests and yours don't line up. I ain't gonna go outta my way to get in your way, but I sure as hell ain't gonna go outta my way to help, either. Neutral. Neutral's fine by me."

"You realize that stance suits you about as badly as possible, right?"

"Ain't about suitin' or not suitin'. I do it 'cause I gotta."

Saying something so entirely unlike himself, Garfiel kicked at the ground in irritation, kicking up dust, and turned his back on Subaru.

"If that half-demon girl clears the Trial, fine, that's that. Once she's taken in, I get it, she's gotta clear the Trial to get back out—even I understand that much. —But whether I follow along outside the opened Sanctuary, that's a whole different story."

"..........."

"You wanna leave, leave, whatever. But don't you go tryin' anything inside. And you don't go steppin' any further onto my side of things than that, either. Do that, and I won't do a damn thing."

"Even if I told you we need your strength out there?"

"...What I want, there's no way in hell you people can give it to me. That's the end of what I got to say. Don't go doin' anything you don't need to."

Without listening to what Subaru had to say, but speaking somewhat more rationally than usual, Garfiel walked away from the spot. Up until now, Garfiel had shown fierce rejection whenever the conversation pressed too deep—but this time, of all times, he hadn't lashed out at Subaru. Where did that difference come from? What was his real intent?

"There's a mountain of things I want to think through... but—"

Running his fingers through his black hair, Subaru simply held the tangled information in his mind, for now. He wanted to gather it, organize it, piece it together, and work out an answer—he wanted that badly.

"—even that, I won't get anywhere thinking it through alone."

If he wandered into the labyrinth of his own thoughts, Natsuki Subaru would be dragged back into the negative spiral once again. To avoid that, what he needed now was—

"Can I lean on you again, too...?"

Just to think of the one person in this world he could confide his troubles to.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

As if driven by some unbearable emotion, Subaru's feet had grown restless.

After parting from Garfiel, his walking pace quickly turned into a run. Breath ragged, sweat on his brow, eyes trembling, Subaru ran. There was one destination: the tomb, lit by the moon hanging in the night sky.

It was right after that exchange. Having said he wouldn't interfere with what Subaru did, Garfiel wasn't about to get in the way. No one was there to stop Subaru as he dashed back into the tomb.

Reaching the entrance, Subaru came to a stop and roughly wiped the sweat with his sleeve. Calming his ragged breath with repeated deep breaths, he faced forward and peered into the darkness of the tomb. What he needed lay within—or rather, in the dream castle that could be called its depths: Echidna's world.

"If I just wish to know..."

That was how the white-haired witch had explained she'd invite him. Clinging to that, believing it, Subaru had come here.

There was so much he wanted to ask, so much he wanted to talk through, so much he wanted her to help him puzzle over, so much he needed help finding an answer for. Things only that Witch of Greed could be told—he wanted to tell her, and have her show him a way forward.

What he had to do and what he wanted to do lined up perfectly. All he needed now was an answer to how to make it happen—something other than agonizing over it alone.

"――――"

Going to Echidna's castle, pouring out every worry, every doubt weighing on him, and clinging to her charity—it wasn't as though he felt no shame in that. Nor was he free of the fear that confiding everything to Echidna, breaking some taboo, might once again let the shadow of jealousy engulf the Sanctuary.

Even so, Subaru hoped.

That the Witch's guidance would become the key to breaking open this invisible dead end of fate.

"The way I am now... I should meet the conditions."

Because he was lost, this thoroughly. Because he was resolved, this thoroughly, to do everything in his power.

If not for the greed the current Subaru sought and craved, what else could be called an Apostle of Greed?

He'd throw his life away as many times as it took. He'd give up his pride too, gladly, however much of it was needed. Because this—pathetic, unsightly, powerless, ignorant—was Natsuki Subaru's very best effort.

"I'm counting on you, Echidna...!"

Steadying his breath, Subaru quietly braced himself and stepped into the tomb. Entering the Trial chamber that had already accepted one challenger tonight, he looked around the square space and walked out to its center.

"No telling where, or what kind of ritual's needed, I guess I'll just wing it..."

The second time he'd been invited into the dream, aside from desperately wanting an answer, Subaru had still been in the same sprawled posture he'd been in right after Returning by Death. He couldn't recall there being any special condition, like offering something up.

For now, Subaru knelt where he stood, clasped his hands together, and closed his eyes. What he pictured in his mind was the white witch's form, and as if calling out to her, he laid out his feelings one by one—pleading his desperate need to grasp at a future that seemed stalled dead.

"――――"

And so Subaru simply kept waiting, in silence, as time passed. The tomb's cold air brushed his bare skin, and yet he could feel the clammy cold sweat beading on his own forehead.

Wanting it. Desperately. Craving it. With everything he had.

If, wanting this much, craving this much, it still didn't reach her— —then was greed simply a desire too vast for human hands to hold?

"—Ah?"

Just before the weakness could slip in, Subaru—eyes closed—had the illusion of a sudden white light breaking into the darkness behind his eyelids. —No. Not an illusion.

"――――"

White light invaded his vision, the pitch-black world eroded away little by little. Before he knew it, his kneeling body had toppled sideways to the ground, and he could feel his consciousness pulling away from reality, dragged toward another world.

—The invitation to the dream castle had begun.

To the castle where Echidna waited, where this time, he wanted to hold a real discussion to seize the future. In his hazy consciousness, Subaru held onto only that single thought, over and over—

"—Look upon the now that should not be."

Just as his consciousness slipped away, he thought he heard a voice like that.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

A sensation like intoxication shook Subaru's every nerve.

He didn't know what had happened. Awareness came suddenly. Like switching a channel.

A change of consciousness that dragged along its noise and clamor, as if flipped straight from one channel into one of an entirely different mood. Right after Returning by Death, Subaru always felt something similar to this.

The gap between the horror of the world just before death and the situation right after Returning by Death carved a sense of wrongness not just into Subaru's consciousness and body, but into his very soul.

『――――』

And upon waking, Subaru realized he was trying to make a sound, and no sound came. He tried to put a hand to his throat, only to realize—belatedly—that he had no sensation of a throat, nor of a hand, either.

『――――?』

Hands, feet, eyes, mouth—more than that, even a body—none of it existed for Subaru now. Only his consciousness floated in the air, surveying the world as though he had become nothing but a viewpoint.

An unnatural sensation, as though in a dream, his body entirely absent. And yet the sense that this wasn't the first time he'd been in such a state—was that, too, simply because this felt like a dream?

By thinking of it that way, Natsuki Subaru tried to detach his awareness from the scene before him. But it was impossible. Without a body, Subaru couldn't turn his head away, couldn't even close his eyes.

All he could do was watch what lay before him—a sight burned inescapably into his consciousness.

"—iar"

A small, hoarse voice. So faint it was hard to make out what it said. And yet—

『――――』

He understood, instinctively. This is bad, some instinct told the consciousness that was all that remained of Subaru.

It was a voice he shouldn't hear. Something he shouldn't have noticed. Something existed there that he wasn't meant to know.

But no matter how strongly his consciousness wished otherwise, the burning scene didn't change. It wouldn't disappear. It only forced its "result" onto Subaru, searing it in.

"Liar... liar, liar liar liar liar liar liar...!"

The repeated word, once unintelligible, took clear shape—and the muttering, as if it had forgotten how to end, began to mix with tears. A heartbreaking sight. An unbearable sorrow was woven through it. Seeing it, hearing it—it was among the very worst of this world's suffering.

『――――』

Why am I here now. Why was I made to notice this. He'd failed. He'd erred. He'd misjudged. He shouldn't have noticed. Shouldn't have known. Shouldn't have been made to realize it.

—If he hadn't kept believing otherwise, then he—

"Liar, liar! Subaru's... liar! Li—ar—!!"

Tears streaming in torrents from violet eyes, Emilia collapsed to her knees, screaming those words. As if condemning a betrayal, as if denying the nightmare before her, hair flying wild like a child's, Emilia screamed as though driven mad.

—Before the bed where Rem lay, Subaru's corpse, dead by his own hand, a dagger through his throat, lay next to the sobbing, screaming Emilia.
