Arc 3 Chapter 26: "A Corroding Spirit"

—A sky of clear, sweeping blue stretched out above Subaru as he lay flat on his back.

Looking back, about a month and two weeks had passed since he'd been summoned to another world.

In that time, how many times now had he ended up staring up at the sky like this? A great cumulonimbus cloud blotted out much of the sunlight, but the blazing near-noon sun still poured down through the cloud's thickness to reach him.

The dazzling light seared the backs of his eyelids. Squinting reflexively, feeling tears well up quietly from deep in his eyes, Subaru found himself musing idly.

"Come to think of it... I haven't run into a single rainy day in this world yet."

He hadn't yet experienced a day where rain fell steadily from morning to night.

There'd been a few nights of light drizzle late at night, leaving the trees and lawns damp come morning, but he had no memory of experiencing real, proper rain here.

The temperature was just warm enough to make long sleeves a bit much—by feel, something like June or early October back in his old world. Given how little it rained, maybe he'd landed in this world's dry season.

"Shall we call it a day, then?"

The voice broke in, abrupt, on Subaru as he sat halting and adrift in his rambling little mind-game.

Still on his back, he lifted his head just enough to glance toward the voice—standing a few meters from where Subaru lay sprawled on the ground was an old man.

He stood a bit taller than Subaru, his white hair—thick for his age—neatly combed back. He was dressed head to toe in a black butler's uniform, and he carried himself with a crisp, disciplined air to match the starched fabric.

His spine was straight as a rod, yet for all the elegance of his posture the man himself seemed entirely at ease, gentle wrinkles etched into his mild, kindly face.

That said, gripped in the old gentleman's hand was a wooden sword with an oddly long blade.

"Nah, not yet. I was just doing a little philosophizing."

"Oh? How interesting. What were you thinking about?"

"Raging fire up top, flooding down below... what is it?"

Swinging both legs up and then down for momentum, Subaru pushed himself to his feet.

There was a heavy ache lodged deep in his body, but the blows had left no lasting damage—their effect was minimal. Taking the moment of rising to roll and check each joint, Subaru spun the wooden sword still in his grip and leveled it at the man in front of him—Wilhelm.

"Alright, one more round, if you'd be so kind."

"Out of curiosity, what was the answer to that philosophy of yours?"

"Ah—wetting the bed, then getting defensive about it."

Even as he spoke, Subaru closed the distance, dropping low to swing the wooden sword up in a rising half-circle.

The tip cut through the air, the gust of wind it stirred carrying enough force to shatter bone—no restraint whatsoever. But—

"Whoa!"

"You're putting too much tension into it. Your hands, feet, neck, hips—and your face, too."

Wilhelm's wooden sword slid into the path of the strike, and the arc of the swing was smoothly bent off-target. In the disorienting sensation of a blow aimed at the head sailing clean over the head of a man taller than himself, the old man pivoted, and a sword dancing in his palm cut a stinging line across Subaru's body.

Jaw, throat, solar plexus, groin—each one a vital point strung along the body's centerline. Wilhelm's wooden sword traced carefully over every one of them, and the mere touch of impact sent Subaru flying.

The force of the blows, being nothing more than his own momentum turned back on him, was slight. But even so, being struck in his vital points was enough to steal the breath from his lungs and enough of a jolt to the pain receptors to flood his vision red.

Subaru tumbled head over heels, knocked back the exact same distance he'd closed. Once again he ended up spread-eagled on the ground just as before, and when his red-tinted vision cleared, there was that same blue sky looking down at him, mocking.

"Shall we call it a day, then?"

Wilhelm's call came flat, without a trace of sarcasm or scorn.

How many times now had Subaru heard that voice asking after his will to continue—

"That's a no, for the twenty-third time."

"My, keeping such faithful count, are you?"

"Course I am. I might forget the times I've done something someone didn't like, but I never forget the times someone's done something I didn't like... that's the kind of man I am."

Boasting about something that was nothing to boast of, Subaru slowly, carefully hauled himself back up. The damage was minimal—Wilhelm's restraint remained flawless as ever.

Using the wooden sword—regripped—as a support to stand, he cleared the dirt from its tip with a practice swing, then leveled it at Wilhelm once more.

"One more bout, please."

"As many as you like. Perhaps let's change things up a bit this time."

The moment the consent left Wilhelm's mouth, Subaru charged in without waiting to hear the rest.

Dropping low, body coiled around his hips, this time his strike would be a point, not a line—a thrust, its striking area reduced to a bare minimum, aimed at revenge.

Matching the mastery of the old man before him—striking four vital points in a single instant—was flatly impossible no matter how he struggled. So Subaru's thrust would be a single strike, and he'd pour everything he had into it instead.

His target was the torso—a large enough mark—anywhere near its center would do. With that intent behind it, the tip of the wooden sword drew slowly, inexorably toward the center of the old man's black clothing—

"It landed—?!"

He'd thrown the strike meaning to land it, but the shock came from never actually believing it would. And yet the total absence of resistance in a sword that should have connected dead-on robbed the tail end of his triumphant shout,

"Fum—ble—?!"

—as the sensation of his footing being swept out from under him stole away all sense of his own weight, and up and down flipped inside out.

His brain couldn't keep up with the sudden release from gravity, and in his spinning vision the ground filled his sight in close-up. Certain he was about to take the full weight of his body on his face with no way to stop it, he shut his eyes against the premonition of incoming agony. And then—

"Majorika!"

An instant before his face would have hit the ground under its own momentum, something hard and rod-like pressed into his stomach instead. With the sensation of slamming full-speed into an iron bar, his upside-down body flipped once more—until, correctly this time, both feet touched down against gravity.

The recoil sent both his arms shooting up reflexively, straight as rods, and Subaru froze in the pose of a gymnast who'd just stuck the landing on a floor routine.

He had no idea what had just happened.

All he had was some mysterious voice in his head going A full rotation?, and it was in the midst of that that Subaru suddenly noticed the wooden sword he'd been gripping so tightly was gone from his hand.

"Ahh—"

The instant he twisted his neck around in a panic to search for it, his eyes met the old man's, smiling right beside him. In the old man's hand, Subaru could see two wooden swords now.

"I—I don't wanna get hurt—"

A countless number of gentle, light body blows struck all over him, and Subaru was once again sent flying without a shred of resistance, tumbling across the ground until he was covered in grass.

The taste of dirt spread through his mouth; he spat it out and waited for the damage report from each part of his body to come in. Arms: functional. Legs: numb but intact. Stomach: starting to get hungry. Face: the bad eyes are just how he was born. That concluded the report—Wilhelm's restraint as exquisitely calibrated as ever.

"Working up quite a sweat, I see."

His twenty-fourth rendezvous with the meadow, all told.

That crisp, dignified voice reached Subaru—who lay face-down, plastering a forceful kiss into the dirt—from somewhere a level higher up.

Rolling onto his side to clear his line of sight, Subaru looked toward the voice. There, leaning against the terrace railing and looking down at him sprawled out in the garden, stood a woman.

"I only heard your voices, but it sounds like you've been going at it with quite some fervor."

"Ah, Lady Crusch. Have we disturbed your work?"

At the appearance of the green-haired woman resting her chin on her hand, weight propped against the railing—Crusch—Wilhelm, looking up at her from the same ground as Subaru, put a note of apology into his voice. Though in truth there was nothing in the sound of it that matched the words literally, and Crusch, on the receiving end, simply waved a hand,

"No, I was just thinking of taking a breather."

She answered Wilhelm so, then added, "Besides—"

"—I would never be so arrogant as to think effort, whether another's or my own, is ever a disturbance. And it hardly does to let the help sit idle. Do let yourself be worked hard, old Wilhelm."

"Understood. Even so—"

Wilhelm bowed deeply at Crusch's generous words, then cast a sidelong glance at Subaru.

"Shall we call it a day, then?"

"I'd like to think I'm not so oblivious as to say yes to ending things after that little exchange."

Brushing the grass off his grass-covered body as he stood, Subaru turned himself over to check for the third—no, the twenty-fourth time that he was in one piece, spat the dirt from his mouth, and said,

"Getting utterly wrecked while a beautiful woman watches really does a number on a guy's pride, y'know. My manly-man gauge is getting scraped down to nothing."

To Subaru, cracking jokes and rolling his wrist, Wilhelm silently tossed back the wooden sword he'd taken from him. Catching it neatly as it spun through the air, Subaru tested its weight in his palm with a wry laugh, and—

"No need to worry on my account. This isn't the first time I've watched you get beaten senseless."

"Ugh."

"I've only been told the circumstances secondhand, but I can't deny that was a touch too direct of Lady Crusch, just now."

At Wilhelm's words, spoken while looking with some pity at Subaru clutching his chest and groaning, Crusch raised an eyebrow with a face utterly devoid of malice and said, "Is that so?"

"It's only natural that one's blade falls short against an opponent one's skill cannot yet reach. And still, if all one has shown is an unbending resolve, then I'd think there's cause for regret, perhaps, but none for shame."

At Crusch's words, spoken with a hand to her chin, Subaru felt a ticklish sort of discomfort. Part of it was surprise that she'd speak of that whole embarrassing display in terms of praise at all; part of it was that no matter how she phrased it, the result itself—one no words could overturn—hadn't changed one bit.

And that was precisely why, from right in front of Subaru, she—

"And besides—if being coached under the watchful eye of a woman is such an ordeal for you, then it's one you've apparently already been enduring, over and over, for quite some time now, hasn't it?"

To Subaru, half-sinking into a sea of thought, Crusch said this and let her lips curve into a knowing smile. Leaning half her body out from the railing, she sent a meaningful glance sweeping across the terrace—a glance that passed clean over Subaru and Wilhelm facing off directly below her, toward the far edge of the garden.

There—

"...It's a different feeling entirely, making a fool of myself in front of family."

"I'd think continuing to lay your hand bare in the palm of someone who may well become an enemy someday poses its own problem... though I suppose, having taken in such a person myself, I'm no different. It seems even one's own heart isn't so easily understood, after all."

Making a conscious effort not to consciously register where Crusch's gaze had gone, Subaru kept her company as she, above him, turned her thoughts inward on herself.

And once she'd finished her spell of reflection,

"Wilhelm."

"Yes."

"I find myself wanting to move my body a little. I'll clear up what remains of my work and come down shortly. It's earlier than planned, but I'll ask for today's instruction."

"Understood. Please, take your time—no need to rush."

"Given my present state of mind, I'm afraid that's a rather difficult request to grant."

With a faint smile, Crusch stepped back from the railing and, her bearing as crisp and composed as ever, turned back toward her office. Her long green hair whirled as if dancing with the brisk motion, glimmering deeply where it caught the sunlight, before vanishing from Subaru's sight.

Watching her go, Subaru let a faint tension escape on a released breath.

He gave a wry laugh at how blatantly his own body relaxed with relief.

However much Crusch might be the type of person he had trouble with, wearing that so plainly on his face could only leave the worst possible impression on her.

In any case,

"Shall we call it a day, then."

"Not a question this time—so I guess that's how it is, huh."

Catching how the question mark had dropped from the tail end of Wilhelm's words as he raised his wooden sword in a loose stance, Subaru understood that this fierce, gentle stretch of time had come to its end.

At Subaru's expression, disappointment plain in his dark eyes, the old man closed one eye.

"Once Lady Crusch comes down, I'll need to fulfill my duties as her instructor. That is, after all, the reason House Karsten employs me."

"I won't push my luck any further than that. You've already been sparing me your free time as it is—I'd be asking for some serious divine punishment if I pushed harder."

There was a certain loneliness in the practice coming to an end, but making the man stop on his account would only be an imposition. He was already in a weak enough position, asking for something this presumptuous as it was. He wanted to avoid earning any displeasure—though it might already be far too late for that.

"—Here I come."

"Whenever you're ready."

True to his declaration, Subaru's body kicked off the dirt and launched forward.

No feints, no probing. For his twenty-fifth challenge—and his last—Subaru chose a single strike, head-on, with no tricks at all.

He raised the wooden sword high overhead and, gripping it firmly with both hands, brought it down with everything he had.

The straight vertical cut sliced through empty air, its tip losing its target and plunging into the earth, and Subaru—unable to fully kill his own momentum—stumbled forward, pitching ahead.

And then—

"——!"

As countless flashes of blade seemed to cut through him all at once, that was how it felt to Subaru as he was beaten down.

Two days had already passed since Subaru had entered Crusch Karsten's residence.

Residence—though in this case, that didn't mean the main house of House Karsten, the duchy Crusch headed. Her true residence, the one befitting a duchess with lands of her own, wasn't in the royal capital at all; the mansion where Subaru was currently a guest was what might be called a secondary residence, used only during stays in the capital.

That mansion stood in the upper reaches of the royal capital—in a corner of the noble quarter lined with high-class residences, one that stood a cut above even that, a district of what could hardly be called mere houses so much as clusters of estates.

In scale, it naturally fell short of Roswaal Manor, which had been carved out with reckless abandon across a vast stretch of countryside, but in interior and ornamentation, even an untrained eye could tell it matched or exceeded it.

Given that it also had to serve for entertaining guests here in the royal capital, a house of ducal rank no doubt had to pay careful attention to such things.

That it wasn't simple nouveau-riche indulgence was clear enough from Crusch's own pragmatic character. Though he also couldn't help thinking the price tag ran a bit steep even to call it a necessary expense.

On that point, the simplicity of the garden—needing none of those little trappings—seemed to reflect the temperament of the mansion's mistress, and Subaru found something oddly comfortable in that.

Aside from the trees ringing the mansion, carefully tended by a gardener's hand, the garden itself was nothing but a spread of low grass—not a shred of excess anywhere. Then again, the garden's simplicity probably suited its actual purpose well enough.

Its main purpose was as a place for the very sort of combat training Subaru had just been undergoing. That it needed no flower beds to please the eye seemed, in its own way, to express a nature in Crusch that favored steel over flowers.

"Can you stand, Master Subaru?"

Wilhelm's voice reached Subaru, adrift in his rambling thoughts, from somewhere off in the distance.

He answered with a silently raised hand, catching the edge of the old man's relief in his ears,

"Thank you, again, for today."

"Think nothing of it. You've merely kept an old man's bones from boredom. If anything, I should be ashamed—I've never been much good at teaching others."

"Isn't that a problem, coming from a guy who's employed as a swordmaster?"

"With Lady Crusch, it's less instruction and more simply helping her build the right mindset for real combat... something along those lines. There's rarely much I need to teach in words."

He answered Subaru's remark with a wry smile, then, his face cool and utterly unsweated, straightened his own appearance and said,

"You're likely too exhausted to move right now. There's still some time before Lady Crusch comes down. I'd suggest staying as you are until you can move again."

"I'll do just that. The feeling under my head is out-of-this-world nice, too! I might be losing on pretty much every front, but this one moment right here? I'm firmly in the winners' bracket!"

Nodding faintly at Subaru, gloating with poor manners as he lay there, Wilhelm straightened his own back and looked down at Subaru and what lay beyond him,

"I'll go make a round of the mansion. After your rest, feel free to do as you like, the same as yesterday. —I leave Master Subaru in your care."

"—Yes. Thank you for your consideration."

Standing close beside the reclining Subaru, a person who hadn't uttered a single word until now answered him so.

Still in her familiar black-based apron dress, she knelt right there on the ground, having slipped her own knees beneath Subaru's fallen head to cushion it softly.

A maid girl with short blue hair beneath a white headdress—Rem.

The mock battle between Subaru and Wilhelm—a battle in name only, really nothing more than one-sided training—she'd watched, silent, the whole way through, from a corner of the garden.

And then, once Subaru had struggled to the very last of his strength, she'd rushed to his side, and was now tending to his effort in the form of a lap pillow.

Her devoted figure was a model of what a servant should be, and in the gaze with which she doted on Subaru resting in her lap, feelings clearly ran deeper than any mere attendant's. But Wilhelm, having taken note of it, chose not to remark on it.

That look was, after all, the very same shine he'd watched countless times before, day after day, in this mansion.

So the old man, without touching at all on what lay in the blue-haired girl's heart, bowed with a polished, practiced motion, and said,

"Well then, I'll take my leave. Ferris should be back by afternoon. It would help if both you and Master Subaru could be in your rooms by then, Lady Rem."

"We're sorry for the trouble. We'll be sure to."

Rem accepted Wilhelm's words with quiet composure. At her demure response, the old man gave a satisfied dip of his chin, then narrowed his eyes just slightly at Subaru.

Then, without another word, he turned his back, and this time he truly did vanish from the garden.

With the old man's uncanny presence gone, the garden all at once flooded with a sense of release, as if something taut had finally snapped.

As the overwhelming sense that even the plants had been forbidden to breathe faded away, the garden transformed back into an open, windswept meadow, with nothing left to block the wind or the twittering of small birds.

In the midst of it, Subaru simply lay there vacantly with his eyes closed, giving over every sense in his body—and the whole of his spirit—listlessly, to the soft feeling beneath his head.

Rem, bearing the weight of him, said nothing.

She simply, silently held the weight of him as if cherishing it, and every so often, as though reminding him that time hadn't actually stopped, slid her fingers into his black hair and combed through it with a gentle touch.

"...I'm—"

Abruptly, Subaru set that near-motionless stretch of time in motion.

At the sound of the voice, spun out on trembling lips, hoarse as a whisper, Rem's fingers stilled. Indulging in her kindness as she waited, just so, for his words to continue, Subaru took his time—plenty of it—before finally saying the rest,

"...I'm weak, huh."

He muttered it, a fact too plain to bother denying.

The wind blew, releasing the bangs Rem had been stroking to the open air, and at the feel of it, he could sense Rem shift her knees just slightly, stirring.

"Yes, I suppose you are."

After a moment's silence, Rem said this in a short, clipped voice.

She'd agreed so naturally that it took him a moment to even register it as a response to what he'd just said—and once he did register it, once he'd taken in the content of those words, Subaru, still in his lap-pillow position, glared up at the upside-down Rem above him and said,

"Hey, isn't this normally the part where you're supposed to say something like, 'That's not true at all. Subaru-kun is incredibly wonderful and cool and super strong and mammoth happy, my heart's just going pitter-patter,' and hype me up?"

"If that's what Subaru-kun wants."

To Subaru's request, delivered in a slightly high-pitched imitation of a woman's voice, Rem answered so without so much as a change of expression. At her attitude, Subaru braced himself with a preliminary "Alright, then—"

"Okay, take two. —I'm weak, huh."

"Yes, I suppose you are."

"Were you even listening to what I just said?!"

"It's Subaru-kun who should ask that—were you listening to Rem's answer?"

At the unexpected comeback, Subaru straightened both legs stiff in protest, and Rem, even at that reaction of his, tilted her head, her expression still one of pure affection.

Subaru's voice caught at her words, and then, slowly, he turned over in his mind what Rem had just said. And then,

"Rem's amazing, huh."

"Yes—though Sister is even more amazing."

"I can't say I get that whole Ram-supremacy thing of yours, but yeah—you're amazing."

Letting the strength drain out from his legs, Subaru imposed on himself a boneless sort of limpness across his whole body and entrusted everything to Rem's lap.

Rem, once more, slid her fingers into the bangs of Subaru, eyes shut like that, playing idly with them, and said,

"Rem is here to do whatever it is Subaru-kun wants, after all."

"...Agreeing with me putting myself down for being weak—is that really what I want you to do?"

"Isn't it?"

Tilting her head the other way from before, Rem asked this with an utterly pure gaze.

Subaru produced no words in answer, only let out a long, low, deep breath through his nose, and let his silence stand as his reply.

Quiet, undisturbed, idle time went on flowing by.

Just before sinking into that sea of thought, on the verge of being swallowed whole by the drowsiness rising up in him, Subaru drifted, half-asleep, losing track of just where he stood between waking and dream.

"Shall we head back soon? We wouldn't want to get in Lady Crusch and Sir Wilhelm's way."

But the passage of time rocked on, indifferent to any of that.

At Rem's words, her knees swaying beneath him, Subaru fell into thought, then twisted his neck, shifting his posture so as to rest his cheek against her leg instead, and said,

"Just a little longer. Your thigh feels too good."

"Alright. —If that's what Subaru-kun wants."

Rem, having begun to tense her knees, let the tension go instead, and Subaru's proposal was accepted.

Indulging in that boundless kindness, Subaru let himself sink deeper and deeper into a swamp-like peace where he need not think of the things he didn't want to think about.

—Three days since the day the start of the Royal Selection was declared. Three days since the day Subaru and Emilia parted ways.

Natsuki Subaru was, right on schedule, rotting away.

  • "力強いヴェーゼを土にかましていた" — rendered as "plastering a forceful kiss into the dirt" (i.e., face-planting). "ヴェーゼ" is an unusual loanword choice for "kiss" whose exact source/pun is uncertain; translated for sense rather than literally.
