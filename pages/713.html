Arc 9 Chapter 18: "A Hundredfold Nuisance"

—What was recounted was days of solitary struggle and regret that could not be wiped away.

A story of history that had to be treated courteously, gravely, preciously — so it had been firmly decided. Even having spilled it all out, the stance of facing forward, refusing to let it go, was fragile.

"The price," she said.

As the toll for leaving this dream world, the "Witch" who said so was hateful beyond bearing. Flashing a glimpse of red tongue, the harmful being who carried an air of mischief seemed, with her hollow beauty, to be toying with me, scheming to lead me exactly as she pleased.

"All right, I've decided."

The first time, she had decreed forgetting; what would she determine this second time? As if mocking me for stiffening instinctively, the flower-scented "Witch" touched with her fingertips a white handkerchief fluttering in the wind.

—Its white fabric trimmed in gold, embroidered with a great spirit in the shape of a gray cat.

"The handkerchief Petra gave me before we left the 'Sanctuary'...?"

I wanted to scream stop until my throat tore. But there was no throat here to tear screaming stop.

I wanted to throw myself between and cut in front of the ghastly "Witch," shouting stop. But there was no body here to throw in front of her.

So I screamed stop, stop in my heart, tried to throw my very soul in the way — and still couldn't stop it.

"I've duly collected the price of the tea party. I look forward to your next visit, from the bottom of my heart."

It had proven its worth as payment, and relief and a faint sense of happiness spread there. More than pride that it was I who had brought that about, far, far, far more — far, far, far more terrifying — was the fact that it had caught the "Witch's" eye. I didn't even know just what I had made myself complicit in.

"So Echidna kept her promise. This time's tea party hasn't vanished from my memory either."

With a white light, the dream world ended, and he returned from the illusion to a dark, cold space. Back in that place, having replayed the encounter with the "Witch" — he noticed something wrong.

"...Emilia, where are you?"

Her absence, when she should have been there, made his heart race, filling him with impatience. An overflowing anxiety and a seeping sense of wrongness — for one forced to repeat this again and again, a deviation from the order of things that should never be allowed.

Driven by a welling bad premonition, he hurried out of that place, the graveyard, at a quickened pace. And the moment he stepped outside the graveyard, into the moonlight, what met his eyes was—

"A shadow... it's a shadow."

The "Sanctuary" sealed in jet black, and the footprints of a "Witch" more terrifying even than the one who must have done this.

Screaming stop, refusing, lamenting, resisting — none of it would end this. Because this had truly happened — it was the ineradicable history of his footsteps.

—In the midst of that, what had I made myself complicit in?

—"Helmet bastard," the voice that called out to him clearly recognized Aldebaran for who he was.

The sense of an ambush Yae had picked up on, and a spirited voice he'd heard before. He didn't need to think hard to realize that the all-out diversion, deliberately splitting off from the main force, had ended in failure. Knowing that, the problem now waiting for Aldebaran was—

"Hey! That voice just now — that's the blonde brat! Reinhard's liege! What's she doing all the way out here...! Weren't we not supposed to be found!?"

Heinkel, looking like he'd swallowed a mouthful of foam, expressed the confusion of the situation quite thoroughly for them. His flustered panic was exactly what Aldebaran felt inside, but thanks to Heinkel unbecomingly making a fuss first, Aldebaran was spared from losing his own composure. Even so, the situation was anything but bright.

"...I updated the matrix fifteen seconds ago."

Periodically updating the matrix in preparation for raids and surprise attacks had been meant as a cautious measure to avoid risk, but it had backfired. From this moment, Aldebaran had only fifteen seconds' worth of chances to try again. That already meant he couldn't avoid being ambushed. —An ambush was, for Aldebaran, a far worse tactic to face than a surprise attack. Whether by sharp insight or by sheer guesswork, they had struck at precisely the worst possible timing.

"If she's the Sword Saint's liege, that'd make her the famous Felt-sama, huh~. ...Guess this is already the moment for Heinkel-sama's turn as human sacrifice, don'tcha think~?"

"—! If you're thinking of using me as a hostage to break through, it's pointless! They want Reinhard to inherit House Astrea. Right now my standing, being here with you people, is bad enough... if they've got a righteous cause, they'll come at me to kill without mercy!"

"Uh~m... then wouldn't that make you all the more fitting for the human sacrifice's role?"

"I'm telling you this ain't the time for jokes!"

It probably hadn't been a joke at all. Giving the shouting Heinkel a look of exasperation, Yae shrugged at Aldebaran with a "So, what'll we do~?" Unlike the two flustered middle-aged men, Yae was taking the situation in front of her coolly. Perhaps a fruit of her training as a shinobi, her disciplined fortitude was admirable. A state of mind Aldebaran doubted he could reach even after dying a few million times—

"Anyway, handing over the old man would be pointless."

If it was Felt and her people waiting outside the woods, then, sadly enough, Heinkel's own grim assessment was probably right — as a hostage he'd be worth next to nothing. In fact, the likely ending was that they'd seize the golden opportunity to trample his life away, with "Here lies the beloved Heinkel Astrea" carved onto his gravestone. That only pessimistic pictures came to mind was simply because there wasn't enough material to paint an optimistic one.

"Yae, old man, wait here. I'll go take a look first."

"Wha—are you out of your mind!? This woman's a shinobi, isn't she? If anyone should go first, shouldn't it be her!? Tell him so yourself!"

"...Al-sama, is that truly for the best?"

"It is."

He answered shortly and gave a nod, and Yae backed off readily with an "Understood~." At that, the red-faced Heinkel still tried to press further, but—

"You're being tiresome, Heinkel-sama. You don't need to worry so much — who else besides Al-sama could even think of using the Divine Dragon Volcanica, let alone actually pull it off? There's no way he's without a plan."

"Th-that's... true, I suppose..."

At Yae's covering fire, Heinkel reluctantly swallowed his words. As expected, the Divine Dragon's prestige was effective in all sorts of ways, senile as its bearer might be. Even in this thoroughly outmaneuvered situation, it made people believe unconditionally that Aldebaran wouldn't go down without a fight. Unfortunately, contrary to what Yae had claimed, he was about as plan-less as could be.

"Well then — will it be a demon or a serpent that comes out?"

Either way, it was a grim prospect — hiding that uneasy thought inside his helmet, Aldebaran left the two of them behind and walked straight out of the woods. What he wanted as material for judgment was, at minimum, confirmation of who the enemy was and what forces they commanded—

"—Heh? Coming out here so boldly — guess you've made your peace with things pretty cleanly, huh."

The view, blocked until now by tall trees, suddenly opened up, and a plain spread out before Aldebaran's eyes. Across that plain, standing with commanding presence and a grin baring a white fang, was a girl with golden hair and red eyes — the very person he'd guessed at from the voice and its content: Felt.

"—"

This was after he'd already had his encounter with Reinhard. That fact itself wasn't surprising. On top of that, from the moment he'd guessed the opponent was Felt, Aldebaran had already begun estimating her forces. —At this point, the two headliners, the "Sword Saint" and the "Gray One," were already accounted for and out of the picture. As for the rest, going by what he knew from the Water Gate City of Priestella, the camp's core assets were the trio of former delinquents, Flam's yet-unseen sister, and the aged Giant who'd stormed the royal castle. Felt's own strength was as yet unconfirmed, but surely she couldn't come anywhere close to Priscilla.

Even Priscilla herself, fighting one-on-one, couldn't match Aldebaran. Even in the unlikely case that Felt was a hidden powerhouse, he could handle it any number of ways. —That had been Aldebaran's thinking, right up until he stepped out of the woods. But—

"...Hey now, what kind of joke is this?"

At the unexpected sight arrayed before his eyes, Aldebaran couldn't help but let out a dry laugh. After all, his original estimate had been five or six people at most. And here, roughly a hundred times that — a party of over five hundred — had turned out to welcome him.

—Felt, raised in the slums, hadn't shaken her poor-man's habits even now that her standing had changed.

These days she no longer wanted for food, clothes, or a bed to sleep in, but none of it truly belonged to Felt herself — it all came attached to Reinhard's House Astrea. Reinhard was certainly Felt's knight, but Felt had no intention of deluding herself so far as to think his belongings had therefore become her own.

So even with delicious food to eat, far better clothes to wear, and the ability to sleep soundly and safely through the night, she hadn't the slightest intention of letting her own original "cards" go to waste.

Felt's "cards," put simply, were the many abilities she had cultivated in order to "live strong." The eye for spotting easy marks and the fleet feet honed in her slum days, the nerve to never flinch at any situation, and the instinct for when things had gone dangerously wrong — all of these, after the incident with the "Bowel Hunter" that had led to her meeting Reinhard, she'd realized needed sharpening further to still be of use, and she'd kept honing them even amid the advanced education she underwent for the Royal Selection.

In fact, every one of her "cards" had become far more useful than before. —Though even so, she'd yet to manage shaking Reinhard off even once.

In any case, by continuing to hone the "cards" she'd always had, Felt prided herself on having broadened her view enough to see things her former self never could have. That pride was what kept Felt from overlooking a faint snag of wrongness.

—Just as it was when the report of a crisis reached the royal capital, sent through the "Divine Protection of Telepathy" from Flam, left behind at the Pleiades Watchtower, to her twin sister Grassis.

"—Felt-sama, I must hurry to the Augria Sand Dunes. Depending on how things go, it may come to a battle with the Divine Dragon Volcanica. Outside the sand sea, the damage would be far too great."

"Wait a sec. ...What bugs me is that they went out of their way to start it in that sand-covered place. That's the spot where you're supposedly weaker than anywhere else in the world, right?"

"...Yes, that's true. The miasma there doesn't agree with me at all. But not going isn't an option. Flam and Ezzo-dono are at that tower. And besides..."

Seeing Reinhard, driven by the Sword Saint's sense of duty after receiving Grassis's report, Felt scratched her head and answered, "Yeah, yeah, I get it." That he made that face at a time like this was one of the things about Reinhard that rubbed Felt the wrong way, but there was no use picking at it now. More importantly, she needed to get Reinhard to swallow her next proposal. That proposal was—

"Reinhard, I ain't telling you not to go. But cut it out with charging off on your own. —Take us along partway."

"Felt-sama, that's—!"

"You gonna go pulling something reckless and not let me do the same? Quit being so full of yourself. Everyone else besides you's got the right to do everything they possibly can too, y'know."

"—"

Reinhard fell silent, at a loss for how to argue back, and Felt could well understand his feelings. After all, the enemy had completely shut down Ezzo and Flam, and on top of that was said to have the Divine Dragon Volcanica in tow. This was, roughly speaking, a crisis that ranked among the worst the kingdom had ever faced. Truly, a great task fit for the Sword Saint to resolve.

"Don't get it."

"Don't get what?"

"Even not knowing about Flam's Divine Protection, if they went and dragged the Divine Dragon out, it'd get found out right quick. And once it did, it's plain as day you'd come flying in. And that helmet bastard went and did it anyway."

"...In other words, Felt-sama, you think Al-dono is acting having already accounted for that?"

"Which sounds more likely — that he's on guard 'cause he figures you'll come, or that he's written you off figuring you won't?"

At Felt's question, one eye closed, Reinhard too began seriously weighing the possibilities. There had to be some countermeasure worked out against Reinhard. In fact, there was a fairly high chance that bringing the Divine Dragon onto his side was itself part of that plan. The unfavorable conditions of the Augria Sand Dunes, and the Divine Dragon's presence — could these be enough to reach even Reinhard?

"Even so. Even so, I must go. Even if the enemy has prepared some formidable countermeasure, if I'm the only one who can overcome it, then..."

"—Reinhard, don't dodge."

She gave that order to Reinhard, kneeling to speak with her at eye level, and the red-haired knight blinked, his eyes going wide with an "Eh?" Since his face hadn't moved as instructed, Felt reached out and took both his cheeks in her hands. Reinhard's eyes stayed frozen wide, his handsome face — the one that had the town squealing over him — thoroughly ruined.

"Idiot, I told you I'm not stopping you. You go and take the bait they're offering. Cut loose with that ridiculous strength of yours and raise hell. —We'll pick up whatever's left over on our end."

"—Ah."

Bringing her face close, close enough their noses nearly touched, she flung the words at him, and just as she watched his blue eyes flood with astonishment, the door to the inn room was thrown open roughly. Standing there, out of breath, were familiar faces—

"Perfect timing. How'd it go?"

"Pant, pant... y-you're always asking for something unreasonable..."

"A sturdy Dragon Carriage that's fine even if it breaks — couldn't we just use one of our own, Boss!?"

"Idiot, the whole point was we can't bring that Earth Dragon — Romy — along, wasn't it? What in the world happened? Grassis wouldn't tell us any of the details either."

"Dragon Carriage, procured. Done."

The trio squeezed shoulder to shoulder through the doorway, with sleepy-eyed Grassis following behind. Looking over the crowd that had come barreling in, Reinhard's eyes widened in surprise once again. At his face, which looked a touch younger than his age warranted, Felt gave a snort. "Heh."

"You oughta get a little more life carved into that face of yours."

"Felt-sama, this is..."

"Don't go getting stuck-up thinking you can do everything by yourself, Reinhard. All on your own, you couldn't even manage holding hands with these three at once, could you."

Saying so, she gave a shrug, and Reinhard blinked his wide eyes rapidly. Then he drew a deep breath, and quietly, slowly let the emotion in his eyes settle—

"—Yes. I am a 'Sword Saint' who can't even hold hands with these three at once."

At last, he nodded, showing not merely the face of the Sword Saint, but Reinhard's own face.

What followed was a race against time, and Felt worked Reinhard mercilessly. She had him shoulder the Dragon Carriage that Grassis and the others had tracked down, and had him fly it in one dash from the royal capital to the Augria Sand Dunes, dropping them off partway at the Astrea domain.

"Off I go, Felt-sama!"

"Go knock 'em flat, good and loud!"

Honestly, even with Reinhard's "Divine Protection of Windshielding" at work, it was an emergency transit she never wanted to experience again — but making him take the detour had unquestionably been worth it. Because the helmet bastard's countermeasure against Reinhard turned out to be far more vicious than they'd imagined.

—They learned that the "Witch of Envy" had appeared in the dunes, and that Reinhard had stayed behind to hold her off, less than half an hour after sending him on his way.

"The 'Witch of Envy,' of all things... that's a trick even I, back in my prime, wouldn't have dared cook up. This helmet fella, whoever he is — I don't know the man, but is he really that far gone?"

Old Man Rom, stroking his own bald head with a large palm, let out a heavy sigh at the report relayed from afar. Old Man Rom, who'd joined them at the domain manor, was — together with the absent Ezzo — one of the camp's two great minds. But at his assessment, Felt shook her head. "Nah."

"From what I saw, the helmet bastard wasn't that far gone. Sure, his looks and way of talkin' were weird, but that ain't so different from Crusch-neechan's cat-ears or Emilia-neechan's black-haired bro."

Toward Al, who had caused all this and turned enemy, Felt held no particularly good or bad impression. —Only, she figured that, just like the other knights, beneath his flippant manner he carried a solid loyalty toward the master he served. So—

"—Did that princess of his die, and now he's thrown caution to the wind out of despair?"

—The death of Priscilla Barielle, caught up in the internal strife of the Vollachian Empire.

The unbelievable report delivered to Royal Lugunica Castle had naturally reached Felt's ears as well, present as she'd been in the capital. Honestly, she hadn't been able to grasp it the moment she heard it, and even now it didn't feel real to her. That was how overflowing with a vitality utterly removed from death that woman Priscilla had seemed.

"...There's no such thing as a person who doesn't die. Us on the outside can force ourselves to swallow that much. But that helmet bastard couldn't."

"But even so, what's he scheming? By Flam's report, he's supposedly got the Divine Dragon under his command — is this some grand, world-dragging suicide brought on by despair?"

"I don't think that line's entirely out of the question, but..."

Falling into self-destructive despair and dragging the world along to mourn Priscilla's death — Felt didn't think it was that kind of blowup, in that direction. If Al's goal really were such a straightforward death wish, then the greatest obstacle to it would, without question, be Reinhard.

"And yet, the helmet bastard didn't team up with the Witch — the Witch of Envy — to just kill Reinhard outright. So for now, I'm guessing the world's destruction ain't the goal."

"Oh, what's this, Rachins? Playing the wise counselor 'cause the teacher ain't around?"

"Don't go making fun of me. Listen, from where I stand, my real feelin' is I wanna turn tail and run right now. A situation where even the 'Witch of Envy' shows up? No way, no way we can handle that."

Flashing his long tongue as if to make a point of it, Rachins snapped that at Felt. Beside him were Gaston and Camberley too, and they, faces tight with worry, chimed in with "Yeah, yeah," agreeing with Rachins—

"Y-yeah, that's right, Boss! Even if Reinhard's holding down the 'Witch of Envy,' the Divine Dragon's still runnin' loose, ain't it!? There's no way we can handle that!"

"Even Felt gets that much, doesn't she. The reason we came back to the domain was to evacuate the people here just in case..."

"Yeah, that's right. Gotta deal with that side of things too."

"See, look. She said it herself... wait, did she say 'that side'?"

At Felt's answer, Gaston, who'd been on the verge of relief for a moment, blinked. At the big man's reaction, Felt laughed and nodded back, "Oh, I said it, I said it."

"Gotta think about the worst case, sure, but we can't go making a huge production out of it and stirring up a panic. I mean, even you lot are scared stiff just hearing the word 'Witch.'"

"'Course we are! I mean, you too, right, Boss!? It's a 'Witch,' a 'Witch'!"

"Your own girl gets called somethin' pretty similar too, doesn't she."

"Toto gets called the 'Vixen of Venom,' and in front of me she's cute, so it's fine!"

"Quit foolin' around! You're gettin' off track!"

Cutting in between Felt and Camberley as they spat back and forth at each other, Rachins sharpened his four-whites stare and slammed a hand down on the map spread across the table. "Listen up, all right?"

"Ain't gonna get anywhere without deciding on a plan. Felt, what're we doing."

"Heh, if it's a majority vote, no need to even say it — runnin' would win easy. But..."

"—All of us here intend to follow your way of doing things, girl."

At Old Man Rom's low-voiced declaration, Felt caught her breath slightly. Looking around, she saw Rachins with his exasperated face, Gaston with his sour scowl, and even Camberley, legs trembling like a newborn fawn, all holding their tongues and waiting for Felt's word. Honestly, when had they become such a dependable bunch of faces?

"Since way back. I always hated it something fierce, when somethin' bad happens, just holdin' my head and waitin' around for someone else to fix it for me."

"Ah well, that's a hot-blooded way of thinking. Whoever could she take after, I wonder."

"Beats me. Why don't the one who raised her put a hand to his chest and think about it."

She said that to the foster father slowly shaking his head, and stuck out her tongue just a little. Then, turning around with vigor, she swept her gaze over the faces waiting on her word. And she declared:

"All right, you lot, get your asses up. —This is the guy who made a fool outta our Reinhard. No need to hold back — let's go smash him good."

—Once they'd decided to fight, Felt and the others moved fast.

Far away, at the eastern edge of the world, the battle between Reinhard and the "Witch of Envy" continued. And Al, who had likely provoked that fight, had left the battlefield and taken the Divine Dragon out of the sand sea — that much of the enemy's movements they'd tracked accurately. The problem was—

"There's a witness account of the Divine Dragon heading north. Taken at face value, the helmet fella ought to be moving with it. ...Which would mean the destination is north."

"If you're sayin' 'at face value,' that means there's a twisted way of lookin' at it too?"

"Hmm. From what I've heard, the helmet fella planned this out thoroughly, countermeasures against Reinhard included. So then, why in the first place did he go out of his way to be conspicuous at the post town by the exit of the dunes?"

"That'd be to plant the impression that he's got a 'Dragon' on his side, wouldn't it?"

"Yes, he wants to plant that impression. —Which is why, I suspect, the Divine Dragon heading north is flying at a height low enough for the naked human eye to spot it."

True to having flagged it as "twisted," Old Man Rom's guess indeed came from an unexpected angle. Most things had a straightforward way of looking at them and a warped one, and most of the time, going with the plain, straightforward view was the way to land on the right answer. But here too, a small snag held Felt's feelings back.

"If he purposely showed the 'Dragon' headin' north, what for?"

"To hide his real objective, of course. The 'Dragon' shown flying off is a decoy card... I'd guess the real target lies somewhere other than north or east — west or south."

"...The princess died in the Empire. If the Divine Dragon lands one big hit on the Empire, it'd kick off a real clash with that country full of tangled grudges, huh? ...Nah, that's not it."

If that guess were correct, it would certainly generate major friction between the Kingdom and the Empire. But that ran sharply counter to the contents of the state letter concerning the future diplomatic relations between the Vollachian Empire and the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, which had been brought back at the same time as the news of Priscilla's death. Of course, there was also the possibility that Al had no intention of dragging the Kingdom in, and simply intended a straightforward act of vengeance against the Empire.

"If that's the case, it's not so different from a suicide bombing that drags in the whole world. Which would mean..."

"—Mean what?"

"—My gut says west. Whether it's due west, northwest, or southwest, I dunno."

"If the 'Dragon' is a diversion, the northwest line can probably be ruled out too. That narrows it to two points. Now, the rest is—"

"Right, the rest is—"

—Betting everything on their read of the situation, Felt lined up the camp's entire strength across the plain. Frankly, the military strength the Astrea domain held wasn't much. This too was a side effect of Reinhard being simply too strong, but she'd assigned that small handful of soldiers to guiding evacuations, to clear the domain's territory that might become the enemy's path. That was fine. Their strength was for protecting. What was needed now was not that, but—

"—You really must be out of your mind, girl."

"No, no, or is that so, hm? For my part I find it a decisiveness much to my liking, yo. Should the need arise, she wouldn't hesitate to drag the likes of us violent men into it, no. As a backer, that makes me quite proud, yes."

"Wherever might your nose be, I wonder? Those tattoos declaring themselves all over your face are so overpowering that, to my eyes, I can't make out your face as a whole at all."

Speaking up one after another like that, each in their own way, were the representatives of the assembled forces. One was a giant of a Pig Human, built with a frame to rival Old Man Rom's own and clad in armor of thick muscle. One was a bizarre-looking eccentric with a blue tattoo of a balance scale covering his entire face. One was a beautiful woman of ripe age, adorning herself alluringly in provocative attire quite unsuited to the occasion.

—The "Black Silver Coin," "The Balance," and the "Flower Prison Garden." Standing side by side, yet sharing not a shred of friendliness toward one another — but there was one thing all of them had in common. —That they had answered Felt's call and gathered here.

They were the big shots who ruled over the underworld of Flanders, the "City of Earth Dragons," northeast of the Astrea domain, and Felt had known them since an earlier incident and had kept in contact since. Every one of them was a scoundrel with skeletons in his closet, but at the same time they were also Felt's backers — a necessary evil, five hundred of them all told, who'd trusted Felt's instincts enough to go along with this formation.

"Ha! What a sight, what a sight! Can't believe you all actually gathered this many."

Shading her eyes with a hand, Felt raised her voice to the five hundred scoundrels lined up in ranks before her. In truth, when she'd first thought of borrowing strength from the people of Flanders, she'd figured she'd be lucky to gather even a tenth of this. And here, unexpectedly, was ten times that.

"And here I am tellin' everyone the enemy's the guy who outmaneuvered Reinhard... geez, this ain't the time for it, but I wish I could show you this, Reinhard."

To Reinhard, who wasn't here, she wanted, if it were possible, to drive the point home. That even in a situation beyond even Reinhard's reach, this many people would come stampeding in, not to turn tail and run, but to clean up the mess.

"Well, guess there's probably some who just couldn't back out, like Camberley here."

Muttering that, cracking her neck with a series of pops, Felt turned back, her comrades at her back. Ahead of her gaze, facing the group lined up alongside her, stood a great stretch of woods — toward it, Felt drew in as deep a breath as her small body could hold, and—

"—Oi! You can hear me, can't you!? I know you're in there, helmet bastard!"

With that, she loosed the declaration of war that would trigger the opening of battle.

—Utterly unaware that such an unexpected series of moves had taken place, Aldebaran could do nothing but stand rooted before the unforeseen sight spread out ahead of him.

"Hey, hey..."

It was a situation that threw off Aldebaran's calculations badly enough to draw an involuntary, dry laugh. A hundred times what he'd anticipated — now that was something. Truly, something to be reckoned with. The group lined up across the plain lacked any uniformity in appearance, and yet they were a mismatched army of steel who shared, solidly, one single target to stand against.

"—"

Still wearing that dry laugh, Aldebaran was at a total loss. Just like this, being so easily thrown into disarray by events outside his expectations — that was the difference between himself and Yae, whose composure he'd only just been admiring. Even the reason Yae held him in some regard was nothing but a fiction wrung out through the cheat of his Authority. Either way—

"Quite something, aren't you, Felt-jouchan."

And Felt, standing at the very center of this astonishment, was the one truly responsible for upending Aldebaran's expectations. Her bearing, which left no doubt of it, had grown sharper still in the mere three months since he'd last seen her. No wonder — she was a prodigy who, in just a single year, had made Priscilla acknowledge her as a rival. Her weight in the world was, at bottom, on an entirely different level from Aldebaran's own. —And yet, his resolve wasn't so cheap that he could use that as an excuse to back down.

"Thanks for the warm welcome. Though, you sure it's all right to bother with me? Even while we're doing this, your camp's precious Sword Saint is—"

"—Sorry 'bout this, but."

"—"

For the moment, Aldebaran tried drawing things into a war of words to pry out even a little information. But Felt cut ruthlessly through his words, scratching her head and closing one eye. And then—

"Me and Old Man Rom, we're of one mind on this — first thing's to shut your damn mouth."

"—!"

In that instant, a welling chill of dread stiffened Aldebaran's entire body. But it was too late. Faster than he could react to that dread at all, a small figure that had been lying in wait at the edge of the woods sprang out, a slender foot-blade striking hard against the nape of Aldebaran's neck.

"Guh, kh...!"

A fierce impact rattled his brain, and the strength drained from Aldebaran's knees. At the edge of his vision, the one who'd landed that blow touched down lightly on the grass, her pink hair tied into two bunches swaying, and a pair of sleepy eyes looked at Aldebaran. A face he recognized — or rather, a girl with a face very much like it. Her true identity—

"Avenging Flam."

From those short, flat words, he understood she was Flam's sister. Crumbling from the girl's single blow, consciousness slipping from his grasp, Aldebaran murmured—

"Not... dead..."

His consciousness slowly fading into the depths of darkness — reflexively, he bit down on poison. The potent poison hidden behind his back molar was a technique Aldebaran had performed more times than any other motion in his life — one soaked into not just his body, but his very soul. This, if nothing else, was a skill unique to Aldebaran alone — one he would let no one interfere with, not even the Sword Saint Reinhard van Astrea.

For there existed no one in this world better at killing Aldebaran than Aldebaran himself—

"—Oi! You can hear me, can't you!? I know you're in there, helmet bastard!"

The sensation of his brain being burned away by potent poison cut off, and what prompted his consciousness to wake immediately after was a spirited voice, brimming with a hostility that was, oddly, satisfying to hear. To the newly redefined matrix point — the moment of Felt's declaration of war — Aldebaran returned, and in his mind counted off the first tally.

"Hey! That voice just now — that's the blonde brat! Reinhard's liege! What's she doing all the way out here...! Weren't we not supposed to be found!?"

Watching out of the corner of his eye as Heinkel flustered and Yae soothed him in a way that only seemed to stoke it further, Aldebaran updated the battle situation in his mind using the information he'd just gathered. The previously estimated strength of the Felt camp needed updating — and a drastic one at that.

After all, his original estimate had been the trio of former thugs, the old Giant, and Flam's sister — five people at most. And here, roughly a hundred times that — a party of over five hundred — had been lying in ambush for him.

"Well now, she's sure got herself one troublesome brain backing her up."

This was, in a form entirely different from the decisive battle against the Sword Saint Reinhard van Astrea, the worst-case scenario for Aldebaran—

"...You're really coming at me, dead seriously, with the one tactic I hate most, Felt-jouchan."

—And so began the unavoidable battle between Aldebaran and an enemy that would come at him with sheer numbers.
