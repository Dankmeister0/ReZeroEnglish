Arc 7 Chapter 86: "Five Peaks"

—The "Giant Eye," Izmail, was a warrior-hero of the Cyclops Tribe, and the hope of his people.

At the center of his valiant face, a single large blue eye shone clear and bright, gazing upon the future without a trace of hesitation. As the name suggests, the Cyclops Tribe possesses only a single eye. Given that most races are equipped with a pair of eyes, having only one grants almost no advantage for survival. Does that make the Cyclops Tribe a pitiable race, inferior in the ability to live?

—On that point, one can say with absolute certainty: no.

Possessing only one eye, the Cyclops Tribe traded away a critical weakness—the fact that losing that single eye meant losing most of their capacity to survive—for a host of extraordinary faculties concentrated within that one orb. Beyond eyesight that could see far further than other races as a baseline, individual variation aside, many among them could perceive the density of Mana or sources of heat, and no small number possessed dynamic vision of an almost abnormal degree. There was another race gifted with unique ocular abilities, the "Evil Eye Tribe," but unlike them—who were often born with frail bodies in exchange for their extraordinary powers—the Cyclops Tribe was physically formidable as well.

In a certain sense, the "eye" reigns supreme over every other faculty in combat. In daily life, it goes without saying that the bulk of one's perception comes from sight; on the battlefield, where survival hinges more directly on such things, this becomes even more pronounced. The Cyclops Tribe, then, could rightly be called a superior race, one whose very soil was suited to producing excellent warriors.

Returning to the matter at hand, let us speak of the "Giant Eye," Izmail. Twenty-one years of age, Izmail was a warrior who wielded a massive battle-axe with his finely honed body. As mentioned, a blue single eye blinked at the center of his face—a sight that made members of the Cyclops Tribe shudder at its elegant beauty, and members of other races shudder at its sheer intimidating presence.

Among the Cyclops Tribe's aesthetic sensibilities, the size and shape of the eye, along with the color and brilliance of the iris, were often the criteria for judgment—and by every measure, Izmail's eye was first-rate. A beautiful, large blue eye. From the moment he was born, expectations rose that he would walk a path apart from the ordinary, and from childhood he was affectionately known by the name "Giant Eye." He grew into the boy, then the young man, then the warrior his tribe had expected of him.

Wielding a battle-axe larger than his own body, cutting a bold swath through the battlefield—had this been an age when the Vollachian Empire raged in constant warfare, the name "Giant Eye" would have thundered not just through his tribe, but across the entire land. But the age did not wait for him to grow; it plunged instead into the lukewarm bath of stagnation and slackness. Many cursed Izmail's misfortune for it. But fate did not abandon him.

The flame of rebellion that began in the eastern lands eventually spread to engulf the whole of Vollachia, and it beckoned Izmail—robbed until then of any chance to fight—onto the battlefield.

"If only I had the chance to fight—"

With this battle-axe, he would achieve feats no one else could hope to match. That was Izmail's pride, and the future his entire tribe believed in without a shred of doubt. The Empire possessed the pinnacle of martial prowess in those called the "Nine Divine Generals"—but should his own chance come around as theirs had, he would leave every last one of them stunned.

And so, when the "Giant Eye" Izmail raised his voice, declaring that the Emperor must be struck down, not a single member of his tribe objected—and they threw themselves eagerly into the search for the black-haired child as well.

The sign of rebellion that had spread across the whole of the Empire was matched by the spread of rumors of a "black-haired crown prince"—not so much whether such a person truly existed, but the fact that this gave one a token of legitimacy to claim. Put simply, procuring a black-haired boy was the entry requirement for participating in the great upheaval now unfolding across the Vollachian Empire, and those who failed to seize the moment forfeited, then and there, any just cause for taking part. To sit back and watch this contest for supremacy unfold from the sidelines would be a betrayal of the Empire's own way of life.

—Let the people of the Empire be strong. This teaching had, to this day, reached every corner of the Empire, down to the people with no connection to the capital. Those who could not grasp that principle had no right to join this great upheaval, nor any right to live in the Empire that followed it. Once Izmail achieved his great ambition, he would tear all of it out by the roots and build a mighty nation. To that end—

"—I shall claim supremacy over this Vollachian Empire!!"

Toes finding purchase on the towering stone wall, Izmail's body rose into the sky. A wall thought to be an absolute barrier—impossible for outsiders to scale, impenetrable to any hostile intent for those within—he crossed it with ease and set his feet down. Seeing Izmail land atop the ramparts, the soldiers stationed there with bows raised fell into confusion, scrambling to draw the swords at their waists. Too slow.

"What miserable soldiers the Empire keeps!"

Gripping the haft of the battle-axe on his back, the muscles of his right arm swelled, and he swung it in a single flash. The massive blade released cut soundlessly through the air, and five soldiers in its path had the upper halves of their bodies blown away simultaneously. Lower halves, missing everything from the waist up, crumpled slowly to the ground as blood gushed forth, and the surrounding soldiers screamed. Hearing in that scream both sharp vigilance and a faint tremor of fear, Izmail—

"How graceless."

A warrior who carried vigilance and tension, he could still respect. But one driven by cowardice earned only disappointment. Izmail blinked his blue eye once, switching the world he perceived. In that instant, the vivid colors of the world drained away, and his vision filled with darkness. —No, not only darkness. Rising up within that dark were lights of red, yellow, and blue. This was the emotion carried by living things, captured as color—a trait particular to Izmail's eye. With the power of this eye, Izmail could glimpse whether an enemy held the will to fight, what techniques they had mastered, the whole history of them as a warrior. Holding that colorless vision, Izmail chose his targets. Namely—

"Cowards, be gone!"

He stepped forward, and the swung battle-axe went for those tinged in blue light—the mediocre ones whose hearts had already fled the battlefield, who prioritized nothing but their own precious skin. He cut down turned backs, severed fleeing legs, crushed faces trying to form words of a plea for mercy, and death and blood spread across the top of the wall. And as he rampaged, watching more and more blue lights fill the world one after another, his despair grew.

"What is this? This is the capital, isn't it—the very seat of His Excellency the Emperor of the Vollachian Empire!"

Turning his battle-axe sideways, aimed at the inside of the wall, Izmail bellowed his fury from atop the ramparts. This was the heart of the Vollachian Empire, the symbol of a mighty nation never to be brought down by anyone—far in the distance, he could make out the Crystal Palace, said to be the most beautiful castle in the world. For Izmail, reaching it would take no more than a few minutes. Storm the Crystal Palace, cut down the soldiers standing in his way, take the head of the Emperor seated on the throne. That had been Izmail's wish. But this—

"This is no cheap honor, won by tormenting weaklings like these!"

His fury sounded almost like a wail of grief, as though betrayed by something he had believed in. And yet, the battle-axe honed by ceaseless training struck true at each enemy's life, and with every step forward another life was extinguished, and the "Giant Eye's" despair only deepened. Following Izmail, fellow warriors of the Cyclops Tribe arrived atop the wall one after another. They too wielded their weapons, chasing down the fleeing soldiers, striking them down, taking their lives. He had wanted to feel proud of that. He had wanted to prove that the Cyclops Tribe stood here.

"But with opponents like you, even that—"

"W-wait, please! Stop, stop it, please—!"

Right before the eyes of a grief-stricken Izmail, a man being chased tumbled to the ground. Fleeing in terror from the warriors, the man collapsed in front of the very last person he should have—and upon seeing Izmail with his craven face, his throat trembled out a shriek. Thrusting both hands forward, scooting backward on his rear, he shook his head at Izmail in desperate refusal.

"Don't kill me! I don't want to die, I don't wanna die!"

"—...Enough. Do not shame yourself further by clinging to life like this."

"Hii, hiiiiii!"

"That's enough."

At the sight of the soldier's pitifully wretched behavior, Izmail's face twisted. Unable to bear listening any further, he raised the battle-axe high overhead. The blade, slick with blood, gleamed dully, and the soldier's eyes filled with despair.

"If you are a warrior at all, at least meet your end with dignity—"

"N-no, I'm not! I'm not a warrior, not a soldier!"

"What?"

The battle-axe stopped a hair's breadth from splitting the man's skull along with his life. It halted precisely at a single layer of skin on his head, and the man let out a "hyuu" of breath, going limp. But it was not some passing whim that spared his life. It was the words spoken just before that stayed the blow.

"What do you mean, you're not a soldier? You're wearing a soldier's equipment right now—"

"I was made to wear it! Put this on, hold a bow, and fight! If I did that..."

"If you did that?"

"There'd be a pardon! They said I'd be released, let out of the cell...!"

"—...."

Izmail studied the desperate man's face. There was no lie in that pale, drained expression. A face attempting deceit, trying to manipulate the one looking at it, always betrays some corresponding tremor. Izmail's eye, which never missed such subtleties, found no such falsehood here.

"Impossible..."

Taking in what he had just witnessed, Izmail's gaze swept across the top of the wall. The Imperial soldiers atop the ramparts, routed and fleeing before the Cyclops Tribe's advance—every one of them carrying a cowardice pitiful enough to make one look away—and yet, no matter how one looked at it, there was simply too little resistance here. On the way to storming the capital, the defense forces they had clashed blades with along the road had put up a far better fight. Izmail had been dismayed, thinking that the regular soldiers charged with defending the capital had fallen to such a state. But what if these were not regular soldiers at all—only condemned criminals, handed equipment? If so, then to what end—

"—...."

At that thought, Izmail suddenly realized something. If his reasoning held true, then the only ones atop this wall right now were the Cyclops warriors centered around himself, and the criminals forced to fight them. Which meant—

"—Burn them all together."

Abruptly, amid a sky echoing with roars of fury and screams, a quiet voice struck Izmail's eardrum. A volume that should not have been audible at all—the very fact that he had heard it pierced his whole body with alarm. It was a rare phenomenon on the battlefield: those with true power carry power even in their voice. Somehow vacant, almost lacking any seriousness at all, that voice reached the "Giant Eye's" ears.

"—...."

Opening his great single eye wide, Izmail looked up at the sky above the capital. Inside the ramparts, protected by that sturdy wall, above a city laid out in orderly, disciplined rows of buildings—there floated a brown-skinned woman, her legs transformed into flame. One eye covered by an eyepatch, her blood-red gaze turned toward him—that woman.

"O-oh, ohhhh, ohhhhhh—!!"

The instant she entered his sight, Izmail let out a battle roar and kicked off the wall, flying into the air. He drew his battle-axe back in a massive arc, charging furiously at the woman floating in the sky. Even without striking a vital point, a mere graze against any part of her body from this blow would send devastating pain through her entire frame. This was no attack like those he'd used against the cowards on the wall—it was everything he had, every last ounce of himself, in one blow. Every cell in his body screamed that unless he unleashed this, unless he landed this strike, he could not win against this opponent.

The blow flew true, drawn inexorably toward the woman's slender body floating in the sky—

"Disappear."

Following her single utterance, a light that filled his entire field of vision with white blanketed Izmail's world.

"Ka—guh, ha...!"

Coughing, wracked with the sensation of his whole body burning and blistering, he forced himself upright. His scorched throat ached; when he reflexively reached for it, his charred skin crumbled away, fingers falling off with it. Witnessing that damage firsthand, he realized keeping his life at all had been a miracle.

—No, not a miracle. He had reflexively used his battle-axe as a shield, protecting himself from the surging flames. Even so, the heat had been so intense it seemed to paint over everything in existence—this was merely the result of that. Still, Izmail understood: he was likely the only one who had barely managed to keep his life.

"—...."

Forcing open trembling eyelids and looking up, he saw the capital's ramparts glowing red-hot, and upon them, the Cyclops warriors who had been locked in battle there, along with the conscripted criminals, had all been burned away together. Most of them had likely been swallowed by the flames without even understanding what was happening. Or perhaps those who did understand, who were able to react, had suffered all the longer for it. That he alone, having suffered, had kept his life—that made their deaths all the more cruel. And what was crueler still—

"—Huh. So there's a survivor after all. Lucky... or, no, unlucky? Unlucky bastard."

Amid the burned-out land, thick with the stench of scorched vegetation and charred flesh, a man appeared, treading across ground littered with cinders. The man, an axe slung over his shoulder, tilted his head, looking down at Izmail in the burnt-out field. Seeing his terrifyingly cold, dry eyes, Izmail understood instinctively. This man was the one who had stationed the criminals on the wall, the one who had carried out that cruel scheme to burn Izmail and the others along with them.

"Y-you, you did this to everyone..."

"—? Don't be stupid, I didn't burn anyone. You think I could pull off something that outrageous? A monster did it. If you want to hold a grudge, hold it against her."

There was no mockery, no jest in the man's voice—only sincerity—and Izmail caught his breath at it. Then, immediately switching his single eye's vision, he strained to see through the man. But what he found left Izmail stunned. —He was blue.

Those who held strong will to fight glowed red; tension, when tinged heavily with anxiety, turned yellow; and fear or dread—anything reluctant toward battle—turned blue. Having neatly trapped Izmail and the others and wiped them out to a man, this man was blue. This man was not even a warrior. He wasn't a coward either. He was something else—something far more repulsive.

"I cannot let you live—!"

Responding to the world his eye showed him and to his own instinct, Izmail sprang up. The flames he'd taken had scorched his innards, and his left arm was gone entirely from the shoulder. Wounds covered every inch of his body without exception, and there was no comparing his movement now to his full strength—it was slow. But even the melted, misshapen battle-axe lying beside him would be more than enough to beat the man to death. Putting strength into it, hearing his charred arm creak and flesh crush with the sound, he drew the battle-axe back straight at the man—

"I agree, I can't let you live either. And—I've been thinking this for a while now."

"—?!"

"A guy with only one eye was never gonna be strong."

Right in front of the charging Izmail, the man threw a small pouch he had hanging from his waist. It burst open in midair, scattering black powder from within. It was some kind of spice, the sort used to blind an opponent, and there was no way to dodge it. His right arm, gripping the battle-axe, couldn't swat it away, and his fallen left arm was of no use at all—as a result, the black powder covered his field of vision.

Letting out a groan of "guh," he cut short his battle-axe's attack and leapt backward. The Cyclops Tribe's superior eyesight had been turned into a weakness against them. But he refused to be underestimated. Targeting the Cyclops Tribe's eye was standard practice, and there was a countermeasure for it. The Cyclops Tribe's large eye could, in an instant, secrete a massive volume of tears to flush out any contaminant—

"—Ohh."

Izmail, stimulating his tear ducts to bring on that flood of tears, never did open his eye again. In the next instant, a Magic Stone Cannon shell struck directly into Izmail's body, his eye still closed, and blew away that "Giant Eye" along with the rest of him.

Watching the Cyclops warrior take the cannon shell head-on and blast apart into pieces right before his eyes, Todd let out a breath. The man had walked neatly into the trap, saving Todd considerable trouble—but had it come to a straight fight, there was no telling whether he would have had the advantage against a master of that caliber.

"Better safe than sorry."

Looking at the blasted Cyclops corpse and the cannon shell's crater around it, Todd raised his clenched fist to the sky, signaling to the gunner atop the wall that no follow-up strike was needed. Let the enemy's vanguard climb the wall, pin them down there with someone who could afford to die doing it, then wipe out both sides together with overwhelming firepower. It was a simple plan. But the effect had been immediate.

"Most of the time, whoever takes point is the guy who's confident in himself."

In a battle involving this many people, whoever volunteers to lead the charge is either someone hungry for glory getting ahead of himself, or someone who thoroughly understands the importance of striking first in war. Whichever it was, letting even one of their thrusts get through would only cause trouble later. There were two reasons to stop the vanguard cold.

"Well, I crushed their opening move—now what'll they do?"

Even with the loudest, boldest of the Cyclops Tribe cut down, there was no shortage of others willing, however presumptuously, to commit regicide against His Excellency the Emperor. It was practically an exposition of every unruly tribe gathered from across the Empire. Beyond the Cyclops Tribe, there were plenty more waiting in the wings—differing numbers of limbs and eyes, wildly varying body sizes, different skin and blood colors, different languages—enough to make just thinking about how to deal with them exhausting. Still—

"No matter how many strange tribes they line up, I doubt it'll make much difference. After all..."

Muttering that, Todd looked up as Arakiya flew overhead, her whole body wrapped in swirling wind. She had meant to strike in coordination with the Cyclops Tribe's charge, but having missed that chance, she seemed intent now on attacking whoever had let the opportunity slip by. On top of that, at the star-shaped fortress wall's other four points ringing the city—each manned by a different guardian than Todd's group—the assigned defenders had already begun their own attacks.

—Purple thorns raged wild, cutting down a great host of Centaur People, half-man half-horse, surging forward in a charge. —An impossibly massive Flying Winged Blade sliced through the sky, grinding apart the Weaponkin, who turned parts of their own bodies into weapons. —Fists of stone golems rising from the earth struck down the pitiable Winged People, born with wings that could not fly. —A band of madmen who had perfected the arts of slaughter annihilated the Glow People, who bore luminous stones embedded in their foreheads.

"When it comes to being a monster, we can hold our own too."

—Becoming one with the great forces of nature, crimson flames burned the Cyclops Tribe to ash as naturally as drawing breath.

Monsters, none inferior to the others, stood at each point of the star-shaped wall guarding the capital of Lupugana. Five monsters at five peaks—was there any way for rebels driven by nothing but momentum and ambition for glory to get past that?

"At the very least, even if someone told me to do it, I'd refuse flat out. Still..."

Measures to keep the enemy from approaching the wall, measures for how to kill those who did get close, measures for those who somehow survived being killed, and measures for retreat—all of it was already in place. Best if things never got as far as that last measure, but the tide of battle was fickle. To begin with, the very fact that the rebels had made it this far, that this defense of the capital was even happening now, already exceeded anything Todd had imagined before the civil war turned serious.

"And because of it, even though I made it back to the capital, I still haven't seen Katya. ...Just how long is someone gonna keep getting in the way of me and Katya's little rendezvous?"

Grumbling a complaint he couldn't quite direct at anyone in particular—if he had to name a target, the world itself, maybe—Todd looked up at a sky thick with the scent of death, and kicked off the scorched earth. More and more, more and more, more and more and more people would die.

Just how many more had to die before someone decisive finally laid down their weapon?

"Enough already, you battle-crazed lunatics."

"—I regret to inform you, but you lack the strength for this! You shall never pass beyond this point!!"

Standing firm atop the wall like a guardian statue, arms spread wide, his entire body writhing. An instant later, the body of Second General Kafma Irulux pulsed from within, and from his arms came a tremendous burst of purple thorns, hurtling forward to strike the herd of Centaur People charging across the farmland—beings with the upper body of a man and the lower body of a horse—cutting their legs out from under them, piercing their torsos, and dragging them down into the earth. Slipping past that barrage of thorns, a Valiant closing in on the wall drew back a spear and hurled it.

The strength and momentum of a horse's legs, channeled through the upper body, gave the thrown spear a force rivaling a Magic Stone Cannon. Unlike the Magic Stone Cannon, which cost a fortune to field in any quantity, the Centaur People's javelins needed nothing more than the spear itself and enough distance to build a running start to unleash more than sufficient destructive force. This wall was the barrier protecting the capital. Inscribed with the Divine Protection of earth, it would not crumble from any ordinary attack—but if hundreds of spears struck it with this kind of force, the damage could well pass through to the inside. However—

"That you chose the peak I guard—that is your misfortune."

Declaring this, Kafma spread the arms that had launched the thorns out to either side. In time with the motion, the thorns swept sideways across the battlefield, finishing off the fallen Centaur warriors. But the thrown spears, and the Valiants who had hurled them, escaped the thorns' follow-up. So Kafma called forth not more thorns, but another "Insect" from within himself.

"—Ugh."

With a sound of grinding bone amid pain, the armored chest plate Kafma wore split open from the inside. White ribs spread outward from within his body, trembling as they aimed their sharp tips downward at the field below. The line of charging Centaur Valiants found themselves lined up in the sights of Kafma's opened chest, aimed between those ribs, and braced themselves, unsure of what was about to happen. But bracing themselves would do nothing.

—An instant later, Kafma's entire body jolted violently, the recoil sliding his tall frame backward. His heels dug grooves into the wall as he endured the pain tearing through him, and looked forward.

Below him, the line of fire targeted by Kafma's ribs had been gouged out entirely, swallowing and obliterating every Centaur caught along its path. Like the thorns, this was another new "Insect," suited for dealing with many enemies at once—one that had, worryingly, come dangerously close to reverting him to a chrysalis at just the moment he needed it most.

"...But I made it in time. As long as I stand, this capital's defense here will not be breached."

Letting out a breath, hand pressed to the chest that had repelled the Centaur charge, Kafma closed his ribs back into his opened chest. This new "Insect" carried great power, but the recoil was proportionally severe. It drained a great deal of mind and body, and rapid-fire use was a double-edged sword best avoided. Still, it was a challenge worth reaching for, even at the cost of exposing himself to such a threat.

"You rebels, so easily trampling the peace His Excellency's own skill has brought about."

As one of the "Generals," he had watched the reign of Emperor Vincent Vollachia from close at hand. As one man of the rare and much-despised Insect Cage Tribe, he had lived within the world Emperor Vincent Vollachia had built. As one individual named Kafma Irulux, he had witnessed firsthand the magnitude of the great achievements Emperor Vincent Vollachia had accomplished.

Why should Vincent Vollachia have to face a blade turned against him? Was there anyone else capable of a feat of supremacy to match his—a man who sought to change, and in fact continued to change, the very nature of this Empire? And so—

"I cannot allow the likes of you to reach His Excellency the Emperor."

"—Now that's quite the resolve, ain't it? Can't say I dislike it."

Dodging both the thorns and the white cannon fire, a figure kicked off the farmland and leapt up onto the wall. Looking down at the short-statured opponent, Kafma narrowed his sharp eyes. A new challenger, one with the skill to reach a place the Centaur Valiants could not. Put simply—a formidable opponent.

By nature, Kafma was not one for holding back. Mercy, or treating a fight as mere play, was not something he was skilled at either. But this opponent, Kafma judged, likely had no room for such leisure either.

"Second General of the Vollachian Empire, Kafma Irulux."

At Kafma's declaration of his name, six transparent wings burst forth from his back, tearing through the cloak he wore from the inside. Seeing that battle-ready stance—no, more than the stance, it was the declaration of his name that mattered. It is only natural for a warrior facing a warrior to announce his name. Of course, it is also true that no small number of fighters disregard that custom on the battlefield, but—

"—Garfiel."

"—...."

"Name's Garfiel Tinzel, the 'Gorgeous Tiger.' —Truth is, I was told not to give my name, but there's times you just gotta."

So said the man with the ferocious grin—Garfiel—bringing together the beautiful gauntlets fitted on both arms in front of his chest, producing a sharp, pleasing clang. Kafma, entrusted with guarding this stretch of the capital's wall, had been prepared to hold out for however many days it took.

—On the very first day of the war, a decisive clash had arrived, and the strongest of the Empire's Second Generals did not fail to recognize it as such.
