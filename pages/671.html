Arc 8 Chapter 54: "Soumatou"

—Before he knew it, Garfiel Tinzel was sitting down in a dark place.

"Ah?"

His sharp eyes going round, Garfiel looked around him, this way and that.
It was a place he had no memory of. A dim, dusky space—Garfiel sat heavily in a chair, elbow on his knee, chin propped on his hand.
Just what in the world had brought him to a place like this?

"The hell is this place...?"

Wrinkling his nose in suspicion, Garfiel gave a snort.
Garfiel's keen sense of smell, while not on par with a Dog Human's, was normally useful for grasping a situation. Picking up on his surroundings that way was practically instinct for him by now—but his nose wasn't working right.
It wasn't that he couldn't smell anything at all—it was more that nothing he smelled would hold still long enough to grasp.
And speaking of things that wouldn't hold still—it wasn't just the smells. The state of his surroundings was the same way.

As already mentioned, it was a dim space. The ceiling was high, and the chair Garfiel sat in wasn't the only one—rows of them lined up to his left and right. What's more, those rows of chairs seemed to continue in ranks both in front of him and behind, and he himself seemed to be sitting somewhere near the middle of the whole space.
Having gotten that far in his thinking, Garfiel recalled something that resembled the scene before him.

"This here's like one'a them theaters where they put on plays 'n junk."

Once he'd said the word "theater" out loud and the image solidified, it became impossible to see it as anything else.
That was it. Garfiel was in a theater, seated somewhere near the middle of the audience. The dimness, the high ceiling—all of it was exactly what you'd need for watching a show.
The moment Garfiel's understanding settled on the situation, as though it had been waiting for just that, a high, resonant bell rang out through the theater. —The signal that the curtain was about to rise.

"——"

Garfiel froze, eyes wide, and leaving his state of mind entirely behind, a change came over the front of the audience—the curtain rose, and the hidden stage was slowly revealed.
And there, upon that stage, drawing the gaze of every spectator, was—

"...A cat?"

Garfiel's muttered words, face scrunched up, summed up the scene on the stage plainly enough.
Indeed, waiting for the curtain to rise on that stage was a small cat. A small cat with gray fur, standing on two legs with its arms spread wide.
It was smaller than even the Kitten Humans Garfiel knew, like Mimi—genuinely palm-sized.

One such tiny cat, then a second, then a third, took their places on the stage as the curtain went up.
Every cat looked exactly the same. As Garfiel sat there, simply swept along by the situation, the three kittens moved—each of them donning a different prop.

One put on a golden wig, another a red wig, and the last a fluffy white tuft on its head.
And as Garfiel blinked in astonishment, the three cats moved to their respective positions on the stage and began an eye-catching sequence of motions.

The golden-haired cat and the fluffy-headed cat collided with each other, while the red-haired cat stood by and watched.
A literal scuffle broke out between them.

"The hell is this...?"

Dumbfounded, Garfiel watched the cats tangle with each other on stage.
The stage props were surprisingly elaborate—a backdrop recreating a stone-built cityscape, with painted houses and walls that broke, crumbled, and were sent flying in time with the cats' battle.
It was flashy and remarkably well-crafted, and he found himself almost charmed by the sheer effort put into what was, after all, only playacting.

"—? Hey, ain't I seen that cat somewhere before..."

Something about the little cat performers snagged on Garfiel's memory. He scratched at the half-closed lid of that memory, and gradually it creaked open, understanding leaping out.
Those little cats looked exactly like the drawings of Emilia's Great Spirit—the ones Emilia and Beatrice were always badgering Subaru into drawing for them.

"Nah, so what if it is!?"

Even having realized that much, Garfiel had no idea what it was supposed to mean, and it irritated him. He'd raised his voice without meaning to, and the little cats on stage froze in surprise.
The three kittens glanced his way, and Garfiel found himself unable to sit still with the awkwardness of it.
He had no reason to apologize, he wanted to insist—but,

"—Idiot. Sit down, Garf."

"Ah—"

"Watching quietly is proper theater etiquette. Do you intend to disrupt the performance?"

Addressed so suddenly, Garfiel turned and his eyes went wide.
There, in the seat to his left where no one had been a moment ago, sat a girl with pink hair—Ram. She tugged at the sash around the waist of the now-standing Garfiel to sit him back down, then said to the round-eyed kittens watching them, "Continue," in her usual imperious tone.
Obeying Ram's instruction, the kittens resumed their tussling on stage. But to Garfiel, Ram mattered far more than whatever the performance was.

"Ram, what the hell are you doin' in a place like this? What even is this?"

"Garf."

"No, this ain't the time for talkin'. C'mon, we're gettin' outta here! Figurin' out where we are can wait 'til I know you're safe."

"Garf."

"Right, tell me what you think too. Honestly, I bet the two of us thinkin' on it'll get us to the right answer faster than just me—"

"—Garf, haven't you noticed?"

Driven by mounting urgency, his tone gradually rising, Garfiel caught his breath at Ram's words.
Before Garfiel's blinking eyes, Ram, her profile as cool and composed as ever, kept her gaze fixed steadily on the stage.
Feeling a pang of frustration that the woman he loved wouldn't even look his way, Garfiel followed her gaze to the stage as well, watching the kittens' unchanging scuffle.
But then he noticed that meaning lay hidden within that unchanging scuffle.

The golden-haired kitten, the red-haired kitten, and the kitten wearing the white tuft faced off, rampaging through the cityscape backdrop and smashing it apart—until at last, shoved by the white-tufted kitten, the golden-haired kitten went tumbling.
The fallen golden kitten ended up pinned beneath a collapsing set piece.
What kind of performance was this—

"Garf."

Called again by Ram's quiet voice, Garfiel turned. The pale pink eyes that had been watching the stage now turned to face him, and their gazes met head-on.
Looking at Ram's face so close up like this would normally have set his heart racing—but this time, what pounded inside Garfiel wasn't love. It was the alarm bell of instinct.
Seeing that realization dawn on Garfiel, Ram let out a small sigh and said,

"You're dying, you know."

And with that, she declared this to be the fleeting dream-theater Garfiel was watching.

—That golden-wigged kitten sprawled on the stage. That was him.

Once he understood that, what a ridiculous performance this was.
The white-tufted kitten was the enemy they were facing; the red-haired kitten behind was the person they were protecting. As he thought that through, his memories gradually came into focus, bit by bit.

"That's right, I'm in the middle of takin' on a 'Dragon' 'cause the boss man asked me to... which means that fluffball's the Dragon!? And the redhead's the old man!?"

"My, aren't they adorable now."

"I don't even wanna know which of 'em you mean by that, but that ain't the point right now! I gotta get back, now—!"

"Wait."

"Gwaaugh!"

Understanding his situation, Garfiel tried to leap up immediately, only to yelp in pain as something yanked the cord of his necklace. It was Ram, forcibly restraining him, and Garfiel spun around toward her, baring his fangs to shout—

"Hold on, hold on, Garfiel. Sis is right on this one. Let's take a beat."

—but it was Natsuki Subaru, seated on the far side of Ram, who stopped him instead.
This Subaru was the long-limbed version he hadn't seen in a while—long only compared to his current state, mind you, not actually all that long—but it was Subaru all the same.

"Hey, were you just thinking something incredibly hurtful just now?"

"Don't sweat it, boss. You're always sayin' your legs are short and your torso's long anyway."

"There's a difference between saying it about yourself and having someone else say it! There's a thing like that, right! Right, Bea-ko? Emilia-tan?"

"Subaru, don't interrupt, big brother's play is just getting to the good part, I suppose."

"Sorry, Subaru. I promise I'll listen properly later, okay...?"

The long-absent, properly-sized Subaru was brushed off in unison by Beatrice, perched on his lap, and Emilia, seated beside him. Both of them were too absorbed in the little cats' every move on stage to spare Subaru any attention.
Listening to Subaru and the others' exchange, shoulders slumping, Garfiel remembered that the little cat he didn't recognize in person was named Puck.
And more than that—he felt like he'd seen a similar exchange between Subaru and Emilia somewhere before.

"That would be a memory from the theater this space is based on. You see, it's the spitting image of the theater they were invited to when everyone went to watch the play based on the Subjugation of the White Whale."

"Garf-san sure did take a real shine to theater, didn't he. I had a great time too, so I get how you feel."

"Otto-nii, Petra..."

Turning around at a voice from behind this time, Garfiel found himself meeting the eyes of Petra, waving cheerfully, and beyond her, Otto with a shrug.
And further beyond Otto, he also caught sight of a woman with long blonde hair—

"Gao..."

"What a pathetic look on your face, Garf. And this time, rare as it is, Ram is talking sense. Sit down and calm yourself."

"C-calm down, she says, but..."

"There, there. Won't you listen even when everyone around you is telling you the same thing? Honestly, just when I thought you'd grown up while I wasn't looking, you're still such a spoiled child, Gar-bou."

"Gah! Gran—hag!?"

"...Why must you always correct yourself in the worse direction, hm?"

Garfiel's astonished eyes landed on the sight of his grandmother Ryuzu, seated right beside his sister.
At that outrageous pairing, Garfiel's mouth flapped open and shut uselessly, and once again Ram tugged at his sash, forcing him back down.

"Well? Calmed down a little?"

"How the hell am I supposed to calm down in the middle of a 'Daguraham's siege net'...! I only sat 'cause my knees gave out. ...What am I even s'posed to do here?"

"Well, it seems you're willing to listen to what Ram has to say, for now at least. That's good enough."

"As expected of you, sister."

"Whoa!?"

As Ram nodded, her expression the usual cool one, the audience member in the seat directly in front suddenly turned around and cut in. Garfiel blinked, realizing it was Rem, Ram's sister with the pale blue hair.
Rem herself, however, ignored Garfiel entirely and turned back to face the front.

"Wh-what the hell...?"

"That'd be it. Inside Garfiel's head, there isn't much of an image of Rem to work with yet, so all he gets is a vague, fuzzy reaction. That falls squarely within my range as an impressionist."

"If that's the level of accuracy we're workin' with, how many more of these am I gonna end up meetin' in this audience?"

Subaru, toying with the hair of the Beatrice on his lap, answered in response to Rem's flat, bloodless demeanor.
It was a vaguely plausible explanation, but if she couldn't be properly recreated as herself, it left the impression that maybe she shouldn't even be in this theater to begin with.

"That's not right, Garf. It's true you don't know Rem very well yet, but that doesn't mean you haven't taken notice of her. That's precisely why she's here."

"That's right. Someone who's caught your attention lingers strongly in the heart. How close you are to them personally isn't all that important—I think that's the crucial point in this case."

"This is bad for my heart, y'know."

Garfiel had begun to understand, more or less, the rules governing this theater, but having that explained to him by Frederica, and, for some reason, by Reinhard seated in the row behind, felt like it lacked any sense of reality.
Well, since it wasn't reality, it made sense that it didn't feel real. Frederica and Reinhard had probably never even been in the same place together, let alone exchanged words like this.
And yet—

"That first bit of panic's died down some. So all I gotta do is stay put here, like Ram said? Feels like I'm gettin' sleepy or somethin'..."

"Ha! Don't say something so stupid. If you fall asleep, you die."

"That just proves this is a dangerous damn place after all!"

"「Shh」"

The moment his voice rose, Beatrice, Emilia, and Petra all pressed fingers to their lips to shush him at once. Garfiel's lips trembled at the rebuke, and he clacked his fangs together and shut up.
Told not to rush back in a panic, yet also told he'd die if he calmed down too much—just what exactly was Garfiel supposed to do?

"What I mean is that going back empty-handed would be a shame, that's all. There's no need to overthink it, is there-e-e."

"Tch, you're even in my damn head..."

"My, my, how disliked I've become-e. Still, blaming me for it would be misplaced, so apologizing for it would be a strange thing to do too."

Seated in the row behind, Roswaal goaded him on, legs crossed, long as ever. Garfiel shot him an irritated glance, opened his mouth to complain—and the words caught in his throat.
He understood that Reinhard, who'd spoken to him earlier, was seated beside Roswaal. But the person seated next to Reinhard was the problem.
The woman in black, noticing Garfiel's gaze, gave an "Oh" and smiled seductively.

"You're giving me quite a heated look. Even in a place like this, I wonder—would you cross swords with me again, to your heart's content?"

"...Wouldn't be no fun even if I did. Bad for Meili too. Not doin' it."

"How disappointing. Though I don't mind the thought that I have a place inside you."

"Elsa, you really are so nasty."

Elsa, wearing a bloodstained smile, was answered by Meili, who took up where she'd left off—seated, across the row from Garfiel, right beside Rem.
Garfiel was starting to understand, in a vague way, the logic behind the seating arrangement. Which meant, naturally, that seated beside Elsa was bound to be the blue-skinned giant with eight arms.

"——"

The giant, his expression carved like stone, glanced briefly at Garfiel and said nothing.
Exposed to that wordless pressure, Garfiel swallowed a small breath and clenched his fists as tightly as he could. His clenched fist was then wrapped up by the small hand of a small shadow leaning forward from the seat in front.

"Garf, you gonna be okay? Can you really do it? Mimi's real worried, y'know."

"Mimi..."

"That's right. Garf's a coward at heart, deep down."

His right hand taken by the round-eyed Mimi, his left taken by Ram beside him.
Garfiel raised his head, looked back and forth between the two faces, and let out one long, long breath.

This wasn't a real scene, and Garfiel himself was on the verge of dying.
And yet, being stopped like this from rushing straight back, being spoken to one after another by the companions inside his own head—it was like the "Soumatou" he'd once heard about from Subaru.

"But Garfiel, a soumatou's normally something where you flash back through your whole life, from the moment you were born up to right now, all at once—it ain't something that summons you into a theater."

"...Then what the hell is this, boss?"

"Well, you're the one who's gotta find that answer yourself."

"As expected of you, Subaru-kun. I'm impressed."

"—Myself, huh."

There was an unnecessary bit of chiming in wedged in there, but Garfiel sank deep into thought.
If this wasn't a soumatou, and the answer was something he had to find on his own, then wasn't this, after all, just the fleeting moment of grace before a half-dead Garfiel got back on his feet?
If he didn't get up right now, wouldn't he fail the duty entrusted to him—

"Uu, I hope both big brothers do their best, I suppose...!"

"That's right. Whatever the outcome, let's watch it through properly, to the very end...!"

Ignoring the panicking Garfiel, Beatrice and Emilia, watching the play, seemed to be building toward their own climax of feeling.
Hearts in their throats, swaying between joy and despair with every turn on stage, the two held hands, their emotions violently stirred by the bizarre sight of Puck colliding with Puck.

On stage, the golden Puck lay fallen, and the red-haired Puck held back the fluffy Puck rushing toward it—it was truly a moment of life or death—

"—Red-hair."

The red-haired Puck stood protecting the golden Puck, facing off against the fluffy Puck.
The stage, whose situation had changed while Garfiel wasn't watching—if it reflected reality, then his face drained of color and he clacked his fangs together.
He had to get back, now, no matter what—

"—Idiot."

It was a familiar insult, but unlike the usual one laced faintly with warmth, this time it carried a clearer, sharper note of rebuke.
When Garfiel's eyes went wide at Ram's words, she pointed to the stage with the hand not laced with his. Drawn along by that, he looked toward the stage and saw that the scene had moved.

The fallen golden Puck rose up, shoving the red-haired Puck aside. It charged straight at the fluffy Puck—and with one blow from the fluffy Puck, the golden Puck turned into light.

"One out. But there's no such thing as three outs when it comes to life. One out, and it's over."

The golden Puck turned to light, and the red-haired Puck, who had turned his back to flee, was chased down and turned to light as well by the fluffy Puck. Only the fluffy Puck remained on stage, and the curtain fell on the act.
Amid scattered applause, the curtain came down, and as the fluffy Puck bowed deeply, the performance came to its end—

"Ehh!? That was totally not fun at all! Mimi's not satisfied with that, no way!"

Mimi, holding Garfiel's hand just as Ram was, stretched her tail straight up and made an unmannerly fuss.
But it wasn't a case of "well, the results are already in, nothing to be done about it."

In the next instant, the opening bell rang through the theater once more, and the fallen curtain rose again.
And beyond that curtain, the golden Puck and red-haired Puck who should have vanished, and the fluffy Puck who should have been erased, faced off once more—the climactic scene replaying, with the fallen gold one being shielded, and the red-haired one facing down the fluffy one.

"This is..."

Garfiel's throat trembled, utterly unable to grasp what was unfolding before him.
And to Garfiel, his thoughts gone blank—

"Just leaping up blindly wasn't good enough, was it. So what will you do?"

Ram, one eye closed, asked the question quietly.
At Ram's question, Garfiel caught his breath and looked once more at the stage.

"Sister is wonderful."

Truly, wholeheartedly, Garfiel agreed with the words of the sister of the woman he loved—someone he still hardly knew.

On stage, the golden Puck turned into light again, and again, and again.
This play, it seemed, allowed for quite a lot of improvisation, and its content was heavily swayed by suggestions from the audience—how about trying this, how about that.
And indeed—

"What about a full-power kick strong enough to flip the entire Imperial Capital upside down?"
"What if, this time, you deliberately took a wound bad enough to lose half your body, and waited for the moment your opponent thought you were dead to strike back?"
"Even a 'Dragon' seems to have some kind of attachment somewhere. For instance, how about-oo using some acquaintance of the Dragon's, who might happen to be in the capital, as a shield?"

"Shut it, all of you! Either you're suggestin' things I couldn't pull off even if I wanted to, or you're spoutin' cowardly schemes I wouldn't even think of in the first place!"

A string of suggestions unfit to ever be adopted for the stage kept coming, and the people in the seats behind him fell silent all at once at Garfiel's furious roar back at them.
Since this was, after all, only Garfiel's own perception of how they'd behave, it did leave him with the rather unproductive feeling that he was essentially just angry at himself.

"At the very least, making use of the terrain will be necessary. The difference in size between you and it may be a fatal disadvantage, but flip it around, and there ought to be openings you can only exploit precisely because you're small."
"Right, that's it. Garf-san's body is smaller than that 'Dragon,' and in a straight contest of strength he'd probably lose—but that's exactly why he can win. That's the story we should be telling."
"That's fine in theory, but what do you actually do about it? Never mind Garfiel—if it were me, there's no way I'd beat a 'Dragon' even if it fought upside down."
"Nobody's expecting Natsuki-san to go slay any 'Dragons,' except maybe Garfiel."

Meanwhile, the companions seated in a row beside him, offering constructive suggestions—even though they were only Garfiel's own perception of them—somehow seemed to speak more wisely than Garfiel himself.
Of course, this was presumably the sort of conversation the real them would actually have—

"Emilia-tan, got any advice for Garfiel?"

"Me? Let's see... Before he got hit like that, there was that moment he couldn't fully absorb Mezoreia's tail strike—I think that was the problem. The footing might have been tricky, but instead of trying to receive it or deflect it, I think he needed to just dodge out of the way entirely."

"That was a way more solid answer than I expected!"

"As expected of Emilia-san."

Watching Puck and the others scuffling on stage, Subaru and Rem each reacted to Emilia's sharp, precise observation. Whether Rem's reaction was "correct" or not, Garfiel couldn't tell—but perhaps her words were a direct reflection of Garfiel's own feelings.

Either way, something was gradually coming into view, it seemed.
But even if he kept building on this, piling it up and up, and carried what he'd built back into reality—just how much of it would actually turn out to be useful?

"Anxious, Gar-bou?"

"...Not so much anxious as, honestly, I feel more pathetic than anythin'. That 'Dragon's' the opponent the boss man entrusted to me, and yet..."

Peered at by Ryuzu, Garfiel confessed his bitter feelings.
He'd charged in full of spirit, nearly died in the middle of a fierce battle, and on top of that, wasn't satisfied with just that—he'd gone and built himself a theater inside his own head, being comforted by a whole crowd of companions—some of whom weren't even companions, but there they were regardless.

"What exactly is pathetic about that?"

"——"

Asked that, Garfiel, brow furrowed, looked up. Beside Ryuzu, Frederica was watching him steadily.
Narrowing the same green eyes as his own, she said,

"What's so pathetic about it, Garf?"

—repeating the very same question.

"It is pathetic, ain't it. 'Cause everyone else is fightin' alone, and here I am, doin' this...!"

"Fighting alone—you're saying it's pathetic not to do that?"

"That's right! No, wait, that ain't it. I ain't sayin' fightin' together with others is pathetic. It's this, gatherin' up my own kin inside my own head like this—"

"—There's no point pretending anymore, Garf."

Cornered and beaten alone, and in that state, clinging to the companions inside his own heart.
To Garfiel, who had been trying hard to shake off that stance, Frederica said it plainly.
Her green eyes narrowed still, Frederica sighed at the frozen Garfiel and said,

"You're a bit of a fool, Garf. Take a look around you."

"Around..."

At her words, Garfiel looked around the theater.
The companions lined up beside him, the enemies seated in the rows behind, the ones in front whose loyalty he'd hesitate to fully claim—and, before he knew it, there was not a single empty seat left in the theater. Every single one was filled with a face Garfiel recognized.

"——"

Ram and Subaru and the others were there, of course, and Roswaal and Reinhard, and Mimi and Rem, and many more besides.
There were people from the Mathers domain, and people he'd come to know on this journey through the Empire.
There were people he liked, and people he thought he could never get close to—but every single one of them had some connection to Garfiel's life, and had secured their own seat here.

"Well now, the theater's packed to bursting. It won't be long before it can't hold everyone at all."

Seeing the same sight as Garfiel, Ryuzu laughed, in high spirits.
Just as his grandmother said, the theater, now full to capacity, even had people standing to watch. Everyone was gazing up at the Pucks on stage, watching the pathetic losing performance of the Puck playing Garfiel's role.
His face burned hot at the realization. It was a pathetic, unsightly way to lose.
Surely everyone was appalled at him too—

"—Idiot."

"Y'know, Garf. This here, it's inside Garf's head, right? But Garf's not all that smart. Same as Mimi! So maybe what everyone's sayin' is like that too?"

Told this in quick succession from both his left and right, Garfiel blinked.
Looking again after that, he found every gaze that had been watching the stage now turned toward him instead. He looked at each face, one by one, recalling what kind of people they were.
Every one of them had spoken up freely, saying whatever they pleased, complaining about the beaten-up Puck playing Garfiel—and yet—

"Nobody's sayin' anything that's convenient for me, and nobody's sayin' anything that seems too outta character for 'em either, huh."

Ram's way of putting things was terse, Mimi's way was vague, but they were both saying the same thing.
If this was a theater inside Garfiel's own head, gathering up family, companions, acquaintances, even enemies, all just to comfort a half-dead, beaten Garfiel—that would be far too pathetic.
But Garfiel wasn't clever.
He wasn't capable of anything so complicated as making people say things they wouldn't actually say, or lining up convenient words like some kind of puppet show to soothe his own heart.

"Just like you figured, whatever gets talked through in here doesn't just carry straight over into the outside world. This is just for peace of mind, that's all it is. But there's nothin' wrong with getting a little peace of mind."

"Subaru begged you to please stop the 'Dragon,' and this is quite the way of putting it, I suppose."

"I'm the me inside Garfiel's head, so...!"

"Does that mean Garfiel actually thinks you'd say something that troublesome? Is that it?"

Asked by a blank-faced Emilia, Garfiel groaned, "Aah."
He looked up at the ceiling, thought it over a while, then lowered his gaze.

"I figure I don't mind the boss man throwin' as much crazy stuff at me as he wants, so maybe that's why there's a boss man in my head who'd say somethin' like that."

"Even if that were true, you clearly know full well the people around you would properly call him out on it. Honestly, I wonder who taught you that kind of cunning..."

"...And you're the one saying that, Otto-sama."

At Frederica's wry remark, Otto pointed at himself with an aggrieved look. Seeing that, Petra laughed, "Ahaha," hand over her mouth, and the laughter spread through everyone around them.
Before he knew it, Garfiel found himself laughing too, despite everything.

"...Guess my head might've gotten a little stiff, or somethin'."

"Oh? Gar-bou, saying that?"

"There's the boss man's request on the line, and there's the whole Empire ridin' on it too. The opponent bein' a 'Dragon' don't help either, and not bein' allowed to lose is always the case anyway, but—"

The hands laced with his on either side—he slowly loosened them, lifted them up, and brought his freed hands together in front of his chest, clapping them with force.
Still in that pose, he clacked his fangs to raise his fighting spirit, and said,

"This is the duty I was entrusted with, but it ain't a fight I'm fightin' alone."

"—Well done."

A low, weighty word of praise layered over Garfiel's surging resolve.
Garfiel didn't bother confirming that it came from the giant seated behind him, arms folded in fours. Right now, there was only one thing he wanted to confirm.

"You said it, didn't you, boss! Fly up there and drop that dragon that's actin' all high and mighty, you said!"

"—Yeah! I said it! Go get it done!"

"Damn right!"

Answering with gusto, Garfiel rose to his feet with force, right there on the spot.
And this time, Ram—who had pulled him back every time he'd tried to stand before—didn't do it. When Garfiel glanced her way, she sat with her arms crossed, watching the stage, and said,

"What?"

"Ha! Nothin'. Whether it's my own damn head or not, you're one tough woman."

"I'm simply not saying things that don't need saying. That's Garf's role, after all."

Shrugging her thin shoulders, not even glancing his way, Ram made Garfiel let out a wry laugh. In her place, Mimi bounced up to stand on her seat.

"Then Mimi'll say it! Garf, if you win here, you'll look real cool!"

Saying that with a laugh, Mimi's hand slapped Garfiel hard on the back.
A beat later, just like Mimi, Subaru, then Beatrice, then Emilia, then Petra, then Frederica, then Ryuzu, all slapped Garfiel on the back in turn.
As though being pushed forward, Garfiel, backs slapping him along, made for the theater's exit.

"Otto-nii! Gimme some advice!"

"Well, let's see... something as reckless as risking your life—I won't allow anyone but me to do that."

"You're the last damn person who oughta be tellin' me that!"

Slapped on the back by his joking older-brother figure, Garfiel headed for the theater's exit.
The one pushing that door open for him was the young brother and sister he'd thought he hadn't spotted in the seats, and, smiled at by their mother with an arm around each of their shoulders, Garfiel raised a thumbs up.

An encounter enough to fill the theater to bursting—it had been one hell of a soumatou.
So, at the very last, he jabbed a sharp finger straight at the Pucks up on stage, and declared,

"—I ain't no cat! I'm a Gorgeous Tiger!!"

△▼△▼△▼△

"——"

The "Dragon" was a being sometimes likened to natural disasters and calamities of the earth themselves, and the "Cloud Dragon" Mezoreia's rampage left absolutely no room to argue with that comparison.
The destructive incarnation reshaping the scenery of the Imperial Capital into something utterly unlike what it had been just minutes before—witnessing a scene that felt like it belonged to another world entirely shattered Heinkel's heart, his knees trembling without end.

For one brief instant, something had nearly changed the course of Heinkel's fate.
But it had lost badly in a direct clash with the "Dragon," taking a full-on roar fierce enough to split the earth and being blown away. Rubble rained down, and it lay buried beneath.
That was exactly why he'd said it was pointless.

Meaninglessly, mercilessly, that golden-haired boy's life had been scattered.
And all Heinkel could do was watch it happen in silence—

"—I'm a Gorgeous Tiger!!"

In the next instant, the mountain of rubble exploded from within, and a furious shout echoed out.
So sudden was it that Heinkel, throat seizing in shock, stared in disbelief at the figure who'd blasted the pile of rubble apart with an upthrust fist—Garfiel.

"Aah, damn it... how long was I out...?"

Shaking his head groggily, tearing off his tattered coat, Garfiel asked the question. At that, Heinkel blinked rapidly and answered,

"...You were buried under rubble for about five seconds."

"—Five seconds, huh. I coulda been killed ten times over in that. Close call, close call."

Letting out one long, deep breath, Garfiel cracked the bones of his neck.
Heinkel was left speechless at the sight of him—hardly the look of someone who'd just taken an attack Heinkel had been certain was fatal. But there was someone even less able to hide their shock than Heinkel.

『Wh-what...!?』

It had finally shot down Garfiel, who had darted about tirelessly, landing unbelievable blows that made a mockery of the difference in their size.
It had killed him for certain. That certainty, shattered in a mere five seconds, drew even sympathy from Heinkel. —Not that sympathy meant a damn thing here, against that transcendent creature, the "Cloud Dragon."

『You... why won't you... why won't you die!?』

"Ah? Don't talk stupid, I was dead. If the old man hadn't bought me those five seconds."

"I-I didn't do anything..."

Garfiel bared his fangs at the astonished "Dragon's" words, but Heinkel shook his head.
It was true that the instant after Garfiel was blown away and buried in rubble, Heinkel had stood there, sword in hand, facing off against Mezoreia. But that had only been because he understood that if Garfiel had fallen, he himself would be next in line as prey—he'd simply met its eyes, nothing more.

『—! Diiiiiiie!!』

Unaware of Heinkel's own self-assessment, Mezoreia's patience reached its limit.
The "Cloud Dragon" surged furiously toward the standing Garfiel, raised its thick, long tail, and brought it down from directly above like a bolt of lightning.
Even Heinkel nearly got swept off his feet just from the wind kicked up by the wings beating to close the distance. That outrageous, otherworldly blow of the "Dragon's" aimed straight at Garfiel—

"—Ah?"

In that instant, the "Cloud Dragon's" body blurred in midair, its entire form slammed into the Imperial Capital's outer wall.

『——!?』

Flipped upside down, crashing back-first into the wall, Mezoreia's breath ragged with confusion at what had just happened—Heinkel too was left with his mouth hanging open, unable to close it.
He had no idea how it had been done. But he knew exactly who had done it.

—Garfiel had deflected the blow of Mezoreia's tail, and thrown the "Dragon."

"Aah, damn it... I actually pulled it off."

Looking down at his own two hands, Garfiel muttered that with an oddly displeased look.
As though he'd just dug up some unpleasant memory of his own, he opened and closed the hands that had thrown the "Dragon," over and over, then turned around.
His green eyes met the twin eyes of the upside-down "Cloud Dragon" head-on.
And then—

"Well, whatever. Let's do it, round two. —Sorry I got a mountain of people cheerin' me on and you don't, but that's just how it is!"

With that, fueling his fighting spirit on logic that made no sense at all, Garfiel's clash with the "Cloud Dragon" resumed after its brief interruption—a passage of myth, moving at last toward its resolution.
