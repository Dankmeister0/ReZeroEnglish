Arc 7 Chapter 98: "Pleiades Battalion"

—The Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, the Vollachia Empire, the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko, the Kararagi City-States.

Each of the nations commonly called the Four Great Nations has developed its own culture suited to its own land. The reverence for dragons in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica and the worship of Spirits in the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko are the most prominent examples, but it's as though the Four Great Nations set out, as if by design, to walk entirely different paths. That divergence isn't confined to national character or culture alone — the same holds true for the quality of their "warriors."

In the Vollachia Empire, "Magic" has scarcely developed at all. Cross the border and people are still people — the aptitude housed in the body, the Gate and so on, doesn't differ from nation to nation. And yet, compared even to Lugunica or Kararagi, the Empire lags far behind in that particular field. That said, the Empire doesn't much concern itself with this fact.

Of course, falling behind other nations in any field is nothing to celebrate. But the fact that no path opened up for magic is itself proof that some other path did. Set against the technological prowess of the Kararagi City-States, the Spiritual Arts and Curse Arts of the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko, and the magical tradition of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, what the Vollachia Empire excelled at was the refinement of martial technique.

"There are times it's faster to just zip in and smack somebody than to bother with magic, right?"

Now, that's a remark no self-respecting Spiritual Arts User ought to make, and it isn't wholeheartedly endorsed — but in the Vollachia Empire, this attitude toward magic is more or less the norm.

Here's a fact easily misunderstood, and one nowhere formally written down: the uses to which the mana dwelling in a human body can be put are not limited to magic alone. Just as it's employed in Spiritual Arts, in Curse Arts, and in the activation of certain "Meteors," no small number of martial techniques are built upon that same foundation.

More to the point, for any warrior of the first rank, using mana to reinforce one's own body is simply natural law. When, every so often, someone is found to possess physical capabilities well beyond the ordinary, the trick behind it is the handling of the mana circulating through the body — a technique, when done consciously, called the "Flow Method." That said—

"Cha-cha-cha-cha-cha!"

A single toe touches down with a light tap, and in the next instant a gap of over ten meters simply vanishes. To anyone watching that figure from a distance, the sheer unnaturalness of the movement might seem like time itself had been stolen — but it is no illusion, and no trick of the eye. What's abnormal is, quite plainly, the blue-haired boy tearing across the battlefield like that.

Following the earlier explanation, the boy's dash — one that vaults clean over the bounds of human capability — is unmistakably rooted in the flow of the "Flow Method." And yet, behind the boy's youthful face and his sharp, striking good looks, there is not a trace of the years of grinding practice such technique normally demands. The world holds, on rare occasion, beings like this — ones who wield what is called the very pinnacle of martial technique, the "Flow Method," unconsciously, from birth, and who leave behind all those bound by the ordinary rules of mastery.

Racing down the hillside in a single breath, the boy plunges into the battlefield in pursuit of the glory of the first spear. Although—

"Well, given that the fighting's already underway, the glory of the first spear is pretty much a formality at this point, but let's not sweat the details!"

Charging onto the field from the western reaches, he takes in, at a distance, the massed army encircling the star-shaped ramparts. The boy's narrow eyes gleam, wet with an excitement that seems out of place amid a battlefield of mingled grief and joy. Wearing no proper footwear, no armor or helmet of any kind, and carrying no cause worth the name — a figure like this, striding onto a battlefield, is the sort of savagery that both sides, those defending the current Empire within the walls and those seeking to tear it down without, would be equally justified in treating as an enemy. And yet—

"A lofty sense of purpose is one of the more attractive qualities in a man, you know. Whether you have it or not changes how a line lands when it slips out of your mouth. Though, mind you—"

"Who goes there!?"

Amid the skirmish between two small bands of a few dozen each, clad in red gear and shabby armament, clearly hostile to one another, a soldier raised his blade and called out at the boy's voice cutting in from the side. Anger, irritation, excitement, unease — all tangled together, but fighting spirit won out, in a splendid tone of voice. A fine countermeasure, rousing the wariness of those around him against the intruder who'd appeared on the battlefield.

"—But fatally slow!"

"Gah—"

Calling out was the right call. Asking who he was, though, was the wrong one. A figure appearing on a battlefield without declaring any allegiance ought, as a first approximation, to be treated as an enemy. The soldier just now should have done more than alert those around him — he should have struck at the same instant he spoke. Even so, this one here was faster. Not that there weren't ten or twenty other faults one could point to, if one wanted to.

"Ah, or if that last line of yours wasn't asking about my allegiance, but was really you having no idea at all what I even am — well, sorry, my instincts run a little too sharp for my own good."

Rattling this off in rapid patter, the boy left a soldier behind him collapsing to the ground, eyes rolled back white. A sharp hand-strike to the neck in passing. He'd meant to take the head clean off — not enough force behind it, apparently. Shaking his arm out, he tilted his head, muttering that something still felt off.

"It's strangely unsatisfying, feeling like you should be able to do something and then not quite managing it. Even I'm not supposed to go mixing up ideals and wishes with what's actually achievable. Well — accidentally killing him would only lower the boss's opinion of me, so let's call this a net positive!"

Having so blithely shaken off his own unease, the laughing boy's form blurs into a haze the very next moment. A blade comes swinging down toward where the boy had been standing — dodged. In return, the sole of the boy's zori slams solidly into the side of the swinging soldier's face. Using the recoil from that kick to spring away, the boy dives into the group behind that soldier, striking a vital point on each in turn before disengaging — no, while disengaging, he slips into the next group over as well, taking down another five or so. Might as well, while he's at it.

Both the incidental and the intentional were carried out by stealing time from the soldiers around him. Which is to say: the boy's control of his own body defies human comprehension, something no half-measure of skill could hope to keep pace with — the ultimate form, one might say, of the Vollachia Empire's development of martial technique.

Naturally, no one on a battlefield has the leisure to make such an analysis, and no one is quick to grasp that a twelve- or thirteen-year-old child stands at such a summit. Above all else—

"—How uncouth."

"—————"

"Trying to expose the finer points of a star performer's technique, the sheen of his good looks, in the middle of the dance — that's not couth at all. All a stage needs is for you to be captivated, enraptured, held prisoner. Nothing more is required. Of course, if you're going to take the stage yourself as well, then you'll need a proper rival... someone of suitable stature, to better assert your own star quality."

Slowly, the boy turns to survey his surroundings, standing at the center of a ring of fallen Imperial soldiers, and the crowd draws a collective breath. Warriors who a moment ago had been clashing weapons in a life-or-death struggle — soldiers of both camps alike had been swallowed up by the boy's sheer presence, robbed of their time, which is to say, of their lives.

"Mmm, there's nothing more gratifying to a performer..."

Stealing another's life — the boy takes real, tangible satisfaction in that sensation. Amid the warriors having their time stolen from them, while the boy savors his sense of accomplishment, one of the shabbily-armed men on the side opposing the mostly-fallen Imperial soldiers opens his mouth to say "you—"

"Whose side are you on? One of ours?"

"Hmm, hmm-hmm, my allegiance, is it? Now that's a rather good question, you. What do you suppose I am? Friend, or foe?"

"—————"

Answered with a question, and a rather roundabout one at that, the soldier who'd asked falls silent. Seeing this, the boy's smile deepens, and he shakes his head. "No, no."

"I wasn't teasing you. I really did think it over seriously, you know. And this is what I always conclude: I wasn't really listening to the boss's explanation, so I honestly have no idea whose side I'm on."

"...Huh?"

"So for now, I'll just clear the path by punishing both sides equally, shall I?"

The soldier's eyes go wide — and that startled face blurs sideways, and in the next instant he's flat on the ground. The boy's grinning form flickers out of sight, slipping this time into the group of warriors opposing the Imperial soldiers he'd just felled, and mows down their footing with furious speed.

Blue hair dances, and just as a sharp crack of impact rings out, the warriors' consciousness winks out one after another without so much as a groan of pain. That outrage — truly like a storm — no—

"'Blue Lightning'..."

"Oh my, how splendid. That happens to be exactly the epithet I've been thinking of claiming for myself."

The soldier who let that word slip out in a shock-strangled breath loses consciousness the moment he finishes speaking. Responding to friendliness and hostility alike, equally, with violence — call it the Vollachia way, and even this nation's own Emperor would frown at the savagery of it.

But no one can stop the boy. And that, in fact, is the conduct held to be most correct, by this Empire's own code.

"Guh, ohhhh—!!"

Spurred by the threat of the small whirlwind the boy is stirring up, the warriors — too late — finally move. Imperial soldier and rebel alike, with no distinction between them any longer, every mind present is united in the single purpose of stopping this boy. In a sense, that alone — the halting of civil strife within this one small space — would count as an achievement. But their courageous resolve was never to be rewarded with the peace of an answer.

Tilting his body to dodge a blade. Stepping back like a dance to avoid a punch. Slipping between legs to dodge an axe swing. Dodging arrows, dodging spears, dodging fangs, dodging shields, dodge and dodge and dodge and dodge, evade and evade and evade and evade, weave and weave and weave and weave, dodge dodge dodge dodge dodge—

"Without even a weapon...!"

"Yeah, that's the annoying part. I do think having something in hand, rather than going bare-handed, would add a lot more color to a proper flourish, but I don't want to carry around some half-baked thing. First-rate recognizes first-rate, as they say. Ah, that's a line I borrowed from the boss, though."

"...Boss?"

"Yes. Our— the head honcho of the Pleiades Battalion."

Effortlessly dodging the murderous intent and hostility flung at him, the boy's smile deepens with pride. At the unfamiliar word that had slipped from the boy's mouth, an unaccountable dread seized the warriors. Every one of them had, presumably, come here willing to stake their life on a battle to decide this Empire's fate. And yet the boy toying with them before their very eyes — they had no idea what side he belonged to, no sense of the limits of his ability, no clue as to the true meaning behind anything he said or did. And knowing nothing at all about him, everything nonetheless seemed to be unfolding exactly as the boy wished it to—.

—In the next instant, a tremendous battle cry rang out from the direction of a distant hillside, far away.

Who could blame the soldier who let out a small "hyip" and flinched in spite of himself? The same unease and confusion spread just as quickly through the other warriors as well. And so, the smile at the corner of the boy's mouth grew wider not out of mockery for the warriors' fear, but for something far more straightforward.

"Now then — the first big scene, boss. Make it a good one, would you?"

Here, an extremely unfortunate fact regarding "Magic" must be explained.

The background has already been laid out — that the Vollachia Empire never developed magical technique, and instead developed only physical martial arts and techniques of the "Flow Method" type. But this is a separate matter. It concerns the reason why, among the six attributes into which this world's magical system is divided, "Yang magic" and "Yin magic" — attributes with few practitioners — came to be undervalued for reasons beyond mere difficulty of research.

The Fire attribute, which manipulates heat energy to produce flame or ice. The Water attribute, which interferes with a living creature's vitality, promoting the healing of wounds and illness, saving lives. The Wind attribute, which shapes the environment by influencing the atmosphere, at times even securing livable zones within dangerous regions. The Earth attribute, which controls the power of the land, capable of both enriching soil and starving it.

Compared to these attributes, each with a direction that's easy to picture in use, the Yin and Yang attributes are generally understood as effects that raise or lower the functions of the human body. Strictly speaking this is a mistaken understanding, but the fact that such an image has taken root is itself a major reason why the development of Yin- and Yang-attribute magic, each, has failed to progress to this day.

And it may come as a surprise, but the one that has, in particular, continued to be judged a dud, a useless attribute — is the "Yang attribute": the power to enhance ability.

Knowing the primary effects of Yang magic, many would want to ask why it earns such an evaluation. But Yang magic carries, the more one tries to make use of its properties, several extremely troublesome problems that cannot simply be ignored.

For instance: take someone granted an improvement to their physical capabilities via Yang magic — how well could they actually perform on a battlefield? For the most part, such people are cut down by the enemy without being able to do a thing. The reason is simple: they cannot handle their own enhanced body at all, and with the same clumsiness as trying to operate someone else's body, they can't bring their own strengths to bear in the slightest.

"Improvement of physical capability" covers a lot of ground — enhancement of the five senses like sight and hearing, enhancement of physical abilities like arm strength or leg strength, enhancement of defense through increased bone and muscle density. But a warrior is someone who is constantly training their own body, who steps onto the battlefield with a full grasp of their own skill. Someone who receives Yang magic must cast aside that familiar self and go into battle in an entirely new body whose specs alone have been improved. As a result, most accomplished little and died in defeat.

The next problem is the fact that the magnitude of Yang magic's effect is heavily influenced by the caster's own skill and by their physical and mental condition on any given day. Even supposing a warrior who received Yang magic managed to adapt to their enhanced body, there is no guarantee they'll receive the same enhancement from the same caster tomorrow, nor any guarantee the effect will be equally strong. Yang magic cannot be counted on for a stable increase in fighting strength — if anything, it carries the flaw of leaving one unable to even perform up to one's usual standard. That is the reason Yang magic is shunned.

Once, there was an Emperor of the Vollachia Empire who took notice of the power of magic and thought to build the strongest army of all. Mulkia Vollachia, the thirty-first Emperor of the Vollachia Empire, schemed to make use of Yang magic to forge his already-strong soldiers into something stronger still. Having accounted for the aforementioned flaws, he paired Yang magic users with elite soldiers, trained them until the enhanced state became second nature, and brought them to actual battle.

The result, however, was a catastrophe. The elite soldiers, operating on the premise that they would be enhanced, were struck down before the Yang magic could even be cast on them; the skill of the magic users themselves, so crucial to the plan, varied wildly, and because the army failed to function as an army, it was overwhelmed by superior numbers, even putting the Emperor's own life in jeopardy. In the end, that conflict was resolved only when Viva "the Dissector," then a close aide to the Vollachian Emperor, struck down the enemy general — but given the scale of the defeat, Mulkia Vollachia went down in history bearing the dishonorable epithet of "the Emperor of Great Defeat."

With that precedent standing, the value of Yang magic — and, by extension, of magic altogether — came to be dismissed within Vollachia, and the result was that the notion of perfecting martial technique as the proof of a strong soldier spread all the more widely. That has not changed even to this day; even Vincent Vollachia, who turns every manner of stratagem over in his mind, has dismissed the deployment of Yang magic in group combat as not even worth considering.

In practice, given that a single Yang magic user can only enhance one target at a time, expecting an army's worth of soldiers to receive the same treatment is simply a pipe dream. And so, in the sense of inflicting an unfamiliar bodily effect upon an enemy, Yin magic has survived, if anything, only as a means of harassment, while magic in general has withered away within the Vollachia Empire — and in other nations too, Yang magic came to be regarded as an unfortunate branch of the art, rarely used for anything but self-enhancement.

—And yet, in all things, there are exceptions.

"Let's go, all of you—!!"

Drawing a deep breath, the boy's voice — still high — is raised across the western hillside, looking out over the distant battlefield. Centered on that boy, the warriors deployed in a line along the ridge — a company numbering well into the thousands — raise, one after another, the banners meant to declare the unity and the resolve of the group.

Never to be lowered, that banner is the very symbol of their fighting spirit. Beside the boy, the great standard is held aloft by a man whose whole body is covered in tattoos, and it's supported by a gray Lizard Human and a plain-looking man with sharp, resolute eyes.

Depicted on the raised banner is a mark, discernible even at a distance, shaped like a star. Neither family crest nor national emblem, it exists solely to prove where each of their hearts is planted — a kind of declaration of war, and nothing more or less than that.

The assembled company was, without exaggeration, made up of every manner of race gathered together. Even on the soil of Vollachia, where a great many races already live intermingled, relationships of cooperation that cross racial boundaries are rare. The demon city of Chaosflame functioned as one of the few exceptional examples of such a place, and in this very siege of the Imperial Capital, the rebel forces held not the slightest shared sense of cooperation among themselves.

But this company is different. One could view it as people simply traveling together toward the same objective. But that alone wouldn't be enough to bring them together beneath a banner meant to declare a shared resolve. It isn't that their objective is the same. It's that their resolve is the same.

And that is precisely why this company — no, why the Pleiades Battalion — is capable of being a trump card.

Leading those banners, and the comrades gathered beneath them, the black-haired boy — Natsuki Subaru — shouts out.

"—We are the strongest!!"

"'The strongest! The strongest!! The strongest—!!'"

The battle cry, raised all at once, thunders across the sky, and a tremendous fighting spirit spreads and catches fire across the battlefield. Beatrice, held in the arms of the shouting Subaru, covers her ears and blinks in surprise. Rui, likewise, clings to Subaru — but the surprise belongs only to the two girls.

"—We are invincible!!"

"'Invincible! Invincible!! Invincible—!!'"

Hiain, Weitz, Idra, Gustav, Orson and the rest, old man Null, Rex, Milzak, Cashew, Moizo, Diroy, Kligkin, Kodoro, Fenmel, Jawsrough, Tanza — all roar aloud. Feet stamp the ground, the earth trembles, the tremor calls forth further tremors, stirring their fighting spirit higher still.

Something hot, deep in the chest. Something hot and hot and hot, something dependable beyond words, races through this whole body. That thing, hot and dependable beyond words, set upon the tongue—

"—Destiny!!"

"'Bring it on! Bring it on!! Bring it on—!!'"

The company, having shouted those words together as one, all turn their gaze forward — down to the battlefield below the hillside. And then—

"—Let's go."

The sensation — of the sky, the ground, the whole world, shaking — was felt in that instant by everyone on the battlefield.

Among them, the ones least able to dismiss it as someone else's problem were those at the western ramparts, who were about to collide head-on with the banner-bearing company charging down the hillside — namely, the Imperial soldiers ordered to defend the nearest position, the Fourth Apex.

"Still—"

We cannot allow ourselves to be broken by mere intimidation, thought Gudda Dialmo, the Second General known as "the Tiger Hunter," standing his ground.

For a moment, he too had been overawed by the abruptness of their appearance and the height of their morale — but the Imperial soldiers, the men under "the Tiger Hunter," were not so poorly trained as to be taken down by some ranged trick like that. More than anything, the attrition suffered by Gudda and his men was, looking across this entire battlefield, about as minimal as could be. The Imperial Capital lies under a ferocious siege, yet the brutal assaults being carried out against the other apexes have had almost no effect here at the Fourth Apex. That, unmistakably, is the achievement of Olbart Dunkelkenn, who reigned as guardian of this Fourth Apex — a result of battle that could rightly be called the true measure of "the Vile Old Man."

"Honestly, the correct move is to cut off the enemy's legs before they even get here. Water, weapons, whatever it is — severing what they can't fight without, that's basic strategy."

There was a cruelty in "the Vile Old Man's" explanation for why the enemy's offensive here was overwhelmingly weaker than at the other apexes — the sort of cruelty that would send a chill down the spine of any officer or soldier who imagined him as anything other than an ally. But even Olbart, upon learning that the other apexes were on the verge of being breached, had to rush to their aid in order to fulfill his role in the Imperial Capital's defense.

And so, the Fourth Apex currently finds itself lacking the Empire's supreme fighting force, the Nine Divine Generals. The superhuman Shinobi that Olbart trained and left behind as a parting gift must be left to deal with the Flying Dragon Squadron that has appeared from the same western sky as the approaching army. With no ranged weapons of their own, the arts and techniques the Shinobi possess are the only means of countering the Flying Dragon Squadron.

And as for the company charging in with such ferocity—

"—I will meet you with everything I have!"

So saying, Gudda Dialmo hefts the two massive iron clubs synonymous with his epithet "the Tiger Hunter" — each nearly a hundred kilograms of solid metal — and glares at the enemy before him. Gudda's epithet comes from the time, back when he was still a Third General, that he wiped out a band of Tiger Humans who had run utterly amok within the nation, and took the head of the enemy commander who had killed the Second General leading the effort against them. At the end of that brutal slaughter, across Gudda's muscle-bound frame, over two meters tall, from the top of his head down to his stomach, a white tiger's claw-mark scar remains carved vertically — and from the tale of that fierce battle, he came in time to be called "the Tiger Hunter."

With their own General struck down, and their army on the verge of collapse, Gudda's fighting spirit did not break even then. So then, with numbers on their side, and with something behind them that must be protected, who among them could possibly quail now?

"Meet them head-on!!"

Letting out a voice like cannon-fire, Gudda's massive body kicks off the ground and closes in on the enemy horde. As if urged on by Gudda's back leading the charge, his men too, each shouldering whatever weapon they favored, plunge toward the star-bannered company sweeping in from the west.

"—That's..."

Closing the distance to the moment of collision on his own two feet, Gudda narrows his eyes and sees, at the head of the enemy — the company arriving with a sound like rolling thunder — a single red Galewind Horse running out in front. What draws the eye isn't the man holding its reins. The man is merely holding the reins; what matters is the small figure riding the Galewind Horse, cradled as if held by that man.

A black-haired boy, holding a girl in his arms, stares straight ahead with black eyes fixed on him. Neither reckless nor overeager, the boy charges forward as though it were simply natural for him to be in that position — and for an instant, a thought flashed through Gudda's mind.

A boy with black hair and black eyes — that matched the "black-haired crown prince" that rumor had spread before the war began.

"—Tch"

Biting down on the thought that had risen in him, Gudda focuses on what he must do. Exploiting that foolish rumor, the rebels had prepared any number of "black-haired crown princes" and, in their folly, thrown them at this decisive battle for the Imperial Capital. And so the Generals charged with defending the Imperial Capital — no, every officer and soldier — had all resolved, as one, on this: whether the black-haired child was genuine or not didn't matter in the slightest. There is only one Emperor whom we are to serve.

"—You traitorous scum, who would raise your hand against Emperor Vincent Vollachia!!"

Brandishing his two colossal iron clubs, Gudda lets out a roar like the tiger he once hunted — the very symbol of his own epithet — and bears down on the boy leading the charge.

Straight ahead: the boy driving the red Galewind Horse, and the rebels following behind him. Meaning to sweep all of it away in one stroke, Gudda — a General himself — drives a blow straight into the enemy's leading edge. The downward strike lets out a tremendous shockwave of impact, enough to burst a weaker eardrum. Even Gudda's own thick arms felt the satisfying jolt of a solid hit — and then Gudda's eyes went wide.

The black-haired boy who should have been crushed, Galewind Horse and all — but what the iron club had caught was neither him, nor the Galewind Horse he rode—

"Protecting Schwarz is my current job, given my profession."

A body even larger than Gudda's own, four thick, powerful arms, and the great shield those arms held — that had caught Gudda's blow, no, both blows, head-on. The physical capability of the Multi-Arm Tribe, even accounting for it, made for a spectacle astonishing enough to catch in Gudda's throat.

And sliding into that instant's pause—

"And... beating down Schwarz's enemies is my job..!"

"—!!"

That voice — muffled, yet valiant, carrying a strong sense of pride and self-conviction — drove a punishing blow straight into Gudda's torso, launching his massive body up into the air.

Letting out a groan of "gah—" as he looked back, he saw it was a man covered head to toe in tattoos who had struck him, wielding a great two-handed hammer swung with both arms. The man was trained, certainly. But with that build, and those amateurish movements — why had he — no, the question of "why" applied to more than just what had happened to him alone.

"'Ohhhhhh—!!'"

Gudda, who had led the charge, was blown away, and after him the collision between rebels and Imperial soldiers continued. He had trained his men well enough that even if Gudda's own head were severed, their fighting spirit would not falter — it was precisely because of that confidence that the sight below him now was so hard to believe.

The Imperial soldiers, almost without being able to mount any proper resistance at all, were shattered all at once by the offensive power of the surging rebel horde, ground down completely. At that shocking a sight — more overwhelming even than the moment he had once watched a General's head bitten clean off before his own eyes — a passion beyond words seized Gudda's entire body—

"—This is madness."

—and so, even a General seasoned by countless battles let slip, in a strained breath, at a sight beyond comprehension.

—In all things, there are exceptions.

That exception is precisely the Pleiades Battalion — a band of terrifying amateurs that shattered the fierce general Gudda Dialmo and the elite soldiers he himself had trained.

They possess no honed technique, no fine weapons or armor, no firm and lofty cause. What they do possess is a strong, truly strong will to stand together, and something like a vow to themselves never to retreat, no matter what comes.

And that, precisely, is the answer to what made the Pleiades Battalion such an aberrant force.

Does anyone still remember the long lecture on the flaws of Yang magic? Its deployment, so easily thrown off by the caster's skill and by numbers, so often disrupting the coordination among its recipients — this was the very folly that once branded a past Emperor with the shameful epithet "the Emperor of Great Defeat," and that even Vincent Vollachia, hailed as a wise ruler, dismissed early on, at the mere stage of consideration.

But what if — suppose, hypothetically — something like this were possible?

Applying one uniform enhancement effect to every single soldier among several hundred, or several thousand — and moreover, having every one of them already lack much in the way of original combat strength or skill, so that none of them suffers from the gap between their old self and their newly-strengthened self. And on top of that, having no need at all to muster as many Yang magic users as there are soldiers to enhance. If such a dreamlike, miraculous set of conditions were ever to align — wouldn't it then become achievable?

That is the very kind of pipe dream Vincent Vollachia would laugh off and dismiss without a second thought. A cluster of coincidences said to be possible only under this wartime condition, in this exact situation, in this exact circumstance.

A miracle that could only be brought about by those who recognized Natsuki Subaru — no, "Natsuki Schwarz" — as their center, who were, from the heart, regarded by him as comrades, and who, from the heart, believed him to be their comrade in turn.

—The Yang-attribute enhancement placed upon Subaru individually, shared through the power of "Cor Leonis" with every comrade acting alongside him.

Misusing the power of the "Small King," originally meant for sharing wounds and burdens among allies, to instead let power that a king alone could never wield be used together with the comrades who support that king.

And so the Pleiades Battalion—

"—I love all of you!!"

The thousands of "non-combatants" marching alongside Natsuki Subaru are transformed into a peerless army, and they begin to utterly overrun this battlefield of the Siege of the Imperial Capital.
