Arc 9 Chapter 47: "Voice"

—The moment he opened his eyes, he had no idea whatsoever who he even was.

'——'

Where here was, when now was, who he himself was, what was happening — none of it made any sense at all. He couldn't see. He couldn't hear. He couldn't move his hands or his feet. His feet weren't touching the ground. He couldn't even breathe properly, and it was unbearably confining — and yet, he could feel a "Voice."

'——'

His sight, his smell, his touch, his taste, and above all his hearing were all shut off — and yet, a "Voice" sounded. Bathed helplessly in countless, innumerable "Voices" with no way to defend himself, he was like a powerless infant thrown into a cradle in hell.

'——'

A storm of "Voices" kept pouring down on him, jumbled and random. Just as a defenseless infant would be beaten by rain, he too nearly drowned in the "Voices." He thought he might as well just let himself drown, right then and there—

'—Young master! Young master, are you safe!? I'll save you now!'

—. ——. A loud "Voice" was heard. Among the countless, innumerable "Voices," one that he could clearly feel was directed at him. He could do nothing. And yet he understood — the strong "Voice" that was aimed at him. So—

'—Otto-nii, clench your teeth.'

That "Voice" reached him, a sense of floating came over him, and then his eardrums were shaken by a tremendously violent sound — a sound that wasn't a "Voice" — and once his tightly closed eyes regained the ability to take in light, he finally came face to face with the "Voice" directed at him, and the ones to whom it belonged.

"'Sorry it took so damn long ta get here. It's all 'cause I'm weak, every bit of it.'"

"'What are you saying, Garfiel-san! If you and everyone else hadn't come, the young master would still be hanging there right now... Ah, young master, young master! Can you tell it's me!?'"

What was there was a blond-haired boy and a giant earth dragon with pale blue dragon-hide. The "Voice" he felt and the voice he heard overlapped, but there was no mistake. The earth dragon pressed close to him as he sat on the ground, and the boy gripped his shoulder firmly.

"'Right now ya don't understand a damn thing, do ya? But it's fine. We're gonna get it all back for ya, all of us together. What I can't do alone, I'll borrow everybody's hands to make up for...!'"

"'——'"

"'So, Otto-nii, you just stay right here...'"

He thinks he meant to say rest easy. Apparently, the boy in front of him knew he was empty, and was trying to do something about it. He probably knew why he was empty, too. He wanted to know that. Wanted to hear it. That urge was there, of course. But it was there, and yet—

"'—Is there anything I can do?'"

He didn't know whether the "Voice" that slipped from his mouth had come out as a proper voice. But he could tell that both the boy and the earth dragon widened their eyes, so he supposed it must have gotten through. Honestly, he understood nothing. He didn't understand, but there was one thing he could say — he was grateful to have been pulled out of that hellish cradle. More than anything—

"'—When people come at you with voices this earnest, you can't help wanting to answer them, can you?'"

"'Ah, well, that's...'"

At his words, the boy and the earth dragon, both wide-eyed, spoke together in one breath and "Voice."

"'—That's just like the young master.'" "'That's just like Otto-nii.'"

At that "Voice," thinking of who he'd been before he became empty, a laugh spilled out of him. —If there was someone out there who had done this to him, "well played," he thought.

"—Teiyaa!!"

A high, silvery-bell voice rang out as she landed a kick whose power stood in stark contrast to its lightness. Rem watched the perfect hit land, gripping the shaft of her Morningstar tight.

It was the result of using ice footholds made with magic both practically and as a diversion, drawing the enemy's attention upward while she devoted herself entirely to supporting Emilia. Of course, if she could settle things with the beloved iron ball Subaru had kept polishing for her, she'd have liked to do just that — but she mustn't get too greedy. Even Subaru, back in the Empire, had properly taken things step by step with Rem. If Subaru had been impatient, pushing himself on her with a "me first, me first"—

"At that point, I might have broken not just his fingers, but his arms and legs too."

Now that her memories had returned, just imagining herself doing something so violent to Subaru was enough to make her faint — but she herself thought that, given her state of mind back then, she might well have done it. That was why procedure and steps mattered in everything. It was just like cooking. For all that she was fairly good at cooking, Rem's bad habit was skipping the steps whenever it wasn't cooking. And speaking of bad habits, there was one more—

"—Don't jump to conclusions!"

Convincing herself too soon that she'd landed the finishing blow and easing up — that came from a lack of imagination. It was Priscilla who had taught her that shutting down one's thinking was a sin — Priscilla, who had guided empty, memory-less Rem in how to set her heart. Even now that she had passed away, that teaching lived on. For as long as that teaching kept Rem alive, it always would. So—

"—! No way!?"

Even when Emilia cried out in astonishment like that, Rem's thoughts didn't stop. Below, Emilia had kicked up from directly beneath Al and the others — their blind spot — and that single strike from her ice-booted foot should have driven into Al with enough force to snap his spine. But Al had blocked it — by enclosing himself and Yae in a box of stone.

"'That's the thing Rom-sama was talking about—'"

Rem judged it to be the effect of "Time Regression." As a snap defensive judgment, covering his whole body like that was too perfect. Casting magic required both an image of the result and the construction of the spell formula. Lacking either, the magic wouldn't take shape, and the more complicated the attempt, the longer it took to form the image of the result. Al's had solidified far too readily for a surprise attack from a blind spot.

"—"

Even though she understood the principle in her head, Rem bit the inside of her cheek anew at that unfair trick. To begin with, it had already been proven that Al's Authority could even dodge Reinhard. If she kept attacking at this rate, wouldn't everything just get deflected? A negative thought began to creep in—

"—Rem!!"

That downward spiral of bad thoughts was shattered to pieces by Emilia's call. Across the stone box hanging in midair, Rem and Emilia's eyes met. Unlike Rem, who had nearly been overwhelmed by the threat of the Authority, the light in Emilia's violet eyes was strong. It gave her courage.

"—"

Strength flared back into her eyes, and the activation of her horn from her Oni transformation sent vitality surging through her whole body. They kept plunging steadily through the sky — thirty seconds had already passed since the fight began. She could measure it precisely thanks to the marks carved into the long stone cliff wall visible far behind them. Agzadd Canyon, a massive rift in the earth located in the Kararagi City-States, had a five-thousand-meter drop from the surface to the deepest point of the ravine, and the fall took roughly a hundred seconds. The marks pre-carved into the cliff wall were spaced ten seconds apart, and they had just passed the third one — seventy seconds left until they reached the bottom.

"Not yet — !!" "—This is where it starts!!"

Emilia and Rem's voices overlapped, and with the courage she'd been given and the strength welling up inside her, Rem resumed the attack. Rem arched her body back sharply, while Emilia twisted hers and conjured ice hammers in both hands. In an instant, the Morningstar shot out with the force of a cannon shell, and the ice hammers wrapped in a raging sidelong gale were loosed at the same moment — together they smashed furiously into the stone box holding Al and the others. Amid the rock fragments scattering with a thunderous roar, the enemy split apart above and below and hurtled fiercely at Rem and Emilia. Al went for Emilia, while toward Rem came the red-haired Shinobi—

"—No, you too."

"Yes, I'm Yae, Al-sama's all-purpose, ever-so-useful maid."

"That title belongs to Rem, for her Subaru-kun!"

Cutting off the introduction of the red-haired maid — no, Shinobi — who called herself Yae, Rem pulled her iron ball back with her right hand while swinging her powerful left arm at the incoming Yae. But Yae braked unnaturally in midair and dodged it. Looking closer, she'd thrown a thread behind her to link herself to Al, using its exquisite tension and slack to control her own movement, while a glimmer thrown from her other hand caught at Rem's neck.

"—Huma."

Feeling the threat closing in on her skin, Rem guarded her vital point with an ice collar conjured by pure reflex. The instant the thread caught on the collar, she pulled her head free, evading having her neck cut. Having saved her own life through a split-second decision, Rem now aimed her iron ball, hauled back up, at the back of Yae, who raised an eyebrow slightly. But Yae dodged the iron ball coming from behind with a precision as though she had eyes in the back of her head, and as they passed each other, she stuck her tongue out at Rem's surprise when the ball returned to her hand, saying,

"It's reflected in your eyes, you know?"

At that provocation, Rem nearly flared up for an instant, but received a scolding from the Katya in her heart. Nodding back at the imagined Katya yowling like a cat, "Stupid, stupid, that's exactly what she wants!", Rem, while giving credit to an enemy skilled enough to read the scene reflected in her opponent's own eyes, said—

"In that case, please, look closely!"

So saying, she wrapped the iron ball of her pulled-back Morningstar in ice, wrapped it, wrapped it again, doubling and redoubling and redoubling its mass, and brought it down as a ten-meter-class block of ice.

"Guess my provoking worked a little too well!?"

With a roar, the block of ice bore down, killing the wind as it came, and Yae, her voice cracking, swung both hands violently, carving the morningstar-ice-block again and again and again, splitting, splitting, splitting its mass to kill its momentum. And finally, once she'd stripped away the ice to leave nothing but the bare, naked iron ball, she caught it in front of her chest with a mesh of stretched thread, scattering the impact, and was sent flying down with force. At the place she went falling toward—

"This time! Unlike in the royal capital! I'm not letting you go!"

"Normally! If you pull! Something like that! I'd have won and run for it!"

Emilia and Al collided, hurling words and weapons at each other in equal measure. Around each of them floated ice and stone weapons, made and left to hover freely, and in the instant, they swung whatever weapon was nearest without distinguishing whose it was, continuing to clash blade against blade.

"Yah! Teriya! Ei! Uya! Toriya! Tei-tei-tei-tei-tei-tei!"

Contrary to her cute little battle cries, Emilia's offense was relentless, leaving no room to breathe. But Al, too, kept parrying the swords, spears, and axes raining down on him — with an axe, with a spear, with a sword — and kept holding on. Against Emilia's acrobatic, almost circus-trick style of attack, Al matched his weapon along the shortest possible path to block it. That, too, was probably a result of "Time Regression" — getting hit once, rewinding to before the hit, and placing his weapon at the spot where it had landed. Over and over.

"—Al-sama!"

Into that seesawing exchange of attacks, Yae, having been struck down by Rem, caught up. Damn it — a two-against-one situation had been created for a moment, and Rem also tried to build a foothold to accelerate her own fall — no—

"Emilia-sama!"

In a split-second decision, rather than catching up herself, Rem adjusted the angle of the iron ball she'd already swung down. Even after having its impact scattered by Yae, the iron ball, still falling at the same speed, leapt toward Emilia in her moment of crisis as though the spirit Subaru had poured into polishing it now resided within it. And before Al and Yae's simultaneous attack could reach her, it let Emilia catch hold of its chain.

"Nng— yaaaaah!!"

The foothold she'd just made — rather than kicking off it, Rem used it to brace herself, reeling in Emilia, who'd grabbed the chain, like a single catch on a fishing line — hauling her up as if splitting the gap between Al and Yae just as they were about to converge. The two pairs were divided into upper and lower sky, and the two allies reunited.

"Thank you! She's, like, so tough!"

"You're welcome! I agree completely!"

Taking Emilia's hand now that they'd risen to the same position, Rem marveled at the enemy's fighting strength. Originally, Rem and Emilia were only supposed to be handling Al, but Yae joining in made things far too troublesome. It wasn't just that their forces were being split — Yae's own skill was no small thing in itself. And what's more, her ability, combined with Al's presence, wasn't simple addition — it was multiplication.

"Yes, almost like me and that person..."

"Huh? Did you just talk about Subaru?"

"My apologies, that'll have to wait until after this is over!"

Answering Emilia's wide-eyed look like that, Rem confirmed that they'd passed one more mark on the cliff wall — sixty seconds until they reached the bottom — and gritted her teeth hard. Her earlier thought was correct. Yae was tough, and tougher still alongside Al. To do something about that—

"I'm going to split them apart. Emilia-sama!"

"Right! Let's do it together!"

Still holding hands, Emilia pointed her free hand and Rem her weapon-gripping hand, each aimed down at the pair below them,

"Al—" "—Huma!!"

Two kinds of Huma mixed together, and amid the groaning creak of the air, ice manifested in midair — of course, simply hurling chunks of it would just repeat their earlier failures. So, to avoid that, they'd devised not how the ice was produced, but where it appeared.

"—!" "Tch."

Blocked by the spreading ice, the click of a tongue inside a helmet went unheard. But judging only by the result, one could be certain a tongue had been clicked. The mass of ice Rem and Emilia had produced — a thin, flat sheet of ice — wedged itself between Al and Yae, who'd been positioned one above the other, blocking their reunion. The ice sheet, while keeping its strength, had been spread to its limit into a disc thirty meters across; diving beneath it to reunite while still falling was next to impossible, and being made flat, it also refused to be cut through by thread.

"Al-sa—"

"—Yae, hold out for a while."

Facing each other across the ice sheet, Al below the ice gave that order to Yae above it. Rem could tell, plain as anything, what feelings Yae reached upon hearing it. Just as Rem fought this battle for Subaru's sake, Yae was the same—

"When someone precious to you puts their faith in you, it makes you want to try."

"You're wrong."

Curtly rebuffing Rem's assumption, Yae folded her knees in a spin, turning her body and baring her bare feet. The ice sheet, owing to its sheer size, caught the wind's resistance and its fall slowed drastically. Yae landed on the ice, and Rem and Emilia touched down as well, flanking her front and back. Barefoot Yae had ten rings fitted onto the toes of her pale feet as well—

"—Magic really is a troublesome thing, isn't it~. I'm grateful for the old man's teachings back at the village."

"Thank you for the compliment. My teacher was a fine person."

"Same here. I was taught — 'Lia, just take it easy and do your own thing.'"

"That's the same approach my teacher took with me back at the village, so I suppose Emilia-sama has a natural gift for it too."

Shrugging with a somewhat wry smile, Yae looked back and forth between Rem and Emilia flanking her, then, prefacing with "Well, you see," said—

"The very fact that I'm here means your plan has already failed... Bol-sama will be rushing over shortly too, so why don't we skip the pointless back-and-forth and you just surrender?"

"Nuh-uh, no can do. I'll admit I was totally shocked that you were with him too, but..."

"Rem and the others aren't playing around either. And besides, if you're counting on the 'Divine Dragon,' he won't be coming — because our lord is keeping him occupied."

"...Your opponent is the 'Divine Dragon,' you know? Isn't that expecting a bit too much?"

"Then don't you have faith in Al?"

To Yae, who tilted her head, Emilia's question seemed to strike straight through her. Yae let out a small "Mm" as she held her breath, then exhaled it, twisting the pale fingers of her hands and feet as she took a fighting stance. Rem, too, drew her iron ball back into her hand, and Emilia gripped a pair of beautiful ice short swords in both hands. A glance beneath the ice sheet showed the distance to Al, falling without wind resistance, steadily widening. And that was because Al had left Yae to buy him time.

"Emilia-sama, Al-san is..."

"Yeah, I know. We really, really have to hurry."

Their intentions conveyed in just that brief exchange — at that, Rem felt an oddly out-of-place thought. That she and Emilia would be so in sync like this — that had once been unthinkable to her. — That kind of thought. Setting Rem's inner thoughts aside, the three of them atop the ice quietly stoked their fighting spirit—

"—Yae Tenzen, the 'Crimson Sakura.'"

"—Emilia, the 'Witch of Ice.'" "—Rem, 'the Hero's Attendant.'"

With their introductions given, Rem and Emilia kicked off the ice at the same moment and closed on Yae. From the hands of Yae, crouched low with bent knees, twenty strands of thread were loosed dancing outward, and batting them aside with her chain and magic, Rem challenged the "Crimson Sakura" standing in her way, so she could reach Al beneath the ice.

"Yaaaah!!"

Rem, roaring as she charged, wasn't putting on a brave face or bluffing — she believed, completely. That Ram and the others were taking on the "Gluttony" pair, and that Roswaal and the others were handling the greatest threat, the "Divine Dragon" — that each of them would properly fulfill their roles.

For that reason, now, she would strike down Yae before her and reach Al. Another mark passed — fifty seconds left until the bottom of Agzadd Canyon—

—That beautiful, fierce clash atop the ice, Al watched steadily as he fell beneath it, and—

"I don't think you're gonna lose... but don't forget, Olta. There's something you've gotta do for me."

Those words, murmured only within his own mouth, never once reached the ice above.

—Aldebaran, betrayer of the sun, and his accomplices.

The battle against the wicked followers who might well pull the trigger on the world's collapse had moved into its next stage — both the fight against "Gluttony" in the mountains and the fight against Aldebaran in Agzadd Canyon. Naturally, the same held true for the third battle, the one expected to be the harshest of all.

Through the Authority of the newly-born "Witch of Melancholy" in this world, the battlefield had been shifted to a stretch of rocky land where no thought needed be spared for collateral damage, and there, the usurper who commanded at will the mighty Dragonhusk of Volcanica — the "Divine Dragon" known as the kingdom's guardian — faced off against the rightful Dragonkin. In this clash between two who each held overwhelming power hidden within them, the rocky wasteland — which had never held anything but rock and sand to begin with — grew ever more devastated, driving away what little human presence it might once have drawn. That was, precisely, the ravages of war that had been expected from a battle against the "Divine Dragon." However—

"—Ngh."

His vision blurred, his consciousness hazy, the ringing in his ears passed beyond noise into silence. His limbs, unable to take any strength, left him unsure whether all four of them were even properly still attached to his torso. Every thin, shallow breath brought a pain like being struck by lightning from the crown of his head to the tips of his toes — proof, probably, that his broken bones were tearing up the inside of his body here and there. Having thought that far, he realized he was lying face-down on the hard, rocky ground.

"—!"

Once he understood his own condition, other things began to become clear too. The violent tremors of the earth, the sensation of the sky bursting apart, the faint change in the smell of the air — all were evidence of a drastic shift in the environment, and not much time could have passed since he'd collapsed to the ground. A minute? Thirty seconds?

"Five seconds, Master. Reporting."

The answer to the question he'd been holding was conveyed to him with precision, but he didn't even have the strength left to move his lips into a wry smile. The other party hadn't expected a reply either — no, the situation simply wasn't one in which a reply could be expected. The tremors of the earth, the bursting of the sky, the violent upheaval of the air — the point of origin for all of it was that other party, and their opponent. The battle between the "Dragon" and the Dragonkin was reshaping the world violently, by brute force, like a natural catastrophe. — At the side of that battle, he lay collapsed, not merely half-dead but at a ratio closer to ninety percent dead, ten percent alive.

"—"

Now, he couldn't even hear voices properly anymore. —Roswaal L. Mathers's life hung by a single flickering flame in the wind.
