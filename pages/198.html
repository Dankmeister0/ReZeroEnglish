Arc 4 Chapter 32: "1/4"

Tilting his steaming cup, Subaru lent an ear to Frederica's words as well.

"The Demi-Human War — to begin with, do you know what sort of conflict it actually was, Subaru-sama?"

"Like I said before, I've never dug into the details. But... it's not like I can't make some guesses from the name and the historical background."

"Oh, how interesting. Would you care to share your thoughts?"

Frederica hid her mouth and smiled at Subaru's response to her question. Hiding her fanged mouth when she laughed was, for her, a habit so ingrained it had become second nature by now. He often saw her doing it. That she smiled so much, and yet seemed unwilling to show that smile to others — that was his impression of it.

Closing his eyes and scratching his cheek with a finger, Subaru said, "Right, well," by way of preamble, then continued.

"I don't even know how long ago that war happened, but I can guess that whatever set it off wasn't unrelated to the 'Witch of Envy.' I know Emilia gets treated like something toxic at the royal castle, and that half-elves are hated by all kinds of people."

The 'Witch of Envy' — a symbol of absolute evil so notorious she appeared even in picture books, said to be known by everyone. Just the resemblance of being a silver-haired half-elf was enough to earn Emilia such unjust treatment. Given that, Subaru could easily imagine ripples spreading outward from it — disputes breaking out over the flimsiest of pretexts.

"A half-elf is basically a child born between a human and an elf, right? If a trend of loathing half-elves took root... it wouldn't be strange for a broader prejudice to emerge too — treating any child born between a human and another race as some kind of heresy."

"...Please, go on."

"This is just speculation, but a movement to reject half-elves would connect to a movement to reject all halfbreeds. And if you take it to an even more extreme conclusion, some people might start thinking the demi-humans themselves — the root cause that could produce a halfbreed in the first place — were something to be feared."

As far as Subaru knew, humans were still the most numerous race in this world. He'd confirmed the existence of elves, and beastfolk like the three beastkin siblings, but from his few days' impression of the royal capital, the absolute number of demi-humans different from humans seemed smaller than that of humans.

And being the majority could, all by itself, become a kind of righteousness.

"I don't think everyone would drift toward that kind of thinking, but the loudest voices tend to stand out no matter where you go, right? And so, hatred of demi-humans... or really, fear of demi-humans, maybe. All that resentment overflowing, and in the middle of all the commotion—"

"Conflict erupted between humans and demi-humans. The smoldering spark that had been building eventually caught, spread, and stretched its reach across all of Lugunica." Frederica took up where Subaru's words left off, her voice heavy with gloom.

Subaru watched her, one eye closed, head bowed slightly. Then Frederica nodded once and raised her face toward him.

"That was a nearly irrefutable line of reasoning — there's hardly anything I need to add. ...Have you truly never heard the details before?"

"Nope. If that's mostly right, then it's a victory for my imagination. Or maybe my reading experience — conflicts between different races are pretty common in light novels and the like."

Even so, in fact, Subaru had of course never been conscious of that kind of issue for himself. His original world had had so-called racial discrimination too, but to Subaru it had always felt like a distant matter — one he regarded with about as much immediacy as the problems of some other world entirely. He could say he'd simply thought coolly, I am I, others are others, and that would be true enough, but in reality it was probably more accurate to say he'd simply looked away from it.

"Still, even if I can imagine how the trouble started, there's no way I could rack my brain enough to figure out how it got resolved. But since you're talking about it in the past tense, at least the Demi-Human War itself is settled, right?"

"Yes, more or less. Even so, the scars the war left run deep, and to this day the seeds of prejudice against children born between demi-humans and humans remain deeply rooted."

Perhaps because she herself had been born into circumstances that made her a target of that very prejudice, Frederica's words carried a weight that couldn't be gained just from hearing the story secondhand. Subaru hesitated over whether he should even ask her to continue, but as if she'd read his intent, Frederica let out a breath.

"My apologies for making you worry over my feelings. Let's continue the story, shall we."

"I want to tell you not to push yourself, but if this connects directly to what I actually want to know, I can't really say that. So... please push yourself."

"My, my. Subaru-sama, you really are skilled at motivating people."

Interpreting Subaru's self-serving remark charitably, Frederica tilted her own cup to wet her tongue, then continued.

"The Demi-Human War began roughly fifty years ago. It went on for nearly ten years from there... and the records say it ended forty years ago."

"Ten years... that's a long time. Back where I'm from, there was, like, a Hundred Years' War and a Thirty Years' War in history too."

He wasn't well-versed in historical fiction or the like, so his knowledge of those was about as deep as glimpsing the names in a textbook. Still, if a conflict earned a name like that, it must have lasted at least that long. Thirty years, a hundred years, fighting on out of hatred for someone the whole time — just thinking about it was enough to send a chill down his spine. And here Subaru was, exhausted after barely two months in another world.

"And I'm already this worn out — no way I could keep whacking away at someone for decades."

"In any case, it was a war that began between a single demi-human settlement and the human populace. By rights, it should have been contained to that one incident. But... because of an event that occurred afterward, the war's heat flared up all at once. Gruesome conflicts, blood answering blood, broke out all across the land."

"That later event — what was it?"

"Immediately after the initial clash, the King of Lugunica at the time, taking the matter seriously, sent a close aide as an emissary to negotiate peace. On the demi-human side as well, chieftains from several races gathered to receive the emissary, and the matter was meant to be settled through negotiation... but..."

Frederica trailed off, and Subaru silently tilted his head to prompt her onward. At the gesture, she closed her eyes.

"Those who attended the parley — the emissary from the royal castle, and the chieftains, all of them together — were slaughtered right there on the spot."

"Slaughtered...? By whom? For what?"

"To this day, the culprit remains unknown. But at the time, both the human side and the demi-human side alike concluded, 'It was the other side's scheme.' As a result, a small spark became a great blaze, one that couldn't easily be stamped out — and so it went on for ten years."

"What were they doing? Couldn't they have just... talked it through properly? Or is that just wishful thinking?"

Considering the feelings of the people at the time, that would be far too much a god's-eye view of things to expect. The king's close aide, dispatched from the royal castle — considering his honor, murdered as he was at the negotiating table, backing down without identifying a culprit would have been a matter of pride. On the other hand, the demi-humans, too, had lost their chieftains, all of them, in one stroke. It was a terrible thing to weigh lives by number, but by pure comparison, their losses were the greater.

And on top of that, between the two sides lay the foundation of racial entanglement born from the existence of the 'Witch of Envy.' Repairing the relationship would have been difficult from the start, and while both sides stood frozen over it, the response to the next problem that arose would have been delayed — falling further and further behind, inviting tragedy. It wasn't hard to imagine.

"The Demi-Human War ultimately ended with the demi-humans' surrender — in a manner of speaking. Though that doesn't mean the demi-humans admitted to complicity in the parley incident; rather, it's more that they were simply the first to acknowledge the pointlessness of continuing to fight."

"Personally, I think whoever backs down first from a drawn-out fight is the smart one. Besides, this was basically a civil war, right? The country wouldn't have gained anything from it."

"That's exactly right. In truth, Lugunica's national strength fell sharply while it was preoccupied with the Demi-Human War. It was fortunate that the surrounding nations were unsettled in their own affairs at the time — otherwise, an exhausted Lugunica might well have been supplanted by another country."

A stroke of luck amid misfortune, one might say — the three other nations besides Lugunica had, at the time, been fully occupied with their own troubles, and so Lugunica managed to avoid being stabbed in the back. A crisis every bit as dire as that era's now bore down on the country once again, this very moment.

"Still, deciding to end a war that had dragged on that long — that's impressive too. Must've taken real courage, and thinking about the backlash from the hardliners, that's not an easy thing to pull off."

"...There was someone on the human side so extraordinary that even the hardliners' resolve broke before them. It's said that every demi-human race bowed their heads before the brilliance of the Sword Saint of that era, Theresia van Astrea-sama's swordsmanship... is something the matter?"

"Nah, I just wasn't expecting a name I actually recognize. Small world."

The name of Wilhelm's wife, which he'd heard a few times now, had indeed been Theresia. The Sword Saint of that era — meaning, what, two generations before Reinhard? Hearing that the woman entrusted with that title had single-handedly achieved enough to bring the curtain down on a war that had raged for ten years — well, it certainly explained the sheer absurdity of what a Sword Saint was capable of.

"Well, anyway, I get the general flow of the Demi-Human War now. And I can more or less guess at some of the problems that would've branched off from it."

"Your earlier speculation was nearly correct in every respect. You seem to be quicker-witted than I'd expected — I confess I misjudged you, and it surprises me."

"Choosing to take that as a compliment and moving on — the Demi-Human War ended, but the prejudice against demi-humans doesn't just vanish that easily. Of course, out where people can see, that kind of hostility probably doesn't show itself openly."

Even in the royal capital, along the main street lined with fruit shops, humans and demi-humans passed each other going about ordinary life. He had no idea how much hardship it had taken for that ordinary sight to become ordinary, but even as there existed places where it had become everyday life like that, there would inevitably be other places — ones the light never reached — where that everyday normalcy could never be built, no matter how much time passed.

"Someplace with a small population, an insular village that doesn't let outsiders in... feels like if someone with even a little bit of a problem showed up in a place like that, they'd catch the full brunt of it."

"My brother and I were, I'd say, in precisely that kind of environment."

Her brow furrowing with the pain of remembering the past, Frederica — for the first time speaking of her brother, Garfiel, so plainly — let her gaze drift somewhere distant.

"My brother and I are siblings by different fathers. That's why we go by different family names... I bear my father's family name. My brother bears our mother's."

"Family name — right, Frederica's is... Baumann?"

"Yes. And my brother should be going by Tinzel. Our mother was... how should I put it, a very poor planner. And, it seems, rather unlucky as well."

Frederica chose her words but couldn't quite finish choosing them. Seeing his blank incomprehension, she prefaced with, "It's embarrassing, but," and continued.

"My mother was about to be sold off to settle a debt when a band of demi-human bandits, targeting the slave trader, seized her instead... and that's where she met my father, apparently."

"Wait, hold on! I don't think I was mentally prepared to hear that!"

"But she was soon parted from that father too, by death, and while she was at a loss, carrying me as still an infant, she was captured by another group of demi-humans. And there, this time, she met Garfiel's father..."

"Wait, wait, my bad! I didn't think it'd turn out to be this heavy a story!"

"Which is why I'm trying to keep it brief, so it isn't too heavy. In any case, that's where Garfiel was born, but again, she couldn't stay together with my brother's father either, and the three of us — mother and two children — were at our wits' end, until House Mathers took us in."

Having laid bare her heavy past so lightly, Frederica let out a breath, a trace of nostalgia rising in her eyes. Then, stroking the armrest of the chair she sat in, she went on.

"At the time, the head of House Mathers was already my master — Roswaal-sama — who had inherited the position while still in his early teens. So to my brother and me, my master is, in the truest sense, our benefactor. It's an honor to be allowed to attend to him like this."

"So the two of you were placed in the 'Sanctuary,' and lived there... By the way, this might be a hard thing to ask, but — what happened to your mother?"

From what he'd just heard of their origins, their mother seemed to be a pure human. Which meant she should be someone who could enter and leave the 'Sanctuary' freely. And yet, he'd seen no sign of her presence — not in the 'Sanctuary,' and certainly not at the mansion. Subaru braced for the worst, but Frederica shook her head at him.

"You seem worried, but please, don't be. My mother, once she'd entrusted my brother and me to Roswaal-sama, left the mansion that very day and vanished without a trace. We never learned where she went afterward. I like to think — if nothing else — that she's well."

"——"

At the offhandedness with which Frederica said this, Subaru found himself at a loss for words, falling silent. To Subaru, the worst-case scenario he'd imagined had been that she'd died — but reality had betrayed the two of them even more cruelly than that.

Still, hearing these circumstances only deepened a question in him. Namely—

"But if that's how your mother left, why does Garfiel go by her family name? Frederica, you go by your father's, right?"

"There are no records left, nothing at all, so this is only my mother's memory passed on secondhand... and it's not the most reliable of things. But the reason my brother goes by our mother's name is... because that child never knew her, and because — for all his rough act — he has an unusually strong sense of attachment."

"A strong sense of attachment..."

Picturing Garfiel, Subaru put the boy's character into words within his own mind. Quick to act before thinking things through, foul-mouthed, with no manners to speak of — but he held to his principles, and it wasn't as though he couldn't be reasoned with. He rated his own intelligence low, but that didn't mean he wasn't thinking, or that his mind had simply shut down. The impression was of a delinquent dragging along the old-fashioned image of a good-hearted thug. If you weighed just the one point of holding to his principles, Subaru couldn't deny he seemed the type to run deep with loyalty and human feeling.

"Subaru-sama. Do you know how the barrier of the 'Sanctuary' selects its targets?"

Frederica's question came suddenly, tossed at Subaru as he sat lost in thought. Unable to grasp its meaning, Subaru's response came late. He looked back at her, blank-faced, and said, "Um," lacking confidence.

"Honestly, I don't get it. I mean, I know for sure the barrier exists, but I've never actually felt it myself. I figure it's something magical that checks whoever passes through, but..."

"The barrier probes the bloodline within the body of whatever passes through it. Human blood, and demi-human blood — it rejects whichever party can be clearly confirmed to carry both. That is the true nature of that barrier."

"...What are you trying to say?"

At this sudden explanation of how the barrier selected its targets, Subaru, unable to read the purpose behind this disclosure, lowered his voice as he tossed the question back at her. In response, she gave one small nod.

"Do you understand why I'm able to pass through the barrier, and be here, outside the 'Sanctuary'?"

"...No, honestly, I don't. Hearing the barrier's conditions just makes it more confusing. On the way back, I saw Ryuzu-san's condition worsen just from being near the barrier, so I know that thing's effect is the real deal — I saw it myself, going into the 'Sanctuary' too."

Right before that intense first meeting with Garfiel, Emilia had passed through the barrier and collapsed. Given how powerful it was, doubting its existence would be absurd—

"—Wait, why—"

At that moment, a bolt of lightning shot through Subaru's mind. It was from the return journey, bringing the evacuees along with Ryuzu. Passing through the forest, through the barrier, heading out of the 'Sanctuary,' and parting ways with Ryuzu, their guide — a sense of wrongness he'd felt then. This was the answer to that wrongness. Subaru had watched Ryuzu keep her distance from the barrier with obvious reluctance, and it had been nagging at him ever since.

"If the conditions are the same... then why the hell was that bastard Garfiel, who'd come right up near the barrier, so damn fine?"

Garfiel, who'd ambushed Subaru and the others as they crossed the barrier, putting on a display of hurling the Dragon Carriage, Patrasche and all. There was no doubt he hadn't been going all out at that point, but there was Emilia, who'd lost consciousness just from passing through the barrier, and Ryuzu, whose condition had nearly worsened just from getting close. Garfiel's behavior differed far too much from theirs.

—As though the barrier's effect simply didn't touch his body at all.

"Because of his atavistic traits, my brother might appear, at a glance, to carry demi-human blood strongly, but that isn't actually the case. — Just like me."

"If the strength of the blood is the condition the barrier uses to tell halfbreeds apart from everyone else... then if either side's blood is diluted enough, you'd slip past that condition?"

"My brother's father and mine are different men, but neither of them was a pure demi-human. Both were halfbreeds themselves, and so, mixed with our human mother's blood, what resulted was a half-formed existence carrying only a quarter of demi-human blood."

"A quarter... that's why you weren't caught by the barrier."

A barrier that rejects halfbreeds — so it wouldn't reject quarter-bloods. It was the kind of logical leap that sounded like something out of an Ikkyū-san riddle, but pieces of various suspicions Subaru had been harboring were telling him now that it was true. Ryuzu's real meaning, too — when she'd spoken vaguely of Frederica's escape from the 'Sanctuary' as an exception — made sense to him now, hearing this.

But that, in turn, stirred up another suspicion. Namely—

"Wait. Then — does that mean Garfiel can leave the 'Sanctuary' too? If he wanted to, regardless of whether the 'Trial' succeeds or fails, he could—"

If that were true, it would be a surprise, but a welcome one. If the premise that the barrier stood in the way of pulling Garfiel out of the 'Sanctuary' no longer held, then a path opened up to bring him out to where his strength was needed against Elsa's onslaught. He'd already all but given up on driving Elsa off this time, and had been thinking only of ways to get everyone remaining at the mansion out safely, but—

"If he can get outside, then that means..."

"It's true, my brother can leave the 'Sanctuary,' the same as I can. When I left the 'Sanctuary,' he even walked with me as far as the barrier, saying he'd come along. But..."

There, she cut herself off, and Frederica looked at Subaru, who seemed to think he'd found a ray of hope. The emotion pooled in her eyes ran so deep that Subaru felt his own excitement cool at once. And to that Subaru, she said—

"My brother stayed behind in the 'Sanctuary.' And I don't believe Garfiel will ever leave it, not unless the 'Sanctuary' itself is released. He has a strong sense of attachment — he's a kind child."

"Strong sense of attachment... don't tell me—"

Subaru's brows rose as the thought struck him. As if to confirm his surprise, Frederica dipped her chin and hid her mouth behind her sleeve.

"He isn't the kind of child who could leave the residents of the 'Sanctuary' behind — the ones who can't leave — and come out into the world himself. For better or worse, he's straightforward... a troublesome brother, mine."
