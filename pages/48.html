Arc 2 Chapter 24: "The Long-Awaited Morning"

With the Contract with Beatrice concluded, Subaru had gained just the smallest measure of peace. But in the fundamental sense—the sense in which he was cornered—nothing about the situation had improved in the slightest, and not even a sliver of a solution had come into view.

As ever, Subaru remained confined to his hikikomori existence, unable to leave the guest room, and Beatrice wasn't the type to stay glued to his side around the clock either. If anything, since he'd negotiated to have more of her protective attention allocated to the fourth night into the fifth day—that critical stretch of time—Beatrice, who would rather not be near Subaru at all if she could help it, hadn't come to his room even once since then.

In her place, the one who visited Subaru's bedside again and again was—

"Good, that's good. So Beatrice really did come to apologize. Well done, well done."

—Emilia, smiling faintly at the bedside as she nodded to herself. Her repeated visits weighed on Subaru's conscience even as they brought a single thread of light into what was otherwise a world of pure darkness—without exaggeration, she felt like a goddess to him.

Even after being so bitterly pushed away, Emilia still closed the distance to Subaru. Even when he'd offered his few, guilt-laden words of apology, she'd said—

"You were probably just on edge, weren't you? Everyone has moments like that, so it can't be helped. Though I'd be glad if you said something like that to Ram and Rem too."

—and let his rude remarks pass gently by, unchallenged. As for the small request she'd made after that, Subaru still hadn't given her a clear answer. If he judged the two of them, who knew the truth, to be simply untrustworthy, then his mouth would be sealed shut by their hands. And yet, even having tasted their excessive loyalty firsthand, the honest truth was that he couldn't bring himself to hate them completely. Though perhaps that was only a flimsy, papier-mâché sort of hope—born from the fact that only one of them was certainly involved, and the optimistic thought that maybe, just maybe, the other knew nothing at all.

Day by day, Subaru wore his spirit thin in the gap between hope and the despair that always outweighed it. Emilia watched him grow gaunt like this, staring intently, and then, glancing sidelong at the meal set beside the bed, she said,

"You still haven't eaten, have you."

"...Sorry."

Subaru was nothing but a troublesome guest. He wouldn't even show his face, and worse, the household staff's very first encounter with him had been met with abuse—and yet the mansion's servants, as a matter of course, never committed the disloyalty of skimping on his meals.

Even though the food set out was left almost untouched practically every time, even though it was plain he wasn't welcome, they never let their expressions change, never voiced a single complaint, and carried out their duty to the fullest. One of them was blunt to the point of rudeness, the other rude in the guise of politeness, and yet he knew—Subaru knew—that neither of them was the type to slack in her work. And yet still—

—What if there's poison in it.

That anxious thought crossed his mind every time he looked at the food. He didn't think either of them was the type to resort to something like that. If they meant to, there had been countless chances across all his previous loops. But their actions had always come directly at Subaru. So he was half-convinced that when they came to kill him this time too, it would be with a weapon gripped in hand.

The reality that someone whose good qualities he knew so well was, without question, trying to kill him—it was from the moment he accepted that reality that Subaru's despair had begun.

In that state of mind, his refusal to eat was purely because tension and anxiety left his insides trembling too much to hold anything down. He was sure the food was delicious. Time and time again, he'd tasted for himself just how good it was. He might even have once thought that someday he'd like to cook that well himself.

"If you don't eat even a little, it'll be bad for your body, you know? I know it might be hard, though."

"My stomach won't take it. ...Though if Emilia-tan fed it to me herself, going 'say ahh,' maybe I could manage."

Subaru aimed a worthless bit of banter at her worried face, cursing his own hopelessness. Toward someone who, from the bottom of her heart, was surely concerned for him, he could no longer even manage to fake his usual flippancy—and hated himself for it. And yet—

"All right then. Here, say ahh."

"—Huh?"

"I said, ahh."

Emilia set the tray of food on her lap, spoon in hand, and looked at Subaru. The spoon, laden with soup still faintly warm, was aimed at his mouth and slowly drawing closer.

Not understanding her intent, Subaru waved his hands frantically.

"No, no-no-no, hold on a second, Emilia-tan. What are you doing?"

"What do you mean, what? Didn't you just say you could eat if I did this? So, eat. I'll do the 'ahh' for you."

"Um, isn't there kind of an aesthetic where things like this end up not actually happening, or, or even if it does happen, it's the sort of thing where a girl blushes bright red and can only manage it once, and that's the part that really gets you?"

"There's nothing embarrassing about feeding a little food to a boy saying childish things like that. Come on, quit talking nonsense."

Against a flustered, stammering Subaru, Emilia forcibly pushed through with her "ahh." In the end, swept along by her momentum, Subaru felt his ears heat with humiliation and said,

"A-ahh."

"There, gulp it down. Next, next. Here, here, here, here, here."

"That's too fast!? There's zero lingering afterglow for my first ever 'ahh'!?"

Her motions were mechanical and efficient, as if competing in some speed-eating contest. Subaru couldn't help taking in each spoonful offered one after another, and in the end, caught completely in Emilia's snare, he arrived at the point of finishing the entire meal by her hand. Having emptied the tray beautifully, a satisfied Emilia deepened her smile and said "mm,"

"There. What do you say when you're done eating?"

"Much obliged, chief."

"That's bad manners. Properly, one more time."

"Thank you for the meal."

"There, you're quite welcome."

Subaru bowed his head deeply, and Emilia bowed politely back. As for Subaru, having crammed food suddenly into a stomach that had gone without for more than two days, a strange fullness now pressed on his insides. He tilted his head, thinking he hadn't recalled eating all that much, and Emilia said,

"Since you haven't eaten anything proper in a while, it's so your stomach doesn't get too surprised. Ram suggested it, and Rem made it. They're such good girls."

Emilia's words, spoken as if boasting about her sisters, pierced sharply into Subaru's chest. The thoughtfulness that should truly have moved him to tears of joy now amounted, for the Subaru of the present, to nothing but a painful delusion—one that brought tears for an entirely different reason.

Kindness shown, familiarity displayed—both had something hidden behind them, something other than what they appeared to be. Yes—once you've been made to learn that lesson once.

"Well then, now that I've fed you, I should get going—staying too long will just tire you out."

"You could always sleep next to me instead, you know?"

"Good to see you're getting some energy back. But I have all sorts of things I need to do too. I snuck out to come here, so I shouldn't stay too long."

Emilia closed one eye, a playful gesture of apology. Subaru suddenly recalled her usual schedule at this hour, and it struck him just how fortunate he was.

At this hour, she should have been buried in study in her own room, without fail. Bearing the weight of a kingdom, striving with all her might toward the future—she should have been piling up effort after desperate effort.

She had nothing but her own body, couldn't advance toward any future, was stuck endlessly repeating the present—she shouldn't have had a single second to spare for a loser dog of fate like him.

Suddenly, something chirped inside Subaru's heart—asking whether it was really all right to just keep losing to fate. As if pushed from behind by that stubborn refusal to lose, so like his own voice, he lifted his face.

"Hey, Puck."

"...I kept quiet because I figured my loveliness wouldn't suit the mood, but what's up?"

The silver hair, cradling the tray, was on her way out of the room. Subaru called out to her back by another name, and watched as a small cat emerged, parting the long silver strands. Meeting those black eyes squarely, Subaru pointed at him.

"Emilia-tan's probably not gonna run into anything, but... watch over her properly, thoroughly, completely. Even if it means using that tiny body of yours as a shield."

"If my size is enough to protect against it, that's a pretty small crisis."

"Shut up. Just—please. This time, I've got my hands more than full just taking care of myself."

He didn't think danger would reach Emilia, most likely. At the very least, Roswaal had no reason to harm her, and if Roswaal had none, then the twins would have no reason born of their own either. The only one whose life was truly in danger in this mansion, at this point, was Subaru, plain and simple.

"Just worrying about your own life, huh. Simple enough. ...No, wait. Now that I really think about it, it's starting to feel like I didn't do all that much last time either."

The likely summary of last time was that some handsome guy had snatched up everything while Subaru scraped by on momentum alone. Both last time and this time—wasn't Subaru just being dragged along the whole time?

"Sorry to interrupt while you're having such a nice little breakdown, but I'll take your warning seriously. ...Yeah, it doesn't seem like a lie or malice."

Puck nodded, his voice dropping just slightly. What he was thinking behind those pitch-black eyes, into which no light seemed to penetrate, Subaru couldn't tell—their unreadable blankness felt equal parts eerie and reassuring, and he ducked his chin in acknowledgment. Left out despite being the one actually concerned, Emilia pouted at their exchange.

"What are you two agreeing on all by yourselves? Are you thinking of something bad?"

"No way, no way—it's just, you're a girl, so watch out for all kinds of things. Watch out for cars, watch out for men!"

"Cars... you mean like carriages or dragon carriages? And what do you mean, watch out for men?"

"Not every guy out there is as devoted and sincere as me, so Emilia-tan, listen carefully to what your father here says and learn to tell who you can trust."

At the return of Subaru's usual tone, Puck nodded along.

"That's right, Lia. From now on, don't think of my opinions as nagging—listen seriously. First off, black-haired guys with bad eyes are no good. If you bring one of those home, I'm not letting him in the house."

"Brutus, not you again!!"

Subaru shouted in outrage at Puck, who'd casually stabbed him in the back he'd trusted. One boy and one cat tilted their heads at the unfamiliar name. Subaru cleared his throat to regain his composure.

"Anyway, that's all. Be extra careful at night especially. Emilia-tan, you're cute enough that you're probably fighting off the fear of a midnight visitor every night."

"I hear the floor my room's on is set up so that a scary guardian gets alerted if someone barges in without permission."

"Yikes. Good thing I have no stamina and pass out cold after work—can't even properly attempt a midnight visit, me."

"I'll leave the lock unlocked, so if you've got the courage, maybe you should try it sometime."

With a small wave, Emilia left it at that and exited the room. As she went, the small cat on her shoulder waved goodbye in kind. At least he'd been able to sound the warning to her and her guardian. This much should be enough—the two of them would probably be fine.

"Ah, crap..."

The moment relief slipped into his heart, drowsiness came crashing down over Subaru's consciousness all at once. The sleepiness he'd been fending off with pain seized the opportunity to dominate his mind, stripping every last shred of resistance from him. The fact that his hunger had unexpectedly been satisfied may have been one distant cause robbing him of the will to fight it off.

I can't afford to fall asleep, he thought, as if biting down on his own lip—and Subaru's consciousness sank, as though drawn down into a doze.

Suspended between dream and waking, Subaru's consciousness drifted like a cloud.

He'd once heard somewhere that the act of dreaming during sleep is a byproduct of the brain sorting through information.

If so, then the fact that scenes disturbing enough to rob Subaru of restful sleep kept unfolding even now, while he slept, made a certain grim sense—it fit the logic of a mind sorting through vivid memories.

The sensation of his upper body being torn away, the instant his skull was crushed, the pain of his legs being blown off, the emptiness of his throat being pierced through, the despair of his belly being ripped open—all of it tormented Subaru, standing at the edge of dreams, even beyond the reach of reality.

Groaning, moaning in his sleep, writhing as his whole body grew slick with sweat, tears spilling from the corners of his eyes, Subaru kept fighting the emotions welling up from within himself.

It hurts. It's painful. It's sad. It hurts. I don't want this anymore. I want it to end.

Complaints, weakness, spilled out endlessly, and with each one his Soul was shaved away a little more. Shaved and shaved, until in the end, worn down completely, surely nothing at all would remain. His heart and body were exhausted enough that it truly felt that way.

Then, suddenly, the tension left Subaru's body. The chill that had been shaking him to his core, the dread, was swept away as if all at once.

The reason was a hand. Someone was holding Subaru's hand. There in the bed, adrift in the borderland of unconsciousness, someone was reaching out from the side of reality, trying to keep hold of him.

It was a warm touch. A gentle sensation. He could feel that he was being cherished. He felt as if he'd been saved. A calm breeze blew into his ravaged heart.

His pained sleeping face eased into peace, and his breathing, forgetting its suffering, returned to something quiet.

Who had it been? What had it been? Had it been real? Or had this too been a convenient dream?

Faintly, in both his right hand and his left, he could still feel the warmth lingering in his palms—

"—Are you just going to lie there snoring forever, I suppose?"

"Buh, wha, gurgh!"

Subaru's pained cry as he was violently kicked off the bed, forcibly roused from sleep. Jolted awake by the hard floor, Subaru grabbed the bed and hauled himself up, and there before him, standing on the far side of the bed, was Beatrice.

She crossed her arms without hiding her displeasure in the slightest, and gave a haughty sniff.

"The appointed hour has come, so I've come, however reluctantly, I suppose."

"There's such a thing as backtalk you don't need to accompany with a kick, you know. This is exactly that kind."

Subaru flung his upper body onto the bed and said this in an exhausted tone. Then, suddenly realizing he'd fallen asleep, a chill ran through him. After all that effort to keep himself conscious, never letting his guard down—

"Falling asleep on the crucial fourth day, seriously, am I just a reckless idiot?"

"Muttering to yourself is annoying, I suppose. Come, hurry up and let's go."

Ignoring Subaru, who lamented with his face in his hands, Beatrice beckoned him along and headed for the door. Subaru scrambled after her, alarmed.

"Go, where... where are we going?"

"You expect me to spend the whole night in a room reeking of you like this? I'll pass, I suppose. Betty's place is inside the Forbidden Archive—that's non-negotiable, I suppose."

Beatrice snapped her fingers lightly and touched the door. There was no visible change, but presumably that alone was enough to complete the preparations for a "Door Crossing."

It was an unexpected offer from Beatrice, but Subaru silently thanked his luck—this was a windfall. Beatrice's "Door Crossing" was a nasty trick for any attacker, since it made it impossible to pin down the location for an ambush. For some reason it didn't work on Subaru, but he'd already confirmed Rem had no effective means of "Door Breaking."

"You actually think things through more than I expected, huh."

"If we don't hurry, the tea will get cold, so let's go back quickly, I suppose."

To Subaru's impressed remark, Beatrice gave an indifferent reply. It seemed his assumption that she'd been considerate of him had been wishful thinking after all. Grimacing at having wasted a compliment, Subaru glanced down at his own hands.

There was nothing particularly different about them, but for some reason, they nagged at him strangely. Come to think of it—while he'd been sleeping, someone had, his hand—

"Beatrice. This is a long shot, but you didn't happen to hold my hand while I was asleep, did you?"

"That is indeed a long shot, I suppose. Even if my brother himself begged me to, I'd have to respectfully decline, I suppose."

"You didn't have to go that far. ...But hey, even so, when I die, I'm dying right alongside you!"

"Absolute, unconditional no, I suppose."

Subaru raised a thumb, showing a weak flash of teeth. It was his usual pose, but his spirits still hadn't fully recovered.

Leaving a sigh of exasperation for Subaru, Beatrice passed through the door. Hastily straightening his clothes here and there, Subaru too crossed the door into the Forbidden Archive.

As ever, Subaru thought, it was a room he could never quite decide was tranquil or cluttered. The room, lined endlessly with bookshelves crammed full of books, never changed its appearance no matter how many loops he visited it.

Beatrice, entering first, went as always to her usual spot beside the stepladder. She sat down on it—directly across from the door, that stepladder shirking its duty by being used as a chair instead.

"Do try to pass the time as you see fit, I suppose. Here, whatever happens... nothing should happen, but I'll honor the Contract regardless, I suppose."

"Pass the time, easy for you to say..."

Tension, and the anxiety that came with it. He'd been trying to steady himself since earlier, but even so, as the critical hour drew near, he couldn't stay calm. Subaru scanned the room roughly, comparing the many books lined on the shelves.

"Oh, right. You got any books written entirely in the I-script?"

"...There are some, I suppose. Don't tell me you can't read any of the other scripts?"

"Embarrassing to admit, but yeah. Though I did try, you know."

Diligently, watched over by the twins the whole time— He'd gouged open his own wound spectacularly and taken massive damage in the process.

"Setting foot in House Mathers's Forbidden Archive, and this is what you're wasting it on—what a treasure squandered, I suppose. Betty inviting someone in here happens maybe once every few years, I suppose."

"Say what you like, but a book I can't read is just a stack of paper, and 'rare chance to get into the Forbidden Archive' doesn't really mean much to me..."

Unfortunately for Subaru, this room held nothing rare or novel for him. Saying so to the current Beatrice would only complicate things, and Subaru, judging it wasn't anything to particularly boast about anyway, held his tongue.

Beatrice's brow furrowed suspiciously at Subaru trailing off mid-sentence. Subaru, to change the subject, raised a finger.

"Come to think of it, do you stay in this room all the time?"

"That is the nature of the Contract, I suppose."

"Contract again, huh. Getting tied up in contracts so young... you must've gotten caught by one heck of a hotshot saleslady. Insurance agents too—the louder their voice, the better they are at the job."

"It was entirely a Contract of my own choosing, I suppose."

Her tone was curt, refusing to allow further comment. At Beatrice's expressionless remark, Subaru nodded quietly and said,

"So the party in question has testified—"

"If you don't shut up this instant, I might just void the Contract, I suppose!"

Beatrice stood, pulled a book from the shelf, and began hurling them at Subaru one after another. Given that she normally didn't hesitate to hurl Magic at him without reservation, resorting to this kind of physical attack was extremely unusual for her.

Subaru caught the frail girl's throws easily, one after another, stacking them on a nearby table. When the attack suddenly stopped, he furrowed his brow—and only belatedly noticed.

The roughly seven books thrown at him—every single one of them was written entirely in the I-script.

Looking up, Subaru turned his eyes toward Beatrice. As if mocking Subaru for being slow to catch on to her intent, she waved a hand airily.

"Ith that all wight, hmm? Ith maybe a widdle too hawd, I th'poth?"

"Aw, thankth. It'th a widdle too hawd for me too, I don't fink I get it, nyeh~"

"If both of you start talking in baby talk, it's disgusting, I suppose!"

"Sheesh. I was just matching your level, you know."

If Subaru were going for a bit, he wouldn't stop at baby talk—he'd go all the way back to fetus talk. Tugging on the umbilical cord, tapping the placenta for communication.

At Subaru's attitude, Beatrice finally shrugged both shoulders and, as if giving up on conversation entirely, began opening a book. Watching her indifferent demeanor, Subaru too settled into a nearby chair and picked up one of the books Beatrice had thrown his way and chosen for him. Too embarrassed for words of thanks to ever leave his mouth, but that consideration had genuinely made him happy.

And so, the silence in the Forbidden Archive passed by gently.

Neither of them spoke; only the slow, intermittent sound of pages turning drifted back and forth through the archive.

The books Beatrice had chosen were, as before, mostly fairy-tale-like works meant for children. Since "written only in the I-script" essentially meant "written only in hiragana," it made sense that the selection skewed toward children's books.

But even that precious reading time ended up being wasted, as Subaru's eyes simply drifted back and forth across the same page over and over.

At first he'd had enough composure to make some progress through the book, but as the minutes ticked by, that mental leeway was gradually stripped away.

—From inside the sealed Forbidden Archive, there was no way to check on the world outside.

Given the nature of the room, the Forbidden Archive had no windows, and was, as Beatrice had once put it, a place where "time was frozen." Not literally—but at least in the sense that it felt that way.

What time was it outside, right now? By the time Subaru had been welcomed into the Forbidden Archive, sunset had already been near. Simply calculated, all Subaru needed to do was spend roughly half a day here before he'd reach the morning of the fifth day.

But immersed in this stagnant place, he found himself utterly unable to gauge even that mere half-day for himself. It felt as though only about an hour had passed since he'd entered this room—or perhaps as though nearly ten hours had gone by.

The breathlessness born of anxiety and tension played its own part in severely dulling Subaru's ability to judge his present state, until he could no longer trust his own judgment at all.

And yet he felt guilty even asking Beatrice about it. It wasn't out of some feminist principle about not interrupting her reading—it was that taking such action, causing change, felt terrifying.

Spending time like this, stagnant, was suffocating too. But if the situation were to suddenly shift dramatically because of something he did—that thought was terrifying as well.

Unable to go forward or back, he could only cower before the overwhelming phenomenon that was time. His fingers, trying to turn the page, went numb; the tip of his tongue cried out with thirst. His heart pounded its familiar rapid rhythm, his ears rang shrilly, and he could hear the sound of blood circulating through his entire body.

How long had he been forced to endure that kind of tension? If the beginning had come unreasonably, then its end, too, arrived without warning.

"—They're calling."

Suddenly, that murmur echoed quietly through the archive.

Snapping his head up, Subaru saw Beatrice closing her book and setting it aside. She rose lightly from the stepladder.

"Am I being called, I suppose."

As if she'd forgotten Subaru existed, startled as he was, she muttered to herself and gave a flick of her fingers. In that instant, Subaru's entire body felt a wrongness, as if space itself had warped. A sensation close to weightlessness assaulted his body from every direction, and though it lasted only an instant, a sluggish fatigue and nausea spread listlessly through his whole body, as if he'd been tossed around.

Catching himself as he nearly slid off the chair, Subaru shot a resentful glare at Beatrice, the cause of it all. She, finally noticing his gaze, said,

"Ah, that's right, you were here, I suppose. I'd forgotten, I suppose."

"Forgetting me when I'm right in front of you—that's a low-tier joke even by joke standards."

"A matter of priorities, I suppose. —My brother is calling, I suppose."

With just that said, Beatrice slipped past Subaru toward the door. As she made to step outside as a matter of course, Subaru reached out toward her back, unable to hide his mounting alarm.

"H-hey, wait a second! If you go outside right now..."

"You're welcome to stay cowering behind, I suppose. It should be safe enough here, I suppose."

Blood rushed to Subaru's head at her undisguised contempt; he kicked back his chair as he stood and chased after her. He put his hand to the door handle—hesitated for only a few seconds.

But then—

"Ah, damn it. What the hell, this much!"

Spurring himself on with foul language, he wrenched the door open violently. The next instant—

"Ah—"

An unwitting, foolish sound spilled from Subaru's mouth.

He shielded his eyes from the light stabbing into his eyelids, meeting the greeting of morning sunlight with a start. As if to confirm it, he waved a hand through the air, and his body staggered forward. Straight ahead down the corridor, through a window overlooking the garden—there, high in the sky, was a sun that had only just begun to rise.

The morning sun of the fifth day. The one he'd craved so desperately, the one he'd failed to reach no matter how many times he'd tried.

"No... way... did I... make it? Past the fourth night...!?"

Unable to believe the result before his eyes, he slammed a hand against the window hard enough to nearly shatter it, and pushed it open. A cool breeze poured in, brushing his bangs, and Subaru drank in the sharp scent of morning. A cold, newborn wind scattered the air of the mansion.

His legs gave out, and he slid weakly backward. His back hit the wall, and he slumped down, all strength to remain standing gone.

Dazed—there was no other word for it. The present situation was so unimaginable he couldn't even tell whether it was dream or reality.

He had given up entirely. He had despaired completely. He had been worn down to nothing. And yet, Subaru had made it past the fourth night, and arrived at the fifth day.

"Ha, haha..."

Dry laughter spilled unbidden from his mouth. Once it had started leaking out, he found no way to stop it.

"Hihi, hahaha. What, the hell. Hey, what the hell is this. This, hey, haha..."

He couldn't think of any way to properly put his current feelings into words. Hugging his knees, Subaru remained crouched in the corridor, continuing to spill out laughter that sounded like he'd lost his mind. He couldn't put it into words. It refused to become words. Finally, Subaru—

"—Subaru?"

That empty jubilation of his was interrupted by a voice like a silver bell.

Reluctantly lifting his gaze, he saw a silver-haired girl standing at the end of the corridor. Emilia. He'd found her, safely arrived at the morning of the fifth day, unharmed. The two of them, together, had made it past the fourth night. That fact stirred up some indescribable emotion in Subaru's heart once more.

And yet—

"Emilia...?"

In contrast to the joy spreading within Subaru, Emilia stared at him, aghast. And then, suddenly, she broke into a run, closing the distance to where he sat.

"Subaru... where were you?"

"No, I was..."

"Because... no, never mind. That's fine. Just... come with me."

Pulled by the hand, Subaru got to his feet, startled by her forcefulness. Without waiting for his reply, Emilia dragged him along and took off running down the hallway.

Bewildered by her sudden behavior, Subaru tried to say something—but the words caught in his throat as he saw her profile. Upon her fine features was an unconcealable expression of agitation and desperation.

An expression that recalled, uncannily, the moment of the battle at the Loot House.

"Hey, what in the world—"

—happened, he couldn't manage to ask. Before the words could leave his mouth, another sound struck Subaru's eardrums first.

—It was, he thought, a scream. Or perhaps a wail.

High-pitched, trailing, brimming with sorrow, it was the cry of a soul that could not help but leave a wound of grief in the heart of anyone who heard it. The cry, like something being torn from one's very body, went on and on, conveying its desperate urgency with painful clarity even to Subaru, who had no idea what was happening.

They passed through the corridor and headed for the upper floor. The second floor on the east side was where the servants each had their private rooms. Passing the room where Subaru had been staying until a few days ago, Emilia, still pulling him along, made for a room even further in—and there,

"Roswaal, and..."

Indigo-colored long hair stood in the corridor, narrowing its eyes at Emilia and Subaru as they hurried up. Right beside him, Beatrice, who had vanished earlier, leaned against the wall—and in her arms, curled up, was a gray-furred cat.

"Inside."

Reaching the three of them, Subaru tried to demand an explanation, but Roswaal answered with only that single short word. What he indicated was right beside him—a private room with its door open.

Turning to Emilia, she too gave Subaru a nod. Her violet-blue eyes were wet with tears, forcing him toward a decision whether he wanted it or not.

Holding his breath, Subaru turned his steps toward the room. Even as he stepped inside, the screaming from before continued without pause. And now it was unmistakably coming from the very room in front of him.

Stepping in, he gently opened the eyelids he'd closed from tension—and Subaru saw.

It was a neatly ordered room. Reflecting its occupant's meticulous nature, the private room, with its few but tastefully arranged furnishings, made clear how efficiently and without waste its limited space had been used. The floor plan itself was nearly identical to the room Subaru had used, and yet it struck him how much personality a room could take on simply from a different occupant.

By thinking that, Subaru managed, for just an instant, to forget the sight in front of him. But that escape from reality was futile, and the world forced its conclusion upon him regardless.

In the center of the private room stood a neatly made bed. And atop that bed—

"Aaaaahhhhh aaaaaahhhhhhhhhhh——!"

—Ram, screaming as if to tear her own throat apart with the depth of her sorrow, tears streaming down her face in torrents—

And—

—clung to by her—

Rem lay there, her life already gone.
