Arc 9 Chapter 27: "The Words of a Young Child"

The twin blades, swung in the shape of a cross, drove a wound into his opponent that decided the outcome. Blood ran from the not-shallow wounds covering his whole body, and yet Wilhelm's mind could not conceal its frustration — frustration at his own swordplay, at the poor quality of the strike he had just landed.

"...Weak."

What spilled from behind his clenched teeth was disappointment and anger at himself, grown old and diminished. The sheer inadequacy of a body that had piled up the years and, with them, its own decline, was enough to make him want to bury his face in his hands. Were he a poet, an artist, the years he'd accumulated would have translated directly into knowledge and skill — his command of words or brush growing ever more refined, reaching heights his younger self could never have touched.

But a swordsman has no such luxury. The years enough to make him feel his own age were nothing but layer upon layer of decline too plain to excuse — his strength, his skill, his speed, none of it comparable to the days when he'd been honed to his sharpest.

—That, and only that, galled him.

He had tasted that same frustration many times before. When, as captain of the Kingdom's Royal Guard, he had decided to step back from the front line. When the White Whale had taken his wife from him, and after fourteen years spent chasing vengeance, he'd been forced to realize he could only face that all-important enemy with a blade dulled by age. And now, the frustration searing through Wilhelm was — without exaggeration — the greatest he had ever tasted in his entire life, rivaling even the moment during the Demi-Human War when he first learned that Theresia was the Sword Saint, and was made to understand just how hopelessly far he fell short of her.

Weak. Far too weak. That fact of weakness was, in a world governed by the logic of strength, an unforgivable sin. In this world, excuses carry no weight. There is only strong or weak — nothing else. Talent, laziness, illness, age — none of it means anything once you step onto the ring where strength is measured.

Naturally enough. A sword cannot choose whose hand holds it. So it follows that the one holding the sword is allowed no excuses — that much is only natural. No, wait — there does exist, in this world, a Sword that chooses its own wielder. And the one who holds that Sword stands at the very summit of this world where no excuse holds water.

—That, precisely, was the opponent that Wilhelm Trias — who could not use his own age and decline as an excuse — had to surpass with these two hands, no matter what.

"—"

Setting aside Wilhelm's own self-assessment, the battle — in which he'd displayed swordsmanship closer to his prime than he had in decades — reached its conclusion, and the fearsomely skilled Shinobi fell toward the depths below. Among all the Shinobi Wilhelm had ever faced, she was, without question, among the very best. He could scarcely imagine how much training the girl must have undergone to reach such heights, but in the world of contests, there are no prizes for effort. That is the cruelest thing about the sword.

Even if one loves the sword above all else, whether the sword answers that love has nothing whatsoever to do with the depth or magnitude of that affection. If anything, the deeper and greater that love, the more thoroughly the heart of one who learns that nothing came back to repay it cracks — breaks beyond repair.

With that sentiment tucked into a corner of his mind, Wilhelm did not let his guard down, even with the outcome already decided. The falling girl — he caught sight of her body catching on a single remaining thread, left dangling in midair. Blood ran down her upside-down body and dripped away, but her slender torso had not been cut in two. That was the reason for his caution.

In a battle staked on their lives, neither side had room to hold back. Wilhelm had given no consideration whatsoever — to her youth, her sex, her circumstances — in the blade he swung. He had unleashed and rained down his sword fully intending to end her life. And yet the fact that her body hadn't been split clean in two proved not only some shortfall in his swordsmanship, but that the girl had, by some means, avoided a fatal blow.

Probably—

"...Thread, is it."

Thread, which the girl controlled with every finger and toe, strung beforehand even through this pit leading to the depths. The crystallized art of a technique that surely demanded rare talent and blood-soaked training to wield freely could become a blade to cut down an opponent, a chain to bind an enemy's movement, or — wound tightly around her own body — even serve as armor to ward off a sword strike. At the moment of his final strike, the girl had moved to shield her neck and heart, and Wilhelm had swept his blade with all his strength through the torso she'd left open — which had been, it turned out, her split-second sleight of misdirection.

For a girl who, in an instant's judgment, had preserved a single thread of possibility for survival, there was nothing to say but well done. His guard would not lower, but the girl's survival itself was something Wilhelm hoped for too. Taking the enemy alive, if possible, was the shared preference among their side.

"...Nngh."

A faint groan slipped from the throat of the dangling girl. Wilhelm, having driven his sword into the wall to secure his footing, was moving to approach the girl below and recover her. Sensing this, perhaps, her trembling eyelids opened, crimson eyes reflecting Wilhelm's form. At that reaction, Wilhelm did not relax his guard. But he was also certain, from the feel of his blade's impact, that a counterattack from her was impossible.

Fifty years he had wielded a sword; in that time, life-or-death battles and lives he had taken were beyond counting. The weight of blood spilled and lives cut down, honest and unfalsified, told Wilhelm exactly how deep his opponent's wound ran.

"—"

So no matter how much the girl's gaze hinted at some trump card left to keep him in check, Wilhelm saw through it as a desperate bluff and did not stop his approach.

"I myself do not know the full particulars of your circumstances, but for your own sake, you would do well not to resist any further in vain. Before long, Master Al's person shall also be secured."

Having seen through the bluff, Wilhelm moved to check any further resistance from the girl. Wilhelm had heard that the girl was one who conspired with Al, the knight of Priscilla Barielle. He'd also heard of the tragedy in which Priscilla's life had been lost in the Vollachian Empire, and the surmise that it was this which had driven Al to his violent scheme.

That state of mind — of having lost someone he loved — Wilhelm understood in Al all too painfully well. To have one's body cut, one's heart torn, one's soul left burning eternally in bottomless helplessness — that must be how it felt. And yet, that could not be allowed as a reason to let him destroy the world. Still less could it be overlooked that a benefactor's name should number among the victims of that rampage.

—Al's whole scheme, every part of it, had to be broken. Which was the same as saying every single person who took part in that scheme, whatever their reason for cooperating with Al, had to be stopped — no matter who among them it might be. This Shinobi girl, too, was one of the gears that had to be removed from that machine—

"...I'm the worst."

On a thin breath, weak, barely clinging to life, those words rode. The dangling girl, apparently realizing her bluff had not worked on Wilhelm, let the words spill out without hiding her disappointment in herself. It was—

"Instead of being of any use, all I've done is drag everyone down."

Regret from the very bottom of her heart — which, were she not hanging in midair, might have come with falling tears. Had that regret been directed only at the outcome of her fight with Wilhelm, he might finally have let go of that last shred of caution. But it was not that. Right after the girl's murmur, a sound came — from above, from outside the pit. A heavy thud, as of something toppling onto a floor. And following it—

"...Gu— hgh!"

The instant he recognized that pained cry as belonging to Felt, whom he'd left in the room above, Wilhelm used the sword driven into the wall as a foothold and kicked off, launching himself upward. Repeating a series of wall-jumps, Wilhelm burst out of the pit — and there, his eyes went wide.

"—!!"

Legs flailing, Felt hung suspended in midair, clawing desperately at her own throat with her fingers. She was hanging by a thread looped around her neck. But the girl who controlled it shouldn't have had any strength left to spare for this. Wilhelm noticed, in the corner of the room, that part of the wall had collapsed — and understood that this was both the source of the earlier sound and the mechanism that had strung Felt up. A thread had been looped around Felt's neck, and a section of wall, hollowed out beforehand at an angle, was set to fall after a set time, pulling the thread taut and hoisting her up — a time-delayed trap. This way, no matter how the fight went, regardless of whether the girl lived or died, the mechanism would trigger and Felt would be strung up.

"Right now—"

—I'll save her, Wilhelm thought, sword in hand, rushing toward Felt. The thread was tough, but without someone controlling it, its strength couldn't exceed a sword strike. Freeing Felt would be easy. And the girl who'd set the trap couldn't escape either. To begin with, her purpose in setting a time-delayed trap must have been this: if the fight with Wilhelm dragged on, to draw his attention away from herself, either to catch him off guard or to make good her escape.

But the contest had been decided before the trap could trigger, and the girl had taken wounds too severe to allow any escape. The trap now gave her neither the chance for a surprise attack nor the grace period to flee. In other words, one might say Felt had been strung up for nothing, caught in the crossfire. So Wilhelm reasoned.

The flow of Wilhelm's thinking in that moment was natural, and no one could blame him for it. After all, at this point, the only ones who correctly grasped the true meaning of Felt being strung up were the very members of the group behind the crisis threatening to overturn the kingdom. Except—

'Instead of being of any use, all I've done is drag everyone down.'

Had there been time to fathom the true meaning behind those words of regret from the dangling girl, things might have gone differently — that her regret lay not in the defeat itself, but in what lay beyond it. What she regretted was not her defeat, but the insurance policy that would follow it.

"...un— run."

An instant before Wilhelm's sword strike could cut the thread, the strung-up Felt, her face a mask of desperation, was straining with all her might to voice something on rasping breath. It was not "Help me." It was "Run."

"—"

It was the instant right after Wilhelm's eyes caught that word for certain that the massive arm of a Dragon, bursting through the wall of the Barielle villa's annex, struck him mercilessly from the side.

—Infiltrate the royal capital of Lugunica and free the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony imprisoned in the Prison Tower.

Toward Aldebaran, who had declared a plan that wasn't merely far-fetched but sounded like the ravings of a madman, and had then gone charging alone into the city without a second thought, Heinkel harbored complicated feelings.

The true root of Aldebaran's purpose had not been shared with Heinkel. It seemed Yae, who followed him, had been given a similarly limited amount of information — but unlike Heinkel, who cooperated with Aldebaran in pursuit of some reward, Yae's part in this scheme apparently stemmed from a twisted attachment to Aldebaran himself.

"A twisted attachment, huh. ...Shameless of me to say that without so much as blushing."

That inexplicable feeling Yae held for Aldebaran — neither love nor hatred — and the fact that he'd measured and judged it by his own yardstick: Heinkel mocked himself for it with a bitter, hollow chill. To begin with, by what standard could one even measure whether something was straight or twisted? If Yae's attachment could be called twisted, then what of himself? What of Heinkel's own attachment, which — in seeking some means to wake his ever-sleeping wife — had finally reduced him to nothing more than a traitor to the kingdom?

"—!"

At this self-directed question mingling self-reproach and self-mockery, Heinkel felt a splitting headache and dug his fingers into his temples as if gouging them, trying to distract himself from the pain. Ordinarily, drink was the best remedy for this kind of fog-shrouded, answerless pain. Wine wouldn't heal the pain, but it could make him forget it for a while, numb him to it. Unhealthy as he knew it to be, Heinkel could barely even open his eyes to look at things properly except within the haze of drunkenness that wine produced.

And yet right now, he could not bring wine to his lips in search of the drunken haze that dulled the pain. Because — the moment might finally have come. The moment of the desperate wish he had pursued for nearly twenty years, craved until his very soul cracked, longed and burned for.

"...Louanna."

Behind his closed eyelids, at the name of his beloved woman spun from his lips, her image rose up. Unchanged since the day they'd met, young, delicate, lovely — his wife's form had not altered one bit since the year she'd begun to sleep. Not because his memory of her had gone unrefreshed, but because her sleeping face truly ignored the passage of every one of those years, remaining just as it had been long ago.

He wanted to see those eyelids open again. Wanted her lips to spin sound once more, wanted to burn into his mind the way she had been — lively, unrestrained despite that delicate appearance, unable to sit still for even a single second. If that were granted, Heinkel would need nothing more from the rest of his life—

"I..."

'—Ah, Pops, you're not going all negative again, are you? Feels like that kind of mood.'

At the voice suddenly reaching him from directly below, Heinkel held his breath. The one who called out to him as he kept gouging at his temples was the wing-spread Divine Dragon. Contrary to its solemn, overwhelming presence, the Divine Dragon spoke with the shallowest possible frivolity, and though it surely couldn't see Heinkel's face riding on its back, it sharp-eyed sensed the nuance of his mood all the same. At that question from the Divine Dragon — whether it counted as concern or not — Heinkel found himself at a loss for a reply. Naturally enough. To Heinkel, being left alone like this with the Divine Dragon was exactly like a Flying Dragon or Water Dragon with its favorite treat dangled right under its nose.

To wake the ever-sleeping Louanna Astrea, Heinkel had explored every possible means, clinging to magic, to curse arts, to Meteors, to Magicreation Tools. And with every one of those hopes cut off, his last remaining hope was Dragon's Blood. With that supreme treasure, said to drive back every plague and bring abundance even to withered earth, he could wake Louanna. And right now, it pulsed near enough that Heinkel could reach it the instant he drew his sword — insisting on its own presence. Though, admittedly—

"...Leave it be. Don't concern yourself with me."

'C'mon, don't get all sour on me. I get that you're itching to hurry, and I know full well it's rough being stuck with me. So I figured I'd show a little consideration, in my own way, to make sure you've got a proper brake on you, Pops.'

"Consideration? Don't tell me you mean this is it."

'That's exactly what I was gonna say it was. That a bad thing to say?'

What the Divine Dragon's words — spoken without malice, in a tone that suggested it might be tilting its head — pointed to was the place where Heinkel and it now were: namely, above the clouds, with the ground left far behind. Heinkel was riding on the Divine Dragon's back, high, high up in the sky. Whether the Divine Dragon was controlling the wind or not, he was exposed to no shaking or gale — a comfortable enough environment, aside from the hardness of the scales. But the "consideration" the Divine Dragon spoke of surely wasn't this comfort. It was, rather, the altitude — maintained high enough that, should Heinkel ever draw his sword, there would be no surviving it.

'Even if you carved out my heart and got the blood, it'd mean nothing if you fell along with me and died. So no matter how alone the two of us are, you can't lay a hand on me up here, Pops. Whaddaya think? Not a bad idea, huh?'

"Don't say it yourself. Besides, that's neither consideration nor calculation — that's just a word for it. And there's still a chance your scheme fails, that I draw my sword without thinking of the consequences..."

'Nah, that ain't happening. 'Cause you're sane, Pops.'

"Wha—"

'A sane guy can properly think about what scares him. I've seen plenty of screws-loose types up to now, and you're not gonna turn into one of them, Pops.'

At the Divine Dragon's oracle — said however it liked, whatever it pleased — Heinkel opened and closed his mouth without managing an immediate rebuttal. Though which was the reason his ears reddened first — anger or embarrassment — was unclear. If the former, then the very fact that he couldn't spit out some heedless curse proved the Divine Dragon's read on him right. If the latter, the fact that being scolded struck a nerve likewise proved the Divine Dragon right. In short, the moment his ears went red, Heinkel's defeat was already sealed.

"Me, sane...!"

Heinkel tightened the grip of the fingers clutching his own head, his voice grinding out against the Divine Dragon's assessment. Sane — considering Heinkel's current position, that was far too ironic an evaluation. What was the Divine Dragon thinking? Would a sane man carry his own selfishness to such extremes as to become a traitor to the kingdom? Heinkel was neither sane nor decent. He was unworthy of an evaluation describing the kind of goodness that most ordinary townsfolk possessed universally.

"That's why, when Lady Priscilla died, I took Aldebaran up on his invitation."

Priscilla Barielle, who had intervened in the Vollachian Empire's national crisis and scattered her life there. Heinkel was in no position to say, even if it cost him his tongue, that he'd sworn her his loyalty. His relationship with her had been nothing more than an alliance where their interests happened to align — and even that much, he no longer knew how much use Priscilla had found in him at all.

Still, though Heinkel had sworn Priscilla no loyalty, he had never once doubted that she held the Vessel of a king — one who could take the throne and lead the nation's people as its new sovereign. Her superior abilities, her foresight and wisdom, even her beautiful appearance — all splendid qualities for a queen. Priscilla had the qualifications to become Queen of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica. And once that was accomplished, he was to receive, as her reward, permission to use the Dragon's Blood.

That hope had scattered, along with Priscilla's life, in that empire. He regretted it from the depths of his heart — both failing to obtain the Dragon's Blood, and the failure of a Lugunica ruled by Priscilla to come to pass.

And to Heinkel, who had lost his hope and been crushed by it, Aldebaran extended a hand. Cooperate with him, and in exchange for making an enemy of the whole world, he could obtain the Dragon's Blood. It was, for Heinkel, a temptation far too sweet.

To have hope that had drawn so near, that should have been within reach, slip away — that was agony. And to have that flame of hope snuffed out just one step short was, for Heinkel, the second time it had happened. The helplessness of that first time, when his own foolish blunder had cost him the chance, was impossible to forget.

Which is exactly why Heinkel clung, shamelessly, to the possibility that this time — this time for certain. To call that sane, as the Divine Dragon had, was the height of missing the mark.

'Sulked back into your shell again, huh. Not that I'm one to say it, but nobody's gonna love a cranky middle-aged man, y'know?'

"...That's a bit late to point out."

'Man, you're really twisted up about it. ...Well, that's a boomerang comin' right back at me too, so I'll leave it there.'

Even something as simple as a shrug came across as absurdly exaggerated on the Divine Dragon's massive body. In that unsettlingly human-like gesture, Heinkel found an unmistakable point in common with Aldebaran, and ignored the headache that grew worse with the puzzlement of it.

Loved by no one — that was exactly what Heinkel wished for. He wasn't fool enough to spout some sulky claim that he'd never once been loved. Heinkel had been born loved, and there had been no small number of people who loved even a man like him. And rather than repay the affection directed at him, he had betrayed it, and now walked on through a drizzling mist.

'—. Say. Come to think of it, we don't get much chance to be alone together, so there's something I've been wanting to check with you, Pops.'

"Can you not simply wait quietly?"

'I'm just not the type who can stand silence. Might be 'cause I always had chatty company.'

The nature of the Divine Dragon was a complete unknown, but it hadn't spoken much of itself, and Heinkel hadn't much wanted to ask. Either way, this was a creature that, once things were settled, would have to die so he could claim its heart-blood. There was no need to go adding reasons to feel guilty about it. Besides, whether out of consideration or not, a change from the previous line of conversation was welcome. If they talked about something else, maybe this headache would ease up a little.

"So, what does the awesome and mighty Divine Dragon wish to ask of me?"

'Ah, it's nothing much, but you're crazy tough, aren't you, Pops? I was wondering if you're actually aware of why that is.'

"—"

'Huh? Did I say something?'

At the Divine Dragon's playing-dumb question, Heinkel gnashed his teeth in irritation. The worsening pain in his head was proof that Heinkel's just-formed hope had missed the mark — proof that, though he'd expected the topic to change, it hadn't.

"—"

Falling silent, Heinkel looked down at his own open palm and let out a breath. The Divine Dragon's question concerned Heinkel's body. Of course it did — his own body. He was aware of its abnormality, and he understood the reason for it perfectly well. Among the many sins Heinkel had committed, this was the punishment for one of the particularly heavy ones.

Louanna Astrea's unwaking sleep — that was without question the beginning of House Astrea's fracture, Heinkel included. But what made it truly decisive was an event fifteen years ago. Theresia van Astrea's deployment in the battle to subjugate the White Whale, the transfer of the Sword Saint's Divine Protection mid-battle that saw the Sword Saint's title change hands, and Theresia's death. And the trigger for it all — the kidnapping of the daughter of Ford Lugunica, then the King's younger brother. On the night of that incident, the unforgivable betrayal Heinkel had committed, and the worst possible failure that rendered that betrayal utterly meaningless — this, the present Heinkel, was heaven's punishment for it.

"There's no particular reason I need to go out of my way to explain that to you..."

'Oh, gonna go there, huh? Fine then — if you won't say it, maybe I go tattle to the other me that you're being uncooperative, and threaten to tear up the deal?'

"You—!"

'Kidding, kidding. Bet you've got fifty, a hundred things you don't wanna say. Same goes for me too, Pops.'

"...A hundred's a bit much."

To the Divine Dragon's banter — which didn't sound the least bit light — Heinkel could only weakly manage that much of a reply. Fifty, a hundred — it said, but what Heinkel carried could be counted on one hand. And even that number small enough for one hand was already enough, fragile enough, to nearly crush him. Even so, piecing together what had cracked, dragging along what had stretched taut, telling himself just a little more, just a bit further — he had kept going, until finally—

'—'

"—? Uh... what is it?"

Sensing some faint shift in the air, Heinkel asked the Divine Dragon. For a moment he hesitated over whether to simply call it Volcanica, and settled on the safer form of address after all. But paying no mind to Heinkel's hesitation, the Divine Dragon turned its head downward — toward the royal capital, below the clouds. And then—

'—Hang on, Pops!'

"Guh— !?"

In the next instant, slammed by a violent wind pressing down from directly above, Heinkel was flattened against the Divine Dragon's back as if prostrating himself. With no time even to gasp at the sudden shock, the Divine Dragon plunged all at once, Heinkel pinned to its back, tearing straight through the sky above the royal capital.

Whatever concealment they'd maintained above the clouds while Aldebaran assaulted the Prison Tower was now ruined. In other words, the need to show itself had arisen. At a speed that never let Heinkel so much as lift his head, the Divine Dragon reached its destination. The raging dragon's forearm swung, and with a violent crash of destruction, a building's wall was smashed clean through.

The reason for so sudden an outburst, its target — none of it was known at all. All that was certain was that the building struck by the outrageous tragedy of the Divine Dragon's blow was mercilessly destroyed, and the dragon's claw smashed to pieces everything within it, root and all—

'—You gotta be kidding me!'

That voice, coming from the very mouth of the Divine Dragon that had carried out the merciless destruction, was pure, unadulterated astonishment. The Divine Dragon, its golden eyes sharpening, had its gaze fixed through the building into which it had thrust its arm, voicing surprise at the result of its own merciless destruction. Small wonder. The Divine Dragon's claw — sharper and tougher than nearly any blade in this world — had been severed, torn away, reduced to particles. And the one who had done this was none other than—

"...Father."

The one standing amid the half-collapsed building, twin blades raised, having just deflected the Divine Dragon's very claw. None other than Wilhelm van Astrea, the Sword Demon.

"...My thanks."

Aware of the numbness in both arms that had barely managed to deflect the dragon's claw, Wilhelm said this to Felt, who had warned him of the threat's approach. Felt, strung up with thread looped around her neck. Even in a situation that endangered her own life, she had not asked Wilhelm to save her — she had ordered him to prepare. Thanks to that, he had managed, by a hair's breadth, to take up a defensive stance in time.

'No, even in time, most guys couldn't have stopped that!'

So exclaimed, against Wilhelm's musing, the massive form standing outside the mansion — an absolute power so fearsome and mysterious in presence that many would fall prostrate at the mere sight of it. The awesome bulk of the Divine Dragon Volcanica peered into the mansion from outside.

"Cough, cough... I'm with the Dragon on that one, too. I said run, not go get him... ehh-hem."

Coughing painfully like that, Felt, of all people, agreed with Volcanica's assessment. In the confusion of the dragon's claw, she'd been freed from her hanging — no, Wilhelm's eyes caught what had actually happened more precisely than that. That Felt had been saved without Wilhelm's help was no coincidence.

"Don't tell me you came to save Lady Felt?"

'Position-wise, hard to say yes or no to that, but if I say yes here, does that make little Felt an official member of Team Traitor-to-the-Kingdom?'

"Not a member, no. But there's no mistakin' you came flyin' here to save me..."

Still faintly showing the aftereffects of having been strung up, Felt answered so, rubbing her own throat. Volcanica offered no denial either. It seemed the Dragon had, by some means, sensed Felt's plight and had come flying in here to save her. In other words, the Shinobi girl had aimed for exactly this when she'd looped that thread around Felt's neck. That said—

"It seemed a bitter choice for her, all the same. After all, coming here means..."

'The me over there can't lend a hand.'

"Indeed. — By the way, your manner of speech has changed a great deal. From when you glanced my way some forty years past, you seem an entirely different person... no, an entirely different dragon."

'There's a saying — three days apart and a man's a new person. Forty years is a downright absurd stretch of time. Summer casts a spell on girls, y'know, Gramps.'

"I see."

So he answered in words, though it was hardly a satisfying explanation for Volcanica's change. Still, Wilhelm had no time to press the matter of that shift in attitude here. There was something more pressing than that. Volcanica, who had come crashing in, was not alone. The dragon apparently carried someone else on its back. That this person had said nothing this whole time, trying to hide their presence, was not to catch Wilhelm off guard, but—

"...Can you not even show your face before me, Heinkel?"

"—!"

At Wilhelm's call, he could feel through the very air that the other had flinched. No matter how he bowed his head, silenced his voice, tried to hide his presence, Wilhelm already knew that Heinkel numbered among those siding with Al, the traitor who had become an enemy of the kingdom.

Al's actions constituted, more than enough, a crisis threatening the kingdom's very survival. Not wielding a sword in such a crisis was, for Wilhelm — once captain of the Royal Guard — simply unthinkable. But setting all that aside, the very reason Wilhelm had resolved to take up his sword and lead the charge himself was Heinkel's presence among them.

"No doubt you were told that if you cooperated, you'd be granted a share of Dragon's Blood. And with it, you mean to wake your wife — Louanna. If so, your judgment has grown far too shallow."

The Dragon's Blood kept in the royal castle did indeed house tremendous power. With that power belonging to the kingdom's supreme treasure, it might well be possible to wake Louanna Astrea, still sleeping from a cause unknown. However—

"Do you truly believe Master Al — no, the man named Al who has turned traitor — will keep his word? That man has betrayed what was left behind by Lady Priscilla Barielle, the master he served, and is even deceiving those around him who worried for his sake in order to set this scheme in motion. Why should he, of all things, grant your wish alone?"

'Well, I mean, I get why you'd be suspicious. But that's a bit one-sided—'

"Noble Dragon of Lugunica, I am not speaking with you right now."

At Wilhelm's tone and manner, showing no room for argument, Volcanica fell silent with a 'Hmph...' To the one hiding on the silent dragon's back beyond it, he went on all the same.

"What's the matter — nothing to say? Is it because I've struck true, or is it that you never even considered it? Ah, that's right. Even from long ago, you've always turned your face from anything unpleasant, covered your ears, and run away. So even now, you refuse to look at anything but what's convenient for you—"

"—Shut up, shut up, shut up!!"

"—"

"Saying whatever you... whatever you damn well please...! Whatever I do for Louanna's sake, you've got no right to say a single word about it, Father!!"

Roaring so, a cry that sounded torn from blood, a figure rose up on Volcanica's back. At that familiar face and voice, at the temper tantrum jabbing a trembling finger his way, Wilhelm let out a deep breath. It was exasperation, disappointment — and a small measure of relief. There were several reasons for that relief. That, spoken to this ill of, there was still enough fighting spirit left in him to raise his face; and that, having sided with traitors, cowering out of sight would have been a coward's act. But taken all together, what could be said was—

"...So you've finally shown your face."

"Ngh..."

"And so? Have you no rebuttal besides bellowing noise? Is the reason you sided with that man exactly as I've surmised? If so, how can you possibly believe that man would grant your wish alone? Have you worn yourself thin with wishing until your eyes have clouded over?"

"I... I—"

At Wilhelm's repeated questions, the figure on the dragon's back — Heinkel — hung his head. Where had the momentum of his outburst of temper gone? His dejection was plain to see. And this shrinking of Heinkel's before Wilhelm was nothing new. It had been so for a long, long time — long before he'd sworn to become a knight, long before Wilhelm had entrusted him with Astrea, his own beloved sword.

"Ain't wrong that the old man's after the Dragon's Blood, aiming to wake up his sleeping wife."

In place of Heinkel, who hung his head unable to form words, it was Felt who answered Wilhelm's question. Lowering the hand that had been rubbing her throat, straightening her back, Felt — the least powerful being present by far, yet with an expression that showed not a trace of it — said,

"It's what the old man told me himself, with his own mouth. Though I didn't hear the whole of it, mind."

"...No — my thanks, in place of my unworthy son, for answering. And my apologies for having drawn you into such a matter."

"Don't need that last apology. Told the old man the same thing — it's just his way's different from mine. Ain't a matter of good or bad."

Despite having been dragged in and having suffered harm herself, there was no anger in Felt's answer. In that dignified way of seeing things, Wilhelm saw in her, unmistakably, the Vessel of a king. Beyond doubt, Felt too — like Crusch — was one of the five chosen by the Dragon History Stone. And yet here the Divine Dragon stood in her way, blocking the path.

"Descending to the ground like this, swinging your claws without mercy — surely many have already witnessed it. The foundations of the Dragon Kingdom will shake for it. Are you aware of that?"

'Yeah, I do feel bad about it. Truly. But even if the Dragon Kingdom reverts back into the Lion Kingdom, that's a whole lot better than the world getting smashed to pieces.'

"I should think your act of rebellion is more than sufficient a calamity to count as the world being broken."

At the very least, there was no doubt a violent shock would soon run through the Kingdom of Lugunica. That tremor might crack the kingdom's precarious foundations, and the fissures it opened might swallow up a great many things. Even supposing that Volcanica and the others' actions were meant to counter something that surpassed even that—

"Even with a supernatural Dragon involved, this is not a matter for a mere handful of people to decide alone. Still less that an immature man who can see no farther than his own feet should be counted among that handful — that is beyond forgiving."

'That's a mighty divine pronouncement, comin' with a noble dragon thrown in for good measure.'

"If so, then I would ask that, just as you once did, you save the many common folk struggling and gasping in suffering, here and now. Beyond that, let the world be saved by the hands of many, not few. And if that cannot be done—"

'And if it can't?'

"—Then I have no choice but to conclude that you too, like my foolish son, have been manipulated by sweet words."

So saying, Wilhelm re-gripped his twin blades and raised them, facing off against the Divine Dragon Volcanica. The numbness from deflecting that first blow had faded from his hands. But if a single exchange left him like that, three more would tear the sword right out of his grip. He would have to dodge cleanly, or deflect at a still higher level of skill.

'Little Miss Felt, don't move. Not confident I can go easy on my touch here.'

"Might not listen to that anymore. Been buildin' up a grudge ever since old man Rom went and tried to trick me good."

"In that case, please forgive the inconvenience a little while longer."

"Don't go gettin' all buddy-buddy just when it comes to how you two handle me!"

Shouting so, Felt glared alternately at Wilhelm and Volcanica and stuck out her tongue. Sparing her only the barest sliver of attention, Wilhelm fixed his focus on Volcanica's every movement. The dragon, too, was in the position of having rushed to save the Shinobi girl. Its form had already been witnessed; the longer time passed, the more the uproar would spread, the harder it would become to move freely. Here too, then — a short, decisive battle.

'Hey now, fallen on hard times or not, I'm still a legendary dragon, y'know? Ain't this the part where you look for some option besides fighting me?'

"Unfortunately, I happen to have just been thinking that I must surpass legend once more. My apologies for making convenient use of you, but I'll ask for your company all the same."

"—!"

As Wilhelm answered Volcanica's warning so, he could tell that Heinkel, who had long said nothing, caught his breath and stiffened his cheeks. Eyelids trembling, Heinkel stared at Wilhelm blankly with his blue eyes.

"Father, don't tell me..."

"—"

To Heinkel, who seemed to have grasped something, Wilhelm closed both eyelids once. Before Volcanica, it was a reckless, defenseless act — but the trust that his opponent would not exploit that opening was a feeling shared between them, born of each other's fighting spirit. Giving himself over to that feeling, behind his closed eyelids, Wilhelm's regret rose up before him.

A cloud-covered sky, and an empty gravestone holding nothing at all. A great crowd of mourners lost in grief, and His Majesty the King's words praising a life given in devotion to duty. A son absent from the place he should have stood, and a weak self without even the will to go and bring him there. And then—

'—It's all right, Grandfather. When she passed, Grandmother wasn't the Sword Saint. So the Sword Saint never lost.'

'Because the Sword Saint mustn't lose. That's what Father told me.'

'So there's no need to grieve — House Astrea is safe, Grandfather.'

"...Just the words of a young child."

The innocent, guileless words of a young child who had only just inherited, along with the Divine Protection, the right to draw the Dragon Sword. Recalling in vivid detail the regret of that day, Wilhelm opened his eyes and looked at the dragon — the Divine Dragon who, by its own account, had a name among legends, who had once saved the kingdom.

He was well aware how improper it was, amid a crisis threatening the kingdom's survival, a situation that would set many hearts adrift. But just as much as saving the kingdom, saving the world, Wilhelm had an important role he had to fulfill with the sword — an important role that could be fulfilled by nothing but the sword.

He knew no other way, Wilhelm — no other way to return the Sword Saint to being merely human. And so he demanded of this sword the same strength as before — no, greater strength than ever before — and challenged even a dragon for it. To surpass, waiting beyond it, the legend that would overwrite legend itself.

"—Wilhelm Trias, the Sword Demon."

'—Volcanica, the Divine Dragon.'

With their introductions spoken, both combatants moved. Sword-light and dragon claw left even sound behind.
