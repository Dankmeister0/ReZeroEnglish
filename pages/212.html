Arc 4 Chapter 46: "Locust Plague"

Seeing the smile turned his way, Subaru, for the first time, found himself genuinely thinking Echidna was cute.

The smile that softened her lips held none of the calculation or sarcasm that colored her usual expressions—it arose purely from goodwill directed at Subaru. There was no doubting she was a woman of striking looks, and when someone like that treated you with real warmth, well, a man's heart couldn't help but open up a little. Of course, Subaru wasn't about to let that feeling shove aside the two girls who already occupied the most important space in his heart.

"Still, gotta admit my heart skipped a beat there, for real…"

"Did you say something just now?"

"Said it'd be a problem if you had a talent for seducing men, is what I said. Kinda awkward having someone obsessed with me when I don't even remember wooing her… ah, but if it means the Six Witches won't cause trouble and'll even lend me their power out in the real world, I'd be grateful."

Turning his face away to cover himself, Subaru voiced that convenient little wish. The only two whose fragments of power Subaru had actually witnessed firsthand were Typhon and Minerva, but between an offense and a healer role, they'd more than pull their weight. Though having his arm torn off and then being healed by a punch to the face each time was something to think twice about.

"Unfortunately, unlike when I was alive, I can't interfere with reality now that I'm dead. This—inviting you to my castle in the world of dreams like this—is the exception."

"Yeah, I know. Just figured it couldn't hurt to ask. Honestly, just having someone listen to me talk is more than enough to save me as it is."

At the very least, having unburdened himself of what he'd been carrying, Subaru felt his mental collapse had been pushed back a little further. He'd also managed to dispel a good deal of his suspicions about the Witch of Envy. As for the parts of the mystery that remained unsolved, he decided not to dwell on them for now. More importantly—

"Can I ask another question about Return by Death?"

"I can only speak in speculation, based on what knowledge I have. If that's fine with you."

Echidna prefaced her answer with an air of self-importance. Her expression carried the bearing of someone grand and consequential. And yet, at the root of it, she seemed to be enjoying the mere format of answering questions so much that her foot tapped an impatient rhythm against the grass, as if unable to wait for Subaru's words, while her fingers restlessly toyed with her own hair. For the embodiment of intellectual curiosity, she was awfully easy to read—Subaru wondered, in the back of his mind, whether that was really acceptable for a witch—but pressed on anyway.

"When I do Return by Death, sometimes the point where I revive from that 'death' changes. So far it's happened… six times? Each time I've revived in a different place, at a different time. What I want to know are the conditions that make the save point shift."

"Save point… the change in the point of revival, is it."

"Basically, once it shifts, I can't go back to the previous point anymore. Say the day before yesterday was my save point—if it shifts to yesterday, I can't go back to the day-before-yesterday save point anymore. Do you have any idea what mechanism decides that?"

Subaru faced this problem with a seriousness rivaling—perhaps exceeding—the seriousness with which he treated the limit on Return by Death itself. The shifting of the save point—that was an unclear piece of the puzzle whose relevance and conditions eluded even Subaru, who had found a certain usefulness in Return by Death.

"It's not that I'm relying on the fact that I can come back even if I die—I don't intend to think it's fine to die however many times. I don't intend to, but… if it comes down to having nothing else to rely on, I'll rely on it without hesitation. But—"

Even so, when he found himself in a situation he couldn't undo, what was Subaru supposed to do?

What crossed his mind was the image of Rem, still unconscious, lying in a bed back at the mansion. Having parted from her after the battle with the White Whale, she was supposed to have rejoined him after the fight with Petelgeuse—but Subaru's Return by Death had never let him get her back. Standing before her sleeping form, driving a knife into his own throat—Subaru would never forget that.

Even now he couldn't believe he'd had it in him to take his own life so rashly, crushed by nothing more than despair. No—it was precisely because he'd lost the will to keep going that he'd killed himself. Blood welling from his throat, caught between pain and suffocation, Subaru's life had ended.

And when he opened his eyes again, he found himself flung back to mere minutes before he'd driven the knife into his own throat. Never had he hated a Return by Death save point update as much as that time.

If it meant not losing Rem, Subaru would have challenged the White Whale, the madman, any number of times over. No matter how many times he died in the process, no matter how many times he was beaten down, he would have kept challenging them.

"Could you tell me in detail about the circumstances when your save point changed?"

"Ah, right… okay. Well, starting with the very first save point—"

Recalling his memories in response to her question, Subaru gave a simple account of the course of his Return by Death experiences so far, along with the time and place he'd been sent back to each time. That meant, in effect, recounting the greater part of his life in this other world up to now. Even skipping over a good deal of the in-between stretches, telling the whole of these overwhelmingly dense two months must have taken something close to an hour, subjectively.

"So right now, the save point is fixed inside the tomb, right after I cleared the first Trial. Based on what you just told me, that holds this time too."

"………"

"Does that tell you anything? At one point I went off the deep end and came up with some crazy theory that the old man was the save point, of all things."

The scarred proprietor of a fruit stall in the royal capital, Kadomon, had somehow been selected—twice—for the role of Subaru's save point in this world. Though maybe the instance in front of Rem should also count toward that number of two.

"No matter how I look at it, I can't make it fit any condition involving people, or objects, or time. If it were time, the number of days or the length of the interval is all over the place; whether it's people or events, I can't find anything in common. The conditions are completely up in the air."

"Indeed, even from where I'm sitting, there's no commonality in the conditions you've described. If that's the case, then what's needed is a change in how we're thinking about it."

"A change in how we're thinking about it?"

"Thinking of it as 'you return there for some reason'—that approach doesn't produce an answer. So what if, instead, we think of it as: you cross that point because you've gained a reason to cross it?"

"Gained a reason… to cross it?"

"That save point of yours—depending on how you look at it, it's less a 'point of return' and more a 'wall you're returned to.' A wall that pulls you back, in the form of death, until you overcome it. A hole, if you'd rather."

At Echidna's words, Subaru knit his brow, then sank into thought over their meaning. A wall, or a hole. Through Return by Death returning him to a point of revival, Subaru had changed a number of situations he otherwise couldn't break through. That meant there had been that many situations that would have ended, unresolved, without Return by Death. And each time Return by Death changed one of those situations, the position of the save point shifted. Which meant, in other words—

"Return by Death shifts every time I change a situation that couldn't otherwise be changed…?"

"In this case, Return by Death is nothing more than a means. What intent that means is given—that comes down entirely to the thinking of whatever entity makes it happen."

"The entity that makes it happen, meaning…"

Subaru fell silent, and Echidna nodded at him. Twisting her lips in something like disgust, she said,

"There's no one but the Witch of Envy. The witch who makes you undergo Return by Death wants you to change the situations that can only be surpassed by your meeting 'death.' And every time you overcome one of those situations, she updates the save point."

"B-but… that doesn't add up. There are things that don't make sense. Because—then what about Rem? I haven't been able to save her. If Return by Death is a power meant to change situations, then why, at a time when I couldn't save Rem—"

It was Return by Death that had overwritten the save point and stripped away the chance to save Rem. It was precisely because of that fact that Subaru was so fixated on the timing of the save point update. To that question of Subaru's, Echidna responded, "I'm afraid," and continued,

"I don't think the Witch of Envy is taking into account this person you call Rem."

"………!"

"What the Witch of Envy wants is, purely and simply, for you to escape the fate of the dead end you find yourself stuck in. Return by Death is the means to that end, and it doesn't factor in the suffering of anyone besides you. Saving someone with that power—that's entirely your own wish. The witch has nothing to do with it."

"Ah…"

"So let me state it plainly."

Even as Subaru reeled under the blow, Echidna went on. Subaru looked up at the white witch with a weak light in his eyes. Echidna closed her eyes once, as if enduring pain, then pierced him with her black gaze.

"Unless you break through the problem standing in your way and change the future in which you die, your soul will, without fail, return to my tomb. But suppose—suppose you allowed a great many sacrifices, and changed the future on those terms…"

"—Then the chance to get back what was sacrificed… never comes around."

"…That's how it would work out, yes."

The only one the Witch of Envy took into consideration was Subaru himself, and Subaru alone. As long as Subaru could overcome the fate of death, everything else was, to her, a trivial matter. No matter how hopeless the deadlock, she trusted that through infinite retries, Subaru alone would eventually find a path to survival. No—even if it proved impossible, Subaru wasn't permitted to give up. Because every condition of Return by Death lay entirely in the hands of the Witch of Envy.

"Fine… if you're going to play favorites with just me like that, then my mind's made up."

"………"

"This blessing you've given me—Return by Death—I'm going to wring every last drop out of it. In the end, I'll reach you having let nothing slip through my fingers. Yeah. Decided. Settled. That's what I'll do. I'm the best there is at making sure other people's expectations come out wrong."

If Return by Death existed by the witch's will, then how it was used was up to Subaru's will. Then let him pile it up, let him overcome it, let him hold onto everything and run through to the end.

If the witch's love saved Subaru and no one else, then Subaru would save everyone else. Only by accomplishing that could Natsuki Subaru land a single blow against the witch.

"A change in circumstance… meaning breaking through both the problem of this Sanctuary and the problem at the mansion. Once those are cleared, no matter how much damage occurs, Return by Death shifts from this point on. In other words, if I'm going to clear it, holding onto absolutely everything while I get past it is a non-negotiable condition."

"It's a bit rich coming from me, but isn't that an awfully harsh requirement? You seem to have swallowed it awfully easily."

"It's just putting into words something I'd already half-suspected. Instead of breaking my spirit, it's actually got me fired up. My rebellious streak's burning bright."

Echidna fell speechless at Subaru's declaration. Watching the witch, momentarily at a loss for words, with quiet satisfaction, Subaru cracked his neck and said,

"Well, there's no squeezing anything more out of the Return by Death question. Anyway, that was a real help. Thanks."

"…I got to have a valuable conversation and satisfy my own curiosity, so on that score, we're even. It's not as if I was showing off my knowledge—I was just stating a hypothesis."

"Well then, this time I actually want to lean hard on that knowledge of yours."

On the subject of Return by Death, since it fell outside her actual knowledge, there had been a lot of ambiguity. But the next question Subaru was about to lob wasn't like that. This one, in particular, needed to be answered clearly, straight from her mouth.

"This time I did Return by Death—do you know how I died?"

"I haven't looked into that part, so no, I haven't seen it. Your firsthand account, having experienced multiple kinds of death, is extremely interesting to me… but it doesn't seem like you're in the mood for that right now."

"Talking about wounds and how I died like it's some war story—can't do that without a drink in me. And all there is here is Dona-tea, so that request's not gonna be granted."

"That's a shame. …Judging from the state of your body out there, it seems you met a death—an ending—that involved considerable suffering, both mentally and physically."

Perhaps it was her own form of consideration, avoiding the word "death." That consideration eased the tension in Subaru's cheeks for just a moment, before he tightened his expression again and nodded. "Yeah," he said.

"Gives me the shivers just remembering it, but… this time, I got eaten alive."

"………"

"Pathetically enough, by a rabbit small enough to sit in the palm of your hand. Seems it's omnivorous, and it was hungry too. Chewed through every single part of my body, no exceptions."

He'd softened his phrasing, but the sheer horror of it defied description. Fangs sinking into every inch of him, the memory of flesh, bone, and blood being torn apart still cried out from some deep wound in Subaru's mind even now. Forcibly suppressing it, Subaru somehow kept his composure. Echidna pressed the back of her hand to her lips.

"…So you encountered the Many Rabbit, is what you're saying."

"The Great Rabbit?"

"Not 'great' as in size—'many' as in number. Many Rabbit, corrupted into Great Rabbit. One of the negative legacies left behind by the Witch of Gluttony, Daphne—one of three."

"Great Rabbit, huh…"

It was a name Subaru had heard before—from Julius, and here from Echidna herself. A name for one of the Witchbeasts. The White Whale, the Black Serpent, and the Great Rabbit. All three had threatened the world for four hundred years, and all three, it was said, had once served under Daphne, the Witch of Gluttony.

"I finally take down the White Whale and now there's a Great Rabbit right after? Give me a break…"

"If the opponent is the Great Rabbit, then the opponent is simply too much."

Before Subaru, head in his hands, Echidna's expression darkened as she learned the true nature of the threat Subaru had faced. Sensing a bad feeling from her manner, Subaru asked,

"I had the same kind of dread with the White Whale too… but which one's worse?"

"If we're comparing raw combat strength, the White Whale is far superior, without question. But if we're talking about which is harder to destroy, it's unquestionably the Great Rabbit."

"Harder to destroy…"

Just as he had with the White Whale, Subaru had been thinking about how to somehow subjugate it. In response to that, Echidna raised a finger. "Listen," she said.

"You all seem to think of it the same way you'd think of an ordinary Witchbeast—just one that's a bit more troublesome than usual."

"I mean, I get that it's not the kind of thing that word 'troublesome' really suits, but…"

"The Great Rabbit is, quite literally, what you might call a natural disaster. It always moves in swarms, a mass of pure instinct driven by nothing but insatiable appetite. Every living creature is prey to them, and they do nothing but devour that prey—nothing else at all. Where the Great Rabbit has passed, all that remains is an empty, uninhabited field. No damage to crops, no damage to fruit—none whatsoever. What it devours is living creatures, and living creatures alone."

Hearing Echidna's description of the Great Rabbit, Subaru swallowed hard, his throat clicking. Echidna's expression was grim, and Subaru already knew her words weren't exaggeration.

The empty, uninhabited Sanctuary. The swarm of tiny rabbits that had sunk their teeth into every inch of his body. If that had been the Great Rabbit, then the Sanctuary's emptiness could only mean the Witchbeast had devoured every single resident who'd been there. Emilia, Ram, Ryuzu, Roswaal—not even Garfiel, transformed into the great tiger, would be an exception.

Locust plague—the word suddenly crossed Subaru's mind. A locust plague referred to the phenomenon commonly called a locust swarm outbreak. Strictly speaking, it wasn't locusts but a swarm of grasshoppers that had undergone a special mutation; the swarm, having proliferated en masse, would devour fields, ruin crops, and cause famine—a recognized form of disaster.

What the Great Rabbit did bore a resemblance to what Subaru knew of that. Though unlike grasshoppers, what the Great Rabbit devoured was living creatures themselves, and its bottomless appetite posed a far more direct threat to life than any locust plague.

"Is there any way to drive them off somehow?"

"Individually, none of them amount to much in strength, but the whole premise of the thing is that it acts in swarms. It doesn't matter how many individuals you chase off, and above all, once it's spotted prey, it won't stop until it either eats or dies. It's 'appetite' itself, essentially."

"Wait, wait, wait. There's gotta be—like, if you're saying the Great Rabbit moves in swarms, there's gotta be some boss controlling the swarm. If we take that down, wouldn't the swarm collapse?"

By the rules of human society, take out the head and the group falls apart. Going by the rules of the animal kingdom, maybe all that happens is the number two moves up to number one. Either way, Subaru wasn't well-versed in the ecology of Witchbeasts. He had no choice but to rely on Echidna, who was. But she shook her head.

"You seem to be under a misconception—the Great Rabbit is a swarm, but there's no concept of a 'boss' among them. All that exists in them is truly just the instinct called 'appetite.' Devouring other living things to satisfy hunger—there's no other mechanism at work whatsoever. They increase by individuals splitting to multiply, and when there's no prey around, they stave off hunger by eating each other. They don't even have a sense of fellowship."

"Eating each other… wh-what in the world did anyone create a monster like that for!?"

"Well, that's something only the one who created it would know. Though at the very least, Daphne herself was never as fixated on appetite as the Great Rabbit is."

Subaru shuddered at the abominable nature of its ecology. Certainly, it's the principle of all living things to follow instinct and devour other creatures to sustain their own lives. On that point, there's likely no difference between humans, animals, or even Witchbeasts. But the Great Rabbit didn't even have that—individuals split and multiplied, yet there was no mating, no producing of offspring. Worse, having multiplied for the sake of increasing the swarm, it then devoured its own split-off individuals to satisfy its hunger—an arrangement utterly divorced from reason.

The Great Rabbit was a monster whose potential as a living creature had, completely and utterly, run its course.

"Suppose… yeah, suppose we wanted to destroy the Great Rabbit—what do you think we'd need to do?"

"Honestly, that strikes me as the same kind of question as asking how one might evaporate every single raindrop falling from the sky."

Presumably meaning it was that hopelessly out of reach. At Echidna's answer, Subaru put a hand to his forehead, feeling dizzy at the sheer height of the wall standing between him and defeating the Great Rabbit.

"If you kill every single one, then no matter how many Great Rabbits there are, they can be destroyed."

"But that's a wildly reckless thing to attempt. As far as I know, the only ones who could plausibly pull that off are Reid Astrea and Sekhmet. Reid would slaughter every last one with his overwhelming swordsmanship. Sekhmet would... well, it's a matter of compatibility, but she'd have wiped them out in a second."

"Those two, I've heard about before, but they really are monsters for real…"

He pictured Sekhmet, whom he'd once been allowed to sit on the lap of. As far as Subaru had seen, she'd left nothing but the impression of someone gloomy, sluggish in every motion—precisely the sort of witch who suited the name 'Sloth.'

"Wait, a—"

Recalling that encounter with Sekhmet, a faint spark of hope suddenly lit within Subaru. It was a thin, fleeting light, but it was enough to illuminate the darkness of the dead end—and, more than anything, it was something he absolutely could not do without borrowing Echidna's power, here and now.

"Echidna."

"What is it, with such a serious face? …No, that won't do. I'm one of the dead, and you're among the living. The crossing of our fates here is a fleeting illusion, a bubble of a dream. So no matter how ardently you gaze at me, I—"

"No, uh, sorry to interrupt when you're getting into it, but that's not it at all. Really, not even close."

Somewhat deflated, if Subaru wasn't mistaken, by that, Echidna's spirits seemed to sag a little, and Subaru, taking the wind out of her sails without meaning to, got back to the actual matter at hand.

"Echidna, I want you to let me meet the Witch of Gluttony—Daphne."

"………"

"Before, at this tea party, you let me meet the Witch of Pride, the Witch of Wrath, and the Witch of Sloth. At the time, you said something about how you had all five witches stored, or whatever it was."

"…Collected, is the word. Their traces from when they were alive, all five of them, do indeed exist within me. If I bring that down into this vessel that is myself, their manifestation here is indeed possible. Possible, but—"

At Subaru's proposal, Echidna knit her brows in evident trouble. Her gaze wandered, and then, looking as though it was hard to say, she glanced at Subaru sidelong.

"I think it would be best if you didn't meet Daphne."

"Why not? I mean, sure, I got a good enough taste of how difficult these witches can be last time, but I'm about as braced for it as I'm gonna get. Whatever comes out, I won't hold it against you."

"It's not even a matter of holding it against me or not—in my judgment, you and Daphne are an extremely poor match. I'd even call it the worst possible match. So even if you were to meet face to face, I doubt any useful conversation could—"

"Only one way to find out. Trial and error—that's a phrase you're fond of, isn't it?"

Taking action in order to obtain an answer—that was something Echidna herself had affirmed as good. At Subaru's words, Echidna made a face as though struck in a sore spot, letting out an "Ugh…" Scratching his head, Subaru pressed on. "And besides,"

"You're the one who gave me your seal of approval that at least in this place, I don't have to worry about my life. I'm not trying to be careless about it, but if I at least don't have to worry about dying, then I figured there's nothing to lose in giving it a shot."

"I haven't ruled out the possibility of your mind breaking and you ending up an empty husk, you know?"

"I trust you'll pull the plug before it gets that far. C'mon, Echidna. When it comes to leaning on other people, I'm the best there is."

Tossing his trust her way with a glib little remark, Subaru flashed his teeth and gave a thumbs up. At Subaru's ever so frivolous manner, the resistance in Echidna's eyes gradually faded, until at last—

"…Understood. I'll let you meet Daphne."

"Alright, thanks."

"Just, let me say this much. Never, under any circumstances, undo her restraints. Also, touching her is forbidden. If possible, I'd like you to avoid even making eye contact."

"If I follow all of that I'm gonna come across as, like, deathly rude to her!"

To begin with, several words in there had been impossible to just let slide. Subaru opened his mouth to press her on them—but before he could, Echidna's preparations were already complete.

Just as before, when Echidna brought a witch down into herself, there was no preliminary motion, no sign of any kind whatsoever. In the space between one blink and the next, a different person now occupied the space where she had been.

And this time was no different. But—

"Hey, now… this is, no matter how you slice it…"

Faced with the presence that had appeared before him, Subaru's cheeks twitched as he muttered those words.

Right in front of him, the Witch of Gluttony, Daphne, stood before Subaru.

—A witch-girl, shut inside a coffin, her entire body bound tight in restraints, both eyes sealed fast beneath a thick black blindfold.
