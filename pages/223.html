Arc 4 Chapter 57: "The Immortality Experiment"

—Immortality. Across every era and every land, in stories without number, it is the one ideal that occurs to any living thing that can think at all.

To never age, never decay, never be caught up in the wheel of reincarnation, and to go on existing, forever, as "oneself." Even knowing it defies the very logic of life, it remains the pinnacle so many individual lives can't help but be enchanted by.

"Immortality... huh."

Subaru murmured the word again, as if testing it, and almost laughed at how utterly unreal it sounded. But his cheeks only twitched; he couldn't manage a proper smile.

Part of him wanted to laugh the whole thing off as ridiculous. But the part of him that knew, as plain fact, that the Witch's experiment was no mere fairy tale, couldn't hide its shudder.

"Guess even a Witch can have surprisingly mundane goals. Immortality's the kind of thing you'd expect some small-minded type clinging to their own tiny little life to chase after."

"Whether clinging to one's life makes one small-minded is a matter of personal taste, but at the very least, it seems the Witch of Greed hardly took some detached, enlightened view of her own life. She feared death same as anyone, and took measures to overcome it. ...In most cases, that would end as nothing more than an idle dream, for lack of power and lack of skill."

"The trouble is, Echidna had more than enough skill. She probably could've thought up any number of ways to do it. And what that clever head of hers landed on was—this."

Subaru looked down at Pico, seated beside him, and bit his lip against some feeling he couldn't quite name.

Pico, looked down upon, showed no reaction whatsoever to Subaru's gaze. She simply waited, silent, in a kind of standby state, as if for some word to be spoken to her.

Watching her, Subaru let out a breath that was almost a laugh.

"I see. No insides means... no personality. That's what it means."

"A puppet-like state, essentially. Precisely the state of a blank vessel, prepared and waiting. All that remained was to fill it with whatever she wished, and her long-cherished wish would be granted."

"But does that actually work, in practice? I mean, I don't get the theory, but roughly, I get the idea of what she was trying to do."

Downloading one's own memories and knowledge into an empty vessel, and overwriting it.

If this were just data of some kind, even Subaru wouldn't feel this much revulsion toward it.

But what was being discussed here was the personality of an actual human being. And to Subaru, both the appearance and the contents belonged to someone he knew.

"Extract your memories from yourself, pour them into an empty body. If it works, and you can repeat it every time the old body's about to give out—yeah, that's a kind of immortality, all right. But..."

Personality, memory, and the possibility of passing them on—that really was close to overcoming death.

If you could preserve a personality like data, then even if the vessel were broken by some accident, you could simply install it fresh into another vessel and be revived.

A duplicable personality, and a duplicable body. —Echidna's immortality was, in theory, achievable.

—But if that were so.

"There's a story that when a person meets their parallel-world self, they panic, and get gripped by this sense of duty that they absolutely have to eliminate the other one."

"............"

"That's how unbearable the sensation of another 'you' existing must be. Just imagining it, I can already picture a future gross enough to make me sick. Right, Ryuzu-san?"

"What is it."

"The fact that you can make multiple... Ryuzu Meyer bodies like this—that means you could stuff the same number of personalities into multiple bodies. Which means, it's not just about making 'yourself' last forever. You could make multiple copies of yourself too."

Going by the theory just discussed, that should be possible as well.

And if you followed the "theory" alone to its conclusion, then when it came to backups and spares to keep "yourself" going forever, the more the better.

Subaru couldn't believe that something he'd thought of, the Witch wouldn't have thought of too.

"I wonder what that'd feel like. Having spares of yourself ready to go, like that. Having the 'insurance' that even if you fail, it'll work out somehow. Can you understand that, Ryuzu-san?"

"...That much, I suppose I could never understand, not in all eternity. The technique for extracting a personality is beyond my ken. I, as an individual, would vanish the moment this one body of mine were lost. In that sense, this one, and you too, Su-bou, are no different—neither of us gets a redo."

"Yeah. That's right, isn't it. ...Ah, of course it is."

At the words Ryuzu let slip, Subaru couldn't hold back a dry laugh. Ryuzu frowned slightly at his reaction, but she would never understand its true meaning.

"So that's it. Ah, I get it now. Now I sort of understand why you were so friendly with me."

Subaru murmured that thought toward the white-haired Witch floating up in his mind.

Preparing a duplicate of herself, transferring her personality into it, prolonging her life indefinitely—that scheme for immortality. To do such a thing was nothing less than attaching "insurance" to one's own life, and—

"Then how exactly is that any different from what I'm doing right now?"

There was no way he could feel disgust at it.

If anything, he felt something closer to kinship. A feeling too shameful to really call kinship—perhaps it was the dark pleasure of finding one of his own kind.

Echidna, who in her own way had arrived at a fragment of immortality.

Subaru, tossed about at the Witch's whim, repeating "death" over and over to accomplish his goal.

Both of them, in the end, stood in rebellion against a "life" that was supposed to be singular, one and only.

If that was so, Subaru thought—

—if that was so, wasn't Echidna the only one who could possibly understand him?

At the very least, if there were anyone who could understand that particular cast of mind, it would be—

"Su-bou?"

"...I understand your position, Ryuzu-san. And I get what Echidna was trying to do, too. So with that said, I want to ask—did Echidna's plan succeed?"

"Her plan..."

"The empty vessels being ready—I can see with my own eyes that they exist. All that's left is overwriting them with her own personality. Did that overwrite succeed? Or, to put it more simply..."

—Was Echidna, somewhere in this world, still alive right now?

The question Subaru couldn't quite put into words.

Ryuzu, understanding its substance, closed her eyes, and shook her head at Subaru's pleading gaze.

Slowly, from side to side.

"No, I'm afraid... the Witch's plan did not succeed. There exists no body of Ryuzu Meyer that inherited the Witch's personality and carried her life onward."

"Wh-why not? Did the download—the extraction of the personality—not work?"

"I don't know what this 'download' is, but the technique itself, for extracting a personality, should have been complete. What failed was something else entirely."

"Something else, like..."

"It's a simple matter. Pour more water into a vessel than it can hold, and the water will overflow. Once part of it has spilled out, what remains can no longer be called the same thing it was."

At the word "vessel," Subaru looked at Ryuzu, and then at Pico. Taking in their small, slight frames, he said—

"Vessel... I mean, this isn't about the size of the body, is it."

"Perhaps it should be called the size of the soul. Every person has a container suited to their soul. To accept the soul of the Witch called Echidna, the vessel of the girl Ryuzu Meyer simply wasn't large enough."

"That... how did she confirm that?"

"She tried to pour her knowledge into the first duplicate, and failed—that was the first time she confronted the problem of the size of soul against vessel. By that point, though, Ryuzu Meyer's own body was already inside the crystal, and the structure for producing duplicate after duplicate was already built. So even though her original purpose couldn't be fulfilled, vessels kept on being born, one after another."

Subaru thought, surprisingly, that there'd been some awfully thoughtless goings-on here.

For someone like Echidna, it seemed a remarkably careless oversight at the crucial step. And the fact that she'd done nothing at all about the Ryuzus continuing to multiply afterward struck him as even more out of character for her.

"So what happened to that first duplicate? Even if it couldn't all fit, some part of it must have inherited the Witch's memories, right? Even incomplete, couldn't you call that a copy of the Witch?"

"When you pour water into a vessel until it overflows, can anyone choose which part spills out? If it were some trivial memory that doesn't interfere with daily life that overflowed, that would be one thing. But if it's the part that causes serious problems that spills out, then what remains no longer constitutes a personality at all."

At Ryuzu's roundabout words, Subaru thought of the failed first duplicate—the first Ryuzu=Echidna. In other words, some entirely different "something," far from what the Witch had intended, had been born, and—

"From what I've heard, the first duplicate born was a complete failure as a personality—utterly broken—and precisely because it had inherited a fragment of the Witch of Greed's power, it was a troublesome thing indeed. Disposing of it was apparently quite an ordeal."

"Disposing... I see."

"Of course, the Witch was neither so quick to give up nor so irresponsible as to throw everything away after a single failure. After disposing of the first duplicate, I'm told she struggled to find a way to alter the total quantity of the soul, so as to transfer her personality into the next duplicate."

"The fact that idea even occurred to her, in terms of the soul—that's incredible."

What Echidna had conceived of, essentially, was nothing other than the idea of compressing a large amount of data to transfer it to another storage medium. Or perhaps trimming away unnecessary parts beforehand to fit the available capacity on the receiving end.

Subaru, who'd had at least some contact with computers and understood the concept of data, could arrive at that idea. But that Echidna, with no knowledge of exchanging data via computers, and moreover conceiving of it on the dimension of something called a "soul," could reach the same idea—that was staggering.

When he'd heard that the first duplicate had been lost, Subaru had honestly felt a pang of disappointment. But learning right afterward that Echidna had been trying another approach, he'd seen a glimmer of hope in that.

But then Ryuzu, prefacing with a "however," said to him—

"Even as she groped for a new method, the Witch never got the chance to try it."

"Wh-why not? I mean, no offense, but the groundwork for testing it was all there, wasn't it? There were already several copies of Ryuzu Meyer—"

"Because before she could continue her experiments in the 'Sanctuary,' the Witch of Envy made her move."

"——"

"The Witch of Envy devoured half the world, and in the process, consumed all six of the other Witches besides herself. The Witch of Greed was no exception. The Witch's scheme to sustain her life through immortality was cut short—by the hand of none other than another Witch."

Subaru already knew the fate of the six Witches other than Envy.

In that castle of dreams, Echidna had spoken of them, and even let him meet them directly. All of them had been destroyed by the Witch of Envy, mere remnants of a dream left behind after vanishing from this world.

Perhaps that lingering presence, keeping only her spirit in this world in that way, had been Echidna's last stubborn defiance—her way of clinging to something, having failed to achieve true immortality.

"...What happened to the 'Sanctuary' after the Witch disappeared?"

"From the start, the management of this land was entrusted to Roz-bou's House Mathers. What sort of contract was made between House Mathers and the Witch, this one does not know. That arrangement continued on down through the years, and now it's Roz-bou who maintains and manages this 'Sanctuary.' Though what Roz-bou actually does amounts to keeping enough goods flowing that the place doesn't die out, and occasionally bringing in a child whose circumstances suit the 'Sanctuary,' as a new resident."

"So everything else on the inside is left entirely to you, Ryuzu-san. You said before that the individuality you've gained is what makes you an individual, but..."

"As far as the constraint of being a duplicate goes, I am, from the start, the third duplicate. I was born with a personality planted in me beforehand, so as to manage the ever-multiplying vessels and inherit Ryuzu Meyer's role in the 'Sanctuary.' Even now, I act in accordance with that role."

"Planting a personality... is that even something that's possible?"

Planting a pseudo-personality into an empty vessel, so it could fulfill a role, was it. Giving a robot a pseudo-AI so it could behave like a human being—even in the world Subaru had come from, that was far from practical reality, nothing more than the stuff of imagination.

In response, Ryuzu nodded.

"It was hardly an easy thing, mind you. It was possible only precisely because it was an empty vessel with no soul present. And even then, in the experimental stage, it seems only the simplest of things could be managed."

"It was a struggle at first," Ryuzu said, a faint smile touching the corner of her mouth.

"Having a role given to you without any memories—it's a peculiar sensation. The days pass slowly, and yet with tremendous speed all at once. It took quite some time before I could even come to think of it as peculiar."

"...What about the duplicates that keep multiplying? Aside from this Pico and you, Ryuzu-san, I've never seen any others around the 'Sanctuary.'"

"Aside from the four who fulfill the role of Ryuzu, the other Ryuzus are scattered throughout the 'Sanctuary.' Given the function of eyes against intruders, and of relaying messages. Between duplicates of the same origin, interestingly enough, thought can be transmitted."

Subaru recalled Garfiel once mentioning something about being the "eyes" of the "Sanctuary." If that referred to Ryuzu's duplicates, scattered about to keep watch over the surroundings of the "Sanctuary," then it made sense that Garfiel had so easily seen through the mass evacuation of the Arlam Village refugees that day. Having gotten that far in his thoughts—

"Wh-wait, hold on, there's something I can't just let slide here. What do you mean, the four who fulfill the role of Ryuzu?"

"Hmm, that's the part that caught your ear? That's simple enough too. Even for a duplicate, sustaining the entire structure of a human body with Mana continuously places an enormous burden on it. When the Mana runs out, the body vanishes. Unlike Spirits, once it disperses, there's no reconstructing it. Or perhaps there is some way—this one simply doesn't know of it."

Subaru recalled the existence of Spirits, who could repeatedly vanish and reconstitute themselves, retaining their memories each time. Though in their case, since there existed an Anchor—a place to "return" to after vanishing—their vanishing wasn't, strictly speaking, a true disappearance. But Ryuzu and the others had no such thing. Which meant death by exhaustion of Mana meant the true death of that individual.

"One of me can only remain active for so long. Once the body's pushed past the point where it can move any further, it takes roughly three days to recover the Mana that was lost. During that time, it would be a problem if Ryuzu Meyer simply ceased to exist within the 'Sanctuary'—that must be avoided."

"So that's why there are four Ryuzu-sans."

"We take turns, one a day, so the role of Ryuzu Meyer comes back around every four days. In every other respect, this one is no different from any of the other vessels. ...A hollow vessel that merely bears the name of Ryuzu Meyer on its back. It might be fair to put it that way."

At Ryuzu's self-mocking words, Subaru found himself at a loss for what to say. Anything he might say felt hollow, like mere pretending to understand. And yet, even knowing that staying silent here would only amount to affirming Ryuzu's own bitter self-mockery, his lips could not form the words.

"Don't let it weigh on you, Su-bou. This one, and the other duplicates as well, accept this role willingly. The same holds true for the very first Ryuzu Meyer."

"The first one... Right, that's something else I wanted to ask about."

"Hm?"

"I understand why you—the duplicates of Ryuzu Meyer—would follow the Witch's words, or want to protect the 'Sanctuary.' But the girl, the original Ryuzu Meyer—why did she cooperate with the Witch in the first place?"

A girl sealed away inside a crystal, robbed of eternity.

Judging by everything he'd heard so far, the attempts to pour Ryuzu Meyer herself into an empty vessel had never been part of the plan. Which meant Ryuzu Meyer had offered up her own body for the experiment, and chosen to let her own soul—her very self—come to an end inside a crystal with nowhere left to go.

That was a decision tantamount to suicide: giving up her soul, her very self, forever, in exchange for endlessly generating an external shell of flesh.

How could that young girl have made such a decision?

Or had the Witch simply chosen her test subject arbitrarily, without ever obtaining the girl's consent?

Praying it wasn't the latter, Subaru asked his question.

"What was Ryuzu Meyer thinking, when she agreed to take part in this experiment?"

"...I'm told that Ryuzu Meyer proposed a trade to the Witch, and that she agreed to participate in the experiment because the Witch accepted those terms. There's no need to worry—it was not done against her will."

"A trade... Can I ask what it was?"

"You may ask, but I doubt you'd understand it even so, Su-bou."

Subaru gave Ryuzu a silent look at her air of holding something back. At his stubborn, childlike stare, Ryuzu grimaced and let out a deep sigh.

"The condition Ryuzu Meyer set before the Witch—it was the survival of the 'Sanctuary.'"

"The survival... of the 'Sanctuary'?"

"That this experimental ground, this 'Sanctuary' the Witch had prepared as a test site, this environment, should be preserved and continue on—that was Ryuzu Meyer's wish. Naturally, since the Witch needed the 'Sanctuary' to remain in order to continue her own experiments, she readily agreed. And even now, with the Witch gone, the promise made to Ryuzu Meyer remains kept—the pact between the two of them is upheld still, by our own hands."

"No, but wait, that's... that's backwards, the order of it."

The "Sanctuary" had existed because Echidna needed it for her experiments, and Ryuzu Meyer was supposed to be the one gathered here to be used in them. And yet that very girl, the test subject, had asked the Witch to preserve the "Sanctuary." The sequence simply didn't line up.

"Even treated as a test subject... the 'Sanctuary' was still a far more comfortable place to be than the land where she'd been persecuted. How does that thought sit with you?"

"...That's... that's far too bleak to call any kind of salvation."

"The salvation lies right here. That is precisely why Ryuzu Meyer offered up her body to the experiment. Whether that bore fruit or not is something you'll simply have to judge for yourself, by looking at this one, and at that girl there."

Subaru could find no words to follow as Ryuzu sipped at her now-cold tea.

Beside him, Pico showed no reaction at all, even once the topic of her own circumstances had been thoroughly discussed. She simply sat there in silence, pinching the hem of Subaru's clothes.

"Why is this kid so attached to me, anyway? I thought there was supposed to be nothing inside, and at first she treated me like I wasn't even there."

"Ah, that would be because Su-bou touched Ryuzu Meyer's crystal. The command authority was rewritten, and Su-bou got overwritten in, most likely."

"Command authority...?"

Yet another unfamiliar term made Subaru frown. Nodding at his suspicious look, Ryuzu raised a single finger and said, "There, now."

"Try giving that girl some kind of order, as a test. Oh, but nothing indecent, mind you. She looks just the same as this one, after all."

"You don't have to tell me that, I'm not gonna get turned on by a loli body, okay? I like girls my own age, in a perfectly healthy way. ...Pico, rub my shoulders a little."

Answering Ryuzu with a sullen look, Subaru then gave that order to Pico beside him. Hearing his voice, the girl raised her face, gave a faint dip of her chin in acknowledgment of Subaru's command, climbed up onto the bed, and moved around behind him.

"Oh, o-oh, that's nice, that's nice... wait? Hold on, Pico-san? That's really strong! Ease up, ease up... whoa, that's bad, Pico-san, ease uuuup!!"

"She knows the concept of rubbing shoulders, but the right amount of force is still unknown territory for her. Things like this need to be learned by experience, or blunders like that will keep happening."

"Y-you knew this would happen and tested me anyway, didn't you!?"

Shaking off Pico, who kept right on trying to knead his shoulders, and directing her back to her spot, Subaru rolled his creaking shoulder in a big circle. Shuddering at the memory of Pico's grip strong enough to nearly crack bone, Subaru tilted his head and said, "Still, though—"

"Just touching it transfers command authority or whatever? Isn't the security on that thing way too lax? What happens if some ill-intentioned Lolicon wanders in there?"

"Stumbling into that place could hardly happen by accident, and more importantly, the transfer of command authority isn't so simple a thing either. At the very least, one must first be recognized as an Apostle of Greed."

"...Hnn?"

Yet another something he couldn't let slide had slipped into Ryuzu's words as she tilted her teacup, for what felt like the umpteenth time now. Crossing his arms, Subaru said, tentatively, "Um—"

"Sorry, but I have no idea what this Apostle of Greed thing is."

"Being recognized by Echidna—that is the qualification of an Apostle. Within the mausoleum, did something of that sort happen to you? Were you given something, granted something, had something put into your body?"

"Within the mausoleum..."

Subaru recalled his encounter with Echidna in that castle of dreams, trying to think whether there had been some solemn handing-over of the kind Ryuzu described. But nothing came to mind.

What Subaru had received from Echidna in that place amounted to a bit of knowledge, some measure of comfort, and a considerable amount of terror. And—

"...Don't tell me it's that Dona-tea thing."

"Hm, Dona-tea?"

"Echidna slipped me something, claiming it was her own bodily fluid, disguised cleverly as tea, and I ended up drinking it. Twice."

"No joke intended—that would be it, most likely."

"That bastard, what the hell did she actually make me drink!!"

Ryuzu soothed Subaru with a "now, now" as he shot to his feet in fury. To Subaru, seething with indignation, she said—

"Even so, it's precisely because of that, that this situation exists now. It wasn't entirely a bad thing, was it?"

"What ticks me off is that she pulled something like that behind my back! Doing that to someone's body without telling them. An Apostle of Greed, on top of everything—things are already complicated enough with the Witches as it is, don't go piling more on. Every single Witch, every one of them..."

Between the Witch of Envy who'd finished the Return by Death Contract without his even knowing, and the Witch of Greed who'd added him to her Apostles without so much as asking—was every single Witch like this?

"In any case, that is how Su-bou came to hold command authority over the duplicates of Ryuzu Meyer here in the 'Sanctuary.' It works even on this one—you can make even me obey."

"Does it actually work on you too, Ryuzu-san?"

"More resistant than those girls with no will of their own, but ultimately, this one cannot defy it either. Well? Wouldn't a boy your age be delighted by that?"

"I told you, I'm not a Lolicon..."

Even with such a suggestive look turned his way, he didn't so much as twitch.

Watching Ryuzu chuckle merrily out of the corner of his eye, Subaru turned back over the mysteries of the "Sanctuary" now brought to light.

A facility quietly hidden away deep within. Ryuzu Meyer sealed inside it, and the system that produced her duplicates. The destruction set to occur six days from now, and the problems attached to it.

And what was indispensable in discussing the very existence of that facility—

"Ryuzu-san, sorry to spring this on you, but... I need a bit of your help."

"What is it? If it's something lewd, better ask the more innocent ones than this one."

"That's enough of that already."

Dismissing Ryuzu, who still seemed bent on turning him into some hormonal teenager, Subaru stood, straightened his back, and looked up at the ceiling.

"Command authority—there's at least one other person besides me who has it, right?"

"——"

"There's someone I want to hear from about that, but there's one more thing bugging me too."

Two people rose up in his mind.

One was Garfiel Tinzel, the great tiger who guarded the "Sanctuary," who had been giving orders to as many as twenty of Ryuzu's duplicates.

And the other—

"Why did you send me through the Door into that facility? I think it's about time I got an answer to that..."

Subaru resolved himself to seize the chance to press that question—to the girl with the cream-colored, curly hair.
