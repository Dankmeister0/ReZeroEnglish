Arc 6 Chapter 9: "Cross the Sand Hour"

The first day's assault on the Augria Sand Dunes was called off the moment the sun went down.

Once midnight drew near, the longest of the three daily "Sand Hours" would descend. Forcing a path through the miasma-laden sand during that time risked having one's body eaten away, or losing the way entirely.

With the Pleiades Watchtower — their one landmark — no longer visible even in the distance, it was wiser to spend the night quietly, recovering strength and gathering energy for the morrow.

"By the way, kind of a late question, but does the witchbeast countermeasure keep working while we're asleep?"

"Of course it does. It'd be too embarrassing to laugh off if you got your bottom bitten by the very creatures you brought along yourself."

Having witnessed the ferocity of the witchbeasts of the Augria Sand Dunes, Subaru was more than a little — well, thoroughly — gun-shy. Meili, delighted at his cowering, was riding high.

Perhaps it was the joy of having her usefulness proven, but ever since the sand-worm incident she'd been visibly in good spirits. Subaru, for his part, had come to realize that keeping her happy was worth risking his life over, and resolved to put real effort into it from here on.

In any case, night had fallen.

For the time being, using a stretch of open sand within the dunes as a base, Subaru and the others began preparing sand defenses centered on the dragon carriage. Though "preparing" made it sound grander than it was — the burden fell mostly on one person, but the method itself was simple enough.

"So, Emilia-tan. I know I keep asking, but if you would."

"Sure, just step back a bit. — Here goes!"

With the same motion as planting a tower of ice, Emilia let out an unenthused little shout and cast her magic. A wall of ice rose up, covering the eastern side of the parked dragon carriage, functioning as a shield against the sandy wind and shutting the sand out completely.

There was no worry of the ice melting, and it also served as protection against minor witchbeasts. The cold — both in appearance and in feel — was a problem, but if one overlooked that, the effect was more than sufficient.

"Continuing to rely on Emilia-sama's power like this sits rather poorly with me."

"It's fine. I told you, didn't I? You and Subaru are working hard while we're on the move. And like I said, I'm feeling really, really good right now."

"Normally one's stomach turns from the miasma, they say — which makes me worry all the more, in its own way."

Julius sighed, glancing at the ice wall, and Emilia made a fist to show off a bicep. Catching sight of her soft, white upper arm out of the corner of his eye, Subaru thought he'd better keep an eye on Emilia's condition too.

In truth, the air of the dunes, said to be steeped in miasma, carried a distinct "heaviness." That weight seemed to be acting on Subaru's body in some indefinable way, and it was likely responsible for Beatrice's and Julius's uncharacteristic quiet. Even Anastasia's and Ram's silence inside the carriage might have something to do with it.

The two who seemed unaffected, if anything in good spirits, were Emilia and the ever-unchanging Meili.

There didn't seem to be anything the two of them had in common, but with the effects showing up this unevenly, vigilance couldn't be spared. If Subaru was honest, he wanted to get through the dunes as quickly as possible.

"Impatience is forbidden. Not that I can't understand your wish to hurry along the way."

"——"

Julius spoke up to Subaru, who stood in silence gazing up at the eastern sky blocked by the ice wall. Feeling as though his thoughts had been read, Subaru snorted and turned his back on the ice wall. Just then, Patrasche, who had drawn near, pressed her nose against Subaru's shoulder.

One after another, it seemed — Subaru gave a wry laugh.

"So you're trying to calm me down too, huh?"

"Ben wasn't trying to do any such thing, she supposes. Though perhaps she wasn't quick enough to beat someone to it."

Beatrice, who had been pressing her back against him and looking up meaningfully from below, turned her face away.

Was his anxiety really that obvious? Subaru scratched his head. Looking over, he found Emilia watching him with gentle eyes too, and when their gazes met she gave him a small wave.

Suddenly embarrassed, Subaru clapped his hands.

"Alright! Thanks to Emilia-tan we've got a place to sleep secured, so let's get ready for tomorrow. The 'Sand Hour' ends toward dawn, so before then..."

"Before that, we need to treat Ram."

"Ah, right. Right. Okay, Emilia-tan, Bea-ko, if you would."

Trying to talk fast and slide past the topic, Subaru was stopped by Emilia, intent on carrying out her daily task. At Subaru's request, Emilia and Beatrice nodded, and the treatment began with the men left outside.

The reason the men were left outside while the treatment took place was —

"— Nn."

Though it came through the door, the faint sound leaking out made it clear the treatment had begun.

The voice, trembling as if stifling pain, feverish in some way, belonged to Ram. Inside the carriage, a faint light rose as mana combining all four elements was used to treat her.

Mana poured into the wound of her lost horn — Ram's condition as she received treatment was pitiful, and yet, undeniably, there was something faintly sensual about it too.

Which was why, ever since witnessing the treatment on the first day, the men had declined to be present.

"Time and again, I can't understand what Roswaal was thinking, trying to make you do that."

"It is, in a sense, an honor of a role, without question. Lady Ram is charming and lovely. Though considering whom her eyes are so plainly fixed upon, I can't bring myself to be entirely glad of it."

"Roswaal's gotta know that too. So why does he do stuff like this?... In this case, is the question why Ram puts up with it even though she's treated like that?"

"The bonds between one person and another cannot always be measured from the outside. All the more so when it comes to the relationship between a man and a woman. To put it in extreme terms — your first meeting with me could hardly be called a good one either, could it? Yet who would have guessed we'd one day stand shoulder to shoulder on a life-or-death journey like this."

"...Well, that's true, I guess."

At the alluring sighs of the treatment sounding from behind him, Subaru rubbed his nose, cheeks faintly reddening. Julius wore a composed expression, but it was more than a little stimulating for a pure-hearted young man. Still, they couldn't afford to wander off and be sniffed out by dangerous witchbeasts. No helping it. It was necessary.

"My my, I can practically see Natsuki-kun's inner conflict."

"WAAAAH!?"

While quietly straining his ears, breath was suddenly blown into one of them, and Subaru jumped. Losing his footing, he tumbled onto the sand, and saw Anastasia had come around from behind the dragon carriage. Julius, who'd been on guard at Subaru's flustered state, broke into a grin at the sight of Anastasia and looked over at Subaru sprawled on the ground.

"Aren't you being a touch too startled? As though you'd been thinking of something wicked."

"Huh? I wasn't thinking anything wicked, okay? Wait, I have no idea what you're talking about. No idea at all. Not the slightest clue. So, Anastasia-san, what did you need? I'll answer anything!"

"Aren'tcha just terrible at hiding it? Well, whatever. Natsuki-kun might be dyin' to know what's going on inside, but I've got somethin' important to talk about."

Shaking off Julius's deeply contemptuous gaze, Subaru gave Anastasia a thumbs up. She gave a wry smile at the reaction, planted her boots in the sand, and —

"Ugh, this is hard to walk in. How are you two so fine with it?"

"We've trained for it. Though I won't go so far as to say it was for this very moment."

"When your footing's uncertain, don't try to test it by stepping slow and careful — plant your foot with some real force and trust the sole of your boot. That's the Clind style."

It was a basic technique for running rough terrain that he'd learned from Clind, the all-purpose butler, during his parkour training.

Anastasia nodded, impressed, and then pulled down the collar of the robe she wore, exposing the lower half of her hidden face. She let out a deep breath.

"I get why the sand's a problem, but it feels like it's gonna choke me. I'd like to get somewhere free of the sand wind soon and take a real deep breath."

"Same here. Also, I want a bath. My head's already caked in sand."

It made sense why desert peoples wrapped turbans around their heads. Measures against sand, and against heat and cold — the way people who lived on this harsh land did things was, naturally, quite reasonable. Subaru had wrapped some cloth around himself too, but it wasn't enough to fully block it out.

"A bath, agreed. Wouldn't it be nice if the 'Sage's' house had a bath?"

"...I take it you don't actually know that much, then?"

"Echidna might know the way there, but it's not like I've ever actually been inside."

Anastasia pulled the robe's collar down further, revealing the fox stole she wore without fail. That Eridna would serve as their guide for the journey was the fundamental premise of this whole expedition.

There was no use doubting her, but it was something worth nailing down all the same.

"This 'important talk' — I can take it that it's about what comes next, right?"

"Of course."

"We shall listen with due respect."

Anastasia puffed out her modest chest at Subaru and Julius as they settled into a listening posture. Taking this in, she turned back toward the dragon carriage with an air of great importance.

"Startin' tonight we're sleepin' in there, but... you two boys are gonna have to sleep up front on the driver's seat, y'know. Just 'cause you're crammed in close with us girls doesn't mean you get to get carried away."

"Enough of that already!"

"Aw, I was just tryin' to ease the tension... well, whatever."

To begin with, sleeping in the same room hardly mattered one way or another at this point. Subaru's own lack of courage, and the other party's lack of any wariness, were both already thoroughly established.

In any case, setting aside Subaru's inner scream, Anastasia got down to business.

"So, today, walkin' toward the tower like this for a whole day... how'd it feel? Any progress?"

"Progress, well... honestly, nothing much to show for it."

Tilting his head at the question, Subaru looked back over the day.

They'd arrived at the Augria Sand Dunes around noon, weathered the morning "Sand Hour," and then spent nearly half a day moving. They'd crossed the afternoon "Sand Hour" in between, and now, with sunset, here they were. As for results up to now —

"The tower really is a perfect landmark, that's one thing. Emilia-tan's marker for the way back, that's another. And Meili's ability being stronger than expected — I guess that counts as a haul?"

"With preparations beforehand and Subaru's quick thinking, our countermeasures for the 'Sand Hour' were flawless. The problem, though, is that the route to the Pleiades Watchtower itself isn't visible... wouldn't you say?"

"The route isn't visible?"

Subaru frowned, his conclusion diverging from what should have followed Julius's words. Julius let out a breath. "You hadn't noticed?"

"To be sure, up to now we've made steady progress with no notable setbacks in our course. As long as that holds, I share the sentiment."

"Don't be so roundabout about it, that's a bad habit of yours. Just say it straight out, bam."

"Then, briefly — Subaru, have you not noticed anything strange about the tower?"

"——"

At Julius's quiet question, Subaru caught his breath.

Then he fixed his gaze on the watchtower — visible faintly beyond the ice wall, its towering, imposing form asserting itself hollowly even in the dark of night.

Even told there was something strange about it, Subaru couldn't tell what was wrong with the watchtower. The tower he'd been staring at all day long should have looked no different now than it had since they'd first arrived at the dunes.

— No different at all. It should have been.

"—!?"

"You've noticed, it seems. That tower clearly hasn't drawn closer, nor moved further away. We've been walking toward it this whole time, but... the distance hasn't closed at all."

Just as Julius pointed out, Subaru finally noticed the anomaly.

The Pleiades Watchtower, seen from afar, remained there unchanged, at a size impossible to overlook. Its appearance hadn't shifted in the slightest.

"No way..."

"Subaru, I understand your shock. But do keep your wits about you..."

"So then, all that hard work today, walking all covered in sand — it was all for nothing!? This is way too much of a pain in the ass to clear!"

"...Um?"

Kicking at the sand underfoot and raising his voice, Subaru left Julius momentarily at a loss for words. Subaru crouched to the ground before the bewildered Julius, scooping up sand in his palm and pouting.

"Hey, hey, hey, hey, seriously? Knowing that just made all my exhaustion hit me at once. Being told all that effort was wasted is genuinely brutal. Ugh, this is the worst."

"Less mental strain than I expected, it seems."

"Where are you even looking! I'm suffering so much right now! I'm super down, I feel super awful about it! If you two had noticed, you should've said something sooner!"

"I thought I'd stay quiet until I was certain. I didn't want to disappoint you needlessly."

"You guys are always like that..."

Tossing away the scooped-up sand, Subaru clapped his hands and stood. Then, glaring up from below at the slightly downcast Julius, he fumed.

"Could you please cut it out with that suggestive, meaningful vibe? I'm not gonna get mad if you just say something off the top of your head! If anything, that might be exactly where the breakthrough comes from! What's with you people and this 'keep it locked away in my own heart' mentality? Has that ever actually made things better even once? At least for me, staying quiet has never once worked out!"

"Ah — ah, my apologies."

"Say even the small stuff right away. I told Ram this too — she's a lost cause on this front. Do I have to say this to every single person, seriously?"

At the sheer lack of proper reporting-and-consulting, Subaru shook his head in exasperation, and Julius looked appropriately chastened. Having, for once, gained the upper hand against the "Sword Saint's" successor, Subaru picked out this one small consolation from a heap of otherwise inconvenient facts, and turned back to face Anastasia.

"So, I take it you have some idea about this phenomenon?"

"I'll admit I explained things poorly... though more than that, what was half-doubt on my end became certainty after today. Glad you're takin' it that way, though — makes this easier."

"Say it while it's still half-doubt, from now on. That's final. So, what exactly is going on?"

Sticking his tongue out at Anastasia for bringing up excuses first, Subaru urged her to continue. Anastasia prefaced with "Don't be too shocked, okay," and then said:

"This stretch of sand between the dunes and the tower — it's probably that the space itself is twisted."

"The space is twisted...?"

"In other words, it's not actually connected the way it looks. Keep walkin' straight ahead like this, and, well, you'd probably never get there, no matter how long you walked."

At Anastasia's breezy revelation of this shocking fact, Subaru's jaw hung open. Given that she was putting this forward as something told to her by Eridna, Anastasia — or rather Eridna — must have known this from the very start.

"So that's what stopped even Reinhard, and every challenger before him."

"The destination itself is right there in view, after all. All that's left is closin' the distance... and if anythin', after fightin' through a den of witchbeasts along the way, that last stretch is probably the one you'd want most desperately of all."

"——"

Subaru could dimly imagine the regret of adventurers who'd fallen partway, their goal in sight before them.

An obstacle that had thwarted even Reinhard from attempting a conquest, blocking anyone who sought the tower. Twisted space, an edge of the sand sea that could never actually be reached — so that was the trick to it.

"How do we break through it?"

"...Talk of 'breaking' it might be a bit off the mark, y'know? This is a natural trap born of the miasma. There's no human hand at work in it at all."

"A natural trap!? This is!?"

Very rarely, nature prepared traps that seemed, in their very design, to harbor outright malice. Mirages often seen in desert regions, snow paths leading to cliffs in heavy-snow country. Broadly speaking, bottomless swamps, even the ebb and flow of the tide, could claim a human life.

But surely, the existence of this watchtower was —

"This is what nature prepared as a trap? Isn't that a bit much?"

"The Pleiades Watchtower was originally built in a place where this sort of phenomenon occurs to begin with. Thought of that way, it's not unnatural at all. And it makes sense, besides."

"Makes sense? Of what?"

"Don't forget — the dunes lead to the watchtower, but the tower isn't the only thing that lies beyond them. To begin with, what is the watchtower even for?"

Julius cautioned Subaru, whose tone had grown heated, and prompted him to think it through. This time, the answer came to Subaru at once. The watchtower's purpose — the sealing of the Witch's Shrine.

And the impassable trap of the dunes would, no doubt, prove an extremely effective deterrent against the Witch Cult and their scheme to resurrect the Witch of Envy.

It wasn't something that could simply be condemned out of hand.

"...! Fine. I get it. For now, never mind the circumstances and purpose behind why that tower was built there. That's a separate problem. What matters is — how do we get through that twisted space."

"You get that there's no cuttin' this off at the root, right? This land's just made that way."

"Fine, understood. Climate and local specialty, noted. Next, let's find the weak point. You wouldn't have offered to guide us without one, right? A way through."

"Without that, there'd be no point talkin' at all... though whether you'd call it a 'way through' is iffy."

Anastasia gave a wry smile at Subaru's continued pressing, putting on her air of great importance once more. Then she nodded toward Subaru and Julius, who were watching her intently, and said:

"During that 'Sand Hour' — there should be a moment when the space connecting to the watchtower splits open. Pass through there, into the true sand sea. That's the condition for breaking through."

— From the next day on, the assault on the dunes was one difficulty after another, in unbroken succession.

The path to the watchtower that Anastasia had revealed, and its condition, were now shared among everyone. Which meant that, for the most part, the march until the "Sand Hour" arrived would be blank space, time expected to hang heavy on their hands.

Such worries, however —

"Even when the crucial 'Sand Hour' comes, whether we reach the split is a matter of luck. Thinkin' about it that way, I don't figure it's so bad, not stayin' in one spot."

"Also, if we stay in one place for too, too long, the animals around here might learn our scent. I'll be fine, but if you ever got separated from me, they'd chase you down to the ends of the earth."

— were put to rest by the opinions of two people: Anastasia and Meili.

The former's reasoning was a bit unreliable, but the latter's was a matter of life and death, quite literally. It wasn't a situation anyone wanted to dwell on, but looking away from danger was mere cowardice. True courage lay precisely in facing fear head-on and standing against it anyway — that was what made it noble.

"Isn't that right, Emilia-tan?"

"—? Yeah, that's right."

"Emilia-sama. Best not to take that too seriously. That's his face when he's being an idiot."

Periodically exchanging words with those inside the carriage, Subaru's motivation hadn't dropped even after hearing yesterday's march had been for nothing.

The food and water prepared in Mirula still had plenty of margin left. The effects of the miasma were being eased by Emilia and Beatrice's Spiritual Arts, so there shouldn't have been cause for worry.

"Honestly, not knowing how many days this job's gonna take is its own kind of stress."

"Subaru, don't swallow any gravel that gets in your mouth — spit it out. Even if it's not much, it's probably sand laced with miasma, isn't it? If you're careless about it, you'll regret it later."

Having grown entirely used to the taste of sand in his mouth, Subaru's guard had slipped, and Beatrice scolded him for it. As always riding in Subaru's arms while astride the dragon, Beatrice pulled a water flask from within her robe.

"Here, rinse your mouth, she supposes. Since you can't bathe, at least keep your mouth clean."

"Yeah, yeah... come to think of it, you stay clean without ever needing a bath, huh?"

"Most of Betty's body is composed of mana, she supposes. Naturally, cleanliness is maintained at all times. Being cute anywhere, anytime, is simply a matter of course for Betty, she supposes."

"Yeah yeah, cute, cute."

Beatrice puffed out her cheeks at Subaru's perfunctory response, but he petted her head anyway, playing with the ringlet curls tucked inside her robe. Today they were in braids.

"Oh, another pack of witchbeasts spotted. ...If they keep their distance like that, they just look like ordinary animals."

"If you got away from me and got close, mister'd get bitten right up, quick as that. It's weird, though — the animals 'round here all keep starin' and starin' at mister."

Bringing Patrasche alongside the carriage, Subaru looked at the pack of witchbeasts gathered off to the side of their path. Meili looked at Subaru with a genuinely puzzled expression and murmured "How strange" just as she'd said.

It seemed she couldn't sense the so-called "Witch's Lingering Scent" that clung to Subaru. He'd assumed it was of the same nature as miasma, but it looked like the matter would remain unresolved after all. In any case, it didn't seem the scent had faded from him, nor had the tendency for witchbeasts to be drawn to it disappeared.

"Oh, hey, isn't that a witchbeast we know from way back? Ram, take a look!"

"I find it deeply unpleasant, having that spoken of as though it were some fond memory. Ram, and Barusu too, have nothing but wretched memories of that lot."

The pack of magic hounds watching them from a distance were kin to those that had attacked the old Roswaal Manor in that witchbeast disaster. Subaru's spirits rose without thinking, but Ram's attitude was sharp and cold.

"Don't say that. Those guys were basically the matchmaker for you, me, and Rem, in a manner of speaking. If it weren't for them, who I am now might not exist. Thinking about it that way, my relationship with them isn't so bad... no wait, it is bad! It's terrible!"

"Stop settin' yourself on fire and then puttin' it out yourself. It's pitiful to watch, wretched to listen to, and pathetic just thinking about it."

The memory of the anguish from a year ago resurfaced at the sight of the witchbeast pack. Ram sighed at Subaru's escalating excitement, and promptly shut the carriage window, cutting the conversation off.

Regrettable, but there was no helping it. In Ram's mind, that incident from a year ago had been repainted as one resolved through cooperation with Subaru, with Rem's existence absent from the picture entirely. In truth it was unclear how the pack of witchbeasts could possibly have been dealt with without Rem, but the memory alteration apparently allowed for no sense of incongruity in that.

If Rem's memories were ever restored within Ram, the missing pieces would fill in, and the conversation would finally line up properly. Though when that time came, Subaru had the distinct feeling he'd only end up treated coldly all over again.

"Whoaaa, the 'Sand Hour' is no joke! The 'Sand Hour' is brutal! This sand wind is insane!"

"I— I made the ice as clear as I could, but tell me if it's not working, okay!?"

"It's already pretty much not working! The 'ice solves everything' theory just collapsed!"

And then came the crucial "Sand Hour" itself. Plunging headlong into the sand wind, charging forward in search of the twisted rift in space leading to the tower, they were overwhelmed by a ferocity of sand beyond anything they'd imagined.

What was particularly unexpected was the utter defeat of the ice wall, which had supported their expedition so powerfully up until now.

"Even with the ice wall up, sand's completely caking onto it and I can't see a thing ahead! Not to mention, catching this much wind with a big flat sheet of ice is basically suicide!"

"Huh!? What!? Subaru, did you say something!? I can't hear you at all!"

"The wind's too strong, it's basically suicide!!"

"Huh!? You want to die!? Don't, that's terrible! Don't lose heart!"

"That's not what I— gwaaaaaah!! Sand just got violently in my eye!!"

"Huh!? What did you say!? Say it again! Subaru? Subaru—!"

"You want me to say it again, that's way too spartan— gwaaaaaah!!"

"Subaru—!?"

The sand wind, now near gale force, was close to a full-blown sandstorm.

Though those braving it were dead serious, the exchange had all the makings of a comedy sketch.

Even holding a thin sheet of ice meant only to block the sand was, in wind striking so head-on, nothing short of suicidal. Better, instead, to wrap the whole body in cloth and shrink as small as possible along with the earth dragon, reducing the surface area catching the wind.

The trouble with that plan, though, was —

"Come on! Come on, Gian! Your horsepower's the only thing we've got to rely on!"

"——"

Taking the sand wind full in the face, forced to charge straight into it, was the earth dragon. Several days into the dune assault by now, the bond that had formed between them had come out in the form of a name.

To put it simply, the Gairas-breed earth dragon pulling the carriage had been named "Gian."

And Gian's efforts now held the key to breaking through the sand wind. After all, there was no way to make the carriage carrying the women smaller against the wind.

As a result, the only strategy left was for Gian to pull the carriage with all his might, horsepower enough not to lose to the wind.

"It doesn't sit well with me, leaning so heavily on the earth dragon's efforts alone... but right now, you're the one we're counting on."

"C'mon, hang in there! I really don't wanna go back to that endless sand plain again!"

Julius, holding the reins, and Meili seated beside him, both sent Gian words of encouragement. The two of them, having watched Gian's efforts up close more than anyone, could throw out those words precisely because they believed in his hidden strength.

"——"

No small window could be opened in this wind, but Subaru could easily believe that Emilia and the others inside the carriage were praying anxiously as well.

As a last measure against the sand wind, Subaru had asked Emilia to fashion the carriage a bit with ice. Plastering a flat sheet of ice across the front would block the sand, but couldn't withstand the fierce onslaught of wind. So, the desperate measure Subaru had come up with instead was to form wings to deflect the wind, or a sharply angled shape — he pictured a single spear, thrusting fiercely into the wind.

"It's an application of my mini four-wheel-drive customizing tricks. Can't shave off weight for lightness since that'd be suicide here, but I did what I could within that. Alright, go, Gungnir — pierce through!"

"—!"

As if answering Subaru's cry, Gian's legs kicked forward with tremendous force. The carriage, now a single spear, tore straight through the wind bearing down on it, forward, ever forward.

Eventually, the carriage bearing the name of that divine spear drove itself into the wound it had torn through the wind, gouging into the sand's fury, and broke through—

"——"

The instant they emerged from the sand wind, what struck Subaru was a silence honed to a terrifying edge.

The wind that had roared so loudly in his ears, the sand that had scoured his whole body, the miasma that had stirred the witchbeasts to activity — all of it vanished as though it had been an illusion.

"——"

The end of a "Sand Hour" was certainly abrupt, but it usually left more of an afterglow. The sand-laden wind would gradually lose its force, then slip away slowly like a retreating wave, leaving only its distinctive scent of sand behind.

But this time, that was missing entirely. Which meant this ending was something different from every "Sand Hour" that had come before.

"——"

Feeling a dryness in his mouth, Subaru turned around.

There, just as he was, were Julius and the others, having come through the sand wind and standing stunned. And naturally, if they were there, so were Gian and the carriage.

"— Julius."

"Yeah."

At Subaru's call, the rigid Julius gave a nod. Then, as if by unspoken agreement, they both raised a hand, clenching a fist to celebrate breaking through the "Sand Hour."

Beside Julius, Subaru could see Meili brushing sand off herself and consoling Gian. Gian, without question, deserved the MVP award of this leg of the journey.

They owed him thanks, comfort, a reward without stint — except—

"Right now, above all — we broke through! We did it! Yeeaah—"

"Enough! It's unbearably noisy right in my ear, she supposes——!!"

Subaru's jubilant chin was struck from directly below by a palm-heel strike from Beatrice.

The blow was a clean hit, his vision spinning, and Subaru toppled clean off Patrasche's back, falling defenseless onto the sand. His head buried in the sand, mouth full of a good mouthful of it, Subaru immediately sprang back up and rounded on Beatrice.

"What was that for all of a sudden!? A guy's trying to celebrate and you just— I got so startled I swallowed a ton of sand!"

"Betty's right here in your arms and you go on hollerin' by yourself, it's a nuisance, she supposes! Somethin' about downbursts and off-road and genesis-shift and burning souls, makes no sense at all, she supposes! My ears've been ringin' this whole time!"

Still astride Patrasche, Beatrice launched a fierce protest at Subaru.

Subaru had plenty he wanted to say in his own defense, but he couldn't deny that he'd gotten a little carried away, shouting out a string of nonsense terms. What even was Gungnir, anyway.

"Ahem. ...In— in any case, we made it through the 'Sand Hour' safely. Let's celebrate that much, at least. C'mon, hooray!"

"...Hooray, she supposes."

Beatrice's sulky attitude aside, breaking through the sand barrier was, without question, a fact.

Subaru stroked the neck of Patrasche, who had done so well, and voiced his thanks to his brave dragon partner, whose dignified face showed no hint of fatigue.

With that done, Subaru dashed toward the carriage, meaning to share in the joy—

"— Mister."

"Huh? What's up? You could stand to celebrate a little more too—"

"Quiet."

Meili's sharp words cut off Subaru mid-jog, stopping him as he turned back toward her.

What was packed into that word was unmistakable — vigilance against clear danger.

— Subaru wasn't dense enough to misread what that meant.

"——"

Facing the silenced Subaru, Meili nodded, a finger held to her lips, and with her free hand, slowly, slowly pointed straight ahead.

Subaru, and Julius and Beatrice too, followed the direction of that finger and looked forward.

They had crossed the sand wind of the "Sand Hour," and cleared the twisted sand sea leading to the watchtower.

Because of that, the silhouette of the watchtower rising up before them was, compared to before, unmistakably larger and clearer.

It could be taken to mean that a concrete distance to their goal had finally been established.

"...What... is that."
