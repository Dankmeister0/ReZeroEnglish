Arc 4 Chapter 130: "Snow Face Mold"

A biting wind lashes past. A cold so severe it cuts into the skin. A savage tide of white devouring the whole field of view. Warmth passing through a clasped palm.

Natsuki Subaru stands here without a shred of hesitation or doubt.

"I made a cool entrance and all, but something about this situation is off!?"

Snow slapping his cheeks, Subaru raises his voice at a scene that doesn't match what he'd planned. The Sanctuary, howling with wind, is—just as it looks—smack in the middle of a curtain of snow. This was a sight Subaru had steeled himself to face eventually, but the timing is wrong compared to what he remembers. The Sanctuary being buried in snow, drawing in the white Witchbeasts—that wasn't supposed to happen until tomorrow morning. There should still have been more than half a day of leeway.

A little behind him and off to the side, a silver-haired girl stands with her shoulders trembling, breathing out white puffs. Emilia has half her body, unable to fully rein in the mana overflowing from within her, buried in ice. Her left side has turned white, and though it must hurt terribly, not a trace of it shows on her face. While inwardly showering the stalwart Emilia with praise, Subaru suspects her to be the cause of this snow.

If so, then it would be a runaway surge of magic she couldn't control that brought the snow, which in turn summoned the Great Rabbit.

"But that would mean the order's backwards..."

The sequence is inverted—uncontrolled magic power and the Great Rabbit drawn by snow. Emilia exercised her magic to fight the rabbits, and as a result took damage to herself. If you trace the flow correctly, the cause would instead be—

"——"

Further behind Emilia, Echidna's tomb sits in solemn repose. And peering fearfully out from the entrance, Subaru spots several pairs of eyes and nods. If the ones inside are Sanctuary residents, then they're unaffected by the tomb's mechanism. That means the tomb's function has stopped—in other words, proof that Emilia has cleared her Trial.

Emilia, who overcame her Trial. Snow falling earlier than scheduled. The state of the Sanctuary's residents, and the resolve on Emilia's face, her cry. And then—

"Roswaal."

"——"

Roswaal sits slumped beside the tomb entrance, staring blankly this way. Ram sleeps in his arms, and there's no time now to confirm whether she's safe. Nothing to do but believe that she is.

"Subaru."

As he stands there thinking, Subaru's hand is tugged by the small hand gripping it. Hearing a familiar voice call him by an unfamiliar name, Subaru instinctively catches his breath and—

"Whoa, y-yes?"

"...Why are you giving such a strange answer?"

"Nah, it's just fresh, you calling me by my name. Can you say it again, bashfully this time?"

"Hah!? You—you're seriously out of your mind! Are you an idiot, at a time like this!?"

At Subaru's playful request, Beatrice rounds on him with a scary face. As expected, even a request doesn't slide past her, and Subaru reluctantly backs down. Then—

"S-Subaru... here, go on."

"Bea-ko, you're so cute."

"—! Fine, I'm never saying it again! Remember this once things are settled!"

Swinging their clasped hands wildly, Beatrice fumes, her face bright red. Watching the girl with a warm smile, Subaru turns his attention to the ring of rabbits steadily closing in around them. He licks his dry lips once.

"So, Beatrice. The enemy's the Great Rabbit—you mentally prepared?"

"Right after contracting. Opponent is one of the Three Great Witchbeasts. Preparation lacking, situation poor. Contractor is a novice. It's Betty's first time being called into actual combat in four hundred years."

"And?"

"A fitting handicap, I suppose."

Beatrice smiles fearlessly, and the Witchbeasts, chattering their teeth in a rapid clack-clack, close the distance in a rush. Stepping forward to meet them, Subaru glances back at Emilia behind him.

"Beatrice and I are gonna blast the Great Rabbit right now. Sorry, Emilia-tan, but I need you to protect everyone from whatever slips past us!"

"I could also—"

Emilia cuts herself off, hesitating for a moment over whether to continue. But she closes her eyes, takes a small breath, and then—

"Understood. Leave it to me. —So, I'm leaving this to you."

"Yeah, leave it to me."

Right person, right place, division of labor—the husband works hard while the wife minds the home front. Emilia exhales deeply and focuses on controlling her own magic while raising a defensive line. Amid the ceaselessly falling snow, she spreads a barrier of ice further out.

Stepping outside the defensive line Emilia has raised, Subaru looks out at the world beyond the white gale. Filling his entire field of vision are red eyes and sharp fangs. Witchbeasts covered in pure white fur, ruled by the most primal, most voracious hunger in this world. Many Rabbits—turned Great Rabbit.

At the clack-clack ringing of fangs, an ache in Subaru's Soul spreads through his whole body. That gruesome death, sunk into by those fangs, innards devoured to nothing. The unbearable pain of a hole torn through his torso, blood gushing, throat ripped out. And then flesh torn away all over his body, limbs lost, ending in overwhelming loss cradled in Emilia's arms.

This loop of Natsuki Subaru's cannot be fulfilled unless he overcomes this Witchbeast.

"—Are you scared?"

Holding his breath as he watches the Witchbeasts, Subaru is addressed by Beatrice. Glancing up at him sidelong, Beatrice wears a composed expression. And yet her eyes, her profile, tell Subaru more eloquently than words ever could.

—Just who it is standing beside Subaru.

"Nah, not scared."

"Is that so."

"Emilia's behind me, you're beside me. I mean, come on—I'm feeling invincible."

"That's how it should be, I suppose."

Her cheeks loosen, and Beatrice smiles faintly. At her expression, as if to say see, you understand, Subaru grins back wickedly too.

The Great Rabbits raise a battle cry and leap all at once at the bold, fearless pair. In response, Beatrice points her right hand—the one not clasped with Subaru's—forward and says,

"First, a little probe, I suppose. —El Minya."

The incantation and the warping of space happen simultaneously, and purple crystals materialize, encircling Subaru and the others. Shaped and gleaming like icicles, these are the magic stakes that once impaled Elsa in a previous loop. In an instant, some forty of them are generated.

Aimed for a split second, then fired silently—the magic stakes strike unerringly, piercing straight through the open-mouthed faces of the rabbits. Still skewering one rabbit, a stake flies through the air and plunges into the pack lined up behind, exploding on impact, its scattering fragments shredding the rabbits at the point of impact as well. The power of a single strike, unleashed forty times at once.

Under the shockwave of destruction converging from all sides, red flowers of rabbit blood-mist bloom across the white world. With the mercilessness of the opening salvo, the pack of Witchbeasts deployed in front vanishes in the hundreds. In the plaza now littered with the marks of destruction, the surviving Great Rabbits let out cries of agony. The infinitely multiplying Witchbeasts still have plenty of numbers left, but even so, Subaru is thrilled—

By Beatrice's overwhelming, beyond-imagination power of annihilation.

"Th-that's amazing!!"

"I-is it, though? It's nothing much, I suppose. In Betty's hands, that's just the opening act. A piece of cake, I suppose."

"No, but, you, this... this magic had this kind of power!? What element!?"

"Shade element, obviously, I suppose. I'm not much good at anything besides shade, I suppose."

Beatrice's expression is far from displeased at Subaru's praise. The blasted Witchbeasts, already gnawing on the flesh of their fallen kin, have resumed multiplying, but Beatrice doesn't seem to give the increase of small fry a second thought.

"Fine, Subaru. As a fellow user of the shade element, I suppose I'll give you a lecture."

"Eh, that's it? That's all we've got going for us?"

"Huh?"

"You and me, was it just that we're both shade-element types...?"

"I—it's not just that, I suppose! We're fellow shade-element users, and contractor, and, um, Betty's Subaru, I suppose. Right, so I'll teach you, I suppose."

Flustered, Beatrice likely doesn't even know what she's saying herself. The girl clears her throat, raises a finger, and deliberately lowers her voice.

"The pinnacle of the shade element—the power of shade magic, the finest in the world."

"What should I do?"

"Just hold Betty's hand and don't leave her alone—that'll do, I suppose."

"Nah, I mean, I think that's important too, but..."

"...Seems you don't understand how a Spirit Arts user works. This bodes ill, I suppose."

Even said with exasperation, what he doesn't know, he doesn't know—nothing to be done about it. Twisting her mouth at Subaru, Beatrice shakes her head, pulls his hand, and steps forward.

"Basically, a Spirit Arts user and their Spirit fight as one, but with two separate minds on the battlefield, I suppose."

"Two separate minds as one..."

He recalls the way Emilia fights. The most vivid image of Emilia fighting alongside Puck is the battle with Elsa in the Loot House. In that fight, Emilia handled offense through Puck, defense through herself—dividing the roles. Also, Emilia would buy time with small spells so Puck could unleash a big one, among other tactics. He also feels like the bald old man beside him once said that this is basically how Spirit Arts users fight.

"So, meaning I should be doing that too. Alright, leave Shamak to me!"

"Having Subaru cast a half-baked Shamak sounds like it'll get us both hurt, so no thank you, I suppose. Besides, right now Subaru's Gate is..."

Beatrice trails off, reluctant to say more. At her demeanor, Subaru reflects that he's made her worry needlessly. Subaru's Gate is, probably, done for. He'd overworked it. There'd been a sense of it breaking. So that too is a pipe dream.

"—Here it comes, I suppose."

Mid-explanation, Beatrice murmurs quietly. Right after Subaru lets out a "Huh?", he notices his feet have left the ground. Beatrice beside him gives a light kick off the ground, and that leaping force, as if spring-loaded, carries both their bodies skyward. And in that instant, the fangs of the Witchbeasts surge into the spot they'd just occupied. The sound of the Great Rabbits' fangs snapping shut echoes out, and one after another the Witchbeasts kick off the ground, chasing the pair who leapt upward.

"Are we flying!?"

"Just a light hop, I suppose. I used shade magic's 'Muraku' to lessen the effect of gravity. If I felt like it, I could ride the wind and actually fly, I suppose."

"But we're falling!?"

"If we just wanted to escape, riding the wind as-is would work... but I suppose we're wiping them out instead."

Buffeted like leaves tossed by the wind, Subaru and Beatrice's bodies are battered by the blizzard. That they don't tumble gracelessly through the air regardless is likely due to something Beatrice is doing. From roughly ten meters up, the two of them slowly, unhurriedly descend. Below, the pack of Great Rabbits waits with mouths open, and another strike of magic stakes is anticipated.

"Subaru, to continue—originally, a Spirit Arts user casts magic not through mana within themselves, but by directly interfering with the mana that exists in the atmosphere, I suppose. This requires a contract with a minor spirit, a condition the current Subaru doesn't meet, I suppose."

"H-hold on, uh, Beatrice-san? Down there, there's a whole lot coming up from below!?"

"Just listen, I suppose. Your own Gate's no good, and you can't use minor spirits either. With Subaru being this hopeless and helpless, is there really nothing for you to do besides stand beside Betty and praise how lovely she is? What are you even here for."

"That's what I want to know!"

"Then I suppose I'll teach you."

Below, the leaping Witchbeasts' fangs are about to reach their feet. Once bitten, a Great Rabbit never lets go of its fangs. Acting all composed and cool only to take massive damage and end up half-crying would be way too lame. At Beatrice dragging things out, Subaru shouts, half in desperation.

"What do I do!?"

"Picture it, I suppose. What you should imagine is the same crystal as Betty's from before, I suppose. That's mana crystallized, a stake woven from tangible magic power. Sharpen the tip, pack ruin inside, and pierce through defenses straight into flesh—an attack like that."

"Pictured it!"

"Then all that's left is to chant it, I suppose!"

Right below, the pack of Great Rabbits with mouths agape. Red eyes, blood-soaked maws, sharp fangs, an instinct that sees Subaru as nothing but a hunk of meat. Everything about them is repulsive, everything about them is hateful—the greatest enemy in the Sanctuary.

"—El Minya!!"

Subaru and Beatrice's incantations overlap, and the summoned magic stakes rain down from above onto the earth. Explosions and destruction blast the ground of the Sanctuary apart, and the hideous Witchbeasts are torn to shreds and blown away.

"Amazing..."

Freezing a Great Rabbit that had slipped past her left flank solid with an icicle, Emilia lets out an awed breath. Her violet eyes are riveted on Subaru and the others, taking on the Witchbeasts beyond the blizzard.

More precisely, Emilia's gaze is fixed on Beatrice, hand in hand with Subaru. Being a Spirit Arts user herself, one who even now casts magic in cooperation with a minor spirit, Emilia's eyes tell her, painfully clearly, just how far outside the norm the magical battle unfolding before her is.

First, Beatrice isn't receiving a supply of mana from Subaru. This isn't because there's no pathway connecting the two of them as contracted partners—Beatrice is deliberately choosing not to. With Subaru's body fresh off the contract, suddenly thrown into real combat, if she drew as much mana as she needed from him, his body would never hold up. Beatrice understands this and handles things so as not to burden Subaru.

Second, far from receiving magic power from Subaru, Beatrice is giving it to him. It's an odd way to put it, but it's the truth. Right now, Subaru, hand in hand with Beatrice, is—with her assistance—casting magic he shouldn't yet be able to use. Not through his own Gate, but by using Beatrice's very existence as a substitute Gate. Subaru surely has no idea how far outside the norm this is. Despite being a Spirit herself, Beatrice is supplementing magic power for two—herself and Subaru—not from some outside source, but from her own stored magic power.

And third, this is witnessing the genuine article of shade magic honed to its peak. For a magic user, one's proficient element greatly determines one's future. Just as the four basic elements each carve out clearly distinct roles when specialized, the two special elements—shade and light—differ greatly in character from the other four even before being mastered. All the more so because, at a glance, shade seems to be an element with little practical use. Problems abound as its negative side: the time it takes for effects to manifest, the sheer amount of mana required, and so on.

Hence, combined with its rarity, few pursue mastery of the shade and light elements to begin with. Unlike the other four elements, many spells have been lost to history, and it's fertile ground for new great magic users to fail to emerge. Beatrice has mastered shade magic, an element defined by nothing but such problems—and she wields ancient spells at that, buried in history, from before they were lost.

"Wow, they're flying so much. Eh...? Did they just vanish...? Ah, already over there."

It's a way of fighting that makes you forget you're watching reality, like something out of a dream or an illusion. The way Subaru and Beatrice hold hands so amicably is surely part of what gives that impression.

She can tell Subaru is fighting desperately, but Beatrice is even smiling faintly. Surely, that girl is truly having fun. Not that fighting itself is fun, not that wielding power is fun. Simply, being like that—together—is fun.

"——"

In the blink of an eye, Subaru and Beatrice's positions shift to an entirely different spot. A limited kind of teleportation magic, something like a small-scale 'Door Crossing.' Purple magic stakes blast apart the rows of Great Rabbits front and back, and a ferocious, roaring Witchbeast, lunging at the pair, catches on something mid-air and is torn apart.

Straining her eyes, Emilia realizes. The scattered fragments of the burst magic stakes haven't vanished—they remain, stitched into the air as if time had stopped there. The leaping Witchbeast tears itself apart on those fragments, shredding itself. The trap of light-catching fragments is scattered everywhere in great numbers, and Witchbeasts leaping aside, rolling sideways, charging boldly at the pair, moving all around, fall one after another into the trap in comically quick succession.

The Great Rabbit is a fearsome Witchbeast, but as individuals their capability isn't all that high among Witchbeasts. Each one on its own is weak; taking one on alone, so long as you're wary of its ferocity, anyone experienced in combat would never fall behind. Their reckless way of fighting, driven purely by the instinct of hunger, has no capacity to learn. Even as their own kind gets caught in traps and torn apart all around them, their own hunger is everything. So they fall into the exact same trap without a thought, and end up exposing their own corpses.

"Hah!"

Another one, slipping through the encirclement, gets a spell hurled at it—a Great Rabbit. Emilia dashes over and, without hesitation, kicks apart the icicle where the Great Rabbit stands frozen solid, feet on the ground. It becomes shards of ice, the Witchbeast dead beyond any possibility of revival. Thanks to Subaru and Beatrice's fierce efforts, the number of Great Rabbits slipping through to her side is astonishingly small. Emilia too can devote her strength to controlling her own swirling magic power.

And yet, even watching Beatrice display such overwhelming strength, the seed of anxiety inside Emilia refuses to disappear. Beatrice's encirclement of traps is powerful and cunning. The Great Rabbits fall into them one after another, building mountain after mountain of corpses. But there's no bottom in sight at all.

Even now, before Emilia's eyes, a crouching Great Rabbit shudders, and another Great Rabbit sprouts as if growing from its back. This repeats, and the Witchbeasts' numbers multiply like a geometric progression. A hundred Great Rabbits become two hundred the next moment, and four hundred the moment after that.

Sheer numbers, and a shameless instinct that knows no retreat. That is why this Witchbeast is counted among the Three Great Witchbeasts, why it has claimed the name of "Calamity" for four hundred years running—

"Subaru, Beatrice."

Calling out both their names, Emilia refuses to let her guard down even at what looks like overwhelming dominance. The dread she felt when she first laid eyes on the pack of Great Rabbits, after returning to the tomb with Roswaal and Ram, is something Emilia is sure she'll never forget.

Those twin eyes that see every living thing as nothing but food for themselves. Something that, in the truest sense, can never be reconciled with—that is how vast and hopeless a gulf it can make you feel toward what stands opposite you. To counter overwhelming unreasonableness, there is nothing to do but bring forth a matching power of your own.

Emilia had, in truth, intended to be the one to do that. The uncontrollable torrent of magic power swirling within her—something that surely isn't hers alone—if she unleashed it, she could annihilate even the pack of Great Rabbits. In exchange, it would cost her own life. At worst, she'd resolved herself to that.

"Subaru..."

Before her eyes, she murmurs the name of the man fighting, under her breath. He knew about the Great Rabbit's coming attack in advance—she can't imagine he'd challenge this menace without a plan. He brought Beatrice out of the Forbidden Archive and has her behaving so vibrantly. He would never do anything to cloud that smile.

So Emilia believes in the man called Natsuki Subaru. The white magic power asserting its presence in her chest, the one that could end everything—she suppresses it, telling herself its turn will never come.

—She believes in that person's words.

To Subaru, casting magic had always been tantamount to shaving away at his own Soul.

Just as Puck and Roswaal had pointed out from the very start, Subaru has no talent whatsoever as a magic user. The first time he used Shamak, he lost control and drained every drop of mana in his body, leaving himself unable to move. After that, he doped himself with the power of the Bokko Fruit. Even when forbidden to use it, he used it anyway in the duel, and in the end pushed himself to the absolute limit until his Gate collapsed. The path of a magic user is, by now, utterly and completely closed to him. There have been many times magic saved him, but for Subaru, each one was an act like shaving down and sharpening an already-thin core. That it eventually snapped, he thinks, was inevitable.

That's exactly why the sight of what he's doing now—firing off great spells one after another—is something he'd only ever pictured in dreams, and had assumed was flatly impossible.

"Hey, Beatrice! Is it really okay to keep blasting away at this rate!?"

The Great Rabbits repeat a cycle of multiplying faster than they're taken down, as if in a counterattack. Devouring the corpses of their own kind, multiplying—it seems the act of multiplying itself consumes energy, because the more they multiply, the more ferocious each individual becomes. A faint hope sprouts that if they just keep stalling for time like this, eventually the energy for multiplying will run dry, but—

"There's no limit to how far the multiplying goes, I suppose. These things were made that way, I suppose. Try to destroy them and they won't be destroyed—not unless you wipe out every single one, all at once."

"Then what do we do? You got any bright ideas?"

"Shouldn't it be Subaru who has some, instead of always relying on cute little Betty?"

Blasting a hole in the pack with an exploding magic stake, a blown-apart Great Rabbit is shredded by the scattered fragments left hanging in the air. Watching it through to the end, Beatrice pulls Subaru's arm and flies lightly. Both the pulling force and the leap are effortless despite how little strength seems to go into them, carrying him along with ease.

Walking through the air, dodging fangs with dance-like steps, weaving through gaps in the trap of fragments—Beatrice. That she's exhilarated yet unafraid throughout this fight is proven, no doubt, by the fact that her lavish dress remains without a speck of dirt or a single drop of spattered blood.

"Crossing, I suppose."

"Right."

A shout, and in the next instant space warps, the two of them undergoing a small-scale teleportation. Crossing space in a manner different from 'Door Crossing,' they reappear directly behind the pack of Great Rabbits. Sniffing the air but having lost track of Subaru and the others, the Witchbeasts are wide open.

"Take the left, I suppose."

"Then I've got the right."

He pictures it. The image traveling through Subaru's palm responds to Beatrice's magic power, and the world, receiving that interference, transforms. It's undeniably the sensation of wrestling in someone else's loincloth, but that's exactly why he doesn't allow himself an ounce of slack.

Following Subaru's imagination, a violet crystal is born—the Minya family of shade-element spells. Subaru carves a spiral groove into the thing as it forms, boosting its penetrating power, and fires it off in one motion. The sensation of something not touching his own hand being loosed according to his will. It feels like drawing a bowstring that exists only in his mind, loosing an arrow with no shape.

Piercing the wind, the propelled magic stake strikes the defenseless pack directly, and with grating shrieks the Great Rabbits are blown apart. On the right side of the pack, likewise, the destruction Beatrice generates annihilates Great Rabbits. A fissure formed in space swallows the pack of Witchbeasts, and the sealed space, like something out of a painting, traps hundreds of Great Rabbits within. The Great Rabbits leap about in what looks like a place on the far side of a mirror. Aiming at that pack, seemingly unaware of anything, Beatrice fires a single magic stake—the flat plane of that world shatters to pieces, and the pack of Great Rabbits on the far side is swallowed whole into oblivion along with it.

Holding his breath, Subaru is stunned by the sheer versatility of Beatrice's magical skill. Even as Subaru repeats Minya over and over like a broken record, Beatrice unleashes shade spell after shade spell, no two alike, annihilating Great Rabbits as she goes. As if showing Subaru every card in her hand. Or as if she herself is rediscovering the techniques she once possessed.

"Soon, I suppose."

"Huh?"

The Great Rabbits' numbers, cut down considerably, spring back up by exactly that much in an instant. Watching this, Subaru feels again the sense of unease he's had for a while now. Combined with Beatrice's murmur, this seems like a good moment to compare notes.

"Beatrice. Those things—they've been coming back by exactly the number we kill, but... doesn't it feel like they're not increasing past the number they started with?"

If there were a thousand Great Rabbits, kill a hundred and a hundred come back. Kill two hundred and two hundred come back. By that logic, no matter how many they've killed so far, the balance of power shows no sign of tipping. But Subaru hasn't personally witnessed them multiplying beyond their maximum number. At Subaru's words, Beatrice tucks her chin and says,

"Probably, even though the multiplying itself has no limit, there's a cap on the maximum population needed to sustain the species, I suppose. So they can't increase beyond that, I suppose."

"Then if we could take out the max number all at once..."

"In theory, they could be destroyed. ...But that's a difficult matter in its own right, I suppose."

Beatrice's face turns troubled at the ray of hope Subaru has spotted. That's only natural. A quantity of Great Rabbits filling the entire field of view. A spell that could scorch the entire visible range might be able to destroy them, but how much power would it take to do that in an instant, all at once, in one go?

Even by the standards of power Subaru knows, it'd be as reckless as blowing this whole area apart with a missile. And even then, if even one survived the burning, it would revive instantly. The risk was too great.

"In that case, then, I guess... that."

"Have you thought of something?"

"Same as always, this ends up depending entirely on you, though."

Watching the Witchbeasts multiply, Subaru leans in close to whisper into Beatrice's ear. Hearing it, Beatrice lowers her eyes as if pondering, then nods.

"Betty was thinking along the same lines, I suppose. But to do that, we'd need..."

"I know there's a catch. But listen! Don't get the wrong idea, Beatrice!"

"—?"

"We don't have to solve this all by ourselves, you know?"

At Subaru's answer, Beatrice's eyes widen slightly. Then, letting out a small breath, she leans into him, resting her forehead against his chest.

"Really... you come up with quite the answer, I suppose, Subaru."

"I promise to keep being a contractor who never bores you, fresh and full of surprises."

Beatrice gives a wry smile at Subaru flashing his teeth with a thumbs up. Then she lifts her face from his chest and says,

"Very well, I suppose, let's do it. Though, to actually carry it out, even Betty will need some time, I suppose. In the meantime, I want you to manage well."

"Consider it smooth sailing. I'm feeling it."

"We'll see who's doing the rowing, I suppose."

Pushing his chest, Beatrice steps away. The girl draws a breath, closes her eyes, and begins raising and focusing her magic power.

Watching this, Subaru fires himself up and kicks off through the snow. Chasing after the running Subaru, the Witchbeasts snap their fangs. Shadows lunging at his feet. But too slow. Compared to the hell he's crawled through these past two days, this last Great Rabbit feels almost gentle.

"Out of the way, out of the way! Move, move! I don't have time to deal with you lot right now!"

Dodging fangs, kicking them aside. Chanting, forcibly carving a path with magic stakes, Subaru, still carrying Beatrice, powers straight through the hole-riddled plaza and races back toward the tomb.

"Eh, wait, Subaru!?"

Seeing Subaru return, Emilia's face shows surprise. Sliding to a stop beside her, kicking up snow, Subaru sets the eyes-closed Beatrice down right next to her and pats her head while saying,

"Sorry, Emilia-tan! Handling this with just me and Bea-ko was rough going!"

"Th-that's fine, but... so what do we do? Should I really..."

"Nah, I've thought of a way to beat them. Emilia-tan doesn't need to use some suicidal finishing move. Actually, don't. All this hard work would mean nothing."

Emilia catches her breath, staring intently at Subaru's face. Did she really think she hadn't been noticed? She probably had. In a moment like this, if truly cornered, Emilia would absolutely try to force a breakthrough without sparing herself. Honestly, what a troublesome girl. Getting herself hurt so everyone else can be saved—spare him that.

"Everyone being safe and everyone being saved is obviously the best outcome, so..."

"...Subaru."

"Emilia-tan, I'm about to ask something a little unreasonable of you. If you can't do it, I'll rack my brain some more, but if you can, I want you to give it your all. —Let's win, all of us together."

"——"

Emilia places a hand on her chest, blinking repeatedly as if something in Subaru's words struck her. To buy time until she makes up her mind, Subaru generates magic stakes and fires them into the pack of Witchbeasts to keep them in check. But it doesn't take long at all.

"Understood. Let's do it, Subaru. Tell me anything."

Her resolve set, Emilia's answer carries fierce determination in her eyes. At that, Subaru clenches his fist and turns back around.

"That's the spirit. Let's do this!"

Subaru felt a tremendous surge of magic power rising on either side of him.

Emilia stands to his left, Beatrice to his right. Each of them extends a hand, clasped with one of Subaru's, the three of them linked hand to hand. There's no particular meaning in taking this formation. It just raises Subaru's morale. And that rising morale is called, in the context of battle, fighting spirit. And high fighting spirit is a crucial factor that can even determine the outcome of a battle.

"Picture it, picture it, picture it!"

He conjures in his mind a vicious, powerful spell strike. Generating magic stakes with sharpened violet crystals, Subaru bombards the closing ring of Witchbeasts. Repeating suppressive fire, striving to keep them from approaching the tomb or the three of them. In this shade magic, Subaru isn't using his own mana. So he's casting magic with no burden whatsoever—except that's wrong.

The mana needed to cast the spells is borrowed from Beatrice, but the control of the magic is unmistakably being done by Subaru himself. Power, targeting, quantity—shaping all of it as an image, giving it form, launching it, and instantly judging the next attack. For a professional magic user, physical fatigue would pile on top of all this, so the sheer scale of that parallel workload and burden is unimaginable. No wonder he was called talentless.

Slamming down a magic stake, the earth explodes and the shockwave sends a Great Rabbit flying, letting out a cry as if in protest. The grating clack-clack of fangs chains together, and amid the blizzard it sounds like nothing so much as the gears of hell turning. A death conveyor sending Subaru and the others to the guillotine, steadily advancing by the turning of those gears.

"Minya! Minya! Ugh, damn it! This spell's a mouthful!"

Grumbling about how hard the spell name is to say, Subaru targets a cluster of Great Rabbits pushing forward. The summoned magic stake fires off, missing the lead Witchbeast's head—instead striking the ground just short of it, and the shockwave shoves them back into the pack once more.

That's phase one of the plan. Subaru is using the magic stakes to hold the pack in check, but not to kill them. This is to avoid breaking the principle of the maximum population, which could trigger multiplication at some unpredictable point. He wants the Great Rabbits pinned right here, while their numbers stay at maximum. Though, of course—

"If you're drawn in by the smell of mana, there's no way you lot can take your eyes off us right now."

After all, present here are two people possessing an amount of mana rarely seen. And both of them beautiful girls, no less. Right now, Subaru's got a flower in each hand. Bound to inspire jealousy.

"Picture it, picture it, picture it... how about it, jealous, aren't you! Come closer!"

Muttering along, Subaru doesn't hold back on taunting even the beasts, who surely can't understand a word. Provoking his opponents is one goal, but Subaru's banter serves even more to fire himself up. By dressing up the extraordinary as the everyday, Subaru just barely manages to hold himself together.

Otherwise, there's no guarantee his knees wouldn't be shaking right now. The warmth passing through both hands. Feeling this touch so close, right now, he can't afford to show anything pathetic.

"Picture it, picture it, picture it...!"

Repeating it over and over, muttering, Subaru narrows his eyes and watches closely. The pack of Great Rabbits surges forward as a whole, and there's only so much holding them back with suppressive fire can do. But it's not quite ready yet. Not Emilia, not Beatrice, and not Subaru's preparations either.

"...Subaru."

The sensation of his left hand being squeezed—looking over, he sees Emilia, eyes half-open, watching him. Does that mean her preparations are complete? Her lips part slightly, waiting for Subaru's signal.

"——"

As if pushed forward by Emilia's gaze, Subaru narrows his bloodshot eyes even further. The curtain of blizzard is thick, and the spots he wants to see keep flickering out of view. Even so, the writhing white shapes faintly tell Subaru apart the Witchbeasts from the snowfield.

—A little more, just a bit more, almost, go, go, go!

Gritting his teeth, he waits for the moment. Checking everything at once—the far side of his vision, both edges, the near ground—Subaru's eyes go wide.

"Now, Emilia! Trace the line—!!"

Shouting, Subaru squeezes Emilia's hand hard. Emilia's violet eyes glare forward with strength, seeing the line Subaru has drawn.

While using the magic stakes to keep the Great Rabbits in check, Subaru had, in parallel, been drawing a line of mana on the ground. Unlike the magic stakes which take on a fixed shape, carving into the earth with formless mana was an incredibly difficult feat. But Subaru, called talentless, powered through that hardship with concentration and a sense of pride far beyond that of an ordinary person. Pride in not wanting to be seen looking uncool.

Four lines had been drawn. A "rectangle traced to enclose" the pack of Great Rabbits before him. A line drawn as a range marker, so Emilia could see it clearly.

"As expected of Subaru! So-o wonderful!"

At the magnificent setup, Emilia raises a cry of delight—something she'd never normally say. Emilia raises her right hand, still clasped with Subaru's, and lays her left hand—increasingly covered in white from the runaway magic power—over it. And then, she chants.

"—Al Huma!!"

Massive magic power surges forth, and the world transforms, following Emilia's incantation. The mana rushing through travels along Subaru and Emilia's clasped hands, leaps out into the atmosphere from there, pierces through the earth, and merges with the line of mana Subaru had drawn. —With a thunderous roar, an astonishing phenomenon occurs.

"Whoa..."

Witnessing it firsthand, Subaru unwittingly lets out a stunned voice. No wonder. Anyone seeing this sight would react the same way.

Emilia's magic power traces the line Subaru drew on the ground—lifting all the snow within that rectangular space, snowfield and all, into the air. Naturally, the pack of Great Rabbits remains entirely intact atop the lifted ground of snow, and the Witchbeasts haven't even noticed that the shock they just felt was their own footing rising into the air.

Limited in range as it is, the floating snowfield must be some twenty meters square. Given how densely packed the Great Rabbits are, the sight of a vast number of white fur-balls trembling atop a floating white ground is a spectacular demonstration of just how supernatural magic can be.

"Emilia!"

"I know! I'm not letting them get away!"

But if it ends there, the Great Rabbits will just leap down from the snowfield, and that's that. To keep them from escaping, one more trick is needed.

Emilia raises her still-clasped hand high, then swings it down in one motion. Taking that motion as its trigger, the floating snowfield begins to shake. What was about to happen to the Great Rabbits atop the snow, they couldn't possibly have imagined.

A violent roar, and a piercingly cold, freezing wind arises. Bathed in it, Subaru and the others don't look away, watching to see the outcome.

—When the wind stopped, there stood a snowfield closed shut, vertically.

Emilia had folded the floating snowfield from both sides toward the center. The ground closed like a book being shut, and the Great Rabbits atop it, utterly helpless, were sealed together between the folds of the snowfield.

Hurriedly, they scan the area around the closed snowfield. No stragglers slipped through. No moving shadows. All the Great Rabbits confined to one place, an extremely narrow range. Under these conditions—

"Alright, time for the finishing blow, Beatrice—!"

Calling out to Beatrice, clasped in his right hand, Subaru announces the end of setup after setup after setup. At this, the girl, who had been quietly continuing her incantation, gently opens her eyes.

Seeing the scene before her, Beatrice smiles faintly. Not surprise, nothing of the sort. Just a smile filled with nothing but trust, and—

"This is the pinnacle of the shade element—Al Shamak."

The moment the incantation is whispered, the world is swallowed in shadow.

—For an instant, it was tossed about by something like a full-body sensation of floating.

But that too lasted only a true instant. The floating sensation ended, and impact came from underfoot. Right after, the sense of restraint that had been pressing against its whole body vanished, and first, it shook its body hard, shaking off the snow clinging to its fur.

Snorting, turning its head. By eye, by nose, by ear—finding prey is, for it, the action that comes before all else. It scans the area with red eyes, searching for prey with a rich, savory scent.

It senses nothing. Just moments ago, right in front of it, there had been prey so appetizing it made the stomach clench with hunger. Flesh soft, blood sweet—prey that surely could have intoxicated it, however briefly, with a sense of fullness.

Nose, nothing. Eyes, nothing visible. Ears, nothing heard. The prey that should have been there isn't. It turns its head. Can't find it.

Something like disappointment is instantly overwritten by immediate hunger. To distract itself from the emptiness and hunger in its mouth, it sinks its teeth, for the time being, into the nearest white mass. Tearing it apart, ripping flesh, slurping blood, gouging out organs. Chewing greedily, devouring it whole, it notices similar meals unfolding all around.

The prey will run out. Not exactly out of any sense of crisis, but following survival instinct, it bites off the head of the white mass beside it, desperate to devour whatever's in front of it before it disappears. Biting, swallowing.

Repeating, repeating, driven by an unquenchable hunger, it devours the neighbor, and the neighbor beside that, and the neighbor beside that, and the neighbor beside—

Eventually, at some point, having devoured everything around it, it finds itself alone. It licks the blood soaking the ground, chews without leaving a scrap—the scattered pieces of flesh, the blood-soaked earth and grass, all of it. Once even the leftovers are cleaned up spotless, this time it is, at last, truly alone.

Inside it, a hunger that won't vanish even with more meat crammed in than its own volume can hold assails it. Crying out, clacking its teeth, it feels as though it will go mad. An inexhaustible hunger, a starvation that can never be sated. Devour and devour, and still an insanity that grants no forgiveness.

Did its mother, too, carry something like this?

For just an instant, a strange thought flickers through the mind of the thing ruled by hunger. Noise-like, it's nothing more than a flicker of emotion, never something that would be codified into words. And that too, in an instant, vanishes forever before the maddening hunger.

It shudders, shudders violently, and screaming at the sensation of its innards being churned, unconsciously gives birth to a being separate from itself.

The suddenly appearing white mass rolls across the ground on its back, as if it had forgotten how to walk. Perceiving the prey with every sense, it bites down on the rolling mass without hesitation. Devouring it whole before it can even scream. After devouring it, hunger tortures it once more. And having writhed and struggled in agony, once again, a being different from itself is born into the world.

Repeating, repeating, it continues this act. Alone. A world with nothing else. There are buildings, there is forest, there is earth, there is air, there is wind, and yet there is no prey—only itself, alone.

It goes on devouring. And eventually, "it" too is devoured by a hunger different from its own and vanishes into nothing.

A new solitude, ceasing to be solitude, repeating, and the world turns.

—An unsatisfied hunger is never satisfied.

For an instant, Subaru held his breath at the presence of the tremendous shadow that blanketed everything before his eyes.

"——"

The pitch-black sphere summoned by Beatrice's incantation swallowed whole the snowfield enclosing the Great Rabbits sealed by Emilia, and as it did, it compressed, shrinking smaller and smaller, until at last it dwindled to less than the size of a marble and vanished without a sound. What that meant—even without understanding the principle, the result was vaguely comprehensible.

Al Shamak—the greatest spell of the Shamak family is one that acts upon space itself. And that spell swallowed the pack of Great Rabbits, snowfield and all, and blasted them into another dimension. Regeneration, multiplication—none of it means anything now. Literally, it's a matter of another world now.

"Like the Forbidden Archive... I did say, can't we send them off to some isolated space, but..."

"Are you dissatisfied, I suppose?"

At Subaru's voice trembling at the overwhelming feat, Beatrice beside him puckers her lips. Hands on her hips, chest thrown out, the girl looks quite put out by Subaru's attitude.

"Seriously, that's amazing..."

Beside Subaru, Emilia too has her eyes wide at the result. In Emilia's case, having a deeper grounding in magic than Subaru, her astonishment likely comes from a somewhat different angle. The freezing of her half-body also seems to have eased somewhat, thanks to using such a large-scale spell. As for the rest of the control, it's probably fine.

Turning his head, Subaru confirms there's nothing left where the Great Rabbits once were. Turning back, he confirms the tomb is safe too. Peeking cautiously out from the tomb are the expressionless Ryuzu clones. It seems all the Ryuzu duplicates managed to evacuate safely. Beside the tomb's entrance, Roswaal leans against the wall, Ram in his arms.

And then, Subaru sees Ram's hand touch Roswaal's cheek, and Roswaal, crying.

"——"

Seeing that, Subaru feels the weight in his chest rapidly lift. There are surely still many things they'll need to talk through. He's left Otto, Garfiel, and the others back at the mansion too. He believes they're safe, but they need to reunite and have a proper talk. There's a lot he wants to hear from Emilia about what happened here, too.

And yet, somehow, all of it feels like it'll probably be alright.

Even with so much still left unconfirmed, seeing Roswaal crying, and Ram watching him with a smile, made it feel like things would be alright.

"Subaru, here."

Letting out a long breath, Subaru's cheek is suddenly poked by Emilia's finger. Emilia smiles at Subaru looking at her, then gestures with her hand toward what's behind him. There, Beatrice still stands with her arms crossed, pouting, and—

"I think you should say a word or two to this hero of the hour, I suppose."

At the childish gesture of her puffed cheeks, Subaru gives a small nod. And then—

"Wah, eek!"

Slipping his hands under her arms, he hoists her light body up in one motion. Ignoring the cute shriek that escapes her, Subaru spins around on the spot, girl in his arms.

"You did amazing! As expected, I love you, Bea-ko!!"

"Wait, hold—wai, no, let, let go, I suppose! Betty isn't like this...!"

"There, there! Cute, cute! Bea-ko's wonderful! Bea-ko's the best! Bea-ko banzai!"

Showering her with praise, Subaru spins around and around, Beatrice still in his arms. Held aloft, Beatrice's face turns bright red, while Emilia watches the two of them romping about with an exceedingly gentle gaze.

And so, spinning vigorously, expressing his joy with his whole body, the Spirit and her contractor, still spinning—

"Ah—!"

—in the end, both slipped and went, together, face-first, into the snow.
