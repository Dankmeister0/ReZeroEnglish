Arc 6 Chapter 86: "Stories of Yesterday"

She had harbored her doubts for a long time now.

In order for Roswaal L. Mathers—the master to whom she had pledged her loyalty—to achieve his ultimate goal, it was necessary to kill the "Dragon" that protected the Kingdom of Lugunica.
That Ram was an indispensable piece toward that goal of Roswaal's, she had been told—by the man himself—nearly ten years ago now.

In the oni village that burned red, Roswaal had saved Ram and ██, and in exchange, he had asked for a price.
And Ram had decided to pay that price, in return for retribution against the oni tribe.
For that, she would cooperate with whatever scheme it took to kill the "Dragon," whatever it turned out to be.

The one point Ram had never settled within herself was the crucial question of how, exactly, one killed the "Dragon."
It would not be easy. No matter what tricks were played or measures taken, it would never be easy. And when that time came, what on earth could Ram actually do?

"You'll understand when the time comes. A role that only you—only you sist██—can fulfill."

She felt, somehow, as though she'd been spoken to in words with pieces missing.
What that meant, oddly enough, she had never thought deeply about until today—

"I finally understand. Roswaal-sama's true aim."

Every single one of Roswaal's actions existed for the sake of the final goal he had staked his long life upon.
For that, he had burrowed deep into the Kingdom of Lugunica. For that, he had saved Ram and ██. For that, he had seized the "Sanctuary" as a touchstone to test resolve. For that, he had tested Natsuki Subaru.
Even if he'd been forced to change his original plan, none of the groundwork laid up to that point would go to waste. No—he was a man who simply wouldn't let it go to waste.

And so, an answer had come to Ram as well.
The reason Roswaal had taken in the young oni sist██ as his final trump card.

██ had been taken in as a replacement for Ram's lost horn.
Ram and ██, sist██—two who made up one single oni—had been kept close at hand for the sake of killing the "Dragon."

Of course, Roswaal too could not escape the effects of the Authority of "Gluttony."
As a result, he too must have forgotten why ██ had been kept in his mansion. But even having forgotten, he must have quickly caught on again to his own aim.
And then, watching Ram and the others struggle to reclaim ██ from the harm "Gluttony" had wrought, he never once spoke that truth aloud. Honestly, thorough to the very end—

"That thoroughness of his never changes, does it."

Even after his plan was once derailed by Subaru's actions, he kept quietly working, biding his time beneath the surface to see his own goal through.
A troublesome nature. Who knew what he was up to right now, out of Ram's sight.
And yet, she also thought this:

—that the moment Roswaal permitted Ram to accompany Subaru and the others on this journey, bringing ██ along, perhaps he had already foreseen the possibility that things might turn out this way.

Maybe she was overthinking it.
To begin with, the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony's appearance at the Pleiades Watchtower had been an unforeseen contingency; there was no way he could have been certain Ram and ██ would end up in danger.
This might all be nothing more than a delusion born of Ram overrating the man she adored.
And yet—

"—Still, I'd rather believe the man I love trusted me. It feels better that way."

That he had believed that if Ram were there, ██ and the others would be protected to the end.
That, if it truly came down to it, he had trusted Ram to notice the scheme he'd hidden and choose, of her own will, to play the trump card meant for killing the "Dragon."
That Roswaal L. Mathers had taken the measure of the woman Ram had spent ten years becoming—thinking that, felt so much better.

So—

"—Right now, I feel absolutely wonderful. So go ahead and get beaten senseless."

The great feat achieved by the Fist King, Neiji Lockhart, was something of a legend on Ginunhive, the Sword-Slave Isle of the Sacred Vollachia Empire. —No, it had been a legend.

Now, having fallen victim to the Authority of "Gluttony" and lost both his "name" and his "memory," there is no one left anywhere who knows of the achievements he built as a sword-slave.
In the spectacles of the Sword-Slave Isle, where strength was put on display, Neiji had survived hundreds of death-matches armed with nothing but his bare fists alone, and in the end had won the rights of a freedman.
With refined fighting spirit gathered into his fists, he caught blades and shattered steel; before those fists, any obstacle was as soft as a maiden's skin—he could well have been called one of the most prolific killers of men in history.

The Carnivorous Beast, Beri Hainelga, was a serial killer without equal, one whose brutal methods once made the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko tremble. —No, it had once made them tremble.

Now, having fallen victim to the Authority of "Gluttony" and lost both her "name" and her "memory," not a single soul remembers the many tragedies and lessons her murders once produced.
Beri had shown abnormal growth from birth, attaining a bodily strength no ordinary human could ever achieve. And she had committed one atrocity after another—embracing the men she fancied and crushing them to death.
Her enormous frame gave her monstrous strength, and her tough skin turned aside even a blade without so much as a scratch. This killer, who craved touch and warmth, had been feared as one of the worst criminals in memory, alongside the "Bowel Hunter."

The career of the Leaper, Dorkell, was a strange one. Originally an ordinary merchant living in the Kararagi City-States, Dorkell was a deviant who had heard the voice of something inhuman and strayed from the beaten path—had been, that is.

Now, having fallen victim to the Authority of "Gluttony" and lost both his "name" and his "memory," the fact that he had once been mocked by so many as a deviant is gone, and no one remembers it at all.
One day, Dorkell had suddenly abandoned his entire life—wife and children included. He chose instead a path of devotion to something that did not exist, becoming the rare oddity who made contact with the Witch Cult.
In the end, Dorkell manifested a power distinct from Magic—one that differed even from Witch Cult doctrine, making him a heretic among heretics, cast out even by the Witch Cult itself.

In short, these were transcendent beings of a kind—monsters, oddities, madmen, each as extraordinary as the last.
No ordinary human could stand against even one of them, and beyond that, none of them ended as themselves alone—inside "Gluttony," they mixed together with every "memory" absorbed, refining into something even greater.

What would happen if Neiji Lockhart gained Beri Hainelga's endurance and Dorkell's power to appear and vanish without a trace?
That would be the completion of a single existence more monstrous than anything seen in recorded history.

Until now, the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony, Lye Batenkaitos, had never done such a thing.

The reason was that mixing every "memory" together in the great cauldron of himself risked his own existence being swallowed up by the contents of that cauldron.
But even if he drank down everything mixed within that cauldron, he himself would not be consumed—or so it seemed, at least, from Batenkaitos's own point of view.
And once that was so, there was not the slightest hesitation left in him to try what he had always refrained from before, to break through the very limits he himself had once set.

For every superior talent, career, and possibility this world had to offer—every last "delicacy"—devoured and hoarded within himself, that was what made him the self-styled gourmet who was Lye Batenkaitos.

And so it was on this day, in the tower rising from the sands at the world's eastern edge, that there was born a Warlock in whom every skill and preternatural ability, whether innate or acquired, had been fused at the highest possible order.

The Warlock possessed arms that could destroy anything, a body that could withstand any attack, sorcerous arts that could turn back any spell, and a wisdom that could grasp all things.
Nowhere in history had there ever existed a being so excellent in every capacity, and none would ever be born again, though thousands of years might pass.

This was the extraction of the world's every possibility, born of that detestable calamity known as the Witch Factor.
It was born from a great cauldron in which only the finest things had been boiled down—the supreme delicacy itself, made manifest—

"—I'll give you three chances."

Before that singular, unparalleled being, a girl with peach-colored hair held up three fingers and spoke.
Blood ran from her forehead, and a cold light lodged in her pale pink eyes. She stood there battered from head to toe, her slender body marked all over with grievous wounds that ought to have hindered any movement.
Beyond that, there were countless other reasons she shouldn't still be standing. He knew that well.
The greatest factor was not the wounds or the bleeding, but the fact that her frail body was not functioning as a proper Vessel for the existence that she was. —How pitiable, he thought.

"I'll let you land the first hit, three times. There's something I want to test. Quite the bargain, don't you think?"

And yet, as if entirely indifferent to such sentiment, the girl kept her three fingers raised, speaking as though to provoke him.
Three times—that was a mortal sentence. To begin with, he shouldn't have been able to endure even a single blow.
And there was no particular reason to refuse the offer, either.

He didn't even consider whether it might be a trap.
She wasn't the sort of girl who would propose such a bargain for no reason. Only, it seemed necessary to convey, in earnest, that excessive confidence could be one's own undoing.
If he pulled her into the same great cauldron, surely there was something to be learned—

"—Big Sister, here I come."

Coiling power into his back leg and exploding forward, he drove home a blow exactly as requested.
It was a single strike distilled from the Fist King's technique and destructive power—far, far more force than needed to destroy one girl—and it was sucked toward her lovely face—

"First—one."

"—?!"

At the very instant he thought he'd caught her clean, the girl's face dodged the fist by a hair's breadth. And catching his outstretched arm, she brought a knee up in the same motion, snapping his right arm at the elbow.

An impossible turn of events. That toughened body, one even a Holy Sword's slash couldn't cut through—the girl put her knee into the one fixed point that existed, the joint of a fully extended elbow, and destroyed it.
But even so—

Riding the momentum of his broken right arm, his left leg snapped up toward the girl's face.
It wasn't payback for the arm, but this too was a knee strike aimed at the face. A long, slender leg, a white knee that should have crushed her soft, delicate nose and ruined that beautiful face—

"Second."

Hearing those words spill from her thin lips, he could hardly believe his ears.
That blow should have made speech impossible. Yet the girl, facing the knee strike sweeping in from the side, simply traced her hand through the air as if drawing a line, deflecting its trajectory gently upward.
The knee that had been thrown sailed clean over her head, as though it had been aimed from the start at empty sky.
And then—

"Third."

Dodging the right arm and the left knee strike, he spun his body and drove a left elbow—sharp and hard—that, had it connected, would have crushed the girl's skull like a piece of fruit and scattered its contents across the tower floor.
That was—that should have been—what happened.
The elbow, aimed at her temple, only sent a few strands of her peach-colored hair fluttering.
And the girl, dodging the elbow as though she'd known it was coming from the very start, reached out again with her hand and seized his face.

"How foolish."

She said it with a coldness beyond anything, and slammed his head into the floor with tremendous force.

Slamming down onto the floor the writhing, twisting form of Batenkaitos as it shifted shape before her eyes, Ram jabbed three fingers straight at his face.

"Anyone who fights believing they have three chances loses all three. Fight without any real intent to win, and even luck itself abandons you."

"Gh—guh..."

"Thank you. That's proof enough. Even Fortune herself is no match for Ram anymore."

The most fearsome enemy, to Ram, had always been sheer, senseless fortune itself.
But now Ram had tamed it with her own two hands, brought even that down to grovel at her feet.
There was nothing left in the world for her to fear.

"—!"

Batenkaitos, sprawled on the ground, kicked both legs up and rose with the momentum of bringing them down. Ram stepped forward without hesitation to meet the man as he swung his broken right arm at her.
Blood flowed from her forehead. But that pain, that sense of loss, made Ram's blood boil from somewhere deep inside her.

Ah, how infuriating. This sensation, this exhilaration—she had always found it so bothersome.
That detestable instinct of the oni: welcoming battle, exulting in the exercise of power, blood boiling with the thirst to kill an enemy that deserved killing—Ram had always hated it.
That was why, that night when the horn on her forehead had broken, she had thought she'd finally been set free.

"How ironic."

Muttering that, Ram seized the arm swung at her and threw the man over her shoulder. She flung him to the floor, kicked his head, sending him sprawling deeper into the corridor.
Behind her, she would not let him get near the injured Earth Dragon and the girl leaned against the wall.

"—————"

The girl who kept on sleeping, unchanged—on her forehead, a white horn quietly asserted its presence, glowing faintly.
By rights, a horn shouldn't function for someone sleeping without any will of her own, even if she were oni.
And yet, even if the feeling of it remained lost to her, Ram and the girl were twin sisters—and that bond called "Synesthesia" was, at times, an intangible connection formed between beings of close blood.

Ram herself had felt that presence over this past year of caring for the sleeping Rem.
It wasn't only the resemblance in their looks—it was that very sensation that had let Ram accept, so smoothly and so objectively, the fact that this girl was her little sister.
But that "Synesthesia" had never brought anything across from the sleeping Rem.
If Rem had so much as had a single frightening dream, that fear might have reached Ram—but nothing like that had ever come through, not once in this entire year.
Which meant the sleeping Rem was not dreaming at all. —She had only been staring, endlessly, into the darkness behind her own eyelids, breathing in a world of nothing at all.

Which was why noticing this misuse of "Synesthesia" here was pure happenstance.
Infuriating as it was to admit, it was thanks to Subaru's idea. He'd used some strange power to take on Ram's burden and lend her the strength to fight, and from that, the idea of doing the same thing had occurred to her.

Those linked by "Synesthesia" could share strong emotions, and sometimes even injury or pain.
The principle was unclear, but there had once been a hypothesis in a book Ram had skimmed through in Beatrice's forbidden library.

—That "Synesthesia" was the effect of Od connecting between two distinct individuals.

Od was the wellspring of power that existed in the depths of a person—another way of saying Soul.
It was sometimes used as a power source for casting Magic in place of Mana, but originally, Od was the pure quality that made a person who they were.
A domain untouchable by anyone or anything—the theory went that when it happened to connect between beings born of the same womb, the result was "Synesthesia."

In the end, that was dismissed as folk theory, never proven.
But if it had been kept in that forbidden library, it probably wasn't complete nonsense either. More than anything, Ram simply liked that theory, personally.

That she and someone else had been connected from birth—what a lovely thought.

"That troublesome horn caused no end of headaches."

The destructive impulse that flowed from the white horn—Ram had loathed it.
It was infuriating, too, how everyone around her had celebrated her as a reincarnation of the oni god. There wasn't the slightest connection between Ram and some hoary, ill-defined ancient existence like that.
Value ought to be placed on Ram herself, not on some thing like that.

Which was why, if she'd been connected to someone else, entirely apart from that "thing," what a lovely thought that was.
And if such a connection to someone truly existed—

"—Surely, Ram would have loved that child from birth, without fail."

Even if it were an infant, she wouldn't have questioned the means to protect that bond.
To protect it, to cherish it, to love it, to adore it—she would have given everything.
So—

"Forgive me. For making even you carry this burden, at a time like this—your wicked older sister that she is."

Through "Synesthesia," Ram shared the strain placed on her own body with her sleeping little sister.
It was thanks to having once experienced Subaru's Authority firsthand. Once she'd seen a thing, Ram could generally reproduce it herself in kind.
Subaru's power worked on the same principle—it likely forcibly linked another person's Od with his own, triggering an extremely one-directional form of "Synesthesia." He could send his own burden over if he wanted to, but Subaru, being a fool, never did.
Instead, he took it all onto himself, trying to lighten the load for his allies.

"—How foolish."

The same words she'd just spoken to Batenkaitos.
And yet, though the words were the same, they didn't carry the same ring at all.

What Subaru had done with his Authority, Ram could only reproduce through the "Synesthesia" connecting her to Rem.
She had no idea what it cost Subaru on his end. But this, at least, would make things somewhat better. Ram, too, could escape the ghastly situation of being permanently linked to Subaru.
In exchange, Ram's burden was now flowing over to Rem instead—

"—————"

The sleeping Rem said nothing at all.
Only, valiantly, using the white horn placed in her clasped hands—the horn hidden within the staff, Ram's own broken horn, serving as a catalyst—she kept sending the mana flowing into her own horn over to Ram, without pause.
There was no telling just how much strain it placed on Rem's body to channel the enormous quantities of mana Ram needed.

—Which was why this had to be a short, decisive battle.

"Hey, Big Siiister—!!"

Roaring, the kicked-away Batenkaitos came bounding back toward Ram.
Using the power of the so-called "Leaper," he could erase the distance between them in an instant with sorcery—but even a technique of appearing and disappearing without a trace was meaningless if his opponent had it completely figured out.

"Slow, you dolt. At this rate Ram will be an old woman before you land a hit."

With "Clairvoyance," she stole a view through her opponent's own eyes.
His focus, the movement of his eyes, where his strength gathered—putting all of it together made reading his aim easier than reading minds.

Dodging the outstretched palm, she slipped past and twisted all five of his fingers the wrong way.
Driving an elbow into his throat just as it started to scream, she sent him crashing into the wall with a sharp spinning back kick.

"Guh...!"

"Though Ram is cute even in old age, mind you."

Saying so, she grabbed the collar of his clothes and yanked him down, slamming the back of his head into the ground once more. Stamping her heel down onto his face, she shattered his nose.
Stepping back from the arms grasping at her, she unleashed a barrage of wind blades.

"—Gyaaaaaah!!"

"Such an ugly scream, for such a pretty face."

Sliced apart all over his body by wind blades, scattering a spray of blood, Batenkaitos was blown backward.
As ever, that body kept restlessly shifting shape, but from the neck up, it stubbornly refused to change from the face she now knew.
It was nauseating. Ram would never do such a thing herself—it had no class.

"—————"

Bleeding profusely, Batenkaitos flung himself back at her again, with no finesse at all.
Or rather, he probably was employing some near-godlike technique. Combining the powers of various transcendent beings, he was surely inventing techniques no one else could ever reproduce.

Ram crushed those superb attacks, honed to excel in every direction, with a violence that surpassed them all.
A hundred grand techniques, a thousand—she twisted them apart with a force of a hundred million. This, now, was simply that kind of fight.

She wouldn't even let Batenkaitos touch her. She was in fine form.
Unlike with Subaru, this body suited her, after all. She let loose two more of her restraints.
Probably around fifty percent of her prime—no, back then Ram had still been young, so factoring in her growth since, she was likely far stronger now than she'd been at her peak.
And not drowning in that strength was Ram's own advantage.

"It really was for the best, having my horn broken."

Without that night, she might have eventually succumbed to the horn's temptation.
She'd like to say it was impossible, but there was no way to know the answer to something that never happened. So Ram took pride in the path she'd walked, deciding that things being as they were now was for the best.
Thanks to losing her horn, Ram had never had to become the "oni" she so despised.

"Besides, if that night had never happened, I never would have met Roswaal-sama—so there's really no comparison to be made."

That conclusion, beyond which there was nothing more to say, was the answer Ram offered as she thrust her palm forward.
Right before her eyes, at the tip of her nose, Batenkaitos appeared, having leapt through space. His face, startled that his landing spot had been read in advance, was seized in her grip.

"You can't jump twice in a row, can you? I've grown tired of your magic tricks. That face of yours, too."

"Wa—"

"I won't wait."

Coldly discarding the word, Ram generated a blade of wind against the palm gripping his face.
In an instant, his eyes, nose, lips, ears—every single part of his face was shredded all at once, and Batenkaitos screamed, spitting blood.

She would let the wind blades carve until that face was gone entirely.
But the moment she carried that out, Batenkaitos's form vanished in an instant from within her grip.
Even so, escape didn't mean escape from the wound, from the pain, from reality itself.

"Ah, ah, aaaaaaah—!!"

Wailing, weeping not tears but a great flood of blood instead, Batenkaitos writhed on the ground.
Watching him, Ram slowly advanced in that direction.

Hearing the approaching sound of Ram's footsteps, Batenkaitos's form changed.
A tall man, possessed of a powerful build. —She knocked aside the fist that came at her and snapped his knee in two.

Kicked down, changing shape again to flee from Ram.
A bearded giant of a man, curling up to harden his defenses. —She kicked that body up into the air, pinned him to the ceiling with a barrage of fists, and turned the flesh beneath his prized skin to mush.

To kill Ram, who dealt out unending torment, Batenkaitos's form changed again.
A bald old man, sorcery to appear and vanish without a trace. —Now pitying a trick whose secret had long since been exposed, she seized him, pressed his face against the wall, and ran along his body as if scraping the whole of it away.

"Ah, bababababababa—!!"

Pinned down by Ram's overwhelming strength, unable to escape as his entire body was ground away, Batenkaitos's form writhed and twisted in her grip, searching for some optimal answer.
Sealing off every single one, Ram laid to rest, by force, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand victims.

For those swallowed up into "Gluttony"—their honed skills, the paths they had walked, the feelings they had loved, all trampled and treated with contempt—she offered this violence as a memorial rite for them.

Their skills, their paths, their feelings—never to be exploited again.
Not a single thing would ever work on Ram.

"Take responsibility for yourself."

Swinging her arm through to the end, Ram flung away the blasphemer, his whole body savagely carved apart.
Rolling across the corridor floor of the watchtower, Batenkaitos twitched and trembled. His form slowly, slowly, slowly began to change.
The countless "memories" he had relied upon for that shape faded away, and—

"Well, it's been a while. I've been wanting to see that face again. Somewhere around third from the top of the list, though."

"—Ah, guh, ah."

Wiping the blood from her forehead, Ram's gaze fell upon Batenkaitos as her lips loosened.
This, in the true sense, was Batenkaitos himself. No longer the small-time being borrowing others' faces and techniques—this was Batenkaitos, restored to his own face and form.

No matter who he became, no matter who he relied upon or clung to, one couldn't run from oneself.
Just as breaking her horn hadn't changed the fact that Ram was oni.
Just as having her "memory" stolen hadn't changed the fact that Ram was Rem's older sister.

"At the very least, at the very end, won't you try resisting with your own strength?"

"—————"

"Come to think of it, you said something interesting on the spiral staircase, didn't you? You have a younger brother and sister, it seems. Why not show a little backbone, for their sake?"

The pained, ragged breathing of the fallen Batenkaitos abruptly stopped.
He hadn't died. He'd reacted to her words.

At the sound of "brother and sister," Batenkaitos's breathing calmed, if only slightly. And then, slowly, he raised himself up where he lay—

"Our... our... little... sister..."

"Don't touch her, is that it? Unfortunately, do you really think you're in any position to make that claim? Have you ever once, even a single time, lent an ear to someone else's plea?"

"Ev-even so..."

"—————"

His blood-soaked face twisted into a wretched grimace, Batenkaitos pleaded in a tearful voice.
At the anguished ring of that plea, Ram's eyes narrowed just slightly. Then, exhaling a breath, she lowered her gaze—

"Think about—"
"—Big Sister, you're too kind."

In the instant her eyes drifted away, Batenkaitos left only those words behind and vanished.
The Leaper Dorkell's spatial leap—without leaving so much as a trace, the blasphemer had vanished from sight.

—He'd fled.

"Hah! Ahahah! Ahaha-hahahaha!"

Using the preternatural power of the Leaper, Dorkell, Batenkaitos had fled Ram's domain.
No more thoughts of devouring Ram—no matter how it looked, he had chosen to run.

He couldn't win. He couldn't win. There was no beating that thing.
Just as he'd thought, that was a monster. Buying time only for her to come back stronger was beyond all reason.
That was a being that belonged on no plate—not fine dining, not the vilest of fare. She simply didn't belong on any plate at all.

"Sorry, Rui, Roy, the both of you—but preparation is everything, when it comes to fine dining."

Even as he nursed the wounds covering his entire body, Batenkaitos sneered at the younger siblings who'd been after the same prey.
Rui had withdrawn from the front lines early on, and Roy was likely still rampaging somewhere else by now. If only that oni would go after the two of them instead, he could make his own leisurely escape.

Roy, with his "Bad Taste," didn't know when to pull back, so he might well end up dead—but there was nothing to be done about that.
If anything, he'd found Roy's "Bad Taste" tiresome. Because that man ravaged whatever feeding ground he pleased, there had probably been plenty of fine dining stolen away from him.

—No, wait. Had anything he'd ever eaten up to now really been "fine dining" at all?

"Useless, the lot of them... Ahh, damn it! Damn it, damn it, damn it! To think a thing like that existed! I wish I'd never known!!"

That was not the emotion of a little sister who adored her big sister.
It was something else entirely, boundless and immense, a longing directed at something vast beyond measure—an intense feeling born from deep within Batenkaitos, a craving that, truly and utterly, came from the very bottom of his heart.

He wanted to devour that. To savor it, with everything he had.
Claiming to be a gourmet, he'd thought he had devoured every emotion, every superior talent this world had to offer. But now that he knew the "real thing" existed in this world, everything else had gone pale and colorless.

Everything Lye Batenkaitos, as the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony, had believed valuable and gathered up, had crumbled away into dust.
That grand banquet table that had once seemed to shine so brilliantly had turned into nothing more than mud pies in a sandbox.

"I want that."

Want it, want it, want it, want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it want it—

For the sake of tasting that, he'd throw away everything, and think nothing of it.
For the sake of devouring that, losing everything he'd hoarded up until now would leave him with no regrets.

He didn't want to taste anything but that. He didn't want to fill himself with anything but that.

"Guh—uegh."

Even as he ran, vomit spilled from the corner of Batenkaitos's mouth.
It wasn't pain or suffering that caused it. It was simply unbearable. What he had believed to be the pinnacle was not, and now that he knew true supremacy, everything that had ever filled him felt filthy by comparison.
Why had he ever thought anything but that was wonderful? Praised it? Savored it?

If loving anything but the truly wonderful made one a "gourmet," then what was he?

"Ahh, that's right, it's true, isn't it, of course it is, of course, of course, of course it is, precisely because it's true, precisely because I want it to be true! Overdrink! Overeat!"

A surging appetite, a hunger being filled, and a heartfelt scream begging to have what he wanted.
To become one, to mix together—if appetite meant taking an outside existence into oneself, then the "Gluttony" driving him was the ultimate love.

"I love her, I love her... yes! I love her!! Big Sister— no, Ram! We love—"

The colossal emotion sprouting within him, the words he tried to shout out—cut off suddenly.
The cause was pain, a fresh agony that had just occurred.

"—Huh?"

Pressing a hand to his cheek, Batenkaitos checked his palm, slick with blood.
A newly opened wound—one that had torn open his cheek as he fled toward the outside of the tower. The cause: empty space, with nothing there at all.

"—————"

Reaching a finger silently into that space, Batenkaitos watched his own fingertip get sliced.
In that empty space, there was an invisible blade.

"Hah."

It was the very same technique Batenkaitos had shown Ram on the spiral staircase.
The technique of setting an invisible blade in empty space was the art of some legendary Shinobi, but whose "memory" it had come from no longer mattered, since he'd already discarded it.
The problem was that a blade he had no memory of placing was here.

"No way..."

Dodging the blade that had shallowly cut his finger, Batenkaitos tried to press further inward—only for his toe to be blown clean off, and he reeled back with a shriek of "Ahhh!"
Then, even the back of his head, thrown backward, was shallowly shaved, and he froze in place, his face going rigid.

—He was surrounded by invisible blades.

"...Haha, seriously?"

He had shown it only once.
Once, in the middle of battle, and it was an invisible technique to begin with, so it wasn't as if she'd actually seen it either.
And what's more, she hadn't even physically come to this spot. And yet, she had anticipated his escape route and set the invisible blades here in advance.

"—————"

Batenkaitos raised a hand—not to the left eye, crushed and ruined by the wind blades, but to the right eye, which had barely survived. —Through "Clairvoyance," Ram was sharing that eye.
She hadn't let Batenkaitos escape. Overlapping with that eye, she wouldn't let him get away.

"Heh."

Batenkaitos laughed. There was nothing left to do but laugh.
He had loved. For the first time, he had yearned, fiercely, deeply, for something. He was utterly smitten by how far beyond all reason she was.
And then—

"Ah! Wait, wait for me! Wait, wait, just a little more! Just a little more is all I need! Just a little more, that's all!!"

No matter how much he pleaded with "Clairvoyance," his voice wouldn't reach—he knew that much already.
So his desperate calling wasn't meant to be heard by her. It was meant to drive himself forward.

In a great hurry, Batenkaitos clung to the wall right beside him.
He'd been rash, tossing aside everything he'd devoured. If only he still had the Fist King's peerless skill, this wouldn't have been such trouble.
Setting even that regret aside for later, Batenkaitos slammed his arm against the invisible blades. Both wrists were blown off, blood spraying from the wounds.
It hurt, it hurt, it hurt, it hurt, it hurt—but the pain didn't matter anymore.

"Take it, please—our feelings!! Bear witness—our wish!!"

Just pressing his blood-spraying arms against the wall, he wrote with all his might.
With pitch-black blood, using his small body for all it was worth, he wrote in huge letters across the wall of the sand tower.

"Bwuhah!"

Then, stepping back on his own, he opened his right eye wide to look at what he'd written.
So that the bloody letters drawn on the wall would reach the one person he wanted, from the very bottom of his heart.
Because she, surely, would watch it through to the very end, still overlapping with him, right up until the last moment.

"Ahh, I love—"

—Before he could finish the words, the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony's head was severed by a blade of wind.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—Fura."

Sweeping her finger, Ram, having loosed the wind blade, murmured only that one word.
The scale of a spell affected its area of effect, but its raw power hardly changed. Especially for something as simple as slicing off a thin neck with the wind blade's edge—the smallest measure of power was more than enough.

Chasing after the fleeing back of "Gluttony," Ram had sent only a single blade of wind.
"Gluttony's" desperate flight had been easy enough to track with "Clairvoyance." She'd used a small trick to slow his footing so her aim would be true, and hastily improvised as it was, it seemed to have worked well enough.

And then, right before that final blade struck home, "Gluttony" had done something bizarre.
He'd sliced off his own arm and used the blood to write letters on the wall.
Filthy, terribly one-sided—it was a vile piece of harassment not even worth looking at, but—

"—————"

At the very last moment, she watched those blood-written letters spin as they flew off, and closed her eyes.
She hadn't been able to feel at ease otherwise, that was all. There was no sense of obligation compelling her to watch it through—not the slightest.

The blasphemer had made the wrong final choice.
If he'd staked his life for his younger siblings' sake, Ram might not have been entirely without mercy. But instead, he'd used that as bait to try to deceive her, turning it into a tool for his own survival.
He probably didn't even know how to return what he'd devoured. If he had, he would have used it as a bargaining chip to beg for his life. He hadn't done even that. So he would face the consequences.

"Those who grasp the sword, by the sword; those who rely on magic, by magic; those who entrust themselves to flame, by flame. And those who pray to oni, by oni—destroyed by whatever they made their refuge."

—That was the principle of retribution Ram believed in.

Lowering her arm, she let out a long breath.
Then Ram turned and made her way back down the badly wrecked corridor. She hadn't been able to fight close by, so she'd deliberately put distance between them.
That distance now felt maddening, and her steps naturally quickened.

"—!!"

Climbing back over the crumbled wall, what greeted Ram was the high cry of an Earth Dragon.
The jet-black Earth Dragon had skillfully used her own body to hide Rem behind her. If, worst case, whoever came back hadn't been Ram, she'd likely meant to serve as a shield.
Battered as badly as she was, still trying so valiantly to keep her word to Subaru—an Earth Dragon far too good for the likes of him, really—

"—No, that's not it, is it. Did you want to protect Rem too?"

"—————"

"I see. ...Good girl, Patrasche."

Gently, she stroked the Earth Dragon's neck.
This wounded girl, too, would need to be carried to the "Green Room" soon. Even an Earth Dragon showing such admirable loyalty couldn't be pushed further with injuries like these.
Ram had no wish to treat her sister's savior—her savior dragon—so roughly, either.

Having offered the Earth Dragon her thanks, Ram went to Rem, sheltered behind her.
The shared use of the horn's power through "Synesthesia" had already been undone, and there was no horn on her forehead now. Still, the toll of shouldering Ram's near-oni-god strength was undoubtedly eating away at that body.
Even Ram herself grew heavy-hearted just thinking of the backlash sure to come before long.
But—

"Right now, in this moment, I won't think about anything so tactless."

Kneeling slowly down beside her, Ram placed a hand against her sleeping sister's cheek.
The bond of sisterhood that had never quite felt real to her before now carried far more certainty than it ever had, and she felt a warmth of affection and preciousness welling up within her.

Having lost her horn, having lost the power of the oni god reborn, she had lived out her days up until now.
As for what that night of fire had been for, Ram had, until now, concluded that it had been for the sake of her becoming herself—and she still didn't think that conclusion wrong.

Only, from this very moment, it had changed.
That the reason her horn had broken that night was—

"—It was so that, today, here, Ram could come to know that she is Rem's big sister."

Through "Synesthesia," through the touching of soul to soul, she could feel it now—that they were twins who had shared one world between them, irreplaceable as sister to sister.

"I want to talk with you more than ever before. About what kind of time you and Ram have spent together. What kind of yesterdays we've piled up. Let's fill in the memories we're missing, together."

Since time never stops, always moving forward, there would be countless memories of the future yet to accumulate.
So that nothing would vanish away, unknown, forgotten—every night, again and again, let flowers of memory bloom between them.

"Let's talk about lots and lots of yesterdays."

The "Sleeping Beauty" gave no answer.
And yet, that silence didn't tighten around Ram's chest with pain—instead, filled with something warm, she smiled.
Still smiling, no longer doubting her own feelings even for a moment, she let her lips move.

"—I love you, Rem."

Surely, no matter what kind of time the two of them spent together from here on, this feeling alone would never betray her.
Strange, that she should speak the very same words as the twisted Sin Archbishop's dying declaration—and yet, even the same sound, spoken this way, could never carry the same ring.

Between one who knows nothing of love, and one who lives for love, the words could never carry the same ring.
They could never, ever sound the same.
