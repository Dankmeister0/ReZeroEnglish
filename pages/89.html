Arc 3 Chapter 13: "Compatriots"

Slumped on the floor, Subaru keenly felt the strength of his own incredibly rotten luck.

Not being found at all would have been the best outcome, sure, but being found by someone he already knew was a stroke of fortune in its own right. And on top of that, the pair who'd found him were people he'd only just met yesterday — life really was unpredictable. Yesterday's encounter with them, in other words, had existed for no purpose other than letting Subaru get through this very moment. That wouldn't be an exaggeration.

"So — in respect for whatever fate had in mind, let's keep this nice and peaceful—"

From a formal kneeling position he bent deeply at the waist, planted both hands on the ground in a V-shape, and gently pressed the crown of his head to the floor. Defenselessness, gracelessness, powerlessness — a triple constellation of Nothing, guaranteed to inspire absolute pity in one's opponent: the ancient DOGEZA technique, handed down since the days of the Great Japanese Empire.

Having come back to his senses and realized he hadn't actually gotten himself out of this mess, the option Subaru settled on was this transparently obvious full-groveling apology. Other options had included forcing his way through, or pretending to be crazy and dancing around to muddy the waters, but he could vividly picture himself getting cut down in either case, so both were rejected on the spot. Having arrived at the choice with the highest apparent survival rate, this was him clinging to his opponent's mercy.

"——"

The silence stretched on, a crushing pressure bearing down on the shoulders of Subaru's prostrate form. Unable to withstand the weight of it, Subaru snuck a glance upward.

Filling his field of view as he peeked up was the sole of a small shoe—

"What a boring fellow."

"Guh—!"

That bored remark came paired with a front kick straight to Subaru's face. With a force that gave no impression of holding back, his kneeling head snapped backward. The back of his skull struck the closet door, and even as tears of pain blurred his vision, the sole of that shoe never left it, grinding down all the while.

The girl to whom the shoe belonged kept her foot planted on the stunned, immobile Subaru as she spoke.

"Sneaking into the royal castle, no less — I wondered just how much resolve that would take, but... how utterly trivial. I've no ear for apologies. If you meant to apologize, you shouldn't have done the thing in the first place."

Flinging words mixed with contempt and mockery, the girl kept gouging Subaru's cheek with her pointed heel. Groaning at the sharp pain, Subaru instinctively tried to knock the foot away, but—

"Don't move, idiot. The princess doing this herself is her being kind. You want my interrogation instead? I'm clumsy with my hands, so it hurts for real."

The tip of a greatsword, drawn without a sound, was pressed against the throat that had tried to move. Before the eyes of a gasping Subaru, the one-armed man who'd done it murmured languidly in a voice hard to make out.

The thick-bodied blade was, in terms Subaru's own knowledge could place it, closest to something called a qinglongdao — a "green dragon blade." He wielded that thick, oversized sword one-handed as though it were nothing. Needless to say, there was no way an unarmed amateur like Subaru could hope to match that level of skill.

While Subaru was left speechless by the professional's ability to seize the initiative without tipping his hand at all, the girl, foot still planted on him, looked up at the retainer beside her.

"Hey now, Al. Don't go pulling out something so dangerous right next to my foot. If my jewel-like skin were to get so much as a scratch, it would be a loss for this entire world."

"Relax, princess. I make it a policy never to do what I can't. Especially not to you — you're the one I owe. Just think of yourself riding on a super-reinforced mudboat."

Keeping his nerves on edge through the master-and-retainer exchange playing out before him, Subaru judged that moving right now, at least, would be unwise. And yet it was equally true that simply letting the situation run its course wouldn't lead anywhere good. He needed to seize some kind of opening—

"A mudboat's gonna sink... put me on a proper ship..."

Squeezing the words out, Subaru staked his life on pointing out the odd thing he'd just heard. It was the orange-haired girl who slowly turned her head at those words. She overlooked, for the moment, the fact that Subaru — whom she was trampling underfoot — had spoken without her permission, and said—

"Quite some nerve, speaking without my permission while you await my judgment. Very well — if you care so little for your life—"

"Whoa, hold up a sec, princess. What'd you just say, kid?"

Stopping his mistress just as she was about to deliver some cruel pronouncement, Al leaned in and asked Subaru. The retainer's rudeness seemed to grate on the girl's nerves, but he, fixated on Subaru, didn't notice.

Inside his jet-black helmet, Al fixed Subaru with a stare from eyes invisible from the outside.

"I had a feeling about this ever since I caught a glimpse of you yesterday... Hey, kid."

Still with his cheek being gouged by the girl's foot, Subaru looked up at Al with the half-open eye he had free. From Al's manner, Subaru judged that his own hunch hadn't been so far off the mark. Their gazes locked, Subaru held his breath and waited for Al's next words. And then—

"When the wind blows—"

"...? The, uh, barrel maker profits?"

"Talk about next year—"

"—and the ogres laugh!"

"Laughter brings—"

"—good fortune!"

Declaring it firmly, Subaru kept looking up at Al's face. Inside the jet-black helmet, whatever change came over his expression didn't reach the outside. But he shook his head, then shrugged his one-armed shoulder.

"I see. So that's how it is, huh. Well, this beats all."

"What's this, satisfying yourself with an understanding all your own? Explain it to me too. What was the meaning of that incomprehensible exchange just now?"

Displeased at Al's air of having understood was the girl, left out of the loop entirely. She glanced back and forth between Al's manner and Subaru's face, which wore an expression that seemed to share not a little of the same sentiment.

"I am not so generous as to laugh off insolence piled on insolence. So answer with care. What was that exchange just now?"

"—If I had to put it into words, it was something like confirming each other's hometown."

"And what of it..."

To the question posed in a low voice, Subaru answered in a similarly low tone. The girl frowned at how little sense the answer made, but almost at once her eyes narrowed as though something had occurred to her.

"—The same hometown? Al."

"Probably, yeah."

Once again, Al confirmed his mistress's question with a shrug. Then he sheathed the qinglongdao he'd drawn, and looked back down at Subaru anew.

"Guess I've got a few more things I want to ask now. How about it?"

"...If it's an interrogation, I guess I'd be happy if you threw in a katsudon or something."

"Me, I'm more a unadon guy myself. Though you won't find either one around here."

So saying, Al made a sound that, from the tone of his voice alone, could be judged as laughter, and held out his free hand. Subaru took the hand and gripped it firmly in return.

"Life sure is unpredictable, huh. For real."

So he muttered, mixing the sense of having narrowly escaped with his life together with an unexpected surprise.

"Amnesia?"

Rising up off the changing room floor, having secured at least a provisional right to keep living, Subaru muttered that in a bewildered voice.

Nodding at that muttered question was the jet-black helmet — Al. With his one arm, he roughly scratched at the seam between his helmet and light armor, around the nape of his neck.

"It's what you'd call a loss of episodic memory. General knowledge, this and that proper noun, all that kind of stuff hasn't gone anywhere. And yet just the part having to do with myself has popped right out. Pretty textbook, right?"

"Having someone else fire off loanwords at me like that feels weird somehow... Guess I've gotten pretty steeped in this other-world fantasy stuff myself."

Answering Al, who seemed to want agreement, with a wry smile, Subaru once again let the fact he'd just confirmed sink in. That is to say—

"You're definitely, without a doubt, someone summoned to another world just like me."

"Judging by what common sense I've got to compare it against, anyway."

So saying, he shook with a gesture of the shoulders — probably laughing, Subaru guessed. Al's manner had softened considerably compared to their exchange just moments before. Subaru could sense from it an intimacy on par with — no, greater than — the one from their meeting yesterday. In a sense, that was only natural. Because Subaru, too, found himself feeling an empathy for the man that was anything but detached.

"I hadn't really thought about it, but I guess as long as I exist, it wouldn't be strange for there to be other people summoned like me. Al-san, do you know of anyone else?"

"Cut it out with the -san, that's a pain, brother. I go by Al around here, so just stick with that. Now, about other people from back home—"

Looking like it was hard to say, Al adjusted the position of his helmet and dropped his head slightly.

"Sorry, but I don't know of anyone else. You're the first compatriot I've ever met myself. Honestly, I even suspected there was a chance I was out of my mind."

Subaru's eyes went wide with surprise. At that reaction, he said, self-deprecatingly, "I mean, it makes sense, right?"

"I've got memories of a world that isn't here, but I don't have any memory of the self that supposedly held those memories. You think I could go without suspecting that this 'me' who just suddenly popped into existence had cooked up some mystery backstory as the end result of pure delusion? Memories of another world — honestly, to me, that could only look like a lonely kind of madness. But—"

His tone as he spoke had been gloomy, but by the latter half it cleared away. Riding that momentum, he slapped Subaru's shoulder hard.

"You were here, brother. Thanks to that, I've been saved. My memories were real. I wasn't some crazy lunatic. Just that alone — ah, I've been saved."

Subaru could tell that mixed into the ring of relief was a note on the verge of tears, and he thought of the weight of the man's far-too-lonely days before him.

There had been no one, anywhere, who could share his memories with him. Subaru was in the same boat in that regard, but Subaru had, as an anchor for those memories, his own recollections of himself to fall back on. Only with both of those things together could the sturdy self called Natsuki Subaru exist in another world. But Al had no such anchor that ought to have been there. Inside him lay memories of another world that no one else knew existed — a world he himself couldn't even grasp as real. In that vast, overwhelming sea of memory, from which he could get nothing but other people's incomprehension, he had gone on drowning, endlessly.

Chilled by the weight of it, Subaru asked, his lips trembling despite himself. It was something he hadn't given much thought to until now, but once he became conscious of it, he couldn't help but press the question. It was—

"Al, just how long have you actually spent in this world?"

The time Subaru had spent in this world, in real time, amounted to no more than about twenty days. His memories of his original world were still plenty vivid, and he was still in the state of an amateur at this other world, where every single thing in it still felt fresh.

At Subaru's question, Al fell silent for a moment, then—

"Since I came to this world — I'm not certain, but — it should be around eighteen years."

"Eigh—?!"

Hearing an unexpected number of years, Subaru's very words of reply caught in his throat. Al confirmed Subaru's shock with a nod.

"I don't have the memories to know my real age... but I figure I'm probably getting on toward around forty by now. I was summoned around the same age as you, I think?"

Touching his chin, Al said this while giving Subaru's whole body a good long look-over. Feeling anew the weight of what he'd just said, Subaru found himself even less able to casually offer some word of comfort for those painful days. There was still plenty he wanted to say and ask. But thinking that digging it up might only lead back to Al's pain, it wasn't so simple—

"So?"

What cut off Subaru's sentimentality was the voice of the third person present, who had stayed silent up till now. He turned his head. Leaning her back against the changing room wall, arms crossed and eyes closed, stood the girl. She tapped her crossed arms irritably with a finger.

"This was worth interrupting my judgment for, I trust. There'd better have been some fruit to it, Al."

"For me, more than I could've asked for. Whether it amounts to anything for you, princess, I couldn't say... but at the very least, I found out that my own ravings weren't just ravings after all."

"Saying that with such a clean face I can't even get angry. —Now then, as for what's to be done with you."

Pushing Al aside with her hand, the girl stood before Subaru. The pressure radiating off the small girl — its sheer scale left Subaru overwhelmed despite himself. A weight he hadn't felt at their meeting yesterday. It was, without meaning to, something that made him keenly feel the vast, deep gulf lying between her and him.

"So you're spouting off that you, same as Al here, came from beyond the Great Waterfall?"

"The Great Waterfall?"

"The very end of the ends of the world. A cursed place of finality. A raging torrent of water that sweeps away everything in this world — that is the Great Waterfall. It's said no one has ever seen beyond it."

Placing a finger under the chin of the immobile Subaru and lifting his face up, the girl went on.

"Every so often, some fool turns up crowing about having come from beyond the Great Waterfall. Most of the time it's some vulgar type just trying to grab attention with big talk... but Al's a bit different."

"I'd love to know the grounds for that judgment..."

"Instinct."

The girl casually cut down Subaru's one attempt at pushback with that single word. Having declared as much to a speechless Subaru, she then turned back to Al behind her.

"One jester to ease my boredom is enough. That is my decision. Do you have any objection, Al?"

"Oh, I've got one, I've got a big one. See, princess, thing is — I'm pretty sure honestly there's basically no one else from back home for me anymore. So if you could show just a teeny bit of mercy, I'd be happy, and a little embarrassed, and my heart'd go all aflutter—"

"You mean to give me orders?"

"No, no, never — I'm just begging for mercy here. My super-gorgeous, super-promising, super-clever, super-wonderful, super-kind princess surely wouldn't make a choice that'd leave me sad."

At Al's glib words, the girl received them with silence and a blank expression. Still with the temperature of her gaze lowered, she looked at Subaru, still awaiting his sentence, with boredom. Her fingers still holding Subaru's chin up, he was terrified beyond help of whatever she'd say next.

Al was on Subaru's side in spirit, but the girl's own moodiness was something Subaru had already come to know in the short time he'd spent with her yesterday. Whether she herself had the power to harm him was unclear, but at the very least, she could certainly handle a dagger and Magic.

—If it came down to it, he'd let loose a Shamak and land at least one blow in return.

But that half-desperate thought of Subaru's—

"Well, fine, I suppose."

—was rendered needless worry by the girl's single bored remark, no different from before.

The girl withdrew the finger that had been touching Subaru's chin, drew a fan from within her robes, and crossed the changing room fanning herself.

"It wasn't something to particularly pique my interest in the first place. Going out of my way to lop off an unneeded head and earn my retainer's displeasure would be idiotic too. Needless trouble, unnecessary labor."

"So that means, uh... no penalty?"

"That's way too convenient for you, no matter how you slice it, brother."

At the wind seeming to blow toward his release, Subaru let all the tension drop from his shoulders at once, but Al gave a wry laugh and drove that nail back in at his display of relief. Then he called out to the back of his mistress, who looked about to leave the room.

"Hey, princess. I'm glad you heard my request out, but seriously, what are we actually gonna do here? There's no way we can just let this slide as-is, right?"

"Yeahhh, figured."

The immediate threat had, quite literally, been a narrow escape from certain death, but it was equally true that what Subaru had gone and done exceeded the line of what could just be overlooked. Al, it seemed, had no intention of softening the line drawn there. At his words, the girl stopped in her tracks and, looking utterly bothered, turned back with just her neck.

"You won't read my intentions, will you, you dullard. —Fine, bring him along with us. If he tries to run, you may take his head, no need to hold back."

"Sure, that's fine, but... no, wait, is it really fine? I mean, where you're headed right now, princess—"

"Quiet."

Al, having so readily accepted the merciless order, then voiced his doubt anyway. Talking straight over him, the girl said—

"For anyone to put in their two cents on my conduct is simply unthinkable. Know your place, Al. In this world, only things convenient to me ever happen. Therefore, there is not a single error in my judgment. Bringing that jester along will work to my benefit. That is simply how the world will choose to turn out, of its own accord."

"Your self-affirmation is downright refreshing to listen to, princess. Got it, understood."

To that self-awareness bordering on arrogant insolence, Al declared his compliance in a favorable tone. Then he turned back to face Subaru and gave a light poke at his left-out shoulder.

"So, that's that — you're a prisoner now, brother. Oh, and don't try running. I'd rather not, but if it comes to that, I'll cut you down."

"What happened to compatriot solidarity?"

"Weigh duty against human feeling, and it's duty that tips the scale in this life we lead."

Delivering a line that made the fleeting sorrow of this fickle world sink in deep, Al pushed at Subaru's back, urging him to follow along. Completely swept along by the flow of the situation, Subaru had no choice but to advance, sandwiched between the small back walking ahead of him and the pressure of his armed compatriot bearing down from behind.

"Well, I do know it's my own fault, so that part can't be helped, but... is this situation actually improving or getting worse..."

"If it had been anyone but me who found you, it would've ended with your head lopped off. If this isn't an improvement, what is? Go on, offer me your utmost gratitude."

"Saved is saved, I guess... but it's a weird thing for me to say myself — are you sure you don't need to ask why I was even in a place like that? Even I think I'm super suspicious and the worst."

He'd have liked to explain himself, but it was hard to say he'd been acting on a reason he could properly explain to someone else. It really had just been the result of a vague anxiety he couldn't sit still with. So what Subaru had said was born of anxiety over not being asked what ought to have been asked — a shell of weakness that amounted to a preemptive defense — but the girl snorted as though she'd read through his shallow inner thoughts completely.

"I've got a rough idea of the reason already. So I won't ask. That my mood happened to be decent enough... and, ah, yes, that's right."

The girl stopped and turned back. Orange hair swept across in front of him, and Subaru, caught off guard by her sudden stop, scrambled to halt as well. Pointing a finger right in front of his face, the girl curled up the corner of her lips in a bewitching smile.

"The appa."

"Huh?"

"Yesterday's appa wasn't bad. You can go on thinking it's thanks to that, that I'm not lopping your head off on the spot."

Having defined the reason for her change of heart as such, the girl set off walking without looking back again. Watching that back go, dumbfounded, Subaru chewed over the words he'd been given, and finally, as he stepped forward, murmured quietly to himself. It was—

"So basically, my life just got saved by the old fruit-shop guy..."

Since even his managing to sneak into the castle had been thanks to Kadomon, it felt like the man's rate of involvement in events around the royal capital had reached some abnormal figure. With that trivial observation, Subaru arrived at a certain measure of acceptance regarding his own narrow escape.

"Hey, is this really okay? Is this fine? Am I not way out of place here?"

"Out of place, or more like — you're trespassing to begin with. The princess is satisfied with it so I'm not gonna go blabbing either, but... don't try anything weird. I'm not gonna go out of my way to cover for you."

To an anxious Subaru glancing around repeatedly, Al, walking beside him, tossed that off curtly. But even Al, looking so composed, on closer inspection kept straightening his posture uncomfortably, sending his gaze ahead toward his mistress's advance.

The girl walking ahead — striding down a corridor in the upper floors of the central tower of the royal castle — bore countless gazes upon her, yet strode along with an attitude befitting her sumptuous dress and elegant beauty. The gazes watching her walk came from the fully armed guards standing lined up on both sides of the corridor. Following the girl down that corridor lined with sword-bearing guards, Subaru walked along briskly, his inner state anything but normal.

After all, Subaru had originally meant to sneak a peek at the castle's interior on the quiet. So why did he now have to be in a spot like this, right out in the open with so many people around, in a situation where his head could fly the moment Al beside him let slip one careless word?

He wanted this corridor to end already. Pushed along by the whirlpool of gazes directed at him from the soldiers, by their silent pressure, Subaru quickened his pace and ended up catching up to the girl's back. No — rather, he'd reached the end of the corridor, arriving at the place where an exchange was about to take place.

"A huge door, at the far end of the corridor lined with soldiers..."

The double doors were tall enough to have to look up at, and their size naturally let Subaru imagine, even from outside, the solemnity of whatever lay beyond them.

The location was the upper floor of the central tower of the royal capital's castle — past the corridor lined on both sides with soldiers — and an unpleasant premonition had been gripping Subaru's head and shaking it around, refusing to let go, for a while now.

Before a Subaru sunk in such gloomy feelings, the girl was exchanging words with a giant of a man blocking the doorway.

The fully armed giant removed his helmet and, with an intelligent gaze, took the measure of both the girl and Subaru. He looked to be around thirty, a man with features more rugged than sharp. His deeply carved, boulder-like face wore both a hard edge and the air of a soldier who'd seen many battles.

"We've been awaiting you, Priscilla-sama."

He bowed his head respectfully, his voice as weighty as his rugged face. At his properly mannered attitude, the girl nodded imperiously, then gestured with her hand toward the two behind her.

"My companions. One's my knight, and the other's... my appa attendant, I suppose."

"Wai—!"

Give me a better title than that, he almost blurted, but Subaru remembered how bad the situation was and clamped his own mouth shut. Glancing briefly at Subaru's cascade of expressions, the knight, his boulder-like face not so much as twitching, looked to the girl.

"—An appa attendant, is it."

"That's right, an appa attendant. A pitiful jester whose mission it is to deliver me red, sweet-and-sour appas. He's harmless. That's fine, isn't it."

"Without first hearing Miklotov-sama's opinion, I cannot allow passage—"

"What I do is, in short, the world's own choice. Such extreme insolence. Let us through, Marcos."

It seemed to be a set line, for the knight offered no rebuttal to the girl's imperious remark. With quiet blue eyes he appraised Subaru, then Al's bearing — and immediately after, those clear blue eyes gleaming brilliantly was, likely, no figure of speech but plain fact. He touched a finger lightly to his own eyes.

"I confirm no dangerous mana response, nor any weaponry. The only arms the knight here is bringing in is that greatsword there, correct?"

"............ Oh, the knight — that's me, huh. Right, right. Yes, yes. If some black-haired guy here starts acting suspicious, I'll split him clean in two with this very hand."

"Should the worst come to pass, at that time, please protect your mistress, Priscilla-sama. As for everything else, please leave it in the hands of us of the Royal Guard."

Given such a grandiose reply to his flippant remark, Al muddied the waters with a vague "sure, sure." Then, at a word from the knight called Marcos, the great doors were opened.

"Everyone inside is already waiting. Please hurry."

"Making the common lot wait is a privilege of mine too. The reverse, I'll never allow."

Making a haughty remark of astonishingly small caliber, the girl stepped through the doors as she was seen off. Watching Al follow after her without the slightest hesitation, Subaru steeled himself and went in too.

—What spread out before his eyes was a vast space carpeted wall to wall in red.

Walls fitted with dazzling ornamentation, a ceiling with lavish lighting casting its glow even in broad daylight. Compared to the sheer gymnasium-like size of the room, how little furnished it was made for a bargain sale of extravagant waste. Though, thinking of the room's purpose and use, that too was only natural. At the very back, in the center of the room, there was a modest raised step, and set upon a slightly elevated spot, a fixed chair. With a wall behind it worked in a design modeled after a Dragon, whoever sat in that chair would look either as though they bore the Dragon on their back, or as though they were protected by it.

This was, without question, the throne room of the royal castle. Which meant that chair could only be Lugunica's throne.

After his gaze was first drawn, unavoidably, to the throne, Subaru fearfully took in his surroundings. Unlike outside, there wasn't a single sword-bearing guard to be found within the room. With the entire Royal Guard standing by outside, the number of figures inside the room was small enough to count on all one's fingers and toes.

And among them—

"—Subaru?"

A silver-haired girl was watching Subaru, who had just passed through the doors into the throne room, with a face full of shock. She blinked as though unable to believe Subaru's figure was really there, her violet eyes wavering with confusion and some other feeling all mixed together.

Bathed entirely in her astonishment, Subaru's heart pounded so hard and high it hurt. It was a mysterious pressure surpassing any he'd felt over and over since sneaking into this castle. Somehow forcing that down, Subaru acted before her next move could come.

Putting a hand to his head, closing one eye, and sticking out his tongue.

"Sorry, I came after all."

A grand, silent weight came to lie heavily over the throne room for a good while after that.
