Arc 5 Chapter 67: "Liliana Masquerade"

In the end, Liliana didn't remember what had prompted her to start singing in the first place.

Liliana's family, going back through her mother's mother's mother, and her mother before that, had never settled anywhere — for generations they had wandered the world. In a rootless trade like bardery, of course, people tire of you quickly. So you never stay in one place — you keep traveling on your own two feet, wherever the wind blows and your whims take you.

Some bards apparently gathered in large troupes to perform together, but Liliana wasn't the type to enjoy running in packs. It wasn't that she disliked company — their sensibilities simply didn't mesh. To put it plainly, it was a difference in musical taste.

Just as her mothers before her had done, Liliana set out on her own. Still, there was no denying that even among bards — a breed given to laissez-faire parenting — her independence had come remarkably early. She left the nest and struck out alone from her parents at the age of thirteen.

"Argh, quit yer naggin'! I can't just sit here smolderin' in a place like this! Ma and Pa can go do as they please, for all I care!"

Trivial or not, there was no doubt she'd stormed out at the end of a shouting match. From the time she turned ten, Liliana had wanted nothing more than to strike out on her own. It was the reckless notion of a dreamy-eyed girl, and her parents — her mother especially — had fought hard to hold her back. But even at ten, young Liliana's emotional maturity already outstripped that of most girls her age — no small thanks to growing up steeped in the many ballads her father played and her mother sang.

To little Liliana, the people who appeared in her mother's songs were figures of longing and admiration. The more she came to know their adventures and challenges, their battles and romances, their struggles and triumphs over themselves, the less she could bear standing still forever in one place.

—While the people I know so well through song get to choose to live so freely.

To ten-year-old Liliana, the heroes and legends who lived in song were her friends. She longed unbearably to walk the same roads they walked, to see the same sights they saw, to stand beneath the same sky everyone had once looked up at, and taste it all just as they had.

Carrying feelings like that for three whole years — if anything, it was a wonder she'd held out as long as she did. Liliana burned through her boiling passion and her one-sided sense of kinship with the people in those stories, all while stealing — shamelessly stealing — her father's skill on the lyulyre and her mother's voice and repertoire of famous songs, one after another, until she'd made them her own. And then, on the night her mother handed down the family's heirloom lyulyre to her, at the age of thirteen, she stormed out after an almighty family shouting match and struck out on her own.

"Wahahaha! Just you watch, Ma, Pa! I'm gonna become the King of Bards!"

Having thoroughly shaken off her parents' pursuit, she stood alone and swore that vow to the night sky. Thus began the great adventure of Liliana Masquerade.

—Looking back on it now, she understands that that family quarrel was her parents' way of showing kindness.

From the time she was ten, her parents had nagged her endlessly, trying to rein in her recklessness. They pointed out her unpolished technique, laughed loudly at her ignorance of song, and every so often skipped her meals.

"Oh-ho-ho-ho! A little brat like you striking out on her own — ten years too early! A cheeky girl who talks like that doesn't get any of the rabbit we caught in the trap!" "Oh dear, oh dear, how pitiful! And the rabbit stewed so tender today too, what a shame to go without! A child who won't listen to her father and mother is a pitiful thing indeed!"

In both the good sense and the bad, she supposed her parents were dreamers themselves. For a pair like that, how much must their only daughter's departure have pained them? Surely, at the moment of parting, all sorts of conflicted feelings had churned inside them.

"Well, that's one less mouth to feed! Now we can eat three meals a day!" "With Liliana gone, I suppose... maybe we'll make one more kid!"

Surely there had been conflicted feelings. Surely she'd been missed. No doubt about it. And besides, that family quarrel was her parents' final gift to her.

So that even if Liliana's dream fell apart and she wanted to come home, she wouldn't be able to. To cut off her retreat, they had hurled such heartless words at one another. When people believe there's an escape route, they grow weak. Cling to the thought of a place to return to, and your fighting spirit will naturally refuse to burn all the way to the very end.

Bards in particular have no hometown of their own. Hometown and family — two anchors that people normally have separately — are one and the same for them. Their unconscious dependence on family runs deep. Severing that is the greatest obstacle to standing on one's own.

Liliana overcame that obstacle through the recklessness of youth and her parents' clever contrivance. It was when she found herself sipping muddy water, gnawing on grass roots, crushed by hunger and helplessness, on the verge of whimpering "I want to go home..." that she realized their kindness.

Had her spirit broken there, she might well have set down the lyulyre by now. She was grateful to her parents. That parting had surely been for the best, for both sides.

"—Ah." "Ugh." "Oh my."

Well, the awkwardness when they crossed paths again in some town a few years later was on another level entirely. All the more so because her parents were holding a little girl Liliana had never seen before. She figured the child must be her little sister, but Liliana exchanged no words with her parents — she simply squared her shoulders, straightened her back, and walked right past.

A few more years down the line, once she'd built up achievements far more worth boasting about, maybe she'd be able to meet her parents again and actually laugh and talk with them. But as she was now, she wasn't there yet. So that was as far as things would go today.

Of course, there was a chance that encounter would be the last time she ever saw her parents. More likely still, she'd never get the chance to tell that nameless little sister that she was her big sister. But that was fine. That was the life Liliana had chosen — one lived alongside song.

Besides, once Liliana someday became a bard whose name resounded across the whole world, those glib-tongued parents of hers would absolutely go around bragging to everyone. And her little sister would no doubt be the first victim of that bragging. Well then — adding that to her list of ambitions felt rather satisfying, didn't it?

"Heh. Now that's a future to make the heart race. Though there's not much up top to pound!"

And so, feeling renewed, she stepped forward once more — this was when Liliana was seventeen.

Now then, Liliana, currently twenty-two years of age, had spent nine years since striking out on her own — naturally enough, one hardship after another, never a life of smooth sailing.

Especially right after she set out at thirteen — the very day after, or the day after that, having sworn to "become the King of Bards," she was already at death's door. If a passing merchant caravan hadn't taken her in and let her earn traveling money as an errand girl for a while, she likely would have died alone in truth.

It was a band of merchants who traveled from place to place trading goods. Liliana was taken in by the group and looked after as an errand girl-cum-entertainer. With meals and a place to sleep provided, it was a far safer and more comfortable way to travel than striking out alone in earnest. Whenever they reached a town, Liliana would shoulder her lyulyre and sing on the street corner to earn her daily coin. She never forgot how moved she was the first time she received a tip after leaving her parents' side.

She stayed with the merchant band for about a year, but it disbanded once its leader had saved enough money to settle down in a town and open a shop. Of the merchants who scattered afterward, a few invited Liliana to keep traveling with them, but she politely declined and struck out alone.

She let go of that safe, comfortable travel and, light and unencumbered, set about sharpening her fangs alone. The days of soaking in lukewarm water were over; the legend of Liliana Masquerade was about to begin. No doubt she'd been fired up with that very conviction.

As for the hardships of the years that followed, let's skip over most of them. As a member of a merchant band, or as a member of a bardic family with a real reputation to carry — that was one thing. But once she was neither, the world's winds turned cold toward a little songstress on her own. It was right around this time that she came to understand the true kindness behind her parents' words at their parting.

And there was one more grave truth about the world that Liliana came to realize at this time. It was the fact that the world she lived in and the world inhabited by the people from the stories she knew so well were never one and the same — and that she was no companion of theirs, nothing of the sort.

There was no special trigger for this realization. It simply struck her, quite suddenly — one night when, as usual, she was gnawing on grass roots, having eaten some unfamiliar red berries in the mountains and given herself a stomachache, suffering alone through endless cramps and fever.

The wonderful heroes she knew from stories never ended up like this. Because those stories were already complete. The days when they coughed up blood, spoke their dreams, cried out their wishes, and swung their swords were all in the distant past — and all Liliana was doing was borrowing the cream skimmed off their footsteps and passing it along to others.

Liliana loved them, but they would never love Liliana back. Her feelings were entirely one-way — worse, the kind that arrived at the dead end called the past and lost all sense of where to go.

—Then what, exactly, was a bard?

Having stormed out of her parents' home declaring "I'll become the King of Bards!" and spent several years calling herself a bard, however imperfectly, Liliana had finally realized she was a fraud. Slamming unguarded into a wall she had never once considered before, Liliana felt as though her pride and every one of her front teeth had been smashed to pieces.

For three days and three nights, Liliana's stomach pains, fever, and vomiting continued. Tossing in delirium, she went on turning that thought over endlessly, whether in dreams or in the hazy borderland of reality.

On the fourth day, waking that morning, Liliana had recovered. She washed her face in a stream and drank from it. The face reflected on the water's surface seemed to belong to someone entirely different from who she'd been before.

The wind rustled the grass and trees, the stream babbled coolly, and she could hear the calls of insects and small birds. That was the first time she'd ever felt song in such things.

Tears welled up, and unable to hold them back, Liliana threw herself into the stream. Insects, small birds, and fish alike startled — and music overflowed from every one of them. Surfacing with her face a mess, Liliana laughed, cried, laughed, and wailed all at once.

Coming down from the mountain, still grimy with mud and water, Liliana took her place on a city street. Everyone gave the shabby-looking girl with her instrument a wide, disgusted berth. The shopkeeper in front of whose store she'd planted herself scowled openly, and the faces of passersby showed plain discomfort. Had she simply stood there for a few more seconds, some heartless passerby might well have shoved her aside.

But once on the street, Liliana moved quickly. It wasn't that she thought she'd be dragged off if she didn't start fast. At that moment, she simply, desperately wanted to sing, as soon as possible.

"——"

When the lyulyre's strings were plucked, how many people even noticed? That in the hands of that shabby, grimy girl was a well-worn lyulyre — and that of her whole self, only those two hands touching it had been kept clean.

How many noticed, exactly, is uncertain. But if one thing is certain, it's that the awareness of anyone who did notice was instantly swept away from it.

"——"

The moment Liliana's performance began, the moment music came pouring out from her delicate, graceful fingering, every last person on that thoroughfare stopped in their tracks, breath caught in their throats. In an instant, everyone understood that something dramatic had changed, bewildered by the great wave that had crashed over their hearts.

When they realized the source was the grimy girl standing on the street, every eye converged on her. Feeling that attention gather, Liliana understood her own rising exhilaration. A stage had been built, and she was racing straight up onto it. And when the heat of her playing reached its peak, Liliana's song began.

"Song" poured out of her — so much so that she found herself wondering what on earth she had been singing until now, unable to believe it was the same throat producing this sound.

Her feelings for the many famous songs she knew came and went, breaking through and soaring on. She watched, with a clear and unclouded heart, as those songs — which she'd always thought of as friends by her side, impossible to part from — ascended to the heavens.

—Song is a gift. To those old friends passed down through singing, she was nothing at all.

And that was fine. That was how Liliana came to understand her own existence, what a bard was. With that understanding, she could go on singing from here on.

She would go around boasting: that this world once held people this wonderful. She'd go around making the misguided boast that there was once a time she thought of such wonderful people as her friends.

And someday, she'd truly become friends with someone amazing, and sing and boast that a person that amazing had once been her friend — she'd make that happen.

"——"

When the song ended, Liliana wept. The people who had listened, dumbstruck, wept just as she did, sniffling.

Thunderous applause enveloped the thoroughfare, and Liliana Masquerade became a bard. And from that day on, Liliana's relationship with music had never stopped.

Remembering the time she first sang after striking out on her own, and the time she first sang after becoming a "bard," Liliana sang atop the burning control tower.

Something close to that reckless, all-or-nothing feeling from back then churned in her chest. She wanted to sing so badly she could hardly stand it; there was too much she wanted to put into words, too much she wanted to turn into sound. Even in the middle of singing, she wanted to sing more. This was, frankly, practically a sickness.

The white flames that chose their prey and burned it to nothing blazed on now without losing an ounce of their intensity. That searing heat couldn't touch Liliana, but the scorching warmth alone tormented her body without end. Even now, a burning pain shot through the soles of her shoes, and her body — having sprinted through a stone tower ringed by such flames — kept screaming. It was agony sharp enough to make her want to drop to her knees and wail this instant.

But wailing was out of the question. Rolling around in pain would be such a waste. Below her were people listening to her song — this throat existed for a singing voice, not a crying one.

"——"

The song she sang out was not one handed down to her by her mother or her family line. As a bard whose true duty was to carry stories on in song, that might disqualify her — but this was the very first song she'd received as a gift, back when she first came to know the music filling the whole world.

When a new morning arrives, the sky is dyed a crimson-gold. Liliana loved that sky, the one that appears when night is chased away and a new day begins. And then, overtaking even that crimson-gold glow of dawn, the blue sky brings the real morning with it.

—The sky that overtakes the morning glow.

No matter what kind of night one faces, morning still comes. That blue sky which overtakes the morning glow, and comes to everyone alike, is the start of a new day.

"——"

Right now, chaos was rampant throughout the city, and many people, swallowed by anxiety and grief, couldn't even move. In a night where they could see neither forward nor back, it was true that everyone was struggling, thrashing about.

But even so, morning would come — that was what Liliana wanted to sing. She wanted to sing it because she believed it, so she sang.

There was nothing more painful to her than living a life where she held back a song she wanted to sing, at a time she wanted to sing it. So she sang, with everything she had, exactly what she wanted to pass on through song right now.

From atop the control tower, Liliana kept singing, her throat trembling. Her fingers danced across the lyulyre's strings, and in fact, she danced as she sang and played, using every inch of the tower's summit so her voice would reach the great crowd surrounding it on every side.

But sadly, her voice could never truly reach the eardrums of every one of those people. It wasn't merely a matter of volume. There was the matter of distance. There was the matter of the listeners' hearts. However much heart Liliana poured into it, there really was a wall — both physical and mental — that made her inaudible.

Liliana believed in the power of song. But a song only truly becomes a song once it reaches someone — only then is it fulfilled.

How many people surrounded her on every side, on the verge of being crushed by anxiety and grief? Hundreds, a thousand — no, it might reach into the several thousands. Liliana had never once had the experience of delivering song to that many people using nothing but her own power, without the aid of a magic vessel.

A mere human had no means of amplifying a voice, no way to deliver it to so many at once. Liliana's challenge was reckless, her wish far too distant.

Once, at ten years old, Liliana's ambitions — too great for her own body — had been called reckless by none other than her own parents. Was this, once again, the very same thing? Was she repeating it now?

Was the power of song real, while she, the one meant to deliver it, remained a fraud? Was it all right to end here, like this?

"—!"

Was it no good — a thought welling up from somewhere within, and it scorched her throat. At that moment—

"Liliana — my lovely songstress. I beg you, with that voice of yours, to hold me captive for all eternity."

The idiotic pickup line of an idiotic man surfaced in Liliana's mind. He was a strange man. Frankly, an eccentric. "Pervert" might be the more accurate word.

There had been others before who approached Liliana's singing with impure motives. Every one of them, Liliana had pushed away. She couldn't lend her throat to anyone who wasn't sincere about song, who tried to use it for ulterior motives. That was a matter of duty, as a bard.

"I was captivated by your beauty. Please, stay by my side!"

So he was the first to approach her with an ulterior motive rooted purely in her appearance. He only learned Liliana was a bard after he had already, out of nowhere, tried to woo her on looks alone. Even when the opportunity arose and she sang before him, his gaze had wandered more over her face, chest, and legs than it did toward the song — and frankly, it had been unpleasant.

Still, it wasn't as if he'd been unmoved by Liliana's singing, and he never once tried to hide behind a lie about his affection for Liliana herself, either. He held affection for Liliana's looks, showed understanding for her voice, and once he got to know her character, he wouldn't let go.

"The city of Priestella has four great water gates. Because of this, the city has a number of shelters prepared in case of emergency. This magic vessel is used to keep the residents' everyday awareness of danger sharp, and to help them make the right call when the moment comes." "Uh-huh... and what about it?" "I was thinking it would be wonderful to have Liliana's song carried over that broadcast. There are still so many people in this city who don't know your singing. This seems like the perfect opportunity."

A singing voice carried through a magic vessel struck Liliana as something close to heresy. A song ought to be sung right in front of the person listening — shouldn't it? Liliana had balked and tried to refuse. But he laughed, his face utterly untroubled, and said—

"Your form, I want to keep entirely to myself. But your singing voice — that should never be monopolized by just one person. The songstress belongs to everyone; Liliana belongs to me. Is it so wrong to wish for both?"

What a face this eccentric could make — smiling without a trace of malice. If he genuinely thought that counted as wooing her, she wanted to snort with laughter.

Liliana knew countless love stories passed down through song in this world. She knew the men and women within them, flushed and lost in love and romance. She knew what words could enchant, what gestures could set a heart racing, how love came to fruition.

So Liliana wasn't naive enough to be swept off her feet by mere words like that. Not naive — not naive at all — but she'd liked the ring of the word "songstress." It was far too grandiose a title, and she couldn't exactly claim proudly that she deserved it.

But he — Kiritaka Muse — had wanted, had expected, Liliana to be a "songstress." That man was the one who had made her the "songstress" of this city.

"——"

Reach them. Resound. Make this feeling tremble through—

No matter how dark the night, no matter how thick the pitch-black with no end in sight. Morning still comes, just as it always has.

Louder than anyone, more fiercely than anyone, she sang it, believing it. Liliana Masquerade, the "songstress" of the watergate city of Priestella.

"——"

The heat and pain she'd felt so keenly before — she no longer felt them now. Every bit of her had been poured entirely into the wrist and hand playing the lyulyre, into these dancing feet, and into the throat and everything above it that kept on singing.

Everything was being wrung out of her, as if it would all wither away entirely. Every last emotion, every limit of her singing voice — all of it.

"——"

Singing, singing, singing her heart out, Liliana didn't notice. That her ears could no longer hear the wails of the people whose hearts had been dominated.

Outside the burning canal, the people gasping under pain and grief were looking up at the sky. No — not the sky. The flame-wrapped control tower, from which the voice came.

Atop it, a small silhouette kept raising its voice, from far, far too great a distance. No one could tear their eyes from her. Pouring every nerve into their ears, everyone held their breath and listened, rapt, to the song.

A voice that by all rights should never have reached them was, instead, clearly audible to every single one. It wasn't a miracle, nor was it some shared illusion everyone happened to experience at once. Still less was it some sharing of emotion by an Archbishop of Sin's Authority.

—It was the true blossoming of the Divine Protection of Telepathy, the gift Liliana had been granted from the heavens.

That Divine Protection's power, unconscious until now, only reached its true strength at this moment. Driven by her skill as a singer and by the resolve to cast away everything she had, right here, right now, it became an immense force pouring down over the city.

Of course, Liliana had no awareness of any of this. And there was no one present crass enough to explain that fact to her.

Liliana simply sang, with her entire soul poured in, single-mindedly. Having become a bard, pouring everything of song into it, entrusting the whole of this moment with the whole of herself.

Here, without question, the song of Priestella's "songstress" rang out.

"—So then, my eye for spotting that one was true after all."

Priscilla, gripping the Sunlight Sword — glowing red-hot as if it held a sun within — was smiling.

The singing voice reached Priscilla's ears as well. With the white-burning control tower as her stage, Liliana was raising her finest voice.

However selective the Sunlight Sword's flame might be about what it burned, the heat it carried was no illusion. Inside the control tower it was hot enough to steam-roast a person alive, and the heated stone tower itself ran fever-hot. Even at this very moment, it should have been unbearably hot enough to make her want to leap off.

And yet, listening to this voice that should be transmitting every one of Liliana's emotions, there wasn't the faintest trace of whining about the suffering, or forced bravado against the pain. It wasn't that she felt nothing — it was simply that the song had, purely and completely, overwhelmed the pain.

This was, frankly, an utterly foolish conclusion. Something only a fool could manage — the very pinnacle of foolishness. Proof that the ultimate talented fool produces results foolish enough to overturn reason itself.

"That one's foolishness is delightful. Folly and stupidity are similar, but not the same. There's no value in living foolishly — but a fool has the redeeming grace of being amusing. And that one has, on top of it, proven her own worth to be more than mere amusement. Therefore, I shall grant her deed a reward."

Before her musing had even finished, burning iron chains closed in from above and from the left. The flame-wreathed iron serpents' jaws bore straight down on Priscilla, who had stopped in her tracks. Crass, and the very picture of ugliness — Priscilla scoffed at the sight.

She raised the Sunlight Sword and swept its red blade in a single diagonal arc. By cutting into the paths of both attacks — from above and from the left — at once, the oncoming chains were forcibly swept aside with a single stroke. Two light, ringing sounds rang out almost simultaneously, and against that brilliance came the demon's spiteful click of the tongue.

"Both you and that girl are irritating, every single time! What's the difference between me and her?! The methods differ, but the essence is the same! It's nothing but proof that people connect through one and the same thing!"

Sirius screamed at the top of his voice, drawing back the burnt-off ends of the chains. Whirling an arm, spewing flame in raw fury, the hem of the coat draped over the demon's shoulders rippling in the heat wave — its bloodshot eyes were fixed on Liliana, dancing atop the burning control tower.

The true power of Liliana's Divine Protection of Telepathy was tremendous, its aftershocks reaching even to the demon. Even to a demon so sensitive to shifts in others' emotions, the effect of that song was coming through without a single discrepancy.

The residents were being freed from the curse of "Wrath" the demon had rooted in their minds. Outside the still white-burning canal, the eyes of the people standing rigid held no trace of madness. What filled their gazes now wasn't raw fury, but only a faint film of tears. What emotion those tears originated from, the demon couldn't pick up — something so bewilderingly complex, never settling on any one thing, forever shifting and shaking.

"If only he were here — if only he were here, I could prove it...! Why do you keep standing in my way like this?! People seek each other out, they want to become one — that's how the world has always gone on! And yet!"

"Take even a single song — how it's felt varies endlessly from person to person. Even the meaning packed into the single word 'wonderful,' said in rapture at a fine song, differs from one to the next. For all your noisy shrieking about emotion this, emotion that, your grasp of the one part that actually matters is shallow indeed... That is what one calls folly, is it not."

"SHUT UUUUUP!!"

Eyes flying wide at Priscilla's merciless words, Sirius roared and brought both arms together. The chains rang out where the palms overlapped, and both hands forcibly tore the chains coiled around each arm loose. Skin peeling from the arms, flesh scraped away — through that agonizing act, Sirius freed both arms and bundled the torn-off chains together, whipping them around in a furious swing.

Flame rode the spin of the whirling chains, the swirling mass expanding to the full radius of their arc. The scorching hellfire turned into a disc, and the tremendous heat caught even Sirius's own body alight.

"Don't tell me those bandages of yours are wound on account of some stunt like this?"

If the bandages were there because of burns, and the cause was exactly what stood before her now, it was folly and nothing else. Faced with the greatest power, the greatest threat yet, Priscilla's composure didn't so much as waver.

Two flame serpents, their combined might already immense — and now a still greater conflagration surpassing even that. Priscilla gazed, looking bored, at a whirlwind of fire that would surely leave not even a shadow behind if she took the full brunt of it.

"The tremor of emotion... the violent surge of the heart, that is to say, fury — that is to say, 'Wrath'!"

Surrendering himself to the emotion he loathed and despised, Sirius's flame became a heat wave. Unleashed with the full force of that spin, the flame no longer bore any resemblance to iron chains. The moment the fire was loosed, the chains' role as its conduit was already finished.

The chains, their duty done, melted away in an instant, leaving only a mass of flame surging toward Priscilla. A scorching mass that seemed to blot out not just her vision but the world itself — an area attack tantamount to a cloud falling from the sky.

Dodging it was impossible; any attempt to guard against it would simply be swallowed whole. Against fire itself, then, there was only one possible thing to be done.

"—Since my will is heaven's will, the Sunlight Sword's ultimate radiance shall follow it as well."

Against the surging wave of flame, Priscilla readied the Sunlight Sword. Not the formless, careless swings she'd used until now — she raised the blade overhead.

"Be erased—!!"

"——"

At the instant of collision, Sirius spat hatred toward Priscilla from the far side of the flames. Priscilla let that fury pass right by her. The only thing that reached her ears was the singing voice.

In the very instant her body was about to be swallowed by the heat wave, a change came over the Sunlight Sword. The jeweled blade, which until then had shone bright enough to put every gemstone to shame, abruptly lost its light — leaving only the crimson mounting and red blade in Priscilla's hand.

And just like that, the sword stripped of its light was slammed straight into the flames.

"——"

Having lost the jeweled sword's brilliance, the blade was reduced to steel with nothing divine about it. So it had no power left to repel the oncoming flames — that would have been the impression of anyone observing the scene objectively.

—But the outcome ended up being the exact opposite.

"—Take a good look, and eat this."

Priscilla's whisper, following through with the Sunlight Sword's swing, should have been swallowed and lost within the flames. But her presence hadn't vanished, even now, having let that whisper slip out. Far from it — her entire body bore not even a lingering trace of the heat wave, remaining exactly as beautiful and intact as before.

That wave of flame, which had boasted such tremendous firepower, had vanished without leaving even a trace behind. As if only the Sunlight Sword — its brilliance restored once more — knew exactly where that flame had vanished to.

"Hm—"

The instant she reset her grip on the Sunlight Sword, Priscilla's expression changed. The cheeks that had worn a bold, fearless smile stiffened, and with a face suppressing a click of the tongue, she broke into a run.

Ahead of her line of sight was Sirius's back — he had already begun running before she did. The demon's fierce legs, sprinting furiously, were pulling away from Priscilla all at once. That start was, quite clearly, a run made without waiting to see the result of that last blast of flame. In other words, Sirius's target had never been Priscilla to begin with—

"Stop that grating song—! Don't you dare selfishly deny my and his 'Wrath'—!"

Sirius, eyes bloodshot, charged in a straight line toward the control tower where Liliana sang. The white flames encircling the control tower were flames that permitted freedom to Liliana alone. Even if Sirius leaped in, it was a certainty his whole body would be burned to nothing by that firepower. Surely even the demon understood that much. Which meant his aim was—

"Fool — what do you think you're doing to what's mine—!"

Kicking off, Priscilla's body, gaining explosive momentum, tore across the plaza. Sirius's speed was formidable in its own right, but Priscilla's surpassed the demon's still further. Sirius's initial head start vanished, and Priscilla raised the Sunlight Sword against the demon's back. Even if it wished to defend, the demon had already lost its weapon. With no chains left on either arm, it had no means of receiving Priscilla's blade.

"Halt, you vulgar thing—"

"SHUT UP, YOU'RE the one who should halt!!"

"—?!"

An instant before the Sunlight Sword cleaved Sirius diagonally in two, Priscilla's body froze in midair. Her entire form locked in place as if forcibly bound, and Priscilla, caught by a shock utterly outside her expectations, choked on her own breath. There, Sirius swung up a leg. From beneath the rolled-back hem of his trousers came the sound of a chain — a sound she'd grown thoroughly familiar with over the course of this battle—

"RUAAAAAAAAAAAH!"

"Tch!"

Not the arms this time, but a strike from the iron chain wrapped around a leg landed square on the immobilized Priscilla. An attack landed while her whole body was frozen in place — this time, there was no way to guard against it.

The chain's furious blow, carrying several times the speed and power of the arm strikes, exploded against Priscilla's fine features head-on. The impact of steel slamming into flesh rang out, the barrette binding her orange hair snapped off and flew away, and her beautiful hair spilled loose. When her face came back into view, there was no wound on it. But her pride had been wounded.

Unable to fully absorb the force, she was flung backward, and the distance between her and Sirius opened up. In that span, Sirius had already closed in on the control tower, and with the same leg-chain that had struck Priscilla, he loaded it with the full weight of his body and all his might in a motion beyond common sense, and unleashed it.

A great serpent wreathed in flame swept the control tower with tremendous force in a horizontal blow, and the tower's very foundation was shattered in a single roaring strike. It crumbled, collapsed — every last bit of the stone tower's material was swallowed by the wave of fire, tilting under the sheer weight of that firepower.

—With Liliana still atop it, the stone tower's balance tilted all at once, and it began to crumble.

Priscilla, her orange hair spread across her back, opened her eyes wide at the crumbling control tower. Sirius's figure was visible. Atop the tilting control tower, Liliana's figure was not.

But Liliana's song continued. Even now, as the ground beneath her feet crumbled and she was swept up in the collapse. Liliana devoted herself entirely to her role, keeping the residents' hearts captive to her still.

"—That resolve — a most worthy cause!"

Stepping forward, Priscilla advanced toward Sirius without hesitation. If Liliana's song broke off, the residents' hearts would fall back under Sirius's influence once more. She made her decision in an instant. The Sunlight Sword's brilliance intensified, and the paving stones exploded beneath a single step from Priscilla.

"You heartless, selfish creature! Don't go justifying yourself for being unable to empathize with anyone! It's you, unable to connect with people, who's the defective one — understanding each other, melting into one another, that's the true nature of humanity!"

"Vulgar thing."

Sirius, having destroyed the control tower, hurled abuse at Priscilla for choosing to charge straight at him. He leaped, and with the force of a heel dropping down, the iron chain came smashing down. Impact — and the flame trailing after it a beat later produced an explosion, the blast wave knocking Priscilla's running body aside. She caught herself, and pressed forward.

Bathed in the heat wave, Priscilla's crimson eyes never wavered. Sirius's madness was the same. The demon's mind, by now, would take in no one else's words. Both were complete unto themselves. Their values, and so the two of them, could never be reconciled — not ever.

"——"

The tilting control tower let out a violent roar, stone fragments caught up in the destruction scattering everywhere, plumes of smoke swirling with flame flung wide, and the plaza turned into a scorching hell. The people in the canal on the side the control tower was falling toward fled, screaming, tears streaming down their faces. But those tears weren't born of sorrow. They were tears shed for something else entirely — for the singing voice.

"Love is becoming one—!"

"Wrong. —Love is generously accepting that it's fine to be different. The thought of everyone looking the same direction, thinking the same thoughts, feeling the same feelings, turns my stomach."

Ducking beneath a horizontal sweep of the chain, Priscilla flew forward in a low crouch. Sirius, clicking his tongue, conjured wall after wall of flame to block her path, but the Sunlight Sword sliced through every single one, its red blade drinking them dry.

The distance closed, and the chain's blows grew both more forceful and more numerous. The clang of steel against steel was swallowed by the thunderous roar of the collapsing control tower. Racing through that sharp clatter, Priscilla's momentum finally reached Sirius.

"This ends now."

"—Now, I wonder about that!?"

In the instant she brought down her raised Sunlight Sword, Sirius threw open the front of his own coat. Exposed within was the demon's chest, tightly wound in chains just like the limbs, and bound to the chains coiled around his torso was a girl with golden ringlets—

"Mmmph—!"

Priscilla had no way of knowing that the girl's name was Tina, or that she had been held captive by Sirius the entire time since this upheaval began. Among the information Subaru had disclosed regarding tackling "Wrath," the matter of the girl had indeed come up — but Priscilla's mind had filed that fact away as trivial.

So Priscilla felt not the slightest hesitation, even confronted with a hostage brandished before her like this. The blade came down without slackening its momentum in the slightest, cutting diagonally through Sirius's body, Tina and all. The Sunlight Sword's blade, boasting tremendous heat, sliced through the chains protecting his body without so much as a sound, cleaving it in two, and accomplishing its purpose.

"—Oh, my?"

"My Sunlight Sword burns what it wishes to burn, and cuts what it wishes to cut."

The chains were sliced through, and the girl's bound body was freed. Collapsing to her knees right there, the girl raised her tear-stained face, stunned at the sensation of the blade that had just grazed her body. On that young girl's body, there wasn't a single cruel wound from the blade anywhere.

What spurted blood instead was Sirius, staggering back under the blow. The demon looked down at its own wound, shook its head slowly, and turning its gaze on Priscilla, said—

"This pain... who are you?"

"Is there any reason I should feel your pain? What do I care for your wish to become one with someone? Go on, die alone, clutching your delusions to the end."

The Sunlight Sword came crashing down in a horizontal blow against the demon's tilted neck. With tremendous sound and force, Sirius's body bounced off the paving stones, scattering blood, sent flying and hurled into the canal, and fell.

A splash rose up, and Priscilla gazed steadily at the Sunlight Sword.

"The sunshine has ended, and the sun's disk has clouded over, has it. What a troublesome one that was."

The instant she finished speaking, the collapsing control tower finished crumbling entirely. Most of it had become rubble, and the upper floor where Liliana should have been had also taken the brunt of that collapse, badly wrecked. The control tower had collapsed as though toppling right over the canal — and naturally, the song could no longer be heard.

"...Um."

A young voice called out to Priscilla, who had been narrowing her eyes at that mountain of rubble. It was Tina. Her face still hadn't quite come to believe she was free, but her body trembled under Priscilla's gaze looking down at her, and tears spilled down in a steady stream.

At the sight, Priscilla let out a breath. The Sunlight Sword had already vanished. The white flames that had been burning the canal had also dissipated, and a great many people were coming this way. Some of them were heading for the collapsed pile of rubble, apparently intending to search for the songstress swallowed within.

"A clamorous night, full of clamorous people. This of all moments called for a poem's part to play, and yet there's nothing here but idleness. —How tiresome."

Sounding bored, as she always did — yet with just a trace of feeling folded into that boredom. Priscilla turned her back on the sobbing child and murmured this while gazing at the canal.

"But it wasn't bad. I'll grant you my praise."

Slowly, slowly, I was drifting along wherever the current took me. My whole body ached, my will to do anything completely wrung dry — how should I put it, battered and bruised all over? That's about the size of it, anyway, and I couldn't move a muscle.

"Ahh— uhh—"

My throat was utterly spent too, and I couldn't so much as twitch a finger. Fortunately, bard's costumes show a lot of skin and use very little fabric, so falling into the canal didn't leave me weighed down with waterlogged cloth — that was my lifeline. For someone with no strength left to swim, like me, just managing to stay afloat at all felt like a small blessing. Though, if I really did just keep drifting like this forever, my body heat would eventually give out completely and things would get very bad indeed! Ah, even shouting inside my own head is exhausting. I just want to sleep like this. Even though I'd die.

"Eeh— ehh—"

The burning control tower, all that blazing firepower. My whole body had basically been smoldering the entire time, so when I first fell into the canal I thought, hey, the chill might actually feel nice. But now I've reached the point where I can't even feel the cold anymore, and, well, yeah — that's probably pretty bad.

Though if we're talking about "bad," honestly, the state of mind I was in — not even trying to escape the crumbling tower, just singing straight through until I slid off into the water — was way more alarming. But but, it felt so incredibly good. Enough to make me think I might have been living my whole life just for that one moment.

I really do hope that wasn't just some delusion on my part, and that it actually worked. Well, my head's still attached, so as long as Priscilla-sama wasn't taken down, I'd like to think it worked out. If so, good.

Yeah, if that's the case, then, well, I'll take it. I've still got a mountain of ambitions left to fulfill as a bard, but in a way, I managed to pull off exactly what needed pulling off, right when it counted. I might not have managed the goal of singing a song that goes down in history, but if the people there were saved, then maybe I left enough of a mark to come up at somebody's dinner table someday, as a small part of how it happened. I think I'm allowed to hope for at least that much.

"Ohh— ohh—"

By the way, the reason I've been making these weird noises this whole time is partly a signal that says "hey, I'm here," and partly because if I don't at least keep my lungs shaking, it feels like the rest of my body is going to give out entirely. Proof that I'm me really does come down to my voice, one way or another, both times. That's about how it is. But I guess even that's about to run out soon.

There's been a lot going on, but all things considered, it's been a fun life. So, thanks for everything, it's been— GLBLRGH!?

"Ow! Owowow! Something just slammed into the top of my head with a vengeance!"

"Hm!? What was that just now — wait, huh!? Liliana!?"

Right as I thought my head had smashed into something with tremendous force and it hurt like crazy, it turned out I'd apparently collided with a little boat or something floating in the canal. And what's more, the voice that came from atop that little boat was a man's voice I recognized.

"Could that possibly be you, Kiritaka-san?"

"It really is you, Liliana! I'm so glad we've found each other again! But why on earth are you in the canal!? No — never mind, I'll pull you up first. Just hold on!"

Kiritaka-san up on the boat thrashed about, rocking the boat back and forth with a creak. Since it was his boat that was holding my body back against the canal's current, honestly, it hurt quite a bit. It hurt, but I was too surprised to even remember to groan about it.

I mean, come on, being fished out by Kiritaka-san after everything that just happened — seriously? There's just no way to describe how awkward this is. Really.

"Just... a little more... there, got you!"

Kiritaka-san shoved both hands under my arms as I floated there and hauled me up. His hand brushed against my chest in the process, but, well, I don't have the energy left to get mad about it right now, so I'll let it slide.

Hauled up onto the boat, I still couldn't move, just lying there limp.

"Your body's gotten quite cold. Hold on, Liliana. I'll heat up a fire mana crystal right now. And you can't stay in wet clothes like this, either."

He brought out a towel blanket and scrubbed away at my hair and face with it. His touch was surprisingly gentle, and I found myself thinking, huh, so all that gentlemanly posturing isn't entirely for show after all — and then a wave of relief washed over me all at once, and the tension just drained right out of me.

"Kiritaka-san... what have you been up to, this whole time?"

"Me...? Ah, well, all sorts of things, yes — playing my part in reclaiming the city, of course!"

He probably swept back that proud forelock of his and flashed a gleaming smile, I'd bet. I didn't have the energy to open my eyes to check, but I could picture it perfectly.

So I couldn't help it, I burst out laughing, and I could sense Kiritaka-san's surprise. I wanted to hear all about his "all sorts of things," and I wanted to tell him about mine too. But right now I'm just so sleepy, and yet, there's just this one thing I want to say first.

"I'm really sleepy, so... I'm gonna sleep for a bit now, I..."

"Ah — ah, all right. I'll carry you somewhere safe, so don't you worry."

"If you can manage not to do anything mischievous to me while I'm asleep... let's talk some more... afterward..."

"Wueeh!?"

He probably wouldn't, but I just wanted to say it anyway. When I wake up, I'm sure I'll end up saying something embarrassing anyway, so in the meantime, at least, go ahead and be flustered.

—Because I'm going to need at least that much preparation before I can say I'm glad to be your "songstress."
