Arc 5 Chapter 30: "Tiger and Cat on a Moonlit Night"

"I'm so sorry. I never imagined I'd be having guests over, so the house isn't very tidy..."

"Nnn, don't worry about it! It's fiiine! It's so preeetty! It's so preeetty! Mimi's room is like, way worse than this!"

"Oh my, my. That won't do. You really must tidy up properly."

The woman patted Mimi's head with an easy familiarity as the girl kicked her feet against the sofa. Mimi hummed contentedly, clearly having let her guard down completely around the woman. Watching this out of the corner of his eye, Garfiel kept his mouth shut and studied the woman.

Long, lustrous golden hair that reached all the way to her waist. Fair skin, and a slender frame that nonetheless retained a womanly fullness. A gentle countenance, and calm, clear emerald eyes. Her youthful appearance could easily have passed for a woman in her mid-twenties, but if what Garfiel knew was correct, she should have been approaching her mid-thirties. That she looked nothing of the sort was one more thing troubling his heart.

"Does Mr. Gorgeous Tiger there dislike tea, perhaps? I'm sorry—here I went and served it without even asking your preference..."

The woman—who had introduced herself as Reala Thompson—knit her brow in concern at Garfiel's continued silence. Her words reminded Garfiel that he hadn't so much as touched the cup of tea set before him, and he hastily picked it up.

"Nah, that ain't it. Just a little... I mean, I was surprised how big your house is, is all."

"Oh my, is that so? It certainly is quite large... which means cleaning it every day is such a chore. I'm terribly scatterbrained, you see."

Reala accepted Garfiel's spur-of-the-moment excuse without a shred of doubt. Laughing elegantly with a hand held to her mouth, she wore a well-tailored dress befitting someone who lived in the grand estate she'd just described.

Her smile, her sweet, coaxing voice, her every gesture—all of it stirred a deep homesickness in Garfiel. And yet Reala said nothing about the way he stared at her. That fact wrung his heart with a pain that felt almost unnatural.

The woman who called herself Reala Thompson was, Garfiel was certain, the very image of his birth mother—Rishia Tinzel—burned into his memory.

Of course, Garfiel had been parted from his mother right after his birth. Too young to even remember, he had, in effect, no memories of her at all. And yet he could vividly recall her likeness—because, infuriatingly, the Trials of the Tomb that had lingered on in the Sanctuary had shown him the past of their parting.

His mother's face, her voice, her love—Garfiel had recalled all of it through that Trial. And at the same time, the Trial had told him that, immediately after their parting, his mother had met an unfortunate death. Which was why, to Garfiel, a reunion with his mother had been nothing but an impossible dream.

So then, who was this woman standing before him now?

"Miss Mimi. The fur around your ears looks so fluffy. May I touch it?"

"Go ahead!"

As Mimi offered up her head, Reala reached out happily and savored the feel of her fur. Her smile was like a young girl's, her manner utterly innocent of caution toward strangers. That much was obvious from how easily she'd invited a shady-looking thug of a man and a beastkin girl into her home.

Every bit of that manner was one more reason it made Garfiel feel his mother's presence.

His mother, Rishia, had been an unfortunate woman. Her family home was seized over debts, and as a child she was sold to a slave trader. That slave trader, in turn, was attacked by a band of Demi-Human bandits on the way back, and Rishia was made a plaything for the half-beastkin men among them. It was there that her elder daughter Frederica was conceived, and once her belly began to swell, the bandits abandoned her without a second thought. After that, Rishia was captured by yet another band of bandits—different from the first—and spent a long stretch of time with them. Frederica, too, must have already been with that band of bandits by the time she was old enough to remember. She has always been reluctant to speak of that period, but given that the group abandoned Rishia the moment she became pregnant with Garfiel, it could hardly have been a good environment.

After a stretch of time that seemed to distill misfortune upon misfortune, Rishia ended up wandering with her daughter in tow while pregnant. Fortunately, she was soon rescued by Roswaal, the eccentric noble. Roswaal guaranteed her safety and provided food, clothing, and shelter on the condition that she raise her children in the Sanctuary. As a result, Garfiel came to be born in the Sanctuary, delivered by Ryuzu's own hands.

Told in mere words, it was a life that could only be called a wretched string of misfortunes. And yet his mother's character—something no bullet-point account could capture—was, in the eyes of those who truly knew her, something entirely different from that unhappy record.

"Rishia was, hm. For whatever reason, an abnormally positive girl. Those must have been days hard enough to die from. And yet that child always said tomorrow might bring something good. Today may be hard, but tomorrow might bring something good. Just as she'd been blessed with two children, maybe something happy would come."

"Mother... might have been the sort others would dismiss as a foolish woman. If I'm honest, there are times I wonder why she couldn't have lived a bit more shrewdly. ...But Mother was a wonderful person. That much is certain, without question. I loved Mother dearly, and I truly am glad to have been her daughter."

"Your mother... Rishia, you mean? Ye-e-es. I never had a-a-all that many proper conversations with her myself, mind... but she was a curious person. Curious, or perhaps I should say inexplicable. She was more sensitive to happiness than most. Even the sma-a-allest things could make her feel happy. She could find happiness even within misfortune. So-o-o, well. I suppose... I didn't dislike her, perhaps."

Ryuzu, Frederica, even Roswaal—none of them ever spoke ill of his mother. And that was the same sentiment echoed by everyone in the Sanctuary who had known her.

He supposed she must have simply had a carefree, happily-tuned mind. Otherwise, why would she have left her children behind to go searching for the man who'd surely put her through such suffering—Garfiel's father—of all the foolish things to do? And to die so soon after, no less—where in any of that was the happiness?

—Even now, he had never found the answer to where his mother's happiness had lain.

"And here I'd given up completely on ever findin' it..."

His claws dug into his palm, his fist clenched so tight it nearly drew blood. He was supposed to have let it go. To have accepted it as something unknowable. He'd thought it was a problem he would have to digest within himself, over a long stretch of time. And yet why—why now, of all times, did she have to appear before him like this?

And with an expression and a manner that seemed to say she didn't know him at all, no less.

—

Careful not to be noticed, he stole glances at her expression and gestures. There wasn't a single unnatural motion. Completely at ease, Reala treated Garfiel's presence as that of a total stranger with a vividness striking enough to leave him spellbound.

Was that her answer, then? That she had a new life now. That she knew nothing of Garfiel. That whatever promise they'd once exchanged had been cast far away, and she was living happily like this instead. That she didn't know him at all—was that his mother's answer—?

"Mama." "Mom, I'm hungry."

Into the living room—where Garfiel sat in silence while Reala and Mimi played together—the two siblings came down, now changed into fresh clothes. The sister shot Garfiel a sharp glare, then hurried over to cling to her mother.

"Hey, Mom. Can't the guests go home already so we can eat?"

"Sis, what a thing to say. Mr. Gorgeous and Miss Mimi looked after Fred, you know? He was playing on a boat and nearly drowned."

"Hmph, who knows. Maybe that Gorgeous guy's the one who rocked the boat in the first place. Worming his way into our house like that so he can squeeze a bunch of money out of us."

"Now, now, Sis. That's a terrible thing to say. But you're right, though—Fred was saved, so we should thank them properly... Maybe money would be best?"

"Mom!"

The sister panicked, sensing that her own words were about to deal a crippling blow to the household finances. Reala, meanwhile, wore a puzzled look, seemingly unable to grasp why her daughter was so worked up. To Garfiel, this heartwarming exchange between mother and children felt like nothing short of walking barefoot over thorns. He drained his tea in one gulp and set the cup down with a clack.

"Seems like I ain't exactly welcome here, so. I'll take my leave now."

"Ehh, why?"

"Ain't no 'why' about it."

Mimi protested as Garfiel moved to leave, but he paid her no mind and started to lead her out of the room. Reala's face fell sadly as Garfiel walked off, while the daughter saw him off with a mocking tongue stuck out at him. Just as he thought to honor that sentiment and go—

"Don't go, Gorgeous Tiger."

The younger brother had grabbed the hem of Garfiel's clothes, blocking his path. For a moment, Garfiel hesitated to shake off those small fingers. He didn't know why he'd hesitated. But—

"Honestly, Fred!"

The sister planted her hands on her hips, fuming at her brother for stopping a suspicious stranger. But before she could pounce on him in a fury, Reala clapped her hands to draw everyone's attention.

"Now, now, everyone needs to get along. That's right—it simply won't do to send our guests off like this. Fred clearly wants them to stay, so don't be difficult about it, Sis."

"But, Mom..."

"No 'buts.' Mr. Gorgeous, Miss Mimi, just a little longer, won't you? I'd be so happy if you could join us for dinner. Tonight's dish is one I'm quite proud of."

"Mama, you always say that's your specialty..."

"Well, of course. Mommy always gives it her all for you two."

As Reala struck a feeble little flexing pose, everyone in the room had the wind taken out of their sails—everyone, that is, except Garfiel, whose expression remained the harshest of all. The mood, just beginning to soften into warmth, was precisely what wounded Garfiel's heart the most right now.

At Reala's words, the children each wore a face—one happy, one resigned. He felt himself on the verge of being drawn in, accepted, and that, right now, terrified Garfiel.

"Sorry to turn down the invite, but I got folks waitin' on me. Get back too late and they'll start worryin'. So I'll be headin' off."

Enduring the ache in his chest, Garfiel prayed his voice wouldn't shake. At his answer, the siblings' faces went stiff, and Reala's brow drooped as she lowered her eyes. But—

"I understand. There's no point forcing you to stay if it only troubles you. As they say, 'For a guest, hospitality and a Soirie.'"

Perhaps that exchange, more than anything else, was what cut most sharply into Garfiel's heart that day. His sense of defeat before Reinhard, even the shock of seeing Reala for the first time—compared to the shock of this single moment, both were nothing.

He found himself pressing a hand to his chest, needing to check that his body hadn't literally been torn apart. Seeing him like that—

"Garf, let's go."

Mimi, who only moments ago had objected to turning down the invitation, now pulled Garfiel's hand. He had no choice but to silently follow the girl's consideration as she tried to lead him outside. Just as he reached for the living room door to head out—

"I'm home. Oh? Do we have company?"

The door opened from the other side, revealing a gentlemanly man with a fine, well-groomed beard. Well-tailored clothes, a bearing full of vigor. From the tone of his voice and the cast of his features, he seemed to be a man of some accomplishment. At his appearance, Garfiel could tell the children behind him lit up with joy. This man was, no doubt—

"Hm, I don't believe I recognize you. And you would be...?"

"Papa, it's Gorgeous Tiger!" "He's a suspicious person!" "...Eh?"

At his children's contrasting reactions, their father cocked his head in confusion. His gaze then drifted to Reala, standing quietly in the living room. At the man's look of affection, Reala's cheeks softened ever so slightly. That was Garfiel's limit.

"It's nothin' much. Don't worry about it. I was just leavin'."

With that, Garfiel left the room, Mimi's hand still in his grip. Shoving past the man, who hastily stepped aside to make way, Garfiel fled toward the entrance.

"Gorgeous Tiger!"

From behind, the boy's sorrowful voice called out to him. But Garfiel no longer had the composure to answer it.

Gorgeous, my ass. Tiger, my ass. What part of him right now looked anything like a golden tiger? A tiger was strong, mighty, unshaken by anything.

What about him right now looked like a tiger at all? A real tiger wouldn't go and get sad over something like this—!

"Garf! My hand, it hurts, I said!"

—

So absorbed in his own thoughts, Garfiel hadn't noticed her cries of pain. Only when his arm was yanked hard, claws digging in, did he realize what he'd been doing. Mimi's small hand had been gripped so tightly in his vise-like one that it had gone completely pale-blue.

"S-sorry... I..."

"Garf was weird in the house. And my hand, it really hurts."

Mimi murmured in a subdued voice, and Garfiel put a hand to his forehead. The damp wind of the sluice-gate city brushed past the two of them in their awkward silence. The sun had apparently set completely, and the city was wrapped in the scattered glow of magic lamps lining the streets.

The surface of the canal, once reflecting sunlight, now mirrored the glow of the magic lamps instead. There was a quiet, ethereal beauty to it, but Garfiel had no heart left to enjoy it now.

"Excuse me, you two!"

A slightly breathless voice reached the two of them as they stood frozen there. Looking up, Garfiel saw the same man from before come running out of the glow of the magic lamps. Having shed his coat, the man arrived before them and braced his hands on his knees, breathing hard.

"Ha... hah, I caught up. Oh dear, this won't do... I used to have far more stamina, but ever since it's all been desk work, I've been sorely lacking in exercise..."

"...You got some kinda business with us?"

Garfiel had no interest in the man's self-deprecating remarks. Though not as much as Reala or the children, this man's presence, too, was undoubtedly poison to him right now. The man surely hadn't missed the faint edge in Garfiel's voice. Putting a hand to his head with a troubled look, he said,

"No, my wife told me. From what I hear, you're the ones who saved my son, aren't you? Letting you leave without so much as a word of thanks would be simply unthinkable."

"...I really didn't do nothin' much. Makin' a big deal outta it just makes things awkward."

"Anything concerning my own child matters a great deal to me. All the more so when he's been saved from real danger. I truly do wish there were something I could offer in thanks. ...Oh, forgive me. I'm Garek. Garek Thompson. Believe it or not, I work at Priestella's city hall, so if there's ever anything..."

"Really, there ain't nothin'..."

Garfiel tried to take his leave of the formal, courteous man—Garek—but the words caught in his throat. If this man was Reala's husband, someone who truly knew her—

"Just one thing... mind if I ask?"

"Of course, anything at all. I only hope it's something I'm able to answer."

Garek answered with a good-natured smile. Reala was the same, and so was his son Fred. Garek included, that whole family was simply too trusting for their own good. The daughter was about the only one with any proper sense of caution. Mulling that over, Garfiel chose his next words carefully.

"It's about your wife... Is Reala her real name?"

—

The air changed. At Garfiel's question, the smile vanished from Garek's face. He turned the question over on his tongue, then, in a quiet voice—

"What exactly do you mean by that?"

"Exactly what it sounds like. 'Reid always meets things head-on,' they say. Beatin' around the bush ain't my style. So tell me straight. Ain't your wife's real name not Reala, but Rishia?"

—

At Garfiel cutting straight to the heart of it, Garek's expression clearly clouded with distress. His mouth opened and closed uselessly before he swallowed audibly, and—

"What... what exactly do you... know about my wife?"

"That's exactly what I wanna know, too."

To Garek's trembling voice, Garfiel answered with his own honest truth. Hearing this, Garek fell silent for a moment, as if lost in thought. Garfiel waited on his words. Mimi took his other hand—the one she hadn't been holding before. When he glanced her way, she gave him her usual carefree smile.

"...It seems I ought to tell you the truth of the matter."

Garek said this with a sigh, to Garfiel who'd been glancing sidelong at Mimi's smile. There was a weariness in his voice, mixed faintly with a sense of resignation. Garfiel knit his brow suspiciously and waited for his next words. And then—

"My wife, Reala... has no memories from before we met, fifteen years ago."

Garek had met Reala back before he'd become a man of influence in the city of Priestella—back when he was nothing more than one merchant among many rooted in the city, or so he said.

That day, driving his Dragon Carriage home from a business trip far away, Garek had come upon a road blocked by a landslide. The negotiations themselves had already fallen through, and struggling as he was to repay his debts, Garek found himself unable to hold back his irritation at this string of misfortune.

—And there, amid the devastation of that landslide, he discovered a woman buried alive.

It could only be called a miracle, he said. That Garek had refused to take the long way around and struggled to find some passable path. That the rain, which had been falling steadily, happened to stop just then, granting him clear visibility. That Garek had arrived just after the landslide occurred, keeping the time she'd spent buried alive short.

Every one of those miracles happened to overlap by chance, and the woman was pulled out alive by Garek. She was covered in mud, with no belongings of any kind. Loading the unconscious woman into his Dragon Carriage, Garek rushed straight to the nearest town, where he had her admitted to a treatment house and waited for her to recover.

"Her condition was extremely dire. She was running a high fever, and being caught in the landslide had left wounds all over her body, along with fractures in several places. During treatment, her heart even stopped once."

As her condition remained precarious, both the treatment house staff and Garek desperately prayed for her recovery. Looking back on it now, even Garek himself couldn't say why everyone had fought so hard to save her. No—Garek had his own reason, but he couldn't explain why the others had truly striven so hard on her behalf. Still, he was now grateful from the bottom of his heart for all their efforts.

"All that effort paid off, and she pulled through. She was still gravely injured, but I remember feeling truly relieved once she'd passed the worst of it. She woke up a week later... and I stayed in that town the entire time, waiting for her to wake."

The deal had fallen through, and the future of Garek's trading house looked bleak indeed. Why, in such a state, had he squandered time equal to money itself? He had no clear answer. Something without any real basis had simply kept his feet rooted there.

And so, after a week's time, the woman awoke. Amid everyone's joy at her waking, she parted her trembling lips and, in a weak voice—

"'Who am I?' That's what she said. I remember it clearly."

The woman didn't remember her own name. No—not just her name. Everything. Who she was, where she'd come from, what she'd been trying to do. What had led her to that landslide, she couldn't recall any of it either. Unable even to remember her own family, everyone was at a complete loss over how to handle her.

The only thing anyone could determine was from the clothes she'd been wearing at the time of the accident. From the lettering stitched into them, it seemed her initial, or perhaps her family name, likely began with "Ri."

"Taking the name of a flower in full bloom, she came to be called 'Reala.' And once her wounds had healed, I took it upon myself to look after her."

As her wounds slowly healed and the day she'd leave the treatment house drew near, Reala had nowhere to go. And yet she remained a cheerful woman regardless. She was lively enough to make the tragedy of the accident seem like a lie, possessing a power that could bring a smile to anyone she interacted with.

There was no way she hadn't felt anxious. Losing one's memory was the same as losing one's very existence. The reason she kept smiling through it all was simply because she needed to.

Perhaps some of it was consideration for the people around her. But the greatest reason was so she wouldn't have to look straight at the thought that she was unhappy.

"I still remember how nervous I was when I asked her to come with me. The time it took to hear her answer was, I daresay, the most suffocating stretch of my entire life. I was even more nervous then than when I properly proposed to her afterward."

And so, accepting Garek's offer, Reala set off with him for Priestella. The reason he couldn't abandon her, the reason he'd waited for her to wake—it was all simple, really.

Because Garek had fallen in love with her the very moment he'd carried her, caught in that landslide, into his Dragon Carriage and wiped the mud from her face.

"Ever since I brought Reala into my life, my trading house—which had been mired in misfortune—recovered with remarkable speed. Everyone credits my own talent, but nonsense. It was all thanks to Reala. She's the one who brought good fortune into my life. That's why I can stand here today as a merchant in my own right, as someone involved in running this city, and as a father."

"I love my wife. Our children are precious to me. There was a time I worried about her past. But I don't think that way anymore. Whatever happened in her past, she is my wife, a woman dear to me."

Having finished recounting everything from their first meeting up to now, Garek closed with that, looking somewhat bashful. Garfiel, who had listened to the whole story in silence, tilted his face up toward the sky.

Stars scattered across the darkness. What did the round moon and the twinkling stars think of him, looking down as they did now?

"I hate to ask you this. But I must."

"..........."

"What... relation are you to my wife, Reala?"

That was—what a cruel question that was.

Garfiel lowered his gaze from the sky to Garek standing before him. Garek's eyes were gentle, yet held a firm resolve as he looked at Garfiel. Garfiel wasn't so dull as to miss the feeling behind them, or the meaning behind the words.

And he knew, too, what the right answer was.

—

He opened his mouth, then closed it. He breathed in, breathed out, in, out, again and again.

His heart raced. His vision threatened to swim. His head throbbed with pain, and nausea welled up inside him. A whirlpool of tangled emotions wrung his chest tight, as though it might collapse at any moment.

—Mimi's hand gently held Garfiel's own through all of it.

"I... am..."

"..........."

"...ain't got no relation at all... to your wife."

He said it. He said it, and it was done.

At those words, the whirlwind of emotion churning in Garfiel's chest rapidly extinguished itself. What remained was a sense of loss, a suffocating emptiness. Before him, Garek lowered his eyes, lips trembling faintly, and—

"I'm... sorry..."

With that, he bowed his head, his face twisted in pain. But Garfiel didn't want to look at Garek's reaction right now.

Enough. He wanted it to stop. He didn't want to be hurt any more than this. What was wrong. Who was at fault. Was he the one at fault, or was it Garek? Who was he supposed to blame? Who was he supposed to crush, tear apart with his teeth, send flying? What would it take for this ache in his heart to just vanish somewhere and be gone?

"Dear...! Oh, thank goodness. Mr. Gorgeous and Miss Mimi are here too."

—!?

He nearly let out a cry. Nearly let out a scream, cracked with anguish. That was how terrifying her appearance was to Garfiel right now—more frightening than any blade.

"Reala, why..."

"You went running off after them in such a hurry, so I figured you must be trying to keep them here. And I thought it would be simply unthinkable to send them off without giving them anything."

To Garek, startled by his wife's arrival, Reala gave a wink and slipped past him. Then she held out a hand to Garfiel, who had frozen stiff in shock.

"Here, this is a Soirie I made myself. It's not much of a thank-you, but I'm confident in the taste, so please, take it. Please."

"...Ah."

An entirely guileless smile. Garfiel remained rigid, unable to say anything, his breath catching in his throat.

Watching the exchange between the two of them, Garek's face took on the look of someone witnessing something painful to see. No one could stop this exchange between Garfiel and Reala—not if they understood what the air between them meant. And so—

"Ohh! Sweets, yay! Awesome, Mimi's gonna show off to Ojou!"

Mimi swiped the bag of sweets right out of Reala's outstretched hand, laughing without a care in the world—a display of tone-deafness that really had to be seen to be believed. Garek looked on, stunned, and Garfiel still couldn't manage a single word. Only Reala smiled happily at Mimi's warm reaction.

"I'm so glad to hear you say that. Do send my regards to this 'Ojou' person too, won't you?"

"Yes, ma'am! Understoood! Under-, understoood!"

Mimi gave a salute with the hand not holding Garfiel's—the one that had gone pale-blue—then tucked the bag of sweets into her clothes and gave Garfiel's back a rough slap. The force of it was enough to make him double over coughing, and Mimi laughed.

"Okay, now we're really heading home! Gorgeous Tiger and Gorgeous Mimi, signing off for real this time!"

"Yes, take care now. Mr. Gorgeous, do be careful not to fall in the canal, too."

Mimi pulled Garfiel along by the hand, and Reala gave a small wave to their backs. Mimi, smiling, waved back vigorously, and Reala waved her arm even more energetically in return. Only the two men watched this heartwarming exchange with faces full of anguish.

Garfiel let himself be led along beside the canal, pulled by Mimi's arm. Mimi said nothing to him either, and they walked on and on until Reala and the others behind them were completely out of sight. Then—

"Oi, squirt..."

"—Hup-yah!"

—!?

Garfiel had been about to call Mimi to a stop, but her sudden action cut him off. Still gripping his hand, Mimi leapt lightly and landed on the building right beside them. Planting her feet on the edge of the three-story stone building, she made for the top. Dragged along, Garfiel had no choice but to kick off the same footholds and follow. In just three bounds, the two of them reached the rooftop.

"Mmm! Feels so good!"

"The hell it does. What was that just now..."

Garfiel circled in front of Mimi, who looked pleased with the wind against her face, meaning to figure out what she'd been thinking. But Mimi looked back at him with the smile wiped from her face. Seeing himself reflected in her round eyes, Garfiel felt an odd discomfort for some reason. As he stood there in silence, Mimi's expression suddenly broke, and—

"Garf, you gonna cry?"

"H-hah? What the hell are you sayin'. Ain't no way that's happenin'."

"Mimi knows Garf's strong, but you don't gotta act tough, y'know? Reala's Garfiel's mommy, right?"

—

At Mimi's unexpected words, Garfiel's breath caught. It was certainly a conclusion one could reach naturally enough, if one had grasped the flow of the conversation and knew Garfiel's circumstances. But Mimi knew nothing of his family situation, and she hardly seemed like a girl with sharp intuition to begin with. That she'd struck at the fact so bluntly, so precisely, left Garfiel shaken.

"W-why... d'you think that..."

"'Cause Garf and Reala's smell, they're super alike. Both of Reala's kids smelled kinda like Garfiel too. So Mimi figured, maybe that's it."

It wasn't reasoning so much as sheer feral instinct that had let Mimi pierce straight to the truth. Words could be twisted around, but pointed at something unchangeable like that, Garfiel had no comeback left. He sank weakly down where he stood and gazed up vacantly at the sky.

The stars and the moon looked down on him just the same as before.

"So, was Mimi right? Reala's Garf's mommy?"

"...Dunno. Is that woman really my mother, I wonder."

At Mimi's words, Garfiel covered his face with his palms. He didn't know. That, truly, was the honest state of his heart right now.

He was certain, without a doubt, that Reala was Rishia. And yet, just as Garek had told him, just as Reala's every manner had shown, she had completely forgotten that she had ever been Rishia. Having forgotten everything, Rishia had gone on accumulating years as Reala, bearing children, living what looked like a happy life.

"Heh, come to think of it, that means those two are my little brother and sister, huh."

He hadn't thought that far ahead, but half-siblings with a different father—that was exactly the same relationship he shared with Frederica. Which meant those two siblings were his own dear younger brother and sister. For Garfiel, who'd always been the youngest, they were the little brother and sister he'd never known he'd been waiting for.

—Except that it was a relationship nobody had asked for.

"Ain't nothin' good comes of me steppin' forward and sayin' who I am..."

Reala had forgotten all of Rishia's years. Even if Garfiel told her everything he knew, the fifteen years she'd spent as Reala wouldn't simply vanish, and the fifteen years she should have spent as Rishia would remain lost all the same.

Reala would be left carrying fifteen years' worth of guilt she had no need to bear, along with the grief of losing the life she should have had as Rishia. Garek, too, would have to watch his wife sink into despair, and the children, knowing nothing, would be unable to understand their mother's suffering.

All of it would be nothing but Garfiel's own self-satisfaction. Even if he forced Reala to acknowledge that she was Rishia, he alone would be the one left feeling like he'd gained something from it.

Neither Frederica nor Ryuzu knew that Rishia had survived, in this form. If Garfiel said nothing, the two of them would surely go on not knowing, from here on too. And Reala's family had no way of learning of her past unless Garfiel spoke up. That happy time of theirs would remain unlost, preserved as it was.

If Garfiel simply locked it all away in his own chest, and let go, truly let go, of what he'd already half given up on—that alone would settle everything. And yet—

"Why, then... am I..."

Why couldn't he summon the resolve to cast it aside, the determination to forget, the courage to shut it all away? Where had the tiger gone? Show me. Show me the right path. The strength to shoulder everything alone, to carry it all, and still stand up regardless. Tell me, tiger, tiger. Because a real tiger is the strongest of all, and loses to no one.

—

Clutching his head, biting back what welled up inside him, his mind churned by grief, on the verge of hurling everything away all at once—that was when Garfiel noticed.

"There, theeere."

That his head had been drawn into a small chest, and was being gently stroked.

—

Mimi had wrapped her arms around Garfiel from behind as he sat crumpled there. Resting her chin atop his head, her small palm stroked his hair gently. Feeling the ache churning through his skull ease at that soft touch, he said,

"What's the... big idea, here..."

"Nnn, Mimi just figured Garf probably wanted to cry. But see, guys can't cry except in some place where it's okay to cry or whatever, right, so annoying! Mimi forgot where, but Ojou said so!"

It sounded like an answer, yet somehow wasn't one at all. Careful not to let his heart shake, not to let his voice shake, Garfiel chose his words with caution. Still holding him, Mimi grinned a loose, goofy grin, and—

"And, um, Mimi forgot where, but it was probably inside a girl's chest, I think? Feel like it was? Yeah, it was! A guy's allowed to cry in the chest of a girl he's in love with!"

"...Who the hell said I'm in love with some shrimp like you."

The woman Garfiel had feelings for wouldn't so much as glance his way, was never gentle with him when he wanted her to be, and only turned suddenly gentle whenever he least expected it—only to collect twice that much back with her fists later. A troublesome woman, through and through. Nothing like the girl in front of him now.

And yet, at Garfiel's answer, Mimi kept right on smiling, and—

"Nnn, but it's fiiine! Even if Garf's not in love, Mimi is! See? A girl who's in love with Garf! Mimi! Right here, this chest! So it's fine to cry!"

"...Ah."

It was too ridiculous an argument to even take seriously. What was that supposed to be? Wordplay? A child's excuse? Nothing but sophistry, plain and simple. And yet, for all that it was nothing—don't screw around with me.

Tiger, tiger, where have you gone. Come back into this chest of mine, right now. Let out a ferocious growl, strike this cowering spine of mine and haul it back up, do something about these feelings. Or else, or else, or else, it'll be too late.

"Mom..."

Stop it, stop it, please stop. He didn't want to make sounds like that—whining, weakness, none of it. He was a tiger. A tiger. The strongest, the greatest, a shield harder and tougher than anyone's. And yet—

"Mom...! M-mom...! Mmom—mom...!!"

"There, theeere."

"Why?! Why'd you have to forget me?! After all this... after we finally got to meet again! Won't you even... even let me... c-call you that...?!"

"It's fiiine. Good boy, Garf, good boy!"

"Mmom... M-mom... ...Mom..."

Tiger, tiger, where have you gone. What do I look like right now? Stars, moon, sky—tell me.

What do I look like right now. If not a roaring tiger, then what am I right now—!

  • "Ojou" (お嬢) left untranslated, per Mimi's established habit of calling her employer Anastasia this nickname rather than a name.
  • The original wordplay on リ (the shared first syllable of リアラ/Reala and リーシア/Rishia, the clue that let Garek's found woman be renamed "Reala") doesn't fully carry over in English since the wiki-standard romanizations are "Reala" and "Rishia" (Re- vs Ri-); rendered the clue as "Ri" to keep it plausible but flagging the mismatch.
  • "とーや！" (Mimi's jump exclamation) has no fixed equivalent; rendered as an invented exertion shout ("Hup-yah!") rather than literal content.
  • Mimi's stuttered "かしこまり／かしこ、かしこまりー" (a playful fumble of the formal "certainly, ma'am") was adapted as a stammered "Understoood! Under-, understoood!" to preserve the comic repetition rather than the specific word.
  • Garfiel's degrading, near-wordless cries of "母さん…かあさんッ…があざんッ" and later "があざぁん……かあ、じゃん……お、かあさん" are rendered as broken repetitions of "Mom" in English; exact phonetic distortion of the Japanese couldn't be preserved 1:1.
