Arc 9 Chapter 15: "Loser"

—Your old man's been taken into custody by an associate of mine.

The instant he played that trump card, Aldebaran felt his own defeat right down to the bone.

132,044 — the number of attempts it took the Matrix to clear a single problem. Counting that number was a habit the Witch had drilled into him, but this was only the second time in his life the count had climbed this high.

And just like the first time, Aldebaran had lost all over again.

He'd laid every preparation, drawn up every scheme he could, and as long as his spirit wasn't broken he had infinite chances to fine-tune the plan — and still he'd lost.

What a weak heart he had, he thought. It disgusted him, truly, deeply.

A weak heart.

More than lacking talent, more than having his effort go unrewarded, more than being loved by no one, that was the venom that made Aldebaran hate himself most of all.

"――――"

Through repeated trial and error against the Dragon, he'd finally managed to draw the Sword Saint's blood.

That taste of success, though, was undone by some absurd Divine Protection — the Divine Protection of Bloodshed, which made him stronger the more he bled — but the sight of both his arms rendered useless was still a battle gained.

Honestly, Reinhard had probably fought battle after battle without ever sustaining real injury, and Aldebaran had hoped that making him taste pain might cast some shadow over his invincibility.

But unfortunately, the strongest man in the world, the strongest of any generation, the strongest in all of history, was strong against pain too.

So even with both arms shredded and shattered, his expression never changed.

So—

"...I know how you feel."

Aldebaran nodded up at Reinhard, who stood over him where he'd fallen in the sea of sand.

There was grit in his mouth, the taste of blood welling from a cut on the inside of his cheek, and every inch of him ached and throbbed from being battered by shockwaves — but even so, he couldn't help saying it.

Across more than a hundred and thirty thousand battles, Reinhard's expression had never changed even with his arms in that state — and yet now Aldebaran had seen that face twist into something terribly pained.

"I know how you feel."

To Reinhard, whose face now looked like a child who'd let go of his parent's hand and gotten lost in the middle of a shopping trip, Aldebaran heaped on more words of understanding.

He piled it on, and went on.

"Family's always tripping us up."

"—! As if I could believe what you're saying is true."

"Why don't you go look? Like... that thing — the Duke's Weathervane Divine Protection. The one that can spot a lie. Use that and see whether I'm telling you the truth."

"That's—"

"Can't do it, huh? —Guess you've got some trauma about taking someone's Divine Protection away from them."

At that remark, something sharp and stinging crossed Reinhard's face.

In the invincible, iron-walled Sword Saint's defenses, a single ant-hole had opened — like water starting to seep from a levee. And the more of that seeping water washed over him, the stronger Aldebaran's sense of defeat grew.

He hadn't been able to win with Body or with Skill. And because of that, he was resorting to the foul move of attacking the Heart.

It was a visibly effective strategy.

After all, something Aldebaran had failed to accomplish across more than a hundred and thirty thousand attempts was about to be achieved without shedding a drop of blood or sweat — simply by being contemptible.

And none of it was any fault of the young man born Reinhard van Astrea, who had been expected to become the Sword Saint.

"Then how about I corner you with logic instead?"

"...Logic?"

"That's right. Logic."

With a grinding effort, Aldebaran pushed himself up off the sand, pressing Reinhard further into a corner.

If the dragon could get back up, there was still an option: keep fighting while the seed of doubt he'd planted held root. But the fallen dragon showed no sign of waking, and he'd already spent the key that unlocked the Heart. In that case, all that remained was to use this key to force open the sealed route the rest of the way. —That was what Aldebaran decided.

"As you can tell by now, I set every hand I could to get ready to meet the Sword Saint head-on. Mountains of plans, the Augria Sand Dunes brimming with Miasma, and—"

"—You brought the Divine Dragon Volcanica onto your side."

"Strictly speaking, that part I pulled off with a cheat code... but yeah, that's right. Even doing all that, though, I had no confidence I could actually finish you off — and in fact, I couldn't. So you get it, right?"

"――――"

"Your old man was one of the Princess's associates. Chances... opportunities, there were plenty."

Of course, Aldebaran was certain that wasn't the reason Priscilla had brought Heinkel into her camp.

Haughty and arrogant, declaring without shame that everything in this world belonged to her, the woman Aldebaran loved forgave beautiful things and ugly things alike, however much like or dislike separated them, with equal measure.

She would never have used Heinkel as a tool to bring down Reinhard, nor scorned the way Heinkel lived his life just to wound him on purpose.

The roots of her true intentions lay hidden deep within a heart as fierce as flame, and so they could never be brought fully to light.

—Or perhaps he should have brought them to light.

Perhaps, heedless of being scorched, he should have reached his one remaining arm — the one left to him after losing his left — into those flames, prepared to have it turn to charcoal, to ash.

If he had, Priscilla Barielle would still be—

"...She's not anywhere anymore."

"Al-dono?"

"There's not one reason left for me to hesitate. I can take that kind of measure without any trouble at all."

At Aldebaran's flat declaration, Reinhard's eyes narrowed slightly.

Even to Reinhard, who for various reasons had never wanted to be granted the Divine Protection of the Weathervane, he should have given enough information to make him believe it.

"――――"

A glance showed that Reinhard's wounded arms hadn't begun to heal.

Blessed by the world, beloved of fate, Reinhard was said to have Spirits from all around swarm to heal him whenever his body took a wound — but here in the Augria Sand Dunes, Miasma, the natural enemy of Spirits, filled the air completely. So even Reinhard's fangirls couldn't gather round.

Perhaps, in all of recorded history, no one but Aldebaran had ever driven Reinhard this far into a corner — in a situation like that—

"...What is it you want from me?"

Squeezing the words out, Reinhard finally sat down at the table of hostage negotiation.

At that, Aldebaran let out one long, long breath, thinking that attacking the Heart had been worth it, and—

"Nothing outrageous. I'm not gonna make some impossible demand like 'side with me the way the Divine Dragon did.' I wouldn't make you betray your master — not something I'd hate to have done to me."

"I don't follow, and I don't believe you. Holding my father hostage, and now you'd talk about conscience?"

"What a shameless bastard, huh. I feel for you, having to deal with a guy like that. —My demand's simple: I just want you to let me go, as I am."

"Let you go?"

To that answer, laced with the sense that it was impossible, Aldebaran nodded.

He had no intention of forcing some outrageous exchange — ordering Reinhard to take his own life, or to shut down the Council of Wise Men's movements in exchange for his father.

He already knew this, after all. —Reinhard would never take a hostage negotiation.

"――――"

He truly, honestly worried for Heinkel's safety; he truly didn't want Aldebaran, his enemy, to die; and he truly worried for Fram and Ezzo, the people close to him left behind in the tower.

And yet, when it came down to it, he could take every last bit of that very human warmth and sacrifice it all on the altar of the cold-blooded necessity of protecting the world's balance.

Reinhard could sacrifice Heinkel, whose safety he genuinely worried over; he could kill Aldebaran, whom he genuinely didn't want to see die even as an enemy; he could let Fram and Ezzo, the people close to him left in the tower, die while worrying about them all the same. —That was what the Sword Saint was.

That was why only someone capable of sacrificing even himself to protect the greater cause could ever become the Sword Saint.

"Let me say one thing — letting me go here isn't going to make the world fall apart. If anything, with two users of cheat abilities gone from the Royal Selection, your master's chances of becoming queen ought to go up."

"Lady Felt would never wish to gain the throne by eliminating a rival."

"Noble of her. But then, of course she would be — if she were the type who didn't mind that, the Royal Selection would've been settled by process of elimination the moment it started."

This wasn't going to be a bargaining chip. He'd known that too.

There had been twists and turns, but in the end Priscilla acknowledged the four Royal Selection candidates besides herself. People who'd met her exacting standards wouldn't ever go for some dull deal.

In the end, this whole hostage negotiation was a farce with a foregone conclusion.

No matter how many words he spent, Reinhard's own shredded arms already proved that Aldebaran was a dangerous existence. Insisting now that he was a safe man wouldn't leave any room to be believed.

There was no way the Sword Saint, Reinhard van Astrea, would ever let Aldebaran go.

So then, what was the point of all this back-and-forth? The answer was obvious. —Everything Aldebaran had set up existed for one purpose alone, still ahead of them.

"—There it is."

That Aldebaran sensed the faint change in the air a beat before Reinhard did wasn't because he was quicker — it was because, unlike Reinhard, who found it wholly unexpected, Aldebaran had been waiting for it, longing for it. —The arrival of the thing that would end his endless battle with the Sword Saint, Reinhard van Astrea.

It was—

"—Impossible."

Half a second behind Aldebaran, Reinhard caught the presence too, and a stunned voice slipped out of him.

In a lifetime perhaps half as long as Aldebaran's, Reinhard had surely already peered into all kinds of abysses that people his own age would never lay eyes on.

Even when the Divine Dragon Volcanica — bound to the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica by Covenant, tied by a bond of deep trust — had turned into an enemy, Reinhard had responded coolly, without losing his composure.

And now that same Reinhard was struck speechless. It was no wonder. Even he was seeing this for the first time.

The Augria Sand Dunes, where the Pleiades Watchtower was built — the reason for that tower's construction was not so the Sage, weary of battle, could retire there, nor because some inquisitive Witch had wanted a tastelessly appointed library built by tricking Od and Lagna into it.

"—The Witch of Envy."

—It was because they needed somewhere to keep watch over the sealed thing itself.

—This was around when Aldebaran's battle, one that would stretch to 132,044 rounds, had just begun.

"There, that's the first aid done, but..."

Saying that, Petra cinched the bandage tight and finished wrapping it, looking anxiously at the two injured men.

The one Petra had just finished treating was Garfiel, limp and unconscious. She and the others had carried him and Ezzo — the two of them found collapsed at the very top of the watchtower, its highest floor — down to the fourth level, and had just finished doing what they could for them.

Petra had handled Garfiel's care; the other one, Ezzo's, had been handled by—

"This one's finished too. Thank you for lending me the supplies."

"Mm-mm, it's totally fine. More importantly, are you okay, Fram-chan?"

"Yes. Fortunately, Al-sama only immobilized me — he didn't do me any actual harm."

Fram said this, gesturing to her own body to show as much.

At the name Fram spoke, Petra's expression clouded faintly. Fram was unharmed, but Garfiel and Ezzo were both critically wounded, near death — and that was something none other than Al had brought about.

He hadn't raised a hand against Petra and the others either, same as with Fram. But that didn't mean what Al had done could be excused, or forgiven.

"Are you both done treating them nowww?"

Petra, who'd fallen silent and clenched her fists in frustration, turned at the voice calling from behind her — Meili, peeking into the room. Meili, who'd begged off nursing duty on the pretext of keeping watch over the tower, still had her little red scorpion perched on her head, and she looked over the bandaged Garfiel and the others.

"Both Fang-bro and Sensei-san are wrapped up in bandages, round and round, huhh."

"...It's because they're both covered in awful burns, all over. I wish one of us could actually use proper healing magic."

"My apologies. Being at the young master's side tends to sap one's motivation to study magic."

"Don't get down about it, Petra-chan. Me, I can't even wrap a bandage properly, let alone use healing magic. Back when I used to do it for Elsa, I was pretty sloppy about it, tooo."

Even hearing an excuse that wasn't really an excuse and comfort that wasn't really comfort, Petra's heart didn't lighten. Part of it was her lack of aptitude for Water Magic, the school used for healing — but the real reason she hadn't mastered the skills needed here was, without question, her own lack of resolve.

"If only Garf-san would just wake up..."

It would mean making him treat himself, but Garfiel was the one in the Emilia Camp who could use healing magic most properly of all. Just standing on bare ground was enough to revive him thanks to his Divine Protection of the Earth Spirit, and being able to use healing magic on top of that while in that state was the strength of tough, sturdy Garfiel.

Which was exactly why neither Petra nor anyone else in the camp had ever imagined Garfiel would be the first one taken out of the fight. —No, wait. This time, the first one who couldn't fight anymore hadn't been Garfiel at all.

"Subaru..."

Pressing her clenched fist to her chest, Petra's lips called out that name.

Subaru and Beatrice — neither of them could be found anywhere in the tower. It seemed the two of them had been rendered helpless by Al before Garfiel and the others, but where on earth had they gone?

It wasn't something she wanted to think about, but she couldn't help imagining lines like them being erased away without even leaving a body behind.

"Judging from how that helmet guy was acting, that doesn't seem to be it, thoughhh?"

"That's just empty comfort..."

"It's not empty comfort. Empty comfort would mean I'm lying for Petra-chan's sake, right? This isn't a lie, it's what I really think."

"――――"

"It's not really the case for me, since I can borrow the power of my little Witchbeasts, but doing away with a body the normal way is a real hassle, you know. So I don't think helmet-guy killed those two — I think he took them off somewhere by some other method."

At Meili's guess, backed by a rather alarming personal experience, delivered with a finger to her lips, Petra frowned.

If, as Meili said, Subaru and Beatrice weren't dead, that would be for the best. And if he'd taken them somewhere, there was at least a chance of getting them back.

Just what was Al thinking—

"—Whatever Al-sama is thinking, his scheme should be crushed soon enough."

"Ohh, saying that with an awful lot of confidence, aren't youuu."

To Fram, who'd cut in with that, Meili tilted her head and closed one eye. Fram, watching Meili fidget restlessly with her long braid, nodded back with a "Yes," and—

"I'll keep the exact method to myself, but I've informed my sister Grassis of what happened here. Grassis is with the young master right now, so the same information should reach him too."

"The young master...?"

"The Sword Saint, Reinhard van Astrea — that's the young master."

Perhaps it was just her imagination, but Fram seemed to swell with pride as she said it.

At the sound of "Sword Saint," Petra's eyes went wide, while Meili, who'd come up beside her, let out a "waah," a reaction that felt somewhere between disgust and sympathy.

"If the Sword Saint's coming flying in, seems like it'll be fine no matter what helmet-guy's plotting. If anything, I'm starting to feel sorry for himmm."

"Meili-chan, saying that much about someone..."

"I mean, the Sword Saint went up against Divine Dragon-chan like it was nothing at allll. Ah, but I guess now that Divine Dragon-chan's on helmet-guy's side, who knows how it'll go, huhh."

"...Even if Al-san gets beaten, I won't think it's a shame at all."

If anything, Petra couldn't help but wish he'd get thoroughly, utterly wrecked.

Having her own feelings trampled was one thing, and a bearable one at that — Petra barely knew Al, so even having whatever feelings she'd had for him betrayed left her with relatively little pain.

What truly hurt was for the people who knew Al far, far better than Petra did.

Both Subaru and Emilia had genuinely worried about Al — worried enough that they'd endured giving up time they wanted to spend together in order to make this happen. —And Al had betrayed that.

"I don't want to forgive him...!"

Every now and then, Petra found herself thinking she must be a terribly narrow-hearted, unpleasant girl.

It wasn't just the anger she felt toward Al right now. Ever since the incident surrounding the Sanctuary, she still hadn't forgiven Roswaal either, and even in the Empire she'd run into things she couldn't bring herself to forgive, more than once.

Petra's heart couldn't forgive any of it, and didn't even want to try. The only people Petra wanted to be kind to were the ones who never did the things Petra couldn't forgive.

Being around people who were truly kind made a girl like that feel like a very unpleasant child.

"Petra-chan, you're plenty kind too, you know. I'll vouch for it myselfff."

Petra couldn't help ducking her head, and Meili patted it lightly, pat pat. At the feel of her palm, Petra looked up to find Meili wearing a strange sort of face.

It was a face caught somewhere between being exasperated with herself and exasperated with Petra, an odd sort of expression.

"...It'll be a lot of work, but let's carry Garf-san down. Since he's got his Divine Protection, laying him flat on the ground might help him heal faster."

Petra proposed that without a word about either Meili's expression or the hand on her head.

Fortunately, neither Garfiel nor Ezzo were especially large for men. More importantly, Petra and Meili were, as you'd expect, un-strong young girls, but—

"Understood. Let's carry Ezzo-sama down too, while we're at it. Splitting up the nursing between upstairs and downstairs would just be a lot more trouble."

As she said it, Fram lifted both their bodies up with an easy heave.

Despite being a girl the same age as Petra and the others, her physical ability was beyond comparison. She was apparently a servant of the Sword Saint's household — was that the reason for her strength?

In any case—

"I don't think this is over yet."

Al, having pulled off something outrageous, had left the tower with the Divine Dragon in tow, and before long he'd be pursued by the Sword Saint Reinhard, once word from Fram reached him.

Both Meili and Fram seemed to think that whatever purpose Al had would end up ruined because of that, but Petra couldn't bring herself to be so optimistic.

Maybe part of it was that Petra didn't know Reinhard personally.

But more than that, it struck her as a little strange that Al, who'd managed to get Subaru under his control, wouldn't also have some countermeasure ready for Reinhard.

And if even Reinhard couldn't stop Al, then—

"—Huh?"

That was just as Fram carried Garfiel and Ezzo out of the room, and Petra and Meili, having packed up the travel supplies they'd spread out, were about to follow after her. —Petra, stuffing tools into her bag, felt something strange at the bottom of it and pulled it out.

It was—

"...A book?"

What settled into Petra's hand, pulled out of the bag, was a single book — one she recognized. Recognized all too well, in fact, since this tower was, quite literally, full of mountains of exactly this kind of book.

There was no mistaking it. This was one of the Books of the Dead lined up in vast numbers in the third-floor library.

That a Book of the Dead existed here — that in itself wasn't strange.

But its being here was strange. Ezzo had strictly warned them beforehand not to take any Book of the Dead out of the library, and everyone had understood and agreed to that.

And yet, a Book of the Dead had turned up in a bag. And not just any bag—

"—Subaru's bag."

The bag in her hands was the one holding Subaru and Beatrice's personal belongings.

In a bag packed with necessities like spare clothes and the Beatrice Growth Journal Subaru kept writing, a Book of the Dead had somehow been slipped in, as though hidden away in secret.

"I... can't read the name?"

Swallowing hard, Petra checked the spine and knit her brow.

She'd heard that the spine of a Book of the Dead carried the book's title — and in the case of a Book of the Dead, that meant the name of the person whose life and death it recorded. In fact, whether or not one had the right to actually read the books in the library, everyone should have been able to make out the titles on their spines.

But this book's title couldn't be read. It had been scraped away, deliberately made unreadable.

"――――"

Petra tried to imagine both the true identity of this book with its scraped-away title, and the meaning behind its being hidden in the bag.

The intent behind scraping off the title was obvious enough — to hide whose Book of the Dead this was. And the reason for hiding it in the bag was surely the same: to hide that this Book of the Dead existed at all.

And given that this Book of the Dead had been hidden in Subaru's bag—

"A book... Subaru hid."

"Petra-chan? I've got this stuff packed up over here. How about over thereee?"

"...Meili-chan."

At that voice, Petra, whose hands had gone still, turned to look at Meili. At first Meili tilted her head at Petra's not-quite-normal look, but the moment she noticed the book in her hands, she froze in shock.

"Petra-chan, that book, is thattt..."

"...It came out of Subaru's bag. Maybe, in this—"

"In that bookkk?"

"—There might be a hint about what's happening right now."

Gripping the book's binding tight, Petra thought that wasn't necessarily a mistaken guess.

Al's sudden rampage inside the tower — his original goal had been to read the Book of the Dead of the late Royal Selection candidate, Priscilla Barielle. Or maybe he'd had some other goal too, but either way, there had to have been some purpose to coming to the tower.

If that purpose was, perhaps, this very Book of the Dead — and Subaru had judged that he must never hand this book over to Al, and hidden it in his own bag—

—and if that had been the cause of the decisive rift between Subaru and Al—

"If I read this book, maybe I'll understand."

"...I don't think I'd recommend it, since I think it's dangerousss."

"――――"

"But stilll, I get it — Petra-chan's feeling that she doesn't want this to end with her having done nothing at all."

To Meili, who said that with a face that seemed to say what a handful you are, Petra stuck her tongue out a little and said, "Sorry."

No matter what Meili said to her, Petra had already meant to do it. —She'd been told, over and over until they were sick of saying it, by both Subaru and Beatrice, that reading a Book of the Dead was dangerous.

For what it was worth, Ezzo had boasted that the trick to reading a Book of the Dead was to shut your heart away and make it blank — but whether Petra could actually manage that, she had no idea.

But Petra Leyte simply wasn't the kind of girl who could allow herself to just crouch there doing nothing, content to be left quietly behind.

So—

"—Meili-chan, if something happens, I'm counting on you afterward, okay?"

"Asking something like that of me — Petra-chan, you really don't have an eye for people, do youuu."

After that exchange, Petra took a deep breath, and slowly turned the pages of the book resting on her knees—

"—Ah."

—and touched a Memory that was forbidden in this world.

Even Aldebaran had no certainty as to when it would happen.

There was a chance that the girls' hearts were more fragile than Aldebaran gave them credit for — that they'd be unable to bear what had happened and simply weep quietly, leaving the face-down card never turned over.

If that had happened, Aldebaran might have repeated his hundred and thirty thousand rounds another hundred and thirty thousand times, still endlessly stacking stones beyond counting, without end.

But—

"I figured the girls would think that far, at least."

The girls left behind — Petra, Meili, and Fram, the three of them — possessed experience and cleverness beyond their years, along with the rare abilities that came from the environments that had shaped them.

After tending to the critically injured Garfiel and Ezzo, they wouldn't simply stop and stand still — Aldebaran had read that they'd start moving, taking forward-looking action.

And in trying to do just that, they would find it. —A single Book of the Dead, meaningfully hidden inside Natsuki Subaru's bag.

To girls with nothing left to rely on, who'd lost their guiding star, it must have looked like a single handful of possibility left behind by Natsuki Subaru. If they could just reel in that thread of possibility, surely, without fail, they'd reach some light that would let them escape the closed-off dead end they were trapped in.

There was no room for them to realize it was a trap Aldebaran had laid out all along.

That Book of the Dead—

"—was Natsuki Subaru's book."

A Book of the Dead that ran against the very laws of this world — truly a thing that deserved to be called forbidden.

What happens when someone reads through one of these — that's something far more instinctively, undeniably grasped by witnessing it firsthand than by any explanation in words.

It was—

"—Impossible."

Reinhard, his blue eyes going wide, let the same stunned word slip out again.

But no matter how much he tried to deny that reality, it wouldn't be overturned. If the Sword Saint Reinhard was a thing blessed by the world, then this was a thing that cursed the world.

Blessing and Curse were two sides of the same coin — not a power balance where one simply outweighed the other.

So—

"Nobody but the Sword Saint can stop that thing."

Sitting himself down cross-legged right where he was, Aldebaran took in, past Reinhard's shoulder, the night sea of sand — a scene lit only by starlight — and the threat still further staining it pitch black.

It was the very end of the far east of the world, further east even than the tower where the Sage had lived, coming from the sealed shrine that every living thing turned its eyes from — the very symbol of a table flipped over, of everything ruined.

Ferociously, like a black tsunami surging forward, it was a demonic hand kneaded together from the Witch's Miasma.

Touching the forbidden, letting the Witch of Envy intervene in the world.

That was the time bomb Aldebaran had planted, meant just for Reinhard—

"—! You—"

"If you mean finish me off first, I'll fight back with everything I've got. Just so you know, if you don't go, the world ends. Not that I'd mind that either way, personally."

"What are you—"

"Even if the world ends, I won't. —Because that thing's going to keep on ignoring me."

That was exactly why Aldebaran himself could never have been the switch for the bomb.

Only someone sharp-eyed and clever, someone with the courage to break through a situation, could ever be that switch.

"Reinhard, I don't want to destroy the world. If anything, it's the opposite."

"Ngh...!"

"I won't touch your old man. —Go."

"Al-dono — don't think everything's going to go your way."

Grinding his teeth in frustration, he'd probably cursed as foully as he possibly could. And still, leaving behind knightly manners far too refined for the moment, Reinhard kicked off the sand.

Turning his back on Aldebaran and the others, Reinhard shot straight — and threw himself ferociously from the side at the hand of the Witch of Envy, closing in to swallow the Pleiades Watchtower whole.

—In that instant, Aldebaran experienced the illusion that sound and color and light and heat, everything that ought to exist, had been torn root and all out of the world.

"――――"

The Sword Saint's white strike collided with the Witch's black destruction, and a color that was neither white nor black was born.

It rivaled the apocalyptic big bang that had come just before, in the battle between the Sword Saint and the Divine Dragon — starlight, bullets of lightning, flame that reduced the night to ash.

And this big bang didn't end with just that one moment. It went on, and on, and on.

Again and again, over and over, countless times, endlessly, without end, knowing no end — heat was born, light was born, color was born, sound was born, colliding as though to remake the world anew, as though to carve a line between the world that had been and the world yet to come.

—It was a battle without resolution, between a thing that would not end and a thing that would not perish.

'...I got put to sleep. What happened?'

The plan had worked — he'd succeeded in pitting Reinhard against the Witch of Envy.

The legendary clash between the Sword Saint and the Witch of Envy was happening near the tower, kilometers away, and yet there was a terror to it, as though the aftershocks reached even this far.

Perhaps sensing that aftershock, the unconscious dragon woke up.

The dragon had been sprawled out spread-eagled on the sand, an unseemly sight for the strongest of dragons, but now he narrowed his golden eyes at the shockwaves reaching him from far away, and—

'Looks like you pulled it off, huh.'

"Yeah. A half-asleep, incomplete Witch of Envy, and a Sword Saint who isn't at full strength thanks to the Miasma and his busted arms... worth crossing a hundred and thirty thousand ropes that could've snapped at any second. You good to go?"

'Aside from my throat feeling scratchy and having burned through a ton of Mana even with a Dragonhusk mana tank, I'm fine. Flying us out over the sand sea's no problem.'

"Good enough. We'll sync up on what happened while you were out later. For now—"

Let's go, he thought, and just as he tried to stand, something came flying in from far off with terrific speed, aimed straight at Aldebaran's leg — and the dragon knocked it down with his tail on reflex.

What was knocked away, tumbling across the sand, was just a small stone. —Probably something Reinhard, even while holding back the Witch of Envy's hand, had flung Aldebaran's way.

'They keep coming, one after another! Might not stop till we're completely out of sight!'

As the barrage of too-small thrown stones kept coming, wrapped up and shielded by the dragon's spread wings, Aldebaran found himself admiring Reinhard's sense of duty.

Understanding his role as the world's savior, and still pouring everything he had into the part that wasn't that. —This Sword Saint seemed greedier than the image Aldebaran had of him.

That was surely the influence of someone at Reinhard's side, he thought, and—

"—Told you, hero. I win in the end, no matter what. Even if I lose a hundred thousand times along the way."

Leaving behind words that would never reach him, Aldebaran, gripped in the dragon's claws, took to the sky once more.

Against the night sea of sand, the Sword Saint holding back head-on the obsession of the Witch of Envy, darker than the night itself, looked like nothing so much as a martyr standing alone against a great tsunami.

Though, if anything, that martyr was the one who'd be left behind alone in the world after everything else had perished.

'We could probably take down the Sword Saint too, right now, catching him in a pincer with that thing.'

"...What good would that do. If Reinhard's gone, there's nobody left to shut the lid on hell. I need that guy to keep being the lid."

'Just saying.'

"I can't afford to have him die on me."

To begin with, Aldebaran had never had the slightest intention of letting Reinhard die.

It was only that unless he attacked with lethal force, he wouldn't get results enough even to wound him. He'd simply understood the Sword Saint to be that high a wall to climb.

"'Don't think everything's going to go your way,' huh."

The parting words of that high wall, that martyr left behind by the world, came back to him.

Was this too some power of a Divine Protection? If so, maybe he'd been granted something like a Divine Protection of Vital Spots or a Divine Protection of Weak Points, and it had just activated.

Certainly, Aldebaran had the power, through infinite attempts, to draw the outcome he wanted toward him.

And yet, with all that power—

"Not once, not a single time so far, has anything I really wanted to go well actually gone well."

Even having reached the far side of 132,044 acts of defiance, within the heart of a thing bound to neither Blessing nor Curse, that sense of defeat never once faded away.
