Arc 7 Chapter 81: "The Heroine and the Clown"

—Under the rule of Vincent Vollachia, the current Emperor of the Sacred Vollachia Empire, the Empire had entered an era of peace unprecedented since its founding.

Small-scale skirmishes still occurred here and there, but conflicts on the scale of thousands clashing in arms — nothing came to mind except Yorna Mishigure's rebellion.
Civil war, of course, and any slide into territorial disputes with other nations besides — as long as the long-standing standoff with the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica remained at a stalemate, such things simply had no chance to occur.
It would be easy to say the times themselves were on the Empire's side, but to say so would do a disservice to the countless labors spent maintaining that peace.

In the first place, was this the kind of feat that could be achieved by the times' favor alone?

To keep war after war from breaking out for nine years of his reign, in an Empire where conflict and the reasons for conflict never ran dry — that was as contradictory an act as building a bonfire atop thin ice.
And yet it was accomplished — through the skill of the current Emperor, Vincent Vollachia, a man even called a "pacifist."
That said—

"—I doubt His Excellency is ever pleased to hear himself called a 'pacifist.'"

"Is that so-o-o? Whatever one thinks of the rights and wrongs of it, surely it can't feel bad to have one's work recognized? And indeed, the stability of the nation is exactly what His Excellency desires, isn't it?"

"Then are you telling me you take pride in being called a 'Demi-Human Enthusiast'?"

"Without question. That name is one I spread myself, after all."

"Well, well, it seems I picked a poor comparison. Lately, every old friend who comes to call on me is an oddball. Though I suppose I can't complain of being bored, thanks to it."

So saying, the woman swept back the bangs falling over her forehead, a wild smile spreading across her beautiful features as she narrowed her green eyes.
She was tall and slender, her body honed to a supple, athletic strength.
Long limbs with smooth skin and a figure rich with feminine curves were bound up in clothing like that of a merchant ship's crew, and beside the fine chair she sat in rested a sheathed curved sword. As proof that it was no mere ornament but a weapon meant for real combat, her whole body brimmed with the particular vigor of a warrior.
Above all, on the left side of her face was a long, white scar from a blade, running so far as to stretch from her forehead down to her chin.

A scar that drew the eye — one that, once seen, burned itself into memory and refused to fade.
And yet, even with it, the impression she gave others was one of beauty — for this woman known as the "Scorching Duke," even that scar was nothing more than an accessory.

—Serena Dracroy, Senior Count, known as the "Scorching Duke."
That was the name and title of the great woman before him — one of the foremost nobles of the Sacred Vollachia Empire.
And for Roswaal, who sat facing her now, she was a friend with whom he'd kept up an acquaintance across national borders long before matters had come to this.

"Assuming, of course, I take you at your word when you call me an old friend, tha-a-at is."

"I've no intention of playing some petty game over whether we're friends or not. In the first place, being a Senior Count means every single person I meet only wants to talk profit and loss. Every now and then I'd like some idle chat free of all that — is that too much to ask of me?"

"I wouldn't call it too much to ask, but that sorrowful plea does tug at my heart, I thi-i-ink."

With a small laugh, Roswaal picked up the cup on the table before him and, savoring its warm fragrance, brought the tea to his lips. Something fragrant and warm danced across his tongue. —Unfortunately, that was the extent of what Roswaal could draw from food or drink.
Tasting nothing no matter what he ate or drank was a burden Roswaal had been forced to bear in order to become who he was now — part of the price he'd had to pay.
Even so, he could hardly fail to offer some word of thanks for the hospitality extended to him as a guest.
So Roswaal glanced sideways at the small figure seated beside him. That person, who had presumably savored the tea's flavor in his stead, let out a small sigh, and—

"Such fine tea leaves, and they've gone to waste. I'd recommend replacing whoever's in charge of serving."

Narrowing her sharp crimson eyes, she delivered her verdict on the tea with an even sharper remark.
At her words, which discarded both mercy and restraint, Roswaal closed one eye, while the one she'd addressed let out an amused "Ha" and laughed, saying—

"You don't mince words, do you. So the server should be replaced?"

"Yes. It's simply too disrespectful to the tea leaves."

"Actually, everyone in my household was busy, so I'm the one who brewed this tea."

"Is that so. Then I'd suggest you never do so again, as long as you live."

"A girl who knows nothing of tact — I like her. You've taken quite the wife."

Baring her teeth at the rapid-fire exchange, Serena shot Roswaal a mischievous look.
As ever, she was a woman of broad-mindedness, if that was the word for it — generous toward rudeness and disrespect alike. For all her standing as a great noble, she lacked the sort of character that clung stubbornly to authority.
That way of thinking, that way of being, hadn't changed one bit since the day they'd first met.

"That said, she's not someone incapable of telling rudeness from insult, so-o-o. She simply understood a joke right on the edge for what it was — mind you don't push it too far, Ram."

"Understood, dear."

"—. Serena, let me correct that: she's my attendant, not my wife, no-o-o."

"I see, so the 'Demi-Human Enthusiast' hasn't finally been caught, after all. Though relations between our two nations are getting tense, I did think it cold of you not to invite me to the wedding."

Roswaal sighed at Ram, who accepted the correction without missing a beat and then went right on calmly closing the distance regardless. Serena's smirking reply, too, felt subtly off from what Roswaal had intended.
Still, Roswaal knew from recent experience that the more he dug into this topic, the worse it would go for him. So he left it there.
In any case—

"Even so, I'd heard of it, but it's still a surprise — that you and Count Dracroy were on such close terms, Roswaal-sama."

"Whether you can call it 'close' when we go years without so much as a word between us, I couldn't say. And then he shows up unannounced in the middle of all this upheaval... Any ruder, and I might be tempted to burn him alive like I did my father."

"Serena, that joke doesn't land so well with anyone who isn't from the Empire, you know."

"Hm? Is that so. It's a surefire laugh with just about anyone in high society."

The remark that even made Ram wince for a moment was a joke poking fun at one chapter of Serena's struggle for succession — one drawn from the true story of how she'd seized control of House Dracroy by burning her own father alive.
Even the scar on her cheek, so it was said, had been dealt to her by that same father before she killed him — and both the nerve it took her to turn such a thing into a joke, and the Empire's willingness to laugh along, gave one pause for thought.

Roswaal had first come to know her back in his teens — when Serena had visited the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica on some errand, and he'd worked to resolve the trouble that arose there.
Put simply, he'd saved Serena's life when she was targeted by an assassin, and had even exposed that the one behind it was her own father, who resented her existence — which meant his ties to House Dracroy ran surprisingly deep.
That said, Serena's personality hadn't changed one way or the other before or after that harrowing experience, so her fierce, fire-like way of being was simply innate to her.

"It's true I'd love to hear all about your exploits in society and swap old stories... but as you've no doubt guessed, there's a reason I've come calling without warning, ye-e-es."

Catching up with an old friend held its own appeal, but for Roswaal, nothing mattered more than the fulfillment of his cherished wish.
Every part of Roswaal's life existed for the sake of the wish sunk deepest within his heart. —And so his visit to the Empire, even each blink and breath he took, was but one more step toward that cherished goal.

"—————"

At present, Roswaal and Ram had parted ways with Emilia and the others and traveled to the territory of Senior Count Dracroy in the northwest of the Empire, where they were meeting with its ruler, Serena Dracroy.
The purpose of their visit was, needless to say, to secure Subaru and Rem, who had vanished from the Pleiades Watchtower and were believed to have been sent far off into the lands of the Empire — though for Roswaal's part, recovering Subaru alone would have sufficed. He wasn't foolish enough to say so aloud, though.
In any case, the journey was one to bring their missing kin back to the Kingdom.

That said, even if bringing them back was the whole of the purpose, the matter was neither so easy nor so simple.
Of the Four Great Nations besides the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, travel to and from each was difficult for its own reasons. The Kararagi City-States, which required connections and a great deal of money, were still the more manageable option, and the Holy Kingdom of Gusteko, too, could be considered within tolerable bounds of straightforwardness so long as one didn't mistake the timing or how one approached matters of faith.
Seen in that light, the nature of the Empire — so at odds with the Kingdom — was by far the most difficult of all.

The relationship between Lugunica and Vollachia had historically been long and hostile, and even now, though a non-aggression pact had miraculously been struck between them, it remained doubtful how far one could trust it unreservedly.
That non-aggression pact was less a proof or sign of friendly relations than it was a warning — that this was a time for each nation to turn its eyes inward, and neither should go sticking its nose in where it wasn't wanted.
This time, the closure of the border seemed to Roswaal to be connected, in its background, to that non-aggression pact.
Or perhaps—

"—Are you suggesting this whole affair shaking the capital is some scheme by a faction wanting to tear up the non-aggression pact? There's a limit to how suspicious a man can be. That's not a thought I'd expect from someone of the Kingdom."

Hitting precisely on what was in Roswaal's mind, Serena narrowed her green eyes.
This formidable woman was easygoing enough in manner, and her skill at brewing tea was catastrophic, but her insight was no false thing — she hadn't been entrusted with one of the Empire's few Senior Count seats for nothing.
The Empire's governing philosophy, so different from the Kingdom's, asked nothing of age or birth so long as one had ability. Naturally, it was a system in which the more capable a person was, the higher a position they attained.
That was how Serena Dracroy had won, and continued to hold, her position as Senior Count.

Her observation just now was, in fact, one possibility Roswaal had already considered and kept in mind.
By the time Roswaal and the others had entered the Empire, the sparks of rebellion had already begun sending up smoke. And the efforts to smother the fire while it was still just a sign of trouble hadn't borne fruit this time.
If it seemed likely to grow into a full-blown rebellion, then naturally there had to be some cause behind it.
And that was—

"—A war of territory against the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica. That's what I suspect lies behind it."

To Serena, who'd just called her suspicious, Ram — hands resting on her own knees — said quietly.
Meeting the glance the great woman shot her way head-on with her own pale pink eyes, Ram continued.

"If Emperor Vincent Vollachia's reign is under threat, then someone out there doesn't want the peace he built. And if that's so, what does that someone want once that peace is broken? It's only natural to think the answer might be the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica."

"Is that so? There are those who rebel simply because they can't stand the existence of someone greater than themselves, you know. The ringleader of the lot who turned a blade on me last year was one such."

"Let's set aside such special cases for now."

"I wouldn't call it so special, but I suppose it does stray from the point."

Running a finger along her well-shaped jaw, Serena weighed Ram's opinion.
Roswaal hadn't interrupted along the way because he'd found nothing in Ram's opinion that needed correcting. He'd never discussed this concern with her before, but it came as no surprise that she'd been worrying over the same possibility he had.
In any case—

"Serena, you shouldn't want a war with the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica either. Unless, of course, you're harboring enough anger at the Emperor to want to rise up against him."

"Fortunately, I've never once had cause for complaint against a meritocrat like His Excellency. As for General Yorna's occasional temper tantrums, I hold territory well away from the Demon City, so it's none of my concern. Only—"

"Only?"

"What's behind this opinion of yours, that we shouldn't want war with the Kingdom? If you're about to tell me the odds are against us, a wind's going to start blowing through my heart, I warn you."

Even as her lips loosened into a smile, a sharp edge crept into the look Serena leveled at him.
It was her own way of expressing pride in response to Roswaal's words. For a woman known as the "Scorching Duke," who held her position as Senior Count through raw ability, being looked down upon was unforgivable.
This, unlike Ram's remark about the tea, was on an entirely different footing.

Serena poured no blood or heart into her tea — but into her family name, she poured every drop she had.

"Well, Roswaal? Is it a misunderstanding on my part? Or a slip of the tongue on yours?"

"Hmm..."

As Serena bore down on him, cutting off every avenue of retreat, Roswaal returned his old friend's green-eyed gaze and noticed Ram, beside him, watching his profile out of the corner of her eye.
She'd no doubt sensed, keenly, from the shift in the air, that the wrong answer here could spell trouble.
And if Serena's mood soured, it would become extremely difficult for Roswaal and Ram to move about the Empire. It wasn't simply a matter of losing a patron or supporter, either — Serena knew Roswaal's true nature.
Weighing that, and factoring in Serena's character and disposition besides, the best answer Roswaal could give here was—

"You're right, I'm sorry for the misunderstanding. Let me correct myself. —If war breaks out with the Kingdom, a great deal of needless blood will be spilled. Vollachian blood, at that. Which is why I wouldn't recommend it."

"Oh?"

At Roswaal's flat, even reply, Serena's brow twitched upward.
The white scar across her face trembled, and her pupils narrowed the slightest bit. It hadn't yet erupted into an outburst of emotion, but everything depended on what came next.
But Roswaal had already decided what came next. He had no intention of trying lies or empty flattery on her.

If Kingdom and Empire came to blows, vast quantities of Vollachian blood would be shed.
And the reason was—

"After all, the Kingdom has the 'Sword Saint,' Reinhard van Astrea. Every last Vollachian soldier who crosses the border will only go to building a river of blood."

"Oh, come now, that's playing dirty!"

"Dirty or not, since he does exist, there's nothing for it but to put him on the table, I thi-i-ink. As you say, he's the sort of trump card that shatters any argument outright. However—"

Here Roswaal paused, closing one of his two differently colored eyes and leaving only the blue one open, and said—

"This particular trump card wouldn't hesitate for even a moment to be played first."

Normally, one held a trump card or ace in the hole in reserve as long as possible — but this was not so where the "Sword Saint" was concerned. Playing him first was the most effective move, and the payoff the greatest.
Above all, the man himself would gladly accept that role.

"Well, well. Not even allowed to run a proper thought experiment — you'd make any strategist weep."

Just then, dispelling the tension of a moment ago, Serena let her weight drop heavily back against her chair. She pursed her lips like a sulking child, and said—

"Living in the Empire, it's all just hearsay to me at best... but from where you stand, is the 'Sword Saint' really a monster beyond all reckoning?"

"The 'Sword Saint' of generations past had a certain charm to him, but the current one — that phrase fits perfectly, I'd sa-a-ay. Though I doubt you'll really feel it without seeing the real thing."

"You say that as though you've watched every 'Sword Saint' there's ever been. But I see..."

With a wry smile at Roswaal's reply, Serena knit her well-shaped brows.
On the grounds of their long acquaintance, Serena trusted that Roswaal wouldn't spout nonsense here, and gave his words serious, thorough consideration.
But not every citizen of the Empire besides her was going to be so reasonable.

"Overestimate one's own nation's strength, or underestimate the 'Sword Saint's' power, and tragedy becomes unavoidable. So I'll go ahead and blow a headwind straight into your heart: you shouldn't fight, no-o-o."

"—————"

"And well — if war is ever picked with the Kingdom, I'll be one of the ones facing you, too. Shall I show you? The mercilessness of a ruthless magic assault raining down from an altitude you can't touch, hand or foot."

He shrugged, playing it for a joke, but Roswaal's own strategic combat power was nothing to scoff at.
Not on the level of the "Sword Saint," perhaps, but simply flying through the air while wielding magic, he could shut down thousands of common soldiers. Choose a battlefield suited to him, and he could push through an entire front line alone.

"That much, unlike the talk about the 'Sword Saint,' I can believe without any trouble. I've seen a glimpse of it myself, with my own eyes."

"And that was more than ten years ago, too. These days, I could show you magic far more refined than back then, I thi-i-ink."

"You're a bottomless one, aren't you. Still, power that stands out that far always comes bound with a fitting shackle. That's why you can't act as you please here in the Empire, I'd wager."

"Correct."

This time it was Roswaal's turn to raise both hands, bested.
In truth, if he carried out the tactic he'd just described, Roswaal could keep firing magic from an altitude no human hand could reach, scorching the Empire to ash until his mana ran dry.
But as wide as the world was today, there was no one besides Roswaal capable of such a feat with magic. Even if he went easy on it, the result would be much the same.
Displaying magical skill of that caliber in the Vollachia Empire was as good as revealing his true identity.

That was why Roswaal had to hold back from any flashy displays during this visit to the Empire.
The same reasoning and restriction applied to Emilia, off acting on her own elsewhere.

"In her case, there's also the chance she'll just fly into a temper and forget herself, though."

"That-a-t part we'll just have to leave to the children traveling with her. Well, given the circumstances, Garfiel should be able to play the role of holding her back too, so let's not worry too much, too much."

Or rather, if he couldn't trust in that much, he'd never have committed to splitting up in the first place.
It was precisely because he trusted her judgment enough to leave things in her hands that this was the current state of affairs. —Though if Otto or Petra heard him call it "trust," they'd no doubt pull quite the sour face.

"—I take it you two aren't the only ones who've entered the Empire under these circumstances?"

Alongside the exchange between Roswaal and Ram, Serena straightened up from her chair and went on. Receiving both their gazes, she traced the scar on her cheek with a finger, and said—

"Something you're searching for... no, someone, is it. It must be something quite precious to the both of you."

"Even between the two of us, that's a question I find hard to just nod along to, tho-o-ugh."

"But your actions speak for themselves, don't they. Otherwise you'd never go to the trouble of crossing a sealed border to come to Vollachia. —Not unless it were fate itself driving you."

Narrowing her eyes just the faintest bit, Serena murmured in a quiet voice.
Roswaal, finding nothing in her words that struck a chord, furrowed his brow at the odd sense of it. But before he could press her on what she truly meant, Ram was quicker to nod and say "Yes"—

"There's someone Ram absolutely must bring back. Someone as good as Ram's own life."

"Ram."

"Whether Ram tries to dodge it or hide it, I doubt it would work on this person."

Roswaal moved to caution Ram for so readily exposing information that could become a weakness, only to be talked back at in turn, and found no counter-argument came to him.
Told that their actions themselves were the answer, well — it was true enough.

"Even so, there's a right way and a wrong way to show a weakness."

"Don't go blaming Ram for that. Isn't it rather touching? What surprised me more, though, was that you went to such reckless lengths for the sake of something precious to her. She must matter to you a great deal."

"Yes. There is no substitute for Ram, after all."

"A fact I won't deny, but the way you put it concerns me a little, I mu-u-ust say."

The person each of them wished to bring back differed between Roswaal and Ram — but with no way of knowing that, Serena seemed to have drawn the wrong conclusion.
Given they wanted her help, the finer details about who they were looking for could be shared later.

"—In any case, I understand now why you warned that we shouldn't start a war."

Reeling the conversation back further still, past the immediate topic to Roswaal's earlier warning, Serena's expression abruptly tightened. Naturally, tension rose in Roswaal and Ram's faces as she studied them, and with an intelligent gleam in her eye she went on, "But—"

"Unlike me, who's willing to lend an ear to your counsel, I can't vouch for how the rest of the Empire's people will react. Tell most of them there's no hope of victory against the 'Sword Saint,' and they'll only fly into a rage."

"That's precisely the difference from the Kingdom, where no one needs the 'Sword Saint's' strength explained to them at all, ye-e-es."

"Quite. In the Kingdom, one glimpse of the 'Sword Saint' Reinhard is all it takes — no further explanation is needed."

Owing to treaties among the Four Great Nations, Reinhard was forbidden from leaving Lugunica, so people from other countries had almost no chance to lay eyes on him.
If only such a chance could be arranged, there'd be no need for such a roundabout way of persuading anyone.

—One look at the "Sword Saint" Reinhard van Astrea, and anyone would grasp at once just how foolish it would be to stand against him.

The only ones who failed to understand that were madmen living in a world detached from all sane reason.

"Hearing you say all that so earnestly, all I can do now is hope His Excellency suppresses the rebellion without incident — or that whoever might replace him from the rebel side has some measure of reason."

The rebellion had already broken out; what lay beyond its aims was where this whole conversation had started.
If the rebels had some clear-headed plan, and no reckless ambition of clashing with the Kingdom, then the war between the two nations, and the great bloodshed Roswaal had described, could be avoided.
Of course, nothing would be better than the Emperor's side crushing the rebellion cleanly.

"However this civil war comes to a close, it would be a problem if its aftermath turned its blade toward Lugunica. A quarrel with the Empire isn't something on my agenda, you see."

—Every single one of Roswaal's actions was a road leading toward the cherished wish he meant to fulfill.
This chance to meet with Serena here in the Empire was no exception.
Wanting her help in bringing Subaru back was part of it, and so was persuading her, as one of the Empire's powerful figures, of the futility of war against the Kingdom.

Subaru's existence was indispensable to Roswaal's cherished wish.
What's more, a war with the Sacred Vollachia Empire had no place on the path Roswaal meant to walk. This was no time to be fighting a war, Kingdom against Empire.
Until the day of the Rite of Dragon Devotion arrived and the Covenant between the Kingdom and the Dragon was renewed, there could be no obstacles allowed.
Any problem standing in the way of the Royal Selection had to be removed.
To that end as well—

"—His Excellency the Emperor needs to put down this rebellion as swiftly as possible, and see to it that this first era of peace since the Empire's founding continues on a good long while, mustn't he."

"Hoh? Haven't seen that scheming look on you in a while. Plotting something wicked?"

Catching Roswaal's words, Serena raised her brows, delighted.
He had no awareness of wearing any scheming look, but if she said so, there was probably something to it. Even so, Roswaal's thinking shouldn't run counter to Serena's own intentions.
She, too, was one of the Empire's nobles, and so long as she didn't take the rebellion as her own personal affair, her position naturally put her on the Emperor's side.

"Count Dracroy."

Just as Roswaal was thinking this over, Ram, beside him, spoke up to Serena of her own accord.
At that address, Serena looked at Ram, and—

"Might I have a moment?"

"Ah, go ahead. Actually, do stop calling me Count Dracroy. I just want to chat with an acquaintance... call me Serena instead."

"Serena-sama, then. —You really do seem quite close with Roswaal-sama."

"Hm?"

Stared at intently by Ram, who'd just changed how she addressed her, Serena blinked in surprise. But she quickly shifted into a mischievous expression and nodded, "I see, I see."
What exactly she "saw," Roswaal had a bad feeling about.

"Seems you're curious how I came to know this man. From the look of it, the two of you go back a fair way yourselves, though..."

"It's been exactly ten years now."

"Then my acquaintance with him runs a little longer than yours. Ram, can you hold your liquor?"

"Not as well as I know tea, perhaps, but I'll have plenty to say about it."

"Excellent, then."

Slapping her palm against her knee as if this was exactly what she'd hoped for, Serena grinned at Ram.
It seemed they were about to plunge into drinking, with old stories as the accompaniment. And what was more, the era when he'd first met Serena included more than a few of Roswaal's own youthful indiscretions.

"Serena, I appreciate the invitation, but we're here on urgent business. As to that matter—"

"That's exactly why you want to borrow my strength, isn't it? All the more reason not to sour my mood, then. If we haven't seen each other in years, the least you can do is share a drink with me."

"...Ram."

Giving up on persuading Serena, whose ears were plainly closed to the idea, Roswaal turned to look at Ram.
Just as Roswaal had come to bring Subaru back, Ram, too, had come to the Empire to bring back her other half, Rem. If anyone could pull Serena — her attention now on her wine cup — back into a proper discussion, he thought, it might be Ram, who'd answered Serena's earlier questioning so sincerely.
But—

"Barusu aside, it's precisely for Rem's sake that Ram doesn't think turning down Serena-sama's offer would be the wise move."

"Is that really all there is to it, tho-o-ugh?"

"Of course, if Ram can also hear stories of Roswaal-sama from before you all met, then even the same delay counts as the wiser move."

Ram showed not the slightest hint of shame at combining purpose with personal benefit.
And much to Roswaal's chagrin, Ram and Serena got along exceptionally well. Their drinking talk would surely flow, and the two would no doubt hit it off.
Which meant, for Roswaal, the start of a thoroughly headache-inducing stretch of time.

"In a sense, perhaps he ought to just accept this, too, as a necessary price to pay."

All of it, so that they might swiftly accomplish their goal and leave the Empire for the Kingdom once more.
He'd been prepared to pay some price from the moment he decided to visit an old friend and ask for her help. He simply hadn't imagined it would take quite this form.

"As it happens, another guest only just left right as you two arrived, passing each other like ships. Quite the lively company, that one, so I was just about to feel rather lonely."

"Were they close acquaintances of yours as well?"

"Strictly speaking, it was that guest's husband I was close with, but yes. They should be back before long, so I'll introduce you then. For now, though—"

Rising to her feet, Serena crossed to a display cabinet in the corner of the room. When its doors opened, bottles of liquor of every size lined the inside, betraying just how much of a connoisseur she was.
Selecting one bottle from among them and bringing back three glasses from the same cabinet, Serena said, coming back—

"I first crossed paths with Roswaal when I was serving as an envoy in my father's stead. In the Kingdom I traveled to, I was targeted by an assassin... one sent by my own father, as it turned out, but that's when he saved me."

"I see. Do go on."

And so, glasses set down on the table were filled with liquor, and the telling of old stories began in earnest.
Between an intensely curious Ram and a Serena who loved to talk, the conversation flowed freely, and Roswaal, feeling rather cornered, resigned himself to it all as necessary time well spent.

"—————"

All of it, for the fulfillment of his cherished wish, and to reclaim the piece necessary to it—

—It was two days later, when word of the black-haired maiden allied with the rebel army, "Natsumi Schwarz," reached Count Dracroy's mansion, and Ram took Senior Count Serena Dracroy's person into custody, that Roswaal's plans went awry, and he found himself drawn ever deeper into the Empire's civil war.
