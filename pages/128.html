Arc 3 Chapter 51: "Entreaty"

Everything was disappearing into a world of white.

Whether his frozen body had melted, or shattered, or would remain forever trapped within a statue of ice, he couldn't say. The fate of the body left behind mattered nothing to him now.

There was only one thing he understood with any clarity. Again and again, he had repeated it, witnessing some miserable end each time, making things worse with every repetition, until at last, with these very hands, he had destroyed the one thing he'd wanted most to protect — and only then did he finally understand.

—That Natsuki Subaru was expected of by no one. Not by anyone else. Not even by himself.

The sensation of his five senses suddenly returning, senses he should have lost entirely, was not something he could ever get used to no matter how many times he experienced it.

Cold had seeped clear through to his core, until even the sensation of cold itself was lost, leaving only a feeling of loss — as though he were sinking endlessly, endlessly, into a white darkness. Then, all at once, that sense of losing everything lifted, and after a single blink, everything was back the way it had been.

Blood flowed once more through limbs that had been so thoroughly tormented; nerves that had been utterly ravaged now worked as though they'd forgotten what pain even was. The bone-piercing cold had vanished, and the sunlight was warm enough that, if he didn't roll up his sleeves, it would almost be muggy.

"——" "—!" "——ah"

Standing amid the crowd, sounds flew past from left and right, dumping a merciless workload back onto ears that should have been dead, demanding they return to service at once. Processing that meaningless noise, Subaru checked the movement of his own limbs. Both arms, once sliced clean off — fine. His right leg, once turned into a bed of spikes — fine. His left leg, once burst apart by a driven stake — fine. He planted his feet firmly on the ground, and his fists closed, hard and strong.

Everything was back to normal. Control of his lost body had returned to him, and Subaru let out a breath of relief. And more than anything else—

"Subaru-kun, what's wrong? You're spacing out."

There, beyond the counter, tilting her head, was a blue-haired girl looking at him with concern. Abandoned by everything, crushed beneath an unbearable powerlessness, despairing under the weight of a disappointment and a loss he'd brought entirely upon himself, having died a dog's meaningless death with nothing he could do about it and been dragged back—

"Rem."

"Yes, it's Rem, Subaru-kun's Rem. ...What's the matter?"

Answering the short call of her name, Rem ducked under the counter and came out of the shop. Coming to stand before Subaru, she reached a hand toward his face and touched his cheek. Knitting her brow with concern, Rem let a shadow of worry cross her fine features.

"I'm sorry, I didn't notice. You must be tired. Forgetting my own duties and losing myself in something else — Rem's a failure as an attendant."

"Tired... yeah. That's... right."

He lifted his own hand and covered Rem's, still resting on his cheek. She raised her eyebrows in surprise at the touch, but seeing Subaru's utterly haggard face and hearing his voice, she held her reaction back. Without even looking at her, though she plainly had something to say, Subaru clung to the certainty of Rem's presence in his palm — to its warmth — as if he could not afford to let it slip away.

"I dropped it, wore it down to nothing... and wore myself out, yeah."

And yet, the her he should have lost was, without question, right here.

"—Subaru-kun?"

—Subaru resolved, then and there, never to let go of the one thing in front of him ever again.

He hurried through the crowd and started down a gentle slope.

He grimaced at the dust kicked up by a dragon carriage passing right beside him, but his gaze stayed fixed ahead. Making for his destination, there was no hesitation in his stride. Come to think of it, throughout these repeating days, Subaru felt he'd done nothing but waver.

He'd wavered over what he ought to be, wavered over where Emilia's heart truly lay, wavered over the meaning of his own existence, wavered over what he needed to do to seize the best possible future, wavered lost in whirlpools of madness, wavered between life and death — and if he traced it back far enough, he was a lost soul who'd wandered lost into another world in the first place.

But Subaru, who until now couldn't even feel sure of the way he put one foot in front of the other, was pressing forward now with a clarity of purpose he'd never had before.

He finally understood. The loop, the days repeated over and over, hadn't been for nothing. The third world, the one where events had truly driven Subaru to his limit — body and soul alike — had made that clear to him.

What Subaru could do. What Subaru had to do.

"—kun!"

His gaze, cleared of all doubt, fixed on his goal, and his feet kicked off the ground beneath him with real force. His body felt light. Freed from every last weight pressing on his heart, there was, right now, nothing left in the world for Subaru to fear.

"Subaru-kun, please listen to me!"

Pulling her along by the arm, once he'd reached the bottom of the slope he made for the main street — one of the widest boulevards the royal capital had to offer. He wove his way between the throngs of people passing back and forth, pushing forward against the tide. As he came upon a road he recognized, Subaru's pace only picked up further. His impatience showed in his breathing, in his stride, in the spring of his every step.

Suddenly stepping into shade, Subaru looked up at whatever was blocking the sun.

Filling his upward view was the great wall encircling the royal capital, and, set into a gap in that wall, the capital's main gate connecting inside to outside — the characters carved above it, still unreadable to him, a combination of the Ha-script and the Ro-script, which he'd once been told read "Royal Capital of Lugunica."

Which was to say, Subaru — no, the two of them — now stood before the royal capital's main gate.

"—Su-baru-kun!"

The feet that had been trailing along behind him without much protest finally stopped, and the arm he'd been gripping was forcibly wrenched free. With that connection between them severed, even Subaru had no choice but to stop.

He came to a halt and turned around to find Rem watching him, her eyes wavering with confusion.

"What's gotten into you? What happened? If you don't explain..."

Rem's plea, her left hand resting on the right arm that had been gripped, her frame seeming, somehow, to shrink a little smaller.

Hearing her words, Subaru had to admit her confusion was only natural. From her perspective, Subaru had suddenly changed and dragged her along by force. Hurried the whole way with not one word of explanation — of course she'd be upset.

"Ah, sorry, Rem. I guess I was in a hurry about a bunch of stuff. There's, like, a lot I've had to think through, you know? So I skipped the explaining. My bad."

"Honestly, that's a problem. Rem knows you've been thinking things through, Subaru-kun, but you still need to tell her, even just a word. ...Though Rem doesn't dislike it when you're forceful."

Rem, cheeks lightly flushed, pressed both hands to her face and let out a sigh of relief.

Hearing that Subaru's voice had regained its normal cadence, its calm, she seemed to conclude that his strange behavior a moment ago had been nothing more than a small overreaction on her part.

Seeing her relief now, her earlier worry made sense.

The change in Subaru right after a Return by Death must have seemed dramatic indeed to Rem, who had only experienced a few seconds passing.

In truth, between her and Subaru lay the gap between "a few seconds" and "several days" worth of accumulated experience.

What's more, Subaru had piled up those "several days" three times over to arrive at this present moment. And looking back on it calmly, the depression roiling inside him during this stretch of time must have been, even by his own reckoning, something he'd rather have looked away from.

He'd made a fool of himself at the Royal Selection, been half-killed by Julius at the training grounds, opened up a fatal rift between himself and Emilia, and been left behind in the royal capital, losing all sense of why he existed.

All while spending idle time at Crusch's mansion, endlessly, fruitlessly, searching for an answer he could never find, to what he could do, what he had to do.

Then, all of Subaru's hesitation had abruptly cleared — literally, in the space of a single blink. To Rem, it must have seemed a bolt from the blue if ever there was one.

"Sorry for worrying you. I'm fine now. Feels like all I've shown you lately is pathetic stuff, getting knocked down and knocking myself down, but I finally get it now."

"No. Thinking about you isn't any trouble for Rem, Subaru-kun. ...You finally get it?"

Rem answered in a voice that seemed to lift, matching Subaru, who spoke with eyes free of all doubt now.

It had truly been a long time since they'd shared an exchange like this, and Subaru couldn't hide the small joy welling up inside him.

And to the question that followed on the tail of her words, Subaru nodded, "Yeah,"

"I agonized over it, went off the rails, caused trouble left and right for everybody, and honestly I'm feeling like the world's biggest sorry right now, but I finally figured out how to wrap it all up neatly. No, actually, thinking back on it, it was there in plain sight from the start, and I bet somebody even told me... but I'm bad at giving up, you know."

"Rem thinks that's one of your wonderful qualities, Subaru-kun..."

Subaru gave a wry smile at Rem's words, trailing off a little.

Then he looked up at the sky, drinking in its breadth and its height deep into his eyes. For the world that had gone on watching over him from above, unchanging all this while, it must have been a maddeningly frustrating stretch of time.

But now, at last, he could put an end to that painful stretch of time too.

The answer had been right in front of him this whole time.

No matter where Subaru headed, no matter what reckless thing he attempted, no matter how far into folly he ran, it had followed at his back without a word of complaint.

That's right—

"I've decided, Rem."

He looked straight into the eyes of the girl standing close enough to touch.

Her blue hair, falling to her shoulders, stirred in the wind, and Subaru's reflection showed clear in her transparent eyes.

The black-based apron dress wrapping her small frame bore not a single wasted ornament, embodying her upright, noble nature. The white pleated cap crowning her blue hair sat crisp and resolute, delicately framing the lovely, neatly composed features of the girl beneath it.

"Yes, Subaru-kun?"

Her pink lips curved into a smile, her narrowed eyes drawing a gentle arc.

The sweetness of her voice was so full of tenderness it threatened to melt his mind, seeping endlessly, seductively, into Subaru as he prepared to speak just a single line.

"First, let's rent a dragon carriage. The counter's a little crowded because of all the Royal Selection chaos, but if even past noon tomorrow would still work, then there ought to be more openings right now."

One that's fast, with plenty of stamina — that'd be ideal.

He needed one that could keep running. Day or night, briskly, without ever needing to rest.

"A dragon carriage...?"

Rem tilted her head, repeating the word Subaru had said.

What showed in her eyes was bewilderment; she likely couldn't see where the conclusion of Subaru's story — one that was rushing ahead a little too fast — was headed.

But Subaru, acting as though he hadn't noticed her confusion at all, gestured toward the gate.

"Picking out the carriage might eat up some time, so maybe we should use whatever's left over to stock up on food and stuff. Oh, but even for travel rations, I can't stand the crumbly, dry kind. I'd rather just drink water than eat that stuff."

Back in compulsory schooling, there'd been a class under the banner of "hands-on learning" where they actually had you eat preserved food and simple field rations, but as far as Subaru was concerned, both had earned an unhappy "no thanks" rating — a memory he wasn't fond of.

If that was the case even in his original world, with all its developed seasonings, then the reliability of preserved food's flavor in this world was surely even lower.

"No, wait, actually, there's a slight, teensy chance that some magical force is at work and preserved food actually tastes great here...!? I mean, I managed to make mayonnaise, so maybe the whole 'try it before you knock it' spirit is alive and well after all..."

"Um, Subaru-kun?"

"Hm, oh, sorry, my thoughts went barreling off in a weird direction there. What's up?"

Correcting the conversation that had begun to veer off track, Subaru shaped his mouth into a gentle smile and looked at Rem, even bending slightly at the waist to bring his eyes level with the girl before him.

At Subaru's attentive manner, Rem fell briefly silent, and then, lips trembling as though shaking off her own hesitation, said—

"It's just — I'm sorry. Because Rem isn't quick enough on the uptake, she doesn't understand what you're trying to do, Subaru-kun. Um, what are you...?"

"Ah! Right, my bad! Sorry, I totally didn't notice! No, I was completely in my own head already talking through the plan like you knew what it was — embarrassing!"

Slapping his knee, Subaru laughed off his own blunder without a trace of self-consciousness.

And after having a good laugh at his own carelessness for a while—

"Things I was made to realize, and things I realized on my own — well, through all kinds of twists and turns I ended up here, but the answer's been staring me in the face for a long time now."

A wry smile — a truly wry smile — crossed his face.

He'd tasted bitter defeat. He'd chewed on regret. He'd wept over injustice and impermanence, been toyed with by a cruel fate, and been smeared with his own blood and the blood of others, ground into the dirt.

He understood the answer to all of it now, clearly.

"Rem."

Calling the girl's name, Subaru slowly reached out a hand to her.

Rem looked at the offered hand, waiting for Subaru's next words. As though answering her posture, Subaru put the feeling welling up inside him onto his tongue—

"Run away with me. As far as we can go."

He had, in plain terms, declared his defeat before fate.

"...eh?"

Whether it was that she didn't understand the meaning of what she'd just been told, all that escaped Rem's throat was a faint, rasping breath.

Thinking her reaction only natural, Subaru shook his head and continued—

"From here, I'm leaving the royal capital and heading way out west... or maybe north. Apparently we can't cross into the empire to the south, so it's one or the other. I'm not great with cold, so personally I'm leaning west."

"Um, no, that's..."

"Honestly, the distance and stuff is pretty fuzzy to me, and this is jumping the gun about as much as it gets, so it won't be an easy trip, I bet. Oh, wait, actually, what do we even do if there's no way to return the carriage? Would it be better to just buy one outright instead of renting from a carriage shop?"

He figured it was probably some kind of rental-car-like system, but the finer details were something Subaru, who'd never reached driving age back home, was fuzzy on. He hadn't been there the last time Rem rented a carriage, either, so he had no idea what kind of paperwork was involved.

He doubted the shop was running a business without accounting for the possibility of someone just driving off and never coming back, so presumably some sort of terms were worked out to cover that — but—

"W-wait, please!"

Rem called a halt to Subaru as he sank deep into thought.

She held her palm out toward him, an unusual look of impatience showing on her face.

"Run away... what, um, what do you mean by that? The way you're talking, Subaru-kun, it sounds like you're about to leave for some country that isn't Lugunica..."

She voiced her guess in a tone that couldn't hide her half-belief, half-doubt, and then Rem let out a small "ah" and her expression brightened just slightly.

"Could it be — you've thought of something amazing again, haven't you? Something that would help Emilia-sama, or Roswaal-sama..."

"There's nothing like that, Rem."

"Eh—"

As though clinging to hope. As though wishing that the true meaning of Subaru's words might really be that, Rem had painted a picture of the future she believed in far too completely.

But Subaru quietly shook his head at that, and clearly voiced his denial.

"I told you, we're running away. Even staying in the royal capital, there's nothing I can do. And going back to the mansion wouldn't change how powerless I am, either. —That's what I finally understood."

Powerlessness, emptiness, the sheer unreasonableness of the world — all of it bore down heavily on Subaru's body.

But the more he'd tried to deny it, the tighter it had wound around him — and once he'd simply admitted it was an immovable fact, how light his body felt now.

"So run away with me, Rem. Staying here is no good. Everybody — every single one of them — kept telling me that. I didn't want to admit it, kept desperately denying it, but... yeah, that's right. I was weak. Nobody ever needed me. Not really."

He thought he must have been conceited.

He'd been mistaken. Misunderstood things, gotten carried away with himself.

He'd fallen into another world, been granted a little power to redo fate, and because things had gone well by luck a couple of times, he'd started to feel like he'd actually saved someone — and that was the mistake.

When he'd never had the strength, the resolve, or even the right to save anyone at all.

"That's not..."

"Yeah, it is. I was told, straight to my face. —Told, over and over."

That someone like him wasn't needed.

In the first world, Subaru had ignored Emilia's wish and stormed out of Crusch's mansion. He hadn't listened to Rem's words trying to stop him, and the result he'd invited was that catastrophe.

In the second world, without ever paying the price for creating that outcome, he'd run from reality until, in the end, Rem's life had been snuffed out in the most horrific way — and once again, he'd saved no one.

The third world had produced the most despicable result of all. He'd dragged a traveling merchant into it along the way, had Rem swallowed by the White Whale, and taken Emilia's life with his own two hands. The Witch Cult had probably been wiped out to the last member by Puck, but if Puck had turned that same fury on the whole country after Subaru's death, the damage would be beyond anything that had come before.

Going back even further, to days that truly could never be redone, at the Royal Selection in the royal castle — even there, hadn't he failed to be of any help to Emilia at all?

Far from standing at her side, he'd butted in beside her and only dragged her down, and rather than making up for it, he'd ended up thoroughly beaten down by another candidate's knight. And on top of it all, he'd had a falling-out with Emilia, hurling at her words he'd never meant to say, wounding her.

"...Ku-ha."

He felt a dry laugh crack open in his throat.

Looking back on it, it was a masterpiece. Calmly reviewing his own actions, his own thoughts, he could feel with painful clarity just how thoroughly he'd been a jinx to everyone around him.

—He'd wanted to be of use to Emilia? He figured he, of all people, could save someone? He was certain everyone would fall apart without him?

What delusional nonsense. What a misunderstanding. Ah, what arrogance.

All Subaru had actually done was worsen Emilia's standing, betray on a grand scale and wound the heart of a woman who'd been considerate enough to worry about him, and get Rem, who'd stayed loyally by his side, killed every single time — nothing but a string of foolish acts.

Amazing — everyone must have known it would turn out this way.

That's why they'd all told Subaru to behave. To do nothing. That his power wasn't needed, that he should step back, that he should just disappear — that's what they were saying.

Everyone around him who'd told him so understood the future far better than he did. Completely unlike Subaru, who couldn't do anything, couldn't understand, couldn't grasp it, even though he supposedly knew.

Was it possible — that everyone else was the one looping, not him?

"If not, then... who else would make a fool of themselves the way I have?"

He was pathetic. There was no limit to how pitiful, how hopeless, he felt about himself.

A clown is a clown only because he braces himself to be laughed at and plays the part accordingly. Which meant Subaru, who hadn't even realized the audience was pointing at him and laughing, wasn't even a clown. —Just a hopeless fool, and nothing more.

"So I've decided to disappear. That should be fine — it is fine. No matter what someone like me does, all that happens is one more corpse... or, depending, even more than that."

A corpse, a corpse, a corpse, a corpse, a corpse.

The corpse of some stranger. The corpse of someone he knew. The corpse of someone precious. The corpse of someone important. The corpse of someone he'd trusted. The corpse of someone he'd wanted to trust. —The corpse of someone he loved.

He was sick of it. Utterly sick of it.

Why did he have to go through this? He'd suffered this much — surely he deserved some kind of reward. Even Subaru understood that things like effort always being rewarded, or any goal being achievable if you just set your sights on it, were nothing but fairy tales.

But even so — even so, was it wrong to wish, in even the smallest way, to avoid the very worst?

"Let's run, Rem. Neither of us — you or me — can stay here... in this country."

Throwing everything aside, treating it all with contempt, Subaru had made up his mind to run.

And into that flight, that decision certain to earn him scorn from every direction, he meant to bring the girl before him — Rem, and Rem alone — along with him.

There was one thing, even amid throwing everything, every last bit of it, away, that he could not let go of.

Solitude was frightening. Solitude was terrifying. In this vast world, in a world of darkness where he understood nothing, even knowing that doing so was the right answer, the fear wouldn't allow him to cast off all protection and run alone.

So he'd held out hope that she alone, Rem alone, might come with him.

Through all the repeated days until now, she alone had stayed by Subaru's side the whole while. She'd watched, together with him, every disgraceful display, every shameful word and deed, every misguided way he'd lived.

Precisely because it was her, Subaru believed she was worth staking everything on, in the end.

Across the repeated time, three times, in every single world, Subaru had let Rem die.

To keep her from dying, he must not return to the mansion. Whether she reached the mansion or died somewhere along the way, the ending was the same: her life scattered in some horrific fashion.

Then would it be enough to keep her held back in the royal capital? No, that wouldn't work either. Even if she spent her time in the capital, once word of the trouble at Roswaal Manor reached Crusch's mansion, she would fly out of the royal capital to save her domain.

And surely, once she'd made up her mind to that, Subaru would have no way to stop her. He alone would be left behind at Crusch's mansion, only to be told how it had all ended after the fact.

He could see it with perfect clarity — himself, having lost everything, left hollow and empty.

To keep Rem from dying, Rem must be allowed to know nothing.

If he truly wanted to save Rem, he could not stay here.

"Even if you suddenly say something like that, Rem can't..."

At Subaru's plea, Rem gave a small shake of her head.

It looked like a gesture of refusal, but in truth it expressed her own hesitation.

Subaru's reasoning was simply too abrupt for her to take in. More than anything, the amount of information he'd offered her, as material to judge by, was far too little.

There was no way she could simply nod along to being suddenly told to abandon everything and run.

And yet, even understanding how unreasonable it was, Subaru still had to make Rem understand and agree.

He no longer even knew what the limit was on how much information he could safely offer.

It felt as though whatever he said might run afoul of the Witch's Curse.

Whatever action he took, he had a premonition it would become another sacrifice to some unreasonable fate.

He was out of moves. Boxed in on every side. Trapped in a dead end of fate.

So the only move left to Subaru was to beg. To appeal, single-mindedly, to her conscience. Even knowing full well how thoroughly cowardly that was — even knowing he was exploiting the dependence Rem felt toward him.

"There's no time. I really am sorry this is so sudden. Really, truly. I feel awful about it, from the bottom of my heart. ...But, please, choose."

"Choose..."

"Me, or not me... choose."

With so little information given, and all of it sprung on her so suddenly, he hated his own cowardice for forcing such a choice on her, hated the urgency of the situation itself.

But it was also true that giving Rem time to think it over slowly worked against him. Subaru couldn't honestly say he wasn't using this situation to his own advantage.

With her time to think limited, and Subaru — the object of her dependence — right in front of her, pleading, what decision would Rem make? He had — he thought he had — a chance of winning.

A chance of winning — or perhaps it was closer to a desperate, cherished hope.

The selfish hope that Rem — she alone — might forgive Subaru's degradation, his flight from everything.

"Once we get a carriage, let's head west. Leave Lugunica, go way out west... Kararagi, was it? We'll buy some little house there and live together, the two of us."

Speaking quickly, Subaru began describing the future he pictured.

An ordinary, peaceful future, one surely untouched by unreason or cruelty.

"I feel bad about dipping into Roswaal's travel funds, but if it comes to that, we can just call it a loan and pay it back someday. Once we've got a place to live sorted out, I'll get a real job, I promise. ...Never actually worked before, but I'm sure it'll be fine."

Less than a high school graduate, and a shut-in on top of it.

An indoor type through and through, with not even a part-time job to his name. As for work since coming to this other world, the most he'd managed was an apprentice's chores at the mansion — practically a child's first lesson in labor.

Landing a proper job would surely be next to impossible, but he'd sink his teeth in and get one anyway. Compared to pain, to suffering, to dying, it was all downright easy.

The more he thought about it, the more Subaru's future opened up before him.

Thinking of all those days he'd struggled and flailed toward just one single future, only to drag down the worst possible calamity every time — how happy this felt, by comparison.

"Even if it's humble, as long as you're there, I know I can do my best. Just having someone waiting at home with a smile — no matter how exhausted I get, if I can just think that Rem's waiting for me, I know I'll...!"

Even if, wherever they fled to, he ended up pointed at and scorned by everyone he'd left behind, he felt sure he could bear it, as long as Rem was there beside him.

So please — please, there's no wish beyond this one—

"Choose me...!"

He pleaded, the words wrung out of him, his hand still held out.

"If you choose me, everything I am is yours. My whole life, all of it, belongs to you. I'll devote myself to you, live only for you... so."

He couldn't look at Rem's face, standing right in front of him, now.

He had no courage left to see what expression she wore. Not a scrap of courage remained in him. If he'd had any, surely he'd have reached a very different ending than this.

Because there was nothing left in him now — a coward, a cheat, a pitiful excuse for a man.

"Run away with me... live, with me...!"

He begged, from the very bottom of his heart, that she alone must not die.

Having poured out the full measure of his feelings in a voice parched dry, Subaru's heart raced and his breath came ragged.

An exhaustion like he'd just sprinted flat out, along with a battered, ravaged mental fatigue, assaulted Subaru fiercely. And then, bearing down on him for having done all this, came a silence.

Rem gave no answer.

The noise of the crowd felt distant now, and how the people around them might be viewing this conversation happening between the two of them in public — such a trivial thing didn't so much as enter his awareness.

Unable to bear it any longer, Subaru cracked open the eyes he'd been squeezing tightly shut, and looked to gauge the expression on Rem's face before him.

Bracing himself against the fear that her answer might already be written there in that expression.

"——"

Silent, not even noticing that Subaru was watching her, Rem held her lips pressed firmly together.

Her face seemed to be straining for blankness, and yet a faint strain showed between her brows and at the corners of her eyes — she couldn't quite hold her usual composure.

He could tell that hesitation, confusion, and reluctance were all swirling within her.

The words Subaru had just spoken, all of them, were shaking Rem's heart — greatly, powerfully, violently.

The silence stretched on.

A stretch of time that might as well have been eternity pressed a slow-burning impatience against Subaru's back, like a fire being lit.

But at last, eventually, even that time came to an end.

"—Subaru-kun."

A gentle sound, full of tender affection, called out Subaru's name.

The moment he heard that tone, that tremor in her voice, Subaru felt certain his plea had reached her.

She would accept him. She would forgive Subaru's weakness, and take the whole of it — the whole of the man called Natsuki Subaru — into her arms.

A flood of emotion welled up in him. For the first time, he felt as though he'd finally been rewarded.

And Subaru lifted his face—

"Rem cannot run away with you, Subaru-kun."

His wish was clearly, plainly refused, by a Rem whose face was etched with terrible sadness.

"Because—"

"——"

"Talk about the future has to be told with a smile, doesn't it?"

With an expression somewhere between tears and laughter, Rem spoke the very words Subaru himself had once said to her.
