Arc 4 Chapter 112: "Instinct That Refuses Weakness"

The battle raged with a ferocity beyond imagining.

Two men faced each other, fists crossing head-on. Flesh struck against hard bone, muscle burst and blood dripped. Groans of pain rose; ragged breath mixed with froth spilled out in ugly sound, searing the raw, muddy brutality of the brawl into the eyes and ears of anyone watching.

"...Subaru."

Standing before the entrance to the tomb, watching over the fight, Emilia pressed a hand to her chest and called the man's name. Her eyes wavered with confusion, her fingers trembling as though groping for something to cling to, however uncertain. The warmth of the reliable presence that had always been at her side—she knew it was gone, and still it went on binding her heart.

There were words Subaru had spoken to her, inside the tomb. He'd declared, flatly, that he liked her, that she was precious to him, and that was why he believed in her. His words had, in truth, saved some part of her. But at the same time, they had deepened her unease.

Even now, moment by moment, the true memories within her were reviving. Believing the wrong memories to be her beginning, Emilia had walked this far. If the beginning that had set her on this path were to change—what would become of her?

Even now, after they had thrown their true feelings at each other almost like an argument, the unease remained rooted inside Emilia. Subaru had shouted that even if Emilia changed, his feelings for her would not. A boy willing to fight to the end for her sake, no matter how wounded, no matter how much he suffered—she had not the slightest doubt about his feelings. He had gone on proving as much, again and again.

What I can't trust—is myself.

Having walked this road with the wrong beginning, her feet had stopped on a path where she should have had the ending in clear sight. Now, wondering if she had made a mistake in how she'd set out, she hesitated even as she looked toward how it would end.

Her feet had stopped, once. Was she even allowed to start walking again? And if she did—would it be a new road she walked, or the same one as before?

"——"

She wandered on and on through a labyrinth of questions with no answer, no exit. Even now, unable to raise her voice, understanding only that she had no right to stop them, Emilia kept her eyes fixed on the fight between the men before her.

"——!"

A groan rose, and blood sprayed, higher than before.

A fist cut the wind, swung through to the end. The knuckles bearing down on his face—sadly, pitifully slow.

"Tch!"

No need for an exaggerated dodge. With the barest tilt of his head, he slipped the punch without so much as grazing it. Into the wide-open torso of the man thrown off balance by his own overswing, he drove his knee up sharply, gouging into the organs beneath. He felt the thin muscle and ribs against his knee, the shock traveling on into the guts behind them. From a mouth that had already emptied its contents more than once, fresh bloody bile spilled out under the merciless force.

"Guh—ah—"

Clutching his stomach, staggering, a pitiful sight. Garfiel brought both raised arms down on the hunched back, then—having attacked from the front—rattled the innards again from behind. A body that had held itself up through the knee strike couldn't withstand the blow from behind and simply toppled. He kicked the fallen body over onto its back and drove his toes into the left flank for another blow.

Intermittent groans. The dissonant rasp of ragged breath mixed with bloody phlegm and vomit. Fist, knee, toe. With every strike the sensation of skin, flesh, bone being ground away came rebounding back up his limbs.

What would normally bring him a surge of exhilaration was, now, nothing but deeply unpleasant. Over and over, this act repeated—what possible meaning could there be in it?

"Just give it up already, will ya!"

He hurled abuse at the pathetic figure coughing, spitting up bloody phlegm. Consciousness hadn't been stolen from him yet. Strike his head, kick his stomach—infuriatingly, that alone he wouldn't surrender. Even with this much of a gap in strength, even after making the body understand it.

"You're a hardheaded bastard, ain'tcha! You never had a chance from the start! Sloppy stance! Wasted swings! Your whole damn center's off—it ain't even worth watchin'!"

"...Yeah. My bad."

"Yeah, damn right it's bad! You got any idea how it feels bein' dragged into your pointless stubbornness and playtime, whether I like it or not? Cut the crap. Cut the goddamn crap."

Spitting, he hurled abuse at Subaru, who—breath ragged—tried to push himself up. Before the raised voice of Garfiel, Subaru braced both arms against the ground, struggling to rise. His face, battered again and again, had swollen; a nosebleed stained the lower half of it. Some of his teeth must have broken or chipped by now. It wouldn't be strange if his organs, his ribs, several things inside him were already wrecked.

Agony should be raging through his whole body, to the point where holding onto consciousness itself should be near impossible.

"What's the point?! You came at me 'cause you thought you had a chance, didn'tcha?! And now you're just—showin' off this beat-up, wrecked, pathetic mess I can't even stand to look at... just lie the hell down already!"

"—Guh!"

Sensing something like obsession, beyond what words like stubbornness or willfulness could capture, Garfiel went on relentlessly punishing Subaru's body. He knocked away the arm braced on the ground and drove his toes into the side of the face as the body collapsed. He kicked the rolling, blood-spitting body up from below, then drove an elbow straight down into it as it floated into the air.

Slammed into the ground, his whole body pounded against the hard earth, Subaru sprawled spread-eagled. Eyes rolled white, breath escaping like air leaking out, the body finally stopped moving—certain at last that he'd taken consciousness from him, Garfiel let out a long breath.

"Now what, makin' a face like it's over already..."

"——!"

Garfiel, shoulders heaving with ragged breath, went rigid, eyes wide, at the voice from below. Before the frozen Garfiel, the man whose consciousness he was sure he'd severed at the root was rising, upper body swaying, back onto his feet.

"You gotta be... jokin' with me..."

"Ah... no joke... here. Get beaten this much and... it ain't even funny enough to laugh off..."

"That ain't what I—"

"—Raaah!"

With a tenacity like something out of a nightmare, Subaru let out a sharp breath and swung his fist through. Of course, even that all-out effort was, to Garfiel, no more than a child's play-swing. He lightly brushed the fist aside to divert its course and, in return, drove his palm straight into Subaru's gut. A blow that pierced the gaps between organs, sending shock rippling through the fluid inside the body—wringing the already-empty stomach, forcing yellowish bile and blood to surge back up.

"K-kuh... hah..."

"Same result no matter how many times you try it, how many times I gotta say it before it sinks—"

He delivered his renewed demand for surrender to Subaru, who collapsed to the ground clutching his stomach. Unable to bear watching such a pathetic display, Garfiel looked away and even clicked his tongue.

"——!"

Past the tip of his nose, Subaru's right fist—for he had not fallen after all—whistled through.

"Wha—?!"

"Hey, Garfiel. Who gave you permission to write me off on your own...?"

Left arm forward, right arm cocked back. At the sight of Subaru still holding a stance that hadn't lost its fighting spirit, Garfiel felt something close to a chill.

Not that he thought, even for an instant, that it posed any threat. It was plain to anyone's eyes that the man was doing nothing but putting up a bluff, and the gulf in raw power separating Garfiel from Subaru could not be closed no matter how he struggled. Even if Subaru went on recklessly throwing himself forward like this, a thousand times, ten thousand times, there was a rock-solid gap in raw strength between them—enough that Garfiel could simply keep dodging forever.

Which meant every last bit of Subaru's struggling was, absolutely all of it, meaningless. No matter how much pain and suffering piled up on that body—struck, kicked, thrown by Garfiel—a body running out of strength would never, ever reach Garfiel.

"You son of a bitch, don't you dare mess around with me...!"

"Ah...?"

Against an opponent he could not beat, the man still rose, armed with nothing but obstinacy. Only the heart refuses to break; only the spirit refuses to lose. Perhaps people sometimes call that quality strength—a strength not dependent on the body. Perhaps there were those whose hearts would be stirred by a figure that rose again and again, no matter how many times it fell.

But if the man was putting on this display hoping it would make Garfiel change his heart—that was an insult beyond measure.

Only the heart doesn't break, doesn't bend. What meaning could there possibly be in that?

"You think if you just keep puttin' up a front like that, even without winnin', I'll eventually pull my fists back on my own? Think I'll get sick of hittin' and kickin', admit your damn spirit, and back off... Think I'll say I lost 'cause I got soft on you, huh?! Is that what you think?!"

"——"

"Don't friggin' joke with me. You and me standin' here like this ain't no game! I beat you down, or you get beat down. There ain't no other way this ends!"

He stamped the ground. The power of his Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits surged up, vitality flowing through the earth into his body. Even more than when he'd pushed his exhausted body here after finishing the fight with Ram and the others in the forest, his body had recovered further. A fight that didn't even rise to the level of a brawl with Subaru did nothing to tire him—it was no different from sitting and resting in the shade of a tree.

Such a puny thing could never shake Garfiel's heart, not even slightly.

"You said it yourself, didn'tcha?! You said you'd teach me I'm weak, didn'tcha?! Well, look at yourself now! What's weak?! What's 'teach you'?! You're the one gettin' pinned down for lackin' power, the thoughtless damn fool—that's you!"

He remembered every word, every syllable, of the taunt Subaru had thrown before the fight began. All of it was idiotic. All of it was a lie. Everything about this man was one big con.

"That brother and Ram, both! The two who came at me for a fight, both of 'em had real spirit—wantin' to actually beat me! That's exactly why I went all out against those two. Twisted my head, worked out a plan, poured everything I had of brains into makin' up for what I lacked in strength... Those two, I could genuinely respect their fighting spirit. What about you, huh?!"

He didn't know all the details, but Otto, who'd turned the forest into an ally through his Divine Protection and driven Garfiel into a corner—the way he'd forced things all the way to that final great spell, pouring out every last bit of will and wit—was, to Garfiel, a fight he wanted to praise as the best a weakling could possibly manage.

And Ram's offensive, which had begun with that ambush afterward—though it had dealt Garfiel's heart a pain equal to betrayal—the fight itself had been, as expected of her, genuinely formidable. A battle against a true, ruthless, unrestrained strength.

Both of them had thrown everything they had against Garfiel, trying to wrench victory from him. That way of living was noble. Noble enough that even as his enemy, Garfiel couldn't help admiring them.

"And compared to that... you're nothin' but pathetic. Gettin' beat down, gettin' beat down, and gettin' up again—so what? No matter how much you get hit, your heart just won't give—so what?! Refuse to run even from a fight you can't win—what's so damn admirable about that?!"

"——"

"You think that's supposed to move me? You think—covered in blood, legs shaking, can't even open your eyes right—and I'm supposed to be so moved I back off?! I'll turn you into meat, you bastard... how much are you makin' a mockery of me, and of the ones who stood and fought for real?"

Right now, Garfiel's heart burned with the greatest fury he had ever felt in his life. The fight had been defiled. He himself had been insulted. And not only that—the nobility, the purity of resolve, of those he had come to respect within that fight—this man's very way of being was trying to smear even that in the filth of humiliation.

"Lie down. Get outta the way. You pathetic, miserable, shit-shit-shittiest excuse for a man. The smartest thing you can do is admit you lost right now and go to sleep, you idiot!"

"............"

Without dropping his stance, Subaru went on taking Garfiel's unbroken stream of abuse. His head swayed unsteadily, his half-closed eyes somehow still fixed on Garfiel. There was no sign the fighting spirit in him had withered—and that was precisely why this stubborn refusal to give in was so unbearable to look at.

If this much—words, fists—couldn't bend his heart, then how was he supposed to break it? If pain wasn't enough, there was only one answer.

"Why don't YOU say it to him, huh?!"

Turning, Garfiel called out to Emilia, standing near the entrance of the tomb, watching. Seeing Emilia's slender shoulders flinch at suddenly being singled out, Garfiel clicked his tongue in irritation at the weakness he saw in her too.

"Say it yourself—tell him you can't stand to watch! No matter how much I say it, he ain't listenin'. Tell him yourself, from the mouth of the woman he's in love with—tell him it's pathetic, it's uncool, it don't mean a damn thing no matter how hard he tries, and that this whole 'kindness' act is shit!"

"...I—I..."

"What, huh?! Can't say it?! You still think he's got a chance, lookin' at him like that? Or is it that you WANT to watch? Wanna sit there soakin' in the illusion that you're loved, watchin' a guy who's in love with you turn himself into a bloody wreck for your sake? Which is it, huh?!"

"——!"

Eyes wide, Emilia froze under the barrage of malice hurled at her. Garfiel's unbearable words pierced without mercy into Emilia, who had been watching the fight from the sidelines.

If it couldn't be stopped by Garfiel's fists, then it had to be stopped by Emilia's words instead. If Garfiel broke his body, and Emilia broke his heart, then this time Subaru would surely fall to his knees.

Watching over the fight, Emilia had again and again twisted her face in anguish at the sight of Subaru being struck. Unlike Subaru, who had steeled himself with some resolve she couldn't even comprehend, Emilia's heart had settled on nothing at all. She was still the girl who had lost to the Trial in the tomb, still weeping helplessly.

Garfiel had no intention of blaming her especially harshly for that. Being struck down by the Trial, by the past—of course that could happen. Who could possibly deny another's memory of their own deepest regret, or say why they should?

That the past, that regret, could be overcome—that was nonsense, plain and simple.

An achievable ideal and an unreachable fantasy, jumbled together by a madman. The boy still standing before them was a true lunatic, chasing nothing but fantasy, pushing it presumptuously onto others, forcing it on them. The same breed as Roswaal—a colossal fool who could see only one single thing.

"Stop him. Do that, and it's all over—for both of us! We're both just bein' danced around in the palm of some shitty Witch's hand. That's all this is."

"I—"

At Garfiel's words, Emilia straightened her back as if struck by lightning, eyes wide. Her strange, beautiful eyes, faintly wet with tears, turned toward Subaru, standing there in his anguished refusal to fall. Her trembling lips, guided by Garfiel's urging, moved to call out and stop Subaru's stubbornness. With that, everything would end. But—

"Emilia."

"——"

Before Emilia could speak, it was Subaru who called her name first. Her mouth, where the red tip of her tongue had shown through, closed, and Emilia strained to listen to Subaru's hoarse voice. Straining not to miss a single word, whatever it might be. To her, Subaru offered just a single line.

"...Watch me."

A short line. A quiet voice, murmured almost only within his own mouth. But hearing it, Emilia lifted her face with a start, and after several seconds of hesitation—

"...Okay."

She nodded, hand pressed to her chest.

"...Hah?!"

Faced with this exchange between the two of them, Garfiel raised his voice in incomprehension. But against Garfiel, eyes wide with fury, Subaru—letting out a long breath—pointed a finger at him.

"...You've been running your mouth this whole time, but you've got it wrong, Garfiel."

"The hell'd you say?"

"Maybe to you it looks like I'm bustin' my ass in a fight I can't win, like some kind of idiot... but no way. Fighting when I already know from the start I can't win—I got burned real bad doing that once before, and I learned my lesson big time. Never again. Not ever."

His face a swollen, battered mess, Subaru nonetheless grimaced with distaste. What exactly that "once before" he mentioned referred to, Garfiel had no way of knowing—but it must be tied to some memory that filled Subaru with something close to hatred. Still, right now, there was something more pressing than that thought to dwell on.

"You're not making a lick of sense. Forget ideals, forget fantasies—you can't even see what's right in front of you. If you'd given up on challengin' an opponent you can't beat... then what the hell is THIS, huh?! What the hell IS this?!"

"Obviously, idiot. I haven't given up on this fight. Not even once."

To the howling Garfiel, Subaru—his voice regaining strength as his mind cleared over the course of talking—declared it flatly. At the inexplicable force packed into those words, fury momentarily choked Garfiel's throat.

"Until I can't stand anymore, I'm not giving up this fight. ...And the moment I can't stand anymore won't come until you kill me."

"............"

"And you, backing off at the decisive moment like you always do—you can't kill me. ...Which means you can't stop me. Bit by bit, I'm getting closer to winning. My win isn't going anywhere."

"That's outta your damn mind...! Whether I can kill you or not ain't the point. How the hell are YOU gonna do it?! However you struggle, how the hell you gonna take me down, huh?!"

Battered to the point of being little more than a rag, no matter how many words Subaru piled up, it was nothing but armchair theory. Subaru's argument was pure extremism. "I won't stop unless I'm killed" was nothing but a statement of intent. Even granting that premise, did it mean that as long as he wasn't killed, Subaru would keep moving and eventually manage to land a fatal blow on Garfiel?

Something like that couldn't happen. And even if it could, how much time, how many miracles stacked one on another, would it take to get there?

"I'll just smash your arms and legs, make it so you can't do a damn thing—and that's the end of it! Whether you're conscious or not, whether you got the will to win or not—none of that matters!"

Garfiel roared, raging. With the vitality drawn up from the earth through the soles of his feet, his exhausted body had recovered to roughly half its usual strength. More than enough to tear Natsuki Subaru apart.

He stamped the ground and closed in on Subaru, who stood there stock-still. As Garfiel approached, Subaru, as if seizing the opportunity, thrust out a fist. Far too foolish. Far too slow. Everything about it fell short. Garfiel dodged with ease and landed one blow to the torso. He drove a knee into the dropping chin, grabbed the reeling body, and hurled it down onto the ground with full force. He stamped a heel down onto the body as it fell onto its back, once, twice, three times, wringing out groans of pain. A relentless barrage of strikes. With this—

"...Is it over?"

"—Guh?! You... what the hell IS with you?!"

Subaru, who should have taken even more severe damage to his body, rose to his feet again. Faced with that sight, an unfamiliar, indefinable sensation began, at last, to take hold in Garfiel's chest.

It wasn't the anxiety of thinking he couldn't defeat him, couldn't win. It was suspicion—that maybe, exactly as this man claimed, simply piling up physical damage really wouldn't be enough to stop him.

"Puttin' your life on the line for somethin' like this—what the hell's the point?! You think if you scrape by somehow and manage to beat me, that half-demon can beat the Trial?! You actually believe somethin' like that could happen, huh?!"

"............"

"You think miracles like that just stack up?! You think somethin' that convenient just happens?! Even in the one-in-a-million, one-in-a-hundred-million chance you actually beat me, that woman ain't gonna change one bit! Everyone's the same! Someone who screwed up, who's stuck carryin' a past they can't do a damn thing about... someone who's spent their whole time starin' at regret they've stewed over and over—there ain't nothin' forward OR backward left for 'em! Why can't you get that?!"

"You're the one who doesn't get it—!"

"——?!"

Lacking the force to be called a roar, too emotional to be called a plea— In the gap where Garfiel lost the thread of his own words, Natsuki Subaru's shout cut in.

"Don't go decidin' everything on your own, Garfiel...!"

"Wha—what're you talkin'—"

"Don't you dare decide Emilia's limits for her. She's not that weak."

Far off, at the entrance of the tomb, Emilia caught her breath.

"And don't you dare decide MY limits and write me off, either. Whether I drop to my knees, whether I throw everything away and curl up on the ground—that's not for anyone else to say. Not gonna happen. Not until I'M the one who gives up."

Spitting blood, the light in Subaru's eyes grew fiercer. And then—

"You—who the hell gave you the right to give up on yourself? You can still fight. You can still push further. ...Some hardheaded conclusion you reached back when you were a kid, arms and legs not even fully grown, not even old enough to have hair down there yet—how much longer you gonna cling to that?!"

The conviction Garfiel had stubbornly clung to, the belief that had bound his heart to the Sanctuary all this while—Subaru declared, right to his face, that it was worthless.

"——"

Garfiel opened his mouth to fire back at once. But as if something had struck him in the chest, no voice came from his throat.

He couldn't say a word. Nothing came out. His mind went blank. It wasn't that he thought this man was right. There was no way he himself was wrong. Someone who had known he was wrong and started from there—there was no way he was wrong.

He must not be wrong. So this man, who claimed Garfiel was wrong, could not be allowed to remain.

"Haah... haah... fine..."

"............"

"I gotta stop you. Not one damn word you're sayin' makes any sense. And yet it makes my stomach turn. So I'll stop you."

He had to stop him. And the means to stop him could only be, in the end, exactly the means this man himself had described.

Unless the breath is stopped from his body, this man will not stop.

"Then... I'll kill you..."

"Can you?"

"Don't screw with me. ...If it's a way to do it, I had one from the start."

If he couldn't stop him without killing him, then this time he would kill him and be done with it. The means to that end—he would choose it, now.

I'll give myself over to the hateful, loathsome beast's blood sleeping inside this body.

"—OOOOOHHHH"

Clutching his own body, Garfiel was seared by a heat that seemed to boil the blood in every vein. A heat so intense he could almost mistake the breath he exhaled for being tinged red. Every cell in his body writhed, muscle swelled, his mass exploding outward.

His limbs thickened like logs, his torso swelling enough to burst the loincloth at his waist. Golden fur sprouted, covering his whole body; sharp canine teeth grew before his very eyes into blade-like length.

His muzzle pushed forward, his pupils narrowed, and the coloring of the world itself changed. His thoughts turned to white heat, and the spirit that had been, until this moment, Garfiel Tinzel, was painted over and erased.

The exhilaration of Beastification, and the sensation of reason being drowned out by beastly instinct. When it was all over and he returned to himself, scattered before Garfiel's eyes should be nothing but shreds of gnawed flesh. That would be Natsuki Subaru's final form.

It had to come to this before it could be stopped. He felt no regret over that. No intention to repent.

The one who lacked the strength was in the wrong. The weak could do nothing. That was all there was to it.

Consciousness faded. The beast's instinct roared in triumph, jaws opening to sink its teeth into its pitiful prey—

—And the beast's reason-stripped sight was swallowed whole by an eruption of black smoke.
