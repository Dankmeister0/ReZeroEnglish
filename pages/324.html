Arc 5 Chapter 8: "Companions in Seasickness"

"Feeling better yet, I suppose?"

"No, hang on a bit more. Whoa, this is bad, the whole world's swaying. It's still swaying right now. I really thought I'd gotten over this, but nope, guess not. Guess the old saying's true — the soul you have at three stays with you till you're a hundred..."

Watching the clear water flow through the canal, Subaru and Beatrice sat side by side, taking a break. They were on a street facing the Grand Canal that led to the city center, sitting at the edge of the road with their feet dangling over the water — and Subaru could tell that passersby found the sight of the two of them rather endearing.

Probably they were being taken for siblings or something. Or maybe as a couple of country bumpkins from somewhere far off, gawking at the novelty of the Grand Canal.

"Can't really say either guess is wrong. ...It's cruel how nobody ever figures out the truth. Ugh."

"If you're going to gag mid-sentence, then just sit still and get some air, I suppose. You needn't worry so much — what do you think Betty's here keeping you company for?"

"So I don't get lonely being left behind, right? Beako's so kind."

"...Well, that's fine too, I suppose. Just hurry up and feel better."

When Subaru leaned his shoulder against Beatrice beside him, she braced herself, determined to hold his weight with her small body. Seeing her try so hard like that only made him fonder of her, strange as that was. Just where was the ceiling on how much he could trust her, he wondered.

About fifteen minutes had passed since the commotion of Subaru going down with seasickness so early aboard the ferry.

Put ashore and forced to make their way to the destination on foot instead, Subaru and the others were holding off on setting out until he'd recovered.

Emilia, Otto, and Garfiel had gone on ahead by boat, and by now they were probably already at the Muse Company, negotiating with Kiritaka.

Originally, Emilia had insisted on getting off the boat with Subaru and going on foot too, but Otto had chimed in with the unnecessary suggestion, "Keeping the person we arranged to meet waiting will only make a worse impression the longer it drags on," so in the end they'd reluctantly decided to leave Subaru behind.

Still, considering it had taken a full fifteen minutes of rest before he'd finally stopped feeling woozy, it was galling that he couldn't really call that decision wrong.

"Well, being wary of Anastasia and her people is one thing, but... surely even they wouldn't try anything shady in broad daylight, in a place with this much foot traffic."

There was some anxiety in acting alone in an unfamiliar city he'd been invited to by a political rival. That was exactly why Garfiel existed as a bodyguard. Now that Subaru's presence carried no small amount of influence within the Emilia Camp, an attack against him would hardly be meaningless.

Even so, as he'd said, he had a strange sort of confidence that Anastasia wouldn't be foolish enough to corner him here. And he didn't mind adding to that a measure of trust that Julius wasn't the type to favor underhanded tactics.

"Given what he's called, I do trust him to choose the knightly path like a proper knight ought to..."

"Subaru. Muttering to yourself while grinning like that is disgusting, I suppose."

"I—I wasn't grinning at all! Besides, the idea that I was smiling while thinking about that guy is genuinely offensive! Argh, forget it, I can stand up now. Let's go."

Raising his voice at Beatrice's remark, Subaru took a deep breath and got to his feet.

He gave his arms, legs, and neck a light stretch, and it seemed the effects of his seasickness had finally worn off. A slight heaviness lingered in his limbs, but nothing bad enough to hinder his movement.

"Well, if something happens, Betty will handle it, I suppose."

"Yeah, I'm counting on you. Though I doubt trouble's gonna come knocking so soon. Only a colossal idiot would try something with this many eyes around."

Precisely because they were Royal Selection candidates who had to mind the public eye, one had to choose one's methods with care. As for Emilia, he always wanted her to play the part of the well-behaved, naturally endearing girl whenever there was an audience — except she didn't need to play a part at all, being a well-behaved, naturally lovely girl to begin with. Which meant she could just be herself.

"Which means the innocence that none of the other candidates could ever hope to imitate is the one true weapon the angel known as Emilia-tan possesses...!"

"There you go, drifting off into strange thoughts again, I see. Subaru, Subaru, come on, the destination's this way, I suppose. Follow me."

Beatrice's way of handling Subaru's habit of going off the rails at the slightest opening had, over this past year, become second nature to her. Taking his hand, she led the way through streets they'd only just arrived in with the same easy confidence as someone strolling through a home she knew like the back of her hand.

Their destination, the Muse Company, apparently lay along the Grand Canal, at the boundary between First Street and Second Street. Subaru had only caught the explanation in a haze while suffering through seasickness, but Beatrice, at least, had surely gotten a proper grasp of it.

If there was a problem, it was the tangled layout of Priestella's streets.

"Along the Grand Canal" was easy enough to say, but the actual route had to dodge and detour around the many small canals branching off from it, so there was no going straight there. Naturally that meant plenty of turns, plenty of bridges to cross, and even the occasional need to scoop Beatrice up and leap over a waterway—

"Look, Subaru. There's a marvelously splendid fountain, I suppose."

"Yeah... Wait, where even is this park?"

The sigh of admiration slipped from Beatrice, hand still leading Subaru along, at a city park with a large fountain set at the center of its grounds. There were no playground fixtures to speak of, but the well-tended flowerbeds and fountain were a pleasure to look at, and here and there children could be seen splashing about near the water.

It was, without a doubt, a peaceful and soothing sight. No doubt about that — but,

"No matter how I look at it, this isn't our destination, right? Like, is some big merchant house's heir off playing high-stakes money games at the edge of this park? If it rains, the bills'll get soaked."

"Does seasickness impoverish the heart too, I wonder. Looking at such a beautiful sight and having such a bleak thought be the first thing that comes to mind... Betty finds you a disappointing partner, I suppose."

"You used to blush harder trying to cover up your own mistakes, but lately you've gotten a bit too smooth-talking, and it makes your old man sad."

"C-Calling yourself Betty's father is four hundred years too early, I suppose! Subaru, honestly, there's simply no other way to put it — you're utterly lacking in awareness on that front, I suppose!"

For some reason Beatrice overreacted more to the latter half of that, but Subaru, satisfied just to see her get worked up, didn't press the matter further. More pressing was the question of how they'd ended up at this park in the first place.

"Hey, Beako. You were leading the way like you had it all handled. Wasn't that confidence supposed to mean you actually knew the route?"

"I knew the destination, I suppose. It's just that the route there was a little uncertain, I'll admit. So, to make sure I didn't get lost before arriving, I decided to try a method I once read about in a book called 'the Left-Hand Method for when you're in trouble.' Was it all a lie, I wonder?"

"What's this Left-Hand Method for when you're lost?"

"You put your hand on the wall to your left and follow it along, and you're guaranteed to reach the goal — a groundbreaking method, I suppose."

"That's a method for when you're stuck clearing a labyrinth!!"

The strategy Beatrice had proposed was a well-known, orthodox surefire method for getting from a maze's start to its goal. Subaru would readily admit it did work — but being such a basic technique, it had plenty of flaws when it came to application. Especially—

"If you try the Left-Hand Method starting from the middle of a maze, there's a chance you'll never get out at all! What if the wall you're touching is an inner wall instead of the outer one!? And besides, this is a city that's like a maze, not an actual maze!"

"Mmh! Even from Subaru, I won't forgive it if you mock the wisdom of our forebears, I suppose. Betty was the librarian entrusted with managing the Forbidden Archive, the repository of knowledge. Making use of accumulated wisdom is indispensable to living — it's the weight of history, I suppose."

"I'm not mocking our forebears, I'm mocking you for not being able to use their wisdom properly! Are you one of those bookworms who reads a book and thinks they understand everything?!"

Well, she was at a curious age (four hundred years old), and it had taken quite a while before she'd come out into the world (four hundred years), and there was also the gap between her own sense of common knowledge and the world's actual timeline (four hundred years) — so some of it couldn't be helped. Even so, she really was a surprisingly unreliable loli.

"If you're going to say that much, then I suppose Subaru must have some plan to break out of this hopeless situation. Go on, say it, I suppose."

Hands on her hips, Beatrice shot Subaru a provocative look.

Subaru, for his part, was well aware he'd just shown her an embarrassing side of himself with the seasickness, and he fired himself up to prove here and now that he could be reliable too.

"Heh. You were right to judge this place as maze-like. But your mistake, left-hand rule and all, was mistaking it for a complete maze. Unlike the left-hand rule, which is hopeless once you start midway, I've got an even more perfect strategy."

"Ho-ho, awfully full of confidence, I suppose. Something already smells like a disaster in the making, but go ahead and say it anyway, I suppose."

"Heh, don't come crying to me later. Behold — the 'You Are Inside a Rock' strategy."

"—?"

Beatrice tilted her head, a question mark practically floating over it. The name was likely too specialized jargon to guess the meaning from, but Subaru cleared his throat self-importantly and began his explanation.

"Listen up. First, you set your current position as the starting point. From there, you head in a fixed direction, and eventually you'll hit a fork, right? When you do, you go down both branches of that fork, all the way to their dead ends. Once you've confirmed whether it's a dead end or another fork further on, you come back to the original fork."

"...Mm, go on, I suppose."

"Then you map it out, calculating the forks and the number of steps as you go. Then you head to whichever branch wasn't a dead end. At that new fork, you do the same thing you did at the first one. Keep doing that while mapping, and naturally you'll fill in the whole route to the goal and the map of that dungeon's floor..."

"That's much too long-winded, I suppose! The sun will set and rise again before we ever arrive!"

"Y-You idiot! What's wrong with prioritizing certainty!? Do you have any idea how many players this method has brought home safely from overly complicated dungeons!? In fact, this is exactly it — this is what you were talking about, the wisdom of our forebears!"

"That's the bad pattern where you rely on wisdom so much you lose sight of the original goal, I suppose!"

That Beatrice, having had her own idea shot down, was now trying to trample Subaru's brilliant plan out of spite — that wasn't the case, Subaru had to admit, even to himself.

It was true the strategy took far too long, and even before that, they didn't have any paper or pen to map with in the first place.

"Guess there's only one method left at this point..."

"What are you planning to do now? My trust in Subaru's opinions has dropped quite low already, I suppose."

He'd meant to win back her trust with a foolproof topic, and instead he'd only lowered it further. It happened often enough — sincere effort going unrewarded.

"Let's switch gears. And as a compromise between the two of us—"

"Between the two of us?"

"Let's just honestly ask somebody."

"That's the way to go, I suppose..."

To begin with, Subaru had never been especially attached to solving things on his own. Beatrice's pride had been the only real obstacle, but it seemed that had been cleared now too.

Fortunately, Kiritaka, who ran the Muse Company, was a renowned merchant and a public figure involved in running the city. Even an ordinary passerby would probably know the place.

Thinking so, Subaru looked around, meaning to call out to whoever was nearby. But—

"Kind of odd how there's barely anyone around, for a park."

"I don't think the timing's bad either, I suppose. It's a perfectly ordinary early afternoon — just the right time for a nap, I'd say."

Subaru wholeheartedly agreed with Beatrice's words, and the temptation to nap in the shade of a tree welled up in him — but he shook it off. Well then, should they head back toward the canal where there'd been more foot traffic? Just as he was considering it, Subaru noticed something.

"—I can hear something."

The sound of the wind, and the faint murmur of flowing water.

Mixed in among them, tickling at Subaru's eardrums, was a human voice — no, a singing voice.

"——"

Coming in fragments, snatches of song and music, it clawed at Subaru's heart for some reason he couldn't name. His feet turned naturally toward the sound.

And Beatrice, having noticed the same thing, went along with him.

And when Subaru and Beatrice, drawn along, arrived at the source, the two of them were so overwhelmed they forgot even to gasp.

—At the far end of the park stood some sort of monument, and before it, a girl stood singing.

She was a petite girl with dark, tanned skin.

She had a lively-looking face and large, round eyes, her bright yellow hair tied into two bunches hanging from either side of her head, her hair and body adorned with ornaments made of nuts and animal bones.

In the singing girl's arms was an instrument, plucked with the fingers, somewhere between the size of a guitar and a ukulele, and as she played it she poured her voice out, throat trembling, singing her song to the fullest.

The sheer energy carried in that song was overwhelming.

As he listened, Subaru could almost feel a wind that shouldn't have been there, an earthquake that shouldn't have been happening. It was in no way a sensation born of being overwhelmed by sheer volume. Hers was a single voice, and the tune she sang was, if anything, a ballad.

Just one lone girl, with nothing but her fingertips and her singing voice, was generating energy to rival an entire orchestra, and it was piercing straight through Subaru's whole body.

"——"

It wasn't only the sight of the girl that startled Subaru, who'd forgotten even to breathe.

There was also the crowd surrounding the singing girl, listening to her voice without so much as a murmur. Their number easily exceeded twenty, and this many people had been holding their breath, absorbed in the song so completely that Subaru and Beatrice, standing at the park's entrance, hadn't even sensed their presence.

In fact, until he'd approached and confirmed it like this, Subaru had thought the girl was alone. That was how completely this place was under the girl's dominion.

And even as Subaru's whole body trembled with the shock of it, the girl's song approached its climax, the crowd's excitement building toward its peak—

"—No money, no future, no dreams, only pride remains. Ah, what do I see? Behind my eyelids, darkness. Beyond the darkness, nothing at all. It runs out, it runs out, the end is coming."

"Hold on, listen closely and this is a horrible song, isn't it!?"

"Hyaa!?"

Jolted back to his senses by lyrics that offered neither dream nor future nor god nor mercy, Subaru couldn't help blurting out a fierce retort.

Instantly, the girl startled and cut off her song, nearly dropping the instrument in her hands, and of course the music stopped as well — and the very next moment, the mysterious sensation that had ruled over the whole place vanished.

Seeing that, Subaru went pale, realizing he'd done something terrible.

"Crap, I really didn't read the room there. That wasn't what I meant— ow!?"

"Subaru, you idiot, you ruined it, I suppose. I was enjoying myself so much, and you go and pull such a tasteless stunt. That's far too much, I suppose, no matter how you look at it."

Before he could even apologize, a sharp pain shot through his toes.

Looking down, he saw Beatrice, her face indignant, grinding her heel into his foot. It seemed she too had come back to herself the moment the song stopped, but she apparently couldn't forgive him for cutting it short.

And then—

"Huh, wha— the song?" "This is a park... just now, I was definitely in the darkness—" "It's not true, it's not true, I couldn't help it back then... because, because—" "When I grow up, I want to defeat Temion and save Drafin...!" "I want to cheer on that dream of yours..." "Tina-chan..." "Lusbel..."

The audience, until then swallowed up by the song, returned one after another from that intoxicated space back to reality. Some were still crumbling under the song's lingering effects, and some boys and girls had used the song as an excuse to get cozy with each other, but every single one of them had, in fact, returned to themselves.

And what did everyone, having returned to reality, do? They all glared as one at the culprit who had cut the song short — namely, Natsuki Subaru, the man renowned for his inability to read the room—

"—Don't go doing unnecessary things!!"

or, freely translated, that was the gist of the abuse the whole crowd hurled at Subaru.

"Heeeey there, thanks for your hard work~. Oh myyy, that was quite the storm of criticism, wasn't it!"

"Don't say that so cheerfully. I got stepped on so much, isn't my right foot a different size from my left now? Is my right foot okay?"

"I wouldn't know, I suppose. This time, Betty isn't going to take Subaru's side, I think."

At Subaru's question, Beatrice turned her face away with a huff, flatly refusing to engage. Left with no choice, Subaru checked for himself, and thankfully his right foot didn't look to be swollen to twice its size, at least.

Having thrown cold water on the outdoor concert, Subaru had braced for a veritable storm of abuse from the crowd — but as it turned out, that hadn't happened, since the singing girl herself had smoothed things over on that front, and things had ended without incident.

The audience members, one by one, exchanged smiling words with the girl, told her how moved they'd been by the song, shook her hand, and then about half of them stepped on Subaru's foot on their way out before leaving.

Since he had a guilty conscience, Subaru couldn't say anything, and Beatrice had silently condoned it all, so public opinion was thoroughly against him.

As for his foot, he probably ought to brace himself for some internal bruising inside his shoe and sock.

"If I get hurt, I end up relying on your healing magic anyway — doesn't that feel kind of empty to you?"

"It would just be a waste of the mana I've been slowly storing up, I suppose. Wait for it to heal naturally, or go ask Emilia, I'd say."

"You always get sulky whenever I actually ask Emilia-tan to heal me, though... But yeah, natural healing, I guess. My sense of that had gotten kind of numb, honestly."

Figuring Beatrice's point was a fair one, Subaru decided to go along with her opinion.

Since coming to this world, he'd been getting hurt more often, and he supposed he'd grown a bit too dependent on the near-omnipotence of the means to heal those injuries. Especially while learning parkour, bruises, scrapes, and sprains had been an everyday occurrence, and yet either Emilia or Beatrice would promptly heal him right up.

His fear of the pain of injury hadn't faded, but it was true that a certain complacency had crept in — a sense that a bit of injury here and there was fine.

"You always tighten the slack whenever I start getting careless like that. Sorry for always relying on you. I'll think it through a bit more."

Saying so, Subaru reached out and stroked the head of Beatrice, who was still facing away. At the gentle touch, she turned only her eyes toward him, leaning her feelings his way just a little, and said,

"W-Well, if you've reflected on it, then that's fine, I suppose. Take that pain as a lesson and bear with it, I'd say."

"Right, right, I'll do that. Oh, right. Sorry for talking this over on our own here—"

To any outside observer, the whole exchange between Subaru and Beatrice would have been utterly incomprehensible. Since he was also the one who'd caused the trouble in the first place, Subaru tried to offer the girl an apology. But he was stopped before he could.

The girl held up a palm toward Subaru and Beatrice, who were looking at her. And then—

"I've had a flash of inspiration!"

"Huh?"

"Please, listen. —I don't care about our age difference."

Leaving the dumbfounded Subaru and Beatrice behind, the girl abruptly began tapping her instrument to set a rhythm. Then, clicking her tongue to catch the timing, she drew a small breath and began to sing.

"Hey, can you see it, can you feel it? The age gap in our love, yours and mine. Everyone says it's strange, but I don't mind that at all. What I always mind is the height gap in our love, yours and mine. Hey, wait for me. Please, wait for me. Just a little, just a little more, if I stand on tiptoe I could almost reach. If the two of us get that close, surely no one will mind the age gap anymore. So please, just two years. Please, just wait that long. The distance of our love, yours and mine. A sweet, melting distance of love."

"The distance of our love closes, becoming a quietly burning love, and soon the stork comes for the two of us, surely bringing us a child, and the future's a bright love story!"

"Ehhhh!?"

Blindsided by the girl's sudden song and its lyrics, Beatrice's head was spinning. But right after the girl finished singing and let out a shout, Subaru abruptly jumped into the song right at its end. It rhymed, in what could be called a rap style, and for something done on pure reflex, it was oddly polished.

That last cry of astonishment came from Beatrice, aghast at watching the two of them — who'd just jammed together with no explanation whatsoever — high-five each other.

"Hold on just a moment, I suppose! Why— yes, why is Subaru suddenly joining in on the song, I wonder? And it's just as strange that you're taking it as though it's the most natural thing in the world, I suppose!"

"Come on now, what are you talking about, Beako. ...Song transcends borders, you know?"

"What a fine sentiment. This Liliana's heart trembles with emotion. Though not quite enough to actually tremble!"

"T-The way you're acting like Betty's the one who's wrong is completely unacceptable, no matter how you look at it, I suppose..."

Beatrice wore an exhausted expression at the two of them — not so much "at their own pace" as flat-out "going their own way." Even Subaru felt that was too much to put Beatrice through, so he turned back to face the tanned girl.

"Just so you know, Beako and I aren't the kind of relationship you're imagining, and besides, even if Beako grows up two more years, she still wouldn't be in my strike zone."

"Eh? But wouldn't that make her around thirteen or fourteen? I may not look it, but I'm actually quite good at gauging people's ages, you know~. I suppose you could call it a gift from all my life experience?"

"Roughly four hundred and two, give or take."

"Oh, come on now~. Just because I guessed right, there's no need to get so stubborn about it, you know~."

The girl, apparently thinking Subaru's claim was just a desperate excuse, refused to take it seriously.

Subaru, for his part, found it too much trouble to correct her and gave up denying it any further. In any case, the conversation had veered far too strange a direction. He wanted to steer it back.

"So anyway, let's get things back on track. That 'I've had a flash of inspiration' thing just now — was that a new song?"

"Whyyy, yes it was! I may not look it, but I have an explosively rich sensibility, so watching the two of you and your exchange just now, I simply couldn't hold it in any longer! I'll be sure to write it down properly on a score to preserve it, so please feel free to be proud!"

Rattling this off in a rapid stream, the girl then put a hand to her mouth and continued,

"Oh, but but! That's not all I should mention. Mister, yes, you, Mister. You matched me there at the end, didn't you? What was that, exactly? I've genuinely never had a reaction like that before, and I got so happy and excited I couldn't help myself!"

"That was the god of rap suddenly descending on me. If you asked me to do it again, I probably couldn't. I don't have that kind of skill or talent. It was just a fleeting spark."

"A fleeting... spark..."

At Subaru's faraway look, the girl too wore a somewhat wistful expression.

Something passed unspoken between the two of them, a mutual understanding neither could put into words. And that was what finally snapped the patience of the other girl, left out of it entirely.

"Subaru."

"Hm, what's up, Bea— gwuh!?"

The instant his sleeve was tugged and he glanced down, a shockwave sent Subaru's body spinning through the air. He bounced across the park's lawn, slapping the ground hard to break his fall, but he couldn't kill all the momentum and ended up rolling to a stop, seeing stars.

Meanwhile, Beatrice, having pulled that off, turned a flat, half-lidded stare on the tanned girl.

"I refuse to let the two of you keep steering this conversation any longer, I suppose. From here on, Betty will be the one running things, I think. Defy me, and you'll end up just like that example, I suppose."

"Fweh... u-um, well then..."

"Be quiet, I suppose. All you need to think about is answering promptly whatever Betty says, whatever question Betty asks. The only reason nothing's happened to you yet is that your song was wonderful, I suppose. But that mercy won't last forever, I'll have you know."

Beatrice's voice showed no mercy even toward the trembling girl.

Watching the girl nod her head jerkily up and down, Beatrice let out a single breath. In the moment before the next words came out, the girl braced herself, stiff with dread at what she might be told.

And then—

"You will guide Betty and the rest of us to wherever this Muse Company's headquarters is, I suppose."

"—Eh?"

"I won't say it twice, I suppose. Your choice is between guiding us, or refusing and having Betty get angry with you, I think."

"I—I'll guide you! Please, allow me the honor!"

Faced with such a constrained choice, the girl raised the white flag at once.

Satisfied at the sight, Beatrice nodded — just as Subaru came back, dusting himself off. At his gaze, Beatrice lifted her chin haughtily.

"If you have a complaint, go ahead and say it, I suppose."

"Is using the mana you've been diligently saving up on disciplining me really within acceptable limits?"

"That depends on the time and circumstances, I suppose."

"You've got some flexible thinking there, Beako. Anyway, about what just happened... I do think it was good that we got ourselves a guide."

There, Subaru scratched his cheek and let his words trail off.

Beatrice shot him a displeased look at the way he'd left the sentence hanging, so Subaru went on.

"That girl's probably famous as some kind of songstress, so I don't think it's a good idea to handle her carelessly."

She'd introduced herself as Liliana just a moment ago, and with singing talent like that, there was little room for doubt. Her personality, however, didn't quite live up to a title like "songstress"—

"H-Heeey there, what can I do for you? Heh heh, I'll guide you, lick your boots, anything at all, you name it. So please, please just spare my life... I beg you, I beg you...!"

There she was, groveling on all fours, abjectly begging for her life.
