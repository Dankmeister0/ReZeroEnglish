Arc 9 Chapter 38: "Scorched by the Sun"

"—In other words, even if this weren't the Sacred Gold Coin but Garf's full-body, full-soul strike for real, Emilia-sama already knows exactly what Garf's going to do. She could dodge his painfully obvious attack without even trying, counter it, and leave him roaring and bawling like a whipped cub."

"I see, so that's how it works... But wait! Making Garfiel cry and roar like that is just too sad. I'd rather get along with Garfiel some other way."

"In that case, there's no need to counterattack at all. If Emilia-sama simply dodges every one of Garf's attacks with a cool, untroubled face, eventually his spirit will break, and things will end peacefully."

"Oh, that's a relief... a relief? Really? Even though his spirit broke?"

Emilia, having just been handed the Sacred Gold Coin by Rem, tilted her head with a huge question mark floating above it.

At the moment, inside her head Garfiel seemed to be busy either wailing like a child or crumpling to his knees in despair, but Rem — who had taken over the explanation duty from Ram — breathed a sigh of relief, having at least managed to convey the outline of Aldebaran's Authority, "Time Regression."

"Well done, Rem, as expected. You got Emilia-sama to understand."

"I'm glad I could be of help to you, Sister. Though Garf had to be sacrificed for it..."

"If it moves things along just by him crying or getting depressed, then Garf should be satisfied with that outcome. I hear he's sleeping without a care in the world, so Ram will go check on him later."

"I— I see. Garf will probably be overjoyed if Sister goes to check on him..."

At Ram's tired shrug of her thin shoulders, a complicated bitterness ran through Rem's heart.

Now that her memories had returned, she remembered — originally, Rem and Garfiel had been rivals of sorts over Ram. Ever since childhood, she'd disliked Garfiel for daring to harbor feelings for Ram far above his station, and the two of them had argued more times than she could count.

But thinking back on Garfiel's efforts in the Empire, and the conversations they'd shared on the road home, treating him the way she used to weighed heavily on her conscience.

Especially now, when Garfiel had failed in his duty to protect Subaru and Beatrice and the others. When he woke, he was sure to be violently depressed, blaming himself.

"Kicking a man like that while he's down would be far too pitiful... All right. Rem will yield this one to you. Right now, Garf needs to recover as fast as possible so he can become a fighting force for rescuing that person."

"That's true. Though it doesn't feel bad to be bound by Rem's possessiveness. If Rem would rather not, we could put Garf off a while longer, you know?"

"You do know that I'm the one who's supposed to say 'please don't do that,' don't you, Sister?"

"I wonder about that."

Rem let out a sigh at the dazzling sight of Ram, faintly smiling, answering with such poise.

How much of this consideration, this care from Ram, had she let go to waste — across the two separate stretches of time when she was asleep, and when she had no memories?

Of course, Ram wasn't the only one Rem had caused worry to.

She'd caused trouble for everyone in the camp, and for people outside it too. — Fortunately, perhaps, Rem had originally had zero social connections outside those directly involved, so the damage in that sense, at least, had been limited.

"If anything, I feel like I was better at properly interacting with people like Katya-san and Mizelda-san during the time I had no memories than I am now that my memories have come back..."

Looking back over the record of her own life as it returned to her, that was the impression Rem was left with.

Though she'd been forced into it out of necessity to survive after being swept off to the Vollachian Empire, it felt strange that she'd been more socially adept while she had nowhere to belong.

And on top of that, she'd pushed Subaru away, over and over, even though he kept trying so devotedly to stay close to her despite the fact that she trusted no one at all — thinking about it made her want to strangle herself.

"If only Subaru-kun had talked to me looking straight into my eyes, at least... ah, but it was me who refused that too, wasn't it, uu...!"

There was the Rem who wanted to excuse herself, saying it couldn't be helped since she had no memories, and the Rem who wanted to convince herself that if only she had faced Subaru head-on, her sincerity would surely have reached him.

Either way, the fact remained that Rem had caused an enormous amount of trouble for the people around her, Subaru chief among them, and from here on she would have to make up for it with everything she had.

"First, I'll resolve this crisis safely. After that, to show Subaru-kun my gratitude, I'll break my own finger... yes, that's it."

"...Doing something like that won't make Subaru happy — in fact, he might be so shocked he'd drop dead, so please, absolutely do not do that."

"Ah, Petra-san..."

Rem turned around at having her secret resolve overheard, and there stood Petra, brows knit in worry. Feeling that she shouldn't have let her hear that, Rem nonetheless held up a palm toward her and said, "No,"

"Please don't stop me. Rem, Rem feels so sorry toward Subaru-kun. Unless I punish myself for my own inadequacy, I can never properly face him again—"

"So Rem-neesama isn't punishing herself because she wants to apologize to Subaru — she's doing it because it makes herself feel better? Is that it?"

"That's not my intention at all—"

"Even if it's not your intention, that's all it ends up being, is what I'm saying. Listen. What Subaru wants from Rem-neesama isn't for you to accept punishment — it's for you to finally receive the happiness you missed out on while you were asleep."

"——"

At Petra's words, spoken with her eyes softly lowered at the corners, Rem couldn't help but catch her breath.

It was because it carried an echo of the same gentle, sweet scolding Subaru had given her that very first day she'd come to feel something special for him — that morning after the incident with the Witchbeast threatening Arlam Village had been resolved, when Rem had been blaming herself.

For some reason, Subaru back then and Petra standing before her now seemed to overlap, just slightly.

"...I suppose that's proof of just how much everyone has been influenced by that person."

"Hm? What's that, Rem-neesama?"

"Nothing. It's just that Petra-san's words stirred up a feeling that's very nostalgic and very precious to me. Do you remember? The time the Witchbeast attacked Arlam Village."

"...Um, yes. I remember."

At Rem's question, Petra's cheeks stiffened just slightly. Then her gaze flicked, ever so briefly, toward a girl named Meili standing in the corner of the room.

Apparently she was a girl who had joined the camp while Rem was asleep, and who had fulfilled some role in the Kingdom while Emilia and the others were fighting hard in the Empire. Rem felt she vaguely recognized her, but why was Petra paying attention to her right now?

"Ahem. Anyway, please, Rem-neesama, stop trying to hurt yourself. If your precious, snow-white fingers got hurt, the whole world would fall into pitch darkness."

"Petra-san..."

"And if you say you absolutely won't stop, then I'll break my finger too, out of shared responsibility!"

"Petra-san!?"

At Petra clenching her small hand into a fist, Rem's voice caught in her throat despite herself. Seeing Rem's reaction, Petra grinned mischievously, and continued, "So,"

"You're not allowed to do that, okay?"

"—. Yes... understood."

Pressed by Petra's insistence, Rem nodded back timidly.

Part of it was that she'd lost to Petra's passion, but more than that, it was really that the Subaru-like impression she got from Petra had sealed off any argument she might have made.

Petra had already seemed to overlap with Subaru a moment ago, but the way Rem was stopped just now — throwing her own extreme back at Rem, who was about to go to an extreme, and cancelling it out — was truly, uncannily similar.

"——"

Feeling, through Petra, just how great Subaru's influence had become, Rem fell silent.

In a way, the way Petra was acting right now was proof that she understood Subaru even better than Rem herself did, and it wouldn't have been strange for Rem to feel some jealousy or envy over that. But strangely, Rem felt not a fragment of such emotion toward Petra.

She didn't know why that was, but right now, while thinking of Subaru, a different splinter had caught inside her.

And that splinter was—

"—Aldebaran-san's... Authority."

The Authority of "Time Regression," which she had just finished explaining to Emilia using the Sacred Gold Coin.

Thanks to Ram's patient explanation, Rem had come to understand how terrifying it was, but as she chewed it over in her own mind in order to explain it to Emilia, a suspicion had come bubbling up inside her.

An Authority that observes a future ahead of the present and, by turning back time, avoids the undesired outcome—

"—Could that person, too...?"

The dim, faint spark of that suspicion grew larger and larger inside Rem, and the fire it spread caught and burned across the plain of her thoughts. And among what remained after the fire had passed, several embers lay scattered — embers that had kindled the flame and fed its strength.

For example, a dense scent of Miasma always seemed to drift from Subaru.

For example, that Miasma often grew even thicker, to the point it almost seemed to wrap around him entirely.

For example, Subaru would sense the situation with an insight impossible to explain, and derive a solution for it.

For example, thanks to Subaru's presence, many deaths had been avoided — and Rem herself had been one of those people.

Each of these things, taken alone, felt like little more than nitpicking or grasping at straws. But the moment a single rule-breaking Authority became the core connecting them, they clicked into place with astonishing precision.

But if — if by some chance — Rem's intuition here was actually correct, then the boy named Natsuki Subaru, while possessing a power that was without equal—

"—only ever used it for someone else's sake. ...What a thankless person to be."

If he could redo any span of time and rewrite it however suited him, he could behave with total selfishness, remaking the world exactly as he pleased.

And yet Subaru hadn't done that, hadn't used it that way. — That much, Rem, who represented the very case where things had not gone Subaru's way, understood better than anyone.

If Subaru had used his power to bend someone's heart to his own will, then surely the very incident of Rem breaking his finger would never have happened.

In other words, that very fact — that his finger had been broken — was proof of Subaru's sincerity.

"After all, Rem should break her finger too, to match Subaru-kun...!"

"You already gave that up once, just now!?"

"You're mistaken, Petra-san. This time it's not the negative reason of not being able to face him — it's the positive reason of wanting to share the same pain as Subaru-kun—"

"Positive, negative, upward-facing, downward-facing, no-o-o way!"

Refused a hearing, Rem's enthusiasm fizzled out unspoken.

In any case, setting the finger-breaking matter aside, Rem's conviction had deepened further that Subaru possessed an Authority of the same lineage as Aldebaran. And yet, even after hearing about "Time Regression," Ram and Emilia and the others didn't seem to have arrived at the same idea Rem had.

That wasn't because Rem understood Subaru more deeply than they did —

"...If anything, it's the opposite."

The reason Emilia and the others, unlike Rem, hadn't reached this idea, was that everyone except Rem, who had been asleep, knew what Subaru's efforts had looked like over these past days.

From what she'd heard, during the more than a year Rem had remained asleep, Subaru had formed a Contract with Beatrice and kept up ceaseless effort in order to become worthy of being Emilia's knight. Precisely because they knew about that effort of his, none of them suspected that some other factor lay behind the results he'd produced.

Rem didn't know about that effort — it was frustrating enough to feel like it was tearing her heart apart. — And that was why there was such a large gulf between her and everyone else's understanding of what Subaru was capable of.

But that didn't diminish Subaru's heroism, not in the slightest.

More than anything, the very fact that things had come to this current situation proved that even if Subaru did have an Authority, he was not omnipotent — and that was precisely why the fact remained unshaken that he had wrung out every last drop of his wits and courage, taking someone's hand and pressing forward.

And even now, as things stood, there was still something Rem could do left to help him.

"Now that I know that... Sister!"

The captive Subaru was calling for help. — Once she reached that certainty in her own thoughts, Rem hurried to share this idea with Ram and the others, to add more material for their discussion.

It would be difficult to get them to accept, given that it was based partly on intuition and partly on a theory premised on the Oni Tribe's sense of smell for Miasma, but surely the wise Ram and the big-hearted Emilia would listen to her seriously.

Thinking so, Rem was about to speak to Ram and the others, who turned toward her asking "What's wrong?" —

"—I have returned. Apologies for the delay."

That was the very moment.

Into the great hall of the Royal Manor where Rem and the others had gathered, two men appeared as if in the blink of an eye. One was a handsome, keen-eyed man wearing a monocle — Clind. And the one Clind had brought with him was a master Rem herself had known for a long time and owed a great debt to—

"Roswaal! You came!"

"Ye-e-es, I heard the details from Clind. Hearing that Subaru-kun and Beatrice are in dire straits, and that the very foundations of the Royal Selection are shaking, I could hardly say my fatigue from traveling the Empire was too-o-o much to bear, now, could I."

"Mm, that's so-o-o reassuring. Let's get everyone talking right away— but before that."

"—?"

Emilia, welcoming the tall figure who had appeared, Roswaal, casually pointed with her hand toward someone standing right behind him — Rem.

At that gesture from Emilia, Roswaal turned around, and his mismatched eyes came to focus on Rem. In response, Rem pinched up her skirt and dropped into a curtsy,

"Roswaal-sama, I have been granted a very long leave of absence. And I must once again offer my thanks for coming to fetch me in the Empire. — Rem has returned."

"——"

At Rem's words of gratitude and her greeting on returning, a faint sound caught in Roswaal's throat. And then, in the next instant, his tall frame swayed, as if struck by a sudden bout of vertigo.

"Roswaal-sama!"

"Ah, no, I'm fine, fine. ...It's just, this so-o-o suddenly, I nearly drowned in information, that's all."

As Ram, beside him, caught his body just in time, Roswaal answered with a wry smile.

It was probably the backlash of "memory" that came from having Rem's "name" spoken aloud.

It was a shame that it hadn't been the power of his bond with Subaru — who had been diligently polishing that iron ball, the Morningstar. But witnessing the empty place inside himself being filled back in didn't feel bad at all. Though perhaps it was inappropriate to say so.

In any case—

"—Welcome back, Rem. Once this situation settles down, let's be sure to talk about the mansion during your absence... about Ram in particular, shall we-e-e."

"Yes. I'm looking forward to it, truly."

With his characteristic comprehension, Roswaal fully absorbed the shock of suddenly remembering Rem. Admiring the strength of his master's spirit, Rem was about to try and get the conversation back on track once more.

But then her sleeve was tugged sharply from behind—

"Milord, wasn't Sister Frederica with you?"

"Ah, I had Frederica stay behind in Barielle territory. Once one takes something on, after all. It seemed too cruel to leave Schult-kun to fend for himself, and Clind was the one who said he himself would carry out the duty expected of Frederica, so, well, there-e-e it is."

"Brother Clind is doing Sister Frederica's duty...? Isn't that impossible?"

"How sharp. All the more reason I must exert myself, to dispel that suspicion. I shall endeavor."

Having confirmed the whereabouts of Frederica, who had not returned together with Roswaal and the others, Petra frowned, and Clind, unperturbed, vowed to rise to the occasion.

Alongside that exchange, Rem, gazing at the profile of Petra — who had pulled on her sleeve — couldn't help but tilt her head and say, "Petra-san?"

Just now, Petra had clearly cut Rem off mid-sentence and brought up a different topic. Of course, Frederica's whereabouts was a topic worth wondering about too, but the action itself felt suggestive of something more.

While Rem puzzled over this, on the other side of the room Emilia had begun introducing Old Man Rom to Roswaal and explaining the need for countermeasures that crossed the boundaries between camps. Petra had been watching that, but then she turned toward Rem and gave a small beckoning wave.

And then—

"...By any chance, did Rem-neesama notice something about Subaru too?"

"—! The way you're saying that, Petra-san, you also noticed Subaru-kun's Authority—"

"Don't say any more than that. ...Because the 'Witch' might be listening."

"——"

At Petra pressing a finger to her own lips as she said that, Rem too found herself falling silent.

Petra hadn't stated it outright, but the way she'd just spoken had hit exactly on what was in Rem's heart — proof that Petra, too, believed Subaru possessed the Authority of "Time Regression." And from the words that followed, Rem could guess that Petra had even more information than she did.

Being "listened to" by "the Witch" was a rather far-fetched way to put it, but—

"Given that I've heard Reinhard-sama is holding back the 'Witch of Envy' at the Augria Sand Dunes, I can hardly laugh that off as an exaggeration."

"Right, it's related to that too. I don't have absolute proof that it's true, but I think it's better not to say this out loud. — Because I really don't want to go through that kind of experience again."

Petra's murmur carried an earnest, almost frightening weight of conviction, one that felt as though she'd personally experienced the cost of speaking that forbidden word aloud.

Naturally, at Petra's quiet plea, Rem swallowed back the words she'd been about to spin. But that also meant giving up her chance to reveal the true extent of Subaru's accomplishments up to now—

"...That sort of thing, that person wouldn't care about, would he."

Before her memories were lost, and after they were lost — Rem had been given two separate chances to know Subaru.

What those two chances had taught her was a sincerity, a devotion so earnest it moved the heart, one for which being fairly evaluated, having everything laid bare, was very much a secondary concern.

That everything Subaru had done and felt should be praised, and that he should be rewarded for it — wishing for that was, after all, only a matter of Rem's own values.

"Rem-neesama, let me ask you just one thing."

Petra said this to Rem, who was swallowing her own silent prayer, the lingering trace of sentiment still on her face.

It hadn't been spoken in any especially hushed voice, but to Rem it felt like a question Petra had asked with the utmost care, meant to be heard only by her and no one else.

Sensing, once again, something in that earnestness that resembled Subaru, Rem gave a small nod, and—

"Is there anything Rem-neesama wouldn't do, to save Subaru?"

Behind that question, Rem sensed that Petra was saying, "I would do anything." It wasn't that Rem felt any rivalry toward that. But her deliberation didn't even take a full second.

"Yes, of course. — Because I am Subaru-kun's Rem."

Answering that way required no hesitation from Rem whatsoever.

—Moment by moment, Aldebaran could feel the exhaustion piling up.

"Food I can scrounge up on-site, and for water I just need to lick ice made by cooling the air, but... losing sleep is the one thing that's seriously brutal."

The relentless sabotage kept being sprung on them, tormenting Aldebaran's party without end.

And what made it worse was that most of the damage from this sabotage wasn't the kind of massive harm meant to injure them or knock them out of the fight — it was purely designed to force exhaustion on them. And then, just when you'd think that, they'd throw in something on the level of that first rockfall at the quarry, a viciousness of method that never once let you drop your guard.

The single biggest problem right now was that there was no perfect way to defend against it.

"A martial-arts-capable steward... a martial-arts-capable steward, huh. Guess that rough-sounding title wasn't just for show."

The Emilia Camp's steward, Otto Suwen, was a mild-mannered pretty boy Aldebaran had seen plenty of times before.

His position in the camp was as the group's punching bag and shock absorber, an everyday, indispensable role — but not someone you needed to be wary of in a life-or-death fight. — That assumption had been dead wrong.

Aldebaran had far too badly underestimated the fact that this man could talk to any living creature. He was paying that price now, at an unbelievable rate and volume.

"And what's more, it's not just the sneaky steward giving me grief... there's also the thing with the old man and Felt-chan."

On top of the sabotage, what troubled Aldebaran even further was the discord that had arisen within the traveling party, a seed of trouble too dangerous to overlook.

Felt hadn't originally been part of Aldebaran's intended lineup, but her presence — necessary to drag along given her connection to "Aldebaran" — had ended up causing serious damage at this point.

That was the matter of Felt touching a core part of Heinkel that stirred his emotions into chaos.

It seemed Felt had touched on something Heinkel really did not want touched.

While his own biological son, Reinhard, held back the "Witch of Envy" at the Augria Sand Dunes, Heinkel had stabbed his own biological father, Wilhelm, when he stood in the way as an obstacle. Felt had carelessly brushed up against that mental state of his, right on the verge of shattering, and had nearly ended up paying for that carelessness with her life. — No, in fact, she had already paid for it with her life several times over.

Had Aldebaran not redone it with his "Domain," Felt's death would have been certain.

That would have earned "Aldebaran's" wrath, and driven Heinkel's position further into a corner. As it stood, this was already an unnatural situation, with Aldebaran's consciousness overwritten onto that of the "Divine Dragon." If something that severely provoked the "Dragon's" instincts were added on top of that, the magic of the "Book of the Dead" itself might come undone.

That's why Aldebaran had forcibly cut off the conversation between Heinkel and Felt, but—

"It's nothing but a stopgap. If I carelessly let the old man and Felt-chan be alone together even once, it's plain to see another bloodletting incident's gonna happen. And leaving Roy's restraint to anyone but Yae is out of the question... damn, this isn't some riddle about ferrying a wolf and a goat across a river by boat. Especially since I'm bad at those."

It was the sort of thing called a logic puzzle, but he'd been placed in a situation not far removed from one.

Having his brainpower siphoned off by a battle of wits that shouldn't even be necessary was hardly desirable, but even so—

"—Whether it's Heinkel-sama or Felt-sama, if you just cut whichever one loose whenever you like, things would get a lot easier, you know?"

"—. I've told you a hundred times, I'm not doing that."

At the light, breezy voice that suddenly cut into his musing, Aldebaran clicked his tongue in reply.

Coming toward him with her hands clasped behind her back, walking with a dancer's light steps, was Yae. Seeing her alone, Aldebaran narrowed his eyes inside his helmet.

"Hey, why are you alone. Shouldn't you have someone with—"

"Felt-sama is with Bol-sama, the Sin Archbishop is hanging up nice and proper, and Heinkel-sama's all tied up. For now, at least, everything's calm, I promise."

"...Even so, that doesn't mean there aren't holes in it, does it."

"True enough. But but, right now the biggest hole about to blow open is over here at Al-sama's place, you know~?"

As she said that, Yae crouched down right in front of Aldebaran, who sat hugging one raised knee. Peered into by her cat-like crimson eyes, Aldebaran felt distinctly uncomfortable.

However brusquely he might try to answer, her read on the situation was correct. — Without question, the one wearing down the most vividly among the whole party was none other than Aldebaran himself.

"But well, that's only natural. The goal I want to accomplish is the biggest and has the most steps of anyone's. So it's only reasonable that I'm the one shouldering the hardship—"

"And if, as a result, Al-sama collapses partway through, isn't that going to be a real eyesore?"

"...What are you trying to say."

"So~o~o, that's exactly what Yae's been telling you this whole time. Before Al-sama gets squashed flat, let's lower the victory conditions."

"That's exactly why—"

"—Why does Al-sama have to insist on such a clean method to begin with?"

Resting her cheek on her folded knee, Yae cut in sharply with that.

Feeling Yae's thread-thin eyes open just slightly, feeling the playfulness drop out of her tone, Aldebaran found himself unable to speak on the spot.

Taking advantage of Aldebaran's silence, Yae continued.

"Your opponents aren't holding anything back. Of course they wouldn't. They've had their allies stolen from them by Al-sama, been hurt, and on top of all that, the fate of the whole world is on the line. Of course they'd fight like they're desperate to survive~. Straightforward, head-on, and not above using methods that go against the righteous path."

"...I know full well I'm putting you through hell. Not just having you take on their harassment nonstop, but running into a clash with the 'Sword Devil' in the royal capital, too."

"That's not it. I'm not here to complain. ...No, well, in a way, I guess it might really be a complaint, at that~."

"What are you—"

Trying to say — the words didn't follow.

Unable to dodge Yae's hand as it stretched straight toward him without warning, Aldebaran had his forehead pushed, and toppled straight over backward. And then, as Aldebaran blinked in surprise, Yae, having pushed him down, slid smoothly over to cover him from above.

"——"

At close range, Aldebaran and Yae's eyes met.

In that instant, Aldebaran instantly flicked his tongue against the poison packet hidden in his back teeth, meaning to redo this very moment through self-inflicted death—

"—Your wife has passed away, hasn't she, Al-sama."

"—!"

"She's passed away. You've said so yourself, many times, haven't you."

Just before he swallowed the poison, with Yae close enough that he could feel her breath, Aldebaran's throat made a sound.

There was no need for her to say it. Priscilla Barielle was dead. That was the premise underlying everything. It was the reason, the cause, and the final restraint that had led Aldebaran to make this decision.

Because she was gone, Aldebaran had cast off every chain that bound him, and begun this — knowing full well it meant turning every living thing into an enemy and plunging the world into chaos.

"Is that really true?"

"Ah...?"

"Is it really true? Your wife is nowhere to be found. So nothing else matters anymore. Whoever you betray, whatever you destroy, however people think of you, none of it matters at all. — Is that really true?"

At the question piling on top of the last, Aldebaran couldn't move.

Even now, with just a flick of his tongue, he could undo this whole moment. That was his insurance — no, even if he had no such insurance, Aldebaran might not have moved regardless.

That was how much power Yae's words and the strength in her gaze held to bind him, more tightly than steel wire.

Really, she asked him. Just what answer was she looking for?

Really, she asked him. Of course it was true. What else could he possibly say?

Really, she asked him. She was questioning the rightness of Aldebaran's resolve.

"The result might be insane. The damage caused might be outrageous, too. But I also think this — your methods, Al-sama, have been beautiful. So very, very beautiful, this whole time."

"Beautiful...?"

"Facing them head-on, out-thinking and overcoming their schemes, and when the battlefield turns against you, exploiting their weaknesses without ever letting anyone die. Al-sama's resolve is beautiful. And that's exactly why things have gotten this terribly, terribly desperate."

"——"

"—Al-sama. Let's stop burning for the sun, shall we?"

Yae's hands slid up smoothly and cupped Aldebaran's helmet from either side. Not a motion meant to pull it off, but one meant to keep his gaze from turning away.

That touch, and the whispered words, bound Aldebaran's soul and sealed his movements.

—Stop. Burning. For the sun.

"Let's crawl on the ground, get covered in mud, stop looking up at the sky, and bathe in blood instead. It's all right. I'm already filthy myself — I don't mind bathing in mud, or blood, right alongside you."

"—. Whatever you say, I—"

"You don't have to give up the things you can't give up. But if that's what you want, then please stop trying to stay beautiful. If you'd just let go of even that — of trying to stay beautiful—"

There she cut herself off, and Yae's lips curved into a loose, crescent-moon shape.

Her smile, faintly tinged with the color of blood, looked terribly fragile — and it overlapped, for a moment, with someone else's smile.

"—No one can beat my Al-sama. No one at all."

—It was the smile of someone who had shaped Aldebaran, carved into his very soul.

"——"

At Yae's declaration — spoken at close range with a bloodstained smile, clinging, cherishing, grieving, pleading, and glaring all at once, and somehow fragile through it all — Aldebaran let out a long breath.

And then—

"—Domain Expansion. Matrix Redefinition."

So as not to let go of the one thing he truly could not let go of, he thought — even if it meant being covered in blood and mud.

"—Ngh."

Struck by a stab of headache sharper than the rest, gouging straight into his brain, Otto let out a faint groan.

More than twenty hours had already passed since he'd opened the "Divine Protection of the Word Spirit" and kept it running continuously. In that time, he wasn't constantly monitoring every open channel, but even without speaking out himself, he couldn't afford to miss even a single piece of information that came flowing in.

As a result, Otto had been keeping himself in a state somewhere between half-open and fully-open with the Divine Protection, waging a lonely battle on and on, sacrificing sleep and food along the way.

"...It seems Emilia-sama has rejoined the Margrave and the others."

There was no comparing it to tracking Aldebaran and his group, but in order to check on his allies' movements too, Otto had, so to speak, sent out animals to scout both ahead of and behind himself.

Part of the reason he wasn't actively making contact with Emilia and the others was that he didn't want his concentration disturbed, but the biggest reason was to avoid being stopped from carrying out his own reckless plan.

Because no matter how you looked at it, if Emilia and the others learned that Otto had already lost more than a cup's worth of blood from his nose, there was absolutely no way they'd let him keep going.

"But this is necessary."

Before long, Aldebaran and his group would arrive at the Grand Mogolade Geyser in the Kararagi City-States, and try to accomplish some objective there. That was probably the limit — the deadline.

It was nothing more than Otto's own intuition, but he was certain: letting them accomplish that would be their defeat condition, the very last chance to bring Natsuki Subaru and Beatrice back.

Which meant it had to be stopped. But the decisive blow to do so wasn't something Otto himself possessed.

The one who held the decisive blow was always going to be someone other than Otto.

All Otto Suwen could do was provide covering fire for that someone else. — And that was exactly why he poured his entire self into that covering fire, even if it meant wringing every last drop of blood from his own body.

"Young master, please don't die."

"...You're not telling me not to be reckless, huh."

"Being reckless is basically your hobby, isn't it, young master. I'm not going to tell you to stop doing something you can't stop even when told to. So just, please, don't die."

Otto gave a wry smile at his beloved dragon Frufoo, who was acting like he understood him so well.

Normally, Otto acted separately from everyone else in the camp, but as a means of transportation and as someone to talk to in a pinch — so that he wouldn't get so absorbed in other creatures' words and thoughts through the "Divine Protection of the Word Spirit" that he couldn't find his way back — he moved together with only Frufoo.

As expected of a dragon he'd been with since childhood, Frufoo seemed to see right through Otto's thoughts and actions, and that reliability saved him more often than not.

Especially at times like now, moving through the mountains far from any human settlement.

"I don't know if it'll help, but why don't you sleep a little? From what you just said, the other side's resting too... so right now, there's no risk of losing track of them, and you could get some rest, couldn't you?"

"What are you talking about. If they're resting, that's exactly when I shouldn't slack off. If anything, now is prime time."

"Calling this situation 'prime time' is your bad habit showing, young master."

Frufoo shook his thick neck side to side as if exasperated, and though his leisurely tone and attitude might seem far too laid-back given the circumstances, once you realized it was a deliberate calm meant to keep Otto from getting too anxious, it was hard to stay annoyed at him.

In fact, while talking with Frufoo, Otto's spirits gradually recovered. — Thanks to that, it seemed he could manage to push himself just a little further.

"I'm not doing it with any particular intention, you know."

"Even without intention, you're helping me. My Frufoo is the best partner anyone could ask for."

"Young master, you sure know how to talk!"

At Frufoo's low, discontented growl, a smile spilled out of Otto, and he took a deep breath. Then, turning his attention back to the channel once more, he resumed speaking to the flocks of birds, insects, and fish he'd tasked with tracking Aldebaran and his group, trying to gather as much as he could of what the other side was doing.

Of course, since it required exchanging words with animals, Otto's tracking ran a little behind real time, with a certain amount of lag built in.

Even so, the fact that he'd managed to keep from losing Aldebaran's group, never once letting up the pace of the chase, owed a great deal to the power of the Zodda Bugs. Most animals feared the presence of the "Divine Dragon" that his opponents commanded, and no matter how earnestly he pleaded with them, they wouldn't venture past a certain point.

But the Zodda Bugs were different. Valuing the survival and future of the swarm over that of any single individual, they were fearless masters at approaching even in the face of the "Divine Dragon" and bringing information back. Once this whole affair was over, he'd have to treat them as generously as possible — including the peaceful sanctuary he'd promised them.

"If you dote on the Zodda Bugs too much, Frederica-ojousan and Petra-ojousan are just going to make even sourer faces at you, you know."

"I know very well that women tend to dislike Zodda Bugs for some reason. But if they knew about the achievements these bugs made this time, maybe their opinion would change."

Still, Otto himself understood there was little hope of that.

Even setting the women aside, given his fight with Otto back at the "Sanctuary," Garfiel too seemed to harbor some lingering resistance toward Zodda Bugs. As for Subaru and Beatrice, once they were safely rescued and heard about the Zodda Bugs' contribution, what kind of face would they make—

"At the very least, I should make sure to convey my thanks directly to the swarm's representative—"

"—That's going to be a bit difficult, I think~?"

"——"

In that instant, a voice that belonged to none of the insects, none of the small animals, and not to Frufoo either, brushed past Otto's ear. The moment he caught his breath at that, Otto's body was abruptly hoisted up into the air.

"Young master!!"

The Dragon Carriage wasn't in use — Otto had been riding directly on Frufoo's back — and as his weight vanished, Frufoo let out a desperate cry upon noticing.

But Frufoo's cry was in vain; Otto found himself strung up from a single scraggly tree in the mountains, thin cords wound around his hands and neck, choking him as his legs kicked helplessly in the air.

He just barely managed to wedge a finger between the cord and his throat to avoid instant suffocation, but—

"Gh, guh...!"

"You don't look like a combat specialist, but that instant reaction was impressive. What do they call it, always being battle-ready in spirit? Mister, you might have made it big as a Shinobi yourself, you know?"

Strung up and dangling, swaying back and forth, a slender silhouette rose into view beneath Otto's feet. It was a girl with crimson hair, wearing a servant's uniform with elements of a wasou — features that matched perfectly.

One of Aldebaran's group, an enemy Otto needed to whittle down—

"See, I told you so, didn't I, Al-sama. — No one, I said, can beat you."

—The crimson Shinobi, Yae Tenzen, said this with reddened cheeks, looking up at the Otto she'd strung up.
