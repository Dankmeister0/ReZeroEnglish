Arc 4 Interlude: "Emilia Camp, Warlock, Spirit, Spirit Arte User"

At the moment of impact, a cheekbone creaked, and a tall body danced weightlessly through the air before being slammed into a wall. Not only that — the shock punched straight through the flimsy wooden wall, sending the battered body flying outside in a shower of splinters. The body skidded across the snow, kicking up white powder, and rolled head over heels.

"――――"

The body that collapsed face-down onto the ground didn't so much as twitch, and the space filled with a silence so complete it made one wonder if the man was dead. Eyes went back and forth between the tall figure sent flying through the blown-out wall and the one who'd done the sending, still standing inside. Catching that gaze, the puncher let out a satisfied breath.

"Ahh... I did it, huh. Did it good."

The blond boy — Garfiel — bared his sharp canines in a fine grin as he declared this. Watching Ram rush to the side of Roswaal, whom he'd struck, Subaru scratched his head.

"O-oh. Yeah. Guess so."

was all he managed to say in reply.

Garfiel and the others, who had parted ways at the manor, rejoined everyone at the Sanctuary half a day after Emilia's "declaration of baby-making" — exactly the morning after the decisive battle with the Great Rabbit.

Six people, including Garfiel, had come to the Sanctuary with Patrasche pulling the dragon carriage. It was a relief to confirm the safety of Rem, Petra, Frederica, and the rest of the women who had gone to rescue the manor, as well as the safety of Garfiel and Otto, who had stayed behind as the rescue party. Except that, as a little something extra that had tagged along—

"Ohh, we meet again, mister."

So said the girl with braided hair trussed up like a bundle of firewood in the corner of the dragon carriage — Meili, the stage director of the witchbeast attack, who had assaulted the manor together with Elsa. Subaru, struck speechless by this unexpected reunion, was greeted with the same shameless familiarity Meili had shown when they'd first crossed paths at the manor.

"Hey, can I ask you something? That blond mister is awful rough with girls, don'tcha think? Tying me up all round and round and just leaving me like this is too much, don'tcha think? You think so too, right, mister?"

"Sure. Though if it were up to me, I'd have gagged you too, since who knows what you'd try. ...Since you got caught, what happened to Elsa?"

"Dunno. But even Elsa probably couldn't survive that huge fire, don'tcha think? If that's how it went, then she finally got to die, and I think that's fine."

Meili showed no particular interest in whether Elsa — who was supposed to be her ally — lived or died. Subaru frowned at her words.

"You've got a lot of nerve demanding prisoner treatment. I doubt a very good future is waiting for you. Considering what you pulled."

"Yeah. But well, it didn't work out, so what can you do. And besides, even if I crawled back with my tail between my legs, Mama'd just get mad at me anyway."

"Mama, huh. I've heard that word a few times at the manor too — so there's some kind of boss figure pulling your strings? ...Well, I guess Roswaal can clear that up for us."

"Just make sure I get three meals, and no peppir in 'em, 'kay?"

Signaling the end of the conversation with her attitude, Meili turned her back in an easygoing manner. Her treatment would probably be decided later. Either way, she was a valuable witness regarding the attack on Roswaal Manor. That Garfiel had had the presence of mind to capture her was, quietly, rather surprising.

After that, they confirmed everyone's safety — hearing about the Sanctuary's wellbeing, and conversely about the villagers of Arlam Village, and how the refugees who had evacuated the Sanctuary ahead of everyone else had been safely taken in at a nearby town. Just as everyone breathed a sigh of relief, that opening blow of reckoning had come to pass.

"One good hit and I'll let it go for now — I think I'm bein' pretty nice about it, y'know."

Looking at the hole punched in the wall, Garfiel shook out the arm he'd hit Roswaal with as he grumbled this. Subaru, for his part, had plenty he wanted to say to Roswaal too, so he thought Garfiel's anger was justified, but—

"If that one hit packs more than ten times the punch of mine, I start wondering whether 'nice' is really the word for it..."

"Don't be so laid back about it, boss. C'mon, you go too."

Garfiel snorted at Subaru's hesitant answer and held something out. Wondering what it was, Subaru looked at his hand — it was a tree branch that could only have been picked up in the forest. Quite an impressive one, thick and long. It looked rather like a wooden baseball bat.

"...What am I supposed to do with this?"

"Even I know it's bad manners to land more'n one hit. One hit's one hit — but if you cram somethin' powerful into it with this, ain't nobody got room to complain."

"If you're telling me to pile on right after what I just saw, that's plenty bad manners already!"

On top of that, using a weapon. If you're going to call that "one hit," you're deep in the territory of sophistry. But Garfiel tilted his head at Subaru's answer and jerked his chin toward outside the hole.

"But hey, even if you think that way, boss, looks like the others don't."

"Huh?"

Pulled along by Garfiel's exasperated look, Subaru glanced outside — just in time to see Frederica raising her fist in front of Roswaal, who Ram had just propped back up on his feet.

"My lord, prepare yourself—!"

Frederica's sturdy arm cut through the air, and her fist buried itself in the same left cheek Garfiel had struck. The pitiful tall man went flying again, this time slamming bodily into the trunk of a tree several meters away and coming to a stop there. The snow piled on the tree, jarred by the impact, came sliding down, burying the crumpling Roswaal beneath it. Frederica watched it happen, nodding in satisfaction as she clapped her hands.

"Wha— Is everyone on board with this whole revenge-visit thing!?"

"Boss, reckonin' is somethin' that's gotta be done. If you don't settle up for what you did, it just gets awkward later. Not that I'm plannin' on refusin' to heal him after, mind you. Lady Emilia's standin' by for that too."

Petra and Otto were also waiting their turn near Roswaal, who had his eyes spinning as he poked his head out of the snow. Ram was the one hauling the beaten Roswaal back up, while Emilia stood by as the healer, ready for when the need arose.

"I mean — Emilia-tan's the healer, right? She's not, like, waiting in line too, is she? I mean, she'd have every right to line up there too, but—"

"Who knows. Might be a tough call for her, personality-wise. Either way, you saw it yourself. So c'mon, boss."

The wooden bat was pushed out to him again. Subaru accepted it hesitantly.

"So if I don't do it too, I don't get to be part of the conversation? Is that it?"

"Well I ain't sayin' it's gotta be that way. But you must want to cram one into that bastard's face too, don't you, boss? That's all there is to it."

Pushed from behind, Subaru walked out of the house. Inside the hole in the wall, Garfiel had his thumb up. It seemed he was mimicking the gesture after seeing Subaru do it. Seen off by his still slightly awkward little brother figure, Subaru slowly took his place at the very back of the line of people meting out reckoning to Roswaal. Incidentally, at that moment Petra had just taken a full swing at Roswaal's face with a wet cloth.

A damp, satisfying sound echoed through the Sanctuary.

"Er, so, once again. Now that the reckoning's more or less done, I'd like us to compare notes on this whole incident and talk about what happens going forward."

Clearing his throat before taking up the role of moderator, Subaru swept his gaze across the assembled faces. Gathered in the great hall were the key figures involved in this whole incident. Even so, that alone amounted to quite a number of people, and Subaru found himself sentimentally struck by how large the group had become.

In the great hall were Subaru, Emilia, Beatrice, Roswaal, and Ram — the original Roswaal Manor members — plus Otto, Frederica, and Petra, the later additions to the manor. On top of that, Garfiel and Ryuzu had come as the Sanctuary's key figures. Rem's sleeping body was currently entrusted to Ryuzu's house, being tended to by Pico and the other Ryuzu duplicates working in shifts.

"Subaru, you're looking kind of far off — you okay? Are you still not feeling all the way better?"

"Nah, it's not that. Just feeling a bit sentimental. The Emilia faction's people just doubled at once. If you count the Sanctuary folks and the Arlam Village folks, our numbers are gonna climb even more."

"...Yeah, that's true."

At Subaru's answer, Emilia's expression turned similarly distant, and she gave a small nod. At the Sanctuary, Emilia's evaluation had surely risen, at least among the Sanctuary's residents and the young men's brigade of Arlam Village, thanks to her showing her own will and standing firm through the defensive battle against the Great Rabbit. Being someone reliable in a crisis wasn't the same as having the qualities of a monarch, but compared to when she'd been kept at arm's length, it was a world of difference. If only there were more chances for people to interact with her, surely Emilia's character would come to be known to them as well. Subaru hoped, for now, that understanding would spread little by little that way.

"And, well, we got off topic. Let's get back to the main thing. For now, we've all shared what happened at the Sanctuary and at the manor, right? What's left is pursuing responsibility from the guy who pulled the trigger on all this — or rather, I want to hear the details..."

At Subaru's words, everyone present turned their eyes toward the culprit in question. At the edge of the great hall, Roswaal, who had been slumped limply against Ram's lap, noticed the gazes falling on him and opened his eyes.

"Oh-ho? After everyone's finished tormenting poor, defenseless me like this, you sti-ill say it's not enough beating?"

"You had it coming, so don't act all put-upon. You even got Emilia-tan to heal you with magic — don't play innocent now. More than that, I was surprised about Ram."

"...What do you mean?"

Sticking his tongue out at the ever-mouthy Roswaal, Subaru turned the conversation toward Ram. Ram, holding Roswaal's hand where it lay on her lap, furrowed her brow at Subaru's words. Meeting that gaze, Subaru began with an "I mean," and continued,

"Even accounting for you not being at full strength, I'm surprised Ram just quietly watched Roswaal's reckoning happen. I figured with Roswaal getting worked over that much, you'd definitely blow a fuse."

"That's a foolish question, Barusu. ...Even Ram doesn't think Roswaal-sama is a man who never makes a single mistake. And yet, even on a mistaken path, I want to stand at his side. It can't be helped that I feel that way. Don't you understand even that much? How foolish."

"Don't call me a fool twice in the same breath, at the start and the end of your sentence! But by that logic, you—"

"Yes. Roswaal-sama did something that deserved being struck by Garf and the others. So it's only natural that he be struck. What Ram does after — treating him gently and with care — is Ram's own business."

As ever, Ram's way of loving was masculine and consistent. No one could find fault with the way she devoted herself to Roswaal. Only Garfiel wore the face of a man caught in a dilemma — no doubt because he'd long since been talked into silence by the same technique.

"What a peculiar girl she is. Burdened with a burn like that on her stomach... If it weren't for Beatrice and the silver-haired girl, the scar surely would have remained as it was."

The one who spoke to Ram was Beatrice, seated primly beside Subaru. The girl, knees drawn up, leaning her weight against Subaru's left shoulder, had worked together with Emilia to heal Ram, whose life had hung by a thread, and had helped restore her enough to reach this point, erasing the scar in the process.

"I'm grateful to Beatrice-sama and Emilia-sama. But I don't want anyone poking their nose into what I choose to love with the life you gave back to me."

"Beatrice isn't kind enough to have gone that far for that, I suppose. Devoting yourself to a man who's done all sorts of things — if you end up hurt again, that's your own business, I suppose."

"—That, at least, I won't do again."

A word for a word. As Beatrice lined up harsh words against Ram's own, it was Roswaal, listening in, who sat up and said that. At the sound of it, the two who had been trading words held their breath, and Subaru swallowed too.

Sitting up, Roswaal now showed a bare face with the makeup wiped away. The handsome man, his clown's paint removed, covered his own left eye with one hand and swept the great hall with his remaining blue eye, then bowed his head deeply.

"I will never again take the arrogant approach of sacrificing someone in this room and, on top of that, thinking I could achieve my aims that way. — I swear it, on the soul of my teacher."

"――――"

"Besides, I'd rather not be struck by Beatrice a third time."

Raising his face, Roswaal said this with a joking air as he looked at Beatrice. Hearing it, Beatrice turned her face away with a huff.

"The second time was punishment for spouting foolishness, I suppose. As for a third time — I know nothing of that."

"That's how I'd like it to stay. I too would rather avoid being retaliated against by everyone again. With how merciless Garfiel and Subaru-kun were, even I braced myself for death."

"You're putting my hit in the same league as that? It wasn't nearly that intense."

Still, it was true that if he was going to hit him regardless, Subaru had wanted it to count. The swing he'd let loose had cleanly grazed the tip of Roswaal's jaw, rattling his inner ear and leaving him unable to stand — that had been quite a sight to see, in its own way. In any case, there had been a certain sincerity to Roswaal's declaration. If so, Subaru didn't mind trying to believe him. Probably, this change of heart had something to do with the sight of Ram, who had risked her life to burn the Gospel — something about that must have moved him.

"After being so obsessed with the Gospel, your current attitude, having cut yourself off from it, is a bit anticlimactic, honestly. I genuinely thought that talking down Roswaal before he went off the deep end was going to be my last job here at the Sanctuary."

"I nipped that troublesome bud in advance for you, then. However I dress it up, I lost my bet with you. The moment I lost myself, defied both the Gospel and our contract, and made it snow. And then, you all thoroughly crushed the result of my going that far."

"...Well, that's 'cause Garfiel and Emilia-tan busted their asses for it."

"That you don't mention your own name at that crucial moment is a bad habit of yours, isn't it. In any case, results are results. You broke through every single one of the Trials, thought to be an impossible task, and the Sanctuary has been freed. ...I've lost."

"—Hey, boss."

Roswaal shrugged his shoulders with a resigned expression. As Subaru struggled for words in response, it was Garfiel who cut in, raising a hand. He kept his sharp eyes fixed on Roswaal, his sharp fangs clicking together as he spoke.

"You seriously plannin' on keepin' this bastard as an ally? Honestly, I ain't convinced. Not even a little."

"Garfiel..."

"Course I ain't! What d'you think this bastard did to the Sanctuary and the burned-down manor, huh!? If it weren't for you and the others, boss, the village woulda been turned into rabbit feed, and sis and the girls at the manor woulda been played with and killed by that bowel-slicin' bitch! Keepin' a bastard around who'd pull somethin' like that — who knows when he'll slit our throats in our sleep next!"

Garfiel's roar came with a slap of his hand on the floor, and the great hall's structure trembled faintly. Garfiel's point was a fair one. Even though everyone had struck Roswaal in the form of "reckoning" and sat down at the table to talk, that was, after all, no more than a ritual to formalize the act of hearing him out.

It was a fact that Roswaal had put everyone here's lives in danger for the sake of his own goal, and Subaru had actually witnessed, more than once, everyone here dying. The fact that everyone could now stand here, safe and whole, together, was owed to nothing but the many worlds sacrificed, and to everyone's ability to cooperate once those worlds had been stacked up. Even Subaru had anger he wanted to hurl at Roswaal, feelings he wanted to press him on. Just as Garfiel said, there was even a part of him that wanted to turn his back and say they could never see eye to eye. But,

"Even so, we need Roswaal's power."

"Boss...!"

"For Emilia to win through in the Royal Selection, Roswaal's cooperation is indispensable. If we lose him as a backer, Emilia falls out of the Royal Selection with no way to fight back. Making him settle up is one thing, of course, but... we can't just say 'well, goodbye then.'"

"You're sayin' I gotta forgive the bastard who tried to kill my family!?"

"――――"

Garfiel's emotional words struck deep into Subaru. Even if Subaru tried to pin down his head with words alone, Garfiel wouldn't be convinced. Frederica, Ryuzu — Garfiel had nearly lost each of them. For a boy who had spent over ten years training himself, all for the sake of protecting his family, Roswaal was surely an unforgivable, mortal enemy.

"I... forgive my lord."

"...Sis!?"

But the one who raised an objection to Garfiel's argument was none other than his own blood relative — Frederica, who had herself nearly been killed. At his sister's words, her long blond hair swaying, Garfiel's eyes went wide with shock.

"What the hell are you sayin'! This bastard, he burned the whole manor down with you in it—"

"Even so, I am alive right now, like this. Thanks to Garf saving me."

"That's just how it turned out! The bastard tried to kill you! And Granny! That's — isn't that everything!?"

"...For over ten years, I was cared for by my lord."

To Garfiel, his shoulders heaving, Frederica narrowed her eyes. Her gaze, full of compassion, almost seemed moved by her grown little brother's anger.

"I took the hand my lord offered, for the sake of my own goal. And in the time since, I've learned a great deal, and that's brought me to where I am now. To put it crudely, I made use of my lord's kindness for my own ends. If that's the case, isn't it not entirely a debt owed — in terms of what's given and taken?"

"A debt of gratitude and a life ain't the same damn thing! And what about next time he betrays—"

"Ah, sorry to interrupt while things are heating up, but may I put in a word?"

As Garfiel tried to press further against Frederica's words, it was Otto this time who called a halt. Garfiel's gaze, brimming with fury, turned to Otto as he raised his hand. But Otto let that gaze slide off him, soothing him with a "calm down, calm down" air.

"Setting aside Garfiel's emotional argument for now, as for the possibility of Margrave Roswaal pulling something like this again... I think you can consider that, for the time being, essentially off the table."

"Hah? What're you talkin' about, huh. You sleepy or somethin'? Want me to put you to sleep, huh?"

"The contract. That's what I mean. Between Natsuki-san and the margrave, there's a contract governing how this whole affair's outcome is to be regarded. Isn't that right, Margrave?"

To Garfiel, on the verge of boiling over, Otto responded with unwavering calm. That he confirmed the contract not with Subaru but with Roswaal himself was, in its own way, a shrewd move. Roswaal grasped Otto's intent and raised his eyebrows slightly.

"Just as Otto-kun says, indeed. As a result of the contract established between Subaru-kun and myself, I cannot go against Subaru-kun's policy."

"Policy, my ass..."

"Give up the Gospel and cooperate to make Emilia queen. That's the content of the contract I made Roswaal agree to. So Roswaal can't pull something like this again."

Picking up the thread at the end, Subaru explained, and Garfiel ground his teeth. The outcome of the wager had been Subaru's victory. The contract bound Roswaal, and having lost the Gospel, he could no longer force the future into a mold that diverged from its writings. Of course, whether that made Roswaal a harmless existence was another matter entirely.

"Even so, that don't wipe the slate clean for what he did! If 'I won't do it again, sorry' were enough to settle things, then 'Logos's revenge isn't enough with just the right hand alone' wouldn't be a thing that happens!"

As if Garfiel's outcry still insisted on the point, opinion was split on whether to swallow Roswaal's conduct or reject it.

Those who agreed on swallowing it were Subaru, Otto, and Frederica. Those showing a stance of rejection were Garfiel and Petra. Those still undecided were, roughly, Emilia and Ryuzu.

As for Beatrice and Ram, they held to a wait-and-see stance regarding which way it would tip. That was because the two of them related to Roswaal in a way different from either camp.

"Petra..."

Like Garfiel, Petra showed her anger toward Roswaal fiercely and without compromise. At Subaru's calling of her name, she gripped the hem of her skirt, her face reddening.

"No matter what Subaru-sama says, I don't like it. My lord — the lord of the domain — tried to do something terrible to everyone in the village, didn't he? Everyone believed in the lord. I thought the lord was a good person too, but...!"

"...That's an earful, isn't it."

Even Roswaal furrowed his brow at the young girl's denunciation. Setting aside the various camps' intentions and detailed circumstances, it was likely Petra's opinion that most reflected the sentiment of the general populace. It wasn't simply because she was a child. Petra's stance vividly conveyed both an honest evaluation of the stance Roswaal had shown as a lord toward his people until now, and anger at how, this time, Roswaal had betrayed that and done what he'd done.

At present, the residents of Arlam Village and the people of the Sanctuary had not been told that Roswaal was the mastermind behind this whole incident. Petra's participation here, in a position resembling a representative, was rooted in trust — trust in the cleverness of a girl who had pieced together the truth from fragments of conversation at the manor, and who had not put it into words even so, until she had gained certainty. If Petra had shown the shallow curiosity of a child her age, digging and prying for every detail, they might have been able to smooth it over with some plausible excuse. But that hadn't happened.

"Like I keep saying, we need Roswaal's power. Cutting Roswaal loose here means closing off Emilia's path. Even if he says he doesn't want to cooperate, we've got to make him cooperate, even if it means tying him up to do it."

"Boss, that's what I'm sayin' — we're at a standstill."

"We're at a standstill. Then we need to find a compromise. What would you have Roswaal do, that would make you willing to swallow this, at least for now? Killing him outright — sorry, but that's something I have to stop."

"—Tch."

As if to check Garfiel, who was starting to rise, Beatrice, seated beside Subaru, stood up. Even a short girl was more than enough to look down on a boy sitting slumped on the floor. Clicking his tongue at the gaze directed at him, Garfiel glared at Roswaal.

"First off — guaranteed food, clothes, and shelter for the old geezers and hags who lived at the Sanctuary. Guarantee safety equally, whether for those stayin' here or those leavin'. That's the real first step toward what sis was already tryin' to do."

"Very well. I'll take that on."

"Don't you dare pull some excuse about the manor bein' burned down and havin' no house or money left, y'hear?"

"What burned down was the Mathers annex. The main residence is elsewhere. I have no intention of exposing myself to some tedious situation like a funding shortage, born of poor preparation."

Even Subaru was surprised at how confidently Roswaal answered. That the burned-down manor was the "annex" was news to Subaru. It made his earlier worry about where he'd even live once he left here feel faintly ridiculous.

"On top of that condition, promise two more things... no, 'contract' to two more things."

"――――"

At Garfiel holding up two fingers, Roswaal fell silent. Garfiel folded down one of the raised fingers toward Roswaal.

"One's what boss just said. Swear, again, in front of everyone here, that you'll keep that condition. That you won't pull no more stupid shit."

"...Ah, very well. And the other?"

"Simple. — If you break it, I'll crush your skull between my teeth myself."

A chilling, bone-deep killing intent radiated out from Garfiel. The killing intent that must have struck Roswaal head-on passed through everyone in the great hall on its mere backwash, seeming to trace along their skin like the edge of a blade.

"Very well. — I'll contract to that one equally too."

And so, only a few seconds later, when Roswaal nodded and accepted, Subaru felt the killing intent recede like a wave pulling back, and let out a breath, releasing the tension from his stiffened body. Garfiel, wearing a sour expression, propped his cheek on his hand, elbow on his crossed knee.

"...That's about it from me, for now. You settle for that too, missy."

"But..."

"Tellin' your folks, tellin' your friends — ain't gonna make nobody happy."

Petra, who still looked like she had more to say, fell silent at Garfiel's words. Then the girl turned a face on the verge of tears toward Frederica beside her, and pressed her face into Frederica's chest as she nodded, stifling her sobs. It was a sight that ached in everyone's chest.

"Anyway — setting aside the remaining issues, like guarantees for both the Arlam Village and Sanctuary residents, or what becomes of our base of operations now that the manor's burned down — can we say that, for this incident, the pursuit of responsibility and the reconciling of circumstances is done?"

Waiting until Petra had stopped crying, Subaru summed things up once more. If no one present had any objections, this would mark the end of the first stage of discussion on the problems surrounding the Sanctuary and the manor. The rest would be a matter of resolving the various remaining issues individually—

"Yes."

But while everyone else chose silence, there was one person alone who raised her hand. None other than the standard-bearer of this whole group, the one who had yet to voice her opinion on how Roswaal should be treated — Emilia. Bathing in everyone's gaze at once, she sought Subaru's permission to speak beside her.

"Go ahead, Emilia-tan. At this point, say whatever you want."

"Then, if you don't mind, I will."

Nodding to Subaru, Emilia looked toward Roswaal. Catching that gaze, Roswaal raised one eyebrow, waiting for Emilia's words with a look of mild surprise. And then, Emilia said,

"Roswaal still hasn't done the most important thing, has he. Until he does, we can't end this discussion."

"The most important thing...?"

Nothing came to mind for Roswaal at Emilia's remark, and he looked puzzled. Subaru too tilted his head, racing to figure out what Emilia was getting at. Watching everyone around her do the same, Emilia let out a small breath, and said,

"If you do something bad, you have to say sorry. That's just how it is."

"――――"

"Everyone's been saying, this whole time, do this or do that as proof you regret doing something bad, or Roswaal's been saying he swears to his teacher he won't do anything bad again — but before any of that, isn't there something he has to say first? Roswaal, have you said it to everyone, even once? I haven't heard it."

Cheeks puffed and reddening, Emilia laid into Roswaal like that. The content seemed so childish that everyone present was left speechless. But Emilia, saying it, was unmistakably, without a hint of joking, genuinely angry as she pointed this out.

Emilia was angry. It was rare, so very rare, for Emilia to truly be angry. And having triggered that rare thing, Emilia was angry. So that everyone — who had all forgotten — would do the obvious, obvious thing.

"Roswaal."

Subaru called Roswaal's name. Roswaal, who had been sitting there dumbfounded, turned his gaze toward Subaru, and at that expression of someone caught off guard, Subaru couldn't help but let his lips loosen. And then,

"Apologize, Roswaal. If we're going to keep working together from here on, that's just the basic decency of being a person."

"――――"

Subaru's agreement with Emilia's opinion, and his intent, carried through to everyone in the great hall. Roswaal, the object of their attention, sensed the meaning and the demand behind the gazes turned on him, and drew a breath.

"—Yes, that's how it should be, I suppose."

Watching Roswaal's apology, Emilia said that and smiled — and it left a deep impression.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

—Looking down at the woman sleeping in the coffin, Subaru wore an expression that defied description.

"That's a ve-e-ery rude face to be making at my teacher, isn't it."

Peering at Subaru's face from the side and finding fault with his expression was Roswaal. Inside the tomb, in the innermost chamber, the two of them — Subaru and Roswaal — stood facing each other across the coffin. With no one else allowed between them, so that they could exchange the words that needed exchanging.

But before that,

"Is this really — for real — your teacher, the 'Witch' Echidna?"

"Indeed she i-is. Her body sleeps on, unchanged from her beauty in life, within the mana ore. That I've been granted the chance to meet her again like this — it makes all the years I've lived worthwhile."

"From where I'm standing, your stalker-esque drive to keep hijacking your descendants' bodies one after another just leaves me going, 'ugh.'"

"That's the same thing Beatrice said to me, you know."

Sorry as it might be to Roswaal, who seemed delighted, Subaru couldn't bring himself to laugh. The perpetuation of life through soul-transfer technology that Roswaal had carried out, as Subaru had heard from Beatrice. He'd made a similar joke once before, but he'd never dreamed it would actually turn out to be true. On the other hand, he'd come to accept, at least, that the whole family's supposed obsession with Echidna had in fact just been Roswaal himself, individually, disguised as a family trait.

"As for the guilt toward the descendants you took over... I guess there's no point asking."

"The Mathers family was, from the start, a bloodline built for that very purpose. I began it, I continued it, I built it up. Whoever might condemn me for it, I feel not the slightest twinge of conscience."

"How admirable. A paragon of stalkers."
