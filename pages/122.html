Arc 3 Chapter 45: "Encounter"

—I don't want to die.

"Hah, hah, haah, haah, hah—"

Gasping for breath, legs tangling gracelessly beneath him, spitting out the grass and dirt that had gotten into his mouth when he fell, drool and bloody vomit dribbling from the corners of his lips, he ran on through a darkness he couldn't see beyond.

The light of the Crystal Lamps had vanished into the distance, and darkness had fallen over the highway, where even the moonlight was blocked out. A silence loud enough to echo inside his skull, and no sound he could rely on to be heard. Not even his own breathing, not even the beat of his own heart—nothing. He'd been left behind in a world of soundlessness.

—I don't want to die, I don't want to die, I don't want to die.

He kicked at the ground, surely moving forward through the darkness. And yet the darkness refused to clear, refused even to make certain where he stood, so that he couldn't tell where the feet striking the earth were even trying to go. He'd already lost count of how many times his legs had tangled, how many times he'd fallen. If each time had knocked him further off the original path, then where, exactly, was he headed now?

There should have been a place he had to go. There should have been people he had to meet. There should have been people who had aimed for that place together with him.

And yet now, surrounded by an endless darkness with no end in sight, he was utterly, entirely alone.

"Somebody—!"

The darkness that filled his vision, the silence that cut him off from the world—it was terrifying, and he raised his voice. From a throat scraped raw, a voice he shook until he could taste blood, surely reached far into the night beyond. But the reply to that scream was only the same punishing, repeated silence.

No one was there. He'd been left behind. No sign of anyone coming back for him. Of course not. No one wants to die. Knowing that staying here, coming back here, staying within reach of this darkness, meant losing your life—who could possibly come back for him?

He could feel despair slip smoothly into his heart. His chest went cold, and his limbs began to stop moving, as if ice water had been poured through his veins in place of blood. His body, recognizing the end, was refusing to resist any further. And yet—

"No... I don't... want to die..."

His heart, his emotions, his soul rejected the way his body was ready to accept the end. Shaking his head, throwing a tantrum against whatever fate was trying to force this ending on him, he dragged forward limbs that carried no fighting spirit, limbs as heavy as if stuffed with lead. Forward—no, he was fleeing. Fleeing, and fleeing still. No destination. Fleeing in a panic. Wandering, endlessly wandering on.

—Far, far away, from beyond the darkness, a sound reaches him.

Shaking the air, pushing the darkness aside, the end drew near, taking on shape. Desperate to put distance between himself and the thing closing in from behind, he kept fleeing, face twisted in terror, at the speed of a crawling turtle.

Exposing himself to this graceless, pitiful humiliation, Subaru thought—

—How on earth did it come to this?

"Every merchant and dragon carriage here—anyone willing to sell me your legs for money, I'm buying, all of you!"

At Subaru's "business proposal," the traveling merchants exchanged glances, their cheeks loosening into distasteful smirks, apparently taking it for some kind of joke. But as if to embody Subaru's intent, Rem lifted the bag of traveling funds she'd been entrusted with, opened its mouth, and swept it around so everyone could see the contents inside—and every one of their expressions changed.

From there, led by Otto, a shoving, jostling scramble of volunteers began. In the end, of the fourteen merchants gathered at that waypoint, ten agreed to accompany Subaru and the others. Negotiations were rocky at first, but Otto's proposal brought it to a neat landing. A method that left no one at a loss—

"The four of you with large dragon carriages and cargo beds will have your cargo entrusted for transport by the others. The proceeds will be divided later through the guild in the royal capital. In that case, we'll reconcile the fare against the sale proceeds, of course."

Having deftly reconciled everyone's conflicting demands, Otto explained this with a beaming face as he stood at the head of the ten who would accompany them. Despite being the youngest of the group, he'd instantly worked out a path everyone could accept, and the way he'd talked it through showed a natural gift for trade. Of course, from his own perspective, it was probably less altruism and more a fierce determination not to miss this once-in-a-lifetime chance to neatly offload stock that could otherwise make or break his career as a merchant.

"I'm glad you're willing to buy my oil, but why exactly do you need so many dragon carriage legs on top of that?"

Watching his fellow merchants transfer their cargo, Otto crossed his arms and put the question to Subaru, who was fretting over their departure time. In response, Subaru prefaced with a "well," then said,

"We're headed back to Mathers territory now. Officially, I'm registered as a manservant at Margrave Roswaal's estate."

"I'm aware. Margrave Roswaal L. Mathers, the one with a taste for Demi-Humans. I hear he's quite the eccentric even among titled Lugunican nobles."

Subaru gave a wry smile at Otto, who kept glancing up at him, gauging his reaction. If it had been Ram who heard that just now, she'd have been furious.

"Well, I won't deny it. He's a weirdo, that part's true."

"Saying that about your employer. Well, I did ask hoping for that kind of answer. Still, Natsuki-san, you don't look much like a noble's servant."

"Still in training. The only things I've passed so far are sewing and bed-making."

Anyway—

"I'll take it on faith that you're on an errand for this margrave... but why do you need dragon carriages for it? Honestly, wouldn't a margrave have his own?"

"Like I said, it's about numbers. There's a lot of what we're carrying, so I want the cargo beds empty if possible. ...Though in Otto's case, buying up the oil is unavoidable anyway."

"Much appreciated. So, this cargo you'll be transporting is..."

His mouth smiled, his hands rubbed together as if trying to read Subaru's mood. But behind his narrowed, half-lowered eyelids, his eyes had gone cold with calculation as they studied Subaru. He clearly wasn't taking Subaru's claimed status as the margrave's errand-runner entirely on faith. And regardless of whether that status was true or false, he seemed concerned about just how dangerous whatever he'd be hauling might be.

A reasonable worry, at that. If Subaru's claim to be the margrave's retainer was true, then he'd be made to carry something that required hiring outside people to transport. If it was false, he'd end up complicit in whatever scheme some group impersonating the margrave's people was running. It was only natural he'd exercise maximum caution.

And Subaru couldn't afford to have the deal fall through over such suspicions. So he decided to lay out his reason for hiring them without holding anything back.

"What we're carrying, see, is people."

"People?"

"Yes. As in, human beings. There's a village near the margrave's estate. A small village—all the villagers together don't even number a hundred. I want to load them up and move them."

That was the reason Subaru had hit on for hiring Otto and the rest. The dragon carriage Subaru and Rem rode in was quite large, and being built for hauling heavy freight, the dragon pulling it was correspondingly sized. According to the shop's people, it could easily pull a carriage even with a dozen-odd people riding in it.

"People... I see. Surely, and I mean surely, you're not asking us to transport corpses, are you? Because if so, I'm very sorry to say I'll have to decline."

"...It's exactly so that doesn't happen that I want to bring you all along."

If so, with multiple dragon carriages, it should be possible to evacuate the entire village.

At first, before he'd run into Otto and the others, Subaru—desperate to rejoin Emilia—had completely forgotten about the villagers' ability to flee on foot. Had he been slower to realize this, in the worst case, he might have had to load only the estate's people, Emilia included, along with the village children, onto the dragon carriage to carry them to safety, then risked multiple round trips at his own peril.

In that sense, meeting Otto and the others here was a stroke of fate's mischief—a rare bit of chance working in Subaru's favor.

Otto, brow furrowing in confusion, seemed unable to shake his distrust of Subaru's words. Subaru uncrossed his arms and, prefacing with "actually," said,

"We're planning a large-scale manhunt—er, beast hunt, sweeping the whole area around the margrave's estate soon."

"A hunt? Sweeping the mountains, you mean?"

Leaning forward at the unexpected word, Otto watched as Subaru gave a solemn nod of confirmation and arranged his face into a grave expression.

"There's a Witchbeast that's lived in that area for ages—a Jagganoth. Up till now we managed to coexist, humans and Witchbeast, by keeping a barrier up between us... but recently, a Jagganoth crossed the barrier and there was an incident. Quite a commotion."

"And that's why it's come to a hunting sweep? But still..."

Something seemed to be nagging at Otto, and as he pressed further, Subaru said nothing and simply pulled up his right sleeve. Seeing it, Otto swallowed a gasp deep in his throat.

What his trembling eyes had caught sight of was Subaru's upper arm, still marked deeply with the traces of lacerations from fangs and claws. And beneath his clothes, several more scars that would never fade remained carved into him.

"Thanks to the margrave's kindness, I got treated in the royal capital after nearly dying. Now that the treatment's wrapped up, this is me on my way back."

"I—I see... So that's it. But then, why wouldn't the margrave himself go around announcing it, instead of you arranging dragon carriages along the road, Natsuki-san?"

"The margrave doesn't plan to relocate the villagers—he means to clean it up quick. But as you can see from my body, there's a chance the Witchbeast could pull some nasty surprise. So I want to have insurance in place. Not that I don't trust my master, mind you."

Lowering his eyes with an apologetic air as he said this, Otto gave a small groan of "hmmm," and after a moment's silence,

"I understand. I'm sorry for prying into something you probably didn't want asked about. I won't mention the injuries—I'll explain things to the others properly."

Otto glanced at Subaru with concern, a bitter expression crossing his good-natured face. He seemed to feel guilty for having unintentionally pried into Subaru's wounds. That, Subaru thought, was a little too soft a spot for a merchant to have.

"Don't worry about it. You can tell them exactly what I told you—it's better than them suspecting something's off."

Otto, apparently good-natured to the core, seemed satisfied with that and nodded a "yes, sir." Watching him, Subaru thought that he himself—quietly clenching a fist inside—was by far the worse person here.

It was roughly an hour after negotiations concluded that they left the merchants' camp behind.

Cargo was transferred onto four dragon carriages, leaving nine merchants with lightened cargo beds, plus Otto—still loaded with his oil—for ten companions in total. Combined with the carriage Subaru and Rem rode in, that made eleven carriages in all—cramped, certainly, but enough that they should be able to carry every villager.

Parting ways with the group staying behind at the camp, who would head to the royal capital once morning came, Subaru and the other eleven carriages set out along the highway in the dead of night. Among those staying behind was the gaunt merchant who'd introduced Otto to Subaru—Ketty. Watching her wave, Subaru thought once again how thoroughly luck was against them ever truly connecting.

"Running through the night without stopping, we should cross into Mathers territory sometime past dawn."

Otto, riding alongside in his carriage, called out to Subaru with a sidelong glance. He didn't seem to be raising his voice particularly, yet it still carried over to Subaru—apparently another effect of the Earth Dragons' wind Divine Protection. If it kept wind and jostling from affecting the riders, it must interfere with things like this too. With cloth wrapped around his mouth against the sand, making it a chore to breathe, Subaru was honestly grateful not to have to raise his own voice. He nodded and answered, "Sorry we can't stop to rest."

"No, no! I've got no complaints whatsoever. Getting to offload my stock and getting a fat fare on top—I'm unstoppable. I've driven three days and nights straight before, you know!"

"You'd probably collapse and pass out the second the deal was done."

"Wha—!? Did you just read my mind!?"

Subaru gave a wry smile at Otto, flustered to have his tried-and-true punchline stolen out from under him, then turned his gaze to Rem, who held the reins. With the sand-cloth Subaru had rewrapped covering the lower half of her face as she stared straight ahead, Rem's expression was even harder to read than usual. For Subaru, who'd started to pride himself on being able to sense the movements of her emotions despite her impassive face, that was a rather unwelcome situation. Especially now, when he couldn't stop wondering what she was thinking. And then—

"—Subaru-kun."

"...! Ah, yeah, what's up, Rem? Something wrong?"

Called out to by the very person he'd been staring intently at in profile, Subaru swallowed his surprise deep in his throat before affecting a deliberately calm voice. Hearing it, Rem gave a small shake of her head. "No," she said,

"You'd gone quiet, so I thought you might be tired. It's a bit rough with the shaking and the sand, but if you're sleepy, it's fine to sleep. Rem will keep watch."

"I'd love to take you up on that, but I'm wide awake. Besides, it'd look pretty pathetic if I made you do all the work."

"But Subaru-kun, you're still recovering."

Subaru found himself at a loss for words at how Rem, even while being considerate, still stood her ground. Her tone was gentle, but her will was firm and unyielding. At the very least, there was absolutely no chance Rem would assign Subaru any role on this carriage that might put a burden on him. Every time she treated him with such kindness, a thorn he couldn't pull out drove itself into Subaru's chest.

Not knowing her true intentions behind serving him with such wholehearted devotion, wanting to know, not wanting to know—his contradictory heart twisted itself further.

"Rem, is..."

"Yes?"

The anxiety had slipped out of him unbidden, and he'd called her name. It seemed to have reached her clearly; she responded at once and now waited for Subaru's next words. The source of the anxiety he'd inadvertently stepped into. Even now, he could retreat with a simple "never mind," and Rem probably wouldn't press him. But not knowing what Rem was thinking that would keep her from pressing him was itself sure to torment him. If that was how it would be, better to make it clear once and for all.

"Rem, don't you have any doubts about what I'm doing? I haven't explained anything to you. Not about the Witch's Cult, not about hiring these merchants."

Gritting his teeth, swallowing down his weakness, forcing down a voice threatening to waver with anxiety, he asked the question. It was something he'd wanted to ask and dreaded asking, all this time. Hearing it, Rem closed her eyes once, then—

"Roswaal-sama left word with me. That in the royal capital, all my actions should follow Subaru-kun's wishes."

She answered this way, letting a servant's face show through, her emotions frozen over. Hearing that answer, Subaru's expression stiffened rigid.

Because Roswaal had ordered it, she followed Subaru's words. That would mean—

"Left word... from Roswaal...?"

"Yes. Though he didn't order anything specific for me to do. Just that in the royal capital, whatever I do, I should follow Subaru-kun's lead. And Rem intended to do so, as much as she could."

Somehow, Rem's supplementary words didn't sink in properly. All that echoed, flat and unrelenting, through Subaru's head was—

"Roswaal's... order..."

That she didn't object to Subaru's actions, that she followed him, was because her master had commanded it. Which meant that everything about her following Subaru up until now might not reflect her true intentions at all.

No—more than that, even the fact that Rem was here beside him right now.

"Subaru-kun?"

Suspicious of Subaru's sudden silence, Rem tilted her head toward him, her brow faintly furrowed. Even that worried gaze she turned on him, Subaru now found himself unable to accept with any honest feeling.

The way she worried over him and looked out for him like this, the way she quietly supported him when he seemed about to collapse, the way she stayed constantly by the side of a Subaru on the verge of going mad with loneliness, the way she stood by him, affirming him as he did what he believed was right—was all of it only because Roswaal's order sat at the back of her mind?

To put it more extremely—did Rem, in her heart, not actually approve of what Subaru was doing at all?

"——!"

Nausea surged up, and Subaru swallowed back something sour rising up his throat. The urge to vomit, dammed up inside his body with nowhere to go, turned into a bomb that spread a chill of horror through his entire body, raging through him. His limbs went numb, his vision flickered, and his brain itched unbearably. The impulse to smash his skull open right now, thrust his fingers inside, and claw at the contents made his breath ragged.

But the fingers digging into his scalp never once broke through his skull. All he could do was grind his teeth against the itch that wouldn't subside, and endure the pressure, as if his stomach were being seized in a fist.

He didn't want to think about it. Didn't want to think. The more he thought, the more he looked back, the more he wanted it—the further what he'd wanted receded, his ideals turning into daydreams, his hope repainted with despair and disappointment.

"Subaru-kun?"

He hated it. He was sick of it. He didn't want to think. Didn't want to doubt. Didn't want to believe. Didn't want to be betrayed.

He buried his head, cutting off every response from the outside world, closing himself off within. Rem called Subaru's name several times, but seeming to think he was trying to sleep in that posture, she stopped calling out further and turned to face forward once more.

At this moment, Subaru had finally, truly, of his own accord, become entirely alone.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

"—aru-kun. I'm sorry, please wake up. Subaru-kun."

At the call, there was a sensation of his consciousness being roused.

The presence of someone's hand touching his shoulder. He lifted his face, rubbed his bleary eyelids with his hand to clear his vision, and reflected in his slowly clearing sight was a familiar girl's face.

"Rem, huh. What's up?"

Consciousness surfacing from unconsciousness, a dull ache struck the depths of his chest the moment he recognized her. But it wasn't unbearable. Straining not to let it show on his face, Subaru waited for Rem, who'd been reaching a hand toward him, to answer. After apologizing, looking sorry for having woken him, she said,

"We should be reaching the fork in the road soon. It's a road that's impossible to mistake in daylight, but right now we only have the Crystal Lamp's light to go by."

As she spoke, she pointed at the light cast from the Crystal Lamp fixed beneath the thick neck of the Earth Dragon still single-mindedly kicking at the ground. In this world without streetlamps or other lighting fixtures, securing visibility at night meant relying on the glow of Lagmite Ore, or the light of primitive torches and the like—or, more naturally but even less reliably, moonlight.

For an Earth Dragon with good night vision, that Lagmite Ore alone was probably enough to run without losing the way, but—

"Unfortunately, it's basically invisible to me. There's a small one on the driver's seat too, technically, but it only lights up what's right at hand."

There was one built into the carriage's interior, but even that wasn't enough to cast light all the way to the driver's seat. In any case—

"So, you're saying you're losing track of the road?"

"No. I want to check the map before I lose track of the road. There's a map packed with the cargo at your feet, Subaru-kun—could you take it out?"

"At my feet, this?"

He groped around in the darkness with his toes, then drew up whatever had touched his foot. He hauled the fairly heavy thing up onto his lap, thrust a hand inside to rummage through it, but—

"Uh... I can't tell which one's the map. Actually, even if I find the map, isn't it too dark to see it anyway?"

"Please don't underestimate an oni's night vision... is what I'd like to say, but I'm not actually that confident about my night vision, specifically..."

Rem's expression clouded, unsure what to do. Then, suddenly, something flashed to mind for Subaru in the darkness. Oh, right—

"Hold on a sec."

Calling out to Rem, Subaru pulled out the small pouch containing his personal belongings. Inside were things like the hand towel Rem had made him carry, and a bag with his spending money. He rummaged through it, searching for the feel of the thing he wanted,

"Found it."

What was in Subaru's outstretched hand was a cold, hard object. Even pulled out, Rem couldn't confirm what it was in the dark and tilted her head, but Subaru, holding something in his hands for the first time in a while, flipped it open and pressed the power button.

"Haven't had this on in a while—better not be dead. ...Oh."

After a moment's silence, the "startup" effect surfaced on the screen. Then, exactly one second later, Subaru's hands were suddenly lit up brilliantly. At the sudden burst of light, Rem raised her eyebrows in surprise.

"Subaru-kun, what is that?"

"A lost technology—no, a technology of the future—a keitai denwa, a cell phone. Barely, but it's still alive, thank god."

Powered on for the first time since the day he was summoned to this world, the phone glowed brightly despite its nearly-dead battery. It was one of the items from his original world he'd been able to bring with him when he fell into this one. He'd brought a few other personal items too, but of all of them, this cell phone was probably by far the most useful. Though, of course, that usefulness came with the condition that the battery had to last.

"I never knew when I'd need it, so I kept it off, but I never imagined the day it'd come in handy would be as a flashlight."

Giving thanks to civilization for a use quite different from its intended one, Subaru lit up the inside of the cargo. In the bag illuminated by the light, he quickly found the rolled-up map and pulled it out, holding it out to Rem's lap.

"I'll keep it lit, so go ahead and check the map."

"Yes, thank you very much."

While lighting the map Rem's eyes were fixed on, Subaru let out a small breath where she couldn't see it. Seems like he was managing not to let this trembling inner turmoil show, he thought.

"Natsuki-san, what is that? I've never seen anything like it."

His voice nearly cracking with excitement, it was Otto who was intensely curious about the phone in Subaru's hand. Otto had pulled his carriage up on their left and now leaned in to peer at it.

"A Crystal Lamp I've never seen before... no, it doesn't look like crystal at all. Actually, I've never even seen the material it's made of."

Drawn by Otto's excited examination, another carriage that had been lagging slightly caught up on the right side. Craning his neck to peer over as well was an older-looking man with a bandana wrapped around his head. Despite his age, his eyes lit up with a childlike sparkle, riveted on the phone in Subaru's hand.

Normally, Subaru would have been pleased by their reaction and cracked a joke or two, but unfortunately, he didn't have the emotional room for that right now. Shaking his head, he prefaced his answer to the two of them, hungry for an explanation, with "sorry, but,"

"It's a secret tool the margrave lent me. If word of the details gets out, this thing might just disappear. Better to forget you saw it."

"Whoa, what's with those shady circumstances that reek of money?"

If anything, that only piqued Otto's interest further, and Subaru inwardly gritted his teeth. But their conversation was cut off by Rem's voice, raised in a "I understand." She lifted her face from the map and pointed ahead along their course.

"A little further and we'll reach Flugel's Tree. From there, if we follow the highway northeast, we should enter Mathers territory."

"Flugel's Tree?"

At the unfamiliar term, Subaru tilted his head, and Otto, still breathing hard with excitement, raised a finger. "Well, you see,"

"Flugel's Tree is the name of this huge tree that towers up as if piercing the clouds, standing right in the middle of the Lifaus Highway. 'Piercing the clouds' might be an exaggeration, but it really is enormous. Supposedly, according to legend, it was planted a few hundred years ago by a Sage named Flugel."

"So that's why it's Flugel's Tree. Any word on why he planted it, or the circumstances around all that?"

"Well, it's a story from a few hundred years back, after all. And honestly, this Flugel fellow, nobody's too sure what he did besides plant that tree."

What kind of Sage was that, Subaru wondered, but didn't say it aloud. As they continued driving straight for a while longer, gradually, even to Subaru's eyes, a huge tree towering beyond the night came clearly into view.

"I see... this is something, huh."

Comparing it to his original world, maybe something on the scale of the "thousand-year-old" giant trees he'd seen in TV specials would match it. This one's age, if Otto's account was accurate, was only a few hundred years, but whether from a difference in the plants' growth rates or something else, in that shorter span it had already achieved a majesty to rival them.

A trunk so tall its top couldn't be seen even looking up. Branches stretching skyward in vast, countless numbers, the thick foliage boasting a volume to match. Supporting that thick, sturdy trunk were countless roots, crawling over the ground like writhing great serpents, sinking into the earth. Rather than standing within some vast forest, this single enormous tree stood alone in the middle of a plain—which meant there could hardly be a landmark more conspicuous than this.

Beside Subaru, dumbstruck at its sheer size, Rem coolly worked the reins, shifting the carriage's course slightly. This, going by the map, would take them northeast into Mathers territory. Watching the great tree draw near and then, eventually, recede behind them, Subaru murmured,

"If the battery had more juice, and I were in the mood, I'd have taken a picture."

Peering at the screen showing only a single bar of battery remaining, he muttered this while choking down his frustration. Catching Subaru's mutter, Otto called back, "Eh, what was that!?" and Subaru answered irritably, "Nothing!" before lowering himself back down onto the driver's seat. Then, pulling his attention away from the great tree, Subaru suddenly noticed something.

"Huh, where'd the bandana guy who was riding on the right go?"

Scanning around, the person who should have been there was nowhere to be found. Wondering if he'd suddenly fallen behind, Subaru checked briefly to the rear, but there was only the carriage that had been driving behind the bandana man's, with that one spot hollowed out completely empty.

"Don't tell me he got so absorbed looking up at the tree that he got left behind."

"What's wrong, Natsuki-san? Looking for something?"

"Not something, someone—one of your people. Where'd that dignified older guy who suited the bandana so well, the one riding on this side just now, go? Did he get nostalgic and climb the tree or something?"

Flinging the sarcasm irritably at the carefree Otto, Subaru pressed him on the oversight. But Otto, hit with Subaru's irritation, blinked in blank confusion, tilting his head as if he genuinely had no idea what Subaru meant.

"What are you talking about, Natsuki-san? Nobody was ever driving on my other side."

—Huh?

Subaru couldn't make sense of the words he'd just heard, and stood dumbstruck, unable even to speak. Watching Subaru freeze in place, there wasn't a trace of joking in Otto's manner. He seemed genuinely, thoroughly puzzled, having taken Subaru's remark completely at face value. But that, precisely, was what made no sense to Subaru at all.

"What are you talking about? Just a minute ago, he was right there next to you, staring at the phone all fascinated. Practically drooling, staring at it."

"Ah, is that what it's called, a 'phone'? Also, is my personal safety guaranteed now that I've heard that?"

"Don't joke around with me!"

Snapping at Otto, who still seemed intent on brushing off Subaru's words, Subaru turned to look right. There, as before, was only empty space stretching out—no sign of the person who should have been there. But he couldn't have not existed. His face, his voice—Subaru remembered them clearly.

"—?"

And then, in the midst of glaring fixedly at that empty space, his vision blurred hazily.

Like a mist had settled over his eyes, an indistinct sensation made Subaru blink several times. But even so, the wrongness wouldn't wash away. A hazy darkness continued running alongside Subaru unchanged, and it struck him as so eerie, so unbearably unsettling.

So Subaru opened the phone he'd closed, and lit up the space to his right once more. Seeking some trace of the person who should have been there, wanting to know the identity of this indescribable sensation that refused to be shaken off—

And then, in the light he cast—

"——ah?"

Subaru's eyes met an eye—unbearably enormous—floating in the darkness.

A roar thundered out, and fog began to settle over the Lifaus Plains.

—Fog settles over the Lifaus Plains.
