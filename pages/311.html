One Day II Part 4: "Birth of the Emilia Camp's Minister of Domestic Affairs, Esquire"

Chipping away at the towering stack of documents piled up before him, Otto listened to Subaru and the others, seated on the reception sofa.

He pulled the papers he needed out from the pile, and now and then set his quill pen scratching across them. On one sheet he scrawled out some calculation, and once he'd worked out the numbers, jotted the result onto the relevant document, cross-referencing it against the notes on a nearby paper before stamping his seal. His hands moved with practiced fluency, and his eyes darted about so busily it was hard to tell whether he was actually paying the conversation any mind at all — but judging by the occasional interjection he tossed in, he wasn't simply letting it wash over him.

While Emilia watched Otto's work with an admiring expression, Subaru gave a full account of what they'd just been up to, and the reasoning behind it. He finished his account at almost the exact moment Otto set his quill pen back into its stand with a click.

"Oh, so it's about patching things up between siblings. ...If that's all it was, you could've just come to me."

"How's that now?"

"What you want, basically, is advice suited to the situation from someone who actually has brothers and sisters, right? In that case, I think I'm a much better bet for the job than you lot of only-children — I've got siblings both older and younger than me. Yes indeed."

Subaru couldn't help but be taken aback by Otto's sheer confidence. He'd never actually asked about Otto's family before, but if his claim was to be believed, he was apparently the middle child, with both an older brother and a younger one. If true, that made him exactly the advisor Subaru and Emilia had been desperate for. Still, Subaru did have one concern.

"But weren't you the one who got kicked out of the house for being useless? If your family was all sunshine and roses, fine, but hearing failure stories from a guy who's been cut off isn't gonna help much."

"Who said anything about being disowned for being incompetent!? I've never once told you a story like that! My brother inherited the family business, so as the second son I simply took the freedom that came with it and went into trading! And for what it's worth, I like to think I'm just as quick-witted as my brother or my little brother — if not more so!"

"That's just what you think — meanwhile back home, they're all relieved they finally got rid of the troublemaker."

"Do you have some kind of problem with me being here!?"

Otto slammed the desk, face reddening, and Subaru shook his head. "Not at all." The thought of Otto not being here was frightening enough on its own. It was just that, before Subaru could put his gratitude into words, some smart remark always slipped out first. That, too, was probably some peculiar virtue unique to a man named Otto Suwen.

"But somehow, Otto-kun just doesn't give off the air of someone you can rely on," Emilia said. "I wonder why... even though he's helped us out so much."

"W-what, even you, Emilia-sama...?"

Emilia, chin in hand, put Subaru's very own thoughts into words. It seemed she, too, was a fellow victim struck by Otto's peculiar virtue. He was a man whose sense of dependability seemed to fade in inverse proportion to his actual competence.

"Making Emilia-tan worry like this — what a sinful bastard you are."

"That is way too much of a stretch! What did I even do!?"

"Anyway, Otto-kun. If you could, I'd love to hear what you think we should do about those two."

"You slid right into the main topic! What is with the two of you, master and knight both!?"

After a bout of overreacting, Otto's face settled into the resignation of a man who'd realized that making a fuss was pointless. His chair creaked as he leaned back, and he ran a hand through his gray hair.

"Well, I think the most important thing, first, is how the two of them actually feel. From what I can see, Garfiel isn't the problem. That stubbornness of his is just a childish thing, and deep down he loves his family — I can easily picture him wanting to make up."

"Yeah, I think so too. I think Garfiel wants to make up. It's Frederica who can't seem to close the distance."

"Frederica-san's position is a bit more difficult, I'd say. As the older sister, she's the senior party, so making up would require her to be the one who gives ground. But from what you've told me, none of this is her fault. Whether she can show the pure generosity befitting an older sister — how much tolerance she can muster for her junior's little tantrum — that's what this whole affair is going to hinge on... What's wrong?"

Subaru had been staring at Otto, wide-eyed and rigid, as he laid it all out so logically. Catching the look, Otto asked what was wrong, and Subaru shook his head. "No, it's nothing—"

"—just, a way more serious opinion than I expected came flying out, and I was trying to figure out where to slip in a joke..."

"It's a serious topic, so of course I followed a serious process to a serious conclusion!"

"My bad. Guess I just don't have what it takes to keep up with your complicated setups..."

"Do you actually want this problem solved, or do you just want to stir the pot!?"

Of course he wanted it solved — it was just that fighting one's deepest instincts was no easy thing. Setting aside Subaru and Otto's usual back-and-forth, Emilia was nodding, clearly moved by what Otto had said. Then she went on, "So then..."

"What we need to resolve first is how Frederica feels, right?"

"I'd think so, yes. Frederica-san doesn't seem the type to be so twisted up that she can't forgive Garfiel, either. In fact, I doubt it'll end up being all that complicated a matter. If anything, it's the sort of thing time alone would resolve—"

"The whole point of what we're doing right now is that we don't want to leave it to time — we want it sorted out quickly. Were you even listening to a word of this from start to finish?"

"I don't deserve to be talked to like that!"

Seeing this was about to end up exactly the same way as always, Subaru gave a scornful little snort. Otto bristled, but Subaru pressed on to needle him further. "In that case—"

"—that generosity an older sibling's supposed to show, when would you actually pull it off, huh? If you've got a younger brother like you say, there must've been times you two fought and you, as the older one, showed him how big your heart was. I wanna see it, hear it, sing it, the whole story."

"Let's skip the 'see it' and 'sing it,' please. Right, a personal story, then. Actually, believe it or not, my family relations were fairly harmonious. Both my brother and my little brother turned out to be decent people, and my parents were kind, too... huh, wait, I can't seem to recall ever fighting..."

"Useless!!"

"W-what a thing to say! What's wrong with a harmonious family!? Are you saying a relationship where you've never fought isn't the real thing? That's not true at all! What could you possibly have to complain about in a relationship that's peaceful and free of trouble!?"

"At least here and now, you drew the worst possible card!"

Subaru had expected to draw out some deeply meaningful tale, only to find Otto's hand was completely empty. He was, after all, someone who could handle even Subaru's usual unreasonable demands well enough day to day, so it was probably no surprise his family relations had never once boiled over into shouting matches. Or perhaps everyone in the Suwen family shared Otto's own knack for being the one everyone teased. Peaceful, sure, but a temporary paradise that only existed because no one there ever played the tormentor.

"Little hothouse flower, raised without a care in the world..."

"I feel like I just got insulted in some seriously creative way — is that just my imagination!?"

"...Heh heh."

Otto shouted back at Subaru, whose insult had taken flight on the wings of his imagination. Watching the exchange, Emilia suddenly put a hand to her mouth, unable to hold back a smile. Her smile drew both their eyes, and she gave a small shake of her head.

"No, sorry. It's just, the way you two go back and forth just now looked so much like a really close, friendly squabble... isn't it just like brothers?"

"I'd like to think my own brothers treated me with a bit more care than that, though..."

"Don't say that, big brother. You just never noticed — back home, this is probably exactly how your brother treated you too. Face reality, bro."

"Shut up!"

Otto no longer had the energy to argue back properly. Subaru, pouting at his reaction, kept rattling off variations — "Onii-sama, Aniki-kun, Ani-i, Nii-ya, Onii-chama, Ani-gimi-sama" — and so on. Watching this, Emilia suddenly clapped her hands.

"Oh! In that case, how do you two usually make up? I get the feeling Otto-kun's always the one who backs down — if we knew how, wouldn't that get us a little closer to an answer?"

"Wow, it's just been naturally decided that Otto's always the one who caves."

"So then, will you be the one to give in first, Subaru?"

"Me... even if I were to yield to anyone else in this whole wide world... to Otto — to Otto alone, I could never, ever yield...!"

"Shut up!"

Otto blew away the little theatrics with a short, sharp bark, then sat scratching his temple in thought. He seemed to be genuinely mulling over Emilia's question, trying to come up with an answer.

"Um, let's see, when Natsuki-san and I get into it, what do I usually do..."

"Well, mostly you just cry yourself to sleep and let it go!"

"The answer came out so obvious I didn't even need to think, and now I don't know what to do with myself!"

Emilia stood and patted the head of Otto, who had it buried in his hands on the desk, as if to comfort him. Jealous of Otto for receiving Emilia's kindness, Subaru nevertheless brushed off his knees and rose from his seat, sensing there was nothing more to be gained here.

"Well, it was some help, at least. For now, let's go talk to Frederica, and depending on how that goes, we'll move forward with Emilia-tan's plan."

"Just so you know, plain old meddling might end up being nothing but wasted effort."

"I figure that beats actually breaking a bone by a mile. You disagree?"

"...Hah."

Hearing Subaru's answer, Otto let out a resigned sigh. But the way the corner of his mouth had loosened was answer enough to the question. Emilia, too, must have read the same thing in Otto's expression that Subaru had. She gave a light stretch where she stood and smiled at him.

"Well then, Subaru and I will get out of your hair. Sorry for interrupting your work when you're so busy, Otto-kun."

"No, no, I was the one who invited you over. Besides, I'm surrounded by a mountain of stifling paperwork here — a little breather now and then makes it easier..."

Responding to Emilia's consideration with that, Otto's expression suddenly changed.

"No, wait, why exactly am I laboring away at Margrave Mathers's territorial paperwork in the first place...? Somewhere along the way I ended up helping with some incomprehensible domestic administration, and now I've even been granted access to view the territory's management records... All I was supposed to be doing was submitting an estimate for the oil purchase calculations..."

"Whoa there, Emilia-tan. Any longer and we're gonna start getting in the way of Otto's work. Let's you and me hold hands nice and cozy and make our exit!"

"Eh? Ah, yes, all right."

They left Otto behind, hand to his forehead, just beginning to have doubts about his present circumstances. Taking advantage of the confusion, Subaru grabbed Emilia's hand and headed for the door out of 'Otto's Office.' Just as he laid a hand on the door to make his exit, Otto called out to his retreating back.

"Ah, Natsuki-san—"

"Ah? What is it. Relax, it's fine. You sitting there isn't a mistake, or hypnosis, or some elaborate suggestion, none of that. It's just that you got led there by circumstance and some skillful talking..."

Subaru turned back, but his words trailed off midway. It was because the look Otto was giving him seemed, somehow, too weighty for Subaru to keep joking through — as if he were about to bring up something serious. Subaru fell silent, and beside him Emilia tilted her head, puzzled. With both of them in view, Otto hesitated for the briefest of moments. But it dissolved away in the short span of time it took Emilia to turn her head.

"...No, it's nothing."

"What, now I'm curious. If you started to say something, just say it."

"It's not that I wouldn't like to... but honestly, right now it's still the kind of thing you can't quite get a grip on, like trying to grab a cloud. I'll tell you once it's taken a shape with a little more hope to it. There's still a lot even I don't know — whether this will end up helping, or just being one more thing to worry about."

Scratching his head, Otto concluded his reason for hesitating with that. For a moment, Subaru tried silently to will Otto into changing his mind, but Otto simply sat back down in his chair and picked his quill pen back up.

"I'll get back to work, then. I'll leave the Garfiel matter in your hands. If the military officers aren't in a state where they can do their jobs properly, us civil officers can't work in peace back at the rear, after all."

"...Got it. But once you can say it, you say it, Mister Minister of Domestic Affairs."

"Yes, of course... wait, civil officer? Minister of Domestic Affairs...?"

"Let's go, Emilia-tan. Any longer here and we'll just be in the way!"

Leaving Otto behind to once again wrestle with the growing gap between his self-image and his actual position, Subaru hurriedly pulled Emilia along by the hand and left the room. Emilia blinked in bewilderment, but just before the door swung fully shut, she turned back to Otto.

"Ah, um, well, Otto-kun, take care and do your best!"

With those words — impossible to tell whether they were meant kindly or as a kick in the pants — she left the room.

Having left 'Otto's Office,' Subaru and Emilia's course of action was, at long last, solidifying. Or rather, into what had already been solidified, Otto's opinion had mixed in like some impurity, and one way or another, it was about to be carried out in that same, already-solidified state.

"Come to think of it, what a waste of time that was..."

"Don't say that. Otto-kun's advice was, well, um, you know... yeah, um, well, y'know... helpful, wasn't it?"

"The way that ended on a question mark really shows off Emilia-tan's honesty she just can't hide — cute."

Praising Emilia for desperately trying, and failing, to cover for Otto, Subaru set off in search of Frederica, following the course they'd settled on. For now, if there was a problem between the two siblings, it lay with the sister. Garfiel's heart was already made up. What remained was Frederica's resolve — and finding the spark that would settle it—

"My, if it isn't Emilie and Subaru. Whatever are you two doing?"

"Ugh." "Ah."

At the voice reaching them from behind, Subaru caught his breath awkwardly, while Emilia turned around with open, honest surprise on her face. Standing where their eyes met was a girl in a dress, her deep indigo hair done up in braids.

She looked younger than ten, younger even than Petra or Beatrice. She wore an opulent dress much like Beatrice did, though its ornamentation was more simply tailored than that of the ringlet-haired girl's. Her sharp eyes and dignified features seemed at odds with such youth.

The girl's name was Annerose Miload. She was the legitimate heir of House Miload, whose hospitality Subaru and the others were currently enjoying, and in the absence of the household's current head, it fell to her to receive them. Of course, the actual arrangements were handled by capable household staff, Clind foremost among them, but Annerose's bearing as she gave the orders was thoroughly dignified.

The mindset of one who governs, who stands above others — she had already cultivated it, despite her tender age. House Miload was a cadet branch of magical lineage, with Mathers — Roswaal's birth family — as its main house. Annerose already carried the resolve of one who would, in time, inherit that legacy.

It seemed to Subaru that this was precisely what stripped her of the childlike charm one might expect, and he wasn't especially fond of dealing with Annerose because of it. Faced with her simply as one human being to another, this girl, nearly ten years his junior, seemed far more accomplished than he was. On the other hand, beside him, Emilia's reaction was straightforward as ever.

"Honestly, Anne. I've told you so many times, it's not Emilie, it's Emilia."

"Oh, do forgive me, Emilie. But if we're placing blame, it's Emilie's own fault for hesitating over her words at our very first introduction. Besides, Emilie is so much easier to say than Emilia, and I think it suits you rather sweetly."

"You think? Well, I don't really mind it either... you're hopeless."

And with that, she went ahead and allowed Annerose to keep calling her that. As one could tell, Emilia had been strangely close with Annerose ever since they'd first met. When Subaru had pressed her for a reason, the answer he'd gotten was simply that the two of them oddly clicked. It seemed Annerose felt something similar toward Emilia, showing not a trace of negative feeling toward her despite her being a Half-Elf. Perhaps the two of them were simply on the same wavelength emotionally — though given Emilia's actual age, that would be its own sort of problem.

"By the way, whatever were you and Subaru doing, Emilie? A secret rendezvous?"

"Oh, did it look that way? Did it? Man, I'm embarrassed — guess we just looked that close and lovey-dovey. This is embarrassing, so embarrassing, embarrassed, Emilia-tan."

"No, nothing like that at all. We were just plotting a little something together."

"You know exactly how I feel and you still cut me down flat, geez!"

To Annerose, who wore an expression of keen interest that might have marked her as precociously worldly, Emilia simply shook her head. Annerose, too, apparently hadn't expected much, and let out a sigh, saying "I see," while giving Subaru a faintly condescending look. That look was pure mockery of Subaru's spinelessness. But he didn't think it was really his fault. He kept up his efforts properly, after all. It was just that Emilia's skill at deflecting them had, if anything, improved even more than before.

"I shall hear about this little plot of yours later... but have either of you seen Clind? I have business with him, and he's nowhere to be found."

"If you mean Clind-san, he was watching over Petra and Beatrice just a little while ago."

"...That's certainly one way of putting it, Emilie."

From that alone, Annerose apparently grasped the situation, her face twisting as if she'd bitten into something bitter. Given how long she'd known Clind, she surely understood his nature well enough. After all, under normal circumstances, his immovable "Loli Soul" was directed at his mistress Annerose and no one else. Just how intense, how sharp, and how utterly hopeless it was needed no further explanation.

"He said he bought some delicious sweets and wanted to let the two of them try some. I wonder if there's any for us, too. I'm just a teeny bit curious."

"...I can't imagine Clind would ever commit such a rudeness, so I'm sure there is. He already brought some to me as well, along with tea, ahead of time."

"Oh, is that so. Wow, I can't wait."

Emilia clasped her hands together in delight, while Annerose looked on with a fond smile. Their respective heights and ages were completely reversed from what the scene suggested. It was a heartwarming sight, but Subaru couldn't help but tilt his head slightly all the same. Noticing Subaru's tilted head, Annerose narrowed her blue eyes.

"Well, in that case, the two of you — Emilie and Subaru, looking so idle — will do just fine. There is a small favor I'd like to ask. Would you be so good as to accompany me?"

"Whoa, hey now. We might look idle, but we're actually not, you know. We might look free, but in truth, we're using this bit of downtime to firm up a genuinely proactive plan to cut off any future regrets at the root, and—"

"What kind of favor is it? If it's something we can help with, sure..."

In place of Subaru, still spinning his long-winded excuse, Emilia went ahead and rashly signaled her willingness to help. Annerose smiled at her for it, then turned toward the two of them a gaze far more mature than her nine years would suggest.

"It's a matter of wanting to surprise dear Uncle, and a small gesture of consideration from me to a maid I've known for many years."

Without ever realizing it, the outer moat closes in around Otto, leaving him nowhere left to run. Which way will tomorrow go?
