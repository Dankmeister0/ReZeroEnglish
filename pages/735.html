Arc 9 Chapter 39: "The Last Line"

'...Al-sama, let's stop yearning for the sun, shall we?'

I don't know what feelings, what intentions, she held when she said those words.

I was the one who'd subjugated her through fear, bound her with hatred, and used her desire for revenge as collateral. From where she stood, surely watching me driven into a corner must have been immensely satisfying.

And yet, for all that, the fact remains that there were more than a few reactions from her that didn't quite add up.

So then, what exactly was the true intent hidden behind her contradictory behavior?

I've stopped seeking that answer. There's only one thing that's certain.

"—Domain Deployment, Matrix Redefinition."

That piece of advice from Yae Tenzen had removed a single shackle from within Aldebaran.

It wasn't a restraint he'd consciously imposed, but an unconscious one — a save point, a hurdle, call it what you like — essentially, a mental chain that put the brakes on the overwhelming power of Aldebaran's Authority.

With that removed, the range of Aldebaran's options widened to something approaching infinity—.

"Target: twelve o'clock to three o'clock. —Do it, Alter."

"Roger, Origin."

Responding without a hitch to the order, 'Aldebaran' — standing by close behind — spread its wings.

There was no hesitation at being addressed as Alter. The update and sharing of intent had already been completed through the Book of the Dead. Being called Origin in turn was, plainly, a trivial matter.

There was no room to voice any opinion on the matter before a white light — a Dragon's breath — was unleashed.

"——"

The power of the breath, drawn from the Dragonhusk of the Divine Dragon at its fullest specification, was ferocious enough that even the Sword Saint himself, Reinhard van Astrea, had barely managed to cancel it out.

That was unleashed to sweep across a battlefield with no Sword Saint present — no, an area that wasn't even a battlefield to begin with — and everything there, the nature, the man-made structures, and above all not just the plants and animals but every life, humans included, was wiped away.

The range of a Dragon's breath, unleashed in earnest, extends for several kilometers, and anyone swallowed by that light of annihilation, which comes without a shred of warning, is erased before they even have time to feel pain.

—And now, Aldebaran had ordered 'Aldebaran' to do exactly that.

"——"

Just as instructed, twelve o'clock to three o'clock — the direction mapped onto what's known as a clock position — was partially burned away, and everything that had lain in its path was reduced to nothing.

The lives lost were probably not in the hundreds or thousands, but likely no fewer than tens of thousands.

"Ha-ha-haaah, now that's the way to do it!"

Witnessing that trampling — nothing short of raw, violent might that crushed everything in the world beneath it by sheer force — Roy Alphard, strung up as he was, laughed with delight. Roy, his arms bound as he hung there, went so far as to clap the soles of his free feet together, cheering it on with foot-applause in place of hand-applause.

But it was only that Roy was the sole one making a racket about it — among those present, not a single member reproached Aldebaran for what he'd done.

What there was, instead, was delight, understanding, submission, fear — every one of them, accomplices, whether by choice or by force majeure.

And bathed in their gazes, Aldebaran turned his eyes up to the sky.

"——"

The Dragon's breath hadn't just scorched the earth when it was unleashed — its effects reached the sky and the air as well.

The clouds were blown apart, the rain and wind that should have come were driven off, and in their place the land bore the deep-etched trace of the Dragon — a claw-mark of Draconic wrath, so to speak.

Naturally, no living thing exists that doesn't fear the wrath of an overwhelming existence like a Dragon.

Since nothing exists capable of standing against a Dragon raging in fury, it's an iron rule that every living thing bows its head to a Dragon's temper, hides its own existence completely, and waits for the storm to pass.

—Which is precisely why birds or insects drawing near that claw-mark of wrath so soon after was, in the extreme, unnatural.

"Wrong guess, huh."

Watching a flock of birds gathering to inspect the damage in the area scorched by the breath, Aldebaran let a murmur slip out, one that died away inside his own mouth.

Most animals are honest, faithful to their instincts. If that honesty gets twisted, if they act in a way unfaithful to instinct, that's proof some unnatural influence is interfering.

Which is to say, proof that whatever's doing that unnatural interfering is still alive.

Having confirmed as much, Aldebaran—

"—Next."

he murmured, and inside that same murmuring mouth broke open a paper packet of poison, downing the virulent toxin.

And then—

"Target: three o'clock to six o'clock. —Do it, Alter."

Next, back at the restart point he'd returned to, he ordered the Dragon to devastate the next area.

"Target: six o'clock to nine o'clock. —Do it, Alter."

"Target: six o'clock to eight o'clock. Minor correction."

"Target: six o'clock to seven o'clock."

"Target fixed at seven o'clock. Adjusting range — breath at eighty percent."

"Seventy percent."

"Sixty percent."

"Sixty-five percent."

"Sixty-five point five percent."

—Seven hundred fourteen.

Told by Yae that he was doing this the "clean" way, Aldebaran reconsidered.

He became aware of an arrogance he hadn't recognized in himself, seriously examined what was truly necessary to accomplish his goal, and arrived at an answer.

—The restriction against killing.

It was something he'd decided, from the very moment he chose to eliminate Natsuki Subaru and follow his own purpose as a created being, that he had to hold to as the bare minimum line he would not cross.

From a world that had already lost so much, a world where even her existence — like the sun — had vanished, he'd decided he would not let it lose anything more.

But in clinging too tightly to that vow, Aldebaran had, without realizing it, clamped a shackle onto his own Authority.

In order to keep the oath of taking no one's life, he'd placed that same restriction on the process itself, too.

With Aldebaran's Authority — the Domain — whatever happens in a world that gets repeated each time never happened at all, endlessly, until a period is put on that matrix.

So then, as long as the books balance out to "no killing" in the end, there's no need to shy away from deaths along the way.

Having kept that conclusion outside of his conscious awareness was the very shackle Aldebaran had unknowingly imposed on himself. Removing it was something he'd gone on wasting time over, fruitlessly. —No, without Yae's words, he might well have remained oblivious to it even now.

"I knew big brother was using his Divine Protection to keep whittling us down. The problem was, there was no way at all to guard against his thoroughness or his endless hand of cards…"

That difficult problem, the now-unshackled Aldebaran broke through with sheer, forceful brute strength.

There was nothing complicated about it. Their pursuer had been borrowing the eyes and mobility of insects, fish, and small animals to keep track of Aldebaran's group's movements and to set up a wide variety of sabotage. But judging from the estimated range of effect of the opponent's Divine Protection, the way he directed those animals had neither the instant responsiveness of telepathy nor the absolute control of brainwashing.

In every case, the pursuer must have been giving instructions verbally, each and every time, as the situation called for it.

And to carry that out, the pursuer doing the directing had to keep pursuing while maintaining a careful, not-too-close-not-too-far distance from Aldebaran's group. —So he'd used the Dragon's breath to forcibly pin down the location of that trailing pursuer.

"Narrow down the area by clock position, measure the distance by how deep the breath reaches. If we go and pull something outlandish, he's forced to play a card just to confirm what happened. Playing a card is proof big brother's still fine. But what about when he doesn't play one?"

A card — proof that he had no room left to move the forces he'd won over with his Divine Protection, because he'd already been erased from this world.

Having confirmed that, Aldebaran looped his Domain back, narrowed down the direction where the pursuer was, measured the distance, and pinpointed the opponent's location down to the wire. Naturally, in the process, mountains and forests and even towns, along with a great many people, were caught up as collateral victims — but every last one of those, none of it had ever happened.

Thanks to the Domain's final settling of accounts, the number of casualties besides Aldebaran was zero. —No, since Aldebaran's own "deaths" don't count, the number of casualties was zero, period.

"Counting the time picking the tower members too, that makes thirteen hundred forty-seven… he really put me through it."

There's no exhaustion quite like having your footing swept out from under you a second time by someone you thought you'd already eliminated once.

In that sense, it wasn't an exaggeration to say the pursuer — Otto Suwen — had tormented Aldebaran even more than the Sword Saint or the Sword Devil had. In fact, if it had been by his design that Emilia was sent against Aldebaran and Wilhelm against Yae back in the royal capital, then the damage their side had suffered went far beyond Aldebaran's mere thirteen hundred forty-seven "deaths."

Of course, it was precisely because he'd judged him a threat of that magnitude that Aldebaran had thrown in his cards without holding back, and had succeeded in stringing up his formidable foe.

"Al-sama, over here, over here~"

Waving a hand back and forth, beckoning Aldebaran's group over, was Yae below. Following her call, 'Aldebaran' touched down on the ground, and he leapt from its back.

Partway up the mountainside, behind the smiling Yae — who'd already reached the target point ahead of him — there hung a green-clad administrator strung up from a tree, and, bound hand and foot, an Earth Dragon left rolling on the ground.

The Earth Dragon appeared to have struggled desperately against its Steel Thread restraints, blood seeping here and there across its pale-blue dragon hide, and even now it writhed, breathing out exhausted, frantic breaths.

"Gotta hand it to 'em, an Earth Dragon's loyalty really is something else. Thinking back on it, the current Patrasche was kind of the same way."

"I did think about making an example of it, just in case, but it seemed like the type that'd get more stubborn than broken, so I held off. What do you think of Yae-chan's judgment call?"

"I think that was the right call too. Besides, an example doesn't mean much if nobody's around to see it."

At Yae, who greeted him with her head tilted, Aldebaran twisted his lips inside his helmet. In Aldebaran's field of view, the administrator hung above the writhing Earth Dragon — Otto — had his eyes and mouth, of course, and everything from his neck down to the tips of his toes sealed tightly in Steel Thread, utterly unable to do a thing.

Normally, a single strand of Steel Thread is about the thickness you'd only just be able to confirm was there if you squinted hard. That it was plainly visible as a restraint made it impossible to even guess how many times over it had been wound.

"No light gets through. No sound leaks out either. Even breathing, let alone whispering, carries the possibility of communicating with the outside. So I bound his whole body without a single gap, and limited his air passage to a single strand from the ankle. This way he's too busy struggling to breathe to have any room left to put intent into it."

"…I get that this isn't you venting, it's proof of how careful you're being. Honestly, big brother's the type where if you give him a chance to talk, there's no telling what he'll pull."

"I'd never act out of personal grudges~. Yae-chan's not that bad a Shinobi, y'know?"

Pressing a finger to her cheek, forming an adorable smile, Yae was in quite the good mood.

Given how much bitter suffering Otto — who specialized in harassment — had put her through up to this point, no matter how much she insisted she held no personal grudge, her resentment must have run fairly deep. To begin with, harassing an enemy and dragging them into a war of attrition was, properly speaking, none other than a Shinobi's own specialty.

"Guess if you've finally managed to pin that down, no wonder you're in a good mood."

"Buu~, Al-sama really doesn't understand a girl's heart at all. Of course it feels good to bring down someone who's been coming at you with sly, thorough, relentless, absolutely rotten-tempered, nagging little attacks!"

"That's a way harsher way of putting it than I ever did."

"But the biggest thing is that Al-sama went and properly played the monster. That you didn't cling to some petty little shred of humanity, and committed fully to being a monster."

"——"

"If it's for that, getting thrown with all Bol-sama's might is nothing at all."

"Sounds like you've got plenty of complaints about the plan after all…"

Correcting what she called Aldebaran's misunderstanding, Yae tacked that on at the end.

The change in how the Domain was used, and the pinpointing of the pursuer's location that resulted from it — once that was done, the method for closing the distance at maximum speed, before the opponent could catch on to their intentions, was this: having the flying 'Aldebaran' throw Yae all the way to the target's position.

It was a rough method, and one that put Yae's life in tremendous danger besides, but given that the opponent was somewhere in the mountains with plenty of natural cushioning around, Yae — who could make her own cushion with the Steel Thread Technique — was judged the best suited for the job.

"There's no way we could've left this job to Heinkel-sama as he is right now, just because he probably wouldn't die no matter how hard he got thrown."

"Even if you punched an old-man-shaped hole clean through a mountain, the old man himself would probably come out of it right as rain, but yeah — trusting him with something this critical in his current state, that's..."

The rather fatal rift that had opened up between Heinkel and Felt. They'd taken measures to deal with the problem for the time being, bringing it to a temporary lull, but that had earned 'Aldebaran's' displeasure, and it was nothing more than a stopgap treatment that couldn't be called a sure thing.

Since it was the sort of problem that could flare back up for any number of reasons, the conclusion Aldebaran and Yae had reached together was to leave it untouched for now.

In any case—

"—Finally. Checkmate."

Looking up at Otto — his movement and resistance sealed away, the man now nothing so much as a silver cocoon — Aldebaran claimed victory in one match of a long, long-running contest.

That said, Otto wasn't the only opponent Aldebaran was playing against — this was a simultaneous match against opponents on the scale of the Kingdom, on the scale of the whole world. Just because he'd managed not to drop this one piece didn't mean he could go on carrying it around as some triumphant moment forever.

More than anything, he had to make the elimination thorough enough that this opponent would never return to the board again.

"Even if I carve off his eyes and ears and tongue and limbs, I can still keep to Al-sama's orders, y'know~"

"—Yae."

"Yes~ ma'am. Al-sama, you're so scary~"

Inflicting a wound from which there's no recovery, letting only the life remain while forcing them off the battlefield — that would be a deception.

For Aldebaran, the restriction against killing wasn't something so convenient that it could be said to be upheld just because the person wasn't literally dead — it couldn't be, and it mustn't be. That would be the same as declaring world peace achieved by taking every hand and every foot from every human being on the planet.

So Aldebaran would take neither Otto Suwen's eyes, nor his tongue, nor his hands, nor his feet. What he would take from him instead was — his reason to fight.

"—Roy. I'm loosening the curse seal's scope. This is your cue."

"—Roy. I'm loosening the curse seal's scope. This is your cue."

That the man looking up at him had spoken those very words — Otto Suwen, bound from head to toe without so much as a gap, had heard it clearly. —On exactly one point, Aldebaran's group was mistaken about how Otto's Divine Protection of Spirit Speech actually worked.

Kotodama — spirit speech, that is, voice — is a vibration of sound. And vibration, even if Otto's ears were stopped up, would still be touching Otto's body as long as he was within its reach. The Divine Protection of Spirit Speech doesn't translate sound that enters through the ears. It senses the vibration of intent that touches the being called Otto, and picks that up.

Which is why—

"Funny how fate works out, isn't it, big brother? Back in Priestella, both Lye and Rui failed to finish eating you, and yet here you are, served up right in front of us after all!"

The voice of the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony, Roy Alphard, licking his lips as he said it, was likewise something Otto could sense with certainty.

"——"

But, to his utter, bone-deep frustration, there was nothing left that Otto could do.

He went on picking up the voices around him through the Divine Protection of Spirit Speech, yes, but with his mouth sealed, his limbs bound, and any means of sending out intent of his own cut off, Otto was, hopelessly, powerless.

"What are you doing to the young master... stop, please, stop it...!"

Just below where he hung, hearing Frufoo's anguished pleading was painful to bear.

For now, it seemed Aldebaran's group had no intention of doing further harm to Frufoo, let alone taking her life. If that was the case, he wanted Frufoo to just stay quiet and focus entirely on riding out this storm. That, right now, was his greatest wish.

As for the rest—

"—!"

In a voice that couldn't become a voice, he worried, from the bottom of his heart, about the companions who weren't there with him.

He'd heard they were cooperating with Emilia and Ram, with Petra and Meili, with Rem and Clind and Roswaal, and with the people of the Felt Camp too. Surely a good plan would turn up. If it were at all possible, he'd have liked to deal the enemy a real blow with his own two hands, enough to cover Garfiel's share too.

And the two of them, captured — Subaru and Beatrice—.

"So sad, isn't it, big brother? So painful, isn't it, big brother? But! All that bittersweet screaming and wailing, I'll take it all in and love it, every bit! In the end, this world is nothing but drunken excess! Gluttony!"

Slowly, he felt the defiled appetite of Gluttony closing in on him. That, Otto had no way to resist — nothing left to do but accept it, and be chewed up.

So Otto Suwen went on, still, praying. Still wishing. Still thinking.

That his own final gambit would land—.

"—Well then, time to eat!"

△▼△▼△▼△

"That hit the spot!"

The effect of Gluttony's Authority activated, and the hanging Otto Suwen was eaten.

The act produced no fitting sort of effect, no flare of magical spectacle, nothing showy of that kind happened at all. It was simply, solemnly, a feast plated up and settled into the stomach of a table-mannerless diner. —Nothing more than that ordinary a meal took place.

"I only just watched it happen, but it really does feel like a cheat move. In an instant, the resistance I could feel through the thread just vanished."

Yae murmured that, not bothering to hide something like disgust in her voice, her fingers touching the ring that spat out the Steel Thread.

What she'd just said meant that the resistance the bound man — Otto Suwen — had been putting up, trying to escape from those Steel Thread restraints, had vanished.

Which was to say, proof that Roy Alphard's Corrosion had taken effect.

"And on top of that, the two of us still remember big brother, properly. —Seems like you actually held back and just ate his 'Memory,' like we agreed."

"Ah-hahaa, come on, what's with that, that's not nearly enough gratitude and appreciation! Just so you know, biting off only the 'Name' and only the 'Memory,' keeping them separate, is a lot harder than you'd think! It's like the kind of trouble it takes to pick out only certain fillings from a sandwich without ever opening the bread, y'know?"

"Don't care. If you can't do it, you're the one who dies. So sort it out and eat neat."

In front of the now-still, limp form of Otto Suwen, wrapped up in Steel Thread, Aldebaran snorted at Roy going on about how hard his Authority was to use.

In reality, no matter how much Roy insisted on how advanced a feat this was, Aldebaran had no intention of lending an ear. —If he botched it, Roy would simply have his Soul burned away and die, by the effect of the Oath's curse seal carved into his body, binding him to Aldebaran.

Of course, since Aldebaran couldn't afford to have Roy die on him right now, he did intend to redo it every time it failed, and go on searching for a way to have Roy pull it off properly.

"Al-sama~, isn't that Sin Archbishop getting a little full of himself? If the curse seal can bind his actions anyway, wasn't there some way to just make him fall in line, coldly and however, without going through all the trouble of coaxing and sweet-talking him around?"

"Carving a curse seal isn't nearly as casual a thing as you seem to think. To begin with, an Oath's curse seal carries risk for the one doing the carving, too. In fact, the chance to carve a curse seal only ever comes once in a lifetime. It's a trump card, for making someone fall in line who genuinely won't listen to reason any other way."

As Yae whispered that into his ear, Aldebaran answered her that way while pushing her forehead back.

As the name suggests, the act of carving a curse seal, a kind of curse art, is something that existed before magic and curse arts ever branched apart, and in terms of its nature it's actually closer to the very source from which magic sprang. If you got dragged along by the image the word "curse seal" conjures up and talked about it carelessly, you'd end up on the receiving end of a good half-day's tedious lecture from the very person who taught Aldebaran how and why to carve one.

"To think you'd use such a precious opportunity just for us...! It's fine, it's great, sure thing, isn't it great, ain't it swell, isn't it grand, because I can believe it's all fine — drunken excess! Gluttony! That's basically love already, isn't it..."

"Wha—! You're telling me you'd pick some Sin Archbishop with crooked teeth over Yae-chan, an adorable, alluring, devoted maid like me?! Al-sama, you cheater!"

"You're the one who shouldn't be falling into sync with a Sin Archbishop like that..."

At the tag-team routine of Roy, whose mouth never stopped running, and Yae, who refused to let hers stop either, Aldebaran grew thoroughly weary.

Yae surely had no intention of actually getting along with Roy, but the way she'd use absolutely anything if it meant rattling Aldebaran's heart was thoroughly Shinobi of her. Meanwhile, Roy, for his part, carried an attitude liable to give the false impression that he was, for a Sin Archbishop, someone you could actually reason with.

But that impression must not be mistaken for truth. To Aldebaran, both Yae and Roy were, equally, tools to be used toward his purpose, and, depending on circumstances, targets who might well slit his throat while he slept.

And that was—

"Roy, don't get too carried away. Hurry up and tell me what big brother, the one you just ate, was scheming. That's exactly what I had you eat him for."

Keeping Roy firmly within striking range the whole time, Aldebaran gave that cold warning.

Roy had had his restraints undone, his broken limbs healed by 'Aldebaran's' Magic, but that was by no means a sign of trust, nor permission to act freely. It simply wasn't realistic, going forward, to carry Roy around still strung up while working out the details of the plan.

For instance, at a moment like this, when he wanted to rely on Roy's Authority as Gluttony, keeping him face-down as a card he couldn't immediately use would be far too poor a move. Vigilance toward Roy was never to be loosened — what was needed, on top of that, was to have a method ready for keeping Roy in line regardless.

Fortunately, the curse seal was fulfilling that role satisfactorily.

"Well, here's hoping we get something useful out of him~"

Saying so, the wary Yae let her slender fingers sway, letting the presence of invisible Steel Thread flicker into view to hold Roy in check.

It was Yae, of all people, who'd shown the most reluctance right up to the end about making use of Roy's Authority; even as she welcomed Aldebaran declaring, on her own advice, that he'd remove his shackle, she had strongly argued that if he was going to remove it, he ought to release the restriction against killing itself, entirely.

And in fact, just as she said, if Aldebaran resolved to lift the restriction against killing and freely decide the life or death of any opponent, the goal itself would be achieved without any trouble at all. It would be enough to simply scatter 'Aldebaran's' breath in every direction and keep the enemy from ever drawing near.

But Aldebaran refused that, and instead argued for adding, as a new option, something short of it — allowing "death" along the way, and making use of Roy Alphard's Authority.

In truth, that was the unleashing of a forbidden move: actively making use of a Sin Archbishop's Authority.

It was a different matter entirely from wielding Aldebaran's own Authority, which occupied what you might call a vacant, unnumbered position. He was making someone use, for his own sake, an Authority that had been handed down through an unbroken chain of malice, one that had already given birth to countless tragedies and sacrifices — and all for the simple reason that it was useful.

This was, in all likelihood, an evil act that, since the Witch Factors were first born into this world, only a single person besides Aldebaran had ever committed.

In short, it was an action that crossed that great a line.

And now, without results to match what he'd done, there would be no meaning to having turned his back on the sun—.

"So tell me. I went out of my way to ruin the man's whole life. Don't tell me there's nothing to show for it—"

"Ahh, wait, wait. I'd rather you not rush me like that. Seems like you've got the wrong idea, but even we can't just suddenly understand the entire whole of someone we've eaten, all at once. Something you knew from the start, right, isn't the kind of thing that just pops into your head unless the situation calls for it? It's the same kind of thing..."

"—I'm not saying this twice."

"...Yeah, yeah, I get it, already. Hmm, I see, I see. This big brother's had a lot more life experience than we expected, and honestly it's got us a little surprised, too—"

Roy, who'd been about to spill out a long-winded excuse, focused instead on recalling the "Memory" of Otto he'd only just eaten. What Roy had been saying could be called a combination of Otto's own turbulent, eventful days as the holder of the Divine Protection of Spirit Speech, and the sheer, abnormal volume of voices he must have heard over the years.

But then Roy's snide chatter cut off abruptly, and Aldebaran grew suspicious.

That was, unmistakably, a sign he'd dug up some "Memory" that caught on something—

"What, what's wrong?"

"Ha-ha! Seems like we were more thorough than we thought. —There's a foolproof, for if he ever got taken down himself."

"——"

A foolproof — a safety device meant to guard against some foolish mistake.

The instant Roy said the word, a ferocious chill shot up through Aldebaran's entire body.

Not a countermeasure for when an attack was closing in on him, but a countermeasure for after he'd already been taken down.

If Otto Suwen had prepared something like that, then now was exactly that moment.

"Yae! Right now—"

Withdrawing from the scene, and looping the situation back through his own death.

Weighing both options in his head at once, Aldebaran moved without narrowing down which countermeasure to take. In one sense that paid off; in another, it led to failure.

And that was—

"—A pleasure to meet you for the first time, Aldebaran-sama."

A lovely girl's voice struck Aldebaran's eardrums like an ambush.

It was, quite literally, a foreign element that had materialized in the blink of an eye, and its appearance came with so little warning that even his instincts couldn't ring the alarm bell that this was bad in time.

He should never have looked at her — the blue-haired girl, her appearance and attire changed from how she'd been in the Vollachia Empire. —He realized that only right after he'd caught sight of her, her maid uniform's skirt fluttering.

In that instant, that maid girl's name, her existence, her very nature, came rushing to threaten his brain.

"——"

A girl who had once had her "Name" and "Memory" eaten by the Authority of Gluttony, erased from the Od Lagna Registry that records the names of every living thing — who had then been spat back out on account of the malice of "Ill-Eating," and left inert ever since, never manifesting any real harm — that fact detonated inside Aldebaran's head.

Struck by the shock of being forced, by sheer brute compulsion, to remember her existence, Aldebaran — who bore the fate of never being allowed to let his thoughts stop — was left behind by his own thoughts—

"—Al-sama!!"

In that instant, Yae's slender arm shot out toward Aldebaran even as she cried out. Catching sight of her white fingers at the edge of his vision, Aldebaran couldn't move.

Unable to move, in the span of that single second where his thoughts had been stolen from him, the situation moved on without him.

—In that split second, Aldebaran's body was severed from the space it had occupied only a moment before, and flung out into a sky that battered him with a furious wind.

"——"

The dazzling brilliance of the sun he'd stopped yearning for burned into Aldebaran's black eyes as he tumbled through midair. —It was the unexpected start of the moment of truth for Aldebaran's group.

—The battle for the Grand Mogolade Geyser, the struggle that would decide the world's fate, had begun.
