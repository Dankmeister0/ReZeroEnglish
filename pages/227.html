Arc 4 Chapter 61: "A Cry From Four Hundred Years Ago"

Her sorrow-laden eyes held Subaru fast, refusing to let him go. Something in Subaru's chest wanted to laugh Beatrice's words off, to scoff them away.

—Just now, what did you say?

All he had to do was repeat back the incomprehensible words that had just brushed his ears, toss them right back at Beatrice. All he had to do was twist the corner of his mouth into a grin and slip in some flippant remark, same as always. It was just—yes, just a little—a bad turn of the wind. Nothing more. Because if it wasn't that, then—

"——"

—then he couldn't deny the "joke" of the desperate-eyed girl standing before him.

"Just now… what did you say?"

After a moment's hesitation, the barest pause, Subaru forced out the line he'd prepared. If he could just keep the smile on his lips now, maybe shrug one shoulder, it would be perfect. And yet—

"…ah"

Subaru's cheek twitched, and not just his shoulders but even his fingertips locked rigid, refusing to move. As if the existence called Natsuki Subaru, reflected in Beatrice's eyes, had been fixed in place within the world exactly as it was.

"Shall Betty say it again, since you want it so badly?"

"W-wait…"

"—Betty wants you to end her. With your own hand."

"Stop it!!"

Subaru's voice rose, cutting Beatrice off. It was, in a way, a comically exact reversal of their positions from moments ago.

Beatrice had refused to hear what she didn't want to hear, and Subaru had forced his own logic on her, wrung a scream out of her. So now that Beatrice was doing the same to him in reverse, Subaru had no right to condemn her for it. He knew he had no right. And yet—

"Do you even understand what you just said…?"

"Shouldn't you be the one asking yourself if you understand what's being said to you?"

"What?"

"Betty's saying you should be made her end. The spirit Beatrice's end. You, made into 'that person'—the one who ends this contract that's bound her for over four hundred years."

You should feel honored, Beatrice added, an ill-fitting, sardonic smile rising on her face. Her smile, parched dry—looking at it, Subaru felt something like clawed, twisted fingertips raking at his chest. It was unbearable, and Subaru pressed a hand to his chest as he said,

"That doesn't make any sense… Are you saying you want to die?"

"'Want to die' isn't, strictly speaking, accurate. Betty wants the contract ended. Betty just wants to be freed from the eternal contract that's bound this body all this time."

"If the means to that is taking your life, how is that any different from wanting to die?!"

Subaru stamped his foot, his throat shaking as he raised his voice in fury. The Gospel pages scattered underfoot were trampled beneath his heel, but he paid it no mind. He jabbed a finger, glaring at Beatrice as he snarled.

"Don't you dare spout something as stupid as wanting to die! Say that in front of anyone else you like—but in front of me, saying it to me, that I will not allow!"

If you die, life can't be taken back. Natsuki Subaru alone could die and start over. So for Subaru himself alone, there was grounds—grounds that could justify throwing his life on the line, that could make sense of self-sacrifice. But Beatrice was different. Everyone else was different.

Once a life is lost, it cannot be recovered. Knowing that, and still saying it in front of him—

"What do you mean, you want it ended?! Don't say such selfish things! Acting just to end things, just to die—no matter who else might allow that, I sure as hell won't!"

"That's, quite the selfish thing to say, isn't it. —What could you possibly understand about Betty?"

But against Subaru's fuming, Beatrice's answer came back cold and hard. She brushed off her skirt and rose to her feet, fingers touching the tip of her ringlet curls, and said,

"Four hundred years since Betty became the Keeper of Knowledge, tending the Forbidden Archive. Four hundred years… Betty has done nothing but obey the contract, waiting for that time."

"Four… hundred years…"

That phrase again, Subaru thought, his face twisting with the urge to click his tongue. Four hundred years. The era when the Witch's power raged fiercest, a cursed age in which every long-lived figure carried some entanglement or another. Beatrice, too, had been born into that era, and had lived on to this very day.

"After making a contract with the Witch and taking shelter with House Mathers, who shared her position, at first it was days of simply, quietly waiting—following the Gospel's account, for the day that was to come."

"——"

"But while Betty just waited like that, time in the outside world kept flowing on, moment by moment. The head of House Mathers who shared Betty's position at the time died of old age, and the role passed to the next generation. Even as Betty watched heads of the house come and go, Betty's own time kept flowing on, utterly unchanged."

What kind of agony must that time have been, for Beatrice? The girl's flat, matter-of-fact way of speaking only reflected all the more vividly just how her heart had been worn away by the inorganic passage of time, and Subaru, listening, felt a chill creep over him.

"Days without knowing anything—not when the promised day would eventually come, not who 'that person,' the one who would come to Betty, would even be."

And yet, Beatrice said, shaking her head,

"There was no anxiety. Because Betty held the Gospel in her hands. All she had to do was trust the Gospel that recorded the future, and wait for the day that was to come to be added, in due course, to its blank pages. If she waited, that time would surely come… that's what Betty kept believing."

"But…"

Looking down at the page still trapped beneath his heel, at its utter, endless whiteness, Subaru realized its cruelty. Beatrice, too, seemed to sense the meaning of Subaru's gaze, and nodded. What had once been hope to her, the Gospel had, before she knew it, become—

"Every day, over and over, checking whether the writing had changed… that time became agony."

"………"

"Betty dreamed, again and again, of new words being added on the page after the last entry. Over and over, imagining the day when 'that person,' whom Betty didn't even know, would come to her, and Betty could finally fulfill the role given to her."

"…Beatrice."

"House Mathers isn't exactly a household with no visitors, is it. There have been people who came to Betty's Forbidden Archive before, plenty of them. Plenty who laid a hand on that door… and each and every time, Betty's heart was betrayed."

Whoever pushed the door open and stepped inside was never "that person." How many times had disappointment piled up? How many times had she repeated that despair? With expectation betrayed again and again, her heart wore down bit by bit, resignation filling her eyes.

The next time, and the next, kept betraying Beatrice's expectations. Eventually, she stopped expecting at all. Because clinging to the hope that it might arrive only meant her heart, having climbed to some height, would be dashed against the ground again, and she couldn't bear the pain of it shattering. It was only natural that the heart that had endured all this time would begin to crack.

"And somewhere along the way, Betty realized. …No, that's wrong—Betty had already realized."

"Realized what?"

"That the Gospel was never going to show Betty its next entry."

Beatrice bent her knee, picking up the spine of the Gospel lying at her feet. With its pages fallen out, leaving only the cover, it gave off an air of forlorn desolation. Tracing the spine she'd picked up with her finger, Beatrice said, "Do you know?" as a preface, then continued,

"A Gospel records the future of its holder. How much detail it records depends on how little discrepancy there is between the holder and the World's Memory—the smaller the error, the clearer it becomes."

"World's Memory…?"

"The world's memory, if you like. —The world already knows everything, not just the present and the past, but even events yet to occur in the future. The Book of Wisdom is a forbidden tome that draws out the necessary knowledge from that. The Gospel, you could say, has inherited only a fragment of that same function."

It had been Echidna who called the Book of Wisdom "the world's memory." There was, without doubt, a close relationship between Echidna and Beatrice. And Beatrice held the black spine out toward Subaru, showing it to him.

"The imitation Gospels the Witch Cult carries operate on almost the same principle. Only the precision differs—the formulae used are based on this one as a reference."

"…Why would that technique have leaked out after Echidna's death? If it's true, only two Gospels—yours and Roswaal's—were supposed to have been passed down."

"Well, Betty doesn't know, and doesn't care. Whoever, wherever, copied the imitations and handed them out to whomever—that's none of Betty's concern."

"Then why bring up the Witch Cult now?"

"Betty had business with the Gospel the Witch Cult holds. You just jumped to conclusions."

Even against Subaru's provocative tone, Beatrice didn't rise to it, maintaining her composure. She asked Subaru, "Do you have the Witch Cult's Gospel?" To which Subaru nodded and answered,

"Not on me right now. I brought it into the Sanctuary, and it's being kept there for now. As for the ones recovered from other Witch Cultists, the collection and storage got handed off to someone better equipped for it than us."

At present, the only Gospel Subaru possessed was the single volume that had belonged to Petelgeuse. As for the other Gospels held by the Witch Cultists who had been Petelgeuse's Fingers, the conscious ones destroyed theirs themselves before their deaths. The handful that had somehow been recovered were entrusted to the Crusch Camp, to handle afterward. Ideally, he would have already brought Roswaal back from the Sanctuary and held a proper accounting of merit for the Subjugation of the White Whale and the defeat of Petelgeuse, together with the Crusch Camp and the Anastasia Camp.

"That one volume—did you read through the contents?"

"Somehow I could read it, before I knew it, so, yeah, more or less. The handwriting was like a worm wriggling around, near-impossible to read, but for the most part it was information written out in bullet points. Though, personally, it looked to me less like a book of prophecy for the future and more like… instructions from the future."

He recalled the Gospel—readable, presumably, thanks to Echidna's influence. The content of Petelgeuse's Gospel had mostly followed a pattern: where Petelgeuse should go, what he should cause to happen. With most of the process leading to the outcome omitted, it could be said to leave the actions needed to bring about that outcome to the holder's own judgment. So the Witch Cult's Gospel seemed like something to be taken not as an omniscient book of prophecy, but as one guidepost toward the future—one among possibly several—and no more than that.

"If the future were predicted perfectly, it seems like we'd have no way left to stop it, from our end. I've already convinced myself that's why it's some kind of incomplete version."

"The content itself doesn't interest Betty much. What matters is whether it recorded the holder's end, or not."

"—I don't think it was really an 'end,' exactly."

Subaru recalled the final page of Petelgeuse's Gospel, as far as he knew it. Separate from the "THE END" Subaru had scrawled in his own blood, the last line the Gospel itself had originally written, in fulfillment of its true function—was short:

"At the Mathers domain, put the Trials to the silver-haired half-devil."

Such an absurdly terse line it was, offering Petelgeuse no clue whatsoever as to what would happen before or after it. So, it seemed, if that was all the information a Gospel could offer, there was no way it could outdo Subaru in the precision of its future-knowledge.

"—That's what Betty thought."

But at Subaru's account of everything he knew, Beatrice nodded as though something had clicked into place. Then the girl bent the spine still remaining in her hand and said,

"Did the Gospel add any writing to it after that?"

"…No, I don't think it did. At least, as far as I confirmed, the last entry was the holder's last action. And besides, there couldn't be any addition after that. Because—"

Starting to voice the conclusion that followed, Subaru realized what he was about to say, and his throat froze. And that realization laid bare the intent behind Beatrice's question. He raised his head. Before Subaru, Beatrice wore a faint smile. In this brief span of their encounter, it was a smile she had already shown him more than once—hollow, one that could only inspire in whoever looked upon it a pure, overwhelming sense of emptiness.

"—The reason nothing more gets written in a Gospel is because the holder's future ends there."

"Y-you're different from him…"

"Is it? In the sense that the Gospel records no more future, the only difference is whether one still exists, or no longer exists. —Can you say it's different?"

"It—!"

The word of denial that burst out reflexively was blocked before it could reach her, deflected by Beatrice's emotionless eyes. Her heart wasn't asking for comfort dressed up on the surface. The answer to this question had already been settled, within Beatrice. Clenching his back teeth to the point of nearly cracking them, blood welling at the corner of his lip, Subaru said,

"Why… do you just—!"

"…………"

"Draw the conclusion all by yourself?! Anyone! Anyone, left alone with their anxiety, worrying by themselves, ends up thinking that way, heading in a bad direction! Thinking there's no other way, agonizing over it… convincing yourself that the worst thing right in front of you is the truth!"

He knew, because he was Subaru—who had run into hardship again and again, lamenting his own helplessness each time. Repeated malice, obstacles that never disappeared, walls of the world that came crashing down as if speaking to him, telling him it was impossible to overcome them. They forced you to keep facing them alone, and even as you fought on solo, they'd wrap black fingers around the heart of whoever kept struggling. So—

"If you were suffering, if you wanted someone to do something about it, then—one word would've been enough! You should've just called out to someone, so they'd understand! If you'd just said you wanted help, that you were sad, then—I would've—!"

When you're cornered into fate's dead end, feeling there's nothing you can do, when despair convinces you that you can't climb out on your own strength, when you think you're isolated—that's when you should look around yourself. Only then, for the first time, can you notice the hand already being held out to you. Only when you take that hand, only when you're pulled forcefully by a body you thought couldn't move—only then can you finally realize:

—There was never any need to give up. Not anywhere.

"How many times have I… so this time, I'll be the one to…!"

"…I want… someone to do something about it."

"That's it… say it just like that."

"I want help…"

"Yes! Yes, yes, yes, yes! Just say that, and reach your hand out—"

"It's sad, it hurts… Betty wants to be saved from this darkness…"

"Yeah, leave it to me—"

Small, trembling fingers stretched out toward Subaru. Carried by an impatient eagerness, driven by some incomprehensible force welling up from within, Subaru reached his hand toward Beatrice's.

In that moment, Subaru had completely forgotten the reason he'd come here. In truth, Subaru had come to call Beatrice because he needed her strength to break through his current dead end. He'd hoped that she, of all people, might be able to help him. But now that he knew the suffering Beatrice carried, the darkness in her heart, he couldn't think that way anymore. Only a sense of duty drove him now—the desire to save a small girl tormented by loneliness. If he took that hand, Subaru would be taking on yet another burden he could never set down. Even as it was, he was already carrying more burdens than he could handle, and yet Natsuki Subaru was, once again, about to take on more. But he didn't care. Because—

"——"

—How could he leave behind a girl looking at him with eyes trembling like that?

Beatrice was trying to rely on him. That, to Subaru, summoned an unbearable feeling. He didn't know why. It didn't matter why. His soul was simply screaming. Save her. Rescue her. She is, to you—she must be.

"I will definitely, I'll—"

"So please…"

His fingers reached the extended fingers. He drew the fumbling touch firmly into his own hand, laced their fingers, pressed palm to palm. He looked straight into Beatrice's eyes. His reflection swam in her moist gaze. And then, from those eyes, Beatrice let large tears spill, and said,

"—Betty wants you to kill her."

—Refusing any easy salvation, she shook Subaru's hand off.

Why, Subaru's throat drew breath to raise the question. He looked at his rejected hand, at the empty fingers grasping nothing, then at Beatrice who had done it, meaning to ask why.

"——"

But he couldn't, because the eyes looking at him were, too much, too much, too much—irrevocably, hopelessly too late for anything.

"Four hundred years… Betty's been alone, this whole time."

"B-Beatri…"

"'That person' who was supposed to come never came, and Betty spent all four hundred years alone."

He couldn't look away from Beatrice's eyes. Even just saying her name—even that much—Subaru now hesitated to do.

"Betty doesn't even know how many times she thought about giving up. Doesn't know how many times she wished she could forget everything. A hundred times, a thousand times, past ten thousand, a hundred million, and still not enough…"

In this narrow, dim room, Beatrice had spent a long span of time in solitude. Hugging her knees, atop that stepladder, she had kept waiting for someone whose face and name she didn't know to come. A sea of books stretching as far as the eye could see—even reading every single book in that sea, still the visitor she waited for never came, and not even the book that should have shown her the future showed her anything. How many times had loneliness killed this girl's heart?

"Want help…? Want someone to do something…?"

"—ah"

"How many times, how many dozens, how many hundreds of times do you think Betty has thought that? Do you think Betty never once thought of it, and just gave up, just like that?"

The halting words gradually began to take on heat. Subaru found himself, in turn, overwhelmed. His throat seized, and the passion that had, until moments ago, scorched his soul from its very core flipped instantly to ice, his limbs heavy as if packed with lead. Too heavy to move for the girl before him, and too heavy to flee.

"If you reach out your hand, will you pull Betty out of this endless, unseen darkness? Will you show Betty the right answer, to this dead end with no way out?"

"………"

"If you… would really do that for me… then why… why—"

She hung her head, her voice breaking with tears. Beatrice's expression became invisible, and suddenly some unknowable darkness took hold of Subaru's heart. His footing unsteady, he felt he might lose his way even trying to reach Beatrice, who stood close enough that his hand could reach her. He faltered, hesitated. And in that gap, Beatrice raised her face. She glared at him. Her mouth opened, baring her teeth, and she said,

"—You left Betty alone for four hundred years?!"

"——!"

"Alone! The whole time! On and on and on, Betty spent all that time here, alone, doing nothing! Lonely! Afraid! Thinking she'd been abandoned, unable to fulfill the role given to her, with the promise never kept, unable even to simply decay away with time… made to believe she'd spend eternity, alone!"

Tears spilled, one after another, from the girl's wide eyes. Trailing down her cheeks, falling in fat drops from her chin to the floor. Each droplet that struck the floor felt like a tremendous blow, pummeling Subaru's heart.

"You'll help me?! You'll save me?! Then why didn't you come sooner?! Why did you leave Betty alone?! If you're only going to say kind words now, why didn't you hold her from the very start?! Why did you let go of her hand?! Why?! Why?! Why do you leave Betty alone?!"

Her words became blades, became flame, became steel, wounding Subaru's heart one after another. In every form, in every meaning, as every kind of pain, they tore into him, condemned him. Beatrice's grievance was, to Subaru, terribly unreasonable. Four hundred years—the vast majority of the solitude she'd endured was a span of time Subaru had had no part in whatsoever. His acquaintance with her spanned barely two months; by her own reasoning, a time so short could only be called early or late—it could never have been salvation. If he wanted to argue by logic, he could have said as much.

But what, in the end, would such a pointless rebuttal save? Not Beatrice. Not Subaru. No one. And Subaru was made to realize, all too keenly, just how much he had underestimated the time this girl called Beatrice had spent.

Four hundred years. —It was four hundred years.

Written out as a number, it might look like nothing much. In stories, in subculture fiction, four hundred years was nothing special. There were stories that threw around even more absurd spans of time, stories where the world itself looped through entire timelines. Next to that kind of impact, four hundred years was nothing at all.

Idiot. Was he an idiot? How foolish, how far beyond saving, was he? A girl who had actually lived four hundred years in solitude, given nothing but an unanswerable riddle as her reason to keep living—how could a mere three characters, "four hundred years," let him see that, understand it, feel it? How much could Subaru's thin, flimsy words possibly heal of her four hundred years of loneliness?

"Words like 'help me'... salvation like 'do something for me'... in these four hundred years, hasn't that wish long since withered away…"

"………"

"Do you think you're the only human who ever tried to take Betty away? Betty is a high-ranking spirit. There have been no small number of humans who sought her power, who tried, one way or another, to take Betty away."

It was news to Subaru. There had been others before him who'd tried to take Beatrice out of the Forbidden Archive, just as he had. And the outcome of those attempts was told plainly enough by her present existence. To Beatrice, who still looked at him with weak, weary eyes, Subaru shook his head and said,

"D-don't lump me in with people like that! I only wanted to—"

"Among them, there may have been some—naive fools—who, like you, disregarded Betty's power entirely and just wanted to save the person in front of them."

"——"

"But none of them could take Betty away. Naturally, wasn't it."

Because, Beatrice said, catching her breath, smiling that fragile, fleeting smile again,

"The contract binding Betty to this place can't be undone by any half-hearted resolve. A contract that has bound Betty to her role for four hundred years… it isn't something a mere human can break so easily."

"What… do I have to do…"

"—Make Betty first."

The words, thrown at him, were so quiet, and yet so sharp. Subaru felt as though his eardrum had been pierced by a thin needle.

"Wha… what?"

"Make Betty first. Think of her first. Choose her first. Overwrite the contract. Paint over the contract. Blot out the contract. Take her away. Draw her close. Hold her."

"——"

"That is something you can never do."

Beatrice's plea—earnest, desperate, heart-wrenching. It was too heavy a wish, at the far end of something too heavy, to be answered with a careless nod.

"Inside you, who comes first has already long since been decided, hasn't it. That silver-haired girl, or the blue-haired maid… either way, pushing the two of them aside to make Betty first—that, you absolutely cannot do. It's something you cannot do."

"Emilia… Rem…"

"A contract is absolute. Absolute, isn't it. To rewrite a contract made, by any means other than fulfillment, requires a commensurate price. Betty no longer believes, cannot believe, that the promise will ever be fulfilled. So there is only one other possibility, besides fulfillment, for release…!"

The two girls made Subaru's heart pound hard. Whenever he thought of them, his heart leapt, raced, burned. That was already an answer carved into his soul, one that could never, absolutely, be changed.

"So, break Betty's contract… and destroy this body, that's done nothing useful, that's spent all this meaningless time, doing nothing at all…"

"The contract… is it really that heavy? If you hate it so much, isn't that something your own will alone could change…?!"

He couldn't find an answer. He had no idea what to say to Beatrice. Subaru's answer, for that reason, was a cowardly one, that shifted the crux of the problem elsewhere. In an instant, a flash of despair crossed Beatrice's eyes. To Subaru, it felt like a fatal misstep.

"That… is the meaning of Betty's life."

"The contract, is…?"

"Betty was born for this contract, and lives for this contract. The role given to her the moment she was born, the contract she has never once fulfilled since the day she was born… and you're telling her, selfishly, to break it…? Is that what you're saying?"

"It's not selfish! Haven't you already fought hard for four hundred years?! Holding to just one promise all that time—who could blame you for that? Who could?! You've already done enough…!"

"I haven't fulfilled even one part of my duty! Throwing away the meaning of my birth, the reason I live—how could I go on living after that?! No one would blame me?! Betty blames herself! Betty absolutely will not forgive it! The spirit Beatrice will not allow such a cowardly way of living!!"

His legs trembling, Subaru stepped forward and gripped the small girl's shoulders, calling out to her. But the girl, raising her face, pushed back at Subaru with an even louder voice, shoving his chest away where their bodies touched, putting distance between them again. Even against the girl's feeble strength, Subaru's body was pushed back. His body wouldn't move with any strength. He couldn't even remember, in his own resolve, what it was he'd been looking at.

"For a spirit, a contract is absolute! A contract with one's contractor outweighs everything! It's the same for Onii-cha! Onii-cha, too—that's exactly why he prioritizes that silver-haired girl above everything else! Values her most! Loves her most! Between Betty and that girl, he would absolutely choose her! Even Onii-cha won't make Betty first!"

As a fellow spirit, Beatrice had clung to Puck more than anyone. Perhaps that had been the mark of a bond, a desperate clinging, to the one being who could share with her the kind of vast time that no human could ever accompany her through—four hundred years. What feelings Beatrice held for Puck, what Puck thought of Beatrice—Subaru had no way of knowing. But what Subaru couldn't know had already, within Beatrice, reached its answer.

She had had time—more than enough time—to agonize over it, and arrive at that answer.

Breathing hard, shoulders trembling, even the girl's carefully arranged ringlets had begun to come undone. Large tears welled in her wide, round eyes, and her quivering lips still flickered with weakness and pleading. Too small, Subaru thought—she was too small a girl. And how had everyone left a girl this small, alone, for so long?

"You know you're not the promised 'that person,' don't you…"

"——"

"But will you become 'that person' for me? Or, not 'that person,' but something else entirely—will you become that, and save Betty instead?"

"——"

Words wouldn't come. He couldn't casually nod, and still less could he impulsively deny it.

In this brief span of time alone, Subaru had come to know a fragment of the doubt and torment Beatrice had carried—something he had never even tried to learn before. But to truly understand her loneliness, in the fullest sense, he would have to live through that same four hundred years of solitude himself. Only then would he understand. Something no human could ever possibly do. Her troubles, her loneliness, her sorrow—these could never, ever be within Subaru's reach.

"That there's nothing to be done—no one understands that better than Betty does."

"Beatrice…"

"So kill Betty. With your own hand. Suicide, too, goes against the contract—something a spirit absolutely cannot do. So even dying, Betty can't do alone."

"Why me…?"

Beatrice reached out both hands, pleading. Afraid that he would take those hesitantly outstretched hands and, powerless, accept them, Subaru covered his face with both his own hands and said,

"Why would you want to entrust your ending, the end of your four hundred years, to me…?"

"Why… is it…"

Whining, weakness, an excuse to flee—Subaru could have condemned it as any of those, however he chose to phrase it, but Beatrice chose none of them. She tilted her head slightly, as if she herself didn't know the reason. Then, after a short pause, she nodded slowly.

"—Ah, Betty understands now."

"…………"

"The reason Betty is entrusting her ending to you is, surely… surely—"

If he heard that answer, there would be no escaping anymore. That certainty took root in him. He raised his head. So as not to have to hear her answer, he had to cover his ears. He had to stop her mouth from moving. His decision came too late. His realization came too late. There was no longer any way, however he tried, to make it in time.

Beatrice's lips began to shape the answer. It was coming. In that instant—

"Sorry to interrupt, in the middle of your conversation."

A voice that should not have been able to be heard spoke, and Subaru spun around, driven by a chill of dread. And then, he saw.

"—Shall I be the 'that person' for you instead?"

Standing in the doorway was the jet-black murderer, a blood-soaked kukri knife dangling from her hand.
