Arc 7 Chapter 69: "He Who Comes from the Imperial Capital"

—Whether to treat the second Sparka or the great banquet afterward as the main event of the day was a tough call, but in any case, three days had passed since that day when both had happened.

In the meantime, nothing noteworthy had happened on Sword Slave Island. What threw Subaru off was that a third Sparka, one welcoming new sword slave candidates from outside, hadn't been held, and not a single ordinary death match had been arranged either.

Ordinary death matches were a kind of practice bout meant to keep the sword slaves from losing their edge in preparation for the shows. That said, "practice match" or not, they were still the kind of thing where letting your guard down could get someone killed, and there were dangerous individuals who might kill someone even without letting their guard down. And unlike the Sparka, it seemed hard for Subaru to interfere in those death matches even if punishment was on the line — so in a sense, it was a relief.

"The guards have been awfully on edge too. Well, maybe they're just scared of you and me, brother."

So said Hiain, putting a rather generous spin on the mood of Sword Slave Island these past few days. This Lizard Human seemed to have been genuinely delighted that Subaru had saved his companions during the second Sparka, and his friendliness toward Subaru had shot up by leaps and bounds since then. Subaru had been the first to call him "brother," but by now Hiain was treating him like one right back.

"Well, I'm not exactly complaining."

Even while thinking, somewhat exasperated, that Hiain had a rather easily-bought personality, Subaru was more than happy to take the change in attitude. Anyone would rather get along amicably than be on edge with everyone all the time. Hiain's softening was striking, but Weitz and Idra's attitudes had shifted too.

Weitz treated him now with a kind of solid, settled trust, and Idra with something like quiet respect. And that shift in attitude had, in the best way, spread to the other sword slaves on the island as well. Which meant—

"Yo, Schwarz, good look in your eyes today too!" "You've got that fighting spirit overflowing!" "The guards are grumbling. Say at this rate there won't be any sword beasts left!"

More and more people were calling out to Subaru cheerfully and warmly like this. And just like with Hiain, this suited Subaru's purposes perfectly. The more people he could talk to, the easier it was to gather the information he wanted.

For now, with no need to worry about Gustav's Cursed Order, the information Subaru wanted most was—

"—the drawbridge control tower."

The sole means connecting the island to the outside world, the drawbridge — how to operate it, and how to get into its control tower. That was the information Subaru wanted most right now.

"The tower itself is somewhere everyone can see, but I can't get in, damn it..."

The control tower itself stood, quite impressively, right at the island-side foot of the drawbridge. There wasn't any particular guard posted there, but the entrance was secured with an extremely sturdy lock, and without that key there was no opening the huge metal door. For the past several days Subaru had been trying to track down where the key was kept, but it seemed Gustav operated the drawbridge himself, which meant there was a good chance he simply carried the key on him.

"Hmm, going up against Gustav-san seems like a bad bet."

Truthfully, Gustav's strength was still something of an unknown to Subaru. The man had four arms on that frame of his, and he'd been entrusted with managing Sword Slave Island — given that he had to keep a bunch of rough, violent sword slaves in line, he couldn't possibly be weak. Of course, fighting him once would settle the question of whether Gustav was strong or weak—

"But honestly, I don't want to stir things up with Gustav-san in the first place..."

By their respective positions, Subaru and Gustav stood in something like an adversarial relationship, sword slave versus warden. But personally, Subaru didn't dislike Gustav. If anything, in an Empire where so many people were hard to reason with, Gustav had never once raised his voice at Subaru and had always been willing to actually talk things through — which left Subaru with a rather favorable impression of him as a rare, reasonable person. At the very least, he was far better than the fake Cecilus, who wandered back and forth across the line between friend and foe. So ideally, Subaru wanted to quietly steal the key from Gustav without a confrontation.

"Still, openly hovering around Gustav-san the way Ceci does isn't really my style either."

Now that the binding of the Cursed Order was gone, in a sense, Gustav — a man faithful to his duties — could be considered no longer a dangerous adversary for Subaru. Even so, Subaru wasn't bold enough to go around openly searching for the key or asking about it carelessly. The worst case for Subaru would be Gustav, finding him a nuisance, locking him away somewhere on the island and sealing off his movements entirely. If that happened, Subaru would have no choice but to rely on his last resort behind his back molar. So—

"Guess all I can do is poke around little by little."

Scratching his head, Subaru muttered that, mouth twisting at the steep road ahead. To get information about the drawbridge, Subaru had been probing the people on the management side — the guards who watched over the sword slaves.

The guards, who answered to Gustav at the top, were fewer in number compared to the island's sword slaves, but their bearing gave off the sense of tough Imperial soldiers — no weakness, no real opening to exploit. Gustav's training and discipline seemed thorough; even when a child like Subaru tried talking to them, the guards' vigilance didn't loosen up at all — if anything it tightened. Subaru suspected the precedent of the fake Cecilus, an even less endearing child than himself, had something to do with that.

In any case, getting information out of the guards was proving very difficult. Whether or not Gustav had passed word about what Subaru might be scheming, the guards' sharp gazes followed him wherever he went, which made him think he might have slipped up a little. Even though it was all to draw out the truth about the Cursed Order, he'd ended up provoking Gustav's caution.

"If only I'd gotten punished over some other topic... nah, no use saying that now."

Reflection was important, but regret didn't produce anything in particular. Regret was just something you did for your own sake. Unfortunately, Subaru didn't have time to feel sorry for his own stupidity. He couldn't waste a single minute, a single second. If one guard turned him down, all that meant was he'd go try ten, a hundred more.

"Right, some great person once said pickup lines are a numbers game. Not that this is picking anyone up, though."

Fist clenched, Subaru rebuilt his resolve by drawing on the wisdom of a professional from an entirely unrelated field. And so, trudging along through the island to make himself unwelcome to guards in yet another area,

"You're restless, aren't you, Schwarz..."

"Wha— Weitz?"

It was Weitz who spoke to him in a low voice as Subaru wandered around searching for guards. Subaru tilted his head at Weitz, who sat on the ground leaning against a wall. The spot was near the passage connecting to the colosseum. Except the colosseum wasn't opened except during a Sparka or a death match, so an iron gate had been lowered across the passage. Which meant that today, with nothing going on, it was nothing more than a dead end.

"What are you doing here? Hide and seek?"

"Don't know what that is, but probably not... I came to have a look at what's going on underground."

"Underground... oh, that place."

Following Weitz's jerked chin, Subaru glanced that way. Right beside the passage leading to the colosseum was a door connecting to the island's underground. As Subaru knew, since the island floated on a lake, the basement didn't connect to anywhere. Well — technically it did connect outside, after a fashion—

"It just connects straight to the lake. Its purpose is disposing of corpses, apparently."

"Sword slaves and sword beasts who died in death matches... lumped together. If we'd lost, we'd have been fish food the same way."

"That's chilling."

Fish food meant the aquatic Witchbeasts living in the lake. Apparently some fairly ferocious ones had been set loose there; while the number one cause of death on Sword Slave Island was death matches, the number two — whether by accident or attempted escape — was reportedly falling prey to those Witchbeasts. If Subaru couldn't use the drawbridge, he too would have no choice but to become their food.

"Have you heard... the story of a sword slave who once escaped from the island..."

"Huh? Ah, yeah, I've heard. Because of that, the previous administrator got fired and Gustav-san was picked as the new one."

"The terrible conditions improved... but the sword slaves' standing never budged. What do you think..."

"What do I think about what..."

"Should we... resign ourselves to being sword slaves? To being ruled over...?"

"――――"

At Weitz's words, Subaru reflexively checked to make sure no one else was around. What Weitz had just said was a remark that skated dangerously close to the edge for a sword slave of Sword Slave Island to make. If a guard with a nasty streak overheard it, it would be more than enough grounds for a warning. Even setting that aside, Weitz, being of the same "Go" as Subaru, was probably watched more strictly than the other sword slaves to begin with.

"Um, Weitz, I don't think you should say careless things like that. I'm fine with it, but there's the question of what people around us might think, too."

Subaru tried, gently, to steer Weitz's thinking elsewhere. If Subaru alone got caught up in the next Sparka, there were ways to handle it. But if Weitz got dragged in too, the difficulty would shoot right up. But in response to Subaru's train of thought, Weitz continued, "Schwarz..."

"Why do you think I came down here...? To lend you a hand."

"—Ugh"

"You intend to leave this place, don't you...? I'll go along with that too. I have no interest in spending the rest of my days here as a sword slave... And I owe you a debt."

Saying that, Weitz slowly rose to his feet. Then, amid a face covered in tattoos, he turned serious eyes on Subaru. Held steady under that gaze, Subaru caught his breath and froze. It wasn't an atmosphere he could joke his way out of, not at all.

"Weitz, I—"

"I know who you are..."

"—Huh"

"But it doesn't matter... Don't lump me in with easygoing Hiain. I have no interest in the crown on your head. I'm only lending you my strength. Remember that..."

Being told that his true identity was known made every hair on Subaru's body stand on end. But the words Weitz followed with numbed his entire body even more than that. Whoever Subaru might be, Weitz had said he'd repay his debt, that he'd lend his strength. That was, that was so very—

"...That's reassuring, Weitz."

"Hmph..."

At Subaru, who grinned and gave a thumbs-up, Weitz looked away and snorted. Subaru took that utterly un-candid reaction favorably too, as very Weitz-like. The reason Weitz had come down here to scout out the basement had been to help Subaru in his search for how to operate the drawbridge — a way out. In the end, there was nothing useful down here after all; it seemed the conclusion would circle back to the same starting point: the drawbridge was the only way out.

Just as Subaru reached that conclusion—

"—Whoa, this shaking!"

"The drawbridge, huh..."

A low rumbling sound and a shuddering tremor, as if the ground itself were shaking, rang out, and Subaru and Weitz reached the same conclusion at the same moment, exchanging a look. There was no mechanism capable of shaking the whole of Sword Slave Island besides the colosseum and the drawbridge. And since the passage to the colosseum was right there, blocked by the two of them, naturally the only remaining candidate was the drawbridge.

"More new people, huh..."

Arrivals meant, in other words, that the next Sparka was about to begin. Tasting a sensation like his mouth going dry, Subaru murmured this, and Weitz looked over at him. Under that gaze, Subaru put on a smile. As if to reassure him that there was nothing to worry about.

"—. Let's go take a look..."

Whether that smile had any effect or not, there was no telling from the feelings of Weitz, who'd already turned his back. Still, following behind him as he said nothing unnecessary, Subaru and the others climbed from the island's lower level up to the middle level, then headed for the high ground overlooking the drawbridge.

"If possible, I'd like to see them open and close the tower entrance too, but..."

"Schwarz-sama, over here."

"Tanza"

Catching his breath from dashing up the stairs, wiping sweat from his chin, Subaru heard a girl's voice. Tanza, who'd arrived at the high ground first, waved to them, and Subaru and Weitz joined her. Tanza, glancing warily at Weitz, said,

"You were with Weitz-sama, I see."

"Ran into him downstairs by chance and was having an embarrassing conversation. What about you, Tanza?"

"...I've been watching the tower. It does seem that Gustav-sama is the one holding the key."

"Ugh, figured as much."

Tanza, too, understood that operating the drawbridge was essential for escaping the island. She'd been let in on the truth about the Cursed Order as well, so the two children were, in effect, plotting their escape together. Just then, Weitz poked his face in between them.

"If we just need to take the key... shall we go at it with numbers...?"

"That's a way too short-fused plan! Besides, pulling something that reckless would get us hit by the Cursed Order."

"—Huh"

"I see..."

Subaru talked Weitz's rash proposal down by holding up the Cursed Order as a shield. Hearing that, Tanza's eyes went wide, so Subaru put a finger to his lips to hush her.

The truth — that there was in fact no Cursed Order — hadn't been shared with anyone besides Tanza yet. Of course, the fake Cecilus, who'd been present at the time, knew too, but conversely, the fake Cecilus had no friends to tell it to, so there was no danger of it spreading that way. Still, Subaru considered this a truth far too frightening to spread carelessly.

"Part of why the sword slaves stay quiet is because the Cursed Order exists."

The fact that their treatment wasn't unbearable, and the fact that defying it meant the Cursed Order would take your life — those were the reasons the sword slaves of Ginunhive stayed obediently under Gustav's rule. If it became known there was no Cursed Order, people who thought like Weitz would surely start appearing. And then, an all-out war would break out between the sword slaves and the management side. Numbers-wise, even with casualties, the sword slave side might win, but—

"All the guards keep Witchbeasts with their horns broken, so the damage would be catastrophic."

It wasn't just that the guards themselves were strong — keeping Witchbeasts under their command was what made Sword Slave Island's guards so troublesome. Since every sword beast that fought in the Sparka was a Witchbeast kept by a guard, that meant there were still plenty more like the lion and the rat Subaru and the others had struggled so hard to defeat. So it would be a real problem if Weitz let his temper get the better of him here.

"Still, waiting for Schwarz-sama to single-handedly defeat every sword beast in the Sparka doesn't strike me as a terribly realistic plan."

"That's just as reckless as the other idea... Besides, if they bring in more sword beasts, my spirit's gonna break. I'm not planning anything like that."

"Then..."

"I've got plenty of ideas going, so don't worry. You don't have to believe in me, but at least believe that I've got someone I absolutely have to see again."

At that, which included keeping something hidden from Weitz, Tanza fell silent. She lowered her eyes slightly, then continued, "The person you wish to see—"

"—would that be a family member?"

"Hm? Well, I guess people as important as family count, but also, uh, a girl I like."

At the oddly pointed question, Subaru tilted his head and answered that way. Hearing it, Tanza moved her lips as if she still had something to say. But before she could, Weitz said "Look..." and pointed down below, at the drawbridge. From both sides, this one and the far one, the drawbridge was being activated — the new residents of Sword Slave Island were on their way over.

"What kind of people are the new arrivals...? Not that it'd happen, but I doubt they're acquaintances of Weitz and Idra this time."

"Anyone I'm acquainted with can all just die, far as I'm concerned..."

"That's scary, and also kind of a lonely thing to say, don't—"

He'd been about to say "don't say that," when the carriage crossing the drawbridge came clearly into view. It was a carriage pulled by a black, armored Galewind Horse, but there was something different about it from the one three days ago — the difference in how calm the occupant was.

Last time, Hiain's companions, Orson and the others, had apparently ridden along quietly in the carriage. This time was different. Someone was standing on the roof of the carriage. On the roof of the swaying carriage, a woman with brown skin and a slender build stood—

"—?!"

The instant he saw that face, those distinguishing features, Subaru choked back a scream in his throat and dropped into a crouch on the spot, hiding behind the railing, making himself small. His heart was pounding with tremendous force, the sound of blood rushing exploding inside his head.

"S-Schwarz-sama? What's wrong, dropping down so suddenly..."

"Ah..."

"Ah?"

"That woman... she's bad news."

Beside the crouched Subaru, Tanza, crouching the same way, widened her eyes in surprise. But Subaru didn't have the spare capacity to worry about Tanza's surprise right now.

"A woman... that woman...?"

Hearing Subaru's mutter, Weitz, leaning against the railing, looked toward the drawbridge. It seemed Weitz's eyes had spotted the figure atop the approaching carriage's roof too. Weitz didn't seem to know who the woman was, but Subaru knew exactly who she was. And knowing it, he thought: worst possible timing.

That carriage — or at least the woman on its roof — wasn't a candidate for the next Sparka. Because her true identity was—

"—Her name won't come to me right away, but she's one of the Nine Divine Generals."

Because she was that overwhelmingly strong woman with silver hair and an eyepatch who'd once caused havoc back in Guaral.

"It seems this round's visitor isn't a sword slave, but apparently an envoy from the Imperial Capital."

So reported Idra, who'd picked up some scraps of talk from a guard. Subaru and the others had been watching the carriage's arrival from the high ground, but the shock delivered by the woman on the roof — an opponent utterly beyond the norm — had been far too great.

"That woman noticed me and looked right this way..."

Beside the instantly-crouched Subaru, Weitz, who'd stayed to watch the carriage finish crossing the drawbridge, said this, his voice shaking as if he'd seen something unbelievable. They must have been a hundred meters apart or so, and yet she'd sensed his gaze even from there — a monster. Since it was apparently Weitz she'd looked at, Subaru had been entirely right to crouch down. Of course, even if she'd looked at Subaru right now, there was surely no way she'd have recognized him.

"A half-beast Dog Human, one of the Nine Divine Generals... almost certainly General Arakiya, I'd think."

"Tanza, you know her too?"

"...She was one of those who once targeted Yorna-sama of Chaosflame. I haven't seen it with my own eyes, but I hear she wrapped an entire district of a city in flame."

"E-even so, that's a pretty rough joke. Right?"

Hiain tried to laugh off Tanza's grave tone. But there wasn't a single person present who could laugh it off. Subaru in particular had taken quite a shock from it.

"An enemy who's just plain strong, with no tricks — that's the kind I have the hardest time with."

Against an opponent with some opening, some weakness that could be worked around with the right approach, Subaru had a chance of winning. But against someone whose sheer strength was simply on a different level, all strategies evaporated instantly. Because they were strong. Little tricks don't work against the strong. Trampling over all of it, scattering Subaru's preparations to the wind — that was the privilege of the truly powerful.

"Schwarz-sama, please don't be too pessimistic. Since she's an envoy from the Imperial Capital, it's unlikely this is about doing anything to us directly. To assume she's an enemy—"

"—is too soon, huh. Yeah, yeah, that's true. That's true, but..."

"――――"

"When I think of the worst case, I just can't help it..."

Hugging his own thin arms, Subaru answered Tanza, who was trying to encourage him. He appreciated Tanza's feelings — she herself surely didn't want to imagine making an enemy of one of the Nine Divine Generals either. To put it mildly, she was on par with Yorna in strength, or stronger still. Trapped on an island like this, there was no fighting someone on that top tier of strength.

"B-but why's brother so worried? If she came from the Imperial Capital..."

"Hiain, don't you get it? The Empire's split in two right now."

"Huh? Oh, ahh! Damn, so that's it...!"

Beside the brooding Subaru, Hiain and Idra were discussing something with grave faces too. Subaru couldn't quite follow the thread of their conversation, but he didn't have the spare attention to worry about that either. Realistically speaking, the chances that he was simply overthinking things were, in fact, fairly high.

"Right now, there's no way that I, or Tanza, could be the..."

"General Arakiya."

"...could be the target of General Arakiya's business here."

Even if Subaru and the others stood out somewhat badly within the island, it seemed unlikely that had spread all the way to the outside — and even if it had, given what they looked like, it seemed odd that it would ever become a problem worth acting on. Even Olbart, who presumably wanted to do something about Abel, had no apparent reason to bother with Subaru or Tanza.

"So then, what business would the Imperial Capital have sending an envoy to the island?"

"What could it be... the island's shows, perhaps...?"

"The shows... this island's original purpose, I suppose."

Weitz and Tanza came up with a plausible-sounding possibility for what had brought Arakiya here. The island's shows — that was Sword Slave Island Ginunhive's original function, putting sword slaves against each other in death matches as entertainment for visiting spectators. A tasteless spectacle, but apparently one useful for keeping the Imperial citizenry's more frightening appetites in check.

"If it's about the shows, th-then we're gonna get made to fight again... but with a rebellion breaking out and everywhere in an uproar, would they really do it now?!"

"There's also the thinking that it's precisely because of times like these... The idea that with the Empire in turmoil, there's all the more reason to tighten the resolve that Imperial citizens should be strong and resilient..."

"His Majesty the Emperor's thinking, is it..."

Hiain's voice cracked, Idra's reasoning carried some weight, and Weitz's murmur was heavy — and each of their gazes turned toward Subaru. It was as if they expected Subaru might actually know the answer, but Subaru didn't have that answer either. All he could do was shake his head.

"Both Hiain's idea and Idra's idea seem plausible to me. Just..."

"Just?"

"I doubt the Emperor's seriously concerned about this place..."

Subaru knew that the Emperor issuing orders from the Imperial Capital was an impostor. Both the real one and the fake one had been in Chaosflame, but with the rebel army having risen up, it seemed unlikely Abel had reclaimed his throne without Subaru's knowledge. Which meant that if this was an order from the capital, it was the thinking of the fake Emperor. And it seemed hard to believe that fake Emperor — Vincent, who could think along almost exactly the same lines as Abel — would waste effort for nothing.

"There has to be some reason for this. Absolutely."

Some reason, specifically, why — at a time like this, instead of Abel, who ought to be the true Emperor — one of the Nine Divine Generals had been dispatched to an island seemingly forgotten by the rest of the world, floating on a lake.

"General Arakiya wasn't accompanied by a large force, was she?"

"Right..."

"I only saw it from a distance too, but what came was a single carriage. Five or six people at most, I'd guess. Even the attention it drew was about what you'd expect for just a General."

"I see..."

Eyes lowered, Tanza, just like Subaru, stumbled over not understanding the other side's purpose. It might well be overthinking things — there was also the view that Subaru and the others should simply keep their heads down and wait for the storm to pass.

"Looked at another way, how about this... hide in the envoy's carriage, leave the island..."

"I feel like we already tried hiding in a carriage back at Chaosflame."

"If the same method works no matter how many times we use it, I see no issue with that..."

Subaru and Tanza took Weitz's proposal seriously. But Hiain, who'd been listening to the three of them talk, raised his voice: "Wait, wait, wait!" The gray-scaled Lizard Human turned everyone's attention toward himself with his loud voice, then said,

"Are you out of your minds, leaving the island?! You'll die!"

"Staying here as we are, we'll eventually be made to fight something and die anyway, coward..."

"But that's not happening today or tomorrow! Schwarz... you've got a brother here too, don't you?!"

"I'm not planning on staying here forever either. If anything, I have no intention of overstaying."

Hiain's big mouth, lined with small fangs, trembled as he was left speechless at Subaru's stance. For Hiain, perhaps living inside the island was actually more comfortable than the outside. It wasn't an exaggeration to say that once you cleared the Sparka and became a sword slave, your safety outside of death matches was more or less guaranteed. Subaru could understand the impulse to feel that way too.

"Squirt! Idra! You two are..."

"Like Schwarz-sama, I too have reasons I cannot afford to stay here long."

"I... if it's possible to leave, I'd like to leave, is how I feel."

Tanza and Idra answered Hiain's words, spoken as if he sensed the tide was against him. Positive or reluctant, the nuance differed, but neither of them thought well of Sword Slave Island either. That seemed unbelievable to Hiain.

"E-even reckless disregard for your own life has its limits...!"

"Sorry for troubling you, Hiain. But I want you to think about it too. Whether we really ought to stay here forever."

"――――"

Hiain looked at Subaru and the others with eyes like he was staring at something terrifying. But if he kept thinking without stopping, thought hard about it, Hiain would surely understand too — that the safety and security you could get here was nothing more than treading water, paid for by sacrificing all sorts of other things.

"...Fine, say we leave — what about the Cursed Order?"

Dropping his flat denial, what Hiain threw out next was that. The invisible chain that bound the sword slaves of Sword Slave Island, the biggest reason the sword slaves here stayed smoldering in place without acting — that was the same thing Weitz had brought up too. Subaru wanted to avoid saying, until the very last moment, that it had already been neutralized. But as long as Hiain was wary of it, he'd keep doubting whether Subaru and the others were in their right minds. So Subaru decided he'd at least try to ease that wariness a little.

"I'm working on the Cursed Order thing too. Actually, I've already got a lead on how."

"—?! Y-you mean the Cursed Order?"

"Yeah."

Judging that holding back here wouldn't do any good, Subaru nodded with a face full of confidence. At that answer, not just Hiain but Weitz and Idra too couldn't hide their surprise. Only Tanza, already knowing there was no Cursed Order to begin with, watched over the scene quietly without a word.

He couldn't tell them the truth, but Subaru puffing out his chest here would give Hiain and the others something to steady their anxiety with. So he couldn't afford to show an unconfident face. Even if Arakiya's purpose as an envoy remained unclear—

"I'll definitely find a way off this island. So—"

When that time comes, I'll need your help. — That's how he meant to continue. But Subaru's words couldn't continue.

To be precise, continuing wouldn't have meant anything anymore.

"—ka"

A rasping sound of breath escaping, and Hiain's eyes went bloodshot as he collapsed.

"――――"

His narrow pupils shot through with blood, Hiain's huge body toppled sideways. With no warning whatsoever, it happened so suddenly that Subaru couldn't react in time — couldn't even catch Hiain as he fell.

"H-hiain?"

With a heavy thud, Hiain's body crumpled limply onto the floor of the communal room. Once down, Hiain didn't move again. Only his hands and feet twitched, spasming. It looked, Subaru thought, like a cicada. A cicada flipped on its back on a road in midsummer.

Moving like a dying cicada, Hiain— And it wasn't just Hiain.

"...Ah"

A lighter sound than Hiain's collapse rang out, and Subaru spun around, dumbfounded. And there they were, collapsed. The three who'd been talking with Subaru — Idra, Weitz, and Tanza — had all fallen to the ground there, trembling the same way.

"Tanza?"

It made no sense. It made so little sense he couldn't even react. Crouching beside Tanza, who'd collapsed right next to him, he turned her face-down body over. Limply, her head lolling, tears of blood streamed from Tanza's eyes. Blood was flowing from her eyes, her nose, her ears too, dripping steadily onto the floor.

"Hi"

A sound caught in Subaru's throat, and he dropped Tanza's body. Hurriedly he checked the other three as well. All of them, blood streaming from eyes and nose, had gone still.

"Wha..."

Why — the questioning voice began ringing enormously loud inside his head. Why, why, why, unable to make sense of the meaning of what lay before him, Subaru's head spun round and round, filling up entirely with that question, his ears ringing at a deafening volume. The illusion of his skull splitting apart tried to swallow him whole—

"Th-this is wrong..."

Pressing a hand to his own head, Subaru looked up. A sharp, stabbing pain struck his head, and blood came dripping from Subaru's nose. The ringing in his ears wouldn't stop either, and there was a wet, seeping sensation of blood welling up.

The order was wrong. Later than Tanza and the others, but Subaru had the same pain too. Had it, and — what was this.

"Something's... wrong..."

Hand on the floor, hand on the wall, Subaru somehow managed to stand. Standing, he went out into the corridor to confirm what was happening. Inside the room, Tanza and the others were already beyond saving. And it wasn't just Tanza and the others who were beyond saving — it was so much more, so much more, everything.

"――――"

The neighboring communal room too, the passages here and there too, the great hall too, the foot of the drawbridge too — everyone, everything, was beyond saving. Dragging his feet, vision flickering, feeling as if the contents of his head were dissolving out through his ears, Subaru, clothes soaked in blood, made his way around the island looking.

Everyone was dead. Everyone, everyone, was dead. Everyone bleeding from eyes, from nose, from ears, dead. Dead.

"Why?"

Why so suddenly, sword slaves and guards alike, indiscriminately, all dead like this. Everyone, all at once, out of nowhere.

"Why?"

Everyone had disappeared. Suddenly, out of nowhere. Unable to believe it, unable to accept it, all he could do was ask, haltingly. Ask whom? The sky. If not the sky, then the water.

"Wh—"

"—Hey."

Unexpectedly, someone answered Subaru's voice, which should have gone unanswered by anyone. Suddenly, with no warning at all, left behind on an island where everyone had abruptly died, bleeding from his own nose and ears, Subaru was anxious. Frightened. So, toward the voice of someone who seemed like they might give him an answer, Subaru turned — and then.

"—What are you doing here, kid?"

There, freezing in an instant the Subaru who'd felt a flicker of relief, stood a man.

"Like you can see, I figured everyone on this island was wiped out, root and branch, but..."

So said the man in the black bandana, cracking his neck as he spoke. His bright orange hair was tied back with a bandana with a red line through it, and his slender silhouette was dressed as an Imperial soldier. At a glance, he looked like he had a friendly, approachable face. But that was a tremendous misunderstanding. A grave mistake. Don't be fooled. The kind-looking face, the voice that sounded eager to get along — all of it, fabricated.

The name of that man, built entirely out of fabrication, was—

"—Todd"

A name kept in a place important enough that Subaru, even as the drawers of his memory were supposedly growing harder and harder to pull open, could draw it out without hesitation. And as for why that name mattered so much—

"...You, kid. How the hell do you know my name?"

Because the man who could switch his whole demeanor at a single word, closing the distance, was a dangerous individual. The man — Todd — strode toward Subaru with heavy steps. Subaru tried desperately to flee from him, but his body, gone ragged on its own, wouldn't let him. His back was caught up with in an instant, and Subaru's body was slammed roughly against the wall.

"Ow...!"

"No memory of you. I don't forget a face I've seen once — and this one's not familiar. — No, wait."

Pressed cheek-first against the rough surface of the building's wall, Subaru writhed at a pain that showed no mercy. Todd brought his nose close to Subaru's hair. Then, right there, Todd sniffed, taking in the scent of Subaru's hair.

"You smell like a scary bastard I know, kid."

Said in that low voice, Subaru had no idea who he meant, and it chilled him to the bone. It felt like a combination of being spoken about himself and pure revulsion toward Todd. A face he'd hoped never to see again — and here it was, on this island, of all places, somehow.

"Why...?"

Why was Todd here, a man who should have fled Guaral, in a place like this? Now that he thought of it — Todd had taken the captured Arakiya and fled with her. He should have realized it. If Arakiya was here, Todd might be too. That was stupid, though. Just because they'd fled together didn't mean it was any kind of certainty they'd be together at the next place too.

Why, how, no, scary, why, here, Todd—

"Why did you... everyone..."

"Kill them, you mean? I won't answer that. From where I'm standing, you surviving is the miscalculation here. Well, might just be a matter of time even if I leave you be."

"—Ugh"

"I'm not a fan of leaving things to chance or coincidence, though."

Todd's voice whispered right by his ear, and something cold was pressed lightly against his neck. It was the cold blade of the large knife Todd had drawn from his hip. A knife suited for skinning some big animal — if he pulled it even a little harder, Subaru felt like it wouldn't just cut his throat, it would take his head clean off. And even if the other party was a child, Todd wouldn't hesitate to do it. Except—

"――――"

There was, just possibly, a chance. A chance that Todd wouldn't kill Subaru — no, wouldn't kill him here, would put it off instead. A chance he'd try to get something out of Subaru first. If that happened, it would be irreversible. Irreversible, and a chance of despair. So, if anything—

"Wait" "—!"

His head throbbed, and the nosebleed wouldn't stop. In the midst of a sensation of his consciousness swimming from blood loss, Subaru somehow probed with his tongue for his back molar, trying to use his last resort.

The next instant, Todd forced his fingers into Subaru's mouth.

"—Ah"

Roughly, forcibly having his mouth invaded, Subaru retched violently. The contents of his stomach surged back up. But Todd didn't care in the slightest, kept moving his fingers. And then, along with the vomit that came back up, he pulled it out.

"...The hell is this. Don't tell me it's poison."

"――――"

"Hey now, what kind of kid are you? Going to the trouble of planting poison in your own mouth — that's a suicide measure for when things go south, isn't it? Something's messed up about treating your own life so cheap."

Wiping his fingers, dirtied by Subaru's own clothes, Todd said this with an easy, unbothered air. But he didn't sound like he was joking. It felt more like he genuinely couldn't believe Subaru's line of thinking, like it was something sincerely beyond him. As though he were saying that suicide was a ridiculous idea in itself.

"Right, then. It's not like I get some kick out of making you suffer. You can use it, if you want."

"Th-that's..."

"Yeah, if you'll just go ahead and die on your own, suicide works fine by me too. Saves me from getting my knife dirty."

As proof of that, Todd shrugged and put the packet of "medicine" back inside Subaru's mouth. Placed neatly right above the back molar, positioned so that biting down hard would break the packet. That would let the contents leak out, and Subaru's life would end. All while knowing exactly that.

"Either way's fine by me. As long as you die."

Grabbed by the shoulder, Subaru was spun around to face him. Todd's figure was right there in front of him, and Subaru's back was pressed against the wall. He wanted somehow to land one blow back at Todd, but strength kept draining out of his body, replaced by numbness.

Slowly, the same "death" that had taken Tanza and the others began eating away at Subaru too. Why it was coming slowly, slowly like this, he had no idea.

"Time's up, suicide, or the knife."

"――――"

"Choose."

The destination doesn't change, Todd said, holding up three fingers, demanding Subaru's answer. So very, very quiet, and Subaru hated Todd with everything in him.

Tanza, too. Hiain, too. Weitz, too. Idra, too. All dead. Orson and the others too. Old man Null too. The guards too. Everyone, dead.

Why was Todd alive. Why was Subaru dying too.

"—!"

Feeling as if his chest was being torn to shreds, Subaru bit down hard on his back molar. The packet broke, and the "medicine" inside leaked out. It seeped, slowly, into the gaps between his tongue and teeth, seeping in, seeping in, and then—

"—Bwahh"

Subaru spat the massive amount of blood pooling in his mouth straight at Todd, standing before him. It wasn't meant to drag him down together. It was meant, with the will to land one last blow, to stain his clothes. But Todd's instincts were sharp. The moment Subaru braced himself, he leapt aside. Because of that, none of the blood reached him. Having only spat out blood for nothing, Subaru collapsed.

He fell, and fell, and then, and then, and then—

"Ah, u, ah, oaaaaaaahh...!"

His entire body convulsed violently, the poison racing through him at tremendous speed. Blood poured from his nose and ears with even greater force than before, his eyes swelling up as if about to burst, every muscle, every bone in his body howling all at once. No, not howling — it just sounded that way. His entire body was screaming.

"I, gi, giggii, giiiiiii...!"

His blood was hot as if it were burning. Hot as though he'd been thrown into a boiling pot. His whole body seethed and roiled, as though his skin had been peeled off and wasabi, mustard, every sharp and burning thing imaginable had been smeared all over him, as though thousands of needles had been driven into him, as though his entire body were being grated on a grater — it hurt, it hurt, it hurt, it hurt.

Just from the pain alone, he wanted to die. Just from the suffering alone, he wanted to die.

"You're something else, kid."

Thrashing like a fish hauled up on a line, convulsing, heading straight toward death. Watching Subaru drown in that sea of blood, Todd muttered something. What he was saying, Subaru couldn't tell — couldn't tell, had no way of telling anymore. Already, nothing made sense anymore. Nothing made sense, anymore—

"—Normally, if you're gonna prep poison for suicide, you'd pick something that doesn't make you suffer."

The voice, distant, and consciousness, flying off toward white, toward white. And still, right to the very end, the very end, the pain and the suffering and blood, blood blood blood, blood, blood blood blood blood, ch-ch-ch-ch, ch——

A snap sounded, and blood, and eyes, could be seen, uh.

△▼△▼△▼△

"I know who you are..."

"――――"

"But it doesn't matter... Don't lump me in with easygoing Hiain. I have no interest in the crown on your head. I'm only lending you my strength. Remember that..."

There had been pain — burning, searing, his whole body scraped away. That pain, just as it seemed to cut off abruptly, Subaru found himself in air that felt chillingly cold. And then—

"Ah, ah..."

"Schwarz...?"

"AAAAAAAAH—!!"

Wrapping his own body in his arms right there on the spot, Subaru opened his mouth wide and screamed like that. Screamed, and screamed, trying to drive the poison out from inside himself. Trying to expel poison that shouldn't be in his body anymore. Poison that felt like it was still there, still going to kill him.

That deadly poison, tormenting Subaru, tormenting him, determined without fail to kill him. No matter how many times he tasted it—

"Gu, hiu, aaahuuuuaaaaaa...!"

The "death" brought on by that drug reminded Subaru there was no easy way to die — it was the guardian of that final, forbidden bastion he must never, ever flee toward.
