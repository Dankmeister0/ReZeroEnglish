Arc 7 Chapter 4: "A Courageous Choice"

"Whoa!!"

The instant a sharp shock ran through his right hand, the whip's grip was torn from his grasp.
He instinctively tightened his grip to resist, but he was no match for the force pulling it away — if anything, he was yanked forward, tumbling face-first and flipping over onto the ground with an "Abeshi!"

"Owww..."

"Every day, nothing but diligent practice. It seems you still lack sufficient training. Pitiful."

"Can't argue with that..."

Sprawled on the ground, Subaru grimaced at the biting remark raining down from above. As he slowly pushed himself up, his eyes met a cold, handsome face looking down at him.
A man with deep blue hair neatly trimmed, his sharp eyes calling to mind something reptilian. True to the impression given by the monocle over one eye and the butler's uniform, he was a meticulous, sharp-edged professional through and through.
His name was—

"—Clind! What an insolent thing to do to Subaru-sama!"

"..."

The young man called Clind wordlessly turned his gaze aside. There, striding purposefully toward them, came a tall woman.

A beautiful woman with a fine figure, her long golden hair swaying, her eyes a lovely emerald green. Clad in a black-based maid uniform, she walked straight past Clind and over to Subaru on the ground, gently holding out a white cloth.

"Are you all right, Subaru-sama? From the looks of it, you landed face-first..."

"Yeah, I'm fine, Frederica. My clothes got all muddy, but that's par for the course."

Gratefully taking the cloth and wiping his face, Subaru was watched with concern by the beautiful woman — Frederica — who pressed on with a "But—" in response to his answer.
But it was Clind who shrugged wearily at Frederica's words.

"As he himself said, this is standard procedure for Subaru-sama's training. If anything, isn't it you who's making a needless fuss and embarrassing Subaru-sama? Meddlesome."

"Wha—!"

Frederica flushed at Clind's remark, baring sharp teeth and narrowing her eyes.
As the two of them began glaring at each other, Subaru felt like burying his face in his hands. Here we go again...

—Clind and Frederica.

Butler's uniform and maid's uniform — both were servants of House Mathers, and though he'd heard theirs was a long-standing acquaintance, the two didn't get along particularly well.
Normally, both were courteous, attentive, and capable servants, but whenever the two of them came into contact like this, an argument was quick to break out over one thing or another.
Sometimes it was a highly sophisticated exchange of views as servants regarding Roswaal's affairs of state; other times it was a pointless squabble over who should yield the right of way in a hallway.
In short, it seemed the two of them simply didn't click—

"Frederica, I appreciate you worrying about me, but I'm fine, really."

"No, it's not that I'm worried about you, Subaru-sama. It's just that this man's smug face, his attitude, his thoughtless remarks — they infuriate me, that's all...!"

"It wasn't about me after all! How embarrassing!"

"Ah, no! It's not that I'm not worried! Of course I worry about you, Subaru-sama. Not just me — Petra does too."

Frederica seemed to have blurted that out without thinking, and at the mention of Petra's name from her lips, Subaru could only scratch his cheek and give a wry smile.
Aside from Ram, the women of the mansion — Petra especially — were constantly worried about him.
The truth of the matter was probably that, since Petra couldn't get away right now, she'd asked Frederica to come check on Subaru in her stead.

"But it was me who asked for this. I knew going in it'd be spartan... No matter what, I've got to shed a layer before Master heads back to Annerose's place."

"—A fine resolve. That's the sort of thing that puts real effort into my instruction. Invigorating."

Hearing Subaru's answer, Clind swung his arm, and the ivory-colored whip in his hand cracked through the air with a snap.
Clind was helping Subaru learn the same weapon, and out of respect for his skill and standing, Subaru called him Master.

"Honestly, there's only so far I could've gotten teaching myself. It's a huge help having someone nearby who can actually use a whip."

"I'm honored to be of use. Delightful. Though I'm not the only one who can handle a whip, you know. A candidate."

"Huh, someone else can use one? You don't mean Ram, like in the sense of wielding a whip of words, right?"

"Subaru-sama, that sort of thinking is... well, I can't deny it in Ram's case, but still."

Frederica made a sour face at Subaru's quip as he picked up his dropped whip, readying himself for the next round. Comparing the two of them, Clind closed the eye with the monocle and said,

"It's a simple matter. The Master, of course. Omnipotent."

"In that case, there's no way I want him teaching me!"

Even as he said it, Subaru trampled the option that had never really existed and swung his whip.
The whip, capable of exceeding the speed of sound at its top speed when swung in earnest, howled through the air, its tip aiming — not for Clind, but for the piece of firewood set atop the stump between them.

To wrap the whip around the log standing upright on the round stump and capture it.
That was the task Clind had set for Subaru, and to this day it remained a high hurdle he had yet to clear even once.
That wasn't purely because Subaru's skill was too immature to catch the log with the whip.

"Your aim is completely transparent. Blocked."

"Guh...!"

Clind's whip struck away the tip of the one Subaru had loosed, with fearsome precision.
Overcoming this interference and drawing the log to hand with the whip was the true condition of the task. This was the real reason Subaru had never once cleared the hurdle.

"Strike where you aim, ensnare what you aim for, produce the result you aim for. You must fulfill the purpose of choosing a whip rather than relying on a sword or spear. Otherwise it's meaningless."

"Gah! Guh! Grr! Dammit! Struck away without so much as batting an eye! I know that without you telling me! Using my own cunning is the only path I've got left...huh!?"

As he tried to draw back the parried whip, its tip was caught by Clind's once more.
By the time he realized it, it was too late. The next instant his balance was thrown off, and Subaru was easily pulled down by a Clind who didn't even seem to be putting much effort into it.

"Oof-splat!"

"That was quite the spectacular fall. Knowing how to take a hit is important too, you know. Caution."

"S-Subaru-sama!"

Frederica rushed over to Subaru, who had once again gone down face-first. This time he'd struck the ground with his face, and blood dripped steadily from his nose.
Frederica hurriedly pressed the cloth to it for him.

"Clind, this is going too far, no matter how you look at it!"

"Given Subaru-sama's position, this can't be helped. Necessary. You mustn't interfere too much with a young man's determination to push himself, either. Understood?"

"'A young man's determination,' he says..."

Frederica looked as though she couldn't believe it, but Clind shook his head. Then, with cold, clear eyes, he fixed his gaze on Subaru, who was wiping away his nosebleed.

"Subaru-sama, I hold your resolve in seeking instruction in high regard. Admirable. As one who stands as a knight at Emilia-sama's side, you need strength — the strength to protect Emilia-sama and yourself to the very end. Essential."

"...Yeah, I know. That's why I'm grateful you're being hard on me."

Even snuffling through his stuffed-up nose, Subaru's morale didn't break.
Seeing that, Frederica pressed her lips together tightly as well.

"Men really are foolish when together, just like Garf..."

"If I could match him in muscle as well as stupidity, that'd be a huge help, but it doesn't seem like that's in the cards."

Pinching his nose, blowing blood out of each nostril in turn, he forced his nosebleed to a stop.
Then, looking at Subaru as he stood up, Clind gave a deep nod.

"Ever forward, never stopping... That attitude is truly a pleasing thing. Long-awaited."

"Much obliged. ...If I get better with the whip, my options increase. And if my options increase, it's not just me — the hands available around me increase too."

"Indeed. But don't place too much faith in it. A situation where Subaru-sama must fight alone is not one that's likely to come up very often. Support."

Clind jerked his chin, and the one he indicated was Frederica.
Starting with her, Subaru had Garfiel and Otto around him, and closest of all, Beatrice. And if it came to fighting to protect someone, Emilia too, of course.
So Clind's point was correct.
Subaru's kind of fighting wasn't about wresting victory for himself, but about making the most of someone other than himself.

"By the way, hypothetically, if I somehow ended up all alone in a situation like that, what should I do?"

"You should run. Headlong."

"..."

Clind didn't laugh at the half-joking quip.
It was a situation almost impossible to occur, but that didn't mean it never would. For a truly desperate, cornered-to-the-last situation like that, Clind's advice was blunt and to the point.

"Run, Subaru-sama. Run headlong, unashamedly, heedless of appearances, with everything you've got. That is the wise course. The only one."

"The way you say that, it sounds less like the best option and more like there's simply no other choice..."

At those words, Clind didn't answer, his expression unchanged.
There was no need to answer. Which, in the end, was probably proof enough that Subaru was right.

"Geez, my master shows no mercy!"

"That man's always been like that. He raises promising people harshly... but the moment he loses interest, he tosses them aside. Coldly, cruelly."

"Delica-san? Your voice just got kind of cold there?"

Sensing a lethal edge sharp enough to cut if touched, Subaru asked timidly, and Frederica snapped, "I don't know!" turning her face away sullenly.
Then she brushed off the hem of her maid uniform.

"I've decided. I too shall root for the success of Subaru-sama's training. Please, do make that insufferably composed face of Clind's twist into a look of misery."

"That's, uh, a pretty tall order...!"

"My, and this from the man asking Emilia-sama to become queen?"

Truly, a capable maid — first-rate even at provocation.
At Frederica's words, Subaru's eyes widened, and he nodded with a mischievous grin, saying, "Fair enough." Then he readied his whip with renewed strength.

"If I can't pull it off after being told that, I'm no man at all! I'll do it — just watch!"

With a valiant shout, Subaru wound up and unleashed the whip with everything he had.
Slicing a sharp arc through the air with a whoosh, the tip of the whip shot straight for the stump—

"Your enthusiasm is admirable, but your skill is lacking. Clumsy."

"Guh, guaaaaah—!!"

"S-Subaru-sama—!!"

Once again, he was hauled down and slammed face-first into the ground.

△▼△▼△▼△

Overhead, a great tree snapped in two, the shockwave of its splitting slamming into his entire body.
Taking the blast against his back, Subaru spat out the blood filling his mouth, its taste like rusted iron.

"..."

In his arms was Rem's body, soft and warm.
His own hands and feet were still attached. It seemed he'd managed to dodge the arrow's strike. Knowing that much, this moment was a passing grade.

"W-what suddenly do you think you're—"

"Shut up, you'll bite your tongue!"

Rem's reaction had lagged behind the suddenness of it all, but he had no time to lend an ear to her protest.
Subaru hauled her body up as he held it, and having fallen forward, next threw himself backward, rolling hard with Rem still clutched to his chest.
He could feel Rem stifling a scream in his arms, but it was drowned out by a far louder sound — the thunderous roar of the snapped great tree crashing down.

The spot where Subaru and Rem had been lying just moments before was crushed beneath the great tree, and the other trees caught up in its fall spread the destruction further through the forest.
As a human being who lived by nature's grace, that fact struck painfully at his heart — but,

"—Right now, this takes priority!"

Mixed into the dust kicked up by the falling tree, Subaru didn't let up his rolling momentum. Rem struggled against him, but he pulled her close by main force, drawing on the strength of desperation.
Rolling on and on, the ground suddenly vanished beneath them.

"Guoah!"

"Kyaa!?"

The sensation of floating lasted only an instant before the ground below caught the two of them as they fell.
A great pit heaped with fallen trees and earth — it was the pitfall trap Rem had dug to catch Subaru.
By deliberately rolling into that pit, he'd cut off their attacker's line of fire.
That said, it hadn't come without cost.

"Guh, gah... y-you... you broke them...!"

His left arm, which he'd wrapped around Rem in a desperate frenzy — the fingers she'd grabbed onto had been snapped. Middle, ring, and pinky — everything except the thumb and index finger was wiped out.
As Subaru writhed in pain, trying not to look directly at his fingers now bent at odd angles, Rem, who'd fallen into the same pit, crawled away to put some distance between them.

"Of course they broke! Grabbing me like that out of nowhere... What in the world is going on!?"

"...Never got around to mentioning it, but there's a dangerous hunter in this forest. Though the chance this was just a stray shot from someone hunting deer is pretty much ruled out now. ...Gah."

With cold sweat beading on his forehead, Subaru straightened his three broken fingers and, for the time being, splinted them with a branch and secured it with a cloth.
It was a small mercy that it was the fingers of his left hand that had broken. If it had been his dominant right hand, the one he used for the whip, his combat effectiveness would have dropped to somewhere around that of a grade-schooler.

"A dangerous hunter... Isn't he on your side?"

"Would an ally give covering fire with this much force? And covering fire for what, anyway...whoa!?"

The instant he gently poked his head out of the pit to check on the situation outside, the fallen tree right in front of him exploded.
It seemed the hunter had begun prioritizing clearing the sightline first, in order to take a clean shot at them. He'd already sized them up as having no long-range means of attack.

"Normally, the standard doctrine is for a sniper to relocate the moment their position is exposed... Damn it, he's underestimating us. Not that I can argue with that."

"...This is the power of a bow and arrow? I can't believe it. This isn't normal."

"Yeah! I think so too! I bet if we get hit, it'll blow a huge hole clean through our chests!"

In fact, being shot through by an arrow and pinned to a tree like a mounted insect specimen was exactly how he'd died last time.
Still, something was off. —Last time, Subaru had ended up in the forest on the opposite side.

"Why the hell are you over here...?"

The pain traveling up from his broken fingers stabbed into Subaru's brain like a drill, over and over.
Gritting his back teeth hard enough that the pain felt like it would split his jaw, Subaru forced his mind to work desperately.

It was hard to imagine this hunter and the one who'd killed Subaru before were different people.
Both had attacked Subaru, and both wielded the same powerful bow. The question was whether this assault was incidental or deliberately targeted.
Either way, the question of why Subaru had been targeted at this particular moment had no end.
It was true that Subaru and Rem had been facing off, and that Subaru had frozen in place at her rejection — but even so, it didn't seem like that had made him a noticeably easier target than before.

What's more, if the attacker had deliberately targeted them, the possibility that they'd been followed came to the surface.
The situation was still frustratingly unclear, but Subaru had no grasp of exactly how he and the others had been sent flying from the Pleiades Watchtower. It wasn't impossible that they'd streaked across the sky like a shooting star and crashed into that meadow.
Of course, in that case — Rem aside — there'd be no explaining how Subaru's fragile body hadn't been smashed to pieces, so it was probably safe to assume a different situation was at play.

"Maybe he's targeting suspicious people who dropped in out of nowhere, or something..."

Or perhaps this was private land, and the hunter was trying to drive off trespassers with excessive force — a hunter whose restraint was poor despite his marksmanship being anything but. How about that theory?

"If that's the case, then maybe we can talk this out! Hey! I mean you no harm! Us being in this forest is just a coincidence—"

"Wait just a moment! That 'us' — does that include me and that girl? I don't want to be lumped in with you!"

"This isn't the time to be saying that—guwah!?"

The response to Subaru's call for a ceasefire was a single arrow that gouged a huge chunk out of the ground.
Its ferocious force was enough to blow away even his squabble with Rem from his mind — and before long, it would clear away the obstacles blocking the sightline and bare its fangs at Subaru and Rem in the pit too.

"It seems he's not the type you can reason with..."

"If he's using arrows, I've got to consider the chance he could hit us with an arcing shot too... If he really settles in and waits for hours like some sniper... no, wait, with a bow it's not like peering through a scope, so maybe he can't hold out that long?"

In movies and manga, there were often scenes of a sniper with a rifle lying in wait for hours on end, but surely a bow and arrow would be a different matter.
Unlike a gun, where all you had to move was the trigger, an arrow had to be nocked and the string drawn and held. Even someone with the absurd, otherworldly stamina of this world had their limits.
Which meant that what the enemy hunter would be aiming for was—

"—A short, decisive battle."

Concluding that the enemy would make some move before too long, Subaru set himself a time limit, knowing he had no room to think things over at length.
Since the enemy had no intention of talking things out, a fight was unavoidable. And yet, for an unavoidable fight, the cards in his hand were pitifully few.

"Just like Master taught me — running is the only move."

Fortunately, thanks to diving into the pit, their attacker couldn't visually confirm their position. If they climbed out the far side and stayed low, they might be able to make it into the underbrush and escape.
Or—

"..."

"...? Why'd you suddenly go quiet? Weren't you just working out how to escape?"

"...I guess with it being an emergency and all, you've decided you're willing to actually listen to me."

"Hmph."

Rem, her well-shaped brows knitting, sat with her knees folded on the dirt and made a displeased face.
But since she didn't lash out right away, it seemed she too understood there was no room to argue with Subaru.
A temporary truce — if her hostility could be set aside even for now, things would go faster.

"Rem, listen. I'll jump out and draw his attention. While I do, you climb up the far side of the pit and get to safety."

"What...?"

"His arrows pack a scary punch, but with all this greenery, we've got no shortage of cover to choose from. I'll buy some time somehow, so you get away."

Fleeing while bringing Rem along, or having Rem flee first.
Weighing which had a higher chance of success, Subaru figured it was the latter. However skilled an archer their opponent was, here in a forest thick with trees, Subaru would be moving with the possibility of incoming arrows in mind. He wouldn't be easy to pick off.

"Once I've bought enough time, I'll skedaddle too. But I don't want to get separated from you once you've fled, so leave a trail if you can. It might be hard to picture, but back where I'm from there's a symbol called an arrow — if you use that to mark the general direction..."

"—Please stop deciding things on your own."

"Rem?"

Subaru laid out his escape plan to Rem briskly and efficiently. But cutting off his explanation, Rem glared at him, sharp and hard.
Subaru held his breath at the intensity of that gaze, bewildered as to what was wrong. And Rem, seeing his confusion, only grew more irritated.

"You decide everything, absolutely everything, all on your own... and in the end, you tell me to run? Me, who's angry at you precisely because you were about to abandon that child?"

Yes — she rejected Subaru's proposal for the same reason she'd first rejected him.
Her logic made sense. It was, one could say, the answer that a good, decent nature would arrive at. But he hadn't expected that same nature to keep her from abandoning Subaru in a situation like this.

"That's... but I—"

"I don't need excuses. There's no time for them anyway. But I refuse any order to have only me flee. And besides, we can't leave that child behind either."

Averting her gaze from Subaru, who couldn't hide his surprise, Rem turned her attention outside the pit.
What her attention had settled on was another great tree, somewhat apart from the one the first arrow had blown apart.

"I was wondering where she'd gone — you hid her over there? How'd you get her to stay quiet with all this ruckus going on..."

"...She was too much trouble to bring along, so I knocked her out. She shouldn't wake for a while."

"You..."

Even now, at this critical juncture, Rui remained a burden weighing down their escape. The resentment beginning to sprout in Subaru over that fact shattered the instant he heard Rem's utterly Rem-like answer.
That somewhat extreme snap decision — there was really no way to describe it except as pure Rem.

"...Even I think it wasn't a great move, you know."

"No, given the circumstances, it was probably a brilliant one. —So, hypothetically, you won't leave her behind and escape together with me, will you?"

"I may not even know who I am, but I'd bite through my own tongue and die before I'd do something like that."

Subaru privately doubted Rem would actually die from something like that, but even if it was just a hypothetical, he had no desire to let her try.
Honestly, abandoning Rui here and escaping with just Rem would have been the best option for Subaru — but Rem herself clearly wasn't going to allow it.

"I curse the soft-hearted fool I was right before we got swallowed by the shadow..."

When the Green Room had been swallowed by the shadow, it was the fact that he'd instinctively grabbed Rui's body along with Rem's that had led to this whole situation. There was no going back and redoing that moment now, but he could loudly and firmly insist that the choice back then had been the wrong one.

"So what will you do?"

"...I'll do it. I'll bring her. That tree, right?"

"...Yes. I've laid her down in a hollow in the tree. Do you have a winning strategy?"

"My master taught me — when you're up against a strong enemy, run without hesitation."

It wasn't a matter of accurately gauging the gap in strength between yourself and your opponent.
In this world, most opponents were stronger than Subaru anyway, so the optimal means of self-defense was to simply assume that everyone he ran into outclassed him.
Which was exactly why running was the top priority. But if running proved impossible—

"I'll use everything I've got. Rem, I know you won't like it, but lend me a hand."

"...If it's to save that child, then yes."

Rem stared hard at the hand he held out, but she didn't take it.
She simply gave a reluctant nod to Subaru, who had worked out their plan.

△▼△▼△▼△

He gave his left arm a firm swing once, testing the state of his three broken fingers.
There was pain. Tasting the sensation of a throbbing, unrelenting ache clawing at his brain, Subaru resolved not to let it get in the way of running.
And then—

"—Hyah!"

Putting his strength into it, he shoved a smallish fallen branch up out of the pit and onto the ground above.
The next instant, an arrow closing in at tremendous speed struck the fallen branch he'd raised. The impact tore it from his grip and sent it flying violently backward.

"Uoooooh!!"

Paying it no mind, Subaru clambered up out of the pit and planted his feet on the torn-up ground.
However confident this hunter was in his archery, there was a limit to how fast a bow could fire. Unlike a gun, an arrow had to be nocked, the string drawn, and the aim set.
That time lag gave Subaru a slim chance at survival—

"—That's too fast!!"

It happened the instant Subaru, having climbed out of the pit, kicked off for his first step.
In terms of time, it had only been two seconds since the first shot blew apart the decoy branch — but for a seasoned hunter, those two seconds were plenty enough to loose the next.

"—!"

The instant he heard Rem's voice, the ground behind Subaru burst apart.
Freezing up at her call rather than moving into a defensive posture had actually worked in his favor. More precisely, his nerves simply hadn't been sharp enough to react to her call at all — but the result was fine either way.

As he ran on toward the tree that was his target, arrows kept coming, one after another, at intervals of under two seconds.
Pursued like that, Subaru would soon be turned into a pincushion.
But—

"I won't let you—!"

The hunter's next attack was interrupted by a clod of earth hurled with a valiant shout.
It was Rem's throw — bracing her back against the slope of the pit, hefting a dirt-caked boulder — no, an attack so intense it deserved to be called a bombardment rather than a mere throw.

"It would've been best if you knew how to use magic...!"

When they'd taken stock of their capabilities, Rem had shown reluctance about using magic or the power of her Oni horn. More precisely, the truth was she simply couldn't remember how to use them.
Still, there was no time to spend trying to jog those memories loose. And so, what Subaru had proposed instead was the raw physical strength that remained in her body even without technique—

Using the innate physicality born of the Oni race, a primitive, brute-force rampage now bore down on the hunter, who had scorned them as weaklings and never bothered to change position.

"They say even a cornered mouse will bite the cat! Take a good, long taste of it!!"

Leaping over the shockwave of an arrow striking right at his feet, Subaru bellowed, and in his line of sight the huge stone Rem had hurled sailed straight toward the direction the hunter had been shooting from.

"A-Aaaaaaah—!!"

Once she turned hostile, showing no restraint whatsoever was one of Rem's charm points.
Pebbles she'd gathered up just in case instantly transformed into terrifyingly dangerous weapons the moment they were flung from her slender arms.

"While Rem's, um, buying us time—!"

Crushing sniper fire with artillery fire — proper weapons deployment in action — Subaru reached the tree that was his goal.
Circling around behind the great tree, he found, inside a hollow opened up by rot, the sight of Rui asleep, wrapped up as if in her own golden hair.

"..."

Seeing her cherry-blossom-pink cheeks and the faint rise and fall of her chest, he confirmed she was alive.
But as he reached his arms out toward her body, Subaru felt a fierce revulsion well up inside himself. Even if he thought he'd made his peace with it, it wasn't his mind but his very soul that rejected Rui.
However innocent her sleeping face might look, this was one of the Sin Archbishops.
An unforgivable, treasonous criminal, and—

"Don't you dare stop moving!!"

"—!"

That hesitation was shattered by Rem's urgent cry.
The instant he heard it, Subaru forced down his revulsion and hoisted Rui's body up. Still holding her light frame, he burst out of the hollow tree, heading for Rem—

"—Ah?"

And there, right in front of Subaru as he burst out, a sudden black shadow blocked his path.
He'd planned to retrace the same path back to the pit. There had been nothing like this along the way, Subaru thought as he looked up at the shadow that had suddenly appeared. —And then he was struck speechless.

"—!!"

A massive shadow that had slipped through the forest without a sound to stand in Subaru's way. Its true form was a giant serpent, nearly ten meters long from end to end.
A great serpent, its entire body densely covered in green scales, with yellow eyes. Seeing the twisted white horn on the forehead of this sudden intruder, Subaru understood exactly what it was.

"A Witchbeast—!!"

Faced with its imposing presence, Subaru regretted his own foolish oversight.
Subaru himself should have been aware of it — that right now, more than at any point since being summoned to this other world, he was giving off the Witch's Lingering Scent more strongly than ever.
That being the case, just as it had been at the Augria Sand Dunes, just as it had been on so many occasions before, it was inevitable that a Witchbeast would be drawn to Subaru.
A dark forest like this, with so little human traffic, was exactly the sort of habitat it would favor.

"—!!"

Jaws splitting wide, the great serpent fixed its aim on Subaru.
That maw looked like it could swallow not just Subaru but Rui in his arms too, and have room to spare — and seeing it close in, Subaru's sense of time slowed to a crawl.

—Ah, this is bad.

That was what Subaru felt, in a sensation as detached as if it were happening to someone else.
The way the world seemed to waver slowly, sluggishly, was close to what people call seeing your life flash before your eyes. One theory holds that this happens because, facing death, the brain searches through past life experience for some means of survival.
But in Subaru's case, no matter how hard his brain searched, he had no experience of narrowly escaping being swallowed by a giant serpent. The closest thing, if he had to name one, was that pack of rabbits — a memory so bad even his life-flashing-before-his-eyes montage wanted to refuse it.

A fleeting instant was spent on such a useless thought, and Subaru flinched, bracing himself.
Cursing his own body for instinctively moving to shield Rui, he plunged head-first into the great serpent's gaping mouth—

"—!?"

"Uh?"

Above the head of Subaru, who had reflexively shut his eyes, something came pouring down with a wet splatter.
An unpleasant thought flashed through his mind — surely not the type that sprayed digestive fluid before eating? — but that wasn't it. What soaked Subaru from head to toe was a spray of dark, inky blood.
It was the massive gout of blood the great serpent had spewed after its body was pierced clean through by a sharp arrow.
