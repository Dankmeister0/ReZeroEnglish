Arc 7 Chapter 68: "The Sword Wolf's Guidance"

"—Wait a moment, Schwartz. I'm reading through to a good stopping point."

Having said that, Gustav didn't so much as glance at Subaru, whom he'd invited into the room. His gaze stayed fixed on the letter in his hands, on the desk before him.

Led by a guard, Subaru had been brought to the topmost level of Ginunhive. Beyond a door that looked lavish at a glance, what awaited him was a simply, plainly furnished room.

The spacious room held nothing but a work desk, a work bookshelf, and a work cabinet for supplies. The room seemed to have more space than it knew what to do with, so presumably it had once held a good deal more.

This governor's chamber, stripped of everything unnecessary for work, gave an impression of being remarkably tidy—and that plain, no-nonsense reflection of Gustav's character was, if anything, reassuring.

"'A good stopping point,' he says, but there's not even a chair."

In that room, Subaru—ordered by Gustav to wait—was at a loss.

Told to wait, there was nothing for it. With nothing to occupy his hands, Subaru idly looked around the room and spotted a figure who was clearly out of place.

Or rather, found him—the man was waving a hand at Subaru with a slack, grinning face.

"Yo yo, Bassy, good work out there. You here to see Gustav-san?"

The fake Cecilus said this in that easy tone of his, and Subaru swallowed a sigh and walked over.

How to put it—Subaru's relationship with the fake Cecilus had gotten a little more complicated again. In a sense, Subaru's entry into the "Sparka" was the result of the fake Cecilus goading him into it.

Even without their argument, Subaru figured he probably would have entered the Sparka anyway, but—

"There's no denying I had a pretty strong urge to prove Ceci wrong, though. Yeah, this right here."

"Oh my, wanting to prove me wrong—now that's a fairly ambitious goal. Was that why you entered the Sparka? Well, sorry about that, then."

"Sorry for what?"

"Well, you see, I didn't actually go watch the Sparka you just fought in."

The fake Cecilus said this breezily, sticking out his tongue, and Subaru's mouth fell open. But what welled up right after was a burning "this bastard" kind of anger.

Even though he'd been the one to fire Subaru up with their argument, even though he'd watched right in front of him as Subaru charged in to prove his point wrong.

"Y-you, you bastard...!"

"Now now now now, don't get mad! No no, I get why you'd want to be mad, but I have my reasons too, you know!"

"Then let's hear them! If it's some lame excuse..."

"Well, I knew the result without watching, didn't I? Bassy shuts them out and that's that. So there was no reason for me to go—it's not like anyone's life was on the line for me."

"—"

Even pressed right up against him, the fake Cecilus's tone never wavered, and Subaru pulled back.

Honestly, Subaru was aware he'd done enough to earn being repeatedly called a creepy brat by Hiain, but—

"Ceci, from the outside, you're a pretty creepy brat yourself, you know..."

"Perhaps those who accomplish great feats are, in every age, difficult for others to understand. Though in this case, that would apply to both you and me, Bassy."

"Well, that's quite the high opinion of me."

"Yes, this whole affair rather confirmed it for me. So feel free to keep on doing unconventional, off-the-mark, audacious, misguided things from here on out!"

"I could do without the 'off-the-mark' and 'misguided' part!"

Subaru twisted his lips in protest at the fake Cecilus, who was stringing together vaguely plausible-sounding words at random. And right on the heels of their exchange came the heavy sound of a hand slapping a desk.

Looking over, the source of the sound was Gustav, who had finished reading the letter.

Gustav rested his chin on two crossed arms while planting the remaining two on the desk. Having four arms lets you do handy things like that, Subaru thought, impressed.

"If you crossed all four arms, would it be too big to rest your chin on?"

"Hmm, who knows. Gustav-san's got a nice big face to match, so it might actually fit four stacked hands just right. Want to give it a try?"

"Do you two not possess the consideration to behave solemnly? By common sense, silence ought to be valued when waiting on someone reading a letter."

"Well, nobody told us..." (both, in unison)

It was a childish bit of sophistry, but Subaru's and the fake Cecilus's excuses overlapped perfectly.

That fact made Subaru feel apologetic toward Gustav. Agreeing with the fake Cecilus meant Subaru had done something senseless.

"Gustav-san, sorry. My bad..."

"Somehow I feel like you arrived at that through some unconvincing reasoning. Still, since you've admitted your own fault, let's just say this is entirely Bassy's mistake."

"Since you voiced an apology, in my assessment Schwartz is the more solemn of the two of you. I might add that I have no recollection of granting Segmunt permission to enter this room."

"Eh, seriously? Then why's Ceci in here?"

"That's because my policy is, of course, to be wherever I want whenever I want. There's no one on this island who can seal away my freedom, so I'd call it perfectly reasonable treatment."

The fake Cecilus said this, casually declaring he'd answer to no one. Subaru glanced fearfully at Gustav, but the island's administrator had his eyes shut, rubbing the bridge of his nose as he endured some inner struggle.

The fake Cecilus's story didn't seem to be a lie, but of course Gustav wasn't happily overlooking the situation either—that seemed to be roughly where he stood.

But still—

"So nobody can stop Ceci, huh."

"Yes, exactly. What you'd call special treatment—in other words, I'm the leading star of the world's stage."

"I don't really get what that means, but okay."

Odd phrasing aside, it wasn't as though Subaru couldn't grasp what the fake Cecilus meant.

Still, if the fake Cecilus's claim wasn't wrong, that in itself created another major contradiction. And that contradiction wasn't something Subaru could afford to ignore.

"Um, so anyway, you said you had me called here, Gustav-san... but is it okay for Ceci to listen in on this too?"

"—. Segmunt."

"Don't mind me. Nobody can throw me out. Don't worry—if it's something relevant to me, it'll reach my ears, and if it's not, I'll forget it right away. My power to forget is really something. There's even a chance I've already forgotten several important things as we speak."

"That's not something at all..."

At the fake Cecilus's shameless nerve as he puffed out his chest, both Subaru and Gustav threw up their hands.

In the end, with the room's cast unchanged, Subaru moved to stand facing Gustav. The fake Cecilus laced his hands behind his head and leaned his back against the wall, watching over the proceedings.

It made for a decidedly peculiar audience of "actors."

"Setting Ceci over there aside—you called me here because—"

"Naturally, this is a warning regarding the incident of you abusing your punishment."

"...Isn't that backwards?"

Cut off by the low voice, Subaru muttered that with a sour expression.

By the rules of the Sword Slave Isle, a warning came first, then punishment, and beyond that, death by the Curse Clause. Setting aside that final capper, having a warning follow a punishment had the order backwards.

At Subaru's pointing this out, Gustav, without so much as a twitch of his expression, said:

"Schwartz, you made a statement in my presence that risked disrupting the order of the Sword Slave Isle. You ignored the warning, and punishment thereby followed. You're aware of this?"

"...Yeah, I am."

"Then swear you will never speak of it again in my presence. —A scheme to escape this island, defying His Imperial Majesty's judgment."

His expression still didn't move. Neither did the look in his eyes nor their color change. His tone of voice held the stillness of some enormous boulder, and yet an overwhelming intimidation radiated from it.

If it had been a bit more emotional—anger, say, or irritation—Subaru would have found it easier to read. But what dwelt in Gustav was neither anger nor irritation.

"An incredibly strong sense of duty."

"—"

"Gustav-san, do you owe His Imperial Majesty some kind of debt?"

"Is your aim to dodge the question?"

"It's not that."

He had no intention of dodging, nor was he in any position to.

Gustav clearly had no intention of letting him go without wringing a firm commitment out of him. So Subaru's question came from pure curiosity and interest about Gustav himself.

Asked, at least in part, from the position of someone who actually knew the man Gustav called "His Imperial Majesty."

"I have no personal connection to His Imperial Majesty. I am simply someone entrusted with managing this island as governor. Diligently carrying out my duties is, given my position, only natural."

"So being this straitlaced is just your personality?"

"Calling the fulfillment of one's due duties 'straitlaced' strikes me as a somewhat irrational way to phrase it."

Subaru hadn't meant to mock him, but taken as an insult, he hunched his shoulders.

Still, Gustav's answer wasn't what Subaru had wanted. Whether this was Gustav's true feeling, or a facade concealing it, he couldn't tell.

Not without knowing Gustav a good deal better.

"In Gustav-san's case, five or ten times through probably won't be enough to get inside his heart..."

Subaru himself suspected that behind the success of his actions so far lay something like a "suspension bridge effect"—people placed under extreme conditions.

In the life-or-death Sparka, with no room to spare, everyone had been pulled along by Subaru's knowing words, as if by the hand, into sharing their own circumstances.

Coming to know them was the single greatest reason Natsuki Subaru had been able to break through the Sparka.

That method, however, was considerably harder to pull off outside the Sparka.

"—Schwartz, what's that face for?"

"Oh, just wondering what it'd take to melt Gustav-san's heart of ice."

"There is no correlation between hearts and ice. I ask that you refrain from speaking like Segmunt. For the sake of not adding to my worries."

Even Subaru's flippant remark was answered with total seriousness, and behind him came the sense of the fake Cecilus laughing.

Ignoring him together, in unspoken cooperation, Subaru and Gustav looked at each other. And then returned once more to the question still unanswered.

"Let me have your oath once more, Schwartz. So long as you act as a sword slave of this island, you will obey me, its governor. —That you will refrain from any act defying His Imperial Majesty's will."

"—"

Closing his eyes, Subaru repeated Gustav's demand in his mind.

To Subaru, who had boldly declared he'd leave the island, Gustav had issued a single warning, and when Subaru still didn't retract it, sent him into the Sparka as punishment.

If what the sword slaves said was correct, a warning was followed by punishment, and if that failed, the penalty of the Curse Clause.

And there was something Subaru absolutely had to confirm. Because it was a confirmation utterly essential to him and the others getting off this Sword Slave Isle—

"Schwartz, your answer—"

"—Absolutely, no!"

"—"

To the low question pressed on him once more, Subaru squared his shoulders and raised his voice, bold and unashamed.

At that answer, Gustav's brow furrowed and he fell silent. Seeing him go quiet, Subaru immediately realized the true meaning of his words wouldn't come across the way he'd phrased it, and corrected himself.

"'No' means I refuse. Sorry, but I can't do what you're asking, Gustav-san. No matter how stupid it makes me look, I've already decided I'm getting off this island."

△▼△▼△▼△

Breaking down words that might not otherwise land, Subaru declared it again, plainly.

Escape from the Sword Slave Isle of Ginunhive—declared boldly to none other than the very administrator of this island, Governor Gustav Morello.

"—"

Subaru's answer was, of course, not what Gustav wanted to hear either. Without his expression or the color of his eyes changing in the least, the grim menace of his stern face simmered and burned.

Simmering, just like magma waiting for the moment to erupt—

"Schwa—" "Ahahahahahaha!"

In that instant, a shrill peal of laughter forcibly kicked away the magma just as it was about to erupt.

Doubled over, laughing his head off, was the fake Cecilus, who had been shamelessly eavesdropping on their conversation from the back of the room.

Laughing uproariously, he wiped away the tears welling in his eyes with a finger even as he laughed, and said:

"Absolutely no! Absolutely no, he says. I'm not quite sure what 'no' means, but I get a real sense of rebellious spirit from it, and personally, I like it quite a lot! Ah, but really, Bassy, just how fearless are you? I don't dislike it—if anything, I like it."

"Yeah? Well, I think you're a creepy brat, Ceci."

"Ahahahaha!"

Stamping his feet in place, the fake Cecilus's laughter showed no sign of stopping.

Even doused by the fake Cecilus's fit of laughter, the words of refusal Subaru had thrust at Gustav didn't simply vanish.

"Schwartz. I gave you a warning, and when you would not retract your position, I meted out punishment. Not only that, but even now, given a renewed opportunity to change your ways, you still claim you have no intention of doing so?"

"...Yeah. It's not that I hate you, Gustav-san. What I hate is the Empire."

"I possess no right to bend your subjective feelings of personal like or dislike."

Unspoken in that was Gustav's implication that he didn't mind being disliked, but it was true that Subaru didn't dislike Gustav. He didn't know the man well enough yet to decide either way.

Deciding about someone from a first impression alone was a ridiculous kind of judgment.

"That is, unless the girl you like kisses your hand or something."

"—?"

"Never mind, that's a whole other story, about someone totally unrelated."

Regretting the words that had slipped out on impulse, Subaru locked eyes with Gustav.

From Gustav's perspective, Subaru had to be about as difficult a child to handle as they came. Even sent into the Sparka under the pretext of punishment, it hadn't fazed him in the slightest.

Gustav must have realized by now that it hadn't functioned as a punishment for Subaru at all.

Which meant—

"Beyond the warning and the punishment—are you going to penalize me, Gustav-san?"

"—. If you will not retract your stance, then in the worst case, I would have no choice but to act in accordance with the authority granted to me."

"I see... Then go ahead and do it?"

"—"

Tilting his head, Subaru said it to Gustav as though it were nothing at all.

Hearing Subaru's words, Gustav's expression moved, just slightly. Not the furrowing of a brow, nor the rubbing of one—his cheeks simply stiffened.

Not the look of someone deep in thought—it was the reaction of a man who'd had a sore spot prodded.

"The Curse Clause you've placed on every sword slave on this island—use that to punish me. I'm an insolent brat who won't obey a word you say, and you're the governor here, so making an example of me should be perfectly fine. Go ahead and do it. But—"

"—"

"You won't, Gustav-san. —No, I don't think you can."

No rebuttal came from the silent Gustav.

Subaru probed at what that silence meant, continuing to press his own reasoning.

The Curse Clause—the single greatest reason the sword slaves of this island feared, the thing binding their every action.

At the will of the governor Gustav, the sword slaves branded with the curse mark could be stripped of their lives. Subaru believed Gustav couldn't actually wield that deterrent.

"I heard from Old Man Null, and from other sword slaves who've been living here a long while. Before you came, Gustav-san, the Sword Slave Isle was a lawless place—more people died from injuries, from sickness. But since you arrived, things changed a lot. Fewer people die now."

"That is simply my due duty as administrator. To begin with, a sword slave's life ought to be spent for the island's spectacles, and death by any other cause must be avoided as much as possible. The same holds for the day-to-day duels outside the shows—I provide occasions for maintaining tension and skill, but death in such settings outside the actual performances ought likewise to be avoided."

"Right, you don't want to lose sword slaves if you can help it, Gustav-san. I've felt that too, over the few days I've spent here. Surprisingly, the benefits are actually pretty solid."

"'Benefits'—another unfamiliar word, how exciting!"

The fake Cecilus latched onto Subaru's line of pursuit, but Subaru had no intention of playing dictionary for him.

This, for Subaru too, was a fairly crucial showdown.

"You allow deaths in the Sparka because it keeps the quality of the sword slaves up, and, I think, to keep the ones already in here from getting complacent."

"—"

"Like I said before, once you become a sword slave, the rules here are pretty easy. But it's a problem if the sword slaves learn to slack off in their life here. So you run the Sparka. That keeps up the tension, and... it also gives people peace of mind."

"Peace of mind—meaning what?"

"The peace of mind of thinking, 'It's a good thing we became sword slaves.'"

To Gustav's question, Subaru answered clearly, looking straight into his eyes.

Weighing Gustav's aims and actions from various angles, that was the answer that felt right. There was, of course, a chance he was off the mark, but there wasn't much to lose in saying it aloud.

If he got laughed off or flatly denied, well, that would be that.

"—"

But what Gustav chose was neither—only silence.

"...You don't want sword slaves dying if you can help it, Gustav-san. But at the same time, it's a problem if they get defiant or start getting cocky. So you use the Sparka for that. When I think about it that way, it all fits."

"It sounds logically consistent. But it does not constitute rational proof that I would not enforce the Curse Clause. That is merely wishful thinking on your part."

"There's one more piece of evidence. ...Ceci here—defiant and cocky as they come."

"Eh, me?"

At Subaru's words, thumb jerked backward over his shoulder, the fake Cecilus's eyes went wide. Gustav's gaze flicked briefly that way, and Subaru gave a big nod.

Including the sheer nerve of boldly sitting in on this conversation between Subaru and Gustav right now, the fake Cecilus's shameless conduct stood head and shoulders above the other sword slaves.

Naturally, things like warnings and punishments must have been given to the fake Cecilus as well, but in a different sense from Subaru, none of it fazed him at all. Which meant it wouldn't be strange if, before Subaru, the fake Cecilus had already been dealt the penalty beyond punishment.

"And yet Ceci's fine. There's a chance he's your big favorite and you're playing favorites, but you don't seem like the type for that, Gustav-san."

"A keen eye. I hold no fondness for Segmunt. Though I have absolutely no intention of letting personal like or dislike color my official authority."

"Wait wait wait? Are you two possibly talking about how I'm disliked? Ah well, sorry for stealing the spotlight of every conversation wherever I go."

"Your mental fortitude is way too strong..."

Even criticized rather directly, the fake Cecilus was utterly unfazed. Subaru figured you probably needed to be that thick-skinned for a personality like his to grow up so uninhibited.

In any case, the fake Cecilus's existence was fairly strong evidence for Subaru's argument.

In other words—

"You haven't penalized Ceci either. I've done things every bit as much of an eyesore to you as Ceci has, if not more. Given that, what are you going to do with me, after I refused what you asked just now?"

"—"

"Punish me with the Curse Clause? ...What'll it be, Gustav-san?"

At Subaru's question, Gustav's eyes narrowed, ever so slightly.

The Multi-Arm Tribesman rested his chin on two of the four arms he'd set on the desk, the other two placed as if embracing it, and Subaru waited, unmoving, for his answer.

Having said this much, his intent must have gotten through to Gustav.

Subaru wasn't provoking him to try it—not quite. He was trying to confirm something.

—That there was no such thing as a "Curse Clause" holding the lives of the sword slaves in its grip.

"—"

Gustav projected intimidation onto those around him through his stern appearance and heavy bearing, but when it came to running the island, Subaru suspected he'd actually been proceeding with great delicacy.

Whatever instruction of His Imperial Majesty—Abel's—he was trying to uphold, Gustav, contrary to appearances, had achieved control over the island's sword slaves not through violence, but through rules.

Once a sword slave survived the Sparka and earned the title, he wouldn't grow careless and run wild in his new circumstances. Run the Sparka periodically to keep spirits tight, make everyone dread having to enter it again, and no one would ever be left to fall afoul of the Curse Clause beyond punishment.

A double safety net—and the chains of a Curse Clause that didn't exist bound the sword slaves' feet all the same.

That, as Subaru saw it, was the true nature of the mechanism of the Curse Clause surrounding this Sword Slave Isle.

And whether this was correct or not would be revealed by Gustav's actions from here.

Provoked this far—if the very existence of the Curse Clause came under doubt—every rule Gustav had laid down would be overturned entirely.

To prevent that, he absolutely had to punish Subaru with the Curse Clause.

"—"

Determined to see it through, Subaru kept his gaze fixed unwaveringly on Gustav.

Under that gaze, Gustav remained silent, for a while, and a while longer, and then—

"—. I will give you punishment. Schwartz, I will send you into the next Sparka."

—So, as if he'd conceded defeat, Gustav did not take Subaru's life.

△▼△▼△▼△

"That was about as decisive a victory as they come, but how are you feeling, Bassy?"

Just as they left Gustav's office, the fake Cecilus, falling into step beside him, asked this.

At that breezy attitude, Subaru let out a great sigh.

"I mean, what are you, Ceci—my enemy or my ally?"

"Oh, now that's an odd question. I'm the eccentric who went out of his way to fish you and the deer girl out of that lake, you know? Considering you might well have died without me, wouldn't my standing be self-evident?"

"Right. So—enemy or ally?"

"Which do you think would be more fun?"

Letting the useless ramble wash over him, Subaru asked again, and the fake Cecilus threw the question right back.

It was an airy sort of question, and its content made Subaru bristle for an instant, but it had an entirely different flavor from the exchange he'd had glaring Gustav down.

His exchange with Gustav had been a critical fork in the road, one that could have decided a great deal going forward.

But this question of the fake Cecilus's—

"Whatever I say, you'll just decide on a whim in the moment anyway, right, Ceci?"

"Correct, full marks. For what it's worth, I'll say I'm leaning ally for now. My gut's whispering that watching your outlandish antics is more fun for the time being, Bassy. That said, even I can't trust my own moods, so do be careful."

"I really wish you'd keep a proper grip on your own steering wheel..."

An ordinary moody type would be one thing, but in the fake Cecilus's case, he was a very dangerous kind of moody.

It felt like keeping a battle junkie or a berserker at his side, and Subaru found himself thinking that protagonists in various games and manga really did an impressive job picking their companions.

Thinking about it that way, he felt a little sorry for himself, having done nothing but struggle over choosing companions ever since coming to the Empire.

"My Emiliase and Beatromin deficiency is about to hit lethal levels."

"Ho-ho, another unfamiliar term. Is that also some local dialect of yours, Bassy?"

"This one's a word I made up myself, I guess—or rather, it's the dream substance that makes up half my body."

Like a medicine half made of kindness, it wouldn't be an exaggeration to say half of Subaru's body was made of feelings for the people he cared about. It was an exaggeration—but he'd say it outright anyway.

Which was exactly why that back-and-forth with Gustav just now had carried so much weight.

"It bugs me to admit you're right, Ceci, but yeah, I got exactly the reaction I was after."

"Participation in the next punishment? Or perhaps..."

"Of course, that he won't kill me with the Curse Clause. The Sparka thing... well, yeah."

Subaru, who had defied His Imperial Majesty's will and openly declared his intent to escape the island—since Gustav hadn't punished that with the Curse Clause, it was all but impossible he'd ever invoke it—no, he couldn't invoke it.

Though in exchange for that confirmation, he'd been handed the punishment of participating in the next Sparka.

"Since you were planning to enter the Sparka one way or another anyway, Bassy, it's no real blow to you."

"I think 'was planning to one way or another' is putting it a little too strongly. Even I'd rather not enter a terrifying event like the Sparka if I could get away with it."

"Ah, I see, I see, I understand completely. That's the standard line for building up the drama, isn't it?"

"It's not that at all!?"

Answering the fake Cecilus's overly cynical read, Subaru ran his tongue along the back of his molars.

If he was going to enter yet another Sparka, just like today's, he'd probably end up relying on this "medicine" again. It couldn't be helped, but it still weighed on him.

Still—

"Anyway, one more piece of my plan has moved forward. I don't know how much longer you'll stick around, Ceci, but don't become my enemy—well, don't get in my way, at least."

"I'll take that to heart as words spat out after all manner of inner conflict. Now then, now then, just what will you get up to next, Bassy... oop."

"—?"

Talking like that, Subaru and the fake Cecilus had come down to the level with the living quarters, packed with sword slaves. At the odd sound the fake Cecilus made there, Subaru widened his eyes too and turned to look where the other was looking—

"—Schwartz-sama."

The one calling out to Subaru like that was Tanza, standing at the edge of a group occupying the table in the middle of the great hall.

In the great hall Subaru and the fake Cecilus had entered, the dining space held more sword slaves than Subaru had ever seen gathered at once, and at the center of them were the members of Subaru's unit.

It struck him as odd to see Vait, who disliked getting chummy, or the stage-fright-prone Idra, or the reserved Tanza in a position like that. Hiain, loud-voiced and always putting up a bold front, felt a bit less strange in such a spot.

But more than the unexpectedness of the lineup, what startled Subaru was—

"Um, what's going on, everyone? Going all quiet so suddenly."

Scratching his cheek, Subaru asked the great hall's occupants that with an awkward smile.

For some reason, the moment he and the fake Cecilus had walked in, everyone had gone silent, turning eyes on them that seemed to be studying them intently.

"—"

Falling silent, they stared at Subaru and the fake Cecilus—no, at Subaru alone.

This was the first thing that felt off. If they'd been staring at the fake Cecilus and gone quiet, the reason for such a reaction would have made sense.

The fake Cecilus was a creepy brat, feared and disliked by the sword slaves.

But it wasn't the fake Cecilus they were looking at—it was Subaru.

"Schwartz-sama. We've been waiting for you to return."

And to Subaru, puzzling over their reaction, Tanza slowly approached.

At her polite words, Subaru answered, "Ah, uh, yeah," his skin prickling oddly. Tanza's words were as they always were, and yet somehow the air felt thick with tension.

Or rather, it wasn't just between him and Tanza—the tension filled the whole great hall's atmosphere. An uncomfortable air, one that made his throat click involuntarily.

"Waiting for me to come back... ah, you must've been wondering why Gustav-san called me. Sorry for worrying you. That got settled fine, so..."

"No, that matters little."

"It matters little!?"

"—More importantly."

Subaru, startled at being cut off so bluntly, was silenced as Tanza stepped in close. He recoiled with an "oaf!" at the force of it, but the fake Cecilus caught his back and held him in place.

Glancing back, the fake Cecilus, looking as delighted as ever and probably thinking nothing at all, was blocking off Subaru's escape route. Infuriating.

"Schwartz-sama. There's something I'd like to ask. Will you answer it?"

"O-oh, yeah, um, sure. If it's something I can answer..."

"—About your father, Schwartz-sama."

"...My... dad?"

Asked this with such a solemn face by Tanza, Subaru furrowed his brow, unable to make sense of it.

Coming at him with such a weighty, deliberate air, only to ask about Subaru's father. Odder still, it seemed this wasn't a question of Tanza's alone.

"—"

The heat in the sword slaves' gazes, fixed steadily on Subaru, was growing more intense.

Hiain, Vait, and Idra, of course, but the other sword slaves too, all seemed to be holding their breath, anxious over how Subaru would answer Tanza's question.

He hadn't the faintest idea what was going on.

Even so—

"Just so you know, once I get started talking about my dad, it's a long story."

"Kindly keep it brief."

"You're gonna ask and then set conditions on it!? Ugh, come on, seriously!"

Scratching roughly at his head, Subaru groaned, "Ahh," in irritation. But even that attitude did nothing to deflect Tanza and the others' focus.

He had no idea why wanting to know about Subaru's father had become the topic at hand, but—

"My dad's someone I look up to. He's incredibly cool, adored by a ton of people, always at the center of everyone. I mean, he's got charisma, or... it's like he's got this natural-born leadership quality—it's amazing."

"—! Always at the center of a great many people..."

"Even though he's relied on by all kinds of people and his work must be tough, whenever he has time he pays attention to me, teaches me all sorts of things. ...I, well, I really respect him. He's cool."

"—"

As he talked, his father's face rose in his mind, along with the face of the mother who loved that father.

A warmth welled up slowly behind his eyes, and Subaru gripped his own chest tight. Here in this other world, remembering the two of them was both a joy and a deep ache.

But his parents, precious enough that the memory hurt, had become the very pillar supporting the core of who Subaru was now.

"Schwartz-sama, um..."

"Yeah..."

Seeing something in Subaru's expression, Tanza hesitated a little over her next words.

But having come this far, whatever she asked, he wouldn't get angry. Thinking that, Subaru waited for her to continue, and after a moment, Tanza said:

"Your father's... name is?"

"That's..."

At Tanza's question, Subaru went to answer, then hesitated.

Still caught up in sentiment, he almost answered simply "Natsuki Kenichi," but that would make things rather complicated. After all, on the Sword Slave Isle, Subaru went by "Natsuki Schwartz," so the combination of family name and given name would come out backwards.

Then again, giving some other name like "Kenichi Schwartz" would only add to the confusion without any real payoff.

So, after a moment's hesitation, Subaru said:

"Sorry. I can't tell you my dad's name."

"—!"

"Tanza?"

The instant Subaru answered that, Tanza's expression visibly hardened.

Her eyes wavered wide, and her round cheeks went taut with tension. Subaru was startled by Tanza's reaction right in front of him—but not only that—

"Seems it's no laughing matter after all..." "Could such a thing really be? No, but then, the way he led us..." "T-that's what I've been saying! See, am I still crazy for thinking it? Huh, hey!"

Led by the members of Subaru's unit, the sword slaves of the great hall all began stirring at once.

Looking at one another, breaking into a buzz of chatter, they left Subaru thoroughly bewildered. What in the world had everyone this shaken up?

"Um, everyone?"

"—!"

The wave of noise receded in a rush the instant Subaru called out.

Subaru felt a faint pang of loneliness at that—but then—

"Schwartz. Whoever you turn out to be, it doesn't change what we think..."

The first to say that, to Subaru's anxious face, was Vait.

Vait, with his fearsome tattoos covering face and body, but the words he'd spoken were, most likely, meant to reassure a worried Subaru.

"I—I share that opinion. Or rather, it all makes sense now. If anything, it only strengthens my resolve to keep working with you, Schwartz."

"Oh, oh, yeah, that's right! What's the problem, huh?! This guy hasn't changed one bit! He's a sword slave same as us, my blood brother!"

Following on Vait's words, Idra thumped his own chest, and Hiain came rushing over to sling an arm around Subaru's shoulders, turning to the other sword slaves to say so.

Idra aside, Subaru's eyes went wide at Hiain's attitude, but—

"—Right! Quite the kid, Schwartz!" "Knew you weren't just anybody! Couldn't be explained otherwise!" "Oh, guidance of the Sword Wolf we serve! Our thanks for this day's blessing and chance encounter!"

Sparked by the words of the three from his unit, a cheer erupted from the once-silent sword slaves.

Their voices, ringing out through the great hall at a volume loud enough to seem like it might shake the whole Sword Slave Isle, only deepened Subaru's bewilderment, spinning it round and round.

But before he could finish digesting his own confusion, Subaru found himself being shoved along by Hiain.

"There, there, we still haven't finished celebrating today's Sparka! You're the man of the hour again this time, so party it up right, brother!"

"Hiain, you flip your mood way too fast!?"

"C'mon, it's fine! Hey, hey, make room for your brother! Bring the meat and drink!"

Shoved along by Hiain's coarse, booming laughter, Subaru was packed in right at the center of the crowd of sword slaves.

There, too, were Orson and the others, back from the infirmary, who'd fought alongside Subaru in today's Sparka. They raised their cups in modest welcome at his arrival.

"Thank you, truly, for today, Schwartz. —I'm so glad you were there."

"—"

Welcomed like that by the teary-eyed Lizard Humans, Subaru's throat caught around the words.

He had a mountain of things he wanted to say, and questions too, but in this moment, the words he'd just been given were more than enough reward for having faced the Sparka at all.

"No, thank you—for making it through."

And with that, he decided to partake in the bone-in meat being carried over one after another, his first shared feast in three days.

△▼△▼△▼△

"—Won't you join in the celebrating too, miss?"

"...My position is a complicated one."

Watching the noisy crowd raising a ruckus together from a distance, Tanza answered the boy who'd spoken to her with a sigh mixed into her words.

A boy who resembled someone she knew in every respect except height and the length of his limbs. Not knowing how much he understood of the circumstances, she hesitated to lay bare her own feelings to him.

That went both for the startling truth of that black-haired boy's identity, brought to light by Hiain, and for Tanza's own complicated feelings toward him.

"Yorna-sama..."

Resting a hand at the fold of her kimono, Tanza thought of her precious mistress.

And her thoughts churned on, ever more tangled, over the wish Yorna held, the person that wish was not unconnected to, and this boy who himself, it seemed, was not unconnected to that very person.

Still, if there was one thing she could say for certain—

"—I have no means left but to rely on Schwartz-sama."

The question of whether she could truly trust him had been swept away, and only that much had become certain.

As for what came after—how far she could go with him—that, she still didn't know.

"I see, that really is an expression of the utmost complexity—quite a melancholy air about it."

"—"

"Well, well, Bassy really is a sinful man, isn't he! Now then, however—"

Utterly unconcerned with her feelings, the boy beside her let a bright smile play at the corner of his mouth. Only that smile looked more unsettling than any smile of his she'd seen yet.

Feeling a faint shiver run through her, Tanza listened to what he said next.

And it was—

"—If things don't start moving soon, my patience just might run out. Or so I say."
