Side Story: "Ram's Refusal of Pickup Attempts"

1

"...Honestly, Emilia-sama is such a handful."

Muttering this, the girl watching the crowds passing before her placed a hand to her forehead as though exhausted.

She was a girl of lovely appearance, with bright pink hair and beautiful pale-pink eyes. Her fine features leaned more toward cute than beautiful, though her piercing, clear-eyed gaze ruthlessly dispelled that impression.

The girl surveyed the street with cool eyes and sighed at the absence of the figure she sought. The reason so many eyes were drawn to her was likely her attire.

Even in the royal capital, where one might spot a man with the appearance of a reptile, a dancer clad in what amounted to underwear, or a diminutive beastman resembling someone's pet, the girl's outfit—a maid's uniform—stood out as especially strange.

Still, she was used to drawing curious stares. Paying the onlookers no mind, the girl's expression instead turned gloomy over the problem at hand.

"I did ask her to wait, and yet here she is, causing Ram no end of trouble. What an insolent noble lady."

The "servant" shrugged haughtily at the fact that the "master" she'd been accompanying was nowhere to be found.

—The girl's name was Ram. She had no family name; she was simply Ram.

She worked as a servant in the household of Roswaal L. Mathers, a noble of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica holding the rank of margrave, and on this day she had come to the royal capital accompanying a certain personage.

This city was the largest in the Dragon-Friendly Kingdom of Lugunica, its population and vitality ranking among the finest in the world. Though for Ram, who had already visited many times alongside her mistress, the novelty had long since worn off—

"Though I suppose it's all still new and fascinating to Emilia-sama. Before, she never would have had the leisure to notice."

Sorting through what she knew about the person she was searching for, Ram continued her search along the capital's thoroughfares.

They had only just gotten separated, so if the one she sought was careless but not foolish, she couldn't have gone too far from where they'd parted.

That said, whether she'd first turned right or left down the street was purely a matter of luck, and if she'd gone the wrong way, reuniting with her would prove extremely difficult. In that case, there was nothing to be done about it.

"She couldn't even wait quietly long enough for me to send a letter—she ought to reflect on her own lack of patience. It isn't Ram's fault at all."

It was a shamelessly self-assured attitude, but there was no one nearby to call her on it. And so Ram's pronouncements went out into the world entirely unedited.

For a servant, Ram's words and conduct were conspicuously insolent, but rather than scold her for it, her master took the position of endorsing it outright: "That's just pa-a-art of your charm, isn't it? No harm done." And since, to Ram, the opinions of anyone but her master amounted to so much dust and rubbish, her disposition remained exactly as it was.

Of course, even Ram—who embodied shameless self-regard as a matter of course—did not find the present situation to her liking.

"We should at least have decided on a meeting place. That was a mistake on my part. And with this many people around, even Ram's Farsight can't handle the strain of picking her out..."

"Heyyy, you there, the young lady in the flashy, sweet maid outfit!"

"...In that case, I suppose it'll have to be asking around and searching on foot. Honestly, what a bother."

"Hey, hey, hey, don't just naturally ignore me like that. Over here! Hey, look over here!"

The noisy voice caught sight of Ram trying to walk brazenly away while ignoring its call and swung around in front of her. —Upon laying eyes on the voice's owner, Ram's expression shifted.

"Whoa there, don't make a face like you're utterly disgusted. Keep that up and this old man's feelings are gonna get hurt, y'know?"

"Anyone would make that face if accosted by someone with such a suspicious appearance and such an incomprehensible manner of speech."

Ram cut him down coldly, but this time, surely no one could condemn her attitude for it.

That's how "strange" the getup of the person standing before Ram was.

He was a man whose face was hidden behind an ornately-crafted jet-black steel helmet. That alone would have made him merely a man of unknown identity, but below the neck he wasn't wearing armor at all—just the ordinary getup of a common townsman, and cheap, mismatched material at that, with sandals woven from straw, called zori, on his feet.

If this wasn't suspicious, then what on earth would qualify?

"The royal capital's public order really has fallen into disarray. Wait right there—I'm calling the guards."

"If you're gonna treat me like a pervert, at least act like you're in danger!? There's no way I'm waiting around!?"

Ram moved to take the hardline measure of reporting evil the instant she saw it. The suspicious man tried desperately to stop her, and in light of that desperation, Ram reconsidered—perhaps she should urge him to turn himself in rather than report him.

"That's not a peaceful look you're giving me. I'm just looking for someone and want to talk, that's all."

"What a coincidence. I happen to be busy with the very same errand. Well then, good luck to you."

"You're wearing a maid outfit, the very symbol of a spirit of service, and yet there's absolutely no getting through to you!"

As Ram tried to end the conversation no matter what, the man reached out an arm to hold her back. As she tried to slip past beside him, Ram noticed—the man had no left arm; he had only one.

"Oh, you stopped 'cause you feel sorry for me, huh. Yeah, that's right, on account of this I've got it rougher than most folks. I live off other people's warmth and pity. Pretty pitiful, right?"

"If you're short a hand, borrow a cat's paw. If you have legs, search yourself. That's all. We're done here."

"That's way too harsh!"

As the steel-helmeted man continued blocking her path, the warmth gradually began draining from Ram's expression. Noticing this, the man waved his right arm in a fluster,

"Wait, wait, wait, seriously, I just wanna ask you something! You seen a princess-lookin' girl, orange hair, about your age, wearing a dress, with a real impressive chest?"

"I haven't seen her. Setting aside how you've treated me so far, that's simply the honest truth."

"That preamble makes it sound more suspicious, not less! Well, guess it can't be helped... So you're looking for someone too, huh? Who is it?"

"I won't say something that would be pointless to say."

"That's not just cold, that's downright curt!"

"That's not what I mean. It really would just be pointless, that's all."

It wasn't that Ram refused to cooperate with the steel-helmeted man purely out of displeasure. Of course, she had more than enough displeasure to go around, but the essential problem lay elsewhere.

—The one Ram was searching for simply couldn't be found by anyone who didn't already know what to look for.

"Haah... fine, got it, I won't pry. Guess this turned into an unproductive little chat for both of us."

"I knew from the start it would be meaningless. All that happened was you held me up. Now then, just what do you intend to do about that... ah, never mind. You couldn't do anything about it anyway."

"Your mercilessness genuinely knows no bounds! Sorry to bother you, then!"

"Hmph!"

Ram snorted and turned her back on the man as he clutched his helmet and stamped his feet in frustration. It was an attitude one could only call scathing, not even leaving a word of farewell. And to that retreating back—

"—See ya, Ram."

Something in the man's voice carried a note of nostalgia, and Ram found herself stopping in her tracks without meaning to.

But when she turned around, the steel-helmeted figure had already been swallowed up by the crowd and vanished. Still feeling some vague unease, Ram gently touched a hand to her own pink hair,

"—Ram was quite sure she'd been careful not to give her name."

she murmured briefly.

2

Even after her unproductive interlude with the steel-helmeted man, Ram's search continued to flounder.

After all—

"Hey, keep quiet. Make noise and you'll regret it."

"Standing out like that on a public street in broad daylight—guess you don't have much sense of self-awareness, huh."

"We're feelin' pretty irritable today, so we might get a little rough, y'know?"

Spouting lines straight out of some third-rate thug's playbook, the three men stood before her, and Ram lowered her eyes in disappointment at their three dim-witted faces.

Unable to find the one she sought on the main street, she had ventured a little way down a back alley, and this was the result. A trio—one big, one of average build, one small—had sprung up out of nowhere, pinning Ram against a wall and threatening her.

"Come to think of it, the royal capital's public order really isn't so different from the wilds."

"The wilds? What backwater are you from, anyway. Though you sure don't look like some hick."

"—I meant it in the sense that wild beasts prowl about in both."

Ram, thoroughly sick of this witless exchange, watched as the men blinked blankly for a moment before glancing at each other and beginning to laugh. They must have taken it as nothing more than a frail girl's feeble attempt at back-talk.

"Hey, hey, don't move."

Unable to bear their vulgar laughter any longer, Ram rolled her wrist, prompting a sharp voice. Looking down, she saw the average-built man held a knife, its dully gleaming point pressed to her slight chest.

"Don't scream. We don't wanna run into some 'Sword Saint' again like earlier."

"Yeah, our luck was rock bottom earlier."

"Well, if we'd fought for real, we probably would've barely won anyway!"

Intimidation, weak-kneed complaint, and delusions of grandeur followed one after another, and Ram, frowning at the words she'd caught, shook her head slowly in an exaggerated "good grief," making sure the men could plainly see it.

"Hey, don't mo—"

"Ram, believe it or not, has been holding back. She kept telling herself she was in the middle of an important task entrusted to her by Roswaal-sama and had no time to waste on detours. —But this is her limit."

The instant Ram muttered this in a low voice, a slender wand appeared clutched in her palm. Drawn from beneath the hem of her skirt, the magic wand glowed faintly, and the power of Mana howled through the back alley as wind.

"You bi—don't try anything funny—gaboom!"

In an instant, a piece of wood sent flying by the wind struck the knife-wielding man square in the face. He went down instantly, and at the sight, the other two froze—Ram leveled the tip of her wand at the big man's face.

"Wh-whoa!?"

Reflexively swatting at the wand, the man had his outstretched arm seized and was thrown with all that momentum still behind it. He slammed into the ground back-first, and as he lay there gasping for breath, a slender leg delivered one sharp kick—the second man, too, was knocked out cold.

And then Ram turned her pale-pink gaze on the last man standing,

"Do you have something to say to Ram?"

"I—I'm sorry...?"

"Yes, very good. —But I won't forgive you."

The small man, his face frozen in a strained smile, went sailing splendidly high into the sky.

—And so, leaving the trio she'd knocked flat in the alley, Ram looked up at the sky with a weary expression.

"The sun's gone and set completely. ...At this rate, the Great Spirit won't be able to come out anymore, either."

Evening had passed, and the sky was steadily taking on the presence of night. The conditions for doubling the helplessness of the one she sought had now been met, and the search would only grow more difficult from here.

Just as she thought this while gazing up at the moon, Ram noticed something.

"That light is—"

Where her eyes fell, a tremendous aurora of light rose into the sky over the royal capital, and Ram felt the illusion that the whole world had shuddered.

Amid the surrounding commotion at the shock of it, Ram instinctively turned her feet toward the spot where the light had risen. She never doubted the conviction that welled up in her—that the one she sought would be there.

"Just when I think she's nowhere to be found, she goes and gets caught up in some commotion... She really is a handful."

So saying, Ram broke into a run, heading for the lower quarter of the royal capital—the deepest heart of the slums.

There, a scene awaited her in which the one she sought was tending to a young boy—and in the end, she would return to the manor together with both the one she'd searched for and that boy. But that is a story for a little further on.
