Arc 7 Chapter 55B: "Fortress City Rhapsody"

Time rewinds a little, to just before the coming of the winged assailants.

"Uuugh, I've gone and failed again, sir... I really am hopeless, sir..."

A boy walked down the street, slowly shaking a head crowned with pink, wavy hair. Short trousers left his pale legs bare — this was the boy who'd come out of the largest mansion in the city: Schult. Wearing a gloomy expression, Schult sighed, opening and closing his thin, soft hands that hadn't a trace of strength to them.

Though still young, Schult had already decided upon the one master he would serve for the rest of his life. Born in a poor village, Schult had been cast out to lessen the number of mouths to feed under that year's crushing taxes, driven from his home to starve to death on the roadside. Gnawing on mud from hunger and thirst — a meal that was about to be his last in this life — Schult had been saved by a dazzling sun that happened to pass by.

Saved by that way of living, one that seemed as though it could burn the whole world to ash, Schult existed as he did now. That was precisely why Schult truly meant it when he wished to devote everything he had, and everything he would ever have, to the master to whom he owed such a great debt. And yet—

"I'm no use at all, and I hate myself for it, sir..."

Even his deep sigh came out weaker than most people's, owing to his limited lung capacity, and Schult ached — in his head, his chest, and his heart all together — over his own inadequacy. Just recently, he had rushed about trying to fulfill a duty his precious master had given him, and had failed. It was such a simple task — just filling the bathhouse with hot water — and yet he'd let the water overflow, mishandled the magic stone meant to heat it, and turned the whole thing into a chaotic mess. He'd ended up troubling Rem, who had only recently begun serving Priscilla and who had trouble with her leg besides, and this time in particular he found himself utterly disgusted with his own pathetic self.

"Priscilla-sama and Al-sama both tell me I don't need to rush to change, sir, but..."

He was glad that kind Priscilla and big-hearted Al comforted him so. But Schult hated that he had nothing to give back to the two of them. In that regard, he felt that Heinkel, who treated him differently from the other two, understood him a little better. It was Heinkel who'd told him the sword training method he'd learned from Al didn't suit him, and taught him a different approach instead. Thanks to that, Schult felt his arm muscles had gotten just a little bit firmer than before.

"Compared to Al-sama's way, where you swing your sword for one day and rest for ten, Heinkel-sama's way of practicing every single day suits me better, sir. But I don't want to disappoint Al-sama, so I can't tell him that, sir..."

In any case, with such signs of growth, Schult had gotten a little carried away with himself. And heaven had not forgiven Schult that indulgence and conceit. That, he reflected deeply, was what had led to the great failure in the bathhouse. Right about now, in the bathhouse he'd refilled, Rem was probably helping their master — Priscilla — bathe. Rem could do anything, Schult thought with genuine admiration.

"It's just like Yae-sama, sir. ...I wonder if Yae-sama is doing well, sir..."

What suddenly resurfaced in Schult's mind was a woman named Yae Tenzen, a lively, cheerful maid who had once worked under Priscilla. She'd taken care of Priscilla's personal needs, always chatted happily with Al, and was kind to Schult besides — he had liked her very much. But one day, it seemed something had happened to her family, and she'd suddenly quit her work at the mansion and gone back to the countryside. Schult hadn't even had time to say goodbye, and it had left him feeling very lonely. According to Al, who'd seen her off last, she'd asked him to say hello to Schult for her.

Perhaps Rem would end up taking Yae's place as Priscilla's personal attendant. If kind, hardworking Rem became that, Schult himself would be very happy. She got along well with Priscilla, too, so surely Rem would enjoy it as well. Above all—

"Being together with Priscilla-sama means everyone's full of happiness, sir."

It was Schult's own personal experience, more than anything else, that spoke to just how great Priscilla's presence truly was. Rem, too, seemed to be at an age full of various worries, often lost in melancholy thought. But surely that dark, troubled face of hers would be swept away by the dazzling sun that was Priscilla.

"Ah, Shuu camE."

"Mm-hm! Indeed, sir!"

Harboring such thoughts, head bobbing up and down as he walked, Schult came to a stop. The reason was a voice he recognized, and the sight of someone pointing straight at him. Right in front of Schult as he walked down the street stood a young girl, her black hair dyed pink at the tips, pointing at him. Beside the girl was a young man, and noticing that Schult and the girl had become aware of each other, he let out a bright, "Well, well!"

"If it isn't the little butler! I take it the little princess isn't with you today?"

"Priscilla-sama is relaxing at the mansion, Flop-sama, sir. Were you out for a walk with Utakata-sama, sir?"

"Uu, being called -sama is rarE. FunnY."

The young man — Flop — smiled softly, waved his hand, and nodded, "That's right," to Schult's question. The girl beside him, Utakata, put a hand to her cheek, seeming somehow struck by the way Schult had addressed her. Flop and Utakata were both people Schult had only come to know after arriving here in Guaral. From what he'd heard, Flop was a traveling merchant who went from town to town doing business, while Utakata was a warrior of a tribe called the People of Shudrak. Both of them were fine people who had a place of their own to stand on.

"Oh? You look a bit down, little butler. That face makes me think you might have something troubling you."

"Eh!? Th-that's amazing, sir! Can you really tell, Flop-sama, sir!?"

"Ah, of course I can tell! After all, most people have something troubling them! Why, even my sister, who looks like she hasn't a worry in the world, has them!"

"MidI didn't seem to have any worrieS."

"But she does! Surprising, isn't it!"

Flop put his hands on his hips and laughed heartily, mouth wide open. Watching Flop's bright demeanor, Schult somehow felt his own spirits lifting too. Flop, like Priscilla, was a person who brightened everyone around him, like the sun. Surely all sorts of colorful flowers bloomed around him.

"You're like a prince of a flower garden, sir."

"Hahaha, a prince is far too grand a title for me. I'm nothing more than a humble traveling merchant! Now then, little butler, what sort of trouble are you carrying around? Why don't you tell me about it?"

"Is that all right, sir? Weren't you in the middle of your walk...?"

Schult felt humbled that Flop, bending at the waist to ask him this, would take the trouble. He glanced over at Utakata, who was supposed to have been out walking with Flop. But Utakata narrowed her round eyes and shook her head, looking not the least bit put out.

"Uu was boreD. Playing with Fuu got boring toO, so Uu'll hear Shuu's worrY."

"I see. Ah, but please don't think of it as just killing time. Any worry is a serious matter to the person worrying over it. So I'll listen to you properly."

Saying so, Flop thumped his none-too-sturdy chest, then led Schult and Utakata to the edge of the road, toward the curb of a flowerbed there. The three of them sat down on the curb and began to talk, the breeze washing over them.

Schult's trouble, as already mentioned, was the problem of his own inadequacy. For Priscilla's sake, he wanted to become someone more and more capable.

"It's just not going well, sir. How did you and Utakata-sama become the fine people you are now, sir?"

"Ho-ho, I see. How I came to be who I am now, hm... how philosophical!"

"Philosophical... is that so, sir..."

At Flop's smiling answer, Schult braced himself at the scent of some academic subject he'd never studied. Would he be unable to get an answer to his troubles without understanding this "philosophical"? If so, he was resolved to study it too.

"The reason Uu became Uu iS, because Maa gave birth to Uu."

"Maa, sir? That would be...?"

"Uu's mother! She was close with TaA. BuT, she stabbed herself and dieD."

"I-is that so, sir...!"

Kicking her dangling feet back and forth, Utakata spoke of the manner of her own mother's death. She seemed unbothered by it, but Schult, hearing something so unexpected, was startled. Flop, seated on Utakata's other side, gently stroked her head.

"I see, so that's what happened to your mother. That must have been hard, Miss Utakata."

"MorE than Uu, it was harder for TaA. Uu was raised by Mii and everyone, sO Uu's totally finE. Grew up like thiS."

"That's true, Miss Utakata has certainly grown up wonderfully. I'm sure your mother is pleased about that too."

"...? Maa's dead, sO she won't be happY. Fuu's saying weird thingS."

Utakata made a puzzled face even as Flop stroked her head. Still, being petted by Flop apparently didn't feel unpleasant to her, and she made no move to stop his hand. At Utakata's reaction, Flop quietly narrowed his blue eyes and said,

"Well now, Miss Utakata's answer was a very good one. Honestly, if I'm to talk about how I became who I am now, I think I'd end up giving a similar answer."

"The same answer... then were you also raised by the Shudrak people, the same way as Utakata-sama, sir?"

"If that were the case, I'd be delighted to have so much family! But aside from my sister Medium, my family is made up entirely of people who have already passed away. A very sad and lonely thing, indeed."

"...I see, sir."

Flop had spoken the first half in his usual tone, but let his voice drop just slightly for the second half. Lowering his eyes at his answer, Schult once again felt keenly aware of his own uselessness. He had asked both Utakata and Flop something terribly insensitive. After all, if Schult himself were asked about the family that had abandoned him, he couldn't give an answer that would make the asker happy either. He should have thought things through more carefully before asking.

"There's no need to blame yourself so, little butler. You can learn as you go from here. Which, actually, ties right into the rest of my earlier answer."

"Earlier... you mean the answer that was the same as Utakata-sama's, sir?"

"That's right. What I mean by 'the same as Miss Utakata' is that I, too, became who I am now because of the influence of the people around me."

Spreading both arms wide, Flop smiled as if showing off his current self. That cheerful smile and reassuring tone of voice — both were essential things that made Flop who he was. Had those, too, been cultivated through others' influence?

"Long ago, my sister and I lived in a truly awful environment. It was a facility that gathered up orphans to raise, but the meals were scant, you got no money for your work, and the adults there would hit the children the moment anything went wrong. A dreadful place."

"Th-that's a dreadful place, sir...! What on earth is a child supposed to do, living somewhere like that, sir!?"

"Uu would burn it dowN."

"Burn it down, sir!"

"Hahaha, that might well have been one option. But that's not how it went."

At Flop's harsh past, Schult found himself agreeing with Utakata's proposal to burn the place down. But Flop, laughing at the two of their answers, had already moved past that history long ago. Just how had he moved past it, exactly—?

"Well, it was thanks to outsiders. There was someone who saved us, someone we owed a great debt to. That person is now part of my and my sister's family who have already passed away, though."

"...You were saved by someone, sir. That's really wonderful, sir."

"You understand, little butler?"

"I understand, sir. ...Because I, too, was saved by Priscilla-sama, sir."

He laid a hand gently over his own chest, feeling with his fingertips the fact that his body was no longer just skin stretched over bone. That was proof of everything Priscilla had given him up until today. The more sturdy, strong, healthy, and splendid Schult became, the more he could prove that what Priscilla did that day hadn't been a mistake. And Priscilla, too, would never have to regret it.

At the gleam in Schult's red eyes, Flop gave a small smile, and said,

"As it happens, I've felt something similar myself. That's why I'm in the middle of working hard to accomplish a great goal of my own. The road is quite steep, but I'd like to keep at it without giving up."

"Please don't give up, sir! Ah! But, I'd like you to tell me a little more specifically, sir. How exactly are you working hard, Flop-sama, sir?"

"Hm, let's see. I've thought about it in various ways, but... here, let me tell you about my sister instead of myself. About my lively sister, who's taller than me and quite skilled with her arms."

"Ohh, sir!"

Flop raised his hand high overhead — perhaps because the sister in question was just that tall. This wasn't the first time Schult had heard about Flop's sister from him, but he'd never actually met her in person. By the time he'd come to Guaral with Heinkel to rejoin Priscilla, Flop's sister had already set off for another place. In any case—

"MidI is big and strong. Mii was impressed, tOO."

"Above all, her loud voice and fearless nature are her selling points. And of course, as her older brother, I'm quite proud of how skilled she is with a weapon too! She worked hard to become strong, you know."

"To become strong... is that so, sir! I admire her, sir!"

Surely she had worked hard toward becoming strong, staying positive and never losing her drive to improve. Never giving up and continuing to try — that might be the key to improving, Schult tried to conclude. But "No, no," Flop said, shrugging his shoulders.

"You'd think so, wouldn't you? But as it happens, no! Medium became strong for a somewhat more negative reason."

"N-negative, sir? What do you mean by that?"

"Yes, it's simple, really. Medium, you see — whenever I got hit by the adults, she would always gently stroke my wounds."

Flop said this with the corners of his eyes softened, reminiscing about the past. Schult was bewildered by the gap between Flop's calm way of speaking and the content of what he was actually saying. Stroking a wound from being hit — he couldn't connect that to becoming a trigger for growing strong.

"Being hit is such a pitiful thing, sir. But when your sister was gently stroking your wounds like that, what were you doing, Flop-sama, sir?"

"Me? I was smiling. Medium's feelings made me happy, and if I hadn't smiled, Medium — who I was protecting — would have worried herself sick over it."

"..."

"So the first time I failed to shield her and she got hit by an adult herself, Medium was shocked to her very core. Because when someone strokes a wound from being hit, it hurts. It doesn't heal, and the pain doesn't go away. A very regrettable thing."

Shaking his head slowly, Flop answered with a lonely air. Hearing Flop's answer, even slow-witted Schult finally understood his true meaning — and why his sister had been able to aspire to become strong without ever giving up.

"If she hated the paiN, there was nothing to do but make sure she never got hit again. Is that what MidI thought?"

"Well, that's about the size of it! For all she's my own sister, it's a wonderfully simple answer. Thanks to that, Medium became strong, and our travels as brother and sister became safer for it!"

Flop's laughter, so exaggerated, looked a little different to Schult this time. It wasn't just cheerful — it looked proud. Surely Flop was proud of his sister from the very bottom of his heart. That was something Schult envied greatly.

"I want to become strong too, sir, just like Flop-sama's sister...!"

"Hearing that, I'm sure Medium would be delighted. Or, well, she might get embarrassed. She doesn't have much experience being someone's role model, and that sounds like fun too."

Flop gave a smiling seal of approval to Schult, who clenched his small fists tight. Listening to the two of them talk, Utakata looked at Schult and said, "Shuu?"

"If you want to get strong, wanna practice with UU? Bow practicE."

"The bow, sir? But I'm also practicing with the sword... no, I'll do it, sir! I'll do both, sir! That way I can become twice as strong, sir!"

"If you manage to see both through, that's exactly right! Clever!"

Flop threw his hands up into the sky in agreement with Schult, who burned with resolve. And at the promise of training together, Utakata puffed up her nostrils and stuck out her chest with pride. Showing Schult the bow slung across her back, she said,

"Uu, tOO, is trying hard like ShuU. Gonna become a master like TaA."

"Um, I'd like to meet Taa-sama too, sir. Why do you want to become a master of the bow, Utakata-sama, sir?"

"Maa told UU. So Uu could kill travelers with bow and arroW."

"I see, sir. ...Sir!?"

The answer was more extreme than he'd expected, and Schult's understanding lagged a moment behind. It had sounded like she meant killing travelers, but what exactly did that mean? Was it all right to ask for more details? Schult, having only just reflected on his own thoughtlessness, agonized over it.

And so, while Schult agonized, the chance to press for an answer was stolen away.

"—Little butler! Miss Utakata!"

"—!?" "Fuu?"

All at once, Flop's expression changed, and a sharp voice called out to Schult and Utakata. Schult's shoulders jumped at the intensity of it, and Flop's arm gripped that shoulder tightly. Then, pulling Schult and Utakata — still seated on the curb — close, Flop shouted:

"The sky's looking dangerous! Let's hurry and run for it!"

When that swarm was first spotted in the distant sky, Heinkel was inside the city hall.

Present there were the effective commanders of the forces stationed in the fortress city. Second General Zikr Osman, originally dispatched to the city, who had joined the banner of Priscilla — or, more precisely, a man named Abel — during the capture of the fortress city, along with his subordinates. And, fewer in number than the imperial soldiers but with individual strength nothing to scoff at, the jungle-dwelling hunting tribe known as the People of Shudrak, along with their acting chieftain, Mizelda.

By the time Heinkel made his way to city hall, these two had already put their heads together and were in the middle of discussing what came next. As for Priscilla's talk of a prophetic omen — Heinkel didn't think for a moment she had the power to foresee the future, but he knew better than to underestimate the perceptiveness possessed by someone so far outside the norm.

Priscilla wasn't unique in this — those who bore special power or fate stood at a different height of vision than ordinary people. A difference in the height of one's vantage point meant, in essence, a difference in the scenery one could see. Ordinary people — no, mediocre people — could never truly understand those who could see farther, who could perceive things invisible to the common eye. That was something Heinkel had learned, painfully, over his forty-odd years of life.

Which was why he never flatly denied something just because he couldn't understand it himself. That was the reason he hadn't dismissed Priscilla's words out of hand, and had come to city hall instead. And then—

"..."

Beyond the window, the first to notice the anomaly was Mizelda, who sniffed the air with an expression like she'd sensed something instinctively. The former Shudrak chieftain had apparently lost one leg in the battle to take the fortress city. She'd fitted her right leg, missing below the knee, with a rod like the tip of a cane, using it in place of the missing limb. Because the cane made a sound with every step she took, the same sound rang out as she turned around now.

The instant her striking, strong-eyed beauty of a profile tightened, Heinkel and Zikr, both present, instantly sensed something wrong and looked in the same direction. And upon seeing the swarm of black dots surging in from the distant sky, Zikr murmured:

"—A swarm of Flying Dragons."

"—! Zikr! Warn the troopS! I'm mobilizing the ShudraK!"

The instant she grasped the danger of the situation, Mizelda shoved Zikr's round shoulder aside, kicked off the floor with tremendous force, and launched herself out from the rooftop of city hall. That said, she'd only recently lost one leg. Rather than leaping straight out the window, she took the stairs at a mad dash, hurrying outside. Caught up in her momentum, Zikr sharpened his usually vacant-looking face and

"Ring the bell! Enemy attack! They're coming from the sky!"

So he shouted, kicking his aide in the back, spreading word of the crisis throughout the entire fortress city. Having watched the whole string of reactions play out, Heinkel too put his hand to the sword at his hip. Then, no less loud or sharp than the ringing alarm bell, he clicked his tongue.

"Heinkel-dono! What of Priscilla-dono...?"

"Miss Priscilla's at the mansion. She'd read that something was going to happen. She'll get moving soon enough."

In response to Zikr, who'd turned to look at him, Heinkel turned his eyes to the enemy shapes in the sky. In the span of mere tens of seconds, they had clearly grown darker and larger, closing in on the city at tremendous speed. There was no longer a moment to spare.

"If this is an attack, do you have any idea who's behind it?"

"...That number of Flying Dragons is far from ordinary. A force capable of mobilizing Flying Dragon Riders on that scale — even in the capital... Given that, there's only one possibility."

"Then tell me what that possibility is!"

At Zikr's roundabout way of speaking, Heinkel's tone sharpened, impatient at the time being wasted. Under Heinkel's pressing, Zikr paused a beat before saying,

"The 'Dragoon General,' Madelyn Eschart... She commands Flying Dragons. In other words—"

"Goddamn it, one of the 'Nine Divine Generals'...!"

At the mention of a name he recognized, Heinkel raked his fingers through his own hair. He wasn't especially well-versed in the internal affairs of the Empire, but Heinkel was, at least nominally, the vice-captain of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's Royal Guard. By virtue of that position, he had far more knowledge of foreign affairs than the average person. He'd at least heard the names and epithets of the Empire's greatest fighting force, the Nine Divine Generals.

Every one of them was said to be a warrior of extraordinary ability, each a match for a thousand — in the Kingdom, the only ones capable of properly standing against them were the very best, starting with Marcos, the Royal Guard's captain. And then, of course, there was the Kingdom's strongest, the Sword Saint — but—

"Isn't this the story about there being some absurd monster who can go toe-to-toe with Reinhard..."

He'd heard that the strongest swordsman of the Vollachian Empire was a fighter who yielded nothing to Reinhard. Naturally, there was surely some variance in strength even among the Nine Divine Generals, but the idea of a being who shared a rank with such a monster coming to attack was, for Heinkel, tantamount to a nightmare.

To begin with, Heinkel had never had the slightest intention of coming to the Empire. Priscilla had simply gone and decided on her own to head for Vollachia, and he'd been dragged along for the ride with no say in the matter. He had no interest whatsoever in whether this fortress city survived or fell. But—

"I can't afford to put Miss Priscilla in a bad mood... If she becomes queen and I've already been driven off, none of this means a damn thing."

Priscilla was capricious, and in the end, no one ever really knew what she was thinking. For whatever whim had struck her, she was keeping Heinkel close at hand for now, but if he failed to prove his usefulness, he could well end up tossed aside empty-handed. That alone was something that absolutely could not be allowed to happen. Priscilla was, after all, the only one left holding the thread Heinkel had to cling to.

"There's no time to keep jawing about this. I'm driving off that swarm of Flying Dragons. Zikr, you stay here and direct the overall response!"

"That was my intention, but what will you do, Heinkel-dono?"

"—I'll do as I please."

Unlike Zikr or Mizelda, Heinkel had no troops or comrades to lead. Even if he had, his title of vice-captain of the Royal Guard was purely ornamental. He'd studied the basics of troop command ages ago and never since, and to begin with, no one would listen to Heinkel's orders anyway. So there was, from the start, only one thing Heinkel could do.

"..."

The instant he made that decision, Heinkel didn't wait for Zikr's reply — he dashed for the balcony of city hall and leapt down from there into the city below. Catching the tips of his toes on the roofs of tall buildings, he kicked off each rooftop and ran, leaping from one building to the next. He kept up this string of jumps through the wind, his destination the top of the wall encircling the city. He leapt onto the section of wall that rose toward the western sky, the direction from which the swarm of Flying Dragons was coming, and steadied his breath. Already the alarm bell warning of the attack rang throughout the city, and here and there amid the townsfolk's panic mingled the furious shouts of the guards trying to calm them and urge them to evacuate.

"...Ah, damn it."

The very smell of the air had clearly changed, and the taste of saliva on his tongue turned bitter. As the scent of the battlefield — blood and steel — drew nearer, Heinkel found himself hearing a high, ringing phantom sound deep in his ears.

Besides Heinkel, imperial soldiers too came rushing to the western wall. If Zikr's guess was right, their attacker was one of the Nine Divine Generals — an opponent holding the rank of an imperial general. With what resolve would these men stand against such a foe? Gathered as they were under the same banner, did they feel no strangeness in that? Or was it enough for them simply to fight? Would dying in battle be satisfaction enough?

"Damn it, damn it, damn it, every last one of you goddamn bastards...!"

A thick, pitch-black heat welled up from deep in his chest, seeping through his entire body. Savoring that dark-colored heat as it began at his heart, coursed through his organs and lower belly, and traveled out to the tips of his limbs and fingers, Heinkel clenched his teeth until they creaked. His hand slowly reached for the sword at his hip — the one bearing the name "Astrea" — and Heinkel gripped its hilt hard. And then—

"—Drop dead, every last one of you, you sons of bitches!!"

Roaring as though venting an unbearable fury, Heinkel's sword flashed once, and the thick neck of a Flying Dragon gliding in to sink its teeth into him burst apart in a spray of blood.

The clanging alarm bell and the screams of fleeing people overlapped in an exquisitely ghastly chorus, and the fortress city of Guaral was transformed into a battlefield ruled by shrieking chaos.

The army of Flying Dragons surging in en masse from the western sky, numbering no fewer than several hundred, shattered the hearts of the residents who had taken comfort in being surrounded on all sides by protective walls. If there was a weakness in those absolute walls — walls that could withstand even the destructive power of a mighty Magic Stone Cannon — it could only be a threat capable of surmounting even so great a barrier.

A being that could straddle the walls outright, or a ruler of the skies that could simply fly over them. It was no exaggeration to say that, on this day, the fortress city had been struck by its greatest enemy yet.

"—This is very bad indeed."

Avoiding the main street, where screams echoed, Flop peered out from an alley and furrowed his brow. In the few minutes since spotting the Flying Dragons in the distant sky, the situation had spiraled downhill, and the fortress city — forgetting its brief spell of peace — had once again been turned into the same battlefield it had been ten days before. Except this time, the change was even worse than it had been ten days ago. After all—

"Mister Master over there tried to keep the damage to a minimum as much as he could... but it seems this time's opponent has no such consideration in mind."

Subaru, who had aimed for the city's "bloodless surrender" through the bold measure of cross-dressing. That goal had ended in failure thanks to an unexpected intruder, but even so, had any plan other than Subaru's been adopted, the amount of blood spilled would surely have been incomparably greater. Flop believed that the fact the sentiments of the fortress city's residents and the stationed imperial soldiers hadn't soured to an extreme degree was the fruit of Subaru's careful consideration. But this time's enemy, it seemed, had no such gentlemanly regard whatsoever.

"Hii—"

A tremendous roar and ground-shaking impact rolled through, and beside Flop, Schult let out a shriek. It was no wonder. The enemy's Flying-Dragon-backed assault was that fierce, and that effective.

"And here they're just having the Flying Dragons carry rocks and drop them from high in the sky."

What they were doing was simple and clear — nothing more than that one primitive attack. But even being struck by a rock the size of a human head would guarantee grievous injury, and the rocks being dropped just now were nothing but massive boulders, one or two armspans across. Their force was enough to demolish stone buildings and gouge huge craters into the street's surface. And of course, should a person be struck, the result would be a tragedy too gruesome to look upon twice.

Flop and the others had hurriedly ducked into the alley, but there was no guarantee this counted as a safe zone. If a safe zone existed at all, it would mean either fleeing underground or heading toward some genuinely powerful individual.

"Mii and the others arE in the big building in the middlE."

"P-priscilla-sama is at the mansion, sir...!"

"Ah, quite right. You're both very clever, and I agree completely. The problem is that where we are right now is just about smack in the middle between the two!"

There was Mizelda and Zikr, dependable holders of the city's defensive strength. And, purely as an individual force, there was Priscilla's power, which hadn't even yielded to a member of the Nine Divine Generals. Which one to hurry toward was a genuinely agonizing question, even for Flop. And even as he agonized over it—

"H-help... waaaaahh!!"

Right in front of the alley where Flop and the others hid, a figure fleeing down the street screamed and vanished. —No, not vanished. A shadow flying at tremendous speed had snatched the man's body up and carried him off into the sky all at once.

It seemed the invading Flying Dragons had a division of labor of their own — split between a unit dropping massive boulders like before, and a unit carrying out direct attacks. There was even a chance that other Flying Dragons bore still other roles.

"Th-that person just now...!"

"I wanted to help him, but it was impossible! And we can't go carelessly out there ourselves either. If we fall prey to a Flying Dragon's claws and fangs, all three of us will be carried off into the sky together."

"Uu has her bow! With thiS, Uu'll bring down flying dragons, tOO!"

Within his arms, Utakata, brimming with fighting spirit, showed off her bow and arrow. If it had been Holly or Kuna, or an adult Shudrak here instead, Flop might have given the idea some consideration. But Flop, who had accompanied Utakata during her bow practice before, knew that her skill wasn't yet a match for this battlefield.

"—Give the children the best part, isn't that right. I understand."

Squeezing his eyes shut, Flop thought of the benefactor who had saved him and his sister from rock bottom. Gruff and rough in manner, that benefactor had nonetheless never once bent from that one principle, no matter how many times he had to repeat it. So Flop wanted to be an "adult" who wouldn't shame that memory. He couldn't keep holding his breath here forever. Whichever direction they sent the children — city hall or the mansion — some kind of countermeasure was necessary.

In order to get Schult and Utakata safely away—

"—Both of you, listen to me. From here, I'm going to—"

Holding his breath slightly, Flop, resolve settling over him, tried to explain his plan to the two of them. It was the very moment the two children looked up at Flop at those words.

"Grrraaaaahhh——!!" "—!!"

Along with a roar fierce enough to seem like it was coughing up blood, something enormous came crashing down from the sky into the street. What was slammed into the ground amid a tangle of roars and screams was a Flying Dragon, black blood pouring from its entire body. Spreading its wings to a span of some three or four meters, the great Flying Dragon flailed its wing — broken by the impact of the fall — desperately trying to flee the scene. And then—

"Don't run! Don't you dare run, you bastard!!"

A figure that had fallen to the ground together with the Flying Dragon, tumbling forcefully across the ground, leapt onto its back. Then he drove his sword into the writhing dragon, skewering its heart from behind. The Flying Dragon's death cry stretched on and on, and the dragon collapsed where it lay, weeping tears of blood. And once he'd confirmed the Flying Dragon had breathed its last—

"Godda...mn it..."

Shoulders heaving with each breath, a red-haired man climbed down off the dead Flying Dragon's back. At the sight of him, Schult, in Flop's arms, cried out, "Ah!"

"Heinkel-sama! Are you all right, sir!?"

"Ahh?"

Called by Schult's high voice, the red-haired man turned around with an ill-tempered air. At once, the sight of the man's entire body dyed a deep red made Schult's throat catch and go hoarse. Then, eyes wide, Schult wriggled free of Flop's arms and ran to the man's side. Watching him come, the man wiped his blood-soaked face with his sleeve and said,

"What, it's you, runt."

"H-heinkel-sama, you're all red, sir! A-are you badly hurt somewhere, sir!?"

"Hurt? Ah, this blood's not mine, it's spatter. Even if I am, it's nothing worse than a scratch."

"R-really? Really, sir...?"

Eyes darting about in bewilderment, Schult patted his hands all over the blood-soaked man's body to check. Not caring in the least that his own hands and clothes got dirty in the process — the man, looking down at him, let out a sigh. At the exchange between the two of them, Flop finally let out the breath he'd been holding along with his earlier resolve, and said,

"No need to worry, little butler. It looks like he's not just putting on a brave face — he really doesn't seem to be hurt."

"You're... that fellow I can't quite figure out. Have you been with these runts?"

"'That fellow I can't quite figure out' stings a little, but that's about the size of it! Although all I've really done is drag the little butler and Miss Utakata around while we ran for our lives. I'm relieved you showed up."

As Flop turned a smile his way, the blood-soaked man — Heinkel — averted his eyes. It wasn't embarrassment — it was clearly a reaction of distaste. He even clicked his tongue. But it was a fact that he'd taken down a Flying Dragon and played a part in setting Schult's mind at ease.

Heinkel, like Schult, appeared to be one of the retainers serving Priscilla. Flop had assumed he could fight simply because he carried a sword around, but he seemed to be far more formidable than expected. The sheer quantity of blood dyeing his body red could hardly be explained by the one Flying Dragon he'd just finished off — it was evidence he'd been drenched in the blood of several.

"I was just about to head to Priscilla-sama's side, sir! What will you do, Heinkel-sama, sir?"

"To Miss Priscilla's? ...That's probably the safest place for you, yeah. But—"

"There are precious few places you could call safe in this city right now. If the enemy is Flying Dragons alone, I'd think a building with a basement, or somewhere spices are kept, would be the wise move."

"SpicE...?"

Cutting into the conversation between Schult and Heinkel, Flop drew a puzzled tilt of the head from Utakata at the odd point in his talk. Looking over, he saw Heinkel too was giving him the same dubious look. If one wasn't especially well-versed in the ecology of Flying Dragons, it likely wouldn't click right away.

"Flying Dragons have an extremely keen sense of smell, you see. They can catch the scent of blood from quite a distance. On the other hand, they're creatures that don't do well with strong, pungent smells. So if you sprinkled peppa all over yourself, you might just manage to avoid getting caught."

"...You know a lot."

"Well, I am a traveling merchant who's been all over, believe it or not! And as it happens, I know someone who just so happened to be quite knowledgeable about Flying Dragons. There's even a chance she was the foremost expert in the whole Empire!"

That said, most of what he'd heard from that person had been about the good side of Flying Dragons, so he'd never imagined he'd end up facing them as a threat like this. Still, so long as the knowledge he'd gained wasn't lying to him, coating oneself in spices as a strategy for escaping Flying Dragons wasn't entirely out of the question. Joining up with a capable fighter like Heinkel was a stroke of good fortune for Flop and the two children, but he couldn't necessarily call it good fortune for the battle over the fortress city as a whole.

"I can't say for certain it's a good idea to use Mister Red for our own bodyguard."

Heinkel, capable of holding his own against a Flying Dragon single-handedly, was precious fighting strength for a city under assault by countless numbers of them. However the battle unfolded from here, he would likely become one of the factors deciding the tide of victory or defeat. It wouldn't do to keep a man like that tied down alongside three non-combatants. Therefore—

"We're going to aim for somewhere underground, keeping clear of the Flying Dragons' eyes and noses along the way. Would you mind coming with us as far as a nearby restaurant or storehouse, Mister Red? Once we've thrown off their eyes there — or, well, their noses, I suppose — we should be able to manage on our own after that."

"Flop-sama...!"

The option of splitting up apparently hadn't occurred to him at all, and Schult's face went slack with shock at Flop's proposal. But in place of Schult's surprise, Utakata nodded and said,

"Uu, tOO, thinks the same as FuU. Uu and the others will protect ShuU. No need to worrY."

"You too, Utakata-sama...?"

As Utakata nodded again and again, Schult lowered his round eyes. Then, after a few seconds, he lifted his face with an expression of resolve and looked at Heinkel.

"I understand, sir. I'll do my best with Flop-sama and the others, sir. So please, Heinkel-sama, be careful not to get hurt..."

"—Quit getting all worked up on your own like that. Why the hell should I have to go along with whatever you all decide?"

"Eh!?"

But Schult's resolve went for nothing, as Heinkel scowled and spat out those words. Wiping the blood off his sword with his own cloak, he indicated their surroundings with his chin and said,

"Let me tell you, I don't owe this city a damn thing. More to the point, failing to protect this runt here is a lot more likely to put Miss Priscilla in a bad mood. And that, I can't have."

"W-wait, wait, wait, Mister Red! Hold on a moment! Are you saying that keeping the princess in a good mood matters more to you than protecting the city? That's..."

"—That's right."

"..."

Answered with a clear, decisive statement, Flop couldn't help but blanch. Staring right into Flop's face, Heinkel let his blue eyes glint fiercely within his blood-red-stained face, and said,

"There's something more important to me than this city, more important than anyone's life. And no, it's not this runt. There's something I need from Miss Priscilla — the woman who dotes on this runt — something I have to get her to hand over to me. And to get it, I don't give a damn how many people die, or who."

"Mister Red..."

"Besides, neither I nor any of you are anything so grand. Know your place. What you can do is limited. Don't go reaching for anything outside that. All you'll get is a fool's reward."

"..."

"Nobody gets to become a sword."

Spitting the words out, Heinkel's hand reached for Schult's shoulder. Schult's thin, small shoulder was seized by that hand, and the young boy turned moist eyes up toward Heinkel. What filled those eyes—

"You look like you're in pain, Heinkel-sama, sir."

—No, what filled them wasn't fear at all. It was something that should be called pity. At the young boy's earnest gaze, Heinkel's cheek stiffened faintly. But it wasn't enough to move his already-settled resolve.

"I'm taking this runt to Miss Priscilla. If you two want to tag along, suit yourselves. But don't you dare mistake it for me protecting you."

So saying, Heinkel, still gripping Schult, turned his back on Flop and the others. He stepped out into the street on those same feet, meaning to head for the mansion where Priscilla waited. Flop, too, hesitated over whether to follow Heinkel's advice and go along—

"—So it's you, killing the dragons."

—In that instant, a shadow fell alongside that single voice, touching down on the ground with a violent roar.

"..."

A cloud of dust billowed up thick, and the sudden intruder had gouged a huge crater into the ground. Right before the eyes of Heinkel, who had been about to take Schult away, stood a small figure blocking the road, short arms folded.

—At a glance, understanding just what it was came a beat too slow. After all, what had just happened and the impression given off by that figure were simply too far apart to reconcile.

A small stature, a lovely face, unblemished white skin adorned by sky-blue clothing that only further set off its charm. In appearance, it seemed hardly different from Schult or Utakata, and it seemed impossible that this was the same figure that had just appeared with an impact fierce enough to shatter the ground. But what had happened was everything, and no one could deny it.

The figure that had appeared narrowed its golden eyes, cast a scornful glare over the four of them standing in the street, and nodded. And then—

"For the sin of spilling a dragon's blood, atone by bleeding out every last drop of your own. ...Fools, all of you."

  • Utakata's (and, in her single line, Mizelda's) speech carries a distinctive katakana-stress pattern in the original (odd kana capitalized to mark accent, clipped grammar, contracted nicknames like ウー/フー/ミー/ター/マー). I rendered this with invented nicknames (Uu=Utakata, Fuu=Flop, Mii=Medium, Shuu=Schult, Taa/Maa=unclear referents) plus capitalized sentence-final letters to preserve the accent flavor. This is an interpretive substitution — the identity of "Taa" (ター) is not established in this excerpt (possibly a Shudrak elder/father-figure) and should be checked against later chapters/wiki.
  • Schult's habitual sentence-ending であります has no direct English equivalent; I rendered it as a trailing "sir" to preserve his stiff, soldier-like formality — a substitution rather than a literal translation.
  • 執事くん ("Butler-kun") → "little butler"; あのお姫くん → "the little princess" — both preserve Flop's teasing -kun diminutive via adjective rather than the suffix itself, since -kun on an epithet (not a name) doesn't transliterate naturally.
  • 嬢 (as in ウタカタ嬢, プリシラ嬢) rendered as "Miss ___" rather than kept as a romanized suffix, since it's not among the honorifics specified for preservation.
