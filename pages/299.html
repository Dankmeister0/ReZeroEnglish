Arc 4 Chapter 129: "—Choose Me"

—Even now, there are times I recall that moment and am struck by regret.

The finger I extended, brushed away. My name, called out as if cherished. In her parting words there was compassion; in the corners of her smiling eyes, there were tears and resolve — more than enough to seal away any voice I might have raised.

"――――"

Even now, I don't know what I should have said. Even now, I can't remember what I was thinking. Even now, I haven't found the answer to what I should have done.

—And so even now, Beatrice remains alone, curled up, unable to move from the Book of Wisdom Archive.

"...Ryuzu."

What spilled from her lips was a fragment of memory so old that even giving it sound felt strangely nostalgic. When her emotions burst forth and that name left her mouth, the time that had been stagnant within Beatrice — four hundred empty years of it — came flooding out all at once.

It was after Ryuzu Meyer, the girl, was lost — her existence traded for the establishment of a place called the "Sanctuary" — and after the Warlock Hector was driven off, that Beatrice shut herself away in the Book of Wisdom Archive and began waiting for the arrival of someone called "that person."

The loss of the one being she could truly call close, someone she had been on genuinely intimate terms with. Beatrice's exhaustion — having lost her right before her own eyes, through her own insufficient strength — was plain to anyone's gaze, and everyone knew that only time could heal that wounded heart. And so her mother's conclusion was simple and clear.

"Eventually, that Warlock will return again to destroy me. I intend to have countermeasures ready by then... though even those cannot be called perfect."

"Yes, Mother."

"Should we meet again, that will be the decisive battle, fought with everything we have. Given the enemy's strength, my chances of surviving are fifty-fifty... perhaps slightly worse than that? Unfortunately, with his Gate so damaged, Roswaal will be of no use in the fight."

Echidna lowered her eyes, but Beatrice's expression remained resolutely flat. It wasn't that she was holding it in — since that day, nearly all her emotions had simply stopped surfacing. How much would the backlash affect her, if a loss too great were struck by the return of feeling? Perhaps it was because her heart understood this that her emotions had frozen over in the first place.

Watching Beatrice, whose expression didn't shift even a fraction, Echidna ran her fingers through her own white hair.

"To begin with, I am among the least suited of the Witches for combat. Once it's clear I can't even borrow the strength of Roswaal, magical genius that he is, only by exhausting every last strategy can I glimpse a chance at victory."

"...What should Betty do, then?"

It was common knowledge that Roswaal had been driven to the brink of death in the battle that established the Sanctuary's function. His Gate had been all but annihilated; his path as a mage was effectively severed. Picturing her comrade lying in bed, still wandering the border between life and death, Beatrice threw her question at Echidna in a voice tinged with something like abandon.

"Should Betty buy time, like Roswaal did, until Mother's ritual formula is complete? Or would it be better if Betty offered herself up as the core of the ritual — this body, a potent lump of Od? For Mother's sake, there's nothing Betty would begrudge giving. ...Please, use me however you like."

Pinching the hem of her skirt, Beatrice bowed, showing her mother the utmost trust. In truth, it was too fragile and fleeting a thing to be called "trust." But Beatrice hadn't grasped her own present state of mind, and even if she had understood herself, the answer she gave would likely have been no different.

A reckless desire for revenge, indignation at her own powerlessness — the only difference lay in whether or not she was aware of it as such.

"—I see. Hearing you say that makes it that much easier for me to ask this of you without guilt. Truly, you are a well-raised child, Beatrice."

"...Yes. Betty is Mother's daughter."

Ordinarily, being praised so by her mother would have brought unrivaled joy. Perhaps knowing this, Echidna rarely voiced such praise for Beatrice outright. Now, those magic words fell into her hollow chest with a strangely empty sound. Perhaps no matter what was said to her, this heart would never again kindle with warmth.

Thinking exactly that, she couldn't react in time to Echidna's next words.

"Beatrice. I am entrusting you with the management of my archive of knowledge. Until the day that must eventually come, stand guard over that knowledge as keeper of the archive. — See to it that no one takes it from you."

"...Eh?"

"Fortunately, you have an unrivaled aptitude for Shadow Magic. Applying the power of Door Crossing, you can connect a sealed-off space to a place you're familiar with. ...Yes, let's call it the 'Book of Wisdom Archive.' I want you to store and protect there everything I know, compiled into books."

Leaving Beatrice wide-eyed with shock, Echidna went on speaking, unhurried. Beatrice, who had been certain she was about to be ordered to accompany Echidna into the coming battle, life on the line, could only blink in bewilderment at being handed a role she had never imagined. Watching her daughter's dismay without pause, Echidna laid out her words smoothly.

"The archive is best connected to Roswaal's manor. I intend to dispose of every one of my research facilities and prepare for the final battle. I'm sorry, but I can't spare personnel to carry the books out. For the bookshelves and the labor, please rely on Roswaal."

"W-wait..."

"I won't set a deadline. Both you and I have already been freed from the yoke of mortality. The length of the passing Seasons means little. That said, considering the possibility that I may be lost, leaving it open-ended would be irresponsible. So then..."

"Wait, please!"

She drew breath and cried out, raising her voice. Beatrice couldn't understand what her mother was saying, right in front of her. No — instinct screamed that she mustn't understand it. Echidna's thoughts were an abyss, forever effortlessly beyond what an ordinary person could grasp. And so her words were the absolute optimal answer; Beatrice had never once considered interrupting her before.

But now was different. It was different. If she let Echidna say everything now, she would surely regret it. Once her mother's opinion was spoken in full, it would be an optimal answer with no room for objection. The world would fall in line with that opinion's current, and Beatrice would be unable to resist it. So the only way to stop it was to cut her off before she finished.

"Mother... what are you even saying. T-the archive, whatever it is — Betty doesn't understand any of it. Betty is! Going to be with Mother!"

"Regrettably, your presence would have little effect one way or the other against the Warlock. Of course, having you there rather than not should raise the odds of victory... but only marginally. A negligible amount. Within the margin of error, one might say."

"E-even so, if it's better than not having me, Betty will help Mother! That way—"

"That won't do. Against a slight, uncertain boost to our chances, the risk that you and I are both destroyed together is far greater. Given that my chance of surviving the battle is lower than half, I believe I must make the effort to leave my knowledge for posterity."

And the act meant to leave that knowledge for posterity was the management of the archive she wished to entrust to Beatrice. In that moment, Beatrice cursed her own power of Door Crossing, her ability to create a private space. If she didn't have such a power, her mother would never have asked this role of her—

"D-don't tell me... Betty's power, was for... this, all along?"

"――――"

"Mother knew from the start this would happen, and... if that's so, then not just this archive place, but— the 'Sanctuary,' too..."

"Having the means to foresee something and choosing to act on it are two different matters. It's true I had both the means to see this path unfold and the means to avoid walking it. But I swear, on my own way of living, that I have not made use of that power. That much, I want you to believe."

Echidna shook her head, denying Beatrice's strained question. Then, stepping toward Beatrice, who bit her lip, Echidna drew a single book from the shelf and quietly held it out to her daughter.

"Wh-what is... this...?"

"An incomplete copy of my Book of Wisdom. The ritual formula within the Book of Wisdom is advanced and complex — I haven't been able to fully decipher it — but it should offer its holder, at minimum, a simple signpost toward the future."

Beatrice took the offered book, tracing its cover with a trembling hand. When she raised her face to look at Echidna, her mother's gaze remained just as it always was — as though looking off into some distance — fixed on Beatrice.

"There are two copies. One is for you; the other I have given to Roswaal. As for what follows, Roswaal will see to it once he reads the book. I'm sorry to ask this selfishly, but I want you to hear me out."

"――――"

Looking down at the book with trembling eyes, Beatrice realized, too late, that she had been too slow. Not letting her speak, not letting her put it into words — that thought alone hadn't been enough.

Echidna, her mother, had already decided every answer. No matter how much Beatrice might cry now, cling to her, beg — it wouldn't change the answer. That was who Echidna was. That was the kind of Witch the "Witch of Greed" was.

"Let's return to the matter of the deadline. Even if I do not return, the archive must eventually be opened by someone. When that happens, I will make it so you can recognize it. One fit to inherit my knowledge should come to fetch you."

"To fetch... Betty..."

"Let's call them 'that person,' for now. The deadline, then, shall be when 'that person' opens the door of the archive and tells you your duty is at an end. — This is my final wish."

Her final wish. At those words, Beatrice caught her breath and looked up again at Echidna's face, watching her once more.

Her mother's expression, unchanging as ever. And yet in that expression, just for this one moment, some unfamiliar feeling seemed to be mixed in.

"Betty. — At the very least, stay well."

Parting from Echidna, Beatrice, exactly as her mother had said, took up residence at Roswaal's manor, exhausted the full extent of her Shadow Magic to create the "Book of Wisdom Archive," and packed it with her mother's books of knowledge. An ocean of knowledge Echidna had spent her entire life gathering and writing down. Shut away in a room stuffed with that knowledge given form as books, one could indulge the illusion of being held by one's mother.

Whether her younger self had truly thought so or not is another matter, but Beatrice kept Echidna's instruction. If she hadn't immersed herself in that duty, she wouldn't have been able to endure the sense of loss that continued, unabated, to torment her small chest. And even so, it went on eating away at the girl endlessly, even within the archive where time lost all meaning.

"Duplicate the soul... overwrite it onto the vessel..."

Exactly how much time she began to spend hollowly, she never properly grasped. But by the time so much had passed that she'd forgotten how long it had been since she'd last had a proper conversation, a now-grown Roswaal began setting foot in the archive.

"Just a li-i-ittle intrusion again today."

A gaunt young man with an unkempt beard dragged his feet into the room. Leaning on a cane, walking with weary reluctance — Roswaal, his entire body wrecked in the battle against the Warlock and most of his Gate's function destroyed, had become someone for whom even ordinary daily life was a struggle. And yet, once some measure of stamina had returned, he began driving that inconvenient body to its limits, exposing himself to something like a slow shaving-away of his own life, facing down the bookshelves.

His flesh gone, his face and body reduced to skin and bone. The refined looks that had once made him renowned as a beautiful man had long since lost their luster; only his sunken yellow eyes gleamed, wet with something like madness.

"—Do as you please, I suppose."

Ordinarily, Beatrice wanted no one at all setting foot in this archive. Until the arrival of "that person" whom Echidna had spoken of, this place was meant to be Beatrice's own "Sanctuary," untouched by any other eyes. And yet, Roswaal alone was different. He alone was a comrade who, like Beatrice, had lent his strength to Echidna's wishes and spent no small stretch of time alongside her.

For Roswaal's wish alone, her heart could permit the archive to be opened. Perhaps it was that small fellow-feeling of Beatrice's that ended up deciding the fate that would befall the man named Roswaal L. Mathers, and his line, thereafter.

"――――"

Setting foot in the archive, drowning in Echidna's sea of knowledge, Roswaal struggled, staking his entire life on finding something. Whether the result of that effort bore fruit or not was not something Beatrice was privy to.

Still, the man named Roswaal L. Mathers, who had studied alongside Beatrice under Echidna, died some ten-odd years after Echidna and Beatrice parted ways — somewhere around his thirties — and the management of the manor passed to the next generation.

"Well, we-e-ell now, a pleasure to meet you, Lady Beatrice. My predecessor spoke to me of you, if only in stories."

"...The Roswaal, is he dead, then?"

"The previous Roswaal has passed away. But please, be at ease. I, the current Roswaal L. Mathers, will carry on your duty and my debt of gratitude to your mother."

So said the second Roswaal, smiling at Beatrice.

—His smiling eyes were heterochromatic: yellow and blue.

Of what came after, there is scarcely anything worth setting down.

A line that kept the name of Roswaal even as it passed to the next generation. Even knowing that this way of being for the Mathers family was meant to never forget its debt of respect to their late mother, Echidna, Beatrice never allowed the Roswaals who followed unrestricted access to the archive. It was only natural. To Beatrice, the only one who could be granted special treatment was, without question, the first Roswaal alone. Anyone bearing the name Roswaal after the one who had shared her feelings was an imitation. In exchange for the manor — Roswaal Manor — being provided to sustain the archive, she extended some measure of convenience, but nothing more than that.

If Beatrice were ever to open the archive willingly again, it would be for one person alone: "that person." The one she waited for — the signpost her mother had handed her — forced upon the girl a long, long span of solitude.

"Your power is magnificent. Please, lend that strength of yours as a Spirit to me."

—Shut up. Go away, wherever.

"Such a long, lonely stretch of time, in a place like this. Too cruel a fate. Whoever ordered it, such a thing shouldn't be allowed."

—What would you know. This is the precious duty Mother asked of me.

"Isn't knowledge meant to be shared? If what's here were spread abroad, imagine how many could be saved. You ought to understand that yourself."

—How many, doesn't matter. What Betty wants to save is only a handful. And the only one who can save Betty now is just one person, too.

Four hundred years' worth of time. Uninvited, unwelcomed, more than a few had nonetheless found their way to the archive. Each of them, men and women alike, hurled their own words at Beatrice, the archive's keeper, and every one of them, in the end, invariably demanded that she open the books.

Their offers, their proposals, their commands — there were times her heart wavered, wondering if they might be right. When the door was pushed open, when she noticed the outside sunlight spilling in, she had thought, more than once, that this time "that person" had finally come.

But every time, contrary to Beatrice's hopes, not one of them knew the duty of "that person," and the book in her hands never once showed that any of them was "that person." And so Beatrice shook off every word, every hand extended to her, rejected them all, and lived out her days up to now with only her mother's words to lean on.

As those days accumulated, Beatrice's heart came to be governed by resignation and despair.

If only she had exchanged more words with the first Roswaal. From the moment she lost the one presence who could share her memories of Echidna, Beatrice had to go on resisting, entirely alone, the sheer concept of overwhelming time. When that happened, with nothing left to rely on, Beatrice hardened her heart, and the only method left to her was to shut herself away behind a rampart of solitude that nothing could break.

As a result, over the long span of four hundred years, the girl's cage took shape. Whether it was locked from outside, or locked from within— —even Beatrice herself was starting to lose track of which.

"Hey, Betty. It really has been a while. It's me — Puck."

That, and the impossible reunion it brought about, might well have been the sole event that thawed even slightly Beatrice's frozen heart.

"N-nii-cha? Why, are you here..."

"My darling daughter got charmed by the Roswaal of this manor, and I came along to be taken care of, too. I never imagined Betty would be here. I'm glad to see you."

Washing his face with tiny paws, bashful — the little cat Spirit's name was Puck. Like Beatrice, an artificial Spirit born of Echidna's hand, and to Beatrice, practically the only being who shared her origin and circumstances — nearly her sole kin.

Four hundred years ago, the time Beatrice had spent with Puck now seemed, in retrospect, both long and short. Puck, made before Beatrice, had left the two of them before the war against the Warlock began and, following his purpose, had presumably wandered the world ever since. Half-believing she'd never meet him again, all but thinking of him as dead, Beatrice found her heart, for the first time in decades, welling up at this reunion with Puck. But that joy lasted only a moment—

"I wandered the world for about three hundred years after we parted, too, Betty, before I finally found Lia. I don't know what you're waiting for, but I'm sure your wish will come true."

"Yes, yes, I suppose. But nii-cha, Betty envies you, I suppose. Betty has, the duty from Mother..."

"Mother? Who's that again?"

"――――"

Puck cocked his head, genuinely puzzled, not joking in the slightest — and seeing it, Beatrice remembered. Several Contracts had been exchanged between Puck and Echidna when he left her side. She didn't know the detailed terms, but in the sight of Puck having forgotten Echidna entirely, one clause of it was plainly, vividly on display.

"...No, it's nothing, I suppose. Betty's glad, to see nii-cha again."

"Yeah, I'm glad too, Betty."

Puck, having fulfilled his purpose and lived out the reason for his own creation, dazzled Beatrice. And she understood, too, that the words she wanted to say would only get in the way of his path. So the girl held her tongue, and with a sorrowful smile blessed the road ahead of the one she called brother.

This unexpected reunion with her own kind brought Beatrice a small measure of joy, and a far greater measure of anguish, pushing the girl's heart toward the end of her four-hundred-year-long days. Comparing herself, who had accomplished nothing, to Puck, who had fulfilled his role, Beatrice was struck dumb by the gulf between them. And she found herself thinking:

"...Surely, Betty can never smile like nii-cha does, anymore."

Beatrice made up her mind to avoid, as much as possible, the Half-Elf girl Puck doted on. If she didn't, surely someday Beatrice would hurl the tangled knot in her heart at that girl. At a girl who had done nothing wrong, whom her beloved brother treasured above all else in the world — surely she would inflict some irrevocable wrong upon her.

Beatrice was skilled at calling her heart to restraint, at killing her own feelings. For four hundred years, the world had greeted the sunrise and seen off the sunset and sunk into moonlight, over and over, without end. Second nature. A practiced habit. A resignation she knew all too well. That was all it was. Into days like that — a foreign body suddenly intruded.

"D-don't make it hurt, okay?"

"Even glib remarks, when carried this far, are almost admirable, I suppose."

It had truly been a long time since a human had barged into the archive without permission. Looking down at the boy who collapsed after having mana forcibly drawn from his body, Beatrice let out a breath and smoothed her own hair.

Tossing the boy who had left the room into a labyrinth, using her power to connect spaces, had simply been petty spite. Spite for having been made, the day before, to help treat that same boy, brought in injured. Spite for having, via Puck, been made to grant the wish of the Half-Elf girl who claimed he had saved her. Her intent was to vent, if only a little, her frustration over a matter she couldn't even complain about, by giving the boy a hard time.

Instead, her Door Crossing had been seen through in one stroke. The boy never noticed how deeply unsettled Beatrice had been, inwardly.

"Well, there's someone Betty doesn't want to get involved with anymore, I suppose."

Chasing the boy out of the archive, Beatrice mused to herself. Why he'd been able to reach the archive on the very first try was unclear even to Beatrice. Likely his magical aptitude was specialized toward the shadow attribute, and by chance his wavelength had matched Beatrice's that day, or so she reasoned. Still, though the boy had aptitude for the shadow attribute, he had no aptitude as a mage to begin with.

He'd only be staying at the manor a few days at most — with that thought, Beatrice managed to ignore the faint knot that had sprouted somewhere deep in her chest.

"Betty. I heard you were mean to that boy. That's no good, now. He's Lia's benefactor too, so you really do need to apologize properly."

The next morning, scolded by Puck when he showed his face in the archive for her behavior the day before, Beatrice found herself having to face, sooner than expected, the very boy she'd resolved to have nothing to do with.

"The hell kind of thing is that to say the second we meet, you loli."

"What is that word, I wonder. Never heard it before, and yet it feels unpleasant, I suppose."

"It means 'too young to be a conquest target.' I don't really have much of a thing for younger types."

"...That anyone could speak this rudely to Betty, it's almost pitiable, I suppose."

A tit for a tat. Whatever thin intention she'd had to apologize vanished completely, thanks to this exchange.

Sitting silently through breakfast, and seeing Puck's helplessly exasperated face, Beatrice let out a breath, thinking she'd somehow been forgiven. Instead, in its place, it was now being decided that the boy would be staying at the manor long-term.

Now truly wanting to curse the situation in earnest, Beatrice decided to excuse herself from the breakfast table and shut herself in the archive. This manor was troubled enough as it was, and it was an emergency besides. A boy with no apparent backbone would surely give up soon enough and be gone. Until then, all she had to do was endure and wait it out.

"Yo, Beatrice. Work's done, I got some time to kill, so I came to play."

Not knowing anything of her own thinking, the vacant-faced boy barged into the archive as if it were the most natural thing in the world, pestering Beatrice for no reason at all whenever he pleased, and repeating the act every single time he found a spare moment.

Faced with such shamelessness, even Beatrice could do nothing but fall speechless. Up to that point, a handful of others had possessed the qualification to enter the archive without Beatrice's permission. But every single one of them had come seeking the knowledge within the archive, or seeking out Beatrice herself, a powerful Spirit. Open their mouths, and they demanded the knowledge be released. Or they demanded a Contract with Beatrice. That was the sort they always were.

"Beatrice. — Can I pull on that drill-curl of yours and go boing-boing with it?"

"Do you wish to die, I wonder?"

Just when she thought he'd say something serious for once, the boy proved to be exactly the same in every regard. When he'd first woken up and started working at the manor, he'd seemed somewhat cowed the first day or two, but his familiarity after that point had grown out of all proportion.

Then, out of nowhere, he suddenly announced:

"I'm cornered, dead-ended in every direction. Not gonna lie, I want to borrow your hand."

—The boy had gotten to the bottom of what had triggered the Witchbeast disturbance surrounding the manor.

Having taken the Curse of a Witchbeast onto his own body, coming to Beatrice to discuss dispelling it and finding the cause, the boy showed her something somehow different from anything she'd seen in him before. And at the same time, she noticed something else:

The strangely warped way the shadow-attribute power within the boy's body was building.

The Witchbeast affair itself concluded outside Beatrice's involvement, and the boy seemed, in the course of the incident, to have bridged some gap with the serving sisters, apparently welcomed as a genuine member of the household. Afterward — the boy's exuberance once he'd recovered, his attitude of pestering her even more brazenly than before, an episode of him savoring his own mysterious homemade condiment mayonnaise — through it all, Beatrice found herself sketching, "perhaps," an impossible daydream.

—A boy who showed no particular interest in either her knowledge, or her power.

Could it be, perhaps, that he was the very presence Beatrice had been waiting for all along? With no grounds whatsoever, Beatrice, worn out from endless days of doubt, tried to settle on that conclusion — only to suppress herself, opening the prophetic book that wrote nothing at all.

As long as the prophetic book showed nothing, that boy couldn't possibly be the "that person" Beatrice was waiting for.

To begin with, the boy lacked so many of the qualities she'd expect of the one she waited for. For one, his eyes were bad. His attitude was bad, too. He had no refinement, and his legs were short. There were things he seemed to treasure more than Beatrice, and he wasn't kind to Beatrice. If anything, there was scarcely a single redeeming quality to be found. What the Half-Elf girl, or the blue-haired maid sisters' younger one, saw in him, she genuinely couldn't fathom.

Since he had no redeeming qualities at all, he ought to just be alone, liked by no one. Then, perhaps, she'd be willing to change her treatment of him, if only a little, whenever he showed his face in the archive.

Or so she'd thought, at times.

"Beatrice. I'm thinking of inviting Lady Emilia and Subaru-kun to the 'Sanctuary.'"

Those were the words Roswaal, having returned from the royal capital, told Beatrice. A whirlwind of doubts of every shape raced through Beatrice's mind, her eyes wide. But Roswaal silenced that flurry of questions with a single gesture. Namely, he traced a finger over the cover of the prophetic book he held.

"...You understand, don't you? Beatrice."

"I- I understand, I suppose. ...Do as you like, I suppose."

That was all Beatrice had left to say in reply. Hearing that Roswaal had turned his back on her and gone ahead alone to the "Sanctuary," Beatrice decided to shut herself in the archive, meeting no one's face.

The entry in Roswaal's prophetic book that summoned a result concerning the "Sanctuary." Hearing of it, it was true she'd harbored some hope that her own book might do the same. But the pages of Beatrice's prophetic book remained blank as ever, and the girl's heart was left stranded in a wasteland.

She knew what had become of the "Sanctuary," as a result of Ryuzu Meyer's sacrifice. She knew that four hundred years had passed without that place ever being freed. That it held within it those cast out from the Demi-Human race, still waiting for their moment of liberation. That it was a wall the Half-Elf girl must overcome, in pursuit of the throne.

—But if that place were to be freed, what would become of Ryuzu Meyer's sacrifice?

What of Beatrice's own helplessness, at having failed to save Ryuzu Meyer that day? What of that unbearable sense of loss, which had been the very thing that led Echidna to bid her farewell?

Feeling emotions with nowhere left to go — feelings that should have been frozen — begin to pulse once more, Beatrice, this time, truly understood the terminus of her own fate.

Beatrice didn't know the particulars of what had happened outside the manor. The boy who returned from the royal capital carried with him a keepsake belonging to someone dear, someone she remembered fondly. Seeing it, feeling once again that she'd been left behind by the world, Beatrice sent the boy and the others off to the "Sanctuary." Resigning herself to the thought that whatever they brought back from the "Sanctuary" would surely be her answer.

"So Betty decided, I suppose...!"

Before the answer could be brought back, she felt violence, bearing "death" with it, sweep through the manor. And when she realized where its source lay, this time, at last, Beatrice understood that she had been abandoned by fate.

"Betty won't break the promise with Mother. ...But there's no meaning left, I suppose, in spending any more empty time than this!"

"That person" would absolutely never come. And yet she couldn't stop waiting. If so, then someone would simply have to take the choice to "wait" away from Beatrice.

To that end, she wouldn't even mind offering herself up to whoever came to take her life. If she could, if there were someone she could bring herself to entrust that role to, even in the smallest measure, then even at the very last, she might believe her small wish had been granted.

And so, when the boy — Natsuki Subaru — threw open the archive door that night, an emotion too hard to put into words swept through Beatrice's heart.

Because it felt, for the first time, like fate — which had never once tried to save Beatrice's heart — was finally repaying her with something.

If her life were to be taken by his hand, breaking their promise — even that would be—

"I'm taking you out of here, Beatrice. — This time for sure, I'm dragging you out into the sun with my own two hands, and we're playing until that dress of yours is caked in mud and black from head to toe."

—What on earth was he suddenly saying?

"That's none of your business, I suppose. No one asked you for anything like that, I suppose."

It made no sense. What was he even saying. Because the boy had never, not once before this, acted the way "that person" should. He'd never snatched away Beatrice's prophetic book and told her, "Sorry to keep you waiting."

"Quit letting yourself get jerked around forever by a blank book and some verbal promise from four hundred years ago. — What you want to do, you get to choose, Beatrice."

"――――"

—So why, of all times, now, did the boy have to stir up the heart of a Beatrice who had already made peace with the end?

She had been thinking of nothing but reaching that end. Seeing the boy return, she had held the hope that it would be by his hand that it all concluded.

And yet the boy was trying to show her a future in a shape entirely different from the hope Beatrice had held. She didn't want that. The heart that wanted such a thing had long since worn itself threadbare over four hundred years.

"I- if you... were 'that person'..."

Even so, listening to the boy's voice, unconcealed in its indignation, something began to shift within Beatrice's heart. As flowers bud in the season of thaw, dormant emotion, trembling, began to show its face.

If she said it aloud, there would be no taking it back. Losing her four-hundred-year obsession with her mother's words that had bound her all this time, only to cling now to something else entirely new. Knowing that full well, Beatrice was about to speak the decisive words—

"Will you, become Betty's 'that person'?"

"Are you an idiot? — There's no way in hell I'm your mystery 'that person' or whatever."

The instant she spoke it, the hope that had budded in her was betrayed by the boy's mocking expression.

After that, she'd driven the boy from the room in a fit of anger, and remembered little of it clearly. But she was aware, at least, of having spoken words she could never take back, and of having extinguished the fire before it became truly irrevocable.

More than anything, this whole trail from door to door felt to Subaru like Beatrice's will, trying to steer him from the hidden passage out of the manor entirely.

Perhaps she understood the manor's current state, and was showing him a path to keep him alive. If so, then even entering the hidden passage might not connect to the archive at all. He might simply be guided straight out of the manor, along the evacuation route, all the way to the mountain cabin — and Subaru would lose his chance to save Beatrice forever.

"—Not even given time to think it through!"

Behind the thinking Subaru, he heard the death cry of a beast taking a decisive blow. The Witchbeast that had unwittingly bought him time so valiantly had, this time for real, had its life taken by Elsa's shadow.

Shaking his head, Subaru threw himself into the hidden passage. A spiral staircase long enough to reach the manor's basement greeted him, but the manor fire's reach had apparently extended even here — the heat and smoke made it a place no person could function in. Pressing a hand to his chest as if to hold back a throbbing pain, Subaru steeled himself and plunged down the stairs in one go. Having just climbed, he was heading back down again. Buffeted by trapped heat, Subaru was now too afraid to even imagine what color his exposed skin had turned.

Eventually reaching the bottom, Subaru, breathing hard, peered into the darkness at the far end of the passage. The smoke, it seemed, had been seeping in partway down the spiral stairs through gaps in the wall; the underground passage, though it still retained residual heat, showed no sign of fire. No danger of burning here, but in exchange, having lost his light source too, Subaru groped his way forward through the dark.

And then, after walking some ten-odd meters, he reached a space with a little room to it, and his feet stopped at the door to the small room he'd been aiming for.

"Th-this is..."

In this hidden passage, Subaru had never before reached beyond this door. That alone meant whether another door existed beyond it was entirely unknown. In other words, for Subaru, this might be the very last candidate door with any chance of connecting to Beatrice. If this one did function properly as part of the hidden passage—

"――――"

Shaking his head to banish his own faintheartedness, Subaru reached for the door handle. If Beatrice's will to keep Subaru alive was what had guided him here, this was a losing bet. Fearing that, even as he touched the handle, Subaru—

"Guah! Not this door again...!"

Crying out at the sensation of his palm being burned, Subaru grimaced and glared at the door. At the door's response, as if reflecting Subaru's own heart awaiting a verdict, something like desperation surged up in him all at once — and then he noticed.

"The doorknob's... hot?"

Even with the trapped heat, there was no sign of fire in this underground passage. Both the smoke and the heat had presumably seeped in through gaps in the stone that formed the stairs. If Subaru's reasoning was correct, it seemed unlikely the room's door should have grown this hot.

This heat was as if the door itself had actually been scorched by flame.

"...Beatrice. If you can hear me, listen."

Careful not to touch the door, Subaru tilted his head slightly upward and murmured. Believing his voice was reaching the girl, though she wasn't here.

"Did you guide me here? If you did it knowing there's no other way out except the hidden passage, I honestly don't even have words for how good a strategist you are."

The play that had led Subaru this far was, he had to admit, impressive. The shadow of Elsa and the Witchbeast along the way surely had nothing to do with Beatrice, but Subaru had had no choice but to be swept along, inevitably, to this point. If he opened this door as it stood and made it to the mountain cabin, Beatrice's aim would be achieved.

"But it looks like things aren't going to go quite that smoothly. ...Even if I open this door, I'm not going to run off the way you want me to. And it's not like I'm insisting I won't run away out of sheer stubbornness or something, right? I mean, that's maybe half of it. But there's something a lot more serious going on."

To a listener who might not even be hearing him, Subaru went on speaking, unhurried. Lightly kicking the door blocking his way with the toe of his shoe, Subaru let out a sigh.

"If I open this door, I'll probably die. You and everyone else might not get it, but on the other side of this door right now, that's the state things are in. Hard to explain in words, but... I, who know the true depths of science, understand."

Setting aside the misfire back in the dining hall, the modern knowledge within Subaru was screaming. The door in front of him right now was in exactly the state of a door you must never touch, the kind that shows up again and again at fire scenes. No joke — Subaru's life was genuinely in danger.

As for the rest — whether this voice was reaching Beatrice. And if it was, whether Beatrice would believe Subaru's words.

"Beatrice. I'm about to open this door. — I'll leave it to you to decide how to take what I said."

Knowing full well something that threatened his life lay right before him, Subaru's heart was, somehow, calm. It wasn't that his nerves had steadied, nor that he'd steeled his resolve. It was something else entirely. Simply, he could entrust his own life, quietly, without reservation.

Because — of course he could.

"—Beatrice. I believe in you."

Saying that, feeling the pain of his palm being burned, Subaru threw the door open. And then—

Less descending the spiral staircase than tumbling down it, the shadow reached the basement.

"――――"

The blood flowing from it was murky as sludge; dragging its crushed leg, its form was a demonic visage unlike anything of this world. In its right arm it held the wickedly black blade; in its left, the heart of the Witchbeast it had killed, gripping it as if to test the sensation, crushing it as it advanced deeper into the passage.

The crawling shadow retained a human shape, but whether human will remained within it was something not even the shadow itself could say. Simply, its body had been destroyed to the point of incapacity, its life shaved away past the point of resurrection, and as a shadow, its vitality had already run dry. As for why it could still keep moving at all — perhaps it was because the personality that had existed before this thing became a shadow had, as its foundation, an obsession fierce enough to sustain even this.

Eventually, the shadow, silent throughout, reached the very end of the passage. A shadow with no will, with no purpose beyond hunting down whatever moved and taking its life. Sensing the presence it had pursued lay just beyond, the shadow, with a slow motion, let its wicked blade flash.

"――――"

With a dull sound, the door before the shadow was cleaved apart. Kicking the door's remains aside, the shadow moved to peer into the darkness beyond the door, and—

"――――"

A faint gust of wind blew through, and the shadow felt the illusion of being drawn into the darkness before it. White smoke billowed from deep within the dark, clouding faintly before the shadow.

And in the next instant — oxygen flooded into the room that had been undergoing incomplete combustion, and combined with the residual fire, erupted all at once into scorching heat.

A backdraft. There was no way a shadow, a mass of destructive impulse stripped of reason, could ever have anticipated that explosive phenomenon.

"――――"

The gushing hand of flame swallowed the shadow whole, the inferno consuming its body entirely. Having lost all power of resurrection and recovery, the body that had only had decay left to it was wrapped in flame that reduced everything to ash, passing beyond mere carbonization and igniting all at once — consumed utterly.

The force of the flames didn't stop at swallowing the shadow — it tore straight through the underground passage, turning the spiral staircase into a sea of scorching heat, then blew apart the study, bursting into a full conflagration.

—This time, at last, Roswaal Manor was collapsing, its final moment upon it.

※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

Seeing the transformed state of the archive he'd been welcomed into, Subaru unconsciously caught his breath.

Cracks ran through the floor near the entrance, and the hole that seemed to lead into some pocket dimension was still very much present. The countless fallen bookshelves showed no prospect of recovery; worse, flames had begun to rise from part of the room. The final state of Roswaal Manor had, at last, begun to affect the archive as well.

"――――"

But noticing the pair of eyes watching him as he entered the room, Subaru swallowed his shock and shifted his focus. For now, he would concentrate only on the one girl before him.

—Because this, surely, was the last chance.

"You're an idiot, I suppose..."

"That's what you open with?"

"Well, it's true, I suppose. Betty did everything she could to somehow get you to escape, and you threw away every single chance and came crawling back. ...There's no door left anywhere in the manor now, I suppose. The fire's starting to spread into the archive too, I suppose."

It was true. Fire had caught on part of the fallen bookshelves, and beloved books, one after another, were turning to ash.

Everything in here was flammable, after all — it would all burn away with astonishing ease.

"Then it's over for both of us, this way, huh."

"...Yes. Over, I suppose. The knowledge meant to be handed to 'that person' has caught fire, and the promise is completely broken now, I suppose."

"I see. Then hear my last words."

"…………"

Beatrice's hollow eyes turned to look at Subaru. There was neither affirmation nor denial in her expression, but that reaction was, at the very least, a sign of willingness to listen. Nodding at her manner, Subaru drew a small breath.

The words he hadn't managed to finish at their last parting. And now, the words he wanted to say — he would say them, all the way through, this time.

"Beatrice. — Save me."

"...Ha, ah?"

He squared his shoulders and declared it plainly. At Subaru, his face covered in soot, saying this outright, a flash of astonishment crossed Beatrice's eyes.

Surely she'd been imagining what he might say. Facing an unavoidable end, Beatrice must have simulated, over and over, the many things Subaru might say to her.

Save you. Not leave you alone. A string of such manly words — perhaps she'd been waiting for some cool, heroic line of welcome, the kind she'd hoped for from "that person."

But if he meant to convey his true, unfeigned feelings, then that wasn't something Subaru could manage.

"I was thinking about saying all sorts of cool stuff, like I'll take you out of your loneliness, or I'll save you. ...But none of it felt like anything but empty bravado, thrown out in the moment. So I really thought about it, honestly. What do I actually feel about you? And because of that, what do I actually want to tell you?"

To a wordless Beatrice, Subaru tossed out his unvarnished true feelings. Setting aside, cowardly, his own decision to leave it up to Beatrice how she'd take it.

"It's not really about saving you or anything — honestly, the truth is, you don't need my power at all. You're strong, you're smart, you're cute... if you wanted to, you could've done anything. If you'd wanted to become anything, you could have become it."

"――――"

"You had more than enough strength to live on your own. Of course you did. Otherwise you couldn't have kept going for four hundred years. So saying I'll lend you my strength, or I'll save you — none of that would've meant a damn thing to you."

"――――"

"But even you — strong, smart, capable of anything you turned your hand to — even you were scared of living alone. It was painful. It was lonely, wasn't it. So nobody can blame you for clinging to the idea of 'that person.'"

"How dare you... rejecting Betty's feelings on your own... what do you even know about Betty...!"

Biting her lip, Beatrice glared at Subaru, an emotion close to hatred kindling in her. But that trembling glare hadn't quite become hatred. Holding onto a fierce emotion that felt about to scatter at any moment, desperately trying to keep it intact, Beatrice shook. Subaru shook his head at her.

"I know. I know you're kind. That if someone's suffering from a nightmare, you'll take their hand to reassure them. That if someone's run up against a problem they can't solve, you'll reach out and open a path for them. That even for someone you can't stand, if someone close to them is lost, you'll grieve for them."

"Talking like you understand..."

"I don't have the power to be any help to you. But if there's anything I, who doesn't want to leave you alone, can actually do, it's just to cling to you and beg."

Before Beatrice's widened eyes, Subaru extended his right hand forward. Burned, blistered, a hand pitiful to look at. Still, better than his left, ravaged beyond recognition by repeated damage. Wiping it off, straightening it as best he could, making it presentable enough to take a girl's hand—

"Beatrice. Save me."

"――――"

"Save me — I can't live without you, and it's lonely as hell. So save me."

If someone overheard it, what a shameful, pathetic threat it must sound like. I can't live without you, so take this hand — that was the threat he was making. Not knowing what he could do for the other person, so instead telling her what she could do for him, and using that as grounds to force her to go on living.

It was an utterly self-serving, unreasonable, hopeless kind of threat.

"That's... unfair. Unfair, I suppose."

"…………"

"Saying it, like that... doing it, like that... now, of all times, to Betty... because you said, you're not 'that person'... you rejected Betty, and yet...!"

Faltering, hesitating over her words, tangled in her own feelings, Beatrice agonized. Unable to tear her eyes from the offered hand, Beatrice clutched the book in her arms tightly.

Tears spilled from the corners of her eyes.

"Four hundred years, always alone...! Living through that solitude, and now, even if Betty took your hand here... you'll die soon enough anyway! A human's lifespan, to Betty, is like the blink of an eye... and now, at this point! To cling to something like that...!"

"I can't even imagine the four hundred years you've lived through. I can't pretend to understand it either. I haven't even lived a twentieth of four hundred years yet. Your fear of the time after I die — I probably can't fully understand that either."

"Then! Then... your words don't solve anything, do they...!"

"But I can hold your hand with you tomorrow."

"――――"

"Tomorrow, and the day after, and the day after that. Even if I can't manage four hundred years from now, I can spend those days with you. Even if forever together isn't possible, I can treasure tomorrow, today, and you."

"――――ッ"

"So, Beatrice. — Choose me."

Subaru had already chosen. And he had presented Beatrice with the choice. The rest was up to Beatrice's decision.

Would she faithfully keep her mother's word, be swallowed by the fire here, and put a period to her four hundred years? Or would she forget the promise made with her mother, abandon the search for "that person," and take Natsuki Subaru's hand?

"Y-you're not... 'that person'..."

"No. Don't lump me in with some other man you imagined up. I'm me. I'm Natsuki Subaru. Forget all about your four-hundred-year one-sided crush on some faceless, nameless guy."

"――――"

"Rather than fearing some parting that might come someday, let's live through the tomorrow that's sure to come, together, with me. I'm weak, and yet my expectations are sky-high... so if you stick with me, you, the caretaker type that you are, will be so busy you won't have time to sit around thinking about how bored or lonely you are."

"...Uh, hic"

"Choose me, Beatrice."

He would say it as many times as it took, until it got through. Because he understood the feelings shaking within this wavering girl, understood what her heart was going through.

So that Natsuki Subaru's own selfishness could shoulder, in her stead, the guilt she felt for wavering, the shame she felt for breaking her promise.

So that this girl would never again have to cry alone.

"You'll disappear anyway, someday..."

"There's no such thing as forever. The future you're afraid of will come, without fail, someday. A day will surely come when I leave you behind, still living on through eternity. But if all we do is dwell on the fear of parting and throw away the joy of being together, then both you and I have far too much of life left untasted."

"You'll leave me behind, someday..."

"Let's be together. Let's try living together. Let's do this, together. Let's pile up so many memories that they can blow away the fear of parting, so many that you can smile with pride and say it was fun. Enough to make up for the lonely four hundred years you spent here, and then some."

"Doing something like that... won't change anything...! Someday, you'll leave me alone again!"

He stepped forward. The distance closed. In the trembling girl's eyes, his own reflection was visible.

Pathetic, shabby, nowhere close to the white-horse prince who'd kept her waiting four hundred years. Just the same old Natsuki Subaru, standing there.

"To you, living through eternity, the time we spend together might be nothing but a fleeting instant. Then I'll carve it into your soul. That instant of mine."

"――――"

"—That a man named Natsuki Subaru was so vivid, so unforgettable, that even across an eternity, he'd never fade into sepia!"

With a sound like cracking glass, the world that was the archive began to collapse.

Before either of them realized it, Subaru and Beatrice had come to be surrounded by fissures in space and by flame. But neither heat nor fear registered now.

Within Subaru now, there was only Beatrice. And within Beatrice now, too, there was only Subaru's existence.

Beatrice's trembling arms clutched the book her mother had given her. Believing that loosening those fingers meant healing her four-hundred-year solitude, Subaru reached out his hand.

He cried out.

"Choose me! Beatrice!!"

"—Ah"

"Because you wanted someone to take you outside, didn't you! Isn't that why you always sat in front of that door!!"

With a definitive sound, the world reached its true end. The archive — the girl's lonely cage — vanished, wrapped in the world's disintegration and flame.

And it was in that instant, just before—

—that a single book fell, with a sound, onto the floor of the archive.

Having passed through the hidden route to the mountain cabin, Otto and the others watched from atop a small hill as the manor burned and collapsed.

"――――"

Otto, Petra, Frederica, and Rem, carried on Frederica's back — the four of them had safely completed their evacuation to the mountain behind the manor via the hidden passage. The mountain behind, particularly near the cabin, seemed to be thoroughly warded with barriers to keep Witchbeasts at bay; there was no sign of wild Witchbeasts nor any of those used in the attack anywhere around them.

And yet no one had the composure to rejoice at having escaped with their lives, to let their voices lift in relief.

Everyone gazed at the manor with a prayer in their hearts, waiting for some visible change to appear there. Trusting in the safety of Subaru, still inside, and of Garfiel.

"――――"

Setting aside even the treatment of his own wounds, Otto watched the manor without so much as blinking. He was aware that Petra, right beside him, was gripping his arm with a strength unimaginable for her young age. She must be worried, worried, unbearably worried. It was common knowledge that the young girl held strong feelings of affection for Subaru. Considering her anxiety, he couldn't help but pray for his safety too.

"――――"

As if to reassure Petra, he gently laid a hand on her brown hair. Smiling at the girl, who looked at him in surprise, Otto turned his gaze back to the manor.

And then, he noticed.

"...That's..."

Somewhere around the middle of the burning manor's main building, perhaps. From the area where the study with the hidden passage they'd escaped through had been, flame burst forth with tremendous force. Windows shattered, the surging flame spreading rapidly to its surroundings, until at last the manor's very shape gave way entirely and it collapsed.

"Ah..."

He heard Petra, witnessing it, let out a sound of despair. Otto too, confronting the same reality Petra had grasped, desperately held back the urge to cry out that it was a lie. If he lost his composure here, it would only trouble the heart of the girl beside him, who must want to cry even more than he did.

But that thought of Otto's was immediately overturned.

"Otto-san, look, that!"

"Owow!?"

Just as Otto's eyes were about to lower, Petra's small palm struck him across the face. Startled by the impact, sparks bursting before his eyes, Otto blinked wildly. But seeing Petra pointing at the manor with a joyful expression, he hurriedly turned to look, and understood.

"H-ha, haha..."

—From the collapsing manor, a single beam of white light stretched up toward the heavens.

That light, like a rainbow, bent at a point high in the sky and extended its tip far off toward the east. As if to say that was where its destination lay.

Otto knew what lay in that direction. So, as his cheeks relaxed watching Petra rejoice, exclaiming "Just now! Was that just now!"—

"The rest is up to you. — Honestly, I'm exhausted."

At the very same moment, Garfiel too — half-naked but for a cloth wrapped around his waist — looked up at that same light Otto's shoulders had slumped in relief over, gnashing his fangs.

Having escaped the burning manor and rushed into the forest, Garfiel put his hands on his hips and roared with laughter. More than that, above all, Garfiel's convictions wouldn't allow him to kill a girl.

"Still, that black woman must've gotten scorched real nice, I bet."

Watching the manor collapse, Garfiel let out a breath. Hurling a Witchbeast to crush someone — an indirect act, leaving no sensation on his hands — but it was still true that Garfiel had, by his own choice, cut down something resembling a human being.

He felt his fingers tremble faintly, a wringing pain in his stomach. But Garfiel forced it down with a single shake of his head, sat down beside the sleeping Meili, and leaned back against a tree.

"The afterglow of winning, the feel of killing — all that's gotta wait for later. There's nothin' more I can do from here no matter how hard I try. ...Countin' on you, Boss."

"Once everything's settled, there's a guy I gotta go crack across the face together with you!"

She'd known it, and yet she'd taken his hand anyway.

She'd known, from long before, that if she took this hand, if she clung to its warmth, there would be no going back to that other, lonely night, alone. She'd known she was warning herself that living by relying on a warmth destined to be lost someday was maddeningly foolish.

—A raging blizzard howled.

A white curtain unfurled thick enough to block all sight, a world of bitter cold where even a breath seemed to freeze the instant it touched the outside air.

The wind that lashed at her was frigid, the fragments of driving snow carrying an edge sharp enough to cut flesh. Even exposed to such ferocity, the silver-haired girl, her hair streaming out behind her, kept her gaze fixed forward, a fierce force of will burning in her violet eyes.

"Absolutely, absolutely not... I won't let anyone be lost!!"

Extending both hands, wrapped in a faint glow, the silver-haired girl released an immense quantity of magical power. Amplified within the raging blizzard, the freezing magic took on light, a pale-blue radiance sweeping across the world like a blade of light, slicing through the white Witchbeasts crossing its path one after another.

A grating, unpleasant sound echoed on and on — the clack of short fangs gnashing together. The very embodiment of hunger — creatures specialized in nothing but devouring their prey, a beyond-redemption calamity from ages past, one that could never coexist with anything.

Before an endlessly multiplying killing intent born of hunger, the silver-haired girl did not retreat a single step. And yet her breathing had grown ragged, and part of the vast mana she still could not fully control had begun to slip beyond her command, white crystal beginning to creep across half her body.

Left as it was, before long the girl herself would be transformed by her own magic into a statue of ice. But even knowing that, the girl could not draw back.

"――――"

Beatrice glanced briefly behind her. There lay everything she had to protect from the fury of the white Witchbeasts.

A crumbling ruin, and the many lives entrusting their hopes to the girl's small back. And, not having entered the ruin, a man watching the girl's battle in dazed silence, and, in his arms, a pink-haired girl lying motionless as though dead.

Half her body threatening to freeze solid. And yet an unquenchable fire lit within the girl's heart.

Seeing that, who could dare voice weakness here? She knew what she stood for, whom she'd been entrusted by, why she stood here like this.

"No one... I won't let anyone decide anyone's end! Everyone joined hands like that, for me... so I'll protect it! I promised Mother that, I did—!"

A torrent of pale-blue light slammed into the mass of Witchbeasts surging from the front. Without so much as a death cry, the Witchbeasts fell still within that white radiance. Witnessing their comrades' pitiful deaths, the Witchbeasts immediately turned to devouring one another, gnawing at the ice.

An unbearable sight to watch. And yet, perhaps, if you squinted, it might resemble the way people cling to hope, too. Even so — even so, all the same.

"As long as I don't forget Mother, and Ju-su, and everyone from today... and the words that person wrote for me, I won't give up."

So even if this body were sealed in ice, at least she would never have regrets.

Pushing through the blizzard, the Witchbeasts' fury gradually, gradually narrowed its range, driving the girl and those who depended on her further into a corner. Should it come to that, the girl was resolved even to throw away her own life if need be.

But just as the girl steeled herself with that resolve, a voice reached her.

"You don't have to push yourself that hard, Emilia-tan."

"――――"

She heard the sound of someone landing beside her from high above. She looked to her side. The blizzard was too fierce, the white curtain too thick to make out that someone's face.

But the girl knew, without a doubt, exactly who it was.

The voice, the manner, and above all — there was no way he wouldn't come, precisely when she needed him most.

"Leave the rest to me, you can step back if you want. — There's a first-battle bonus, you know."

"Sorry. I have no idea what you just said."

A hint of a wry laugh. The figure stepping forward brought with it another, smaller shadow at its side.

And two voices could be heard. Voices that seemed, in some way, to have been waiting for this very moment forever, with a note almost of eagerness in them—

"Well, whatever happens now, don't blame me, I suppose."

"Yeah, we'll make it work somehow. — You and me, together!!"

The opening bell of the first battle had been rung — for the Spirit Beatrice and her Contractor Natsuki Subaru, the two of them, who would go on, again and again, hand in hand, fighting side by side from this day forward.
