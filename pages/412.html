Arc 6 Chapter 10: "Like a Flash of Light"

—A flower bed of riotous colors scattered across the dune's earth.

It was nighttime, and strangely, no starlight could be seen in the sky above the dunes. There was no sign of clouds, yet the sky's condition couldn't be discerned—because, as explained beforehand, the miasma hanging over the ground blanketed this vast sand sea entirely. Whether it had been Beatrice who explained that, or Emilia, or Julius, Subaru couldn't quite recall.

What was clear now, though, was an instinctive alarm bell telling him to pay attention to the ground rather than that inscrutable sky, and the uncanny, inexplicable gaudiness of petals blazing bright enough to defy the night's darkness. Flowers vivid to the point of gaudiness—almost poisonous-looking—were hopelessly out of place in a sea of sand.

"A flower bed... in the dunes..."

Even saying it aloud, the words felt wrong together, and yet Subaru had a sense of having heard this before. What rose in his mind was the tavern in Mirula where they'd gathered information before entering the dunes. The one-legged proprietor there had told a story about a flower bed that appeared in the dunes.

If you see a flower bed in the dunes, he'd said, run without looking back. —Because it marked the territory of a vicious, hideous witchbeast: the Courtesan Bear.

"But even if he says run without looking back..."

His voice trailing off weakly, Subaru murmured this while staring ahead and backing away. That the flower bed was a witchbeast's territory—that much was useful information. Given how vicious and dangerous the beast was, the advice to flee immediately made sense. But if there was a problem, it was the scale of the thing.

"—————"

Subaru's party fell silent, holding their breath. Having weathered the onslaught of the "Sand Time" and broken through the distortion in space, before the party now lay the sand plain leading to the Watchtower—and filling their entire field of view straight ahead, that very flower bed spread out. It was, quite literally, so dense there wasn't a place to set a foot.

A paradise of flowers, suddenly sprung up in the middle of the sand plain. It reeked of trouble to a degree beyond suspicious. Even without the tavern keeper's warning, they would have gone out of their way to avoid it. If flight through the air had been an option, they would have swerved around it without a moment's hesitation.

"There's no way to go around this."

In a whisper barely distinguishable from a ragged breath, Subaru spoke to Meili. As things stood, the party relied entirely on Meili for dealing with witchbeasts. Whatever judgment she made would determine their path—but the girl glaring at the flower bed had a face gone quite pale. Cold sweat beaded on her skin, and the color had drained from her fair complexion. Even without words, it was plain as day that they were in a very bad situation.

"—————"

Slowly, with an almost unbelievable quietness for her massive frame, Patrasche drew close. Seeing Beatrice on her back signal with her eyes to "get on, I suppose," Subaru put his foot in the stirrup and, killing what sound he could, mounted the dragon once more.

Whether advancing or retreating, Subaru's own legs would be swallowed by the sand and be of no use at all. Judgment might be made by the reins, but any dash would depend on Patrasche's sturdy legs.

"...For now, it looks like they're all sleeping."

"—————"

How long had passed while they held their breath, waiting on Meili's judgment? Whether it had been a few dozen seconds or something closer to an hour, it was hard to say, but at last Meili let that whispered assessment slip out. Hearing it, Subaru felt the tension drain from his shoulders and brought Patrasche closer to the carriage.

"That flower bed is the Courtesan Bears' territory... right?"

"Cross-referencing it with what we were told beforehand, it's almost certainly correct. Though... the scale is somewhat larger than I had imagined."

"That difference in scale doesn't look like a cute little double or triple, I'd say."

Letting out a breath, Subaru and the others put their heads together to compare impressions. Julius and Beatrice, too, seemed to have been struck speechless by this reception far beyond their expectations. Inside the carriage, the voices of Emilia and the others celebrating their breakthrough of the "Sand Time" had likewise gone silent, and fearful faces peered out through the small window to see what had happened.

"Subaru, did something happen? I can't hear the sand wind anymore..."

"We cleared the 'Sand Time' beautifully. And just when I thought we'd passed the first checkpoint, the second one was already waiting for us. See for yourself."

"See for yourself, huh...? Ah—"

Taking in the flower bed through the small window, Emilia's throat froze in shock. She, too, had heard the tavern keeper's story. Naturally, the same information would come to mind for her. As Emilia's eyes went wide, Anastasia and Ram peered out from beside her as well. Both of them likewise gazed at the flower bed, their faces creasing at the sheer nastiness of this trial on the way to the Watchtower.

"We got past the sandstorm and the 'Sand Time,' and now this obstacle is a flower bed. Anastasia, you don't happen to know anything about this Courtesan Bear territory, do you?"

"Guidin' the way's my job—don't go expectin' more than that outta me, now. But yeah, honestly, this here's a bit of a problem. What should we do?"

Subaru glanced sideways, hoping unspoken for some input from Eridna, but going by Anastasia's shrug, there seemed to be no breakthrough idea there either. Subaru let out a long breath through his nose and looked to Meili.

"Meili, with your constitution you can call witchbeasts off, right? Can't you get these Courtesan Bears to pull back from that flower bed?"

"...That's hard. Ten, twenty, even a hundred or so, I could manage somehow. But past that, there'll be some who don't hear my orders."

"There's a limit on numbers?"

"There's a limit on quality, too. I can't exactly go making unreasonable demands of something like the White Whale or the Great Rabbit. And Courtesan Bears, well—my compatibility with them isn't great. Big difference from the Shadow Lion."

"The Shadow Lion... ah, the big one that showed up at the mansion."

Cutting the trip down memory lane short, Subaru glared toward the flower bed, weighing Meili's assessment. An uncountable, sprawling paradise of petals—there was no telling just how many witchbeasts lurked within. Still, it was certain to easily exceed Meili's limit of a hundred.

"As things stand, I'd say our options come down to two: 'advance' or 'retreat.'"

From the driver's seat, Julius held up two fingers, addressing the silent Subaru. Looking at those raised fingers, Subaru let out a breath.

"Retreat was never on the table to begin with. Sure, it'd be a waste after clearing the 'Sand Time,' but more than that, it wouldn't solve anything. Putting it off doesn't mean anything."

"If we dove into a different 'Sand Time,' we might be able to challenge the Watchtower from another route. Isn't it a bit rash to shut off that possibility without even considering it?"

Julius's argument was that among the three "Sand Times" that occurred each day, one of the others might hold the correct path. The party had come through the midnight "Sand Time"; there were also morning and afternoon ones—could passing through a different one change where they ended up? No, Subaru thought, shaking his head.

"Even if we came out somewhere different, I can't imagine it'd secure us a safe path to the Watchtower. Here it's a flower bed, but somewhere else could have a different obstacle entirely. I doubt there's some easy route just waiting for us."

"...True enough. That was, perhaps, a touch too optimistic a hope."

"Plus, think about it—with Meili here, facing down Courtesan Bear territory of all things, this might actually be our best possible chance."

"And now you swing too far pessimistic. As ever, you have quite the talent for tearing something down right after you build it up."

"Wasn't trying to pull off anything fancy. Anyway, Meili. What about you?"

Talking circles around Julius's unfavorable odds, Subaru turned the question to Meili. Silent, staring intently at the flower bed, Meili responded to Subaru's words with a weak "Mm..." and lowered her head.

"The numbers are rough, but if you're telling me to do it, I'll give it a try. I don't have to make every single animal I can see listen to me, after all."

"You don't need all of them...?"

"To get through the flower bed, I just need to quietly clear out the bare minimum of Courtesan Bears. Get them to open a path, then let the rest stay peacefully asleep. As long as we don't provoke them, that much I should be able to manage."

As she answered the question, conviction seemed to settle into her own thinking, and strength returned to Meili's voice. At the very least, she seemed ready to respect Subaru's choice to "advance." That was honestly a relief. Subaru nodded to Meili, then looked around at everyone's faces.

"I think we should go forward. Either way, there's no reward to be had without diving into the risk. We won't find a solution by running from the problem forever."

"For someone who mostly relies on others, you sure talk big."

To Subaru proposing they push forward, Ram tossed out a biting remark. Wryly amused at how "her" the jab was, Subaru gave a thumbs up and said, "You know it."

"In the end, it all comes down to relying on Meili. And if worst comes to worst and we end up provoking the witchbeasts, then it'll be relying on Emilia-tan, Julius, and Bea-ko next. Sorry about that."

"—————"

"Oh, and Patrasche too. My bad, my bad."

As if displeased that her name hadn't come up among the reliable members, his beloved dragon lifted her head, and Subaru gave it a pat. At Subaru's pathetic little declaration, Ram shrugged with an unabashedly exasperated look.

"At least you're self-aware, though that doesn't save you. If worst comes to worst, the very least you can do is serve as a decoy."

"That's a nostalgic way to be talked to. Though you probably don't remember it."

"—————"

At his words fondly recalling some bit of the past, Ram only looked puzzled. In the witchbeast forest, or on the morning they'd set out for the royal capital for the Royal Selection—there had been similar words thrown at him by Ram before. Now that Rem's existence had been erased from memory, and the memories tied to it altered along with it, where had that exchange from that day gone?

"Let's go get it back. I don't want to take even one more second of a step backward."

"—Yeah, that's right. I think Subaru's right too."

At Subaru's resolve, fist clenched, Emilia agreed with a face full of resolve. She touched the pendant hanging at her neck, letting her fingers brush against her feelings for the spirit sleeping within the great mana crystal. Then her violet eyes fixed straight ahead, on the flower bed.

"Beyond this flower bed is the key to a precious memory. Let's not stop moving. It's all right. I'll definitely protect everyone."

"Ain't it awful uncool, gettin' told that by a girl?"

At Emilia's dignified declaration, Anastasia looked over at Subaru and said as much. He wanted to snap back "shut up," but honestly, thinking that things would probably play out exactly as Emilia said, it was a rather difficult thing to argue with.

The distance to the Pleiades Watchtower had shrunk, by eye, to something like a few kilometers. Before crossing the "Sand Time," while blindly advancing through the Augria Sand Dunes, the estimated distance to the visible Watchtower had never once changed from something like a dozen-odd kilometers. Compared to that, this was an overwhelming reduction in distance—arrival could almost be called imminent.

"Though all of that hinges on getting through this Courtesan Bear nest first."

The flower bed filling their view sat there brazenly atop the sand sea, its wrongness palpable. These flowers lacked the basic ecology one would expect—plants putting down roots into the earth. The way their stems and roots seemed to float slightly above the ground was because the flowers weren't growing from the earth at all, but from the flesh of that ghastly beast itself.

"—————"

In crossing the Courtesan Bears' territory, the instructions Meili gave Subaru's party were simple and clear—silence, as much as humanly possible, and nothing more. By her account, the Courtesan Bears were currently in a dormant state. They rarely woke, but in exchange, the ferocity of a witchbeast freshly roused from sleep was apparently beyond measure.

"Bad animals are at their most vicious right when they've just started movin'. Even that White Whale they took down, the moment it came out with the fog is s'posed to have been when it was angriest and most troublesome."

So went Meili's side note about the White Whale. Thinking it over in light of her lecture, the moment Subaru's group had met the White Whale in battle was the very instant it appeared on the Lifaus Plains—in other words, the absolute worst possible timing, right as it woke. The memory of that ordeal came flooding back, and belatedly, Subaru gave himself credit for having somehow won that fight. That aside, though—

"Wakin' a sleepin' kid up angry is the same whether it's a person, an animal, or a bad animal. So, y'all be nice an' quiet, 'kay? If you can just do that much, I'll handle the rest."

Following Meili's reassuring words, the party's breath-held march began. The formation switched from how they'd tackled the dunes—now the carriage went first. Seated in the driver's seat, Meili communicated her intent to the Courtesan Bears, and through that intent, Julius guided the carriage forward. Subaru, controlling Patrasche, ran alongside the carriage, keeping neither too close nor too far.

"—————"

Leaning slightly forward, holding his breath, Subaru held Beatrice's small body close. Her body heat came through to him, but she too seemed stiff with tension. Glancing up at the carriage, he saw a figure atop its roof. Not some suspicious stranger—the familiar slender silhouette was Emilia.

"—————"

Noticing Subaru's gaze, Emilia turned her violet eyes his way and raised a hand. The reason Emilia was lurking up on the roof like that was so she could move instantly the moment any trouble arose. Subaru would have preferred she stay quietly inside the carriage, but with Julius's spirit arts unreliable right now, Emilia was the only ally capable of high-firepower, wide-area attacks. Beatrice could manage it too if pushed, but the moment she fired off one big shot, Subaru would run dry.

Depending on someone else for combat strength—someone he ought to be protecting, no less—was one of Subaru's inferiority complexes.

If that weren't the case, if there were no need for it to be that way, then perhaps even Rem could have— That kind of weakness lived in Subaru always, impossible to shake off.

"—Ah"

"—————"

While he was wrestling with such irreconcilable feelings, his chin was suddenly jerked upward by the back of Beatrice's head. Subaru started, and Beatrice gave a snort. Focus on what's in front of you now—that was clearly the message. Looking up again, he saw Emilia too pointing a finger at him, urging the same caution.

"—————"

Neither agreement nor acceptance could be voiced; it only turned into a long exhale. He really wanted to slap his own cheeks to shift his mindset, but with even a gesture like that potentially fatal, restraint was mandatory. And then, just as Subaru made up his mind after his usual roundabout thought process, the vanguard of the party reached the flower bed at nearly the same moment.

"Move."

Once, the carriage pulled by Jaian came to a stop, and amid the creak of the wheels, Meili's quiet voice was cast out toward the flower bed. At first, there was no response at all to Meili's call, and Subaru clenched his back teeth.

"Move."

Meili repeated the command. Even so, still no change came over the flower bed. In the maddening stretch of time, Subaru's stomach lining seemed to itch with unease. And then—

"—————"

Slowly, as though the very ground itself were peeling back, the flower bed "rose up."

"—————"

Unthinkingly staring straight at that rising witchbeast, Subaru's throat gave a faint spasm. The witchbeast called the Courtesan Bear resembled, as its name suggested, a bear. But it was decisively different from any bear Subaru knew. Too different.

The risen Courtesan Bear's body length was probably somewhere between two and three meters. Its legs were short; in exchange, its arms were abnormally long, long enough to drag along the ground. The arms, seemingly covered in black fur, were thick, and at their ends bristled row upon row of claws like hooks. Vivid petals swayed across its back and sides—striking at first glance—but seeing the beast up close, the eye was drawn not to its back but rather to the front of its body. The torso appeared covered in black fur, but what crawled all across it wasn't fur at all. What blanketed the flesh, densely and without pause, were thin, tangled flower roots. Like veins bulging up under skin, fine roots covered the beast's body entirely.

"—Ugh"

Though the body was densely wrapped in roots, the parts the roots hadn't reached were abnormally desiccated, flesh and skin shriveled completely. The face had lost so much moisture that the shape of the skull was visible, and the round, bulging eyes were bloodshot, as if hating everything in this world. It looked for all the world as if the very flowers rooted in its own body were sucking the life out of it.

The Courtesan Bear did not coexist with the flowers. Clearly, it was being killed by them.

"—————"

The witchbeast, unbearable to even look at, sluggishly obeyed Meili's words. Slowly it peeled its body from the ground, roots tearing free from sand that offered nothing to draw up, the sound of tearing chaining together in countless echoes. More than a dozen Courtesan Bears stirred to clear enough space for the carriage to pass. And the further they advanced, the more their numbers grew, at an accelerating rate.

"Hah."

A faint exhale escaped Julius, and the carriage resumed its advance. Even Julius, it seemed, hadn't been able to suppress a shudder at the sight before him. Falling in at the rear of the carriage, Subaru and the others entered the path the Courtesan Bears had opened.

"...Ugh"

The instant they entered the Courtesan Bears' territory, a violent scent of flowers assaulted Subaru's nose. It was nothing like the stench they'd faced with the Sand Earthworm, but the sheer chaos of countless differently-scented sweet fragrances mixing together was every bit as violent in its own way. His nasal passages felt assaulted, and he had the illusion that the gears in his brain were gumming up, scorched by the sweetness.

"—————"

Truly, without exaggeration, a nauseating stench that induced dizziness and even headaches. He'd take that troublesome sandstorm back gladly at this point. If it could just blow away this poison-like scent clinging to his entire body, he genuinely wished for it.

But Subaru's wish went unanswered; with no wind at all, the party pressed on, deeper and deeper into the flower bed.

"—Hm"

Suddenly, as if noticing something, Beatrice tugged at Subaru's sleeve. Wondering what was wrong, he looked at her and saw her repeatedly glancing behind them. Feeling an unpleasant premonition at that gesture, he turned around—and understood the meaning of Beatrice's reaction. The gap in the flower bed opened by Meili's command, once it had seen the carriage and Patrasche pass through, was steadily closing back to its original state from the edges inward.

Once Meili's command was fulfilled and the carriage had passed, the task was complete. Return to their original positions and go back to dormancy—was that the logic driving them? Even without any intention of turning back, this meant stopping partway would be next to impossible. Not that resting in the middle of this witchbeast's nest was something Subaru wanted to entertain, even as a joke.

"—?"

Just as that thought crossed his mind, the carriage's advance came to a halt. Looking ahead to see what had happened, the cause was obvious. A single Courtesan Bear stood risen and motionless directly in front of the carriage, its hollow eyes turned toward the two on the driver's seat.

"—————"

As that one bear made its suspicious movement, a change came over the nearby Courtesan Bears as well. Their previously docile, shuffling movement stopped, and as if tracing the behavior of the one bear staring fixedly at the carriage, gazes began gathering toward it, one by one.

—Bad, Subaru realized instinctively.

Looking back, they had already advanced several hundred meters from the point they'd entered the flower bed. The path behind them was blocked, and it was clear they were surrounded on all sides by Courtesan Bears. Their numbers could easily reach into the hundreds, perhaps even a thousand. An overwhelmingly violent disparity in numbers.

"—————"

Having thought that far, his gaze locked with Emilia's up on the roof. Emilia was looking down at the Courtesan Bear standing before the carriage, hesitating over what response to take. If open conflict was truly unavoidable now, the best option would be for Emilia's magic to blast a path forward and simply charge through. But she didn't want to rush and mistime it.

"S—"

While Subaru and the others hesitated over the decision, it was Meili who remained the calmest. She put a finger to her lips, calming Subaru and the others first as they wavered before the witchbeast. Then she thrust that finger, still held to her lips, forward and glared at the Courtesan Bear watching her.

"Tsk, tsk, tsk."

A finger extended, and a sound like clicking her tongue slipped from Meili's lips. It was exactly like the sound a person makes when soothing a kitten. If the other party really were a kitten, it would have been an endearing sight, but against a monstrous witchbeast, it had no calming effect whatsoever. And yet Meili's fingertip swayed in time with the clicking sound, and gradually the Courtesan Bear's gaze shifted—away from the two on the driver's seat, toward Meili herself, and then to her finger.

"Tsk, tsk, tsk... tch."

Once she was certain the Courtesan Bear's focus had settled on her finger, Meili slowly turned her arm, finger and all, to the carriage's right. Drawn by that arm's motion, the Courtesan Bear blocking the carriage's path turned its face that way and took a slow, shuffling step.

"—Oh"

A sigh of relief escaped Subaru's throat at the Courtesan Bear's gesture of leaving the carriage. Emilia and Beatrice, who had gone rigid, also let the tension drain visibly from their shoulders. As the one bear blocking the way moved off, the other Courtesan Bears too began returning to their own respective movements away. And so the march through the flower bed would resume—or so it should have.

"————!!"

At that moment, the thread of tension snapped, and no one could blame whoever it was that let out a sound. The heart isn't strong enough to endure being suddenly forced into an extreme deadlock. That's true for humans, and it's just as true for earth dragons.

Jaian, having borne the brunt of the Courtesan Bears' negative pressure up close, let out a low growl and, as if throwing a tantrum, stamped the ground.

"Oh n—"

At Jaian's roar tearing suddenly through the silence, the dormant Courtesan Bears reacted all at once. The one that had been turning away from the carriage spun back around, and that reaction spread through those around it—the witchbeasts opened their jaws, baring fangs, and leapt at the carriage. An instant before—

"—El Huma!!"

Mana rapidly took physical form, and the tip of the resulting ice spear pierced straight through the witchbeast's head. The sharp ice point drove into the open, gaping mouth, tore through its interior, and burst out through the nape of the neck. Its brain scrambled, the Courtesan Bear died without so much as a sound and toppled backward. Caught in its momentum, several other Courtesan Bears were knocked sideways all at once.

"Run—!!"

The instant he recognized it as Emilia's preemptive strike from atop the carriage, Subaru shouted. In answer to that cry, Julius worked the reins and the carriage lurched forward with force. Naturally, Patrasche followed suit, plowing through the frozen witchbeasts from the side and charging ahead with fierce momentum.

"—!"

"They're comin', they're comin', they're comin', they're comin', they're comin'—AAH!!"

A beat later, a tremendous, furious roar tore through the night of the Augria Sand Dunes. The vast flower bed reared up all at once, and the Courtesan Bear horde, fangs and hideous faces bared, surged forward toward the charging carriage.

"Subaru! Hold on tight or you'll get thrown off, I suppose!"

The illusion of being battered by a fierce gale was really the pressure of murderous intent and hunger radiating from countless Courtesan Bears. Thick, claw-tipped arms were raised high, and a merciless blow was aimed straight at Subaru atop his dragon. A direct hit would strip a human body clean off, guts spilling out—no question about it. But an instant before it connected, a pale blue gleam cut in and buried itself in the witchbeast.

"Hyah! Yah! There! This... take lots of Huma!"

As ever, with a rather deflating battle cry, mana swirled, and pale blue light danced wildly across the black sky, transforming into countless ice blades pointed sharp-end down at the ground below. After a moment's stillness, they launched as though gaining sudden propulsion, tearing apart the heads, arms, torsos, and legs of the Courtesan Bears swarming the carriage, sending up sprays of blood mist.

"Whoa-ho! As expected of Emilia-tan! I'm falling for her all over again!!"

"This isn't the time to say things like that! Just run!"

"Yes ma'am!"

His life saved by Emilia's covering fire, Subaru pushed Patrasche to accelerate. In moments of sudden combat like this, Emilia handled the fighting with astonishing composure. Thinking back, even though Emilia avoided fighting, she'd never once flinched from combat itself. Within her, the aversion to fighting was hesitation that could be swept away the moment battle actually began.

"Barusu, drive like your life depends on it. Unless you want to die!"

"Right, of course— wait, Ram!?"

Emilia's valiant efforts, the pale blue light sweeping the carriage's front all at once, froze solid a cluster of Courtesan Bears attempting to circle around to the front. As Jaian smashed through the ice statues with its charge and pressed on, seated in the driver's seat holding the reins was, before Subaru even noticed, Ram, who had moved over from the passenger compartment. Watching Ram skillfully handle the reins, controlling the agitated earth dragon, Subaru's eyes went wide as his gaze darted about.

"Where's Julius!?"

"Apparently, without a means of ranged attack, he's no use up in the driver's seat. So."

Glancing back at Subaru's shout, Ram looked behind her. Drawn by her gaze, Subaru turned to look as well, and was utterly astonished by what he saw. Clinging to one of the ornamental fittings on the carriage's side, amid biting sand and groaning wheels, was Julius, swinging his knight's sword to cut down any approaching witchbeast.

"I can't very well leave all the burden to Lady Emilia, now can I."

"Are you doing this to spite me!?"

"I have no such intention—"

Cutting himself off there, Julius met an approaching witchbeast's claw with his blade. The strike threaded the gaps between the hooked claws and cut deep into the Courtesan Bear's arm. The dancing sword-tip then flashed like lightning through the throat of the shrieking beast, destroying its brainstem and ending its life.

The technique—destruction minimized, executed with real elegance.

"None at all."

Felling witchbeast after witchbeast in succession, Julius finished his reply to Subaru and flicked his sword clean. The gesture left Subaru with both a sense of reassurance and, simultaneously, a taste of defeat, and he made a sour face. Still, with Emilia handling mid-to-long range and Julius sweeping close range, if that arrangement held, the carriage's safety could be set aside as a lesser worry for now. The problem, rather, lay in a direction that was anything but someone else's concern.

"Bea-ko, you good!?"

"It's Subaru who should be watching not to run dry, I—!"

Straightening up atop Patrasche, Subaru held the reins with one hand and, with the other, lifted Beatrice up, setting her small body standing atop the dragon. Then, as their hands clasped firmly together, something stirred deep in Subaru's belly. That stagnant something took on heat and slowly flowed from Subaru's body toward Beatrice.

"Minya!"

With the incantation, a violet crystal formed and took aim at the witchbeast blocking the jet-black earth dragon's snout. The moment aim was found, acceleration was instant, and the result followed at once. With a sound like shattering glass, the witchbeast struck by the direct hit reeled back, and the crystal shattered into pieces. But in the next instant, the flesh of the witchbeast that had taken the direct hit shattered too, just like the crystal.

"There we go, nice, Bea-ko!"

"But I can't spam this, I suppose! Use it carefully, sparingly— Minya!"

"Carefully and sparingly!?"

Beatrice's combat power depended on Subaru's remaining mana. To counter the overwhelming sheer volume bearing down on them, even Beatrice, who normally championed conservation, couldn't afford to hold back. Tasting a sense of loss like his very soul being shaved away with each spell, Subaru cracked jokes and played it cool, easing Beatrice's own hesitation. Of course, she likely saw right through that consideration of his.

"Ugh, geez, geez! Why's it gotta be like this! This was s'posed to be my trump card, y'know!"

And then, the final weapon that had kept silent up until now rose to its feet and stamped in frustration. In the driver's seat, next to Ram, Meili—eyes brimming with tears, face red—surveyed the surrounding horde of Courtesan Bears. Then she thrust a finger at the witchbeasts that would not obey her.

"I'll punish you, you bad, bad animals! Come on out, Sand Earthworm!"

"—You're kidding me!?"

The instant Meili spat that out like a sulking child, the sand plain—already upturned by the Courtesan Bears rising up—was suddenly heaved up further from beneath, and a massive body burst out. Caught up in the force of the giant beast thrusting its entire body up from underground, several Courtesan Bears were flung skyward. Those that fell from that height back into the sand had their bones crushed, and one unlucky enough to be sent straight upward was swallowed whole by that mighty maw and chewed to pieces.

Bursting through the sand plain to ravage the ghastly flower bed was that very same Sand Earthworm. Trailing its foul stench, letting out an eerie cry that split the darkness of night, the Sand Earthworm marked the Courtesan Bears clinging to the carriage as prey and hurled its massive body about, crushing them one after another in an instant.

"Go, go, Sand Earthworm! Get 'em, get every last one of 'em!"

"You've gotta be kidding me! Are you kidding me, kidding me, kidding me, hey, hey, hey!"

Weight enough to shake the ground itself thrashed across the sand sea, and the screams of Courtesan Bears crushed beneath that massive body echoed out. The Courtesan Bears were by no means small themselves, but they were utterly no match for the Sand Earthworm's colossal body, over ten meters long. The overwhelming disparity in mass decided every clash, and one after another, Courtesan Bears turned into corpses.

The scene had become something like a clash of kaiju, and Subaru had no proper words left for it. Emilia and Beatrice's magic, Julius's swordsmanship, Meili's strange gift—barely managing to carve out a path, the carriage and Patrasche pressed steadily onward through the flower bed.

"Aah! The Sand Earthworm—!"

Meili's scream rang out. Looking over, the Sand Earthworm, now swarmed by numerous Courtesan Bears, had claws sunk into its huge body, spilling copious amounts of fluid from all over, and collapsed to the ground. The slick, dripping outer skin of the Sand Earthworm was not something sturdy like that of a snake or an insect. The soft flesh beneath its skin fell easy prey to the hooked claws, and its death throes echoed across the sand sea.

"Next! Next, next! Keep 'em comin'!"

With the huge one down, Meili, face gone pale, clapped her hands. As if drawn by it, the sand heaved up again, and another Sand Earthworm poked its head out. This time, six emerged at once, but every one of them was two sizes smaller than the first, and surrounded by the Courtesan Bear horde, they were hunted down in the blink of an eye.

"At this rate..."

They were being crushed under sheer weight of numbers. Gripped by a bitterly hopeless feeling, Subaru frantically scanned his surroundings for any way to turn the tide. And then, he noticed.

The Watchtower, which had been so distant, now loomed close enough to almost call it right in front of them.

"Just a bit more! If we can just reach the Watchtower like this—"

That wouldn't necessarily make the witchbeasts back off, but it felt like an oddly powerful hope all the same. There was no particular special reasoning behind it in that moment.

He simply felt that if they reached the Watchtower, there would probably be some power there to break this deadlock. A hope clutched at like the last straw. And in fact, that feeling wasn't wrong.

"Just like this—"

Except, that power wasn't the sort of convenient thing one could count on. It was a hammer of judgment, falling impartially on any impudent souls approaching the Pleiades Watchtower.

"—?"

Straining his eyes desperately open, clinging to Patrasche while supporting Beatrice, Subaru glared at the Watchtower visible dead ahead. Sweat trickling down, and in the instant he narrowed his eyes slightly, Subaru felt a faint sense of wrongness.

At the tower's center, something seemed to have flashed.

"What..."

The last syllable never came.

"—————"

—Light streaked across the sky, and, without missing its mark, struck Subaru's head dead-on. In that instant, everything above Natsuki Subaru's neck was blown away by the impact, evaporating before he ever lost consciousness.

"—————"

No one in that place raised their voice at witnessing that instant's carnage. Because both the one who might have seen it, and the one who should have screamed, had been wiped out, root and all.

With a sound, the headless earth dragon collapsed. Caught up in the momentum of the carriage violently overturning, the human body, likewise missing its head, was crushed, snapped, and reduced to shreds of flesh.

Only a pool of blood rolled across the sand, and the dry sand swallowed the flowing blood. And then, slowly, slowly, the fine grains of sand dragged everything that decayed in the sand sea down into its belly, hiding it all away.

Only flowers of blood remained, vivid, as though marking the trace of their journey. But even that crimson bloom vanished, swallowed into the sand that consumes everything.

—And here, the party met its total annihilation.
