Arc 7 Chapter 71: "Water"

—An atrocity that should never have happened: the massacre on the Isle of Sword Slaves, triggered by the activation of the 'Curse Clause.'

In the final moment, it was the cold sharpness of a knife that took Subaru's life. But just before that, the sensation of slowly dying from the extremities inward had eaten away at him without question, working to kill him cruelly.

In the end, Subaru still didn't know why everyone else—the sword slaves, Tanza and the others included, the guards and beast-gladiators who were supposed to be running the island, and even Cecilus—had died in an instant. Why was it that Subaru alone, exceptionally, died later than everyone else?

That was probably a question with no answer, no matter how much he thought about it. There was no one to ask who'd actually answer, and it wasn't worth spending time and brain cells on. More than that, he needed to focus on the information he'd actually managed to pick up for certain.

"That reaction of Todd's... the Curse Clause is the answer."

It had been a coin-flip choice made with a blood-starved brain moments from death, but he'd drawn the right answer. If he hadn't reconsidered back then, and had stayed possessed by the idea that it was Arakiya's magic, he wouldn't have been able to come back with any certainty.

It was precisely because the truth had struck home that Todd decided, in that single instant, to kill Subaru. Because he'd judged Subaru not as a dying child, but as a danger that had to be killed right now. That very speed of Todd's switch was the basis for Subaru's conclusion that it was the Curse Clause.

"But why the Curse Clause?"

Now that he'd ruled out poison gas and had evidence backing the possibility of the Curse Clause, he had no intention of clinging to the idea that the Curse Clause was just a bluff and didn't actually exist. But if that was the case, then Gustav's behavior became far too unnatural.

The basis on which Subaru had judged there was no Curse Clause was that Gustav hadn't inflicted its punishment on Subaru, dangerous as he was, defying the rules of the Isle of Sword Slaves and trying to break its order. Certainly Gustav didn't want to let sword slaves die, but weighing a single Subaru against the order of the whole island, hesitating to use the Curse Clause didn't add up. So, thinking about it the other way—

"—When I provoked him, Gustav-san wanted to use the Curse Clause but couldn't. But when everyone on the island got killed, the conditions for using it were met?"

For now, that was as good a sorting-out of events as he could manage with the information he had.

"...So Gustav-san is an enemy, huh."

Given that the Curse Clause had been used, nine times out of ten Gustav, the island's administrator, was an enemy of Subaru and the others. In other words, the enemies could be counted as three: Todd, Arakiya, and now Gustav added to them. Being able to confirm that Cecilus wasn't an enemy was a gain, but there was no room to be glad about it. That Curse Clause had been a threat that could take even Cecilus's life.

"——"

In exchange for the information he'd gained, bad ingredients kept getting tossed into the pot one after another. Could he strain the contents of that pot, boiling black and roiling, and see through to the bottom? And bad ingredients, as it turned out, had a way of piling on relentlessly. Because—

"Schwarz...!"

"—!"

"This tremor..."

Subaru crouched, pressing a hand to his chest, unwounded but still carrying the shock of having been stabbed. Weitz, who'd been worried about him, startled at the tremor rising up from underfoot. Of course, Subaru already knew this was coming, and knew exactly what the tremor was. He knew, but there was a big problem.

—The grace period before the drawbridge began to move was shorter than it had been on the previous do-over of his 'death.'

If he looked away from what he didn't want to think about, and covered his ears, his spirits wouldn't have to sink. But if his heart turned into a loser that way, there'd be no way left to beat the defeat closing in on him. So no matter how unpleasant the reality he faced, he couldn't afford to shut his eyes and cover his ears.

—Even if the restart point had been pushed back by over a dozen seconds.

"——"

The abnormality that had struck Subaru's Authority of 'Return by Death' was still hard at work causing mischief. Not only was the restart point set somewhere out of character, but this time a slight time discrepancy had occurred too—a shortening of the grace period by a dozen-odd seconds. It was a different kind of abnormality from the previous cases where the grace period had been extremely short—the sort of situation perfectly suited to stoking Subaru's anxiety and despair.

This time it was only a dozen-odd seconds, but there was no guarantee it wouldn't shrink further. If this abnormality kept stacking up again and again, until eventually the time before death fell below a single second, or 'Return by Death' stopped working altogether, what could Subaru even do?

Even if he didn't want to make 'Return by Death' a given, he still had to factor it into any plan he came up with. Now that he'd shrunk, Subaru was even more powerless than before his height had shrunk. This imperial land wasn't kind enough to let him sit back and coddle himself out of self-preservation. So—

"H-hey, Schwarz... are you serious about holing up here?"

"I'm serious. Please, just this once, for this big one, don't chicken out on me, Hiain."

"I ain't chickening out! I ain't... but—!"

His voice shook so badly that anyone listening could tell he was putting on a brave face—Hiain, right beside Subaru, gritted his teeth and hesitated over his words.

Subaru understood why Hiain, frightened, wanted to make him reconsider. If they were found out, it wouldn't end well, and Hiain had no idea why Subaru would go this far. But if he told Hiain honestly what was going to happen, whether or not Hiain believed him, there was no doubt it would cut off any path to his cooperation.

So Subaru had deceived Hiain, getting him to cooperate under a false pretext. That it was to get information Hiain couldn't ignore, even if it meant taking some risks. And that information Hiain couldn't ignore was—

"Damn it, an envoy from the capital coming to talk about the show...? Give me a break...!"

"—. I've asked Weitz to stall for time. We're going to get the full story."

"I know, I know that much...!"

Hiain, thick with impatience and irritation, was worried about the island's shows—the death matches between sword slaves put on display, the very reason Ginunhive existed. Having survived the 'Sparka,' and with an environment set up to make it hard to die even in death matches meant for training between sword slaves, the shows were where the greatest danger to life lay. Subaru had lied about having information on the shows to ask for Hiain's cooperation. And as a result—

"—Come in. Let's hear what you have to say."

Along with that stern voice, the sound of a door opening echoed through the room. Two log-like arms threw the entrance open, and the air grew heavy with the return of the room's master.

"——"

At that entrance Subaru shut his mouth and tapped the shoulder of Hiain beside him. Hiain answered wordlessly too, seeming to steel himself to the fact that there was no backing out now. Naturally. Subaru and Hiain could no longer afford any clumsy moves. Because—

"General Arakiya, and..."

"Todd."

To the words of Gustav, the large figure who'd opened the door, a curt woman's voice answered. There was a hint of a wry smile at that short, unhelpful answer, and—

"Private First Class Todd Fang. For now, at least, until this job is finished."

So said, next, the voice of the man that felt like it could crush Subaru's soul in its grip. Utterly indifferent to how Subaru felt about it, the two who'd been shown in—Todd, and a dark-skinned woman with an eyepatch, Arakiya—stepped into the room. This was Gustav's office. Into the very room Subaru had been summoned to a few days prior for a stern warning, Todd and the others now set foot as envoys from the capital.

"Pretty spartan room, for someone with a position like Governor."

Standing by the reception sofa in the middle of the room, Todd looked around. Certainly, there wasn't much in Gustav's room. It was a room with nothing but a work desk and bookshelf, and whatever necessities befit the name "office." Subaru understood why that would catch someone's eye. But he really wished Todd wouldn't look around. Especially not toward the bookshelf in the corner of the room.

"—. It's an office. Everything this officer needs is here. Is your purpose an inspection?"

"Not at all. I'm just attending General Arakiya. The 'General' here is quite particular about people, so I'm her only subordinate."

"Small. Elite."

"'Elite' is far too generous a rating for me."

Todd shrugged at Gustav, who closed the door and headed to his desk. He kept his expression unchanged even at Arakiya's follow-up remark, and slowly sat down on the sofa. With that, his gaze finally left the bookshelf, and Subaru heaved a huge, huge sigh internally.

It had been an incredibly tense moment. No matter how much he told himself surely not, Todd was the sort who could overwrite even that worst-case fear. So he was genuinely, deeply afraid that he might be noticed after all.

—The presence of Subaru, covered by Hiain camouflaged into the surrounding scenery beside the bookshelf, straining his ears at this very situation.

Having asked Weitz to hold up the arriving dragon carriage, Subaru had used that time to sneak into Gustav's office together with Hiain, camouflaging themselves to hide there—that was the current state of things. The stalling itself had precedent for working, but whether the camouflage would go undetected was something he couldn't know without trying. As for the mechanics of Hiain's camouflage, even though he thought he'd grasped it from the first 'Sparka' and their conversations afterward, understanding and confidence were two different things.

The precision of Hiain's camouflage was affected less by his physical and mental state than by whether he had the nerve to stay perfectly still. If he had the guts to remain rooted in place, once blended into the scenery he was practically invisible. Subaru too, small enough to be held against him, could receive the same effect. But the instant he moved, the camouflaged look of his scales would fall apart completely, leaving him as obviously visible as a child playing a bad game of hide-and-seek.

In that fight against the lion-like beast-gladiator, that very timidity had gotten him killed again and again. It was Hiain's own weak heart that killed off his hard-won strength. —But this time, his camouflage should be one step toward fixing that weakness.

"If we're not found, there's nothing to worry about happening to us. The envoys coming are one of the 'Nine Divine Generals' and a sharp-eyed aide, but as long as your camouflage doesn't waver, we'll be fine. And besides—"

"A-and besides...?"

"—I've got your back."

He'd said that beforehand, before storming into the office, looking Hiain firmly in the eye. Even coming from a cheeky kid with short arms and legs whose voice hadn't even broken yet, it had reached Hiain, whose life and comrades' lives had been saved before. It seemed to have really struck home.

—So this time, for the "first" time, Hiain's camouflage went undetected.

What was about to happen next—the reason Arakiya, bringing her aide Todd, had come to this island to speak with Gustav—was about to be spoken of for the first time.

"General Arakiya, won't you sit?"

"...Standing. Also, can't settle."

"Can't settle?"

"Says there's been a lot going on since coming to the island. Probably because the Dragon's nest is close by."

Even offered a seat, Arakiya shook her head and remained standing. It made for an odd arrangement—the aide Todd sitting on the sofa, with Arakiya standing behind him. Even so, the fact that the picture of Todd with his superior standing behind him didn't strike Subaru as strange was, perhaps, because he'd built Todd up in his mind as something too overwhelmingly powerful and terrifying.

Though, honestly, it would be unreasonable not to be wary of someone who'd come at you to kill the instant Hiain's camouflage so much as flickered—

"If that's fine with you, this officer has no objection. Now then, the purpose of this visit—"

"Getting straight into business is appreciated. We don't want to take up much time either. General, may I hand over the letter?"

"Do as you like."

"Much obliged. Governor, this is a letter from the Chancellor, from the capital."

Leaving Arakiya standing there literally like a piece of furniture, Todd drew a letter from inside his coat and handed it to Gustav. In Gustav's hands, the letter bore a wax seal, and it looked like it carried a proper stamp identifying the sender too. Though Subaru couldn't make out the mark of the stamp itself.

"The Empire's... Chancellor..."

At Todd's words naming who had entrusted him with the letter, Subaru's mouth went dry. If he remembered right, from what he'd heard from that insufferable Abel, the person holding the position of Chancellor was supposed to be one of the ringleaders who'd driven Abel out of the capital. A letter from that villainous Chancellor who'd driven out the ill-tempered Emperor had arrived for Gustav. And what's more, the one who'd carried that letter was that devil of a man, Todd.

Thinking of the massacre that would soon happen on the Isle of Sword Slaves, it was a letter that gave off nothing but bad omens.

"High Earl Gustav Morello, I understand your posting to this island as Governor was by order of His Majesty Emperor Vincent Vollachia."

"That understanding is correct."

"Even if it was His Majesty's order, isn't administering the Isle of Sword Slaves, Ginunhive—such an unpopular job—unbefitting a blood relative of Vollachia's hero, 'Eight Arms' Kurgan?"

As if backing up Subaru's premonition, the air in the room turned sharp and dry. The cause was Todd's blunt way of putting it, which felt almost discourteous. And from his past dealings with Gustav, Subaru knew exactly what angered him the most. What angered Gustav wasn't being spoken ill of himself, but rather—

"—That way of speaking—are you questioning our Emperor's judgment?"

It was disloyalty toward His Majesty the Emperor—that is, Abel, or Vincent—that made him angry.

"——"

At the sharp air Gustav gave off, Arakiya smoothly stepped up to stand beside Todd. Arakiya's face gave nothing away of what she was thinking, but that single action alone conjured the image of Gustav's righteous loyalty being crushed by brute force. However, it was none other than Todd himself who held back Arakiya's show of intimidation with a raised hand and a word: "General."

"That was me speaking out of line just now. The Governor's anger is justified."

"...But I won't let you die. You have a friend's revenge to settle. Right?"

"—. Yeah, of course I do. That's what I'm living for right now."

Even while reining in Arakiya's attitude, a flicker of anguish crossed Todd's face as he answered. At the sight of Todd, called out by Arakiya over a friend's revenge, Subaru—trying not to miss a single word—couldn't help but twist his lips.

Todd and "revenge" seemed like an awfully poor match of words. Not that Subaru knew Todd well enough to say that, but did it mean Todd, too, had people who properly mattered to him? And yet here he was, leaving behind the fiancée he'd been so desperate to see, trying to kill a great many people on this island.

"Sorry for the tactless way I put that, Governor. Both General Arakiya and I have something else weighing on our minds right now. That's why our manner was poor. My apologies."

"...Apology accepted. But loose talk invites disaster."

"Can't argue with that. I feel it keenly, believe me."

Having held Arakiya back, Todd made a bitter face at Gustav's warning. Paying that reaction no mind, Gustav cracked the wax seal with a thick finger, and the letter was opened. The contents of the letter, from the Imperial Chancellor—the one complicit in the current ravaging of the Empire—were—

"—This is..."

"About the true meaning behind my rude question earlier—there was no ill intent. I just wanted to say that if the Governor had grown weary of managing the Isle of Sword Slaves, this would be convenient timing."

As Gustav's eyes ran over the letter, small creases carved themselves into his rock-like face. As if widening a crack in real stone, Todd closed one eye. Leaving the silent Arakiya where she was, Todd went on wearing the face of someone acting as the representative envoy.

"'There is a risk of internal unrest among this island's sword slaves. His Majesty the Emperor wishes them disposed of, all together.'"

Flatly, with all the indifference of someone discussing which day to put out the trash, Todd demanded of Gustav that the sword slaves of the Isle of Sword Slaves—several hundred people—be disposed of.

"——"

That his worst prediction had proven true, and Subaru could still bear it, was purely because he'd already witnessed the actual massacre in which everyone on the island had been killed, and had steeled his heart for it. And that Hiain, blindsided out of nowhere, hadn't screamed was a miracle. Or rather, it would be more accurate to say he was too dumbstruck to even process it.

"——"

It was a remark so lacking in any sense of reality that Hiain forgot even to flinch. Still, even though the camouflage had held by sheer accident, Subaru could do nothing but grind his back teeth. This request was what led to the activation of the Curse Clause that would kill everyone on the island.

Just as he'd feared, Todd, Arakiya, and now Gustav would become enemies. Unless he stopped these three from committing their atrocity, there was no way to prevent that massacre.

"'Risk of internal unrest,' you say?"

"Unrest is unrest—reckless folk who dare to darken His Majesty's glory. That said, the Chancellor doesn't believe Governor Gustav is harboring them. The Governor's loyalty is genuine."

"——"

"Which is exactly why it must be painful. Word is that since the Governor took his post here, the caliber of sword slaves shown off at the events has become incomparable to before."

Even while ordering those very sword slaves' disposal, Todd's manner was one of praising Gustav's efforts. Gustav's expression, eyes lowered to the letter, didn't move. How Todd's words were landing inside him, Subaru—watching the situation with such limited view—couldn't tell.

"—. Reduce needless risk of death, teach the fundamentals of combat, and have them stand shoulder to shoulder rather than undercut one another—then the caliber of the sword slaves who survive rises."

That the running of the Isle of Sword Slaves had been carried out according to Gustav's own philosophy—so he answered quietly, and Subaru thought he could sense something like pride in the tone of his voice. Rather than treating the sword slaves as mere spectacle, Gustav had trained and raised them, remaking the island's rules so as to meet the standards of the Emperor and the Empire's citizens. Probably, none of that included anything soft like attachment or sympathy for the sword slaves themselves. There was simply Gustav's own resolve, as a man placed in the position of Governor.

"Talk, over?"

Arakiya asked, addressing Gustav, who had set the letter down and closed his eyes, all four of his hands resting on the desk. It was a question so tactless, so utterly ignoring the flow of conversation, that if Subaru had been in Gustav's shoes he might have flushed red with anger. But Gustav didn't object, and Todd took it as a given too.

"I've heard about the Curse Clause, so convenient for this kind of disposal. Sounds like you were given a Curse Tool by General Groovy Gumlet of the 'Nine Divine Generals.' Supposed to be able to round everyone up in one sweep, no?"

"It's possible."

"Then this is quick work. Let's wrap it up fast and head back to the capital together. Getting new sword slaves ready will take time too, so let's close Ginunhive for a while—"

Todd spoke with all the weight of putting away classroom supplies at the end of a lesson. That lack of gravity in his tone was getting in the way of restarting Hiain's still-frozen mind. But even if Hiain hadn't rebooted yet, the underlying clock itself seemed about to run out.

If Gustav activated the Curse Clause exactly as the order Todd and the others had brought demanded, it would be total annihilation. He knew that future wasn't going to stop on a whim, so the only way to prevent it was for Subaru to act here and now. But how should he move?

He already knew perfectly well that showing himself with no plan would get him killed instantly. And yet, to take Gustav—who would presumably use the Curse Clause—hostage, Gustav himself looked to be stronger than the two of them, Subaru and Hiain, combined—

"—I refuse."

With that, Gustav refused Todd and the others' demand, and Subaru could ride on that. Ride on it. Ride on it—ride on it how?

"...What?"

Subaru, desperately trying to get his head working, found his thoughts churned up just like Hiain's. In contrast to Subaru, whose thoughts had ground to a halt, Todd's voice was cold and sharp.

Subaru felt the air in the room thicken, viscous, as if it had suddenly gained weight. It was a change of a fundamentally different temperature from when Gustav had grown angry at Todd's way of speaking earlier. Not a matter of heat or humidity—more like the color itself had changed. Wrapped in that air, Todd fixed his gaze on Gustav, whose hands rested on the desk, and—

"Is there some discrepancy between the content of the order and our understanding of it?"

"There is none. The letter states exactly what you've just told me, word for word. The Chancellor's name and seal are both correct, without error."

"Then what exactly is this show of feeling supposed to mean?"

Spreading both hands, Todd questioned Gustav, who had rebuffed his demand. Even Subaru couldn't understand what was happening. Gustav had refused the demand from Todd—from the capital itself—to use the Curse Clause, to wipe out the sword slaves of the Isle of Sword Slaves. As for why, it was—

"—This officer was ordered by His Majesty the Emperor to devote himself to training sword slaves, both body and mind, in preparation for a crisis."

"The Chancellor's seal reflects His Majesty's own will. Are you saying you can't make a judgment because it contradicts an earlier order? This directive is genuine."

"I don't doubt its authenticity. I accept it as the Chancellor's order."

"...Then we're not getting anywhere, are we?"

Frowning, Todd turned on Gustav the look of someone regarding something incomprehensible. Just this once, Subaru agreed with Todd. Grateful as he was that Gustav was brushing off the demand, he had no idea whatsoever what Gustav's true intent was. So, before Subaru and Todd, both caught in the same bewilderment, Gustav continued.

"I received this command directly, in person, from His Majesty."

"Which is exactly why this order rescinds that and tells you to follow the next command..."

"This was at the time I was appointed Governor of this island, by His Majesty's own command."

Cutting off Todd's words, Gustav stated the grounds for his choice. Of the time, several years ago, when he'd taken his post as Governor of the Isle of Sword Slaves. It was—

"His Majesty said this to this officer: '—Even should a command come from me, do not let it override this, my first command.'"

"——"

That was, that was precisely, the very words meant to guide Gustav through this exact situation. Words that had anticipated Gustav being placed in this exact situation—a command that could never have come together without a foresight so precise that "thorough preparation" didn't begin to cover it.

A move laid down for the future, by the Vollachian Emperor, Vincent—no, by Abel.

"Because of a past command from His Majesty, you can't follow His Majesty's present command—is that it?"

"He also told this officer there was no need to know the particulars. This officer agrees. Whether or not I know them, the role I must fulfill doesn't change."

Shaking his head, Gustav answered Todd's question with stubborn resolve. At Gustav maintaining that unyielding stance, Todd narrowed his eyes slightly. That gaze wasn't the look of someone thinking about how to persuade him. After all, Todd had no reason to exhaust his words trying to persuade a stubborn man who wouldn't listen. In other words—

"Arakiya. —Negotiations have broken down."

"—!"

So Todd said, addressing Arakiya in a flat voice. In the next instant, before the fatal moment could arrive, Gustav grabbed the four corners of the desk with his four hands and, hoisting it up, tried to throw it at his two visitors. The thick, powerful muscles of his arms bulged, and a desk that looked like it weighed close to a hundred kilograms was lifted with ease—

"Fwsshh—"

With a laughably half-hearted shout, Arakiya pointed the wooden branch she held at Gustav. An instant later, water erupted from the tip of the branch with tremendous force, blasting away everything from Gustav's neck up—his whole rock-like face—and turning his life into nothing.

There came a heavy sound as the desk, briefly hoisted off its legs, dropped to the floor. A beat later, as if flinging itself bodily onto that desk, Gustav's headless body collapsed. From the wound where his head had been, a great gush of blood came bubbling out, mercilessly staining the office floor. The spreading crimson seemed to insist, almost too simply to be believed, on the reality of Gustav's death.

"Multi-Arm Tribe, huh—no matter how many arms you've got, doesn't make a difference. Should've grown a few extra heads instead."

Looking down at the dead Gustav, Todd cracked his own neck. Then he turned back to Arakiya, tilting his head at her as she stared intently at the tip of her branch and asked, "What's wrong?"

"Don't tell me you're actually surprised by your own power?"

"No. But surprised. ...Just plain water, this much force."

"Ah, that. For hitting a lot of people spread out, fire's better, but for one target, water's the clear choice. Next time, a cup's worth is plenty—just put the water right inside the target's skull."

"Got it. ...Todd?"

Even as he proposed something horrifying, Todd walked over to Gustav's corpse. Arakiya called out to him, brows drooping, as he drew a large knife from inside his coat. Not answering that call, Todd circled around behind the corpse on the desk and, without mercy, plunged the knife into its thick back, slicing it open lengthwise.

It was a merciless technique, as if he were filleting a fish. The blood that had flowed from the neck was already so great that the spatter from the already-dead body was minimal, but even so, drenched in no small amount of blood, Todd dismantled Gustav's corpse.

"——"

Unable to help himself, Subaru looked away from the sight and became aware of his own trembling. Until now, Subaru had thought of Todd as a terrifying enemy, but this was the first time he'd witnessed this kind of grotesque behavior. —Dismantling a human being like an animal.

He'd assumed Todd's violence ran in a straight line, aimed purely at eliminating enemies and threats. If Todd had a hobby of mutilating corpses like this, then had Subaru's own body, after all the times he'd been killed before, been mutilated the same way? He couldn't help but wonder. Thinking that, an unbearable chill of horror and revulsion took hold of his entire body.

Amid sounds so unpleasant he wanted to cover his ears, Subaru couldn't even do that. Even in a scene where every instinct told him to shut down his heart, he couldn't afford to, because he didn't know what might be lying in wait there. Maybe, when the time came to flee from Todd, having a corpse around might draw his attention away. Thinking even such foolish thoughts, if only to keep going—

"—Found it."

"—? Found what?"

Abruptly, that unpleasant wet sound cut off, and the conversation between Todd and Arakiya shifted. Arakiya too had kept her voice low at Todd's grim hobby, but Todd answered breezily, wiping blood spattered on his cheek with the back of his hand, and—

"A Curse Tool, a Curse Tool. Figured a guy like this would have it embedded in his body somewhere."

"A Curse Tool..."

"Something needed to activate the Curse Clause... ah, doesn't matter if you don't get it. Either way—"

As he spoke, what Todd pulled out from inside Gustav's body was a black sphere that appeared to have been embedded right in the center of his large frame. Todd called that sphere, no bigger than a golf ball at most, a Curse Tool. One necessary for activating the Curse Clause, at that. In other words—

"With this, whether the owner lives or dies doesn't matter. —What a stupid thing to have tried."

Toying with the Curse Tool in his hand, Todd spoke to Gustav's corpse, its back split open and its head gone, in a state of utter gruesomeness. There was no pity or sympathy in his voice. Only a flat note of exasperation.

"Thinking you could stand a chance against a monster—that's what's funny."

Todd's muttered remark, head tilted, was probably referring to Arakiya, who'd killed Gustav in a single breath. But to Gustav, who'd lost his life, and to Subaru, present there too, it couldn't help but seem as though that 'monster' referred to Todd himself.

"——"

—And now, that Curse Tool had to be taken from that 'monster,' no matter what.

"Arakiya, give me some water. Once I've washed off the blood, the ones on the island—"

Having cut open Gustav's corpse, Todd's attention turned toward Arakiya, wanting to wash his blood-soaked knife and hands. Setting the Curse Tool down on the desk for a moment, Todd turned toward Arakiya.

—Instinctively, Subaru judged: this was the moment, and no other.

"Ah."

At that word from Arakiya, her mouth rounded in surprise, Todd turned around. His eyes went wide as they found a child, suddenly appearing in the room, reaching for the Curse Tool on the desk.

Hiain's camouflage had done its job to perfection. So much so that neither the meticulous Todd nor the monstrous Arakiya had noticed Subaru and Hiain's presence.

"Hiain!"

The instant his fingers hooked onto the Curse Tool, Subaru called out to Hiain, blended flat into the floor. But his gaze was pointed in a completely different direction—the office entrance. Raising his voice toward it—

"Blow the whole room up!!"

"—! Arakiya!"

Hearing Subaru's urgent shout, Todd instantly called for Arakiya. Arakiya, wary of the door, pressed herself right up beside Todd. So as to be able to respond even if some outrageous attack came slamming in from the door's direction. But—

"—A bluff!"

After one second in which nothing happened, Todd saw through Subaru's aim. But if he could just get that one second, Subaru could accomplish his goal. Grabbing the Curse Tool, and then—

"Woraaaghhh!"

Roaring, Hiain leapt in and grabbed hold of Subaru, who had the Curse Tool in his grip, around the waist. Then, cradling Subaru in his arms, Hiain charged—not toward the entrance, but the opposite direction—the office window. Throwing themselves bodily at it, Subaru and Hiain burst out, smashing through the window frame. At the very instant they burst out—

"Splooosh—"

With a shout every bit as half-hearted as before but power beyond imagining, a flash flood tore through the office. The tremendous pressure of the water blew apart the upper level of the Isle of Sword Slaves, mercilessly sweeping away and scouring the bookshelf that Gustav, the room's former master, had kept so meticulously arranged, along with all its furnishings. But—

"Not here."

Watching the contents of the room pour out one after another through the shattered wall, Arakiya, who'd caused it, stepped onto the soaked floor and murmured. Peering out below, past the ruined office, a strip of white cloth had been tied along the wall, visible from above. So that, gripping it and sliding one's hands along it, one could climb down.

"This..."

"Set up and lying in wait, perfectly prepared, huh. —What was with that brat?"

Beside Arakiya, Todd, looking out past the wall as well, muttered with a hand to his mouth. Narrowing his eyes, looking thoughtful, Todd let out a small breath, and—

"Arakiya, go get the Curse Tool back. You can kill him."

"Got it. What about Todd?"

"You don't need to know what I'm about to do."

Shaking his head, Todd brushed off Arakiya's question like that. But Arakiya didn't dwell on it, leaning out through the broken wall and simply dropping. No tricks, no aid of any kind, straight down several dozen meters.

Seeing Arakiya off as she went to chase the intruders, Todd let out a sigh, and—

"...That was a kid with an unpleasant smell to him."

—he murmured with colorless eyes, and clicked his tongue in irritation.
