Arc 3 Chapter 2: "A Servant's Tea Talk"

Seen off by the coachman, the two of them entered the mansion's entrance hall, where they were greeted by—

"Welcome back, Emilia-sama,"

said Rem, bowing at the waist, all emotion wiped from her well-formed features and all warmth drained from her voice.

The blank expression on her face as she straightened up was one Subaru hadn't seen in quite a while now. Lately, inside the mansion—mostly in front of Subaru, at least—she'd taken to showing smiles (or, one might say, smug faces) more often, which made it clear that what she wore now was a mask put on for outsiders.

The fact that she addressed only Emilia, disregarding Subaru's presence entirely even though he'd come back with her, was further proof of it.

"I'm back. Sorry for being away from the manor. —Looks like we have a visitor?"

"An emissary from the royal capital has come to call. Roswaal-sama is receiving them at the moment, but might I ask you to join them?"

"Of course. It's my problem, after all—I can hardly be left out of it."

Emilia nodded back at Rem, who'd answered her question, and turned her feet toward the stairs leading up. But before that, she turned back to the bewildered Subaru,

"Ah... um, what will you do, Subaru? Can you make it back to your room on your own, properly?"

"I'm not a kid, you know. I mean, is there any chance I could come along too...?"

Subaru made the offer to Emilia, whose tension eased slightly as she tilted her head. But the one who cut off his words wasn't a troubled-looking Emilia—

"Nee-sama is already present at the meeting. There is no need for any further servants to be there. You understand, don't you, Subaru-kun?"

"Ngh..."

Rem's words left no room for argument, and Subaru's throat closed up.

Unable to come up with a rebuttal on the spot, Subaru just stood there, and Emilia raised one hand slightly.

"So that's how it is, sorry. Rem, please show the way."

"Yes. Subaru-kun, please go back to your room."

With her apology tossed out, Emilia didn't look back at Subaru again. Rem delivered the finishing blow to the now aimless-looking Subaru, and, following her lead, Emilia disappeared upstairs, her silver hair swaying.

"Ah, damn it, this sucks."

The moment the two of them vanished from sight, a sigh-laden curse slipped from his mouth.

He'd been left behind. —Both physically and emotionally.

"Sure, it's true that a guy with my pitiful knowledge of this other world wouldn't be much use even if I were there, I get that."

Two weeks had already passed since he'd come to the mansion (though it felt more like a month and a half), and in that time what Subaru had learned amounted to little more than some basic housework and how to read and write. In other words, about on par with a village child helping out around the house.

To think he could involve himself, at that level of ability, in dealings between nobles—let alone the fringes of an exchange that would decide the very top of a nation—was far too grand an ambition. He knew his place, at least that much.

So he could accept being left behind. Not that he had no complaints about it.

"That said, I'm not obedient enough to just go back to my room and sulk myself to sleep."

If he followed Emilia and Rem's suggestion, the sensible thing would be to go back to his room and either go back to sleep or review last night's writing practice. Or, as a second-best option, it wouldn't be bad to go complain to the indoor girl holed up behind whichever mansion door she was hiding behind today.

Not bad, but surely there was more he could do.

"Well, if that's settled, kitchen, kitchen it is."

Having worked out his plan of action, Subaru's feet started moving, his cheeks twisted into a scheming grin.

His destination was, just as he'd said, the kitchen—there, he'd start by brewing a nice hot pot of tea.

"Isn't it boring, waiting outside like this the whole time? Care for a cup, sir?"

The old man seated on the coachman's box looked down at Subaru, who'd returned tray in hand as he spoke, and blinked, his eyes widening slightly in surprise.

The setting was once again outside the mansion, right beside the dragon carriage parked in front of the main gate.

Still a little unnerved, as ever, by the unfamiliar bulk of the lizard, Subaru held out the tea and looked up with a half-smile at the old gentleman's startled face. Then—

"Ah, forgive me. It was a touch unexpected, and I'm afraid I was rude enough to look down on you from above."

The instant he recovered from his initial surprise, the old gentleman leapt lightly down from the coachman's box to the ground. The motion made almost no sound on landing, and Subaru caught his breath faintly.

The coachman's box was high enough up that "leapt down" was the accurate way to describe it. The seat itself was positioned only a bit higher than Subaru's eye level, and getting down from it without support seemed like something you'd have to be braced for a numbing jolt to the soles of your feet to attempt.

But contrary to Subaru's assumption, the old gentleman walked over with unhurried composure, and, bowing gracefully before the proffered tray,

"I'll take you up on the offer, then. My throat was indeed feeling a touch parched."

"Ah, thanks. I didn't know your taste, so I just went with the priciest tea we had, for now."

As Subaru held out the tray, the old gentleman's face creased into a mild, gentle smile. Watching the age-appropriate wrinkles appear around his mouth, Subaru studied him closely as he approached.

He stood a little taller than Subaru, his hair gone entirely white but thick and full, with no hint of thinning. His attire was black formal wear, far too dignified for a mere coachman. His back was ramrod straight, and even through his clothes Subaru could tell that the body beneath was honed to a degree that belied his advanced age.

In short, this was a man who made you tense up just by standing in front of him, whether you liked it or not.

"Well, that just instantly rewrote my Different-World Senior Citizen Rankings. Farewell, Grandpa Rom. Though honestly, I never even got to see you in action."

For someone with a backstory as deep as "member of the Giant race," he sure exited without a single moment to show for it.

Subaru hadn't been able to confirm what became of him afterward, but he couldn't help wondering, just a little, whether Rom hadn't hit his head hard enough to end up even worse off. That was the one small thing that nagged at him.

"—I'm merely an old bag of bones. There's no need to be wary of me."

A gentle voice suddenly addressed Subaru as he mused vaguely on such things.

The old man had taken the cup and, before bringing it to his lips, offered a word. Caught staring rudely, Subaru felt awkward, and the old man laughed at him.

"This is a fine flavor. I imagine you splurged rather considerably on it..."

"No exaggeration at all, it's genuinely the priciest tea we've got. Probably enough that, if the pink-haired maid found out I helped myself to it, she'd fly into a real rage."

For all she looked careless, Ram actually had a surprisingly refined palate when it came to things like tea leaves.

If it came out that he'd used the top-grade tea leaves marked "strictly do not remove" without permission, he could expect a proper lecture waiting for him. Though, from Subaru's point of view—

"I didn't take the tea leaves out of the kitchen—I properly brewed it inside and only carried the tea itself outside... maybe she'll buy that Ikkyu-san logic, huh?"

"Hmm, quite the tactician."

"Unfortunately, whether that piece of sophistry actually works on her is entirely down to luck."

And a bet where he was about seventy percent certain to lose, at that. Even attempting it was foolish.

But this tea-drinking chat was only happening because he'd judged it worth taking on that much risk.

The old man tilted his cup for another sip, and let out a deeply appreciative breath.

"Now then, with this tea as bait, what is it you want from this old bag of bones?"

He asked the question with one eye closed, his expression gauging Subaru.

Subaru's shoulders slumped at how sharp the man was, and he said,

"Hmm. Out of everyone I've met so far, you're the second hardest to deal with, after Roswaal."

"To be put in the same category as Margrave Mathers—I'm honored."

"That means you're basically being ranked alongside a pervert, you know? I said that fully braced to get yelled at, just so you're aware."

He was breezily deflected, that cool expression never wavering, and Subaru once again felt the wind knocked out of his sails. Seasoned, one might call him—this was not a man Subaru, thin on both the depth and breadth of life experience, could hope to match. A completely different breed from a certain large, bald old man he knew.

That being the case, all Subaru could do was raise the white flag.

"You win. —My name's Natsuki Subaru. I'm currently a servant-in-training here at Roswaal Manor. At the very least, I'd like to ask your name."

The most he could manage, being the greenhorn that he was, was to admit as much and then throw himself on the mercy of his elder.

As Subaru bowed his head honestly, the old gentleman's cheeks eased into a faint smile.

"Well, well, how courteous of you. My name is Wilhelm. I currently serve House Karsten, employed in their service, I suppose you could say."

At the old gentleman's introduction of himself as Wilhelm, Subaru rolled the name once over his tongue before saying,

"Thanks for telling me your name. And while we're on the subject of thanks, could you maybe let me in on the reason for today's visit... no, go so far as the actual content of it?"

"As for that matter, I believe the emissary is discussing it inside as we speak."

"Right, but I got told real smoothly that I'm not allowed to participate. It wouldn't be much fun letting the whole event go on without me, so I figured I'd try my own approach."

He understood, both from his first impression and from their conversation so far, that this wasn't a man who'd easily let his tongue slip. But pushing his way boldly into someone's personal space regardless was Subaru's specialty. His extensive experience being isolated for failing to read other people's feelings wasn't for nothing.

Wilhelm was briefly at a loss for words at Subaru's greedy attitude,

"Not flustered when your scheme is seen through, but pressing forward with that outcome already factored in. And when your thinking is seen straight through, rather than showing any shame, you brazen it out instead. —Depending on the company, that's a personality that would earn you nothing but ill will."

On the surface, it was harsh. But Wilhelm's expression as he said it was gentle. Going by the content alone, Subaru's attitude should have been unwelcome to him too, but it seemed the man's largeness of spirit as an elder let it roll right off.

Well then, thought Subaru, and he lifted his face,

"Not even a taste of it, huh?"

"Not knowing what position you hold within the manor, I can't afford to say anything careless. I hope you understand."

Even faced with Subaru's rather thoroughly rude posture, Wilhelm responded courteously, and Subaru threw up his hands. He had absolutely no negotiating skill capable of bending the will of someone this stubborn.

At this rate, all he'd accomplished was fulfilling the conditions for getting yelled at by Ram over sneaking out the expensive tea, Subaru thought, at a loss. Then—

"Though, I could tell from your manner just now that you're on close terms with Emilia-sama, a candidate in the Royal Selection."

"O-o-oh, you saw that? You saw me and Emilia-tan having our lovey-dovey, giddy, blushing little chat?"

"...Tan?"

Wilhelm knit his brows in puzzlement at the odd form of address, then gave a wry smile at how quickly Subaru's mood had flipped.

"You're walking a treacherous road. She may well end up the next Queen of Lugunica, you know."

"Right now she's just an incredibly cute girl, and I'm just a lousy servant, that's all. The future's got endless possibilities—if you only ever talk about maybes, you'll never be able to move at all, you know? Wilhelm-san, did you not marry your wife because you thought, 'what if she's maybe the cutest woman in the world' or something?"

"My wife—"

At Subaru's extreme way of putting it, Wilhelm faltered for just a moment.

But he quickly turned his gaze back to Subaru, a flash of something like admiration lighting his eyes.

"I see, you're quite right. I too believed my wife was the most beautiful woman in the world. It felt as though everyone's eyes were on her. Worrying I might not be a match for her—how pathetic that would have been."

"Right? Rather than hand her over to someone else, even if I don't think I'm worthy, I'll make her mine. Somehow or other keep her within arm's reach, and then work on making myself a match for her, day by day—that's the win-win ideal."

That was more or less how Subaru had lately been picturing the future of his own feelings.

That his sincerity still hadn't reached her heart was surely because Subaru hadn't yet come to possess what it would take to get there.

So then, he'd figure out some way to become that, he thought, for now.

"That's why I want to avoid staying completely hands-off when it comes to Emilia-tan's affairs. She's already walking way out ahead of me, and if I let my guard down she breaks into a jog—so to catch up, I want to use every full-out sprint I can, sure, but shortcuts too, anything I can get, that's just how I am."

"You're a man who moves on some remarkably interesting logic. Truly fascinating. But if you ask more of me than that, I'm afraid it puts me in something of a bind."

He turned his palm toward Subaru apologetically, and yet Wilhelm shook his head.

Without ever letting his sincere demeanor slip, he went on, as if soothing Subaru,

"I am, first and foremost, simply a coachman. I'm not so deeply informed about the circumstances involved. I don't think I can be of use to you."

"I dunno about that. You noticed who Emilia-tan really was even with her hood up, so don't you think 'just a coachman' is a bit of a stretch as excuses go?"

"—"

At Subaru's breezy remark, Wilhelm's expression suddenly went blank, and he fell silent. Watching him out of the corner of his eye, Subaru held up a finger.

"I mean, sure, you could probably make a rough guess just from someone approaching the manor, but that's not the same as being certain, right? But Wilhelm-san, you spotted Emilia-tan right away."

"Perhaps I'd simply met Emilia-sama on some previous occasion."

"If that were true, Emilia-tan would've said hello, wouldn't she? Emilia-tan, who loves explaining stuff, not introducing an acquaintance to me standing right next to her—hard to imagine. And besides, the moment you identified hooded Emilia-tan as Emilia-tan in the first place, the whole 'just a coachman' premise gets pretty shaky, don't you think?"

A silent look demanded an explanation from Subaru. Subaru waved a hand, saying, "No, no,"

"Apparently the robe Emilia-tan was wearing has some intricate spell woven into it, and it seems to have this effect that messes with people's perception of her or something? Unless Emilia-tan gives permission, or you're someone capable of breaking through that effect, you apparently can't even see her as herself."

Come to think of it, she'd been wearing that same robe the very first time they'd met, back in the royal capital.

By now Subaru understood, more or less, that it was meant to avoid the trouble her lineage—most likely, the fact that she was a Half-Elf—might otherwise invite.

Enough, at least, to sense the surface of just how brutal a handicap she carried, living in this world as a silver-haired Half-Elf.

Unaware of Subaru's private musings, Wilhelm let his shoulders sag, as if resigned by the explanation. He gave a weak shake of his head, murmuring, "Well, well," in surprise.

"To think you'd already been sizing me up from the very start... you're quite the ill-natured one yourself."

"It's not like I'm that clever, though. I just happened to think 'huh, wait, that's odd' while I was brewing the tea back at the manor. Pure coincidence, coincidence."

Still, that he'd been able to grab hold of that thread at all was thanks to Subaru's own resolve—to make sure Emilia never came to harm.

Between everything and the incident with the Witchbeast, Subaru had been made painfully aware of just how full of danger this world was. Doing what little he could—drops of water on a hot stone, however futile—to keep those fangs even slightly further from her was the very most Subaru could manage to show his regard, for now.

So he'd grab hold of anything worth grabbing, and confirm anything worth confirming.

Grinning in that flippant, easygoing way of his, Subaru had no intention of letting Wilhelm off the hook. The moment the old gentleman had accepted that first cup of tea, he'd already been dragged into Subaru's own little pocket dimension.

A convenient little Subaru-verse that ignored the other party's position and circumstances entirely, and shoved only Subaru's own convenience onto them.

"So 'just a coachman' won't fly as an excuse, then. ...As you've guessed, I am indeed connected to the Royal Selection—no, perhaps I should say connected to someone connected to it."

"Connected to... someone connected..."

Wilhelm had effectively been talked into a corner. To fulfill his obligations as the loser, he began speaking slowly, choosing his words with care.

Chewing over the substance of it, Subaru worked through the meaning in his head, then said,

"So basically, position-wise, you're in a spot kind of like mine?"

"That my reasons aren't born of infatuation would be the difference between you and me, I suppose."

"Well, sure, if you've got the most beautiful wife in the world, you're not going to be tempted to cheat, are you. Though I do think Emilia-tan's got her beat on cuteness."

"No, my wife should surpass her in cuteness as well."

What Subaru had meant as teasing got firmly rebutted, and he couldn't help but flinch back.

Twisting the corner of his mouth into a grin, Subaru glared at Wilhelm, who'd declared it so flatly,

"You're surprisingly stubborn about weird stuff, Wilhelm-san. ...You're more playful than I figured."

"If you'd take it as an old man's pride, refusing to be talked down by his junior, I'd be glad."

That aside, it had sounded pretty serious for pride's sake, Subaru wanted to point out, but he held it back and turned his mind to drawing out something even more substantial.

He felt like if he could just hook the man somehow, appeal cheaply to his sense of human feeling, it might just work—

"—It seems, however, that time's run out."

"Huh?"

A dumb sound escaped Subaru. In response, Wilhelm pressed his lips together in silence and gestured toward the mansion with his hand.

Turning to follow the gesture, Subaru saw that, in the distance, the mansion's front door had opened.

"Coming out was Rem and... who?"

Emerging from beyond the double doors was the familiar blue-haired maid. And, accompanying her, an unfamiliar figure.

Given the flow of the conversation and Wilhelm's manner, that person was presumably the very emissary they'd been discussing.

"How should I put it—this is like, straight-up fantasy, huh?"

That impression slipped out of his mouth unbidden, maybe because this person's appearance carried an air far too unsuited to the word "emissary."

Noticing Subaru's stare fixed on them, the figure broke into a mischievous smile and came striding straight toward him—toward the dragon carriage—

"Hey now, hey now. Just because there's a beauty around doesn't mean it's polite to stare like that, y'know."

The instant they stood right in front of Subaru, they leveled a pale finger at him and declared as much, complete with a one-eyed wink.

The figure who was presumably the emissary was a lovely-faced girl with flaxen hair trimmed to a medium length.

She was tall for a woman, about level with Subaru. But her build was slender in a way that made comparison pointless, delicate, and every single gesture carried a femininity—or rather, a sparkle you might better call girlishness.

Her flaxen hair was decorated with a white ribbon, and with her large eyes shining bright with curiosity, she carried a charm almost cat-like—and, sure enough, atop her head was—

"Finally, actual contact—cat ears that aren't just background extras."

"Nya-nya?"

Twitching as if in reply to his mutter were the beast ears, the same color as her hair, asserting their presence atop her head. In a world where Demi-Humans were simply a given, Subaru had never had the chance to interact with any Demi-Human beyond what he'd seen in the royal capital, but seeing the real thing like this was overwhelming.

After all—

"The soul in me that lives to pet fluffy things won't stop craving the being right in front of me... Damn it, settle down, my right arm...!"

Even from a glance, he could practically imagine the fluffy, downy feel of those cat ears, and her mere presence in front of him was practically deadly poison.

With a face set for a desperate struggle, he held down his trembling right arm and somehow managed to back away, putting distance between them.

She watched him off with a blank, bewildered look at his suspicious behavior.

"Aw, huh, did I get myself hated already? Ferri-chan, fail~"

The girl gave herself a playful little bonk on the head with her fist, sticking out her tongue.

A different kind of shudder ran through Subaru at the gesture, his throat freezing in astonishment.

—A knock on her own head followed by a tongue-out wink!?

It was a classic, worn-out, legendary "cutesy act" pattern so old-fashioned it was rarely even seen back in Subaru's own world anymore. Something so calculatedly cute that he'd sworn to himself he'd smack anyone who actually pulled it off for real, someday—

"But faced with the real thing, its destructive power is too high, I can't do a damn thing...!"

He meant it two ways: that its sheer calculatedness robbed him of even the will to lash out at it, and that, coming from someone who could pull it off, it suited her far too well and was far too cute to touch. The two meanings overlapped, and as a result, all Subaru could do was let it pass.

Leaving Subaru trembling with that thoroughly misdirected shudder to himself, the girl bowed silently, then turned to Wilhelm, who'd come to receive her,

"I'm back, Grandpa Vil. Sorry for making you wait outside. Bet you were bored, huh?"

"Not at all. This young man kept an old bag of bones company in conversation, so I've had a more enjoyable time than expected."

"Fumyu?"

At Wilhelm's way of dressing up their earlier exchange, the girl reacted sharply, pressing a finger to her cheek. Her cat-like pupils narrowed, and her mouth seemed to take on something like a deformed little ω shape. Then—

"Ohh-ohh-ohh, I see, I see. Right, right, so you're the boy Emilia-sama was talking about. Ah-hah, I get it, I get it."

The girl looked Subaru up and down as if devouring him with her eyes, then clapped her hands as though satisfied. Under that gaze, Subaru instinctively covered his body with his hands, twisting to escape her stare,

"Hey, quit it. Just because I'm a guy doesn't mean it's not embarrassing to be stared at like that."

"Don't say that, honey. You've got a nice body, not much wasted flesh on you at all. If you didn't already belong to somebody, I'd almost want a taste."

As Subaru hammed it up, the girl played right along, wiggling her fingers at him.

Normally, this was exactly the kind of giggly back-and-forth with a girl that would have Subaru hitting his stride. But—

"For some reason, I'm oddly not feeling it at all. What is this... some kind of physiological thing?"

It wasn't dislike exactly, but Subaru's instincts had been loudly insisting something ever since a moment ago.

That the mischievous-eyed girl standing before him was, unmistakably, a natural enemy to a person named Natsuki Subaru.

A slug to a snake. A frog to a slug. A snake to a frog. Between Subaru and her, the compatibility was such that Subaru would always be the weaker party and she the stronger—he grasped that instinctively.

So none of Subaru's usual context-blind antics were forthcoming from his side. But in contrast to a Subaru who'd completely lost his footing, the girl kept coming at him, pedal to the floor.

Specifically, she reached out her hand, gently wound her arm around Subaru's neck, and pulled him into an embrace.

"Wha, huh, huh!?"

"Don't move. I'm checking something right now."

Since they were nearly the same height, her face, pressed close in the embrace, ended up right beside Subaru's own. Her voice was whispered right by his ear, driving a ticklish sensation through his entire body.

Subaru's face reddened at the feel of the embrace. But his expression, sweeping around as if searching for help, spotted Rem standing near the mansion's entrance, and he went pale all at once.

Even the professional blank expression Rem had been wearing for work had, he could tell even from a distance, lost yet more warmth.

As Subaru's mouth flapped open and shut helplessly, Rem suddenly raised both hands and made some kind of small, deliberate gesture.

There'd been no prior agreement about any kind of signal between them, and yet Subaru understood with perfect clarity exactly what her declaration of intent meant.

'—I'm telling on you to Emilia-sama.'

"Stooop it—!!"

His body moved on instinctive judgment, and Subaru somehow managed to pry the clinging girl off of him. Though it wasn't a rough shove or anything—he just grabbed her shoulders and put distance between them. If anything, it was Subaru who ended up staggering backward and landing on his rear.

Unable to process, all at once, the whirlwind of feelings from the last ten-odd seconds, Subaru, sitting sideways on the ground, pressed his sleeve mournfully to the corner of his eye, saying,

"Why does this keep happening, no matter what I actually want... and here I am, devoted to Emilia-tan and no one else."

Subaru lamented reality while faking tears. Then his shoulder was suddenly, gently patted. Looking up, he found it was Wilhelm resting a hand on his shoulder,

"I sympathize."

he murmured kindly, a white handkerchief clutched in his hand. Subaru pressed the offered cloth to his eyes, saying "thanks" as he got to his feet.

An adult's capacity for warmth—witnessing it firsthand, he'd felt an unbidden urge to cling to it, but Subaru barked a mental reprimand at his own weakness and reined it back in.

"I mean, that guy's got a wife he loves more than anyone else—and there, with that little act, his feelings had finished sorting themselves out. So, can I ask what that was just now?"

Returning the handkerchief, which hadn't actually wiped away anything, to Wilhelm, Subaru turned to the girl and asked for an explanation of what she'd just done.

At his question, the girl pursed her lips thoughtfully,

"—Just like I heard, the flow of water inside your body's all stagnant. I'd like to do something about it, but there's no time, so no can do for now, I guess."

Leaving that suggestive remark hanging, the girl resolved the matter to her own satisfaction.

Before Subaru could press her on what that was even supposed to mean, she stretched her hand toward the sky, turned around, and said,

"Well then, let's hurry back to beloved Crusch-sama, Grandpa Vil. Stay away too long and there's no telling what she might do."

"—As you say."

Wilhelm followed, with few words, the girl's brisk instructions.

He gave Subaru one final bow, set the now-empty cup back onto the tray that had fallen to the ground, and said,

"Thank you for the meal. Well then, Subaru-dono, take care of yourself."

With a light leap, Wilhelm bounded lithely back up onto the coachman's box, taking the reins that guided the lizard in hand.

As Subaru watched his motions off in wordless silence, this time it was the girl who pressed her hands together as if in prayer,

"Well then, we haven't even properly said hello yet, but I'm busy too, so sorry about that."

Leaving behind a well-practiced wink, the girl turned her body toward the dragon carriage. Subaru instinctively reached a hand toward her back, trying to voice the question flitting through his mind, but—

"Wait, hold on. I've got a mountain of things I want to ask..."

"I think you'd do better just asking Emilia-sama honestly. Well then, if fate wills it, let's meet again in the royal capital. Bye-bye."

Cutting off the question flat, the girl left a smile behind and disappeared into the carriage.

Understanding he'd been swept up entirely in her pace, Subaru withdrew his hand with regret. Wilhelm left him with a "Well then," and the carriage set off with a cry from the lizard.

The carriage frame creaked, and the wheels began to turn.

The lizard planted its feet firmly against the earth a few times to push off—and the acceleration came immediately after.

Once the wheels began moving forward and the frame started to shift, everything after was swift.

The dragon carriage picked up speed before his very eyes, surging over the road and vanishing into the distance all at once, kicking up a cloud of dust.

In the end, all that was left behind on the spot was Subaru, a thoroughly outmaneuvered loser, and nothing but the fading scent of expensive tea that had gone almost entirely to waste.

Meanwhile, aboard the dragon carriage drawing away from Roswaal Manor.

"—Were you able to fulfill your duty as emissary?"

"Of course. There's no way Ferri-chan would ever fail something Crusch-sama asked of her. Honestly, Grandpa Vil, you worry too much."

The exchange took place up on the coachman's box, where the carriage was being driven.

Wilhelm sat on the box, guiding the lizard without difficulty. Directly behind him, the flaxen-haired girl had her face poked out through the window of the compartment the lizard was pulling.

The dragon carriage should have been traveling at quite some speed, but, cloaked as it was in the Divine Protection of wind, it was unaffected by the strong wind, and there was no worry of their conversation being drowned out by noise.

In a way, there might be no condition better suited to a private, secret exchange.

At the girl's reply, Wilhelm gave a wry smile and answered, "Then it was needless worry on my part." At that, the girl came back with, "More importantly,"

"What surprised me more was that Grandpa Vil was actually talking with someone while he waited. I mean, Grandpa Vil hates talking with people, right?"

"That's quite the outrageous misunderstanding."

At the immediate denial, the girl waved a hand, saying, "Ah, no, no,"

"You're right, sorry, sorry. —It's just that you like cutting people down more than talking to them, huh."

"...That's a terrible misunderstanding too."

At the girl's teasing words, Wilhelm, however, offered nothing further.

Dissatisfied with how little reaction his provocative remark drew, the girl pouted,

"Boring. Is talking with Ferri-chan less fun than talking with that boy just now? Did you really take such a liking to him?"

Her voice carried the sulk of a child not getting enough attention from a parent.

As if trying to coax more of a reaction out of the sparingly responsive Wilhelm, the girl leaned even further out the window, bringing her body closer to him,

"I didn't feel anything special about him, but did something strike a chord with you, Grandpa Vil? Like, he seems like nothing much but he's actually crazy strong, and you caught a glimpse of that talent!"

"No. My assessment of him doesn't differ from yours by much. He's an amateur—a green amateur, wet behind the ears. Nor does he possess any talent striking enough to catch the eye. There's no mistaking that he's an utterly ordinary sort."

"Then why? Trash like that is exactly the sort of thing you hate most, isn't it, Grandpa Vil?"

The girl seemed determined, at every turn, to paint Wilhelm as some kind of broken personality. At her words, Wilhelm didn't even manage a wry smile, and quietly raised a hand to point at his own eyes,

"His eyes."

"—His eyes?"

At the girl's questioning echo, Wilhelm dipped his chin and simply lifted his gaze, as if remembering,

"That boy's eyes caught my attention, just a little. Those are the eyes of one who has stepped into the realm of death several times over. There are those who turn back at the last instant and return. But..."

He cut himself off, and Wilhelm quietly closed his eyes,

"I know of no one who returns from the realm of death not once, but several times over. That, I suppose, is what drew my interest."

"Hmm, I don't really get it."

But the girl cut clean through Wilhelm's admiration-tinged words with a simple admission of incomprehension.

This time Wilhelm did manage a wry smile, but the girl went on, "Still,"

"Whatever Grandpa Vil's words just now meant, that boy definitely won't get to walk any smooth, easy road, will he."

The girl too narrowed her eyes, as if recalling something, and then fixed her gaze steadily on the broad back seated up on the coachman's box,

"Being taken a liking to by the 'Sword Devil,' Wilhelm van Astrea, is a misfortune no different from being bewitched by a Witch, after all."
