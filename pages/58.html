Arc 2 Chapter 34: "The Witch, the Spirit, and the Mage"

—Satella, the Witch of Envy.

A supernatural being feared by everything in the world, who once wiped out the witches who had made the world howl in grief, then destroyed half the world with the power she seized from them. Her body was sealed inside a crystal by a hero's hand, and even now, unable to be destroyed, she remains sealed away, or so it's said.

An absurd, far-fetched story, Subaru thought, with the sensibilities of a modern kid. Measured against the common sense of Subaru's original world, it was simply unbelievable. If it were just that her evil deeds had been passed down through legend for hundreds of years, fine, but the idea that such a being was still slumbering somewhere in the world even now—

"Well, this is the kind of era where people don't know the president's name but know the most popular member of the national idol group, so maybe common sense isn't much of a yardstick."

As for Subaru, he'd been the type utterly uninterested in either—a prime example of a bland, unmoved existence in his old life.

Mulling over that thought, he ran back through what Beatrice had told him, propping his chin on his hand as he glanced beside him. In the garden where the morning sun poured in, there was Emilia, sitting on the green grass, faint lights drifting around her.

No matter how many times he saw it repeated, it lost none of its dreamlike charm—one of the finest sights this world had to offer. Emilia's morning routine.

Not wanting to disturb her, Subaru scratched his head a little way off, and in his butler's uniform, swallowed back a rising yawn with a soft exhale.

A night had passed since his talk with Beatrice in the forbidden archive, and now morning of the third day had arrived. Having powered through yesterday afternoon by sleeping like the dead, Subaru's body was, physically speaking, in prime condition. From the dead of night into the early morning, he'd had a mountain of things he wanted to ask Beatrice, but—

"It's bad for your skin, so it's time you went to sleep, I suppose. Honestly, it's your fault I've stayed up this late in the first place, isn't it, I suppose."

—the girl had snapped at him, veins practically bulging, and thrown him out of the archive. Thinking back on it, she was always in that room, but where in that room did she actually sleep? Setting aside the bookshelves, there was only Beatrice's usual perch on the stepladder, and the one desk Subaru occasionally sat at. Nothing else notable in the place. Surely, given her personality, she wasn't sleeping directly on the floor.

"Does she even have a room somewhere? Even so, I've never once run into her. Not that I'm expecting some lucky perverted encounter with a little girl like that or anything."

If she was keeping it a secret, that was no fun. Next time, if the chance came up, he'd secretly tail Beatrice for a day. Once he was out of the loop, once life at the mansion could go on peacefully.

—Another reason to keep pushing forward, and Subaru's lips loosened into a smile. And then—

"You're making a much better face these days, nya."

A voice called out to him, and when he raised his head, there floated a little gray ball of fur right in front of him. Or rather, a small cat with a pink nose. Puck. He was making the gesture of washing his face with his short paws, the way ordinary cats do.

"Honestly, yesterday I could barely stand to watch. I'm a little relieved now."

"Yeah, sorry for worrying you. But I'm still nowhere near recovered, this naive heart of mine. I need you to comfort it. Specifically, by way of pet pet pet pet."

"Well, if that's the extent of the bravado, I think you'll be fine. Good. That means Lia's lending you her lap wasn't wasted."

Subaru pulled the palm-sized cat close and indulged fully in his fluffiness. Last time he'd discovered the wonders of the ears; this time his focus was the tail. He noticed the fluff-rank was especially high near the tip and the base. Savoring the supreme sensation under his fingertips, nose practically drooping from the fluffiness, Subaru met those black eyes and asked,

"Hey, did you happen to see that lap pillow thing too?"

"For something that went on that long? Yeah. Sitting seiza for hours must be rough, and I offered to take a shift a few times, but... you can rest easy. Lia never handed the job off to the end."

At Puck's seal of approval, Subaru couldn't help flushing red with embarrassment. Seeing that reaction, Puck—

"Ei."

"Ow—! Why'd I just get clawed!?"

"As Lia's mother, so to speak, I found it heartwarming. But as her father, so to speak, I felt a discomfort toward you that's hard to put into words."

"You're torn between your mom-heart and your dad-heart? You're complicated too, huh!"

"It's hard on a parent, having a cute daughter. Oh, and speaking of which, could you scoot a little further away from Lia? Little more. There."

"That's blatant sabotage! This distance is a line I won't budge on! The fact that I can't quite reach even if I stretch out my hand—that's the elegant part, please!"

Bowing and scraping, Subaru somehow won a pardon and managed to preserve the status quo. Then he turned to look at Emilia again. She seemed absorbed in conversation with the spirits, apparently unaware of the exchange between the one man and one cat busy wrecking the morning quiet. Watching her profile—calm, and every so often startlingly beautiful when she smiled—Subaru murmured under his breath, just moving his lips.

"—A silver-haired half-elf, huh."

One of the details Beatrice had told him about the Witch of Envy's origins. Someone whose birth shared common ground with the witch who had shaken the world, who was feared by everyone alive. How heavy a burden that must be to carry through life. Subaru, with no personal frame of reference for such a feeling, couldn't begin to imagine it. But he could sense it hadn't been an easy road, not by any measure.

And yet, she had grown up into someone this honest and forthright. A way of living as a flower that never lost its delicate charm—if she'd managed that, it was probably because,

"Either she was blessed with an unusually good environment. Or maybe..."

"It's because she had a good parent raising her. Fufu-n."

Puck laughed, hands on his hips, puffing out his chest, stroking at his whiskers. A cat who could read emotions. Combined with Subaru's mutterings just now, he'd clearly grasped exactly what Subaru had been thinking.

Beaten to the punch, Subaru fell silent, and Puck, still floating in midair, began spinning in slow vertical rotations.

"Well, a lot of it comes down to that girl's own nature, born and bred. I'm not going to tell you her whole story, but she's had it ten thousand times harder than you're imagining. —Still, that's exactly why she's so precious to me, going on the way she does."

No rebuttal came to mind, not even a fragment of one. It was a fact that Subaru didn't know Emilia, and the days he didn't know about were days Puck had shared with her. And whatever Subaru's imagination could reach was, at the very end of the day, only the limit of imagination belonging to someone who'd been steeped his whole life in lukewarm water—while reality came crashing in, blithely vaulting over anything he could picture. That's how powerless people become, tossed about by fate. Subaru had been made to feel that keenly, again and again, since coming to this world—and surely Emilia had tasted it more times than he could count as well.

"Hey, Puck, do you know about the Witch of Envy?"

"There isn't much I don't know."

"Well then, I wanted to ask... when would someone pose as the Witch of Envy? No, wait, 'pose as' makes it sound bad. Maybe I should say, when would someone borrow the Witch of Envy's name?"

What surfaced in his mind was what had happened on the very first day of the summons, back at the start of the loop. The very first, first thing. He looked back on that moment—when Emilia, to a still-ignorant Subaru, had falsely given her name as "Satella." He had a vague guess as to what her true intentions had been there, but Subaru wanted someone else to confirm it for him. And it would be even better if that someone was none other than the person who understood her best.

Not grasping the intent behind Subaru's question, Puck was silent for a while before slowly opening his mouth.

"Either way, I'd call it reckless to the point of not caring whether you live or die, wouldn't you? There's no shortage of people who still carry grudges and resentment toward the witch, and the fear and despair carved into their hearts hasn't faded either. To claim the witch's name under those circumstances—I can't call it anything but out of your mind, nya."

"That's it. That's what I needed."

"?"

Puck cocked his head in confusion at the finger suddenly pointed at him. Leaving him behind, Subaru pumped his fist internally, having gotten from Puck an opinion that confirmed his own theory. Yes—at that point in time, claiming to be the witch would naturally get you branded as out of your mind. It just hadn't turned out that way because Subaru happened to be abnormally lacking in common sense himself. Normally, that's exactly what would have happened. So then, why had Emilia known that would happen and given that name anyway?

"If you scare someone off by making them think you're crazy, you don't have to drag them into something as dangerous as hunting for that insignia, huh."

She'd meant to push him away, he figured. That must have been her thinking. Emilia's intentions in arranging things that way had already vanished into some other dimension, and Subaru was the only one who knew that exchange had even taken place. And even Subaru would never again have the chance to go ask her what she'd really meant by it. Still, he had no choice but to believe that was it. And believing it, he found he'd been given enough by her to trust it fully as the answer.

In short—

"Emilia-tan is seriously a Bodhisattva of Mercy."

"Don't go lumping people together with things you don't even understand properly."

Subaru, having transcended dimensions to become certain of Emilia's maternal saintliness, found the woman herself approaching with a wry smile. She seemed to have already finished her chat with the spirits—the faint lights that had been drifting around her were gone. In their place, Puck, who until just now had been swimming about at Subaru's side, climbed onto her shoulder.

"Home base, home base. This really is the most comfortable spot after all."

"Yes, yes, welcome back. Sorry, as always. But when you're around, it makes the other spirit kids shy."

"Making spirits shy around me is beyond an honor, it's downright unfair. It's basically going to give people the mistaken impression that Puck's some big shot."

"Fufu-n, I am a big shot, believe it or not. I demand even more reverence! Specifically, how about dried sardines as tribute, nya?"

"Straight-up cat behavior..."

Puck began bobbing his head Peekaboo-style. Emilia stroked his gray head with a finger, then turned to face Subaru properly once more.

Conscious of her beauty standing right in front of him, Subaru realized he was almost instinctively looking away from her.

He felt awkward. Of course he did. After all, he'd had his head in her lap, sobbed his eyes out, been stroked on the head for hours, and then they'd parted. Only a single night had passed since then. What face was he even supposed to wear to see her—honestly, he wanted to hug a pillow and roll around on his bed agonizing over it. That he'd come to see her anyway showed just how serious his condition was. Severe Emilia-addiction. He could no longer live without her.

"Um, this feels a little embarrassing somehow. ...Are you feeling all right? Physically, I mean?"

"I had some withdrawal symptoms, but they've just now been cured, so I'm perfect. What about you, Emilia-tan... um, well. For everything, I'm so—"

He started to voice an apology, faltered, swallowed the words, and rephrased.

"Thanks for everything. My head and heart were a foggy labyrinth, but I think—maybe—it's gotten a little better."

"You don't seem fully over it, but the fact that you're at least trying to get over it is progress. Yeah, if I helped even a little, that's good enough for me. If you ever feel like you're about to fray again, come tell me. Big sister will be nice to you."

She put a hand to her chest, laughing as if teasing him a little. That, too, was probably her consideration, meant to lighten Subaru's guilt. Watching her face—looking so pleased at playing the older woman—it almost seemed like she genuinely meant it, and that thought wavered a little in his mind.

Anyway—

"As long as you're doing well, that's what matters. You've got work to do today too, right? You went to sleep at a weird hour—not feeling sleepy at all?"

"No worries there. A shut-in like me basically sleeps during the day and stays up at night as a rule. Lately I've been a little too healthy about it."

"You say that sometimes, but what exactly is a 'shut-in'?"

"Someone who lights a charcoal brazier in their room and gambles on whether a pink-haired mage will come diving in to save them."

That, frankly, was the kind of stunt pulled by people a whole tier higher on the soul-level scale. At the strangeness of what Subaru was describing, Emilia nodded, looking a bit taken aback. Watching her reaction, Subaru suddenly had a thought.

"Speaking of mage—you're one too, aren't you, Emilia-tan? What kind of magic can you use?"

Asking "what kind of magic can you use" was the kind of talk that could only ever happen in another world. Subaru almost laughed at himself for saying something so unexpectedly fantastical. But Emilia shook her head at the question.

"Strictly speaking, I'm not a mage. My case, like my Contract with Puck, makes me a Spirit Arts user. What I use isn't magic, it's Spiritual Arts. The principle is almost the same, though."

"A mage and a Spirit Arts user are different?"

He tilted his head and looked at Puck, sitting on her shoulder. Noticing the conversation had turned to him, the little cat fiddled with the fur near his belly.

"A mage uses the mana inside their own body to cast magic. A Spirit Arts user, on the other hand, uses mana from the surrounding air to work their arts. The results you can produce are similar, but the path you take to get there is quite different."

"So what kind of difference does that actually make, professor?"

He raised his hand, tossing the question to instructor Puck. The cat straightened up, cheeks loosening as if pleased,

"It comes down to whether or not you use a Gate, so this difference is actually pretty huge. For a mage, the size and number of Gates directly relates to their aptitude as a mage. But for a Spirit Arts user, that hardly matters. Since they draw on outside mana independent of the Gate."

"So you take in mana from outside through the Gate, store it inside, and use that stored mana to cast magic. And the Spirit Arts users don't have that."

Chewing over Puck's explanation, Subaru tilted his head at a "hm?" that occurred to him partway through.

"Wait, wait, that would make Spirit Arts users way too one-sidedly advantaged. I mean, mages have fuel stored internally, but Spirit Arts users can bring in fuel from outside as much as they want... unlimited MP wouldn't make for a fair fight at all."

"Quick on the uptake. But it doesn't work out that conveniently. The mana filling the atmosphere isn't infinite, and..."

He trailed off, and Puck lifted his gaze to look at Emilia. Receiving that look, she picked up where he left off.

"How powerful a Spirit Arts user's arts can be depends on the strength of the spirit they command. And to begin with, forming a Contract with a spirit isn't something that happens easily, so I don't think it's really a matter of which is superior, mage or Spirit Arts user."

"Hmm... but if you've got a Contract with a really powerful spirit, that's pretty great, right? Emilia-tan, isn't Puck actually kind of amazing?"

"Well, I won't deny I rank pretty high up there, nya."

"You act all mellow, but when it counts you say what you mean, huh... You really don't waver when it comes to standing your ground."

Subaru had some opinions of his own about strong egos, but even he was taken aback by how unshaken Puck was. Probably a difference in years of experience. Between a green kid and a Great Spirit, the years cultivated were simply on a different scale.

Despite that, the Great Spirit seemed unused to being praised, grinning bashfully, looking rather pleased about it. Subaru pointed at him.

"Now that I think of it, what kind of spirit even is Puck? At the Loot House you were shooting out ice left and right, but... as I recall, there wasn't an 'ice' attribute to begin with, was there?"

Back in the bathhouse, when Roswaal had lectured him, what Subaru had heard about were the standard four—fire, water, earth, wind—plus the ominous yin-yang system on top of that. The one who answered Subaru's question was Emilia, who'd been stroking the head of the bashful Puck. She turned her right palm upward.

"Ice, but actually, that's fire mana. Fire is mana primarily tied to heat, so both hot and cold get broadly classified under fire in the grand scheme."

A cracking sound split the air, and an icicle appeared just above her palm. An icicle about the size of a traffic cone, its sharp tip aimed his way, began spinning like a drill.

"Even if there's no malice behind it, that's terrifying!? If I had a fear of sharp points I'd have wet myself just now!"

"Oh, sorry. I was trying to explain it clearly and I just... it was fine making it, but then I didn't know what to do with it."

"The fact that 'threaten someone for no reason' is where your mind went is the scary part! I like Emilia-tan being demure and lovely!"

"Yes, yes."

With a dismissive murmur of agreement, Emilia clapped her hands and the icicle dissolved into mist. Not melting or shattering—it literally scattered into something like powder and dispersed. A strange mechanism indeed, but that fantastical quality was exactly what kept tugging at Subaru's curiosity.

Maybe that itch showed on his face. Watching the suddenly quiet Subaru, Puck spoke up out of nowhere.

"Hm, could it be you want to try using magic?"

"Can I!? Me!? If I could, then—Ultimate Annihilation Spell—"

"No, you can't. Whether it's magic or Spirit Arts, you can't skip the basics. Magic wasn't built in a day."

Puck folded his arms, striking down Subaru's rearing curiosity from straight above. Subaru slumped, his momentum broken. Perhaps feeling sorry for him, Puck added a "but" while fiddling with his whiskers,

"I could probably let you have a simple taste of it, though?"

"Meaning what, exactly?"

"Basically, since you want to use magic, either Lia or I just need to assist you. We'd use the mana in Subaru's own body and cast the magic through Subaru. To us, it's just a question of whether the mana used comes from the air or from Subaru—the magic itself would come out through Subaru's Gate. How about it?"

"Hold on, Puck, don't go making promises so easily. It could be dangerous."

Emilia scolded her partner, tempted as he was by the sweet allure of the idea. She seemed to be genuinely worried about the safety of the whole thing, but—

"Sorry, Emilia-tan. I'm glad you're worried about me. Super glad, actually... but I'm doing it!"

Thumbs up, teeth gleaming, Subaru flashed Emilia his best triumphant grin. At Subaru's attitude, stripped of every trace of anxiety or hesitation, Emilia blinked in astonishment, unable to hide her surprise.

"W-why are you so set on this...?"

"Even knowing it's risky, even knowing full well it's dangerous, there are times a man's just gotta do it. ...If I pass up a chance like this, the chance that I might actually be able to use magic, then what was even the point of getting summoned to another world!?"

A soul-deep roar, fist clenched. Right now, Subaru was more fired up than at almost any other point since arriving in this other world. For someone whose only benefit from this fantasy world so far had basically been feasting his eyes on gorgeous people, passing this up would mean he couldn't really say he'd experienced another world at all.

Realizing it was pointless to argue against Subaru's enthusiasm, Emilia resigned herself, adding a firm "I'll stop you the second it looks dangerous" as a condition. Receiving that warning with a bright smile, Subaru turned back to face Puck properly.

"So what do I do first? Draw a magic circle or something? Do I have to offer up Betty as a sacrifice?"

"Good to see you're on friendly terms with Betty. Well then, first let's check your attribute. We won't get anywhere without knowing what kind of magic you can even use."

At Puck's suggestion, the brightness that had been on Subaru's face clouded over instantly. Puck blinked his black eyes at the striking reaction, but Subaru waved a hand vaguely, saying "nah,"

"Ore no zokusei wa kitto 'hi' dayo?" (My attribute's probably fire, y'know?)

"Why are you suddenly talking like that?"

At Emilia's question, Subaru shook his head slowly side to side, so slowly it seemed like it should creak. He didn't want to remember anything more about that. But Puck, ignoring all of Subaru's feelings on the matter, took off from Emilia's shoulder and floated in front of Subaru's face.

"Well then, pardon me."

"Fuwaa, what is this new sensation."

Puck extended his tail, a length rivaling his own small body, and touched the tip to Subaru's forehead. Presumably, he was examining the mana inside Subaru's body. Subaru recalled that when he and Emilia had done their heart-to-heart before, they'd pressed foreheads together—and more than the difference in method, what he felt now was envy toward that lucky moment of forehead contact with Emilia.

"Not that pretending otherwise changes reality... no wait, hold on, let's think positive here, the way I do. Come to think of it, wasn't Roswaal's attitude that time kind of unnatural? Yeah, he was jealous of the rare, once-in-a-generation talent for magic sleeping inside me. Oh, Shit! Yeah, that's it, he was jealous of me. That's why he fed me a bunch of half-truths, trying to shut the door on my glorious path as a mage..."

"That's rare, you went straight down the 'yin' attribute path."

"Second opinion—!!"

Transcending dimensions, receiving confirmation from a completely different source, Subaru sank into despair. Unless Puck and Roswaal were somehow colluding behind the scenes on this, he had no choice but to believe the result. Given that this laid-back cat—one who seemed like he'd go around living by the philosophy Subaru's own mother used to preach, "other houses are other houses, our house is our house!"—how likely was it, really, that he'd lied out of jealousy over Subaru's talent?

"Debuff specialization, huh... I wanted to blast a bunch of lightning around, blow away an army of ten thousand..."

"Oh, and also, no talent to speak of, really. Small Gates, and the only thing you've got going for you is a slightly-better-than-nothing number of them. A lot of them aren't even fully open, so the airflow's pretty bad too."

"I know, shut up! Don't describe a person like an unwashed screen door! By the way, how would you rate the talent numerically, out of curiosity?"

"If you devoted twenty years purely to training in magic, you might get to just shy of first-rate, maybe."

"Half a lifetime for less than first-rate... I'm giving up on this path...!"

At Subaru's declaration, holding back tears, abandoning his dream, Emilia looked at him with exasperation. But it couldn't be helped—effort and hard work were things Subaru's dictionary preferred to leave nothing more than a scratch mark on, not an entry.

Still, sentiment aside,

"I still want to try the magic experience lesson, so please. What do I do?"

"You're a 'yin' attribute, so—that's a bit unexpected. Simple 'yin' spells, let's see, what do we have, Lia?"

"For yin attribute, I think there's Shamak, the blinding spell... sorry, I'm not too confident about it."

Emilia lamented her lack of expertise. In front of her, Puck clapped his hands, saying "ah, right,"

"Shamak, huh, now there's one you rarely see used. Well then, let's make that our goal for now."

"Wait wait wait wait. Don't just casually move forward like that's a given. To begin with, what kind of spell even is Shamak? What if I use it without knowing the effects and get turned into a frog by some incomprehensible power? If that happens, Emilia-tan, turn me into a prince with a kiss, okay?"

"Am I the only one who feels like that logic doesn't connect at all?"

Under her narrow-eyed look, Subaru shrank back sheepishly. Meanwhile, Puck nodded, granting that Subaru had a point.

"True enough. It probably is a little scary to try out a spell you don't know anything about. All right, let me demonstrate. This is Shamak."

Puck waved his short arm and completed the brief incantation. And then, suddenly, Subaru's vision was swallowed in darkness. The instant after he blinked, even opening his eyes his sight stayed as dark as if his eyelids were still shut. He let out a small startled sound without meaning to—but even that voice, which should have been his own, didn't reach his own ears. The darkness that had arisen completely cut Subaru off from both his sight and any connection to the outside world, sending a chill down his spine like a shiver of dread.

"Aaand, done."

"Mommy—! ...huh? Am I back?"

"You're back. Although, Subaru—you were so scared just now, weren't you calling for your mom?"

"I wouldn't do something so unmanly! That was, y'know, that thing! Like when you accidentally call your teacher 'mom'!"

His vision returned, and seeing that beautiful face and the little cat lined up in front of him, he breathed a sigh of relief. He touched his own eyelids, confirmed there was nothing different about them, and then—

"So that's what blinding is, the Shamak spell, huh. Pretty understated, but it seems like it could be really effective, no?"

"Not really. Against anyone equal or lower in strength, sure, but a real difference in power just brushes it right off, and it won't last long either. If I'm the one casting it on Subaru, though, I could keep you in the dark for the rest of your life."

"That idea is terrifying!? Even without going that long, I'm confident I'd be a total wreck if that state lasted even just one day!"

Laughing awkwardly, Subaru quietly moved his trembling fist behind his back. That instant when everything had seemed to recede, replaced by a feeling of being utterly abandoned in the world—that memory was still fresh. A stretch of time where he'd believed he was completely alone in the whole world, with not a single ally anywhere—remembering that, even just a flash of it, had made his hand shake.

Cursing himself for being pathetic, Subaru bit his tongue and, forcing a smile, said,

"Anyway, whether it's effective or not aside, that means I can use that magic too, right? I want to try it right now!"

"Sure. All right, I'll be the one assisting. Lia, stay back—in the highly unlikely event the mana goes out of control and Subaru pops, your clothes would get dirty."

"That is a genuinely 'highly unlikely' scenario, right!? That's basically the worst-case failure imaginable, isn't it!?"

Puck smiled silently and cheerfully, and Emilia, with a slightly grim expression, said "don't overdo it, okay," before actually stepping well back.

In the midst of an alarmingly ominous setting, and leaving behind Subaru's slightly hesitant mood, things began to move forward.

Puck settled on the top of Subaru's short black hair, adjusting his seating position.

"Prickly, uncomfortable head to sit on."

"Never thought the day would come I'd have someone riding up there. Can't offer you a cushion for hospitality, but make yourself at home."

"Nah, I already miss being in Lia's hair, so let's wrap this up quick and get back. So then—mentally ready?"

At the question, Subaru hesitated for only the briefest instant. But then his cheeks twisted into a grin, and he nodded slowly. Plenty of anxiety still lurking, sure, but even so, he couldn't resist his curiosity.

Receiving Subaru's affirmative response, Puck nodded once, deeply. And then, suddenly, Subaru felt a sensation like heat spreading through his entire body. Circulating through his body, something unlike blood—this raging, formless torrent surging inside him must be what they called mana. Through something transmitted from Puck atop his head, he could feel the energy inside his body taking on direction, beginning to move.

"Subaru, try picturing it. Right now, I'm grabbing hold of the mana flowing inside you and moving it. Part of it, I'm going to push out through the Gate, out of your body. Out there, it'll take shape—like before, becoming a black cloud."

"Imagine it, imagining it, huh. Leave it to me, I'm good at delusions."

Subtly misinterpreting Puck's advice, Subaru sought, in the depths of his own fantasies, a destination for the energy squirming inside him. He pictured the Gate—that thing called a gate—at the center of his body. The gate swung open its heavy doors, letting energy overflow from the inside out. That energy went out, and following Subaru's will, was to be sublimated into phenomenon—

"Huh, uh oh. The Gate's suddenly acting a little—"

Just as things reached their final stage, Puck's mutter came out of nowhere. There wasn't time to even ask "acting a little what."

The very next instant—

"Both of you!?"

Emilia's scream rang out, and a few seconds later, a corner of the Roswaal Manor garden was engulfed under an explosive eruption of black haze.

—He hadn't quite popped, but the end result was, all the same, a spectacular failure.
