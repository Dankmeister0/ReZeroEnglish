Arc 4 Chapter 81: "Ray of Light"

Scolded and encouraged in equal measure by Otto and Patrasche—two of them (one person and one horse)—Subaru was, for the moment, pulled back from the brink of his own heart's collapse.

Honestly, there was still a great deal about what had happened in the castle of dreams that he hadn't managed to swallow. But he'd have to chew through it all, piece by piece, and turn it into fuel for who he was now.

"First off, I can't count on any more help from Echidna..."

Echidna, the Witch of Greed, had worn a mask of friendliness toward Subaru while she quietly observed him thrash about. Now, after their final exchange, he wanted to believe that wasn't the whole of it—but even so, a witch was a creature that could not bend its own principles, and that much hadn't changed.

He'd felt that truth all the more keenly watching the other five witches—Sekhmet, Daphne, Carmilla, Typhon, and Minerva. None of them, in Subaru's eyes, had been beings steeped entirely in evil. But he couldn't say, not even under threat, that they were beings of pure good either.

Even Minerva, whose creed was to heal others, was no exception. The way she'd thrown everything away—even her own limbs—for the sake of healing others had been less noble than it was harrowing.

And then there was the last witch, Satella. He wanted to put off thinking about her for now.

The bewildering emotion that had welled up inside Subaru at the sight of her—he understood, on instinct, that answering that question wasn't just something he didn't have the luxury for in his current state; it was something dangerous. The last words they'd exchanged as they parted. The last sight he'd had of Satella—just remembering them was enough to make his chest feel like it would tear apart from the inside, clawed at by a maddening swirl of feeling.

So Subaru deliberately steered his thoughts away from Satella and turned them instead toward other problems. Namely: Echidna's parting advice, and whether he should take Satella's words at face value.

"'Treasure yourself,' huh. Easy to say, but..."

Facing Satella, in the overlap of the first and second Trials in the tomb, Subaru had learned a fact: that the people who didn't want him to die would grieve his "death" just as much as he grieved theirs. And in the process, he'd been forced to acknowledge his own true feelings—that he didn't want to die.

But so what. Nothing had changed about how few weapons he had at his disposal. Nothing had changed about how the sheer number of problems remained unsolved. If anything, the number of people he could rely on to help solve them had only shrunk. His heart had been patched up, a little—but in every other respect, he'd made no progress. If anything, he'd slid backward.

"'Rely on the people who care about me'... how the hell am I supposed to do that..."

Did it mean he should confess everything and lean on them? But the one forbidding that was none other than Satella herself—no, wait. If he followed the thread of that conversation, wasn't it the personality of the Witch of Envy that had forbidden him from speaking of Return by Death? Did Satella's stance differ from that other personality? What had that last appeal of hers even meant—

"—Enough. This isn't the time for that."

He caught himself as his thoughts threatened to drift, smooth as flowing water, back toward Satella. He hit the brakes.

What he needed was concrete solutions to each of his various problems—laying them out, thinking them through.

"Garfiel is afraid of the outside world... huh."

That was the last piece of advice Echidna had given him—information she'd offered up because it would presumably be necessary to break the deadlock of his situation.

That Garfiel had undergone the first Trial in the past was practically a confession already, given his own behavior; Echidna's words just seemed to corroborate it. The question was what he had seen in that past that made him afraid of the outside world.

The fact that he hadn't gone with Frederica when she tried to leave the Sanctuary to take shelter at Roswaal's mansion probably wasn't unrelated to that trauma either.

It didn't seem like a problem he could solve by confronting Garfiel head-on and asking him honestly.

"Which means I've got no choice but to get it out of someone who might know... but Frederica and Ryuzu-san both seemed pretty tight-lipped about it..."

If Frederica's own account was to be trusted, she'd been unable to chase after Garfiel when he entered the tomb, and so, as a result, she was in no position to know the contents of the Trial. Conversely, Ryuzu—who'd dashed inside and dragged Garfiel back out—could be assumed to have had at least one of her duplicates undergo the Trial herself. It seemed likely she'd know the contents of whatever Trial Garfiel had seen.

"In that case—much as I'd rather not—the surest way is probably to get command authority through Ryuzu Meyer's crystal and order her to talk."

Ryuzu, who served as the leader among the duplicates and the appointed representative of the Sanctuary, had said herself that even she couldn't ultimately defy whoever held authority over her. That power carried a compulsion strong enough to force action even against one's own will.

Given that it would also mean gaining more than twenty allies, that act should hold value beyond simply extracting information. That's what he wanted to tell himself, anyway.

"—————"

What surfaced in Subaru's mind was the burning Sanctuary, the swarm of white rabbits. The shameful memory of himself, frantic to escape, sending Ryuzu's duplicates out one after another to shield himself.

I have to get to Emilia. Using that as his justification, Subaru had ordered Ryuzu and the others around in a desperate frenzy, and rushed into the tomb battered and bloodied. He'd never once looked back on what he'd done that day, never regretted it—but looking back on it now with a clear head, even he was appalled at how reckless it had been.

All the more so now that he'd acknowledged to himself that he didn't want to die—that he loved himself enough to fear it.

Wouldn't he, more arrogantly, more baldly this time, make them throw their lives away for his sake again? Even if he told himself he didn't want to do that, he couldn't fully trust himself not to.

"My head's getting all tangled up... I'm swinging way too negative, damn it. Trying to think about everything at once is what's blowing past my brain's capacity. One thing at a time—crush what I can crush."

There were problems where question connected directly to answer. He should start by clearing those away one by one, then link the solved problems together toward solutions for the bigger ones.

For now, Subaru searched for a problem that might have an upward trajectory. First—

"Freeing the Sanctuary—that's something I should handle, since I've already cleared the first Trial. No reason to put any more burden on Emilia. Besides, if she's under too much pressure, her mental state's gonna take a real hit—so that's a no-go."

He recalled the image of Emilia in the snow-covered Sanctuary, pressing up against him with such affection. It was self-evident that continuing to challenge the Trial and having her heart broken over and over was what had brought her to that state. Making her keep confronting the Trial wasn't going to lead anywhere good.

"So if clearing the Trials is my job... the next thing I need to worry about is the second Trial. That one really did a number on me, but... to begin with, does that even count as cleared for me?"

A present that should not be—that was the pretext under which he'd been made to experience worlds where his choices had gone differently. So-called parallel worlds. That was what the second Trial put him through.

The Trial that let him experience, in simulated form, any number of timelines diverging from the true course of events—for anyone other than Subaru, it might have amounted to little more than observing parallel worlds from the outside. But for Subaru alone, the Trial had bared its fangs in the truest sense.

By showing him, in the form of what came after, all the worlds where he'd made the wrong choice.

Every shape regret could take, every form catastrophe could take, every kind of anguished cry—all of them had mourned Subaru's "death."

Witnessing it, feeling it against his own skin, had shattered Subaru's heart into pieces. Even now, just remembering it sent a chill through his whole body, a numbness through his hands and feet that he couldn't shake off.

And it was while he'd been curled up, swallowed by the screaming of his own heart, that he'd been dragged into the castle of dreams. But as a practical matter—what had become of the Trial?

There was no way he could call it "cleared," that much was certain.

But then, what would it even take to clear the second Trial? Unlike the first one, he had no idea.

"No use agonizing over it. ...I just have to do what I can do."

Shaking his head, Subaru pushed his hesitant heart upright and stood. He pressed a hand against the tomb wall at his back and glared at the darkness of the entrance.

He'd been lost in thought alone; Patrasche and Otto, who'd been watching over him worriedly until just a little while ago, weren't beside him anymore.

Their final exchange had left him feeling embarrassingly self-conscious, so he'd had Otto take Patrasche's reins and head back to the stable. He'd felt saved by the concern in Patrasche's eyes, watching him worriedly right up to the end—but he'd wanted some time alone to gather his scattered thoughts.

"The main problems I've gotta deal with: the Sanctuary and the mansion. The Sanctuary's the Trial, Garfiel, and the Great Rabbit. The mansion's Beatrice and Elsa... there's a limit to how multi-front this thing can get."

Every single one of them was a hard problem with not even a hint of a solution in sight, which was enough to make him sick at heart. But he didn't have the luxury of sinking into that. Knock the problems down one by one, steadily.

Without indulging in some world-weary line about his life being expendable.

"First, let's confirm the state of the Trial. If the second Trial starts up again, fine. If it's marked complete and the third Trial begins, even better."

At the very least, if the barrier came down sooner, the situation would shift significantly. Garfiel would have to reconsider his own approach, and worst case, even if the Great Rabbit attacked, evacuation to the outside would become possible. Even Garfiel, formidable as he was, seemed unlikely to keep digging in his heels with the Great Rabbit's threat staring him in the face.

The Sanctuary's problems would probably move toward resolution once the barrier was lifted.

Having thought this far, Subaru felt a faint gleam of light open up before him, and something in him eased.

He'd been plagued by a swarm of problems enough to scramble his brain, but at least he felt he could now make out something like a path toward an answer.

"—————"

Standing at the tomb's entrance, he held his breath before the stone passage visible beyond the gloom.

If he stepped inside and the Trial began, he might once again be made to face a present that should not be. That sight was not something he'd ever get used to, no matter how many times he saw it.

But he knew it wasn't something he was allowed to ignore, or to forget.

If he couldn't run from it, then he had no choice but to face it.

Subaru took a deep breath, held it, and stepped forward.

He stepped into the tomb, moving to challenge the Trial that would free the Sanctuary—

"————!?"

The instant he stepped in, a sensation like a fist slamming into his skull suddenly rocked him.

A pain like a needle driving straight into his brain shot through him, and the moment he thought he saw light scattering before his eyes, his footing gave way beneath him. His upper body swayed violently, and unable to stand any longer, Subaru crumpled to the ground on the spot.

A violent nausea surged up; his stomach twisted with pain as though wrung out, and bile spilled from his mouth. He coughed, again and again, but his body showed no sign of easing up.

Alarm bells, alarm bells, alarm bells were ringing.

Amid the chain of malaise and discord wracking his body, Subaru gasped and rolled his way out of the tomb, back outside. He understood it instinctively: if he pushed deeper inside, this malice eating into his body would only rage more fiercely still.

"Ugh, guh...hah, uh, uwah"

Tumbling out of the tomb, Subaru braced his hands against the grass and retched over and over.

The moment his body was fully clear of the tomb, the agony that had been tormenting him receded. The headache, the nausea, the numbness in his limbs all began to fade, and Subaru, eyes brimming with tears, lifted his face.

"Ah, uh... what... what was that just now...?"

He looked back toward the tomb's entrance, and as he reached out a hand to crawl toward it, a visceral, fundamental sense of rejection welled up inside him.

It wasn't some trauma born of dread over the Trial's contents—it wasn't a problem on that level at all. He understood clearly: the tomb was refusing him.

"Wh-what..."

Once he grasped that he was being refused, Subaru immediately understood what had happened to him.

Patrasche, who had stepped inside to carry him out, had been injured. Roswaal too, entering the tomb to attempt the Trial, had suffered grave wounds. The tomb rejected those unqualified to undertake the Trial. If that same effect had now been turned on Subaru—

"That can't be... because that would mean..."

He stood, and on unsteady legs, tried once more, boldly, to push into the tomb.

But the instant he took a single step inside, the headache and nausea came flooding back, crushing him beneath an overwhelming sense of wrongness that made it impossible even to stand.

"Haah... haah, haah...!"

Staggering backward, putting distance between himself and the tomb's entrance, breathing hard, Subaru had no choice but to accept the fact this latest attempt had confirmed.

"That rotten..."

What surfaced in his mind was the white-haired witch in mourning dress.

At their parting, she had indeed put this question to him:

Take her hand, or take Satella's.

And Subaru had not taken Echidna's hand. He had taken Satella's.

If this was her retaliation for that choice, then it was just too—

"The one gleam of light I finally found, and—!"

He'd thought, right up to the very last moment, that she'd given him that hint out of pure goodwill—had even started to think a little better of her for it.

"—I'm not exactly wrong, though, am I?"

A voice that shouldn't have been audible—the witch's voice, mischievous as ever—rang in his ears, and Subaru looked up at the night sky.

"Stripped of my qualification—nobody told me about that! Echidnaaa!!"

—Natsuki Subaru had lost the qualification to undertake the Trial that would free the Sanctuary.
