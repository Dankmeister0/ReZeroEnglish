Arc 7 Chapter 34: "A Commotion in the Carriage"

Not quite leisurely enough to call pastoral, the carriage rolled on down the highway.
The Galewind Horse, its chestnut-coated bulk swaying, escorted the carriage bearing Subaru and the others with a delicacy that belied its sturdy frame.

"As expected from Ready, not Lady... She really does live up to being Zikr-san's beloved steed."

Watching the horse's spirited form, Subaru mused on its owner as he said so.
The borrowed mount, Zikr's beloved horse, was named "Ready"—a near-miss for the word meaning "lady," and Subaru could only be struck anew by the sheer force of destiny surrounding a self-proclaimed "lover of women."
In any case, thanks in no small part to Ready's efforts, the party's journey toward the magical capital of Chaosflame was proceeding smoothly. At this rate, they'd arrive in about four days.

"Still, arriving isn't the only goal here. There's the reputation of the Nine Divine Generals we're going to meet, and honestly the very ring of 'magical capital' sounds kind of scary."

A city crowned with the word "magic"—the "Magic Capital." It hardly seemed like the sort of place one could deal with easily.
He'd heard it was a salad bowl of diverse races, but did that alone earn a city the label of "chaos" and "magic"? Add to that the fact it was governed by the Nine Divine Generals, who seemed to do nothing but rebel, and all Subaru's instincts screamed danger. That, too, was part of why he hadn't brought Rem along.

"I wonder just what kind of chaos is waiting for us..."

"Speaking of chaos, my head's still all mixed up. That getup of yours and the brother I know just don't line up right—it's giving me bugs."

"Ah? You're still on about that?"

Subaru, who'd been gazing out the window at Ready's back as she trotted along briskly from the carriage's front seat, turned around, gently smoothing back long black hair blown by the wind.
At that gesture, Al—seated at the very back of the carriage—raised his chin as if exasperated.
Sprawled lazily across the three-person bench he'd claimed for himself, Al hooked a finger under the chin-guard of his helmet and tilted his upturned face back down.

"That's gotta be on purpose, that."

He grumbled bitterly at Subaru's mannerisms.
At the sharp rejection in Al's tone, Subaru felt a flash of indignation and shrugged. Surprisingly, Al was second only to Rem in having complaints about Subaru's cross-dressing.
Abel and the Shudrak folk, the O'Connell siblings, even Zikr—once they'd accepted it, they'd all naturally let it go. Yet Al kept needling him about it.

"You said the same thing with the same mouth you used to swear you'd back me up... Was that whole heartfelt exchange just a lie?"

"It ain't a lie, and my decision to lend you a hand's real too, but that's a separate issue from this. Why's cooperating gotta mean I go all rosy about your cross-dressing?"

"If you're on my side, I'd like you to accept me as I am, at least for now."

"And that getup is the real, as-is you? For real?"

Put that bluntly, even Subaru felt it was a bit much to claim.
Natsuki Subaru and Natsumi Shubaruzu—two names housed in one body, sure, but there was a clear difference in bearing, in stance. To put it plainly, Natsuki Subaru was himself with both feet on the ground, while Natsumi Shubaruzu was more like an idealized image drawn in soft focus.

"Of course, it's not that I want to become a woman. It's more about confidence, that kind of thing."

"Didn't ask!"

Argued back at with raised voice, Subaru looked down at himself and reconsidered.
Currently reconstructed via wig and makeup, Natsumi Shubaruzu now needed to project something more intellectual than the dancer disguise from before.
So the costume and makeup had been steered accordingly.

The red-based, collared outfit was a one-off piece modeled after the military uniform of the Vollachian Empire—specifically, the one Zikr wore—with similar design touches. Prioritizing mobility, the cape stayed off except when needed, but Subaru was rather proud of how convincingly bluffing it was.
Trousers below, sturdy boots on his feet, and atop his head a military cap adorned with a bird's feather.
This was the completed picture of the "Lady Strategist" Natsumi Shubaruzu.

"Well, more lady soldier than lady strategist, I guess. When you picture a woman in military dress, isn't cross-dressing as a man practically the standard, though?"

"A cross-dressing brother dressed up as a man—just looking at those words is deepening my chaos even further."

"I wanted to avoid standing out too much, so I went with an imperial flair. Since it's men's dress, I borrowed a little from Crusch-san's taste too."

"That thing where you say you took hints from people you've met—I genuinely don't know if the Duchess would be flattered if she heard that... Am I the only one who thinks this is weird? What d'you think, Your Majesty?"

At Subaru explaining his concept, Al threw up his right arm in surrender. Then he pointed that raised arm forward, directing the question at the figure in the front seat.
Seated in the middle of the carriage—that is, positioned squarely between Subaru and Al's endless back-and-forth—was Abel, gazing out the window with a stern expression.

He'd seemed to be lost in some difficult thought, but at the careless nickname Al used, his brow twitched, and without turning around, he coldly pronounced, "Clown."

"I recall granting you no permission for such a manner of address. Know that one careless word from you could shake the very survival of the Empire. Else you shall lose your other arm as well."

"Ugh, give me a break. I'm mostly used to it, but missing one's still a pain. Lose the other, and it stops being just a pain and starts being a whole different problem."

"Then know your place."

"Heeey, no room for jokes with this Imperial style, huh. Rough on comedian types like us. Right, brother?"

"Don't ask me to agree with that."

The total absence of jokes or banter, and the taut, prickling air—Subaru found it a bit much, though he had no intention of blaming the whole Empire for what might just be a cultural trait.
At most, he acknowledged Abel personally as someone hard to deal with. Even so, their exchanges themselves were sharp enough that it wasn't a dissatisfaction he felt on the surface. Of course, once matters of principle or values came up, it wouldn't stay that contained.

"So, brother's getup—Abel-chan, what do you think?"

"——"

For a moment, a crease of displeasure flickered across Abel's brow at the new nickname.
Even Subaru was startled at Al's reckless closing of distance, but after a brief silence, Abel dismissed the disrespect with a "hmph" through his nose.
Perhaps he judged it better than being called "Your Majesty." Unable to grasp Abel's exact calculus, Subaru swallowed his inner nervousness with a sigh.
Meanwhile, Abel, apparently intending to properly answer Al's question after all, turned his gaze toward Subaru.

"It is indeed a ridiculous getup, but so long as results are produced, I have no complaints to voice. Ability and personal taste ought to be considered separately."

"Don't pretend to back me up and then shoot me in the back. How many times do I have to say it's not personal taste, it's necessity? You think I like cross-dressing?"

"——"

"Don't go quiet on me!"

The meaningful silence only deepened the unjust weight of suspicion cast on Subaru.
Naturally, this was an utterly unwelcome perception as far as Subaru was concerned. If he didn't have to, he'd never want to cross-dress again in his life.
Still, if there was even the slightest chance it could break the deadlock of their situation, he simply couldn't remove cross-dressing from his list of options. That was all there was to it.

"Every single one of you... I've got proper theoretical backing for this, you know. Listen up."

"You just called it 'theoretical backing.' Well, I guess it kind of is a genre convention, isn't it. An emperor driven from his throne gathering allies to reclaim it... with that flavor, having a mysterious woman tagging along is basically a given."

"Yeah, right. Strategist, sorcerer, that kind of position—shows up all the time in this genre as the heroine— not a heroine, dammit!"

"Don't say it yourself and then get mad at yourself. I mean, forget me, everyone else'll be shocked."

Subaru fumed, but his point likely wouldn't land with anyone but his fellow countryman Al.
Then again, most of this kind of framework wouldn't land even with people from the same country. The fact that even this got through to Al only reinforced what Subaru had suspected for a while now—Al's stock of knowledge was awfully close to his own.

"...I never actually asked what era you got sent here from."

Up to now, though Subaru and Al had shared the fact that they hailed from the same homeland, they'd never talked about which era each of them had been pulled from.
Once before, Al had mentioned being summoned to another world nearly twenty years ago. And that in the process, he'd suffered terribly—even lost an arm.

The confession itself had been delivered lightly, but the reality behind it surely wasn't anything so breezy.
The anguish and despair Al must have carried, clinging endlessly to his days. A man who'd been made to suffer the harshness of another world far more than Subaru himself, Al.

Talking it through would only make Subaru realize how fortunate he'd been by comparison. And at the same time, it would surely stir up the agony of wondering what exactly separated him from Al.
Afraid of that, Subaru had never tried to go deep with Al until now. And Al, in turn, had never tried to push further either.

Still, considering the words they used and the subculture references that landed between them, the original world Subaru and Al had lived in must have been the same era—and if Al had been summoned twenty years ago, then at the time, they must have been roughly the same age.
The current age gap between Subaru and Al was purely a product of how much time had passed in the other world since their respective summonings. That was exactly why, despite the age gap, their conversational wavelength matched so well.
The vague, nagging ache Subaru felt whenever he was around Al—that, it seemed, was the cause.

"—Speaking of genre conventions..."

"Huh?"

"A shady sorcerer at the emperor's side... isn't that a convention too? What d'you think?"

Al's idle remark yanked Subaru's wandering thoughts back to reality.
Blinking, Subaru nodded "ah" at the "convention" Al had brought up.

"That's definitely a convention. A bewitching female sorcerer seduces the emperor, slowly turning him into a puppet dancing to her will... and then the once-prosperous nation collapses."

"Don't go collapsing it on your own say-so. Whatever happens, I will reclaim it with these hands. Only—"

"Only?"

"There was no sorcerer, no woman, but it is true there existed something called a 'Stargazer.'"

"—A 'Stargazer'?"

Subaru's and Al's puzzled voices overlapped at the word from Abel's mouth.

An unfamiliar word, yet strangely, Subaru's mind could roughly guess at the characters behind it. And once the characters fit, its role came vaguely to mind as well.

"Could it be like a feng shui master or something like that... a fortune-teller type position?"

"More like a prophet type, ain't it? Even in the Kingdom, the stone tablet filled that role."

"Ah, the 'Dragon History Stone,' right..."

Nodding along with Al's words, Subaru dredged up what he knew of the Kingdom's prophecy tablet.
He'd never seen the actual object, but apparently that stone tablet recorded future events that would befall the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, and was said to be a convenient artifact that even indicated solutions to the problems it foretold.
Except, despite foreseeing the deaths of the royal family from illness, the solution the tablet proposed was the selection of the next monarch—that is, the very Royal Selection that Emilia and Priscilla were now competing in.
Frankly, it was a result that made him tilt his head even at the notion of calling it a "helpful" prophecy tablet.

"If it really wanted to give advice to save the Kingdom, you'd think it'd just tell them how to cure the illness or stop it in the first place..."

Through deepening his understanding as a knight to a Royal Selection candidate, Subaru had learned that the Lugunican royal family showed no signs of tyranny or foolishness—if anything, they seemed to have been a royal line genuinely loved by the people.
Many among the vassals and citizens had grieved their deaths; no one had rejoiced. Why the "Dragon History Stone" had abandoned them remained, to this day, wrapped in mystery.

"When the other party's a stone tablet, checking your answers is a pipe dream, I guess."

"With a stone slab, sure, but the 'Stargazer' was different, wasn't it? What kind of role did that person play at court, Abel-chan?"

"—. It is not so far removed from your assumptions. The Stargazer's power existed to sustain the Empire, for it peered into tomorrow."

Abel's answer to the question was, by and large, consistent with the impression one could take just from the name "Stargazer." Even so, if there was a problem, it was this one thing.

"...But it didn't foresee this, did it? You're the one who got driven out."

"I already told you. The Stargazer's power exists for the sake of preserving the Empire. —That is not necessarily compatible with my own safety."

"That means..." you being chased out was for the Empire's benefit."

"At the very least, that is what the Stargazer must have judged."

Answering flatly, Abel quietly crossed his arms.
The stone tablet and the Stargazer, two things bearing similar roles, each seemingly having overlooked the crisis of the most exalted figure in their respective nations. It was a situation that could invite suspicion of some connection between Kingdom and Empire, but—

"Before that, I'm starting to think the real problem is you, getting abandoned by your chancellor, your right hand, and your prophet all at once. Are we really okay putting you back on the throne?"

"Whoa there, that's a straight fastball to the face, brother."

"No, I mean, doesn't hearing all this background make you nervous!?"

"But hey, there's this—the Afro General vouched for Abel-chan, didn't he?"

"Ah, right. Then it's fine, I guess..."

At the mention of the Afro General—that is, Zikr—Subaru quietly settled down.
He'd been made to worry about all sorts of things, but Zikr siding with Abel was reassuring. Conversely, whether he could have kept traveling with Abel harboring doubts, without Zikr, was highly questionable.
No matter how much this was for the sake of getting himself and Rem safely back to the Kingdom, it wasn't as if he didn't care what became of the Empire.
Honestly, the Empire held far more bad memories for him than good, but there were plenty of people he wanted to see happy—everyone among the People of Shudrak, and the O'Connell siblings.

"Whether you count him among that is starting to make me pretty uneasy, but..."

"Whatever you say, whatever the Stargazer sees of tomorrow, my answer is already decided."

"——"

"Even if the Stargazer judges that I ought not sit upon the throne, I will not obey. If the Stargazer sings that the Empire falls because I exist, then I alone shall overturn that with these hands."

Abel's declaration, delivered with more heat than "flatly" would suggest.
It carried the same intensity as every other time he'd sworn to reclaim his throne. That, it seemed, was Abel's uncrossable line, his unshakable conviction.

Because it felt like it came not from mere vengeance or rebelliousness but from something larger and purer—a genuine belief—Subaru could, at that final line, avoid doubting Abel.
Though honestly, what he really wanted was for all the other, more peripheral parts to be dressed up too, so he could believe wholeheartedly.

"—Ah! Everyone! Hey, hey, look ahead, ahead!"

"Hm?"

Just as the conversation had reached a lull, a lively voice suddenly carried back from the front of the carriage—from the driver's seat—drawing everyone's attention that way.

The one calling out so energetically was Medium, gripping Ready's reins from the driver's seat.
On her journey of two with Flop, she apparently hadn't gotten to hold the reins much, so she'd volunteered eagerly, saying "I wanna do it!" and taken up the role of driver.
Medium, having tucked her long, slender frame into the driver's seat, held the reins in one hand while pointing ahead with the other.

"Something's going on up ahead, looks all busy and stuff! Can you see it, Natsumi-chan?"

"Um... I can see something that's smaller than a speck, maybe."

Leaning out the carriage window, Subaru still couldn't confirm what Medium meant by "busy." Might be an angle problem, or simply an eyesight problem.
Subaru had thought his own eyesight was pretty good, but in this other world, that meant nothing.
The eyesight of people from another world was on a different level entirely. Emilia probably had something like 20/10 vision. He recalled seeing a TV program back in his original world about how people from certain parts of Africa had extraordinarily sharp eyesight.
On that logic, he had high hopes for the eyesight of Taritta, one of the Amazoness types of this other world.

"So then, Taritta-san, how about you? Can you see it?"

"Wait, pleasE—that iS..."

Taritta answered Subaru's call, though her figure was nowhere to be found either inside the carriage or on the driver's seat.
Leaning out the window, Subaru had called up to—of all places—atop the carriage. Positioned on the roof of the moving vehicle, Taritta was serving as lookout, keeping watch in all directions from up there.

"Is it just me, or is it kind of unsettling how casually people are riding on the roof?"

"Can't speak for Abel, but I'm used to it thanks to our Garfiel."

Al remarked on Taritta working atop the carriage, but for Subaru, who was familiar with Garfiel wanting the same role, it wasn't particularly strange.
Fairly seriously, staying vigilant during travel in this other world was essential, so it likely served a proper purpose beyond mere chuunibyou posturing. The fact that Abel didn't stop her was proof enough of that.

"...It seemS Imperial soldiers have gathered. It lookS like an ox-cart or something ahead has been stoppeD."

"That means..."

"—A checkpoint."

At Taritta's report, Abel, having sensed the situation, murmured as much.
At the word "checkpoint," what flashed through Subaru's mind was the one held at Guaral's main gate. Except, unlike a city entrance, a checkpoint set up mid-highway nagged at him.
Checking traffic in and out of a city was natural enough, but a checkpoint set up on the open road carried a strong impression of responding to some kind of incident.
In short, there was a good chance they were searching for something—or someone—specific.

"Don't tell me it's us? Arakiya got away too, and word spread from that?"

"Naturally, it is unavoidable that details will eventually leak from Arakiya. But I cannot believe that woman could act this swiftly and this precisely. Furthermore, the magic capital lies in the opposite direction from the imperial capital."

"So positionally, the report wouldn't reach here?"

"Correct. —That said, there is the matter of her companion."

Arms still crossed, Abel expressed concern not over Arakiya herself, but over her companion—that is, the person who had taken her out of Guaral.

"The guy who dragged off little missy Arakiya. Given how he strolled boldly into the heart of enemy territory in that situation, he's probably sharp."

"Sharp..."

At Abel's concern and Al's remark, an unpleasant thought crossed Subaru's mind.
Limited to the imperial soldiers who'd been at that fortress city, Subaru did have someone in mind who fit "sharp." And that someone—an object of terror for Subaru—had been nowhere among the soldiers who surrendered after the city hall's fall.
He was probably mixed in among those who'd fled the city and abandoned it.

"No way... it couldn't be."

However unlikely, he didn't want to consider that the one who'd taken Arakiya away was that man.
Please, no more deepening entanglements—that was something he'd very much like to be excused from.

"—. Focus on the problem in front of us for now. What do we do? Can we detour around it?"

"RighT now, I don't think they've noticed uS yeT..."

Medium and Taritta, the two with sharp eyesight, had spotted them first.
If they got past before the soldiers ahead noticed them, they could leave the highway and avoid the checkpoint. If there was no skeleton in the closet to worry about, they could pass through boldly, but as people who very much had something to hide, avoidance was the only option they could countenance.
And yet—

"—Wait. Do not detour."

"Ah?"

Just as the order to leave the highway was about to be given, none other than Abel himself called for a halt.
At that stopping call, Subaru gave Abel a suspicious look.

"Don't detour, and do what instead? If you're found, it'll be a disaster."

"I wish to know their purpose. If this concerns Guaral's fall, their movement is far too swift. If it concerns my whereabouts, I wish to learn what the soldiers have been told to search for."

"So-I'm-say-ing, what happens if you get found out? You'll be stirring up a hornet's nest, and we'll end up in a full-blown car chase."

Of course, the possibility of getting arrested on the spot had to be assumed too.
Of the five people in the carriage, only three—excluding Subaru and Abel—could actually fight, and their total combat power wasn't much different from the dancer squad that had infiltrated Guaral. If it came down to a fleeing chase by carriage, losing pursuers on a wide-open highway would be no easy task.

"The odds are terrible. Or do you have some good plan?"

"I do. —You."

"Huh?"

Weighing risk against reward and thinking the balance unfavorable, Subaru gaped.
He'd thought they were discussing the odds of surviving a dangerous gamble just now.

"How does it come down to me? What do you mean?"

"There is already precedent from the fortress city. The carriage has been disguised so as not to be recognized as military property. It is as we agreed upon in advance."

"But that was a setup for making small talk with other travelers, wasn't it!?"

What Abel stated so shamelessly had been intended as a cover story for chatting with fellow merchants or travelers along the way—not as a premise for deceiving vigilant imperial soldiers keeping a sharp eye out.
And yet Abel paid no heed to Subaru's near-screaming protest.

"I shall hide beneath the carriage floor. You will pry loose the soldiers' tongues."

"Wait, wait, wait, wait, are you serious!?"

"Do not let the soldiers peer beneath the carriage floor. You value your own life too, do you not?"

"Why are you being so high and mighty about this...!"

As he spoke, Abel rose from his seat and set a hand to the floor. There, a faint gap ran along the seat's underside—a mechanism allowing one to lift the floorboards and descend into the space beneath the carriage.

Originally, this was a carriage meant to be drawn by the second general Zikr's own Galewind Horse.
Though the exterior had been swapped out and disguised so it wouldn't be recognized as connected to the Imperial Army, its functionality hadn't been sealed off along with it. A carriage meant for use by high-ranking figures came equipped with a mechanism for removing the floorboards to hide in case of emergency.
Surely not even the craftsman who built it could have imagined an emperor would be the one hiding there.

In any case, Abel promptly pried up the floorboard and squeezed his slender body into the cramped space beneath. It seemed he genuinely intended to leave everything else to Subaru from here on out.
Bold, perhaps—but more than that, arrogant.

"Well, well, Abel-chan sure is pushy. Still, guess we've got no choice but to do it, brother."

Al's comment, directed at Abel, who'd said his piece and promptly retreated. His following words were prompted by the checkpoint's reaction, visible along the carriage's course.
It seemed the other side had spotted their presence as well.

"Natsumi, they've noticeD us as well."

"Well, guess we gotta go for it then! Alrighty, let's do our best, Natsumi-chan!"

Helpfully enough, Taritta and Medium's cheerful call-and-response eliminated the detour option entirely. Turning around now would practically be announcing "we have something to hide from a search."
So there was nothing for it but to proceed, however irksomely, exactly as His Imperial Majesty wished.

"Brother, ready?"

"—Yes, yes, understood! Very well, then!"

"...That's a savage switch."

Lightly slapping both cheeks, Subaru flipped the switch inside himself.
Slap too hard and the makeup would smudge. Scratch his head and the hair would muss. Fail to shelve his inner irritation and his words would turn coarse. That wouldn't do—not if he meant to charm the imperial soldiers.

Fulfilling the role demanded of him, seeing through the duty expected of him.
That, precisely, was the first step toward Natsuki Subaru reclaiming lost trust.

"My, gentlemen of the army, you must be exhausted. Whatever business brings you here?"

Leaning out the window toward the imperial soldiers halting the carriage, Subaru mustered up his most charming, lovely smile and waved brightly.

△▼△▼△▼△

—The imperial soldiers who halted the carriage did not take the presence of the vehicle seriously—one carrying only Al alongside the three "women," Medium, Taritta, and Subaru.

What they asked about was the party's identity and their travel purpose.
As arranged beforehand, this was handled as: Subaru, the daughter of a minor count, had hired Al and Medium as her bodyguards, while Taritta served in the position of Subaru's attendant.
As a precaution, before leaving Guaral, they'd trial-and-errored Taritta's outfit, changing it from the Amazoness style that would immediately mark her as a member of the People of Shudrak.

Currently, at Subaru's preference, Taritta's attire was a butler-esque men's outfit, earning Al's glowing praise: "Brother's a cross-dressing man dressed as a woman, and Taritta-chan's a woman dressed as a man... I don't even know anymore."

In any case, the checkpoint's imperial soldiers found nothing suspicious in Subaru and company in that mood.
It was only when told their destination was Chaosflame that they showed a meaningful hint of unease, saying "That's..."—it seemed the magic capital had a poor reputation even among them.

"I wouldn't recommend heading to the magic capital, miss, if I may say so."

"You may say so all you like, but I'd only earn Father's scolding otherwise. Ours is a lesser house, even if noble... we can't afford to be picky."

"Hmm, I see. Well, you seem sharp enough, miss, so I suppose there's no need to worry."

"My, my, aren't you kind."

Smiling with the back of her hand at her mouth, Subaru gracefully deflected the soldier's suspicion.
On the whole, clearing the checkpoint seemed to pose no problem. Now then—

"Even so, everyone seems rather on edge... has something happened?"

"Ah, nothing major. There was a skirmish recently near Badheim to the north, and we've got some deserters, so we're searching for them. ...Also looking for a wanted man."

"A wanted man, is it?"

At the sound of it, Subaru frowned carefully, taking care not to let his voice go rough.
Fortunately, the soldier seemed unaware of Subaru's roiling insides and nodded, "Ah."

"There's word that a man wanted in the imperial capital was spotted around here. Man in his fifties, blue hair, they say. Any idea?"

"—. No, I'm afraid not. But hearing that even the mighty Imperial Army has deserters coming out of its ranks—it makes one worry the winds of the Empire have grown unsettled."

"No worries there, Natsumi-chan! We've got each other, no need to be scared, none at all! Let's chin up and go!"

"My, Medium-san, how reassuring you are. Ohohoho."

Not that it was intentional, but Medium's frank remark loosened the soldier's expression all the same.
There had been a risk of arousing suspicion by probing too much, but that worry, too, seemed to have been dodged neatly. Taritta played her role as the silent butler who never interrupted his mistress's words to perfection—wordlessly.
In her case, given her tendency toward stage fright, the silence might have been closer to genuine.
And then, aside from the women, the one most likely to draw suspicion was the shady-looking Al—

"Well now, tough work for you soldiers, huh. That guys like me can go about our loafing business is all thanks to you fine folk working hard."

"...We work solely for the peace of the Empire. Certainly not for the sake of weaklings like you."

"I know, I know. Know my place and all, right."

Al, too, executed a flawless performance of being an insolent lowlife looked down on by imperial soldiers.
Whether it was genuine or acting was hard to say, but it was efficient. And the unnaturalness of hiring a one-armed man like Al as a bodyguard, Subaru had already preempted.

"He was hired as a bodyguard—one needs that pretext for appearances' sake, does one not? That arm of his—no doubt he served as a soldier for his country once, before this."

"...If he left service due to injury, then it's not our place to say anything more. Still, wickedness isn't always ruled out by that, so do take care."

Treating Al's missing arm not as a weakness but a strength, Subaru reinforced the grounds for his flippant attitude.
Even among imperial soldiers who scorned weakness and revered strength, there was some consideration for those who'd stepped back from the front lines due to a wound that never healed.

—Including that exchange, the checkpoint was cleared without any major trouble.

"Wow, Natsumi-chan, you were amazing! I'm impressed!"

Waving from the driver's seat at the retreating imperial soldiers, Medium showered her with praise. At her words, Subaru shook her head, "Oh, not at all."

"It was nothing much on my part. Had they been conducting the checkpoint for a more pressing reason, getting through would've required all manner of cunning tricks... in that sense, I suppose you could say all of us got lucky."

"You think? Then maybe so. Big bro says I'm lucky a lot too! I feel that way about myself too!"

"Ehe, Medium-san and Flop-san are both geniuses at finding happiness, aren't they."

Hand to her cheek, Subaru smiled at Medium's refreshing answer.
Just then, at the exchange between Subaru and Medium, Taritta—looking utterly exhausted in the frontmost seat of the carriage—turned around. Now that the tension had eased, she'd been sighing in relief, but her gaze carried something meaningful that made Subaru tilt her head.

"What is it, Taritta-san? Something the matter?"

"No, nothing's wronG, buT... Natsumi, how are you able to stay so composed like thaT? I've wondered ever since the plan to sneak in as a dancer."

"...I suppose so, yes."

Taritta had never quite gotten used to how different Subaru became in her cross-dressed form.
Ever since infiltrating Guaral, Taritta was, alongside Abel, one of those with the longest association with Subaru's cross-dressed appearance. And yet even she still found it mysterious somehow.

In a sense, Abel and Flop, who'd pulled off their transformations into Bianca and Flora without any switch at all, had cleared every obstacle purely through their natural good looks.
Naturally, Subaru's take on Natsumi Shubaruzu didn't have that same foundation to rest on.
The rawness that makeup and clothing alone can't fully disguise—that was a problem inevitably faced by those who don't already possess the raw material. Bridging that gap took effort.

"So, I just work hard at it with everything I've got."

The completeness of Natsumi Shubaruzu was, in part, the fruit of humanity's accumulated pursuit of beauty.
Had there been no pioneers before her, Subaru's cross-dressing wouldn't pass muster even at a school festival. That it did was thanks to the wishes and effort of countless people who came before.
And because of that, there was also a sense that she couldn't treat what had been made possible so casually.

"...I envY that you have confidencE. I don't have anY."

"Confidence, hmm... What I have isn't confidence, exactly, but trust in Natsumi Shubaruzu—that's a separate thing entirely."

"Eh? Eh? Eh?"

"Hey now, brother, ease off. You're confusing Taritta-chan here. That level of thinking's a bit beyond even me, y'know?"

In place of the blinking, bewildered Taritta, Al called a halt to Subaru's theory.
Subaru hadn't been aware of talking about anything difficult enough to confuse two people. It was simply that while believing in yourself was hard, believing in an idealized self you'd built up within your own mind—that, you could do.

"What you picture is always your best self—that's it."

"I—I understand this is a difficult way of thinking for mE..."

"But honestly, I'd recommend it as a mindset. Comparing yourself to someone you admire, that's nothing but suffering... but picturing your ideal self, that you can actually do."

"——"

Hand to her chest as she spoke, Subaru drew a small widening of the eyes from Taritta.
It seemed something in her words had struck a chord, at least a little.

"An ideal self, one can picture..."

—Enough that she seemed to quietly tuck the thought away in her heart.

"Still, deserters aside, what'd this wanted man do, I wonder. Big relief Abel-chan's situation hasn't spread, but that was a pretty large-scale operation."

"Who knows... A man in his fifties with blue hair, that description could fit plenty of men, I'd imagine—I can even think of a few candidates myself, honestly."

Blue hair didn't occur naturally in Subaru's original world, but in this other world, starting with Rem, it wasn't such a rare hue.
The wanted man from the checkpoint just now—for instance, that heavy drinker whose help they'd once borrowed in Guaral, the mercenary named Rowan—also fit that description.

Unfortunately, Rowan's chance to show off his real skill had fizzled out due to Todd's ambush, but Subaru still believed he'd have been quite the formidable fighter in a fair match.
That said, he didn't seem like the sort of wanted criminal who'd warrant mobilizing so many imperial soldiers. So it was probably someone else with similar features being sought.

"Either way, word of Abel's situation still hasn't reached the rank and file... With no chaos in the imperial capital, it seems safe to assume a body double is standing in for the emperor."

"Hard to say if that's good or bad, really. Honestly, might've been more convenient for us if there was chaos going on with the emperor missing."

"Feels like a mixed bag, doesn't it. Regardless of how he'd be treated if found, the difficulty of Abel being searched for doesn't change either way."

Whether it would work in their favor for Subaru and the others to navigate an Empire thrown into confusion by the emperor's absence being made public was, at present, unknown. At the very least, it was Abel's own assessment that they lacked sufficient strength to make any move to reveal himself yet, and Subaru largely shared that opinion.
Which made it all the more nerve-wracking that he'd insisted on pushing through the checkpoint regardless.

"Anyway, we're through the checkpoint, so let's get Abel-chan out."

"Indeed. And I've got a complaint or two I'd like to lodge as well."

Nodding at Al's suggestion, Subaru crossed her arms, asserting her fake bosom, indignation plain on her face.
The cause of Subaru's indignation was, of course, having been forced into that reckless gambit to get past the checkpoint, but even more than that, it was Abel's recklessness—thrashing about restlessly beneath the carriage floor throughout the checkpoint stop.
While talking with the soldiers, noise had echoed up from the carriage's underside multiple times, and covering for it had taken considerable effort.
In the end, Subaru had settled things by playing the part of a famished young lady saying "even a lesser count's daughter has it hard..." and attributing the noise to her stomach growling—but it was an outrage that had put a dent in the reputation of the elegant Natsumi Shubaruzu.

"His Imperial Majesty seems rather lacking in awareness of his own position."

"Ohh, brother, you're mad. Well, I'll grant that's fair to be mad about."

At Subaru's indignant resolve to give the reckless emperor a piece of her mind, Al voiced his agreement.
And so, as Al lifted the floorboard and Subaru leaned down to peer into the swaying space beneath the carriage floor, intending to hurl her complaint at the face that would appear—

"Excuse me, Abel, what exactly do you mean by this? After all the trouble we went through to cover for that ruckus of yours—"

"Uu!"

"Eeeeek—!?"

The complaint she'd meant to voice was swallowed by a shriek, and Subaru leapt back into Taritta's arms. Caught up in a reflexive princess-carry, she had no room to spare admiring Taritta's excellent balance.
What had appeared wasn't a smirking, laughing Abel—but a small golden-haired girl.

That girl was one Subaru recognized all too well, along with plenty of bad memories attached.
It was the girl who should never have been there. Rui.
The very embodiment of everything irregular about the Empire, Rui Arneb, had come energetically bursting out from beneath the floor.

"W-w-w-what..."

"Ah, uu?"

"Why! Why are you in this carriage—"

"—It seems she has been hiding beneath the floor this entire time. To think no one noticed until now. This is likely Utakata's doing."

Blinking rapidly, voice trembling in a fluster, Subaru could only stammer. Picking up on her confusion, it was Abel who emerged from beneath the floor next, following after Rui.
He beat the dust from his soiled traveling clothes and combed his thoroughly disheveled black hair back into place with his fingers. Then, noticing Rui edging closer toward Subaru, who was still being held aloft in Taritta's arms, he said—

"Hmph. It seems she missed you rather dearly. She chose being with you over Utakata or your own woman, after all."

"Wh-what are you saying so calmly... More importantly, were you truly all right down there with her?"

"How could I possibly be all right? Thrashing about needlessly beneath the floor, even I felt my blood run cold. Because of that girl, the soldiers nearly noticed—I had no idea how I would explain myself."

Setting the floorboard back and returning to his seat, Abel's words clicked something into place in Subaru's mind.
While the imperial soldiers had been inspecting the carriage, the ruckus that had so thoroughly rattled Subaru's composure—its cause had apparently been none other than Rui's presence.
Presumably, Rui, who'd been hiding beneath the carriage floor, had been clinging playfully to Abel when he descended to hide there. What kind of soothing Abel had attempted was unclear, but apparently he had no talent whatsoever for calming children.

"Then what, exactly, can you do...?"

"You continue speaking of me most dishonorably. What of it?"

"What of it, he says..."

"I'll have you know, there's no turning back now. That checkpoint, and time itself, won't permit it."

"Well, that part Abel-chan's got a point on."

Touching the seam of his worn helmet, Al nodded along with Abel's words.
Meanwhile, Subaru, set back down on the floor by Taritta, pressed a hand against the forehead of Rui, who was giggling "auu—" and pressing up against her, agonizing all the while.

"How did this even happen..."

Confusion and bewilderment swirled through her head.
On the other hand, there was also a part of her that felt relief at Rui being here—that this unsettling factor hadn't been left behind at Rem's side back in Guaral.
Of course, Rui tagging along with Subaru's group did nothing to lessen her wariness about the girl's true nature, about whatever she might be scheming.

"Subaru, there's nothing to be donE. We have no choice but to bring Rui along as she iS."

"Even you're saying that, Taritta-san?"

"As Abel says, there's no option of turning back noW. And we can't leave Rui behind eitheR. I know your relationship with her is complicateD, buT..."

"——"

Glancing between Subaru's face and Rui's, Taritta hesitantly offered a neutral opinion.
Beyond the infiltration mission into Guaral, there had also been the time spent together at the People of Shudrak's settlement. Even if not to Rem's degree, most of the Shudrak knew of the wariness and hostility Subaru held toward Rui.
Even taking all that into account, Taritta was saying there was no option of abandoning Rui.
And, frustratingly, that was the same conclusion Subaru had reached herself.

"We can't leave her here to inconvenience unsuspecting people who know nothing about her..."

"Uu?"

Rui tilted her head, her face utterly uncomprehending.
As it stood, ever since being sent to Vollachia, Rui hadn't shown even once the malicious face of the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony. But that didn't mean it would never surface again, and if that true nature ever did emerge, whatever harm resulted, Subaru believed the responsibility would fall on her.

Subaru held the certainty that this girl was dangerous, and had had countless chances to prevent, beforehand, the great harm that might come of it.

Even now, this very instant, she could simply choke that slender neck and sever her future.
That alone would be enough to keep countless seeds of anxiety from ever sprouting.
And yet—

"——"

"Well, dunno exactly what's going on, but that kid's attached to you, ain't she, brother? I get worrying she'll be dead weight going forward, but ain't it also a matter of how you look at it?"

"Al..."

As he spoke, Al tried to lend a hand to Subaru's agonized decision-making.
He casually crossed the carriage floor and approached Rui, standing beside Subaru. Reaching out to gently pat the golden-haired head of the girl—

"Having one kid along might actually make it easier to wave off suspicion if we hit another checkpoint like that. Traveling with a kid in tow ain't such a bad—OW!"

"Gauu—!"

Just before Al's hand touched her head, Rui, quite literally, bared her fangs.
Opening her mouth wide, she bit down hard on Al's outstretched hand. With a yelp, Al yanked his hand back, and Rui retreated behind Subaru, growling like a beast.
It was the sort of ungrateful act you'd expect from a feral cat biting the hand offering it food.

"Ow, damn that hurts! Tried to help and got burned for it!"

"Y-you actually did it...! So this is exactly the sort of thing you do—feeding off others, just like that!"

"Uu! Ah! Auu!"

"'Ah' and 'uu' don't explain a thing!"

Clinging to Subaru's waist, Rui shook her head furiously back and forth. Subaru struggled to pry her off, but the corset was too tight to move properly.
Fortunately, Subaru could tell, by "name," who Al was, and Al himself hadn't apparently lost himself after having his "memory" taken. But confirming the extent of the damage would have to wait—

"—Please, calm down, everyonE!!"

"Hyaaan!?"
"Uu!?"

As Subaru and Al's commotion grew louder, Rui, caught up in the noise, began wailing shrilly as if throwing a tantrum of her own.
Cutting through the chaos, it was Taritta who raised her voice and stepped in between them.
Sliding her hands under Rui's arms to lift the girl up, she leveled a gaze at Subaru and Al—sharp-eyed, much like her sister's.

"Grown adults, acting so unseemly in front of a child! PleasE, behave more like calm adults. The little one will copy yoU."

"Uh, gh... b-but, Taritta-san, this girl is—"

"No 'buts,' NatsumI. I understand your relationship with this child is complicateD. Even so, what good does it do to make such a fuss?"

Scolded so directly, the sheer simplicity of it hit Subaru hardest.
Told she was acting unbecoming of an adult toward a child, how could such plain reasoning be argued against? Except, such adorable logic held no sway over a Sin Archbishop.
If she knew the danger posed by Rui Arneb, surely Taritta too would voice her opinion—

—Then, why not tell everyone the truth of Rui's identity, right here and now?

"——"

Her throat closed at the murky emotion welling up from within her own chest.
At Subaru's stiffened expression, Taritta and Al exchanged puzzled looks.

In the end, all of this had been brought about by Subaru's own wavering resolve.
To make them understand the wariness she held toward Rui, there was no way but to reveal that Rui was the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony. Unlike Rem, who'd lost even her wariness toward the Witch Cult along with her "memory," the people gathered here would surely grasp the threat once told.
Once they knew the gravity of it, they'd surely stop treating Rui as the ordinary child she appeared to be. And then, to remove the destabilizing element—

Remove her—by doing what, exactly?

"——"

"Uu?"

Still held in Taritta's arms, Rui tilted her head at Subaru's gaze.
The moment Subaru and Al's commotion stopped, Rui calmed just as quickly. Over their time together, Subaru had come to understand: Rui made a fuss when the people around her were noisy.
When those around her were calm, Rui didn't try to make a racket on her own, either. Her good compatibility with Utakata was because Utakata was, exceptionally, a calm-natured child herself.

Was that appearance, too, a kind of camouflage—Rui Arneb's disguise for blending into her surroundings?
The malicious, irredeemably evil girl who had confronted Subaru in that white world she'd reached within the Pleiades Watchtower—was this composed demeanor simply the mask that girl wore?
He knew, rationally, that he ought to treat her as such and act accordingly. And yet—

"—I'll have you know, our purpose here is neither entertainment nor leisure."

Beside Subaru, still tormented, Abel—alone still seated—spoke coldly.
For a moment, Subaru couldn't grasp the true meaning behind his words, but soon understood it as Abel's opinion regarding Rui's treatment.
This journey was not a game, Abel declared flatly. Declared it, and the instant Subaru glimpsed the black eyes he turned on Rui, she caught her breath at their coldness.

There was not a shred of warmth in the eyes with which Abel regarded Rui.
That, in turn, was proof that Abel saw no value in extending any consideration or mercy toward Rui whatsoever—proof that he made no distinction between her and a roadside stone or wildflower.

The heat—or rather, the absence of heat—in those eyes made Subaru understand exactly what would happen if Rui's true identity were revealed.

"—I'll bring her along."

"——"

The instant she made that decision, Abel's black eyes shifted from Rui to Subaru. The sharpness of that gaze felt like it carved into her chest, but Subaru bit down on her back teeth and drove the feeling out of conscious thought.

"Since this isn't a pleasure trip, show us you're of use. ...That's the gist of it, isn't it?"

"Aye, I care not either way. —I expected you would say as much."

"...That's a hard evaluation to interpret."

Whether it carried some note of disappointment, or something else entirely, was impossible to tell.
Naturally, Abel made no move to reveal his true meaning, and Subaru, too, feared that pressing further might lead to a different, worse conclusion.

"Hey, hey, did you all settle things? If you're done, Rui-chan should come over here too!"

"...Is that all righT?"

Sensing the taut air between Subaru and Abel, Taritta asked for permission to respond to Medium's voice calling from the driver's seat.
Abel had already washed his hands of the matter entirely, and while Subaru had decided to bring Rui along, she had no desire to actually hold the girl's hand herself, so she chose to rely on their goodwill instead.

"Alright, Rui-chan, wanna hold the reins? Let's try steering Ready-chin together!"

"Uu!"

"Let's not do thaT!"

The driver's seat grew suddenly lively, and Rui appeared to be warmly welcomed by the two women.
Since Subaru was the only one who held such strong wariness toward Rui to begin with, their reaction wasn't particularly surprising. If anything, the one thing that nagged at her was whether Rui's absence had thrown Rem—left behind in Guaral—into a panic searching for her.

"I doubt that girl could have snuck into the carriage all on her own. If someone helped her, that person will surely have explained the circumstances by now."

"...That's true, I suppose."

Perhaps sensing the shape of Subaru's worry from her expression, Abel coldly dispelled it.
As he said, if Utakata had helped Rui hide away, that information would likely already be shared with Rem and the others in the city.
Though whether Subaru and Rui traveling together would actually ease Rem's worry was another question entirely.

"I wonder which is more reassuring to her—not knowing where Rui is, or..."

"—? Well, brother having her along's gotta be more reassuring, I'd think. Though from what Taritta-chan said, sounds like it's kind of complicated."

At the murmur Al happened to catch, Subaru's feelings grew complicated.
It wasn't something that could be resolved so simply—the relationship between Subaru, Rem, and Rui, the three of them.

"...But, well, I guess so."

Picturing Rem's face behind closed eyelids, Subaru found an excuse within herself.
Regardless of the rights and wrongs of Rui's true nature, Rem would surely worry about the girl. Whenever the right moment came to tell her the truth, doing so without Rui present would be only half the story.
So, Rui needed to stay safe and well.

"Brother?"

"No, it's nothing... not quite nothing, though. Al, I have a request for you too. —Please, would you keep an eye on Rui as much as you can?"

"...Setting aside how weird it is to be asked that, why? What's up with that runt?"

Having just had his hand bitten, Al too seemed to have developed a distaste for Rui. He responded to Subaru's request with a reluctant tone, questioning the true reason behind it.
A reasonable question, but for a moment, Subaru hesitated over her answer.

If it were Al, he'd probably believe her about Rui's true nature more readily than the others.
But if Al learned Rui was a Sin Archbishop, how would he act? Would he understand the danger and become a confidant who shared the same worry as Subaru?

"—. If that girl gets hurt, Rem will suffer for it. Please, I'm asking you."

"——"

For an instant, Subaru wavered, but in the end, concealed the truth just as she had with everyone else.
Hearing Subaru's answer, Al fell briefly silent, then—

"Ah, got it. Bit worried about my remaining arm getting eaten too, but I'll manage somehow."

With that answer, Al settled himself into the frontmost seat of the carriage as if to honor the request. Listening to the three girls making a racket up in the driver's seat, he sprawled his legs out lazily.
It was, in its own way, a very Al-like manner of accepting a favor.

"—? What is it?"

Glancing idly at the back of Al's head, Subaru suddenly noticed Abel's gaze on her.
Arms crossed, leaning back in his seat, he reflected Subaru in those unreadable black eyes, his brow faintly, almost imperceptibly, furrowed in what looked like displeasure.
It stirred up a deep unease in her, and her question came out sharper than intended.
But Abel merely gave a small "hmph" through his nose and said nothing.

"...In any case, I've yet to even hear a word of thanks for getting us safely through that checkpoint."

Directing her irritation at Abel's attitude, Subaru smoothed back her long black hair and settled into a seat set a little apart from both Al and Abel.
With unease about what lay ahead weighing on her, the road toward the magical capital of Chaosflame continued.
Still, listening to the cheerful voices centered around Rui drifting merrily from the driver's seat, she murmured—

"Honestly, whose fault does he think it is that I'm suffering like this."

—words of complaint that reached no one, swallowed instead by the sound of the carriage rolling on.
