Arc 4 Chapter 103: "The Beginning of the Sanctuary, and the Beginning of its Collapse"

"The memories are fragmentary, but I've put them in order as best I could, old fool that I am. I believe they follow roughly this sequence, though..."

"............"

To the halting words spoken from θ's mouth, Subaru answered with silence.

There was nothing else to do but stay silent. There was simply too much information to take in — he couldn't even begin to form words until he'd sorted through it all.

The origins of the Sanctuary. The era in which a girl named Ryuzu Meyer had lived. Which was to say, four hundred years ago — and in that place, as a matter of course, there had been a Witch.

The Witch of Greed. Echidna herself.

"Honestly, I can't picture an era where Echidna's just wandering around like it's normal."

"To a Su-bo who never knew that age, the Witch-sama's existence might seem a distant thing. Well, even to me she only feels close at hand — it isn't as though I knew her directly, mind."

"Talking about stuff you just picked up secondhand like you actually lived it — feels like the first sign of getting old. So... Echidna came to the Sanctuary a lot?"

"Well, they aren't truly my own memories, after all. But judging from the scenes I saw and the words exchanged, I'd guess it was fairly often."

Unlike θ, who had seen the memories firsthand, for Subaru — hearing it all secondhand — the story felt thin, lacking reality.

And of course, there was another point from θ's account that nagged at him—

"Beatrice and Roswaal were both in the Sanctuary four hundred years ago too..."

"Beatrice-sama, as I said just now, as Echidna-sama's daughter. As for Roz-bo — the Roz-bo who appears in this account is the founder of House Mathers... the very first Roswaal, the one who, in the truest sense, set House Mathers on the path to greatness. The name Roswaal, you understand, is passed down generation to generation."

"...Was he close with Bea-ko?"

"From what I saw, it seemed a heartwarming sort of relationship."

What rose in his mind from θ's telling was Beatrice exactly as he knew her — unable to be honest with herself.

Had that girl, unchanged since four hundred years ago, gone on being brusque and unable to be honest with others this whole time? Was that why, even now, four hundred years later, she still carried herself that way?

Never once showing her true feelings to anyone, keeping all manner of thoughts hidden away inside that small body.

Remembering the girl who stood in the Forbidden Archive, a sharp pang of feeling passed through Subaru's chest.

He pressed a hand to his chest, and, holding back the sentiment, shook his head.

"It's a surprise that Echidna used to take Beatrice around with her. From the way she talks, it didn't sound like Beatrice was someone she felt anything like family love for."

"I never met the Witch-sama in person myself, mind. But from what I glimpsed in Ryuzu Meyer's memories, the Witch-sama seemed to have something like human warmth in her."

"Yeah, somehow... I feel the same."

It didn't line up with the image of Echidna that Subaru knew, true enough. But four hundred years — even granting the divide between her life and her death — was still that much time passed.

Was it really so strange, that inside that dream castle, she might have come to see life with a certain detachment?

"She's someone I broke with completely and for good, and here I am still wanting to hope for something from her..."

Even to himself, it felt like an incorrigible weakness.

It wasn't like it had been with Ram or Rem. Echidna had understood Subaru's feelings, his hopes, and had judged it right to trample them anyway. There was no way, at this point, that a future where they joined hands could exist.

"Anyway, so far this is just a warm little trip down memory lane. Doesn't seem like the kind of terrifying development that θ-san would go out of her way to hide from everyone else."

"――――"

"Tell me what comes next. What happens to the Sanctuary — the one that was so peaceful on the surface?"

At Subaru's low-voiced request, θ sipped at tea that had gone completely cold.

Under her breath, just within her own mouth, she muttered, "Bad, this is..."

"What happens next, is it..."

"――――"

"Ruin. That's what happens. And you come to learn the true reason the Sanctuary was made."

"The true... reason...?"

At the weight of those words Subaru caught his breath, and θ gave a small nod.

Then, haltingly once more, as though prying open the lid on a memory, she narrowed her eyes.

"At that time too, the Witch-sama and the first Roswaal were in the Sanctuary. There was a strangely tense air about the place, as though something out of the ordinary was about to happen — and it wasn't just me who felt uneasy about it. Everyone in the Sanctuary did."

—A heavy tension hung in the air, and Ryuzu felt the illusion of her throat going dry.

"We should flee this place immediately. The preparations aren't ready yet. —If he learns the Sanctuary's location now, it means the collapse of the entire plan."

"――――"

"Sensei! Even this time we're spending now is precious! He's— he's already almost here!"

The one slamming the table inside the cabin, raising his voice, was a slender-faced boy.

On the face of the boy who always carried himself with composure and elegance, there was now an urgent, carved-in impatience.

Listening to his appeal with her eyes quietly closed, hands folded together, was the Witch Echidna. The boy leaned forward at her silence, appealing again with a broad sweep of his hand.

"There's no time to hesitate! His power is overwhelming! I still can't be of any real use to you, Sensei...! If you told me to be your shield, I would gladly become one. But as long as we haven't yet established a way to counter him, wouldn't volunteering as a shield just mean dying for nothing—"

"It's not that there's no way. —I already have some idea of how, to a certain degree."

Cutting off the boy — Roswaal's — words, Echidna opened her eyes and glared down at the grain of the tabletop.

At her words Roswaal let out a breath, dumbstruck, saying "Eh—" and Echidna gave a quiet shake of her head.

"I've come to the Sanctuary again and again to build the theory. As for conditioning the barrier, it should work with fairly high probability."

"Th-then...!"

"—Only, the 'core' needed to activate the barrier is missing."

"――――"

Roswaal, whose face had just found hope, caught his breath at the pained note in Echidna's voice.

"Without a core, the barrier won't activate. And without the barrier, it's impossible to keep him away from this place. If we can't secure a safe zone, sooner or later he'll find us and destroy everything."

"That's why we took such careful, painstaking time to prepare the Sanctuary like this... and yet, after coming this far... when we're just one step away!"

Groaning in frustration, Roswaal slammed his fist against the table.

The legs of the old table creaked, and blood welled up on Roswaal's hand.

Silence fell over the cabin.

Time seemed to slow, and the air filled with a heaviness that felt almost mucus-thick.

Within it, a girl timidly raised her hand.

"Um... this 'core' for the barrier... could it not be me?"

"――――"

"I've heard, from before, that it might turn out this way. That among the conditions for the barrier Echidna-sama would build, I was the one who matched them. ...That was why, I thought, you had taken such an interest in me."

"—From Beatrice, was it?"

"Yes."

The one dipping her chin with quiet resolve was Ryuzu Meyer, her pale-pink hair grown long.

Her cheeks stiff with resolve, the girl looked straight at Echidna, whose expression had gone unreadable.

"Beatrice-sama told me that you, Echidna-sama, had been confirming whether I matched the conditions. The times over these past few months when my mana was extracted — that must have been part of it too."

At Ryuzu's question, after a faint pause, Echidna gave a small nod.

"It's true — your compatibility for constructing the barrier is high. If we keep you within the Sanctuary, maintaining the barrier should be possible. I've completed the theory for that. Given more time, to let the Sanctuary's soil and your mana grow accustomed to one another, it should work."

"But it's not possible yet, is it. Not now."

"This isn't just any barrier. This one can't be allowed to be broken. I've proceeded with the utmost care, the utmost caution. Keeping the halfbloods I've spent years gathering within the Sanctuary, conditioning the barrier around them — your existence is meant to be the decisive push behind that. But..."

He cut himself off, and Roswaal's face was one of barely-contained frustration.

Ryuzu didn't understand the finer details, but it seemed that even with clever people like Echidna and Roswaal working together, a difficult obstacle stood in the way of completing the plan.

Was there really no way to solve it?

—No, there probably wasn't, Ryuzu judged, drawing on what little experience of life she had.

"Isn't there... some decisive method?"

"――――"

"...I am someone who was saved by you, Echidna-sama, and by you, Roswaal-sama. Coming to this land, I've been able to live a life where no one scorns me, no one shuns me — and I've been happy. If I can repay even a little of the time I was given, then I believe that is where the meaning of my life lies."

Bit by bit, she laid her heart bare.

At Ryuzu's words, spoken with her white hands clenched so tight they went whiter still, the warmth drained from Echidna's black eyes as if freezing over. In her place, it was Roswaal, standing beside Echidna, who was tossed about by a storm of complicated emotion.

"S-sensei..."

It wasn't a call meant to leave the decision to Echidna. It was a call that carried the nuance of surely not, surely you wouldn't.

But either way, the trigger would be pulled.

"—If we crystallize your Od and make it the very core of the Sanctuary itself, we can shorten the process of acclimating the mana to the soil. The barrier would be completed."

"If we do that, will the Sanctuary be saved?"

"Avoiding devastation from the threat closing in on this place — yes, that should be possible. If we can just buy time, we should be able to work out countermeasures too."

"――――"

Echidna's answer wasn't empty comfort. She never spoke in wishful thinking, never offered idle reassurance.

If Echidna judged that something could be done, then it could be done.

Which meant that offering up her own life here, in this place, held certain, real meaning in protecting this land. That her devotion — her repayment — would surely be fulfilled.

"...When do we begin?"

"—If possible, I want to begin preparations right away. I'll prepare an anchor for the crystallization on this end, and then build up the formula. As for buying time against the threat..."

"That would be my role. I'll do everything within my power. ...Ryuzu-kun."

Roswaal, who had worn a grief-stricken face, looked up. There was no longer any trace of weakness left in his expression. He looked straight into the eyes of Ryuzu, who had steeled her resolve, and said:

"I'm sorry. It's my own lack of strength that's failing to help Sensei."

"No. Roswaal-sama, you too are someone I owe a debt to, for giving me time I could never replace. I'm only grateful for it — there's nothing in it for me to resent."

Ryuzu pressed a hand to her chest and shook her head.

Roswaal drew a small breath, let it out, and looked at Echidna.

"I'll head out right away. Sensei, please handle the preparations, and... call for Beatrice."

"...Wouldn't it be better not to tell Beatrice?"

"If we don't call Beatrice here now, both you and I will spend the rest of our lives resented by that child. ...Though even if we do call her, that might still be true."

"Is that so. ...Understood. I'll summon her afterward."

Having watched Echidna nod, Roswaal turned toward the door of the cabin. On his way out, he set a hand on Ryuzu's shoulder and gave it one firm squeeze.

Feeling the faint press of his fingers, Ryuzu let herself take in the fact that Roswaal grieved to lose her, and closed her eyes.

"...Beatrice-sama."

A word, murmured softly.

Thinking of that girl, absent from this place, Ryuzu's chest gave a small, aching creak.

Once more, the scene shifted.

"――――"

—The moment she witnessed its overwhelming might firsthand, something more terrifying than the resolve to face death took root in Ryuzu's heart, and would not be uprooted.

"—Gh, hff—"

Letting out a groan of pain, spitting up a clot of blood, Roswaal's body flew horizontally across the ground.

Landing shoulder-first, tumbling and kicking up a cloud of dust, his figure left Ryuzu with no choice but to stare on in blank shock, forgetting even to breathe.

Roswaal L. Mathers — one who wielded all six colors of magic, and who, while still in his mid-teens, had climbed to nearly the highest peak of magecraft attainable by the human race.

With overwhelming magical power he could scorch the earth to ash, with blades of wind he could shred even stone walls to pieces, the currents of water he called forth could push back the flow of a great river, and with earth and stone under his command he could raise up even a castle.

And yet here stood a presence of power so overwhelming that even one who wielded such strength as his could not stand against it.

"...Still going, huh?"

Still wearing that languid air, the boy walked along, his dull, tawny-brown head swaying.

He looked to be about the same age as Roswaal. His hair, a brown so dark it was near black, hung just long enough for the bangs to brush his brows, and his features were fine enough to be mistaken for a woman's. His eyes, narrowed in a drowsy, half-lidded way, were black, and he wore a white shirt and black trousers — an exceedingly plain getup for the person he was.

—And every time he took a single step, idly kicking a pebble as if to pass the time, Roswaal's body jerked, spraying blood. Jerked. Was flung.

"Gah! Gh! Gohh!"

"Noisy. Annoying. Irritating. Bothersome. Draining. Depressing."

Muttering in a low tone, the boy strung together one negative word after another. And yet, with every murmur, every step he took, Roswaal's groans deepened in color, and the creak of bone reached even from a distance.

Roswaal's fallen body sank into the ground, as if the very air above were pressing down to crush him. In fact, his limbs were already half-buried in the earth, his flesh split open, and from his bloodshot eyes tears of blood streamed.

"You can quit now, you know? You did good. You can't beat me, but you tried, you really tried. That's enough, isn't it — you tried. ...Not that trying does any good anyway."

"Th-that's... foolish... If I don't stop you... here... why would I ever... guh! Aah! Gyaaahh!!"

"Haaah... That's exactly the kind of thing that makes my head heaviest. Turns my stomach. Sinks my mood."

At Roswaal's refusal to heed his call for surrender, the boy folded his knees and crouched down where he stood. Letting out a deep sigh, he touched the ground as though stroking it. Following the movement of his fingertips, the limbs of the fallen Roswaal crumpled, twisted, and the sound of tearing flesh rose along with a scream.

"Ugh, I really hate this. It's dragging me way down. Feeling this low — it's been so long since I felt this low, it's honestly the worst. The worst, the worst, worst of the worst of the worst of the worst. —Truly, melancholy."

"Ga, ahh—!"

At the end of the boy's words — words that dragged one's spirits down just to hear them — the instant that decisive word was murmured, Roswaal's torso, unable to bear the pressure any longer, crushed inward.

The middle of his torso went flat, and he vomited so much blood it seemed as though his very organs had been spat out through his mouth. Roswaal's eyes rolled back to white, his limbs convulsing. The price for a will to fight that had never once wavered to the very end was the grinding-out of the young mage's lifeline.

"Ahh, ahh, ah-h. What is this. Wha-a-at is this. This state? This is how it ends? Ahh, I really didn't want this. My stomach's turning. My mood's sunk. My head's heavy. Melancholy. Melancholy, melancholy, gloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloomgloom—"

Blood dripped audibly from Roswaal, who no longer even groaned. Gazing at his battered body with a bored expression, the boy laid a gloomy irritation into his murmurs.

Roswaal's ferocious end, and the sheer, boundless otherness of the boy who had brought it about. Ryuzu, who had had no choice but to simply watch, only now — with things come to this — remembered she had forgotten to breathe.

"—Hah."

Her lungs, wrung out to their limit, worked to draw oxygen for her brain and body. One breath, to take oxygen into her body, and then she had to hide again within silence. Even a single tremor in the air felt as though it might brush the edge of that boy's awareness, and it terrified her.

Even as she watched a man she owed everything to crushed mercilessly before her eyes, rather than avenge him she chose to protect herself. Even her own wretched attachment to life was, right now, beyond her own comprehension.

"Ahh? Is there maybe someone over there?"

"—!"

As if to confirm exactly what Ryuzu feared, the boy tilted his head, and his gaze turned this way.

Ryuzu had been watching the clash between Roswaal and the boy in the clearing from inside a cabin set a little apart, peering through a knot in the wall's timber, watching through the narrowest of gaps.

That the boy's remark seemed to have grasped that meager gap from such a distance sent a shudder through her. There was no way he could have found her. The knothole was smaller than the width of a cat's forehead. There was no way, none at all, he could have caught sight of it.

And yet the boy came walking toward her with a stride that betrayed not a moment's hesitation, saying:

"It's not like I really want to do this, you know? Even if I killed everyone here, it's not like anything go-o-od comes of it. ...It'd help if you spared me the trouble, though."

"...Hih."

"Hmm-m. Well, about there, I guess... nah, might as well take the whole lot at once. Ugh, I really just want to throw all of this away right now and go home. My stomach's turning. My mood's sinking. Melancholy."

The boy turned his palm toward the cabin where Ryuzu was, pronouncing her death sentence in a gloomy murmur.

In that instant a chill raced up Ryuzu's spine, and a sharp pain like a needle driving into the crown of her head shot through her. Tears welled at the corners of her eyes, and an unbearable scream choked her throat.

Just like that, an invisible weight began to crush Ryuzu's small body, bones and all—

"Al... Goa!!"

A roar that seemed to spit blood — no, that literally spat blood — rose up, and an explosion of flame dyed the clearing crimson.

Even at a distance, an immense heat that seemed to scorch the face sprang into being in an instant, originating from the upraised palm of Roswaal — still down on the ground — and aiming to burn the boy to cinders from behind.

"――――"

Even that boy showed a flicker of unease before the overwhelming wave of heat, turning to look. But even having turned to face that hellfire, it was no violence on a scale that human wisdom could reach.

Just like that, powerless to stop it, the boy's body was swallowed into red light.

"Sweat's unpleasant. —Irritating."

At the word murmured just before it struck, even the concentrated crimson mana was struck down to the earth.

The white-hot sphere that should have burned the boy and erased him from the world without even leaving a shadow, without so much as dispersing its heat into the surroundings, shrank into a small round red lump and rolled along the ground.

"Ah, uh, hff..."

"Still hasn't gone out, huh — how much power did you put into this. Don't make me use more strength than I have to. The more I do, the more I want to die, you know."

Grumbling, the boy clenched the raised palm of his hand tight. As if obeying the gesture, the shrunken red sphere on the ground imploded all at once.

A dry crack rang out once, high, as the heat snapped against the air, and with that the energy scattered completely into nothing.

Not even a blow from Al Goa — the highest tier of fire mana magic — could reach him.

Roswaal, who had staked everything on a last, desperate gambit even while at death's door, stared in shock at the outcome. This — right here, in front of him — was the result of every last drop of strength he had wrung out.

Ryuzu, too, whose moment of death had been deflected by the narrowest of margins, felt keenly that Roswaal's death, and her own, had only been postponed by the barest sliver of time.

"Demon... no, Warlock, damn you...!"

"That's an unpleasant thing to be called, it drags my mood down. You think I chose this — chose to become like thi-i-is?"

"Whatever, whatever way your life was twisted... out of what limited choices you had... it was you who chose this moment. Don't put on the face of a victim... Hector of 'Melancholy'!"

"Hearing what's right stings the ears, makes it awkward. Really, I ju-u-ust can't stand you."

Crouching beside the fallen Roswaal, the boy reached his palm out toward Roswaal's head.

That much devastation, and he hadn't even touched him yet. If his fingertips made contact, and from there his invisible destruction transmitted itself, Roswaal's body would be crushed beyond any recognizable shape.

"Guh... Ul..."

"Too slow. Won't make it in time. Not gonna let you."

The boy said it flatly, cutting ahead of Roswaal, who was gathering mana to activate a spell.

And then those death-bearing fingertips reached Roswaal, delivering certain death into his dying body.

"—Ru, bh—"

"Bones, rattled to pieces. Guts, mashed to mush. Heart, cracked clean through. How's that for a fini-i-ish?"

A brief cry of pain. That was Roswaal's death rattle.

Looking down at Roswaal, now utterly unmoving, the boy dusted off his knees and stood. Then, this time for certain, he turned to face Ryuzu, who had been unable to move at all.

This time, without so much as a warning, he turned his palm toward her and unleashed that invisible weight.

"—!"

There was no enduring it, not even for a single second.

Pressure bore down from directly overhead, far exceeding her own body weight. That she was able to fall forward onto the ground was pure luck — had her posture been even slightly different, her joints would have been bent in directions they were never meant to go, forced flat against the earth regardless.

"If you can't resist, you're not Echidna. If you're not that ki-i-id, then it doesn't matter anymore."

"—Ah, hih."

"Just get crushed like this, buried in the dirt. Save yourself the trouble of digging your own grave—"

The illusion of unseen hands gripping the skin of her entire body, on the verge of tearing it from the ground itself. Just as she thought the boy's voice, heard distantly, might be the last words she'd ever hear in this world, the weight abruptly vanished.

Gasping for breath, her face streaked with tears and drool, Ryuzu lifted her head to see what had happened. And there, in the direction of her gaze—

"This is hardly a situation I'd call having made it in time."

"No-o, not really. Your disciple held me up, put up a plucky little fight to buy time. Thanks to that, nothing went the way I wanted, and my mood's been thoroughly soured."

"That way of speaking — you really haven't changed at all. Just the same as when we parted."

"Your way of talking hasn't improved any either. Why'd you go and start talking in such an unlovable way? You used to be so cu-u-ute."

The boy shook his head as if grieving. Standing before him, blocking the space between the boy and Ryuzu, was a pale woman clad in black — Echidna.

The Witch turned her eyes to the fallen Roswaal, narrowing them just slightly.

"An unexpectedly painful sight, this. Even though failing to stay objective about the results I've witnessed is, to me, a shameful sort of confusion."

"Processing it all coolly with just your feelings — seems like the kid wouldn't rest easy that wa-a-ay, in this case. Not that I'd know. If you want to cry, take that much time at least. I'm not that cold-hearted."

"And you say that with what face, I wonder."

The words they exchanged were thorny; and while it was clear the two of them were acquainted, it was equally clear, even to an onlooker, that theirs was in no way a friendly relationship.

Bit by bit, they faced off, gauging the distance between them. Ryuzu did not doubt Echidna's strength — but even Roswaal, who had believed in that strength just as much, had been crushed with ease before this overwhelming power.

Given that, not even Echidna's title could be declared, with any certainty, a source of comfort.

"—How much longer do you intend to lie there so pitifully, I wonder."

"...Eh?"

Abruptly, a hand pulled up Ryuzu, who lay face-down with her head raised, from behind. Drawn by that force, Ryuzu let out a startled sound and turned around.

There stood a lovely girl wearing the sullen expression Ryuzu knew so well.

"Be-Beatrice-sama..."

"This is no time to be making dazed noises, I suppose. While Mother's buying us time like that, we should be getting away from here, and quickly, I suppose."

"B-but... Roswaal-sama and Echidna-sama told me to wait here..."

"It's that Roswaal's blunder that got us sniffed out by him in the first place, I suppose. Never mind that — just follow Betty, I suppose. Mother's orders were to bring you along, I suppose."

"Echidna-sama's...?"

Beatrice knit her brow in irritation, but even her expression was, understandably, stiff with tension. Even she, usually so headstrong and full of confidence, was being overwhelmed by the presence of that unfathomable boy.

And still, she was far stronger than Ryuzu, who could only crouch here trembling, making herself small.

"'The preparations are ready.' That's what Mother said, I suppose. She said just telling you that much, you'd understand, I suppose."

"—I... understand."

At the message relayed from Echidna, Ryuzu held her breath ever so slightly and nodded. Beatrice narrowed her eyes suspiciously at Ryuzu's reaction, but there was no time to press the matter.

Behind the two of them, a torrent of mana was swelling. It was only a matter of time before Echidna and that boy clashed outright. The outcome of that unpredictable battle — to draw it, with certainty, toward victory — Ryuzu's resolve mattered above all else.

"Let's go. Beatrice-sama, where are the preparations?"

"...In the old stone room, further in, I suppose. Mother told me to, so I carried it over, but even for Betty's Door-crossing it was a rough job, I suppose."

Speaking of her own achievement without quite saying so, Beatrice took Ryuzu's hand and set off.

Following the bounce of Beatrice's hair, Ryuzu glanced back one last time at Echidna, and bowed her head to a back that paid her no mind at all.

—For surely, she would never again have the chance to exchange words with her.

It was a crystal, clear and blue, beautiful enough to make one tremble.

"Don't get entranced and go touching it without thinking, I suppose. Wherever you touch, you'll be swallowed up from there, and turned into part of this crystal, I suppose."

Forgetting even the situation she was in, Ryuzu let slip a breath heavy with fascination. Beside her, arms crossed, Beatrice warned her against any careless move.

Ryuzu, who had very nearly done exactly the careless thing described, hastily pulled back the fingers she'd half-extended at the warning, and said, "Ah, I'm sorry."

"It's nothing you need to apologize for, I suppose. ...So, what happens from here, I wonder. Betty was only told to carry this crystal here and bring you along, I suppose — nothing more."

"Beatrice-sama, how did you carry this crystal here...?"

"Once you're as good as Betty, moving something this size without touching it is easy enough, I suppose. Even Mother praises Betty's Door-crossing, both for precision and for distance, I suppose."

Beatrice wore her usual composed face, but there was something proud about her all the same, and Ryuzu's cheeks loosened despite herself.

She had, by now, grown quite used to exchanging words with her like this. In the beginning, she remembered, she used to take Beatrice's remarks entirely at face value and end up mortified, over and over.

But after spending some time together, she'd found that the true heart of this seemingly high-handed girl was, surprisingly, easy to read — and once she understood that, they could laugh together, because underneath it all Beatrice really was just as adorable as she looked.

How wonderful it would be if she could simply go on talking with Beatrice like this, the way they always did.

Forgetting all about the calamity bearing down on the Sanctuary, forgetting the fate waiting for Ryuzu, and just staying like this— though of course, no such thing could ever be.

"...? You're smiling right now with a really unpleasant look on your face, I suppose."

Perhaps her sentiment had shown through, for sharp-eyed Beatrice pointed it out to her.

That she could tell the difference between this smile and her usual one meant Beatrice had been watching her, truly watching her, closely enough to know. The moment Ryuzu realized that, tears welled at the corners of her eyes.

Beatrice's eyes went wide. Ryuzu hastily wiped the tears away with her sleeve.

"I— I'm sorry...! Just a little something, in my eye..."

"It's— it's not something Betty's bothered by, I suppose. —Betty knows well enough that this is the kind of situation where telling you not to be afraid would be the harder thing to ask, I suppose."

It was off the mark, but there was no mistaking it for anything other than Beatrice's own kind of consideration.

Warmth spread deep in her chest. To be given so much strength by her words — that, right now, felt like something to be proud of.

"Beatrice-sama."

"What is it, I wonder. If your preparations are taking too long, Betty will go lend Mother a hand too, I suppose. That Roswaal looked like he was about to die anyway — even a fool like him needs saving..."

"Thank you, for such a very long time, for everything you've done for me. But— this is where we say goodbye."

"—Eh?"

Beatrice let out a hoarse sound, blinking her eyes in evident incomprehension.

Inside the cold stone room, Beatrice and Ryuzu — two girls — faced each other.

Beatrice blinked again and again, glaring at Ryuzu who looked straight back at her. Sharp as that gaze was, Ryuzu, who knew the warmth beneath it, did not so much as flinch.

"What do you mean, goodbye, I wonder. Are you saying you're running away?"

"No, that's not it. If I fled and lived on somewhere else, I might someday be able to see you again, Beatrice-sama. But this goodbye is one for this life. I won't have another chance, ever again, to speak with you like this."

"..........."

Pressing her lips together, Beatrice searched Ryuzu's eyes as if trying to probe her true meaning.

Faced with a confusion in Beatrice that she had never seen before, Ryuzu quietly chose her next words.

"The preparations Echidna-sama spoke of are for casting a barrier around this Sanctuary. Ordinarily, it would need time — time for the barrier to take root in the soil of the Sanctuary, or so I'm told. But because of what's happening now, there isn't enough time left for that."

"Not enough time... the barrier won't be ready in time? Are you saying that barrier was the means needed to keep that man away, I suppose?"

"Yes. I saw for myself, clearly, how dangerous that man is. He's a real danger. Now I understand why Echidna-sama would defeat every last obstacle to bring him down. And Roswaal-sama's devotion, too — he must have known that nothing less than what he did could save Echidna-sama."

That was how overwhelming that boy had been.

Ryuzu didn't know exactly how, in the end, activating the barrier would benefit Echidna, or how it would lead to a conclusion where that being was eradicated.

But there was one thing, and one thing only, that Echidna had promised her.

"Echidna-sama assured me that as long as the barrier is activated, it would be possible to protect this Sanctuary. ...And so, I will offer up this body of mine for that purpose."

"D-don't say such foolish things, I suppose. Offering up your body — what, how could you, someone with no aptitude for magic at all, even..."

With a flicker of panic in her eyes, Beatrice began to say it in a rush. But clever as she was, partway through putting her own words into shape, she stumbled, unintended, upon the answer to her own question.

Her eyes went wide with shock, and she turned to look at the blue crystal standing beside the two of them.

"They're going to bind your very existence to the core of this crystal... spread it out as the central Od across the whole of the Sanctuary... so that there's no need to spend time letting it settle in...?"

"Yes. That's what Echidna-sama said as well."

It was the conclusion they had reached, in discussion with Echidna and Roswaal, before the attack had even begun.

Beatrice stood frozen, wordless now. Ryuzu stepped around so as to enter her field of view, and smiled at her.

"My affinity with the Sanctuary's mana... that's something you yourself vouched for, Beatrice-sama."

"—!"

At Ryuzu's words, Beatrice's head snapped up as if struck.

She bit down on her pink lips, a faint trace of blood welling up on her skin, and said:

"Th-that's not... Betty never— Betty never meant for it to be like that, when she told Mother... wait, no, wait, I suppose. Wait, I suppose. B-Betty will go argue with Mother directly, I suppose. Mother's a soft touch when it comes to Betty, so surely she'll listen if I just talk to her..."

"There's no time for that now. This has to be decided this very moment."

"Then Betty will go help Mother right now, I suppose. If Mother and Betty work together, a guy like that should go down easy, I suppose. And Roswaal too — heal him up quick, and with three of us together—"

Shaking her head no, no, Beatrice's words trailed off into nothing.

Beatrice herself had realized just how unconvincing her own words were.

Beatrice truly was amazing. Ryuzu deeply admired the fact that a girl her own age could wield magic with such skill, and never once neglected her daily training.

Ryuzu had watched her, all this while — between doing the laundry, sewing, cooking — watched her adore her mother, bicker constantly with Roswaal, and dart about nearby, throwing herself into magical training.

Having spent such dense, close time together, Beatrice understood her own strength correctly — and precisely because of that, she understood, clearly, the gap in power between herself and that enemy.

She could not bring herself to make a decision that would risk her mother's life on comfort that wasn't even comforting.

"—Betty's Door-crossing — everyone could just escape from here through it, I suppose."

"..........."

"See? Let's do that, I suppose. It'll take a bit of a push, but Betty can manage that much, I suppose. While Mother's buying us time, gather up everyone in the Sanctuary and get them to Mother's mansion, I suppose. Grab Roswaal when there's an opening, and once Mother and Betty go through the Door too, we'll have gotten away from him entirely. ...Yes, this is the way to go, I suppose."

"And then, having fled, will we go on living in fear of being hunted down by that man again? Give up this place of peace we finally reached, after being shunned by so many people — and start all over somewhere new? How long do you think it would take to build a time like this again?"

To Beatrice, who was working out an alternative, Ryuzu shook her head, and her words, though gentle, were severe.

Seeing hurt spread across the girl's face, a sharp pain ran through Ryuzu's chest.

She had to trample, had to kick aside, the kindness of a girl who worried this much for them, for Ryuzu herself, and push her own will through regardless.

What a cruel, selfish thing to do — a betrayal, it felt, of all the days that had come before.

—With the accumulation of all those past days, she would betray the feelings of all those past days.

"Beatrice-sama. I love the Sanctuary. I'm truly glad I was able to live in this place. I love the smiles of everyone who lives here. I don't want to lose that."

"――――"

"I've already been given more than enough warmth. For someone with such tainted blood running through her as me, it was a happiness beyond what I deserved. And so, I'm already content."

"That can't be true, I suppose... N-no matter how you all felt about this place, the true meaning of it isn't what you all think it..."

"Yes. I know."

"—!"

Ryuzu nodded to Beatrice, whose face showed regret the instant those words had slipped from her mouth.

She had known. The true meaning of this Sanctuary.

She had known well enough that Echidna and Roswaal hadn't gathered outcasts like themselves, from all the various races, purely out of goodwill.

A paradise where even those who had been shunned and scorned could live with their heads held high — she hadn't been living her life clinging to such a hope as mere surface comfort, but there had been a part of her that wanted to believe it all the same.

Now, in this very moment, it was a sentiment she understood, together with a certain resignation — that there was no more use in gazing only at what shone bright.

"This place exists for the purpose of somehow dealing with that man who's after Echidna-sama, doesn't it."

"..........."

"That this place exists for that reason, that we exist for that reason — I understand that now."

"Then... if you understand that, then why—"

Beatrice shook her head, unable to understand.

At the near-pleading look in Beatrice's eyes, Ryuzu's lips relaxed into something soft.

"It's all right. Maybe that's how it began. But not every moment we spent here was caught up entirely in Echidna-sama's designs. The time I spent here, the conversations I had with you, Beatrice-sama — not all of it, not every single piece, was like that."

"――――"

"What matters isn't how something begins. It's how it ends, and everything you feel along the way to that end."

"――――"

"My time here was happy. And so I will go, in order to protect that time. To you, Beatrice-sama, again and again, and now as well, I give my thanks for all your countless kindnesses."

From somewhere far off beyond the stone room, a roar echoed.

Shaking the ground, trembling the very air, it was the aftershock of the clash between Echidna and the boy, taking place at the heart of the Sanctuary.

Faint, yet unmistakable, and drawing gradually closer — it was proof, more than anything else, that Echidna, fighting to keep him from reaching this place, was losing ground.

"—!"

Ryuzu closed her eyes, her quiet resolve set. Before her, Beatrice's shoulders rose and fell as she desperately worked her mind, searching for words.

Some magic words that might break Ryuzu's resolve, turn her feelings around, overturn her decision.

But no such convenient magic existed anywhere in this world.

"Beatrice-sama."

"...What is it, I wonder."

"Please be careful not to eat too many sweets, all right?"

"――――"

She was a girl who could never stop reaching for tea sweets during tea time, and it would be such a shame if she got plump and ruined how cute she was, when she was already so lovely. And her teeth — Ryuzu hoped she'd keep them properly clean, too.

She rarely showed it, but when she smiled, she truly was the most adorable girl.

Turning, Ryuzu approached the blue crystal without another word.

The crystal's deep glow was enough to entrance anyone — a light that would truly, if touched, swallow her whole.

Would there be pain? Suffering?

Even having resolved herself to her own end, she didn't know what form it would take when it came.

If she had to put it simply: she was afraid. That much, at least, was true.

When she was swallowed into this light, she would be able to make this Sanctuary real.

If, in that real world, everyone could go on living gently, peacefully, with kind hearts.

If Echidna and Beatrice would go on watching over such a Sanctuary from here on.

"――――"

Suddenly, she felt a tug at her sleeve.

When Ryuzu turned, Beatrice was standing right beside her.

She wore an expression Ryuzu had never seen on her before, tugging at her sleeve with uncertain fingertips.

The pinch of her fingers was faint, and surely even Beatrice herself didn't know what she wanted, reaching out and touching her like this. And yet that hand, which had reached out despite herself, seemed to express the true feelings of this girl who could never put her emotions honestly into words.

"――――"

Gently, Ryuzu loosened Beatrice's fingers from where they gripped her sleeve.

From where their fingertips touched, each other's warmth passed between them, and at the end, Ryuzu let a smile rise to her face.

And then—

"Thank you. —Goodbye, Betty."

—With those words as her last, Ryuzu's consciousness was swallowed into the blue light.

And vanished.
