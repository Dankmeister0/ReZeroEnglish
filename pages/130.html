Arc 3 Chapter 53: "The Cards Dealt"

The hall was filled with silence, and a taut, straining tension.

Tasting that tension against his skin, Subaru wet his dry lips with his tongue, and first clenched his fist at the fruit of the actions that had arranged the first stage of the situation. As a precondition, what mattered most to Subaru now was that the people gathered here in this place were all present and accounted for.

He had no strength. His wisdom fell short. If there was anything a man lacking in ability and connections could do, it was only this: not letting his deaths until now go to waste.

"It seems I'm finally starting to understand the reason you had us delay supper to gather here."

Seated in her chair, hands folded on her knees, the beauty in men's clothing—Crusch Karsten—broke the silence, murmuring this with a look of understanding rising on her dignified face. The First Knight standing beside her narrowed his eyes slightly at his liege's words, puffing out his lovely cheeks lightly, and said,

"Is that so-o? Honestly, Feri-chan's still a bit skeptical, nyan. What could've happened to make such a spineless boy suddenly get eyes like that, nya~?"

Though his tone and expression feigned a joking air, the gaze he—Ferris—turned on Subaru as he spoke held no carelessness whatsoever. Even if his strength fell short, his resolve to keep his liege far from danger came through more than clearly.

"—————"

Still keeping his silence, not joining the exchange between lord and knight, was—opposite Ferris—Wilhelm, standing with his back straight at Crusch's left.

A sword at his hip, eyes closed, only a honed edge of swordsman's intent drifted from him now; not a trace remained of the kindly-old-man air with which he'd greeted Subaru and Rem on their return. Now, as a public figure, he was wholly absorbed in his role as the single blade his liege Crusch wielded.

The setting was the upper reaches of the royal capital's noble quarter, in the annex House Karsten used during stays in the capital. Within a residence whose decor had, in keeping with its master's wishes, been kept as free of ostentation as possible, this was the reception hall, appropriately adorned to welcome guests.

That the aforementioned three, all connected to the household, stood arrayed there was only natural. And as for the faces in the hall besides them—

"Coming back after being sent off is a rather uncomfortable thing. I do hope you'll give me a story, Subaru-dono, that dispels this discomfort of mine."

He must have noticed Subaru's eyes casually sweeping over everyone's faces. The gentle-looking man who smiled thus at Subaru's sidelong glance, with dusty blond hair and a fashionably trimmed chin beard, was Russell Fellow—the shrewd operator who worked the abacus of the capital's commerce.

At Russell's opening gambit—which amounted to a probing jab—Subaru shrugged and said,

"Right now Rem's gone to call one more person, so bear with me a bit longer. It's not certain they'll come, but... I've got good odds."

"I'll look forward to their prompt arrival, then. Incidentally, might I ask the basis for those good odds?"

He'd more or less already guessed the identity of the person Subaru was waiting on.

To Russell's question, Subaru shook his head and said, "It's simple," before continuing,

"You said yourself you've got a nose for the smell of money. If that's true, they'll definitely show their face. You're the same type, aren't you, Russell-san?"

"Well, well... you've caught me on a sore spot."

Russell put a hand to his forehead, acting as though he'd been outmaneuvered. Of course, this exchange was made with the understanding that each of their hands was, to some degree, already visible to the other.

Any sense of security taken at face value would surely be nothing but a fiction; and in the first place, Subaru had neither the skill nor the composure for that sort of subtle maneuvering.

For now, all that supported Subaru was borrowed courage, and nothing more.

"Everyone, thank you so much for waiting."

Just a few minutes later, the one who opened the hall's door and appeared was the blue-haired girl in a maid's uniform—Rem. She bowed her head so everyone in the room could see, then sent a glance toward Subaru and said,

"O-kay."

Making a small circle with the fingers of her right hand as she said this, Rem walked up beside Subaru, rose slightly on her toes, and whispered into his ear,

"They say their arrival will be a bit delayed, but that they will certainly come."

"I see. Alright, good work, Rem."

Snapping his fingers at the report, Subaru nodded, having gained the means to turn a second-best situation into the best one. Then, surveying the assembled group waiting,

"Word is our last participant's arrival will be a little delayed, but the cast is assembled regardless. No sense keeping everyone waiting any longer. —Let's begin."

At Subaru's declaration, each of them, sensing the situation was about to change, showed their own reaction.

Crusch smiled faintly; Ferris pressed her—his—lips into a firm line. Wilhelm remained utterly silent, his expression unchanging, while Russell settled back leisurely into his chair.

Bathed in the full weight of their gazes, Subaru stamped one foot loudly and drew his own spirit taut.

He could feel his heart beating high and hard. Blood coursed through his entire body, and at the same time a great unease reared its head, threatening to darken his vision. But—

"Subaru-kun."

Gently, Rem, standing beside him, touched his sleeve as if to reassure Subaru in his unease. It wasn't skin against skin, only contact through cloth—and yet Subaru felt from it a sense of security as if he'd received the aid of ten thousand allies.

Rem was watching. There was no way he could do anything uncool now, of all times. Grinning fearlessly, hiding his terror behind that smile, Subaru challenged the first wall.

To slip through conditions as narrow as the eye of a needle, and spin out a happy ending. To draw even one step closer to the hero that the girl who had told him she loved him believed in.

"There is one thing I would like to confirm, Natsuki Subaru."

Crusch's voice called out to Subaru, who had steeled himself and looked forward, one finger raised. She waved that raised finger side to side, and, catching Subaru's gaze, said,

"The purpose of this gathering. —From your own mouth, that is."

Her keen gaze rested on Subaru, elbow propped on the armrest, cheek resting on her hand. Though she surely already understood it, her insistence on having Subaru state it himself held not a shred of leniency throughout. Even in something as small as how the conversation began, the contest had already started. He understood that now—precisely because he'd lived through a string of failures to get here.

"Well, of course—"

So Subaru swung his arm wide, holding himself together so as not to be swallowed by Crusch's piercing gaze, and, smiling boldly so as not to repeat past failures, said,

"An alliance between the Emilia Camp and the Crusch Camp, on equal terms—this is the scene for negotiating that."

The first challenge, meant to clear the many walls standing in his way, was about to begin.

—In his exchange with Rem before the royal capital's main gate, Subaru had resolved in his heart to begin himself anew.

Because he could think of it as the sincere way to face the girl who, even after he'd bared everything within his heart, had still told him she believed in him.

"Given that, though... there are way too many walls I've gotta get past."

Thinking anew of the many walls standing in his way, he could understand the weakness that had nearly broken his heart just moments before. The situation hadn't changed; it still felt like nothing but one step short of checkmate.

Even so,

"I've gotta do something about it, right. Lend me a hand, Rem?"

"Yes. Whatever you ask, Subaru-kun."

He scratched his head and spoke before he'd even finished gathering his thoughts, and Rem nodded at once, just like that.

From Rem, gazing at him with fervent eyes, poured an unchanging trust, welling up both courage and a sense of duty within Subaru's chest.

He deliberately chose not to point out that the corners of her eyes were faintly red.

Having moved from the main gate to a corner of the lower district, the two of them stood with their backs against a wall, amid the crowd's bustle.

Looking back and recalling the exchange in front of the main gate, he'd hesitated to continue the conversation any further right there. After all, he'd bared absolutely everything, complexes included, half in tears and not caring who saw. And once Rem's heart, responding to that, had been laid bare as well, continuing to be exposed any further to the stares of the onlookers who'd been eavesdropping would have snapped the limits of his heart's endurance.

Holding Rem to his chest, he'd pushed past the lukewarm stares and finally reached a place beyond the reach of onlookers' eyes, letting out a breath. In any case,

"It's definitely a loss of time. Even if it's only about an hour... right now, that's precious."

As he'd verified before, the limit before the overwhelming tragedy at the mansion occurred was five days. —In reality, it amounted to roughly four and a half days net.

If nearly two of those days were to be spent on travel, the time he could actually use came to two days.

"And the walls I've gotta clear—one, two, three, and a whole lot more, huh."

The checkpoints he had to break through were many, and their nature was on an entirely different level from before.

Each alone was hopeless enough on its own, and with them all bearing down together, it had become a problem beyond words to describe.

The first, naturally, was the Witch Cult. Unless he somehow stopped the fanatics led by Petelgeuse, not a single soul—at the mansion, or in the village—would be saved.

The second was Rem's death—one that came by different means each time, but always, without fail, came. Whether she traveled with Subaru or, hypothetically, went ahead alone, the fated ending she arrived at converged on death. The first time, she'd died somewhere out of his sight. Then the despair of losing her right before his eyes, the second and third times—it would not be an exaggeration to say that the shock these dealt him had driven Subaru down the road to giving up.

And the third was a wall that, by its very precondition, was unthinkable not to clear—one that would surely be triggered by Emilia's death: the Great Spirit Puck's indiscriminate act of vengeance. Thinking back on it, it seemed that Subaru's causes of death in this loop had, every single time, ended with freezing at Puck's hand. That, in turn, produced circumstantial evidence that in every one of these worlds, Subaru had not once made it in time to prevent a situation where Emilia's life was in danger.

Every one of these walls was a condition he could not afford to drop, not even one. If he let even a single one slip through his fingers, the world that followed would not be the future Natsuki Subaru wished for, and it would also betray the image of the hero that Rem believed in.

"—A few, then."

Subaru, having been sorting through the problem points, murmured this softly. Across from him, Rem, watching over Subaru as he sat lost in thought, gave no answer to this murmur. She understood that his mutter wasn't seeking a reply.

To hope for the best judgment from her most beloved hero, and to make the greatest contribution she could. That was how she kept her heart steady now, and it was the greatest expression of love she was capable of giving at present.

Watched over by Rem, within his limited time, Subaru replayed memories that leapt beyond the bounds of that limited time, racing his thoughts in search of even a single fragment of a foothold. Believing that somewhere there slept a possibility to move this seemingly hopeless, checkmated situation—even by the smallest degree.

—Rack your brain. Set your heart ablaze. His body, his abilities—right now, they couldn't keep pace with the ideal. So at the very least, if he couldn't manage this much, then even a single step toward being a hero would be a joke.

—Remember. Think. Don't let your three deaths go to waste. Don't let the will of the girl you let die three times go to waste. Everything of the three worlds that ended weighed heavily down on Subaru's shoulders—

The people he'd met, the conversations they'd shared. Partings, encounters, rage, madness, sorrow, the Witch. And then—

"There's... a possibility, isn't there?"

What suddenly flickered across his mind was nothing more than a handful of possibility. Each individual thread was thin and fragile, forcibly connected, and there was no telling when it might snap. Far too unreliable to stake everything on.

Precisely because of that, it was worth going all in.

"Rem. There's some things I've gotta tell you, and some things I want to ask."

"Yes."

To pull together a plan he'd only just thought of, Subaru needed Rem's cooperation. Now then—before he began disclosing information to her, he first sorted out only what wouldn't run afoul of the Witch's Curse—

"The word getting out that Emilia's entering the Royal Selection is what set up the conditions for the Witch Cult to move. For some incomprehensible reason, they're going to go after Emilia—target the mansion and the village and run wild. I want to stop that."

"The Witch Cult..."

For a moment, at the sound of that word, a dangerous emotion rose in Rem's eyes. But she reined it in with self-control, giving a faint dip of her chin at Subaru's words, and said,

"The possibility of the Witch Cult moving—Roswaal-sama had considered that as well. Rem hasn't been told the details, but countermeasures should have been thought through."

"But that's not enough, not by itself."

As a matter of fact, exactly what preparations Roswaal had made against the Witch Cult remained unknown. Whether it had ended in a dud, or whether it had been triggered but failed to reach in time, wasn't certain. But in the end, those advance measures bore no fruit, and the white hell unfolded regardless. Knowing that future as he did, what Subaru needed to do was secure a means of self-defense that didn't rely on Roswaal, and by extension, a means of protecting the lives of everyone at the mansion and in the village to the very end.

"The Witch Cult should be coming for a short, decisive battle. Rem, what's the mansion's fighting strength?"

"...It's difficult to say, but right now there's a high chance Roswaal-sama isn't at the mansion. After returning from the capital, he was scheduled to go straight to visit an influential figure within the domain."

Speaking hesitantly, hedging her words, what Rem told him was the report of Roswaal's absence that had stunned Subaru the previous time. Hearing it, he confirmed once again the fact that the mansion would likely hold only Emilia, Ram, and Beatrice.

Only three people, and given Beatrice's uncooperative attitude, whether she would even join the fight against the Witch Cult was highly doubtful.

He remembered the conversation he'd exchanged with Beatrice last time, brief as it had been. How she'd looked at Subaru, who'd asked her to kill him, with eyes like a small child whose expectations had been betrayed—

"Sorry, but that's gotta wait for now."

Aching at leaving that on-the-verge-of-tears gaze behind, Subaru, prefacing with "So," said to Rem, who waited for his words,

"Two people who can fight. Even if you and I go back, it's a drop in the bucket."

"It can't be denied that most of the main house's fighting strength depends on Roswaal-sama's own abilities. If Frederica had stayed behind, things might have been different, but..."

Nodding back at Rem, who looked frustrated, Subaru worked out the finer details so no gap in information would arise between him and Rem at this preparatory stage. On the Witch Cult and the mansion's holding forces, there probably wasn't much more to cover. So then, the next topic was the main matter.

Straightening his posture, Subaru looked directly into Rem's eyes. And, watching Rem lift her face as she sensed the air had changed, he said,

"—Rem, whatever it is Roswaal ordered you to do here in the capital. Tell me all of it."

"—————"

Subaru had expected her to show a puzzled expression, or perhaps surprise at being caught off guard. But Rem's reaction upon hearing those words betrayed every one of his expectations.

"—Yes. As you wish."

She nodded at Subaru's words, and, above all, a smile of heartfelt joy rose on her face as a single tear spilled from the corner of her eye.

"An alliance... is it."

Crusch murmured this at Subaru, who, receiving the full weight of everyone's gaze, had just stated the purpose of the meeting. She dipped her chin slightly as if lost in thought, then sent a brief glance toward Rem. Quietly sensing the meaning behind that probing look, Rem shook her head slowly and said,

"Just as Roswaal-sama instructed, Rem has said nothing. —Everything was something Subaru-kun arrived at on his own."

"I do not doubt your loyalty. But, I see..."

Crusch showed understanding—less convinced than as though something had simply clicked into place. She now turned those eyes, understanding rising in them, toward Subaru, and said,

"Then this time, authority as negotiator has been delegated from Rem to you—Natsuki Subaru. Is that correct?"

"Yeah, that's right. Though I gotta say, Roswaal... my lord, pulled a pretty nasty little trick."

Adding an exaggerated sigh for effect, Subaru clicked his tongue at the nasty smile of the indigo-haired figure that rose in his mind. The policy for action in the capital had been handed down privately to Rem alone, without a word to Subaru. And it had been strictly ordered that unless Subaru realized it himself, it would never be let slip to him from Rem's mouth.

"It's not like there was nothing bugging me from the start. To begin with, it's self-evident our camp's chronically short-handed."

As far as Subaru knew, the Roswaal Camp backing Emilia suffered from a fatal shortage of manpower. After all, at the main house, the personnel amounted to just the Ram and Rem sisters, Roswaal, and Beatrice—who he wasn't even sure should be counted. There seemed to be other associates whose names he occasionally heard, but even scraping together those he'd never met, how many could there really be?

Under such circumstances, the fact that Rem had been left behind in the capital together with Subaru—even under limited conditions—had always nagged at some corner of his mind as something off.

Of course, ostensibly, it could be explained as: since Subaru had saved the domain from crisis, they couldn't in good conscience commit the rudeness of leaving him alone in the capital, not when they owed him treatment and other forms of compensation.

"But I can't see that pervert letting Rem go just out of the goodness of his heart like that. There's gotta be something behind it—and once I followed that line of thinking..."

"Naturally, the arrow of suspicion lands on this house, which had the most opportunity for meetings, is that it."

Recrossing her legs, Crusch picked up where Subaru's words left off and stated the conclusion. Nodding as if to affirm it, Subaru, prefacing with "Plus," said,

"I'd heard that Rem and you, Crusch-san, were apparently having secret meetings, night after night. Though it makes me sick just how much of an idiot I was, not even wondering what you two were actually talking about."

Just how much had he been seeing only himself? Nothing but self-mockery came to mind. That Rem had really, subtly, been scattering hints so that Subaru would notice the hidden intent behind it all—it took him remaking the world four times over to finally catch on, that's how dense he was.

"The content of the nightly talks is about concluding the alliance. As for the terms being offered from our side... I've heard the gist of it from Rem."

"The magic ore of the Elior Forest—the sale of its mining rights is the main bargaining chip, is it."

Crusch cut in over Subaru's words, which he'd meant to get away with just faintly hinting at, as though it weren't worth hiding anyway. The instant he heard it, none other than the following had his eyes light up.

"My, my. That's not a story one can afford to miss."

Russell, who like Crusch had settled into a chair—the leather-upholstered ones provided for guests—leaned slightly forward, his eyes gleaming, and said,

"Mining rights to magic ore hold more value than ever right now, precisely as demand for ore is on the rise. All the more so, naturally, if it comes from land still untouched."

At the merchant's bite, sharper than expected, Subaru couldn't hide his inward surprise. When he'd first heard this from Rem, Subaru had even felt a sense of crisis, thinking, "If this doesn't work, we might need to reconsider the terms."

"What's this about demand for ore rising?"

"The season is about to finish the Red Days and move into the Yellow Days, then the Blue Days. This year in particular, the influence of water mana is expected to be strong, so demand should be high for use in heating equipment."

Raising a finger, Russell answered Subaru's question in a resonant, ringing tone. The Blue Days and Red Days he spoke of were terms in this world referring to spring, summer, autumn, and winter. Just as it sounded, Red Days meant "summer," Blue Days meant "winter." Yellow Days would be "autumn," and Green Days presumably "spring." In any case, it seemed fair to understand it as: heating equipment starts selling ahead of winter.

"Magic ore itself is a pure crystallized form of mana, of magical power. Depending on subsequent processing, one can impart elemental directionality to it, allowing it to be used differently according to purpose. Superior in strength, and if used correctly, reliable in terms of service life as well. There's hardly a more manageable commodity than this."

"In exchange, the absolute quantity is small. And once directionality has been imparted, there's no redoing it. Most of the mining sites have their land itself managed by the kingdom, and a considerable portion of the ore is diverted to public works. What's distributed to the common populace is an exceedingly tiny amount—to put it more plainly, it circulates only among the state and the wealthy."

Against Russell, who had listed the good points of magic ore's value, Crusch listed the bad points of how that value was actually put to use, moving to shoot him down. But Russell, undaunted even by that, shook his head and said, "Precisely because of that,"

"I can't help but leap at the discovery of an untouched mining site. And this is said by the envoy of Lord Mathers, who built his fortune on the excavation and trade of magic ore—and, I might add, the knight of Lady Emilia, a Royal Selection candidate. The credibility and trustworthiness are extremely high."

Speaking with a fervent tone, Russell said this while eyeing Subaru sidelong. It was clear his manner concealed, beneath a pretense of shameless eagerness to snatch up the mining rights, a strong measure of pressure directed at Subaru. In other words, having brought this material up in front of the very man himself, he was cutting off any retreat—implying that there could be no backing out now. That said,

"Yeah, you can trust me on that one. In a Royal Selection that's gonna take a long time from here, we're not so far off the mark that we'd bluff the very first partner we try to team up with."

Since this was a condition Roswaal himself had proposed, there was no part of it where Subaru's own will came into play. That also meant there was no room for unease to intervene—to put it plainly, it came down to trust in Roswaal as a lord. Though that was something he could never say to the man's face.

In any case, at Subaru's reply, Russell nodded, eyes holding conviction, and said, "I see,"

"It seems there's no need to worry about your resolve as a negotiator, after all. I apologize for the discourtesy of testing you with such talk."

"Nah, it's fine. In the talks going forward, I'm planning to lean on your input pretty hard anyway, Russell-san."

To begin with, Subaru held not a shred of expectation that Russell's impression of him would be high. At the point of their first encounter in the third world, this was a man who already knew about the disgraceful spectacle Subaru had put on at the Royal Selection ceremony. Naturally, Subaru had figured Russell would harbor doubts about his ability in this negotiation and would find an opportunity to test the point.

He'd schemed to prepare an easy topic to needle him on, hoping to dodge that scrutiny when it came to the main matter—but he couldn't hide his relief that Russell had taken the bait at an easily deflectable moment.

Though, if that relief showed too plainly on his face, he'd surely fail to meet Russell's standards, so even after accepting his apology, Subaru had no choice but to answer as though he were some big shot.

"That said, now that you've apologized like this, I'd like a slip or two of the tongue overlooked in return, wouldn't you say?"

"So that was the expectation with which Russell Fellow joined us, was it. You make surprisingly shrewd calls yourself."

Crusch let this slip at Subaru's ingratiating smile, apparently scoring, in her own way, the exchange just now between Subaru and the merchant. Given that the talks hadn't been cut off, it was perhaps a bare pass, just above failing.

Rendering that judgment even as he kept up his ingratiating smile, Subaru put a hand to his head and said,

"Dangling a profitable deal to secure an advisor was of course one of the aims, but... the reason I called you here, Russell-san, is because it's connected to the main matter."

"Oh? The main matter, is it—"

At Subaru's words as he scratched his head, the air in the room shifted entirely. Crusch, who up until then had kept the demeanor of merely sizing up the negotiation, straightened her posture, closed her eyes quietly just once, and then slowly bathed Subaru in the full force of her sharp gaze.

Before a pressure so intense it gave the illusion that a wind had blown through, Subaru nonetheless refused to flinch and pushed back against it. He'd been made to endure violent, oppressive pressure to the point of disgust before now. Compared to that, there wasn't a trace of negative emotion in Crusch's gaze meant to frighten or cow him. All there was, was a majesty that straightened one's spine and tightened up slack thinking.

"I will acknowledge it, Natsuki Subaru. That you are Lord Mathers' proxy, and Emilia's formally appointed envoy as well. That whatever is exchanged between you and me at this negotiation shall stand as though exchanged directly between Emilia and myself."

Simply by standing and facing her, a person could be pressured this much. Crusch wasn't deliberately intimidating Subaru right now. She had, purely and simply, switched her mindset from the private individual Crusch she'd been until then, to the public figure Crusch Karsten. Which was to say, this was proof that the pressure radiated by the head of House Karsten held this much power in itself.

This was the true form of the heroine who now, in this Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, stood closest of all to the throne—

Amid an admiration that raised gooseflesh, Crusch extended a hand toward Subaru, who stood rooted where he was, and herself struck the opening blow of the negotiation whose beginning she had announced.

"You should already have heard this, but let me ask again all the same. The negotiations between myself and your attendant—Rem—including matters such as the sale of mining rights, have not yet reached agreement. You are fully aware of that point, I trust?"

"...Yeah."

He'd heard from Rem's own mouth that the negotiations had run into difficulty, and that with only the authority entrusted to her, no agreement had been reached. Even as the image rose of Rem lamenting her own lack of power, half his grief was also for his own lack of perceptiveness, for having remained utterly oblivious to her anguish.

Combining these two griefs into regret, Subaru made full use of the fortunate advantage of having already obtained the regret of the future in advance. That was the difference in how things unfolded, compared to before.

"I want to confirm something too—in practice, the terms up to now really aren't enough, right? The sale of Elior Forest mining rights, without excessive interference into either camp's affairs. And on top of that, working out an agreement for the handling of the extracted magic ore itself as well."

"A draft has been presented from Rem's side. As one would expect of Margrave Mathers, I suppose. It secures ample profit for his own camp while letting more than sufficient benefit flow to this side as well. Under normal circumstances I would leap at it and have a letter of agreement drawn up at once, but..."

On matters of exchanging figures at that level, there was nothing Subaru could contribute. If pushed into a corner, he might well blurt out something like, "Fine, take all the mining rights!"—so all he could do was pray the fine details wouldn't come up.

"In this case, the concern lies chiefly with the party on the other side of the deal. You understand, do you not?"

It probably wasn't that his prayer had been answered, but what Crusch, pausing her words, went on to say was something other than that particular worry. Even so, it was certainly not something to be welcomed, and,

"It's not... that you can't trust Roswaal, is it."

That was nothing but a hopeful guess. If Roswaal's conduct were indeed the problem, Subaru would from now on be forcing the man to lead a clean, upright life—but that wasn't the point Crusch was raising as an issue. It was a problem that could not be avoided, one that would go on clinging to Emilia endlessly, and,

"A rival candidate in the Royal Selection. And moreover, dealing with Emilia—a Half-Elf, subject to accusations of being half-Witch. Even with an eye to what comes later, we cannot help but be cautious."

At her stating this in a low voice, Subaru felt something unexpected and was disheartened. The impression Subaru had held of Crusch, if put into words, would fittingly be something like "dignified and commanding" and "sincere." Precisely because she had carried through, at the Royal Selection ceremony, a bearing and statements that embodied those very words, there was something jarring about her now seeming to worry over reputation—

"Don't tell me, this is just a pretext for turning us down...?"

"Subaru-kyun? Blurting something like that out in the middle of an important negotiation—Feri-chan thinks that's not so good, nya~, or something like that? Or something like that?"

Ferris, smiling all the while, had a vein bulging on his forehead. Feeling a strange, masculine sort of menace in the frightening part of that smile, Subaru hastily clapped a hand over his mouth and bowed his head.

Watching this exchange, Crusch loosened the corner of her mouth faintly and said,

"When you answer so plainly, it starts to feel as though I, who responded with a pretext, am the one who ought to be ashamed. This has been instructive for me. When one deals only with the same familiar people day after day, opportunities like this are rare to come by."

With logic like that, difficult for Subaru to quite follow, his present rudeness was let pass. Even so, relying on her leniency time and again would only pile up a debt owed, and could, in the end, invite disadvantage to his own side.

Subaru gave a small nod and said,

"So the pretext is a pretext, and... underneath, at heart, can I take it that you, Crusch-san, have no aversion to forming an alliance with Emilia itself?"

"Natsuki Subaru, let me correct one notion of yours."

Raising a finger, Crusch pointed it toward him and said,

"A thing's worth is decided by the state of its soul, and how it is made to shine. Origin and circumstance are not the decisive factors that determine that thing's essence."

Of course, she wasn't so ignorant of the world as to deny that such things could become an indirect factor. Nor, naturally, did she lack the imagination to consider just how much unreasonable harshness Emilia's circumstances, the reality of being a Half-Elf, had forced upon her.

Which was why Crusch gave a single nod and said,

"At that Royal Selection ceremony, there was no falsehood in the words Emilia spoke. Precisely because there was genuine resolve and pride behind them, I recognize Emilia as one corner of the rival candidates."

"That's complicated. So, meaning what, exactly?"

"The theatricality is just a matter of my own taste. Forgive it."

She seemed aware of her own grandiose manner of speaking; Crusch let her lips ease into a small smile, then, after a single blink, tightened her expression again and said,

"That Emilia is a Half-Elf is not something I would use as grounds for refusing to form an alliance. If anything, since we are not politically at odds, Emilia's existence can be said to be, for me, an opponent I have no active need to stand against. I am not reluctant to ally, either."

"Then..."

"Don't rush the answer, Natsuki Subaru. It would not be an exaggeration to say whether I accept your proposal hinges on what you answer next."

Restraining Subaru, who had leaned forward at the promising response, Crusch put the question to him once more. That is to say, what Subaru—entrusted with negotiating authority—would bring to the table.

"The mining rights to the Elior Forest bear great fruit for this side. But on the other hand, I feel there is no need to rush the affairs of the Royal Selection along. The deadline is three years. Moving matters forward too hastily could well leave a source of trouble for later."

"So the merit of allying with Emilia doesn't reach the level of its demerit?"

"Not quite. As things stand, merit and demerit cancel each other out. As my house sees it, what we want is one more push—a pretext to tip the scale."

As for Crusch's own will, she seemed inclined to favor concluding the alliance. On the other hand, that not everything could simply be moved as she willed was itself likely one of the entanglements of a position grown as large as a ducal house.

Which was why she was asking for it. Something that would move the situation, something enough to silence the voices around her.

"—————"

As he tried to push the words out, Subaru felt a slight jolt of surprise at the sensation of his own throat closing up. Tension and unease swelled within his chest, blocking the throat of a Subaru trying to step forward.

He drew a breath once, and turned over again in his mind the content he was about to speak. He hadn't confirmed its certainty with anyone. He couldn't even deny the possibility that it would prove to be a misguided, wide-of-the-mark thing to say. But she'll take the bait—Subaru trusted his own judgment on that.

"Toward concluding the alliance, what we offer from our side is the mining rights and... information."

"—Information."

Hearing this, Crusch touched her own long hair and urged him on to continue. No judgment had been passed yet. From here on was the true test.

"Yeah, that's right. What I can offer is a certain piece of information."

"Let me hear it, then. Whether what you speak of is truly enough to move me."

Extending toward him the hand that had been touching her hair, Crusch waited on Subaru's words. Naturally, a faint tremor rose in his legs and fingers. But the faint warmth he felt near his elbow canceled it out.

Because Rem had rested her fingers against Subaru's arm, setting a flame to his borrowed courage,

He drew a breath, and in a single stroke Subaru spoke it aloud.

"—The location and time the White Whale appears. That's the card I can play."
