Arc 7 Chapter 58B: "Those Without a Place to Belong"

—It was humiliating.

Her clenched fangs creaked, her innards boiled over with rage, her soul cracked with grief and fury. Toyed with by lesser humans, driven into a corner without accomplishing what she should have, forced even to play the trump card she knew she shouldn't have — what was she even doing?

"What a disgrace, cha."

A state that a being of the Dragonkin should never so easily reveal — and yet she'd bared that shameful sight for all to see. If there had been other Dragonkin present, they would have covered their faces at such a sorry state, cursed it as a disgrace to the Dragonkin, and — in the rage directed at both her and themselves — probably killed Madelyn where she stood, half-dead already from indignation.

But that would never happen. There were no other Dragonkin. Madelyn had always been alone. And so—

—and so, no matter what, she had to kill the one who had killed the being she'd wished to make her mate.

"Flop-san, please help me. We need to carry this child inside..."

At the edge of her hazy consciousness, she heard someone's voice say that. Not that it mattered who — it was a human voice. With no other Dragonkin around, anything that spoke in human words was human, nothing more than a being fundamentally different from herself. And her head burned with the most immediate humiliation, inflicted by those different beings.

The crimson woman in the dress, and the silver-haired woman trailing snow. Those women, who had unleashed a terrifyingly precise combination attack, burned white across Madelyn's consciousness. As though drawn by something, she raised herself up and reached her arm toward the direction of the voice.

The slender back before her — she would tear it open, and then—

"I don't mind being called a huge fool."

A different voice, someone else's, reached her, and in the next instant the figure before her eyes switched places. The slender back was shoved aside, and someone else slender stood before her instead. Her claws swung toward that person, and the sensation of no shallow sharpness gouging into flesh rebounded through her hand.

"——"

Blood scattered, and the slight frame struck by a single blow of Dragonkin strength collapsed, helpless. She didn't think got him. Ironically, it was the very lack of resistance in the blow that made her realize the slight frame she'd torn open was not the object of the hatred scorching her innards. And yet—

"Who's next...!"

Baring her fangs, she moved to roar, as if daring someone else to be torn apart by her claws. She meant to kick aside the body of the one she'd just struck down and give herself over to the ferocity that sought its next prey. And then, seeing the fallen figure—

"...Eh?"

A man lay collapsed, his pale skin dyed with blood, his long golden hair spread across the ground. He was a man she didn't recognize. She could only tell human faces apart in the roughest terms, but among the faces she could tell apart, his wasn't one of them. So what caught her eye wasn't the man himself, but something he carried.

On the fallen man's thin chest, torn open and bared, something lay soaked in blood — an ornament made from a beast's fang, which he'd worn hanging from his neck.

—No, that wasn't a beast's fang. It was, it was, it was, it was, it was, it was, it was.

"——"

Shoved by the shoulder by Flop as he came rushing over, Rem tumbled onto the grass of the garden. It was unrestrained force from a completely unexpected direction. With no chance to brace herself, Rem's body toppled sideways onto the green. But there was no need to demand of Flop, who had done this, why he'd suddenly acted that way. Because a far more eloquent answer was already spreading before her, in vivid red.

"Flop-san!"

Bracing her hands against the grass, Rem's voice cracked at the sight. Lying faceup right in front of her, in the very spot she herself had occupied seconds before, was Flop — his slender body carved with deep gashes running from shoulder to hip. Four blade-sharp wounds sliced raw and vivid across the front of his body. And Rem understood at once that these were the wounds he'd taken trying to shield her.

Just as she understood that it was the very girl she'd rushed over to help who had done this.

"——"

The sky-blue-haired girl stood frozen mid-swing, blood dripping from the tips of her claws — blood gouged from Flop's flesh. Flop had taken his wounds protecting Rem from the girl about to commit that violence. Rem tried to raise herself at once, thinking she had to look at the wound, when—

"Move! Flop-san! Let me see the wound— Kyaa!"

But as Rem tried to approach Flop, the girl's slender arm blocked her. Grabbed by the shoulder and shoved down, Rem watched the girl take a step closer to Flop. Surely she didn't mean to finish Flop off — the thought drained the blood from Rem's whole body. The girl's arm seized Flop by the neck as it was and roughly hauled his body upright. And then—

"You — why are you wearing a dragon's fang... Carillon's fang, cha!?"

Her profile etched with desperation and anguish, the girl demanded it like a scream. For a moment, Rem's thoughts froze, unable to grasp the meaning of the question. But even as they did, the girl kept pressing the closed-eyed Flop with "Why, cha!?" again and again. Flop gave no answer; his consciousness remained sunk in darkness. Yet blood flowed ceaselessly from the wounds on his hoisted body, and there was no doubt the last embers of his life were guttering weaker.

"Answer me, cha! Answer! If you can't do that, then...!"

"P-please, stop! He's unconscious! He's going to die!"

"—!"

Rem clung to the girl's arm as it manhandled Flop, grabbing on for dear life. The girl whipped around, her golden eyes flaring with anger at the interference, but Rem withstood that glare with sheer force of will. With Flop's life on the line, how could she afford to be overwhelmed?

"Please let me treat him right now! Otherwise, Flop-san will..."

"What good will first aid do, cha!? If he's going to die anyway, before he dies..."

"I'll cast healing magic! I can use healing magic!"

At Rem's desperate appeal, the strength in the girl's arm loosened slightly. Ordinary first aid offered no way to save Flop from such a grievous wound. But healing magic was another matter entirely. For the first time, the girl's golden eyes properly focused on Rem.

"...Can you save him, cha?"

"—. I'll do it. No matter what it takes...!"

"Then hurry up, cha."

Whether it was that she trusted Rem's words, or simply understood there was no other option— the girl shoved Flop's raised-up body onto Rem and issued that one-sided order. But there was no room to push back against her attitude.

"This is awful..."

Checking over the wounds on the Flop now handed to her, Rem murmured at the sheer brutality of it. As though torn by a beast's claws, four wounds were carved into Flop's pale skin — deep, painful gashes, like a tree branch gouged through muddy earth. Pressing down the blood that still gushed steadily with the cloth wrapped around Flop's head, she activated healing magic over the wound — and immediately, a wave of exhaustion assaulted Rem's entire body.

"—!"

She had already cast healing magic on the many people wounded in the Flying Dragons' attack. She'd limited herself to only those who absolutely needed healing arts, to conserve her strength, but anyone who needed it that badly required that much more power, and the drain was unavoidable. And on top of that came treating the near-death Flop. The mental strain alone was immense.

"Even so, giving up isn't..."

—an option, she told herself, and focused her consciousness on Flop's wound. As Rem concentrated, Priscilla's words suddenly resurfaced in her mind. They were advice given to Rem as she pondered her memories, her way of being, and the role she was meant to fulfill. Words spoken sometime during the days she'd spent at Priscilla's side, after parting from Subaru and the others to remain in the city—

'Look not at the wound, but at life itself. In any case, a living body has more coursing through it than mere blood — all manner of things invisible to the eye. What healing arts must grasp lies beyond that.'

'What that is, is not for one such as I, who wields no healing arts, to unravel. For convenience, I called it life. How you grasp it, what you call it — that is yours to decide. Only—'

'Remember this, Rem. Pitiable girl, who lost her memories and lost her place to belong. That you, who ought to have lost everything, still possess the power to heal others — that is your very essence.'

'—No one can flee from themselves. Do not, under any circumstance, forget my words. Now, hone yourself.'

"—Look at the life."

Not the painful wound itself, but the life flowing out of Flop, the life that had to be held in place — she would dam it, or amplify it, and tether that life in place. Healing arts were not merely about filling in gouged flesh, closing wounds, and easing pain. At their core, healing arts were not magic that mended wounds, but magic that saved lives.

If she reached for that awareness and that technique — the ability to intervene in life itself—

—then surely it should be possible to tether and pull back Flop's nearly-lost life.

"——"

Seeking the best use of what little strength remained, Rem's healing arts, honed to their absolute limit, showed their true worth. She joined the threads of life that needed joining, dammed the flow of life that needed damming, and coaxed renewed strength into the guttering flame of a life about to go out. —She pulled Flop's life back up.

"Flop-san...!"

His breathing, thin and weakening, and the color drained from his cheeks. As both gradually began to stabilize, Rem reached for that solid sense of progress. At her call, Flop's long lashes trembled, and his blue eyes opened a sliver. Flop's consciousness was still hazy. But if she kept up the healing arts like this, she could pull his life back completely.

Just as Rem was about to let out a breath of relief at that thought, Flop's lips trembled. And with a faint breath, he told her this:

"...You mustn't... heal me all the way."

"—Eh?"

Struck in the eardrums by words she could hardly believe, Rem let out a rasping breath. Her concentration scattered from the sheer shock, and it disrupted the casting of her healing arts. As Rem hurriedly re-steadied her focus, Flop's eyelids fell shut again, his consciousness sinking away.

This time Flop was truly, completely unconscious. Even as she poured her healing magic into his wound, half of Rem's mind was thrown into confusion, unable to grasp the meaning of what he'd just said.

Why had Flop said such a thing? What could he possibly have meant, saying she mustn't treat him?

"Was his mind hazy, and he just said something incoherent?"

That was possible. Given how much blood he'd lost, it was a miracle he'd regained consciousness even for an instant. It wouldn't be strange for him to blurt out something meaningless in such a near-vacant state of mind. And yet, even in their short acquaintance, Rem's trust in Flop ran deep. Flop was always someone who thought things through earnestly before voicing his opinion.

So what if those words of his just now had likewise been squeezed out from that same earnestness? Wouldn't it be far too dismissive of her to write it off as mere nonsense?

"——"

Why had he said she mustn't heal him? —No, wait, what exactly had Flop said? It wasn't that she mustn't heal him. To be precise, it wasn't mustn't heal him at all.

"Mustn't heal me all the way?"

The phrasing — heal all the way — was strange. He wasn't telling her to stop healing him, only to stop short of healing him completely. But if she didn't heal him all the way, Flop's life would remain in danger. Rem's mana had its limits too. In the first place, she couldn't grasp the point of doing such a thing. She would properly save Flop's life, and then—

"—Ah."

Thinking that far, Rem realized her grasp of her own situation had gone askew. In her desperate focus on treating Flop, she'd completely lost sight of everything around her.

"——"

Standing frozen behind Rem, who was healing Flop's wound, was the very girl who had inflicted it. More than that, above the abruptly frozen city, a swarm of Flying Dragons still wheeled through the sky, and fierce battles continued to rage throughout the city. Not a single thing about the situation had improved — even if Flop's wound healed. And Flop's plea had brought a certain possibility to Rem's mind. It was—

"—Just who, and from where, are you?"

Rem asked the girl behind her that question without turning around. It wasn't a question spoken face-to-face, but the girl surely understood she was the one being addressed. At once came the sound of the girl's fangs clicking together, and—

"You shouldn't be saying things like that right now, cha. If you've got time to worry about other things, hurry up and heal that man already, cha!"

"I want to heal him too! But..."

"But what, cha!?"

"But! I'm too worried about who you are and where you're from to concentrate. At this rate, I might not be able to keep the magic going."

Unable to think of an excuse on the spot, Rem had blurted out a painfully childish retort. If it provoked the other's anger, it wouldn't be strange for the claws that had torn Flop open to turn on Rem next. But at Rem's clumsy retort, the girl let out a rough breath, and—

"...Madelyn, cha."

"...What?"

"Madelyn Eschart! One of the Empire's Generals, cha!"

In a tone that could have been either anger or impatience, the girl — Madelyn — answered Rem's question. There was surprise that an answer had even come back, but the greater shock was Madelyn's title. She'd named herself one of the Empire's Generals, and Rem knew exactly what standing that held in this country. And that understanding matched up with the seed of a suspicion taking root in Rem. Namely—

"—? What are you doing, cha? Why..."

"——"

"Why'd you stop treating him, cha!?"

Taking one deep breath, Rem withdrew the hand she'd held over Flop's wound. Naturally, the healing arts mending Flop's wound cut off, and Madelyn snapped at the fact of it. She grabbed Rem by the collar and forcibly turned her around to face her. Even cowed by that raw strength, Rem stared back at Madelyn with unbroken resolve. And then—

"Answer me, cha! What are you playing at, cha!"

"—. If you want me to keep treating Flop-san, I have a condition."

"A condition...? What are you suddenly on about..."

"—Call off the swarm of Flying Dragons attacking the city. Unless you agree to that condition, I cannot continue his treatment."

Thus, Rem struck a bargain head-on with the girl who commanded the swarm of Flying Dragons.

—You mustn't heal me all the way.

Rem interpreted Flop's baffling words as meaning exactly this. She didn't know why, but Madelyn was fixated on Flop's survival. Whatever reason lay behind her obsession existed within Flop, and as things stood, only Rem could keep hold of it.

A negotiation using Flop's life as a shield, to force an end to the attack on the city. This outrage, one that might well earn her the epithet of a monster, Rem carried out with resolute will. She made no comforting excuse to herself that Flop of all people would surely want this. She simply believed that using Flop's life this way could save the walled city from its desperate straits. Even if the true meaning behind Flop's words was something else entirely, Rem had already set this in motion.

"Wha—!"

At the demand thrust upon her, Madelyn fell speechless, as though she couldn't believe her ears. Small wonder. Rem herself had been just as confused when she'd heard Flop's words. The very same thing was now befalling Madelyn — she could understand it. But she would spare not one shred of sympathy. Rem had no room for that either.

"——"

Little by little, the anxiety of having halted Flop's treatment scorched at Rem's chest. In her heart, she wanted to resume her healing arts this very instant and continue the work of pulling Flop's life back up. The fading life balanced on her fingertips stood right at the brink of whether it could return or not. Even as she stood here like this, Flop's chances closed a little more with each passing moment.

"What will you do? Please decide, quickly."

Careful not to let that urgency show on her face, Rem pressed Madelyn for a decision. She maneuvered things so as to make it seem that she herself wasn't the one cornered — that Madelyn was. By doing so, she hoped to preserve even the slightest bit more of a chance for Flop.

The effects of that white light which had shaken the entire city earlier still lingered heavily, and there was no sign of Kuna or Holly, who had been guarding the mansion. It would take time before they returned. She couldn't look outside this place for a way to break the deadlock. Everything rested on Rem's own two arms.

"Madelyn-san, time is—"

"—Don't push your luck underestimating a dragon, cha."

"Ngh...!"

Her further push for a decision was cut off by the sensation of a palm crushing her throat. Madelyn's small hand clamped down on Rem's throat, letting her utter no further words. —No, in the first place, Madelyn refused to permit even the intent to negotiate.

Golden eyes burning with fury cursed the reckless nerve behind Rem's demand. Bathed in a terrifyingly overwhelming malevolent aura, one utterly incomparable to her childlike appearance, Rem's very core recoiled, and she wanted to retract, right then and there, this act she'd carried through on momentum alone. An impulse rose to beg forgiveness for such presumptuous behavior, to prostrate herself. But—

"——"

She remembered the sight of Subaru standing in front of her to shield her from an opponent every bit as overwhelming. Dressed up in a ridiculous getup, cutting a figure almost too pathetic to watch, Subaru had still stood his ground, risking his life to protect Rem. —No, and not only in that one moment. Even by Rem's own eyes, Subaru had never been strong or special. And yet, even while flinching before danger, Subaru had never once retreated. Having watched that side of Subaru, Rem now scolded her own frightened, shrinking self.

I can't just hang my head and give up, she told herself. It wasn't with such a spoiled mindset and soft resolve that I decided to stay behind.

"If you... kill me..."

"What, cha."

"...even if you kill me... you still lose..."

Madelyn's eyes widened at the gasping declaration Rem forced out even as her throat was being crushed. If Madelyn let her anger drive her to kill Rem, she would lose forever her chance to exchange words with Flop. In the end, the substance and nature of the choice pressing on Madelyn wouldn't change.

It was a choice between two: destroy the city, or save Flop. As one of the Empire's Generals, fulfilling the role she'd been ordered to carry out was surely Madelyn's duty. And yet, in Madelyn's attitude and words, Rem had glimpsed a chance to win, however slight.

There were words Madelyn absolutely wanted to exchange with Flop. Something she wanted to confirm. Just how important was confirming that to her? Rem had seen in that a value worth staking everything on—

"—So, what will you do?"

"——"

"—Choose. Will you kill a person, or let them live?"

—Her consciousness, dyed white, gradually cleared, and the world slowly began to take on color again.

"—Ngh."

As consciousness returned, what her whole body felt was a terribly heavy sense of pressure. It was as heavy as though rubble were piled on top of her body, weighing down on her entire frame and making it hard to breathe. She moved her arm somehow, trying to push it off—

"Nngh... there...!"

As she stretched her arm from that cramped position, the weight fell away with a loud crash. Only then did she realize it hadn't been her imagination — she really had been buried under rubble — and it made sense why breathing had been so hard. Once she understood that, she tried to recall how she'd ended up buried under rubble in the first place—

"—Right. I was fighting Madelyn together with Priscilla."

The events just before came flooding back, and Emilia looked around at her surroundings. What spread before her was a ruined cityscape, stripped bare to the point of desolation. Buildings had collapsed, the fallen rubble scattered wide, the ground flattened out completely. It looked like a pile of raked leaves scattered by a violent wind, and Emilia caught her breath softly at the sight.

The white snow drifting down in flickers was the lingering effect of the space of ice Emilia herself had created — the finishing move Subaru had named "Ice Age." But what had dyed Emilia's consciousness white was a different white thing that had fallen from the sky. That was—

"—it had a feeling similar to Volcanica."

The white light that had rained down from above the clouds onto the earth had been a strike triggered by Madelyn's will. Even without knowing what was coming, Emilia had acted on instinct, coordinating with Priscilla, raising a shield of ice to meet that white light head-on. Even that ice shield, layered many times over, had been shattered, and in the end Priscilla's red jeweled sword had flashed—

"And after that, I got buried... Oh no! Where's Priscilla!?"

Remembering what had happened, Emilia began searching for the nowhere-to-be-seen Priscilla. Since Emilia herself had been buried under rubble, it wouldn't be strange if Priscilla, who'd been right beside her, had met the same fate. And if so, unlike the strong Emilia, there was a chance Priscilla might be buried and unable to get out. She had to help her quickly — it would be too pitiable if Priscilla were trembling in helpless fear.

"Priscilla! Priscilla, where are you!? Answer me! I'll get you out right away, so..."

"—You're chirping like a squawking bird, half-devil."

"Priscilla!?"

Pushing through her aching body, Emilia flipped over the nearby rubble. Amid her searching calls, an answer came, and Emilia scrambled to run toward the voice. There, on the other side of a mound of piled-up remains of the collapsed city wall, she found Priscilla. As Emilia rushed over in a hurry, Priscilla, seated among the rubble, gave a small snort.

"Tenacious, aren't you. So you're alive."

"Yes, thanks for worrying about me. And you, Priscilla — you're not exactly fine, are you. But I'm glad you're at least somehow okay."

"Hmph."

Right before Emilia, who breathed a sigh of relief, Priscilla let out a sigh, looking somewhat more tired than usual. Her flamboyant red dress was torn here and there, and traces of dust and bruises remained on her pale skin. Precisely because Priscilla always kept herself immaculate, the sight looked all the more painful. Still, when Emilia's brows drooped with concern at that, Priscilla looked up at her with displeasure and said, "Don't underestimate me," then—

"Don't make me laugh, you worrying about me. In the first place, you and I are enemies vying for the throne, are we not? Wishing for my downfall ought to be your true intent."

"Mm, there you go saying mean things again. I don't wish even a little for you to get hurt, Priscilla. Besides—"

"What."

"You really do think of me as a proper rival. That surprised me, just a little."

Emilia had been utterly convinced that she wasn't even on Priscilla's radar. Precisely because Emilia was aware of how far behind she'd fallen compared to the other Royal Selection candidates like Crusch, Anastasia, and Felt, that assessment was unexpected — and pleasing too. Being placed on the same level as such formidable people felt a bit like having her efforts recognized.

"——"

"Ah, but if you're safe, Priscilla, we need to move right away. I don't know where Madelyn went, and I have to stop the danger to the city too...!"

There would be time to celebrate Priscilla's safety once they'd gotten the city out of the crisis it faced. Ideally, she wanted to make Madelyn call off the Flying Dragons' attack, but since Madelyn hadn't turned up even while she was searching for Priscilla, there was a strong chance she'd already fled. If they couldn't rely on Madelyn, then Emilia and the others would have to drive off the Flying Dragons themselves. With that in mind, Emilia had been thinking of hurrying off to lend aid wherever the Flying Dragons were thickest, but—

"—Wait, half-devil."

"I'd like to wait, but there's no time to rest. If I don't hurry..."

"That's not it. —The situation is shifting."

Stopped just as she was about to dash off in a hurry, Emilia turned around, her eyes widening with an "eh." Priscilla, who had held Emilia back, jerked her chin while still seated on the rubble. Following that gesture, Emilia looked up at the cloud-heavy sky, still shedding white snow. And then she understood what Priscilla meant.

"...The Flying Dragons... are pulling away?"

As Emilia murmured that, she could see the Flying Dragons — who had been trampling the city's skies and attacking people indiscriminately — spreading their wings and slowly withdrawing from above the city. For a moment she couldn't grasp what that meant, and then Emilia thought, could it be—

"Maybe Madelyn... listened to what we asked..."

"Fool."

"Guess not, huh. But I thought Madelyn was the only one who could order the Flying Dragons to withdraw..."

With the most welcome possibility shot down by Priscilla, Emilia fell to pondering. Of course, even Flying Dragons following someone's orders wouldn't commit suicide by throwing their lives away for nothing. They would retreat if defeat looked certain, and if, say, the leader of the pack lost, it was entirely plausible they'd panic and flee.

But would Madelyn's defeat even count as the pack leader losing? Even supposing Madelyn had lost, there was still the matter of that strike from above the clouds — the one that Emilia and Priscilla together hadn't been able to stop.

If anything, it was Emilia and Priscilla who had been in danger. Madelyn could have kept fighting on and on if she'd continued alongside the Flying Dragons. And yet—

"Then it must mean some reason arose that forced her to pull back."

"A reason she had to pull back? Like what?"

"Even I cannot see through everything. Perhaps an order to withdraw came down to Madelyn because the existence of the 'Cloud Dragon' was exposed. Or perhaps..."

With a hand at her lips, pondering, Priscilla trailed off, and Emilia waited for what came next. Letting Emilia's anticipation hang for a few seconds, Priscilla, watching the retreating Flying Dragons, said—

"—Or she found something she had to prioritize over the capital's orders."

△▼△▼△▼△

—Whether Priscilla's reasoning was right or wrong would be revealed only after the swarm of Flying Dragons had departed.

The swarm of Flying Dragons that had driven the walled city to the brink of annihilation, inflicting casualties on countless residents and defenders alike, and the surprise assault led by the "Flying Dragon General," Madelyn Eschart. Add to that the arrival of a brutal ice season unbefitting the Vollachian Empire, and the terrifying white light from above the clouds that had wrought destruction across the entire city — amid a despair in which everyone believed the city's ruin was inevitable, those threats abruptly withdrew, and the city found itself reeling with an unexpected reprieve.

Even attempting to rebuild, they first had to confirm the depth of the wounds inflicted and whether those wounds could heal at all — a mind-numbing ordeal, like counting every drop of blood that had been spilled. But before the survivors of the walled city could begin that work, there is one fact that must be added.

The "Flying Dragon General" Madelyn Eschart, who had withdrawn along with her swarm of Flying Dragons— from the walled city she had flown away from together with her dragons, two others vanished at that very same moment.

—A merchant named Flop O'Connell, and a girl named Rem: their figures had vanished without a trace.
