Arc 8 Chapter 29: "I Never Wanted to Love"

――On a hilltop, the man stood and gazed out at the distant horizon.

Time had passed since the sun had set, and the poorly lit backwater village could not stand against the darkness of night.
More than that, since many of the able-bodied men had been wounded, there were fewer bonfires than usual, and to the girl's eyes, the sight of the man standing in that darkness looked very strange indeed.
The bandits who had been ravaging the neighboring villages one after another had finally turned their spearhead against her home, and it had suffered greatly — livestock and food stolen, men wounded.
Even so, that the village had not been destroyed outright was a stroke of luck amid misfortune, and the figure standing atop the hill could be counted as part of that luck.

When the bandits had attacked the village, the men who resisted were killed or wounded, and the women and children were captured one after another.
The girl too had been on the verge of being sold off, or subjected to something worse, when the soldiers dispatched from the capital — among them the man on the hill — had appeared.
They surrounded the bandits in an instant and wiped them out, barely allowing any resistance.
Afterward, citing protection and reconstruction assistance as their reason, they had stationed themselves near the village and handled everything briskly and efficiently; the village ended up with a stronger palisade than it had even before the bandit attack.

Once the men's wounds healed, the village would be safe for the time being.
The girl too had been running about busily just like the adult women, tending to the wounded, bringing meals to the soldiers, looking after the village children.
It was on her way home, once all that had settled down for the moment, that she found the man on the hill.

"――――"

Silently straining his eyes into the night darkness, the man's figure could have been called comical.
Straining one's eyes at a darkness in which nothing could possibly be seen was the height of futility. An act that led to no result was worthless — that was the harsh way of the Empire.
And yet, to the girl's eyes, the man did not look comical at all.

The man gazed single-mindedly into the distance, as though trying to make certain of something no one else could see.
And what exactly he was trying to see — that, she found, she desperately wanted to know.

"Wouldn't counting the stars in the sky bring more peace to your heart than staring into pitch darkness?"

Before she knew it, she had called out those words to the man's back.
When he turned, there was a faint flicker of surprise in his eyes, and somehow that made her proud.

――That was the meeting of the girl and the king that would be told and retold, again and again, as a fairy tale.

△▼△▼△▼△

――That reunion was one no one could have foreseen.

Call it a cruel trick of fate, or the strange workings of destiny.
In the Vollachia Empire, exposed to unprecedented peril, nearly everyone had come to learn, all at once, the full shape of the "Great Disaster."
The march of the dead, risen as Undead, had thrown every living soul into chaos.

And yet there were those who, having tasted that inescapable fall into chaos, clawed their way back up.
Beings who rose as the strong in spirit regardless of physical strength or weakness — those who, to the eyes of the ordinary and mediocre, appeared as heroes or extraordinary figures. More often than not, such strong-willed people also happened to be physically strong, and their actions weighed heavily on the fate of the imperial capital.

The blow of the "Great Disaster," the worst possible surprise attack the living could have suffered, ended up falling well short of the damage that might simply have been expected.
But even if that spoke to the living's hard-fought struggle, it did not mean the best possible outcome had been achieved.

Because there was one being who, by rights, should surely be counted among those extraordinary figures — and the initial surprise attack had swallowed her before she could claw her way up out of that first chaos, faster even than anyone else.

"――Nn."

A faint breath escaped her lips, and her consciousness drifted slowly toward waking.
Her eyelids, framed by long lashes, trembled, and like a sun fearing the dawn, her eyes slowly opened to reflect the world. Once, twice, her blue eyes blinked — and in that instant, the fragile bubble of a dream popped, and her consciousness settled firmly into reality.

"――!"

When she hurriedly sat up where she lay, what came into view was an unfamiliar place.
The room, built with a high ceiling, was of first-rate material and craftsmanship in both its walls and floor. A fine room held furnishings befitting a fine room, and one glance told her this was a place of noble bearing.
The fact that she herself was lying in a soft bed within that spacious room only reinforced that understanding — but the very fact she now understood was bizarre in itself.
Held up against her most recent memories, it was a fact too unnatural to make any sense of.

"Wacchi was, in the battle for the imperial capital……"

She had been there, she recalled of herself.
At once, what surfaced was a world dyed crimson from earth to sky, and the memory of scrambling about, together with the girl she'd achieved a miraculous reunion with, trying to soothe a wailing, thrashing child.
It had, quite literally, been a handful — but that attempt itself should have ended in success.
And yet, here she was, in a place like this. That made no sense at all――

"――My kimono."

It was only belatedly, having gotten that far in her thoughts, that she noticed the unfamiliar feel of the hand she'd placed on her own chest.
Looking down at her body lying in the bed, what her limbs were wrapped in was not the kimono she was used to wearing, but a blue dress, also made of fine, high-quality cloth.
Her hair, which she always wore up, had been undone, and it seemed her hair ornaments and earrings had been removed as well.
Every one of those was irreplaceable to her, something that must never be taken from her while she was unconscious――

"――You've awakened, my Star."

Just as she was about to rise from the bed to search for her missing ornaments, that voice reached her.

"――Ah."

The instant that voice struck her eardrums, every bit of her mind's activity was seized away, without exaggeration.
The voice had come from the doorway of the room, and between here and there stood arrayed all manner of elegant, dazzling furnishings that should have caught the eye. And yet none of it registered at all.
As if her ears had been stolen, as if her heart had been stolen, her consciousness was drawn entirely in that direction.
For her — for Yorna Mishigure — that was simply unavoidable.

"――――"

Eyes wide, Yorna, still in the bed, stared fixedly at the figure standing in the doorway.
There stood a small figure. In height, he was considerably shorter than Yorna, who was tall for a woman, giving an impression of childlike slenderness — and yet his features were sharp, handsome, unmistakably those of a grown man.
Hair near-black with a tinge of green, reaching to his shoulders, and eyes with deep, unhealthy-looking shadows beneath them that seemed to speak of displeasure — these seemed to vividly reflect a personality that kept others at a distance.
And yet, in truth, Yorna knew it was only that people feared him — not that he pushed people away. In fact, he had never once tried to push her away.
Even faced with that final moment, he had refused, from the bottom of his heart, to be parted from her.
And that was precisely why, Yorna knew, she was here now.
Because she knew――

"……Your Excellency, is it truly you?"

"You speak strangely. But I shall permit it. Everything concerning your Soul, I shall permit."

To Yorna, who asked this unable to fully believe it, the small man answered so.
His voice, dreadfully blunt and curt, tinged with a gloomy weight, carried within its short words feelings so intense they seemed ready to burst.
It was an attachment beyond what any human should bear, and the origin of that attachment lay in love.

The man before her — loved Yorna Mishigure.
This was not something she understood merely because her own Noh specialized in being loved; it was an emotion so plainly, blazingly obvious that anyone present, whoever they might be, would have seen it at a glance.
Though of course, it was utterly unthinkable that anyone else could ever be present in this place.
After all, this was――

"An assignation with an interval of nearly three hundred years between us. I will let no one disturb you and me."

"――――"

"Let me see your face well. Whatever your form or shape may have changed to, I wish to watch you blink, up close."

Slowly, he approached, and at his words Yorna's heart trembled.
She herself could not say clearly what emotion drove that tremor. Of course there was the impulse to rejoice at this impossible reunion, to fling herself into his arms.
But at the same time, she had three hundred years' worth of reasons why she must not do that. Chief among them, most immediate, were the last several decades — the greatest reason keeping Yorna from acting on impulse.
And so, her lips trembling with these conflicting emotions as the man drew near, Yorna――

"――Your Excellency Eugard Vollachia."

At her call, the man's feet came to a stop, and Yorna pressed her own lips tightly together.
If he stopped upon being called that, there was no mistaking him. There was no way she could possibly mistake him to begin with. Whoever else in the world might fail to recognize him, Yorna alone could never mistake him for anyone else.
Yorna alone — no, this Soul, which had begun with a girl named Iris — could never mistake the man once called the "King of Thorns," Eugard.

――Iris and the King of Thorns.
It was a fairy tale told since ancient times in this world, and at the same time, a tale rooted in true history.
It was a story known for depicting the meeting and parting of a girl named Iris and the Vollachia Emperor called the "King of Thorns," and its tragic ending.

The being whose soul — Iris's — never ascended to the heavens, but was instead bound to the earth of the Empire, reborn again and again through countless lives, was Yorna Mishigure. And the one who had bound Iris's soul to the imperial earth was none other than the "King of Thorns" himself, Eugard Vollachia.
Which meant this was the untold continuation of "Iris and the King of Thorns" ――

"As if. It's far from so beautiful a tale as that."

Slowly shaking her head, Yorna suppressed the impulse rising within her chest.
This reunion with Eugard, from whom she had been parted against her wishes — it could be said this had been Yorna's long-cherished wish. In a sense, this could be called the very moment that wish had come true.
But no. This was not it. The reunion Yorna had pictured in her heart had never taken this shape.

"I never wished to see Your Excellency wearing such a face, such eyes."

At the mocking cruelty of fate, Yorna looked at Eugard with a sorrowful anger.
Having stopped, receiving Yorna's gaze — her beloved Emperor, his skin pallid, his eyes now a pair of golden orbs — had changed so utterly from the man Yorna knew.

Specifically what had happened to him, she could not say.
Only that Eugard's appearance was far from ordinary, and she was certain it could bring nothing good to her, or to her Beloved Children.
Eugard, restored to some changed form, achieved an impossible resurrection — and if this unfamiliar place she'd awoken in was, by chance, a chamber within the Crystal Palace, then even the worst possibility crossed her mind.
The possibility that something monstrous had occurred, and the very shape of the Empire itself had been altered.

"Your Excellency, my lord, whatever could have happened to bring you to this……"

There was so much she wished to say, endless as unstaunched tears.
But Yorna shook that off and tried to ask what needed asking.
However――

"――My Star."

That question of Yorna's was silenced by Eugard raising a single finger.
It was not that the gesture itself had the power to seal Yorna's mouth. Rather, accompanying that gesture came a sharp pain that gripped her heart, and it was that which silenced her.

"Gh, hh……"

Pierced through the heart by that sharp pain, a groan spilled from Yorna's throat in place of her question.
Reflexively, she pressed a hand to her chest, and when she looked down, she saw a design that had not been on her dress before — gray thorns had been added.
Those thorns coiled at the center of Yorna's chest, slipping through her white skin to reach within.
They drove thorned barbs into Yorna's very heart, and a tremendous pain overtook her whole body. And yet, when she tried to touch it with her hand, her fingers slipped right through — she could not even feel it.

Amid thoughts bleached white by that pain, what crossed Yorna's mind was Eugard's other name — successive Vollachia Emperors were called by various names for their manner of rule and the great deeds they accomplished, and the name that marked Eugard was the "Thorned Emperor."
Just as the name said, Eugard bound others with the pain of thorns, and made them submit.
With untouchable thorns planted within them, Eugard made the imperial people submit, and using pain and fear, expanded the Empire's territory to its present form — that was the kind of Great Emperor he had been.

Tormented by unbearable pain, Yorna's throat trembled as if gasping for air.
And then she remembered. ――In the midst of that battle that had raged through the imperial capital, after she and Priscilla together had subdued the weeping, rampaging Arakiya, what blunder had she herself committed?

It was nothing complicated.
Yorna's attention had been stolen by the sight of Eugard appearing there, and she had failed to evade the binding of his thorns. And so, carrying the bound Yorna and the unconscious Arakiya, Priscilla had been left to face Eugard, and the many gold-eyed beings who followed behind him――

"――Is Priska safe?"

The question was asked in the midst of fierce, sharp pain.
That lips which had only managed groans of endurance against that pain could form a meaningful question at all was purely because her feelings had, at last, outweighed the pain.

In truth, the pain given to her did not waver in the slightest.
Eugard did not loosen the binding of his thorns. That held true whether the one bound was Yorna, or Iris. To begin with, what Eugard did was not meant to vent anger, nor to punish.
Planting thorns, binding others — for Eugard, that was as natural as breathing.
Just as a person walks on two legs, Eugard bound others with thorns.
The cries and the flowing blood of those pierced deep by his thorns were, to him, nothing more than the means by which he touched other people.

And so, to be with him meant to be with this pain, always.
Recalling that with her whole body, Yorna even managed a faint smile at the corner of her mouth as she asked her question.

"The two who were with me in that place — are they safe?"

"If you mean those who were with you, they did not enter my sight. What enters my sight is my Star — you, and you alone."

"I see, is that so……"

To her repeated question, Eugard's answer was not what she had hoped for.
But seeing Yorna, whose voice had dropped in tone because of it, Eugard seemed to have some thought of his own, and narrowed his eyes, before saying—

"……However. The 'Witch' brought living beings into the castle. Among them may be the ones you speak of."

At that addition from Eugard, Yorna caught her breath faintly.
Unlike the indigo eyes she remembered, Eugard's eyes now held golden light within black orbs — and yet the gentleness with which he gazed at her, and his clumsy consideration, were unmistakably the same man she remembered.
Feeling her chest tighten with a pain unlike that of the thorns, she said,

"Then I have a request of you, Your Excellency."

"A request?"

"――. Was it the 'Witch,' you said? I would ask that you confirm whether the ones this 'Witch' brought into the castle are indeed my companions."

To confirm that Priska and Arakiya were both safe.
For Yorna, who had not yet grasped the full state of things, that was, for now, the highest priority — the safety of those two.
If that could be granted――

"――Will you give up on death for that, my Star?"

The words Eugard spoke pierced Yorna's chest with yet another kind of pain.

"――――"

Silent, Yorna lifted her face, and Eugard looked down at her.
At those words of his, Yorna could say nothing in that instant. She could hardly say something so foolish as "what nonsense." For his words struck, unmistakably, at the very truth of her heart.
Her soul bound to the imperial earth, reborn again and again under new names in new bodies each time she died — that was the wish held by Yorna, by Iris, who had repeated this cycle so many times.
And Eugard Vollachia had seen through it, correctly, entirely.

"……Blood, it seems, cannot be denied."

And Vincent Vollachia, too, had seen through that same truth.
That was precisely why Yorna had chosen to join hands with Vincent, and throw herself into the fight to reclaim the imperial capital from the rebels. Of course, the matter of the fallen Capital of Magic weighed heavily in that choice as well — but Yorna believed that to hesitate over which motive truly held her heart was itself a kind of dishonesty.
And so, Yorna nodded to Eugard's question.

"If it means those children's lives can be saved, then my own death is such a small thing."

"Very well. I shall hear your wish, my Star."

To Yorna, who smiled through the pain, Eugard nodded without any change of expression.
And then, resuming the steps he had once halted, he came to stand directly before Yorna, and gently reached out a hand to touch her cheek.
Contrary to that gentle touch, his fingers were cold, and with the distance between them closed, the thorns' barbs dug in all the deeper.

"Wait here a while."

Releasing his hand from Yorna, who tasted pain piercing both heart and body, Eugard said only that before turning his back.
As ever, once he decided to act, he acted swiftly.
Feeling that fond, familiar thought, Yorna called out a question to the back retreating toward the door of the room.

"What became of the kimono I originally wore, and my hair and ear ornaments?"

"Not to my taste. But they are the things that adorned you. I have kept them."

So saying, Eugard indicated with his hand a cabinet beside the bed, and without spending another word, ended his answer to Yorna.
The way Eugard left the room after that, so terse and unadorned, was precisely the man Yorna knew. In life, he had always lived just so, forever rushing as if pursued by time.
And it was to a man like that, that Iris had wished to say there was no need to hurry, walking always at his side――.

"……Speaking like some frail little girl."

Slowly shaking her head, Yorna rose from the bed and reached for the cabinet.
When she opened the drawer, there lay Yorna's kimono and obi, carefully folded, along with a drawstring pouch holding her hair and ear ornaments — and a breath of relief escaped her.
Perhaps because that relief was so overwhelming――

"――Ah."

An unbecoming, thin sound escaped her throat, at the very same moment heat traced down her cheek.

"Kh."

Folding forward at the waist, Yorna clenched her teeth hard to hold back her sobs.
She must not shed tears. That was a curse that would drag the firmly-built "Kaleidoscope," Yorna Mishigure, mistress of the Capital of Magic, back down into being nothing but a village girl named Iris.
To return to being Iris meant narrowing down the ones she loved to just a single person.
It meant treating the whole of these three hundred years — as someone's child, someone's parent, someone's wife, all the days she had lived through as those things — as though they had never been.

"Why, Your Excellency……why now, of all times?"

She wanted to remain herself — the self who could love Priska, the people of the Capital of Magic, the many living souls of this Empire.
And that was precisely what the pain of the thorns, insisting on its barbs within her chest, sought to make her forget.

That sweet pain, achingly, maddeningly nostalgic — Yorna found it unbearably, terribly frightening.
Unbearable. It was simply unbearable.
