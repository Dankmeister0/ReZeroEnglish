Arc 4 Chapter 114: "Turning Lies into Wishes"

The high cry of an Earth Dragon rang out, and the sound of a battle's conclusion echoed across the sky of the Sanctuary.

The jet-black Earth Dragon sent the battered, exhausted Garfiel flying with the final, decisive blow. The Earth Dragon Subaru called Patrasche had come racing to this place as if she and Subaru were of one mind, and in the final moment, she'd pulled off the finest assist imaginable.

"――――!"

This was, in fact, the second time Garfiel and Patrasche had faced off within the Sanctuary. On the first day they'd arrived, Patrasche, pulling the Dragon Carriage, had clashed with Garfiel—who'd appeared to drive off intruders into the Sanctuary—and suffered an utterly crushing defeat. Of course, there was a line between the two of them—a warrior and a non-combatant—and no one could blame Patrasche for being left unable to do a thing, made to eat dirt. Subaru, naturally, never held it against his beloved dragon for something like that.

But how the person in question—no, the party in question, Patrasche—felt about it was another matter entirely.

Unable to protect her master, drenched in the humiliation of that day. For a Diana of proud draconic lineage, descended from the Progenitor Dragon, that was a disgrace that had to be wiped away no matter what. Between Patrasche and Subaru, who couldn't exchange words, there had been no clear, explicit understanding that this opportunity had come so soon. And so, Patrasche proved it through action—including the parts that words could never fully convey.

The Earth Dragon's cry was offered up to her master and to the Progenitor Dragon.

That something like accomplishment and satisfaction was mixed into it was something anyone could have told, just by looking at the female Earth Dragon nuzzling her nose against the unconscious Subaru.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

Listening to Patrasche's cry of restored honor, Emilia let out a deep breath.

It had felt like an illusion in which even breathing itself was forbidden. No—it had been a battle that made her forget to breathe. Told by Subaru to watch the fight through to its conclusion, Emilia, keenly aware of her own powerlessness, had kept her eyes fixed to the last on the fierce, desperate struggle of the two men.

Subaru coughing blood, groaning in pain, collapsing. How many times, each time that happened, had Emilia nearly cried out and rushed to his side?

But every time Emilia's weak heart threatened to give up on waiting, Subaru's words—"watch"—and his gaze, which met hers as if he'd timed it perfectly, held her back.

A scene where she was never allowed to lend a hand, never allowed to speak up. A scene that was maddening, nearly unbearable—and yet one she must never look away from.

No one had told her so directly. But Emilia's heart had quietly understood that this was something she must absolutely never do.

She didn't know what the feeling was that rose in her chest, holding her feet in place. Subaru had been so stubbornly proud, Garfiel had roared and raged so fiercely, and the fight had been settled at the end of a grueling, muddy exchange of blows between men—what exactly had underpinned that struggle? As a bystander to it, as a woman who found men's logic hard to grasp, Emilia could only understand it in fragments.

Still, in this fight, Subaru had shown his conviction and his pride, and with the help of many others, he had defeated Garfiel. That much was certain, and it was an undeniable fact that some inexpressible emotion was swelling within Emilia's chest.

Driven by that emotion, Emilia acknowledged the two men's fight as something worthy of praise. Which was exactly why the meaning of their battle must never be allowed to be defiled. And so—

"...Roswaal."

Closing her eyes once, and leaving her hesitation behind for good, Emilia turned to face forward. Straight ahead, her gaze leapt past Subaru and the others lying below, to the gap in the trees beyond.

—There, a single Warlock stood in silence.

"When you stay quiet like that, it makes me uneasy. It makes me want to suspect you're up to something."

"No-o-ow this is unexpected. To be accused of such a thing, when I forced my injured body out here for Lady Emilia's and Subaru-kun's sake."

"If that's really how you feel, I'd love to be reassured, but..."

Before Emilia, who trailed off mid-sentence, a tall figure emerged from the underbrush—Roswaal. In the days since arriving at the Sanctuary, she'd only ever seen him confined to his bed; that he'd come out and made his way here now stirred a faint wariness in Emilia.

Ordinarily, Roswaal was, for Emilia, the Royal Selection's sponsor—and in terms of raw power, arguably her only ally. It was he who had led Emilia out of the forest and shown her the path to the throne, he who had presented the possibility of saving everyone in the village sleeping beneath the ice. So until now, setting aside his more than eccentric personality, Emilia had never truly viewed him as an enemy, never found him tiresome, never seen him as a danger. But that, too, had only held true until just a moment ago.

"The lesser spirits have been restless and agitated for a while now."

"...Ho-o-oh."

"They all say they can feel something terribly ominous. ...And now, I can see it clearly too."

Her voice low with tension, Emilia slowly descended from the entrance of the tomb into the plaza. Subaru lay collapsed with a satisfied look on his face; Garfiel, his mouth twisted in bitter regret. Emilia moved to stand beside Patrasche, positioned between the two of them, taking up a place from which she could protect all three if it came to that. That she felt she had to think in terms of protecting the three of them—that alone showed how alien the aura Roswaal was giving off had become.

The air around Roswaal was warped by a dense, abnormally concentrated mana. How great was the density of the mana he was compounding within his own body? Roswaal L. Mathers, known as the finest mage in the kingdom, a man who wielded all six elemental attributes. When that man unleashed his magic to its utmost limit, what manner of miracle could he wreak?

"――――"

Feeling almost intoxicated by the dense mana, Emilia caught her breath. Beside her, Patrasche too had positioned herself as if to hide Subaru from Roswaal's line of sight, stretching her long neck and letting out a low growl to menace the Warlock. Patrasche, too, sensed a threat in Roswaal's extraordinary presence. Seeing the two of them on guard, Roswaal shrugged with his usual air.

"Frightening, frightening. I-I-I'd rather you not look at me that way. It seems I have a rather unfortunate knack for being disliked by animals—Earth Dragons included. If it's the same with spirits, then it would explain why Beatrice and I never did get along, wouldn't it, no-o-ow."

"Don't joke around. Besides, I'm sure this girl's bad mood has a different reason. ...Unless it's been that way since long ago, in which case I wouldn't know."

"No-o-o. Long ago—truly long ago—that wasn't the case at all, no-o-ow. Back before there were Earth Dragons, I used to travel to this very place riding a Phantom Bull—a Falo."

"Before there were... Earth Dragons...?"

Unable to grasp the meaning of what Roswaal had said, Emilia furrowed her brow. Emilia wasn't well-versed in the subject either, but Earth Dragons were already thoroughly woven into daily life, and their culture and history—their long-standing connection to humanity—surely stretched back a good while. Did Roswaal mean "within Lugunica"? Since when Earth Dragons had come to be so relied upon in Lugunica was a gap in her knowledge, admittedly.

Seeing the question mark hovering over Emilia, Roswaal let out a small sigh. It was a sigh that somehow carried the sense of a disappointment he'd already known was coming.

"Surely even you know this, Lady Emilia. We-e-ell, I suppose not. Even granting that you carry the blood of the long-lived Elves, you're still only a little past a hundred years old... and having spent the greater part of that asleep, I imagine you wouldn't remember the world as it was back then, no-o-ow."

"...That's a strange thing to say, Roswaal. If you're going to bring that up, you're much younger than me too. Well—in terms of time actually spent awake, I suppose I lose."

Having spent roughly a hundred years within the ice, left behind by the world's flow of time, was, for Emilia, a shameful part of her past. By any measure across the world, she ought to be counted among the elders—and yet she'd stored up none of the experience or knowledge that ought to accompany that age. Including her failure to pass the Trials—it was one more piece of the many "insufficiencies" of herself that she'd come to newly recognize during her days here in the Sanctuary.

But Roswaal scoffed derisively at Emilia's modest worry. That reaction was unexpected enough that a startled Emilia's brow shot up at once.

"Hold on, Roswaal. What do you mean, laughing like that?"

"――. My apologies. It's nothing much, no-o-o. ...Only that ignorance, at times, gives rise to circumstances so sadly comical."

"...You're making fun of me. Even I can tell that much."

At Roswaal's oddly arrogant words, Emilia's wariness grew, her brow arching further. The abnormal mana surrounding Roswaal. As if she herself were being intoxicated by what she was compounding, the attitude he directed at her carried a change unlike anything before.

Never before had Emilia experienced Roswaal directing such plain malice at her. The Roswaal she knew was always joking around, always making cynical remarks, always trading in the same kind of pointless banter as Subaru or Puck—but like them, he had never once spoken in a way meant to demean her. That was because, in service of whatever goal Roswaal held, Emilia's existence was necessary to him as a collaborator—someone he needed to treat as a figure to be placed above himself, as a future "Queen."

Which meant, put the other way around: the Roswaal before her now no longer saw that value in Emilia. Had he grown impatient with her endless failure to pass the Trials, and simply given up on her? If so, that would still be all right. That, she could still accept. But what Emilia feared more, right now, was something else entirely.

"Roswaal... how long have you been watching Subaru and the others fight?"

"—How long, you ask?"

"I noticed you were there... a little while ago. Around when Subaru and Garfiel started trading blows... right around when Subaru used Shamak, I think."

Pushing his ragged Gate past its limits, Subaru had used magic—for what must have been countless times by then. Wringing out the last dregs of his mana, casting the spell as if squeezing out his very last strength, only for it to be unleashed clumsily and dissipate into nothing.

Come to think of it, that must have been the moment Emilia had truly almost rushed to his side. Once before, in front of Emilia, Subaru had used Shamak in the same desperate, all-or-nothing way, and at the end of it had suffered as overwhelming a defeat as could be imagined. That earlier scene and this one overlapped, and no one could blame her for nearly crying out. But when Subaru drove his hidden trump card—the crystal stone—into Garfiel, tipping the balance to something like even odds, an emotion other than pure desperation was born in Emilia's heart as well.

And it was only once she'd lost that particular sense of urgency that Emilia noticed it. The alien presence of someone else, watching the two men's battle from the shadows, just as she was.

"At first, I thought maybe you'd step in too, once Subaru reached his limit. Ram and Otto-kun seemed to be doing something to stop Garfiel, so I thought maybe you'd come to help Subaru too. But..."

"Just as you say, I did come to lend Subaru-kun aid. Th-o-ough I doubt you'll believe that, no-o-ow."

"Even without Puck, I can still sense the flow of mana. I could tell you were watching the fight, ready to strike at any moment... and which of them you had your aim fixed on."

"――――"

Roswaal's mismatched eyes—one blue, one yellow—narrowed as they regarded Emilia. Those same eyes had narrowed just like this while watching the earlier battle. Always ready to unleash the magic he'd built up, his aim fixed on the struggling Subaru.

"Answer me, Roswaal. —What were you planning to do to Subaru?"

Emilia raised her palm and pointed it at Roswaal as she asked. Puck wasn't at her side. She had doubts about her control over mana. The lesser spirits, sensing the ominous power wrapped around Roswaal, were crying out to Emilia in strong fear. If she couldn't fully borrow their strength, then she would simply have to do it herself.

"Please, answer me. Otherwise, I..."

"Even now, at this point, you-o-ou still hesitate to judge. What an endlessly carefree person you are. Or perhaps you simply place too much faith in the good will of others? After days upon days of nothing but malice heaped upon you, how can you still leave yourself so wide open?"

"――――っ"

Emilia's near-pleading words were overwritten by Roswaal's merciless malice. As Emilia faltered, Roswaal—harboring ill will in equal measure in both his differently colored eyes—refused to yield. The same held true for the several currents of mana swirling chaotically within him.

The ominous, threatening atmosphere. Emilia reflexively reached a hand to her chest, then gritted her teeth, remembering the familiar presence that was no longer there. It was infuriating, this weakness of hers, unconsciously trying to hand her anxiety off to Puck. As if to cover for that weakness, Emilia poured more force into the glare she leveled at Roswaal.

"I understand you have no intention of answering my question. In that case, I—kyaa!"

She would question Roswaal's true intent without holding back. Resolved to do so, the instant she tried to focus her mana, something struck her lightly on the side of the head.

Startled by the sensation of her silver hair being mussed, Emilia turned to the side, and found an Earth Dragon's snout there. Patrasche. It was more forceful than what could be called a mere nudge, and Emilia's eyes went wide. Then the jet-black Earth Dragon, her expression retaining its noble bearing, nudged her forehead once more with the tip of her nose.

"You..."

Though they couldn't exchange words, to Emilia it felt like a cheer of encouragement from the Earth Dragon.

—Calm down. And, staying level-headed, do what you need to do.

She felt as though that sharp gaze were telling her exactly that, and Emilia realized she'd let herself grow heated. Closing her eyes, and when she turned back to face Roswaal again, her hand was no longer at her chest.

"Truly... I have no-o-o fond memories where Earth Dragons are concerned."

Seeing Emilia's changed expression, Roswaal remarked on Patrasche with irritation. Patrasche's concern had been effective enough to that degree in breaking Roswaal's scheme. Which meant, in other words, that Roswaal had wanted Emilia to strike at him just now.

"I have no idea at all what you're thinking right now, Roswaal. Even now, if this girl hadn't stopped me, I probably would have... And yet, it's like you wanted that to happen."

"Just so you know, I dislike being made to suffer pain as much as the next person, you-o-ou know?"

"...? Everyone dislikes that, don't they?"

At Emilia's furrow-browed reply, she saw Roswaal's lips loosen into something sardonic. What that smile meant, Emilia had no idea at all. In any case, the means Emilia ought to choose here was not force.

"Talk to me, Roswaal. I can tell just by looking that you, right now, aren't your usual self. Tell me why you're acting like this... like you've given up on everything."

"...Given up, is it? Hmm, now this is unexpected indeed."

"You're standing there so calm, like it wouldn't matter if I hit you with magic out of sheer recklessness... and you expect me to believe you haven't given up on everything?"

Emilia herself could understand the destructive impulse of hating oneself so much that one wished everything would just fall apart. The only difference was whether one turned that impulse inward or outward. Emilia was the type to turn it inward. Was Roswaal the same, perhaps?

"If that's how it is, then talk to me. I don't know what I can do, but I'll help. Because, up until now, you've helped me so much, and..."

"—No. That's quite enough, Lady Emilia."

But the hand Emilia extended was rejected by Roswaal's hoarse voice. Emilia looked up at the flattest tone she'd ever heard from him, and found his frozen eyes looking down at her. Beneath the clown's makeup—beneath the paint that made him look as though he were smiling—she could see that his true face had killed off its emotions to a painful degree, and Emilia caught her breath.

Roswaal's expression seemed almost like the face of someone who had thrown everything away entirely.

"Quite enough... what do you mean?"

"Exactly what it sounds like. I have no intention of making you understand my intentions, and these two's injuries, the Trials of the Tomb... before even that, the Royal Selection itself no longer matters in the slightest, no-o-ow. —Because this is a world that's already ending, after all."

"A world that's ending... and what do you mean, it doesn't matter? The Royal Selection and the Trials don't matter... Roswaal, what are you saying!?"

At the sudden outburst, unable to grasp its true meaning, Emilia raised her voice in a shout. Roswaal, who had been giving off such an ominous air—but now his expression was filled with a sense of nihilism, and the mana wrapped around him was filled with an emptiness so profound it felt as though it would crush her.

Roswaal's heart, right now, was at the very extreme of instability. Even understanding that much, his claim was not something Emilia could accept.

What he was trying to throw away was, to Emilia, everything that mattered—and it was the very thing, impossible to put into words, that Subaru had risked his life to prove.

The claim Garfiel had made in opposing Subaru had been made unmistakably clear by his cries. Garfiel had tried to destroy the tomb, to stop the Trials from continuing. What had he been seeking, by refusing to let the Sanctuary be released from its barrier? Emilia somehow understood that he'd been seeking days without change. And Garfiel's claim wasn't something Emilia found impossible to sympathize with, either.

To remain unchanged, to be able to remain unchanged—that was a kind of peace, an easy path. If that meant a peaceful place, an everyday life spent with people who mattered, then no one could deny the wish to remain steeped in that time forever.

And yet Subaru had flatly denied it, had shown his stubborn pride and thrown it aside. In terms of position, Emilia too was on Subaru's side—the side urging change upon the Sanctuary. But she hadn't established her reasons for doing so as firmly as Subaru had, nor had she taken in the feelings of the Sanctuary's people the way he had. It was merely territory she'd stepped into because it was a necessary step toward her goal. What did the people around her think of the change it would bring—Emilia, who tended to look only inward, hadn't even asked herself something so obvious.

Hadn't it been Subaru, just now, who had asked and pressed that question in her place? Once again, she had let Subaru shoulder the burden that should have been hers, and let everything slide.

It was precisely because she keenly felt that, that she stood here now. And yet Roswaal was trying to throw away this very now that Subaru had carved open at the risk of his life.

"Roswaal... what is it you're trying to throw away? Wasn't this something... something you and I started together? To just abandon it partway through... that can't be allowed!"

At Emilia's biting words, Roswaal's brow twitched in response. He recovered a faint measure of strength in his eyes, then covered his blue eye with one hand, letting his yellow eye tremble.

"Something you and I started, Lady Emilia...? What are you talking about?"

"Eh...?"

"What was started, was started by me and Sensei. —Certainly not by you-o-ou. Which means bringing it to an end ought to be a matter of my and Sensei's freedom as well. It's decided."

"There's no way something so selfish should be allowed!"

Overwhelmed by his abnormal presence, Emilia still raised her voice. Glaring at the clown's face, teetering on the edge of losing his emotions, Emilia raised her arm.

"Maybe it was you and this 'Sensei' of yours who started it at first, but... it isn't just a matter between the two of you anymore. It isn't just a matter for me alone, either. So many people have been drawn into it, so many people have been troubled by it, and that's how it's continued this far! There's no way it can just be brought to an end on your own terms!"

"The path already leads nowhere but to an ending. In that case, wh-o-o's to say there's anything wrong with bringing it to an end here, before it reaches that ending on its own? This time around, we-e-ell, it seems we failed after all... let's place our hopes in the next 'us,' and in Subaru-kun."

"In Subaru...?"

What did it mean, to place hope in Subaru? Glancing at the fallen Subaru, Emilia renewed her resolve that she must never let Roswaal's words come to pass.

Of course not.

Because Subaru had already done more than enough of what needed doing. If it was hope Roswaal wanted, Subaru had already answered it more than sufficiently. There was nothing more that should be demanded of him now. What he'd been given should be met with the reward he rightfully deserved.

"How can you say that the road Subaru opened leads to an ending? A path that was closing—Subaru and the others pooled their strength and pried it back open. Isn't that what this fight, right now, was about?"

"Right or left, both lead to dead ends. It was nothing but a futile effort, straining so hard before a fork like that. The truly correct path is recorded, in its entirety, right here."

As he spoke, Roswaal drew a black-bound book from within his priest's robes. Seeing that unfamiliar thing, Emilia felt, for some reason, an urge welling up within her chest to claw at herself, and her eyes widened.

It was a book that filled her with a terribly unpleasant feeling. She couldn't see its title or its cover—it looked like nothing more than an unremarkable book—and yet, for some reason, merely looking at its binding gave Emilia a crushing pressure that seemed to destabilize her very spirit.

"That book is..."

"A duplicate of the Book of Wisdom. Or perhaps I should call it a fine Gospel. To anyone but me, it's nothing but scribbled nonsense—characters no one else can read. But to me, it's different. It's a guiding book that records the correct history to be followed."

"The history to be followed... you mean, something like a Dragon History Stone...?"

"I'm told by Sensei that, traced back to their roots, they share the same principle."

Sensei. Only when he spoke that word, as he had just now, did emotion return to Roswaal's eyes. It was a tone of voice that let one sense a great deal—as if picturing someone dear to him, as if he continued to harbor feelings, in those four syllables alone, that he could never quite kill off. So it seemed even Roswaal could think of someone that way, as a matter of course. And yet, despite being able to, he was trying to declare everything before him worthless.

"So because things are proceeding in a different form than what's written in that book, you're going to ruin it? What good would that do? If you searched for a path—one different from the book, one that isn't a dead end—"

"You're saying the same thing as Subaru-kun. Is that his influence too, by any chance?"

"――っ!"

At Roswaal's faint smile, Emilia felt as though he'd struck a nerve, and her throat tightened. Seeing Emilia's expression, Roswaal let out a bored-sounding sigh.

"Borrowed words, a position prepared for you. Even taking on the Trials—you were pushed into thinking you should, weren't you... ye-e-es, I won't blame you for it. It was I, and those around you, who told you to do so—not you yourself. Words spoken knowing full well that you, empty and with nothing of your own, had no choice but to do it that way... Subaru-kun really does demand cruel things of people."

"Cruel things, that's not..."

"Knowing him, he probably didn't bother explaining the logic of why taking on the Trials was necessary—he probably just scolded and encouraged you, didn't he? Forced his self-righteousness on you, just spouted some sheer willpower nonsense about how you can do it if you try. I understand. I understand only too-o-o well. After all, he and I are the same sort, no-o-ow."

"Subaru and you, the same? What do you mean?"

"I mean that we both force our ideals onto the woman we love."

A flat declaration. Closing one eye, gazing at Emilia with only his yellow eye, Roswaal kept his weary smile fixed in place, continuing to hurl words at the silent Emilia.

"What sort of words were thrown at you? Nothing but words pleasant to the ear, weren't they? You were coddled, had ideals forced onto you, handled ever so delicately and carefully, as if touching something fragile and gentle? He never once considered that the real you is weak, fragile, prone to fear and the urge to run—that you're an utterly ordinary person at heart, did he? He has not the slightest interest in the real you. What he loves is only the you that shines inside his own head. —Isn't th-a-at so?"

"――――"

"It's the same for me. I too looked only at ideals, where she was concerned. Wonderful, magnificent, no one could ever surpass you—praising her like that, saying it over and over, pouring in love as if touching glasswork... something so meaningless."

Speaking rapidly, Roswaal turned his gaze away with something like irritation. Whether he was talking about Subaru, or narrating his own experience, perhaps even Roswaal himself could no longer draw a clear line between the two. Overwhelmed by the force of it, Emilia drew a small breath. Even feeling pressed down by Roswaal's current demeanor, she did so in order to say what needed to be said.

"...Is that all?"

"――――"

"Is that really the only thing you think Subaru and you have in common?"

At Emilia's question, Roswaal turned a suspicious look toward her. The doubt that had arisen within him. His failure to continue speaking was itself a clear answer to Emilia's question. If so, then she had to say it after all. She had to correct his thinking.

"If that's all you have to say..."

"――――"

"Subaru and Roswaal aren't alike at all."

Because Subaru, who had chased Emilia even into the depths of the tomb, had indeed laid out one idealistic argument after another, and he'd never once explained to her what it meant to free the Sanctuary. But he had never once simply piled empty platitudes and pretty phrases on her, either.

"Subaru told me I was a troublesome woman, you know."

"...What?"

"He said—while he was doing all sorts of things himself, all I did was cause trouble, and who did I think I was. He said I keep clinging to things that are already past, that all I do is make people expect things of me and never deliver. He said I'm all talk, that I'm lacking in everything, that it's unbearable to watch. —That's what Subaru said to me."

"――――"

"Subaru actually looks at me, properly. And now I think I can't keep showing him only my unsightly side either. That's why Roswaal—pretending to look ahead while actually seeing nothing right now—is nothing at all like Subaru."

If Natsuki Subaru had only ever pictured an idealized version of Emilia, seeing right through her, she'd surely still be sitting with her knees drawn up inside that tomb right now. And Garfiel, too—if Subaru hadn't confronted him while knowing full well everything beyond mere ideals, and still voiced his idealistic arguments anyway, Garfiel would never even have lent him an ear.

Subaru had seen Emilia's weakness, and still said he loved her regardless. Subaru had known Garfiel's kindness, and still told him to change regardless.

To everyone, anyone, who wanted to keep clinging to the place they stood, Subaru kept going around, scolding and pushing them onward. Telling them: you can't stay here, there's more you can do, lift your face, look ahead, raise your fists, you don't have time to stand still.

—That one could never simply keep standing still forever.

"Natsuki Subaru, making the right choice here in the Sanctuary...? Something so absurd... if that's true, then this Book of Wisdom's account..."

"My memories came back, and I was afraid. Puck disappeared, and I felt like I was being crushed."

Roswaal was clearly bewildered by the gap between his own thinking and the answer Subaru had shown. Emilia placed a hand to her chest—not to check for that familiar presence, but to confirm the existence of herself, the heartbeat pulsing beneath it.

"When I remembered everything, I was sure I'd become something different. I was sure that when that happened, the 'me' who'd made it this far would become something wrong."

Even now, the lost fragments of memory in her own mind still cried out. Once they formed a clear shape, something irreversible would appear.

Gaining that, Emilia's world would change utterly. That change—Emilia had feared it, had wanted to reject it—but she'd come to know it wasn't necessary to fear it at all.

Whatever changed, the path she'd walked so far wouldn't simply vanish. However Emilia might change from here, even if it meant becoming a self different in shape from how she'd begun, the feelings she held right now, in this moment, would not become a mistake because of it.

Even if she stumbled, even if she came to a stop, she could start walking again. Looking toward whatever direction felt like forward, and heading forward.

"When I want to try, when I think I can change, when I want to do it—someone taught me that it's still all right even then, that there's someone who'll take my hand and pull me along."

"Delusion...!"

"It isn't a lie. I want to believe in Subaru, who told me he believes in me. What he said might seem like baseless nonsense right now. You might want to call it a lie. But... making sure it isn't a lie is the thing I have to do."

She would never let anyone slap a label on him—on Subaru—calling him a liar who ran himself ragged preaching false hope to a hopeless, helpless Emilia who could do nothing. Natsuki Subaru had declared to Emilia, "I'm sure you can do it."

As Emilia stood now, that would make it a lie. But if Emilia broke through her shell, and accomplished that "I'm sure you can do it," then the lie would no longer be a lie. People have a word for that. They call it a "wish."

"Turning a lie into a wish—that's what I have to do now, and what I want to do."

What Subaru had desperately, earnestly taught her. That thing which she couldn't put into shape within herself was finally connecting to words.

She didn't know if this was truly the right answer. Shaping what hadn't yet taken form into something correct—that was Emilia's task to carry out. And she felt that she wanted to go forward with that, without hesitating any longer, without wavering any longer.

"――――っ! That's madness!"

At Emilia's answer, it was Roswaal whose color changed, who stepped back. Casting aside both his usual composure and the nihilism he'd shown just moments before, he wrung out his voice with an almost despairing expression, quailing before her answer. He raised his arm, pointed at Emilia, and shouted in a voice that cracked.

"Why—how! You arrive at that answer, here, now!? The very thing I could never convey to Sensei—why has Natsuki Subaru managed to convey it to you first!? For him, now! At this stage! He has no right to have reached that feeling!"

"I've remembered what it was I was afraid of. And I'm in the middle of steeling my resolve to face it now, too... what is it you're afraid of?"

"Obviously! Straying from what's recorded! Failing to proceed according to this recorded history, and losing the reunion that was promised! What else could there possibly be!"

"But it seems to me that what's troubling you right now is something different from that."

"――――!"

A flame of anger lit in Roswaal's eyes. What he voiced was fury at his inability to sympathize with Subaru's state of mind. Roswaal had wanted, somehow, for Subaru to feel the same thing he himself had once experienced in the past. The words he'd spoken—that he and Subaru were of the same kind—might not have been mockery or a mere figure of speech, but something Roswaal had stubbornly, firmly believed ought to be true.

Perhaps he'd believed that Subaru and Roswaal, in how they faced the one they loved, ought to trace the same course, ought to bear the same wound. That foundation was now shaking, his confidence on the verge of crumbling.

That was how Roswaal's current distress appeared to Emilia.

"Ah, what is this...! That it should come to this, of all things."

Roswaal pressed a hand to his mouth, twisting his cheeks without caring that his makeup was coming undone.

"Was I being made to dance in the palm of someone's hand from the moment the wager was made? That Garfiel's loss, even Emilia's recovery, were all accounted for from the start? ...Was the mistake made the moment I schemed against the sage's deterrent...? Then what was it all for, what have I—"

"Roswaal?"

Buried in a dizzying whirl of thought, Roswaal pushed Emilia's presence out beyond the edge of his awareness. With a desperate expression, what he was thinking of was how to correct the course of a world that had strayed from his design. But no such method existed anywhere in this world any longer.

Emilia had no idea what was recorded in the prophetic book he held. For Roswaal to be driven this far—it must have diverged, magnificently, wildly off course. So much so, in fact, that it was as good as an entirely different world now.

"—Ah, I see."

A murmur slipped from Roswaal, who had until now been caught in a whirlpool of confusion. There was a ring of clarity back in his voice, and Emilia allowed herself to hope his confusion had settled, if only for the moment. A rational conversation, searching for common ground—

"There's n-o-o need to worry over any of it. Either way, there's the 'Contract.' Whether he can reach the 'genuine article' or not—there was never anything to agonize over."

"What are you talking about? Roswaal, now what are you—"

"Nothing. Nothing at all, Lady Emilia. My apologies for worrying and confusing you so. You need only act as you wish, exactly as I expect you to."

Bowing at the waist, Roswaal scattered his clownish manner about him once more, turning a smile toward her. Of course, there was no way Emilia could accept this. A complete reversal from his distress of moments ago, back to this same unchanging demeanor. That his earlier distraught state had seemed far more "sane" by comparison was itself something strange.

"...What you expect of me—what exactly do you expect from me?"

"Of course. —That you take on the Trials as your heart dictates, and produce a result."

Which result, Roswaal didn't specify. And which one he was hoping for, Emilia had some vague sense of, all the same.

What she didn't understand was why Roswaal wished for it. And the reason he'd grown so reckless, and then swallowed it back down and reined himself in. There was nothing but things she didn't understand. But—

"That's something you're not going to tell me right now... are you."

"…………"

"Fine. I won't force you to tell me. Because I'm sure I don't have the right to, not now. —But don't think you can keep it hidden forever."

"—How reassuring. Whether you can maintain that bravado once you've learned the truth and reclaimed yourself remains to be seen."

That last exchange, tinged with malice—and yet, to Emilia, it somehow felt like a plea. With that, Roswaal turned his back on her and began to walk away. His destination was, presumably, the temporary lodging where he'd been recuperating. In the end, Emilia never learned why he'd come all this way, to watch over the fight. The swirling density of his mana remained unchanged even now, still yearning within Roswaal's body for its moment of miracle.

"That's right. —Lady Emilia, one piece of advice."

"What?"

Roswaal stopped, raising a finger, and Emilia's brow lifted. At how defenselessly Emilia exposed herself to someone who, only moments ago, had all but severed ties with her in a nearly hostile exchange, Roswaal gave a wry smile.

"As for Garfiel... you'd best not underestimate how deep his roots run. His obsession isn't shallow enough to change its mind just because he's been beaten down."

"—Understood."

Emilia accepted it readily. Leaving her behind, Roswaal finally departed for good. Once she'd seen him off, all that remained in the plaza was Emilia's breathing, and the snorting breath of the jet-black Earth Dragon who kept glaring at his retreating back until the very end. And beyond that, only the deep breathing—almost like sleep—of the two unconscious, fallen men.

"—Haah."

Emilia's shoulders trembled, and she let out a long breath. Noticing Patrasche's eyes widen beside her at the reaction, Emilia gave a wry smile.

"No, I'm fine. Thanks to you, I managed to stay calm. ...But that was so-o-o tense. It might have come to a fight with Roswaal."

"――――"

"Yeah. I hate fighting without even knowing why. I wonder why Roswaal got like that... maybe Subaru knows."

Responding to Patrasche's concerned gaze, Emilia knelt beside the supine Subaru and gently raised his blood-soaked body. Brushing away the blood that had begun to dry with her fingers, she gently stroked his swollen face. His expression twitched faintly, as if he'd felt a flicker of pain, or perhaps ticklishness.

"I need to treat you. Neither you nor Garfiel like pain, right?"

"――――"

"Oh, you don't need to look so worried. My control's a bit shaky without Puck, but I can still manage simple healing with the lesser spirits' help."

As she spoke, Emilia called out to the lesser spirits around her, borrowing their power as a faint glow wrapped around her. A soft light enveloped Subaru and Garfiel, beginning to heal their wounds.

Subaru's expression eased, somehow. Emilia gave a small smile at that, and gently laid his head upon her own lap.

How many times now had she lent Subaru her lap like this? Though she'd received so much more than that from him besides, more than she even knew how to begin repaying.

"When you wake up, I've got so-o-o many things I want to ask you."

Murmuring quietly, Emilia gently slipped her fingers into Subaru's forelocks. At the way his face scrunched up, her cheeks loosened just slightly.

—It was about ten minutes later that Otto, together with Ram carried on his back, joined them from the direction of the forest.
