Arc 4 Chapter 96: "With Rouge on the Lips"

"The fragment of the past that Garfiel saw..."

"In the truest sense, it's nothing more than a fragment. I was the one who carried that boy out of the tomb while he babbled deliriously and sobbed. So I know the outline of it, more or less."

Subaru held his breath and turned back to face Σ.

Garfiel's past — it wasn't what he'd expected to come out of this talk with Σ, but it was still one of the problems Subaru would have had to face sooner or later anyway. The order of things had gotten scrambled, but the information he could gain here now was nothing to complain about.

"Can you tell me? What did he — what did Garfiel see in the past, and why did he give up on freeing the Sanctuary? Why does he refuse so fiercely to let the villagers out?"

"—What that boy saw was the moment he parted from his mother."

"The moment... he parted from her."

A sharp, twisting pain gouged at Subaru's chest.

A parting from one's mother — in the context of the Trial, that phrase struck a chord that wasn't unrelated to Subaru either. But unlike Subaru, who could look back on that memory with a clear heart, Garfiel's wasn't the same. That was precisely why he was bound to the Sanctuary with such maddening intensity.

"Does Su-bou know anything about that boy's mother?"

"...I guess, just the gist of it, from Frederica. Their mother was an ordinary human, and with a half-elf and... well, things happened, and she ended up having the two of them. And then she left them both behind in the Sanctuary."

"And went off, alone and heartless, to find happiness for herself away from here... is that the shape of it?"

Filling in the part Subaru found hard to say, Σ let a fleeting, fragile smile touch her lips. What she'd added matched exactly what Subaru had heard from Frederica about their mother's circumstances. Her turbulent life was, no doubt, the work of considerable bad luck.

If he recalled right, Frederica and Garfiel each went by a different parent's surname — Frederica took her father's name, Baumann, while Garfiel took his mother's, Tinzel.

"But I remember Frederica saying the two of them were still small when their mother left... so even if he's being shown a memory from back then, how vivid could it really be?"

"This is a Trial devised by Echidna, keeper of the Book of Wisdom, you know. Rather than lean on something as unreliable as a hazy memory, it would reach into the person's most primal memories and shape the world from those. ...The past that unfolded should have matched, to the last detail, the scene that little one saw as a child."

"................."

Subaru reconsidered — his objection had been thin ground to argue from, given what Σ had said. In fact, relying on Subaru's own memory, Echidna had reproduced things this world had no equivalent for — the streets of his old world, even his old school building. In the sense of drawing out the deepest parts of a person's memory, Subaru himself, of all people, was living proof of it.

"Fine. I'll grant you that. But the real question is... why would seeing that scene of his mother leaving all over again do enough damage to break him down into tears like that?"

"................."

"I don't mean to be harsh, but for him that's a parting from way back when he was small — he's surely spent far longer apart from his mother than with her by now. Clinging forever to something that far in the past just doesn't fit his character, I'd think—"

"Does Su-bou think that for a small child, the memory of being abandoned by his mother is really so shallow a wound?"

Σ's words, cutting in as Subaru tried to press a point he couldn't accept, carried an edge sharp enough to draw blood. Subaru felt as if a blade of words had been laid against his throat, and he fell silent despite himself. Watching him, Σ lowered eyes heavy with grief.

"Though truth be told, even those of us close by — myself, and his sister Frederica — never knew how large the thorn buried in that child's heart really was. Perhaps even Gar-bou himself wasn't aware of it, but touching that past again through the Trial brought the wound to the surface. ...I believe that's the reason for his excessive, obsessive caution now."

"...So, what, then? He refuses to free the Sanctuary — not so much over the outside world itself, but out of... negative feelings toward the mother who chose the outside world over him?"

"He must hate it, I'd think. The outside world. The world that took his mother and left him behind. Even if he wanted to chase after her, he couldn't bring us with him past the barrier into the outside world. For a child who wanted to cherish both his mother and the people of the Sanctuary, that's a contradiction he can't bear."

"Does he hate her, then? His mother, for leaving him behind and going out into the world?"

It was a pain Subaru, who'd never been abandoned by a parent, couldn't truly understand. No matter how much of a mess Subaru made of himself, no matter how much he insisted he was hopeless, his parents had never once given up on him, never once written him off.

He'd been saved by that blessing — and now, that same blessing was what tormented him.

"How to say it, I wonder..."

Σ avoided giving an immediate, clear answer to Subaru's question. Her gaze wandered, and after searching for words with some hesitation, she let out a small breath.

"I've never once managed to draw out how that child truly feels about his mother, deep down. I was too much of a coward for that. I was afraid that asking would only remind him of that time... of the way he cried and whimpered there in the tomb. So I never could bring myself to ask."

"Σ-san..."

"But even knowing all that past, when he gives his name, he calls himself Garfiel Tinzel. He attaches his mother's family name to his own. I believe, surely, he carries it so as never to forget."

"So as never to forget..."

Nodding at Subaru's words, Σ looked fondly around the ramshackle hut — the ill-made little hut Garfiel had built with his own two hands, working so hard at it —

"So as never to forget the feeling he had, seeing that past. —Whether it was fury or sorrow, even now, I still don't know."

"You've got an awful look on your face, Natsuki-san. Are you even aware of it?"

Seeing Subaru as they met up in the middle of the settlement, that was the first thing Otto said, with a wry laugh.

It was a rather rich thing to say right off the bat, and Subaru just shrugged and looked him over.

"Look who's talking. In the half a day since I last saw you, you've turned into a proper grubby mess. Running wild in the forest at your age — grow up a little, would you?"

"Nobody's doing this because they want to end up looking like they've been mud-wrestling, you know!"

"Don't shout, it's pounding on my sleep-deprived skull. Speaking of sleep-deprived, those bags under your eyes are no joke either. Playing in the mud when you can't even spare time to sleep... are you gripped by some obsession to cram in everything you'll regret leaving undone before your remaining time runs out, or what?"

"Could you please stop making it sound like some tearjerker melodrama that was popular a while back!?"

From Otto's outburst, Subaru picked up the useless bit of knowledge that apparently, even in this world, there'd once been a fad for stories chronicling the final days of the terminally ill.

Once all the surrounding mess got sorted out, he thought privately, it might be worth importing a bunch of stories from his old world — but for now, Subaru just said, "Anyway," and pressed on.

"Let's leave the horseplay there for now... so, how's it actually going?"

"I won't deny it's been a struggle without you around, Natsuki-san... but even so, I think I can call it a job well done, if I do say so myself. This is the part where you're allowed to praise me, you know."

"Good boy, good boy. You're the only one I can count on. Keep it up."

"That was an incredibly hollow cheer! I could practically feel the air go dry!"

Watching Otto's exaggerated reaction as he flailed both arms up and down, Subaru privately never stopped feeling grateful for his cooperation. Actually saying so out loud was far too embarrassing, though, so he meant to carry that gratitude to his grave without ever voicing it.

"Anyway, it's good news that things are moving on that front. By the way, how's the other one coming along? Looking usable?"

"That one... it's rough, in this short a time. There's simply not enough hours. If I cut into my sleep, I might just barely make it by the day after tomorrow... something like that, I'd say."

"You're willing to cut into your sleep for it? I've got no words for how hard a worker you are."

"Nguaah! I walked right into that...!"

Otto clutched his head, looking as if he regretted the slip. But even without Subaru pressing the point like that, he probably would have chosen, on his own judgment, to scrape the missing time out of his own sleep regardless.

It was that unstinting sincerity that Subaru trusted in him, as a "friend."

Not knowing he was being thanked in a way that never once showed on Subaru's face or manner, Otto wiped the pained look off, said "So then," and turned back to face Subaru.

"What about you, Natsuki-san — is the stage going to be set on your end? With the trouble that's come up with Emilia-sama, honestly, part of me was tempted to just pack up and skip town in the night."

"I'm confident the missing pieces are steadily coming together. What worries me is I still can't be sure exactly what picture they'll form once they're all fitted in."

"I-is that really okay? There's no time left, you know..."

"If the pieces don't all fit into place by the deadline, we'll fill the gaps in with love, courage, and friendship. In the stories I know, that always somehow does the trick."

"Listen, Natsuki-san. I did say I'd set the odds aside, sure, but that was on the assumption it still counts as a real contest to begin with. Betting on something with zero chance of winning isn't just foolish — it's practically the act of someone with a death wish, so..."

Otto kept grumbling on about something or other, but Subaru ignored him and started walking. Otto, reluctantly, matched his pace and followed.

Without either stoking his anxiety or scolding him for it, Subaru headed for the building where Roswaal was waiting — not that it was strictly necessary, but it was where he meant to bring word of their progress.

Having finished his talk with Ryuzu Σ, Subaru had come away with a fragment of Garfiel's past. Σ had no further information beyond that, but she'd told him the reason she herself couldn't bring herself to actively support freeing the Sanctuary was Garfiel's rejection of that past, and the fact that θ — who had handed the tomb's affairs off to Σ in her place — had been in such an unstable state.

Going by the clones' rotation, the θ in question was due to appear tomorrow. Whatever θ had seen in the Trial was presumably a memory of Ryuzu Meyer's encounter with Echidna — but within it, what exactly had she seen that made her refuse the world beyond the Sanctuary?

Bringing that to light should reveal a way to unravel θ's stubborn heart. And if that were possible, the one whispering ideas into Garfiel's ear would be gone, and there should be a chance to talk things through with Garfiel before he went out of control.

Garfiel put his crude, violent side front and center, but at his core he wasn't so simple-minded as to affirm violence wholesale — at least, that was Subaru's impression of him. If he really were that simple, believing it right to solve every problem by force with his own two hands, he could simply have wiped out, root and branch, anything that threatened the Sanctuary.

Short of Emilia with Puck at her side, or Roswaal at full strength, there was probably no one who could be counted as a real match for a beastified Garfiel. Right now, within the Sanctuary, the one who held the most power was, without question, Garfiel — and if he bared claws and fangs and attacked, that alone would violently settle every problem at once.

That he didn't was simply because, appearances aside, he was the sort who sought a reasoned solution.

"So we get to take advantage of that softness of his and scheme away behind the scenes... Put that way, it kind of makes us sound like the villains."

"Personally, I don't dislike this method of using whatever's lying around. Though yeah, I'll agree it's not exactly the hero of a fairy tale or a light entertainment novel."

Catching Subaru's mutter, Sidekick Villain B — cooperating in this less-than-heroic, small-time scheme — gave a shrug of his shoulders. As Villain A, all Subaru could do was figure that if B was on board, that was good enough.

In any case, the various troubles surrounding Garfiel amounted to the finishing touch on all the factors tangled around the Sanctuary. If Subaru could talk him around, Major Problem B would be as good as solved.

And then there was Major Problem A — the Trial. Emilia's side of things.

"Though I'm going to go see her face again after this."

Evening had already passed, and lights for the night were beginning to come on here and there through the Sanctuary. The sunset had sunk halfway below the western sky, and the world was shifting into a blend of orange and purple.

Night would soon fall — the hour when, if nothing had gone wrong, they would have been facing the Trial.

But Emilia's waking still hadn't been reported. Even if she woke in time for the Trial, given how long it would take for her heart to settle, tonight's attempt was surely off the table.

Or, in the worst case, even tomorrow's attempt — the one Subaru was really banking on — might be in jeopardy. After all, even Puck, who had triggered the whole matter with Emilia's heart, hadn't been able to say for certain how long it would take her to absorb that first shock.

"I know it all comes down to how I follow up with Emilia once she wakes up... but as long as I don't know how much damage she's taken, I can't shake the feeling of just leaving things hanging."

"It's not exactly persuading Emilia-sama, but do you think you can manage something close to it? I only heard about her state secondhand, so I can't really say either way."

"Well, for one, she was confused enough she couldn't even tell who was next to her — that much's for sure. I've never seen her that shaken, like a little kid throwing a fit. As for whether I can pull it off... honestly, I just have to."

"So everything's still a tightrope walk, huh..."

Otto let out a breath at Subaru's uncertain answer, but he didn't seem to have any intention of running from it. The awareness that he was dragging Otto into a fight with such thin odds only deepened Subaru's guilt toward him.

Even so, the way Otto stood his ground and refused to abandon him was, honestly, a genuine comfort to Subaru.

"If that's how things stand with Emilia-sama, what about Garfiel? Did you manage to get any leverage to soften his attitude at all?"

"On that front, I think it's fair to say I've got a foothold to start negotiating from. The problem is getting everything ready to actually bring him to the table still looks like it'll take some doing."

How the talk with θ played out would change how they approached Garfiel. Whatever θ did in tomorrow's meeting — that was the only place they'd find a thread leading to winning Garfiel over. The hard part was that, for either negotiation, all they could do was stack up what information they had so far and gamble on the moment.

Though if you wanted to put it plainly, it was just tightrope walking, over and over, every single time.

"—Here we are. This time, you're coming in on the talk with me."

Standing before the house they'd reached — the building where Roswaal waited — Subaru said this to Otto beside him. Otto screwed up his face in distaste at the words.

"Yeah, well, I get it. This time, unlike before, there's no reason for me to stay outside. So I just go along quietly and... ahh, and declare I've thrown in with the enemy of a margrave, huh. I wonder if my neck's still going to be attached to my shoulders even after all this gets sorted out."

"Relax. If your neck's on the line, mine is too, or I'll end up as rabbit feed. I'm not about to let you die alone!"

"That was somehow zero percent reassuring!"

Otto let out a scream at Subaru, who gave a firm thumbs-up as he declared it.

Just as they were trading banter like that at the building's entrance, the door was suddenly pushed open from the inside — and out stepped a maid with a cold gaze.

"What are you two making a racket about, right outside the door, at an hour when night's already drawing in? It reflects on the good character of Roswaal-sama, your master. Get a hold of yourselves."

"Yeah, sorry about that... but wait, you're here? Is Emilia alone?"

After apologizing for Ram's sullen, sharp tongue, Subaru pointed out how strange it was for her to be here. Ram let out a small sigh and gave an exaggerated, exasperated shrug.

"You don't need to worry so much, Ram will be heading back soon. Ram was called here to help prepare, since Barusu and the others were coming to see Roswaal-sama. —Putting a burden on Roswaal-sama like this is a real nuisance."

"So it all comes back around to being our fault, huh... But wait, what kind of preparation does Roswaal need? He's still stuck unable to get out of bed, so there shouldn't be anything to prepare."

In truth, Subaru knew perfectly well Roswaal had enough energy to get out of bed and kick Garfiel's skull in, but here he played dumb, feigning ignorance.

Ram closed one eye at him and, with an irritated "Just come on," slipped past between Subaru and Otto.

"Roswaal-sama is waiting, so hurry up and go in. Be careful not to drag the talk out and become a bother to him. ...Once that's done, you'll be taking over watch by Emilia-sama's side from Ram."

"You're in an awful hurry. Is Roswaal, pretty much back to his usual self, really that much of a worry? I'd rather you put Emilia first right now. If she wakes up and she's alone, she'd—"

"It wouldn't do for Ram to be at Emilia-sama's side when she wakes up. Don't you even understand that much?"

This time, with a look that genuinely regarded him as a fool, Ram left those words behind and walked off, leaving a taken-aback Subaru standing there. As Subaru silently watched her go, Otto beside him elbowed him in the side.

"If that wasn't you trying to make her mad on purpose, that was a pretty rough exchange, wasn't it?"

"...Yeah, I know. Even as I was saying it, I thought, was that really okay."

"Well, if you understand that much, I suppose I can just leave it at 'that was pretty mean-spirited' and be done with it."

Brushing off Otto's elbow without a word, Subaru went in through the open door and into the building. Otto followed along behind him. Bringing him along, Subaru headed for the bedroom deep at the back and gave a light knock on its door.

"Roswaal, it's me. Mind if I come in?"

"Oh-o-oh, there you are. Do-o come in, do-o come in."

A clownish voice came back through the door — one that sounded, to Subaru's mild surprise, like the manner he used to affect before. Subaru opened the door.

And then—

"Ah. So that's how it is."

Catching sight of Roswaal on the bed, Subaru gave a small nod of understanding. He'd finally grasped the reason behind the "preparations" Ram had mentioned.

Beside Subaru as he nodded in understanding, Otto poked his head in for a look and let out an "eep." Come to think of it, this was probably his first time seeing it.

"My-y, coming all this way just to report on your pro-o-gress, how admirable of you. I don't dislike that about you, no, not at a-a-all."

Laughing in his drawling, clownish way, Roswaal spoke — his clown makeup on for the first time in quite a while.

His arms spread wide, as if to welcome the two of them in.
