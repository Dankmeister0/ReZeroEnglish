Arc 3 Chapter 36: "Roar of Blood"

—The running Earth Dragon's head was blown clean off at its base, and as the Dragon Carriage it pulled followed the collapse of the now-will-less mass, it swerved wildly off the road and flipped over.

The carriage, thrown onto its side, gouged the ground violently, throwing up clouds of dust and roaring as it went. Timber snapped and splintered, and when the fallen Earth Dragon's body was dragged into the wheels, the sickening sound of flesh being torn apart scattered a mist of blood, and the scene turned to carnage in an instant.

The location was deep in the mountains, past the Lifaus Highway and onto a forest trail, right at the entrance to Mathers territory. Crossing a few stretches of woodland and hills, another two hours of travel would have brought them to their destination.

But the Dragon Carriage had been mercilessly destroyed along the way, and now only the hollow clatter of a freely spinning wheel echoed through the wreckage.

The Earth Dragon, reduced to a heap of meat, lay sprawled; the carriage itself had become nothing but debris; and the dust cloud carried the mingled reek of earth and blood, an unnatural stench hanging over it all.

Into that scene—

"...Ugh, ahh..."

A boy, thrown clear and covered in scrapes here and there, let out a groan.

He had landed some distance from the wrecked carriage, in a patch of grassland just off the trail. The lush greenery there was tangled with vines, which had apparently cushioned his fall somewhat. Despite having been at the very center of such devastation, the boy's injuries were, almost miraculously, minor.

Still, that didn't mean the wounds from being flung about so carelessly didn't hurt.

Scrapes and a few bruises. Mercifully, there were no broken bones or heavy bleeding, but the pain was more than enough to curl a will-less child into a ball.

"Ahfu, ugh... ah!"

The black-haired boy rolled out of the grass, groaning in pain, tears streaming down his face. His face was smeared with blood from a gash on his forehead scraped by the dirt, made worse by tears and drool. Where he'd wiped at it roughly, the reddish-brown stain had spread across his whole face, and red had soaked into the sleeves of his mud-caked clothes.

As an unsightly display for a grown man—limbs sprawled out—it was hard to look at, and combined with the wreckage of the carriage, the whole scene inspired nothing but a sense of tragedy in anyone who saw it.

Into that scene—

"——"

Black-robed figures rose up one after another, and a dozen or so surrounded the boy and the carriage. The shadowy figures inspected the headless Earth Dragon lying prone, and once they confirmed the mass of flesh was indeed dead, they turned quietly to look at Subaru, lying there crying.

The black-robed figures wore their hoods pulled all the way down, their faces—even their sexes—impossible to make out. These shadows, whose very breathing was uncertain, closed in around Subaru with a swaying, gliding motion, looking down at him as he reacted to nothing but pain.

"...Ra."

One of the figures muttered a single word under its breath. When one let it slip, another spoke the same word next. As this went on, the chain of the word spread out one after another, and all those encircling him let the whispers cycle through, murmuring in turn.

In a world utterly devoid of insect song or any sign of living things, there was only the sound of wind stirring branches and leaves, and the whispered chanting of the black shadows—a warped scene, as though the world were complete unto itself with nothing else.

And then—

"—Agh. Aah! Aaah, ahbaaah!"

Gradually, as the whispers layered atop one another, a change came over Subaru, who had been groaning in pain. He writhed, arching his back where he lay face-up, letting out cries of agony—still, on the surface, no different from bearing pain. But the quality of it was clearly different from before.

It was a suffering on an entirely different plane from the pain of scrapes and bruises—as though he were holding back something trying to burst out from within, as though a seed of agony rampaging through his body were trying to sprout and torment him.

Anyone watching would have realized it was responding to the whispers of the black shadows surrounding him. Looking down at the suffering Subaru, the shadows still showed no sign of stopping their incantation. Only, as though they had reached some conclusion from the way the gasping Subaru looked, they kept up their whispering as they reached their hands toward his body—

"—Bo."

A hurtling iron ball, screaming through the air, blew apart the head of the man reaching for Subaru.

Blood and skull fragments scattered around, raining down mercilessly even onto the suffering Subaru. The shadow's collapsing body was accompanied by the light ring of iron chain, and the writhing, serpentine thing rampaged around the corpse-turned-shadow—straight at the other black robes.

"——"

But the black robes' judgment was quick. They instantly cut their awareness free from their comrade's sudden death, and scattered soundlessly to avoid the chain's follow-up strike. As they did, what the black robes drew from within their robes were blades shaped like crosses. Dully gleaming, of a tasteless design with a blade fitted to the tip, the black robes held one in each hand, warily watching their surroundings.

There were eleven of them. All took up positions where they could cover each other's backs, forming a formation that eliminated blind spots and was ready to answer an ambush—commendable, to be sure.

But that held true only against an opponent one could face on the same plane.

"—Shh!"

Overhead, an apron dress flared as it kicked off the trees.

Leaping at tremendous speed, the girl's body, launched diagonally downward, arrived a hair faster than the shadows' awareness could catch up to the sound.

The pommel of the swung-down weapon punched straight down through the top of a black robe's head. With a sharp crack, a hole was bored through the crown of its skull, and the shadow, blood and brain matter spilling from the wound, toppled over, swaying.

Kicking that body to block the sightline of a nearby black robe, the girl leapt back. But the shadow whose comrade's corpse had just been kicked showed no hesitation either. It charged, skewering the oncoming corpse of its ally on its cross from the front, then thrust to impale the retreating girl, corpse and all.

That shadow's body—corpse and all—was blown apart by a whirlwind of spiked iron.

The girl had thrown the iron ball straight. The instant the chain reached its full extension, her body tilted forward as though overcome by the weight of the ball—and the cross that had been thrown passed right through the spot where her forward-tilting head had just been. A hard clack rang out as the blade buried itself in a tree trunk, and the girl, gaining initial momentum from her tilted posture as it was, leapt horizontal to the ground and detonated the earth beneath her.

"Ruaaaaah!"

Screaming, baring her fangs, the girl howled.

On her head, where her blood-soaked blue hair swayed, a pure white, sharp horn jutted out, gleaming.

A black robe caught mid-leap was swept up in the girl's blazing high-speed movement. The arm that swung around its waist shattered its hip into fragments, and with the momentum unbroken, she slammed the poor creature—now certain to be paralyzed from the waist down—into a tree behind it, wringing the life from it as most of its innards spilled from its mouth and rear.

Flinging the dead black robe's corpse aside with a single swing of her arm, the ogre drew the iron ball back to hand and turned around. Her adorable face stained with blood, eyes gleaming fiercely, she glared down the black robes. And when the boy lying at the feet of the still-silent black shadows came into her view—

"I won't let anything happen to Subaru-kun."

She declared it in a voice brimming with fighting spirit, and the girl—Rem—dragged her blood-dripping left leg as she circled the iron ball overhead, making it howl.

The wound was one she'd taken failing to fully dodge the splintering timber when the carriage overturned. Had Rem been alone, she might have escaped unscathed—but that had not been possible.

The instant the Earth Dragon's head was blown off, Rem had instantly understood the carriage was about to violently overturn. Taking the situation seriously, her only thought in that instant had been how to protect Subaru—sleeping on her lap—from the wreck without harm.

Spinning her head at dizzying speed, sweeping her gaze around, she'd spotted a spot at the edge of her vision where grass and vines overlapped, and thrown Subaru there. That alone had cost her the fraction of a second she'd needed to escape herself, and Rem had ended up sharing the carriage's fate as it turned to wreckage.

The result: a gash on her forehead, and a shard of timber driven deep into her left thigh. Her dislocated left shoulder she'd forced back into place, and moving it now sent white-hot pain numbing across her pale cheek.

But Rem stepped forward with a gait that betrayed none of the effects of those wounds.

"Witch Cultists...!"

She glared at the cluster of black robes and spat the words out in a voice saturated with hatred.

In response to Rem's challenge, the black robes still showed no reaction. They maintained their same unreadable bearing, whether conscious or not, standing motionless with their crosses trained on the fang-baring girl.

The balance broke in an instant—.

Rem, who had been circling the iron ball overhead, struck first, judging that nothing would be resolved otherwise. The ball, its centrifugal force compounded by her own arm strength, was a steel apocalypse that would smash utterly anything in its path. A single blow would carry off not just bone but life itself, wherever on the body it struck, sweeping everything away all at once.

The blow, chain extended to its full length, snapped trees and bore down on the black robes amid a hail of splinters and clods of earth. But the black robes evaded it—leaping, or dropping low and sprinting—and, having drawn out that one strike, surged as one toward Rem's now-undefended body.

Rem, having overextended and left a wide opening, twisted her body, yanking hard to pull the iron ball back to hand. But the cross-blades would reach her before the ball returned—.

"—Ruah!!"

The jaw of the lead black robe was gouged clean off by Rem's toe, snapping up from below.

To call it a kick would be too gentle a word—she had, quite literally, scraped the lower jaw off from directly beneath with the tip of her foot.

The jawless shadow, its pink tongue dangling loose, still lunged at Rem before it with a motion that paid no mind even to its own fatal wound, swinging its cross down toward her chest.

—Before that motion could be completed, the iron ball Rem had yanked back blew away the shadow's remaining upper jaw, skull and all.

The iron ball flew free amid the scattering brain matter. Rem caught it, bearing down on her own face, in an upraised palm—the spikes punched through her left hand, bright red blood staining her pale cheek. But gripping it hard regardless, Rem spun at once and smashed the ball into the face of a black robe swinging its cross at her from the side, crushing it flat.

That made six in total. Cutting the assassins down from twelve to half their number, Rem, breathing hard, turned bloodthirsty eyes on the remaining foes.

A spear of stone with a razor-sharp tip came hurtling toward her face. She tilted her head, dodging by a hair, but her trailing hair and the side of her head were grazed and shredded by the rock's surface, pain and impact blurring her vision.

Her sight flooded red, the blow to her head robbing Rem of her judgment. The ground suddenly felt soft underfoot. Instinct reacted to the chill of sinking, and with a slight bend of her legs she gained the recoil to leap diagonally off her right leg alone. She landed on the branch of a large tree ahead, thinking to set up another ambush as before, but—

"Oh n—!"

She cursed her own recklessness for leaping so carelessly in front of an enemy with ranged attacks.

The branch she'd meant to land on was scorched by searing flame, and beyond it a fireball emerged, having burned clean through the great tree's trunk—the sensation of her skin being seared by intense heat spread across her front, and Rem instinctively raised her blood-soaked left hand before her.

"Hyuma!!"

A thin membrane of water formed in front of her, and the instant it collided with the fireball, white steam burst upward, the death-cry of water being boiled ringing through the forest.

But though the fireball's force was somewhat weakened, it was not crude enough magic to be dispelled entirely by a hastily cast incantation.

"——!!"

Her clenched left hand now had a hole punched through the palm, and there was no telling whether it would be usable in the fighting to come. So she drew back her fist and, without hesitation, drove it straight into the still-advancing fireball, detonating both the flame and her own fist together.

"—Uwaah!"

Caught in the mid-air explosion, Rem's body spun and was flung into a tree trunk. Tumbling down into a pile of roots that writhed beneath her, she raised her face, letting out a groan at the throbbing pain in her left arm.

Her left arm was charred black from the wrist down, her little finger and ring finger carbonized. The skin up to her elbow was hideously red and raw, and past the shoulder there was no sensation of pain at all anymore.

Unless she found a mage as skilled as Ferris, that arm would likely never move again.

Forcing herself up despite such a grievous wound, Rem pulled her awareness back to reality and set the pain aside. She swallowed her groans and roared back instead, stoking her own fighting spirit while showing the enemy that her will to fight had not waned in the slightest—if only to draw their attention even a little further away from Subaru.

But—

"——"

A soundlessly approaching black robe, bare-handed, drove its palm into Rem's torso, slamming her once more into the great tree behind her with tremendous force.

Her internal organs were wrung and crushed, her breastbone groaning under the impact, and Rem's mouth spewed a great gush of blood.

Her throat scorched by the searing heat of the blood she'd coughed up, her body sagged under the agony wringing through her whole frame. The withdrawn palm thrust out again, aiming to crush her skull, and she barely managed to dodge. The trunk behind her was blown clean through by the palm strike, the massive tree spinning through the air with unbelievable ease.

A black robe capable of cracking the ground with a single step forward was clearly of a different order than her previous opponents. Dodging the follow-up with a sideways leap, Rem, rolling on the ground, spat out the blood remaining in her mouth and let her eyes dart around searching for the iron ball she'd dropped.

"——!!"

A spear of stone grazed the side of her face, and her staggering body was struck from behind by a hammer of stone. Her spine groaned violently, her small frame bouncing off the ground and sent flying.

Where she was knocked to, the bare-handed figure that had overwhelmed her before waited. It now held the iron ball Rem had let go of, and swung the iron mass up, aiming for her as she bounced toward it—

"—El Hyuma!"

The blood she spat out in her incantation froze solid, and the blade of pure blood sliced halfway through the arm of the man holding the iron ball. His prize dropped from his hand at the pain. And then—

"Ga-rururu!"

Slapping the ground to control her fall, the bouncing Rem reached out her right hand and caught the grip of the iron ball the black robe had dropped. At the same time, she kicked the ball that had fallen to the ground, sending it flying behind the shadow, wound the chain around its thick neck, and hauled it tight with a vice-grip force.

A wet sound rang out as the chain wrung the flesh, and the black robe's neck, shin-bone and all, was wrenched off. The face, turned a hundred and eighty degrees backward, looked at Rem, and its eyes lost their light.

As that body sank down, Rem, having felled a formidable foe, let herself relax just slightly—and at that instant—

"——!!!"

The black robe's body, which should have lost all power, moved, and a kick of devastating force swept Rem's torso aside.

The kick that struck her left flank obliterated the ribs on her left side entirely, snapped her left thigh as well, and slammed her into the ground. And with that one final blow, the black robe truly expired at last. Its body, fallen face-down, stared up at the sky—the finished product of a gruesome corpse.

"Uu, ahh..."

Groaning, coughing up blood, Rem forced herself upright, berating her left side—including her arm—which no longer obeyed her.

She had likely dispatched what was, among the enemy's ranks, the most formidable fighter. But the price paid for it had not been cheap.

Five remained. Since none had joined the bare-handed one, there likely wasn't another skilled at close combat among them. If so, she should be able to finish it by closing in and wringing their necks one by one.

Could she manage it, in her current trembling state, covered in wounds, with only the right half of her body functioning?

Shaking her head, swallowing down her weakness, Rem scolded herself for wavering.

It wasn't a question of whether she could. She had to. So what if her left side was dead? Her right side still moved. If her right arm gave out, she'd stomp them to death with her legs; if her right leg gave out too, she'd bite them to death.

If she killed every last one of them and Subaru survived, then Rem would have won.

Already, a battle on this scale, and on top of that, inside Mathers territory. Even unconsciously, there was no way her sister wouldn't sense emotions this thoroughly soaked in bloodlust. Before long, her sister would surely track this place down.

Whether she herself lived or died by then didn't matter. As long as Subaru's life was protected, what hesitation could there be about using herself up here?

A faint lingering attachment warped Rem's heart, just slightly. To kill off that last hesitation, she glanced toward where Subaru should have been lying.

If she could just catch one glimpse of him, then whatever happened afterward wouldn't matter.

And yet—

"—Subaru-kun!?"

He wasn't there.

Subaru, who should have been gasping in pain, agony, and terror, was nowhere to be seen.

Panicking, Rem swept her gaze around. Could he have been caught up in the aftermath of the fighting and been thrown somewhere? But search as she might, she couldn't find him anywhere.

And then, with a start, Rem realized.

"One... is missing..."

Five of the black-robed group remained. But now, no matter where she looked, there were only four.

The black robes, cross-blades hanging from both hands, planted themselves to block the game trail ahead, and began sliding smoothly, as if to hide their comrade retreating from Rem's field of view.

—Distancing her from the comrade who had fled, carrying Subaru.

"You... people..."

Trembling words spilled from her trembling lips.

Her lips, drained of color from the heavy blood loss, were painted with the rouge of blood welling up from inside her mouth. Amid that harrowing war-paint, Rem's lovely features twisted into a truly demonic visage as she said:

"Not satisfied with robbing Rem of her reason to live..."

She bent her right arm, gripping the iron ball, crouched on one leg, coiling up explosive force. As the black robes ahead tensed, the instant they leapt toward her—

"—now you'd rob me even of the reason to go die right here, right now?!"

Rem's roar exploded outward, and with a kick that seemed to blast the ground apart, her body launched into flight.

Deployed directly ahead of her, in front of the charging Rem, as a decoy behind the cross-wielding black robes, were fist-sized fireballs that had been chanted in secret—except now there were so many of them they blotted out her entire field of vision.

"————!!!"

A scream exploded outward, and immediately after, crimson brilliance chained through the forest in the light of dawn.

Searing heat raged, burning the trees away, the world crying out in its death throes as the land turned to scorched earth in that furnace—

—and onto the burnt-out field fluttered down the charred remains of a white apron dress, which, an instant later, burned away to nothing.

—Sway, throb, sway sway, throb throb.

Slung over a black robe's shoulder, jostled without resistance, Subaru drooled.

The wounds from falling out of the carriage—he barely felt their pain anymore. Not that he felt nothing at all, but his consciousness was dominated by a pain so vast that those wounds no longer mattered, and even the will to move had been stripped from him, leaving him sunk in stupor.

At the scene of the carriage crash, the black robes had surrounded Subaru and begun whispering their incantation.

As he listened to that whispering, Subaru's body had nearly been devoured from within by some unknowable sensation swelling up inside him, and his head had been overrun by a chaos far beyond what the word "ringing" could describe.

Something black had rampaged in his chest, in his stomach, inside his skull, at the very center of his heart, as though trying to churn his very existence into pulp.

The thought of what might have happened if that had gone on just a little longer made him shudder.

That pain was the kind that shatters a person's heart. The kind that twists a person's heart out of shape. The kind that changes a person. The kind that turns a person into something no longer a person.

If he had stayed like that—Natsuki Subaru would have become—

"Fhihe, hihihi, hehehehe..."

All at once, as though remembering, mad laughter spilled from the corner of his lips along with drool.

As his awareness, which had been dominated by pain, began to return to himself, his mind naturally exposed the fact that it remained shattered along an axis quite separate from the pain.

His body ached all over. His head especially throbbed as though splitting open.

Blue wasn't there. Blue wasn't beside him. He was being carried by black. What was black? Where had blue gone? Where was he being taken now, like this?

Pushing through the forest, the black robe ran as if flying along the game trail.

Despite a slender build that showed no particular sign of rigorous training, the figure ran like the wind, carrying a limp person—a sight utterly divorced from reality.

Exactly as if it were a shadow gliding wherever the sunlight moved it, the black robe carrying Subaru tore through the mountains, running the unmarked game trails as though they belonged to it alone.

And then, perhaps after a dozen or so minutes of running—

In the deep green darkness where sunlight never reached, the black-robed figure suddenly came to a stop. Ahead of it towered a sheer cliff face, its moss-covered rock conspicuous, so high one had to crane one's neck to see the top—hardly something one could scale without tools.

Before that cliff face, the black robe swept its gaze around and turned toward a particularly conspicuous mass of rock. Then, pressing a palm to that rock mass—

"——"

Hearing the shadow murmur a word under its breath, Subaru's body stiffened in tension.

It was a reaction born of fear that the incantation that had rung through his mind back there was about to be repeated. But the single word muttered this time seemed to be different from the earlier incantation, and its effect did not wrack Subaru's skull the way the other had.

"——"

Before the black robe that had murmured the incantation, that massive mass of rock suddenly vanished.

As though it were some dream or illusion, dissolving into the air, the black robe accepted its disappearance as a matter of course, resettled Subaru on its shoulder, and stepped into the cave that had opened where the rock had been.

In the chill, cold air of the cave, only the regular sound of the shadow's footsteps, treading slowly over the ground, echoed. Some dozen meters into the cave, the light that had been reaching them from behind was suddenly cut off. Likely, the cave's entrance had been sealed once more by the mass of rock.

In the darkness of the cave, where the cold air pricked at the skin, the black robe showed no sign of hurry, nor any faltering in its stride.

Then, the dim gloom, before the shadow advancing without hesitation, was abruptly swept aside by a faint, hazy white glow. The glow of Lagmite Ore.

Set into the walls of what appeared to be a passage, at even intervals, were pieces of ore, and one after another, lights switched on as if guiding the walking shadow's path.

Deeper and deeper into the cave, into darkness upon darkness, they were made to advance.

Whether the gooseflesh rising on his blood-streaked skin was from the cold, or from some other cause, was hard to say.

Still slung over the shoulder, Subaru trembled faintly, hot tears spilling from the corners of his eyes, and kept on laughing, hehehehehe.

And then, the end of that dark, cold rock passage came into view.

The glow of the Lagmite Ore grew slightly stronger there, its lighting properly arranged—a natural chamber, far larger in area than anywhere else in the cave.

And it was there that Subaru would come face to face with a mass of pure malice.

—He was a gaunt man.

Surrounded by black-robed men, the man himself was likewise clad in black priestly vestments. He stood slightly taller than Subaru, his dark green bangs trimmed to just brush his eyes. His cheeks were hollow, and he seemed to possess barely enough flesh and skin stretched over bone to maintain the shape of a human being—a body so devoid of vitality that the description seemed apt.

That is, if not for those eyes, glinting with a maniacal, glaring light.

The man leaned his body over, staring fixedly at Subaru, bound and seated against the wall. With his bent waist twisted further, his head tilted a full ninety degrees, his bulging eyes gazed at Subaru with utter shamelessness, exposing a bizarreness beyond all norms—and indeed, the man's words and conduct set him apart entirely from ordinary people.

"Naa-a-a-aow, I see... my my, this iiis, indeed, quite interesting, De-e-esu."

Having looked Subaru up and down thoroughly, as if licking him with his eyes, the man nodded as though satisfied, showing agreement to the men around him.

The black-robed figures dipped their chins at the man's affirmation and seemed to wait in silence for the rest of his words.

The man showed no reaction to the shadow's silent posture, and instead, as if lost in thought alone, gripped his left hand with his right—twisting his thumb into a wound on his wrist, and, paying no mind to the blood dripping from it, dug into his own flesh and blood.

"Yo-o-ou... surely, you couldn't be 'Pride,' could you, De-e-esu-yo?"

Still bent over, he turned and looked back up at Subaru in that strange posture, and as he spoke the question, he slid the blood-stained thumb from the gouged wound into his own mouth, tasting the iron flavor on his tongue with a rapturous shudder, his stagnant, glowing eyes trembling.

Subaru, thus addressed, gave no answer.

He merely gazed up vacantly at the face of the man standing before him, wearing a grin utterly incongruous with the situation.

Watching that, the man drew his finger from his lips with an audible sound, and said:

"Aa-a-ah, I see-e. My my, how rude of me. Here I am, and I haven't even given my greetings, have I, De-e-esu-ka?"

As if answering Subaru's grin, the man let his pale, colorless lips split quietly to the side, and, laughing malevolently, slowly and courteously bent at the waist.

"I am of the Witch Cult, an Archbishop of Sin—"

Still bent at the waist, he skillfully craned his neck to gaze straight at Subaru, and said:

"—in charge of 'Sloth,' Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti... De-e-esu!"

Announcing himself, pointing at Subaru with both hands' fingers, the man—Petelgeuse—cackled with laughter.

Kehkehkeh, kehkehkeh, kehkehkeh.
