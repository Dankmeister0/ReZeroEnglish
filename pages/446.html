Arc 6 Chapter 41: "The Scent of Peace"

・

—The first thing Subaru felt, upon regaining consciousness, was suffocation and pressure.

"Kohf—"

He coughed, spat out the unpleasant sensation pooled in his mouth—saliva thick with grit—and forced his heavy eyelids open. He'd lost consciousness. Not dozing off—something more forceful, an irresistible wave that had swallowed his awareness whole.

Fighting the heavy sensation, as if sand pressed down even on his eyelashes, Subaru cast his mind back to what had happened to him, to the moments just before. He was fairly sure there'd been a horrible bout of derangement, and then he'd tried to leave the tower, and he'd made it out without anyone stopping him—that much was certain, but after that—

"The earthworm, and… white light…"

He recalled the terrifying events that followed, a string of vivid, high-impact images, and found himself doubting whether he was really still alive. Caught up in a catastrophe like that—did he really have the heavenly luck to survive it? Though, even supposing he had died there, whether he could really die at all was itself questionable—such was the nature of the unwanted outsider currently going by the name Natsuki Subaru.

"After the needle, I fell into an antlion pit…"

He remembered the ground collapsing, being swallowed underground along with the giant earthworm's corpse. He'd stretched his neck, straining for air, but in the end the sand had swallowed him too, and his consciousness had faded. He'd fully expected to suffocate to death after that. But he hadn't.

"Is this… underneath the desert?"

He looked around, but the space seemed to have no light source at all—a darkness so total he could barely make out his own hands. Prying his heavy eyelids open, he pulled a sand-caked palm up in front of his face. He could just barely make out that it was there, but the world was so unreliable he couldn't even see his own lifeline.

—Whether he was truly still among the living—even that felt uncertain.

"…Not exactly a comforting thought, you idiot."

Thinking that far, Subaru slowly moved his arms and legs, freeing his half-buried body from its heavy confinement. The pressure crushing his whole body turned out to be a bed of sand that had swallowed him from the waist down—small mercy that it hadn't been his upper half buried instead. It was something like the trick of collapsing a sandcastle without knocking over the little flag stuck on top. To avoid clumsily burying himself further, Subaru carefully cleared the sand away and pulled himself free.

"—————"

Once he'd finally clawed his way out of the sand, what hit him was an exhaustion heavier than he'd expected. Probably from the cold sand leeching away his body heat while relentlessly pressing down on him. Not even knowing how long he'd been unconscious was, in itself, terrifying.

"Lucky no other earthworm came along and took a bite out of me while I was out…"

Talking to himself, Subaru noticed his throat was parched and reached for his waist. When he'd fled the tower, he should have had a waterskin secured there for crossing the desert. But his searching fingers found nothing; no matter how many times he groped around, the waterskin wasn't there.

"Guess that makes sense, buried up to the waist like that…"

It seemed the waterskin had come loose when he'd been swallowed by the sand. The pouch of emergency rations was gone from his side too. That should have meant something fatal—but strangely, his heart stayed calm. Maybe his heart had already gone numb to the cruelty of the situation, to the point that reacting to every little thing felt pointless.

"If it's at least nearby…huh?"

Kneeling, he ran his hands over the sand mound he'd crawled out of. Given how much sand there was, it was a halfhearted gesture, one he didn't really expect to pay off—but right around where his upper body had been buried, his fingers found something soft. He checked it with his fingers, startled to find it was the waterskin he'd been looking for. And then—

"Empty inside—guess that's surprise number two."

From the weight and feel of the skin, he could tell right away it was empty. Just in case, he pressed the mouth of the skin to his lips, but only the faintest drop moistened the tip of his tongue. Even that pathetic trace of water was precious now. Savoring the meager taste on his tongue, Subaru fastened the waterskin back at his waist and resumed feeling around the area.

"—?"

If the waterskin had turned up, maybe the food would too. That hope was granted—and betrayed—the same way the water had been.

—Scattered around the sand mound, as if devoured and left in scraps, was his food.

"—————"

It was too dark to make out the scene around the mound clearly. But he could tell the food he'd brought was scattered here and there across it. And the remains resting in his palm hadn't kept their original shape—plainly put, something had eaten it and left it strewn about.

Subaru's parched throat clicked as he swallowed. Around him lay scattered, half-devoured food. This fact clawed at Subaru's supposedly calmed mind, gouging it with a horror like fingernails raking flesh. The terror he'd felt toward the earthworm—the fear of being preyed upon—welled back up, and cold sweat, spending precious moisture, ran down his forehead and back.

This place, to begin with, was where that giant earthworm had crawled out from—a monster's thoroughfare, so to speak. Its master worm was surely dead now, but there was nothing strange about other monsters living in symbiosis with it also making their home along this same passage.

"I need to get out of here, now—"

Move away, Subaru thought. But having thought it, he still couldn't see a thing around him. With no wall to guide him, he could only drop to all fours and crawl, relying on the ground itself—confirming the ground was there, confirming a wall was there, confirming a path was there, confirming his own existence was there, crawling and scraping his way to escape.

"Ow… damn it, it hurts…"

His right ear, still grazed raw from the white needle; the nails on his right hand, still partway through regenerating; every pain-signaling organ in his body crying out—bearing all of it, Subaru crawled, dragging himself away from that place. Away from something abnormal, something not of this world. Even without knowing what, if anything, waited at the far end of all this desperate flight.

For now, all he could do was keep fleeing, to put distance between himself and a fear he couldn't even name.

"—————"

Sniffing, Subaru let his hands and knees carry him forward as if drawn by something. It grew gradually stronger, becoming a distinct sensation, guiding him deeper.

—This was roughly an hour into his crawling flight.

Fleeing aimlessly, Subaru had slid down sand slopes more times than he could count, been blocked by walls along paths that weren't really paths, and repeatedly flinched and rolled away from occasional showers of sand falling from above.

Somewhere along the way, Subaru realized he was inside a hollow space beneath the desert. Realizing it hadn't been any kind of salvation, but it was right after that realization that the first true sign appeared.

—It was a strange, oddly captivating scent that suddenly slipped into his nostrils.

He could never call that strange scent fragrant, exactly. Under normal circumstances, smell was a pretty low-priority, unreliable piece of information for judging a situation. But with his sight sealed off by darkness, in a place with nothing, where even sound had become ambiguous, that sudden scent felt intensely vivid.

And so Subaru, like a small child feeling along a wall, began crawling toward it, drawn in. Sniffing, moving on all fours like a dog, he let the scent guide him deeper into the hollow—no, whether it was deeper or shallower, whether he was even heading toward or away from anything, he had no way to know.

"Haah, haah, haah…"

Before he knew it, Subaru had his tongue out, breathing hard, exactly like a real dog.

He'd heard dogs stick their tongues out to regulate body temperature, but that wasn't what was happening with Subaru now. He stuck his tongue out and pressed forward through the dark with a dog's grim expression because he wanted to catch even the faintest breeze on his tongue, to wring out whatever scrap of information he could.

"—————"

In the dark, after only a few hours, Subaru felt the illusion that he'd become a creature adapted to the darkness. Naturally, that borrowed sense of omnipotence was nothing like the truth. As ever, the darkness gave Subaru nothing; in that lonely space, only his own breathing and the faint scrape of sand echoed—and yet, that was somehow comforting to him now.

—Scared. Terrified. I need to do something.

He'd only felt that ordinary, human fear at the very beginning. Now, Subaru found this silence and darkness, this space of solitude with no one else in it, precious.

Here, at least, no one could hurt him. Here, all he had to do was keep struggling to escape. Here, he didn't have to think about anything—there was simply something he needed to do.

A stagnation like sinking into lukewarm mud embraced Subaru's heart, pinned as it was in such a desperate situation, and he felt it saturating him like soft, yielding clay. Wouldn't it be fine, really, if he just melted into it and disappeared, just like that?

—So he thought, steeped in the escapism of inertia and languor.

"Damn it, what is this…"

Subaru had been making steady progress crawling through the unseen hollow. Partway through, he'd come to a fork in the path, agonized endlessly over which way to go, finally chosen the right—and from there, an even longer, longer journey chasing the scent had followed. Would there be somewhere to feel safe at the end of it? Somewhere bright he could finally reach? With no such prospects in sight at all, Subaru had pushed on regardless. And yet—

"A dead end, now, of all times…?"

Enduring a path that grew narrower and narrower, a slope that grew steeper and steeper, what awaited Subaru at the end was a passage so tight he could only literally crawl through it, and at its very end, a wall of sand—in other words, the terminus of the scent.

"What was even the point of all this—no, wait."

Angered by the dead end, Subaru was about to punch the sand wall when he stopped himself. Instead, he wrestled with the contradiction between the scent he'd followed and his current situation.

Subaru had unquestionably come to this place relying on the drifting scent as his guide. That scent had ridden the wind and reached him even from far, far away. Which meant, if there was room for wind to flow, there was no way this could be a true dead end here in the depths of the sand. The wind had to be flowing in from outside, into the hollow.

"Then—!"

Patting his hands over the surrounding surface, Subaru groped his way backward along the dead-end path. Left, right, straight ahead, down—there was no way he'd have missed any of those. So where might he have overlooked?

—Wasn't it, perhaps, above—the direction he'd been too afraid to look, having been forsaken by the Stars?

"—There."

An hour of backing up later, his hand, brushing the ceiling, cut through empty air, and Subaru's eyes lit up. He stood and thrust his arm upward into the space—even with his shoulder in, he didn't hit a ceiling. There was a hole. Its size was more than twice his own girth—easily wide enough to fit his whole body through. Meaning, if he braced his back and legs against the sides, he should be able to climb.

"—————"

A faint breeze flowed down from above the hole. The scent Subaru had clung to as his only lifeline this whole time was riding that very breeze down into the depths. Having sniffed it out and confirmed it, there was no possibility of Subaru not climbing.

"Grh… nngh…!"

He fit his body into the hole and pressed his back against the wall. Of course, since the wall too was made of sand, one wrong angle of pressure would collapse it instantly and ruin the whole attempt. Carefully, so as not to let that happen, yet boldly whenever he planted a foot, Subaru braced his rear and legs in quick bursts of motion, inching his way up the hole bit by bit.

"—————"

Fortunately—if that was the word—the walls of the hole had hollows here and there, giving the lone climber just enough support. Someone with superhuman physical ability might've been able to use these hollows as footholds and simply bound their way up to the top.

"Heh."

But Subaru had no such physical ability, and could only press on with this plodding effort. Even so, the body that had been trained through hours Subaru himself had no memory of seemed to have been conditioned to endure exactly this kind of unnatural exertion. Like getting used to driving a new car, his body—its engine effectively swapped out—gradually adapted. By the time he'd cleared the halfway point of the hole, he'd gotten the knack of it, establishing himself as a fully competent climber.

Though, before he could put that competence to full use—

"—An exit."

He had no idea how many meters of wall he'd climbed, but by the time he noticed, he'd surely climbed high enough that a fall would be fatal. As the reward for his single-minded effort, Subaru finally conquered the wall.

"—————"

There was less emotion in it than he'd expected. It wasn't some bored thought like once you actually do it, it's nothing special, either. He simply thought—that climbing the wall was a trivial thing, set against what now lay before him.

Emerging from the hole, stepping onto new sand, Subaru rose to his feet. And facing the imposing presence standing before him, he murmured, short and quiet:

"—A door."

—There, standing alone, reliant on nothing else, was one strange door.

—Standing before that single door, Subaru was at a loss.

"I can see."

The top of the hole Subaru had reached didn't lead straight to the surface. This was probably still inside the hollow—if the hollow itself had layers, then the point where Subaru had fallen was B2, and where he stood now was B1, so to speak. And in a space hidden by means less grandiose than simply sly, stood a single door—not fused with any wall or floor, just standing there, aimlessly.

Was there anyone who could look at this and understand its meaning at a glance?

"—Besides me."

Gazing at the door, Subaru spoke those words with a strangely arrogant certainty. Hand to his chest, eyes wide, Subaru was fully aware of it as he faced the door he'd so suddenly encountered—this is a door meant for me.

He had a strange conviction. The conviction that this door belonged to him.

The wind that had flowed underground, the scent that wind had carried, and himself, who had followed it here. Everything had been drawing Natsuki Subaru toward this place.

And now, before him, the door glowed faintly, asserting its presence even in this darkness, waiting for the moment it would be opened by Natsuki Subaru's own hand.

"—————"

As if reaching to touch a beloved lover, Subaru approached the door, his heart racing. It looked like a wooden double door, about twice Subaru's own height. Looking closely, he could make out a handle at the center of the two panels, meant for pulling them open, and on it, a strange pattern.

—What he saw were seven jewels.

"—Ah"

Gazing at those jewels, Subaru touched the door. At once, the jewels on the door began to glow brilliantly. —Four of them.

Four of the seven jewels shone brightly, welcoming Natsuki Subaru's arrival—

—but the door's master did not recognize the visit of one who held no key.

—The instant after he blinked, Subaru had no idea where he was, and blinked again.

"Ha—"

Letting out a dazed breath, Subaru looked down at his own hands. He could see them. Of course he could. There was light here, color here. He could see the ground beneath his feet. He was standing on a stone floor. —A stone floor.

"—!"

Body stiffening, Subaru hastily looked around. Around him were reddish-brown walls, walls, walls—the walls of a tower built in a circle, and behind him, the great doors leading outside. This was inside the tower—the fifth floor of the Pleiades Watchtower.

He'd been thrown back to the very place he'd supposedly fled with such fierce resolve.

"Ba—"

ka (idiot) wouldn't follow. The words simply wouldn't come. It should have been the instant right after that blink. In the space of a single moment, Subaru found himself standing in a place like this. Unable to shake off the shock, he grew dizzy, his footing unsteady. His bare feet—his discarded shoes rolled and bumped against his toes—and Subaru clenched his back teeth hard.

This was no time to stand around in a daze. He needed to flee right now—no.

"Why the hell should I have to run?"

A turbid, roiling emotion boiled up within him, and Subaru looked up, swept along by a fierce surge of rage. A hatred, hot as molten magma left to reduce, was churning inside him before he even knew it.

It was a hatred without cause—for the end of his time alone, the end of the dark's peace, the end of the scent he'd clung to—for having everything that place had given him stripped from him by force.

"—————"

Why had Subaru had to run in the first place? Because someone had schemed to kill him, and had taken his life not once but twice. How could such a person go on hiding that fact behind a mask of calm, brazenly playing the part of a decent human being, in a place like this?

—The only suspects were inside this tower.

Was he supposed to play detective and pick out the culprit from among them? Was that why he'd run, because he couldn't? Idiot, idiot, idiot—you understand nothing.

—If one of the suspects was the right answer, then just beat them all down until he found the one that was.

"—————"

Fortunately, every last one of them was letting their guard down around Subaru. The culprit among them—whoever it was—surely hadn't dropped their guard toward Subaru. But that same culprit had no way of knowing that Subaru now suspected them.

Here lay the advantage of having died once already. Having died once, he now knew someone was trying to kill him. So before that murderous act could be carried out again, all he had to do was kill the one trying to kill him first.

"Hihh."

A malicious grin surfaced, and Subaru pressed a hand to his mouth. A brilliant plan had struck him, and he felt as though he'd just been granted a heaven-sent gift, saving himself by his own hand.

"—————"

With that settled, he needed a weapon, something to finish the job quickly. Once Subaru had reached a conclusion, he moved fast to act on it. Briskly, he made his way down the spiral staircase, further below—toward the sixth floor.

His memory was hazy on this, but he recalled that the tower's lowest floor, the sixth, was supposed to hold a lizard—one that he and the others, the original "Natsuki Subaru" among them, had borrowed to cross the desert on their way to the tower—along with something like a traveling carriage. Naturally, if such things were down there, some of them ought to be useful in an "emergency" like this.

"—————"

Reaching the lowest floor, Subaru found the carriage he was looking for—but stopped short. The reason was right beside the carriage: a massive lizard, collapsed and already lifeless.

Its head had been obliterated, the headless body lying on its side on the floor. Whatever had done this must have been tremendously powerful, because the blasted-away head was nowhere to be found. Instead, blood that had poured from the huge body stained the ground, but it had already dried, showing that some time had passed since the massacre.

"—The needle."

In that instant, what surfaced in Subaru's mind was the clash with the earthworm right after he'd left the tower, and the mysterious white needle that had blown apart its head, its whole massive body. The impression of that white needle attack matched the lizard's corpse lying here. If so, there was a high chance that attack had come from someone inside the tower—one of the suspects. And whoever had so mercilessly slaughtered a creature that was practically their own livestock—

"—Means they've stopped bothering to hide who they are."

Perhaps Subaru's escape had been the deciding factor, and his attacker had grown rash. Shuddering at the realization, Subaru rifled through the untouched carriage—apparently used as a sleeping space—and from among the assorted supplies, he found a large knife.

Something close to a survival knife, probably, in terms of use. More a large blade for clearing obstacles than a weapon proper—but in this situation, it would serve splendidly as the instrument of Subaru's purpose.

"Whoever the enemy is, I don't care anymore…"

—I'll make them pay.

Burning with pitch-black emotion, Natsuki Subaru, knife in hand, headed for the upper floors. A twisted smile pulled at his cheeks, hatred driving him forward. Believing that if it meant salvation, if it meant escape, even murder could be justified, he pressed on.

—Never noticing the tears of undiminished fear tracing down those same twisted cheeks.
