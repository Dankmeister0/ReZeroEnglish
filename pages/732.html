Arc 9 Chapter 36: "Proposition"

"I'm sorry! I've caused you so much trouble...!"

"No, if anything, it's Rem who owes the apology. Having you cry with such joy over me made Rem happy too — the same way it did when Sister did the same for me... ah."

Petra bowed her head over and over, apologizing for the unseemly way she'd lost her composure. Rem, trying to comfort her, stiffened at having said something ill-advised, and glanced toward Ram. At Rem's look, Ram smiled gently and said, "It's fine."

"After all, Ram is just a pathetic older sister who couldn't even keep her composure during such a moving reunion. It's only natural if Rem goes around calling me a crybaby."

"T-that's not what I meant! It's a misunderstanding to call Sister a crybaby! Besides, I was happy that Sister cried for me!"

"That's right, Ram. After all, you finally remembered Rem. Even if that made you cry, no one's going to think you're a crybaby. Cheer up!"

"Rem, Emilia-sama... boo-hoo-hoo."

Following Rem's lead, Emilia joined in the comforting, and Ram pressed a hand to her eyes and turned her face away. Watching this, the two of them only grew more worried — but from where Petra stood, the farce had gone on quite long enough. Of course, there was no denying that the situation was joyful enough to move Ram to tears.

"...Sister Ram, I understand you're happy, but you shouldn't tease the two of them so much."

"Eh!?"

"Tease...?"

Petra managed to steady her voice enough to say so, and Emilia and Rem both blinked in surprise. The wicked Ram, having deceived those two honest souls, stopped her fake crying and shrugged.

"That's true. Since your memories came back safely, I just couldn't help wanting to see all sorts of expressions on Rem's face, so I was a bit mean. Will you forgive your wicked sister?"

"Sister... honestly, it can't be helped. Just this once, all right?"

"Yes, thank you. Emilia-sama, is that all right with you too?"

"Honestly, if Rem's already forgiven you, I can't very well go on being angry forever. Really."

"Amaaazing. She wasn't humble at all, and still got away with it just by acting sweet."

Despite an attitude that showed not the slightest hint of remorse, Ram's high-handedness was easily forgiven by Rem and Emilia both, and Meili, witnessing it, muttered in exasperation. Petra shared in Meili's exasperation. It was very like Ram — but she was getting a little too carried away.

"I'd love to throw a victory party for Rem too — Sister Rem, I mean — but right now is a really critical time, so Sister Ram, please show some restraint too."

"Restraint, hm. That sounds like a perfectly reasonable opinion at first glance — but what about you, Petra?"

"...? What do you mean? I'm already regretting what happened earlier, you know."

"I'm not trying to blame you for losing your composure. What I'm curious about is something else. — The way you've naturally ended up settled right between Rem and Emilia-sama since a while ago."

At Ram's remark, one eye closed in a wink, Petra's eyes went wide. "Eh?" She looked to either side of herself, at the profiles of Emilia and Rem sitting there. The three of them were now seated in a row on a long sofa, with Petra in the middle. Petra had an arm looped around each of them, holding on tight as though determined never to let go.

"Huh?"

"It's not 'huh' at all. After you hugged Big Sister Rem, you just sooo naturally pulled her over there with you. And you dragged Big Sister Emilia along too."

"S-sorry. I guess my affection for both of you just... overflowed."

At Meili's continued exasperation, Petra looked back on herself and felt properly chastened. But her excuse was exactly the same as the cute little act Ram had just pulled, so Ram laughed at her with a sharp "Ha!"

"It's all right, don't worry, Petra-san. Rem has been wanting to properly fill in, one by one, all the blank spaces that were left behind until now."

"Sister Rem..."

"And besides, um, Rem had thought she hadn't gotten to be very close with you and the others living in the village, so it makes Rem happy to be cherished so much."

Still held in the crook of Petra's arm, Rem blushed faintly and smiled a bashful smile. By Rem's own reckoning, her acquaintance with Petra and the others had peaked around the time the Royal Selection began — that one month after Subaru had been welcomed into the mansion — and before that, they'd simply been people who passed each other by. So Rem's bashful understanding of things wasn't wrong. The reason Petra held such enormous feelings for Rem was less Petra's own emotion than the outsized influence of 'Subaru.' Still, even so, she couldn't help but think—

"Sister Rem smiling all bashful like that, it's so precious..."

"Petra-chan, your ears are sooo red, all the way. Are you okay?"

"Ah, it's not that! I'm not being unfaithful — Sister Emilia is still number one!"

"Eh? Ah, right. Since I'm the most important person in the camp, I do need to keep myself in check."

Caught between panic and happiness, Petra blurted out something unnecessary, but Emilia took it exactly the way an angel, a goddess, or some hopelessly self-flattering protagonist might, and let it slide right past her. At that, Petra pressed a hand to her chest to still her racing heart, telling herself, "My number one is Subaru, isn't it...!" as she stood up. If she stayed sandwiched between Emilia and Rem like this any longer, her heart was going to be torn apart.

'Well done, Petra! You held out admirably!'

"Subaru had better be ready for me to give him a whole lot of grief later. Cheater."

'This is way too cruel to a me who can't even touch Emilia-tan or Rem!'

Having somehow cut off her own weak-willed lingering feelings, Petra directed her pent-up frustration at Imaginary Subaru. She let out a sigh at 'Subaru,' who was expressing the explosion of emotion he felt for the memory-restored Rem by dabbing at tears with a handkerchief in one hand while whirling round and round the reception room. Then—

"I know I've been causing a commotion over myself, but it would help if we could settle down here for a moment. — Let's talk about him."

"—"

At Rem's quiet, level opening, the air in the room visibly tightened. It hadn't quite gone slack, but the mood had been heating up in contrast to the urgency of the situation — and it was Rem herself, the very cause of it, who dispelled that heat. Very admirable. Very well done.

"Did you and Ram hear the story from Clind-san?"

"More or less. And we were also shown the state the royal capital's in. To think it would turn into such a disaster... Barusu really made a mess of things, didn't he."

"Sister Ram, that way of putting it..."

"I'm sorry, let me rephrase. — All of us made a mess of things, didn't we."

"—"

Ram's assessment was heavy, and it landed with a weight on everyone's chest. It was something they'd already confirmed with each other before Ram and Rem joined the group, but even so, the sheer scale of the damage Al's actions had caused was enough to make one want to cover their eyes.

"That said, there's nothing to be gained from excessive self-blame. The responsibility for what happened lies, first and foremost, with the one who caused it. That's the conclusion we've reached."

"That may be how you all feel you should carry yourselves. But things aren't that simple. Am I wrong?"

"...Not a girl to be underestimated. Makes me wonder what all that mischief a moment ago was about."

Grumbling so, Old Man Rom rubbed his bald head, making a sour face at Ram's pressing question. At this exchange between the two of them — who seemed to understand each other perfectly well — Rem tilted her head. "Sister?"

"When you say it isn't simple, do you mean about where the responsibility lies?"

"Yes, that's right. Rom-sama seems to want to be the one to explain the details. I'll leave it to him."

"Tossed the troublesome part right at me, did you... well, fine. Just as this girl says, whatever our own resolve may be, the current state of things is bad news — for us, and for you lot too."

Left with the job of explaining, Old Man Rom pointed one thick finger at himself, then at Emilia. Watching that huge fingertip, Emilia blinked her violet eyes.

"Of course that's true for us, but — Rom-jii and the others too... Felt-chan as well?"

"Our Ezzo was at the tower too. Ezzo had been entrusted with the duty of managing the tower until the kingdom's official investigation team could be dispatched, and he failed in that duty. And as for Reinhard, he wasn't able to stop the helmet bastard or the 'Divine Dragon' either, and the royal capital ended up taking damage for it."

"But, thanks to Big Sister Emilia, the big rock didn't end up falling on the city, riiight? And she still gets scolded for it?"

"If shattering a thrown rock is enough to win favor, Reinhard could do the exact same thing over and over, endlessly. Things like this, Meili — they must be judged fairly not only by the result, but by the process as well."

Old Man Rom shook his great head slowly, his face troubled, and Meili pouted her lips at him. Behind Meili's unconvinced attitude was a hidden frustration that Emilia's great feat wasn't being properly recognized. Understanding this, Emilia smiled and said "thank you" to Meili, which only made the pouting girl turn away all the more.

"Let me tell you the bare minimum we must be certain to accomplish. First, the helmet bastard — the man calling himself Aldebaran — must be stopped by our own hands. This can't be left to anyone else, or to the kingdom. Only once that's done can we start talking about making up for our losses."

"Why does it have to be us specifically? Of course it's a given that we'll try our very hardest — but if someone can stop Al before he does something terrible..."

"You don't understand, Emilia. This is no longer a matter of Aldebaran alone. He has the 'Divine Dragon' with him. That is a grave matter that shakes the very foundation of the Royal Selection... to overturn it, there's no way but for a Dragon Maiden, a Royal Selection candidate, to draw the 'Divine Dragon' back. If that can't be done..."

"...The Royal Selection itself gets undone, as if it never happened?"

At Emilia voicing that decisive possibility, Old Man Rom drew his chin in with a grave expression. Having listened this far, Petra too, belatedly, caught up to the sense of crisis. To begin with, the Royal Selection that would decide the next ruler of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica was founded on the Covenant with the dragons, and on the prophecy of the Dragon History Stone needed to maintain it. In other words, it was a ritual that relied on the mighty power of the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica — and without the 'Divine Dragon,' the Royal Selection could not stand.

"And so, it must exist without fail. A resolution in which none other than a Royal Selection candidate herself stopped, with her own hands, the Aldebaran who led the 'Divine Dragon' astray."

That was the fundamental premise for bringing this great crisis to a close and allowing the Royal Selection to continue. The words of the sage of the Felt Camp, the elder Giant who had watched over the world for so long, carried a persuasiveness no one else could refute. They had to prevent the world from being destroyed at Al's hands. But that didn't mean that so long as the world wasn't destroyed, it didn't matter what became of everything else. After the battle ended, there had to remain—

"We have to make sure there's still a place left for Subaru-kun to come back to."

"Rem..."

Emilia gazed at Rem, who had murmured this after hearing Old Man Rom's account. Under that gaze, Rem's pale blue eyes filled with a sense of purpose as she answered, "Because, isn't that right?"

"He did that for me. Rem's room in the mansion was left exactly as it had been before... and because Subaru-kun thought of me so strongly, Rem was able to remember what she'd forgotten. It's thanks to this Morningstar, the one Subaru-kun polished every single day...!"

So Rem declared, forcefully, holding up the chained iron ball — the Morningstar. Overwhelmed by the boisterous jangling of the chain and Rem's sheer momentum, Emilia tilted her head.

"...Thanks to the Morningstar?"

"Yes. This may surprise you, but it happened the very instant I touched this Morningstar. All my memories came flooding back in a rush... I had thought it was just his obsessive fixation, but that was a misunderstanding on my part. ...There's so much I want to apologize for, too."

Lowering her eyes as though ashamed of herself, Rem began recounting the circumstances of how her memory had returned. At Rem's fervent appeal, Emilia's eyes went wide with confusion, her hands fluttering helplessly as she floundered. It was probably a matter of timing. — Whatever it was that 'Gluttony,' whom Al had taken out of the Prison Tower, had done, that was most likely connected to the restoration of Rem's 'memories' and her 'name.' And that had happened at the very same moment Rem touched the Morningstar back at the mansion. And so Rem had convinced herself that it was the Morningstar that had awakened her.

"Um, Rem, I want you to listen calmly, okay...?"

"Please rest easy, Emilia-sama. Rem will use this Morningstar to become the strength that brings Subaru-kun back, without question. After that, I'd love to talk with you about all sorts of things — what happened in the Empire, and what comes next..."

With her fist clenched tight, Rem's forceful manner left Emilia's troubled expression unchanged. Meanwhile, Meili glanced over at Ram and asked, "Aren't you gonna stop her?"

"It can't be helped. Even Ram didn't expect this one. — Who would've thought that Barusu's tall tale about polishing that thing every day would actually turn out to be useful."

"...I really don't think it's a good sign that I'm the closest thing to a sensible person in this group."

Meili lamented the situation — that even Ram of all people was overestimating the Morningstar's power. In any case, the matter of 'Gluttony' — including the parts that hadn't gone beyond guesswork — needed to be shared with Rem and the others. That could be left to Emilia, Petra decided, and keeping the surreal duet between Emilia and Rem at the edge of her vision, she turned back to face Old Man Rom. And then—

"Big Brother Clind still hasn't brought the master and the others back yet... but there's no time. We're allies, aren't we — us and you, Grandpa Rom?"

"...Mm. No mistake about that. Continuing the Royal Selection is our shared will."

"Then please, tell me. What is it Grandpa Rom noticed about Al-san?"

Old Man Rom had joined them out on the plains and accompanied them in flying to the royal capital. Because of the constraints of Clind's 'Compression,' he had chosen to act separately from Tonchinkan and the others, and in laying the groundwork for cooperating with Emilia's group, he had hinted that he'd grasped some critical piece of information about Al during their one clash.

By this point in the conversation, the Emilia Camp and the Felt Camp were already bound to share the same fate. It was about time, Petra felt, for them to open their hearts to one another, and so she pressed Old Man Rom for it. At Petra's request, Old Man Rom gave his bald head a few light slaps.

"This story's going to sound pretty outlandish, downright absurd. I wasn't holding it back to seem important — it's a matter of credibility."

"It's all right. I believe in you, Grandpa Rom. Seriously — enough that it'd probably surprise you if you knew just how much."

"...You're more than plenty strange yourself, Petra."

Perhaps taking it for mere flattery, Old Man Rom lowered the corners of his brows and muttered so in exasperation. But there was no lie in what Petra had said. Petra trusted Old Man Rom. Because she knew he cared deeply for Felt, held hostage by Al and the others, and that he possessed the kind of fatherly heart that would let him throw away his life for her without hesitation. So the hurdle of trust that Old Man Rom had been worried about had already been cleared, right from the start. Unaware of all these particulars behind Petra's confidence, Old Man Rom, still keeping half an eye on Emilia and Rem as they worked to reconcile their understanding of things, said—

"This is something I'll tell Emilia and the others too, later... but that Aldebaran fellow is most likely using something called an Authority — a cheat move, plain and simple."

'—An Authority.'

At Old Man Rom's carefully chosen words, it was 'Subaru' who reacted before Petra did. 'Subaru' stopped his midair spin, his face stiffening as he stared fixedly at Old Man Rom's face. That reaction of Imaginary Subaru's was only natural. As it happened, they'd just heard the word from Clind's own mouth — but both Petra and 'Subaru' recognized the word 'Authority' in a far worse sense than that.

It was a word that had been spoken by the Sin Archbishop of 'Sloth,' and by the 'Witch of Greed' sleeping in the mausoleum. A mighty right, an authority, that interfered with the very nature of this world, overturned concepts, and rewrote its laws at will. And the reason Old Man Rom had judged that Al had been wielding it was—

"—that fellow, it seems, can go back through time that's already passed. Doesn't seem to be for very long, mind you, but still."

—In the end, just what was it she had to do?

That 'proposition,' one she'd been living alongside endlessly ever since she was small and weak, kept trailing after Felt with the same easy familiarity even now, dragged around by Aldebaran's gang.

"Damn, it's annoying as hell."

Scratching her head in irritation, Felt spat out the frustration pooling deep in her gut. Felt knew that feelings like helplessness and resignation weren't the least bit useful for staying alive. And yet, despite the fact that they'd never quench a thirst or fill a stomach, these things kept welling up by every trick they had, trying to fill a person's whole body with themselves, getting in the way.

"—Tch."

Clicking her tongue, Felt sank her teeth into the dried meat she'd been given for dinner and tore off a bite. The taste was way too salty for her liking, and she felt a clear stab of dissatisfaction, then found herself exasperated at how spoiled she'd become. It had been barely two and a half days since she'd been taken away, and already she was missing Flam and Grassis's cooking. Even the heavily seasoned bachelor cooking Ezzo and Rachins occasionally set out on the table was far better than travel rations. They didn't let Reinhard cook — Flam and the others got angry about putting the right man in the right job — but if you secretly had him make sweets, he had a surprisingly decent hand at it. Unfortunately, both Felt and Old Man Rom's cooking skills were catastrophic — it was a wonder they hadn't died of food poisoning in the nearly ten years they'd spent haunting the Loot House. Perhaps that was even the reason Felt's growth lagged behind others her age. Whatever the state of the camp's cooking situation, though—

"I can't cook worth a damn. So what can I do?"

Licking a stray grain of salt off her fingertip, narrowing her red eyes, Felt's thoughts circled back to the same 'proposition' she'd started with.

—At present, Aldebaran's group kept moving from one base to another, working to recover from the toll the fighting in the royal capital had taken on Aldebaran and the 'Divine Dragon.' At first, Felt had assumed that once they'd fled the capital, Aldebaran and the others would head straight for their destination, the Grand Mogolade Geyser, but it seemed it wasn't simply a matter of getting there — they showed every sign of waiting for the right moment. That said, the rest they needed while waiting for that moment was by no means going smoothly — they were under relentless sabotage from a merciless pursuer: Otto Suwen.

"It's way more than just talking to bugs — that's one seriously terrifying Divine Protection. ...Actually, no, it's not the Protection that's terrifying, it's that Zodda Bug guy himself who's terrifying, probably."

Otto had a mild, dopey-looking face, but his true nature, it seemed, made him a person to watch out for above all else. Ultimately, any Divine Protection was nothing more than a kind of tool. However fine a sword you were handed, if you lacked the arm strength to swing it, the opportunity and drive to learn how to use it, and the courage to wield it when it mattered, the sword was nothing but a fine ornament. Otto had the strength, the opportunity, the drive, and the courage. — And Felt had to prove that she had those same things too, even without a sword in hand.

"That guy's whole strategy is just wear-them-down, no other option... he thinks up some nasty stuff."

Through his 'Divine Protection of Kotodama,' Otto had apparently won over insects, birds, and fish as allies, and because of him, Aldebaran's group was allowed not a moment's peace, forced constantly to deal with one thing or another. The buzz of insect wings, the howls of beasts, tainted water, contaminated food — his underhanded tactics ranged wide, and because they couldn't even secure proper sleep, Felt, dragged along for the ride, had a headache of her own to deal with too. Still, being caught up in this much was cute compared to what could happen, and Felt had already factored it in. If she could, she'd have liked to use her position traveling with the enemy to help Otto's schemes along without mercy.

"Just being the one holding the 'Divine Dragon' back does have some meaning to it, I guess..."

What made clear that this alone wasn't enough was the battle between the 'Divine Dragon' and the 'Sword Devil' in the royal capital. Even if Felt's presence shaved off a tenth of the 'Divine Dragon's' power, the remaining nine-tenths was still more than enough for the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica to unleash disaster-level force — that was the true measure of him. Considering the chance that Old Man Rom, having lost once, might challenge Aldebaran's group to a rematch further down the line, what she had now wasn't going to be enough. For the moment, that was the 'proposition' the captive Felt had to fight against.

"No, wait, there's one more, isn't there."

Murmuring so, Felt turned her eyes toward the other 'proposition' she faced from where she stood.

"—"

Across from her, on the other side of a crackling campfire, was a man facing Felt. — No, "facing" wasn't quite right. After all, the man had his face lowered and hadn't so much as glanced her way. Clutching a sheathed sword to his chest, hunched over as still as if he were dead, was the red-haired man scattering gloom out of every inch of his body in all directions — Heinkel.

At the entrance to a natural cave hollowed into the rocky terrain, Felt and Heinkel were alone together, sharing a gloomy stretch of time as they waited for the others, who had gone off to check the surroundings for safety and clear away any interference. Technically, Heinkel was supposed to have been assigned as Felt's watchman, but going by how often each of them actually looked at the other, there was no telling which of them was really the watchman. His thorough rejection of the outside world — not touching so much as water, let alone his dinner of dried meat — kept grating on Felt's nerves with everything it had.

"—"

Felt had declared that she had no ruler by which to measure Heinkel's actions as good or bad. There was no lie in that, and she wasn't changing her stance now — but she did have a ruler for like and dislike. At the very least, an attitude like a Hollow's, in someone who was still alive, earned a firm verdict of hate.

"Hey, if you're not gonna eat it, gimme that dried meat."

"—"

"You can hear me, right? Don't just ignore me—whoa, whoa."

Thinking it'd be too combative to start off snapping, she'd used the dried meat as a conversation opener — only to have it flung at her, as if to say "shut up." She caught it in a hurry, but Heinkel still hadn't even looked her way. All at once it felt idiotic that she'd opened her fist at all, and Felt called out "Hey" again.

"Why the hell did you butt in like that back there?"

Bluntly, without hesitation, Felt cut straight to the cause of Heinkel's crouched-over state.

"—"

Heinkel stayed silent, but he had thrown the dried meat at her, so he hadn't shut the outside world out completely. With that in mind, Felt gave a small snort and went on.

"Your old man — man, that's an awkward way to put it. It was a fight between Reinhard's grandfather and the 'Divine Dragon.' I ain't exactly a fighter myself, so I don't know all the fine details of who had the upper hand in that fight. But you're supposed to be on the 'Divine Dragon's' side, right?"

"—"

"And the fact that you're the one who cut in means the 'Divine Dragon' was in trouble. That's already a hell of a thing, but there's something off about it. — If your goal is the 'Dragon's Blood,' then the grandfather looking like he was about to win should've been the perfect chance."

"—"

"If the dragon died, you might've gotten your hands on the blood — but you got in the way instead. Why?"

Taking full advantage of the fact that the man raised no objection, Felt laid out her words without holding back. Just as she'd said, Heinkel's interference in the fight between the 'Divine Dragon' and the 'Sword Devil' was, given his stated goal, extremely unnatural. She had also considered the possibility that a deep grudge against his father was the motive, but right before taking flight with the 'Divine Dragon,' Heinkel had asked the dragon to heal Wilhelm, the very man he himself had stabbed. In other words, in that battle, Heinkel had wanted neither the death of the 'Divine Dragon' nor the death of the 'Sword Devil.'

"It's way too inconsistent. Just what the hell are you—"

—was the very moment she was about to add "thinking." In an instant, she was shoved down onto the hard ground, a drawn blade pressed against her throat.

"You talk too damn much, kid."

Right in front of her were Heinkel's bloodshot blue eyes. Maybe he'd bitten the inside of his own mouth — his breath, reeking of blood, washed over her, and Felt grimaced. Even as this happened, Heinkel slid the blade along her skin as if to threaten her further.

"If you don't wanna get hurt, keep your damn mouth—gwaaugh!?"

In the gap of that threat, Felt drove her fist in without a moment's hesitation, punching Heinkel square in the nose. "Ngh, ah?" Heinkel's eyes went wide, more from shock at being punched than from the pain itself. Felt used the momentum of getting back up to kick him square in the chest, and looked down at Heinkel as he stumbled and fell back onto his rear.

"Like I'd be scared of you, at this point."

So saying, Felt picked up Heinkel's sword, which had fallen close by. For an instant Heinkel stiffened with a flinch, but Felt just sighed and tossed the sword back to him. Heinkel caught it on reflex, eyes widening, and Felt only shrugged at that.

He was probably wondering why she'd given the sword back, but as far as Felt was concerned, it was an obvious call. Even if Felt kicked up a fight here, she couldn't beat Heinkel, and if, by some accident, Heinkel happened to cut her down, it was plain to see how thrilled Yae would be about it. To begin with, the very fact that they'd been left alone together, just the two of them, made Yae's aim obvious. Given half a chance, Yae would like nothing better than to cut both Felt and Heinkel down. To avoid playing into her scheme, kicking up trouble here wasn't a smart move. — Though even without all that reasoning, there was also the simple fact that beating Heinkel senseless just wasn't Felt's job.

"Though whether that makes it Reinhard's job, I'm not so sure about that either."

"—!"

At the mention of Reinhard's name, Heinkel flinched again, exaggeratedly. A man more than twice her age, cringing in fear at Felt's every word and gesture — it was an appallingly ugly sight. But Felt wanted to know the reason behind that ugliness. Even if it brought her, Felt, a taste that was bitter beyond words.

"Same as before, you don't have to answer any of it. I'll just talk on my own and draw my own conclusions."

Brushing the dirt off the hem of her clothes from having been shoved down, Felt didn't wait for a reply and looked up at the sky instead. Through the gap in the rocky terrain, the night sky peeking through held any number of stars glittering with an almost inappropriate brightness. Glaring at the irony of the sky's beauty, Felt began, "Here's the thing."

"What's been bugging me about the fight between the 'Divine Dragon' and the grandfather — that's everything I just said. You want the 'Dragon's Blood,' but what you're doing looks all contradictory. And there's one more thing that looks contradictory to me. — 'Gluttony.'"

"—"

"Never mind for the moment that he's got the worst damn personality and never shuts his mouth. Even setting that aside, a Sin Archbishop is no-good scum, nobody's welcoming that... but if there's a reason to hate that bastard, you've got a whole lot more of one than I do, don't you?"

Heinkel, staying silent, answering nothing, held his breath at that question. Even without a word in answer, his reaction made everything far too obvious, and Felt narrowed her eyes at him.

Roy Alphard, freed from the Prison Tower and brought along by Aldebaran because his plan required it — a Sin Archbishop who'd caused countless tragedies had endless reasons to be hated and despised, but for Heinkel there ought to be a clearer, absolute reason for hostility. Because the harm suffered by the woman who was Reinhard's mother and Heinkel's wife — Louanna Astrea, who lay in eternal sleep — bore a striking resemblance to the harm suffered by 'Gluttony's' victims.

"Told you once already, but I know your — Reinhard's family situation. That the old man's wife has been asleep for over ten years, and that no way's been found to wake her. Well, I figure that side of it gets settled once you've got the 'Dragon's Blood'... but what about making things right?"

"—"

"How are you gonna settle things with the bastard who's kept your wife asleep for over ten years?"

Following the attack on Duke Crusch Karsten and the Battle for Priestella, the sluice city, the harm caused by 'Gluttony's' Authority had come to light. The 'Sleeping Beauties' — tragic figures discovered in a sleep from which they would never wake, unknown to anyone around them, every connection to them severed — had turned out to be, of all things, victims of the Witch Cult. Naturally, that must have reached Heinkel's ears too. And yet, in spite of it, Heinkel had never once shown any interest in 'Gluttony,' who might be the cause of his wife's sleep.

—Because obtaining the 'Dragon's Blood' and waking his wife came before anything else? —Because he had no room left for it, shaken by the fact that he'd stabbed his own father with his own hands and been drenched in his blood?

Or perhaps—

"If you seriously don't have that bastard on your radar at all, then why?"

"W-wait... what are you..."

"Don't need an answer. Told you already. I'll just talk on my own and draw my own conclusions."

His hand trembling around the hilt of his sword, Heinkel looked at Felt with a face the color of ash. Meeting those blue eyes descended from House Astrea against her own red ones, Felt found her certainty confirmed. Deep in Heinkel's eyes was a dying ember, terribly faint, and yet one that refused to go out entirely. — That was probably the same kind of warmth that lived in Old Man Rom's eyes when he looked at Felt.

"—"

Heinkel Astrea looked like a self-destructive, self-centered, selfish man, one who didn't know his own place, whose very core wavered without end, a contemptible man beyond all help. — But if that faint warmth she'd just barely glimpsed at the very depths of him was always, in some part, behind this man's actions—

"The reason you won't even look at 'Gluttony' is because you know he's got nothing to do with your wife falling asleep. You know it'd be pointing the blame in the wrong place, so you won't even raise your sword against him. But—"

—The harm behind Louanna Astrea's status as a 'Sleeping Beauty' was decisively different from that of the other 'Sleeping Beauties.' Unlike the 'Sleeping Beauties' cut off from every connection across the whole world, Louanna was remembered by Reinhard, by Heinkel, by every single person connected to her, all of whom mourned her sleep. In other words, it was different. Louanna Astrea was not a victim of 'Gluttony.' And Heinkel, knowing that fact, had never told a single soul. Why? Because it could never be allowed to be known. — The true cause that had put Louanna to sleep. And that cause was—

"—Reinhard."

"—"

Frozen still, his wide-open eyes, his expression stripped of voice, his breath drained of all life — together they became an answer more eloquent than any words, and pushed Felt's certainty over the edge.

What she herself had to do. — To know: that was Felt's 'proposition,' here and now. The bitterly bitter truth that the resolve and certainty she'd brought to that 'proposition' had led her to, was—

"—So it was that Reinhard bastard himself who put Reinhard's mother to sleep."
