Arc 4 Chapter 101: "θ, Part 1"

One of the representative personalities of Ryuzu that Subaru calls θ.

She had entered the mausoleum and touched upon the past of Ryuzu Meyer, the original from whom they had all been derived, but those memories were terribly fragmentary, with even their chronology sparse and uncertain.

That was surely, θ thought, because her own existence was one formed by cutting the soul of the original Ryuzu Meyer into many shapes. If that were so, then might the other Ryuzus — the three called α, β, and Σ, or perhaps even the copies whose personalities had not yet properly developed — be able to glimpse a different past?

Even if that thought were true, θ had no wish to send them into the mausoleum. —Showing the shape of the past she herself had seen to another copy of Ryuzu was, for θ, an unbearable, near-intolerable agony.

"—What is it? You can stare all you like, I'm not giving you anything, I suppose."

The memory begins with being glared at by a girl with pale-colored hair.

A girl of lovely features. Hair the color of something dissolving into light, pale and delicate; skin white and translucent. Her eyes, faintly tinged blue, were round and large, easily dressing the girl's appearance in the word "charming." Her hair, parted into two, was done up in large, long curls — one could tell just by looking that running fingers through it would meet with a silky smoothness and a springy softness.

The dress, of a subdued color, seemed a touch too mature for a girl of such a young appearance, but given the flashiness of the girl's own features, harmony had clearly been intended between her coloring and the dress.

"——"

Pierced by the lovely girl's sharp, thorny gaze, Ryuzu shrank back. More than that, compared to the girl before her, her own appearance and clothing were shabby in every way. Being of about the same age only made her own wretchedness stand out all the more, and she felt so ashamed she could hardly bear to stand there.

"Hmph. A coward, I suppose."

The girl snorted in disappointment at Ryuzu, who could say nothing back and simply fell silent. Even that gesture, from such a lovely face, was itself a charming motion — but for Ryuzu it brought a pain that squeezed her chest more than any insult could have. Before she could even register that this was something close to fear — fear of displeasing the girl before her —

"Beatrice. What is that attitude? I don't recall teaching you to behave that way."

A gentle voice called out, and the girl's expression stiffened. The voice came from behind the girl — that is, from the direction Ryuzu had been looking.

Emerging from a small shack at the back of the settlement was a woman who was "pure white." Long, flowing white hair. Skin so translucent that even light seemed unable to reach it. Only her eyes and lips, and the long-sleeved dress and skirt she wore, held any color at all, quietly announcing to those around her that her presence was indeed real.

The figure slowly walking closer was a being of great benevolence to this village — the Witch, Echidna-sama. The girl reacted dramatically to Echidna's voice. The one called Beatrice turned around, her face still stiff.

"Ah, eh... That's not it, I suppose, Mother! Betty didn't do anything... it's just, this girl—"

"I never taught you to make excuses in such an unseemly way either. Tell the facts, accurately. If you're certain you've done nothing wrong, there should be no hesitation in doing so. Isn't that right?"

"That's... not wrong, I suppose..."

There was no rise in emotion in Echidna's voice, but there was a quiet, relentless severity to it. Beatrice let her shoulders drop, pressed her hands together, and with a sulky expression said,

"Just as Mother told me, Betty was waiting quietly outside, I suppose. And then this girl came along. She was watching from a distance... I thought it was rude, I suppose. So I called her over and demanded to know what she wanted, I suppose."

"Hmm. I see. And is that correct, from your side?"

"Ah...! Y-yes, that's right. I'm terribly sorry. I—I was the one who was rude..."

Beatrice's account was accurate. Ryuzu had been at the edge of the village, gazing absently at the girl leaning her weight against the fence. Beatrice, no doubt waiting for Echidna's business to finish. Her figure, her eyes, had looked somehow lonely, and it had made Ryuzu's chest tighten. But saying such a thing aloud would surely only earn her a scornful laugh. Ryuzu made her small body smaller still and tried to weather the storm by bowing her head.

"You don't deny it, then. In that case, as Beatrice says, it seems it was young Ryuzu who was somewhat rude."

"That's right, Mother. So Betty isn't at all wrong—"

"However, treating her with enough overbearing force to frighten her this badly was a failing on Beatrice's part. I've told you before, haven't I. You are certainly special, but that is not a reason to look down on others."

"Ugh, mmh..."

Beatrice, who had been about to ride on Echidna's agreement, fell silent at the immediate rebuttal. Watching this exchange, Ryuzu felt a trembling deep in her chest at the fact that Echidna had remembered her name.

Small as this settlement was, she herself was an insignificant child. That the greatly benevolent Witch-sama remembered the name of such a girl was, for a resident of the Sanctuary who owed gratitude and reverence to the "Witch of Greed," an honor far beyond her worth.

"I'll leave the correcting of that particular habit to Juice once we're back at the mansion. I'm sure he'll throw himself into it eagerly."

"...Betty doesn't like Juice all that much, I suppose."

"Since he considers being disliked to be his very role, that's likely exactly what he wants."

Echidna gave a faint smile to Beatrice, who was scowling in distaste, and then turned her face toward Ryuzu. Ryuzu's heart gave an involuntary leap. Having missed her chance to join the conversation and wondering when she ought to slip away, it came as a shock that Echidna's attention was still on her. Echidna walked closer to Ryuzu, who remained frozen, shoulders still raised in surprise.

"Sorry to have startled you, Ryuzu. This child is Beatrice, and... something like a daughter to me, you might say. As you can see, I'm a bit embarrassed that her manners still aren't quite what they should be."

"Not 'something like,' I am her daughter, I suppose."

"Well, so it would seem. From now on, I expect I'll be bringing her along to the Sanctuary with me now and then. You'll likely have more chances to interact, so I hope you two can get along."

"Y-yes! Please leave it to me, Echidna-sama!"

A hand was placed on her shoulder, and Ryuzu nodded, her heart trembling with joy. Echidna gave a satisfied dip of her chin at Ryuzu's agreement, while behind her, Beatrice muttered sulkily, "...Not that Betty would mind being on her own, I suppose."

"You there, sorry to bother you. Echidna-sama should have arrived by now — have you seen her?"

At the call, Ryuzu, who had been carrying a laundry basket, stopped and turned around. Then, realizing who it was that had called out to her, she nearly let out a startled cry. As she nearly dropped the laundry basket in her arms from surprise, an arm shot out and caught it for her.

"Oh, wh—"

"Wah, ah, I'm so sorry!"

Ryuzu bowed her head to the boy who had closed the distance in a smooth motion and steadied the basket. Seeing this, the boy, brushing back his long indigo hair, said, "No, don't worry about it," and continued,

"It's I who should apologize — I didn't notice you were carrying something. That was thoughtless of me."

"Not at all... It would be far too much, from Mathers-sama."

"Whoever it may be, one must never forget consideration for a lady. ...If I may correct one thing — I'm not overly fond of being addressed by my family name. I'd much rather you call me Roswaal."

Saying this to the deferential Ryuzu, the boy — Roswaal — closed one eye in a wink. He was perhaps four or five years older than Ryuzu, a full head taller. And yet his limbs had not quite finished stretching out, and his clear, carrying voice had not yet finished changing into a man's. He carried a kind of allure found only in that brief interval between boy and young man — a naturally graceful bearing so refined that even young Ryuzu could sense it.

And no wonder. Young as he was, Roswaal was already head of House Mathers, ruling over multiple territories, and moreover a remarkable figure who cooperated with the Witch Echidna in managing the Sanctuary.

He was, in a sense different from Echidna, a person who contributed to the maintenance of this settlement called the Sanctuary, and it was often said that one must never be discourteous toward him. Ryuzu, blushing at Roswaal's wink, desperately tried to gather her thoughts back to the substance of his question.

"So, um, about Echidna-sama... I haven't seen her yet today. Beatrice-sama wasn't in her usual spot either."

"I see. Perhaps her arrival has been delayed. Whatever the case with Echidna-sama, it's hard to imagine Beatrice not coming to see you the moment she arrives."

"Um, well... I think it's just been a coincidence that Beatrice-sama has had so many chances to talk with me..."

"'Coincidence' is just what Beatrice says it is, isn't it?"

At Roswaal's question, Ryuzu nodded silently. Ever since being introduced, she had crossed paths with Beatrice, Echidna's daughter, many times. Beatrice, who accompanied Echidna on her visits to the Sanctuary despite her busy schedule, tended to wander about the Sanctuary while Echidna attended to her business. In the course of that, she and Ryuzu crossed paths quite often. When Ryuzu was bringing in the laundry, or going to gather wild greens, Beatrice happened to pass by rather frequently.

At Ryuzu's answer, Roswaal laughed, unable to contain himself.

"Beatrice isn't honest with herself. I hope you don't dislike her for it."

"Dislike her, not at all. She's been kind to someone like me. If anything, I worry I'm always the one putting Beatrice-sama in a bad mood... I even worry sometimes whether she dislikes me."

"Then you needn't worry. Beatrice's 'dislike' isn't very trustworthy. If she truly disliked something, she wouldn't come up with all sorts of excuses just to keep following it around."

Is that really so? Ryuzu wondered, tilting her head as she listened to Roswaal's words. When Beatrice interacted with her, she was almost always grumbling some complaint or another, and eager to find fault with Ryuzu's every little action. Seeing only that side of her, it was hard to believe she didn't genuinely dislike her, no matter what anyone said. Ryuzu, along with many of the Sanctuary's residents, had often been singled out as targets of such malice and hostility out in the wider world. Beatrice's version of it was, compared to what Ryuzu knew, overwhelmingly gentler and warmer — but sharpness was still sharpness.

"I do hope that someday, you'll all come to understand that too."

Watching Ryuzu fall silent, Roswaal murmured this quietly. Seeing the lonely smile that rose to his lips, Ryuzu felt a chill of alarm, wondering what she could have possibly done to put such an expression on his face. But before she could stammer out some words of apology, Roswaal blinked his yellow eyes as though noticing something, and

"Ah! Sensei! I heard you'd arrived, so I came running!"

Raising a hand, Roswaal cast off his mature bearing from a moment ago and took off running with the delighted face of a child. Ryuzu's eyes followed him as he shot past her, and standing where he ran toward was a single woman — Echidna. Echidna, noticing Roswaal rushing toward her with a face full of delight, raised her eyebrows slightly.

"Roswaal. I don't recall ever giving you permission to call me 'sensei.'"

"Today, I won't back down so easily. That assignment you gave me before — I've mastered it properly now. Equalizing the convergence rate of the four colors of mana to produce rainbow-colored mana. From there, I've managed on my own to reach the point of adding the remaining two colors. What do you think?"

"You reached six colors combined all on your own, through self-study? My, my... that's a rate of mastery — and a tenacity — frightening to behold. Goodness, what a troublesome student."

It was rare to see Echidna show surprise. At the very least, Ryuzu was seeing it for the first time. Roswaal puffed out his chest proudly, waiting for Echidna's words. Even to Ryuzu, younger than him, the sight was somehow endearing. From his bearing, his gaze, and every small detail, it was plain to see an unhidden reverence and affection for Echidna overflowing — and something even beyond that.

"What are you standing there gawking for, I wonder."

"Ah... Beatrice-sama..."

While Ryuzu had been watching the two of them from a distance, Beatrice, who had come to stand beside her unnoticed, suddenly peered into her face. Ryuzu instinctively took a step back, and Beatrice settled into her familiar pose, arms crossed, and gave a snort in Ryuzu's direction.

"Staring like that — how rude of you, as usual, I suppose."

"I—I'm terribly sorry. That was thoughtless of me."

Scolded, Ryuzu felt embarrassed at her own shamelessness. Yet at Ryuzu's apology, Beatrice's frown only deepened further. Hers was a face that, if only she weren't always either smiling or pressing her lips into a frown, would have looked much softer. And yet it was always Ryuzu's own careless behavior that seemed to leave her scowling — for which she felt sorry indeed.

"How long are you going to stand there making that face and looking down, I wonder — it's irritating. If you have time to keep carrying that laundry basket around forever, you should hurry along to your next task, I suppose."

"Y-yes. I'll do that. Then, please excuse me."

Bowing her head once more to the sharp-tongued Beatrice, Ryuzu hurriedly took her leave. But as she quickened her pace and walked off, Beatrice came trotting after her, the hem of her dress dragging along the ground.

"Beatrice-sama...?"

"It's nothing, I suppose. Simply killing time."

To Ryuzu, who turned only her head to look back, Beatrice responded with a haughty, composed expression. Ryuzu was about to turn her head back forward as usual, when she recalled her earlier exchange with Roswaal. Beatrice, according to that boy's assessment, was by no means averse to talking with Ryuzu —

"Beatrice-sama. If it wouldn't be too much trouble, would you help me fold the laundry?"

"...Hah?"

Mustering her courage to suggest helping with chores, Ryuzu was met with a displeased growl from Beatrice. Beatrice's large eyes went wide, her expression shifting into something of surprise mixed with a hint of anger. Seeing this, Ryuzu nearly regretted having let Roswaal put the idea in her head.

"—If you can't manage it alone, I suppose there's no helping it, I'll help you, I suppose."

"Eh?"

"I'm not saying it twice, I suppose. Now come on, hurry up, I suppose."

With that, Beatrice brushed past the momentarily frozen Ryuzu with quick steps. In the instant she passed by, Ryuzu caught sight of Beatrice's mouth curving — half in exasperation, half in some other feeling entirely. Something warm blazed up deep in her chest, and Ryuzu hurried into a light trot to catch up and walk alongside her. Then, peering at her profile, she said,

"If it's all right, could you carry a little of the laundry for me?"

"Don't get carried away, I suppose. ...Well, it can't be helped. Just a little, I suppose."

So saying, Beatrice reached her hand out toward Ryuzu, her expression reluctant all the while.
