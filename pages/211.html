Arc 4 Chapter 45: "The Conditions of the Tea Party"

"—here."

Subaru squeezed his eyes shut and clenched his teeth, bracing for the searing pain that was sure to come to his fingertips.

But that grim, almost tragic resolve—

"...Eh?"

Despite having said it to the end, nothing changed in Subaru's body at all.

"...Hm."

Subaru raised his head, touching his own chest to confirm nothing was wrong. Across from him, Echidna, recrossing her legs, furrowed her elegant brow ever so slightly—no different from moments before.

He hurriedly checked her over, but there was no notable change in the way she looked at him. Her breathing, her mannerisms—both were as calm as ever. Bracing for the worst possibility, he stared fixedly at the chest of her mourning dress, at which—

"I don't know if I can live up to your expectations, but I don't have much confidence in the size of my chest. If it were Sekhmet or Minerva, you might have gotten to experience a physical defect like shoulder stiffness from the weight... but from a purely curious standpoint, that's the one disappointing part, I suppose."

"...That's not why I was staring at it. No, more importantly—"

Echidna spun out her words as if it were the most natural thing in the world, and Subaru's thoughts stalled before he could respond. He pressed his palm to his mouth, desperately suppressing the emotion that threatened to make his voice tremble.

"I... when I die, I go back in time and redo the world. I'm doing a 'Return by Death.'"

"I heard you. And before I heard it, I'd already read it. I see—what a rare situation."

Echidna nodded, breezily digesting the fact Subaru had just declared.

But it was precisely that attitude of hers that constituted, for Subaru, an abnormality on par with a bolt from the blue.

—Once spoken aloud, no matter how firmly one tried to hold one's heart steady, the punishing hand would snap even that resolve like a twig, with ease.

Breaking that taboo, and having either himself or the person he confided in suffer heart-searing agony as a result—that had become an ineradicable trauma, ensnaring Subaru completely.

Which was why, for Subaru, doing this as some kind of "test" was simply not an option. Without Echidna's push, he would have flinched and never been able to force through that final decision.

It had been that great a resolution. And yet it had gone this smoothly—

"Why isn't that hand coming...?"

"That almost sounds like you're disappointed it's not coming. Which would make it sound like you're upset you missed your chance to murder me—how hurtful."

"That's not what I— damn it!"

"I know, I know. It's a wonder you can be driven this far when your heart has no room to spare."

Ignoring Subaru's heart, shaken to its core, Echidna kept to her own unshakeable pace. Unable to bear his irritation at her any longer, Subaru clicked his tongue and narrowed his already sharp eyes further.

"Answer me straight. No matter how many times I say I'm doing 'Return by Death,' that hand—the Witch's hand—isn't coming here? Is that what I should believe?"

"So you do understand that was the Witch's hand. ...Ah, yes, that's right. This is my dream, my castle. The dream and illusion I see while dying. No one may enter without my permission."

"Can you guarantee that?"

"How cautious of you. —I guarantee it. The only witches who can exist here are the six: Greed, Gluttony, Lust, Pride, Wrath, and Sloth. There is no room here for Envy to intrude."

Echidna's answer came firm and forceful, in response to Subaru's insistent confirmation.

Hearing it, Subaru held his breath for a moment, then, after a stretch of silence, went slack in his chair. His shoulders dropped, his face bowed, and he let out one long, long breath.

"So... that's how it is... I see... I see, huh...!"

"—————"

Covering his face with his palms, Subaru simply repeated the same words, over and over. As if confirming them. As if refusing to let go of them. As if clinging to them.

Because both being freed from the Witch's fingertips, and being able to speak of the taboo he'd carried on his back all this time, were things happening for the very first time in his life in this other world.

"What a remarkably moved expression you're wearing."

Echidna remarked, her lips curling faintly as she watched Subaru tossed about by an endless wave of emotion. Running her fingers through her white hair, she went on.

"I suppose it just goes to show how much it's continued to torment you. Given the sort of obsessive affection you've been on the receiving end of, I suppose that can't be helped."

"Sorry, yeah, something welled up in me there. I'm calming down. ...Yeah, calmed down. Now that I have, there's stuff I want to talk about. A mountain of stuff."

The restriction on speaking of 'Return by Death' simply didn't apply here.

Just when he thought it had taken time to accept that fact, this time his heart raced too fast after accepting it.

Everything he'd carried until now, everything he was carrying now—the sense of liberation at being able to put it all into words. Into the deadlocked, suffocating feeling, something like a ray of hope shone through. However—

"You seem to be under one misapprehension."

"—?"

"It's true that within this castle of mine, the demonic hand that binds you cannot intervene. So I understand why your heart races at being able to disclose secrets you've never been able to reveal to anyone else. But... whether I choose to lend an ear to whatever circumstances you're carrying, or conveniently cooperate, or offer advice—that's an entirely separate matter, you understand?"

"Eh...?"

Her words doused cold water over Subaru's heart, which had been building up steam until then.

Subaru fell silent, while Echidna wore the expression of someone who'd simply stated the obvious. Faced with her unexpected response, Subaru couldn't hide his confusion and dismay.

He let out a sound that was neither "ah" nor "uh," his gaze wandering.

The light he'd thought he'd seen, the sense of liberation he'd hoped would let him break through the dead end—both slipped from his grasp. Not knowing what to do about it, he simply sat there when—

"When you make a face like an abandoned child, it troubles me too, you know. I don't believe I'm demanding anything so very difficult."

As she spoke, she tilted her head with a troubled expression, and tapped the white table three times with an extended finger, as if flicking it. Drawn along, Subaru's gaze followed to where her finger, having struck the table, now pointed to a single spot—the untouched cup that had been served to Subaru.

"You were invited to a witch's tea party. If you intend for our discussion here to blossom into something, shouldn't you first offer proof that you've accepted the invitation?"

"...Ngh. You're hard to read, you know that?"

"I believe I pointed it out as clearly as could be, right at the very start."

Now that she mentioned it, thinking back, Echidna had indeed complained about Subaru flying into a rage without reading the mood of the tea party, without so much as a glance at his cup.

As retribution for that, one might say the emotional wound Subaru had suffered was rather disproportionate.

"Damn it, fine, I get it!"

He snatched the cup off the table and poured the amber liquid swirling within straight down his throat in one go. Despite the time that must have passed since it was poured, it hadn't lost its ideal temperature—perhaps that alone spoke to it being something served at a witch's tea party.

He drank it down so fast he barely tasted it. Wiping the drops running down the corner of his mouth roughly with his sleeve, Subaru said—

"There. Drank it dry. Does that make you willing to recognize me as a participant in this tea party now?"

"To have my bodily fluid gulped down with such vigor... yes, it does make my body run hot."

"Guh—I forgot about that!"

Subaru fell for the same Dona-tea trap that had caught him at the very first tea party.

Echidna watched, amused, as Subaru pressed a hand to his mouth, fighting down the urge to gag, and then, carving a smile onto her finely wrought, beautiful face, she said, "I recognize you."

"Your question of 'why' served as your qualification, and so the door to the tea party opened. And having drunk the tea the Witch offered you, you are now a bona fide participant. As the host of this tea party, I am obligated to entertain you. —Now, go on, speak."

She clapped her hands lightly together, her eyes gleaming with curiosity.

"Racking my brain to produce an answer—that, too, is a form of bliss for me."

—Rather than the start of a tea party, it might be better called the start of a Q&A session.

Echidna, the witch possessing vast knowledge through the tome called The World's Wisdom.

Being able to speak freely, without any hindrance, about the "Return by Death" he'd never been able to confide in anyone, and about the information he'd gained through its use—that alone was a tremendous blessing.

Facing someone who could get to the very heart of things, Subaru let flow from his mouth a flood of questions that he'd long since set aside as pointless to even ask. The first one he fired off—

"What's causing my 'Return by Death'... it's safe to say it's the Witch of Envy, right?"

"Broadly speaking, that recognition isn't wrong. As for the actual mechanism of 'Return by Death,' honestly, I wouldn't know without observing your death myself. Though since I can't take your life within my castle, that's a condition that can never be fulfilled."

"So I can't die here?"

"It's nothing more than a fleeting dream in the end—a temporary perch that draws in only your spirit for a moment. Do you really think that just because you die within a nightmare, your actual body dies as well?"

"Well, I feel like if you had a bad enough nightmare, you could die of shock or something..."

In fiction, there were plenty of stories where dying within a dream world was reflected in reality. Using that as an example, it didn't seem so strange that death within a witch's mental world might connect to actual bodily death.

"Well, I guess it'd be weird for me to insist I should die when you're telling me I won't. Anyway, so no matter how stupid I act here, I don't need to worry about dying."

"Though it's possible your spirit could be shattered to pieces, return to your body, and leave you a vegetable."

"Living-corpse mode is the same as dying... except I can't 'Return by Death' from it, which makes it way worse!"

Once, in the loop that began in the royal capital, Subaru's spirit had come to the very brink of collapse. Whether that had been some self-preservation instinct, or an act he'd put on of having his ego crushed, he himself wasn't entirely sure—he'd been swept along by the situation until rage brought him back to himself.

Still, he could intuit that having his spirit crushed here wouldn't be the kind of thing curable by that sort of shock therapy.

"A situation where I can't even choose the timing of my own death by my own will—no thanks. ...Though obviously it'd be best of all if I just didn't die at all."

"That even 'death' can be considered a tool for drawing your victory conditions closer to you—that's a set of values unknown even to me. Even I, of all people, cannot observe my own 'death' and store it away as knowledge."

"...? But you're actually dead, and doing this self-bound-spirit thing, so don't you remember what it was like when you died?"

She'd already explained to him, from her own mouth, that she was an intangible being, one of the dead. That her soul, upon dying, had been sealed away and imprisoned by the divine dragon Volcanica.

"Strictly speaking, that's not quite right. It's true that my body—the vessel that held my soul—died and was lost. But my soul itself was sealed by Volcanica before it could face death."

"...So basically, your body died, but your soul didn't. Since what's inside never died, it's a bit different from having actually experienced 'death'?"

"That's about the size of it. Which is why, self-serving as it may sound, I envy your current state. To be able to observe one's own 'death'—and moreover, not just once, but many times over—is a privilege granted to no one."

"...Depending how you take it, that's one hell of a masochistic thing to say. There's not a single time dying has ever felt good."

Echidna surely meant no harm, but her sheer curiosity wore on Subaru all the same.

"Death" was the result awaiting certain actions, and to Subaru, that result had only ever been proof that he'd failed to achieve his goal.

His revulsion toward "death" had never faded—that feeling hadn't changed since before he'd ever experienced it. Though he wouldn't deny that he'd grown somewhat accustomed to facing it by now.

At Subaru's remark, Echidna gave a small laugh, saying "How harsh a way to put it," before continuing.

"A state in which you can redo your own 'death.' No—rather, a power that denies you even the peace of 'death.' There's no doubt it belongs to Envy. When did you realize this?"

"Plenty of people have said things that sounded relevant, one after another, over time. Honestly, I don't have any memory of meeting this supposedly jealous witch face to face myself... but if her name keeps coming up at every turn, I can at least guess that the 'hand model' that shows up in certain scenes is her."

Both arms, and a black shadow whose overall silhouette had gradually become visible.

Whenever he spoke a forbidden word, it tormented Subaru even as it behaved with a contradictory sort of affection. Torment and love, together—to Subaru, it was a sadistic obsession without a single shred of anything to be grateful for.

"I've got no idea why someone I've never even met would be so fixated on me like that. ...Do you happen to know?"

"Hmm, who's to say. Understanding her mentality isn't something I—or anyone else, for that matter—seem capable of. And even if I could, I'd rather not."

"For someone who says she wants to know everything in this world, you're pretty harsh about the Witch of Envy. Well, I guess given the damage she's caused you, that's only natural."

Echidna seemed transcendent, and in fact, she existed at a height no mortal could reach.

Forming an entire world like this, continuing to exist unchanged even after death, the vast wealth of knowledge that let her answer Subaru's questions without hesitation—all of it was proof that she was a being beyond humanity, the Witch of Greed.

And yet even a being so transcendent couldn't escape feelings of like and dislike. To Subaru, that felt like an unresolvable knot he simply couldn't shake off.

"You seem to be overestimating me, but even witches started out human. We can't escape the emotions of joy, anger, sorrow, and delight, nor likes and dislikes of things, nor the strengths and weaknesses of those we deal with. Compared to the other witches, though, I'd like to think I have a considerably broader capacity for tolerance."

"Well, guess it makes sense—if someone who claims never-ending interest in others turned out to be picky about people, her curiosity wouldn't exactly get satisfied easily, huh."

"Just so. Which is why I can accept the great majority of beings. No matter how base or unremarkable a being might be, with nothing worth looking at, I make the effort to extend a certain understanding toward their mentality. In order to know everything, to be fulfilled. But—"

She cut herself off, and lowering her gaze slightly, her expression changed.

The witch who always wore a composed, unhurried smile, who never forgot to watch him with observing eyes—for the first time, Echidna's mouth was overcast with clear revulsion, and the anger that came with it.

"That Witch of Envy alone is the exception. Her alone I refuse to tolerate, refuse even to make the effort. I have never seen a being so utterly unforgivable, not across the span of my life and the time since my death combined, not even now, having glimpsed 'The World's Memory.'"

"...It's not just about being killed, is it. Not with anger like that."

"'Death' doesn't carry so great a meaning for me, all things considered—not when I consider that I still exist like this. But she did something to me that I find even harder to bear than that. I believe every action is an effort made toward obtaining some result... but even so, that is something I absolutely cannot accept."

Having vented her pent-up feelings completely, Echidna closed her eyes and shook her head.

With that single confession and gesture, every trace of intense emotion was blown away from her expression as if extinguished. What welled up afterward was the familiar smile of an observer.

"Let's set that witch's story aside. As for the principle behind your 'Return by Death,' speculation is possible, but there's no certainty. And even understanding it wouldn't have the slightest effect on your 'death' itself. Do you have any questions outside the matter of cause?"

"Outside the cause, huh..."

Having had the very root of his 'Return by Death' affirmed so readily left Subaru all the more troubled. Regardless of the intentions behind the source of this Authority dwelling within him, he was aware that he was using this power—or perhaps being made to use it—effectively, in exchange for pain.

Frustrating as it was, there were several results he never could have obtained without this power.

And it was likely one of the things he would have to keep relying on going forward. Taking that into account—

"—This power of mine, 'Return by Death'—do you think there's a limit on how many times I can use it?"

"Hmm... I see. Naturally, once you learn it's a power that lets you redo 'death,' that's the question you'd arrive at."

As far as Subaru could recall, he had already died sixteen times in this world.

Every one of those deaths had come with pain and a sense of loss, yet each time, having tasted that, Subaru had returned and redone the world.

And though he had gone on to seize results this way, there had always been a fear that accompanied the arrival of "death"—the natural feeling that this time, this might truly be the end.

"That's... a pretty obvious thing to worry about, huh."

He had already overturned, many times over, a "death" that should originally have been final, once and for all.

Precisely because this was a world he could never have carved a path through without that power, he couldn't help but imagine the helplessness he'd feel if that power were ever lost.

Until the very instant he returned by death, Subaru never knew whether this would be the end for him.

The despair he'd tasted so many times, of being crushed halfway to his goal—if everything simply snapped and vanished after that, once and for all, then just how terrifying a thing was "death"?

"Let me preface this by saying it's nothing more than my own speculation. I've formed a rough hypothesis about the principle behind your 'Return by Death,' so allow me to speak in line with that prediction, first of all."

"...Yeah, let's hear it."

"Your 'Return by Death.' I believe it operates under limited conditions, but as for the limit on its number of uses—"

Subaru held his breath.

He met Echidna's gaze head-on as she looked back at him, and that brief pause of hers felt to him like an eternity.

The conclusion he yearned for that badly—and then—

"—Probably, there is none."

"............"

"There is no end to your 'death.' No matter how many times you die, no matter how many times you rot away, each time your soul will go back through time, permitted to redo it all until you overturn the fate that brought about your 'death.' No matter how mercilessly you're killed, no matter how thoroughly your body and mind are broken."

Hearing the conclusion Echidna had reached, Subaru's mind went blank for a moment.

And once that blankness had washed his mind clean, what began to fill his now-empty head next was—

"—I see..."

The effort needed to control the trembling running through his entire body, as he quietly took in the fact he'd just been told.

Pressing a hand to his mouth, Subaru bowed his head, eyes closed.

He wasn't falling apart, nor was there any glimpse of relief at learning there was no limit to his 'Return by Death.'

Echidna, seeming to feel a faint puzzlement at this, knit her brow.

"You seem less surprised than I expected."

"............"

"For you—no, for anyone, really—overturning the absoluteness of 'death' should be an enormously significant thing. All the more so given how powerless you are, yet how forbidding the obstacles standing before you. I expected more of a reaction."

"Sorry my reaction's too weak to live up to your expectations... but."

The possibility of infinite retries—that had been one of the optimistic conclusions Subaru himself had already reached regarding 'Return by Death.' Which was why having it affirmed by someone else hadn't come as an absolute shock. It hadn't, but—

"Swallowing that and immediately jumping to some idiotic conclusion like 'no matter how many times I die it's fine, this other world life is a total cakewalk!'—that's not gonna happen."

"So you've arrived at the answer you'd predicted, and you're already looking past it. You seem to be a sharper thinker than I'd given you credit for. Whether that's a good thing or bad, I couldn't say."

"Every time I repeat 'Return by Death'... or more precisely, every time I try to confide in someone about it and feel the Witch's presence draw near, I can tell that black shadow is getting closer."

The first shadow Subaru had seen, when he spoke the forbidden words, had been a single arm reaching for his heart.

But every time he repeated that act, the shadow had gradually, gradually shifted its outline from faint to distinct, and now it sometimes even showed its entire body, vaguely.

What would happen if, going forward, that shadow's outline became even clearer?

"When that thing takes on a clear form, that's the limit of my 'Return by Death.' When that shadow becomes real, I don't think I'll be able to refuse it."

"Hmm, and your basis for that?"

"Gut feeling."

At those two short words, Echidna's eyes widened.

Subaru propped his chin on his hand on the table, looking up at her from below.

"It's a sense you can only understand if you've actually met that shadow. If there's anyone besides me who'd understand it..."

Only a madman, no longer among the living, who'd tasted the same suffering as Subaru and had his heart broken by it.

Subaru felt a sentimental ache well up in his chest. Echidna, watching him, narrowed her eyes as if peering into something deep within him, but then quickly shrugged.

"It's a bit maddening not to be able to understand that sense of yours, but broadly speaking, my own speculation supports it. Your 'Return by Death' has no limit. However, it operates under a limited condition."

"Limited... condition."

"What causes your 'Return by Death' is the Witch's obsession. Unless the source of that obsession is severed, you won't even be permitted to reach an end."

"The source of the obsession... meaning?"

"Either you kill the witch who's obsessed with you, or the witch loses interest in you and gives up on you... either way, watching you now, it seems to me the difficulty is about as extreme as it gets."

Killing the Witch of Envy—that had been the world's long-cherished, unfulfilled wish four hundred years ago, one that even the combined efforts of a Dragon, a Sage, and a Sword Saint had failed to achieve.

It meant he would have to surpass that past feat—one that hadn't even managed to kill, only seal away, the Witch of Envy who had once shrouded half the continent in shadow.

"Feels like it'd be a whole lot easier for her to just get sick of me..."

"Don't you already sense, on some level, that that's an absolute impossibility?"

"............"

"It's a feeling only you, who has actually encountered the Witch's shadow and dealt with it firsthand, could understand—but as someone who knew that thing from long ago, I can't help but think the same."

Echidna's expression turned bitter. He didn't know just how deep the enmity between her and the Witch of Envy ran, four hundred years ago, but it was clearly a rift that time had done nothing to soften—if anything, one that had deepened all the more, the longer it had festered.

Unable to fully deny her words, Subaru let out a breath through his nose, the back of his chair creaking as he leaned into it.

Just as Echidna said, no matter how he looked for it, Subaru simply couldn't find any hope that the Witch of Envy would ever let him go.

He'd never once imagined that someone he'd never even properly met, whose face he didn't even know, could love him this blindly. A being that, quite literally, had defiled the world to the point of consuming it whole.

"You accept that awfully readily, don't you."

"—Huh?"

"Not to put too fine a point on it, but everything I've said up to now is a mass of my own conjecture. I can't offer any proof, and I'd think that's far too little material to simply believe outright."

Echidna closed one eye, apparently unable to fathom why Subaru had accepted her opinion so readily.

True enough, Subaru thought as well.

His acquaintance with her was short, and above all, she was a "witch." Considering that, it would hardly be an exaggeration to say there wasn't a single element here that warranted trust.

And yet—

"That's a gut feeling too, I guess."

"...A gut feeling."

Subaru realized he had, without quite meaning to, come to vaguely believe that whatever content she spoke was something he could trust.

Perhaps it was because, over the course of their short conversation, whenever she disclosed some piece of knowledge she possessed, the reverence she showed toward that knowledge had convinced him she was someone worth believing.

Her thirst for knowledge sometimes went too far, coming off as tactless or prurient—he'd noticed those flaws more than once, but—

"If I just close my eyes to that, I get the feeling you don't lie. That's... how I feel, anyway."

"...Is that a gut feeling too?"

"Guess so. A gut feeling. Well, if you say a gut feeling from a guy who's died and come back sixteen times isn't worth much, fair enough, I can't argue with that."

Scratching his head as he said it, joking around, Subaru tried to lighten the mood with a bit of self-deprecating humor.

Hearing that, Echidna held her breath for a moment. Then she reached for the cup on the table, brought it to her lips, and moistened her tongue before speaking.

"You may have a talent for wooing witches."

And with that, for the first time, she carved a smile onto her lips unlike any she had shown before.
