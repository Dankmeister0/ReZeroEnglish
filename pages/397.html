Arc 5 Chapter 80: "Leaving Ripples on the Water's Surface"

"—All right, let's begin."

With her profile faintly tensed, Emilia let her clear, beautiful voice ring through the room. With that silvery-bell voice of hers, she spoke to everyone present—or perhaps was speaking to herself—and raised her slender arms.

"——"

Eyes closed, Emilia began gathering mana into her upraised hands. A vast whirl of magical power, and the extreme concentration needed to control it with precision. Lacking either would mean falling short of the goal—an attempt only she could make.

"——"

Countless gazes fell upon Emilia as she faced the great feat of magic with a serious expression. Holding their breath, watching her every move, were the women and children huddled close together. Some of them clasped each other's hands; others closed their eyes as if praying, as if wishing. All they shared with one another was anxiety and hope, and they trembled together.

"...This is rough."

And Subaru, standing at the edge of that same space, quietly watched over Emilia as she bore the weight of so many tangled emotions alone.

The place was a corner of an underground facility in the city of Priestella. Originally a warehouse for storing emergency supplies, now that most of its contents had been cleared out, it suited its true purpose once more. With nothing left in it, the stone-walled underground space felt all the more dim and cold for its emptiness. And yet, perhaps it was precisely because of that that it suited today's purpose.

"Not that I'd call it a good thing."

"Would you stop muttering sentimental things? It's not good for anyone to overhear, and it'll throw off Emilia's concentration, I suppose."

Beatrice's admonishment came in response to Subaru's unintended mutter, from right beside him. The girl, holding Subaru's left hand while toying with one of her ringlets with her free hand, stared fixedly at the white ritual unfolding before them. To Subaru, those pale blue eyes seemed, somehow, to be enduring some kind of pain.

"Emilia will be fine. Don't worry so much."

"...Aren't you misunderstanding something, I suppose? What Betty's worried about isn't Emilia—it's Subaru. Sympathizing with just anyone's feelings, indiscriminately, is a bad habit, I suppose."

"Is that right."

The grip on his hand tightened, and Subaru's mouth twisted into a frown at the girl's consideration. He understood what Beatrice meant to say, understood that she was worried for him. But his decision—made with full awareness of all that—was still his judgment now. On that point, he wouldn't bend. Even knowing it would only cause trouble for others.

"——"

Setting aside Subaru and Beatrice's quiet exchange, Emilia's ritual continued. Pouring in every ounce of concentration she could muster, Emilia breathed out white breath, a faint sheen of sweat rising on her brow. Body and mind alike, she poured everything into controlling that immense mana.

Around Emilia's hands, a pale blue-white light began faintly enveloping the underground chamber. The cold was thick enough to blur the air white, and yet its chill didn't sting the skin—it carried instead a softness, as if cradling something raw and exposed, a bared heart.

They say that people on the verge of death from hypothermia forget the cold. That extreme chill robs the body of its ability to properly sense temperature, offering warmth as a final farewell gift even as it steals away life. Perhaps something close to that existed in this white world, Subaru thought vaguely—before shaking his head, deciding the thought was merely tasteless.

The pale blue-white light filled the space, and the cold gathered toward the center of the room. And at the center of that light—

"——"

Curled up, wings folded, a great black form lay there—the Black Dragon. The strangeness didn't end there; crowding around the Black Dragon were flies as large as a person, swarming together, the whole scene calling to mind something like a nightmare.

But Subaru felt no revulsion at the sight. —No. To be precise, he consciously forced himself not to feel revulsion at the shapes of the Black Dragon and the human-flies.

They were victims—innocent people who had done nothing wrong. Victims of the malice of the Deadly Sin Archbishop of "Lust," people transformed into something inhuman. The means to restore those altered bodies to their original forms did not exist within the knowledge Subaru and the others currently possessed. That was precisely why this measure had been chosen instead.

"It might only buy time, though..."

"Having time, in itself, can be a kind of salvation, I suppose. When things press in close, one's field of view narrows, and options that should have been available never even occur to you. Not noticing that at all, or noticing it too late... either way is cruel, I suppose."

Beatrice answered Subaru's mutter as though speaking to herself. In that small, thin sigh there was a resignation and a sentimentality that only someone who had truly spent long, long stretches of time in contemplation could carry. Sensing that in the edge of her words, Subaru said nothing to Beatrice—only reached out silently and stroked her head.

"...What is it."

"Nothing."

No matter how much time one spends, it doesn't guarantee the right choice will be made. Sometimes, even after spending that time, one still fails to choose correctly. Even so, one can still act so that the choice made becomes the best one, after the fact.

That was the answer Subaru had arrived at, set against Beatrice's four hundred years. And he hoped the time granted to the tragedy that had struck this city would prove the same.

"——"

As that thought of Subaru's overlapped with the peak of the cold filling the underground chamber, at last a sound like cracking air rang out, and—

"...It's done. Everything's fine."

Breathing out white breath, Emilia turned around. Her breathing faintly ragged, she gave a small bow where she stood—and behind her lay lives, their entire bodies now sheathed in white crystal, their very souls sealed within the ice.

"—!"

Family members collapsed in tears; lovers let out choked sobs. Before gratitude could come, anguished wailing burst out first, echoing mercilessly through the underground chamber. Long, long, an endless parting from loved ones whose duration none could know—the grief seemed to have no limit, and it went on reverberating without end.

"Well, for now, it looks like Lady Emilia's plan worked—I suppose we can breathe a little easier, can't we?"

Having received both reports—on the discussion at the meeting hall, and on Emilia's subsequent freezing of the transformation victims—Otto nodded, looking relieved.

The place was a private room at the treatment house, having escaped the shelter and, well, at least been carried this far. His condition on the bed looked unchanged at a glance—the bandages wrapped around both legs were painful to look at. Still, having escaped the field-hospital-like conditions, and with both legs now suspended in traction, one could say things had improved considerably. Given his position as one of the people who'd contributed to the city's defense, Otto probably deserved somewhat better treatment, but since he himself never brought it up, Subaru figured he was probably being considerate of others and decided not to say anything about it.

"Hospitality that reads the room without needing words spelled out... that's the true essence of wabi-sabi."

"It's business as usual for Natsuki-san to be here in body but not in spirit, so that's fine, but... Lady Emilia, thank you for your hard work."

Ignoring the nodding Subaru, Otto offered his thanks to Emilia for her efforts. At his words of appreciation, her brows drooped, and she said,

"No, that's fine. More importantly, I'm sorry for going ahead with things without consulting you, Otto-kun. But I thought it was something only I could do."

"Ah, that's fine. I'm not angry, and what you did was without question a noble, good deed. Beyond that, it has great value even from a calculated standpoint."

"Calculated...?"

"It'd probably be better if you understood, but if you don't, that's fine too... no, actually, I'm not sure which is better. Honestly, that's a tough call for me to make."

"Don't think. Feel. That's E-M-T."

Emilia, seemingly not quite conscious of the implications of her own actions, left Otto holding his head in his hands—Subaru breezily brushed past it with his magic words and moved on, saying "More importantly—"

"Your leg. Still looking like it'll be a while, huh?"

"Given the current state of Priestella, further treatment is difficult. The number of healers in the city hasn't kept up with the sheer number of wounded. I've been thinking it might be better to transfer to a treatment house in another city, but apparently Kiritaka-san has already sent messengers to every nearby city he can, calling in healers. So it's probably wiser to just wait quietly here for outside healers to arrive, or head back to the mansion."

Laughing weakly, Otto resigned himself to being sidelined from the front lines for a while. An injury as deep as Otto's couldn't easily be healed except by a healer with quite advanced skill in healing magic. Someone on the level of Beatrice back in her library days, or Ferris.

"Ferris is stuck glued to Crusch-san's side, and our resident healing-type shock trooper is flying all over Priestella. ...Though I guess the reason for that is that family."

"The little brother and sister, and their mother, right? Since the one who looked like a dragon was their father, I think it was a family of four."

The shock trooper not present here—Garfiel, that is. He was probably off somewhere right now, flying around the manpower-starved city, throwing himself entirely into the recovery effort. Garfiel had always been a straightforward, kind-hearted boy at core. Even with no particular attachment to this city, he'd lend a hand without hesitation to anyone in trouble. Still, even accounting for that, his focus on Priestella was on another level entirely. And Subaru had a vague idea of why.

"If he's not telling us, then there's probably a lot of complicated stuff going on."

"Yeah, that's true. ...Oh, right, changing the subject—but doesn't Garfiel look a little similar to that family? Their hair color and eye color match up so neatly."

"Emilia-tan, that's not changing the subject at all, is it?"

"Eh!?"

Emilia's shock aside, that was the situation with Garfiel. Under normal circumstances, he himself should have been carrying damage across his whole body that could hardly be called light, but between the "Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits" and his bottomless stamina, he showed no sign of resting. On top of that, he had Mimi tagging along after him, reopening wounds and causing trouble for her brothers—it was quite the racket, to put it mildly.

"Well, Garfiel's real reasons will probably slip out on their own eventually. No need for us to go digging for them. More importantly..."

"Hm?"

"Ah, no—since neither of you two were bringing it up, I hadn't said anything either, but why is Beatrice-san sulking like that over there?"

Leaning forward, Otto directed the conversation toward the corner of the sickroom—where Beatrice sat with her cheeks puffed red, visibly displeased, shaking her head side to side. At that, Subaru nodded with an "Ah," and said,

"That's because—see, you sent her on an errand to the restoration artisan, and she got turned away at the door. She's sulking about that. ...Looked at from a few angles, isn't that kind of your fault?"

"Now hold on, I don't know about that... right, Lady Emilia?"

"Mm, that's right. Looking after your own spirit is a contractor's natural duty. So it's Subaru's job to soothe Beatrice, not anyone else's."

"You said 'soothe,' and now that you bring it up, I don't really have many memories of you looking after Puck, Emilia-tan."

"Don't nitpick! Besides, I did plenty of things you never saw, Subaru. Grooming him, polishing his claws, sleeping while holding him..."

Whether or not that was a good reference for how to properly care for a spirit was questionable, but Emilia's expression as she talked about Puck was bright and cheerful. Up until now, the sudden separation at the "Sanctuary" had stuck with her, and whenever she thought of Puck, her expression had carried a strong shade of melancholy—but that seemed to have passed now.

—At Emilia's chest hung a crystal ornament, fashioned from the colorless great demon-stone.

It shared the same design as the one she'd worn against her skin at all times before parting with Puck, and combined with the brightness in her expression, it felt like Emilia's true self had returned. She touched the crystal with her slender fingers and said,

"There's still not enough power right now for Puck to come back... but my contract with him isn't broken, so once enough mana builds up for him to manifest, I'll be able to see him again. Just a little more patience, that's all."

"That's thanks to Beako's efforts too and... well, Kiritaka's generosity, I guess."

The original reason Subaru and the others had come to the city of Priestella was to obtain the great demon-stone. In truth, it was supposed to end in negotiations over whether to hand it over or not, but instead it had turned into quite the roundabout deal. Still, in the end they'd actually gotten the thing, so all was well.

"So c'mon, Beako, cheer up already."

"Betty isn't sulking at all, I suppose. Subaru's mistaken. Hmph."

"Aw, Beatrice is so cute..."

Adding a clear sound effect for emphasis, Beatrice turned her face away from Subaru's attempts to win her over. Behind her, Emilia was swooning, and Subaru agreed—but being cute and being willing to talk things out were two entirely separate matters.

"Dartz-shi does seem like the artisan type. I understand not being able to half-finish a job once he's taken it on."

"But even so, that level of professional obsession seems a bit much, don't you think? The guy was apparently working in his workshop the whole time the chaos was going on. Way too much of a workaholic."

"That's what makes him a true artisan."

"That's what makes him a true artisan, huh."

Subaru wasn't entirely sure why Otto looked so proud of that statement, but somehow, once it was declared that flatly, it felt like it made sense—boys really are simple creatures. Artisan spirit is cool, after all. Still, at the two of them nodding in agreement, Beatrice shot them an irritated look and said,

"But even so, completely ignoring the client's words is unacceptable, I suppose. Betty said she'd pay double to get it back, and he wouldn't even give a proper answer, I suppose."

"A little girl waving a wad of cash at someone's cheek to get her way isn't going to work on anyone except a real connoisseur of that particular kink. Emilia-tan, back me up here."

"Right, you shouldn't think that way, Beatrice. If you go wasting money like that, I'll have to take away your allowance."

"How rude of the both of you, I suppose!"

Indignant, Beatrice grabbed the curtain and wrapped herself in it, hiding away. Then Emilia, unable to hold back, hugged the Beako-wrapped-in-curtain, and a shriek of "Giya, I suppose!" rang out.

Setting aside that heartwarming little interlude, Beatrice's feelings weren't hard to understand. What Otto had commissioned the restoration artisan Dartz to do, and what Subaru and the others were trying to recover, was the damaged Book of Wisdom. Given that its owner, Roswaal, had gone so far as to try to obstruct the future ahead of Subaru, there was naturally great interest in what its contents actually were.

"Even without the obstruction anymore, that guy's still just as evasive as ever."

Even with the sabotage exposed, Roswaal's outward manner remained unchanged from before. Of course, given how much scheming had gone on behind that easygoing façade, vigilance was never let down—but it was also true that some of the venom seemed to have drained out of him. Even so, he remained a bystander of sorts, hardly what one would call cooperative.

"If we could at least see what's inside that Book of Wisdom..."

If it could be confirmed that Roswaal wasn't plotting anything—that regardless of what had happened before, it should be fine to walk the road ahead together—that certainty would likely have a good effect on how the group operated going forward.

"That's what I'd like to argue, at least."

"You don't need to defend yourself so earnestly—both Lady Emilia and I mostly agree with you already, Natsuki-san, so it's fine. Though Garfiel alone might... well, he's got a personal grudge, so even once the facts are clear, his attitude might not change."

Whether that personal grudge was about the "Sanctuary," or about Ram, wasn't something Otto touched on. Without pressing the matter, Subaru watched Emilia and Beatrice horsing around, and said,

"That book isn't something Beatrice can treat as somebody else's problem, either. I think she does want to confirm it, if she can. Being able to bring her out of the library is one thing, and being at peace with the past is another matter entirely."

"I did think about consulting you all several times, you know."

"I'm not blaming you."

Recovering the Book of Wisdom, trying to have it restored, and trying to see the whole thing through by himself—all of it was, in Otto's own way, a decision made out of good intentions. And fundamentally, Otto's judgment rarely missed the mark. Subaru knew full well he wasn't someone who acted out of self-interest.

"You really aren't cut out to be a merchant..."

"Would you please leave me alone!? More importantly, what did Dartz-shi say?"

"Says it might be the biggest job he's ever taken on. Said the fee can stay the same, just wants to be allowed to see it through properly to the end."

The lack of a set deadline was a little worrying, but the man was a craftsman—Subaru figured it wasn't his place to push. He just hoped the man wasn't the type to keep dragging his feet even past a deadline.

"So then, in the end, with the matter of recovering the Book of Wisdom still hanging over us, I suppose it's pretty much settled that I'll be staying in Priestella."

"Planning to keep Garfiel here for a while too, for recovery work and city defense. We've more or less agreed that we drove them off for now, but with those bastards, there's no telling if that was just a feint before another attack."

They were the sort of people who'd gleefully pile malice on malice, again and again. And it seemed Subaru wasn't the only one aware of that—everyone involved remained on guard. Forcing them to endure needless tension and suffering might well be part of the enemy's aim too.

"Once you start thinking like that, there's nothing to be done about it anymore."

"Either way, I think it's necessary to keep watching how things develop. Once my leg's in better shape, I'll go around looking into things myself. Just..."

While discussing their plans going forward, Otto suddenly cut himself off. He forced himself to sit up from the bed and looked up at Subaru, who had one eye closed. Then, tapping his temple with a finger, he said,

"I'll say it plainly—I'm going to object."

"...Well, figured you would."

At Otto's flat declaration, Subaru gave a wry smile. That he'd say that and object was something Subaru had fully anticipated.

After all, Otto Suwen assessed Natsuki Subaru accurately. Subaru himself was the one who felt his own powerlessness most keenly, but there weren't that many people who properly understood just how lacking Subaru could be. At most, Beatrice and Otto. Patrasche too, perhaps. Or, though he wasn't in a position to think about it right now, maybe Ferris as well.

Precisely because of that, Subaru had expected opposition from Beatrice and Otto, both in the same camp. If Patrasche could speak words, he thought she'd probably object in just the same way. Still,

"If you understand me that well, then you should already know my answer too."

"...Beatrice-san's bad mood isn't really just about Dartz-shi, is it?"

"Hmm, who knows. Even I can't really see all the way down to the bottom of Beako's heart."

Shrugging as he bluffed his way through it, Subaru saw Otto give him an exasperated look. Naturally, sharp-eared as he was, Otto surely lacked nothing in his knowledge of legends and rumors. He undoubtedly understood full well the danger of the choice Subaru was making. Knowing all that, Subaru prefaced his next words to Otto with "Sorry about this," and said,

"Gonna go meet this so-called Sage, with the white fox as my guide."

And with that, he smiled.

"—Come in."

Knocking on the door as a matter of basic courtesy, Subaru heard a quiet voice answer from within. It was a voice he knew well, but one lacking its usual vigor, and for some reason that irritated him no end.

"It's you, Subaru."

"Something wrong with that?"

"Strangely enough, seeing your face right now brings me a great deal of relief."

"Kaah, ptooey."

Stepping into the room, Subaru capped off their opening exchange of insults with just such a theatrical gesture. Even while putting on that attitude, the hand with which he closed the door behind him was considerate. Closing it without a sound was the bare minimum courtesy owed to those sleeping within.

"If making noise would wake them, that would be a mercy by far."

"If that were true, would you put on some kind of one-man round of applause and cheering to try it? Now that'd be a rare sight. Makes me even more furious at 'Gluttony' for making me miss out on it."

"Heh."

At that breathy laugh, Subaru tilted his head without meeting the other's eyes. Then he glanced idly around the room, narrowing his eyes at the row upon row of beds lined up. Simple beds, thin towel-blankets—that was all the care given to those who slept there. And Subaru knew that nothing more than that was needed, either.

The people sleeping here had been forgotten by memory, cut off from ordinary life, left to remain as incomplete existences that were simply not-yet-dead.

"Julius. Not that it's my place to say it, but you shouldn't be here so much."

"——"

"Staring won't bring back what you can't remember. Not even for your beloved sister... not even for someone who's practically your other half. It's the same either way."

Without resorting to easy words of comfort, Subaru called out to the young man—Julius. Seated at the edge of the row of beds, at the bedside of the last one, he lifted his face, his refined features unable to fully conceal the melancholy within.

"There's a great difference between knowing something as knowledge, and knowing it as felt reality. I wasn't being conceited, but until now I never thought of myself as someone who lived too much in his own head. That I couldn't realize it until it came to this—it's beyond reproach."

"——"

As he spoke, Julius looked down at the bed right beside him. Naturally, there too lay a victim of the Gluttony's hunger who had lost his "name," his consciousness and memories severed from the world. And so Julius Juukulius could not recall that this person—a slender young man with long purple hair—was his own younger brother, Joshua Juukulius.

"Joshua, huh."

The reason he could even say his brother's name at all was because Subaru had told him the relationship, told him the name. The moment it was reported that numerous unidentified people, unconscious and unresponsive, had been found—victims of the Authority of "Gluttony"—Subaru had been certain that people had suffered the same fate as Rem. And he'd thought that he, of all people, might be able to remember those whom everyone else had forgotten. Clinging to that faint hope alone, he'd visited the sickrooms and found Joshua asleep there.

"It's a strange thing. Even having been told by you, and even with enough shared traits to judge with certainty that he is my blood kin, there's not a single fragment of my brother left in my memory."

Keeping his emotions off his face, Julius closed his eyes. Among Gluttony's victims, the only acquaintance found was Joshua alone. The remaining thirty-some victims had nothing to cling to even in Subaru's memory—no one worried after their safety, no one grieved for them, and they simply kept on sleeping.

Considered that way, perhaps Joshua, worried over by his brother, was the fortunate one. Even in circumstances where the brother he'd once adored so dearly had forgotten him entirely, and that same brother now came to the sickroom clinging to nothing but the mere form of brotherly affection, calling out to a sibling who felt like a stranger.

Being forgotten, forgetting, being absent from memory, existing only as a bare fact—either way, it was nothing but painful.

"...Damn it all."

He'd known it already. He'd understood it all along. That the Authority of the Deadly Sin Archbishop of "Gluttony" was the most despicable sin in this world.

So too was "Wrath," which bent emotions to its own will at whim. So too was "Lust," which shattered human dignity, form and all, and trampled it underfoot. So too was "Greed," which denied everything but the self, forcing its own self-satisfied omnipotence onto others. So too was "Sloth," which used the single word "diligence" as an excuse to paint over the lives of others with its own self-serving love.

Not one of them was anything but the worst kind of malice, unworthy of life, without exception. But could there possibly be any other existence that desecrated every form of life the way "Gluttony" did?

"—Staying here's only going to eat away at you. Don't make me say it again."

Nothing but unpleasant thoughts kept flickering through his mind. Riding that irritation on his tongue, Subaru called out to Julius. At those words, Julius rose to his feet, and reaching out to touch the thin chest of the brother he no longer recognized, he said,

"He's breathing. He's alive. It's a strange thing."

"That's how it is. But he doesn't eat, doesn't need the bathroom, doesn't need a bath either. ...Doesn't laugh, either."

"Nor does he grieve at being forgotten. —Perhaps that, at least, is a mercy."

"A mercy...?"

At the words Julius let slip, Subaru raised an eyebrow in response. Turning back, Julius let the corner of his mouth ease slightly, a faint, weak smile rising, and said,

"If he doesn't realize he's been forgotten, then he doesn't have to fear being left behind either. Having people who should be close to you sever the relationship unilaterally... that was quite hard to bear."

"——"

"Subaru. Being forgotten, and forgetting... which do you think is more painful?"

"How would I..."

At that question, Subaru's throat caught. It wasn't that he was stuck for an answer. The answer had already formed in an instant. So what cut off Subaru's words wasn't confusion. It was fury. Subaru glared at Julius, who wore a cynical smile.

"How the hell would I know that? Cut the crap, don't go wallowing in it."

"...Subaru?"

"Forgetting, being forgotten—to hell with both of 'em! Don't you dare try to rank which pain hurts more, that's about as backward-looking as it gets! Making that face like you're the unluckiest person in the world. Wanna compare misfortunes with me? I'll win, hands down!"

"——"

At Subaru's sudden transformation—pointing a finger, raising his voice—Julius was struck speechless. Eyes wide, he could say nothing in the face of Subaru's abrupt fury. Watching Julius fall silent like that, Subaru lowered his pointed finger, his shoulders rising and falling, and said,

"Don't you dare make that weak face. I know it's hard on you, I know you feel like you've got nowhere to belong now that you've been forgotten... but I won't stand for you looking weak."

"——"

"Did you forget, Julius? —No. Don't forget, Julius."

Biting his lip, brimming with raw emotion, Subaru glared at Julius. Placing a hand over his own chest, he declared it flatly, just as he had once before.

"My eyes know your strength. My own shame knows it. No matter who else forgets it."

"——"

His breath came ragged; the sensation of blood rushing to his head hadn't faded. Truly, how long had it been since he'd gotten this worked up? Not since Regulus. He was stunned to realize barely half a day had passed since then. Just how much strain was this whole Priestella incident going to put on his heart and lungs? And then—

"Hah, hahaha..."

"What?"

"Haha... no, you really are an outrageous man. Realizing that all over again..."

The astonished expression that had been on his face vanished, and Julius suddenly doubled over, bursting into laughter. Overcome by the urge to laugh, he kept laughing in front of a displeased Subaru. Then, as the impulse gradually subsided, Julius let out a long breath and said,

"I see. Yes, that's right. It seems I haven't been left behind by everything after all."

"It's not so much 'left behind' as... you're still ahead of me by like three horse-lengths."

"Would three horse-lengths be enough?"

"I'll knock your teeth in! Me and Beako, together, as a pair—we're way, way ahead of that!"

Raising his middle finger, Subaru spat at Julius, who was starting to regain his usual composure. Julius elegantly dodged the flying spittle and, with a small bow, said "I see," and,

"Then allow me to look forward to that grand boast of yours."

"...Yeah, you do that. And you'd better pull off something impressive enough that everyone's shocked, once they get their memories back too."

At the affected, dramatic manner, Subaru retaliated by flipping his raised thumb downward this time in provocation. At that crude gesture, the man Subaru alone knew to be the "Sword Saint"—no, the "Finest Knight"—laughed gracefully, and said,

"—Then first, let me make it my effort to astonish you—the one who still remembers—more than anyone else."

So saying, he firmed his resolve to accompany the group to the "Pleiades Watchtower" that awaited them.
