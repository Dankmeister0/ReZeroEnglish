Arc 5 Chapter 71: "Sword Demon VS Former Sword Saint"

The battles raging across the city were, little by little, drawing to a close.

The damage in each place that had become a battlefield was severe, and there were no small number of sites that had taken blows severe enough to cripple the city's very functions. That fact alone was proof of the scale of what had struck the city of Priestella — proof, one might say, of the sheer menace of the Witch Cult that had brought its malice down upon this place.

And yet amid this ongoing storm of destruction, there was one battlefield of an entirely different character.

Perhaps it wasn't even fitting to call it a battlefield at all.

There, nothing existed but the ceaseless ring of clashing blades, twin gleams of white steel seeking nothing but each other's lives. Every needless thing had been pared away, until only the barest, truest desire remained — two swordsmen pointing their blades at the one thing they each truly wanted, in a clumsy, halting courtship all their own.

"——"

The flashing sword-light still caught and threw back the moonlight, and the two swordsmen went on exchanging love through steel. The sharp ring, the scattering sparks, white hair and red hair dancing beneath the moon. Both drew the eye, both ensnared the heart, a sword dance so beautiful and refined it might have drawn a gasp even from the God of the Sword.

From the flowing, water-parting grace of the longsword came a ferocity of force impossible to imagine. The twin blades that batted it aside and spun with the wind struck back like thunderbolts breaking through.

"——"

As if performing some fixed, rehearsed dance together, the two blades kept meeting, again and again. Wilhelm took head-on the sword strokes of Theresia — beautiful still, unchanged from the days of her youth — before him, answered with strokes of his own driven by the same momentum, and felt, in the shock against his palms, only grief.

Welling up from the depths of his body was the acclaim of a heart that, for all the years it had aged, remained unripened still.

—It was boiling over. —It was rejoicing. —It was blossoming with joy.

Let it be said plainly, without evasion. The sword demon Wilhelm, calling back the days now past, yearned — with a heart nearly burned to ash by the yearning — for this moment, crossing blades with his wife in her youth. So completely did the wish occupy his mind, that if only this swordplay, this stolen tryst, might never end, he felt he could cast away everything else for it.

"But——"

Such selfish desire was a blasphemy he had no right even to hold.

It was a blasphemy against the endless days the sword demon Wilhelm had devoted to the sword. A blasphemy against the vow he had made when he defeated Theresia, once the Sword Saint, and took that title from her. A blasphemy against his loyalty as a swordsman to the liege lord to whom he owed so profound a debt.

The love that burned in Wilhelm van Astrea's breast, if he let himself follow it blindly, would be a blasphemy against the entire world living in this very moment.

—And so, the reckoning must not be put off. —However pleasant a paradise this stolen hour might be for a sword demon.

"——" "Aaaaaaah!!"

A silent storm of sword-light bore down like a tempest, and he met it with a barrage of strokes of his own.

Her long crimson hair streaming, her white robes flaring, Theresia's movements held no hesitation at all. Like a leaf riding the current, from within that utterly natural bearing, deadly strokes were loosed.

Above, below, left, right — the lethal blows chose no fixed angle. And yet, as he traded blow after blow, Wilhelm felt a faint sense of wrongness. And in the very shock of impact, he found something like understanding of that wrongness as well.

Theresia van Astrea's skill as a swordsman was without peer. Even Wilhelm, in the full bloom of his physical prime back then, would never have matched her in a pure contest of swordsmanship — of that there was no doubt.

Now, in the blade of this silent Theresia facing him, that same skill of old was certainly present still. The art of the Sword Saint, that showed no mercy to its enemies, and brought even those it protected a sense of relief edged with rapture.

—And yet there was one decisive difference between the woman before him now and the woman she had once been.

"—Light."

The twin swords and the longsword clashed head-on, and amid the grinding sparks, the sword demon let the word slip out. Locked in the pressing struggle of their guards, Wilhelm glared past the blade at the blue eyes beyond it.

"There's no comparison — you've grown light, Theresia. Set down the burden you carried, and is this how light your sword becomes?"

"——"

At those words, tinged with something almost like disappointment, her beautiful face did not so much as twitch an eyebrow. Theresia only gazed back at Wilhelm, her clear blue eyes lit with nothing but emptiness. No rebuttal there, no resentment, no hostility.

She had been a woman who laughed often, raged often, sulked often.

Silent, she was beautiful as a blade — but she was almost never silent. She had been a woman like some great flower blooming beneath the sun.

—And now, that absence was simply, purely sorrowful.

"——"

Only the silent husk of his wife stood there. Every time he crossed blades with the form he had loved so long, Wilhelm's heart was thrown into a thousand pieces.

It leapt as if returned to days gone by; it wavered, understanding it could never return to those days; it drowsed in a fleeting, foolish dream, unable to let those days go.

—Fifteen years. What kind of days had Theresia spent, in all that time?

Thinking of the years he'd lost her, the years he had spent seeking vengeance for her, the wound on Wilhelm's shoulder — the one that never healed — asserted itself once more.

The wound the Divine Protection of the Death God had dealt would never fade.

That was a Divine Protection separate from the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint — one granted to Theresia by the sword god, one might almost call it a heavenly blessing meant to bring an end to that age of endless war.

A single cut could summon a river of blood; one brilliant stroke could raise a mountain of corpses. And so, to sever the thread of a life, no cunning trick, no cleverness of any kind, was ever needed.

To surpass Theresia, there had been no way but to attain swordsmanship beyond even hers. The Wilhelm of those days had pared himself down to the utmost limit, and through an ordeal severe enough to turn himself into a sword itself, he had reached it, and achieved it.

Under the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint, with her talent for the blade drawn out to its absolute limit, there had been no other way to defeat Theresia. And now, crossing blades with her in her youth, Wilhelm understood.

—Her skill with the sword was still peerless, still at the height of mastery. And yet, there was a great shadow fallen across that swordsmanship's force.

"You agonized before taking up the sword, but once you'd taken it up, you never hesitated again. You were a woman who understood so much more than I ever did."

"——"

"Do you remember our parting? At the time of the Great Conquest, you shook off my hand as I tried to stop you, and you carved this wound into my shoulder, the one that never healed. — I have not forgotten a single word you said to me that day."

There was no answer. He hadn't sought one. This was only a ritual, one Wilhelm performed alone, in remembrance of that day.

Along with the pain in his shoulder, the carved memory rose up again. As she set out on the Great Conquest, on a journey from which she might never return, Theresia had literally pushed Wilhelm away, and said:

—When I come back, let me hear the words I couldn't hear that day.

"I've come to keep that promise from that day——!"

The twin swords howled, and Theresia's longsword was knocked aside. The former Sword Saint used even that recoil to swing her blade, but Wilhelm didn't so much as watch the counterstroke — he read its path perfectly and evaded it.

He understood. Where the blade would come — he understood it, almost lovingly well.

"Raaaaagh!"

The same habits. The same techniques. The swordsmanship of the Sword Saint that he had once pictured in his mind, chased after, through an ordeal that shaved away at his very soul. Swearing to overcome it, to steal it for his own, yearning to reach that realm, yearning, yearning until his soul itself burned.

It was the same as the figure of her that had once set this heart ablaze.

"——"

Even at Wilhelm's plea, her crimson, beautiful face did not waver in the slightest. Silent, wordless, emotionless sword-light streaked out, and Wilhelm shot down every last stroke of it.

He had loved her so much he could have known her blows with his eyes closed. And so, precisely because of that, he would not close his eyes — he would watch, and love her fully, to the last.

"—!"

—High, then reversed, a thrust, an upward sweep, a diagonal cut.

He caught the falling blade, let the reversing stroke slide past, dodged the loosed thrust, spun his body from the upward-sweeping point, hooked the diagonal cut on his raised twin swords, and turned it into a counterattack.

His sheer number of strokes overwhelmed her flowing defense, and a strain crept into Theresia's speed. Unable to bear it, Theresia fell back, and in that opening, Wilhelm did not hesitate to close the distance.

"——"

For one instant, something like emotion seemed to flicker across the eyes with which Theresia looked at the sword demon. No — an illusion. Just this weak, foolish heart of his, dredging up a memory from another time, when the exact same situation had occurred.

—Before the eyes of countless citizens, Wilhelm had defeated the "Sword Saint," the star of that ceremony, and stolen the girl named Theresia away from the sword god.

The exact same situation as back then, playing out again. Then surely, the ending would be the same too.

"Theresia——!!"

The longsword rose to meet Wilhelm as he closed in. The sword demon caught it up with his twin blades, and the strain cracked the longsword's edge — but at the same instant, it was knocked straight upward, and Theresia's body opened wide, exposed.

Drawing a great half-circle, Wilhelm's twin swords swept back around. This was the greatest opening Theresia had shown since this stolen tryst began, and it lay open before him now. The muscles of his arms swelled, his grip tightening until the sword hilts groaned.

And with everything he had, with a single all-consuming stroke, he meant to end this reunion that never should have been —

—end it, he meant to.

"—!"

A surge of emotion welled up and choked his throat, and in his wide-open eyes, a dozen faces seemed to rise up before him. A weeping face, an angry face, a sulking face, a smiling face — all of them the same beloved woman's face.

Shaking off every one of them, Wilhelm brought his blade down. The stroke ran true, straight for the woman's neck and torso —

"——"

An instant before the blade struck home, a figure flickered at the edge of Wilhelm's vision.

In the midst of that ultimate concentration, it was a wavering of awareness that should never have happened. But it was, in the end, only that — nothing that should have any effect, nothing but a thing to be ignored. In a swordsman's life-or-death struggle, fought with everything on the line, there should have been no room for anyone else to intrude. He should have poured everything into the presence before him, and struck home with a blow worthy of a sword demon.

That was what he should have done. That was what should have been possible.

—If only the figure that had flickered there had been a stranger, nothing more.

"—...Old man?"

There was distance between them. It was not a distance from which a whispered question should have been able to reach Wilhelm at all. And yet the voice reached him, clear, as if spoken right at his ear.

A red-haired man with blue eyes, staring this way. Heinkel Astrea was watching the final moment of this fight.

Watching, dumbstruck, the life-or-death duel between his own father Wilhelm and his mother Theresia — watching for the outcome of that mutual slaughter.

—In that instant, the sword-light dulled.

"—"

It should have been the decisive stroke. A blow more than sufficient to tip the balance of this fight, to bring this long, dreamlike hour to its end — and that single stroke dulled, and room for a counter opened up.

"——"

Theresia arched her body back sharply, and with a flick of her wrist, the returning longsword knocked the twin swords aside. The ring of clashing steel sounded out, and the stroke that had promised a fatal blow degraded into a sloppy strike, mind and technique and body all out of alignment, scattering sparks as it strayed from its mark.

"Ngh...!"

—Why. Why had he noticed.

Catching the returning stroke, absorbing its full weight with everything he had, Wilhelm confronted the doubt rising in his chest head-on. If he hadn't noticed Heinkel's presence — if he could have ignored that presence, if he could have stayed wholly focused on Theresia — he would never have exposed such an unsightly failure just now.

He should have resolved, with his entire life on the line, to steal Theresia back from the sword god. And was the result of that grand, solemn resolve to be this — this present state of himself?

Once more, the light ring of clashing blades began to chain together. But the clear, transparent clang of steel from before, that sword-dance, was already lost. Impurities had mixed in.

The illusion — that they had been, with all their strength, refining the purity of their steel with each exchange, that they were nothing but two blades and nothing more — was now lost, gone far beyond reach. What remained was only a single aged sword demon, cutting down the wife he loved before the eyes of his son.

Unable to become the sword fully, unable even to remain wholly a sword demon, as father, as husband, as swordsman, as man — inadequate, insufficient in every way. He had come to recognize, at last, his own immaturity, in that he could not commit fully to even one of those roles. He had failed to stop the impurity from creeping into the sword-intent he should have poured wholly into his blade.

And so, perhaps, this outcome had been inevitable.

"—!?"

He knocked aside the two strokes, and a following impact shook the straight sword in his grip. Meeting the force of the blow head-on like a fool, a contest of raw strength arose between him and the now-stationary Theresia — and the instant he stepped in to bear her down, her slender body spun before his eyes, and a gap opened.

His foot stepped forward; a half-step opening was born.

"——"

Immediately after, a sense of death closed in from behind. A stroke of overwhelming force, swung in a straight horizontal line, was caught, without a breath's delay, by the sword he swept behind him.

He couldn't fully absorb the piercing shock, and the flat of the blade he'd caught with bit deep into his own shoulder. He staggered, and as his body tipped forward, blood sprayed. Bone groaned, and the tearing of muscle sent lightning roaring through his skull.

He had caught it with his right blade. His left blade remained. Blood spilling from the corner of his mouth, Wilhelm used the right blade to hoist Theresia's longsword back upward once more.

True to his aim, Theresia's longsword was thrown up overhead. At the same moment, the sword slipped clean out of Wilhelm's right hand. No matter. If his right hand was empty, then all that remained was to pour every ounce of his being into the left blade, and strike.

He drove the left stroke into Theresia's exposed back. Tracing a clockwise arc, the fierce cut drove straight for her —

"——"

Sparks flew. And a high, ringing sound echoed out.

The weight of steel in his hand halved, and Wilhelm was made to face, once again, his own failure — the weakness in himself that he should have already recognized, over and over, throughout this fight.

At the instant he drove that blow into Theresia, Wilhelm had, unconsciously, made a choice. Whether to loose the blade in his left hand counterclockwise, or clockwise.

A trivial difference. The smallest, most negligible thing. And yet, at the same time, for two who had reached the very pinnacle of swordsmanship, it was a difference fatal enough to decide everything.

Speed, choosing the left. Or power, choosing the right.

If he had simply hesitated between the two choices, and erred in the end — there would have still been some salvation in that.

What Wilhelm had actually hesitated over, in that split second, was whether or not to look at Theresia face to face.

"——"

What had taken the sword demon's blow was a blade that should already have left Wilhelm's hand. Theresia had caught it, still in mid-air, and thrust it into the path of that stroke. The instant the blades caught and locked together, the longsword swept down with tremendous force. It passed along the flat of Wilhelm's blade, and severed the steel without the slightest resistance.

The longsword's stroke split his sword in two, and Wilhelm understood, in that instant, that he had lost his weapon. That he instantly gripped the broken hilt, bracing for the next blow, was the work of a swordsman's instinct alone.

But even that resolve could only bear fruit where there was no vast, unbridgeable gap in purity as a swordsman between the two combatants. And in that sense, the woman before him now was the worst possible opponent.

A sword demon without a sword, and the "Sword Saint" beloved of the sword god. The gap between them was plain, needing no words at all.

—In an instant where he forgot even to blink, Wilhelm watched the longsword pierce clean through his own right leg.

"——"

It was a sword strike beautiful enough to enrapture.

The blade pierced clean through the base of the old swordsman's right leg, staining its edge with the barest minimum of blood. Without unnecessary destruction, threading the gaps between muscle fiber and nerve, it was a masterstroke of technique so refined it robbed the leg of function and nothing more.

A sensation as effortless as sinking a blade into water. Having that demonstrated on his own right leg sent a shudder down Wilhelm's spine.

Whether that sensation belonged to admiration, or vexation, or love, even he himself could not say. All he knew for certain was the reality now thrust upon him: defeat.

"Guh... hh..."

The blade still buried in his right leg slid free, and his knee split vertically. Just as when the longsword had entered, it was drawn from the flesh without a sound, and the pain, arriving late, made Wilhelm cry out and collapse.

Blood poured from the wound in his leg, and strength drained from his lower body.

Once the power of the Divine Protection of the Death God was invoked, no healing magic of any kind could mend the wound. The closer one stood to the one who bore the Divine Protection, the greater its effect grew, turning even the slightest wound into a curse that ate away at life, forcing the enemy to bleed on and on without end.

"——"

The wound in Wilhelm's right leg was no shallow scratch one could call trivial. Left untreated, it was a deep wound that threatened his life, and the Divine Protection of the Death God forcibly refused any recovery.

His remaining span of life, one had to conclude, had been set terribly short.

"...How mortifying."

Even as pain seared through his mind, what escaped him first was not a groan of agony, but a sigh. His sense of pain screamed in ceaseless, violent protest, yet Wilhelm himself let none of it show on his face beyond the faintest furrow of his brow.

It wasn't false bravado, nor was he simply gritting his teeth out of pride. No matter how sharp the pain to his flesh, it could never outweigh the darkness now blanketing his heart.

When disappointment, despondency, and disgust at his own inadequacy scorched his very soul, what meaning could mere physical pain hold for this old swordsman?

"——"

Letting the sword in his hand fall from his grip, Wilhelm pressed a hand to the wound. Bleeding was the outflow of life itself, and as the loser here, he had no intention of shamefully turning from that fact. But as a matter of simple decency, he could not accept an end as undignified as death by blood loss.

He had fought as a swordsman, resisted as a swordsman, and now, as a swordsman, he had lost. Then the life of the vanquished ought, rightly, to be claimed by the victor's blade.

"Theresia, I..."

"——"

The crimson swordswoman, bearing her longsword, looked down at Wilhelm. In her eyes, still, there was no feeling of any kind. To the very end she would remember nothing, feel nothing — she was a death god of the sword, come to reap Wilhelm's life.

He looked up at that face, beautiful enough to enrapture. Theresia raised her sword quietly before Wilhelm. When that blade fell, Wilhelm's life, too, would come to its end.

But——

"Not alone. Never...!"

At the instant the longsword began to fall, Wilhelm thrust out his right hand. There, fallen, lay one half of the pair of twin swords — the blade Theresia herself had discarded. Wilhelm caught it up with his fingertips, struggling to the very last, unwilling to give up even now.

Defeat — that was fine. That, he could accept. But he could not leave Theresia here, could not go into death alone and leave her behind.

He could not, with his own hand, stop his wife from being forced to wield a sword against her will — and he could not let her advance upon Crusch and Subaru and the others, to whom he owed so profound a debt. If burning through this life of his was not enough to stop it, then let his very soul be destroyed after death; he did not care.

—And yet, that resolute final stroke —

"——"

"Theresia...?"

Sword still raised, Theresia had leapt back, far. Out of reach even of the thrust of the blade gripped in her right hand. Out of reach of the leg-wounded Wilhelm, Theresia tilted her head, ever so slightly.

In those emotionless eyes, seeing a dreadfully hollow color there, Wilhelm felt fear for the first time. It was a fear that appealed directly to instinct — to Wilhelm's instincts as a swordsman.

That there was no need to force a killing blow upon prey already dealt a fatal wound. It was the judgment handed down by a cold, pitiless death god alone — one who had long since abandoned any pride as a swordsman.

"Wait... wait, Theresia!!"

At the terror of being left behind, Wilhelm screamed. His leg no longer hurt at all. Forgetting the pain in his right leg, Wilhelm tried to drag himself after the retreating Theresia. But though the pain was gone, the wound was real. His strength failed him, and he fell. Even as he struck his shoulder hard against the ground, the old swordsman raised his head — could such a thing be permitted?

Her long red hair swaying, Theresia moved away, farther and farther. And in the direction her feet were carrying her stood Heinkel, frozen in place.

Her longsword, its will to fight not the least bit diminished, had set its sights on its next quarry. Having cut down a man she did not recognize as her husband, she would now cut down a man she did not recognize as her son — for that purpose —

"Stop, Theresia! Do you think... do you think a thing like that could ever be forgiven!? Fight me! Look at... look at me! Look at me, look at me, Theresiaaaaa!!"

His voice raw enough to draw blood, Wilhelm called Theresia's name. Over and over, again and again — the name he had longed, so many times, to call out before her — now in a form utterly unlike anything he had ever imagined, filled not with love but with rage, not with passion but with madness.

But the woman did not turn back. Bearing the blade that housed a death god, she drifted, slow and steady, toward Heinkel. Heinkel gasped at her approaching form, and drew his own knight's sword with a trembling hand.

"W-wait, wait a second. Y-you're... Theresia, this is a joke, right? There's no way... there's no way you're my old lady...!"

"——"

"No, even if you're not my mom... that's not it! Dad, look at him, he's... shit! What the hell! What the hell is going on, what are you doing!"

Bearing down before his eyes was Theresia in the bloom of her youth. That figure and the image of his mother in Heinkel's mind refused to align. He shook his head from side to side in denial, spilling out disjointed words in a desperate attempt to reject the scene before him.

His knees shook, his gaze unsteady, his grip on the sword weak. Facing down the former "Sword Saint" like that, he wouldn't last a single exchange.

At this rate, Heinkel would, without question, be cut down by Theresia.

That alone — that could not be allowed to happen.

"Theresia! Over here! I'm still alive! If you're going to kill someone, kill me first! Heinkel, you can't win this — run, now!!"

Bracing on his sword, Wilhelm forced himself up with the desperation of a man clawing at stone. There was no leeway left to press the wound in his leg, and the strain sent more blood spraying out. The cobblestones, already dyed crimson with spilled blood, trailed a thread of it behind him as Wilhelm chased after Theresia's back.

Too far. Far too far. Too slow. Far too slow.

Once again, Wilhelm would not make it in time. Once again, Wilhelm could not reach.

"Hii...!"

"——"

Theresia's longsword traced an arc, and Heinkel's knight's sword, raised with hunched shoulders, met it. Without the slightest pause, the knight's sword was torn from Heinkel's grip as if it were nothing, and clattered away across the cobblestones with a high, ringing sound.

"S-stop... stop it, please, M-mom...!"

Disarmed, the terrified Heinkel fell backward onto the ground. He scrabbled desperately with his hands and feet, trying to crawl away. But his trembling fingers, his frightened heart, and Theresia's emotionless eyes bound Heinkel's mind and body fast with fear, leaving him almost unable to move from the spot.

His throat seized, cold sweat pouring off him, Heinkel's face had gone deathly pale. He might even have lost control of his bladder. But even the composure needed to feel shame over that had been stripped from him, and Heinkel could only stare at the point of the longsword being raised above him.

—As if splitting the moon itself, the straight blade rose true toward the heavens.

At the very brink of his own life, Wilhelm, utterly powerless to act, was being forced to watch the very instant his wife would cut down their son.

He raised his voice. It didn't reach. He stretched out his hand. It didn't reach.

"Theresia——!!"

From the sword demon who had failed to pour everything into his blade, there was nothing but a powerless cry. The longsword, without mercy, began to fall, to end Heinkel's life —

"—That's far enough."

The voice cut through that raw, searing tension, sudden yet unmistakably clear. In that composed voice there was not a shred of hesitation, not the faintest trace of mercy either. It was a voice with a natural gift for striking overwhelming presence into all who heard it, for making its will known beyond question.

Wilhelm froze. Heinkel froze. And even Theresia's movement stopped.

At the point where all three gazes converged stood a single young man.

Hair red as blazing flame, blue eyes like a clear, radiant sky captured whole. His white attire stained with blood and mud, yet his upright bearing needed no word of adornment beyond valiant.

The young man walked forward, slowly, into that place. In his hands, a sheath deeply scored with sharp wounds, and the knight's sword drawn from it.

Its blade polished to an almost unnatural brilliance — he held the dragon sword, Reid.

—And Wilhelm thought, for a moment, that he could hear the sword god's laughter, echoing loud in the sword demon's ear.
