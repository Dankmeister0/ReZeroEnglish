Arc 3 Chapter 21: "The Price of Rashness and an Old Man's Resolve"

The shock that pierced Subaru as he watched that person being led away might have been, of all the shocks he'd taken in the throne room, the sharpest yet.

Driven by nothing but the desire to be of use to Emilia, he'd stepped forward into the center of the room—only to make a fool of himself.

Far from helping her, he'd dragged her down with him in spectacular fashion, and in the end, had it all witnessed by Emilia's own eyes, looking at him with disappointment.

All of it inflicted a pain on Subaru that was almost unbearable. And yet, he hadn't accepted that the fault lay entirely with him. His words had come from the bottom of his heart, no question about that, and there was no way he could wholly affirm everything Julius, so rigidly fixated on the honor of knighthood, had said. His argument had lacked coherence, mired in raw emotion—but that didn't mean everything he'd said was wrong.

Bluntly put, no small amount of frustration remained lodged inside him: frustration that, however many words he'd spent, he still hadn't been understood.

Even so, he'd withdrawn, because he'd known that baring his heart any further in that room would have meant nothing. In that scene Subaru had no right to speak; whatever he said was doomed to fall on deaf ears—that much had been plain to see. Normally, Natsuki Subaru was the kind of man who pushed his own way even in a situation like that, but the truth was, even the will to do so had been broken out of him.

And yet—

"Hey, hey…"

At the same time, the sight of Rom Jii, bound and restrained before his eyes, carried a weight that indicted every reckless, thoughtless thing Subaru had done.

The knight pointed at the huge old man and called him a suspicious intruder who'd tried to sneak into the royal castle. There was no reason for Rom Jii—who'd been in the noble district searching for Reinhard's estate to spirit Felt away—to have any business sneaking into the castle.

Unless, that is, he hadn't come to the noble district alone.

"Don't tell me… he came after…"

The decisive words—came after me?—wouldn't form.

But the question born in his chest needed no answer to feel like certainty.

To begin with, his plan had been far too shallow. Subaru had known, should have known, just how good-natured that old man was, how mismatched his character was for the vicious environment of the back alleys. That was exactly why he'd talked him into helping with the infiltration of the noble district with a smooth tongue, and why, afterward, he'd exploited the old man's good nature to split them apart and go it alone.

As for the risks that followed, that had all been the consequence of Subaru's own choice. Whatever situation he ended up in, he'd figured he could handle it himself, at least at a bare minimum. Worst case, he could drop Roswaal's name; even if that caused Roswaal no end of trouble, he'd at least held onto the minimal assurance that he could save his own skin.

But there was no way Rom Jii—who had parted from him with no explanation at all—could know anything about that shabby bit of self-preservation Subaru had been counting on.

Rom Jii could have abandoned him. He had people who mattered to him, a purpose of his own that mattered. So after they'd split up, Subaru had simply assumed the old man would weigh the priorities, write Subaru off, and act for his own ends.

The result of that failure to consider another person's feelings was the scene before him now.

It wasn't hard to imagine that Rom Jii, wracked with regret over having sent Subaru into such a reckless infiltration, worried for his safety, and come all the way to the royal castle after him. If anything, given the old man's character, it was an easy thing to imagine.

"Rom—"

"—"

Reflexively, Subaru's lips began to move to call out that name.

But something stopped him—and it was none other than Rom Jii himself, who, even as he was led away, had noticed Subaru standing at the edge of the corridor, flanked by a knight.

The moment he registered that Subaru was there, a flicker of surprise crossed those gray eyes—followed, unmistakably, by a look of relief meant for Subaru alone.

Then, so that only Subaru could see, he pressed a finger to his lips: stay quiet.

The meaning of that gesture was plain. Rom Jii had been caught trying to break into the castle. If Subaru admitted to knowing this suspicious old man, it would only put Subaru himself in a worse position—Rom Jii had read Subaru's current standing in an instant and made that call.

I got caught 'cause I was a fool, is all. Ain't nothin' for you to fret over.

No words were actually spoken, but with that finger still pressed to his lips and his head giving the smallest shake, it felt to Subaru as if Rom Jii's inner voice had reached him directly.

Caught up in that illusion, Subaru missed his chance to call out to the group passing by. Surrounded tightly by the knights, Rom Jii's figure vanished down the far end of the corridor.

Would he be dragged out to the hall and denounced? If it came to that, who among people ignorant of the real circumstances would ever speak up for him?

And above all else—

"Why didn't I say anything, just now…?"

No one had actually spoken aloud to stop him; the words that had crossed his mind afterward were nothing but a shape given to his own imagination. Maybe Rom Jii had been asking for help. And even if he hadn't, Subaru had every reason to act as though he had.

Because it was none other than Subaru who had driven the old man into a state of mind where breaking into that place seemed necessary—who had dragged him into all of this.

"—Wait, please!"

It took time to work up the resolve, but somehow his throat managed to cry it out.

The group that had nearly vanished around the far corner stopped, and he sensed them turning to look back, puzzled. He'd managed to stop them. The situation was as bad as it could get, but at least he'd avoided sinking below that. The question now was what to do from here.

"Is something the matter?"

"I've got a bit of business with the old man just now…"

Responding to the knight escorting him, who'd asked with evident concern, Subaru walked toward the group—toward Rom Jii, still restrained.

The group seemed uncertain how to treat Subaru as he approached, but when the knight accompanying him announced, "He is connected to one of the candidates," they all straightened at once, turning respectful attention toward him.

Feeling the weight of that respect even as he found it grating, Subaru nodded his thanks—grateful, for once—and stepped forward. He reached Rom Jii's side, close enough now that—

"—!"

—close enough to touch, and Subaru found he had no idea what to say.

Acting on impulse without a plan was nothing new for him, but this time wasn't so simple. Anything he said to Rom Jii, caught red-handed breaking into the royal castle, risked amounting to a confession of Subaru's own illegal entry. He needed to be careful.

And yet—once again, no plan.

Standing helplessly before the old man he knew, unable to do anything but flounder, Subaru realized he was, more than he'd ever admitted, a person who lived moment to moment. He'd always thought of himself as the calm, collected type who thought a few steps ahead—but given the process and outcome of everything he'd done today, it was almost funny how little face he had left to make that claim. Even now, he couldn't think of a single word to say.

"—Subaru-dono?"

A voice, wary of Subaru's trembling lips and his inability to produce a single word.

Naturally, the knights around them began to fix Subaru with harder looks. Feeling their rising suspicion prickle against his skin, Subaru scrambled to think of something—anything—to say, when—

"Hmph, you nobles sure got some rotten tastes! Laughin' at some fool old man who tripped up and got himself caught—don't seem like the same kinda creature as the rest of us, with blood runnin' through your veins the same!"

Subaru's mounting panic was swallowed whole by a voice loud enough to ring from one end of the corridor to the other.

Spitting as he spoke, in a coarse tone and with an even coarser expression, the one who'd shouted this was, of all people, Rom Jii himself. Still bound, he twisted his body and looked up rudely at Subaru's face from directly below.

"If a mug like this suits you, take a good long look! A face like this—crusted in the grime of the slums—ain't nothin' a soft-raised brat like you'd ever have call to see!"

Stunned, dumbstruck—whatever the word, Subaru's thoughts simply stopped in their tracks from the shock.

Rom Jii, hurling abuse no one could bear to listen to, had aimed it squarely at Subaru. The knights around them froze for a moment too, but a few seconds later, having come back to their senses—

"—Watch your tongue!"

"Guh!"

A fist came down as punishment for the criminal's insolence toward a person of importance like Subaru.

Shackled, movement sealed off, the old man had no way to defend himself. Struck without recourse, he was made to pay the price for words that overstepped his current standing.

Unable to hide his shock at the excessive punishment unfolding before his eyes, but knowing he had to stop it regardless, Subaru reached out—

"Wait, there's no need to go that far—"

"Real gentle of you, ain't ya, brat. C'mon now, what's the matter, knights? That's an order from your precious master—won't you wag your tails and obey like good dogs, ah—guh."

"You'll keep talking, you vagrant?!"

But Subaru's words to stop them were drowned out once more by Rom Jii's abuse. In response to Rom Jii piling insult on insult, the punishment came down harder, more savage than before.

Why—the question surged through Subaru's mind like a flood, nearly spilling out of his mouth. But something held the words back at the last instant: even as he took the blows, Rom Jii never once looked away from Subaru's eyes, and in that unwavering, clear-headed gaze, Subaru read his intent.

—Even here, even now, Rom Jii was trying to protect him.

To speak up for Rom Jii, caught in the act of unlawful entry, would only give people cause to go digging into Subaru's own tender spot. He was well aware that, pressed hard enough, his own position wouldn't hold—and Rom Jii knew it too, knew it perfectly well. That was exactly why he was hurling more abuse than necessary, playacting at estrangement, trying to erase every trace of the connection between them.

A wave of bitter regret washed over Subaru's chest. If it was his own rash actions that had driven Rom Jii into this corner, then it was his own lack of foresight, too, that had forced Rom Jii into this agonizing choice now. Rom Jii was being made to clean up the mess Subaru himself had left behind.

"Subaru-dono, let us leave this place."

"Ah… no…"

The knight touched Subaru's shoulder as he stood frozen, voice full of concern—likely mistaking Subaru's stunned silence for shock at the abuse hurled at him. Subaru gave some vague reply and turned his eyes back to Rom Jii's.

Somehow, I have to get myself out of this.

With that thought poured into his gaze—though he hadn't the faintest idea how he'd actually manage it—duty pressed down on him regardless: I have to do something.

But Rom Jii's answer to that resolve was rejection, a silent shake of his head.

"—Don't need your help, brat."

The small, hoarse mutter sounded, to the knights, like nothing more than a continuation of his earlier abuse. But to Subaru alone, it was unmistakable: of everything Rom Jii had said in that place, this alone held no deception—his true feelings, plain and unguarded.

With that, his eyes turned away, and Subaru swallowed back the words he'd been about to say, understanding that the conversation was over. Having his outstretched hand refused left Subaru feeling, once again, as though he'd been rejected. Just like in the hall. Only urgency pushed at his back, insisting he had to do something—and yet the one person who mattered most had no need of it.

Before the silent Subaru, Rom Jii turned his body and told the knights, who'd stopped to wait, "That's enough, get on with it." The knights glanced toward Subaru, but seeing him standing there, head bowed, unable to say a word, they said, "Excuse us," and resumed their march.

And so, Rom Jii was taken away.

Curling his massive Giant frame small, turning his bruised, blood-streaked face aside, Rom Jii was pulled away from Subaru—and Subaru let the last chance slip right past him.

"Subaru-dono, let's go. The rest is for the Captain to judge."

The knight's voice was quiet, but couldn't quite hide his own indignation.

He probably believed Subaru's silence was because of the baseless abuse Rom Jii had hurled at him. That wasn't entirely wrong—but in essence, it was completely off the mark. And yet explaining the truth of it would only spit on Rom Jii's resolve.

—That, too, was nothing but an excuse.

Was he really respecting Rom Jii's consideration? If things were left as they were, what awaited Rom Jii was condemnation and censure from the knights in the hall—and there was no telling what punishment would follow.

At the very least, if Subaru had spoken up here about Rom Jii's alleged crime, he could have made some use, however small, of his standing as someone connected to the Royal Selection.

"Just got caught in the crossfire, huh…"

Had he not gone along with Rom Jii's ploy, there was no doubt suspicion would have fallen on the stammering, tongue-tied Subaru as well. If he'd had enough silver-tongued skill to talk Rom Jii free with whatever followed, he might—to put it in extreme terms—have avoided even being forced to leave the hall in the first place.

Even that thought was nothing but an excuse.

"I…"

Once again, he hadn't been needed.

Rejected by Emilia, rejected by Rom Jii now too—his outstretched hand had nowhere left to go.

"Why am I even here…?"

What would happen to Rom Jii, wherever they were taking him?

Shaking his head, Subaru tried to drive away the ugly imaginings. Picturing the faces of those gathered in the hall, he told himself, piling excuse upon excuse to comfort himself, that things would surely turn out better this way than if he'd made a scene here himself.

Among the people in that hall, only three knew Rom Jii's face. And all three of them were candidates, the central figures of the Royal Selection itself. Subaru knew just how much Rom Jii mattered to one of them in particular. So surely, things wouldn't turn out badly.

Surely they wouldn't. They wouldn't. So—

"What was I even here for… I…"

A murmur spread through the throne room as the great doors were pushed open and an old man appeared from beyond them. Leading the shackled old man was Marcos, the captain of the knights, who had been called out of the room a short while before.

A few minutes earlier, during the proceedings of the Royal Selection, a single knight had come in to summon Captain Marcos. Marcos had frowned at the request for instructions, mindful of standing before the sages, but seeing his subordinate's urgency, he'd excused himself and left the room.

The proceedings had been halted, kept waiting five minutes—and now, this was the result.

"Hm, and who might this gentleman be?"

Seeing the old man Marcos had brought with him, Miklotov, up on the dais, voiced the question. It spoke for everyone in the room, all of whom were struggling to guess Marcos's intent in bringing this person here.

Bathed in that mixture of bewildered stares, Marcos bowed with due reverence.

"My apologies for the disturbance. It seems this man attempted to break into the royal castle."

"Then why have you brought him here, of all places? He's unrelated—no, this is simply the wrong venue."

The one who made no effort to hide his displeasure at Marcos's explanation was the stern old man—Bordeaux. Most everyone else in the room seemed to share the sentiment, their expressions clouding over, unable to read the true intent behind Marcos's actions.

Amid it all, three people—though they too had no idea of Marcos's intent—wore expressions distinct from everyone else's: expressions of shock.

One was an arrogant girl.

Recognizing the old man's face, the girl said nothing to her attendant about her own acquaintance with him, watching the unfolding scene instead with the corner of her mouth twisted into something cold and cruel. Had Subaru been present in that room, her behavior might well have been different. But with the one person who knew of her connection to the old man now absent, there was, as yet, no reason present in that room capable of moving her heart.

One was an earnest girl.

Taking a moment to place him, she recognized the old man's identity, then quickly cast her gaze in another direction—toward one particular candidate. As far as she knew, there existed a bond between that person and this candidate that could not be severed. Hearing that he'd tried to break into the castle, she understood at once that this candidate must be tied up in the old man's scheme somehow. Her lips trembled with indecision over what she ought to do, even as she kept herself braced, ready to act the instant anything happened.

And then there was the girl receiving the least fitting reaction of anyone in that room.

"—Rom Jii?"

Her eyes went wide, her golden hair swaying as her gaze wavered with astonishment, fixed on the old man.

In that gaze swirled unlooked-for joy, incomprehensible shock, and a tangle of emotions too tangled to put into words.

The voice with which she called the old man's name was murmured only within her own mouth—but there were more than a few sharp-eared people who caught it regardless.

"—Felt-sama, are you acquainted with this old man?"

Marcos, speaking with a tone that all but confirmed he already knew the answer, was one of them.

At his question, Felt closed her mouth for a moment, then stepped forward with all her usual bravado.

"Don't play dumb with me. You already knew that, that's why you dragged him all the way here."

"…I wouldn't say I took it at face value, but this old man did speak your name. My apologies if it came across as testing you."

"That's a load of crap. But damn, pulling a stunt like this…"

Marcos bowed his head without any change of expression, and Felt spat out her irritation without hiding it. Then she turned to look at the bound Rom Jii.

"Guess we both ran into each other in a hell of a strange place, huh, Rom Jii."

Her way of speaking was as coarse as ever, but the affection woven into it was something everyone present could surely feel. This girl, who up to now had done nothing in this hall but hurl insults, had for the first time spoken with genuine warmth.

At that, the bound old man raised his head. For the two of them, it was, in fact, a reunion after more than ten days apart. Surely this should have been a meeting overflowing with emotion.

But—

"Oi, Rom Jii, what happened to your face?"

Seeing the old man's beaten, blood-streaked face lift toward her, the girl's expression shifted in an instant. Her fierce, upturned eyes sharpened into anger, and she glared at Marcos, who held the chain linked to Rom Jii's shackles.

Under the weight of that furious gaze, Marcos, his expression unchanged, replied,

"That would be the result of a scuffle with the soldiers who stopped him during his attempt to enter the castle. My men were only doing their duty; there is no cause to blame them."

"Ha, real fine words, Captain of the Knights, sir. Makin' excuses before I even said a damn thing—ain't that proof enough that you know somethin's off here?"

"There was a possibility my men might face undue criticism, so please, laugh at this cowardly soul of mine. And if you wish to lay blame, direct it at me alone."

Whether it was a difference of experience or simply of stage presence, Marcos remained unshaken by Felt's provocations. Sensing further argument would be pointless, Felt let out a single sigh, setting her indignation aside for the moment.

"Fine, whatever. I get it. Just untie Rom Jii already. It's not like he stole somethin' or killed anyone, right?"

Waving a hand as if to say we'll talk after that, Felt gave Marcos the order. Her intention, clearly, was to speak with Rom Jii first and get the situation under control. But Marcos's answer to that was—

"—I regret that I cannot comply."

Low and unyielding, a flat refusal of her order.

"—Ah?"

Felt's face twisted at the reply, a vein bulging at her temple. Her red eyes, colored first with shock and then, in the next instant, with fury, seemed only a matter of time before the words trembling on her lips burst into flame.

Even under that furious glare, Marcos didn't so much as flinch. Before Felt's rising temper could erupt into a shout at his stubbornness, a red-haired young man stepped forward from the ranks of the knights.

"Captain, that seems rather lacking in explanation…"

"Reinhard, be silent. I understand you have something to say to me, going against the will of the one you've sworn your sword to. But that only holds if the one you've marked as your liege has the will to accept that sword in the first place."

"—"

Reinhard's mediating counsel was shot down, once again, by Marcos's words. As captain, Marcos's reasoning was sound, and even Reinhard couldn't argue against it without cause—he could only fall silent and watch how things unfolded with Felt.

Felt showed no disappointment at Reinhard being talked down so easily; trembling with rage, her eyes hadn't even registered the exchange. Her fury was focused entirely, single-mindedly, on Marcos—and in it was not just anger at this exchange, but a dense, concentrated color of resentment, as if every frustration from the long, twisted road that had brought her here had all been boiled down into one.

"I'll say it again. Let Rom Jii go. We talk after that."

"I refuse."

Holding back an anger threatening to burst free of its restraints, Felt formed the words quietly. But Marcos, answering with the same brevity, paid not the slightest heed to her resolve.

An air of unease was beginning to spread through the entire hall, and Felt's patience, too, was reaching its limit.

"—Knight Marcos, this is beginning to border on insolence."

The one who cut into that standoff was, once again, the person in the room most skilled at handling such situations, holding the most authority—the old man who had already sighed more times today than could easily be counted.

Miklotov reprimanded Marcos, whose stance toward Felt, one of the royal candidates, had grown severe enough to even hint at the possibility of a "rightful strike" against her.

"That the release of a man who attempted to break into the castle poses some problem as an order is one matter—but even in refusing, that manner of speech was discourteous. At the very least, you owe an explanation for such an impertinent attitude."

"As you wish."

The stubborn stance Marcos had maintained until now shifted in an instant, obeying Miklotov's instruction without the slightest resistance. That quick change of heart only added fuel to Felt's temper, but the surge of that fury lost its momentum the moment Marcos turned and looked her straight in the eye.

If Felt's red eyes burned with the flame of fury, then what spread across Marcos's own were the still, unbroken waters of a lake without a single ripple.

Feeling, quite genuinely, as though she'd been doused in cold water, Felt fell silent as Marcos, prefacing his words with "if I may be so bold," continued,

"In the course of these Royal Selection proceedings, Felt-sama has openly declared her own lack of intention to participate in the Selection. To relinquish that qualification is also to relinquish, here in this room, the right to give orders to us knights."

In a resonant voice, Marcos stated his reason for refusing to obey her command.

It might be called a leap of logic, plain and simple—but the fact that Felt's conduct up to this point had failed to leave a good impression on the officials and knights gathered here, and on the members of the Council of Wise Men as well, came back to bite her now. Naturally, many nodded along with Marcos's reasoning, showing their agreement.

"Captain, that is far too extreme a conclusion. To begin with, regardless of whether she holds the qualification, Felt-sama may well be of the royal bloodline of this kingdom—"

"A possibility is only a possibility. Stop speaking in idealistic terms so unlike you. What is not clearly established holds no weight here; what is clearly established is that she herself chose to relinquish her claim."

Paying no heed to Reinhard's words, Marcos sent a glance toward Felt as if to ask, "Do you understand now?"

At that, Felt, who had kept silent until now, dropped her gaze, thrust her hands into her golden hair, and raked it roughly. She let out irritated groans of "ahh" and "ugh," as if venting her frustration, before saying,

"This is gettin' complicated, so let me sum it up simple. You're basically sayin' you won't listen to a damn thing I say, 'cause I got no interest in the Selection. That about it?"

"—As the core of the matter, yes, that would be correct."

"Well, well. First time we've actually understood each other. Ahh, I see. Fine, fine. Ahh, yeah, yeah. I get it, I get it… You piss me off, y'know that?"

Felt's narrowed, cat-like pupils bore into Marcos as he affirmed her question.

The gust of near-murderous hostility carried on that gaze couldn't so much as stir the rock-solid expression on Marcos's face, though—not even as much as a breeze.

A battle-hardened veteran, against a delinquent girl raised and hardened only by the slums—it was a hopelessly mismatched confrontation in terms of experience. But Felt wasn't the type of cute little thing to be cowed by that alone.

She understood the reason now. And she understood the terms.

Her irritation piled up at an accelerating rate, raging through her body in search of somewhere to explode.

Trading insult for insult and giving the man in front of her a taste of his own medicine—that wasn't such a bad idea, either.

Just as that rash impulse detonated, just as Felt was about to let herself be carried away by the momentum—

"Who gives a damn 'bout all that! —Hurry up and help me, dammit!!"

A pain-wracked, cracked shriek from the old man, who had kept his silence until now, rang out through the hall.

Not Felt, whose anger had been on the verge of erupting; not Marcos, standing right beside the one who'd screamed; not a single other person in that hall could react in an instant to that cry.

And the man himself seemed entirely unconcerned with how those around him might react.

"Felt, it's me! Cromwell, the one who worked alongside you in the slums! Not real sure how, but you can do somethin' about this now, can't ya? Save me!"

Kneeling on the carpeted floor, the old man pasted an ingratiating smile across his face and begged. Had he not been restrained, he looked ready to go crawling right up to the girl's feet.

At that display, bordering on outright disgrace, even Felt lost her words, staring speechless at the transformation of someone she knew. Naturally, disgust rose in the gazes of those around her too, aimed at the pitiful figure of the old man.

"We been through all sorts of stuff together, ain't we!? Who was it that taught you how to survive when you were just a helpless little kid who didn't know nothin'? Me, that's who! And here's your benefactor, gettin' treated like this—do somethin', will ya!?"

Spitting as he spoke, shelving his own guilt entirely, he shrieked for help, faster, faster.

Only someone of exceptional mercy could have felt sympathy or pity toward that. To any ordinary person, it was nothing but a string of self-serving remarks that inspired pure disgust.

In the span of just a few brief moments, the old man had, one could say, made enemies of most everyone in that hall.

Every last person had lost their words at such shameless behavior.

Among them, only one person formed words entirely unrelated to that sentiment.

"—This may be turning into a problem."

Only the young man with the swaying red hair, even as he took in that unsightly cry, gave a small click of his throat at the unease welling up in his own chest.
