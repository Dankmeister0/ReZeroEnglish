Arc 4 Chapter 29: "Omnivorous Man"

Having touched on the heart of the information he wanted, Subaru tipped his teacup back.

He let the hot tea slide down his throat, swallowing a few questions along with it and letting them settle in his stomach, then seriously weighed what he ought to say next. And then:

"How much are you actually willing to answer my questions?"

"...You seem to have already sensed as much, but don't expect too much. Because of the Contract, there's little information I can give you, Su-bou. I've been strictly ordered to avoid interfering with the Trials from outside the tomb."

"I get that just saying that is already scattering hints my way... but 'strictly ordered,' huh."

"—————"

Ryuzu chose silence in response to the repeated question.

Judging by her behavior and remarks so far, the candidates who could exert that kind of influence over her were limited. Nine times out of ten, Subaru figured it was Roswaal pulling the strings, but—

"If that's the case, I understand his aim even less. Does he want me — want us — to clear the Trials? Or not? I can't even tell if he intends to cooperate or not."

"I'd guess it's that he'd prefer you arrive at the answer on your own. Roz-bou's always been a roundabout, hard-to-fathom little brat. He's only grown taller — deep down, I doubt he's changed at all."

Perhaps because of how long she'd known him, Ryuzu spoke of the young Roswaal with easy familiarity.

Hearing that he hadn't changed sent a chill through Subaru, who knew Roswaal only in his current, thoroughly weird incarnation — did that mean he'd been like that even before his limbs finished growing? He couldn't quite hide his dread, but—

"Well, surely he still had a bit more cuteness left back then. Yeah, probably. Maybe."

"As for Roz-bou's childhood... hmm, that touches on secrets of House Mathers, so I can't say anything more."

"Guh, now I'm curious... no, wait, am I really curious? It's not like I need to know his personality in that much depth."

Crossing his arms and cocking his head, Subaru found himself questioning the very source of his own curiosity, and—

"Alright, I'll shelve that one. What I need is to know his intentions, not to understand him."

"My, that was quite the swift dismissal."

"There's no point worrying over things that can't be helped by worrying. People aren't built to understand each other one hundred percent, from zero to a hundred, in the end. Wanting to understand anyway — that's probably what love is. And I can love Emilia-tan, but Roswaal? Not so much!"

Ryuzu nodded, almost impressed by how cleanly he'd cut that off, then, murmuring "In that case," placed a hand to her own chin.

"...It seems like there'll be hardly any topics left that I can talk to you about."

"Whoa, hold on, my question time — carefully feeling out the edges of what doesn't violate your Contract, Ryuzu-san — is far from over. I'll set Roswaal's personality aside for now, but there are other personalities I want to ask you about, seeing as you're the know-it-all granny of the Sanctuary."

"Hmm. Well, listening costs nothing, so I'll hear you out. But mind you — I'm not the only one who'll be in trouble if this runs afoul of the Contract. Don't you go forgetting that, now."

The little girl leaned forward, glaring up at him as if to intimidate him.

Honestly, her cute appearance robbed the gesture of any real menace, but Subaru hugged his own shoulders and put on a shivering, terrified reaction, eyes going misty as he said, "Eek, scary," before—

"Alright, now that we've established the scary-perpetrator, helpless-victim dynamic, it's question time."

"I don't much care for being cast as the villain just now."

"The wise ones of old said, that's that and this is this. Anyway, my question — you said Frederica and Garfiel are siblings, which means Frederica was here in the Sanctuary, right?"

Waving off Ryuzu's dissatisfaction, Subaru cut to the heart of the matter with a placating smile sandwiched in. Seeing her expression shift at the question, he wagged a raised finger at her.

"Frederica, who should've been here in the Sanctuary, is now over at Roswaal's manor, decked out in a maid uniform, serving away. But when I connect the dots with what I know, something about that doesn't add up."

"Hmm. How so?"

"If Frederica is Garfiel's sister, that makes her a half-blood too. And if she's a half-blood, then as long as the barrier of this Sanctuary hasn't been undone, she shouldn't be able to leave."

The Sanctuary's barrier reacted to half-blood blood, drawing it in and refusing to let it go.

It had knocked Emilia unconscious and kept Garfiel and Ryuzu trapped inside indefinitely — its existence remained one of the great walls standing in Subaru's way.

Even as he racked his brain over how to deal with it, there was an exception who had somehow crossed that wall and gotten outside. Which meant—

"Either there's some loophole in the barrier, or the barrier's a complete fabrication to begin with."

"'Fabrication'... that's a bit uncalled for. In truth, I have never once laid eyes on the outside world since I was born. And that, every bit of it, is because of that very barrier."

"Or maybe you've been bound by a Contract to claim that... that's a nasty way to look at it, I know. If the risk of testing whether it's really there is high enough, the idea of even trying to confirm it might never come up in the first place. But—"

The possibility that every resident of the Sanctuary was lying in unison about the barrier's existence.

Since the existence of the tomb's Trials had already been confirmed without a doubt, that possibility was, in practice, almost nonexistent. Though he couldn't fully rule out some petty scheme to keep the Arlam Village refugees pinned here and pad Emilia's reputation.

"But given the backlash if the truth came out, the downside's too big — it's not realistic. So the latter possibility gets automatically ruled out... which leaves only the former."

"A loophole... is it. But what do you mean to do once you've wormed it out of me?"

"If the loophole can be applied to everyone, then even if it takes time, we could use it to get every resident of the Sanctuary out — and then nobody would even need to take the Trials."

At Subaru's blithe proposal to bend the rules, Ryuzu's mouth hung open in astonishment. To her, Subaru raised the fingers of both hands, waving them side to side with a "Listen up, okay?"

"Sure, there's probably something to be gained from taking the Trials. Honestly, having gotten a bit of benefit from it myself, I can't deny that outright. But the Trials — let's just call it 'the past' in this case — even facing that past, there's a right timing and a right person for it, and it shouldn't be forced."

"And you're saying that's Emilia-sama? But hardship doesn't come knocking at a convenient time. Faced with a calamity that's bound to come eventually, if she runs now, then what—"

"I never said run away for good. This is retreating in order to properly prepare a counterattack. A strategic withdrawal, you might call it. Sure, there'll be times you have to fight on unfavorable ground — but scrambling to arrange the most favorable ground you can is exactly what shows the mettle of the person involved and the people around them, isn't it?"

Talking over Ryuzu as she tried to get a word in, Subaru kept piling on justification after justification for running. Turning your back on something, he insisted, was not something to be ashamed of.

"Even if she doesn't face it now, Emilia will inevitably face her past someday. Ironic as it is, the Trial already made her remember it. So whether she forgets it or swallows it down, she's the one who has to choose. Given that, my job is to arrange things so she can suffer as little as possible while she does."

"...For all your effort to keep her away from suffering, you won't let her escape the one place that hurts the most."

"If she wanted to run from that too, that'd be her choice, I guess. But that girl... I believe Emilia won't do that."

"How can you believe that so completely? At least, I can't. Not after seeing her come out of the tomb in such a state of distress — I couldn't possibly hold out that kind of hope."

"Because I'm head over heels for Emilia-tan."

To Ryuzu, who was heavily trying to pile on more denials, Subaru declared this as if it were nothing at all.

Sure enough, Ryuzu — who had already worn a dumbstruck expression several times over the course of this conversation — froze with the biggest slack-jawed look yet.

At her blank, stunned face, Subaru offered an embarrassed smile, scratching his cheek.

"I like Emilia, and I think she's super cute. And that super cute girl I like — I believe she's strong enough to overcome anything painful, anything hard, in the end. I expect it and expect it and expect it, and I believe she'll live up to it."

"Th... that's not an answer at all. No matter how fond you are of her, there are things in the heart that just can't be helped. Surely you understand that, Su-bou—"

"Of course I understand. Emilia's a girl too. It's not just the strong parts — there are weak parts, and probably some tiny, ugly parts too, however small. It's not like they don't exist."

"Are you going to admit that or not..."

"Believing that the strong part will still shine through, weak parts and all, wrapped up together — isn't that what devotion is?"

Devotion — to Subaru, that was etched into his heart as the strongest expression of love there was. He remembered the happiness of having it offered to him, wholly and with all one's heart. And so:

"I'm going to devote myself to Emilia. I believe she'll overcome her weak parts and lift her head, resolved to be strong — and I'll work to make that happen so I can grin and say I was right to believe it."

"...Even so, facing the most painful part of one's own heart is something only the person herself can do."

"Well, obviously? I can't be there beside Emilia while she faces her past. If I'd been there in the past, I could've taken her hand as she crouched there crying, pulled her up, and cheered her on with everything I had — but I wasn't there. I can't have any part in what happened in the past. Reach out my hand and it'll just pass right through — a viewer can't reach into the drama playing on the TV screen."

It was the truth. The past was the past, and there was no touching it.

Even the past Subaru believed he'd faced and overcome was nothing more than a fiction — none of it had reached his actual parents; it was merely a matter of self-satisfaction within Subaru himself.

But—

"It's got to be better to have even one thing rewarded than nothing at all. The real me can't lend a hand while she faces the past. But the present me lending a hand to the lacking self of the past — that doesn't break any rules, does it?"

"—————"

"I can't lend her a hand, but my words, my actions, my affection... well, I'd be happy if she put me first, but besides that, there's all sorts of things she's gotten from all sorts of people — the Emilia of now has that. At the very least, she's carrying more than the Emilia of the past did. If you've got more weapons to use, some of them can reach back into what the past was lacking. This Trial — its difficulty is set on the assumption you'll clear it that way, isn't it?"

Because he'd experienced that himself, firsthand, Subaru could weave the same words over and over, without end.

"I'm going to help Emilia. I'll do everything I possibly can so she can overcome her past with who she is now. For that, I'll take any loophole, bend any rule, pay for any cheat item — whatever it takes. That's my way of devoting myself."

"What a... thoroughly self-centered way to love someone."

"Not an herbivore, not a carnivore — call me an omnivorous man—"

He snapped his fingers, flashed his teeth, gave a thumbs-up and a wink — his signature pose.

At Subaru capping off the conversation with that pose, Ryuzu let out a long, long sigh, as if giving up.

"That's quite the string of flowery words you've lined up, all just to excuse cheating."

"Heheh."

"Foolish boy. ...Sorry, but I can't tell you the details of that loophole. But if you're counting on it, let me tell you plainly — it's not possible. Frederica being able to leave the Sanctuary was an exception. There's no taking everyone out that way."

Shaking her head, Ryuzu moved to deny Subaru's hopeful outlook. At her words, Subaru — rather than slumping his shoulders and sinking to his knees in disappointment—

"Well, can't be helped. I figured if it worked out it'd just be a bonus, so I'll take that as settled. In that case, next question..."

"Odd thing for me to say, but don't let it get you down, Su-bou."

"If I folded over something this small, there's no telling how many whale stomachs I'd have ended up in by now. Not to toot my own horn, but stubborn refusal to give up and a whole lot of love are basically my only weapons."

There was something almost refreshing in the way Subaru brazened out his own powerlessness. Facing Ryuzu's exasperated look — one he'd already seen several times over this past hour or so — Subaru clapped his hands and said, "Anyway, moving on."

"I know that, without all this scheming, the most heroic-tale-like outcome would be for Emilia to bring her full strength to bear and clear the Trial as-is. As a veteran Trial challenger, Ryuzu-san, don't you have some opinion on that front?"

"I feel like I've been given a rather unwelcome title just now. Besides, if you ask me, all I can tell you is that I don't know. After all, I've never taken the Trial myself — there's no way I'd know the answer, is there?"

"Huh?"

Subaru cocked his head, responding to Ryuzu's answer with a blank sound. But that last remark was one he couldn't let pass. After all—

"Did you just say you've never taken the Trial?"

"Why the sudden formal speech? Of course I haven't. The Trial is customarily given to outsiders — it's not permitted for me to take it. The same goes for the others. As far as I know, for at least seventy years, nothing like that has happened. You're the first, Su-bou."

"No, no, no, no, that's weird, weird, weird. Wait a second, stay, wait, this is weird, the information's all tangled up. Give me a sec to check my mental notepad."

"Only five minutes, mind you."

Grateful for Ryuzu's surprisingly patient reply, Subaru nodded and pressed his fingers to his temples, desperately tracing back through his memory.

He went back to the day before the previous loop, and to right after he'd cleared the Trial the last time, working through the discrepancies in the finer details of his memory for himself—

"...Huh?"

Only now did he realize that a discrepancy — one that shouldn't have existed — had already crept into his memory.

His face stiffened at the realization, and he barely managed to keep the question — "What does this mean?" — as nothing more than a mutter under his breath. But the seed of doubt that had surfaced could not be stopped from sprouting, stretching its stem, and blooming.

In Subaru's memory, Ryuzu on the eve of the Trial had said this:

—No one had ever taken the Trial before. Not once since she was born had there been a challenger.

In Subaru's memory, Ryuzu just before and just after the Trial had said this:

—She herself had taken the Trial, and though she hadn't completed it, she was unharmed. Proof that there was no danger in attempting it.

In the span of a single day, her statements had flipped to complete opposites.

If this were merely a matter of feelings, it would be one thing — but when it came to accounts of personal experience and matters of fact, that changed everything. A reversal this drastic could only mean one of the two accounts had been falsified, that a deliberate lie had been told.

Still deep in thought, he glanced over at Ryuzu. She waited for him, toying with her pale pink hair, feet dangling off the bed, too short to reach the floor, swinging them to pass the boredom — a sight utterly childlike, in stark contrast to how much of an old woman she was on the inside.

Given how she'd treated him so far, he didn't want to believe she was toying with him out of malice.

"If one of them's true, which one should I believe?"

When faced with conflicting information, Subaru's usual stance had been to believe whichever he wanted to. But he couldn't make a call this rash when Emilia's fate hung on it. What's more, this was, quite literally, a matter of life and death. He had to be careful.

This was the third time he'd broached the subject of the Trial with Ryuzu.

Once she'd answered that she had taken the Trial; twice she'd denied even knowing it. Not that majority ruled, exactly, but perhaps he ought to trust the side of her opinion that claimed no knowledge of the Trial after all.

Thinking about it that way, he could take it positively — that when she'd claimed to have taken the Trial, it might have been a consideration meant to reassure an anxious Subaru. Though if that were the case, he thought, she could've at least said a word about it afterward—

"Either there was a reason she couldn't... or maybe her mind's starting to go..."

"You were just about to arrive at a fairly rude conclusion about me, weren't you?"

"If you could respect the effort Subaru put into trying his hardest to take it charitably, and call it even, that'd make Subaru happy — or so Subaru insists, with a very serious face."

Ryuzu sighed at Subaru mixing wisecracks into his excuse, then shook her head wearily with a "Good grief," and said:

"So, did you finish consulting with that heart of yours and arrive at an answer?"

"Not so much an answer as a 'maybe this is how it was' kind of conclusion. So, um, was I — was Ryuzu-san maybe going out of her way to look out for me, back at the tomb and all?"

"Look out for you?"

Ryuzu echoed his question back. Subaru nodded — "Yeah" — while pressing his own fingertips together in a bashful, girlish gesture.

"Like, here just now you said you'd never taken the Trial, but back at the tomb you said something like you'd taken it and it wasn't a big deal, right? I only just now realized — maybe that was you being considerate toward me, since I was kind of freaking out, or being a worrywart, or whatever..."

"—Ah. I see, so that's it."

Ryuzu's voice, as Subaru rattled through the awkward conversation trying to hurry past it, carried a sudden note of understanding. At that tone, Subaru knit his brows suspiciously, and she smiled at him.

"Su-bou, let me tell you something good. Just this once, special treatment."

"Something good?"

"Because of the Contract, I'm made unable to tell a 'lie.' So when I'm asked something inconvenient, the only way I have to dodge an answer is to choose silence. No exceptions, and it doesn't matter who it might be for. I'm forbidden from lying. Not just me — every resident of this Sanctuary, all of us."

"You... can't lie...?"

There was nothing for Subaru to do but be bewildered at the sudden confession.

Because if that were true, it would overturn the very premise of everything he'd been thinking. No, before that even — if what she'd just said was true, then the doubt he'd had about her a moment ago was, in its very existence, a contradiction.

—How could she, someone who supposedly couldn't lie, have created a situation that could only exist if she had lied?

"Um..."

"What I just told you is the very limit of what I'm able to speak aloud. It's no use asking me anything more than this. Breaking the Contract brings misfortune to both sides. If you want to know what lies beyond this, you should press someone who went further, not one like me who stopped here."

Preempted like that, as if pinned down by a warning, Subaru had no choice but to close his half-open mouth and fall silent.

Rejected like that, Subaru — who had no recourse but to rely on her goodwill — was, quite literally, out of moves. But even to a Subaru like this, she had extended a hand.

Picking up on just that fact alone, Subaru felt firmly certain of at least this much: that her true wish to be freed from this place was no lie.

"Got it. I won't ask any more about this. Let's change the subject. Something else."

"You're an odd one. Even knowing you can't pry out anything at the heart of it, will you still keep an old woman company over tea?"

"I missed breakfast, and I'm basically free until Emilia-tan wakes up anyway. Getting irritated looking at Roswaal, having Garfiel bare his fangs at me, wearing Otto out making him do all the retorts — none of that sounds great, so I'll go deepen this precious friendship with a loli-granny instead."

Standing up, Subaru collected the two teacups, both gone completely cold. Then, seen off by Ryuzu's gaze, he headed for the kitchen.

"No need to look so worried — believe it or not, I've been living as a servant at Roswaal Manor, short as it's been. I've been taught at least how to brew tea."

"Hmm. Well then, I suppose I'll wait with high expectations."

"Whoa, pressure."

Talking all the while, Subaru brewed fresh tea into the cups, handed one to Ryuzu, and returned to his original spot. Facing each other once more, they each took their first fresh sip.

"Not bad craftsmanship at all."

"Leaves are leaves no matter who brews them. Anyway, changing the subject—"

"If it's a continuation of the last topic, I can't answer. But instead... well, if it's something else, I'd like to answer as sincerely as I can."

"Well then, I'll take you up on that."

Taking advantage of Ryuzu's slightly guilty-looking, childish profile, Subaru let a sly grin spread across his face.

"Is there anything — like a weakness of that Garfiel guy's, or something he hates, or something that'd make him keel over just looking at it?"

"Su-bou, do you have any self-awareness that your way of devoting yourself might be just a little twisted?"

And with that, he drew out the strangest expression Ryuzu had worn in this whole hour or so.

Once the tea-time chat was over and the cups washed and put away, Subaru left the building, leaving behind the solitary loli-granny who said she still wanted some time alone.

He figured it had been a bit longer than "about an hour." Before he knew it, the sun — which just a while ago had only just been peeking out — had climbed higher in the sky, and he could feel the temperature had risen somewhat, making things more comfortable.

Going by his own sense of time, it was probably somewhere past ten in the morning.

"This is just the right amount of sunlight for airing out futons, what a waste... man, that thought process has me sounding like I've completely graduated from being a shut-in."

That the first thing to come to mind upon basking in sunlight was laundry was a deeply unwelcome outcome for someone who was supposed to be a poster child of unhealthy living. In any case, setting that reflection aside—

"Well, it's about that time — Emilia should be waking up soon too. After what happened just yesterday, she's probably feeling anxious, so I'll take advantage of her being down and imprint my presence on her while she's vulnerable."

Even as he made that rather underhanded remark, inside, his feet hurried on, genuinely worried about Emilia, no pretense about it.

He wanted to get to her side as soon as possible, and stay there as long as he could, to put her at ease.

After all—

"Tomorrow, I'm going to have to leave her side again for a while."

Sometime this afternoon, Roswaal would probably formally propose releasing the Arlam Village refugees. Once that happened, preparations would be arranged quickly, and they'd likely leave the Sanctuary as early as tomorrow.

Subaru would have to accompany them again, and return to the manor.

"If you want to know what's deeper than this, ask someone who went further, not someone who stopped here... quite the roundabout way of putting it."

It was a position where, unless things were phrased that indirectly, even what she wanted to convey couldn't be conveyed.

Feeling a pang of pity for how troublesome that must be, Subaru quickened his pace and let out a breath.

"I'm looking forward to hearing all about your no-good little brother and our troublesome hometown, Frederica."

Rendered literally as "omnivorous man" since Subaru explicitly spells out the herbivore/carnivore contrast in-line, preserving the joke without needing substitution.
  • 「たぶんメイビー」("tabun meibi"): a bilingual pun mixing Japanese "tabun" (probably) with English "maybe." Rendered as "probably. Maybe." — the joke is muted in English since the bilingual mismatch (the humor of mixing languages) doesn't carry over as distinctly.
