Arc 6 Chapter 80: "The Death of the Mind"

—There's a scent of anger in the air.
A fragrant, mellow, high-purity scent of anger. It was intoxicating, and it stirred this thin, small body of hers, pushing her higher and higher — she could feel it.

"Hahaa."

Facing each other up and down the spiral staircase, Batenkaitos sneered darkly at Ram's fierce challenge.
The girl with strong emotion lodged in her pale pink eyes — Batenkaitos drank in the fury, the hatred, the killing intent she directed this way, breathing it in until her chest was full, savoring the ecstasy of it.
Without mincing words, it would hardly be an exaggeration to say she lived for this single breath.

"Nice, nice, so nice, love it, keep it coming, don't stop. Nobody ever aims a face like that at us normally, y'know. It's such a rare, precious feeling. Get it? Do you get it — how happy we are right now?!"

Stamping her feet in an expression of bursting excitement, Batenkaitos licked her lips.
Yes, this encounter with genuine emotion was something the usual Batenkaitos could never hope for.
The "memories" and "names" stolen by the Authority of Gluttony peeled away from the world, becoming unrecognizable to anyone but Roy and Rui.
The sorrow of losing someone precious, the anger of having something taken, the empty sense of loss — all of it vanished cleanly away, leaving nothing to be done about it.

"Which is only natural, of course it is. So it's not like I ever paid it any mind before."

Operating as a despised member of the Witch Cult, one met plenty of people who directed their hatred at the organization. But no one ever learned of Lye Batenkaitos's deeds and came to hate Lye Batenkaitos as an individual, or sought to kill Lye Batenkaitos specifically.
That natural law born of the Authority of Gluttony, that unspoken rule taken for granted — was now being overturned.

—Because a third party unaffected by the Authority of Gluttony's influence had surfaced.

"Natsuki Subaru...!"

Muttering the name of the man Rui was so obsessed with, Batenkaitos felt a sweet ache stir deep in her thin chest. It wasn't that Rui had rubbed off on her. She had known of him before that.
Long before Rui, far longer, far more deeply, she had thought of him.
And that was —

"— Sister dear understands too, doesn't she?"

The cause of this sweet ache in her chest, and the girl glaring down at her, shared a deep connection. — No, "deep" didn't begin to cover it. One mind, one body; an irreplaceable other half, if you like.
Surely a sister like that would understand. This sweet, faint, too-powerful longing. This very sensation where love and hatred intertwined, and the ache in her chest yearning for release.
So, with that conviction and expectation, she split her mouth into a crescent smile to speak —

"I told you already. — Die squealing like a pig."

"—!"

The next instant, Ram kicked off the stairs and came diving down, her opening move sending Batenkaitos leaping backward.
She pulled back just in time, but the tip of the thrust-out staff had been aimed precisely at her right eye. Had she been a moment slower pulling her head back, her brain would have been stirred right through the eye socket.

"Sister dear is merciless! If I'd taken that, I wouldn't even manage a pig's squeal — I'd just be dead, no can-do at all!"

"I reconsidered. If something lower than a pig imitated one, even the pig would be put out."

Leaping across the spiral staircase, Batenkaitos's landing was delayed by the difference in the steps. Declaring cold murderous intent, Ram's relentless assault pursued her with tremendous force.
Spinning her slender body, Ram thrust with her staff, lashed out kicks, drove elbow strikes. Batenkaitos dodged every one with pure body control, but the fierce gusts that grazed past her ears carried enough force that a single hit would have surely cost her the function of some part of her body.

"Ha! Scary, scary! Scary, but — !"

Facing the unhesitating assault, Batenkaitos laughed as she weaved away. It wasn't composure — it was exhilaration. — No, there was a sliver of composure too.
Ram's martial skill was formidable. No mercy, no hesitation to kill. And yet Batenkaitos could still dodge with room to spare. Why? It was simple.

"Because we know full well that sister dear is capable of something even more amazing than this."

Among the countless "memories" tucked away inside Batenkaitos, there was one — passionate, adoring — that looked up to Ram as an elder sister and yearned after her limitless potential.
That memory's insatiable trust and expectation toward Ram conveyed to Batenkaitos, with sad precision, the gap between that ideal and the Ram standing before her now.
To put it plainly —

"— A sister dear without her horn is nothing but a knockoff that even Rem could substitute for, huh."

"— — — —"

"Oh, mad? Did that make you mad? If you wanna get mad, go ahead and get mad, sister dear. Come to think of it, we've never actually had a proper sisterly spat before."

Batenkaitos flaunted the memories as if they were her own. Ram's cheek stiffened at her words, but her attacking hand didn't slacken.
She's really pushing herself, Batenkaitos marveled inwardly. She had no idea how much suffering it entailed, but it was well known that an Oni's body, once robbed of its horn, could no longer be properly handled by its owner. Ram, too, had endured that penalty endlessly, without end.

She couldn't count how many times she'd wished to take that suffering upon herself instead.
But at the same time, she'd also thought — that she herself could never withstand the suffering Ram carried.
If the agony of losing one's horn depended on the strength the Oni had originally possessed, then whatever Ram bore must be among the greatest such agony this world could produce, something no other Oni alive could share.

And so — praise, for the figure who pushed through such suffering and dove in so boldly of her own will. And gratitude, for the posture of desperate, single-minded pursuit that twisted that composed face out of shape.
She had no idea whom she ought to thank for the chance to witness the moment when the finest, the greatest, the most exquisite delicacy matured and released its rich, fragrant aroma.

—For the miracle of encountering such a feast, gratitude, joy, and a downpour of hallelujahs.

"Truly! Sister dear, you're just wonderful!!"

A dance of murderous intent, love and hate intermingled, unfolding across the spiral staircase. Savoring it to the fullest, Batenkaitos blessed Ram's very existence.
And then, letting her body move exactly as the "memory" desired, tilting, weaving, ducking to dodge —

"And now, ta-daa, counterattack tiii—"
"Be quiet."

Batenkaitos had circled to the outside of Ram's outstretched staff, aiming for her pale white throat. Just as she was certain she'd slipped into a blind spot, her gaze crossed with Ram's as the latter turned.
In that instant, seized by a chill of dread, Batenkaitos aborted her attack, throwing herself in a great leap sideways. But an instant after her right cheek felt the wind, it sprayed blood.

"—Guh"

Tracing the gash torn across her cheek with a hand, Batenkaitos briefly held her breath.
It was a wound she shouldn't have been able to take. At the very least, going by the "memory" within her — belonging to the one who knew the child prodigy Ram best of all — there was no future in which she would have taken this wound.
And yet —

"I told you to squeal pathetically, but even a squalid opponent's voice can be hard on the ears. I've had enough of putting up with this, so I've decided to kill you."

"Sister dear, this is..."

"If you were judging Ram based on your 'memory,' your judgment's still far too soft."

Ram, watching Batenkaitos with her cheek smeared thick with blood, ran a hand through her own pink hair. Sending her a chillingly cold sidelong glance, she said,

"Did you think you'd exposed the real Ram underneath? If so, that's laughable. — Ram's possibilities are limitless. Because —"

"— — — —"

"— Ram is Rem's elder sister."

Baseless bluster, and yet it rang with an abnormal persuasiveness — for no other reason than that the "memory" inside Batenkaitos itself perceived its elder sister exactly that way.
Recognizing that fact, Batenkaitos let her smile drop, her cheek twisting with irritation.

Not fun. Not amusing. She doesn't understand.

"Anger and hatred are just spice for an even finer delicacy. But if the spice is too strong, if its assertion overpowers everything, it ruins the whole dish. I guess that's a metaphor sister dear, who never cooks, wouldn't get at all, huh?"

"That's not true. Believe it or not, Ram's specialty is steamed potatoes."

A memory vividly recalling just that surfaced, and her stomach growled.
Deliberately ignoring that plea of hunger, Batenkaitos cast aside her playful mood.

Ram had still not shown the true worth of her power. Which meant — she hadn't revealed everything even to a sister who was practically her other half.
In other words —

"— Sister dear can't even trust her own sister, huh. Talk about the ultimate individualist."

"...What a shallow way of looking at things. I don't know how many people's lives you've savored, but it's a shame for all of them. Though anything besides Rem is a trifling matter."

Rejecting Batenkaitos's philosophy of feeding, Ram narrowed her pale pink eyes.
Then, stroking her own thin chest, she said,

"Do your best, Barusu,"

calling out to the boy who wasn't there, her cold expression settling into merciless resolve.

"A shared fate — how chilling. But there it is."

Pulling the dust-guard cloth up over his mouth so as not to breathe in the sand, Subaru inhaled deeply.
Goggles would have been nice too, honestly, but that was too much to ask for. He had no choice but to brace for grit flying into his eyes and keep straining to see through the whirling dust.

"Bea-ko! Meili! Weather it!"
"I know that already!" "Geez, you sure like ordering people around!"

Crossing through their sand-blurred field of vision was a vicious presence clad in jet-black carapace.
Let it close in and take a hit from its great pincers — that was death. Give it distance and let it wind up to fire its tail stinger — that was death too. There were plenty of other conditions for death besides.
As for conditions for losing, if that were all that mattered, there were more than enough to spare in their current situation.

"Still, that's nothing new..."

Natsuki Subaru's battles were always a series of razor's-edge tightropes.
Even knowing what lay ahead thanks to Return by Death, it was still suffocating, because the abilities he had and the strength of the enemy he faced were nowhere near evenly matched.
Having too few cards in hand was the usual state of affairs. Trusting in everyone's strength, hoping they'd adapt and fight flexibly, Subaru himself fought hard to live up to everyone's expectations in turn.

"This isn't so much my fight as it is me and my delightful friends' fight!"

"Is now really the time to say that?! Geez! Do your best, Gama-oh-chan!"

At Subaru's pointless shout, Meili, riding alongside him, gave the command to her Witchbeast — the Famished Horse King.
The centaur-shaped Witchbeast let out an infant's cry as the two of them, carrying Subaru and Beatrice on one and Meili on the other, tore across the sand sea at full speed.
And pursuing hard after their sand-kicking backs came the great scorpion, scraping sand with its many legs, whipping up dust clouds as it lunged for them.

"—!!"

Just as she'd sensed before, keeping distance brought the tail stinger, closing in brought the great pincers — both aimed at their lives. They had to maintain a careful in-between distance, handling the enemy's attacks as they came.
Compared to the losing conditions they'd counted, the winning condition they were allowed was a change that would come at the end of this delaying battle: waiting for Emilia to clear the tower's first floor and rewrite the rules that had been presented.
As for whether that was even possible, that much wasn't even settled —

"If that's not possible, then it'd be strange for it to be built into the tower's rules in the first place, I'd wager."

"Exactly, Bea-ko. The fact that the rules go out of their way to say 'it's fine to break the rules' means these rules are premised on being broken."

And if it was possible, it was surely meant as a reward for whoever cleared the tower — a right granted only to those who cleared it through legitimate means. Thinking of it that way, it was a very good thing that Emilia had broken through Reid fair and square. If she hadn't won, the means to save Shaula wouldn't have so much as brushed their fingertips.

"That guy who sexually harassed Emilia-tan, I'll never forgive him, but...!"

It was an infuriating thing to recall, but he'd left retribution against Reid to Julius.
While hoping Julius would give that violent man a thorough pummeling, Subaru kept the towering watchtower in view, entrusting himself to Beatrice and Meili's hard-fought struggle.

"Onii-san! It's hard to move around here! I think it'd be easier for the beasties to rampage if we got farther from the tower!"

"Ngh...! I get what you're saying! I get it, but it's impossible! I can't get away from the tower. If I go any farther, I'll lose track!"

Rejecting Meili's plea, Subaru gripped his own chest and gritted his back teeth.
The effect of Cor Leonis was still active, and even now the locations of his companions inside the tower registered within Subaru's perception as faint points of light. But this Authority, still largely unknown in so many respects, gave no telling what might trigger it to unravel.
As for the effect of the Authority, distance was surely the most straightforward factor.
In fact, Subaru couldn't sense the location of the companions outside the Augria Sand Dunes. If distance was the cause, then just how far could he get from the watchtower?

The thought of losing track of his companions' whereabouts, of their safety, was frightening in itself.
But the biggest problem wasn't confirming their safety so much as being unable to shoulder their burdens in their place. Emilia, Julius, and the others throwing themselves into brutal battles were naturally a concern, but what gripped Subaru's heart even more tightly and wouldn't let go was Ram, facing her enemy in pursuit of revenge.

"— — — —"

He had confidence he'd placed each companion in the optimal spot, but among them, he'd known from the start that Ram's battle would be the harshest. That was because her opponent was Lye Batenkaitos — the very one who had turned Rem into "Sleeping Beauty" — and because of Ram's own condition as well.
Anything less than a fully capable Ram would stand no chance against Lye Batenkaitos.
On top of that, he wanted Ram to be able to beat Batenkaitos down to her heart's content. For that, Subaru needed to shoulder the burden that constantly tormented her in her daily life.
And that was exactly why —

"I can't carelessly get away from the tower! Sorry it's all handicap matches, but bear with me!"

"~~! This is absolutely, definitely going to come back to bite you later!"

Pressing her lips together at Subaru's plea, her face reddening, Meili shouted from atop the Famished Horse King's back.
Then, glaring up at the sky, the girl clapped her hands toward the presences circling high in the deep blue heavens.

"Wing Earth Dragons! You're up!"

At her shrill command, small silhouettes wheeling through the sky plunged sharply down toward the sand sea. Seeing them target the great scorpion on the sand, Subaru instantly shouted, "Bea-ko!"

"Match them up with those guys!"
"—! Got it! 'Vita'!!"

Sensing Subaru's aim, Beatrice raised her small palm to the sky.
The chant's effect activated — but its target wasn't the great scorpion. It was the Wing Earth Dragons diving down toward the scorpion instead.
Along the falling Wing Earth Dragons' path, a faint ring of light formed — the instant a Wing Earth Dragon passed through that ring, it accelerated, wrapped in light.

"— — — —"

The piercing impact drove into the great scorpion's outer shell, a violent sound echoing out.
Up until now the great scorpion had taken the Wing Earth Dragons' charges like a passing breeze, but at this direct hit of undeniable force, several of its legs came to a halt.

"What was that just now... Beatrice-chan, what did you do?"

"I made the Witchbeast you're controlling heavier right before impact, I'd wager. The speed and hardness don't change, but making it heavier alone changes the result."

Beatrice answered Meili's astonishment.
That was the reason behind the Wing Earth Dragon's sudden reinforcement — force was the product of mass and velocity.
The Wing Earth Dragon, capable of burrowing through even the earth with its sturdy horn, a Witchbeast that soared freely between sky and underground — its life-risking charge, backed by this unbelievable destructive force, succeeded in halting the great scorpion in its tracks.

The great scorpion's adaptability was impressive, but it couldn't respond evenly to an attack it was seeing for the first time. And to outpace even that reflexive adaptability, Subaru spun his mind into overdrive.

"Sorry to the beasts, but it's a trade-off between life and time. The number and variety of usable Witchbeasts is plentiful, and with Bea-ko's assist, the possibilities are limitless...! With this method —"

We can do this — that solid conviction had Subaru gripping his fist and Beatrice's hair. In his grasp, Beatrice protested, "That hurts, I'd wager!" — And it was at that very moment.

"—Ah?"

Aiming to buy a bit more time, Subaru looked up at the watchtower and let out a hollow sound.
Hearing that empty voice, Beatrice turned and asked, "Subaru?" but he couldn't answer.
What had seized Subaru's attention wasn't the visible exterior of the watchtower, but its interior — the tower where he sensed faint points of light scattered about, his companions' presences, the cause of it.

Among those presences, presumably in the midst of fierce battles, one reaction had vanished. — Emilia's reaction, the one heading toward the summit.

"— — — —"

In that instant, Subaru's heart slammed violently, as if about to burst.
The fact that Emilia's reaction had vanished threw Subaru into terrible confusion. Surely — surely in this single instant Emilia hadn't just been lost, there was no way something like that could —

"—! Get a grip, you idiot."

Just as his vision was starting to darken, he berated himself and held it back.
It was too soon to despair over reality. He needed to trust in the power of Cor Leonis more. Even if its origin traced back to the worst of men, even if the power was still newly sprouted, this Authority was on his side.

Going by Cor Leonis's reactions up to now, the brightness and warmth of the faint light was thought to indicate his companions' condition.
If someone was in the throes of intense excitement, the color and intensity of the light would change. The reverse held true as well.
So then, did Emilia's vanished reaction mean her "death"?

"If that's the case, the speed at which the reaction disappeared is way too instantaneous."

Suppose Emilia had encountered some terrifying enemy capable of erasing a presence in an instant, and suppose she'd fallen at that enemy's hand — even so, he couldn't imagine her vanishing without a fight.
He couldn't imagine it, and didn't want to imagine it. He believed in her. So this was, in all likelihood, something else.

Something had happened to Emilia, racing up the watchtower toward the first floor, that took her outside the range of Cor Leonis's influence.
That was why Emilia's reaction had slipped, smooth and instantaneous, out of Subaru's perception.
He had to believe that —

"Subaru!"
"Onii-san! This isn't the time to space out!"

Biting down through his cheek flesh to his back teeth, disciplining himself with pain, Subaru was called out to by the two young girls.
Hearing it, Subaru answered, "I know," both to himself and loud enough for them to hear,

"I don't know where she is or what she's up against. But I believe she'll come back."

—Far below on the sand, at the very moment Subaru spoke those words of prayer.

Subaru had thought he didn't know what Emilia was up against, but even his imagination could never have unraveled the situation Emilia now found herself in.
Following Subaru's instructions, Emilia had headed to the first floor to rewrite the rules of the Pleiades Watchtower — and what she encountered there was a mighty presence clad in gleaming blue scales.

A presence unknown to no one in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica — no, in the entire world.
If the Witch of Envy was a symbol of terror, then this must be called a symbol of hope and trust. That was how many accomplishments this presence had continued to pile up for the world.
The name of this mighty and magnificent presence was —

"— The 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica."

Repeating the name given to her, Emilia felt a chill sweep instantly through her whole body.
It might sound strange to say, but Emilia had a constitution with no relation to nervousness. Of course, when made to stand before others or discuss something important, her body sometimes felt a little heavy, but the moment she actually put herself into action, she could quickly forget those effects.
She'd been praised often enough, by Subaru and Ram alike, for being quite the imposing figure.

But even that imposing Emilia found herself frozen still before the "Divine Dragon."
It made her feel as though she shouldn't even breathe without the other's permission. That was how overwhelmingly the presence of a true Dragon stood out, how thoroughly it seized control of the world around it.

"— — — —"

Holding her breath, Emilia looked once more over Volcanica's entire form.
The deep blue-tinted scales glittered like jewels, each one seeming sharper than a finely forged treasure sword. The thick fore and hind legs bore claws like blackened stone, and the face — much like an Earth Dragon's — carried a sense of longevity, golden eyes that had gazed upon an unbelievable stretch of years. Two thick, great horns grew from its head, white as freshly squeezed milk.
The "Divine Dragon's" body was perhaps fifteen or sixteen meters long. It wasn't standing, but crouched in place with wings and tail folded, so its exact size was hard to tell. And yet, even in that posture, it was this enormous — no wonder it couldn't fit fully within the tower.
It was surely for Volcanica's sake that the first floor had been built as an open space.

"...The third floor was the 'Sage,' the second floor Reid, and the first floor Volcanica."

『— — — —』

"Could it be that all of the Three Great Heroes from long ago are involved in the tower's 'trial'?"

Looking back over the content of the "trials" so far, Emilia found a common thread among them.
The "Three Great Heroes" — three heroes who, long ago, had devoted themselves to sealing the "Witch of Envy" and gone down in the history of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica. Shaula, Reid, and Volcanica were exactly that.
Granted, Shaula had said the accomplishment actually belonged to Flugel, Reid had been terribly violent and foul-mouthed, and Volcanica wasn't even human, but a Dragon.
Even so, if all three of them were connected to this place —

"Still being such good friends after hundreds of years — that's so, so lovely."

If promises and bonds, things like that, were what connected the three heroes, then that seemed like an awfully wonderful thing to Emilia.
Volcanica had originally been a being who exchanged a "Covenant" with the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, and in fact, records showed that decades ago, when a black dragon had rampaged within Lugunica's borders, Volcanica had wielded its power then too.

Emilia was fond of beings who placed importance on promises.
She thought it was a very good thing to properly keep one's promises. Subaru meant a great deal to her, and she trusted him, but the way he sometimes failed to keep promises — she thought that part of him was hopeless.
What would she do if Beatrice started imitating him like that?
Emilia didn't want to scold either Subaru or Beatrice for breaking promises.

"Ah, no good. This isn't the time to be thinking about that. ...Um, Volcanica! I came here to take the 'trial'! The first floor's 'trial'! I'll take it on!"

『— — — —』

"I don't know how difficult a 'trial' it'll be, but... please, hurry! If I don't do my best, Subaru and the others will be in trouble. Bring it on, whatever it is!"

Slapping her own cheeks with both hands, Emilia steadied her wavering resolve.
Unexpectedly, it was thinking of Subaru and Beatrice that had served as the trigger. Even if those two broke a promise and got scolded by Emilia for it, that was a story for the future, sometime after today.
To reach that future, Emilia needed to hold her ground here.

『— — — —』

The silent "Divine Dragon" stared fixedly at Emilia with those golden eyes.
Without exaggeration, they were eyes of such depth she felt she might be sucked right in. It had been four hundred years since the battle against the "Witch of Envy" and the forging of the Covenant with the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica — but the years this great dragon had lived surely stretched far longer than those four hundred years.
Perhaps approaching a thousand years — that pair of eyes had gazed upon the world for that long.
What Emilia's presence looked like to such a "Divine Dragon," she had no way of knowing. She didn't know, but —

"I'm used to being judged by people. There are people who dislike me just for being a Half-Elf, and there were mean ones too, like Echidna... but there are also people, properly, who believe in me — Subaru, Ram, Beatrice, and the others."

As she spoke, Emilia touched the crystal stone at her chest with a finger.
Even now, her precious family member, still sleeping, had been the very first to affirm Emilia, who had so often been viewed through such prejudiced eyes.
Starting with that family member, there were companions within this tower who accepted Emilia for who she was.

"So no matter what kind of look you give me, I won't mind!"

Before the "Divine Dragon," which had briefly overwhelmed her, Emilia declared this with fierce conviction.
The soul that had nearly been crushed by the difference in presence, the limbs that had trembled as if about to shrink away, the being called Emilia that had nearly been swallowed by a presence that knew eons of time — she would not lose to any of it now.
—She absolutely could not afford to lose.

『— — — —』

Clenching her fists tight, her violet eyes shining with spirited resolve, Emilia stood firm.
Bathing fully in Emilia's gaze, Volcanica slowly blinked. Then, moving its dragon jaw with majestic grandeur —

『— Thou who hast reached the tower's summit. One who treads the first floor, petitioner of omnipotence.』

"— — — —"

『— I am Volcanica. By the ancient Covenant, I shall question the resolve of the one who would reach the summit.』

"...Huh?"

Holding her breath, having braced herself for whatever difficult question Volcanica might pose, Emilia tilted her head at these weighty words, exhaled together with the dragon's breath.
It was because the content sounded familiar.

"Um, wasn't that the same thing you told me just a moment ago? I'm the one who came to the first floor, and you're Volcanica... is that right?"

『— — — —』

"...Ah! Could it be because I haven't introduced myself? I'm sorry. I'm Emilia, just Emilia. It's a bit of a bother when people ask me to prove it, since lately there happen to be a lot of people who don't remember me, but — I really am Emilia!"

『— — — —』

"...Is that still no good?"

She'd thought perhaps skipping the greeting had displeased it, but even after properly introducing herself again, Volcanica's reaction remained no better.
Had this been Subaru, he'd have despaired to the point of thinking he'd been stripped of his qualification to take the first-floor "trial" just for skipping the initial greeting — but Emilia didn't think that way.
Even when something bad happened, Emilia was the type to believe that if you apologized, the other party wasn't so heartless as to hold it against you forever.
So this time too, she didn't think her own lack of manners was what had bought Volcanica's silence.
What Emilia considered instead was the possibility that Volcanica wasn't angry at all. And if that were the case, it might actually be far worse than anger.
Because —

"Could it be..."

Staring steadily back into those golden eyes, Emilia stepped forward, hesitant.
One step, two steps, advancing across ground so close to the sky, closing the distance to the "Divine Dragon" Volcanica. Toward a presence whose very breathing felt solemn, Emilia approached with full composure.
Then, gently reaching out a hand, she touched the scales of its foreleg.

"— Cold."

The scale beneath her touch was cold as ice, or like chilled steel.
Just how much time spent standing still would it take for something to lose this much warmth?
It wasn't a sign of biological "death." What long-lasting stagnation robbed a being of wasn't only the body's vitality.

『— Thou who hast reached the tower's summit. One who treads the first floor, petitioner of omnipotence.』

"— — — —"

『— I am Volcanica. By the ancient Covenant, I shall question the resolve of the one who would reach the summit.』

Looking down at Emilia touching its foreleg, Volcanica repeated the words once more.
Repeated with a frequency one could only call "over and over," these words of greeting. They weren't meant to reproach Emilia for lacking proper etiquette.
Volcanica's eyes, gazing into such depths, seemed to reflect Emilia and yet didn't reflect her at all.
The reason for that was clear.

"Could it be... you're so old that you've forgotten about the 'trial'...?"

Not the "death" of the body, but the "death" of the mind, had come even for the long-lived dragon.
And that, for Emilia, who needed to overcome the first floor's "trial," would come crashing down as a challenge perhaps even greater than the true test of the first floor itself.

The Great Library Pleiades, First Floor "Maia" Trial.

—Trial, begin.
