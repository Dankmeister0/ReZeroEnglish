Arc 6 Chapter 59: "The White World Laughs"

――That's that, this is this.

The discussion settled along those lines, and Subaru and Julius came away sharing a peculiar sensation. Honestly, Subaru had no confidence he'd managed to meet Julius's complicated feelings halfway. Surely there had been better words, a more perfect approach, a more graceful way of handling it — that's what he couldn't help thinking.

Something that wasn't a crude conclusion like "that's that, this is this," but more refined words — a way of overwriting an absurd reality with something better.

"But it's like you. For better or worse."

"...Yeah? How much overlap 'like me' has with the me from before yesterday is honestly pretty relevant to my identity right now."

"Speaking in terms of establishing one's sense of self, I too was greatly shaken not so long ago. Shall I offer you some advice, as a senior who's tasted the same hardship? ――That's that, this is this."

"Shut up!"

Julius, smiling thinly and needling Subaru about their conversation a moment ago, drew a bark from him. Julius merely shrugged at the retort, unshaken as ever. So it seemed some genuine progress had been made in patching up his feelings, at least a little. The pitcher's words had been clumsy and unrefined, but perhaps it helped that the catcher was skilled.

At any rate, while the two of them were going back and forth like that――

"――Master～, are you done yet～?"

"Oh? Shaula?"

From behind, a face peeked over the top of the stairs to the third level, calling out to them: Shaula. Peering down at the floor below, she let her black braid dangle from her shoulder as she pressed her ample chest against the floor to look at them.

"Why're you being so reserved? Is that even your character?"

"Nyah, whenever Master's talking to someone one-on-one, it's usually about something pretty core, right? And ages ago you told me that when I'm around, it breaks the mood, so stay back. I've been keeping to that. Did you forget...... oh, Master, you forgot everything!"

"Getting to the core of things and breaking the mood, huh......"

At Shaula's blithe laughing words, Subaru knit his brow suspiciously. Ignoring his reaction, Shaula went "hup" and leapt down the stairs in a single bound. Clearing more than twenty steps in one jump, she landed on the floor without a sound. She scurried right over to Subaru's side and hugged his arm to her chest.

"I've told you a bunch of times, cut that out. Emilia-chan's gonna get the wrong idea."

"Fufu. Emilia-sama, is it?"

"Don't 'fufu' me. There's at least some possibility, isn't there? Even if it's some ridiculously slim chance, like finding a needle dropped in the desert, it's not zero."

Saying so, Subaru forcibly pried Shaula's arm loose. At that treatment, Shaula puffed her cheeks in discontent, going "aww."

"Master's so mean. Is this any way to treat a girl starved for warmth?"

"If you're starved for warmth, go cling to Meili or Beatrice. They're both kids, so their body temperature runs high...... Beatrice's four hundred years old and still pretty warm, though."

Weighing the shocking fact he'd just learned against the fact he'd already confirmed multiple times by hugging her, Subaru trembled at the sheer magnitude of Beatrice's little-girl power. Conclusion: no matter how many years you've lived, a little girl is a little girl.

"Hmph. Well, to such a cold-hearted Master, I won't give my hard-earned report. Teacher's-pet strike. Management should listen to the voices of labor!"

"Ah, fine, fine, I get it. I'll look into improving working conditions in good faith, so let's hear the report. What happened?"

"How likely you'll actually follow through is dubious, ya know. But but, I'm an honest, adorable pupil, so I'll answer! ――Reid's book has been found."

"......Huh?"

Bouncing, her braid and shoulders bobbing along, Shaula said it with a smile. At the abruptness of her words, Subaru's eyes went wide. Julius, likewise, blinked in surprise.

"Miss Shaula, what did you just say?"

"I saaaid, Reid's book has been found. Honestly, a book with a scumbag's name like that on it, I think it'd be best to just burn it right away. Don't you agree, Master?"

"Ha, ha, ha......"

"Hahaha? Master, are you laughing grandly?"

Bending at the waist, Shaula peered up at Subaru from below. Meeting her innocent, guileless black eyes, Subaru opened his mouth. And shouted.

"――Say it sooner!!"

"Ah! Subaru, look! Reid's book was found!"

Noticing Subaru's face peek out from the stairs, Emilia's face lit up brightly, and she declared it in her silver-bell voice. What she pointed to happily was the thick tome Ram held in her arms. It seemed that was Reid's Book of the Dead. That was a remarkably quick find.

A library stretching as far as the eye could see, packed with an enormous collection of books――no telling from what era it dated, but if this really recorded every dead person in this world, then without exaggeration, the collection felt as vast as the stars themselves.

"You managed to find the one you were after out of all that? While I was doing Julius's counseling... therapy? Aromatherapy? That kind of thing."

"Counseling......? Well, I'm not quite sure, but, fufu, impressive, right? But I'm not the one who found it."

Emilia puffed out her chest, insisting the credit wasn't hers. What that meant became clear soon enough from her next action. Emilia gently pushed forward the dark-blue-haired girl clinging to her waist.

"The one who found it was Meili. Quite the achievement, right?"

"Don't hear 'quite the achievement' much these days...... but anyway, Meili found it! That's a real achievement, all right! Nice work."

Taking up Emilia's words, Subaru praised the unexpected contributor. Meili, who'd had her arm around Emilia's slender waist and had been having her head petted, looked away bashfully at Subaru's words.

"I-It's nothing special. It's just, it was a book that caught my eye, so I happened to mention it. Found it just a little faster, that's all, not a big deal, I think."

"Don't talk nonsense. When you've actually done something yourself, you're allowed to be proud of it. Well done, Meili. That's already knocked the minus of trying to shove me off a cliff back down to zero!"

"You can wipe that out that easily!?"

Meili's eyes went wide in shock. Subaru rubbed her head roughly, without any restraint. "My hair's getting messed up!" Meili complained, unimpressed, but Subaru's cheeks relaxed into a grin.

"It's fine. It was only an attempt, so let's call it made up for by a big achievement."

"――――"

"So, about Reid's book...... nobody's looked at it yet, right?"

At Subaru's smiling question, Meili seemed to want to say something, but pressed her lips shut instead. Catching that hesitation of Meili's at the edge of his vision, Subaru turned the topic toward the discovered book for the moment.

The volume Ram held――a crest drawn on its spine, in characters Subaru couldn't read. But at a glance, at least, there was no uncanny sense of Reid emanating from the book itself.

"No matter how unconventional the guy himself was, doesn't mean the book recording him is gonna be flashy too, huh."

"If the binding were pointlessly gaudy, or the title lettering aggressively self-assertive, it would have made things easier, wouldn't it. All the more reason we're grateful Meili found it."

"See, look, Meili! Even Ram's praising you! Ram, of all people! This Ram! You should be happy too—guh."

"Be quiet."

Jabbed in the side with a finger, Subaru writhed on the spot in agony. Having silenced Subaru thus, Ram gave a light shake to the book in her arms.

"There's the matter of Barusu. If we carelessly rush ahead and end up like Barusu, losing memories, that would be terrible. So, we haven't shown it to anyone yet."

"W-well, it's not exactly confirmed that my memory getting wiped was because of the book......"

"Hah!"

Even to himself, the statement rang hollow, and Ram snorted it away. In truth, there was hardly any possibility that the memory loss and the Book of the Dead were unrelated. Even with the target book found, it was inevitable there'd be friction over how to handle it.

"So, the book's been safely found. Now the question is how to use it, right?"

Following the same line of thought as Subaru, Beatrice brought that up. At her words, a tension overlaying the earlier joy of discovery pressed down on everyone in the library. Mishandle it, and it was a book that could well blow away someone's memories. That was no different from being told to drink some unidentified, suspicious medicine. Some reluctance was only natural.

"Let's talk through a few risks we can guess at."

"Risks we can guess at...... is there such a thing, Echidna?"

"Only inferences from what's happened and fragmentary information, mind you."

Saying so, Echidna shrugged and raised a hand. Holding up her index finger, she said:

"First, the danger of the Book of the Dead...... as Natsuki-kun's current state plainly shows, there's the possibility of losing one's own memories. From what Natsuki-kun's told us, it seems the memories that spill out are fragmentary...... or in this case, would it be more accurate to say the memories that remain are the fragmentary part?"

Between the amount lost and the amount remaining, the former was overwhelmingly larger. Echidna's words were correct. However, there was a slight discrepancy with the facts here — Subaru was using the memory loss and retention as camouflage to hide the truth about Return by Death.

In truth, the very first time he'd genuinely lost his memories, Subaru had lost everything――with the caveat of "since arriving in this other world"――all of it, gone. Given this, it was best to assume that whatever memory got erased received no special mercy. The fact that he hadn't lost his very self made his memory loss, relatively speaking, the milder case.

If it wasn't just a gap in episodic memory, but he'd lost even himself. The thought alone was chilling.

――At the same time, a question also sprouted within Subaru: why hadn't the memories of his original world, rather than this other world, been lost instead?

"As for why the memories vanished, that too allows for speculation. First, that losing memories is the price paid for reading the Book of the Dead. This is hard to credit, given the difference in symptoms between Natsuki-kun, who read one book, and Julius, who likewise read one."

"...... I don't much want to think about it, but there's a chance she's lost a small amount of memory too, I suppose. If the amount of reading is proportional to the amount of memory lost, that inference could hold, I suppose."

"Agreed. In other words, the difference between my case and Subaru's circumstances would be the difference in the number of books read...... which would line up with what Miss Meili witnessed of Subaru's actions last night."

"――――"

Echidna's inference met Beatrice's counterargument, which Julius then affirmed. Carefully following the conversation among the sharper minds present, Subaru nodded in agreement with the reasoning.

"So basically, you're saying the amount of books read might be tied to memory loss?"

"That's one way to look at it, is all I'm saying. If we accept this inference, then the ones who ought to look through Reid's Book of the Dead would rather be those of us who've never read a Book of the Dead at all. Natsuki-kun and Julius, who've already read one, might be at risk."

"――――"

The opinion that the experienced should read it, and the opinion that the inexperienced should read it, stood in direct contradiction. Both lines of reasoning had merit, and it was hard to say either was wrong. But what nagged at Subaru was――

"So then, what about my case, where my memory's already been wiped once? If the condition for memory loss is the accumulation of information from the Book of the Dead, am I reset, or not?"

"That's a grave concern. Having Barusu's memory slip away again, and having to explain everything all over again...... just imagining it is chilling."

"I'm chilled too, but watch how you say it!"

"Mm...... that, I'm really worried about too. I hate the thought of Subaru forgetting all sorts of things about me again."

Emilia and Ram each affirmed Subaru's worry from their own angle. In truth, it remained unresolved. Whether the person who'd read more would vanish, or whether the one who'd read less was more at risk — that, no one knew. Though he couldn't say it aloud, Subaru also carried the memory, from the previous loop, of having read Meili's Book of the Dead. Did that count as one of the books he'd read this time around too?

"...... What? Are you looking at me like you think maybe I should read it, as atonement or something? Ow!"

"No way I'd think that. Say something that stupid, I'll hit you."

"That's abuse. My treatment as a prisoner's worse than back at the mansion."

Catching the glances aimed her way, Meili had made an unfunny joke, and Subaru scolded her for it. Meili puffed her cheeks at that, grabbing Emilia's and Shaula's hands and hiding behind the two of them. Rather mercenary of her, but honestly, kids were more endearing when they acted like that. Subaru gave a wry smile at Meili's attitude and turned back to Echidna.

"So, any other thoughts?"

"Let's see. Whether it's us inexperienced ones, who have more room than Julius given it's just one book, who read it, or Julius, the experienced one, who reads it, or perhaps Natsuki-kun, who's already possibly overflowed once......"

"Mind if I say something real self-serving? ――I think it's best if I'm the one who reads it."

"Subaru......"

Before Echidna, listing candidates, Beatrice gripped Subaru's hand at those words. Meeting the girl's gaze, more colored by worry than anxiety, Subaru winked.

"This isn't the time for jokes, I suppose. Subaru, your memories......"

"No, of course I don't wanna lose my memories. But in terms of risk management, this call makes the most sense. However you slice it, I'm the one least likely to end up putting everyone else in danger if my memory gets wiped. I'm the weakest one here, after all."

Well, he'd have liked to say he wasn't weaker than Meili at least, but it was common knowledge he couldn't beat a single other person in this group. Easy enough to restrain, and since he'd already lost his memory once before, easy enough to handle the aftermath too. The problem was that Subaru also had four loops' worth of memories he mustn't lose, piled up inside him. So――

"I'm not going in planning to forget. But we're a team. Everyone needs to shoulder some role, for the sake of everyone else."

"――――"

"Julius is the combat role, Echidna's the intellectual, Ram's the sharp-tongued one, Meili's the cute one, Beatrice is the cute one, Emilia-chan's the beautiful-girl heroine, and Shaula's the gravure-scene role — think of it that way, and this is the role I should take."

"Somehow it feels like there were an awful lot of useless positions in there......"

"That's not true at all! Scene transitions, fade-to-blacks, fanservice scenes are important! I'll strip for Master and for the sake of art!"

"Nah, if you strip any more than that I'm gonna back away, so don't push yourself."

"The ladder's been pulled out from under me!"

That this was Subaru's own particular way of trying to put everyone at ease — the people gathered here understood that much. The first to sigh at Subaru's indulging in it, leaning on that understanding, was Beatrice, still holding his hand. She let out a long breath, fixed Subaru with a narrow-eyed look, and said,

"Honestly, once Subaru digs his heels in like this, he won't budge for anything, I suppose. That part of you hasn't changed one bit, memories or no memories, I suppose. Did you learn nothing from what happened with Meili, I wonder."

"Hehe, but you like that about me, don't you? I'm blushing."

"Don't get carried away, I suppose!"

Whap, whap — Beatrice, her face flushed, smacked him about the waist. Still, there was no real note of denial in her words. And it seemed the same held true for everyone else besides Beatrice.

"Forget your promise to Ram, and Ram will grind you into paste."

"You pick now, right in the middle of a nice little monologue moment, to say that!?"

"Now, was it, I wonder."

Ram gave a small snort through her nose and pressed the book she'd been holding into Subaru's arms. Feeling the heavy, weighty presence of the book settle into his arms, Subaru gave a wry smile.

"Even if I tell you not to be reckless, Subaru, you go and do it anyway. ...... That part of you, I think it's really unfair. I'm always worried."

"About that, all I can say is I'm sorry. But, I think... I worry about you just as much as you worry about me, Emilia-chan. Is that presumptuous of me?"

"I'm happy that Subaru thinks that way about me. So I'm really conflicted. Come back for sure, okay......? — but if I make you promise, you'll just break it, so I won't make you promise."

"Kind of thrilling, having so little credibility with the me from before yesterday. What did I even do?"

Shrugging at Emilia's happy words, Subaru asked the room, "Right?" At that, everyone but Shaula and Meili looked away. Whatever the case with his own faction of Beatrice and Ram, it seemed even Julius and Echidna were well aware of Subaru's promise-breaking habit. A serious repeat offender. At any rate――

"No objections to me being the one who reads it, right?"

"...... In the end, no matter what inference we make, it doesn't go beyond guesswork. If it were possible, I would have liked to choose whichever option carried the fewest bad outcomes for everyone here."

To Subaru, lightly lifting the book, Echidna lowered her brows apologetically. That her words were sincere, and that she'd genuinely tried to consider everyone's wellbeing, was beyond doubt. So Subaru could honestly tell her, "Don't let it weigh on you."

"Well then, I'll go ahead and get this over with. If my memory does end up blown away, freeze me solid right away and lecture me thoroughly, would you."

"No one's going to do something so violent, I suppose......"

"I know. 'Cause you're kind."

Patting the worried Beatrice's head gently, Subaru gave her broad forehead a little push. Beatrice puffed her cheeks in discontent and took a step back. Then, gathering everyone's eyes upon him, Subaru plopped himself down right there, crossed his legs, and let out a long, deep breath.

――On his lap, Reid Astrea's Book of the Dead.

"――――"

Focusing on it, he could feel a distinct malevolence radiating from the book. He'd felt something similar when trying to read Meili's Book of the Dead, but the sense of oppression coming off this book surpassed that. So, whose book you picked up really did change how the reader felt, huh. ――What kind of life was he about to be made to read, he wondered.

And could Subaru's memory withstand it?

"――――"

Placing a hand on the book's cover, Subaru glanced once toward everyone watching over him. Beatrice, Meili, Ram, Echidna, Julius, Shaula — all watching. And then――

"――Subaru."

"Well, here I go. Might be late getting back, so go ahead and eat without me."

"...... Idiot."

Seen off by Emilia's smile, Subaru flipped open the cover of the Book of the Dead. In that instant, the characters inscribed in the book seemed to rise up, and he was struck by the illusion that information was being driven straight from his eyes into his brain. In an instant, his consciousness was drawn into the book――

――his awareness cut away from the library, into darkness.

――The sensation from when he'd read Meili's Book of the Dead had grown rather hazy by now.

The sights he'd seen, the trajectory of the life she'd walked, remained reasonably vivid. But his own memory of himself, during the time he'd actually been watching those sights — to put it plainly, he'd been forced to become one with the person named in the Book of the Dead, reliving their subjective thoughts and scenery. In short, the journey of tracing through the contents of a Book of the Dead was a merging with the other person.

In that moment, the Natsuki Subaru tracing the contents of the book had been "Meili Portroute." That, presumably, was why a strange self-awareness, carrying Meili's shadow as "I," had lingered at the edge of Subaru's consciousness ever since.

If that was the Book of the Dead's effect, then what Subaru should be seeing in this moment was Reid Astrea's life — the subjective world of a man with an incomprehensible way of thinking.

What he thought, what he liked, what he hated, what he loved, what he despised, what he did.

He was supposed to become one with Reid Astrea's philosophy, made to witness that life laid bare. Which was why Subaru noticed the anomaly right away.

――The place he was in now was, clearly, not Reid's past.

"...... Ah?"

He stood in a white, white place. All around, a vast, boundless white expanse of empty unreality spread out, and Subaru, at a loss for where he was, stood dumbfounded.

He looked at his hands. His feet. Turning his neck, he found he had a torso, a waist too. In other words, Subaru's body was there. Already, at that point, it didn't line up with what had happened in Meili's Book of the Dead. He'd been thrown into a mismatched, unnatural situation.

From what he could tell, Subaru's clothing was the same as when he'd decided to read the Book of the Dead. Was that the result of Subaru's own mind recognizing "the present state" as taking this form, or had some other will — something like the book's own spirit — been at work, reproducing Subaru in this shape? He didn't want to believe that the moment he'd read the book, his physical body itself had somehow been drawn in――

"――Oh my? If it isn't Onii-san. Back again already?"

"――!"

Subaru's shoulders jumped at the sound of a voice — not his own, someone else's. The voice came from behind him. Reflexively, Subaru dove forward, rolled, and spun around warily. At Subaru's convulsive reaction, the figure behind him blinked in surprise.

"――You're......"

Laying eyes on the one before him, Subaru muttered, his bewilderment and confusion plain. It was a completely unexpected encounter, someone he never could have imagined — a total stranger.

Standing there was a girl Subaru had never seen before. Pale, translucent hair the color of gold thread, grown long — truly long. It spread across the white floor, filling the space at the standing girl's feet like a golden sea. Large, round blue eyes, and limbs white and smooth like translucent porcelain. And yet her body was wrapped in an ill-fitting garment that looked like scraps of coarse cloth stitched together, spoiling the otherwise beautiful impression.

"――――"

A girl he'd never seen. That should have been the case. And yet Subaru narrowed his eyes at the sight of her, roughly rubbing his eyelids with the back of his hand. It was the gesture of someone trying to clear a blurred vision, but the girl before him remained unchanged. Looking again, still — a girl he didn't know. ――Though something, faintly, seemed to stir at the back of his memory.

"Calmed down a bit, Onii-san?"

"This place is...... no, who are you? Which should I ask first?"

"Greedy, aren't you, Onii-san. But I don't dislike it, that honesty of yours, blurting out that you want to ask both. We love greedy people, you know."

So saying, the girl bared her teeth at bewildered Subaru, her lips splitting sideways into a laugh. Yes — a laugh was the only way to describe that smile.

She looked to be thirteen or fourteen, though there was something even younger about her impression. Combined with her well-formed features, she was, without a doubt, a girl a smile ought to suit. And yet, to Subaru's eyes, her smile looked malevolent.

As though instinct itself sensed that this girl's soul had trampled countless lives underfoot. And as Subaru trembled before her, she declared:

"This is the lonely, white terminus of souls. The cradle of Od Lagna. ――The Corridor of Memories."

"The Corridor of...... Memories?"

"That's right, the Corridor of Memories. And――"

At the unfamiliar term, Subaru's eyes widened. Looking satisfied at Subaru's reaction, the girl said it.

Malice, shaped like a girl, laughing, said it.

"――We are the Witch Cult's Sin Archbishop of Gluttony, Rui Arneb."

"――――"

"Well, it'll be short again this time, but let's get along, Onii-san."
