Arc 3 Chapter 25: "Alone"

Being happy — what does that even mean?

Not changing, staying the same — that's one definition of happiness, or so Subaru thinks. That tomorrow comes unchanged from today, that today comes unchanged from yesterday, that days go on without ever changing — that, Subaru thinks, is what happiness is.

Of course, nothing can go on forever without changing. The passage of time breeds change, and through attrition, arrives at one of two ends: evolution or decay.

—So then, having arrived in this other world, is he moving forward? Or is he retreating backward?

If change is the word for it, there's no change greater than the one that knocked him out of the ordinary and into the extraordinary. Changes in environment, changes in circumstance, changes in standing, changes in way of life — Natsuki Subaru has feared them, hated them, shunned them more than anything else.

That's why, even in a world that had changed past all recognition, he'd been desperate, frantic, to find something that wouldn't change. Not wanting to let go of what he'd once gained, clinging to it, holding on with everything he had, and even so—

Even so — I...

When he came to, Subaru frowned at the unfamiliar ceiling looking down at him.

It wasn't the ceiling of Roswaal Manor, whose pointless extravagance he'd grown used to, nor the plain but well-kept ceiling of an inn. And certainly not the grimy, dear ceiling of his own room.

Feeling a faint, meaningless sort of wonder at the needless height of the ceiling, Subaru drifted for a few seconds in that hazy, half-formed state of consciousness. For someone who woke up as cleanly as Subaru usually did, that muddled just-woken feeling was a foreign one. He savored the small deviation from his norm, groping his way toward buried memory.

What had happened before he fell asleep? And where, in the first place, was this place?

A throbbing ache pulsed at his temples, and his half-open eyes welled faintly with tears. Whether that was from the pain or simply a byproduct of waking up, he couldn't say. He wiped the welling drops away gently with a raised sleeve, and then Subaru noticed something strange about his right wrist, caught in the corner of his eye.

Peeking out from his sleeve cuff was his right wrist — and on it, a garish scar he had no memory of. Carved into both the inside and outside of his wrist, it came with a faint, lingering numbness—

"...I remember now."

How pathetically he must have been beaten to end up with a scar like that. And the fact that the scar remained meant—

"...So I didn't die."

Subaru didn't notice how arrogant, how presumptuous, it was to say those words aloud. Nor, further, did he notice what it was he was unconsciously hoping for.

Just then—

"Subaru, are you awake?"

That silver-bell voice, one that ought to tickle the ear pleasantly, now felt terribly uncomfortable to hear.

Tilting his head, Subaru turned his gaze toward the source of the voice. Beside the bed he lay in, Emilia was walking toward him.

Her walking figure looked no different from how she'd appeared earlier in the Royal Selection hall. Her outfit, white-based, lacked the gaudiness of nobility, but suited well the pure, clean bearing she carried herself with. The white robe that had become so familiar whenever she went out was folded neatly now, held in her arms.

Comparing her appearance to the orange sunlight slanting in through the window, Subaru judged that not much time had passed. A few hours, probably. Given that the Royal Selection had begun before noon, he'd been unconscious for maybe four hours at most.

"How are your wounds? Ferris did the healing, so I don't think anything's off, but..."

Emilia drew close, worry in her eyes. When her amethyst gaze fell on Subaru's wrist, she narrowed her eyes painfully at the ghastliness of the scar left there. Even though her gaze was one of concern for his condition, Subaru felt as though something he didn't want seen was being seen, and quietly hid his right hand behind his back.

"Ah, ah, well, at a glance I can't really find anything wrong. I mean, seriously, even the feel of my teeth — which were supposed to be broken — being back is kind of terrifying."

He worked his tongue around inside his mouth, shuddering at the sensation of front teeth that should have been gone. He regretted having been unconscious during the actual treatment. Had the broken teeth been regenerated, or reconstructed from scratch? Beyond the state of his mouth, he could tell that his injuries all over his body had been thoroughly treated, and Subaru marveled anew at the sheer omnipotence of this world's healing magic.

If broken bones, lacerations, and the retirement of permanent teeth could all be fully healed in four hours, hospitals back in his original world would be out of business.

"No wait, hold on. I can't imagine it can do anything about disease. Come to think of it, hospitals still have the edge there, at least a little—"

"Water-attribute magic works on things inside the body too, like illness, so I don't think you need to worry about that, Subaru."

"Seriously, magic-is-omnipotent theory confirmed. Farewell, Big White Tower. Say hi to BJ for me."

He tossed off the idle joke, trying to get the dryness out of his mouth and get things back to normal. But Emilia seemed to have sensed the undercurrent of his true feelings anyway, and the sorrow mixed into her gaze didn't shift at all. And of all things, being pitied was what he could least bear right now.

"Um, so, come to think of it, did the Royal Selection talks wrap up okay?"

Trying to avoid that gaze, and the silence, Subaru offered up a topic. Emilia gave a small nod and replied, "Yes."

"Mostly. We'd already said what we needed to say to each other in the hall, so all that was left after that was really just the fine details of the Royal Selection. It ended with Roswaal basically just nodding along the whole time."

When it came to the finer points, it was probably more than Emilia — still in the middle of learning statecraft — could handle comfortably. Catching a note in her voice that sounded like she was lamenting her own inadequacy, Subaru noticed himself feeling relieved at that. Relief born of a pathetic kind of empathy — that Emilia, too, had felt out of her depth in that Royal Selection hall, had felt her own grief.

As if to cover up the dark feeling that had welled up inside him, Subaru lifted his face and, averting his eyes quickly, spoke fast.

"So, where'd Roswaal-sama go? He felt pretty like part of the furniture in the hall too."

"Once his duty as my sponsor was done, he went off to his job as court mage this time. He's like that, but apparently he's relied on here. ...Not that I'm one to talk, since I can't do anything without Roswaal."

Quite unlike the way she'd boldly asserted her own views in the hall, Emilia's thinking now was somehow downcast and weak. If she was comparing herself to the other candidates and feeling low as a result, Subaru thought she was undervaluing herself far too much.

Certainly, if education received, or birth itself, were made conditions of evaluation, Emilia's position was quite poor for the sole reason of her being a half-elf. But her argument — that these things shouldn't be weighted so heavily, that she wanted to be judged on how she'd conduct herself going forward — had been coherent, and there had been something in her bearing at the time that had visibly impressed even that stiff, stubborn old man.

The things she lamented lacking in herself could be acquired later, in any quantity. The thing most necessary for her to seek the throne — Subaru believed she'd had it from the very start.

So Subaru thought her grief was misplaced, and that what she lacked in taking on the Royal Selection wasn't anything like that. And, as if afraid of her becoming aware of that—

"Well then, once Roswaal's talks over there wrap up, let's head straight back to the inn. Gotta collect Rem and hash out what to do next. Oh, is there still stuff to do here at the castle?"

"Subaru."

"If not, good. No telling what eyes and ears are set up around here, so ideally we'd get all the way back to the mansion. And there we can talk more in detail... no, actually, since we've come all the way to the royal capital, maybe we should focus first on winning over influential people..."

"Subaru..."

"No, actually, come to think of it, maybe it'd be better to exchange some terms with the other royal candidates while we're here. Timing and method are the tricky part..."

"—Subaru!"

Emilia called out sharply to Subaru, who was rattling off a barrage of stopgap words at speed. Startled, he cut the barrage off, and Subaru met the gaze he'd been avoiding. Steady, straight on, her violet eyes were looking right at him.

"—Let's talk."

Quiet, but so unshakeable that it landed on Subaru with heavy weight.

※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※

Sitting up, using the bed as a makeshift chair, Subaru faced Emilia, who leaned her back against the wall near the entrance, clutching the robe in both arms in a tight grip. That grip was proof, however unconscious, that her fingers already knew the conversation about to begin wouldn't be one they could get through with easy smiles.

"There's a lot I wanted to ask... but earlier wasn't really the place for it, so I couldn't. ...Really, a lot."

"...Ah, well, yeah, I bet."

Groping for where to even begin, Emilia's lips trembled hesitantly. Subaru could read her hesitation, her wavering, as clearly as if he could touch it. After all, it was his own doing that had caused all this. Every bit of it must have been beyond anything she could have imagined, and there must have been so much she wanted explained.

So if he were to put it into words, what Emilia ought to be asking wasn't for an accounting of everything Subaru had done today — it was for the truth behind it. And to that question, Subaru could give one answer, without shame, immediately. But—

"Um, so then... why did you end up fighting Julius, of all things?"

The topic she chose wasn't the one Subaru had hoped for. And of all things, it was exactly the kind that made his brow furrow at just being asked. Naturally, a bitter smile rose at the fact that she'd chosen, even while picking her words carefully, to call it a "fight."

"...What's with that super sour face?"

"No, it's nothing. Just, thinking back, I can't really say what happened was some elegant thing you could dignify with a cool word like 'fight,' so, y'know."

Given how one-sidedly he'd been beaten, it would be more accurate to call it a massacre. Or, as Julius himself had put it, something more like "chastisement."

Whatever Subaru's own thoroughly soured feelings about it, Emilia's expression turned pained at his answer. It was only natural for her to be startled — Subaru, who would normally answer with some quip, had said that with naked self-mockery instead. Her pale eyes clouded with worry, she said,

"There must have been some reason, right? Knowing you, Subaru, it must have been something important..."

"A reason, huh..."

Julius had appeared before Subaru as he sat crushed in the waiting room. When Subaru had been lured along to the training grounds at his invitation, he'd judged instantly that this was payback for his rudeness in the hall. He'd also read, more or less, the gap in strength between himself and his opponent Julius. Ordinarily, Subaru's personality was the type to make an excuse — a stomachache, say — and slip away, quietly foiling the other man's intentions. And in fact, some part of him had thought that was exactly what he should do.

And yet, as a matter of fact, Subaru had taken up a wooden sword right there and challenged an opponent he had no chance of beating — and as a result, ended up discarded like trash. Why had he done such a thing? The answer to that was—

"I wanted to land one hit back."

"...Eh?"

"In the hall, in the waiting room, he... that bastard said I wasn't... good enough. I know that. I'm well aware of it myself. That I'm not strong enough — I knew that better than anyone could tell me. But even so!"

He raised his face. He looked up at the silver beauty standing before him, at her bewildered eyes.

"I wanted to show him. That I'm not completely worthless. No — that's not it either. I just... wanted to stand beside you. If I could show him even a scrap of that, I thought I'd... be a little better, maybe..."

His words wouldn't come together, and Subaru hung his head against the raging emotions churning through his mind, clenching the fists resting on his knees, biting his lip. He hated himself for not being able to put it into words better. If he could just hurl every last smoldering feeling in his chest out exactly as it was, he wouldn't have to feel this frustrated.

"Subaru..."

"It was pride. That's all it was. I'm pathetic, useless, a burden — he was trying to cut me away from you, and I hated him for it... I wanted to make him take it back, so I challenged him."

In the end, he thought, that was really all it was. Julius, who had denounced Subaru more fiercely than anyone else in that hall, saying he wasn't fit for Emilia. But that wasn't something anyone needed to tell him — Subaru himself understood it better than anyone. He'd been desperately trying to keep that from showing, putting up a front. And he couldn't forgive the man who had so easily torn away the mask he'd worked so hard to maintain. He'd been desperate, somehow, to cover back up what had been exposed to the light. And the result of that, he supposed, was ending up sunk in a pool of blood like that, pathetically.

At Subaru's listless answer, hanging his head, Emilia drew in a small breath. It clearly wasn't the more substantial answer she'd been hoping for. He didn't know what ideal answer she'd wanted. But whatever ideal she'd wanted to believe in, the reality of Subaru's pride had betrayed it. That disappointment slipped faintly from between her lips.

"For... something like that?"

"That 'something' mattered to me...!"

That "something" was, to Subaru, a thing he couldn't yield on. If the people around him just acknowledged that trivial thing, Subaru could hold his head high. If he became conscious of that trivial thing's absence, shame would seize him, and he'd be unable to move. He'd be left unable to catch up to that back walking briskly ahead of him.

But that clinging feeling of Subaru's went unrecognized by Emilia. She covered her mouth with her palm at Subaru's wrung-out answer, and, as if forcing trembling words out through the gaps between her fingers,

"That's... that's the reason you fought like that?"

"...Yeah."

"After going through all that pain, all that suffering, and still?"

"That's right."

"—Even using the magic I told you not to use?"

Face lowered, eyes averted, looking every bit like a sulking child, Subaru's expression stiffened. Trump card, ace in the hole, last resort — whatever way you put it, it had been dismissed before Julius as an immature ploy.

The one certain power Subaru had gained, however imperfectly, since coming to this world. The power he'd relied on more than anything, that second loop — the incident at the mansion. That it had been broken so easily had dealt Subaru no small shock. Almost as if everything about his days since coming to this world had been dismissed wholesale.

"Hey, Subaru, answer me."

"――――"

"Was that really — really the reason you used your magic?"

"...Emilia-tan, you—"

The trembling words pressed a confession out of Subaru, tormented as he was by helplessness. She, asking the question, had no idea how cruel, how merciless, an act that was. So Subaru, voice still weak, unable even to look at her,

"—You wouldn't get it, Emilia."

"――――"

That's what he said.

The moment he said it, Subaru realized this was nothing but a hopeless act of taking it out on someone else. And more than that, the worst possible thing to say — rejecting her stance of trying to understand, severing the connection between their hearts.

Silence fell. Subaru knew his own words were the kind that shut down any further pursuit, and it must have come through to Emilia just as clearly.

He wanted to avoid, more than anything, being pressed any further on the reason for that fight. No matter where he dug, no matter where he looked back, there was no answer Subaru could give that would satisfy what Emilia was looking for. To dig any further into this topic now would do nothing but deepen the wounds on both sides.

"—I see."

A voice like an exhaled breath reached Subaru, who still couldn't lift his face. Closer to a sigh than a spoken word, it was an acceptance of what Subaru had said, and also a sign that she had no intention, for her part, of pursuing the topic any further. Feeling relief at that response, Subaru let the tension out of his shoulders and exhaled. And then, thinking that he needed, at the very least, to make himself useful to her going forward, since he hadn't been able to give her the answer she wanted—

"Subaru, let's talk about what happens from here."

"Hm? Ah, yeah, I was thinking we needed to do that too. For now, I can't really make any calls until I hear the situation more calmly, but first, let's link up with Roz—"

"Roswaal and I will be heading back to the mansion, as early as tomorrow. And you, Subaru, will be staying in the royal capital."

Unable to process the meaning of what she'd just told him, Subaru gave voice to his confusion with a plain "Huh?" To Subaru, tilting his head in incomprehension, Emilia said, in a voice with emotion suppressed as much as possible,

"That was the arrangement from the start, wasn't it? The reason you came to the royal capital, Subaru, was to get your worn-out Gate treated. I've already arranged that with Ferris, so all that's left is for you to receive her treatment and focus on recovering your Gate."

"No, wait a second."

"While you're staying in the royal capital, you'll be looked after by Ferris — or rather, by House Karsten, under Crusch-sama. Rem will stay behind with you too, so don't worry. Once the treatment's safely finished, we'll talk after that."

"So, wait, I said."

Emilia rattled off the plans for what came next quickly. Understanding that his own will had no place in any of it, Subaru raised his voice to call out to her. He raised his face, trying to look her full in the face after having averted his eyes all this while. But—

"Emilia..."

"It's already decided. Focus on your treatment."

Turning her back to him, Emilia headed for the door as if to say that settled the matter. Her expression unreadable, her feelings unconveyed — that Emilia sent a bone-deep chill through Subaru, and he reflexively reached out to grab the hem of her retreating robe. A wave of dizziness from his blood-deprived body swam through his vision, trying to hinder the motion, but somehow his fingers just barely reached her retreating back.

"Why so suddenly? I mean, I—"

"...Because, isn't it that whenever I'm around, Subaru, you push yourself too hard like that?"

To Subaru's pathetic, clinging voice, Emilia answered without turning her face toward him. As Subaru caught his breath at those words, Emilia lifted her face, looking upward,

"It was like that when we first met too. It was like that at the mansion too, with everything that happened. And today too, wasn't it... All of it, every bit, because you're with me, right?"

"You don't have to put it like that..."

To call it sulky would be far too cute a word for it. It was, in plain terms, a way of speaking that carried a clear note of displeasure. At the irony laced with negative feeling — so rare a thing for Emilia — Subaru answered with a weak trailing voice.

The way Emilia put it made it sound like Subaru was getting far too little in return, however you looked at it. The content might be the same as what Subaru himself had said, but the path leading up to it was different.

He'd at least wanted her to understand that much.

"That's not what I'm trying to say. ...I just—"

"Just?"

"I just wanted to do something for you, Emilia-tan, that's all, and so—"

"For... me?"

At the words repeated back to him, Subaru nodded, meaning it as affirmation. It was a gesture she couldn't have seen with her back still turned to him, but the movement must have been conveyed to her by presence alone. With her face still tilted slightly upward, whatever Emilia thought upon hearing it, a few seconds of silence fell between them, and then—

"—For your own sake, isn't it?"

At the ring of the words spoken next, Subaru could do nothing but fall speechless.

"――――"

Silence gave way, and a blankness swept through Subaru's mind. Confusion was pushed aside by incomprehension surging in, and before words could form, before emotion could well up, his heart was above all buffeted by a hollow ache.

He didn't understand what had been said to him. He didn't even understand what he wanted to say. Was he sad, was he in pain, was he frustrated, was he grieved, did he want to be angry, did he want to cry?

"I, I just... for you, that's all..."

He wanted to do things that would make her happy. He wanted to be of help in whatever she hoped for. He wanted to protect her from every cause that might make her sad.

Subaru had believed, without question, that this was the foundation underlying his actions. And he'd believed, unquestioningly, that these actions would surely convey themselves to her, even without words.

That kind of self-satisfied conviction, blind to another's feelings—

"—Wabuh."

Something soft slammed into his face, and Subaru, in his stunned state, let out a startled cry. He reflexively brushed away whatever had struck his face, and caught, in the corner of his vision, the pure white robe with the hawk embroidery, fallen onto the bed. He realized it was the very thing that had been in Emilia's arms until a moment ago — and understood immediately, too, that it had been thrown at him.

But that violent action refused to connect, in his mind, with the figure of Emilia. Even as his reason accepted that it was Emilia who'd thrown it, his feelings refused to acknowledge it.

Because in front of Subaru, Emilia was always gentle, always overflowing with a motherly kind of consideration — and though she was stubborn and refused to admit it herself, she was a girl too good-hearted to ever stop giving of herself to others.

So Subaru couldn't accept the reality in front of him. His mind stalled out at having been dealt such feeble, pathetic violence by a girl he'd been so sure would never do such a thing.

"E-Emilia-tan...?"

Unable to process reality, Subaru called her name and lifted his gaze.

Right in front of him, close enough to reach with an outstretched hand, Emilia still stood. Having turned her back to him until a moment ago, she now faced him again, her long, beautiful silver hair swaying as if to express her emotions, her violet eyes looking straight down at him.

For the first time, he saw those violet eyes shaken by waves of emotion. For the first time, he saw her stiffened face, biting down on lips that seemed about to tremble with violent feeling.

Why was it — an expression, a gaze like that suited her not at all. And yet there was no doubting that both of those were aimed at him.

Even knowing it was an inappropriate thing to feel, Subaru thought her, in that state— Thought her, with her emotions laid bare like that, beautiful.

"Stop lying like that, saying it's all for my sake——!"

The waves of emotion turned to tears, filling those violet eyes. Shaking her head slightly, Emilia, as if expelling everything she'd been holding in,

"Coming to the castle, fighting Julius, using magic... you're saying it's all for my sake? I never once asked for any of that!"

"――――ッ"

"Everything I've ever wanted you to do for me, Subaru, I would have asked for, straight out!"

"――――"

"Hey, do you remember? What I asked of you."

"I... I..."

Having her deny his actions so plainly from her own mouth, Subaru's mind froze solid. So he couldn't search through his hazy memory for her question.

To Subaru, unable to give an answer, Emilia apparently hadn't expected one anyway. She breathed a small sigh, closed her eyes tight, and,

"I asked you to wait at the inn with Rem."

"――――"

"I asked you not to use magic, because I don't know what could happen to your body if you push your Gate any harder."

He remembered both of those "requests." Both had been said out of her concern for his well-being, out of a wish for him to just stay quiet and safe. Subaru had heard them — and then acted anyway. All of it, pushing through his own poor condition, driven by a near-abnormal obsession that he had to act in a way that would save her, no matter what.

Disregarding those requests, he could always paper it over later if he brought back results substantial enough to trample right over them. He hadn't consciously thought it in so many words, but that kind of careless thinking had always been there, underlying everything. But as it turned out, Subaru had disregarded Emilia's requests and, on top of that, failed to produce a single decent result, ending up doing nothing but holding her back.

But even so, even so, the wellspring of his actions, their true beginning, had surely been—

"I know it was wrong not to listen. I'm sorry. Really. I really am sorry. But! No, that's not it. That's not it. I wasn't doing it for myself, I—"

He wanted, at the very least, for her to understand that the feeling at the root of it was genuine. But his tongue seized up as if numbed, refusing to string together any further words.

At his own body's rebellion against his will, Subaru couldn't hide his irritation and covered his mouth with his hand. Emilia watched his pitiful display with a sorrowful expression.

Under that gaze, under the discomforting sense of being trapped by his own doing, Subaru shrank in on himself and said weakly,

"Emilia, don't you... believe in me?"

It was an unbelievably selfish, self-centered, self-satisfied thing to say. A thing that shouldn't have been said. It wasn't the kind of thing someone who, just moments ago, had refused to let the other party even try to understand him, had any right to say.

"I want to believe in you. ...I want to believe in you, Subaru."

Her voice sounded on the verge of tears. In fact, she might well have been crying.

But Subaru didn't have the courage to check. He couldn't muster the courage to raise his face and look the girl he supposedly loved in the eye.

Even though she might be crying, even though he might have made her cry. Even though he'd been running all this time precisely because he didn't want to see that expression on her face, at the single most crucial moment, Natsuki Subaru—

"I want to believe in you... but you're the one who wouldn't let me!"

Her emotions exploded. Calm, rational, and though she hadn't been entirely without moments of anger before, she'd never once let that restraint break and become fully emotional — or so it should have been. But that restraint broken, Emilia, pouring out the overflowing emotion just as it was, said,

"You haven't kept a single promise, Subaru! You promised... and then you broke every single one, so easily, and ended up all the way out here like this, didn't you!?"

Trampled underfoot. The promises they'd made — meaning, trust itself. All under the banner of a justification that held meaning only to himself: that it was all for her sake.

"You won't keep your promises to me, and yet you still want me to believe in you... I can't do that, no matter what you say. I can't...!"

He wanted to shout at the top of his lungs that it wasn't like that. But in reality Subaru's throat only trembled, producing no sound, and his head, as if weighted down with lead, wouldn't move, staying bowed.

Trembling, shedding tears, tossed about by emotion, he had no choice but to turn his back and keep on betraying the girl who surely wanted a sincere answer from him.

And to the hand Emilia still held out to him even now, Subaru, with a childish show of emotion, brushed it aside. At his stubborn attitude, Emilia repeated a few short breaths, and,

"...Hey, Subaru. Why do you try so hard to help me?"

That, surely, was the question Emilia had tried to voice many times before, and hesitated each time. The words reached Subaru without a single stumble, so smoothly that it was clear just how many times, in the space of that short breath, she must have turned it over in her mind already.

She must have watched Subaru, again and again, running himself ragged until he was covered in wounds, laughing it off with an easy grin, or gritting his teeth through the pain, throwing himself into the jaws of death — and held that question in her chest each time, and even so, kept her mouth shut.

So for it to finally come out here, now, in this place — that was simply the inevitable course of things. If she couldn't get it out here, Emilia would have gone on forever keeping that question locked away in her chest, her heart aching without ever understanding why, as Subaru kept wearing himself to the bone in service of her, unchanged.

Subaru realized that Emilia had, clearly, shown him a path to salvation in asking this. He'd assumed every word would ring hollow, that nothing could possibly reach her now, from him, someone who'd trampled their promises underfoot — but even so, this was a question he could answer sincerely.

—Why did Subaru serve Emilia to this extent, even like this? —Why had he been so obsessed with her, ever since coming to this world?

That was—

"Ever since we first met, you've saved me, Subaru. I haven't forgotten what happened at that Loot House. And at the mansion too. It's been like that, all this time, hasn't it. Hey, why? Why do you do so much for me, Subaru?"

That Subaru kept giving so much to her was, to Emilia, utterly incomprehensible. From her point of view, Subaru had appeared out of nowhere, suddenly saved her life, served her devotedly without ever explaining why, and then, the moment she let her guard down, betrayed that trust and did something foolish. That's the kind of incomprehensible existence he must have seemed.

But Subaru had a clear reason. She seemed convinced she'd never done anything for Subaru, but that was far from true. Subaru had been the one continually given to. And all he was doing, desperately, was trying to give back even a fraction of what he'd been given in return. The beginning wasn't with Subaru. The beginning was with her — she was the one who'd given first.

So Subaru's answer was—

"The reason I want to do something for you is because you saved me..."

"I... saved you?"

"That's right."

Suddenly dragged into another world, at a loss with nothing to go on, exposed to violence he had no way to avoid, in a world that might well have ended right then and there.

"You have no idea how much what you did for me back then saved me. But I... I was saved by that more than I can put into words."

What Emilia had done for him back then wasn't just a matter of saving his life.

What Emilia had saved back there wasn't his life — it was Subaru himself. No matter how he tried to put that into words, it was the kind of thing that resisted words. So Subaru could only give some vague, half-formed shape to it and convey it, along with his feelings for her, as best he could.

"I don't understand, Subaru..."

Weakly, Emilia let her voice trail off as if fading away, her lips trembling. As if reaching out to grasp at that fading figure, Subaru said,

"It's okay if you don't understand. But it's true. I was saved by you. So what I'm doing is repaying that debt of being saved... but right now—"

The words he wanted to add — it's not just that —

"—I said I don't understand!"

Didn't reach her, as she shook her head, silver hair flying, her emotions exploding. Tears still in her eyes, Emilia looked at Subaru, breath ragged with violent feeling, shoulders trembling,

"I saved you? ...There's no way that's true. You and I met for the first time at that Loot House — there's no way I had any connection to you before that!"

"That's not, listen..."

"If there was a connection before that, if that's really true, then I... I'd..."

Covering her face with her palms, hiding Subaru from view, Emilia had no ears left to listen. She was completely closing herself off into a shell, and Subaru's voice had no power to stop it.

He had no idea what part of her he'd touched, what nerve. Not knowing didn't excuse him from finding words. So Subaru, driven purely by emotion, pressed on,

"You might not understand, but listen anyway. It's the truth! Since coming to this world, you were the first person I ever—"

At that instant—the world stopped, and Subaru realized he'd touched a forbidden word.

Time froze solid, all motion in the world stalling out. Even the sound of his own racing heartbeat, even Emilia's voice that had been audible until a moment ago, receded, and even the lingering trace of a shrill ringing in his ears faded away, leaving a soundless world.

Subaru couldn't hold back his fury at his own carelessness, and at the shadow of the executioner, blind to the situation.

A shadow is born. Regardless of the fury raging inside Subaru, the merciless executioner shows itself.

The right arm, formed more vividly than before, instantly takes shape from fingertip to shoulder and closes in on Subaru. And likewise formed, the left hand — only as far as the elbow, unbalanced.

The left hand gently, lovingly strokes Subaru's stiffened cheek. The right arm slips into Subaru's tensed chest, touching, as if cherishing, the beating heart that plays out his life.

Teeth clenched, jaw locked in place, agony tears through Subaru's entire body. Pain like being torn apart, a sense of loss violent enough to make him feel he might disappear entirely, suffering that allows no screaming, no writhing — all of it descends one-sidedly as punishment upon Subaru, who broke the taboo the shadow decreed.

How long that agony lasted, he had no way of knowing. Seconds? Minutes? Or hours? Counting time in a world where time itself has stopped is a foolish exercise, one that could never serve as a means of distracting from pain — just a mere game of thought, a mere fluctuation.

The shadow vanishes, and at once the world begins moving again. Emilia, before him, is still caught in the same confusion, and Subaru, who had been desperately resolved to somehow stop it, can only respond with cold sweat pouring off him and ragged breath.

Pain, agony, the shadow of the Curse — all of it obstructs, gets in the way of Subaru's feelings. The words he'd been about to speak tumble back down his throat, and the sincere feeling he'd meant to convey, with nowhere left to go, settles like a leaden weight on both his shoulders.

"...You're still not going to tell me anything."

Emilia's voice struck his eardrum, cold with something like resignation, something like disappointment. Tearful passion, emotion trembling in her chest — all of it surged forward like a wave, churning up Emilia's heart, and swept it away.

And Subaru, the one existence who might have held back that tide, chose silence instead of reaching out a saving hand to her. Even if that wasn't the truth of it, that was all she could feel. And if he tried to deny it, to say that wasn't so, the black shadow would surely close around his organs again.

An anger that had no business surfacing welled up in him. A sorrow with nowhere to go swelled fit to tear his chest apart.

What was he supposed to do? When he tried to convey his feelings sincerely, Emilia wouldn't lend an ear to them. When he tried to explain the entire path that had led here, the shadow of the Curse got in the way, obstructing him.

"That I want you, want to do this for you, it's because you, you saved me first, you..."

"...That's not me, Subaru."

Weakly, haltingly, the words spun out while cowering before the black demonic hand, and Emilia denied them, unmistakable sorrow bleeding into her voice,

"You're just seeing someone who isn't me, inside me."

"No, that's not... that's not it..."

Head bowed, face covered by his palms, Subaru let the denial that couldn't reach her spill out feebly.

Emilia, the girl who had first picked Subaru up when he fell into this world. That memory didn't exist within this Emilia before him. That history, that world, existed only within Subaru. But even so, even so, that had surely, certainly been Emilia.

If the same circumstances were set before her, she would, without a doubt, take the same action. When Subaru, in that back alley, had been on the verge of ending his life in miserable defeat, achieving nothing, about to be extinguished in vain — the moment she saved him would surely, inevitably come again.

Even if it hadn't happened in this world, it had certainly, truly happened. Even in a history where it hadn't happened in this world, Subaru had gone on, endlessly, being saved by her.

There was only gratitude, more than any words could exhaust. And yet there existed no words anywhere in this world sufficient to convey it.

Even so, he'd believed it had reached her. Wanted to believe it. Wanted to convince himself that was why he was allowed to remain by her side.

"Why won't you understand...?"

"...Subaru."

"I thought... if it was you, Emilia-tan, you'd understand, I thought...!"

"The 'me' inside you is amazing, isn't she."

A sad kind of severance, a gulf of the heart, was packed into that single line.

Stunned, Subaru lifted his face, and before him Emilia averted her eyes, turning to the side. What rose to her lips was a lonely smile — one that seemed self-mocking, and also one that seemed to pity Subaru, throwing a childish tantrum over not understanding.

"Everything, all of it, she can understand it all without even being told. Your pain, your sadness, your anger, Subaru — she can feel it all as if it were her own."

"...Ah."

"—If you don't tell me, I won't understand, Subaru."

Denied. Shattered. The illusion crumbled to pieces. His shoulders grew heavy, something unseen weighed down over his entire body, and a violent nausea assailed Subaru. His vision spun in circles, murky, clouded emotion churning in his chest—

Subaru had believed that Emilia would understand everything without a word being said. Subaru had believed that Emilia would feel his pain, his sorrow, just the same as him. Subaru had hoped that Emilia would take in all of his feelings, everything, and accept them.

—That had been, all along, a fleeting, arrogant, self-satisfied imposition.

"My..."

Denied. He'd been denied. The very thing he'd believed in, relied on as a foothold, ever since falling into this world.

Wringing every ounce of his life out, gritting his teeth through pain, wiping away tears to push through sorrow — all of it, every bit of it, had been to keep protecting the idol he'd built up in his mind.

And now, feeling that idealized fantasy that never truly existed come crashing down with a roar—

"All this time, everything, I've—"

His lips trembled. His eyes burned hot. His tongue spasmed, his heart pounding violently, deafeningly. He lifted his face. His eyes met Emilia's. Her violet eyes held only sorrow, watching him. His own face reflected in those eyes was so pathetic, so far from saved, that—

"—Haven't things worked out because of me!?"

He shrieked, his voice cracking, a shout loud enough to shake the waiting room.

"Even at that Loot House where the crest got stolen! I saved you from that damn dangerous murderer! I put my body on the line! It was all because you mattered to me!!"

His fingers, gripping the sheets, trembled, blood beginning to well up in his palm where his nails dug in. His shouting voice cracked here and there, adding to the ugliness that rang out.

"It was the same at the mansion! Chewed up all over, I fought desperately! My head was split open, my neck blown clean off, and even so I saved everyone in the village, didn't I! Rem too, Ram too, it must've turned out the best it possibly could! Because of me, because I was there, right!?"

He listed off his own accomplishments. Every achievement he could think of, he enumerated, chasing after her fading shadow.

"If I hadn't been there, things would've been so much worse! No one would've been saved! No one, no one, no one! Everything, everything, everything! It was all me! Because I was there!"

At the Loot House, Emilia would have met her end. So would Felt. So would old man Rom. Even surviving that, at the mansion, Rem had died, the villagers had died out, and the Curse might have turned even against Roswaal, Beatrice, or even Ram, who remained at the mansion. Naturally, Emilia's own life had been at risk there too.

Eliminating all of that — that was his own achievement. Something to be proud of, something deserving of reward — the result of Natsuki Subaru's actions.

Having done this much, having devoted himself to this extent—

"You've got a debt to me that you can't ever pay back—!!"

Betraying even the very feeling that had supposedly been at the root of his own actions, the outburst tore free.

Feelings that had never been rewarded, a vain desire for praise, a craving to be filled, a selfish wish to be loved — all of it drove Subaru, at the very depths of his confusion, to say that.

And that, for both of them, was the decisive final line.

"...That's right, isn't it."

A murmur, a trembling voice, was addressed to Subaru, sweat beading on his forehead, breath ragged. That tone held acceptance, resignation, resolve — in short, it was an ending.

"I owe you, Subaru. So much. So, so much."

"Yeah, that's right. So I—"

"So let's pay all of it back, and end this."

At those words, spoken plainly, Subaru's head jerked up as if startled. And seeing only emptiness spreading in Emilia's eyes as she looked at him, reflecting on what he'd just said, Subaru realized he'd said something irrevocable.

Swept along by momentum, carried off by a torrent of emotion, he'd said something that should never have been said. He'd trampled even his own purest feeling, the very root of that emotion, ruining everything with an anger no better than a child's tantrum.

To frame the relationship between himself and her as one bound by "debts owed" meant that—

"No... no, no no no no, that's not what I meant to say..."

Once the scales of "debt" balanced out, that would be the end of the relationship, full stop. An act that should have begun from a wish to give freely, without expectation of return, could only end up that way once calculation was introduced into it.

"It's fine now. —Natsuki Subaru."

Affectionately, ever since they'd first met, she'd called him by his first name. The moment she called him by his full name instead, Subaru arrived, far too late, at the understanding that there was nothing left to be done.

To Subaru, hanging his head, Emilia didn't extend a hand. Shaking her silver hair as if casting something off, she turned her back to him,

"Rem will come later, so follow what she says. I'll leave everything about what happens while you stay in the royal capital properly in her hands."

He couldn't answer. And he wasn't being asked to, either. Emilia, walking away, grew more distant. Far from reaching a hand toward her retreating back, Subaru couldn't even summon the courage to watch that back go.

A physical distance, growing greater. And beyond that, a distance of the heart, grown even greater still.

"You know..."

Suddenly, Emilia's hand, which had reached the door, stopped, and that murmur dropped into the room. Not so much speaking to Subaru as murmuring to herself in a small voice,

"I was hoping... that maybe, just maybe, Subaru — that you, alone, wouldn't treat me as something special. That you'd see me the same as anyone else, an ordinary person, an ordinary girl, without drawing any distinction..."

She was the one who, in the Royal Selection hall, had asked to be treated fairly. The fact that she was a half-elf must have forced her to endure enough painful time that even a wish that small became something to hope for. But,

"That's impossible."

Quietly, Subaru too murmured a small reply. Emilia's words hadn't carried the ring of something asking Subaru for an answer. So Subaru's murmur, too, wasn't offered in the form of an answer to her words — it was something meant only for himself to hear.

Turning her words over in his mind, Subaru shook his head weakly, feebly, and,

"Even if you dragged out every single person in the whole world, it'd be impossible. Emilia... you, only you, there's no way I could ever look at you with the same eyes as anyone else."

That much, at least, was true beyond any doubt — his genuine, honest feeling.

The sound of the door closing came, and the air abruptly fell still. Left alone in the room, Subaru curled up on the bed, his gaze wandering aimlessly.

Suddenly, his eyes caught on the robe, caught at the edge of the bed, on the verge of slipping to the floor. He reached out, drew it toward himself, and held it close. Feeling as if the warmth of the person who'd been holding it still lingered there, Subaru clutched it to his chest as if trying to hold on to something on the verge of vanishing,

—And so, on this day, Natsuki Subaru, for the first time, was truly, completely alone in another world.
