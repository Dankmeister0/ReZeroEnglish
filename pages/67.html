Arc 2 Chapter 43: "The Conditions for Struggling to Live"

He swallowed the words he'd just been told, chewed them, worked them over. It took only a few seconds for them to pass down his throat, settle into his stomach, and circulate as blood and flesh up to his brain. Slowly savoring their meaning, he closed his eyes.

"You're less shaken than I expected, I suppose. I thought you'd be bawling and screaming by now."

Perhaps because Subaru's reaction was quieter than expected, Beatrice murmured this in a voice tinged with surprise. At her words, Subaru gave a small nod — "Yeah" — before continuing.

"There are about three possibilities I can think of."

As he spoke, he held up three fingers, showing them to Beatrice standing before him. As if putting on a show for her silence, he folded down the first of the raised fingers.

"First, a black joke — some kind of sick prank. Honestly, it's not funny at all... but if a placard's about to pop out saying 'Gotcha! Prank successful!' I'll laugh. Now's the time, if it's coming."

He made the demand with one eye shut, but Beatrice's expression didn't change. Subaru accepted that the gentlest possibility had just been crushed, and let out a sigh as he folded down his second finger.

"Second, the wound was just too severe. I actually saw my guts spilling out. Even if you told me it's just life-prolonging treatment keeping me going, it wouldn't quite click... but I could accept it."

"There's no way Nii-cha and Betty's treatment could be deficient, I suppose. A scar will remain, and it hasn't fully healed yet, but given time it should mend. Betty wouldn't let someone she managed to reach in time die needlessly, I suppose."

"Yeah. That you can save someone even with their guts hanging out — that's already been proven... Though, wait, that was only three or four days ago, wasn't it?"

Thanks to the repeating days, things weren't entirely clear in his head, but on the correct timeline, Subaru's blade wound at Elsa's hands should have happened four days ago. —And now, just four days later, he'd wound up with his stomach torn open again. A person who dropped their intestines this often in such a short span might just be a rare specimen indeed.

"Not that I'm happy about it at all... Anyway, that being the case—"

Subaru waved the one remaining raised finger side to side and named the last possibility he could think of.

"There's only one cause of death I can think of that fits."

Among the many possible deaths Subaru had suffered during the repeating days at Roswaal Manor, the one that matched his current condition was—

"A curse's revenge, huh."

"It seems you had a whole batch planted in you when the Ulgarm pack got you, I suppose."

Backing up Subaru's guess, Beatrice answered while crossing her arms. The pale scars left here and there on his body — marks from the fangs and claws of multiple Ulgarm. After checking them over with irritation, Subaru said—

"Just to be sure, I'll ask — can't the curse be broken? You're not just holding out on me for dramatic effect, right?"

If handled poorly, she was the type who might well say something like, "I didn't do it because you never asked, I suppose," so he probed at that worry. But she shook her head at Subaru's question, laced as it was with wishful thinking.

"If it were something that could be undone, Betty would have already saddled you with a debt you could never repay, I suppose."

"Hey, give me a break. It's already piled up so high I'm having trouble paying it back as it is."

All he ever did was receive, never able to give anything back. Last time, the time before that, and this time too. At Subaru's reminiscence, Beatrice looked as though she wanted to ask something, but Subaru waved a hand to brush it off.

"Can I ask why exactly this curse can't be broken?"

"...I suppose it's only fair you know how you're going to die. It's a simple matter, really. —The curses laid on you have overlapped too many times, and become far too complicated, I suppose."

"The curses... overlapped?"

Subaru cocked his head, unable to picture it, and Beatrice spread both her hands wide. Then, to Subaru as he blinked at her—

"Let's say a curse is a thread, I suppose."

As she spoke, a red thread suddenly appeared strung between her two palms. Indicating the taut thread with her eyes, Beatrice tied a knot into it.

"Let's say this knot is the curse's formula, I suppose. Simply put, breaking the curse means untying this knot, I suppose. But—"

Beatrice's fingers moved deftly, and the number of threads crossing her palms increased. Blue, yellow, green, pink, black, white — one after another the threads multiplied. She tied knots into each of these as well, and further tangled them all together in complex ways—

"With just a single curse, you could trace it back and untie it. But once multiple threads get tangled together like this—"

Reaching out, Beatrice gestured for him to take the tangled threads. The threads were draped over his outstretched hands, and as Subaru looked at the bizarrely intricate pattern—

"—pulling or tracing does nothing, there's no way to untie it, huh... Ah, damn, this really is high difficulty."

Even to undo them one at a time, he had no idea where to even start. Of course, given enough time, undoing it wasn't necessarily impossible, he thought.

"You put a condition on it — within half a day. What's that prediction based on?"

"That's simple enough too — it's just that after half a day passes, the Ulgarm will get hungry, I suppose."

Beatrice raised a finger and pointed it at Subaru as she continued.

"The Ulgarm's curse excels particularly at 'stealing mana from a distant target,' I suppose. And a Witchbeast's diet is mainly mana... which means the reason for casting the curse should be self-evident, I suppose."

"They attack people because they're hungry, huh. I see, real wild animals — simple as that. Guess I should be grateful their stomachs haven't growled until now."

He wanted to vent his frustration somewhere, but unfortunately his hands were already full of thread. Directing his hard-to-put-into-words emotions at the thread's stubborn knots, Subaru struggled clumsily to untie them, and Beatrice, looking puzzled, said—

"Aren't you scared, I wonder?"

"Huh?"

"What Betty just told you is a declaration of your remaining lifespan, I suppose. And even though Betty and Nii-cha have a means to save you, we're not using it, citing lack of time as the reason."

The time left to Subaru was roughly half a day — depending on how empty the beasts' stomachs were, it wouldn't be strange for it to trigger at this very moment. And presumably, the curse's formula eating away at Subaru's body wasn't simple enough to yield results if attempted under such time pressure.

That was why Beatrice wouldn't take the gamble, and the same went for Puck. While Subaru understood the rationality of their judgment, he couldn't grasp her intent in going out of her way to voice it like this.

Beatrice looked straight at Subaru. Toying with the thread in his hands, Subaru suddenly felt he understood what she wanted. It was—

"What, is it— you want me to blame you?"

"—"

Beatrice fell silent. Normally she would have snapped back a quick retort, but there was no telling what was going through her mind as she chose silence instead. She neither denied it nor affirmed it. At her attitude, Subaru gave a wry smile.

"Your judgment, and Puck's — from a human, emotional standpoint it feels cold-hearted, but from a rational standpoint it's the obvious call. Giving your all for someone whose death is a foregone conclusion — as a story, maybe that's beautiful, but it's only skin deep."

Pouring everything into a life you know you can't save. As a way of facing life, that might be beautiful. But looked at practically, there was no denying it would be wasted effort. Of course, it's precisely because feeling gets tangled up in it that people throw themselves into such acts.

"I don't think you two are cold-hearted. You're right. —There's something else I want to ask now, is that okay?"

"...What is it, I wonder."

"Does Emilia-tan know about the curse on me too?"

The girl sleeping even now in the room where Subaru had lain, worn out from nursing him. How she would face a situation that Puck and Beatrice had already given up on — that alone weighed on his mind. At Subaru's question, Beatrice shook her head.

"That half-blood girl doesn't know, I suppose. The reason Nii-cha won't lay a hand on your curse either is so she won't find out it exists, I suppose."

"...Ah, I see. Puck can't get away from Emilia-tan while he's breaking a curse, so if he tried, she'd naturally realize I'm cursed, and that a means of curing it is out of reach, I guess."

If they kept quiet until the curse actually triggered, the emotional wound dealt to her would be limited to just the once. If Puck, prioritizing Emilia above all else, had judged the tragedy unavoidable, it was an understandable way of thinking. Reading through to the shrewd calculation behind Puck's appearance, Subaru then said—

"So, then."

He said it to mark a break in the conversation, pointing a finger at Beatrice. Watching her brow furrow faintly at the gesture, he went on—

"I don't peg you as someone mean enough to come all this way just to hand down a death sentence and leave it at that, though?"

"...And what exactly would you know about Betty, I wonder."

"At the very least, it feels like we've known each other about four times longer than you think."

Watching the girl deepen the crease between her brows along with her suspicion, Subaru thought back on the long-yet-short stretch of a little over two weeks he'd raced through. His relationship with Ram and Rem was as good as it had been since the first loop. His standing with Emilia was fine too, setting aside how much the bawling in her lap might have affected her opinion of him. He'd grasped the true identity of the Curse Arts user who'd tormented him so, and he'd managed to save the village children's lives. Looking back over this repeated loop, this attempt's score was close to perfect. If only Subaru's own life — the crucial thing — were factored into that count.

"When those dogs chewed me up all over, honestly, I thought I was dead. Pathetic."

That sensation of life spilling away, which he'd tasted so many times before — Subaru had given in to it. He'd grown too accustomed to dying. He'd developed a habit of giving up. Even after coming this far, even after all this. More than anything—

"You, Rem, and Emilia-tan treated my wounds, right? If you'd meant to abandon me because of the curse and all that, you wouldn't have patched me up this properly."

To think otherwise would be an insult to the girls who had desperately kept him tethered to life, even after he'd selfishly given up on it himself. Including the little cat who'd used her own short limbs to stitch Subaru's body back together with thread.

"You guys are just terrible liars."

"...It's a fact that the chance of saving you is extremely low, I suppose. And it's true that Nii-cha is trying to keep that girl from getting involved in the method, I suppose."

"And so you play the villain to take my anger on yourself, huh — way too much thinking ahead for a little girl. —Let's hear it, then, that microscopic chance."

He made a tiny circle with his thumb and index finger, showing it to Beatrice to draw out an answer. After a moment's hesitation, she let out a breath as if giving up, and said—

"Since it was an Ulgarm that cursed you, its nature must be to strip away your mana entirely, root and all, I suppose."

That much he understood. And Subaru had already felt its power firsthand. Which meant that the instant the Ulgarm that had cursed him began its "meal," Subaru's fate would be sealed. After all, once triggered, there was no way to guard against the curse—

"Wait. Then how do you explain curing the kids?"

The children found deep in the forest had been debilitated. That condition should have been nearly identical to the curse Subaru bore. His thinking hadn't gotten that far at the time, but—

"If the curse had already triggered and the kids' mana was being drained, the pattern should've been to drain them dry. And yet those kids were fine — don't tell me even that was a lie, meant to put me at ease?"

If that were the case, that was one thing even Subaru wasn't sure he could stomach. But Beatrice stopped Subaru's hasty conclusion by holding out a palm, and said—

"Jumping ahead. You're supposed to hear a story out to the end, I suppose. But you're close to the heart of it, I suppose. —The village children's curses could be lifted. And that method is the same as the slim chance that might keep you alive, I suppose."

"The difference between the kids and me...?"

At Beatrice's explanation, Subaru's mind raced. The difference between his curse and the children's. Theirs was a single curse each; Subaru's, multiple. A curse triggered. A curse not yet triggered. A curse that steals mana. Debilitation from having mana stolen. The mana that was stolen. The stolen mana as a Witchbeast's meal. —If that was so, then—

A single possibility flashed through his mind like a bolt of lightning, and Subaru lifted his face to turn back to Beatrice.

"If the caster itself dies, where does the stolen mana go?"

Beatrice's expression didn't change at the question. She only nodded once and said—

"Lose its destination, and the curse loses its effect in the first place. That's because this formula is a curse whose purpose is to steal mana, not to kill the target, I suppose — that's the condition."

At Beatrice's confirmation, Subaru found understanding within himself. The children's curses — the reason their progression had stopped was that the Witchbeast which had cast them had died. And once the caster died, what remained in the children's bodies was merely an inert formula, which was easy for Puck or Beatrice to undo.

The number of Witchbeasts whose lives had ended in that forest the previous night should have been considerable. Starting with the one Subaru had finished off at their first encounter, no small number had been slain by Rem's hand during their flight as well. If the Witchbeast that had cursed the children was among them, there was, for now, nothing to disprove this reasoning.

And that certainty was, at the same time—

"Ah, I see. So that's how it is."

Having acknowledged that his reasoning had become rock-solid, Subaru realized something. The meaning behind why Beatrice still stood before him now, in a stance that could be read as grief.

Since breaking the curse through ordinary means was impossible, there was no way to undo the curse laid on Subaru other than killing its caster. But—

"Whoever cursed my body — there's too many of them to narrow down."

Turning around, Subaru looked toward the forest where the Witchbeasts made their home. A barrier had been strung up, separating the village from the forest by something intangible. Deep within that forest, the Witchbeast pack would surely have scattered by now. Finding them and wiping them all out within half a day — that was impossible.

"Ah, damn it..."

Almost laughing despite himself, Subaru pressed a hand to his forehead and hung his head. He'd been made to understand. He'd stared straight at a reality he couldn't turn away from.

Unless every last Ulgarm was wiped out, Subaru's life couldn't be saved. The difficulty of doing that within half a day. And above all, the danger of setting foot into that forest and facing down a pack of Witchbeasts — these were what had made Puck hesitate, and the reason he'd refused to be the one to tell Emilia the truth.

"Nii-cha is..."

"You don't have to say it, I get it. If Emilia-tan found out, knowing her, she'd definitely push herself past her limits. That makes me really happy... but it's really scary too."

Because she was the type to sacrifice herself for someone else without hesitation, Subaru — even while seeing that she was capable of it — couldn't bring himself to ask that of her, of all people. Because if he ever lost her right in front of him, even by the slimmest chance, Subaru was certain to suffer an agony that having his own body torn apart a hundred times over wouldn't equal.

"The difficulty's seriously monstrous. No way, not a chance. Guess I'll give—"

"'So, are you giving up?'"

A voice like that rose up in the mind of Subaru, mid-sentence. Mixed in with static, it was distant — a thin voice, as though it had echoed from the depths of the unconscious.

He looked up with a start and glanced around. There was no one else here besides Subaru and Beatrice. And yet, that voice just now—

"'Is there another way to help—'"

A voice clinging, pleading. But somewhere in it, that voice also held a grim resolve.

"Got a headache, I wonder? Small wonder, I suppose."

"'Is that all, I wonder. As for the rest, do as you please, I suppose.'"

Over the voice of the Beatrice before him, different words from that same Beatrice overlapped. He didn't know where. But a conversation he had definitely heard somewhere flashed back to him. His vision flickered, and clutching his head against a pounding alarm bell and headache, Subaru, breathing hard, nearly dropped to his knees—

"'—I will save you, no matter what.'"

At the resounding voice of determination and resolve, he steadied his buckling knees. That voice which had spoken those words, that voice speaking those words — Subaru recognized it. And then he realized. There was something he had to ask.

"—Where's Rem?"

Not once since this morning had Subaru laid eyes on the blue-haired girl. She should have returned from the forest together with him. She was the one who'd carried back Subaru, wounded all over, along with the children — Puck had said as much clearly. But that account only covered up to the moment they'd returned.

Was her wound too severe, and she was resting somewhere even now? —Whatever the effects of her oni transformation, he'd been told she had no external injuries.

Then was she out with Ram, going around the village, devoting herself to caring for the villagers? —If Rem were in the kitchen, Ram's specialty of steamed potatoes wouldn't have come out.

Then where was she now — unwounded, yet nowhere near the kitchen?

"Bea-ko... Beatrice, where's Rem?"

At Subaru's halting question, Beatrice smoothed a lock of her ringlet curls and said—

"If you were in the same position, what would you do, I wonder?"

"That's not an answer!"

At her evasive phrasing, Subaru stepped forward to press her — but perhaps as a backlash from moving so abruptly, his blood-deprived body tilted wildly, blocking his advance. Staggering, Subaru stumbled in place. He knew it was nothing more than venting frustration at the wrong target. And he understood too that Beatrice was acting this way precisely to receive it. Even knowing her consideration for what it was, he was furious at his own shabbiness for so readily trying to lean on that kindness.

Just then—

"—Ah, so you two are over here. Sorry to bother you, but do you know where Rem is?"

Perhaps drawn by the raised voice, a pink-haired girl appeared from a corner of the square. She looked at the two of them facing each other with a puzzled air before tilting her head, as if to remind them of the question she'd just asked.

At that gesture of hers, Beatrice kept up her familiar indifferent expression. But Subaru couldn't manage that kind of poker face. Unable to bear looking at the face of the older sister, the spitting image of the girl he had pictured in his mind, he reflexively turned his face away.

That blatant reaction of Subaru's, and the place where the two of them had been talking. Perhaps she found some correlation in that, for Ram's expression suddenly clouded over with a "don't tell me—"

"—Clairvoyance, Eyes Open."

Slipping a hand into her hair, Ram covered one of her eyes and murmured this. Immediately after, witnessing the transformation that came over her, Subaru let out a groan of shock.

With her left eye covered and her right eye opened wide, veins bulged densely across her face. Blue-green veins standing out against pale skin was grotesque enough on its own, and the state of Ram's right eye — no longer merely bloodshot but more like a pool of blood — only heightened the horror further.

Ignoring Subaru's astonishment, Ram, now transformed like this, trembled her lips and said—

"—I can't see her. No way... Rem, don't tell me — beyond the barrier!?"

Turning around with her eye still bloodshot, Ram's feet carried her toward the forest, its barrier now restored. At that, Subaru instinctively grabbed her shoulder—

"Wait, you can tell where she is!?"

"If I can just get inside the barrier... don't stop me, Barusu!"

Ram fiercely tried to shake off Subaru's hand on her shoulder. But Subaru, too, was desperate not to be torn away. Holding her movements in check, he turned back and called—

"Beatrice!"

"—Betty presented a possibility, that's all, I suppose. Nii-cha, who doesn't want to put the half-blood girl in danger, and Betty, who's already this far from the Forbidden Archive, are both out of the running as fighting strength, I suppose. And you yourself going wouldn't help matters one bit. The options were limited, I suppose."

"That's not the point... So then, Rem really did—"

—She had gone alone into the forest where the Witchbeasts lived, intending to wipe out the pack single-handedly.

Listing off who could be counted as fighting strength in this situation, it might indeed have been the correct call. In fact, Rem's power had overwhelmed the Witchbeasts in the nighttime forest. But—

"How many of them can use magic? And in the first place, how's she even supposed to confirm my curse has been broken? Killing them one after another after another — that's not just 'blind,' it's on a whole other level!"

"What do you mean? Barusu's curse should have already been broken..."

At Subaru's blood-spitting cry, Ram's own bloodshot eye clouded too. Then, turning to Beatrice, Ram rushed to her side.

"What... does that mean, Lady Beatrice. What is Rem—!?"

"To undo a Witchbeast's intricately tangled curse, she went into the forest to annihilate the Ulgarm pack, its casters, I suppose. —It's a method by which both the human here and your little sister have a chance of being saved, I suppose. Though the odds are vanishingly small."

At Beatrice's terse words, Ram was momentarily struck speechless. But she quickly switched her expression from grief to resolve, and began to run, meaning to plunge into the forest after her sister without hesitation.

"—Wait!"

Subaru leapt into her path, spreading both arms wide to block Ram's way. The pink-haired girl shot him a sharp glare at the gesture.

"Move, Barusu. Ram has no room to spare right now, so I can't be gentle."

"I'm not telling you not to go without thinking at all! I've got a few things I want to ask, so answer them — honestly."

"There's no time for that..."

"If you want to save Rem, and if you think of me as an ally even a little, listen to me. I want to raise the odds even a fraction."

Hearing that this concerned a means of saving Rem — her sister, likely in mortal danger this very moment — Ram's obstinate stance wavered slightly. Seeing her forward momentum ease, Subaru pressed on, driving the point home.

"There's only two things I want to ask. First — with your Clairvoyance, can you find Rem's location in the forest?"

"...Yes, I can. Ram's Clairvoyance shares the sight of any being within range that 'shares Ram's wavelength,' so moving along through the sights I see, one after another, will get me there without fail."

"So it's less an eye that sees through things, and more an eye that surveys from multiple vantage points, huh. I can't quite picture how it actually looks, but if it means you can link up with Rem, that's good enough."

Nodding at the first condition being met, Subaru went on to raise a second finger.

"Okay, second one. —Ram, are you the type of maid who can actually fight?"

"What kind of question is that supposed to be?"

Ram narrowed her eyes and questioned him back, and Subaru, prefacing with "well, I mean," said—

"Until we link up with Rem, there's no telling where we'll run into Witchbeasts. If you can't defend yourself, this whole thing's a non-starter. Fair warning, if it comes to a fight, I'm gonna be dead weight, big time."

"W-Wait a second. Are you even planning on coming along, Barusu?"

At Subaru declaring his own inadequacy with total confidence, Ram spoke in an uncharacteristically flustered tone. Nodding to show he understood her agitation, he said—

"I get why you're rattled, but it's a necessary condition, you know? Well, honestly, if the only goal were bringing Rem back alive, I guess I'm not strictly needed, but..."

The latter half of his sentence trailed off, and Ram, apparently unable to catch it, wore a suspicious look. At that look, Subaru hurriedly waved both hands and said—

"Since it's come to this, I want all of us to get through to day five together. Only then will all these repeated attempts have been worth it. So do me this one favor."

Subaru pressed his palms together, pleading, and Ram trembled her lips as though unsure what to say. In the end, though, she sealed all of it away and let out a sigh instead, saying—

"—If you're expecting me to fight on the same level as Rem in her full oni form, that's impossible."

"Meaning?"

"Unlike Rem, Ram is 'Hornless,' so I can't achieve a full oni transformation. Unlike Rem, I'm not particularly good at close combat either — I can just use wind-type magic, a little more violently than usual."

So she answered, and as Ram gave her finger a light flick, a strong wind stirred Subaru's hair. If this formula, already interfering with nature, turned even more vicious, it would presumably become something with the force to gouge into his right leg or throat, as it had before. Thinking of that made even this gentle breeze send a chill down his spine. But as fighting strength on their side, nothing could be more reassuring.

"Beatrice! I'm heading into the forest with Ram now. If Emilia-tan wakes up before we get back, cover for us somehow."

"...Bringing that blue-haired girl back means giving up on your own life, I suppose. Do you understand that, I wonder?"

"That's a little off. Let me correct you."

To Beatrice questioning his resolve in a low voice, Subaru wagged a finger side to side, going "tsk tsk tsk," and said—

"Getting used to dying, developing a habit of giving up — that's crap. Life matters. You only get one. I get that now, because you all fought so hard to keep me tethered to mine. So I'm gonna go on struggling, however unsightly it looks."

Once already, a life he'd selfishly given up on had been kept alive for him by others. So the fact that Subaru's heart still beat even now was the result of so many people reaching out their hands to him. This was overtime, granted to him at that cost, so—

"Let's pull off a comeback. We've clawed our way back this far from a state that bad. Greedy as I am, being true to myself, I'm just dying to see an epilogue that includes me in it."

A ridiculous piece of logic, an explanation that made no proper sense. An answer that didn't align even the tiniest bit with what Beatrice had intended — and yet Subaru puffed out his chest with the smuggest look imaginable.

"I don't understand what you're thinking, I suppose. But do as you like, I suppose. Betty presented the options. What you choose to pick from them is for you to decide on your own, I suppose."

"So that's how you sent Rem off too, huh. Well — thanks, Bea-ko."

Turning back to face the forest, he thought of the girl who, even now, was surely fighting somewhere within that deep darkness.

—That meddling, jumping-to-conclusions girl. Always going and imagining other people's feelings on her own, jumping to her own hasty conclusions, doing whatever she pleases on her own. What a nerve.

"I'm saying that at least as much as you're willing to do something for me, I want to do something for you too, you know."

Cracking his knuckles to spur on his resolve, he made his declaration toward the Witchbeast forest. To the pack of black beasts that dwelled within it, and to the supernatural existence that had dragged Subaru into this fate — a declaration of war he had once made and nearly forgotten.

"All right, let's get to the final showdown. —Bring it on, Lady Fate!"

Stage 2, "Roswaal Manor," Day Four — the fifth attempt begins—.
