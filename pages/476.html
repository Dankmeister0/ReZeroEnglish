Arc 6 Chapter 70: "A Single-Minded Star"

In the collapsing tower, struck by Reid's blade, Natsuki Subaru's life burned out.

Literally burned out — that's the correct way to describe his final moment.

There are plenty of things one might want to say, to question — how had he been securing footholds in a tower that was crumbling to pieces, how had he been holding off the encroaching density of shadow, what had he intended to do afterward — but all of that gets tossed aside.

The only certain thing is that Reid's final flash of the blade swallowed Subaru whole and vaporized him. No pain, probably, he thinks. Of course, he doesn't think for a moment that this was some mercy on Reid's part, but the fact that the moment of "death" carried neither pain nor fear was, even for Subaru — who had died so many times in so short a span — a rare occurrence.

Right. No pain, no fear. — Only anger.

"—I—"

Just how many more times would he keep stacking up meaningless "deaths"?

As long as there was something to carry back, some hint toward a breakthrough, then Subaru's "deaths" weren't meaningless, and the deaths he kept piling up weren't in vain.

That was one hell of a self-deception.

That was nothing but an excuse so he wouldn't have to look his own powerlessness in the eye. He simply didn't want to think he'd died meaninglessly, powerlessly, randomly, mercilessly, for nothing. So he wanted a reason for his own "death" — wanted to believe he hadn't just died, plain and simple.

If only he weren't weak. If only he were smarter. If only, instead of being himself, he were strong, clever, capable.

"But..."

Because here, there was only a weak, foolish, pathetic Natsuki Subaru. Because that Subaru had never, ever been left alone by anyone. Because that was what was pushing his battered, scarred resolve forward, right now.

"So, I—"

So, Natsuki Subaru—.

"—Hey. If I told you to die, would you die for me?"

At the moment he spoke, it wasn't that he had no hesitation.

That was because, at the instant he posed this question, he couldn't predict how the other party would react. — No, that wasn't quite right either. He could predict it, in a way. He'd pictured a few possible patterns and figured it would be one of those. So then, what exactly had that hesitation, felt in the instant of asking, been hesitation about?

In any case—

"—? If Master says die, then I'll die, ya know?"

Shaula answered breezily, pressing a finger to her own cheek, and it struck Subaru's chest like a blow. A scream rose up deep in his chest, as if he'd been stabbed, as if something inside were cracking apart, agonizingly.

"——"

Gripping at his chest, where he felt the illusion of a raw, aching wound, Subaru let out a long, deep breath. It struck him as absurdly comical that he, who had gone and wounded himself on purpose, should now act as though the pain were somehow unfair. Watching Subaru's reaction, Shaula blinked, looking puzzled.

Her attitude was like someone innocently answering a question about tonight's dinner menu. Her response was, among the patterns Subaru had predicted, the second worst. Shaula, accepting without the slightest pretense the choice to discard her own life on command. That this wasn't a lie or a joke, but unmistakably her genuine feeling — one look at those straight, unwavering eyes made that clear at a glance.

If anything, it might have been a relief to Subaru's heart to catch even a glimpse of some dark calculation or hidden agenda in her. But reality offered him no such escape route. Whether that was mercy or mercilessness, he couldn't even tell at this stage—

"...I see."

"Master, d'you want me to die? Hmm, well, I'd do anything Master asked, so as far as I'm concerned it's totally bring-it-on, but this is a pretty weird timing to get all resolved about it, isn't it? Right now the tower's a total mess and..."

"I know. I know."

Hearing Subaru's hoarse reply, Shaula tilted her head, finger still on her cheek. With the motion of her head, her long braid — her Scorpion Tail — swayed. Scorpion Tail. Come to think of it, that was an ironic name too.

Just like "Shaula," meaning the scorpion's tail in the constellation, she really lived up to her name in every sense. And that was surely true not just of her name, but of many other things, all displayed brazenly out in the open. That was surely because she had no intention of hiding anything. So then—

"—Shaula. Can you turn into a giant scorpion or something?"

Without any roundabout phrasing, Subaru cut straight to the heart of the matter.

Among the five obstacles besieging the Pleiades Watchtower — one of them, the existence of the massive jet-black scorpion. Subaru was already half-convinced that this was Shaula. But that was, in the end, only a sensory answer arrived at through Cor Leonis, an Authority whose nature remained mysterious even to Subaru himself.

If he wanted to turn that sensory answer into certainty, the fastest way was to simply ask her directly. Of course, if he'd had more time, he might have chosen a different method, and if her answer proved untrustworthy, he'd have needed to seek some other means regardless. But in response to Subaru's question, Shaula, head still tilted, said,

"Turn into one, huh — the nuance's a little different, but yeah, I can do it. Ahh, but it's not real pretty, so I'm not that fond of it. I think this form here, that Mother and Master designed for me, is the best."

Despite the question coming out of nowhere, Shaula answered it, once again, without the slightest hesitation.

"——"

She had no intention of hiding anything. That much about Shaula's own nature could now be considered proven. At the same time, he now had certainty that the giant scorpion that had appeared within the tower — its true identity — was her. When the effect of Cor Leonis had let him perceive the positions of his allies within the tower, the fact that one of those perceptions had pointed to that giant scorpion — so that really had been the mechanism behind it.

"Master? You okay? Your color's not so good, y'know? Want a lap pillow, or an arm pillow, or a chest pillow, or a hug pillow, somethin' like that, to refresh yourself?"

"...Don't say something so brainless. Besides, you're the one who said there's no time for that kind of leisure."

"It's true the tower's a mess, but for me, Master ranks way higher priority than that, so everything else can just wait, allll of it. So if Master wants to get tangled up with me in all this chaos, I'm totally on board. I'll burn for it."

"Don't burn. Go pour some water on yourself."

"Master, you're so mean~"

Shaula pouted, putting on a sulky expression. If you cut out just this exchange, just this expression on her face, you might mistake the situation Subaru and the others found themselves in for something peaceful.

"——"

But reality was anything but kind to them. Nothing like peace. Even as Subaru and Shaula leisurely talked like this, Emilia and Ram were running themselves ragged to hold back "Gluttony," with Beatrice joining them as reinforcement. Julius was clashing with Reid=Roy on the second level. Echidna should have joined up with Patrasche and be taking refuge with Rem toward "Taygeta." And Meili was holding back the horde of witchbeasts — the best moves currently available against the five obstacles were being played.

But even that was nothing more than a deception, a stalling tactic for this conversation with Shaula. Because, this time around, Natsuki Subaru—

"M-Master? Somethin's seriously off with you, right? Lookin' at me with such a serious face like that, four hundred years of waiting's gonna make me lose my patience...!"

At Subaru's steady gaze fixed on her, Shaula wrapped her slender arms around her own body. It seemed like an attempt to feign her usual self, but it wasn't that. She genuinely seemed uneasy about Subaru's demeanor. Unlike her — no, that wasn't right. This was probably her true feelings, laid bare.

Even told to die, she showed no shaken composure — yet the moment Subaru showed some strange change, her heart wavered with startling fragility. — Like a baby bird, innocently devoted to its parent.

"——"

When Subaru had first posed his question to Shaula, he'd held several possibilities in mind. The worst among them was that the instant Subaru hurled some heartless words at her, Shaula would transform, revealing her true nature, and kill Subaru on that very impulse. — If everything about Shaula's behavior up to now had been an act, all of it a lie.

It wasn't an impossibility. Before he'd asked his question, he hadn't been certain, but she did have a track record — transforming into the giant scorpion and killing Subaru, Echidna, and Beatrice. Or perhaps, in the loops where Subaru had found no survivors at all, it was highly likely she, as the giant scorpion, had been involved in that tragedy too.

So that first question had been a gamble for Subaru as well. Having spoken it, there was a real chance that the moment Shaula swallowed the question, Subaru's head would simply vaporize the next instant — that kind of gamble. And that gamble, he could say, he had won.

But the gamble wouldn't end with just one round. The debt Subaru had unconsciously piled up, the stakes that "Gluttony" and the Pleiades Watchtower had set on the table — to recover from the losses accrued this far, small victories stacked one on another wouldn't be enough.

To win big, he had to bet big. Hence—

"Shaula, sorry for all the questions, but there's something I want to ask. From what I've heard, this Pleiades Watchtower's 'Trial' should have several rules, right?"

"You're askin' me that now, while I'm all fired up!? ...Well, sure there are. I told you before, back when Master bashed his head on the toilet..."

"Let me hear them."

Not hiding her sulkiness at Subaru's demand, Shaula gently pressed her fingertips together in front of her chest. But she quickly raised those pressed fingers, murmured "let's see," and,

"One — leaving without completing the 'Trial' is forbidden. Two — violating the rules of the 'Trial' is forbidden. Three — disrespect toward the archive is forbidden. Four — acts of destruction against the tower itself are forbidden. — That's it, ya know."

Counting off on her fingers, Shaula explained in an oddly fluent tone. Of course, when it came to smart remarks, she was plenty glib, so there was nothing strange about her giving such a smooth explanation. Nothing strange — and yet, something snagged.

It was partly that her tone had been unlike her usual self, unusually serious. And it was partly that, at the very end of her finger-counting gesture, she'd raised her last finger — and stopped there.

"—What's the fifth?"

"...There isn't one, ya know. Master, weren't you listening? I only said up to four. Master, can't you even count anymore? That's no good~. I'm not great with numbers either, but even I can count that far..."

"Shaula."

"——"

With a scraping sound, Subaru took a step forward, closing the distance to Shaula. The two of them had originally been standing face to face, straight on, but now that distance closed to where, if either reached out a hand, they could grab the other. — This act, too, was a gamble for Subaru. Of course, the gap between their combat strength wasn't so shallow that closing or widening a single step's distance would change anything.

"Master... could it be you're playing with my heart? Speaking for myself, but if Master's the one closing in like this, if you want to pry my mouth open, well, y'know. If you just went ahead and hugged me with this momentum..."

"If that would really get you to talk, I'd do it. Might even call it a perk. ...But if I trust my unreliable gut, I don't think it would."

"——"

"Shaula, I'm asking you again. What's the tower's fifth rule?"

Even as Shaula gently rebuffed him, Subaru pressed the question once more. It wasn't a physical distance he was closing now, but an act of stepping into her heart. Even if the price paid in return was a sharp, painful one, he had to ask. Bracing himself with that resolve, fists clenched, before him Shaula let out a small breath, and,

"—NG, ya know."

"...NG?"

Shaking her head, Shaula crossed her arms in front of her ample chest, forming an X. The gesture itself was childish, but her eyes were anything but playful — deadly serious.

"——"

Staring fixedly at Subaru, standing on such precarious ground, Shaula's eyes filled with intense emotion. That quiet yet bottomlessly deep passion was something fragile and fleeting — something that deserved to be called a plea. Reluctantly, she shook her head again,

"NG, ya know. No, I don't wanna talk about it. The fifth rule? Who cares about somethin' like that. It's got nothin' to do with me and Master's honeymoon..."

"Of course it has something to do with it. I'm challenging this tower's 'Trial,' and so is everyone else. I can't be optimistic and say it's fine not knowing the rules of the 'Trial.' So, Shaula—"

"...No."

"Shaula!"

Like an unreasonable child, Shaula covered her ears and turned her face away. At that attitude of hers, Subaru spoke with a forceful tone.

"You must have a role here in this tower too, don't you? The tower's Star Watcher...? You've been doing that the whole time, haven't you. I don't know if it's true or not, but four hundred years! Then—"

"—Four days, ya know."

"...Huh?"

The whispered words that spilled out brought Subaru's thoughts to a brief halt. A span of time incomparably shorter than the number of years Subaru had pressed her about. Surely — surely her earlier claim wasn't a lie, surely the time Shaula had spent in the watchtower wasn't really that much shorter — no, that couldn't be.

Shaula, eyes still filled with pleading, trembled her lips and continued.

"It's only been four days since Master and everyone came to the tower. Of those, the first two days Master was laid up sick, so it's only been two days that me and Master have met, talked, gotten close... After waiting four hundred years! Only two days, ya know..."

"Shaula..."

"I thought, even a single instant, a single glance, would be enough."

Shaula had started to lower her gaze, but immediately stopped herself, as if it would be a waste to let Subaru out of her sight even for an instant. — No, come to think of it, that had always been true. As far as Subaru could recall, whenever Shaula was in the same place, the same space as him, she was always, always watching him. That wasn't for some dry purpose like monitoring Subaru's every move, but surely because—

"Four hundred years, I waited for Master in this tower the whole time. I thought if I could just see you once, that'd be enough for me. — But that was a lie."

"——"

"'Cause Master is everything to me. I'm made up of everything about Master, everything about loving Master. Even using up all four hundred years, I couldn't tell Master everything. And that ends after just two days...? I don't want that."

"...Is that why you can't tell me the fifth rule?"

A poignant sorrow enveloped Shaula's entire being, shaping the very existence that was her. Four hundred years — the weight of those words, which he'd only ever grasped as mere characters on a page, finally felt to Subaru like something real, something certain.

Because to speak of four hundred years, her manner had always been far too light, far too breezy. Maybe, he'd thought, she simply lacked whatever organ was needed to feel things like pain or suffering or sorrow. Maybe, he'd thought, even her heart, her very spirit, was as inorganic as that scorpion.

"I don't wanna tell you the rule. NG. Because if I tell you this..."

"——"

"If I tell you this, Master will figure out how to clear the 'Trial.' So if I tell you, if I go and tell you... my time with Master will end."

Desperately, Shaula hugged her own body, baring her heart to Subaru. Her voice, as if coughing up blood — no, as if holding back sobs — pierced Subaru's heart.

It was an answer he hadn't anticipated. Just as with his first question, Subaru had considered several possible patterns for the answer to this one as well. Shaula's true motive for hiding the Pleiades Watchtower's rules. — If she were in cahoots with whoever had made this ill-natured tower's rules, perhaps she too had some agenda of her own. Or, unrelated to any such dark scheme, perhaps her silence about the rules was simple caprice or forgetfulness, with no particular meaning behind it at all.

But the truth was neither. Shaula had her own reason for not wanting to speak the tower's rules. And that reason had nothing to do with the thinking of the "Sage" who'd built the tower — it was something far more heartfelt, far more desperate.

— Four hundred years spent in solitude, Shaula, who had gone on waiting for the reunion with the one she waited for.

That wish had come true, and she'd been so happy, and so she wanted that time to last even a little longer. If it meant granting that small, humble desire—

"Master, are you gonna hate me now, for lying to you?"

"——"

"Gonna hate me, don't even wanna look at my face anymore... is that what you're thinking?"

Why did she look more pained now than when she'd been asked "would you die for me"? Why did she place more weight on whether he'd hate her than on whether she'd live or die?

— Why, despite having waited four hundred years, did she treat this moment as though it were the finish line for everything?

"...I won't hate you."

"——"

"Because you stayed silent, I probably went through some seriously rough stuff, and honestly, there are times I think I wouldn't have been cornered like this if you hadn't."

Facing the silenced Shaula, Subaru spoke his honest feelings. There was no lie in this. It was the truth. Because Shaula had deliberately hidden information, Subaru surely hadn't been able to make the necessary deductions, hadn't reached the answers he needed, and as a result had met with gruesome deaths again and again. Not just Subaru. Others too — Emilia, Beatrice, and the rest.

He couldn't forget the despair, the disappointment, the helplessness of those moments. So he'd thought that if there were some root cause behind all that evil, some being responsible for bringing about those moments, surely he could never forgive them. So then — could he think the same thing of Shaula, standing before him now?

"—No."

Shaula, who had spent four hundred years in solitude, who had savored just two days so thoroughly that she felt the very meaning of her birth had been fulfilled, happy — he couldn't think of her as some root of all evil. If there were a root of all evil, it was unreasonable fate itself, whatever had created the circumstances that forced her into this — the "Master" who had commanded Shaula to wait four hundred years—

"—Ah"

Suddenly, a hoarse breath escaped Shaula's lips.

"Shaula?"

"A-Ah... aaah, ah..."

Sensing something wrong in Shaula's state right before him, Subaru called out her name. But Shaula didn't respond to his call, covering her face with her hands. From her throat came a voice unlike her, trembling painfully.

"N-no... this is bad... Master! Master, Master, Master, Master...!"

"Shaula!? Shaula, what's wrong!? So suddenly..."

"—Someone broke a rule."

"——"

Subaru rushed to her, reaching to shake her pale shoulders, but instead his arm was caught. Shaula gripped his wrist with her slender arm, hard enough to hurt, and said this. Looking her straight in the eye as she said it, Subaru caught his breath.

— A strange change had come over Shaula's dark, round eyes.

Within her round eyes, the black pupils split into three, beginning to pulse a deep red. This transformation occurred simultaneously in both eyes, meaning her pupils had split into six in total.

— Three to a side, six compound eyes.

"Master...! There's still time, ya know..."

"Time for what?"

"If it's now, if Master commands me, I... I can kill myself."

Shaula, her eyes pulsing red, began to let off white steam slowly from her entire body. Her white skin gradually took on a reddish tinge, and even Subaru, standing right beside her, could feel the abnormal rise in her body heat. The mechanism was unknown. — But Shaula's body was heating up, undergoing a transformation. Probably the initial stage of her shift into the giant scorpion's form.

"If I finish changing, it'll be too late. I'll become a killing machine without a drop of blood or a single tear, and I'll kill Master. Because I want Master so much... I want Master so badly I can't stand it... so..."

"Before that happens—"

"Tell me to die, please. ...If you do that, then I..."

So I won't have to kill you — that, Shaula didn't say aloud. But instead of words, her eyes, her trembling voice, her very soul poured out with all its might, said the same thing.

"——"

An indescribable chill crept up through Subaru's entire body. It was surely the instinctive human reaction in the face of a terror that felt utterly beyond this world. The "human" that was Natsuki Subaru was afraid of the "monster" standing before him — Shaula. And so, Subaru—

"Shaula, tell me the fifth rule."

"Master, this isn't the time for..."

"If I can hear that—!"

Subaru cut off Shaula's pleading words with a loud voice. Shaula's shoulders trembled at his forcefulness. He grabbed those trembling shoulders. Hot. Shaula's body temperature had climbed so high it felt like it would scorch his palms, like flame itself. But he didn't let go. He wouldn't release the thing that was now searing her body and mind.

"If I hear that, I'll give the order. — Don't worry. Before you turn into a monster, I'll be the one to command you."

"——"

Hearing Subaru say that, straight and unwavering, Shaula's eyes went wide. Then she called out, "Master," and,

"Master, you player."

"I have no idea what you're talking about..."

"Then Master's a Shaula-player. My own personal player, specializing just in me..."

Smiling faintly, Shaula gently laid her own hand over Subaru's, where it gripped her shoulder. And then—

"—Five: destruction of the 'Trial' is not forbidden, ya know."

"——"

"See, your eyes changed color. — Into Master, the one I love."

Saying that, Shaula shoved Subaru's chest, hard. The force of it was greater than expected, and Subaru, unable to keep his grip on her shoulder, stumbled back. Coughing lightly, he looked up to see Shaula, hugging her own body, crouching down where she stood—

"A-ah... aaah, aaaah...!"

From her whole body, blood-red steam began to rise. The steam's color shifted, a dangerous sign. Shaula's eyes, too, lost their black pupils, transforming before long into something a deep, solid red.

"M-Master... hurry. Before I... stop being me..."

"——"

"Say it, please... tell me to die! If Master says it, I..."

With the same mouth that had just pleaded that she didn't want the 'Trial' to end, didn't want Subaru and the others to leave the tower, Shaula now moved to snuff out her own life, showing Subaru and the others — no, showing Subaru — a path where he wouldn't have to kill her. Hearing Shaula's desperate voice, Subaru let out a breath. And then,

"Shaula."

"Master..."

"—Sorry. What I said before was a lie."

"Eh?"

At Subaru's words, Shaula's eyes went wide. Watching her reaction to the end, Subaru held his breath, and in that same instant, leapt backward with all his strength. That Shaula had shoved him away was, in this unfortunate situation, a stroke of luck. — If she'd still had his wrist in her grip, there was no way he could have pulled off what came next.

— Subaru's body, vaulting over the edge of the balcony, launching out into open air.

"O—"

Shaula's reflexive cry was swallowed instantly by the roaring sandstorm winds. And just like that, with nothing at all to hold onto, Subaru's body plummeted, nonstop, hundreds of meters down.

"——"

It wasn't that he had some plan to survive the fall. He had no plan for survival prepared at all. What Subaru had done was a pure and simple leap off the edge. This was something he'd never wanted to do, never wanted to even say aloud — but from the very start, this had been his intention.

If this course of action were possible this time around, he'd resolved to do exactly this, without fail. Because this way, Subaru could believe in his own choice without a shred of doubt. Because—

"—Master!!"

Leaping off the balcony the same way, Shaula came after Subaru. Eyes wide, arm desperately outstretched, Shaula chased after the falling Subaru — not to kill him with her own hands, but to save his life, diving down after him.

— The true identity of the giant scorpion had been Shaula. — Shaula had deliberately hidden the tower's rules. — Shaula had killed Subaru and his companions again and again, standing in their way as one of the five obstacles.

But even so—

"—I get to save you, don't I."

"——"

He wouldn't forget it, right to the very end — the sight of her, undergoing a transformation against her own will, pleading with him to give her an order that would kill her, all so she wouldn't have to kill Subaru. It was an ugly thing to admit, but he'd wanted to confirm that.

Who to save, who not to save, who to defeat, who to protect, who to love. He'd thought that without confirming that, Natsuki Subaru couldn't move forward any further than this.

He would no longer hesitate over who he was allowed to love.

"—Masteeeeeer!!"

Reaching out her hand, trying to catch up to Subaru, Shaula's form changed in midair. Her outstretched arm swelled, transforming into a massive pincer wrapped in jet-black carapace. Her white skin vanished without a trace, covered instead by that same rough, coarse shell, her body expanding as if bursting from within. For an instant, a grotesque, agonizing transformation of flesh, as if blood and muscle were exploding outward — then, like a tape rewinding, it converged, until finally that ominous, alien form was complete — the giant scorpion.

And then, the giant scorpion's tail swiftly took aim at Subaru. That white light-like stinger would surely be loosed from there, and in an instant, burn Subaru's life away to nothing. Falling through open air, Subaru had no way to dodge it. But—

"—Shaula would cry. So I'm not letting you kill me."

"——"

Faster than the stinger could be loosed — the fall reached its end. Both Subaru and the giant scorpion came crashing down, furiously, onto the horde of witchbeasts surging toward the sand tower below. Subaru would never see the outcome of that.

A being like Natsuki Subaru could not withstand an impact from hundreds of meters up. He would burst apart, his life scattering. But an instant before that life scattered, with just one final word—

"—I'll definitely come save you."

— There was just barely enough time for the sandstorm to sweep away a message that would never reach the giant scorpion.
