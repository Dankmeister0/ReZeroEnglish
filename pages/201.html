Arc 4 Chapter 35: "A Girl's Gospel"

This chapter marks a memorable 201st episode. Thanks to everyone's support up until now, I've been able to make it this far. The choking smell of old books filled the closed-off room.

Stepping through the open door, Subaru's mouth fell open in stunned disbelief as the musty smell and the sight before him registered — and only half a beat later did he realize his feet had landed on a floor other than the one he'd meant to reach.

—And that delay in realization became, in turn, a fatal delay.

"The Forbidden Archive...!?"

He had wandered the whole mansion searching for this very place and never found it — and now, here he was. The timing was unwanted, the chance unlooked-for, and it left a blank space in Subaru's mind, stealing away every last moment before the door behind him swung shut with a bang.

"—!"

As though caught by a gust from outside, Subaru's body was sucked into the archive. The door slammed shut with the same force, stirring up a breeze that tickled the nape of his neck.

He spun around at the violent sound, confirmed that the door now stood between the hallway and the room, and understood.

Understood what it meant for the Forbidden Archive to have opened here, and for that door to have shut—

"O-open it—!!"

He reached for the doorknob, then caught himself, remembering his right arm no longer even held the shape of an arm, and fumbled instead with his left. Fingers slick with blood wrenched at the knob with a harsh clatter, but for some reason the turning handle refused to pass its intent on to the door. It spun uselessly, the noise only stoking Subaru's mounting desperation.

"No matter how much you struggle to get out, it's useless, I suppose."

The voice came flying at Subaru's back as he wrestled desperately with the door.

He whirled around as if struck, back pressed to the door, and looked straight at the girl who had appeared from the depths of the archive ahead, her face turned toward him, cold and composed.

Long cream-colored ringlets, a luxurious, glittering dress. A small frame, a face as endearing as it was infuriating. Every bit of it was exactly the girl Subaru knew.

"Beatrice..."

"What an awful state you're in, I suppose. You're getting the archive floor dirty, so try not to move around too much..."

"Right now—! Open the door! Get me outshide—!!"

Ignoring every word she said as she surveyed his wounds with cool detachment, Subaru bellowed. Deaf even to her warning not to drip blood everywhere, he swung his ravaged right arm, still spilling blood without pause.

"Why—why now, of all times, do you show your fashe!? Why now!? It has to be now! Shend me back! Hurry! Now! Right now—!!"

"...And what would going back accomplish, I wonder. Going back in that pathetic state, what exactly do you think you could do? Betty hasn't the faintest idea, I suppose."

"I know better than anyone there'sh nothing I can do!! But—even sho!!"

It wasn't that he wished to go back there and face Elsa. He only wanted to enter the room he'd meant to reach, to be at the side of the sleeping girl within, and then—

"If I came into the archive, and the Door Crossing happened... then that murderer—she's in the room...!"

If that had happened, the madwoman would surely be puzzling over Subaru's vanishing act. But before she went prowling the mansion in search of the vanished Subaru, she was bound to find the girl sleeping alone in that room. And what that indiscriminate murderer would do upon finding a girl defenselessly baring her sleeping face — there was no need to even think about it.

"That'sh why—!"

"It's already too late, I suppose."

Subaru raised his voice in a shout, as if trying to shake off the ugly premonition climbing inside him. And onto that Subaru, the girl's anguished murmur was abruptly cast.

At the sight of Beatrice lowering her eyes and shaking her head slightly, Subaru froze for an instant. Then his brain replayed what she had just said, and his thoughts stopped cold.

—What had this girl just said?

"Too late... what... do you mean"

"The reason you want to go back to that room — it just now ceased to exist, I suppose."

"—"

To Subaru's broken, halting question, Beatrice answered flatly, just like that.

His throat closed, his eyes went wide as they could go, and before he knew it, Subaru had collapsed to his knees on the spot. His shoulders slumped, his head hung low, and a terrible ringing echoed inside his skull.

Pain, pain — the pain he'd forgotten was called back up, and static ate away at Subaru's consciousness. He genuinely wished everything would be churned up and swept away by that noise, and simply vanish. He didn't want to understand any of it. He didn't want to realize it. And yet—

"Let me see your wounds, I suppose. They're too pitiful to look at, I suppose."

The girl came closer and knelt, frowning as she took in the wounds on the slumped Subaru's right arm, left side, and right shoulder. A faint glow enveloped her palm, and the light was first directed at the most severely damaged right arm. —A sensation like itching ran through the arm, which until now had only ever pleaded pure heat, and with a faint, wet sound, the regeneration of muscle fiber began.

First the bleeding stopped, and then, little by little, in step with the glow, a membrane formed over the surface of the wound, and cells whose recovery had been accelerated began to fill in the gouged-out portions. Only—

"It'll take time for it to return to its original thickness, and more importantly, the fingers you lost won't grow back, I suppose. ...Your hip, and your right shoulder, too."

"...What... are you doing"

In a low, hoarse mutter, a flat, emotionless voice slipped from Subaru's mouth.

Beatrice, her attention fixed on treating the wound, knit her brow at his voice, and held out her palm — the one radiating healing power — where Subaru could see it.

"I don't want to, but I'm going to treat your wounds anyway, because there's no helping it, I suppose. Betty's the only one in this mansion who can heal a wound this bad, I'd wager. You'd best be grateful, I suppose."

"Healing... the wound...? What... for...?"

"Left alone, it's the kind of wound that would cost you your life, I'd think. It's no particular concern of Betty's whether you live or die, but having you die here is simply not something Betty can allow, I suppose."

Closing one eye — perhaps dismissing Subaru's words as delirious nonsense brought on by his wounds — Beatrice answered curtly and moved to continue the treatment. But—

"—!"

"Ah—"

Even as he felt the wound closing under a distinct wave of healing, Subaru wrenched his injured arm free, wringing a small cry of surprise from Beatrice.

Straining his trembling knees, he rolled sideways, smearing the archive floor generously as he put distance between himself and the girl, and glared back at her with an expression of sheer, terrible intensity.

Breathing hard, the stake still lodged in his hip fell to the floor from the motion. A shrill clatter. There came the sound of pooled fluid draining out, and blood welled up from that wound as well, running down his thigh, a river of blood streaming across the floor where he knelt. Baring his teeth at Beatrice, who watched, holding her breath, Subaru snarled,

"I don't need my woundsh healed...! If it doesn't matter whether I live or die... then why the hell are you trying to shave me!?"

"That's... because you're so pathetic, it's more than Betty can stand to watch..."

"Why—why me!? If you're willing to move, willing to try and shave someone, then why didn't you shave Petra... Frederica!? With this power of yoursh, you didn't even have to fight — jusht running would've been enough... there were so many waysh...!"

With the Door Crossing severing the connection between a room and the outside world, she could have shaken off even that relentless pursuer, Elsa. Applied properly, there was no ability better suited to escape than this. Petra, who'd been too slow to flee; Frederica, who had gone to hold the line; and Rem, bedridden and unable even to move—!

"You could've shaved them...! Because I'm a fool, because I'm weak... the place I couldn't reach, you could've reached... and yet, why..."

"Why would Betty do such a thing... Betty has no reason to save these three people you speak of, I'd think. Betty doesn't know them. None of it is any concern of Betty's, I suppose."

"Then—! You had no reason to shave me either, did you!?"

Beatrice shook her head, denying Subaru's plea. Piling denial on denial against her, Subaru struck the floor with his half-healed right arm.

"Why'd you shave me!? Why'd you help me!? Wash it a whim? Then why only me — what's the difference between me and the other three! Rem was always such a good girl, Frederica had things she still wanted to do... Petra was still jusht a child... they all had so much more than me...! A reason to live... value!"

"Value? A reason? Why should Betty have to respect such after-the-fact self-satisfaction? That is an appalling degree of conceit, Human!"

"Then what the hell is with your inconsistent behavior!? All that time I was shearching everywhere for you, you never even showed your face, but now, of all timesh, when I'm in danger, you come butting in...! If you don't shee any value in me or those kids either, you should've jusht shtayed shut up in here alone the whole time!!"

Why show herself now, only after everything had already become too late?

She could have hidden completely, without letting anyone so much as sense her presence, and yet if Elsa noticed the abnormality that had befallen Subaru, there was no guarantee Beatrice's existence wouldn't be exposed.

If it came to that, there might well be a future in which even this girl was cut down by that murderous blade. Why had she taken that risk, just to bring in someone half-dead like him?

Why had she thought to save someone who had given up on living, who had wished for death?

"It doesn't matter if it wash a whim or whatever else... if you really mean to shave me, to shave me... if even a scrap of the intent to help me still remains in you... then right now, kill me... kill me..."

"Wh-what... are you saying, I wonder..."

"Right now! Kill me! Before everything gets overwritten, before this moment where I lose it all becomes set in shtone! Kill me! Kill me! Kill meeee!!"

Spitting, coughing up blood, clawing at the floor with his ruined right hand and remaining left, Subaru screamed his plea.

Before the very meaning of Subaru's life was torn out by the roots — before the future arrived in which simply going on breathing, uselessly, made everything irrecoverable.

He screamed for someone to put an end to this useless, hopeless lump of incompetence and impotence.

But Beatrice would not accept that scream from the depths of his Soul, that plea.

She shook her head, her face showing both confusion and revulsion.

"Betty doesn't understand, doesn't understand at all, I suppose. Betty can't make sense of a Human like you. Why would you... you're alive right now, so why would you say such a thing?"

"Shaving a life ishn't the only kind of shaving there is!? Being alive right now is agony for me! I shouldn't exisht, I shouldn't be here... if you're not going to shave me, then..."

Without relying on anyone else, right now, with his own hands, he would end this miserable stretch of time—

Seeing Subaru hold his breath in resolve, Beatrice let out a thin cry — "Ah—" But hearing it, Subaru, without hesitation, stuck out his tongue from his mouth.

"—!"

He bit down on it with all his strength, tearing it off, and carried out his act of self-destruction.

Agony. Pain on a different dimension entirely from that of his right arm, or his hip and shoulder. No matter how much of it he tasted, there could be no building tolerance to pain. Every wound born in any place, every pain arising from anywhere, was always different, always new, always agonizing, always cruel — there could never be growing used to it. Pain, at any time, in any circumstance, simply behaved as pain, and treated everyone the same.

Blood flooded his mouth, and Subaru's eyes rolled back white as he collapsed on the spot.

He fell, his vision spinning in a wide circle, his limbs trembling as convulsions began. Agony — his breath stopped. The torn-off tongue blocked his throat, and he fell into respiratory distress.

"—wh-what have you done!"

It was not the kind of wound that killed outright. Sharp, dull pain stabbed intermittently through his brain, his limbs trembled without end, and tears of blood ran down his cheeks in his agony. His tongue, hanging limp, half torn through, caught at the corner of his lips — a symbol of just how insufficient Subaru's resolve had been when he'd gone through with his self-destruction.

In this other world, this was the third time Subaru had chosen self-destruction.

The first had been in the midst of the loop at the mansion, self-destruction as a resolve to reclaim something irrecoverable.

The second had been amid the loss of self, upon learning he had let Rem's existence slip away, ending the loop that had begun in the royal capital. He had driven a knife into his throat, but nothing had changed.

And now, the third self-destruction. There was no guarantee anywhere that he could go back, but continuing to live on through this stretch of time was something Subaru simply could not do. It was too heavy. Impossible. So then, even if it meant betting on a single grain of possibility, in order to take back what he had let slip away—

"...No. Don't... leave me..."

A trembling voice called out to Subaru from somewhere in the world that had begun growing distant.

Even that voice grew distant, and more distant, and at last vanished—

—When he opened his eyes, the first thing to strike Subaru's nostrils was the smell of dust.

"Ngh...?"

Waiting for his consciousness to surface fully, Subaru shook his head with his eyes still closed, and understood that he had awoken.

His body lay sprawled sideways on the floor. Tasting the chill, cold feel of the ground across his whole body, he thought hazily — right, the restart point was inside the tomb.

He raised himself up, opened his eyes in the dim light, and looked around. His vision, so soon after waking, was still unreliable, blurred with tears, unable to make out what he was looking for.

Still, it seemed he had managed to Return by Death once again, and he felt relief at that. If the place he'd returned to was the tomb, then the restart point hadn't changed.

In the chamber of the tomb's Trial — timeline-wise, this should be immediately after Subaru overcame the Trial and returned. Emilia should be collapsed beside him in the room, and everything should begin from waking her up.

"My head... it hurts..."

Rubbing between his brows, shaking his head lightly, Subaru scrambled to get his thoughts in order.

There had already been countless things he needed to think through, and now the previous loop had added even more to the pile. Not a single proper solution for what to do came to mind right now. Even the ray of hope he thought he'd glimpsed felt, in hindsight, like nothing more than a moth-lamp luring him into a fresh trap.

It felt as though, having tried to skirt around a pitfall of despair and clear it, he'd only fallen into a different pitfall.

"And a nasty one too, complete with a bed of spikes at the bottom, no less..."

As a figure of speech meaning "lethal," it wasn't wrong.

The Trials of Sanctuary. His relationship with Garfiel. The attack on the mansion. The mystery of the vanished grace period that should have existed, and his desire for revenge against Elsa — establishing a way to save Rem and the others.

Problems piling up until his head threatened to boil over — but simply being given the chance to agonize over how to somehow deal with them was already a blessing.

It was a situation where he hadn't been entirely without the resignation that this might be where it all ended. Merely having broken through that — how much of a saving grace was that alone—

"It's rough, having to face Emilia again pretending I don't know anything—but—"

Even as he spoke, Subaru noticed his hazy vision beginning to clear. Sniffing at the musty smell, he told himself he first had to find Emilia.

Thinking that, he lightly raised his right hand to his forehead like a visor — and only then did he finally notice.

—the fact that his right hand was missing three fingers.

"Wha—!? Ah!?"

At finding an injury that shouldn't exist, a scar that had no business carrying over, Subaru's throat groaned in shock. And the fact that he had been looking at a world far too convenient for himself was finally driven home as his trembling eyes took in the world around him.

A cold floor, a dry stone wall. A settled, moldy smell. The tomb's space, the one Subaru had wanted. But what actually lay before Subaru in reality was a corner of an archive lined with bookshelves crammed full of books, a room drifting with the distinctive scent given off by parchment aged with time, and—

"The Forbidden Archive... why..."

The inexplicable fact that his own body was still placed in a spot he thought he'd said goodbye to. Bracing for the worst-case scenario, Subaru first checked over his own body.

The worst possibility — that the repetition of the world had become fixed at the very moment Subaru had stepped into the Forbidden Archive.

His expression unable to hide his dread, Subaru stared hard at the right arm he'd raised to touch his own face. Three fingers missing, and an arm that had lost fully a third of its thickness compared to the left. But the wound on that arm had already closed over, the twisted mounds of flesh and discoloration telling him plainly that his body was in the middle of regenerating.

There was no conspicuous external injury left on his hip or right shoulder either, where the stakes had pierced him — only a lingering sensation of tightness in the skin, and an occasional unpleasant twinge.

At the very least, this couldn't be the moment right after he'd stepped into the Forbidden Archive, fresh from his injuries. Which left, by process of elimination, only one remaining possibility.

"—Awake at last, I see."

For Subaru, who had already grasped the truth of it, that was the voice he least wanted to hear right now.

Composed in manner, ringing as though weary of the world, and yet unable to fully erase its interest — a soprano that seemed, despite itself, to be seeking connection.

Still seated on the floor, Subaru turned his head.

Behind him, with a wish he couldn't quite discard, he hoped for the illusion that a silver-haired girl might be there. As if to shatter that fantasy, there sat instead a girl in a dress, perched on a wooden stepladder.

Looking exactly as she had before he'd lost consciousness, Beatrice sat looking down at Subaru, a book in her hand.

A hollow breath escaped him unconsciously. In front of Subaru, she snapped the book shut with a sound and climbed slowly down from the stepladder.

"You went and did something so foolish, it really put Betty through the wringer, I suppose. Your arm, your shoulder, your hip, your tongue — Betty healed them all together. There shouldn't be any lingering problems, I'd think."

"..."

"Speechless at the fact that you got to keep your life, I suppose? Well, let this be a lesson, and don't go doing anything so foolish again..."

"Do you... even understand what you've done?"

"Huh...?"

Lining up words that practically demanded gratitude, Beatrice had drawn closer to the silent Subaru. He hit her with a low, strangled voice, and the instant her face creased into a frown—

"—!"

He rose to his feet, and his outstretched left arm seized hold of Beatrice's ornate dress. He yanked her close as her mouth fell open in a startled "Ah," and thrust his face at hers as if slamming it there.

"Who the hell asked you to save me—!! When did I ever ask for that!!"

"—ah"

"Do you even understand what you did!? Because of you, everything's ruined! Everything — everything that should have been fixable — is wiped out, because of you! Why couldn't you just let me stay dead!? I survive, and then what — what happens then, huh!? Answer me!"

By spending his life without holding anything back, quite literally, Subaru should have earned the right to try again.

But that had been blocked by the very hands of the girl in front of him, and it had never come to pass. All that remained in Subaru's hands was an indescribable sense of loss, and an endless anger toward Beatrice.

"Saving me on a whim, healing my wounds too... and that satisfies you? You want me to thank you!? Fine! Thanks a lot! Thanks to you, my life got saved! Everything else — every single thing but my life — I dropped it all, and only my life got saved!"

"B-Betty just... just..."

"What now, at this point!? I've got no end of thank-yous for you!? Go on, put on that composed, superior face like always and look down on me like it's the most natural thing in the world. You're good at that, aren't you? You love it, don't you? Looking down on the likes of us humans and sneering—ah—"

Pouring in every ounce of hatred he had, cheeks twisted into an ugly grin, Subaru showered the Beatrice he'd pulled close with a torrent of abuse. He behaved contemptibly, trying to bury the dissatisfaction, disappointment, and sense of loss filling his heart. But the tail end of his words abruptly broke off.

"—hic"

"Ah..."

Because he had just witnessed fat tears spilling from the eyes of the girl he held, so close.

The instant he saw it, the blood that had rushed hotly to his head drained away all at once, and he was seized with unbearable horror at the ugly, malice-filled words that had just poured from his own mouth.

His slackened fingers released Beatrice's body from that grip of hatred, and the girl's body, falling back as though shoved away, struck the bookshelf behind her, her knees giving out on the spot.

A violent nausea welled up in him. Fully conscious now of what he'd just said, he couldn't bear the ugliness of it, couldn't bear himself.

It was ugly. Twisted. What was it, if not simply lashing out at someone else? From Beatrice's perspective — knowing nothing of the circumstances of Return by Death — all she had done was heal the wounds of a Subaru at death's door. She deserved to be thanked as the one who'd saved his life; there was no reason on earth she should be cursed at.

With his reason, he understood that. But his emotions refused to acknowledge it. Tossed about by the two extremes of feeling warring inside him, Subaru still raised his face, telling himself he had to say something to the fallen Beatrice.

"No... I— sorry. I didn't mean... to say that. It's not... it's not your fault..."

If there was one person to blame, it was, without question, Subaru himself.

He had known what would happen, and yet, carelessly, he had stepped into the tiger's den and trodden on its tail. To make the people around him pay the price for that, while blustering that he alone had no reason to be blamed — that was arrogance beyond measure.

Emotionally, there was still a part of him that wanted to blame her for lacking the information. There was still a feeling he couldn't swallow, at how she'd hidden herself from Subaru only to show herself at that one moment.

But at the very least, that was no free pass for Subaru to loudly condemn her.

"I'm sorry. Thanks for healing my wounds. But, I need to, right away..."

At the very least, he had to disappear from in front of her and choose self-destruction somewhere else.

Subaru had no reason to keep going in this world. Here, he had lost far too much. Subaru could not be strong enough to go on living in a world where he'd lost what he should never have lost.

So Subaru offered Beatrice his thanks in few words, and let his gaze wander, meaning to take his leave of the Forbidden Archive—

"—"

—and noticed, lying beside the crumpled Beatrice, a book bound in black.

A plain cover. A thick binding. About the size of a dictionary, with a weighty look that seemed like it would be somewhat unwieldy to carry around. All of it — Subaru's eyes were riveted on something he recognized.

Why was that here, now, in this place?

"But the... the Gospel is supposed to be in the Dragon Carriage... on hand with me... there's no way it'd be in the archive..."

The Witch Cult's Gospel that Petelgeuse had carried had been collected as spoils and was in Subaru's own possession. But it wasn't being treated as a book to be shelved in the archive — it was simply being kept under strict caution as an item of unknown purpose, and there was no way it should be here.

Shaking his head at the incomprehensible situation, Subaru reached out toward the fallen Gospel, wanting to check its contents, wanting this unease of his to be proven wrong. But—

"—No!"

Right in front of Subaru as he reached out on impulse, the Gospel was snatched away from the side.

Looking over, he saw Beatrice, the hem of her clothes in disarray, breathing hard, clutching the Gospel to her chest with both arms as she put distance between herself and Subaru. Keeping her distance, her throat still catching with sobs, she looked down at the Gospel in her arms and let out a breath that resembled relief, tracing its cover with her fingers.

That gesture — a gesture like touching something dear to her — stirred up Subaru's bad premonition to the utmost degree, and so—

"Why are you holding that like it's something so precious?"

"..."

"That's the book those Witch Cult bastards had... isn't it? It's not, right? It just looks really similar, but it's a different thing, right? It looks so alike it'd cause misunderstandings, so you just moved it away so I wouldn't get the wrong idea, right? Right? I mean, I jump to conclusions easy, I get fixed ideas, I've got a foul mouth, nasty eyes, a twisted personality..."

"..."

"Hey— just deny it. Please."

To Subaru, rattling off a defense of her at breakneck speed, Beatrice kept her silence. In the end, Subaru's words became nothing but a plea.

In front of that Subaru, Beatrice let out a small breath, and thrust the book held in both hands forward, out where Subaru could see it.

"It's exactly as you imagine, I suppose. ...This is a Gospel. The same thing the Witch Cult you speak of carries — a guide to happiness. A place to anchor one's life. The one and only truth, I suppose."

"Wh-why... do you have something like that? Where do they even sell it? Telling you the future, that's way too lucky an item to have. A real-life strategy guide — how much does that break the game balance... hey, come on."

"...Betty hasn't been instructed to answer that question, I don't believe."

To Subaru, tossing out his question in a trembling voice, Beatrice replied coldly, flipping through the pages. Watching the girl drop her eyes to the book's contents, Subaru felt a numbing sensation on his tongue as he said,

"So you won't do anything unless it's what that book says?"

"That question isn't written in the book either, I suppose."

"What about healing my wounds? Or hiding me, when I was about to be killed, here in the archive?"

"That question isn't in the book either, I don't believe."

"What about talking to me like this, right now? Stopping me, when I tried to die...?"

"—I don't know."

Eyes still fixed on the book, Beatrice tossed back her answer, utterly without emotion.

At that doll-like figure, that manner stripped of all emotion, Subaru raised his voice, gripped by a dread that made his lungs seize and a dizziness that threatened to make him forget how to breathe.

"You're saying you can't do anything at all unless that book says so!?"

"...I suppose so. That's right. Every single thing, Betty follows the guidance of the Gospel, I'd think. Doing so is the meaning of Betty's existence, and Betty exists only in order to do that."

"Was saving me... like this, too, only because the book told you to!? Saving me when I nearly died in the Witchbeast forest! Trying to save me when my heart was worn down to nothing! Fooling around together, shouting at each other, all that time we spent goofing off like idiots... none of it had a shred of your own will in it anywhere... is that what you're telling me!?"

"That's what— that's what Betty's telling you!!"

Subaru's words, clinging almost like a prayer. Overlapping the very end of them, Beatrice, her face red with anger, screamed. Stepping forward, she pointed at Subaru.

"Everything Betty has done up until now, everything Betty has seen, everything Betty has said — all of it was written right here, I'd think. You... someone like you could never move Betty's heart. You'd best rein in that conceit of yours, Human."

"—"

"Betty will carry out what is asked of Betty — the meaning of Betty being kept alive. This life, this time, everything, has been spent for that purpose, and Betty exists only, only to do that, I'd think. ...As if Betty would let someone like you deny that...!!"

"Bea..."

Beatrice's emotions poured out as though a dam had burst. Subaru moved to speak on reflex, but he was cut off by a sudden pressure bearing down from in front of him.

Feeling as though caught in a gust of wind, he staggered back, and Subaru realized his own body, unable to resist, was being pushed toward the door. —At this rate, he'd be flung outside.

"Wait— Beatrice!"

"Everything Betty is, is for Mother's sake! The bond with Mother is all Betty has! Betty doesn't know anything about you... doesn't know..."

"—"

"Betty doesn't know you. Betty hates you. Hates you. —Hates you more than anything!"

Shaking her head, hiding her tear-streaked face, the girl's scream sent Subaru's body flying.

The door swung open. The space itself was trying to drive Subaru out of the Forbidden Archive. Subaru's right hand caught the edge of the doorframe. But it was missing three fingers. He barely managed to support his body on his index finger alone, but only a few seconds' grace remained.

Raising his face, Subaru tried to call out to the sobbing girl—

"Beatri—!"

"...-sama"

Drowned out by that thin, faint voice, Subaru's words never reached her.

He was blown away. Erased. Space warped, and Subaru's body was flung out from where it belonged to a place it had no business being.

"—"

The door slammed shut with a sound, the wind that had been blasting through fell still, and silence dropped over the archive.

Left behind was the girl, alone. Her face still fighting back sobs, she made her way with slow steps toward the back of the room — to the stepladder, her usual spot — and sank down onto it unsteadily, drawing her knees up and, on top of them, opening the Gospel with trembling fingers. And then—

"Why... there's nothing here for Betty... nothing..."

Before a blank page with nothing written on it at all, only her sobs continued to echo, hollow, through the quiet room.
