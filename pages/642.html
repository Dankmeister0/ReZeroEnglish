Arc 8 Chapter 26: "The Poison Princess"

—This was the same moment that Petra Leyte's slap left Natsuki Subaru's cheek swollen, and Flop O'Connell's bombshell declaration threw Medium O'Connell into confusion.

"—Guess they really couldn't wait 'til we arrived, huh."

Chewing on his pipe, the strongest Shinobi of the city-state tilted his face upward and murmured, sounding weary.
Slouching his already sloping shoulders even further, the Wolf Human sitting cross-legged—Halibel—was out on the roof of a dragon carriage amid the streaming night scenery, keeping a quiet evening watch.
There had just been an incident where the Coupled Dragon Carriages themselves had been targeted.
Thanks to the children noticing the surprise attack before even Halibel had, disaster had been averted—but that kind of luck wasn't going to strike twice. And making sure it didn't happen again was Halibel's job.

"After that, little Anna's been giving me some pretty harsh looks. Gotta make up for it somehow."

The girl he'd known since childhood through family connections had paid an extraordinary price for this trip to the Empire.
Halibel's company, and the role of envoy for the city-state—one might think such a prize would set a merchant's soul ablaze with opportunity, but her real purpose had been searching for a lost friend.
She was a girl who hated showing weakness. Of course, she'd never say as much out loud.

"That's exactly why an old middle-aged man who's known her since way back oughta lend a hand."

Saying so, Halibel slowly rose to his feet where he stood.
Multiple dragon carriages linked together and hauled at once by numerous Earth Dragons—the Coupled Dragon Carriages, a truly greedy pinnacle of Imperial engineering that showed off the absurdity of the Divine Protection of Wind Evasion without reservation.
Even so, Halibel couldn't get used to this unnaturalness.
The scenery streamed by, the wheels bit into the bumpy road, and yet he felt no wind, no jolting. In Karagi, Earth Dragons were few in number and Beast Carriages were more common—the jostling, the slower pace, those were what felt familiar.
And speaking of unfamiliar unnaturalness—

"Man, walking corpses were already something else, but... guess Flying Dragons turn into 'zombies' too, huh."

Slipping a finger into the gap of his wasou robe, Halibel grumbled while scratching his belly with unkempt abandon.
Reflected in his thread-thin eyes fixed on the night sky was a swarm of corpse-turned Flying Dragons—'Undead Flying Dragons'—their once-mighty bodies and spread wings now scarred with pitiful cracks.
A swarm thick as a black cloud blotting out the stars was slowly closing in on the Coupled Dragon Carriages.

"—There's one troublesome fella in there, huh."

A presence lurking within the swarm of Undead Flying Dragons earned that assessment even from Halibel.
Sensing that ghastly enemy's presence, the Earth Dragons pulling the carriages began to grow unsettled as well.
Even Earth Dragons, known as humanity's loyal friends, hadn't lost their wild instincts just from being domesticated. They felt alarm at an approaching threat, and fear besides.
The Coupled Dragon Carriages were a fine invention, but crises like this were an ever-present risk. Earth Dragons were, after all, unusually empathetic creatures. If one grew afraid, the terror would chain outward all at once—

"—!!"

Just as Halibel voiced that worry, a whinny sliced through the evening dark and rang out.
It was not the fatal cry of the approaching threat meant to freeze the Earth Dragons in terror.
The source of that whinny was the lead carriage—among the dozens of Earth Dragons harnessed to haul the Coupled Dragon Carriages, it was the cry of a single jet-black Earth Dragon, tirelessly running in the single most crucial position.

"Heh, quite the gutsy kid we've got there."

Thanks to the black Earth Dragon's quick thinking, the spreading contagion of fear among the Earth Dragons was stopped in its tracks.
Thanks to that, the worst-case scenario—a frightened Earth Dragon abandoning its post and the Coupled Dragon Carriages breaking apart mid-run—was averted. And just as he was feeling impressed, Halibel suddenly noticed something.

"Huh, whaddya know, that black Earth Dragon's a girl. Not gutsy, more like... spunky, I guess."

His narrowed thread-eyes widened for once as he let that slip, and Halibel gave a small "heh heh" of a laugh.
Laughing, the Wolf Human Shinobi kept up his casual pace right off the side of the still-running carriage—stepping out into empty space—and leapt into the sky.

To meet it head-on. To keep that difficult foe, one even the 'Sin Archbishops' would call troublesome, from getting any closer.
And that difficult foe was—

"Hey hey hey hey, what the hell is that!?"

Plastered against the corridor's window frame, Subaru's voice cracked as he stared at the sight outside.
It was right after he'd taken Petra's slap—right after finishing the conversation with Rem and Rui, no, with Spica, in the cabin, and rejoining Emilia and the others who'd worriedly waited for him, in the middle of giving his report.

"This one's for me, and just a little bit for Otto-san too!"

That was the physical punishment he'd taken, one blow—as he chewed on the worry he'd caused them, and the worry he'd no doubt keep causing going forward, planting his feet firmly back on solid ground.
Just then, a sudden roar shattering the night sky was heard, and Subaru and the others plastered themselves to the window.

"Is that a black Flying Dragon... no way, it's way too damn huge to compare to a Flying Dragon!"

"That size isn't a Flying Dragon, I suppose. No mistake, it's Dragon-class. And what's more..."

"—That Dragon has three heads!"

Just as Emilia, plastered to the window above Subaru and Beatrice who stood side by side at the glass, shouted—it was a sight so monstrous that not just Subaru but even someone from another world would recognize it as an aberration.

Amid the night scenery, flying alongside the Coupled Dragon Carriages, swinging around its massive body and three heads, was a terrifyingly enormous black Dragon.
Cracks ran across its mighty body and the great spread wings, and three pairs of golden eyes that asserted their presence even against the night sky proved unmistakably what it was.

"A Dragon Zombie...!!"

"A black Dragon with three heads... could it be the 'Three-Headed' Valgren?"

"You know it, Sister!"

Still pressing their cheeks to the window glass, the three of them—Subaru and the others—turned back to Ram behind them. Ram, arms crossed hugging her own elbows, glared at the Dragon outside with her pale pink eyes.

"It's the notorious Blight Dragon that once rampaged in a city on the border between the Empire and the Kingdom."

"Knew it! Uniquely-named monsters with cool nicknames like that are always tough as hell, that's just how it goes..."

"A few years after the 'Demi-Human War,' it's the Dragon that nearly burned a commercial city to the ground. And what's more..."

"What's more, what? Is there something else too?"

"...It was the battle in which the previous head of the house, Roswaal-sama's grandmother, lost her life."

After a moment's hesitation, Ram's words made Subaru and the others catch their breath.
Hearing that a close acquaintance's relative had been a victim suddenly made the enemy's threat feel viscerally real. Especially since it was Roswaal's grandmother. There was no way this thing was weak.
Such a formidable Blight Dragon was, for now, being held back by Halibel ahead of them, but—

"Um, who exactly are those Wolf Humans over there?"

Peering through the same window with Subaru at the center, Tanza couldn't hide her astonishment at the battle unfolding outside.
Her astonished way of putting it seemed wrong, and yet wasn't wrong at all.
Her describing the fighting Halibel as "those Wolf Humans" was, in fact, correct.
Because—

"There's like, three Halibel-sans..."

"No, four, Subaru. There's been one the whole time trying to shear off the Dragon's wings from its back."

"I don't think the exact number's really the issue here, I suppose..."

Billed as the strongest Shinobi of the city-state, Halibel had erased Sphinx's lethal attack in an instant. That alone lived up to his reputation, but the battle now unfolding in Subaru and the others' sight was more than fitting of that reputation too.
It was nothing complicated. Halibel, true to a ninja, had split himself into clones and was engaging the Blight Dragon in aerial combat.
Combined with the sheer speed of the fighting, it was no wonder Tanza, who'd never met Halibel before, mistook the strongest of the city-state for a group of multiple Shinobi called the 'Sin Archbishops.'
In any case—

"Uaw!"
"—I don't think that Blight Dragon is the only enemy. Shouldn't we regroup with everyone?"

Spica raised her voice, and Rem, holding her hand, proposed they regroup.
Ideally, Subaru would have liked to share the matter of how to treat Spica with everyone under calmer circumstances, but with the situation already in motion, he couldn't afford such luxuries.

"Right, yeah. ...Let's get to everyone else for now! Abel and the others should be moving too. Right away—guh!?"

"Subaru!?"

Following Rem and the others' suggestion, he'd started to say they should move—right in the middle of that.
A sudden burning pain in his chest cut off Subaru's words mid-sentence, and as he started to double over, Emilia caught his body to support him.
But he wasn't the only one to feel that strange affliction.

"...Are you all right?"

"B-being supported by you is humiliating, deer girl..."

"Even Beatrice-sama...? Barusu, what happened?"

Just like Subaru, Beatrice staggered from the sudden pain, and displeasure crossed her face at Tanza, who'd reflexively caught her.
Watching that exchange out of the corner of her eye, Ram asked her question, and Subaru answered, "I—I don't know," continuing:

"All of a sudden, my chest just started hurting bad... ugh!?"

Saying that, Subaru pulled down the collar of his own clothes and his eyes went wide.
Seeing the same thing Subaru had, Emilia and Ram's expressions twisted into puzzlement as well.
There, on his skin, was a red welt like an angry insect bite—except it wasn't just any welt.

"What is this... someone's eye shape?"

"Ah, that's what it looks like to you too, huh? This is like an eyeball mark... wait, don't tell me, Bea-ko! Yours—"

"...The same mark's on Betty's soft skin too, I suppose."

What lay on Subaru's chest was a mark about the size of a child-sized palm—a stylized pattern that looked like a drawn eye.
The same thing had apparently been carved into Beatrice as well; peering into the collar of her opened dress, Tanza nodded deeply.

"But just me and Bea-ko? Nobody else has anything?"

"Perverted."

"It's pure concern!? I'm not saying anything dirty, not in this situation and at this size!?"

Confirming that the still dully-aching mark hadn't appeared on anyone else, Subaru rubbed at it and frowned.
It was an abnormal sign that had surfaced under these circumstances. Obviously, this was clearly the action of someone hostile to Subaru and the others—but the intent behind it was unclear.
Or perhaps, since Subaru and Beatrice were connected through their Contract, something cast on one had also manifested on the other's body.

"No good, thinking about it isn't getting us anywhere. Best to have someone with knowledge take a look. Anyway, we need to get out of here right now—"

"Ahhu!!"
"—! Don't move!"

Once again, he tried to propose moving from this spot, and once again something interrupted.
Spica and Rem, looking frantic, shoved Subaru and the others all at once out of the way. A sudden act—but the right call.

—Because the huge scissor-blades of an enemy silhouette, bursting through the roof directly above them, violently tore apart the exact spot where Subaru and the others had just been standing.

Otto too, seeing the mark, clouded his expression, saying "That's..."

Glancing briefly at Petra beside him, Otto's look made her instantly grasp its meaning, and she shook her head vigorously side to side.
Judging by that reaction, this pattern had appeared on neither Otto nor Petra.
That in itself was cause for relief, but—

"...What comes to mind are something that slowly eats away at life, or something that reads the target's thoughts. Or else, something for constantly pinpointing one's location."

"You're deliberately telling us in order from worst to least bad, aren't you. In that case, the most likely one is the mark for pinpointing location, then?"

"My my, you two really are quite capable, ye-e-es."

Calmly picking through the possibilities laid out and reaching that judgment, Otto and Petra prompted Roswaal to close his eyes.
That judgment wasn't wrong. The most dangerous kind of curse-mark, the life-eating type, wasn't something that could be carved remotely no matter how you looked at it, so it hardly needed to be considered a candidate.
Still, being marked even just for location-tracking alone was troublesome enough as it was.

"We may need to consider splitting up so our movements aren't laid bare to the enemy. There's also Halibel-dono's support outside. You two should part from me and—"

"—No, it seems it's already too late."

Right after answering in that low voice, Otto pulled Petra beside him close by the shoulder.
Petra's eyes went wide reflexively as, in that instant, the window right beside her shattered and a massive body burst into the corridor of the Coupled Dragon Carriage.
It was an enemy with a menacing presence, its whole body covered in black armor, wielding huge scissor-blades—an Undead.

"—!"

Petra bit back a scream and, from within Otto's arms, pointed a finger at the enemy.
While praising the girl's bravery in trying to seize the initiative even in this sudden situation, Roswaal stepped forward and drove a spear-hand strike in before the enemy could raise its huge blades.
Thrusting a knife-hand into the gap of the helmet covering its face, jamming his fingers into its eye socket, he chanted:

"Goa."

In that instant, the head burst into swelling flame, exploding from within along with the armor.
Despite its flashy entrance, the intruding enemy was allowed to do nothing at all. Judging that the best move, Roswaal turned around thinking he'd played his best hand—only to realize he'd been too soft.
One after another, from the carriage roof, from the corridor, soldiers in the same equipment came pouring into the cabin.
All at once, the Coupled Dragon Carriages themselves had become a running battlefield.

His stern face, covered in scars and beard, twisting in fury, Goz Ralfon roared like a lion.
In Goz's field of view, the swarm of Undead Flying Dragons filling the sky was visible, dropping the Undead they held in their claws one after another onto the Coupled Dragon Carriages.
This bold method of transport was the worst possible mechanism by which enemy soldiers boarded the Coupled Dragon Carriages.

In fact, nearly half of the soldiers failed to attach themselves and instead fell straight to the ground from the Undead Flying Dragons, the impact turning their revived bodies to dust in an outrageous, repeated waste.
And yet, a deployment of force that would be pure suicide for living soldiers became, for dead ones, an efficient method of transport that need not fear attrition at all.
And the one making this possible was—

"—So this is Lady Lamia's 'Pruning Squad'!"

Rushing to protect the Earth Dragons in the most critical position at the lead carriage of the Coupled Dragon Carriages, Goz glared up at the sky at the worst possible combination brought by the swarm of dead Flying Dragons—Undead.

The 'Pruning Squad' was a group of madmen remembered in Vollachian history for carrying out the Great Purge.
Every member wore armor that obscured their faces entirely, wielding huge scissor-blades, pruning away those unnecessary to their lord's great ambitions—hence the name, Pruning Squad.
The reason that group left its name in Imperial history was that the Imperial family's daughter—Lamia Godwin, then still only nine years old—had used them to slaughter the entire retinue of a rebelling mid-ranking count under her authority.

The victims of that purge suffered a fate so gruesome it could only be described as this world's hell.
Naturally, even those doing the bloody work bore a heavy burden from such an atrocity, yet by some means the still-young princess had made every last one of her subordinates carry it out, completing a mad squad bound by steel loyalty.
Just how thoroughly she had seized the hearts of those soldiers, this tactic itself proved—the fact that even in death, Lamia's poison still had not drained from their souls.
And—

"—Oh my. To think, even after all these years have passed, you still remember me, and remember my darling beasts too—how lovely."

A sudden shiver split the sky, and a sickly-sweet voice descended from above.
In that instant, Goz threw all form aside and swung his warhammer up, deflecting a crimson-red trajectory head-on before leaping back. An instant later, the warhammer ignited in Goz's hands.
Naturally so. —For the radiance of the 'Sunlight Sword' illuminates all things brilliantly.

"To think you would descend to the battlefield yourself...! Lady Lamia Godwin!!"

"Is that really so surprising? Given this body, isn't that the best option, don't you think?"

Gritting his back teeth, glaring straight ahead, in Goz's field of view stood a noble Undead smiling bewitchingly.
A sumptuous dress red as blood, dazzling jewels beyond counting, all of it beauty rendered worthless by comparison against the innate, God-given beauty peculiar to the Vollachian Imperial family—though even that beauty was, paired with pale, cracked skin and eerie golden eyes, but a shadow of what it once was.

Once, in the 'Imperial Selection Ceremony,' she had contended with Vincent Vollachia for the throne, and might have held the seat of Empress—the 'Poison Princess,' Lamia Godwin—as if to prove how utterly she had changed, she looked down at her own half-body, shattered by Goz's counterattack.
And yet, at a blow that would reduce any other Undead to dust, Lamia's body did not head toward collapse—instead, the cracks mended themselves, and it returned to its original form.

"That body of yours..."

"An undying body that won't disappear no matter how crushed or destroyed... it doesn't seem convenient enough to boast about quite that much, though."

Calmly stroking the restored part, wielding the 'Sunlight Sword' with that same hand as proof of her healing, Lamia—in her arms, the unyielding brilliance of the sacred sword felt all the more like a sacrilege.
At the fact making Goz's face twist, Lamia murmured "How unpleasant," and continued:

"We couldn't talk calmly back at the Crystal Palace, but would you mind not making such a frightening face, Second General Ralfon?"

"Grh..."

"Ah, but it's First General now, isn't it? I heard about that. Vincent, my dear brother, revived the 'Nine Divine Generals' system, did he? So very like him."

Resting the hand not holding the 'Sunlight Sword' against her own chin, Lamia's lips split sideways.
That gesture, that manner of speaking, was unmistakably that of the Lamia Goz had known in life, and the ghastliness of an Undead reproducing her former conduct stirred both revulsion and fury in him.
Heedless of his inner feelings, Lamia looked Goz's golden armor up and down from head to toe and said:

"The 'Nine Divine Generals'—just a system for climbing to the top using strength alone as the yardstick, isn't it. A perfect method for assembling easily-handled pawns with empty heads."

"...Indeed, it's true that behind Your Excellency's revival of the 'Nine Divine Generals' lay the intent to prepare countermeasures against this so-called 'Great Disaster.' That much, even I concede."

"The way you say that, it sounds like you have an objection?"

"With respect, I beg to differ!"

Lamia's remark carried a ring of praise for Vincent's thinking.
Before their falling out in the 'Imperial Selection Ceremony,' Lamia had reportedly been quite devoted to Vincent. Life and death had separated the paths the two walked, but that trust had not been damaged even in death.
However—

"How much does Lady Lamia know of what happened during the time your life was gone?"

"What an odd question. —Unfortunately, nothing at all of the time I was dead. I'm in the middle of relearning what's happened, you know. You could tell me yourself, if you like."

"Then allow me one thing! With all due disrespect, permit me to correct you!"

"————"

"Just as Lady Lamia surmises, the current 'Nine Divine Generals' are all formidable hands, every one a strength I myself scarcely measure up to... but not a single one of them, not one, is an easily-handled pawn!"

Puffing out his chest clad in golden armor, Goz declared this loudly, boldly.
If Vincent, preparing to fight the 'Great Disaster,' had revived the 'Nine Divine Generals' out of a desire for pawns that would simply do his bidding, then that plan could be called a failure.
Every single General was a hardened, formidable character in their own right, never allowing for anything so easy as convenient obedience.

"The Empire's splendor is not, as Lady Lamia seems to believe, something concentrated in Your Excellency alone!"

"...Are you saying that as an insult to my brother Vincent?"

"No! No, indeed! That is not it at all, Lady Lamia!"

Narrowing her eyes, her voice edged with a certain sharpness, Lamia's words made Goz shake his head.
His feelings toward Vincent angered Lamia, but Goz too was the sort who couldn't abide insult or disrespect toward His Imperial Majesty. And yet, since the decision to abandon the Capital, his footing on that matter had shifted somewhat.
All of this, too, was the result of a change—not in Goz, but in Vincent.

"He told me that he was counting on my efforts."

"...I beg your pardon?"

"Lady Lamia, the Vincent Vollachia you knew was already exceptional! But! Even now! Still! His Imperial Majesty continues to change, seeking to become even greater!!"

"————"

"The Vollachian Empire shall achieve ever greater development from here on! For that very reason, we cannot allow those who have died once to hold us back any longer, unchecked!!"

In the sense of demanding perfection of their Emperor, Goz and Lamia were kindred spirits. But the decisive difference between the two was whether or not they knew the flow of these nine years' passage of time.
The fact that those whose time had stopped would be left behind by those whose time kept moving.

"The wise man who does not stop his steps! Surpasses even the expectations and predictions of those who have stopped theirs, and presses on still further beyond! That is our Emperor, the very pinnacle of our Vollachian Empire!"

Raising his flame-tipped warhammer, Goz shook off his hesitation and surged forward.
Until just a moment ago, however changed her appearance, his reverence for Lamia as a member of the Vollachian Imperial family had bound Goz's body. —Now, that chain was torn apart by a loyalty stronger and fiercer still.

"Ohhhhh—!!"

Letting out a furious roar, Goz's single swing arced in a half-circle and came crashing down upon Lamia.
Goz's all-out blow, delivered with unmatched brute strength, could not be easily stopped even by one of the 'Nine Divine Generals'—much less by a woman's slender arm, even wielding the 'Sunlight Sword.'
Indeed, Lamia made no attempt to block Goz's blow with the 'Sunlight Sword' in her hand.

"For a not-so-clever head, you talk quite a lot, First General Ralfon. —You've genuinely impressed me a fair bit, so let me tell you something."

Her thin lips moved, and Lamia gazed at Goz with a bewitching smile.
And then her figure, taking the full brunt of the warhammer's descending tip, shattered like porcelain without leaving the slightest resistance, blown apart into countless splinters.

Leaving behind only the promise of teaching him something, she was smashed to pieces beyond any hope of repair, as her final act.
Of course, there was also the matter from the earlier war council. Even if an Undead were defeated, there remained the possibility of revival through the so-called nucleus insects the Kingdom's mages had discovered as a vector.

"Even so..."

Striking down Lamia here should shift the situation by one crucial move.
If no one remained to openly command the 'Pruning Squad,' then the presence or absence of a commander—no, the difference in ability—would tip the balance in their favor.
That conviction of Goz's was—

"—Are you listening, First General Ralfon?"

"—!?"

The instant that sickly-sweet voice grazed his ear again, Goz's warhammer swung straight behind him, striking the torso of an enemy silhouette in its path, splitting the body top from bottom with a sound like shattering porcelain.
Catching sight of it out of the corner of his eye as it was knocked away, flying off, Goz caught his breath.

The one he'd struck down in that reflexive attack, the one now crumbling into dust and vanishing—had an impossible face.
Because that was, just now, someone he himself should have already finished off.

"How rare, that you'd say something clever like that—you're quite right."

"Impossible..."

Before that abnormal sight that should never be, the hand gripping the warhammer trembled violently.
Whether from rage, or from some other emotion, even Goz himself couldn't tell. Only one thing was certain: that this was a sacrilege.
A sacrilege against the Vollachian Empire, to which the 'Lion Knight' Goz Ralfon had sworn his loyalty—.

"'''—This is a war of annihilation between us, left behind in the past, and you all.'''"

So saying, the Lamia Godwin who should have been shattered smiled at Goz.

—Countless 'Poison Princesses,' countless Lamia Godwins, each wielding a 'Sunlight Sword' of her own.
