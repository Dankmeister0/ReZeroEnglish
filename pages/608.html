Arc 7 Chapter 103: "The Countdown of the Stars"

——The ground shook the world, the sky split into white and red, and the clash of lives reached the point of triggering cataclysmic phenomena, and it was then that Rem made up her mind.

"If I'm going to move, it has to be now."

Amid the imperial capital, where a battle of unimaginable scale was surely raging, Rem remained confined within Berstetz's residence, her freedom forbidden.
Feeling the smell of scorching grow steadily stronger day by day, Rem had been unable to do anything effective. Ashamed of that fact to her core, she could no longer accept the option of simply standing still here in silence.

In the end, she had never once come face to face with Berstetz—the master of the house and the very man holding her captive—since that first encounter. Because of that, she had no way of knowing how the old man, who had toppled Abel from the throne out of his own particular philosophy for the empire's future, felt about the great war now engulfing the entire nation.
Still, even if the chance had presented itself, she and he would never have understood one another.

"Because the ground Berstetz-san and I stand on is different."

Cold as it might sound, that was, in the end, the conclusion Rem had reached.
Only the strong, or those with room to spare, could afford to consider the circumstances of someone standing on different ground and feel for them. Rem was neither, so she had to choose.
Who to make an enemy of, and who to call an ally.

Setting that aside, Rem held no hostility toward either Berstetz or Madelyn. If anything, Abel's habit of leaving things unsaid, or Priscilla's unreasonable demands, gave her far more trouble.
So what Rem was about to do now was not born of hostility or rebellion against them—it was simply the result of the position she had chosen to stand in.

"——"

The Battle for the Imperial Capital had already begun. Berstetz's mansion should have stood in a fairly secluded part of the city, and yet even here the aftershocks of the fierce fighting could be felt.
Naturally, the residence too had been placed under strict security, and Rem, nominally a prisoner, had been ordered to remain in her assigned room—bound more tightly than usual.
Still, from the guards' point of view, Rem was, after all, nothing more than a girl with a bad leg, so the watch was not so severe as to post a guard outside her door around the clock.

——Taking advantage of that lapse in security, Rem slipped quietly out of her room.

Holding her breath, she climbed from the skylight of her room up onto the roof of the mansion.
Thanks to her care not to make a sound, the guards had noticed nothing of her covert movements. It would be harsh to call that their negligence—after all, none of them could have imagined that a girl who walked with a cane would escape her room by such means.
Yes—if Rem truly had been a girl who could not walk freely without her cane.

"I meant this to be something kept in reserve, just in case…"

Of course, it was not a lie that Rem had, until now, suffered from a bad leg and relied on her cane.
But she could not go on forever leaving her leg as it was, causing trouble for those around her because of her incomplete "memories," and so she had kept practicing walking all this time.
That effort had borne fruit, and once she'd regained some ability to plant her feet firmly, a thought had occurred to her—if she let people go on believing her leg was still bad, it might prove useful someday.

She hadn't actually thought through how, in practical terms, she would make use of it.
If anything, there was every chance that if this needless secret were discovered, it would only make people warier of her than necessary. But having actually put it to use like this, she could say she'd won her bet.
Though of course, carelessness was still the greatest enemy. Only once this action led to some result could she truly say she'd collected on her winnings.

Having gone to the trouble of placing the bet, there was no point unless she won.
As for who exactly had influenced her into doing something so bold as placing a bet in the first place, Rem tried not to think about that.
In any case——

"——If I can reach the annex without being found."

Rem, now out on the roof, was not aiming to escape the mansion.
That too was an option not easily discarded, but Flop, besides Rem, was also being held captive in the mansion. She couldn't leave without him, and besides, the building was surrounded by a high wall. Unlike the roof, it would be difficult to get over, and there seemed little point even considering it.

So the goal of Rem's escape from her room was the annex within the same grounds—where the various "Crown Princes" gathered from across the land were being held. Making contact with them was her aim.
From what she'd heard, the "Black-Haired Crown Prince" had been held up as the figurehead of the rebellion. It seemed unlikely he was truly Abel's biological child, but he surely had his own thoughts on the current state of the empire.
If she could make contact with them, perhaps she'd see a glimmer of hope for breaking through her present circumstances.

"I have to secure the safety of Flop-san and Katya-san, along with my own."

For that, Rem judged, she needed allies she could cooperate with.
Carefully making her way across the roof toward the annex, she paid the utmost attention to not making any sound. No matter how firmly she could now plant her feet, there was no guarantee she could outrun the guards if spotted.
So as not to waste this hard-won chance, she pressed on with a do-or-die resolve—if caught, there would be no getting off lightly. And all for no reason beyond the fact that she simply could not sit still.
And then——

"——!"

Suddenly, some anomaly erupting in the distant sky made Rem's body jerk violently.
At the edge of her vision, amid the battle raging across the imperial capital's outer walls, Rem watched, breath caught, as the sky looking down over it all twisted into an abnormal spectacle.
Far too distant for her to make out the details, but at that very moment, the "Cloud Dragon" Mezoreia was descending upon the peak of the star-shaped wall protecting the capital, the "Spirit Eater" Arakiya was burning away the clouds in flame, and the wall itself—which was, in truth, none other than Moguro Hagane—was rising to its feet.

Though she couldn't grasp the reality of it, Rem sensed the danger by instinct alone and stiffened. In that same instant, the strength drained from the foot she had planted on the roof.
A faint, cracked "ah" escaped her at the same moment her body slid down the roof's slope. She was nearly flung off toward the floor below, but caught herself just in time, gripping the edge of the roof with all her strength.

"That was… close."

If she'd screamed, or fallen to the floor below, there would have been no excuse that could cover it.
Dangling from the edge of the roof by the barest margin, Rem let out a breath at how close it had been. Then, slowly, as she tried to pull herself back up onto the roof——

"——? What is this place…?"

The annex, her goal, was still far off, and this dangling had been nothing but a stumble on the way there. But in the eyes of Rem, clinging to that edge, a door set into a dead end of the mansion caught her attention.
Having been granted relatively free movement about the mansion, Rem had looked around a fair amount—yet this was something she was seeing for the first time, a door in a section, like the annex, that was off-limits. In working her way around the mansion via the roof, she had wandered into an area she normally could never reach.

"A hidden door…? What could possibly be…"

—inside, the question rose in her mind, and Rem was caught in a moment's hesitation.
The goal she'd set beforehand was contact with the "Crown Princes" in the annex. That path was clearly the one more likely to yield a concrete clue toward breaking the deadlock of her situation.
On the other hand, this unplanned hidden door might, after all that build-up, turn out to be nothing more than a wine cellar—an anticlimax. Was it really worth the risk of venturing in?

"——"

There was no need to think about which was the more rational choice. ——And yet, Rem chose the irrational one.
Releasing her grip on the roof's edge, she dropped down into the passage where the hidden door stood. She did her best to descend as quietly as possible, though some small sound of footsteps was unavoidable. Still, the disturbance in the sky that had startled her moments ago was still ongoing, and the mansion's guards had their attention seized by it as well.
Relying on that, Rem boldly set her hand to the hidden door and threw it open.

"Stairs leading underground…"

The door hadn't been locked—not out of carelessness, but because there had been no need to.
This was, by design, a place never meant to receive uninvited guests. That Rem, who was, at least nominally, a guest of the household, had made her way here was hardly cause for complaint.

Greeted by the chill air rising from the stairway down, and by the dim, lightless space below, Rem swallowed a small breath and made her way down, one hand against the wall.
Something unimaginably terrifying might be waiting for her.
Something monstrous and unspeakable that Berstetz had sealed away might be confined down there.
And yet——

"——Is someone there?"

With a strange, inexplicable certainty, Rem called out into the darkness of the basement she had reached.
There was no light, but by feel alone she could tell it wasn't a particularly large room. And that somewhere in its dimly visible depths, there was a large presence.

Large—though not in the sense of some monster.
An unusually large presence, even for a human, chained and confined to the far wall of the basement.
And upon hearing Rem's question, that figure spoke——

"His Excellency… must be…"

"…You are—"

"Vincent Vollachia, His Imperial Majesty—must be protected…"

The low voice, the color of blood, shook the cracked air of the basement with painful force.
The earnestness woven into it was filled, thoroughly and utterly, with a loyalty that gave no thought to itself.

"——"

At those words, Rem steeled herself and closed the distance, one step, then another.
As she did, the figure of the great, chained man rose faintly out of the darkness. It was truly an enormous body, its face carved all over with white scars——

"…Goz. Ralfon."

A beat late, Rem realized the murmured name was, in all likelihood, exactly that—a name.
And that she recognized the sound of it.

It was, surely, the name of one renowned as among the strongest in the entire Vollachia Empire—one of the "Nine Divine Generals"——

"His Excellency, from the 'Great Disaster'—must be protected——"

The man growled fiercely, like a lion, his scarred face twisting.

"—Abel, was it? You're the one who spread the rumor of the 'Crown Prince,' aren't you?"

Asked this by the formidable woman Serena Dracroy, arms crossed, eyes turned to the sky over the battlefield at the rebel army's main camp during the Battle for the Imperial Capital, Abel slowly raised his face, still covered by the demon mask.

With the arrival of Serena's Flying Dragon Squadron from far to the west as reinforcements—elite Flying Dragon Riders skilled in the art of "dragon-handling"—control of the battlefield skies had swung decisively to their side.
Of course, there were still stretches of the battlefield where the Flying Dragon Riders were at a disadvantage, owing to the presence of the "Cloud Dragon" and the "Spirit Eater," but there was no need to dominate the entire field.

What was needed was a single breach—even the mightiest of imperial walls begins to collapse from just one hole.
Abel believed there existed a means of bringing that about—at least, that much he had concluded. And so, well aware of the stalemates and setbacks scattered across the field, he continued issuing his orders in fits and starts.

"Do you imagine I have time to indulge your idle chatter right now? You, too, should be keeping an eye on the movements of your own subordinates."

"Of course I should. But I take offense at being dismissed as idle chatter. I, after all, decided my own position on the strength of your letter and came rushing here as a result. If my arrival was factored into your plans, I think it wouldn't kill you to treat me with a bit more importance."

"You speak like some tedious commoner."

"I wouldn't go so far as to call myself vulgar, but I do have a taste for the vulgar on occasion. Your earlier reaction, for one, has me curious."

Shrugging, Serena tore her gaze from the sky and turned it toward Abel.
The "earlier reaction" she spoke of was, in all likelihood, in reference to the western reinforcements—specifically, forces other than Serena's own Flying Dragon Squadron.

Unplanned reinforcements, whose leader Abel had had some idea of—even so, he found himself irritated at his own earlier carelessness in letting a reaction show.
And that, no doubt, was the very thread Serena had picked up to lead into this chat.

"The 'Black-Haired Crown Prince'—you're the one who spread that story. And that 'Crown Prince' is none other than the one leading that group currently in the midst of raising all manner of hell on the battlefield, is it not?"

"So you're suggesting the ones causing that ruckus on the battlefield are the emperor's bastard child?"

"No, no, forgive me. That was merely a figure of speech. I don't actually believe the 'Black-Haired Crown Prince' is truly His Excellency's own child. In fact, His Excellency has no children at all, I'd wager."

Shaking her head at Abel's words, Serena answered with striking confidence. At that answer, so full of certainty, Abel's brow creased, and she added "after all," continuing—

"His Excellency has taken no consort, and there's never been so much as a whisper of him bedding a woman. I even tried seducing him myself once, and was utterly ignored. That's fairly strong evidence, wouldn't you say?"

"…Are you in your right mind?"

"Quite serious, I assure you."

"I didn't ask if you were serious. I asked if you were sane."

Given grounds that were rather difficult to accept as such, Abel's eyes hardened.
Serena was one of Vollachia's foremost Senior Counts, and it was precisely because he valued her capability and ambition so highly that he counted her among the essential forces for this decisive battle. But if she judged matters by standards Abel could not follow, that too warranted reconsideration.

Just as Abel's eyes were trying to see through to her true intent, Serena said "wait," unfolding her crossed arms and raising one hand.

"I'm not joking, but that's merely the single largest piece of evidence. That His Excellency wasn't tempted by me is a bit of a wound to my pride, but it's also suggestive."

"——"

"His Excellency has no interest in women. To put it more plainly, he has no intention of producing an heir."

Abel remained silent, and watching him, Serena continued, "Isn't that so, though?"

"I won't claim he ought to be a womanizer, but His Excellency hasn't produced a single child. Not one, at least so far. But even supposing His Excellency preferred men, if he had any intention of producing an heir, he could still manage it. Which leaves two possibilities—either he's incapable, or—"

"He deliberately refrains from producing children."

"Just so. Either way, I don't believe any 'Crown Prince' truly exists. Which means the whole story of a bastard child was dubious from the start. And yet that very dubious tale has managed to spark a civil war of this magnitude—it does say something rather glorious about the state of our nation."

Twisting her cheek slightly, Serena shaped her smile into one of genuine amusement.
Abel said nothing regarding either her attitude or the theory she'd constructed. Nor did Serena particularly need his agreement.
People of her sort could affirm their own thinking without needing affirmation from others.
Which meant what she wanted from Abel was not confirmation, but——

"To what end did you fabricate the existence of this 'Crown Prince'? Once we win this battle, who do you intend to seat on the empty throne, and what do you mean to do with the empire?"

——She was asking whether he intended to entrust the future of the Vollachia Empire to that "Crown Prince."

"——"

At Serena's quiet question, Abel quietly closed one eye.
Some might say this was hardly the moment for such an exchange, with the battle at its very peak. But if one meant to fight, it would be the height of folly not to consider what came after victory.
Naturally, since he fought intending to win, a vision that accounted for the aftermath was necessary as well.

"Why ask me?"

"Because I believe that 'Crown Prince' is no fraud—not His Excellency's bastard child, no, but your true intended successor, the one driving this vast civil war. If, after the war, you and that 'Crown Prince' join forces, isn't it plain to whom I should be putting questions about the empire's future?"

"—Ha."

At Serena's continued reasoning, a breath escaped Abel's lips.
At the same time, though it was the mask covering his face—with its effect of "cognitive obstruction"—doing the work, he found himself impressed, even as it concerned himself, at just how powerful that effect truly was.
That it could conceal both his true identity and his true intentions this thoroughly—he found himself impressed.

"What's so amusing? Did I say something mistaken?"

"I wasn't laughing. If anything, I'm impressed by your insight. It's only that, your conclusion being wrong, your reasoning too has gone rather far astray. Though that's hardly unreasonable of you."

"Mm—"

At Abel's manner of speaking, Serena's lips twisted in displeasure.
But true to his word, he was genuinely impressed by her powers of insight. If one assumed Abel's purpose was simply to win this rebellion and seize the throne of the Vollachia Empire, it was a natural enough conclusion.
Put forward a figure claiming to be the "Crown Prince," hand him the throne, and hold the real power behind it. If possible, one could even assassinate the puppet emperor at some later point and claim the throne for oneself once more.
But——

"Doubt it as you like—I have no intention of giving that one the throne. Your assessment is mistaken."

"Then all of my inferences were wrong?"

"Not all. As you say, the reason I spread word of the 'Black-Haired Crown Prince' was, indeed, to draw out the one raising havoc on the battlefield. But the emphasis is different."

"Emphasis?"

"The emphasis between drawing him out onto the stage, and keeping him at hand. The latter is of little importance. ——In any case, that one's softness could never tolerate the sacrifice of others."

Serena tilted her head, caught in a bewilderment beyond her understanding.
But Abel felt no obligation to explain everything to her from beginning to end. For now, he had presumably resolved the answer she was after—her concerns regarding the aftermath of the war.

Depending on circumstances, Serena could, right here, have struck off Abel's head, carried it to the Crystal Palace, and ended this rebellion then and there.
That seed, he had plucked. At the cost of some measure of his own private satisfaction.

"Cast aside both hesitation and mercy, and make the fullest use of that softness. That, surely, is where your true talent lies. ——Natsuki Subaru."

The person Serena called the "Crown Prince," presumably even now still tearing up the western battlefield.
The image of the one whose name now sat upon her lips refused to settle, even in Abel's own memory. Bare face and cross-dress, and even the form of a small child—a restless, unruly thing even within memory itself.

At the image of Subaru scrambling about in a chaos of circumstance forced upon him, exposing his softness and immaturity as he ran himself ragged, Abel's lips twisted. With intense loathing.

——No—perhaps it would be more accurate to call it hatred, that burning emotion.

"—Who goes there!"

Just as Abel, hiding his dark emotion behind the demon mask, and Serena, about to press him with a further question, were interrupted—there was movement at the camp's curtain.
Several soldiers guarding the main camp drew their weapons, baring sharp vigilance. Looking closer, their weapons were leveled at a slender figure who had appeared just outside the camp.

That figure raised both empty hands, grinning affably as he looked over the faces of the soldiers leveling their weapons at him.

"Ohh dear, I never imagined I'd get such a warm welcome. Seems I'm quite the popular fellow."

"Don't mock us! Where in the world did you come from?!"

"'Led here by the stars,' shall we say? A touch too poetic, perhaps?"

Given such an evasive, unserious answer, the soldiers, feeling mocked, swelled with fury.
And yet, even faced with their menace, the man's manner did not change in the slightest. He was clearly a type well used to not letting others' anger alter his own bearing.
And so——

"——Lower your weapons. That one poses no danger."

"B-but, sir…"

"Even armed with a blade, he could do nothing. He's a clown whose only talent lies in the manipulation of words."

"Ohh-ho, how cruelly put. You've wounded me deeply, you know."

Calling out to the wary soldiers, Abel had them lower their weapons. But the effeminate man he'd just rescued pouted his lips as though he had a complaint about that description, protesting to Abel.
At that, Serena, standing beside Abel, also turned a suspicious eye toward the newcomer.

"Why are you here? Aren't you the 'Stargazer'—"

"——Ubilk, if you please. A pleasure to make your acquaintance, Senior Count Dracroy. Although——"

The effeminate man who introduced himself thus—Ubilk—smiled with a bewitchingly refined, almost unnaturally perfect face.
Ubilk, who ought by rights to have been in the imperial capital, within the Crystal Palace itself, drew out his pause to the fullest, milking it for all it was worth, before delivering the rest of his sentence.

"——Whether that will still hold true after the 'Great Disaster' rather depends on whether you're still alive to hear it, wouldn't you say?"
