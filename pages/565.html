Arc 7 Chapter 59B: "Flop O'Connell"

"—Value your own life, Flop. Self-sacrifice is something only complete fools do."

That was the advice given to Flop by Miles, the benefactor who had pulled him out of a wretched environment.

The orphanage that Flop and Medium had taken shelter in as young children turned out to be, it seemed, a far worse institution than they had realized.
An orphanage that gathered up orphans with no family, nowhere to go, and no one to rely on, and gave those children a life with a roof and walls—that was what the adults there liked to repeat, over and over.

"In a world overflowing with people who have neither food nor work, you children are blessed."

And in fact, Flop had believed it to be true.
Life with his little sister Medium, the two of them abandoned by their family and sipping muddy water to survive, had been harsh. Food meant grass and insects; on the rare occasion they caught something like a rabbit it was cause for a great commotion, and at the worst of times they'd gnawed on dirt and moss just to stave off hunger.
Compared to that, life at the orphanage was far more fortunate.

They were given a blanket, even if it was thin and threadbare, and meals were properly provided, even if it was just flavorless soup and a single scrap of bread. They were assigned simple, repetitive labor where numbers did the talking, and while the adults would sometimes turn violent on a whim, at least it wasn't every single day.
There was a grain of truth in the adults' insistence that the outside world was far harsher. So the thought of running away never even had the chance to take root.
Still, Flop couldn't bear to see Medium or the other children exposed to the adults' beatings and kicks, and so he learned to behave as cheerfully as possible, in a way that would catch the adults' eyes.

Every day, drawing the adults' attention more than anyone else—Flop resolved in his heart that this was his role to fill.

If the conspicuous Flop was well-liked, the adults' attitude toward the other children would soften too. And even on days when the adults were in a foul mood, it was the conspicuous Flop who caught the brunt of it first.
Standing out, all on its own, could become a weapon.
A big voice, big gestures. Exaggerated expressions and mannerisms. Fortunately, making a habit of these wasn't especially difficult. It seemed he had a natural gift for drawing the eye.

—It was on the night he'd diverted an adult's anger away from a younger child and ended up half-beaten to death, the night Medium spent stroking his wounds until morning, that he became certain of it.

This was the role he had to fulfill in this place.
In the midst of a pain that threatened to make him lose consciousness, Flop told himself as much—

"Like hell it is, you dimwit."

"Eh...?"

"I show my face here again after all these years, and it's the same irritating old home as ever, and on top of that some idiot kid's off pulling some idiot stunt. One lunatic brat was already plenty, what with Bal-bou alone."

Flop, having recognized his role and resolved to single-handedly bear the orphanage's dark side.
But that scheme of his was abruptly shattered one night.

The person who appeared, straddling a flying dragon, was not, by any flattering account, a well-groomed individual.
Making no effort to hide his rough, ill-bred upbringing, scratching irritably at his gray hair, he was, to a child's eyes, the sort of person you didn't want to get involved with. His features, which brought to mind something of a sneaking rat, only reinforced that impression; normally, he was the sort you kept your head low around to avoid a beating.

That rude first impression of Flop's was betrayed—very reluctantly—by the man. Miles.

"Originally, I came out of this same place, same as you lot. Well, back in my day it was a hellhole too. So I got the hell out quick and survived on muddy water."

At night, so the children couldn't escape while they slept, the door to their room was fitted with a crude lock.
Miles broke that lock roughly and peered inside, and at the sight of nearly twenty children crammed into the cramped room, he clicked his tongue in tremendous irritation.
Then he ushered the children—startled and frightened by the sudden appearance of an unfamiliar adult—out of the room, and leaving behind only the words "Wait here," headed for the adults' quarters.
And then—

"It was day after day of will-I-die-or-won't-I, but somehow I caught a break. And then, a few years later, I got to thinking about this cursed home of mine again, so... here we are."

With the adults tied up and left rolling on the floor, Miles kicked their faces as they cursed him for an ingrate, wearing a vulgar grin, and spat, "Serves you right."
He must have suffered terribly at these adults' hands. Even so, Flop couldn't help but wonder whether it was right to go this far.
Turning to look at Flop, Miles raised one thin eyebrow.

"What, you wanna get in on it too? Then go get your revenge. Pay it back a hundredfold."

"Eh, no, I—"

"—I'll do it!!"

At Miles's demonic whisper, Flop hesitated over his answer.
But Medium, darting out from behind Flop, went straight for one of the bound adults' heads with a stick she'd picked up off the ground, without a moment's hesitation. —No, it wasn't only Medium.
Every child there, save for the dumbstruck Flop, became a raging rebel.

"It always hurt!"
"I hate you!"
"This is for Big Bro!"

Stop it, stop it—the adults' screams, bound and unable to resist, were swallowed up by the children's furious shouting.
They clawed at the adults' faces, slapped their cheeks, and finally even urinated on them, exploding with all the anger they'd stored up over the days.

"Buhahahahaha! Look at their faces! What a masterpiece!"

Beside Flop, who watched his sister and the others' rebellion in a daze, Miles roared with vulgar laughter.
Flop, for his part, couldn't bring himself to laugh at all. He could only reel, wondering what would become of them now that they'd done this to the adults.

"Well, now, do as you like from here... is what I'd like to say, but if I just tossed you out after this, there's no telling how badly Count Dracroy would chew me out."

Looking over the children who, their rebellion carried out on pure emotion, had now come out of the facility, Miles continued, "So," to his young juniors,

"For now, I'll take you to the Count's place. What you do from there is up to you. Doesn't matter to me if you don't come along, either."

At Miles's rough-and-ready invitation, the children looked at one another.
What showed on their faces was anxiety and confusion. Miles had said even whether to come along was up to them, but that was a "choice" that Flop and the others had never had before.
Children who had, until now, always followed the adults' instructions had never had the right to choose anything. So, suddenly handed that right, they were left fumbling, unsure what to do with it.
At the sight of the children like this, Miles shrugged and said, "Come on, now,"

"What's this, now, of all times? Pathetic. —You've already gone and gotten your revenge on them, haven't you. You lot can choose whatever the hell you want."

Told this by Miles, the children realized something for the first time.
That, just as Miles said, they had already chosen to rebel.

—In the end, not a single child chose to stay behind at the orphanage.

Naturally, Flop, even though he hadn't taken part himself, couldn't bring himself to suggest staying at an orphanage where such things had just been done. To begin with, Medium had immediately jumped on Miles's offer with an "I'm going!" and had called out to Flop too, "Let's go!"
There was no way he could refuse Medium's wish, and no way he'd choose a path that separated him from his little sister. For reasons somewhat more passive than the other children's, Flop left the facility.

They had done something outrageous.
Or perhaps he had been dragged into something outrageous—regret gnawed at his chest.
And yet—

"G'night, big bro."

On the road to Miles's so-called master, sleeping wrapped together with his sister in a blanket taken from the facility, out in the open with no roof overhead, those words struck him in the chest.
His sister, nestling against him as if at ease, and the outside scenery with no prison called eaves or walls.
He realized he no longer had to worry about being beaten, no longer had to fear making his sister cry.

"—Heh."

Realizing it, Flop cried.
He cried and cried, sobbing, savoring the salty taste of freedom.

—Far off, wings beating, the flying dragons receded into a sky blanketed in gray clouds.

Making sure it wasn't merely the preparatory motion for a next attack, Zikr kept up his guard until the receding shapes shrank to the size of beans, and only then did he let the tension leave his body.
The reason was unclear, but the flying dragon swarm had withdrawn. Since the city hall had not been completely brought down, there were two possibilities for their withdrawal—either they'd achieved their operational objective, or they'd judged it too difficult to achieve.

From Zikr's point of view, the former would be preferable.
Weighing the many dramatic events that had occurred in the roughly one hour of fierce fighting, it seemed objectively that the former possibility was more likely.

"The sudden drop in temperature, and the catastrophic damage to the southern part of the city..."

Running a hand through his own luxuriant hair, Zikr turned over the two anomalies he couldn't make sense of.
In the midst of the battle, the temperature of the walled city had dropped visibly by the moment, and when white snow had finally begun to flurry down, he'd doubted his own eyes. Snow could apparently be seen atop very high, high mountains, but if one wanted to see snow in Vollachia, one could only hope for some kind of natural catastrophe.
In other words, the snowfall that had occurred during the battle was nothing short of a natural catastrophe.

That said, it had worked out as good fortune for Zikr and his men.
Flying dragons were weak to sudden changes in temperature, especially cold, and their troublesome aerial fighting power had visibly dropped as a result. Without that, the damage would surely have been far greater and deeper.
However—

"That white light, though, just what was..."

An utterly unmanageable, hopeless destruction had flattened and blown away the southern part of the city.
It was a sight so extreme that even the words "natural catastrophe" felt too gentle—one of the scenes you might expect at the end of the world. Even without further damage following it, that single blast alone had been more than enough to wound the city.
The chain of command had been thrown into confusion, and judgment of the battle situation had stalled. If the flying dragons had pressed the advantage as things stood, the city might have collapsed by sheer inertia.
All the more reason, then, that the dragon swarm's withdrawal was both a stroke of good fortune and something strange.

Zikr therefore surmised that something must have happened on the attacking side, the enemy's side.
Had someone dealt the enemy a heavy blow in place of Zikr and his men, whose hands were tied up in the city's defense? If so, the likely candidates were Mizelda, who led the Shudrak, or Priscilla, who had the proven feat of driving off the Sole General Arakiya.
Whichever of them had done it—

"Women truly are magnificent. And yet, I have no wish to accept as a matter of course the shame of being protected in a woman's shadow."

That women were superior beings was no license for Zikr himself to be inferior.
He should revere and cherish the wonder of women, while holding his own failings to account.
In any case—

"To think we'd be exposed to an onslaught of this magnitude—our advance preparations proved their worth."

"...If it's a matter of taking a walled city, using flying dragons is standard doctrine. Even so, I should have anticipated that it wouldn't be merely a Flying Dragon Squadron, but the 'Dragon General' herself who'd be sent."

At the words of his staff officer, wounded and bleeding from the head, Zikr shook his head.
His foresight had been too soft. The anti-dragon weapons prepared against the flying dragons had, anticipating an attack from the direction of the capital, been concentrated on the western ramparts—but the dragon swarm had come at them from all four directions.
He had reckoned that after the withdrawal of the Sole General Arakiya, it would take time before the next Sole General was dispatched, but this too had been a miscalculation—though, considering the circumstances, that too was only natural.

This was not merely one city's rebellion—it was the herald of a far greater upheaval.
From the perspective of the treacherous minister in the capital who grasped that fact, he would surely be pouring all his effort into stamping out the fire of rebellion while the spark was still small. Zikr should have kept the possibility of successive Sole Generals being committed one after another in view.

"No—reflection can wait. With the ramparts breached, taking the city would be easy now even without flying dragons. Confirm the extent of the damage. Check whether the wall can be repaired..."

"—Lower your weapon this instant! That's an order!"

"Hm."

Zikr swiftly drew out the factors deserving reflection and filed them away at the edge of his thoughts.
Just as he was turning his mind to confirming the damage and planning their future response, a sharp, tense voice shook the frigid air.

Looking over, there at the city hall—the place used as their command post, where they had beaten back the dragon assault—was a single figure surrounded by defending soldiers.
Drawing to himself all the weapons and wariness that had until now been aimed at the flying dragons was a man glaring imperiously around at those surrounding him, his manner foul. In both his hands he gripped longswords, blood dripping from their tips.
Though that blood was not human blood, but the blood of flying dragons.

"That's..."

"One of the soldiers we brought up from the cells as a countermeasure against the flying dragons. A dual-blade wielder..."

"Ah, I saw him myself. His fighting was worth a thousand men. —Hey, stop!"

Nodding at his staff officer's remark, Zikr spread his hands and gave that order to his men. At Zikr's instruction, one of his subordinates raised his voice: "Second General!"

"It's dangerous! Given the circumstances we brought him out of his cell, but..."

"And once the threat of the dragons had passed, you meant to put him straight back in his cell? He'd hardly accept that. The casualties we'd pay for it are the real problem."

Answering his tense subordinate thus, Zikr advanced toward the surrounded man.
The dual-blade wielder's skill was considerably higher than that of an ordinary imperial soldier. In fact, without his furious exertions, there was no certainty they could have driven off the flying dragon swarm that had assaulted the city hall.
If those blades were turned this way, the damage would surely exceed what was warranted.

"Of course, should you resort to such an outrage, your life would be forfeit as well. So..."

"So, what?"

"—"

The man answered Zikr, who had opened the conversation, with a voice and manner sharp as thorns. Worse, he turned one of the two blades in his hands toward Zikr, laughed fiercely with a "Hah," and said,

"You want me to surrender quiet-like? That's no different from what the rest of them are saying, is it, 'Skirt-chaser' Second General."

"You dare! Mocking Second General Zikr!"

Zikr took pride in a title that sounded like a dishonor, but he could sense in the man's remark a clear intent to sneer at that title. Because of this, his subordinates flared up, their hostility deepening, but Zikr held them back again with a hand as he said,

"Your surrender would be welcome, but that is not what this is. Your service was splendid. Whatever our respective positions, the fact remains that you contributed to the city's defense. Therefore, I release you."

"—. You mean that?"

"Reward for merit, punishment for wrongdoing—that is Vollachia's law, and His Majesty the Emperor's own wish."

An emperor of Vollachia who judged a man by his own ability and deeds—that stance was the standard of the Empire's meritocracy, and it formed the very foundation of the loyalty Zikr held in reverence.
But the instant the man heard Zikr's answer, his manner turned blatantly hostile.
A demonic aura poured out of his entire body, and his gaze—the man, his right eye covered by an eyepatch, poured the whole of his emotion into his left eye and glared at Zikr.

"For a rebel 'General' who betrayed His Majesty and the Empire to side with the enemy to say that without a hint of shame...! If it were me, I couldn't stand being so shameless—I'd cut my own belly open."

"—"

At the man's remark, which suggested that the source of his fierce hostility lay in loyalty to the Empire, Zikr held his breath for a moment, then gazed steadily at the man.
This soldier, thrown into the underground cells, must be one of those who, when Vincent had taken Guaral by stratagem, had refused the order to surrender and resisted to the very end. In other words, he could be called a man possessed of an especially strong sense of belonging to imperial ideology.
If so—

"If I were to say that I, the same as you, have sworn unchanging loyalty to the Empire and to His Majesty the Emperor, would you be willing to lend an ear to what I have to say?"

"Ahh?"

At Zikr's question, the man widened his left eye and let out a rough, thuggish sound. But taking in the sight of Zikr, unflinching before his glare, he paused a moment, then threw his sword down onto the floor.
The blade rang out shrilly as it clattered across the floor, and the now-weaponless man raised both hands.

"May I take that to mean you'll hear me out?"

"For now, I'll quit tearing this place up ready to die gloriously. If I went at it for real, I bet I could take the head of a traitor 'General' at least..."

Sweeping his gaze around, the man looked provocatively at Zikr's subordinates. Their wariness rose under that gaze, but the man snorted with a "Heh,"

"I'll go easy on you, though. But if this turns out to be some boring story..."

"It's meant to be a story worth your interest. ...By the way, your name?"

Judging that, with the weapon laid down, they could now approach one another, Zikr asked the man his name. For a moment the man hesitated over whether to answer, but scratching his head as if seeing no point in dodging the question,

"—Jamal Aurélie. Corporal."

he stated his name and rank.
Receiving this, Zikr in turn dipped his chin deeply.

"Jamal, is it. You likely know already, but I am Zikr Osman. I hold the rank of Imperial Second General, and I am also called 'Skirt-chaser.' Though—"

"Hah?"

"These days, being called 'coward' is what really stirs my blood."

That epithet, which he had once considered a disgrace, had become something that shone with a special light within Zikr.
Hearing Zikr's answer, the man—Jamal—twisted his face in incomprehension. Zikr judged him to be the type who was skilled with his arms but not one for using his head or working through subtleties.
If that was the case, then appealing to duty and righteousness might be enough to get him to listen.
Just then—

"—Excuse me, would this be where the representative of this city is?"

Into the city hall, released from a tension that had seemed about to snap now that Jamal had surrendered, slid a voice that seemed to have been waiting for its chance to cut in.
Its quiet, soft tone struck the eardrum in a way that seemed to draw out relief in whoever heard it.
Even so, at a voice he didn't recognize, Zikr turned and knit his round brows.

The owner of the voice showed himself on the stairs connecting the top floor, now serving as the command post, with the floor below. Raising both hands to show he had no hostile intent, the gray-haired man let his gaze sweep around and said,

"Would that gentleman there be the 'General,' Zikr Osman?"

From among all those clad in the same red uniform, he had, without hesitation, singled out Zikr as the 'General.'
Of course, given the cape draped over his shoulders and the rank insignia there, it was easy enough to pick out Zikr as the highest-ranking man present. —The question was the nerve it took to point it out.
The gall to show his face in a command post where battle had raged only moments before and single out an unfamiliar commander by name. Zikr eyed the man—reserved, yet unafraid—with suspicion as to his affiliation.

Since he was nowhere among the city's residents, it seemed he must have slipped in during the fighting.
In that case, the most likely explanation was—

"An envoy from the capital... a messenger to us, perhaps?"

"Eh? Ah, no, no, not at all! We're more, hmm, complicated to explain, but we're not affiliated with the capital or anything like that."

Waving his raised hands from side to side, the young man hastily denied Zikr's suspicion. If that was to be taken at face value, then the young man's position became all the more unclear.
In place of Zikr, whose furrowed brow deepened further, Jamal ground his teeth with a "You bastard,"

"We had first dibs here! Coming in after and yammering on...! I'll knock your block off!"

"I do feel bad about that, honestly. I don't expect we can have a calm conversation right at this moment either. So if I could just get your permission for something—"

"Permission? Permission for what?"

"Treating the wounded, repairing the city afterward... I suppose you could call it helping out with the post-battle cleanup. My companions and I should be of at least some use."

Answering Jamal, whose face twisted, the young man sighed, "Although," and, looking somehow drained, as if tired or exasperated, he loosened his cheeks slightly.

"We've already gone ahead and started some of it, so part of this is really asking forgiveness after the fact."

"—. The offer itself is welcome, but that—"

"It's not a difficult matter, Zikr. This man's words hold no lies."

Before Zikr could press further with his questions, a valiant, arresting voice cut him off.
A sound like a staff striking the floor rang out sharply, and a figure climbing the stairs came up alongside the young man. It was Mizelda, her whole valiant body drenched in blood, her fierce beauty only heightened by it.
By the look of it, the blood staining that beautiful body was all someone else's, and aside from the leg injured earlier, there were no other conspicuous wounds. Zikr felt relief at her safe return.

Mizelda, having returned like this, slapped the young man's slender shoulder companionably, and said,

"This one's companions have already begun their work. I, too, can vouch for it."

"Lady Mizelda, I'm relieved above all to see you safe. As for him?"

"Don't know. He's not our enemy, and he's a good-looking man, so I let him through."

"Lady Mizelda..."

Mizelda's aesthetic sense differed somewhat from Zikr's own way of seeing things, but certainly, just as she said, the young man's features could be called gently well-formed.
The air about him was somehow androgynous, yet there was, strangely, a single strong core felt in the light of his eyes.
Zikr, too, thought he was not a bad person. Only, he was likely also holding something back beneath the surface.

"In a situation like this, I don't believe help comes without expecting something in return. Who are you?"

"As I said just now, I'm someone in a rather complicated position to explain. But I have no intention of standing against you. —I'm only looking for someone."

"Someone you're looking for..."

At Zikr's echoing remark, the young man nodded, "Yes."
Then he removed the green hat he'd been wearing, pressed it to his own chest, and bowed—a courtesy not in the imperial style, but one that showed a posture of respect all the same—

"My name is Otto Suwen. —I'm looking for a friend, and my friend's little sister."

he said, stating his purpose with eyes like a hyena's—not to be underestimated.

—Miles's master, Senior Countess Serena Dracroy, was a fierce and formidable woman.

Young as she was, she vied for position among the upper ranks of the imperial nobility, and following the iron-blooded law that was the very expression of imperial ideology, she placed great weight on a meritocracy that raised up those with ability.
At the same time, she also possessed a nobility of spirit that would not countenance the strong humiliating the weak at will, and it was because of this that the children Miles brought back were not treated poorly.

"I've received the report. For Miles, who avoids trouble whenever he can, to bring you back himself—he must have found your circumstances truly pitiable. In my domain, you may live as you please."

Welcoming Flop and the other children, Serena, having answered thus, laughed magnanimously.
Standing before a presence that felt larger than her actual stature, an intimidating force that made a child's heart want to prostrate itself, and confronted besides with the splendid, vast mansion interior—the likes of which he'd never seen—Flop was overwhelmed.

"Someone with as much room to spare as the Countess has got no reason to go out of her way and pick on the folks below her. In the end, the adults who hit kids are the ones who got hit themselves back when they were kids."

To Flop and the others—doused in hot water, gorging on food until their bellies were full, changed into clean, pleasant-smelling clothes—Miles answered in his usual coarse manner.
Unlike Medium and the others, who were dizzy with a world where everything they saw and touched was new and fresh, Flop was deeply impressed by an unfamiliar world, an unfamiliar way of thinking.

In particular, the influence he received from the philosophy Miles tossed off without a second thought was immense.
That there existed in this world ways of seeing things, ways of grasping them, that Flop had never even conceived of—he could never have known this if Miles hadn't taken him out into the wider world.
Above all—

"Oh, a fellow companion, I take it? You folks too, one of Big Bro Miles's pickups?"

Given his freedom, chasing after the backs of his sister and the other children as they ran about the mansion, Flop came to a stop in the beautiful garden within the vast grounds.
That soft voice called out to his back as he stood overwhelmed by the sight of large blossoms flowering in dignified splendor.
Startled, he searched for the source of the voice, but no one was to be seen in the garden. As Flop tilted his head at this,

"Down here, y'know. Sorry 'bout that—pardon me for comin' at ya from below."

"Whoa!"

"Oh, and down you go."

A head popped up right in front of him, just beyond the fence Flop had been leaning against to peer into the garden, and Flop reeled back on reflex.
He was laughed at as he landed square on his backside, and Flop blinked.
The one laughing at the fallen Flop was a boy somewhat older than him but still with a childlike look about him. Twelve or thirteen, perhaps, a brown-haired figure with an endearing face.

"...Laughing at me like that, that's pretty rich."

"Oh, sorry, sorry. You went down so nice and proper, I couldn't help it. I'd like to help you up, but I'm not really able to move right now, see."

"Not able to move...? Ah."

Brushing off his backside and standing up, Flop went around the fence to stand before the boy. There, the boy, sitting on the ground, had a round mass resting on his cross-legged knees.
Seeing it, Flop's eyes went wide.

"Is that... an egg?"

"Yep, yep, quite the big egg, ain't it? Truth is, this here's a flying dragon egg."

"What are you going to do with a flying dragon egg?"

"Hatch it, o' course."

Cradling that white egg, big enough to fill both arms, with his entire body, the boy broke into a grin at Flop.

—That was how Flop and Medium O'Connell first met the person with whom they would exchange a lifelong vow of sworn brotherhood: Balleroy Temeglyph.

—One could say she had gained an understanding of the flying dragons' threat that went beyond mere knowledge.

The menace of their claws and fangs, the breadth of tactics made possible by their wings, the ferocity that let them harm an enemy without a flicker of hesitation—she had felt each of these more than enough, from the sight of the ruined city and the people who'd been attacked.
Honestly, she couldn't understand the thinking of anyone who would tame such a dangerous creature and put it to important use. An enemy utterly beyond reconciliation—just like that huge serpent she'd encountered in the jungle—it seemed to her nothing more than a danger, plain and simple.
And yet—

"—"

At the combination of things present in the scenery she gazed on outside the window, Rem quietly narrowed her eyes.
In the front garden of the grand mansion, there was a single flying dragon resting its wings, and beside it stood a soldier feeding it. The flying dragon, which should have been terrifyingly savage, instead let out a soft-eyed look and a voice almost like a coaxing whine, being fed by the soldier's own hand.

For Rem, who had been told they were violent and vicious, and had in fact witnessed that danger with her own eyes, the sight of a flying dragon so tame around a person like that was, in a word, astonishing.
She had also been told, at great length, by a girl who seemed unusually well-versed in dragons, that proud dragons never grew tame around humans. She could hardly be blamed for thinking this wasn't what she'd been told at all.

"You look pensive."

Suddenly, a voice spoke behind Rem as she gazed at the garden like this.
Though, since the other party had approached with audible footsteps, Rem, who had already noticed someone coming, wasn't startled.
Still, it was an unfamiliar voice, and given the place she was in, some tension remained as she said,

"I don't know if 'pensive' is the word, but I do have thoughts on the matter. This isn't a place I came to because I wanted to."

"Heh, quite the plain speaker, aren't you. A refreshingly forthright person."

"...And you are?"

In the room she'd been assigned—in a building so opulent that even the room given to a captive was lavish—Rem made the back of a chair, too luxurious-feeling to sit comfortably in, creak as she asked after the identity of the old man who had appeared.
He was an elderly man with white hair, a white beard, and eyes narrow as threads.
His dress was far from that of a warrior, and she could only guess that he was a person holding some sort of high rank.
From his bearing, his age, and the fact that the soldiers guarding Rem straightened their posture and bowed their heads, it was evident he held a position of no small standing.

At Rem's unreserved gaze, the old man gave a light wave of his hand, urging the guarding soldiers to leave the room. The soldiers obeyed at once, bowed, and withdrew from the room.
Then, once the old man was alone with Rem in the room, he gestured with his hand to the seat facing hers, and said,

"Might I sit?"

"...Go ahead."

Before Rem, who dipped her chin, the old man settled into the seat with an unhurried motion.
Facing Rem directly like that, he stroked his own chin with a finger and said,

"Have you heard nothing at all about this humble one?"

"No, nothing. Nothing except 'heal,' 'rest,' and 'if you do anything on your own, I'll kill you.'"

Turning over the instructions of the mansion's mistress—Madelyn, who wouldn't listen to reason—Rem shook her head.
Placed under guard and strictly ordered not to escape—a state of house arrest. Rem had no intention of escaping in any case, but it was true that the situation was far from pleasant.
At this, the old man nodded at Rem's answer with an "I see," and said,

"That was, I must say, a somewhat problematic attitude to take toward a guest. I shall have a word with First General Madelyn about it myself."

"...You're able to reprimand her?"

At the enigmatic old man's words, Rem couldn't help but widen her eyes.
During the time—neither especially long nor especially short—that it had taken to be brought from the walled city to this mansion, Rem had spent it with Madelyn, and had been thoroughly troubled by how little her words seemed to get through.
Stubborn and headstrong, though she'd been saved somewhat by a certain honesty that meant she could, to some degree, be talked around.

"I thought she was someone who hated being told what to do by others. The type to fly into a rage and turn violent the moment anyone said anything to her..."

Having said this much of her own impressions, Rem laid one of her hands over the other.
On calmer reflection, she began to feel embarrassed at the face she must be making while saying this—coming, as she did, from someone who had once broken another person's finger without listening to a word they said.
But at the sight of Rem engaged in this bit of self-reflection, the old man gave a small laugh, "Heh," and said,

"It's not, as it happens, a mistaken assessment. It's a rather painful thing for this humble one to hear, too. In fact, this time as well, my instructions weren't properly followed. Although—"

"—"

"This time it seems it wasn't First General Madelyn's whim so much as another factor entirely."

The old man, his lips still relaxed into what looked like a smile, yet with all trace of mirth erased from his voice.
Pierced by an appraising gaze from behind those narrow eyes where the pupils could not be seen, Rem held her breath faintly. That gaze spoke eloquently of regarding her as the "other factor."
And it also spoke of having brought about a result undesirable to the old man before her.

"...Who are you?"

"My apologies for the delay in introducing myself. In this Sacred Vollachia Empire, this humble one has been entrusted by His Majesty the Emperor with the duties of Prime Minister. My name is Berstetz Fondalfon."

"Prime Minister... Berstetz..."

In answer to her question, the old man—Berstetz—bowed respectfully at the waist.
The instant she heard that name and title, Rem's cheeks tensed further, and the strength in her shoulders increased.
Both the name and the title, she recalled having heard before.

"Then you're the one who..."

"Yes. —This humble one is the enemy of His Majesty Vincent Abelux."

"—"

Said with not a shred of hesitation, it left Rem once again unable to find her next words.
It was a confirmation of what she'd already suspected, but she hadn't expected it to be stated so plainly. At the same time, she couldn't help feeling it was terribly out of place, even ironic, that she—dragged into this whole enormous affair against her will—had ended up stepping into the very closest point to its eye.
By rights, the one who ought to be here wasn't her, but Abel, or Priscilla, or Subaru.
Thinking this far, Rem decided it was for the best that Subaru wasn't here.

"If he were, he'd definitely have gone and done something reckless on his own..."

There was no doubting the black-haired boy's boldness, or his knack for going somewhere well past what anyone could imagine. Even in a situation like this one, he'd surely bring about some outlandish turn of events beyond anything Rem could picture.
In that sense, it was the right call that he hadn't been in the city under assault by the flying dragons. She couldn't imagine he'd have been able to do anything about that, and it would only have meant more needless wounds.
Still—

"—"

Still, if he learned that she'd been carried off by Madelyn, what would he think?
That thought tormented Rem's chest terribly, with the pain of a sharp blade, in her imagination.

"—. So you know that Abel is the real emperor."

Forcibly ignoring that pain slicing through the depths of her chest, Rem pressed Berstetz with the question. To that question, Berstetz answered flatly, "Yes,"

"Of course. Allowing his escape was a blunder, but events have proceeded according to plan since then. No—I should say they had proceeded so. In fact, Guaral's destruction was left unfinished, partway through."

"Why Abel, though? I can't begin to imagine how great a thing it is to resolve on treason... Was it because of his character?"

"For the peak of a nation, for an Emperor, one does not ask after character. Personal feelings and attachments are trifling matters from the standpoint of running a state. If there is anything one asks for, it is only ability, and the trust and track record that he will fulfill his responsibilities."

Shaking his head slowly, Berstetz answered in a voice and with an expression that betrayed no emotion.
An unwavering tone, an expression that didn't shift in the slightest. Quite apart from Rem's own scant life experience, this was because Berstetz's superior negotiating skill let none of his inner mind show through at all.
Whether the lack of emotion meant a lie, or the truth—she couldn't even tell that much.

Still, being told that the rebellion hadn't been raised because of Abel's personality was an opinion she wanted to believe.
She had witnessed so much blood and death at the walled city with her own eyes. That the trigger for it all was simply Abel's bad personality—that was something not even Rem could accept, let alone the dead.
And so—

"Are you saying you cast him out because he was unfit to be Emperor?"

"'Casting out' was never our true intention. That was merely how it turned out."

"What was lacking in Abel, then? I don't know the weighty burden borne by the position of Emperor, or what abilities it requires, but... I do think he's a capable person."

Why she felt she had to defend Abel, Rem herself found strange, but she chose her words to settle some raw, splintered feeling within herself.
In truth, setting aside his combat ability, Abel stood out for the depth of his thinking and knowledge.
That Mizelda, Zikr, and even Subaru were all inclined to follow his lead was proof that he also had the leadership to make others accept his views.

Rem didn't know the particulars, but she too thought this must be the quality of someone fit to stand at the top.
Surely it was something needed even by the one who stood highest in a nation.
That—

"I haven't yet had the chance to ask your name, healer."

"...Rem, apparently."

"Hm."

With some resentment toward Berstetz mixed in, she'd ended up giving her name as if reporting hearsay.
Even so, Rem had by now come to accept that her name was "Rem." Since the moment she'd firmly declared it to Priscilla—or perhaps since the moment she'd allowed Subaru to call her that.
In any case, letting Rem's declared name settle on his tongue, Berstetz let out a small breath and said,

"Lady Rem, how many heirs would you suppose His Majesty the Emperor currently has?"

"H-heirs...? Um, do you mean children?"

"Yes."

As Berstetz nodded quietly, Rem pictured Abel in her mind.
Rem had no memories, but that didnot mean all the knowledge she'd acquired before losing them had vanished too. How humans reproduced—that, too, she properly remembered as knowledge.
That said, it was doubtful whether Abel could properly build that kind of relationship with anyone. To begin with, Rem couldn't quite picture any woman standing alongside Abel.

"I can't imagine it. Wouldn't he have none?"

"—. You understand well. Just so—he has none."

"Ah, so it was as I thought. Though—that might be rude of me to say about Abel—"

Rem, about to continue with "might be," cut herself off there.
It wasn't that Berstetz had said anything, or told her to be quiet. It was simply that, in the silence, the old man's narrowed eyes opened a fraction, an expression that let his pupils show through from within, and Rem's throat, facing that dead-on, was silenced by an unbelievable air of malevolence.

Berstetz sat quietly, hand resting on the table between them. What poured from his entire silent body was an anger so intense it beggared imagination.

"He has none. An heir. —And that is a problem."

"—Ah."

"The Empire must be strong and unyielding. If it is not, then this nation—"

Before Rem, who let out a rasping breath, Berstetz cut himself off there, opened the fist he'd placed on the table, and exhaled.
Then, hiding his eyes once more behind his lids, the old man looked at Rem.

"My apologies. This is, after all, this humble one's first time as a traitor as well, and I must admit I find myself rather unsteady on my feet."

"...Why tell me that, just now?"

"—"

"It should have been a story with no need to be told to me."

Berstetz's words, into which something not emotion but heavier and clearer had been poured.
She hadn't felt it to be a lie, but for that very reason, Rem had a question. Why had he let her hear such a thing?
Rem didn't think of herself as being in some position of such importance, and in fact she wasn't.
She had happened, at times, to find herself placed close to Abel or Priscilla, but that was only how circumstances had played out—not because Rem herself was anything special.

"So why..."

"...You are a healer, and an Oni besides. If possible, I would like to bring you into the fold. You are a valuable presence."

"—"

It wasn't a lie, but it felt like an answer that wasn't the whole truth either.
But to Rem, seeking an answer beyond that, Berstetz seemed to have decided against sparing any further time. Slowly, the old man rose from the chair where he had settled.

"I would like to speak with you a little longer, but even one such as this humble one has duties to attend to. You'll have to bear with some inconvenience a while yet, but I shall instruct the household staff to accommodate you as best they can, so please do make yourself at ease."

"...What exactly are you to Madelyn?"

"A collaborator would be the most fitting term. Though, from her point of view, no doubt the impression is that she's simply making use of a clever human. This mansion, too, is this humble one's own residence, you know."

Shrugging with unexpectedly sturdy shoulders, Berstetz gave his answer, and Rem looked over the room and the garden.
Since Madelyn carried on as though the place were her own, Rem had simply assumed it was her mansion, but it seemed even that had been a mistake. Still, it explained the refinement of the building and its furnishings.

"Even so, I neither think I can relax here, nor do I wish to."

"Such plain speaking—truly refreshing. Well then, once more, Lady Rem—do keep well."

With a small laugh, Berstetz bowed once more where he stood, and withdrew from the room.
Rem thought of calling out to stop him, but she couldn't find the words she ought to say, and thinking he probably wouldn't stop his steps in any case, she said nothing.

In place of the departed Berstetz, the guards who had been sent outside now returned.
With their stern gazes turned on her, Rem looked outside once more.
Just then, the flying dragon, having finished its meal, was carrying the man who'd fed it on its back, slowly beating its wings as it rose into the sky.

"—"

Ferocious and fearsome as the flying dragon was, flying while carried on its back looked exhilarating.
Of course, given the situation, she had no room to enjoy it, but she'd been carried to her destination from Guaral in less than a single day, leaving her only to wonder what all her journeys until now had even been for.
Treating the injured Flop all the while, the place Madelyn had brought her to—

"—The imperial capital, Lupugana."

The city from whose throne Abel had been driven, and which he had resolved he must reclaim.
With the Crystal Palace at that city's heart visible in the distance, Rem remained a captive. Touching the window that wouldn't open, confirming the feel of the glass with her fingertips, she suddenly found herself thinking,

"...Him..."

If he learned that Rem had disappeared, would a pain shoot through his chest?
A pain in his chest, just like the pain in Rem's own, as though a blade had been driven deep within her.

What feeling within herself gave rise to that thought, Rem did not know.

—Slowly, Flop opened his eyes, and found the ceiling of an unfamiliar room above him.

"—"

For a moment he spent time gathering his thoughts, then immediately began observing his surroundings to grasp the situation.
It was a habit from his life as a peddler who camped out often. Of course, his sister, whose senses were far sharper than his own, usually handled the vigilance, but that was no excuse to slack off entirely.
His own poor reflexes could, after all, mean the difference between life and death. So keeping himself quick to wake was a skill he'd naturally acquired as a means of survival—but—

"Where am I... uguu!"

The instant he tried to look carefully around him, a tremendous pain seized his chest, and he cried out.
If this had been out in the open at night, it would have been a terribly foolish thing to do, giving away his position to any beast nearby. Still, what was done was done.
To make up for it, Flop, breathing hard, tried to sit himself up, and said,

"W-what's this... my body won't move at all...!"

It wasn't just that his arms had no strength; the fluffy bed absorbed his weight besides. His whole body sank in soft, and the state of things was such that he could well believe someone telling him this bed was acting as a cage rather than a bed.
Even so, he twisted and writhed his body desperately, trying somehow to escape, and said,

"Come on, come on...! This is quite the formidable opponent!"

"—. You're a fairly strange human, aren't you."

"Wha—?! Someone's here?!"

Behind him, as he wrestled with the bed, he heard a high voice and tried to turn around. But the effort was in vain; his uncooperative body wouldn't even manage a proper turn.
At the sight of Flop floundering like a fish washed up on land, a deep sigh could be heard, and:

"It's the dragon. Your wound's still in the middle of healing, so don't go thrashing around for no reason."

Saying so, the person walked up beside the bed, and at the sight of them, Flop let out an "Ah."
Sky-blue hair, golden eyes, and two black horns growing from her head—the girl was Madelyn. An Imperial First General, the very "Dragon General" who had assaulted the walled city.
And, in Flop's last memory—

"If I recall, I believe I had quite a painful time getting scratched by your claws..."

"That's right. The dragon's claw gouged out your life. ...That girl healed you, ya know."

"That girl... ah."

Madelyn muttered this, looking away, her face awkward. A face came to mind at her words, and Flop found himself understanding what had happened to him.
At the same time, he recalled that, in his fading consciousness, he had asked something terribly cruel of her—of Rem.

"Miss Madelyn, was it? I'd like to ask—what became of Guaral? A town exposed to a very large explosion, and your friends' assault."

"—"

"Given that I've barely managed to hang onto my life like this, I'd guess my wife and I both survived. Although, that outcome ranks as the second-worst possible one, as far as I'm concerned. The very worst, of course, would be both my wife and I dying."

Holding up a finger, Flop rattled off his questions at Madelyn in a rush.
Watching that finger out of the corner of her eye, Madelyn's awkward expression continued unabated. Taking this as a trace of events having unfolded in a way she hadn't wanted, Flop pressed further.

"Well now, Miss Madelyn. That face of yours looks dissatisfied, or maybe sulking. My sister does that too sometimes, fidgeting away while she sulks. The cute part is that, being as big as she is, her fidgeting never manages to look the least bit modest. Are you the same sort?"

"...cha."

"Hm? What was that?"

"The city's fine, cha! We didn't manage to destroy it all, cha! That good enough for ya, cha?!"

Baring sharp fangs, Madelyn roared back at Flop's words with a thunderous shout. Feeling as though he'd been struck by a ferocious blast of breath all over his body, Flop let out a long breath of relief.
"Fine" hardly seemed an adequate answer given the state Guaral had been in, so far as Flop's memory went, but Madelyn had said she hadn't managed to destroy it completely.
If that was so—

"Then the missus made good use of my life, after all..."

Amid a hazy consciousness and ringing ears, Flop looked back on what he himself had done in the moments just before he'd blacked out.
At the corner of his eye, he'd caught sight of Madelyn, greatly shaken at the sight of him bleeding and on the verge of losing his life. She had seemed to be pressing him about something, clinging to it.
And so he'd thought he could make use of that. By saving Flop's life and promising to give Madelyn the answer she wanted, it should be possible to escape the crisis Guaral had been driven into by the assault.
He hadn't known whether that peace-loving Rem could make such a merciless decision, and even now, with things having apparently worked out, he suspected he'd placed a terrible burden on her.

"Where's the missus who healed my wound?"

"...Brought her back with me, cha. That was the dragon's condition, and that girl agreed to it. A dragon keeps her promises. And a promise made with a dragon, she's gotta keep too, cha."

"A promise..."

"This here, cha."

Told that Rem was safe, Flop felt relief. What was thrust before his eyes was an ornament made from a beast's fang, painted red—no, an ornament made from a dragon's fang.
A precious item Flop normally wore around his neck, never letting it leave his person.
It was thrust right up to the nose of Flop, lying face-down on the bed.

"Ah, you picked it up for me? Thank you, truly. It's an incredibly, incredibly important thing to me. If I lost it, I don't think I could go on living with any kind of composure. So..."

"—Carillon."

"—"

Reaching out, Flop tried to take the fang back. But Madelyn easily evaded his hand, and instead of returning it, spoke a certain name.
At that name Flop held his breath, and Madelyn went on.

"This is Carillon's fang, cha. Why do you have it, cha?"

Carillon—a name there was no mistaking, and Madelyn had said it twice now.
There was no way he could have misheard. Because that name was—

"Answer me, cha! Why do you have Carillon's fang..."

"When Carillon was born, I was there too. I helped come up with that little one's name, along with Big Bro Miles and Balleroy."

"—!"

"When that little one's fangs came in new, I was given one as a keepsake. As proof of our bond as sworn brothers... as family. That's why my sister and I both have one of Carillon's fangs."

Quietly, with the feeling of opening a treasured box he kept deep within his chest, Flop answered so.
The flying dragon fang swaying in Madelyn's hand—that was the answer to where it had come from. A memento of precious family: his benefactor and his sworn brother, two people already gone from this world.

Hearing that answer, Madelyn was struck speechless, her lips trembling, her eyes going wide.
From her reaction, and from the fact that she'd known Carillon's name, Flop, forming several guesses of his own, prefaced what he said next with "May I ask?" and continued,

"Where did you come to know Carillon's name? Recognizing that little one's fang at a single glance—I don't think yours was a shallow bond. And besides, you're..."

"—Ah."

"You're the one who took Balleroy's place, who became the 'Nine.' Could it be that you knew Balleroy and Carillon before all this?"

Madelyn's reaction was painful to see, and it hurt Flop's heart, as though he were cornering someone who looked so young.
But Flop's own heart had once been tormented by a pain far greater, over this very same subject, than what he saw now in a wounded Madelyn. Nothing in this world was a more potent poison than the sight of his little sister's sobbing face.
So he felt no hesitation in cornering Madelyn, in drawing out the feelings she carried in her heart.

—Balleroy Temeglyph.

One of the Vollachia Empire's 'Nine Divine Generals,' sworn brother to Flop and Medium, a rebel who had raised the banner of revolt against the Emperor and lost his life for it, a man who had loved his dragon Carillon and his benefactor Miles with all his heart—that gentle, dear man.
What kind of relationship had there been between him and Madelyn?
And then—

"Why did you become one of the 'Nine Divine Generals'?"

"—Revenge, cha."

There was a beat of hesitation before the answer came, but the words, once spoken, were unmistakably clear.
Madelyn, her lips trembling and her eyes wide, saw that expression slowly change, and in her golden eyes there dwelled the same fierce passion as that change—a blazing wrath.

The fierce anger of the girl who commanded dragons seemed to sear the entire body of Flop as he lay on the bed.
So intense was Madelyn's anger that it created that very illusion.

Madelyn had revealed that she'd taken the seat of one of the 'Nine Divine Generals' for the sake of revenge.
The word "revenge" was one that meant something to Flop too. —Because it was, for Flop, the very goal of his life. Except that the target of Flop's revenge was not an individual, but the world.
Revenge against the world itself—a world that forced someone to kneel and submit.

But the anger dwelling in Madelyn's eyes was something entirely different from that.
The passion burning in her golden eyes knew exactly whom it should be directed at.

"Whose revenge do you seek?"

"...Revenge for Balleroy—for the man who was meant to become the dragon's mate, cha."

"—"

"Whatever killed the dragon's beloved, the dragon will never, ever forgive, cha. And for that—"

That she would carry out revenge for the dead Balleroy, Madelyn answered, her small frame flooded with rage.
How Balleroy and Madelyn had met, what experiences they had gone through together, what form the bond between them had taken—Flop did not know.
He only knew that Madelyn mourned his death, grieved it, from the very bottom of her heart.

So, so, so—

"—Who is it that killed Balleroy? What must be done for that revenge to be carried out?"

If it had been Medium here, she would surely have thrown her arms around Madelyn, face to face.
Medium, who had wept torrents of tears over the deaths of Balleroy and Miles, wailing at the top of her lungs—she surely could have taught Madelyn how to cry.

Someone precious to her had been killed, and Madelyn was raging with fury at that fact.
She didn't know how to grieve. A being who commanded flying dragons, who bore black horns, who held some tremendous power within her—for her, the only way she could think of to grieve was through rage.
And that was just the same as Flop, who had forgotten how to cry.

And so—

"The one who let Balleroy die was—"

To Flop's question, Madelyn, clenching her small hands, answered.
Robbed of her beloved, her love left with nowhere to go, Madelyn had packed it all into that small body, and instead of shedding tears, had let it burn as a flame of rage—resolving to carry out her revenge.

"—"

And Flop, hearing the name from Madelyn's mouth, closed his eyes.
Closing his eyes, he fell perfectly silent. Just this once, he forgot even the pain of the wound Madelyn's claws had carved into his chest, still mid-treatment, and gave himself over entirely to the darkness behind his eyelids.

When he closed his eyes, he could still, even now, call to mind the faces of the people dear to him.

'Both you and Medium don't think things through enough. You could've just stayed with the Countess, but no—can't look after either of you, honestly.'

'That's Big Bro Miles worryin' in his own way, y'know. Thinkin' if you stuck around here, he could look after ya forever. Not too honest 'bout it, is he?'

'Shut it, Bal-bou! Don't you go comin' back here all the time just 'cause you made somethin' of yourself!!'

'Nah, that ain't for me at all. Spendin' time here with Big Bro Miles, and Flop, and Medi and everyone—that suits me way better. Right?'

On the day of their departure, Balleroy, who had gone out of his way to stop by from some distant place, and Miles, who was supposed to have been out late that night—their exchange, that nostalgic memory, came flooding back.
Waving his hand broadly, together with Medium, he'd left behind the land that had cared for them so long, and then—.

And then, Flop O'Connell arrived at the imperial capital, and lit a light within his blue eyes.
With those eyes still lit, his lips wove the words.
They were—

"—So you're the one who killed Balleroy. Mr. Village Chief... no—Emperor Vincent Vollachia."
