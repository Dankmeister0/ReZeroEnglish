Arc 4 Chapter 54: "I Know Hell"

Subaru understood the position he'd put himself in the moment he let the question fly, striking at the heart of the matter.

The question left his mouth, and the instant it struck true against Garfiel's eardrums—the blond youth's expression changed. And seeing that, Subaru's mind went blank for a fleeting instant.

That fierce face, always wary of Subaru, its eyes perpetually narrowed sharp at the corners. Garfiel, who kept up that hard exterior without fail, who never once let a hint of weakness show through.

That face, now, twisted—unmistakably.

Like something precious he'd been hiding had been exposed, leaving him looking like a child on the verge of tears.

"...The hell did you just say?"

But that fragile, about-to-vanish expression lasted only an instant.

Garfiel squeezed his eyes shut once, hard, as if to grind away the weakness that had surfaced, his teeth clicking together, and from his whole body radiated a pressure like something demonic as he glared at Subaru.

The air that had been flowing through the forest, calm and even faintly lonesome, changed entirely in an instant. Under that glare, a crawling, goosebump-raising sensation climbed up Subaru's skin.

All of it was nothing more than Subaru's own body, taking the initiative to express the danger it sensed. And that excessive reaction was—

"You're awfully bloodthirsty all of a sudden."

—more than enough material to convince Subaru that this was something inconvenient to have touched on.

At Subaru's murmur, Garfiel's silent eyes only grew fiercer.

Not a trace of that momentary weakness remained; the gaze now fixed on Subaru carried the razor edge of someone looking at an object of hatred. Bathed in a stare sharp enough to kill, Subaru's whole body kept sounding its usual alarm bells. But he consciously ignored it, keeping his flippant attitude intact.

"Don't make me repeat myself. I saw a girl who's the spitting image of Ryuzu-san wandering around the Sanctuary. I'm pretty sure it wasn't Ryuzu-san herself, though."

"...I don't get what you're sayin'. Ain't that just the old lady walkin' around? If she was wanderin' around in the middle of the night, that'd be a problem for a different reason, but that's got nothin' to do with—"

"—Two."

"Ah?"

Garfiel tried to counter Subaru's claim with logic, but Subaru cut through his earnest deflection, holding up two fingers on his outstretched right hand.

"What I saw was two Ryuzu-san lookalikes, acting at the same time. Even if one of them was the real Ryuzu-san, the other one... I wonder who that was—"

—The instant he finished speaking, Subaru realized he'd lost track of which way was up.

"—Gah!"

Something hard slammed into his back, and a groan and the air in his lungs were wrung out of him at once.

What he felt against his back was a rough, thick, uneven surface—he'd been pressed against a tree trunk with considerable force. And what's more, his body was held aloft so he couldn't run, his posture pinned perpendicular to the ground.

A hand pressed against the center of his stomach served as the fulcrum, keeping Subaru's body suspended in midair as if nailed there. And Garfiel, who'd pulled this off through sheer balance and arm strength, glared into Subaru's eyes from point-blank range as Subaru grasped his situation.

"—Where'd you see that, huh?"

"Where else... In the forest, I..."

"No, that ain't possible. You got any idea how damn careful we are, so that don't happen? Otherwise some nosy bastard like you, sniffin' around where he don't belong, could catch us out."

The pressure of the palm bearing down on him increased with a shove, and Subaru drooled from the corner of his mouth under pressure that felt like it was wringing out even his stomach's contents. He thrashed his limbs, but it didn't budge an inch.

Like an insect specimen pinned to a board. The image alone was horrifying.

"Keep this up and your belly and your back're gonna go squish together, y'know? Before that happens, how's about you tell me the truth, nice and proper."

A sadistic smile rising to the corner of his mouth, Garfiel increased the pressure of his palm further still, as if driving the lesson into Subaru's body.

The pressure began to make his bones and organs creak, enough to convince him the outcome being spoken aloud wasn't a joke. Subaru gasped, breath coming out ragged.

"That'll... depend on your attitude..."

"Ain't that somethin'. You still think you're my equal, in this situation? I coulda sworn I taught you from the start that was just you gettin' ahead of yourself."

"If you finish me off right now without asking anything... nothing gets solved."

"............"

Forming his words in broken fragments, Subaru could see that Garfiel had begun listening to what he was saying.

—To Subaru, this fierce reaction from Garfiel was half as he'd expected, and half not.

Across all the loops so far, the only time Subaru had ever laid eyes on a duplicate of Ryuzu—a "Ryuzu clone"—was in the final battle against the Witch, at the very end of the previous loop.

In every other instance, not only had he never seen a clone, but nothing within the Sanctuary had even given him cause to suspect their existence. Looking back now, if he assumed the clones' existence as a premise, there were several moments where things clicked into place that hadn't before—but that was beside the point for now.

In any case, the clones had been hidden that thoroughly well within the Sanctuary. With at least twenty of them in existence, it was hard to imagine Garfiel alone was sheltering them within the Sanctuary. In all likelihood, it was common knowledge among the Sanctuary's residents.

If anyone on their own side knew, it would probably be Roswaal, and possibly Ram, though he wasn't sure which.

Either way, this was content he'd suspected might touch a nerve if spoken aloud. That he'd been able to confirm as much was, first, exactly as expected.

And what was unexpected—

"...Somethin's wrong with you."

The moment Garfiel spat that out, the hold on Subaru's body released.

"Whoa!" Subaru yelped in surprise, unable to break his fall, and dropped to the ground. He rolled through a mouthful of grass and dirt, then spat out gravel and stood back up to glare at Garfiel.

"H-hey, don't just let go like that out of nowhere, it's gonna scare a guy."

"Shut up, you lunatic. I ain't jokin'. You were testin' me, weren't you?"

"Testing you?"

Subaru tilted his head, feigning ignorance, and Garfiel clicked his tongue in irritation.

He glared up at Subaru from below, at Subaru still playing dumb.

"Just now, you said that thinkin' I might kill you for it."

"............"

—What was unexpected to Subaru was that Garfiel hadn't immediately moved to kill him.

That, even after touching that nerve, he'd still been given the chance to keep talking.

Which meant, in other words, that Subaru had made his statement with the possibility of death already factored in—and Garfiel, sensing that, kicked violently at the dirt.

"No damn joke. Actin' like it's normal to bet your own life, actin' like that's nothin'—what am I supposed to call that if not crazy, huh? It's disgustin'."

"Now that hurts... It's not like I'm doing this without a care in the world, you know."

Subaru laughed weakly at Garfiel's words and scratched his own head. Doing so, he could feel for himself the faint tremor still running through his fingers.

Even now, though Garfiel had stopped his hand from hurting Subaru, he hadn't loosened even a fraction of the hostility-like emotion directed at him.

Which meant nothing other than that Subaru's body was still screaming with primal fear, that the agony of his innards being wrung tight was still ongoing.

Of course it was. He'd deliberately provoked Garfiel to fury and was standing right in front of him. Which meant he was, in effect, half-accepting a confrontation with that golden great tiger—the one that had rampaged through the night forest and slaughtered so many of the villagers.

Thinking of those fangs, those claws, that terror, of being trampled beneath them, made both body and heart shrink back.

But even so—

"If my life alone is enough, then it's a fair trade for the result."

If the only sacrifice paid was Subaru's own heart wearing thin, then what a bargain that was.

A happy ending bought that cheaply didn't come around often.

Subaru's resolve looked ready to snap at any moment—and yet the foundation beneath it, however meager, was hardened solid enough to support it.

Garfiel seemed to understand that too. He wrinkled his nose in distaste.

"I ain't got a single good impression of a guy with eyes like that. So really, I oughta just crush it right now, but..."

"That'd be a problem for me, so if you could see your way to letting it slide, I'd appreciate it. That ties into what I was just asking, too... so?"

"............"

"Are you actually willing to answer my question?"

Brushing the dirt off himself, Subaru pressed the question again.

Having a topic he'd tried to cut off with violence dug back up, Garfiel didn't hide his sour expression. He looked away from Subaru, who never broke eye contact.

"Don't wanna say."

"That so. Well, guess that can't be helped."

Hearing that stubborn, childlike answer, Subaru shrugged and backed off easily.

Garfiel looked almost let down by that reaction, as if he'd been braced for something else.

"You... weren't you tryin' to hear it...?"

"You don't want to talk, right? And I don't have the strength to force it out of you, and not enough leverage either. I could beg and grovel, but that doesn't seem worth the risk, so I'll save it for later."

"...What're you..."

"Don't look so puzzled, Garfiel. No matter how desperately you try to bury it, I'll always dig it up. Because I have to."

Garfiel's face jerked up as if struck, and Subaru met it with a level gaze.

Their eyes crossed, but this time, Subaru wasn't overwhelmed by it. Garfiel's stare was weaker than before, and Subaru's resolve was set.

"Garfiel. I will find out what you—what all of you—are hiding in the Sanctuary. No matter what. Now that I know I have to, that's absolute."

"...Shut your mouth. If I shut it for you right here, right now, there ain't no 'absolute,' no 'no matter what,' is there?"

"Sorry, but that's exactly what makes it absolute, no matter what. As long as I don't give up, a secret I already know is hidden isn't a secret anymore. If you want to blame someone, blame your own carelessness."

Not understanding the meaning of Subaru's words, Garfiel's eyes wavered in confusion.

Naturally, he couldn't be expected to understand the word "carelessness" he'd just been accused of—because that failing of his hadn't happened yet, not at this point in time.

"...Guess talkin' more won't do any good."

Watching Garfiel fall silent, Subaru concluded the conversation was over.

Garfiel's guard was raised to its absolute maximum, and on top of that, he'd dug in his heels. For now—or at least, in this particular loop—there was probably no more unraveling his heart to be done.

There had been almost no prospect of clearing things in this loop from the start, but having it collapse so completely still stung.

"Still, though."

—Even so, he had to endure it.

Having decided to wager his own life on this gamble, this sense of loss was something he'd have to face over and over, again and again, from here on.

Getting used to this pain, or forgetting it—neither should be allowed to happen. That was what he believed.

If he ever grew so numb to repeated "death" that he could no longer wholeheartedly wish for the future beyond it, then surely his heart would be swallowed by shadow and never come back. That's the feeling he had.

"You still planning on stopping me, Garfiel?"

"............"

"If you are, it just means the long way round. I'd rather you didn't."

Even if he were killed right here, Subaru would simply return to the tomb, hours earlier. Whatever followed would likely feel like a foregone conclusion—but even if he ended up dragged out here by Garfiel again, he could probably navigate it more smoothly next time.

Of course, if he could avoid Return by Death altogether, so much the better.

Garfiel gave no response to Subaru's warning.

Subaru turned his back on him and started to head from the forest he'd been dragged into, back toward the Sanctuary. He needed to sort out tomorrow's plans and what he still had to confirm.

However many chances he had left was purely a function of his own nerve—but that didn't mean he could afford to waste them.

"You..."

Garfiel's strangled voice stopped Subaru just as he was walking away.

He halted, but didn't turn around. To that Subaru, Garfiel said:

"What the hell d'you want to do with the Sanctuary... with this place. What're you gonna do to us, huh? Answer me."

"I already told you my goal. I'm going to save Emilia. I've got no intention of doing harm to the Sanctuary. ...And no intention of doing anything to you all, either."

At the very least, he knew of the calamity that would befall the Sanctuary. He wanted to save Ryuzu and the residents from that calamity, and he intended to act toward that end. Naturally, that included Garfiel.

But that was, in the end, only the final result.

"Between now and getting there, I'll probably make you feel like hell more than a few times. I'll apologize in advance for that. ...Sorry about it."

"...I don't get it, whatever the hell you're sayin'. Even that part, you're just like them, ain't you—the spittin' image, all of it."

Faced with Garfiel's attitude—like someone confronting an incomprehensible, unsettling thing—Subaru thought, with a kind of resignation, that it couldn't be helped.

He wanted to be understood. But he didn't expect to be.

"I'm not trying to pick a fight. Starting tomorrow, just act normal—or if you can't do that, fine, just don't get involved with me. Bundle up warm tonight and get to bed early. Sleep in tomorrow morning if you want, forget your morning routine. A little extra sleep now and then—"

Midway through tossing out his parting words as he walked off, Subaru abruptly cut himself short.

Something he'd just said himself had snagged on something in his mind.

And that snag, inside Subaru, took an unexpected shape—

"...Is it worth it?"

"...Ah?"

"Anyway, we're done here for tonight, Garfiel. Whatever's got you anxious, whatever's worrying you—I'll take care of it, I promise. So hang in there and wait until then."

"—! You...!"

At Subaru's words, which looked not to "now" but to "later" the whole way through, Garfiel finally raised his head.

His face flushed red with fury, fangs bared, spit flying as he shouted:

"Talkin' down to me, actin' all high and mighty...! Who—who the hell asked you to fix anything for me?! Don't go stickin' your nose in! You don't know a damn thing—not about this place, not about the old ladies, not... not anything! You don't know anything...!"

"It's exactly because I don't know that I'm going to learn. That's what I think I'm doing here, for that very reason."

"You think you can understand somethin' knowin' just the surface, just what's on top?! Grinnin' like an idiot, spoutin' off pipe dreams, coverin' it all up with sweet-soundin' words—you damn con artist!"

"————"

"You don't know a damn thing about real pain, real suffering—don't you dare talk like you understand—!!"

Garfiel screamed at Subaru's know-it-all face, the words tumbling out fast.

The curses vanished into the night forest sky as if swallowed whole.

Talking down to him. Acting like he understood, with words and attitude alike. Meddling, despite knowing nothing.

—And indeed, every word of it was entirely true. Not a single point left room for rebuttal.

And yet—

"...I know."

"————"

"I know 'hell.' —I've already seen it. Many times over."

If there was a hell in this world, it was the world Subaru had seen.

Across the countless times the world had come to its end, Subaru had witnessed a hell he wanted to look away from, over and over, burning it into his eyes, having it driven home to him, again and again.

Which is exactly why—

"It's fine if I'm the only one who knows 'hell.' That's what I'm here for."

—That's what he believed. For now.

After having his heart clawed raw by Garfiel's anguished cry, Subaru didn't return to the cathedral that served as his bed.

Normally, he'd have gone to bed and taken his time carefully working out his plans going forward. In fact, until just a little while ago, that had been his intention.

There was a reason he'd abandoned that plan and set off on this trek instead.

"...I think it was around this way, if I remember right."

Pushing aside the thick, overgrown vines with his arm, Subaru muttered to himself as he made his way through pathless terrain.

The moonlight was blocked by the trees, leaving visibility poor, and underfoot the grass stood knee-high or taller, making footing unreliable. With no proper path and the ground uneven in height, he had to move carefully to avoid tripping, which naturally slowed him down.

"I'm pretty confident in my sense of direction, but my memory's kind of hazy on this one, I'll admit. It's not like I had the luxury of calmly looking around at the time, so I guess it can't be helped."

His self-justification came out weak as he scanned his surroundings, groping his way forward.

Right now, Subaru was in the forest outside the Sanctuary—though not the spot where he'd been talking with Garfiel. He'd gone back to the Sanctuary once from there, then entered the forest again, separately.

As for why—

"That time, Garfiel came out of the trees from around this direction, I'm pretty sure."

Before the loop began—meaning, the story rewinds to before his very first Trial in the tomb.

On the morning of the day he underwent the Trial, Subaru had brought Otto along to the tomb, to check whether he himself had the qualifications to take the Trial. As a result, the tomb had accepted him, and that night he'd gotten the chance to challenge the Trial and settle things with his past—but that wasn't the issue here.

That morning, in front of the tomb, while Subaru and Otto were trading their usual banter, Garfiel had emerged from the forest and joined them. When asked, he'd said he'd been making his daily rounds patrolling the Sanctuary.

"What bugs me is the timing, and the fact that he came out from right there."

The timing had been suspiciously convenient, and the spot he'd emerged from was the problem.

Recalling the image of Garfiel stepping out of the brush at the side of the tomb, Subaru had felt a sense of familiarity about it. Digging through his memory, pulling out what exactly had snagged on him, Subaru realized what it was.

—The mysterious facility Beatrice had teleported him to from the mansion, into the Sanctuary.

When Subaru had made his way back to the Sanctuary from that facility, the spot he'd emerged into felt close to the place Garfiel had come back from that time.

So Subaru had made a guess that there was something at that facility, and entered the forest. Right now, he was searching the night forest for the path to that facility. And—

"A path worn down by footsteps... that means—"

Seeing a stretch of cleared ground connected in an unbroken line through the brush, Subaru judged it proof that someone walked this route regularly. Following that path deeper into the forest, letting his impatience carry him into a brisk pace—eventually, the view brightened, and—

"...Found it."

A dilapidated stone building. He could make out a fossil-like structure, on the verge of collapse, built deep in the forest with its back to a rocky outcrop.

Approaching the building, hard to make out in the dim light, Subaru gave its exterior a quick once-over and tilted his head.

"Huh, I could've sworn it was more wrecked than this, last time I saw it..."

He couldn't mistake its age, but the state of its damage seemed less severe than what he remembered. Put plainly, what Subaru had seen before was a ruin, but the building in front of him now barely still held together as a building.

Which meant—

"Unless my memory's playing tricks on me, does that mean sometime in the next six days, something's going to happen that wrecks this place even further...?"

Matching his memory against the current state, that was the only conclusion he could draw.

And if the facts lined up that way, then it wasn't likely that this place had nothing to do with whatever was going to happen in the Sanctuary, just as Subaru had hoped.

Holding his breath, Subaru carefully killed his presence and turned the handle at the entrance.

The door opened with a surprisingly quiet creak, and he stepped inside, greeted by a sour, musty smell.

As before, the scattered equipment and neglected, ruined entryway greeted him—something with the air of a waiting room or reception area. Passing through it, Subaru made for the room that was his real target.

Through the corridor, to the innermost door—the room where Beatrice's hand had teleported him before, the one with the bottomless-looking pit torn open in its floor.

Bracing himself for a pitfall waiting the moment he opened it, Subaru slowly pushed the door open, poking his head in to check the state of things inside, and—

"...Well, I'll be."

At the sight spread before his eyes, a voice slipped out that he couldn't quite hold back.

Lit by a pale blue light, refracted and scattered, Subaru narrowed his eyes against the glare and looked directly at it.

What existed at the innermost point of the facility he'd sought out, in the room he'd been aiming for, was—

"...Ryuzu-san? Is that... you?"

—A crystal giving off a faint blue glow, of a mass large enough to embrace in both arms, with a small woman sealed inside it.
