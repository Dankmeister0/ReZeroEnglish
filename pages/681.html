Arc 8 Chapter 62: "The Power of Love"

— Its entire body bursting into flame, the "Witch" crumbled to dust.

The power of the "Sunsword," finally unveiled, was terrifying — the ferocity of flames that could burn away even a soul with just a graze exceeded even the imagination of Subaru, who had gone along with the scheme to make use of it.

"So this is the 'Sunsword' showing its true power..."

The crimson flames had spread not only to the "Witch" that had been cut down, but to the other two "Witches" as well. Seeing the result they wrought, Subaru could only hold his breath.

Subaru had witnessed the "Sunsword" being wielded a few times before now, but on those occasions, its owner had always been Priscilla. That said, it would be too simplistic to conclude from this that Abel was a superior wielder of the "Sunsword" compared to Priscilla.

The opponents Priscilla had wielded the "Sunsword" against had always been powerful enemies. This time's "Witch" — Sphinx — was likewise a formidable foe, but to defeat her, Abel had engaged in an unbelievable gambit.

By never once using the "Sunsword" — through every battlefield, every juncture, every dire crisis up to this very day — he had erased that option from Sphinx's awareness. No, from everyone's awareness.

Subaru couldn't even imagine how much sheer nerve it took to see that through to fruition.

"What is it, that unconvinced look on your face?"

"I'm just shocked. Even after all we've been through together, more things about your character keep popping up that I want to complain about...!"

"And here I thought you'd say something worthwhile... Don't tell me you mean my keeping my cards hidden? If so, you too lent your wits to the very same scheme."

"Mine's not a matter of character, it's the result of wisdom, courage, and cheating — so it's fine! Now that I think back on it — the first 'Lifeblood Ritual,' when we clashed with Zikr and the others in Guaral, when Arakiya came at us after that, when we butted heads with Yorna in Chaosflame, the game of tag with Olbart, the Coupled Dragon Carriages — all of it! You were holding it back the whole time!"

"And?"

"'And'!? Ow, ow, owww."

Subaru was flabbergasted at Abel, who affirmed it without a hint of shame, crimson sword in hand — and immediately after, the pain in his right arm, broken by Sphinx as an object lesson, came surging back all at once.

As he cradled his broken arm with tears in his eyes, Beatrice came to his side, said "Subaru," and held out her hand, casting healing magic on him.

And so, as Beatrice gently treated Subaru, she said—

"No matter how much you say it, it won't get through to that man, I suppose. His capacity to feel guilt is probably broken, I suppose. He's the same breed as Roswaal, I suppose."

"Grnnnhh...!"

"There, that should be a bit better, I suppose."

Having described Abel with what was, for her, the harshest insult she could muster, Beatrice finished up Subaru's treatment.

The pain still lingered, but he could feel that the broken bone had been set. He wouldn't be able to move it unreasonably, but it would take time to heal completely, and there were other priorities besides.

"Will Jamal Aurélie live?"

"As long as his breath hasn't stopped, Betty will manage something, I suppose. That's the job of Subaru's partner, I suppose."

With that, Beatrice trotted off with quick little steps toward Jamal.

Of the three Sphinxes that had appeared after the first one was defeated, Jamal was the one who had provoked them the most and taken the worst beating for it. His method of provocation had been the crude sort — hurling unbearable abuse at them without pause — but even that was the doing of his loyalty to the Emperor.

"Uhwaw."

"Oh, Spica, you did great. How's your body? Nothing bad anywhere?"

"Awu!"

Spica nodded firmly and smiled so as not to worry him. He wiped her dirt-smudged cheek with his hand, then went on to pat her head, praising her for how hard she'd fought.

This was, unmistakably, a victory won by everyone present fulfilling their own role.

That Abel's trump card had contributed to it more than anything else was something he had to admit, but—

"Even so, I'm going to hold this against you for the rest of my life."

"If it's driven you to say as much, then the one who proposed this scheme is surely satisfied as well."

No matter how resentfully Subaru glared, Abel let it pass with an unruffled face.

That cold treatment was infuriating, but devising the scheme to keep the "Sunsword" hidden, and seeing it through on faith alone — for that, Subaru had nothing but words of praise.

— That Abel could use the "Sunsword" yet had gone without using it was something Subaru himself had only learned within a loop after the battle with Sphinx had already begun.

Frankly, it was a war-crime level of secretiveness.

Originally, the only trump card expected to be capable of stopping the "Great Disaster" that threatened to destroy the Vollachian Empire was Spica's "Star-Eating" — which required combining her strength with Subaru's.

But if Abel's "Sunsword" could reach the soul at the very core of the Undead, that changed everything.

Because it meant the two could operate the "Sunsword" and "Star-Eating" as twin trump cards for defeating the Undead — and by extension, for repelling the "Great Disaster" itself.

"So that's it, you'd already realized that beforehand, and that's why you joined our group, huh?"

"Uuh?"

"Listen, Spica, this sneaky Emperor here could use the 'Sunsword,' and he knew it was the key item for taking down zombies. And that's the same for you too, right? In fact, we had to actively use 'Star-Eating' to whittle the zombies down. So what happens then?"

"Ahh, ah-awu?"

"That's right. The enemy's main force comes to stop us. We try to use your 'Star-Eating' on that main force somehow, because we don't know of any other way to win. And if your hand reaches them, great. But if it doesn't, then..."

"Awuaah!"

At the end of Subaru's explanation, Spica's eyes went wide and she pointed at Abel's "Sunsword." At her understanding, Subaru said "Exactly right!" and patted her head.

Then, standing beside Spica, he turned a look at Abel like one might give human garbage.

"Listen up, Spica. Those are the eyes of a person with a twisted personality. You've gotta be that pitch-black to become Emperor of Vollachia. ...Maybe it really would be better if the Empire fell?"

"Switching sides the instant the enemy's ringleader burns to ash? Even as betrayals go, your timing is far too poor."

"Hey, I'm begging you, don't go and get yourself deposed in a coup right after we save the Empire together..."

Necessity or not, the Empire was far too bad an influence on Spica's emotional upbringing. Abel was practically the poster child for that, making him the very last person Subaru would want to keep nearby if circumstances allowed otherwise.

Spica ought to spend more time with good, wholesome company — people like Emilia, Rem, Beatrice, and Tanza, children worth emulating.

"In that case, I'd better keep Otto and Roswaal away too. Sis is... I like her, but more than one of her at once is rough. Garfiel seems like he'd be a good influence, though..."

"Subaru! Treatment over here is settled for now, I suppose!"

"Oh. Got it!"

Following Beatrice's voice, Subaru and the others went together to Jamal's side.

But there was no voice from Jamal to greet Subaru and the others as they arrived — he lay sprawled limply on the ground, limbs splayed out.

"For now, he's out of mortal danger, I suppose. But treating him further will take time, and it also depends on how much strength Betty has left, I suppose."

"Your remaining strength, Beako... you mean like leftover mana?"

"I suppose so. ...Thanks to a little irregularity I've been able to splurge more than usual, but I don't want to keep relying on that so readily, I suppose."

"Irregularity..."

Hearing Beatrice's answer, Subaru gazed down intently at his own hand.

What she meant by irregularity was the amount of mana remaining in Subaru's body — for Beatrice, his Contracted spirit, whose own available MP was directly determined by however much mana Subaru held, the amount currently being channeled to her really was irregular indeed.

Subaru had given various thoughts to the reason for this himself.

As possibilities, there was the pattern where his bond with everyone in the "Pleiades Battalion" was having a positive effect on him too, and the pattern where his shrunken body was causing some abnormality in his Gate.

Though, in the latter case, it didn't seem likely that even Beatrice wouldn't know the cause, so he suspected the former possibility was more likely.

"I'd personally be happier — and it'd be way cooler — if it's everyone's bond giving me strength."

"Subaru..."

"Don't worry, Beako. That said, it's probably not some convenient situation where I can use magic without limit, so I agree we should conserve it as much as we can. But, hmm, this is a problem."

While respecting Beatrice's judgment, Subaru gazed steadily at the fallen Jamal.

Naturally, they couldn't just abandon a man who'd contributed to their victory. But if Jamal's consciousness didn't return, someone would inevitably need to carry him.

"I'll say it now — I won't carry him."

"You... jumping ahead of me like that— You've seemed awfully lively ever since you picked up that sword. Weren't you just having a scrappy catfight on par with me, and now you're zipping around leaping all over the place... Carrying Jamal ought to be nothing for you."

"Even if I could, I won't. Now that Jamal Aurélie has fallen, I am surely the most agile among us here. In a position that demands vigilance, I cannot afford to carry baggage."

"Don't call him baggage! Argh, fine! I'll—"

"Uu-awu."

Angry at the unreasonable Emperor who nonetheless spoke reasonably, Subaru moved to shoulder Jamal himself. But Spica slipped past him and took the initiative, lifting Jamal's body up herself.

The sight of a small girl lifting a grown man with such ease was a rather pitiful one to look at, even though Spica herself didn't seem to be straining much at all.

"Well, I guess it'd look pitiful no matter who but you did it. What do you think?"

"Well done. Continue as you are."

"Just like Beako said, this guy has zero sense of guilt!"

"Didn't I say so, I suppose. ...Spica, don't push yourself either, I suppose."

Beatrice sighed at Abel's high-handedness, and Spica answered her concern with a smiling "U!" It was a relief that she really didn't seem to find Jamal heavy at all.

In any case, though the reality of having defeated the formidable Sphinx was gradually sinking in, they couldn't afford to celebrate unreservedly.

Because—

"— It seems there was meaning, after all, to keeping my hands free so promptly."

As Abel spoke and jutted his chin, shapes could be seen streaming one after another into the street where Subaru and the others stood — Undead, apparently drawn by the sound of the fight with the Sphinxes just now.

Subaru gritted his teeth at what this meant, and at the infuriating correctness of Abel's words.

For Undead to appear here like this meant—

"...Even with Sphinx defeated, the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' hasn't broken off, I suppose."

"Damn, so the safest bet — that the magic would vanish once we beat the caster — was off the mark. Beako, does this mean..."

"Even fallen this far, she's still a being made in aspiration of the 'Witch of Greed,' I suppose. She probably built the formula so the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' would keep functioning even in her absence, I suppose. Unless we destroy the system itself, the limit of the 'Rock Mass' will remain the limit of this country, I suppose."

"And the most likely place for that to be is, after all..."

Taking in Beatrice's words, Subaru's gaze turned to the far north of the capital — the Crystal Palace.

It was the place the Sphinxes had made their stronghold, and if something symbolic were to be placed anywhere, it would be the most fitting location. Of course, there was also the chance that Sphinx, indifferent to such romance or drama, had placed the mechanism somewhere else entirely.

"— No, it must be the Crystal Palace. Whatever that 'Witch's' intentions, if a connection to the 'Rock Mass' is required, then contact with the Crystal Palace's core... Moguro Hagane... is necessary."

"Wasn't Moguro one of the 'Nine Divine Generals'...?"

"He is. He's currently kept asleep within the Crystal Palace. Or, to be precise, he's been made to sleep as the Crystal Palace."

"You are absolutely, definitely explaining that to me properly later!"

Subaru said this to Abel, who clearly still had plenty of secrets beyond just the "Sunsword," though he welcomed the fact that their course had solidified around the Crystal Palace being the right answer.

—

If he was honest, even knowing that whatever lay at the core of the "Great Disaster" was in the Crystal Palace, he wanted to confirm the safety of the comrades he'd sent to other battlefields. Especially Emilia and Tanza.

Their deployment was the one part Subaru hadn't been able to fully confirm.

That anxiety and impatience of Subaru's—

"— Well now, all of you look beat to hell. Even the Emperor here's sweating and covered in mud — surprisingly the hardworking type, huh."

A black shadow drifted down and, in a tone that sounded almost carefree, forcibly swept it all aside.

The figure landed between Subaru's group and the approaching Undead, then strolled over with an air of nonchalance and crouched down in front of Subaru and Beatrice.

Then, still holding a golden pipe between his teeth, he grinned and held out his cupped hands.

"For you hardworking types... here, a little souvenir."

So saying, he opened his joined hands, revealing several strange crystals about the size of coins resting on his large palms.

Subaru had no idea what they were, but Beatrice, whose hand he was holding, realized at once.

"Those are Core Insects...!"

"Yup yup. From those folks."

He nodded, unbothered, and pressed his palms back together, rubbing them briskly against each other.

In the next instant, the Undead down the street let out cries of anguish and crumbled to dust all at once — because the cores reproducing their bodies had been crushed, and they could no longer maintain their forms.

The one who had accomplished this in a single instant, without so much as blinking, was a tall, black-furred Wolf Human—

"Halibel-san!"

"Oh, oh, what a warm welcome. I do like a lively kid who can raise their voice."

The one who nodded at Subaru's astonished voice was Halibel, who had been acting separately.

Subaru had entrusted him with what might be called the single most critical task in this decisive battle for the capital — eliminating the enemy that had to be removed for everyone to bring out their best performance.

And the fact that he was here now, like this, meant—

"You can relax, I got the job properly done. Can't let Ana-bou lose face, after all."

"—! That's a huge help! Thank you! Can I ask you for one more thing right away!?"

"Mm, you're not the type to hold back, are you. Go ahead, go ahead, say it."

"I want you to head to the castle with someone! Though, on the way, I want to have you cover for some of the others too. We'll work Abel here half to death on this end!"

"How insolent..."

Abel muttered something displeased at Subaru's proposal, but he deliberately ignored it.

In any case, if Halibel had finished his job, that was a stroke of luck. Emilia's group was where the biggest cause for worry lay, but with Halibel heading there, it would be like adding an iron club to an ogre — or flower effects on Emilia.

"Olbart-san should be at the castle too. So I want you to look after Emilia and Tanza—"

"Wait, Natsuki Subaru. Is that a strategic judgment? If it is not—"

"If it'll make you happy, then sure, that's exactly what it is! It has to do with my performance, after all!"

Subaru shot back at Abel, who had cut in, and the two glared at each other.

Beatrice, gripping his hand tightly as if to say she was on his side, was a reassuring presence. Then, into that tense exchange of glares, Halibel cut in with a "now, now."

He slid a hand-chop between Subaru and Abel, as if cutting the thread of their staring match, and said—

"No need to get so worked up. And, about the fighting at the castle — I don't think I'm needed there, y'know?"

"Huh?"

Subaru's eyes went wide at Halibel's remark, made while stroking the fur beneath his own chin.

At the look demanding grounds for that statement, the "Extoller" turned his thread-thin eyes toward the distance, and—

"I guess this is one of those 'the power of love is great' kind of stories, huh."

—he said, laughing with a face mixing exasperation and admiration in equal measure.

— An impossible sight was unfolding before her.

She, who could no longer define whether she was Yorna Mishigure or Airis, could only watch it, holding back the tears that threatened to blur her sight.

"— Tanza."

The name her trembling lips wove out — the Deer Human girl it belonged to, flame kindled in one eye, the hem of the kimono they had chosen together flaring out as she fought back against the swordsmen among the Undead surging fiercely upon her.

It was a fight far, far more agile and powerful than the Tanza she knew, one brimming with confidence.

Tanza had been a girl without confidence.

So clever and capable at that young age, and yet a child who rated herself far too low, convinced she was never enough. Back when she herself had truly been the same age as Tanza, she could never have done what Tanza did.

A foolish child who, getting carried away with the idea that fulfilling the work her parents and the people of her home village had entrusted her with — so as not to become a burden — was her role, failed to notice the consideration all around her.

Compared to that self of hers, Tanza had been far more admirable — and now, at a time when even her own footing had grown uncertain, she had been reunited with the Tanza she had thought lost to death.

Priska, the Empire, the Magic City, Tanza — and Ugarde. Her mind was in the greatest state of confusion it had been in across more than ten lifetimes.

The only comparable moments she could think of were when she first learned she had been reincarnated by a Curse, when she learned Priska was alive, when she was reunited with Ugarde, and when the living Tanza wouldn't listen to a word she said.

"Too much, all at once, it is...!"

But now was far too frantic a moment to lament a present life so overloaded with shock after shock.

Amid the continuing weight of all that mental strain, Tanza's back — boldly making use of the effects of the "Soul Marriage Technique" and something more besides, to protect her who could not recover the will to fight — looked immense.

Just how much had happened since they'd been parted in the Magic City?

She had come to Chaosflame together with her sister Zoey, and ever since, she had believed she'd watched over Tanza's growth from the closest vantage of all. But had the Tanza standing here now truly grown at her side?

Someone who was not her had stayed close to Tanza, pushed her forward, and made her strong.

She felt it in Tanza's powerful gaze, in her stride, in the fist she drove into the swordsman's face.

"Tanza-chan!"

"Yes, Emilia-sama, let's press the attack."

Ringing out with a voice like silver bells, the silver-haired girl — Emilia — worked in tandem with Tanza.

Her long hair dancing, swinging weapons made of ice to knock aside the Undead swordsmen and cover the attack and defense, she and Tanza moved in flawless unison.

And yet the time Tanza and Emilia had had to build their bond couldn't have been all that long.

Before the unexpected prowess of Tanza and Emilia, the blue-haired swordsmen — the "Undead Sword Saints" — were shattered one after another, scattering fragments all around.

But—

"Ahh, ahh, ahh, how sad — the limits of the living, is it not. You wield your strength with quite the fine display, but how long do you reckon it'll hold out?"

"Oh, honestly! Don't act so high and mighty when you're the one cheating!"

Emilia rose to the provocation of an "Undead Sword Saint," its golden Undead eyes gleaming as it grinned provocatively.

When she raised both hands and struck the ground hard, in the next instant ice spread out from the street like branches extending, avoiding Emilia and Tanza, and bound the swordsmen's arms, legs, and torsos.

— It looked, for a moment, just like thorned vines.

"—Ngh."

"Yorna-sama!"

For an instant, a pang shot through her chest, and she pressed a hand to it, crumpling down where she crouched.

Catching her careless lapse at the corner of her eye, Tanza cried out her name like a scream. Even if only for an instant, it meant Tanza's attention had left the battlefield—

"Greedy for victory, one must be."

"—!"

A powerful front kick was unleashed, sending Tanza's small body flying backward.

Tanza's body bounced and flew, tumbling toward where she herself crouched. She reflexively lifted her face, about to call Tanza's name — but what good would calling do?

It would only pull Tanza's attention away again, pointlessly.

"—Tanza!"

"Yes, Yorna-sama."

Even so, unable to hold it back, she cried out — and in answer, the bouncing Tanza righted herself in midair. An instant before colliding with her, Tanza caught herself, and that small yet immense back was right before her eyes.

Toward them, the merciless blade of an "Undead Sword Saint" closed in to cut the two of them down together—

"—Emilia-sama!"

"Right!"

Emilia responded to the call without a moment's hesitation, and Tanza swung her leg up.

That upraised leg — wearing ice geta she'd switched into — caught the "Undead Sword Saint's" blade head-on.

They were thick-soled geta, exactly like the ones the "Kaleidoscopic" Yorna Mishigure wore so masterfully.

"Yaaah—!!"

Having stopped the blade with the sole of her raised geta, Tanza sprang off that same leg, spinning vertically through the air — and the other ice geta slammed the head of the now-frozen "Undead Sword Saint" down into the ground.

The street caved in a circle nearly five meters across, centered on the Undead's head as it was driven into the ground, the opponent turned to dust by the ferocious blow, as Tanza's kimono hem swirled through the air.

—

At the sight of Tanza looking so dashing, she found herself staring, speechless. Noticing her gaze, Tanza turned back, her eyes softening slightly at the corners as she smiled.

Those lips moved as if about to call out "Yorna—"

"—! No!"

In that instant, Emilia, some distance away, thrust out both hands — the shriek of freezing air, and behind her, the sense of two "Undead Sword Saints" being pierced by ice and turned into ice statues.

But she couldn't voice her gratitude to Emilia.

More urgent than that was stopping the brutality of yet another "Undead Sword Saint" that had appeared behind the turned Tanza — no, there wasn't time. She had to protect Tanza.

"I've...!"

She had overlooked so much, let so much slip through her fingers, disappointed so many people, and stood here now atop so many lives.

Even she, who had never repaid any of that debt, could at least do this for Tanza.

—

She reached out and pulled Tanza into her arms, tilting her body to shield her from the blade, resolving to protect her through to the end.

She had moved, and moved well. If only for this one moment, she was glad she wasn't the trembling, frozen self she'd been.

All that was left was to hope that her body — taller now, its limbs longer than before — could shield this small Beloved Child, the Beloved Child of the Empire that he had once cherished and protected so dearly—

"— It is, after all, only you who could surprise me this much."

That soft voice rang out sweetly, as if whispered right at her ear, so gentle it seemed impossible for a battlefield raging with the wind of blades — melting away, as if it had never been there, the heart that had, just a moment ago, resigned itself to death.

—

Before the "Undead Sword Saint's" blade could land, it had been stopped by a crimson sword thrust into its path.

And seeing with her own eyes the one who had done it, she could only catch her breath.

It was an unexpected, unwanted reunion.

Not knowing whether to rejoice or grieve, her heart had felt cracked ever since that moment.

In the end, she had reached this moment without ever finding an answer.

But right now, in this one instant, she could say one thing clearly.

"That tense face of yours is lovely too, my star."

"Your Majesty...!"

For before the man who said this, stroking her cheek with the hand not holding back the enemy's attack — Ugarde Vollachia, his skin warm with living blood just as it had been in life, his eyes holding a heart within them — neither Yorna nor Airis could hold back the overwhelming tenderness that welled up in her.
