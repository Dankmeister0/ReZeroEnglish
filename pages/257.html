Arc 4 Chapter 89: "A Memory of Snow"

——After finishing his secret talk with Ryuzu, Subaru walked alone through the forest.

Ryuzu had said she had something she wanted to think over and had stayed behind in front of the crystal. A girl with the same face as herself—what could she possibly be brooding over, standing before her own original?

It was a subject Subaru's curiosity couldn't help but latch onto, but he had no idea how far he was allowed to pry. In any case, the Ryuzu he'd just managed to talk to was, for now, someone on his side.

There were four Ryuzu duplicates. According to what Ryuzu had told him, two of them had never undergone the Trials of the Tomb. And two Ryuzu had. And Subaru suspected that one of the Ryuzu who had taken the Trials was the one feeding Garfiel some kind of idea.

There was a chance the four duplicates weren't of one mind. He'd made the mistake of assuming all four Ryuzu were the same existence, simply because they were premised on playing the same single person. But even though the four of them were performing the role of one and the same person, they were separate beings, each with her own way of thinking.

If that was so, then it was hardly strange for one of them—especially one who had gone through an experience the other Ryuzu hadn't—to have arrived at a different conclusion.

For convenience, Subaru decided to call the four Ryuzu α, β, θ, and Σ respectively. The Ryuzu who didn't know the Trials would be α and β. The Ryuzu who did know the Trials would be θ and Σ. He'd actually wanted to call them Eins, Zwei, and Drei in cool German, but he couldn't come up with a fourth, so that plan fell through.

Anyway—

"The earliest I can meet the problem children, θ and Σ, is two days from now, at best..."

The deadline before the Great Rabbit attacked the Sanctuary—or, more precisely, the deadline for luring away the Great Rabbit, which had already come close, using the large-scale Magic of the great blizzard—was five days out. Since a solution wasn't going to spring into being the moment they talked, three days of remaining time could hardly be called generous breathing room.

To stop the Sanctuary's barrier from being lifted, Garfiel would, in the end, slaughter the villagers. Given that Ryuzu θ and Σ were willing to make him go that far, their resolve wouldn't easily be shaken. Factoring in the need to persuade them as well, the road ahead could only be called dark.

"Still, in exchange for more obstacles... I think I can see a way through on Garfiel's end, huh? Basically, if I can somehow talk the two troublemaking Ryuzu down, the problem just goes away."

If Ryuzu θ and Σ's influence was behind Garfiel's rampage, then persuading them was, in effect, the very condition for winning Garfiel over. Since Subaru had had no way to work on Garfiel directly, this was a faint and uncertain thread, but undeniably a ray of light.

At present, Garfiel commanded not just his own individual strength but also had command authority over the Ryuzu duplicates beyond the representative ones—twenty mindless, pure copy-bodies. The copies were mechanical forces that wouldn't even hesitate to die, and Garfiel felt little resistance to using these will-less things. That cold pragmatism struck Subaru as far too harsh to stomach.

Setting that sentiment aside as a separate issue, he wanted to avoid clashing with Garfiel if at all possible. It was something he'd already known, but silencing Garfiel by force simply wasn't possible. If Garfiel, egged on by θ and Σ, led those copies and turned against them in full, fully committed as an enemy, their odds of winning would drop even further.

"After making that big speech and even signing a Contract with Roswaal, I can't afford to fail. Don't intend to, either. Whether or not the safety net actually works is a separate question—wherever I can hold the line myself, I've gotta hold it."

Subaru slapped his own cheeks to keep them from sagging into weakness and shook his head to steel himself. Then, lifting his face forward, what he saw was—

"Either way, dealing with Ryuzu θ and Σ is going to have to be improvised on the spot. Trading a blow or two with Garfiel first to get a feel for him wouldn't be a bad idea, but... that'll have to wait."

He passed out of the forest and back into the Sanctuary's settlement. The sun had fully risen, and here and there throughout the village, the residents and the evacuees from Arlam Village were starting their daily routines. Glancing at them sidelong, raising a hand now and then to return a greeting, Subaru made his way, without deviation, toward a place set apart from all that everyday bustle.

A place set slightly apart from both the great cathedral and the settlement. —To the bedchamber assigned to Emilia, where she was staying.

—Even now, if she closed her eyes, she could recall it vividly.

It had been a white, white world. Across an endless expanse of silver, young Emilia walked all alone.

—You mustn't remember this!

A voiceless voice rose up, but it couldn't reach the small Emilia, who walked with her head bowed. Looking around anxiously, glancing this way and that, disappointed again and again as her hopes were betrayed, she trudged onward through the snow, dragging her feet.

—Turn back! Please, I'm begging you! Don't do anything more!

Young Emilia let out a white breath and watched the mist curling from her own mouth with curious eyes. Again and again, over and over, she breathed out. She wore nothing but a thin undergarment of light cloth and a cloak-like garment that wrapped her body entirely. It was a hopelessly thoughtless way to dress for surviving in a world of bitter cold, but there was nothing to be done about it.

After all, for Emilia, seeing snow at all, and a world this cold, were both entirely new experiences. The world she knew was a forest overflowing with green and warm light, nothing at all like this world now buried under snow and ice.

A place that should have been familiar was showing her an expression she had never seen before. That fact struck the young Emilia as strange, and it made her forget the reaction she should have had.

—No! Not that way! Turn back! If you won't, then please, just—!

Even pleading until her throat tore, until it felt crushed, until she coughed up blood, young Emilia's feet did not stop. The voice went unheard, reaching nowhere, and mercilessly, the girl kept walking forward.

On the unfamiliar snowy path, her bare feet—having lost their footwear somewhere—stumbled painfully onward. Her bare soles, long past feeling cold or pain, were cut by branches and stones hidden beneath the snow, leaving drops of blood to mark the girl's path.

And yet, desperately, forgetting even the pain, hiding her fear of this unfamiliar world, what was it she was pressing forward alone for?

—Stop, please... I don't want to see it anymore... please...

The pleading went unheeded. The wish would not come true. Hope was only ever cut short. The reality she should have already known held true even in the world of dreams, cruelly forcing itself upon her—by showing her, of all things, her past self and the shape of her greatest sin.

"——!"

Young Emilia's violet eyes lit up as though she'd glimpsed hope beyond the swirling curtain of snow blocking her view. What lay at the end of her gaze was, as far as young Emilia knew, the trunk of the tallest great tree in the world. That massive tree, called the "Great Tree of Prayer," was a sacred tree to which prayers were offered up to something unseen and holy, a cherished, irreplaceable presence to everyone throughout the settlement. Young Emilia, too, had stubbornly believed that merely touching that great trunk let her feel its vast blessing against her skin.

In that moment, how reassuring it was to Emilia that the great tree still stood in its usual place, keeping its proud form intact. In the midst of a familiar landscape transformed into somewhere unknown, how much comfort she drew from the great tree, which alone kept the ordinary world going.

Breathing out great clouds of white, Emilia scrambled toward the great tree on unsteady feet. The piling snow reached up to young Emilia's knees, and in the short distance before she reached the tree, the girl fell again and again, leaving the imprint of her body scattered across the pure white snowfield.

At last, having fallen on her face so many times that even her nose had turned red from the cold, Emilia arrived at the base of the great tree. In relief, the tension in her face eased ever so slightly. But even that was only the faintest of tremors, since the muscles, stiffened by the cold, had already gone rigid.

"——?"

Then, reaching a hand toward one of the great tree's roots, young Emilia's face showed she had noticed something. She slid her hand along, tracing the root, and dug with frozen fingertips into the snow burying what lay beyond.

—Stop it!!

She kept digging, kept digging. Purely, driven only by a swelling sense of urgency, young Emilia dug into the snow.

—Stop! Stop, stop, stop, stop, stop it!

She didn't want to see. She didn't want to remember. She wanted to turn her face away, shut her eyes, cover her ears, tear the world apart with her voice. But there was no face to turn away, no eyes to shut, no ears to cover—nothing to deceive.

Young Emilia's fingertips reached something within the snow. And slowly, with her own hand, the girl brushed away the last fragment of snow—

—Stop, please!!

"—To think I actually saved someone like you."

"——"

"Proof of sin. Proof of defilement. Cursed and cursed and cursed again, and at the end of all that suffering..."

"——"

"Just die already. —You witch's child."

"—Emilia? Emilia? Hey, are you all right?!"

"Ah, eh... ah, Su-baru...?"

As he kept shaking her shoulder and calling out to her, Emilia's eyes fluttered faintly open, and she said Subaru's name. She shook her head for a moment, as if trying to shake off a hazy consciousness, and then—

"Why is Subaru here...?"

"Do I need a reason to show up? I could stare at Emilia-tan's face all day long and never get tired of it."

"That's not what I meant... um."

Perhaps her mind still wasn't entirely clear—there was a lingering unease in the way Emilia responded. Wanting to chase away that unease of hers, Subaru slapped his knee, stood up, and smiled at her.

"You shouldn't go dropping your guard like that in front of me, you know. I appreciate the trust and all, but I'm a man who keeps a ferocious wolf under the coat of reason, myself. I'd like Emilia-tan to be a little more aware of me as, y'know, a guy."

"...? I'd never forget about you, Subaru. It's just that I just woke up, so my answers came out a little strange... but wait, when did I even doze off...?"

There seemed to be a sense that the true meaning of his words hadn't quite landed, but there were clear signs of full wakefulness in Emilia's reply now. Having confirmed as much, Subaru jerked his chin toward her, smiling wryly at Emilia's furrowed, troubled brow.

"I get that you've got fatigue built up, but if you're gonna sleep, you should sleep properly in a bed. When I find you sleeping on the floor like that, my heart nearly stops the second I walk in."

"...Ah, sorry. I worried you, didn't I?"

"I'd only just renewed my resolve, and then I seriously panicked thinking some new event had popped up to ruin everything. I'll go ahead and say it—Emilia-tan's sleeping face today hit me even harder than usual."

In truth, words could hardly do justice to the shock Subaru had felt upon entering the bedchamber and finding Emilia like that. He'd knocked, gotten no answer, and figured she might be out, so he'd let himself in—only to find Emilia collapsed on the floor in front of the bed, her silver hair spread out around her. No one could blame Subaru for the color draining from his face, for teetering on the edge of despair. Though the moment he'd scooped her up and felt the warmth of her body, her steady breathing, her heartbeat—all of that had turned out to be needless worry. Still—

"If that'd been all, I would've just let you keep sleeping like that... but you seemed to be having a really bad time of it. Sorry for waking you up?"

In Subaru's arms, the sleeping Emilia had beads of sweat on her forehead, twisting in obvious agony in her sleep. Subaru knew from experience—from a truly agonizing nightmare, there's no way to escape on your own. A call from outside the dream is the only way to break free quickly from that misery. Having exercised that very remedy, Subaru watched Emilia shake her head and say, "No,"—

"Thank you so-o much for waking me up. It was a little... no, a really bad dream, so... yeah, thank you."

"Man, what a cruel dream, tormenting Emilia-tan like that. Well, mind telling me a bit about what it—actually, never mind, looks like even remembering it feels awful."

"——"

Watching her fall silent and then give a strained smile, Subaru turned over in his mind what might have caused Emilia's nightmare. Probably it was some accumulation of negative circumstances that had given rise to the dream. Whether it had come with some concrete image attached, he couldn't say, but—

"...I see. Then I won't force it out of you."

From the way Emilia averted her eyes from him, clearly avoiding further questioning, he judged that the nightmare must have had a distinct, vivid shape to it. A vague, formless nightmare would be easy enough to brush off with words. That she couldn't do that was itself proof of just how clear its shape had been.

It had gotten off to an unlucky start with Emilia, but given how he needed to steer the conversation from here, that was difficult too. While Subaru, frowning, agonized over how to bring it up, touching the tip of his own nose, Emilia looked up at him.

"So... what did you come here for, Subaru? Don't tell me you really came just to look at my face for no reason?"

"I wouldn't call that such a surprising thing to do, honestly."

"No, it really would be. Because Subaru's always so busy, running around like crazy. You couldn't possibly spend that much time on just me, could you?"

"Just how diligent an image do you have of me in your head, Emilia-tan? I'm a man famous for being such a lazybones that I'd wholeheartedly support instituting a 'National Day of Gratitude for Laziness.'"

Not as a joke, not as exaggeration—Subaru knew perfectly well that his own nature was hopelessly lazy. Without some kind of role to fulfill, Natsuki Subaru was a man who would sink into endless indolence. That was precisely why, even back in his old world, he'd never neglected his daily muscle training or his cultivation of countless pointless hobbies and skills. Because he knew that the moment he let himself slack off, he'd become someone utterly hopeless. —Though a person who could keep up that kind of effort with no particular goal in mind could hardly ever be called lazy, Subaru remained oblivious to something so obvious.

As if she had something to say about Subaru's self-deprecation, the warmth in Emilia's gaze after hearing his answer grew a touch lukewarm. Subaru frowned slightly at that reaction, but in the end Emilia didn't pursue the matter any further, and instead said,

"Never mind. Just tell me why you came, Subaru. Come o-o-on."

"I don't get why you're being so childish about it all of a sudden, but... ah, right. I just thought I'd take a walk outside to clear my head a little—"

"——"

"—which, I guess, wouldn't really clear anything for you, huh."

Seeing Emilia fall silent, Subaru realized his own slip and scratched his head. As was clear from the very fact that her bedchamber had been placed apart from the rest of the settlement, all by itself, Emilia's treatment in the Sanctuary was far from good.

The Sanctuary's residents and Emilia should, in theory, have been fellow outcasts, both fallen away from their original peoples—but even among them, it seemed the bad image surrounding the existence of a Half-Elf carried special weight. Even to the evacuees from Arlam Village, the credit for having safely escaped the Witch Cult belonged to Subaru; it did nothing, in itself, to change their direct assessment of Emilia. Emilia's treatment in the Sanctuary was, in the end, no different from being treated like a sore that needed hiding, just as in the royal capital.

She managed to put on a brave face while she was with Subaru, but that hardly meant she found any of it pleasant. How was she coping, all alone, with the stares directed at her from those around her? As long as he hadn't yet managed to improve the situation, taking Emilia outside would do nothing but place an unnecessary burden on her.

"This is exactly why I..."

Furious at his own thoughtlessness, Subaru knocked a fist against his own forehead. Using the pain of knuckle against skull to reset his feelings, Subaru then turned back to face Emilia, who was staring at him wide-eyed at what he'd just done.

"Emilia."

"—Yeah."

Seeing the change in Subaru's expression, Emilia too seemed to sense the shift in the air. She straightened her posture, her violet eyes holding a quiet emotion as she looked back at him. Faced with that attitude and expression, Subaru understood there was no point in dancing around the subject. After a brief moment's hesitation over how to word his first line, he said—

"Do you feel up to telling me what you saw, in the Trials at the tomb?"

—Subaru saw it with his own eyes, quite clearly: those violet eyes welling up with fear and grief.
