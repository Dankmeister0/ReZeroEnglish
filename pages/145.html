Arc 3 Chapter 68: "The Sloth Capture Front"

Subaru felt an unpleasant sweat slowly trickling down his forehead.

How many times now had he pushed alone through a pitch-dark forest like this? If he counted the times he'd been chased by wolgarms, or that dusk chase with Rem, running through bare mountain dirt was almost something Subaru had grown used to.

That said, the circumstances whenever he did so were always dire, and he had no memory of ever walking an animal trail with a calm heart. The feel of grass underfoot in those still-fresh memories had of course been the worst kind of feeling, and nothing had changed about that now, even with the same goal in mind. No—now, with his hatred for the person he was about to meet mounting, that feeling might have tipped even further, further into the worse end of things.

He breathed in, he breathed out, trying to rein in his racing pulse, and the coldness of the sweat beading on his skin made him think of the malice waiting ahead, dampening his spirits.

He wanted to calm his heart. He wanted to be a man with a heart of steel. He wanted a spirit strong, sturdy, and unbending—one that no violent passion could shake and no malice could make quail. If he could do that, if he were like that, surely he could have avoided hurting so many people, and handled so many things so much better—or so he thought. Weak, fragile, utterly hopeless—why was someone like him standing in a place like this? Why did it have to be him?

Faintheartedness tried to crush Subaru's resolve. Against the calamity waiting ahead, his trembling knees were trying to hold even his body back. It doesn't have to be me, does it? That feeling was there. But—

"You told me it had to be me."

Right now, that was the only thing propping Subaru up. He steeled his trembling knees, scolded his crumbling heart, and kept his face turned forward. He wiped away his sweat, clenched his teeth before they could chatter, and found his destination was already right before him.

"――――"

The shadows surrounding Subaru bowed their heads reverently, as if showing him respect. Being treated as a superior like that had once filled Subaru with strong revulsion. But not anymore. Now, at the respect shown by these hateful beings, he smirked to himself in satisfaction. —After all, you're the ones who made it this way.

"This is actually the ninth time I've said this in this forest, but get lost. Go back to your nest and stay quiet, don't show your faces for a while. I've got important business up ahead with 'Sloth.'"

"――――"

At the wave of his arm and the words of a Subaru who seemed, somehow, to have shaken something off, the black-robed figures dissolved into the darkness. They were as inscrutable a bunch as ever. Compared to Petelgeuse, whose face and nature were known, they posed less of a threat—but their very facelessness might make them the more unsettling presence.

Leaving such musings behind, he pressed straight ahead down the path the vanished black robes had made. He leapt over slick-looking moss, stepped across writhing tree roots, and when his view suddenly opened up, what spread before him was a sheer, towering cliff blocking the way—a precipice. And standing right in front of it was the malice come to greet him.

"I have been awaiting you, yes—disciple graced with love—"

There stood Petelgeuse, arms spread wide, his face awash with the color of rapture, striking a pose of welcome. This was the third time now that Subaru had heard this same greeting. Unchanging as it was, it somehow conveyed all the more clearly that the words carried the bastard's true feelings.

"I am the Witch Cult's Sin Archbishop in charge of 'Sloth,' Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti... yes!"

I know, Subaru nearly spat out in irritation, and he only barely held himself back from interrupting the man's self-introduction partway through. Despite having resolved to face him calmly and solemnly, Petelgeuse's madness alone was enough to fray Subaru's nerves, rubbing them the wrong way as badly as anything could.

He breathed out, he breathed in, and Subaru reminded himself of the weight resting on his own two shoulders. Doing so brought back a measure of composure, and he loosened the corner of his mouth into a lazy grin.

"Thanks for the warm welcome. Having a Sin Archbishop come out to greet me personally—I'm honored... though I don't think I'm worth all that myself."

"There's no need for modesty, yes. The dense, overflowing grace pouring out of your very body—it isn't something you could hide, yes, and surely you're not unaware of it yourself? The affection clothing you already rivals that of a Sin Archbishop, yes!"

Waving his arm at Subaru's words, Petelgeuse stomped the ground as he delivered his impassioned speech. His eyes, lit with a mad gleam, blazed brightly, and with his tongue extended, his gaze literally seemed to lick its way over Subaru from head to toe.

"When I first sensed it, I thought, surely not, yes—but even so... could it be that you are 'Pride'?"

"Even if you call me 'Pride,' I don't really follow. Mind filling me in on some of the finer details? Like about the Sin Archbishops... or, say, about the Trials?"

This exchange—the word "Pride" popping up in the flow of conversation, Petelgeuse suspecting Subaru of being it—had happened several times over the course of the loops so far. Riding on that, Subaru cut in, trying to see if he could pry out some information about the "Trials" that Petelgeuse had brought up so many times. The Trials, to put it another way, were probably the very substance of the attack plan this time around. If that information came out, it would be easier to prepare countermeasures, and if asking about it set Petelgeuse off—well, that would just mean raising the flare to start the battle a little early.

In response to those words of Subaru's, which had taken no small measure of resolve,

"That's quite fine, yes. Word of the road's blockade will probably still take some time to spread. The same goes for the start of the 'Trial'—there is still, still plenty of time, yes."

"Huh... the road's blockade, is it."

Far from flying into a rage, Petelgeuse seemed instead to warm to Subaru's attitude, which could be read as cooperative, and he stepped toward Subaru, clapping his hands together happily. Feeling his back grow damp with cold sweat at the madman's approach, Subaru nonetheless kept up his air of composure, tilting his head.

"When you say the road's blockade, does that mean some kind of trick?"

"'Mist,' yes. I should think that alone explains it well enough, yes?"

"I see. That's plenty."

Subaru answered just as briefly to match the short reply. And from that exchange alone, he could confirm that Petelgeuse and the others, at the very least, hadn't yet gotten word of the White Whale's defeat. He'd already known the White Whale was a tool of the Witch Cult—in this case, of "Gluttony"—but how well they coordinated with each other had been an unknown, and now that part had become clear.

If they were operating under the assumption that the road had been sealed off by mist, then there was almost certainly no other Witch Cultist lying in wait along it. That meant that having Emilia and the others cut straight through the road, in order to make their escape, was a viable option.

"So you seal off the road with mist, clear out anyone who'd get in the way, and then it's the 'Trial,' huh. Pretty crafty way of going about it, Mr. Petelgeuse."

"The Trial is sacred and inviolable, yes! No matter what hardship stands in the way, if we do not sweep aside every obstacle and face it, it would be a betrayal of love, yes! Yes! To love! To the love directed at us! To the love bestowed upon us! We must answer it, yes!!"

"Ah, crap, I flipped his switch."

It seemed his careless choice of topic had flipped Petelgeuse's "love" switch. Arching his back, tilting his face upward, eyes bulging as he roared, Petelgeuse was beyond hearing anything Subaru said. He stretched his arms toward the heavens with single-minded devotion, as though something invisible existed there, and amid his madness, a single tear spilled down his cheek.

"Everything is for love—we die for love, yes! Upon the silver half-demon whose very existence is an outrage, I must test whether she can still walk beneath the weight of her sinfulness! Whether she is truly worthy must be tested, yes! Whether she can be diligent, and not slothful! That path—I, with these hands, with this body, with this love—yes!!"

"Test whether she's worthy...?"

"Yes! That is what the Trial is for, yes! Therefore she must be tested, she must be tried! Whether she is a body fit to take in a Witch Factor and become a Vessel—!"

Reaching into his robes, Petelgeuse pulled out a book bound in black. The madman turned its pages lovingly with his fingers, touching the text that packed every inch of it as though caressing each word in turn.

"My duty, as written in the Gospel—the proof of a love that must be carried out! If you truly are 'Pride,' yes, then you should understand this swelling passion of mine, yes! For all of us Sin Archbishops to be gathered at once—why, that alone hasn't happened in four hundred years, yes!"

"Wait! Witch Factors, 'Pride'—we're still not on the same page here..."

"The Gospel! I demand you present your Gospel, yes! I want to burn the love bestowed upon you into these very eyes of mine, yes! Yes, yes-yes-yes-yes-yes-yes-yes-yes-YEEEEEEEEEES!!"

Petelgeuse closed in, thrusting his face so near that his breath brushed Subaru's skin. A rank stench of breath reached him, and feeling that extended tongue was about to touch the tip of his nose, Subaru, in a reflexive burst of disgust, hopped back a small step. As if that reaction of Subaru's were somehow unexpected and unwelcome, Petelgeuse tracked him with his eyes, tilting his head ninety degrees into a twisted posture, and said,

"Present... the Gospel—"

Hearing those words said in a quiet voice, with mad eyes, Subaru judged that this was as far as it would go. There were still countless things he wanted to ask, and what he'd managed to draw out so far could hardly be called well-organized. But there was no pushing this any further, not here, not now. So—

"Alright, hold on."

He slipped a hand into his own robe, answering Petelgeuse's demand. He wasn't about to pull out a Gospel, of course, but against the madman demanding he present proof of where he belonged, he had an answer to show just who Subaru really was.

"This—is my answer!"

He shouted, thrusting the arm he'd drawn straight up overhead—and an instant later, a glowing Lagmite Ore let loose a white light bright enough to be seen from anywhere in the forest. Petelgeuse, his eyes seared by the light, threw up an arm to shield them—but he must have instantly judged Subaru to be an enemy.

"To bear such grace within your body, and yet... what blasphemy——!!"

Petelgeuse glared at Subaru with a face twisted in fury, and his shadow burst outward, rearing up into pitch-black, murderous hands of magic. The magic hands surged straight for Subaru's throat; ducking low, he leapt sideways, slipping past their grasping fingers, and—

"Wilhelm! Twins!!"

At Subaru's call, out from the forest sprang Mimi and Tivey, who had been trailing behind Subaru all this while with their presence concealed. The siblings kicked off trees at the same moment, taking to the air, and spinning as they sailed clean over Petelgeuse's small frame to land before the sheer cliff face—there, they opened their mouths, and a roar thundered out.

"Whooo—!" "Haaaaah——!!"

A resonance wave tore up the ground, and the rock wall, swallowed by the wave of vibration, collapsed with a violent roar. The second live burial had gone off without a hitch, and Petelgeuse blinked at the sheer destruction of it. And then, from his wide-open, defenseless back—

"To turn your back on an enemy——!"

Wilhelm tore through, low to the ground, as if crawling across the earth itself. At the speed of the old swordsman turned wind, Petelgeuse noticed his approach and turned—but it was far, far too slow.

Before his shadow could even direct its palm at the sword demon closing in, the blade he'd raised high came down far faster. With a dull sound, the strike sheared off Petelgeuse's outstretched right arm at the middle of the upper arm. While time seemed to freeze at the vividness of the wound, the blade alone did not stop moving, flashing white as it flipped over—and the flat of the sword struck the back of Petelgeuse's head with a solid blow.

"—Guh!"

At the sharp blow, Petelgeuse's eyes instantly lost their focus, and he crumpled to his knees on the spot without a shred of resistance. In the same instant, blood gushed in a torrent from the wound where his right arm had been severed.

Subaru stared blankly for a moment as the ground soaked through crimson, then hurried over to Petelgeuse. He pulled a cloth from his robe, thinking he couldn't afford to let the fallen madman bleed out, and moved to stop the bleeding—but,

"Master Subaru! It's still dangerous!"

"Dangerous, but—guh?!"

Wilhelm's sharp voice stopped Subaru cold, and as he turned to ask what he meant, Subaru's face filled with shock at the sight of Petelgeuse springing upright right before his eyes. The madman swayed his upper body wildly, his eyes still rolled back to white, foam trickling from the corner of his mouth, and yet he still managed to turn his head, saying,

"The response... from my fingertips... has cut off... what did you... do, yes?!"

Even as fatal blood loss poured from him, Petelgeuse paid no mind to the fact that it was cutting his own remaining time short, and stuck out his blood-soaked tongue in a display of madness. He'd probably staved off losing consciousness through the pain of nearly biting through his own tongue. It was a method possible only because of a mindset that didn't shrink from self-harm—but right now, it was an extremely bad sign.

"Wilhelm! We can't let him die—knock him out and tie him up!"

"Subdue without killing—not exactly my specialty, but as you wish!"

The sword demon, answering the call, shook his body up and down and leapt forward with a bizarre, uncanny motion. Petelgeuse, his face still unfocused, turned it toward Wilhelm and stretched his swelling, shadowy magic hands toward the old swordsman. But—

"It's scattered—must be because of the damage to his head!"

The number of extending arms kept climbing—thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, one after another—but the crucial thing, their movement toward Wilhelm, lacked any coordination. What arose instead was aimless swaying, trembling, rippling motion, and it couldn't catch up to Wilhelm, who ran like the wind without even needing to dodge. And above all, Wilhelm's bewildering footwork wouldn't allow it to.

"Seriously?!"

Wilhelm's form, swaying up and down, blurred faintly, and in the next instant, the old swordsman's figure existed simultaneously both high and low. One half kicked off the ground to come from above, the other lowered its body to sweep at the legs below—a body-doubling technique whose sheer feat sent even Petelgeuse sputtering foam, as he said,

"For a pitiful fool who knows nothing of grace, to dare——!"

"I know nothing of the love you speak of. This body of mine has its hands full loving just one woman, and that is more than enough——!"

A blade-intent that outstripped even his bold retort crashed down, and Petelgeuse, his face gone pale, swung his invisible hands. Whether rage had simply overridden the damage, they caught up to both of Wilhelm's split images, high and low, without a moment's hesitation, their fingers coiling around the old man's frame in a raging fury to tear him apart.

"Got you—yes!"

"I'm afraid not."

But Petelgeuse's triumphant grin flipped in an instant, as the image beyond his gripping fingers vanished. The figures of Wilhelm that should have been attacking from both above and below vanished—and instead, neither high nor low, the image of the old swordsman running straight across the ground came together, right before the madman's eyes.

"Impossible...!"

"An application of killing intent, blade-intent, and footwork—did you enjoy the finer art of swordsmanship, hm!"

Twisting his body, the flat of his roaring blade struck Petelgeuse's left temple dead on. A cracking sound of steel striking flesh rang out, and blood spurted for just an instant from his split forehead. But even after taking that blow, Petelgeuse still didn't lose consciousness, moving his neck while still feeling the cold touch of the blade against his left cheek, and said,

"The keenness of your blade is tremendous... You truly seem a diligent one, yes... bleh?!"

"My apologies, but—"

Petelgeuse had tried to stretch his tongue toward the blade still resting against his cheek. But the opposite side of his face had already been struck by the sheath of Wilhelm's treasured sword, drawn from his hip.

"This happens to be borrowed from my lord. —I can't very well return it soiled with a lowlife's spit."

"Y-Yes... yesss...!"

With a swing of both hands, he slid the treasured sword back into its sheath with a light click. The moment he returned it to his hip in one smooth motion, Wilhelm turned a faint smile toward Subaru, who was watching in stunned silence, and bowed at the waist.

"As instructed, the Sin Archbishop—has been taken alive."

The instant the old swordsman transformed back into an old gentleman, the unconscious madman finally, truly collapsed to the ground.

"He's really out cold, right? For sure? Safe to get close? No, wait, if he dies that's a problem too, so I've gotta patch him up quick..."

"Being scared like that is kinda lame, mister. This guy dying wouldn't really matter, would it?"

"You were told it'd cause trouble if he died, weren't you, sis. Anyway, I've at least tried to stop the bleeding."

Around the fallen, unmoving Petelgeuse stood a nervously wary Subaru and Mimi, who pointed at him and laughed. Leaving those two useless ones to their own devices, Tivey briskly wound cloth around Petelgeuse's arm, treating the wound to close it up. Meanwhile, Wilhelm had been surveying the collapsed cave, and when he returned, the old swordsman nodded magnanimously at Subaru, who prompted him with his eyes for the result, saying,

"From what I could tell, it's completely buried. Since it doesn't seem the cave had another exit, I think we can safely assume the Witch Cultists inside have been wiped out."

"I see... Guess that's not really news to pump your fist over, though."

"Why not?"

Enemies or not, this was a situation involving people's lives and deaths. Subaru hadn't yet been dyed so thoroughly by this other world's common sense that he could throw both hands up and welcome that outright. That seemed to strike Mimi as strange, and with her adorable features paired with a coldly practical view of life and death, she said,

"There's no end to it if you go thinking hard stuff every time you beat an enemy. You beat the enemy, you save your friends. You get paid, you sleep in your futon. I think it's better to just do that than sit around thinking hard stuff."

"Simple, and good for it... though I guess that just means I'm too soft. I'm not gonna argue the point, though. More importantly..."

Sending his gaze toward the forest, Subaru thought of the others, who should have moved into action at the signal. Since this was tracing almost the same course as last time, the surprise attacks should have succeeded for every team. Still, this time there were two more hideouts to attack than last time. On top of that, since he'd had several people head to the village ahead of everyone else, forces at each site were reduced by that much. He'd taken pains to assign Ricardo to a different position from this one, so as not to weaken the strength of whichever team had seemingly struggled last time—he wanted to believe no mistakes had happened.

"Besides, the crucial thing is we've got Petelgeuse pinned down over here."

He flipped over Petelgeuse, drained of blood and taking shallow breaths, with his foot. His limp, strengthless body, combined with his thinness, looked frail—without the madness he showed while conscious, he could easily have passed for an invalid. His black vestments were now soaked through with his own blood, making him look all the closer to a corpse.

"So, what will you do with him? Post a watch and leave him behind?"

"That's rough too. There's no guarantee he won't wake up and escape with 'Unseen Hands,' and if we let him out of reach we might not be able to handle it. ...Guess there's nothing for it but to drag him along, for now."

"'Unseen Hands'... is it. It seems crude to remark on the Witch Cult's strangeness at this point, but a technique distinct from both magic and Spiritual Arts is unsettling indeed."

"Yeah, and it's got a different feel from curses and stuff too. Measuring it against my own common sense, I'd say it's closer to something like psychic powers. Unseen Hands, if you want to put it that way, you could call it some kind of variant of psychokinesis, I guess."

In accordance with the caster's will, hands invisible to others carried out the act. That was, indeed, exactly the sort of thing that psychokinesis would fit, if he applied the modern knowledge of his original world. Though the fact that even Subaru could see it probably meant it belonged to a different broad category altogether.

"Anyway, there's no way to guard against it. Even tying up both his hands probably wouldn't do anything against 'Unseen Hands.' Speaking of which, how do you restrain a mage and people like that?"

"If the opponent is a mage, all you need do is gag them so they can't chant. If it's a Spiritual Arts user you're up against, though, that becomes tricky, since the spirit itself can handle mana."

"Ah, gotcha. Yeah, Puck definitely doesn't seem the type to sit quiet if he heard Emilia-tan had been captured."

After having seen that true form of his, his impression of that small gray-furred cat was quite different now. Whatever Puck's usual behavior might be, his heart was tilted entirely toward Emilia. Even in the situation just described, blowing away anything that wasn't Emilia would surely be no trouble for him at all. Whether he could exchange words with that easygoing Puck again, staying calm all the while, Subaru had no confidence in, at present. He didn't want to think that anything had changed, but...

"So, what've you been up to while I've been off in my own head, little one?"

"Not 'little one'—it's Tivey. Yes, I've just been checking his belongings, a bit."

Rummaging about, Tivey searched through Petelgeuse's robes now that the treatment was finished. The inside of the black vestments seemed to have surprisingly deep pockets, and as Tivey searched, one item after another that had been tucked away inside came out.

"Travel rations, and Lagmite Ore. Oh, and he's got a wallet too, huh."

"Surprisingly ordinary inventory for the guy, huh. And also, that's not very polite, you know, mercenary or not."

"I like to think of myself as the brains of the band, but at the root of it, I'm still a mercenary too. Taking spoils off a loser is just... hm, what's this?"

Voicing yet another grim dose of reality, Tivey tilted his head at the black-bound book he now held. Subaru recognized it at once, and let out an involuntary "Ah," saying,

"That's the book that Petelgeuse guy called the 'Gospel.'"

"—! This is the Gospel?! Whoa, I touched it!"

Tivey reacted with instant rejection at Subaru's words. He let go of the Gospel he'd been holding as though it had suddenly burned him, threw it to the ground, kicked sand over it, and backed away. The sight of him was, quite literally, like a flustered kitten.

"Treat books with a little more care... as someone who loves books, that stings a bit. Even if it's mostly light novels I read."

Recalling the countless stories he'd devoured over the years, Subaru picked up the fallen book and brushed off the dust. But Tivey shook his head at him in quick little motions, saying,

"Y-you'd better let go of it right away, I think. A Gospel is like proof of initiation that reaches the hands of a Witch Cultist. Keep holding it, and your head might go screwy!"

"I don't think my mental damage resistance has dropped so low these past few days that a single book could drive me crazy. ...I can't read what's inside, though."

He flipped through it, glancing at the contents, but going by the characters written there, it was some mystery language that matched neither the I-script, Ro-script, nor Ha-script he'd been studying. It looked, if anything, a bit like hiragana written in an overly elegant hand—but it was too elegant to read, so grasping the content would be difficult. In any case,

"I'll go ahead and hold onto it, at least. Just because I can't read it doesn't mean no one else can."

It was, after all, a Sin Archbishop's possession. Perhaps this so-called Gospel might yield some clue toward understanding their true nature. It was with that hope in mind that he decided to keep it, but Tivey's eyes, watching him, still couldn't hide their fear.

"You don't have to be so scared. Besides, for someone spooked by a book, you sure searched through his robes pretty eagerly."

"Between a cursed person and a book that might curse you, the latter is scarier. A cursed person's no big deal to beat, as long as it's me and sis, anyway."

"Ohh, that's right! It's a piece of cake for Mimi and Tivey!"

As ever, not really engaging with the substance of the conversation, the sister agreed with her brother's words through sheer attitude. Feeling almost charmed by the exchange, Subaru, still cradling the Gospel, looked down at Petelgeuse lying on the ground, and said,

"So, was there anything else worth noting on him? If he had a map, maybe there'd be a rendezvous point for his underlings, or if we're lucky, even a Witch Cult meeting place."

"No belongings like that to be found. Aside from that Gospel, his getup looks like he just walked out with only the clothes on his back."

In the sense of traveling light, Petelgeuse's outfit was indeed something worth wondering about. But no answer was going to appear just from cocking his head over it. Judging there was nothing more to be gained here, Subaru turned around and said,

"For now, let's bind him up tight and keep watch to make sure he doesn't wake. Every time he comes to, we knock him out again with a hit. It's brutal work, but Wilhelm, can I count on you?"

"I too am called a sword demon—I'll take it on. After this, evacuation, I take it?"

As Wilhelm bound Petelgeuse up with rope and hoisted his small frame onto his shoulder while asking the question, Subaru nodded in confirmation, saying, "That's right."

"Mopping up the Witch Cult can wait—getting Emilia-tan and everyone else evacuated comes first. Once we've gotten everyone away from 'Sloth,' we'll decide what to do with this guy."

"Dropping him, unconscious, from the Great Waterfall out into the outside world is one option. Or sealing him in a stone, the same as the witch those people worship, might be another."

"I want to hear the details, but let's save it for later. It's a good thing to have options to choose from."

The fine details of what to do with Petelgeuse would be worked out later. The chance of him escaping, and the chance of suicide, Wilhelm would snuff out with his watchful eye. All that remained was for the other camps' attacks to have gone through, and for them not to bungle the finish.

"Alright. Then, let's finally, after all this time... go see my goddess."

In a sense, this required even more nerve than facing off against Petelgeuse. With his chest burning at that resolve, Subaru and the others advanced toward the village, aiming to reunite with the rest.

Passing through the trees and merging onto the road along the highway, Patrasche and the others, who'd been left waiting, came out to greet Subaru and the group. The plan had been for each team to mount the Earth Dragons left behind here, in order of whichever succeeded in their attack first, and rendezvous at the village, so by the time Subaru's group arrived, only a single team remained outstanding. There was some anxiety about that, but once he realized the remaining team was Ricardo's, which included Liger, that concern was swept away too.

"Anyway, I think we're past the worst of it. Your revenge from last time is settled too. Feel free to praise me, Patrasche."

Last time, she'd met her end with her thick neck wrenched off, though Patrasche of course knew nothing of that; as Subaru told her, sounding like he expected gratitude, the Earth Dragon, seeming to indulge her rider's meaning out of a kind of resignation, rubbed her snout against him.

"Whoa, hey, so you've actually got a cute side after— ow, hold on, ow, your skin, your scales are all rough and it hurts, hurts, hurts, hey, c'mon, cut it out...!"

Pushed over by the force of it and knocked flat on the ground, Subaru had Patrasche relentlessly rubbing her body against him even so. As a result, he was rolled across the dirt again and again with no way to resist, and Natsuki Subaru's one good outfit ended up caked in dirt and in tatters. He got up resentfully and glared at Patrasche, but the noble Earth Dragon feigned ignorance in the face of her grimy rider.

"There were days I thought our hearts had connected, and this is how you treat me? Just you remember this, you overgrown lizard! One of these days, I'll ride you so well I'll have you crying uncle!"

"I've been meaning to mention this for a while now, but the Earth Dragon you ride, Master Subaru, is female."

"You were a girl?!"

Subaru was stunned at this unexpected revelation of his comrade-in-arms's sex. Once it came to that, all the skinship from just now started to take on a different meaning. If this were a light novel, he might even wonder if a humanization arc was waiting up ahead—

"Sorry, but my hands are already full with a heroine, no can do! I appreciate the feelings, but even if you got humanized I couldn't handle— bwuh!"

A sideways swing of her tail caught him nicely in the side, and Subaru writhed, the wind knocked out of him. Patrasche looked down on that scene with the same cool, unchanged eyes as ever. It seemed there was, after all, only an affection parameter between Subaru and her, nothing more.

"J-just... keep being good to me, from here on out."

As if to say that was how it should be, Patrasche lowered her back and neatly took the unsteady Subaru onto it, and the whole group set off together toward the village. Along the way, there was a moment where Wilhelm knocked the stirring Petelgeuse back into unconsciousness with a blow gruesome enough to make one look away, and amid all that—

"The village is coming into view."

"—It seems the others have already arrived as well."

Just as the village's shape began to flicker into view from a distance, Wilhelm narrowed his eyes and reported that much to Subaru. As ever, no matter how hard Subaru squinted, he could only make out the village's shape as a hazy blur—just how different could their bodies possibly be built? In any case, hearing that the comrades who'd been leading the waiting Earth Dragons had arrived safely, Subaru let out a relieved breath, easing his shoulders.

This time there was no worry of Petelgeuse having gotten to the village ahead of them, and they had his body in custody. If they could safely evacuate Emilia and the others from the village, they'd be able to face the Witch Cult with nothing left to worry about behind them—

"...Something like the smell of trouble."

"Eh, no way. Why? We were doing pretty well this time, weren't we?"

But leaving Subaru's sense of relief behind, Wilhelm's face clouded with something sour. It seemed unlikely he'd tell a pointless lie here, so Subaru urged Patrasche onward and dashed straight into the village grounds all at once. And there, what Subaru saw was—

"I humbly beg of you, on bended knee. —My name is Julius Juukulius. One of the Royal Guard Knights, belonging to the Royal Guard of the royal capital of Lugunica!"

Kneeling, bathed in the gazes of the villagers and merchants surrounding him, was the fine figure of a man declaring his name aloud in a ringing voice—Julius.
