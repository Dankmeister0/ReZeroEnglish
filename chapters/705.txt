Arc 9 Chapter 10: "The Limit is Three Days"

——The Great Library, Pleiades.

That was the tower's formal name, spoken by Shaula the first time she'd welcomed Subaru and the others here.
Shaula didn't seem like the type to coin such a scholarly-sounding term on her own, so presumably she'd learned it from the "Master" who'd charged her with the tower's upkeep.
And that word, that necessary explanation, Shaula had faithfully carried in her memory for four hundred years, finally getting the chance to trot it out at last.
And now——

"——The stacks of the Great Library, Pleiades, that hold the 'Book of the Dead.' This is Taygeta."

So said Ezzo, arms spread wide, and behind him stretched row upon row of bookshelves. The shelves ringed the staircase leading up from the fourth layer below, arranged in circles that repeated column after column, dozens deep.
Even accounting for how large the Pleiades Watchtower's circumference actually was, the space being used here was more than it should have been able to hold — some kind of spatial expansion must have been worked upon it.

"It's not beyond the power of shade magic, I suppose. Though, weaving a spell structure that holds for centuries without a caster to maintain it — that's a feat beyond human skill, I suppose."

Beatrice answered Subaru's unspoken question with a solemn face.
As a great spirit of the shade attribute, it was only natural that she'd sense the sophisticated magic laid upon the tower and feel unsettled by it.
In any case——

"——"

Garfiel and Petra, visiting the stacks for the first time, stood wide-eyed and speechless, stunned by the sheer number of shelves — no, by the sheer number of books held within them, each one a "Book of the Dead."
And no wonder. When Subaru had first learned the truth of the books housed in Taygeta, even understanding it in theory, he'd been struck dumb sensing the vast number of the dead it represented.

"Are all of these... books of people who died?"

Petra let the words slip out in a small, stunned voice.
Her eyes wandered faintly, her reaction one of blank astonishment, and Subaru found that only natural. Lately, Petra had grown considerably steadier of nerve, showing a composure that put adults to shame in the face of nearly anything. Even when caught in the "sand hour" that had tormented so many who'd challenged the Augria Sand Dunes, she'd dealt with the sandstorm briskly and efficiently.

Yet even Petra couldn't stay unshaken by the strangeness that pervaded Taygeta.

"——!"

The same was true of Garfiel, who gave a small click of his throat as his eyes darted around the stacks.
If anything, being one of the two — alongside Emilia — most likely in their camp to take things at face value, he seemed less struck by the eerie solemnity of the "Book of the Dead" itself than simply overwhelmed by the sheer volume of the collection.
It probably didn't help that, just before this, in the Vollachian Empire, he'd witnessed an army of the undead — the mass resurrection and second death of countless souls — which likely sharpened that feeling all the more.

Petra's and Garfiel's reactions were a concern to Subaru. But what worried him most was the person who, like the other two, was visiting here for the first time, and who had a purpose of his own——

"——"

Al let out a small breath, turning his head slowly to survey the stacks.
His astonishment was more subdued than Petra's, and the sweep of his gaze around the stacks was slower than Garfiel's — but precisely because of that, there was a tension to it.
It was enough that Subaru's mouth went dry, hesitation rising, as he tried to think of something to say.
But in that moment of hesitation——

"——Can't read it."

Al, having wandered up to one of the shelves without much apparent thought, pulled out a book with a natural motion and, propping it deftly against his elbow, flipped it open.
The gesture was so utterly unhesitant, so natural, that Subaru's own reaction — a startled "Wait—!" — came a beat too late.

"W-wait wait wait wait! Don't just dive right in! You'll give me a heart attack!"

"Hm... ah, sorry. Just, when it was right there in front of me, I couldn't help it."

"Isn't diving straight into something unknown the scarier option!?"

Saying this, Subaru hurriedly snatched the book from Al's hands.
He was thoroughly startled by the man's snap decision, but at the same time it drove home once more just how precarious Al's state was. Throughout their journey here, he hadn't shown it much in his manner, but it was probably a measure of how strong his craving for the book was.
Subaru wanted to be understanding of that. But at the same time, he couldn't just let the recklessness slide.

"I told you properly, didn't I? The books here — they hit you way harder than you're picturing. I nearly turned into Meili myself."

"Whaaat kind of joke is that!? Don't say scary things like that!"

Meili, hearing her name invoked, raised a voice of protest — but this was no joke on Subaru's part; it was simply a side effect of the terrifying sense of reality the "Book of the Dead" carried.
On a previous loop, in the one where Meili had lost her life — back then, Subaru had read Meili's "Book of the Dead," placing a tremendous strain on his mind, immersing himself so deeply in her life that he'd ended up carrying a Meili who didn't exist within himself.
Honestly, the fondness and affection Subaru felt for Meili — enough to bewilder her — probably wasn't entirely free of the "Book of the Dead"'s influence. If people come to feel closeness toward another through empathy and understanding, then the "Book of the Dead" was the ultimate matchmaker.

Of course, under normal circumstances, there was never any chance of meeting the dead again while they lived — so that sort of turn of events could only ever happen to Subaru.

"So handle it with care. You could end up not being yourself anymore."

"Not being myself, huh. ...In a way, maybe that's what I want."

"Al..."

"Kidding, kidding. A crappy joke that isn't even funny."

At the raw pain in Al's somewhat self-destructive words, Subaru's expression stiffened.
Hoping it might serve as a turning point for him to pull himself back together, Subaru had granted Al's request and brought him to the tower. But now his doubts deepened — was this not drastic treatment, but instead just deepening the wound?

"Just as Master Natsuki cautioned a moment ago. Let us consider Master Al's careless conduct already reprimanded — but I ask that Master Garfiel and Miss Petra also exercise due caution. Careless exposure to a book's influence could leave a wound on the heart that never heals."

Behind Subaru and Al's exchange, Ezzo issued that warning to the first-time visitors.
Ezzo's words carried a nuance that only someone who'd tasted the feedback of the "Book of the Dead" — the same as Subaru — could impart.
Unmistakably, he too, like Subaru and Julius, was one who had touched the "Book of the Dead"——

"It's not given much weight, but wounds of the heart are troublesome things. There's no prospect of recovery through healing magic, and because they're invisible from the outside, they're often dismissed too lightly. Even setting that aside, this land is heavily under the influence of miasma, which tends to weigh on one's spirits. If anything happens, come to me — your elder — right away."

"Th-thank you, Mr. Ezzo..."

"An admirable attitude for our most senior member, Master Ezzo. Befitting someone with the depth of experience of rolling his eyes back, foaming at the mouth, and wetting himself the first time he read a 'Book of the Dead.'"

"Miss Flam!? That was supposed to stay unspoken!?"

"He was in the stacks, trembling and convulsing. I rolled my eyes back myself, just watching."

Ezzo, who had been giving his own chest a confident thump, now had his eyes bulging as his supposed ally Flam exposed his disgrace. At this account of Ezzo's experience, Petra's expression — which had briefly held a look of respect — began to cloud over with a "Mr. Ezzo...?"
At the girl's gaze, Ezzo flailed his hands about in a fluster.

"Ah, no, that's not it! Well — it's not not true, but Miss Flam's account makes it sound far more intimidating than it should be! It's true that in the very earliest days I couldn't bear it and blacked out in that sorry state, but once I learned the trick to reading them, I've shown no such disgrace since!"

"The trick to readin' 'em? There's somethin' like that?"

"There is! Put simply: empty the mind. Close the heart. If you can only place yourself in that state of mind, you can perceive the information flowing in as mere information, nothing more."

"You say it as though it's simple, but no matter how many times I hear it, I don't understand it."

Shaken by Flam's slow head-shaking, Ezzo looked to the others as if seeking agreement. But Petra, Garfiel, Meili — of course — and Subaru and Beatrice too, didn't understand either.
At the lack of a favorable response, Ezzo looked aghast, muttering, "Impossible...!"

Setting aside Ezzo's shock, if he really had succeeded in protecting his mind and reading the "Book of the Dead" by the method he'd described, it would be a remarkable feat.
Subaru, having actually read a "Book of the Dead" himself, knew just how outrageous that was. Honestly, even hearing the trick now, Subaru couldn't imagine himself managing the same thing.

"See, this is why. Careless handling could lead to something irreversible. So, um, touching anything besides the book you're after..."

"Better not to. ...Got it. I'll take the advice to heart. Even setting that aside, it's thanks to you brothers that I made it to the tower without any trouble. I won't grumble about it."

"...Well, all right then."

As he spoke, Subaru returned the book he'd taken from Al to the shelf. The name on the spine belonged to someone he didn't know, but a sense like touching a gravestone made him handle it with care.
Yes, the "Book of the Dead" might be something close to a headstone, a grave marker — that sort of feeling. That was probably why any commotion or roughness here came with a strange sense of resistance.
In any case——

"Finding the book you're after out of such an immense number will be quite the task. Best to settle in for the long haul. — After all, this collection may well be adding more books this very moment."

That remark, tacked on by Ezzo, spoke to the harshness of this journey through a sea of books — a journey of a different flavor than crossing the sand sea, and one with no surefire strategy for victory.

"——I am Volcanica. By the ancient covenant, I ask the resolve of those who would reach the summit."

A voice grand, solemn, the kind that shook the very soul of the listener — the voice of a being on an entirely different plane of existence.
Against the backdrop of a deep blue sky, the majestic dragon that had made the very top of the impossibly tall tower its perch — the "Divine Dragon" in all its dread majesty stood before him, and Garfiel could only stand there, stunned.

This wasn't the first time he'd stood before a dragon.
Far from it — in the recent "Great Disaster" in the Vollachian Empire, entrusted by Subaru and the others, Garfiel had gone head-to-head with the "Cloud Dragon" Mezoreia and traded blows with it.
Given the nature of that fight, he didn't consider it a victory. But he still firmly held onto the fact — and the pride — that he had never once been beaten.

Dragons were synonymous with the strongest living beings in this world, reigning at the top of a ranking that encompassed every kind of creature — humans, demi-humans, witchbeasts, spirits, everything.
Having, in however rough a fashion, traded blows with one corner of that summit, Garfiel had come away with the sense that he'd broken through a shell of his own.
And yet——

"——You who have reached the tower's summit. You who tread the first tier. All-powerful petitioner."

Calmly resting its wings at the tower's peak, the pale, glowing "Divine Dragon" Volcanica. Instinct understood at once that this was a dragon on a level distinctly different even from the "Cloud Dragon" Mezoreia.
Nothing about its appearance made it obvious, but its seniority as a "dragon" was as different from Mezoreia's as an adult's is from a child's.

"...Last time we just crossed paths, so I only heard about it secondhand, but wow, this really is something else."

Looking up at the dragon in the same way, Subaru gave that admiring remark from behind Garfiel.
Last time at the Pleiades Watchtower, Subaru had been flung off to the Empire before he'd even had a chance to encounter Volcanica. Even though he'd had the opportunity to see the "Cloud Dragon" in the Empire, it seemed his sense of awe standing before the "Divine Dragon" Volcanica now was the same as Garfiel's.
And Emilia, apparently, had actually fought a battle against this very Volcanica.

"Lady Emilia, that was way too reckless of her..."

"Really, even Betty had no peace of mind over it, I suppose. Back then it wasn't just Emilia — Subaru, Ram, Anastasia and the others too, everyone had a rough time..."

"Yeah well, that time I put Bea through the wringer too with the worrying, huh. Thanks for that."

Recalling that time, Beatrice patted the head of a solemn-faced Subaru — though as the story went, this wasn't the scale of resolution that such a heartwarming picture might suggest.
Back then too, it seemed, there had been a huge uproar — Subaru losing his memories, Meili betraying them, clashes with the "Sage" and "Gluttony."

"If I'd been there, big bro wouldn't've had to get flung all the way to the Empire..."

"Hm, is that something you should be saying? For Garfiel, that means being able to handle problems even Betty and Emilia and the others couldn't, doesn't it, I suppose?"

"Uh, no, that ain't what I— that ain't what I meant."

"That was Betty's pretty little joke, I suppose. I understood just fine."

Flustered at the thought he'd said something careless, Garfiel was met with Beatrice's laughter. As Garfiel breathed a sigh of relief, Subaru added, "Besides—"

"You put everyone through the wringer with worry. I'm not gonna say it was good that I got sent flying, but... when I think that if we hadn't been there, the Empire might've fallen, well."

"You could call that a happy accident, I suppose. Well, thinking about how Al must feel about it, I suppose."

"It ain't just him. Schult, and the old man too, same thing."

Scratching his cheek with a finger, Subaru said this, and Beatrice and Garfiel followed suit.
In the back of Garfiel's mind rose the image of the camp, fractured by Priscilla's disappearance.
Heinkel gone, Al chasing after the "Book of the Dead." Even Schult, the most forward-looking of them, was falling apart just trying desperately to hold his heart together. Leaving it all to Roswaal alone would've been not just worrying but downright cruel, so it was a relief that Frederica was staying by his side.
If only he could help each of them, one by one, find a way to look forward again——

"Sorry for draggin' you along, big bro. Wanted to get a look at the 'Divine Dragon' myself, just once."

"I was curious too, it's not something you need to apologize for. When you find out a celebrity's staying on the floor above you at a hotel on a trip, getting curious is just fan psychology... no wait, going to meet them like that is bad manners, isn't it? Is this, like, overstepping our bounds here?"

"No reason for anyone to scold you for it, I suppose. If anyone has the right to object, it'd be the dragon itself. But—"

"——I am Volcanica. By the ancient covenant, I ask the resolve of those who would reach the summit."

"Just like that, I suppose."

Hearing Volcanica's reply, Beatrice shrugged at its vagueness.
Garfiel too had been half doubtful, but having actually met it, it was true that Volcanica's long, long lifespan had made the world hazy to it. Even so, the sheer, extraordinary presence it carried was proof enough of why it stood apart as an unparalleled "dragon."
In any case——

"Guess there's always someone stronger up top. Gotta make sure I don't get it twisted."

"...You're strong, Garfiel. Out of everyone I know, you're closer to the top of the list than the bottom."

"And makin' sure I carry that all the way to the very top — that's my job, ain't it?"

Checking his own footing, and to show he wasn't sulking, Garfiel jabbed a fist into Subaru's chest. Subaru gave a light "ugh" at the impact, then nodded back with tears in his eyes.
And as they talked like this——

"——Ah, there you are, at the 'Divine Dragon's' side after all."

Ezzo joined them then, coming up the stairs.
Ezzo looked at Garfiel and Subaru's group, and beyond them at Volcanica.

"I understand the appeal. The mysteries of this tower alone stir the探究心 — the spirit of inquiry — greatly, but the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica, an existence indispensable to the founding of the present Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, is likewise an object of fascination!"

"Ha, might not be quite the same feeling as yours, but I've got a thing for dragons too. Volcanica's a bit too huge, but something like a dragon rider — that's boyhood romance right there."

"Dragon rider...!? Big bro, tell me more about that later, in detail."

"Sure. Though, I mean, it was more like the Empire's 'Flying Dragon Riders' I had in mind."

At the word Subaru let slip with an approving nod, something in Garfiel's gut caught. He sensed, instinctively, that it was something really, thrillingly cool.
Watching Garfiel and the others go back and forth, Ezzo touched his chin and tilted his head.

"I see. I'd hoped we might discuss the significance of the 'Divine Dragon's' existence from a more historical or scholarly angle, but I'll look forward to that another time. — Now then, Master Natsuki, as requested, I've finished explaining the stacks to Master Al. Miss Flam is keeping him company for the moment."

"Thanks, that's a huge help. I still haven't had a proper talk with Flam — is that okay?"

"No need to worry on that account. She's the sort who says plainly she dislikes something if she dislikes it. It's slightly concerning, as a servant, how often that occasion arises, but..."

"I'm used to maids who say they hate what they hate, I suppose."

"Ah, yeah. True enough."

At Beatrice's remark, Garfiel and Subaru exchanged a glance and nodded.
The object of Garfiel's affections, for all her haughtiness and strong self-regard, was indeed a woman who — setting aside her position as a maid — spoke her mind plainly. He loved that about her.
Thinking about it that way, maybe pink-haired women in general tended to be strong-willed — Flam included.

"Incidentally, Miss Flam has a twin younger sister named Grassis, and I must say it's troublesome how alike they are — not just in appearance, but in temperament as well."

"Felt's household's got quite the variety going on too, between old man Rom and Tonchinkan and all... Mr. Ezzo, how'd you end up with Felt's group, anyway?"

"I once received a favor from Miss Felt and Master Reinhard. Through that connection I was taken under their wing — 'picked up,' would be the fitting term, I suppose."

"If that's the case, sounds like a good find. Kinda like our Otto."

The expression "a good find" was one Otto himself often used, but the thing Otto and Ezzo had in common was, perhaps, being greenish — Otto in his clothes, Ezzo in his hair.
A green find that turns out excellent and reliable. Thinking that, Garfiel couldn't help but let out a "heh heh."

"You seem to be enjoying something, Master Garfiel."

"It's nothin', somethin' stupid. More than that, bein' called 'Master' like that's ticklish. 'Master' makes it sound way too high and mighty."

"Is that so? I don't see it that way, myself... well then, shall I address you as simply Garfiel, as I do with Rachins and the others?"

"Yeah, we ain't exactly strangers meetin' for the first time. I'll take you up on that."

Garfiel gave a casual wave of his hand, and Ezzo nodded, "Understood."
Watching their exchange, Subaru said, "Come to think of it," glancing between the two of them.

"You two know each other, right? From where?"

"The 'Priestella Incident'... you know, the affair where the Witch Cult, led by an Archbishop of Sin, attacked the water gate city. I was called into the city by Miss Felt to help with the aftermath. That's where I got acquainted with young Garfiel here, and Master Otto too."

"I see, so that's when..."

"Yeah, there was a whole mess back then too... well, it's a long story."

That was, in fact, during the time Subaru and the others had been off on their journey here, to the Pleiades Watchtower.
Left behind in Priestella to recuperate along with the injured Otto, Garfiel had helped with the city's reconstruction, and in the process assisted with excavating the "Witch's Remains" that had been sealed beneath the city. One member of that excavation team had been Ezzo — and, incidentally, the rest of the members had been Garfiel, Ricardo, and, for some reason, Liliana, who'd tagged along.

"Just hearing about it, sounds like quite the eventful cast of characters, I suppose..."

"It was demanding, but a time I learned a great deal from. And of course, young Garfiel here helped me out more than once. I feel a bit awkward saying it in front of him, but he's a splendid young man."

"Heh heh, aw, come on now."
"He really is quite something, I suppose."

At Ezzo's words of praise, given without a shred of embarrassment, Subaru and Beatrice looked even more pleased than Garfiel himself, which was rather ticklish to witness.
In truth, Garfiel too had come to appreciate firsthand that Ezzo wasn't just book-smart, but a capable and battle-tested mage. It was frustrating, in a way, that having far more exceptional mages close at hand made it all too easy to underestimate Ezzo.

"——Now then, deepening our friendship like this isn't a bad thing, but let's settle the matter at hand first. It concerns Master Al, whom Miss Flam is currently watching over."

"——. Al, huh."

"Indeed. There is one thing I'd like to confirm... I suspect Master Al's aim may be the resurrection of Lady Priscilla Barielle. What do you think?"

"Wha...!?"
"Huh?"

Garfiel and Subaru's voices overlapped at Ezzo's remark, spoken while touching his own chin.
At the sudden appearance of the word "resurrection," Garfiel, at least, couldn't quite keep up. Amid Garfiel's confusion, Beatrice narrowed her round eyes.

"What do you mean by that? If you're talking about resurrecting the dead — the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' — we've had quite enough of that already, I suppose."

"You mean the matter in the Empire I heard a bit about. The forbidden art known as the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' interests me greatly as well, but that isn't what I mean to raise right now. As it happens, I have a concern about the method Master Natsuki mentioned, half-jokingly, just a moment ago."

"Something I said... as a joke?"

"——A pseudo-resurrection of the dead, achieved by reading Lady Priscilla Barielle's 'Book of the Dead' and overwriting oneself with her existence."

"——"

Ezzo's words, flat in tone, carrying no unnecessary emotion, sank into Garfiel's mind.
A pseudo-resurrection of the dead — Ezzo's account didn't quite click for Garfiel. But that it wasn't something to be laughed off as far-fetched nonsense was plain from Subaru's reaction — shock plastered across his face, sweat beading on his brow.
Subaru, at a loss for words. Watching him, Ezzo continued.

"As I've said, I myself have already read roughly a dozen 'Books of the Dead.' My earliest attempts were, as Miss Flam's remark suggested, a disgrace too shameful to recount. And going by the sensation I experienced then... one could argue that overwriting the mind is indeed possible."

"...I did hear from Subaru about a close call, where 'memories' nearly got mixed together. But it seems hard to believe that would completely erase the consciousness of the one reading it, I suppose."

"If we're talking about mixing, about being stirred together, then yes, that's accurate. But what if the reader's mind were already prepared to accept it? If, from the very start, one held the mindset of wanting oneself to disappear — wouldn't the mind read in from the book take root without resistance?"

"——"

"Of course, this is purely armchair theorizing. I haven't actually tried it. It's simply the notion that occurred to me, considering that somewhere out there is a person who's lost someone precious to them, and pondering what motive might drive that person to seek out that someone's 'Book of the Dead.'"

As Ezzo laid out his reasoning at length, deep, uncharacteristic furrows formed on Beatrice's brow.
Listening beside her, Garfiel found no room to interject. He worked his mind to try to keep up, and felt he'd more or less grasped the gist of it.
But even if, hypothetically, a dead person's memories, thoughts, everything of that sort, were poured wholesale into someone through the "Book of the Dead"——

"That still wouldn't mean they came back to life."

"Oh? And why is that?"

"'Cause... 'cause, y'know. The soul! The soul's different. It ain't— it ain't someone from inside a book."

His head in a jumble, Garfiel clicked his canine teeth together and gave that answer.
It wasn't a matter of logic, of a proper, well-reasoned explanation. But he couldn't believe a person was made up of nothing but "memories" or "information," and so that was his answer.
At Garfiel's answer, Ezzo narrowed his eyes.

"Indeed. I share the same opinion as young Garfiel here."

"...Huh?"

"What's with that dim-witted face and voice? It quite ruins your fierce look. Chin up."

Seeing the troubled, agonized expression on Garfiel's face after he'd managed to wring out an answer, Ezzo raised a finger and continued, "Listen."

"Young Garfiel's point strikes precisely at the heart of the matter. Even if the 'Book of the Dead' recorded the entirety of the deceased, even if one could relive the experiences and emotions attached to that record as though they were one's own, even if the overwriting of the mind were to actually occur — that would not be the resurrection of the dead. It would be the birth of a different being, one sharing the memories, experiences, and feelings of the dead."

"A different being, sharing the memories, experiences, and feelings..."

"I don't consider that, in any true sense, the resurrection of the dead. I suspect most people would feel the same way, instinctively. So I want to confirm it. — Does Master Al desire the resurrection of the dead? If he chooses the 'Book of the Dead' as his method to that end, then his wish will not be fulfilled."

"——"

"Though, if what he wants is simply to keep Lady Priscilla enclosed within his own heart, then that too is one approach."

Shaking his head slowly, Ezzo brought his line of thought to a close.
At his thought-provoking words, Garfiel clicked his teeth together, then glanced over to check on Subaru.
Subaru should have been the one, above anyone, standing closest to Al's heart, trying hardest to find some way to help him. What did Subaru think, hearing Ezzo's words just now?

"I'm an idiot. No, I am an idiot."

"Subaru..."

"Until you said it, I never even considered that possibility. ...I just, simply figured what Al wanted to know was Priscilla's state of mind at the very end. And that maybe, while searching for the book, he'd find some way to make peace with it, too — I even let myself hope for that much..."

Pressing a fist to his own forehead, Subaru berated himself for failing to think of it. At the raw sight of his self-reproach, Garfiel said, "Big bro," and caught the arm pressing the fist to his forehead.
Hurting yourself like that wouldn't fix a thing. Garfiel knew that from the white scar on his own forehead.

"I don't mean to blame you for not thinking of it. This is a use I only conceived of because I've actually experienced reading a 'Book of the Dead,' combined with my habit of examining things from every angle. Whether Master Al has even conceived of this himself is doubtful. Still——"

"I get it. I get it, Mr. Ezzo. — If, by some chance, Al's thinking that if he disappears, Priscilla will come back in his place — that can't happen."

Clenching his back teeth hard, Subaru answered Ezzo's words, strength returning to his eyes. Then Subaru looked to Garfiel, who still held his hand.

"Sorry, made you worry."

"No need to apologize. This time, worryin' about you is my job. Got asked to plenty by big bro Otto and Lady Emilia both."

"That's a whole separate issue, come on."

Garfiel let go of his hand and shrugged, and Subaru gave a wry laugh, shoulders slumping. Then, with a small "Alright," Subaru murmured,

"Let's not bring this up with Al directly, at least not for now. If he hasn't thought of it himself, we could end up pushing him toward it unnecessarily. But — we're not leaving Al alone. Not for a second."

"If he goes lookin' for the book while he's alone and actually reads it, there'll be no stoppin' him."

"Ideally, we'd find the book before he does. Whether we show it to him or not afterward, having it in hand gives us the initiative."

"...Wish we didn't have to think about it like some kinda power struggle."

Weighing the possibility that Al might act rashly, Subaru murmured this weakly amid the discussion.
Originally, the whole reason they'd brought Al along to the tower like this was to keep him from being driven to such extremes. And yet, for coming here to become the very reason he might be driven to extremes — that would be putting the cart before the horse entirely.

"——"

Garfiel dug his heel into the tower floor, resenting the Pleiades Watchtower.
For what purpose did this tower house the memories of the dead in the form of "Books of the Dead," bewildering the hearts of those who sought them, and now troubling Subaru and the rest of them besides?

"Is Al searching the stacks for the book he's after right now, I suppose?"

"Indeed, that should be the case."

"And the girl you left with him, Flam — what sort of girl is she?"

"She serves as attendant to Lady Felt... or rather, she's a servant of House Astrea. A child of a family that has served House Astrea for generations. Young in years, but not to be underestimated — a formidable talent. Without magic, even I would be hard-pressed against her."

At Ezzo's explanation, Garfiel, having observed Flam's bearing, found himself convinced.
He hadn't seen her move with any particular agility, but Flam's poise as she stood was strikingly beautiful — a straightness of bearing rivaling Ram's. He wouldn't say her actual strength matched Ram's, but given that she worked for House Astrea, it was reasonable to expect she was quite the formidable talent.

"Master Al will likely search for the book without regard for sleep or meals. We intend to propose that we split up to help as well, but... how long is the stay at the tower?"

"— Three days, that's the plan. Al's on board with that too."

Three days — that had been the length of stay originally set for the Pleiades Watchtower.
Even if Priscilla's "Book of the Dead" wasn't found within that time, three days from now they'd leave the tower, and Al would return to Barielle territory to stay at Schult's side.
So it was Subaru's hope to create some chance for Al to find his footing again before then, a point Garfiel agreed with as well.

If only these three days could become time used to help Al move forward.
And ensuring they didn't become anything else——

"That's the job I gotta do here."

Clenching his fist hard enough to make his bones creak, Garfiel firmly took up that role as his own.
To fulfill what had been entrusted to him by Emilia, Frederica, Otto, Ram, and Rem — none of whom were present here. — And, I suppose, by Roswaal too, for what it's worth.

"——You who have reached the tower's summit. You who tread the first tier. All-powerful petitioner."

And so, watching over Garfiel and the others as they resolved to blot out any hint of ill possibility, the "Divine Dragon's" eyes — fixed on some point impossible to determine — looked on, unchanged in their ever-present ambiguity.
