Arc 4 Chapter 97: "Before the Morning"

"Do sit down, won't you. Whether this ends up long or gets wrapped up short, let's have ourselves a proper, settled-in talk, sha-a-all we?"

Roswaal skewered the two of them standing in the doorway with a yellow gaze, one eye shut.

Caught in that sticky stare, Otto beside him visibly gulped, as though he'd been doused in something uncanny. That same touch of malice reached toward Subaru too, but since he'd already been keyed up, one cleared throat was enough to keep him standing his ground without being swallowed by it.

"Coming out to greet us in full makeup — gotta say, all that effort to doll yourself up makes a guy blush."

"No-o-o need to mind it at all. After all, wasn't it you who told me to? To put this makeup on again, that is-s-s."

"Ah, right, I did say that."

After the Contract and the wager had been struck, before leaving the room, Subaru had indeed said as much to Roswaal. He hadn't imagined the man would actually follow through on it, but—

"Originally, for me, painting this face was something like donning armor for battle. To push things forward exactly as the Gospel dictates, I had to move about without ever letting my guard down. Wearing this makeup was my me-e-eans of self-suggestion, to keep myself perpetually braced."

Hearing this unexpected bit of backstory, Subaru found himself staring hard at that face despite himself.

The white-painted skin, the eerie eyeliner. The rouge-colored lips, the distinctive mark under the left eye. To think all of it carried more meaning than simply mocking whoever he was talking to.

"Or are you just saying that to sound meaningful, huh?"

"I'd love it if you'd just believe me outright. We-e-ell, I'll leave it to your imagination. Once I diverged from what the Gospel described and gave up on this world's continuation, I stopped wearing the makeup too... and what it means that I'm wearing it again now, like this — that, you can think over as well-l-l."

At Roswaal's roundabout way of putting it, Subaru clenched his teeth to keep his expression from shifting.
If what Roswaal had just said about battle-armor was true, then the reason he now stood before Subaru and the others with his face painted again was plain.
He must have reclaimed the will he'd lost — the will to go on living in this world.
Even if that will sprang from the same backward-facing hope Subaru himself had once harbored — ending this world and pinning his hopes on his next self in the world after.

"Wouldn't it have suited you better if I had no motivation left, hm-m-m?"

"Not really. Emilia-tan can't become queen without your name, your pedigree, your authority, and your power. I can't have you turning into an empty husk. I'm gonna get you fired up again, and then I'm gonna knock that fire right back out of you and show you a different hope to pull you out by the roots."

"How reassuring. ...If you can manage it, do give it a try, won't you-u-u."

As Subaru piled declaration of war upon declaration of war with such bravado, Roswaal twisted the corner of his mouth, pleased. Then, keeping that smile fixed in place, he said "By the way—" and looked to Subaru's side.

"The one standing beside you, spacing out — that would be Otto-kun, whom I was introduced to, yes-s-s? Compared to how lively he was the other day, he looks awfully wilted again, doesn't he?"

"Not so much wilted as flat-out terrified. The guy he greeted trying to get on his good side, next time he looked at that face it had turned into clown makeup. Course he'd get scared enough to piss himself, right?"

"What is this man trying to pin on me now, saying I pissed myself of my own accord!? And don't go around saying I was 'obviously trying to get on your good side'! That'll ruin my reputation, won't it!"

"Reputation, schmutation — the moment you're cooperating with me, the guy who's outright opposing him, there's no reputation left to salvage anyway. Let's at least make sure you're recognized as a formidable enemy, so when the popularity poll comes around you can crack the top ranks."

"I have absolutely no idea what you're talking about anymore!!"

Watching the two of them kick up their usual fuss, Subaru could see some of the tension had eased out of Otto. Otto himself, realizing that the exchange had been meant to loosen him up, wore a complicated expression.
And Roswaal, observing Subaru and Otto communicate their intent through eye contact alone, ran a finger along his own cheek and smiled meaningfully.

"Understanding each other with just a look — my, aren't you two the close friends. And here I thought I'd known you longer, Subaru-kun. Makes me a touch jealous, doesn't it-t-t."

"Don't say creepy stuff like that. The number one and number two slots in my heart are permanently sold out. There's no room for the likes of you — much less some guy — to squeeze in, you bastard."

"How disappointing. But then, that would mean... Beatrice goes unrewarded as well, wouldn't it-t-t seem. I wonder what she'd think of that."

"—————"

As a warning shot, that word had about as much effect on Subaru as anything possibly could.
He hadn't been on guard against Beatrice's name coming out of Roswaal's mouth here of all places, and combined with his distaste at that knowing look, it hit him like a slap across the face.

"About Beatrice—"

"That girl, in her own way, is following the Gospel's guidance too. In that sense, I suppose you could call her something of a comrade to me-e-e. Though of course, my active stance toward the Gospel's text and her passive one make for rather different ways of facing it, don't the-e-ey."

"So if she just sits and waits, she's supposed to get results out of it? Talk about a raw deal — and yeah, I feel the same way. Only in her case, the curse behind that order to 'wait' is way too strong."

"That would seem lik-k-kely, wouldn't it."

Obeying the orders of her creator, Echidna, Beatrice continued waiting inside the forbidden archive of Roswaal Manor for "that person," who didn't even exist. As a Spirit given, from the moment of her birth, that single purpose and nothing else, clinging faithfully to that order was her entire reason for being.
In terms of sheer stubbornness, Beatrice was, in her own way, close to Roswaal.

The memory of her begging Subaru to let her die was still fresh.
So was the look on her face when she'd shielded Subaru, at the very end of everything that had happened afterward at the mansion.
And so—

"I'll break that curse of hers too. I'll drag her out of that musty room and air her out in the sun, along with her books and her dress. — Whether she ends up rewarded after that is up to her."

"—————"

At Subaru's flat declaration, Roswaal narrowed his eyes as though gazing at something dazzling. Then he let out a short breath, and turned his gaze toward Otto, who had been watching for a chance to enter the conversation.

"So-o-o then? Putting together the fact that you had him present for this, and everything said up to now... I suppose I should take it that Otto-kun here is a card on your side, ye-e-es?"

"N-no, I'm simply here as a kind of arbiter, to make sure the conditions are equal for both sides. I have absolutely no intention of committing any disloyalty toward the Margrave by voicing an opinion against him—"

"Don't chicken out at this point! Tell him straight! Say his clown makeup pisses you off, and once you've beaten him, you're gonna paint him up like kabuki with red and white!"

"I never said any such thing, and what's 'kabuki'!!"

At Otto being made to spout a bold declaration he had no memory of making, Subaru gave a pointless thumbs-up. But at Subaru's attitude, Roswaal closed one eye thoughtfully and sent a meaningful glance toward Otto.

"If I had to guess... it seems your presence has something to do with the change in Subaru-kun's resolve. My, how touching that is-s-s."

"I won't deny it... but thinking about it that way, isn't it a little off, narratively, to get revived with the help of a male character's strength? Shouldn't it normally be the main heroine who's the one to pick me back up?"

"You give me that accusing look and there's nothing I can say in response."

Subaru answered the defiant look with a wave of his hand, and told himself there was nothing to be done about it.
The strength to stand back up, he'd borrowed from elsewhere. This time, it was Subaru's turn to take the main heroine's hand and pull her to her feet. At the very least, that's what he'd resolved in his heart to be.

Subaru closed his eyes once, then opened them. Noting the change in the sharpness of that gaze, Roswaal straightened his posture, and the air in the room shifted.
Fingers laced together atop the blanket, Roswaal opened his mouth to begin. "No-o-ow, then."

"Being isolated like this, under the pretext of convalescence, leaves me with nothing to do. Unbearably dull. Which is why... I've taken to doing nothing but listening in."

"That's a lousy hobby."

"And you, meanwhile, seem to be busy hatching your own little scheme, don't you-u-u. ...Have you come to feel, even a little, just how this 'Sanctuary' came to be, and how tangled up its surrounding circumstances are?"

"Tangled and complicated as hell, that's for sure — it's giving me a headache. Throw your and Emilia's Trial into the mix and 'dead end at every turn' doesn't even cover it."

Subaru admitted the difficulty of the situation honestly, but he'd already factored in the low odds.
Repeating to Roswaal what he'd already confirmed with Otto, Subaru raised a finger to make clear he had no intention of giving up on improving things.

"The Contract, the wager — the deadline's three days out. I'm planning to make a big move tomorrow and the day after... so don't go interfering too much."

"That's not something I can promise. The fulfillment of the Gospel is my life's deepest wish. I don't think I need to explain that to you again and again, do I-i-i?"

"Within three days I'll get Emilia through the Trial and talk Garfiel around. A bigger camp isn't a bad thing for you either. I'll guarantee you a brighter future than anything you'd get from relying on that Gospel."

"If it's you saying it, that future's probably not something to scoff at."

Even at this point, Subaru kept piling on words of persuasion he knew were futile. For a moment, Roswaal lowered his eyes and murmured something, as if letting his thoughts wander toward the future Subaru offered.
But when he raised his face again, there was no hesitation in those mismatched eyes.

"There's no certainty in it. — For me, that alone is reason enough to reject the future you're bringing to the table. Follow the Gospel, and the future I want is guaranteed. Even if the number of things sacrificed to obtain that future is great, it doesn't matter to me. What I desire is only one thing."

"For that one, precious, all-important thing, you'd toss aside everything else that's looking at you? What happens to the people who support your position now — what happens to Ram?"

"O-o-oh come now, that's enough, Subaru-kun. — What good does it do, questioning my resolve at this late stage? My answer remains unchanged, and so does what you need to do."

"—————"

The result of the persuasion, entirely predictable. The words of refusal, entirely predictable.
Roswaal wasn't listening to Subaru's words. Just as Subaru had rejected Roswaal's proposal, Echidna's Contract, Roswaal's own "best" course was already fixed in his heart.

"...I really can't become you, Roswaal. Not ever."

"Is that so. ...Well now, that truly is a shame, Subaru-kun."

Leaning his weight back against the pillow behind him, Roswaal let out a quiet breath. Whether the faint disappointment in it stemmed from expectations Roswaal had once held for Subaru, was hard to say.
The expectation, perhaps, that a Natsuki Subaru with every possibility sealed off would surely arrive at the same place he himself had — Roswaal, who'd thrown himself into a lonely battle, willing to cast away even his own life.

"Let's bring the conversation back to the main point. Is that all right, Margrave?"

It was Otto who stepped forward and spoke, into a room where silence was on the verge of settling. Roswaal turned his yellow eyes on the gray-haired young man, and his silence stood in for consent.
Otto turned just his head back toward Subaru, giving a small nod, then continued.

"I take it as agreed that both sides regard three days from now — the event that will shake this 'Sanctuary' — as the deadline. Likewise, that the Margrave, while keeping Natsuki-san in check, will observe how things unfold without interfering."

"'Observe'... I don't believe I ever gave my word to anything of the sor-r-rt."

"But surely the fact that I haven't been reduced to ash right here can be taken as a statement of intent on your part? A man of your renown — the Chief Court Mage of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, Margrave Roswaal L. Mathers — could surely turn me into a scrap of Mana in an instant with your power. Within the limits that don't void your wager with Natsuki-san, that's the simplest way to derail his objective."

"Oh?"

Watching Otto rattle off his logic with fluent care, Subaru held his breath and watched his back. Otto's tongue had never caught fire like this even in their rehearsals.
Reading the intent behind Otto's words, Roswaal too wore a look of surprise.

"You're interesting, you know. I believe I evaluated you the same way once before... but let me correct that assessment. — You're a far more interesting talent than I'd imagined."

"Am I... allowed to take that as praise?"

"It's praise with no hidden barbs, I assure you. Aha-a-a, splendid. By standing here alongside Subaru-kun, you've proven your logic at the risk of your very life. Truly-y-y, that's the sort of decision I find precious, and to my taste. Not many could imitate it."

"That's... thank you, then."

Though somewhat overwhelmed by the sheer nerve of it, Otto faced Roswaal without flinching. Just as he himself had said — facing down a Warlock who could, if he pleased, reduce him to ash with a single fingertip, and under conditions where that might well happen.

In fact, if Roswaal were truly willing to abandon all restraint to force the Contract with Subaru through, all he need do was carry out exactly the cowardly act Otto had just named. Or, short of that, if he simply burned down the Sanctuary ahead of schedule, or brought forward the heavy snowfall to summon the Great Rabbit early, Subaru would have no way to stop him. The wager would end in Subaru's defeat, settled.

Otto, not knowing about Return by Death, seemed to think Subaru's survival was essential to the wager's outcome — but between Subaru and Roswaal, who both shared the common knowledge of Return by Death, even Subaru's survival wasn't a necessary condition. In that room where the Contract had been sealed, whether he'd get burned alive from behind had arguably been the single greatest point of concern for Subaru.

Roswaal's refraining from that outrage — what else could you call that, if not observing without interfering?

"In any case, we'd like your firm assurance, Margrave. That whatever we plot, however we go about executing it... until the deadline, three days from now, you will not resort to any extreme measures."

"Demanding an assurance — now that's asking for quite a lot, isn't it-t-t."

"It's a gamble that's hard enough to pull off without having to watch our backs the whole time, worrying about interference. If Natsuki-san is placing his whole wager with confidence that he'll lose, then refraining from lifting a finger shouldn't be much of an issue for you, should it?"

"—————"

Gesturing with his hands, the corner of his mouth curled up, Otto met Roswaal's sharpening gaze.
Holding his breath, watching the negotiation play out, Subaru sensed that Otto had staked everything on this hand. With that provocative way of putting it, if Roswaal "took the bait," it would mean their loss.
A suffocating silence hung heavy over the room for a while. But then—

"I se-e-ee. Subaru-kun, you've been blessed with a fine friend, haven't you."

"...Yeah. He's a good buddy — the kind who'll slap me across the face if I'm about to go and make a mistake."

At Subaru's response, Roswaal, his expression softening, gazed off into the distance for a moment.
It occurred to Subaru, suddenly — had Roswaal never had an Otto of his own? An Otto to strike him and set him right, when he was about to err?
Had there never been anyone to correct Roswaal's stubbornly twisted way of thinking, in just that way?

"So it can't be helped. — Four hundred years too late, but we're going to be the ones to do it."

"—————"

"Roswaal! That look just now — I'll take that as agreement, all right!"

At Subaru's insistence, Roswaal gave a wry laugh. Tilting his clownish face at an angle, he said,

"Very well, I'll make you that promise. In any case, I'll need to knead Mana to bring down snow three days from now regardless. I'll tell you now, even someone of my caliber finds it quite the chore to control the weather — temporarily, and in a limited area, at that."

"So you're off to meditation time now, huh? For reference, it'd help a lot if you'd tell me what you'd hate to have happen while you're meditating."

"I'm not in the habit of handing my enemy that much of an advantage, no-o-o. — I'm simply confident that, without my needing to do anything at all, your goal won't be reached."

On that one point, he wouldn't budge.
At the sarcastic remark, Subaru snorted, and Otto, looking exhausted, let out a sigh of his own.
Still, one of the major reasons for coming here had been successfully secured.

Making sure Roswaal wouldn't interfere was an essential condition for going all-in on the real gamble.
Just having gotten that assurance was itself a gain. And it owed much to Otto having spoken far more persuasively than Subaru had ever anticipated.

"By the way, Subaru-kun."

"—Huh?"

"I heard that the Great Spirit has parted ways with Lady Emilia."

A change of subject that felt like being struck from above, right as his thoughts had just started to loosen with relief.
Straightening his slightly bent spine, Subaru nodded to Roswaal's question with an "Yeah,"

"Something went wrong with Emilia and Puck's Contract, and the bond got voided. Looking at it from a Spirit-user's standpoint, Emilia doesn't have the qualification to be called that right now."

"I heard her grief was quite something... At this critical juncture, on top of a heart already broken, to have it cracked further still — will she be all right?"

"...I don't know. At the very least, tonight's Trial will have to be skipped. How it'll affect things from tomorrow on, I won't know until Emilia wakes up."

In truth, Emilia's grief, despite Puck's prior warning, had been overflowing with a sorrow that made him want to look away.
To drive Emilia into that state with only two days left was, for someone already gambling on such poor odds, nothing but stacking another bet atop an already thin chance and narrowing the gate further still.
And yet — the mere fact that a hole letting the wind through had been opened in what should have been a sealed gate was the one faint hope Subaru could still believe in right now.

"Still, my head's heavy over it, that's for sure. But whatever happens, I..."

"It struck me as a bit strange, actually."

Cutting Subaru off mid-sentence, Roswaal waved a raised finger side to side. At that gesture, Subaru fell silent despite himself, and Roswaal continued with "No,"

"Watching you now, it certainly looks like you're racking your brain over how to help Lady Emilia get back on her feet... but as for the Contract between the Great Spirit and Lady Emilia being severed — you don't seem all that surprised about it. What would that mean, I wonder?"

"—————"

At what seemed like an offhand question from Roswaal, Subaru fell silent for a moment.
That Roswaal, through his own eyes, saw little surprise in Subaru was because this had already been known information to him. If Puck hadn't told him beforehand to expect this situation, Subaru, faced with a wailing, screaming Emilia, would have had no choice but to stand there helplessly.
Though even having known in advance, it hadn't made much of a difference, even now.

"You... already knew about this situation, didn't you? The fact that the Great Spirit would part from Lady Emilia's side. Setting aside how you came to know it, of course."

"Even if that's true... you've got no business lecturing me about it. I'm going to use every bit of power I have to win this bet. There's no cause for you to blame me for..."

"No-o-o, that's quite all right. — That was the answer I wanted to hear, that's all."

Turning his palm outward, Roswaal nodded with a satisfied look. His previous words sealed off by that attitude, Subaru let out a breath through his nose, carrying an unresolved feeling.

"Still, tho-o-ough... if it's a foregone conclusion that the Great Spirit would part from Lady Emilia's side, this-s-s presents quite the troublesome development for me, doesn't it."

"...Really? If anything, from where you're standing, isn't that a good sign—"

"Not at all. It's true that I can't bear to watch Lady Emilia's spirit broken, and as a condition for stoking the fire in your heart, it certainly fits the bill... but Lady Emilia losing her power as a Spirit-user means, in effect, that she can no longer bring snow down upon the 'Sanctuary.' That's a rather significant blow for me, isn't it."

"Ah..."

What Roswaal had just voiced was a discrepancy with the Gospel.
Three days from now, snow was to fall on the Sanctuary, summoning the Great Rabbit. To stay true to what was written, Roswaal needed to blanket the Sanctuary in snow.
But if that were the case—

"Roswaal, there's something I've been thinking about for a while now..."

"Hm? What might that be-e-e?"

"About the flaw in that complete edition of the Gospel you're supposed to have."

"—————"

At Subaru's words, the faint smile that had lingered on Roswaal's lips vanished. The faint sense of the air drying, of skin prickling, originated from the Warlock's gaze before him.
Beside him, Otto's shoulders drew in slightly, and Subaru too felt, for an instant, a pressure clinging tight to his throat. The Warlock, focusing the full attention of his mismatched eyes on Subaru,

"Do go on, Subaru-kun. This flaw in the Gospel, you say... yes-s-s, you did say that."

Reaching an arm into the pillow he'd been leaning against, Roswaal drew out a book bound in black — the Gospel. The same sort of thing Beatrice had held in the forbidden archive; the mere fact of its appearance here made the air in the room grow strangely heavy with pressure.

"I-it's just... something about how you follow the Gospel's account to the letter always felt off to me. But there was so much else more pressing to worry about that I kept letting that feeling slide. ...But once I took the time to calm down and really think about you and the Gospel, and pushed that feeling of wrongness as far as it'd go... I realized something."

"Let's hear it, then."

"The unhelpful nature of the content in the incomplete version the Witch Cult carries — let's set that aside for now. The problem is the decisive flaw in the Gospel you hold."

"—————"

At the word "flaw," the corner of Roswaal's eye twitched faintly.
Even so, that he kept his silence was likely because his interest in what Subaru was about to present hadn't run dry. For Roswaal, the Gospel was, quite literally, the lifeline supporting his entire purpose.
Hearing of a defect, a flaw in it, he had no reason not to listen.

Taking a small breath, his insides trembling under the pressure lodged in his throat, Subaru poured strength into his gaze, glared at Roswaal, and said,

"From what you've told me, the Gospel records the whole chain of events here in the Sanctuary, and the path all the way to how it resolves. In your version, that's the flow of 'I take over conquering the tomb, in place of Emilia, who fails to conquer it.' To make Emilia fail, you drive her into a corner as a condition — you bring down the snow, have the Great Rabbit attack the Sanctuary. To push me toward my resolve, you corner the mansion and strip away the 'waste' that is my existence. — That about right?"

"By and large, that's correct. And the flaw, you say?"

"...You shouldn't have known that bringing the snow would summon the Great Rabbit. So your Gospel shouldn't have anything written past the point where the snow falls. It just simply says 'snow falls,' and you carry it out because it's content that needs to happen — a puppet dancing to the book's strings."

"I'm aware of that. Even if that's what I am, it doesn't bother me. By following what the book says, I can obtain the future I desire. So what hesitation could there possibly be in dancing to letters that have neither heart nor form?"

With a flat, unbothered attitude, Roswaal tossed back what sounded like agreement to Subaru's reasoning.
What Subaru was trying to say, his eyes swayed as if unable to grasp it — but for Subaru's part, having his reasoning up to this point affirmed only confirmed that the hypothesis forming inside him was gaining a real sense of substance. — If, hypothetically, that were the case, then—

"You and I are both true clowns, then."

"—Oh?"

At Roswaal narrowing his eyes, the air in the room grew heavier by another degree. That it even felt as if the ambient temperature had dropped might have been because the Mana in the air had lost track of where to settle, unsettled by the Warlock's quiet emotional tremor.
Either way, whether or not it touched Roswaal's reverse scale here, addressing this wrongness about the Gospel was a necessary matter — both to resolve Subaru's own doubts, and to cast doubt on the state of the heart of a man so obsessed with clinging to that record.

"Let's get back to it. The Gospel has a line that says 'snow falls.' Let's assume, originally, that's the sort of thing Emilia is meant to bring down. Since Emilia can't do it, or won't do it, you bring the snow down in her place. In accordance with what the Gospel says."

"You're repeating yourself. Get to the point. What about that content is flawed, defective..."

"What that means is — if Emilia doesn't bring the snow down, then without the Gospel, you wouldn't bring the snow down either. Which also means no snow falls on the Sanctuary at all."

It was a simple conclusion, following naturally from the facts.
The reason Roswaal brought the snow down was because the Gospel's account said so. If it weren't written in the Gospel, or if the Gospel didn't exist at all, Roswaal would have no reason to take that action.
On the side of history as originally recorded in the Gospel, why Emilia would bring snow down on the Sanctuary is unclear — but if the Gospel is truly correct, there must be some reason compelling Emilia to blanket the Sanctuary in snow. Ignoring that "something" and simply bringing down the snow means nothing at all. Much less an original history that can't even hold together without the Gospel to prop it up—

"A prophecy that can't come true without the prophecy book itself — what kind of prophecy is that supposed to be?"

"—————"

"A prophecy book is a prophecy book precisely because it records things ahead of time, no matter how unbelievable, how impossible those things might seem. The incomplete version the Witch Cult holds has its text added to over time. So for that one, I get the logic — the writing takes shape in response to a changing history. But what about the one you have? The so-called complete edition, wearing that grand crown of a title."

"—————"

"What's the use of just lining up the wording to match, after the fact, to fit what's already been written? How can you believe that the exact time recorded will simply tick by as written, while ignoring everything else — all the other factors — that would have to bring about that result? You're smart, Roswaal. There's no way you hadn't noticed something like that."

"—————"

"What you're doing is just refusing to think, Roswaal."

Pointing a finger, Subaru declared it flatly.
Roswaal, on the receiving end of those words, kept his silence for a long, long while. But the oppressive weight that had been pressing down over the room until then abruptly vanished, and Subaru could tell that Otto, freed from the pressure, was trying to steady his racing heart with quick breaths.

Eyes closed, Roswaal remained silent, as if lost in thought. Whether his rouge-painted lips trembled ever so slightly was, perhaps, simply wishful thinking on Subaru's part — a hope that his words had actually had some effect on the man.
But he was given no chance to press further and find out.

"A prophecy book — the Gospel — is a history premised on its own existence. That things move in accordance with it is only natural, given that premise, and the content of the Gospel is recorded as a matter of course..."

"Yeah. Figured you might come back with something like that, honestly. — So I made sure to prepare my counter to that too, just in case."

It was an unusually halting, un-Roswaal-like way of speaking. And because of that, it was scattered away with ease before Subaru's voice, which had anticipated it in advance.
Closing one eye, Roswaal looked at Subaru with only his yellow one. Time and again before this, Roswaal had skewered Subaru with that eerily gleaming yellow gaze.

So this might have been the first time that yellow gleam had ever burned with a light this feeble.
Registering that fact only vaguely, Subaru said,

"Show me the Gospel you're holding. If I can see what kind of text is actually written in there, I might just be convinced by what you're saying."

"—! That, I cannot do. The Gospel refuses to reveal its contents to anyone but the one it recognizes as its owner. The owner of this Gospel is me. Even if you looked at it, you wouldn't be able to understand what's written, and there's even the risk that anyone not acknowledged by the book would have their brain burned to a crisp if they touched it..."

"You're awfully talkative all of a sudden, Roswaal. That worried about me seeing that book, huh?"

Watching Roswaal falter over his words, Subaru's eyes widened in surprise at his own realization.
He couldn't read Roswaal's complexion, thanks to the white paint covering his face, but Roswaal's reaction to Subaru's offer was rejection so excessive it bordered on overkill.
At that sudden shift in posture, Subaru realized his remark had struck a weak point in Roswaal far beyond what he'd imagined.

There was something in the contents of the Gospel, or in what was written there, that Roswaal was hiding, something that must never be known. Was his stubborn insistence on obeying the surface of the prophecy rooted in that very hidden something?
If not, how else could someone of Roswaal's caliber possibly fail to notice the contradiction in how the Gospel functioned?

"Natsuki-san, that's enough..."

Otto, who had been objectively observing Roswaal's attitude and Subaru, about to push further, thinking now was the moment to press the attack, reached out a hand as if to cut off any further pursuit.
Subaru turned just his head to shake off that restraint, but faced with the earnest look Otto was giving him, he gave up on charging toward a rash conclusion.

There was something Otto could see that Subaru, caught up in the heat of the moment, couldn't. And what Otto's eyes told him was that this was the moment to wrap up the negotiation.
Subaru lacked the recklessness to push past that and ruin everything they'd achieved in the discussion so far.

"—Roswaal, three days left. Tomorrow, the day after. And then, the final day. By then, I'll have settled everything, once and for all. As for you — chew over what I've said, why don't you."

"Are you under the impression you've put me in your debt for this? — You ought to thank your friend, Otto-kun. He was the one who found the boundary line that would've been bad for both of us, you and me alike."

His expression wiped clean, Roswaal tucked the Gospel back behind his pillow. Then, shrugging his shoulders, he tossed one last remark at Subaru and the others as they headed for the door.

"From here on, I'll begin weaving the incantation to control the weather. To make the Gospel's account into recorded history. — That will be my guiding compass."

"Do what you want. If you're gonna stubbornly cling to it that hard, guess there's nothing to be done."

Hand on the doorknob, Subaru turned his whole body back around and fixed his eyes on Roswaal.
Meeting the Warlock's gaze head-on with his own, he said,

"Once everything's settled, I'm gonna dump ink all over that Gospel of yours and blot it out black. — Only then will you finally be able to look ahead with your own eyes."


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—Having finished a discussion with Roswaal whose worth, meaningful or meaningless, was hard to judge, Subaru parted with Otto and made his way to Emilia's room.

"—Barusu, you've come."

"Yeah, I have. Sorry for leaving you in charge for so long."

Seeing Subaru arrive, Ram, who'd been watching over Emilia inside the room, made a bored face. Her expressionless face, devoid of any warmth as usual, was just as it always was — but was it Subaru's imagination that her profile, there in the dimness, looked somehow tense?

"The talk with Roswaal's over. I'll watch Emilia, so you can leave here."

Without touching on that, Subaru pulled out a chair and moved to a spot where he could see Emilia, just as Ram had. Ram gave Subaru, now beside her, a brief sidelong glance before murmuring,

"Ram's worried Barusu might try something indecent with Lady Emilia while she's sleeping."

"I'd rather not think your trust in me is so low that you'd suspect I'd pull something dishonorable in this situation."

"Your trust would naturally be low, wouldn't it. Considering you've raised the flag of rebellion against your master, Roswaal-sama, and stand opposed to him within this cramped little 'Sanctuary.'"

"…………"

Naturally enough, it seemed Ram, too, knew about the Contract — the wager — struck between Subaru and Roswaal.
Ram, who to the very last, held a loyalty that made her willing to devote herself, body and soul, to Roswaal. For Roswaal, as a pawn to be used without hesitation to make the Gospel's contents certain, she might be the one person he could rely on without doubt.

"What do you think about it?"

"—————"

To a question with no stated subject, Ram gave no answer. She simply turned her body, which had been in profile toward Subaru, to face him fully, waiting for the rest of what he had to say.
At that posture, Subaru sorted through the tangle of feelings inside him, choosing his words carefully.

"I don't know how much you know about what's in the Gospel. But if Roswaal follows through with what's in that book, the Sanctuary's gonna be torn apart. Whether Garfiel, Ryuzu-san, and everyone else even come out of it okay..."

"If you're trying to use that as leverage to persuade Ram, that's shallow of you, Barusu."

Ram's sharp words cut off Subaru as he tried to pile on more. Her pale-pink eyes fixed on him, skewering him with a light that didn't waver.

"Within Ram, there is only one thing that belongs at the very highest place. That will never waver. Nothing else will ever shake that position — absolutely nothing. So you can't expect that word of yours to change Ram's heart."

"…………"

"Besides — the matter on Ram's end has already been entrusted."

Just as Subaru began to regret having chosen his words wrong, Ram let that slip. At those words, Subaru raised his head, about to press for the true meaning behind them — but Ram was quicker to rise from her seat.
Brushing off her knees, Ram looked once more at Emilia's sleeping face, then turned back to Subaru.

"I'll leave Lady Emilia to you. I'll come again in the morning to look after her."

"Ah, yeah... got it. And, um, what you just said, what did you mean by—"

"Now, I wonder."

That she had no intention of answering — that non-answer was itself the answer.
Leaving it at that, Ram left the room without a shred of lingering hesitation. Subaru tried to call something after her back, but nothing came to mind, and he could only watch her go.

The door closed, leaving only Subaru and Emilia alone in the room.
With Emilia still asleep in bed, all Subaru could do here was keep watch over her sleeping face.

It had been over ten hours since he'd held Emilia, deranged and wailing, and watched her consciousness fade as if she'd fainted — the one saving grace being that her sleeping expression showed no sign of being tormented by nightmares.
If she were being driven back into the past and made to suffer even in her dreams, then she truly had nowhere for her heart to find peace.

Even if she woke, what awaited Emilia was the reality of her bond with Puck severed. How much time would it take to face that?
Subaru had no idea how much his presence could ease that suffering. That he wanted, fiercely, to be of help to her — that much was certain. But how much that amounted to—

"...ru."

"—Huh?"

Subaru's mind, sunk in the sea of his thoughts, suddenly responded to a faint call.
Looking over, he saw Emilia's head tilt slightly in the bed, her violet eyes opening a sliver, gazing at him. — She had woken up.

"Oh, you're up... Emilia, are you all right?"

"—Subaru."

"Yeah, it's me. Your body, is it okay? You've been asleep this whole time... hang on, let me go get you some water."

"It's okay."

Emilia called out to Subaru as he stood and began to move toward the door.
Surprised by the unexpectedly strong tone of her voice, Subaru dropped his half-risen hips back onto the chair without meaning to.

"...Emilia?"

"It's okay. I'm fine, so... stay here."

To Subaru, his voice dropping in concern, Emilia responded with a firm, steady answer.
From the events of the past few days, he'd come to understand that Emilia tended toward low blood pressure and was weak in the mornings. Her mind's sluggishness right after waking was no ordinary thing, and there was no reason to think now, waking from such a long sleep, would be any exception — and yet, there wasn't a trace in her violet eyes of dragging along that long dormancy of consciousness.

"Do you understand the situation?"

"...Yeah. I slept through the whole day today, didn't I. Everyone's already waiting on me as it is, and here I am, what am I even doing... sorry."

"That's not— ! Nobody's blaming you at all. More than that, the problem is..."

As Emilia voiced her apology, Subaru hesitated over whether to bring up Puck. It was the topic he'd expected her to raise first thing, the moment she woke and grasped the situation. That she'd set it aside in favor of showing concern for those around her, as things stood now — could it be that, from the sheer shock, she'd lost the memory that her Contract with Puck had been severed—

"Don't worry, Subaru."

"Huh, wha—?"

"I haven't... forgotten that Puck is gone. I remember it properly. I'm not running away by forgetting."

"Is that... really true?"

"Mm..."

With that brief nod, Subaru understood that Emilia hadn't forgotten the fact. But if that were so, how could she keep such a composed face?
Knowing how she'd looked right after learning Puck was gone, Subaru found her current composure almost too surreal to believe.
And yet, to Subaru, still unable to easily swallow the circumstances that way, Emilia said,

"Sorry, Subaru. I showed you so many parts of myself falling apart... I must've made you worry so-o much."

"No, you can make me worry as much as you want. I don't mind that at all. I don't mind, but... if anything, right now it's you who's..."

"—So much, yeah. There was a lot to think about. I was able to. In my dream."

In a quiet voice, Emilia cut off Subaru's inner conflict. At the sight of her lowered eyes, Subaru found himself holding his breath, drawn in. Her long lashes trembling, Emilia turned her gaze back up to Subaru,

"I want to believe that by morning, I'll be all right... so, Subaru, please."

"...O-okay."

"Hold my hand. Will you stay here with me until morning? If you do, I'm sure I'll..."

Hesitantly, Emilia's pale fingers reached out from beneath the towel blanket.
Seeing that outstretched hand, Subaru grasped it in his own without a moment's hesitation. Wrapping her slender, delicate fingers firmly in his,

"If that's all it takes, that's easy. But, Emilia..."

"Sorry, Subaru. There's probably so much you want to talk about, so much you want to ask. But... please wait until morning. If you do, I'll be able to keep going."

"—————"

"So, please, just like this until morning — Subaru."

At Emilia's pleading voice, Subaru said nothing more.
He simply pulled his chair closer to the bed, bringing Emilia's clasped hand up near her pillow. Then, nodding to Emilia, who watched him with trembling eyes, he said,

"Got it. — Let's do that, Emilia."

"Mm... thank you."

Exchanging only those brief words, Emilia's eyes closed once more.
Unlike the loss of consciousness that had continued since morning, this time it was sleep entered by her own will, to rest her wings. When she woke from it, what words would she have to offer?

Emilia had overcome, by her own strength, the worst-case scenario Subaru had feared. Feeling saved by that fact, Subaru kept watch over her sleeping face.

"...because I believe."

Catching, faintly, Emilia murmuring those words mixed in with her breathing, Subaru let out a sigh.
—Once morning came, things would start moving again.

The real morning was still hours away.
And then—


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"How did the talk with Barusu go?"

"A draw, I suppose you'd say. For both him and me, it was rather a give-and-take. To begin with, it was a situation where I preferred to avoid stirring things up too much anyway."

In the dark room, master and servant exchanged words at a closeness far greater than necessary.
Drawing Ram close beside the bed, cradling her head against his chest, Roswaal reflected on the discussion just past.

In truth, shaking the resolve of a Subaru who had steeled himself was no small feat. Though a clear vision of the future ahead had yet to be fixed, at its foundation Subaru's will had felt like steel.
Otto's presence, standing beside him, had also been a blow for Roswaal. Grasping the situation objectively and reining Subaru in at effective moments, Otto had thoroughly fulfilled the role of balancer — both in the sense of not letting the discussion collapse, and of keeping neither Subaru nor Roswaal from going too far.

Perhaps, had it not been for his presence, Roswaal might have used his sharp tongue to twist Subaru's thinking to his liking — but—

"I'd taken him for nothing but an audience member who'd never step onto the stage... but my, what a capable one he turned out to be, hasn't he. Subaru-kun's found himself a fine friend."

"...Shall Ram see to it, if he's in the way?"

"Let's not. If that decision were to be made, it should have been made back at the discussion just now. Having not done so then, I have no just cause to make that choice now. More than that... the thing I asked of you?"

Closing one eye, Roswaal looked down at Ram with his yellow gaze. In his arms, held by her master, Ram shook her pink head, looking up from within his chest.

"Without a hitch. It was carried out just as Roswaal-sama wished."

"Is that so. If the timing lines up, I'd hoped as much... now, what will come of it, I wonder."

At Ram's nod, Roswaal nodded back, satisfied, stroking the girl's head with his free arm. Ram, receiving it with a look of dazed, dreamy contentment.
Roswaal, for a moment, cast a look almost like pity at the girl in his arms who adored him so, before saying,

"I do hope you won't think ill of me, Subaru-kun. I did give my word not to lift a hand directly. But — surely you'll forgive me for scheming a little, right here from my bed."

"—————"

"Now then, I wonder how this will play out."

A faint smile, brimming with amusement, rose to the corner of Roswaal's mouth.

"If that pure-hearted girl were to learn she'd actually overheard our little conversation, so full of scheming, just now... I wonder what kind of face Subaru-kun would make."


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—It was a dark room.

Darkness had settled over the room with the lights out, and until morning came from beyond the window, the room was filled only with the hush of night and the footsteps of dreams.

The door was shut, the room sealed off from the world outside, silence ruling the space.
And in the center of that room, there in the dimness, a bed had been placed, and upon it lay a single girl, eyes closed.

A stillness so deep not even the sound of breathing could be heard — but then, a sound broke through it, faintly.
It was—

"...Liar."

A murmured, hoarse, feeble voice.
A modest, quiet grievance, spoken by the girl lying on the bed.

"Subaru's a liar."

The whisper repeated once more.
Clenching her free hand tightly, Emilia, left alone in the room, denounced the lie of the boy no longer present.

—The real morning was still only a few hours away.
