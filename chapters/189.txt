Thanks to that, the evacuee group made it back to Arlam Village in exactly eight hours. Having departed past noon, it was now several hours after the curtain of night had fully fallen over the world.

"My butt hurts from sitting the whole time... but, thank god."

Climbing down from the carriage and rolling his hips, Subaru let out a sigh of relief.
All around, cries of joy at reunions echoed through the nighttime village, and some among them were even weeping with happiness. Considering the disaster that had struck this village, Subaru understood that such a reaction was hardly excessive. So there wasn't a single person laughing at it.
Arlam Village, after days without its people, had its vitality restored now that the villagers had returned, even at night. The villagers who'd worn such gloomy faces in the "Sanctuary" now wore smiles, all of them.
The remaining villagers who'd stayed behind to greet them also seemed to breathe a sigh of relief now that their family members had safely returned.

"Natsuki-san, are you heading right back this instant?"

Otto came jogging over, having been looking around at his surroundings, to where Subaru stood apart from the surrounding commotion, watching over everyone.

"As expected, Natsuki-san! You get it!"

For the time being, the village folk should be fine. As for the merchants' claims, Otto would compile them, and Roswaal would be the one to process them. Looking forward, just a little, to whatever unpleasant face Roswaal would make at that, Subaru turned his steps toward the manor.

Fifteen minutes on foot from Arlam Village—at the end of that walk stood Roswaal Manor, all alone.
In the darkness of night, only the manor's lights asserted its presence, floating in the shadows, and seen from a distance like this after sundown, it did have a certain eerie atmosphere, Subaru thought.

"Otto handled the paperwork, but it's probably piled up again since then, over this past week."

Thinking of her toiling away at deskwork like this into the middle of the night, Subaru could sympathize with the hardship.

"I really do need to drag that bastard Otto down into the depths and work him like a beast of burden, as the Emilia Camp's document-processing machine. Let's turn him into a clerical machine."

"Sorry to bother you this late. I'm here from the fire department—" he trailed off.

"No answer, huh."

He'd fully expected someone—Frederica most likely—to answer at wind-like speed, so this reaction left him rather deflated.

"If it's the study, Frederica should be there. For now, I'll see Petra first... and, I need to find Beako too."

The three people currently remaining at the manor rose one after another in his mind.

Last time, the way they'd parted had been the way it had been.

He hadn't managed to get a single answer to the questions that mattered, and instead had been thrown out amid tears and a crumpled face, and that had been that, ever since.

For that purpose too—

"First, a preliminary skirmish... or so I thought."

Knocking, then flinging the door open with force to surprise her—another letdown here as well.

"Leaving the light on when you go out doesn't seem like reliable Petra... but if she's not here, don't tell me she's studying in the study?"

That would be plausible, given Frederica's Spartan streak.

"No, no, I should still have the edge when it comes to basic arithmetic! Don't underestimate modern Japan's compulsory education, you hear!"

"——"

Even accounting for everything, this was too strange, Subaru thought, raising his guard yet another notch. Covering with idle chatter, Subaru's gaze swept from one end of the hallway to the other, then to the door of the study. He pressed his ear to the door, straining to catch any sound from within, but no sound reached him through the thick door from the other side. The chance of gleaning any information this way, from outside, seemed low.

—Petra's room hadn't been ransacked. It remained neat and orderly, the bed made as if she'd been about to go to sleep.

"——!"

A bookshelf against the wall, and a draft blowing through—. The window was closed. Yet he felt the sensation of a cold wind blowing through. Instinct told him that was wrong.

Slipping into the room, Subaru chased after the source of that draft—and noticed.
A shelf at the back of the room had slid sideways, revealing a hidden door, normally concealed, set into the wall, just large enough to pass through. Beyond it lay a spiraling staircase, one that continued long, long down, far below into the depths—

"That's right. There was a hidden passage like this. I remember, I remember it."

It was from a previous loop.
After the villagers of Arlam Village had been slaughtered to the last by the Witch Cult's hand, and, in the depths of despair, he'd found Rem and Ram's bodies at the manor, Subaru, his ego on the verge of collapse, had made his way here.
And then, passing through this hidden passage, he'd gone down underground, and there—

"Got encased in ice by Puck, I think."

He had no certainty about it. Only that, presumably, there had been a row of Witch Cult members, frozen solid in ice, who seemed to have pursued Emilia and the others as they fled through that same passage, and Subaru remembered meeting the same end as them, dying and undergoing a "Return by Death."
Afterward, it had been an underground passage he hadn't thought important enough to check on again, and had even forgotten existed. But—

"Why is it, now, of all times..."

The fact that it had been used at all meant that, at the very least, there'd been a need to evacuate.
And as for who had used it—naturally, it would be someone at the manor who knew of this passage's existence—Frederica, most likely. The simplest conclusion was that she'd taken Petra and fled somewhere through this passage. The question was—

"Fled from what?"

Given how sharp she was, her decision must have had some solid basis.
And given that there were no signs of an attack within the manor itself, that meant whatever danger had been approaching must have been detected beforehand. At that information, Subaru briefly let the word "Witch Cult" flicker through his mind, but he shook his head, chasing it away.

"If it were that, it's way too strange that Frederica wouldn't leave so much as a note. And the people of Arlam Village hadn't noticed anything either... if something as dangerous as the Witch Cult were coming, she'd have taken action to avoid dragging the villagers into it."

At the very least, Frederica had never harbored any doubts about supporting Emilia, whom Roswaal had thrown his lot in with. So she should be taking whatever measures were best, within what she was capable of. And the fact that the villagers knew nothing of this meant it wasn't the Witch Cult.
Anyway—

"Frederica and Petra are probably out of the manor. ...In that case, I..."

For a moment, he took a step toward heading through the passage to try to catch up with Frederica and the others, but what stopped his foot was a girl whose name hadn't come up in his thinking so far.
Even supposing Frederica had judged that fleeing through here was necessary, would that girl really have gone along with such a flight?

"The Beatrice I know isn't some brat who reads the room like that."

That cheeky, ringlet-haired girl would surely have rejected Frederica's suggestion.
She'd shut herself up in her own private forbidden archive, insisting nothing could touch her no matter what happened, kicking away all concern or consideration from others—and then, without fail, wear a lonely expression. Because that was without a doubt what she'd do—

"I'll drag her out...!"

Even if no one else pulled her out, Subaru resolved that he, at least, would do it.
It didn't matter how impregnable she believed her own castle to be, alone within it.
Knowing danger was closing in on this place, there was no way he could leave a little girl behind here alone.

"Now that that's settled—!"

Turning his back on the hidden passage, Subaru let out a sharp breath and burst out of the study.
The surest way to find Beatrice was to go through the manor opening every single door, one after another. With Subaru, he'd have some vague sense along the way of which doors seemed to lead to Beatrice's forbidden archive. Relying on that, all he had to do was find her.
First, starting with the top floor's doors, one after another—

"Whoa?"

Just then, his feet, driving forward with momentum, suddenly tangled, and Subaru tripped.
More than any sense of pathetic-ness at such a poor start, embarrassment came first. Just when he'd tried to look cool, this happened, and it fell rather flat.
Bracing his hands against the hallway carpet, Subaru turned to look back at whatever he'd tripped on. Looking, he saw something lying just a little ways past the study door.

The pink thing was quite long, extending all the way to Subaru's feet, several paces away. Tracing it, wondering just how far it went, he followed it back—and there was nothing complicated about it at all.
—It was spilling out of Subaru's own torn side.

"—Huh?"

The left side of his clothing had been cleanly torn open, and pink innards were spilling out from within.
It started from just outside the study door, and now, at Subaru's feet, tangled and tripping him up—which meant, somewhere along the way, without his even noticing, his abdomen had been cut open.

"...Ohb."

The instant that registered, a clot of blood surged up, blocking his throat, and his vision was dyed crimson.
Unable to withstand the pressure in his abdomen, his innards spilling out, he tried with trembling fingertips to push them back inside, but lacked the strength, and collapsed to his knees on the spot. His body couldn't hold itself up, and his upper body toppled over.

He had no idea what had happened. Just now, Subaru had, without question, been running.

"—I told you, didn't I? We made a promise, didn't we?"

A voice suddenly reached him.
From straight ahead, from somewhere above the head of the collapsing Subaru, someone was casting down their voice.

He had no strength left to lift his face. His consciousness, amid the spilling innards and flowing blood, was desperately trying to grasp hold of a world slipping further and further away.
His body temperature dropped rapidly, and each time he coughed, his face was smeared with the clot of blood blocking his throat, as he strained with clouding eyes to make sense of the world.

Instinct told him: this is the end.
Understanding that somewhere in his heart, Subaru nonetheless scolded himself that he couldn't let it end like this.
He had to seize hold of something, or he couldn't let it end. He had to grasp at even just one thing, or he couldn't be allowed to end. Something, something, something, something, something, anything at all.

Footsteps sent ripples through the pool, and a black silhouette stood over the blood-red-stained hallway.
Black attire. Slender build. Black hair. A sultry gaze, looking down at him with something like affection.
He recognized them, and, remembering the sensation of "having his stomach cut open," Subaru understood.

Not the Witch Cult—but certainly, a real threat. It was—


"Until we meet again, do take good care of those intestines for me."


A declaration of love beyond all reason.
In that, Subaru grasped hold of something certain—and his consciousness faded.

Fading, fading, fading, dimming, dimming, and then, at last.
Everything vanished, ended, and then—began again.

—The fourth loop raises its curtain.
