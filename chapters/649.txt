Arc 8 Chapter 33: "Judging Love"

——Warden of the "Isle of Swordsmen," Ginunhive, Gustav Morello.

The weight of a position bestowed by the emperor of the great and sacred Vollachia Empire was not light.
In Vollachia, where in peacetime the teaching that "the people of the Empire must be mighty" lived and breathed, the role expected of the events staged on the isolated island — spectacles fought by sword slaves — was exceptionally large.
It was not for mere entertainment's sake. Under the reign of the current emperor, who could stamp out even the embers of internal strife before they caught, it existed so that the hearts of the people would not be kept too far from "struggle," so that the fangs of the Empire's people would not be dulled and rounded off.

It had never been stated so plainly in words, but that was the duty Gustav had taken it upon himself to fulfill when he was appointed warden by Vincent Vollachia, the emperor himself.
And indeed, in accordance with that understanding, Gustav had fulfilled the office of warden of the isolated island more faithfully than any predecessor before him. —Until this very day.

"—Gustav Morello, reporting with the sword slaves of Ginunhive, as ordered to rejoin."

Before him, looking down at him, stood the emperor, Vincent Vollachia. Gustav, a member of the Multi-Arm Tribe, pressed all four of his arms to the ground and knelt as he spoke those words.

—The great civil war that shook the entire Empire had had its very premise shattered by the interference of the dead.
To escape the raging army of the undead, a mass evacuation of the capital and its surrounding residents had been carried out, and warriors — with no distinction made between the regular army and the rebel forces — had been forced into cooperation to resist annihilation.
Even as the dispersal of the overwhelming number of evacuees was pushed forward, more than half of their total number had still ended up sheltered in the fortress city of Garkla. Having joined in supporting that great mass of evacuees, and having entered Garkla themselves immediately after, Gustav was granted the opportunity for an audience with Vincent.

"——"

In a chamber of the great fortress, beside the kneeling Gustav stood Idra Missanga, his face a picture of utter bewilderment.
The band of outlaws that made up the sword slaves had renamed themselves the "Pleiades Battalion," organized with Natsuki Schubert as their figurehead and Gustav serving as their strategist. And it was this Idra whom Gustav, in his role as strategist, had chosen to assist with the duties that fell outside simple brawling.
Among the sword slaves with especially deep ties to Schubert, Idra possessed a fitting measure of thoughtfulness and composure, making him extremely valuable within a band otherwise composed entirely of rough sorts.
That said, there was no showing off that composure now, thrust suddenly before His Imperial Majesty.

"I have received a report on your deeds from Zikr Osman. That one... Natsuki Schubert, too, was piping on about his accomplishments."

"P-piping...ah."

Perhaps from sheer overstrain of nerves, Idra blurted the word out before he could stop himself, his face going bloodless.
Vincent, narrowing his black eyes at him, was famous for a temperament as severe as his features were elegant and delicate. For citizens of the Empire who knew their emperor only through rumor, it was understandable that simply drawing his attention upon oneself would feel like a sentence of death.
And yet, Gustav thought, still looking up at Vincent from his knees—

"Your Imperial Majesty, this one is Idra Missanga. One of the sword slaves, but he has assisted this humble servant with work that none of the others could manage. If it would please Your Majesty, once this present turmoil is settled, a pardon—"

"You speak with unusual freedom today, Gustav Morello. But—"

"—uh."

"If you say he is valuable, then valuable he must be. Strive to make the most of him, then. If you would speak of what comes after the war, then leave behind achievements and a life sufficient to be spoken of when that time comes."

"Y-yes! Yes, Your Majesty! I am overwhelmed with gratitude!"

Idra prostrated himself on the spot with such force that his forehead struck the floor.
Overcome by the sheer relief of having his life spared, Idra — once merely the son of a miller — failed to notice that he had just been shown an extraordinary degree of consideration by Vincent.
Of course, Gustav believed Idra's contributions merited such treatment. But what he felt at the same time was a change in Vincent — a certain generosity now extended toward others.

Vincent had always been a wise emperor — indeed, an emperor almost too wise. Because of that, there had always been a coldness in him toward those who could not share in the far-too-clear view he alone possessed. Precisely because of that, the people around him had been driven to strive, at the cost of their lives if need be, to understand the landscape the emperor saw.
The emperor who had accepted that as right now seemed, somehow, to have become something more than that.

"And yet, you have done something quite reckless."

As if suddenly cutting through Gustav's train of thought, Vincent's voice rang out.
At that single remark from the emperor, Gustav, still kneeling, closed his mouth — its jutting fangs impossible to fully conceal — and waited for what would follow.
Seeing Gustav's silence, Vincent narrowed his black eyes.

"What I commanded of you was the office of warden of the 'Isle of Swordsmen.' That, in this time of crisis, you should abandon that post, lead the island's sword slaves, and come surging all the way to the capital in the distant east... It is a wonder you dared show even the one head you possess before an emperor whom that very act should make you tremble to face."

Seated in his great chair, chin resting on one hand propped against the armrest, Vincent spoke, and beside Gustav, Idra's throat made a faint sound.
It was the very height of disrespect toward the emperor — a situation that any citizen of the Empire would have despaired of, certain they had thrown their life away.

"—May I offer a few words in my own defense?"

"I permit it. Choose your words with care, however. Even should you lose two of your arms, you would still, I imagine, perform as well as an ordinary man?"

Yet Gustav did not sink into despair, and, with all due awe, offered his reply to the emperor. Vincent permitted it with a gaze that could be read as either sadistic or provocative.
Before that gaze, Gustav found himself astonishingly calm — perhaps, if anything, the influence of a boy more bold and fearless than anyone he had ever known.

"Your Imperial Majesty says that this humble servant abandoned the duty of warden, but that is not true. And there is one more matter — it is not this humble servant who leads the sword slaves, but—"

"Not you?"

"—Your Imperial Majesty's own son."

Gustav would never, for the rest of his life, forget Vincent's reaction in the instant those words were spoken.

"——"

For a single moment, Vincent's black eyes widened, and an expression utterly stripped of its usual edge crossed his face.
It was, in every sense, an expression that could only be described as caught off guard. That it happened to Vincent was proof that both the speaker and the content had been entirely beyond his expectation.
That fleeting expression, Vincent erased almost at once, a hand rising to his mouth.

"I had valued you for your fidelity to duty, and your lack of slack."

"This humble servant shares the sentiment. And yet, had matters unfolded exactly as Your Imperial Majesty and I both assumed they would, this one would have had no choice but to watch this great crisis of the Empire from the far western edge of the realm."

Had Schubert not committed the outrageous act of seizing the "Isle of Swordsmen," Gustav would even now, amid this great crisis, still be on the isolated island, wholly absorbed in the management of the sword slaves.
No matter what befell Vincent in the capital, he would have remained obedient to his assigned duty — perhaps thinking to leave behind, as a parting gift, an achievement no one would ever come to praise.
That it had not come to that — that he had been spared from leaving behind such a version of himself — filled him, from the depths of his heart, with relief.

Even if this outcome was only the result of having been goaded by Schubert into a self-serving interpretation of the emperor's decree, exploiting to the fullest possible degree his own discretion in matters of "crisis" in order to race to the capital.

"—And if, along with the capital, the emperor had died as a result, that one there would surely have made good use of the lot of you regardless."

"Your Imperial Majesty?"

Whatever the merit of his own judgment, Gustav had at least believed he could affirm it for himself — but the words Vincent let slip carried, it seemed, some other implication entirely.
Vincent, however, did not allow that hint of an implication to be pursued further.
Instead, he shook his head.

"Very well. I shall be talked into it by your glib tongue. In the midst of a struggle that decides the very survival of this Empire, see to it that you win, splendidly, both your own continued standing and a pardon for your sword slaves. —You are dismissed."

"Yes. This humble servant shall exert himself to the very limit of his arms."

Between Gustav and Vincent, an understanding had been reached regarding how the directive concerning "crisis" was to be treated.
Whether Gustav had truly overstepped the bounds of his duty as warden of the "Isle of Swordsmen" would, from this point forward, be proven by his own conduct and that of the Pleiades Battalion.
He had no certainty that he could see it through. But at this moment, at least, Gustav felt he need not regret having let himself be goaded by Schubert.
And it was just then, as Gustav, the corner of his mouth twisting slightly, made to take his leave of the emperor—

"F-forgive me, but there is something this one wishes to ask of Your Imperial Majesty...!"

Gustav could not help but catch his breath as Idra, forehead still pressed to the floor, abruptly spoke up.
Voice trembling, addressing Vincent with all due awe, Idra — still prostrate — had somehow managed to tilt his face upward, his eyes, on the verge of tears, fixed on the emperor.
Idra's courage, which had shown its mettle even in the island's "Sparka," was admirable, but to press further against an emperor who had just commanded him to withdraw was, frankly, far too reckless an act for a man who valued his life.
And yet—

"—What is it."

At the utterly unexpected response from Vincent, Gustav was once again struck with astonishment.
That opening, Idra — never one to hesitate in seizing an opportunity — did not let slip. Working the intuition of a sword slave accustomed to acting at the very brink of death, he moved his parched lips and voiced the question he had been given leave to ask.
It was—

"After this battle is over, what does Your Imperial Majesty intend to do with your son... with Prince Schubert?"

"Missanga!?"

It was a question so out of place, so ill-timed, that only the bravest man in the Empire could have possibly asked it.

The rumors of the "Black-Haired Crown Prince" that had spread across the land could not be separated from the great civil war now etched into imperial history — a war whose circumstances had since been overwritten by the struggle between the living and the dead.
To begin with, the very reason the Pleiades Battalion had been formed was because Schubert had shown his intent to clash head-on with Vincent — that, in fact, was the battalion's grand objective.
It was only natural, then, that Idra should want that answer from Vincent himself.

The trouble was that Idra, in his overwrought state, had failed to realize just how far this constituted the ultimate form of disrespect — the kind that could well cost him his life.

"——"

At the silence that followed, Gustav became aware of an agitation he rarely allowed himself.
Looking back, nearly every instance of agitation he could recall from recent memory had originated from either Schubert or Cecilus — that Idra should now join their ranks was, frankly, a thing to lament for the world.
Even as he thought so, Gustav himself very nearly fled the reality of the present moment—

"—Idra Missanga."

Vincent's lips spoke Idra's name.
At that, not only Idra but Gustav too widened his eyes and swallowed. Before the two of them, the emperor recrossed his long legs and continued.

"Whether that one's continued standing is right or wrong is not a matter for my concern."

Vincent's answer was one that could hardly be said to make sense.
That Vincent, standing at the very summit of this Empire, should have no right to decide the matter of Schubert's standing — that was simply not something one could accept at face value.
But it was not something Gustav could allow Idra to press any further, either.

"Withdraw while your head is still attached to your neck. —Anything further would be disrespect."

At this clear line the emperor had just drawn — that he would overlook matters up to this point, and no further — Gustav hastily hauled Idra up, lifting him with two arms while clamping his mouth shut with the other two.
Holding aloft what was likely the luckiest man in this fortress city, perhaps in the entire Empire, tonight, Gustav bowed his head deeply to Vincent.
And then—

"We humbly beg your forbearance regarding Prince Schubert's standing, whatever it may be."

Riding on the coattails of Idra's good fortune, Gustav's final word closed out that one scene of pleading his case, having strayed as he had from the "Isle of Swordsmen."

"To make even Gustav Morello say such things. Truly, that man defies easy reading."

Watching the back of Gustav, who had carried Idra off like a condemned criminal and added, at the very last, one remark he needn't have made, Vincent let out a quiet sigh.
That the man had not only flown off, unexpectedly, to the "Isle of Swordsmen," but had bent to his will the entire complement of sword slaves stationed there, along with Gustav himself — a man doggedly, almost foolishly faithful to his duty — was a fact worthy of astonishment.
Frankly, no matter how much the boy himself denied it, it seemed almost more consistent to assume he was, in fact, a "Stargazer."
In any case—

"Even with those added to the city's defenses, we remain far from secure. Nor can we afford to be careless in selecting who strikes at the capital..."

There was much for Vincent to consider in how matters should be arranged.
As for the personnel from the allied Kingdom, their selection could be left to that side's own men of learning; but this side, too, needed to turn over cards of corresponding worth, or there would be no discussion to speak of. Leaving Berstetz and Serena at the fortress city, and with Goz on hand for matters of tactics, that alone should function more than adequately.
In simple terms of fighting strength, the shortage of personnel available to fold into the assault force was a genuine pain, but—

"Those skilled in large-scale tactics cannot easily be replaced. On that count, that alone is worth more than having ten of that Cecilus fellow remain behind. ...Ten of him would be nothing but a nightmare in any case. As for the reason he still has not rejoined us — he likely saw something in the capital that piqued his curiosity. I would prefer, if at all possible, to avoid having the nation destroyed on his account."

The danger of the "Stone Mass," and Cecilus's own nature — these were concerns shared in common by everyone who knew him: the great slaughter of the undead at Cecilus's hands, and the resulting depletion of the great spirit Mana.
Just as Halibel had held back the Black Dragon with the Coupled Dragon Carriages, Vincent did not think Cecilus would fall behind the undead even with his limbs shortened as they were — but the fact that he would not fall behind was itself the problem.

"Should the nation happen to fall on account of that, the blame would fall, I suppose, on Chisha, who picked him up that day. That his own scheme should go awry is, in itself, no small satisfaction..."

He was not so mad as to be able to laugh at that as amusing, nor was it clear whether there was even enough distance from the matter to laugh at all.
Just as Vincent reaffirmed, within himself, the reason for his own urgency—

"—H-hold on, wait a minute! I was told not to let you in, Your Imperial Consort!"

"Th-e-en! I never said that was fine yet!"

An unexpected raised voice sounded through the door, mixing coarseness into Vincent's train of thought.
At the grating, gruff voice and the shrill, angry retort, Vincent looked up just as the door to the room was flung open from the other side with tremendous force.

"Abel-chin! Lend me your face for a sec!"

"I shall not."

Charging in as if she meant to kick the door clean off its hinges was a tall woman with long, dancing golden hair. With two curved blades strapped to her waist, she strode straight before the emperor in an act of sheer, sanity-defying audacity — Medium O'Connell.
Behind Medium, peering in from where he had been stationed at the door, Jamal Aurélie's pitiful face showed itself, and Vincent skewered him with a cold glance.

"I believe I ordered that only those I named be admitted?"

"Y-yes, sir, that's true, but... when it's Her Imperial Consort we're talking about, a lowly soldier like me just doesn't know what to do..."

"Then if a 'General' of rank above yours were to appear harboring treasonous intent, would you be powerless?"

"Yes! No! I mean — between Your Majesty and a 'General,' Your Majesty is definitely higher-ranking, in my thinking. But Her Imperial Consort's... position isn't really clear to me, is the... thing! Sir!"

Choosing his words carefully despite the roughness showing through at the edges, Jamal made his case. Vincent gave up on him for the time being and turned his gaze to Medium, standing planted before him like a guardian statue.
Having stormed in with such fierce breath, Medium made a rather different impression than the account Vincent had been given.

"I was told you had shut yourself in your room, weeping quietly."

"Who said 'weeping quietly'!? That's totally not like me at all! I mean, sure, maybe I cried a little... a little, maybe...!"

"Your brother. Flop O'Connell."

"Bro! Bro! Why would you say something like that~!?"

"It's simple, dear sister. Naturally, so that His Imperial Majesty here would develop a strong interest in Medium and a strong urge to protect her, in preparation for the coming contest for the position of Imperial Consort!"

At Medium's fierce spin to face him, prompted by her turn, it was the vacant-faced Flop who thus confessed his own scheme without the slightest hesitation.
Toward Flop, who had likewise been let through without a word from Jamal, there was nothing left to say — but there was no leisure to indulge these two in a sibling spat here of all places.

"I told Jamal-chin already, but I still haven't agreed to that! I don't dislike Abel-chin, but I don't know anything about being an Imperial Consort or whatever!"

"I see, I see. But sister. You are my sister, yet did you know what a sister ought to do before you became one? You became a sister without knowing much of anything in particular... isn't that so?"

"Eh? Oh — now that you mention it, maybe so..."

"In that case, whether you know how or not isn't what matters in becoming something. What matters is the will to become it, and the surroundings around you. A sister and an Imperial Consort are, at the root, the same thing!"

"Ohh~! I see, you're amazing, bro... wait, no, I'm not falling for that, bro!?"

Having very nearly been swept along for a moment, Medium turned and bore down on Flop fiercely, and Flop set a hand to his own forehead with a limp expression, muttering, "Well, I suppose that one didn't work~."
It did not, in any case, seem to be the outbreak of a sibling quarrel.

"Do not bring matters unresolved between yourselves before me. I am busy. Jamal, remove these two."

"Ah, wait, wait! Whether I'm gonna be Imperial Consort or not can wait for now! What I wanted to borrow Abel-chin's face for wasn't that..."

"What is it."

"I want you to take me to the capital too!"

With a bang, Medium struck her own chest with her hand and stated her demand plainly.
Vincent's brow furrowed at the content of that demand. Then, beside the fiercely determined Medium, Flop raised a single finger. "May I?" he said.

"I'm sure this came as a surprise, but my sister didn't blurt this out on a whim. Right before we arrived in this city, there was something we saw at the very end, aboard that dragon carriage."

"—Balleroy Temeglyph."

"Yes, that's right."

At Flop's nod, unsurprised, Vincent recalled the final struggle aboard the Coupled Dragon Carriages.
Subaru had made the girl he'd taken to calling Spica do something, and as a result, the resurrection of Lamia, now an undead, had been left incomplete — and it was into that incompleteness that Vincent had driven a blade with his own hand.
And it was Balleroy Temeglyph — the "Magic Bullet Marksman," now commanding a dead Flying Dragon — who had spirited away his dying sister once more.
Having witnessed Balleroy there with her own eyes, Medium had indeed spoken.

"Bal-bro..."

A weak, thin murmur from Medium.
Her voice cracking, she had called out again and again to the retreating Balleroy.

"Medium and I go back a long way with Balleroy. We used to be in the employ of Senior Count Dracroy. We knew Balleroy there... I never imagined we'd meet again in a form like that."

"To call that a reunion is, I would say, rather too cruel an irony."

At those words of Vincent's, Flop, in a rare subdued tone, replied, "That's true."
The complicated feelings Medium and Flop held toward Balleroy, now an undead, were understandable — should someone close to you appear utterly transformed from the person they had been in life, it was only natural that it should weigh on the heart.
And yet—

"——"

Vincent would never, under any circumstances, claim to fully understand and sympathize with such feelings.
At the very least, it had been by his own hand that he had delivered the final blow to his own resurrected sister.

"So then, how does the connection between you two siblings and Balleroy connect to your earlier request?"

"Abel-chin, you're smart, so you totally get what I'm trying to say, right? So don't talk like that, it's a really nasty way to put it, you should stop."

"——"

"I want to meet Bal-bro and talk to him. Even if he's become like that, even if I have no idea what he's thinking anymore, I still want to talk to him. Because—"

At first with force, but gradually more haltingly, choosing her words carefully to convey what was truly in her heart, Medium let her words trail off once.
Thinking seriously, trying to choose, correctly, the most important words of all—

"Because I wanted to be Bal-bro's bride."

"—. That is nothing more than sentiment."

Responding thus to Medium's tearful plea, Vincent turned his gaze to Flop.
Medium, on the verge of tears, was the very one Flop had recommended as a candidate consort. His true motive, surely, leaned far more toward concern for Medium's safety than toward ambition.

"Do you not intend to stop your sister from heading into a place of death?"

"Oh dear, Your Imperial Majesty, it seems you don't quite understand how much my sister and I value dialogue in our daily lives. Of course, I tried with everything I had to stop her on the way here, and was thrown off by sheer force, and just had to rewrap my bandages!"

"So you were overpowered by force, in other words. That you would fail to heed your brother's counsel is unexpected."

"I love my bro a ton, and what my bro says is usually pretty much right. But me and my bro are different people, so sometimes I want to do something different. This is one of those times."

Declaring that neither force nor words would stop her, Medium's voice regained its former strength.
At that forceful appeal, Vincent fell silent in thought for a moment.

In terms of simple ability, Medium's fighting strength was, at best, only a notch above that of an ordinary imperial foot soldier.
Even Jamal, who could not manage to keep a simple order, was likely her superior in swordsmanship. It seemed unlikely that bringing her along would contribute dramatically to the course of the battle.
But by the same token, that also meant her presence would not sway the outcome of the war one way or the other.

"—Do as you please."

"—! Really? Abel-chin?"

"Do not act suspicious of your own request being granted. Your presence, whether it exists or not, will not affect the course of the battle. But precisely because of that, the burden you bear will fall on you alone."

"That means..."

Startled by the answer, Medium blinked at Vincent's following words. At her reaction, Flop cut in with "In other words—"

"Protect yourself, by yourself. There will be no strength to spare to protect Medium — that's what he means."

"That's right. Without that resolve, plunging into the midst of battle is—"

"Oh, if that's it, then it's fine! Having to protect myself by myself is something me and my bro have been doing this whole trip anyway."

"——"

Vincent had meant to tell her, on the grounds of her status as a candidate consort, that if she expected to be protected she had come to the wrong place — but his intent was cut short almost at once, by Medium herself.
Medium, who had seemed braced for something dreadful to be said to her, now let out a breath of relief, pressing a hand to her chest as though she'd just been handed a trivially easy condition.

"Geez~, I was so nervous about what you were gonna say~. But I'm glad it wasn't nearly as mean as Abel-chin made it sound like it'd be."

Having been told almost exactly what he'd intended, Vincent's lips twisted faintly in displeasure.
In any case, Medium had voiced her own demand, and declared that she accepted the danger that came with it.
There was, then, nothing more for Vincent to add.

"Tomorrow morning."

"Eh?"

"Tomorrow morning, we depart for the capital with the selected personnel. Make your preparations."

Having stated this briefly, Vincent then turned his gaze to Flop.

"You surely do not mean to say you intend to accompany us as well? I will say now, I have no intention of debating the merits of an aspiring suicide who cannot even protect himself."

"Thanks for the concern. I certainly have no intention of going off to die myself. Nor do I want to be a burden dragging my sister down. ...As for Balleroy, I'll leave that to Medium."

"Bro..."

"—. Though there is no certainty you will even be able to meet him."

Without offering any opinion on Flop's decision, Vincent nonetheless touched on the possibility that the hope in question might never be realized.
And yet, at Vincent's words, Flop only smiled.

"It's fine. I'm sure we'll meet."

"Why do you think so?"

"I believe in fate. He's got a mean streak, but I don't think he's without a sense of style."

Groundless as it was, the conviction carried about as much persuasive weight as Medium's own sentiment.
Yet Vincent chose to avoid remarking on it any further. If he said anything, he suspected, these two siblings would each fire back twice as much in return.
Besides — surely they already knew, without his having to say it.

"Well then, Abel-chin, see you tomorrow! Get some proper sleep, those bags under your eyes are no joke!"

Waving broadly, Medium turned on her heel with a flourish.
In that ramrod-straight back, there was not the faintest trace of the tears she had shown only moments before. If it hadn't been a false show of tears, then all that remained was the renewed impression of a girl whose emotions kept remarkably busy.
And then, just as Flop — who should have followed behind the departing Medium — made to leave, he stopped, for a moment.

"Your Imperial Majesty. Thank you."

"For what, might I ask?"

"For taking Medium's feelings into account — no, ours. And for not reproaching Medium for saying she loved Balleroy. Even though she's a candidate consort."

Lowering the corners of his eyes, Flop set a hand to his own head in that manner of his.
So Vincent, with a snort, answered as if to ask just what Flop thought he was talking about.
As for not reproaching the sentiment behind Medium's wish to see Balleroy again—

"Judge love, you mean? To commit so tasteless an act would surely leave the one who did it more pitiable than anyone else."

At that answer, the fact that Flop broke into an oddly delighted smile was, to Vincent, an irritation to behold.
