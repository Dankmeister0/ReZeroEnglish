Arc 5 Chapter 18: "Between the Dance and the Song"

Having finished his important yet trivial talk with Julius, Subaru left the Water Wing Inn with Emilia and Beatrice in tow.

"You and Julius looked like you were getting along so-o well over there in the garden. What were you talking about?"

"'Getting along with that guy' is already a contradiction in terms, but what do you think we were talking about?"

"Where to hang out next time?"

"Like we're school buddies!?"

Their relationship wasn't nearly as easygoing as Emilia made it sound, and even if Subaru and Julius had somehow attended the same school, the sheer gulf in the campus hierarchy would guarantee they'd never end up in the same clique. A school is a painfully legible class society.

In a sense, wasn't there something just as exclusionary lurking in noble society, too?

"Come to think of it, this world and that one are both about equally cutthroat, huh..."

"C'mon, c'mon, what were you talking about?"

"Just scoping out the enemy camp — picking up on recent gossip here and there. An extension of the conversation from the parlor. What's he into lately, how'd that thing turn out, stuff like that."

"Isn't that just... being friends?"

Emilia tilted her head, puzzled, and Subaru found himself tilting his in turn.

Now that she mentioned it, the sheer pointlessness of the topics did sound kind of friend-like. But this was him and Julius — it couldn't possibly be that. Not friends. Something far more dreadful than that.

He couldn't put a name to exactly what, but—

"Well, we're not friends. That much's for sure."

"Stubborn..."

Emilia gave him an exasperated look, then glanced over at Beatrice beside her. Beatrice answered that look not with words, but with a silent, knowing sigh.

The two of them being so in sync gave Subaru a distinct feeling of being left out.

In any case, what he and Julius had actually discussed in the garden — Reinhard, Wilhelm, and the whole House Astrea mess surrounding that Heinkel — that was something Subaru's conscience balked at relaying to Emilia unfiltered.

Part of it was hesitance to casually air another family's dirty laundry, but the bigger factor was probably not wanting to burden Emilia with needless worry.

It was the kind of thorny problem that couldn't be solved by anyone. The curse carved deep into House Astrea's bones wasn't something an outsider could go poking at lightly.

Julius surely understood that too, which was why he'd confided in Subaru alone. It was also, in its way, an acknowledgment — that Subaru had grown capable of at least this much consideration.

—Somehow it left him with an itchy, nauseated feeling in his gut.

"So, Subaru. I'm happy you invited me for a walk, but what are you scheming?"

"——"

While Subaru wrestled with an irritation he couldn't put into words, Emilia, smiling faintly, suddenly said that.

For a moment, surprise stole the words from him; he blinked, then shrugged.

"That's slanderous, Emilia-tan. Scheming, nothing — I'm purely acting on my heartfelt desire to stroll lovey-dovey through the beautiful water capital with you. I've got only the tiniest, itty-bitty wicked scheme to lure you to some spot where the Water Dragon splashes water everywhere so I can admire a soaked, see-through Emilia-tan."

"Hmm, is that so? Subaru, you really are a stubborn old grump. Even I can tell it's not something as fluffy as that."

"............."

Emilia puffed out her cheeks in a sulk, and Subaru pressed a weary hand to his forehead. Looking to Beatrice for help, the girl walking between him and Emilia looked up at him and, wearing the exact same expression and eyes as Emilia, scolded him right along with her.

Facing a solo battle with no allies, Subaru gave up almost instantly and raised both hands.

"Fine, I surrender. Sorry. I give up on the soaked-and-see-through Emilia-tan plan."

"Su. Ba. Ru."

Called by name in an angry voice, Subaru lowered his raised hands and surrendered again, this time for real.

"Subaru's a pervert."

"Coming from the guy who taught you that word, I gotta say — perfect timing, Emilia-tan. Except right now it's less a scolding and more a reward for me... yes, yes, I mean it in the bad way, really."

"Ohh, honestly!"

Emilia raised a hand, showing the wind-up of a swat before actually swinging, and Subaru gave a wry laugh.

"It's not like I was really hiding it or anything, I just wanted to surprise you. We're headed to the city park in Priestella right now — yesterday I ran into the 'Songstress' there."

"Ooh, that 'Songstress' lady! Um, so, maybe today too?"

"Your eyes are all sparkly, cute. Ye-eah, I wanna make contact with the rumored 'Songstress.' I mean, it's not that I don't trust Otto's negotiating skills, but I trust his talent for screwing things up outside of do-or-die moments just about equally. So this is insurance."

"I see. So you're going to get friendly with the Songstress lady, and have her ask Kiritaka-san on your behalf to hand over the demon ore."

"Exactly. Well done."

Subaru drew a circle above his head to mark her answer correct, and Emilia beamed innocently.

In reality, it wasn't quite as clean a transaction as Emilia made it sound, but there was no need to point that out and deflate her enthusiasm. Emilia was purely looking forward to getting friendly with Liliana. Whatever squirmed behind the scenes, Subaru could handle in secret.

"All that's left is to watch out for Emilia-tan and Liliana causing some kind of chemical reaction..."

"Chemical reaction?"

"Just thinking you two seem like you'd hit it off."

"You think? Fufu, I hope so."

Sorry as he felt toward Emilia's pure excitement, Subaru was already a little worn out just imagining the exhaustion waiting down this road. Praying Liliana would be at the park, while also harboring the faint, contradictory wish that she wouldn't — it was a strange state to be in.

Of course, if she weren't there, it'd just turn into a date with Emilia, which he'd rather avoid. —Though honestly, a plain date sounded fine too.

"Hey, Emilia-tan, wanna go cruising on the Water Dragon with me instead? Feels like that'd be more meaningful, and better for our future together."

"I don't know what cruising is, but if it involves a boat, you'll get seasick, Subaru. I don't want to carry you on my back — sounds tiring."

"And besides, the park's right there. You should give up, I suppose."

Hand in hand, Beatrice tugged the reluctant Subaru forward with brisk steps. This time they reached their destination without getting lost, and once the park entrance came into view, Subaru had no choice but to abandon resistance.

The city park, with its central fountain, was already bustling at this hour between morning and midday. That was because there were even more spectators clustered at the far end of the park than there had been yesterday.

"We got here early, so I figured there was a chance Liliana wouldn't be around, but..."

Judging by the crowd that had formed, that worry had been needless.

Today, too, the Songstress's recital seemed to be a hit — the enthusiastic audience's clapping and calls of encouragement rang out ceaselessly, dominating the air of the park.

"Clapping and calls of encouragement?"

"Sounds like it's a lot livelier today than yesterday, I suppose."

Struck by the same question as Subaru, Beatrice tilted her head hard, too.

Like the broadcast over the Magic Vessel that morning, Liliana's singing generally wove itself softly around the heart, stripping the listener of any sense of reality. Given that impression, the fervor in front of them felt jarringly off.

Like something foreign mixed into what should have been pure.

"Everyone looks like they're having so much fun. As expected of the Songstress."

Emilia watched the commotion, eyes lit with anticipation, but Subaru felt a vague sense of dread. Something told him that going toward the spot the crowd was blocking, the place they'd encircled, would lead to regret.

"——"

But the words never took concrete shape, and Subaru couldn't voice a warning. Besides, Emilia was looking forward to whatever lay ahead. Could he really betray those violet eyes shining with anticipation? Subaru's resolve, at any rate, came too late.

The crowd's fervor, the clapping, swelled at last into a thunderous ovation. Which meant that whatever they'd been encircling had reached its finale. And it meant the spot everyone rose to their feet for, cheeks flushed with excitement, their gazes fixed upon it, would finally come into view.

And there—

"What a wonderful dance! I very nearly wet myself at such extraordinary footwork!"

"You as well were quite something — a song and performance skilled enough to entertain even me. Well done. It's been a good while since I've had this much fun with the performing arts."

There stood the 'Songstress' and the woman in red, clasping hands firmly together.

It was exactly as he'd dreaded.

Today, too, the audience filed by ceaselessly, tearfully relaying their impressions of Liliana's performance and singing.

If there was one difference from yesterday, it was that Priscilla, standing beside Liliana, was also on the receiving end of cheers like "Your dance was amazing!" "I was so moved!" "I'll come see you again!" To which Priscilla, in high spirits, fanned herself with the fan in her hand and nodded back with magnanimous grace.

Once the round of fan interaction wound down, only the pair in question and Subaru's group remained. Noticing them standing there rather awkwardly, Liliana's twin tails bounced up. By what mechanism, exactly?

"Oh-ho-ho! If it isn't Natsuki-sama and Emilia-sama! And Natsuki-sama's little lady is here too — what brings you all this way, might I ask?"

"What's a 'little lady.' Subaru, explain it, I suppose."

"Make the guy who said it explain himself. Here, have a candy and be good."

"Rolling this sweet thing around in my mouth won't distract me... lick, lick..."

Leaving Beatrice, who was already busy rolling the sweet around in her mouth, alone, Subaru walked up to Liliana, who was whipping her twin tails around and around like a dog's tail. Beside him, Emilia's eyes widened at the Songstress's over-the-top reaction, but this was only the beginning of Liliana's true form.

"Been a day since we last met — you're packing them in again today. I take it Kiritaka kicked you out again this morning?"

"Nooo! Well, more or less. Being the beloved type that I am, when someone begs so sincerely, showing them what a woman can deliver is simply my duty!"

"And you ended up giving a recital wherever you got tossed out to, huh..."

Beside Liliana, who was thrusting out a nonexistent chest and stroking a nonexistent mustache, Subaru looked over at Priscilla, arms folded beneath her ample bosom. Noticing his gaze, she snorted, plainly looking down on him.

"What is it, you. I'll forgive your eyes being drawn to my dance, but to keep casting such vulgar looks upon me afterward is nothing short of rude. That men fall prey to my allure is simply their nature, and I'll overlook it — but the only permissible expression of that is to swallow hard, breathe in what lingers of my scent, and lose yourself in fantasy."

"I wasn't watching your dance, and I'm not excited. I'm into pure, wholesome types like Emilia-tan. Someone flaunting her body as hard as you actually kills the mood."

"That you prefer that scrawny half-demon to me — well, there's truly no defending that. Still, even I am not so narrow-minded as to deny the world its share of depraved appetites. And if you know nothing of true beauty, I suppose your shallow judgment can't be helped."

Whatever he said, Priscilla would spin it right back, and Subaru realized the futility of fighting back. It was a clash of values. Against Priscilla, who believed the truth of the world resided in herself, Subaru's common-sense thinking stood no chance.

In any case—

"Dance — you mean Priscilla was dancing?"

"You'd do well to regret missing it. Even I rarely take to my feet unless the mood strikes. That it did so this time is testament to something in this bard's song."

Priscilla answered Emilia's surprised look by gesturing at Liliana. At that answer, Subaru was stunned, and Liliana's eyes rolled back in her head. Ignoring Liliana's reaction, who looked about ready to foam at the mouth, Subaru studied Priscilla closely.

"You danced, huh. Hard to believe, honestly."

"Then how else do you explain the fervor of those fools just now? That bard's voice carries something bewitching in it, true, but without my dance, the audience becomes mere dolls only listening. That, too, is one way to enjoy a song, but not one I favor. Since they're fools anyway, ignorant and dull, it's only fitting that some spectacle color my mood accordingly."

"...So basically, if fools are gonna be foolish, they might as well have fun being loudly foolish about it?"

"Ho. Even a mediocre mind can turn a wheel, it seems."

Priscilla's tone was admiring, but it brought Subaru no joy. Besides, there was every chance Priscilla had already forgotten she'd even seen Subaru at the parlor to begin with. As long as she remembered Emilia, no real harm done, he supposed.

"But, but, Priscilla-sama, Emilia-sama. To have such celebrated figures both grace me with a visit — I'm truly moved!"

It was Liliana's reaction that barely managed to keep the mood, which had been turning hostile, from souring further. Well aware of the animosity between the two, she seemed to be doing her best to balance out the awkward tension for Subaru's group. Her ditzy remarks were surely deliberate.

"Heh-heh. Does that mean my singing is just that wonderful? Oh, gosh, I'm blushing, ehehehehe."

"No, wait, this is her being genuine."

Watching Liliana get carried away and turn all dreamy-eyed and smug, Subaru dropped his shoulders — it had just been wishful thinking on his part. Then, noticing that no one else stood around Priscilla, he added,

"You alone? Where's Al, or the bastard, or the cute little butler kid?"

"Schult loses his way if left to wander. Diligent though he is, that he never manages to accomplish a single thing is precisely what makes him endearing. Al I sent on an errand — kept close, his nagging grows tiresome. As for the bastard, I know not. He's likely haunting some tavern."

"Guess 'the bastard' is a universal term..."

Subaru was surprised to get such a straightforward answer. Along with it came surprise at how poorly Heinkel — supposedly a member of her own camp — was treated.

He understood the man deserved to be treated that way, but if so, why bring him into her camp in the first place?

"Let me guess, you'll say it's because it's amusing or something..."

"You understand well enough. Well, the reason I took him in is trivial. To begin with, I merely judged that the man who came peddling himself might prove useful for entertainment. Should he become a nuisance, I'll cast him aside whenever I please. He, at least, ought to understand that much."

"I dunno... he didn't look like he understood it all that well."

Probably it was precisely because he hadn't understood it that he'd earned Priscilla's displeasure and gotten beaten senseless. Or perhaps, to Priscilla, even that had already faded into oblivion.

"But hey, isn't it dangerous or something? Not that I've got much room to talk, seeing as I'm out walking around with Emilia-tan myself."

"What danger could possibly befall me for the loss of three attendants? Even with them present, the only benefit was, at best, having someone to watch my back."

"Ah, is that so."

It was a ruthless enough assessment that it almost made Al and the others pitiable, being written off as combat assets. But having actually seen Priscilla move, it was hard to dismiss it as anything but plain fact. What Priscilla had shown in the parlor had stepped well beyond the bounds of human capability.

"Come to think of it, I've had my jaw kicked to pieces by you once, haven't I."

Somewhere within the loop centered on the royal capital, Subaru had once earned Priscilla's displeasure and gotten his face kicked in. That time, his body had been sent flying nearly to the high ceiling of the room, and a single blow had driven him to the brink of death — that much was undeniable fact.

Recalling it now, his jaw gave a phantom throb of pain.

"So you left Al and the others behind and wandered off, and ran into Liliana?"

"The cityscape alone proved a fair enough diversion for my ennui. Unlike the cramped construction of the royal capital, this city has sights worth seeing. And so, as I was enjoying the flow of the water, a song happened to reach my ears."

"Well, I admit when she first suddenly barged in dancing, I did wonder what on earth was happening. It happens sometimes, you know — people recklessly mixing themselves into my performance and throwing off the mood. Usually I beat them into submission and repentance with my singing, but—"

"You really don't seem like much of a 'Songstress'..."

Repelling intruders with song — how rock and roll. And honestly, Priscilla's sheer nerve, suddenly barging into a performance with a dance, left him a little stunned too. That she'd managed to captivate the audience that thoroughly meant she must have put on quite the dance.

"To draw the hearts of the people away from me is presumptuous in the extreme, but your song had value enough to warrant it. So then. Come with me. At my estate, I shall grant you the honor of performing your song whenever I so desire."

"——"

Priscilla, apparently quite taken with Liliana's voice, presented her with an outrageous demand entirely characteristic of her. In effect, it was an offer to bring her in as a dedicated musician — but from Priscilla, it carried a strong undertone of coercion.

The more she liked something, the greater her fury at the prospect of it not becoming hers. That kind of terrifying implication lurked beneath the words.

And Liliana—

"Th-thank you so much! What an honor of an evaluation, I'm truly delighted! But! But! But! I must decline!"

Unaware of the terrifying side of Priscilla, and not exactly the type to read the room either, Liliana turned down the offer with the fearless cheer of someone utterly oblivious to danger.

"Ho. You refuse? Why."

Sure enough, the tone of Priscilla's voice dropped in response, and the color in her eyes shifted.

A chill, like the flat of a blade drawn across the back, struck even Subaru, who wasn't even the one involved.

The air had turned to one where a single wrong word could cost a life. In that taut, tension-strung world, Liliana stroked the case of the instrument in her hands.

"I am Liliana, a bard. Even now, entreated as I am, I remain in this city — but in time I am a wanderer bound once more to drift on the wind. Not tied to land, not tied to people — that is the way of life I've chosen as my trade."

"And so you'll not accept my invitation, is that it."

"My mother, and her mother before her, and her mother before that — my whole line has lived this way. We are a family who leaves nothing solid behind, who live only to leave song in people's hearts. No one can cage the wind, no one can silence a song. And so—"

"——"

"Your invitation is an honor, but I must decline. Even I don't know where my song will land — I leave that to the wind."

Holding her instrument aloft, Liliana spoke with proud conviction and not a trace of hesitation on her face.

Gone, for that moment, was the foolish, world-mocking air she habitually wore, and the atmosphere of hers that rubbed at other people's nerves like breathing. There was only the pride of a bard — a creature whose purpose was to carry stories forward through song.

Hearing Liliana's answer, Priscilla stood with arms crossed, one eye closed. And with the remaining eye — red as molten, boiling magma — she stared straight through Liliana.

Then, at the unwavering look on Liliana's face, Priscilla let out a soft breath.

"—Very well. Such conviction amuses me. Forgive me; the crassness here was mine."

"Nooo, not at all. If anything, I'm the one who should apologize, truly."

To Priscilla's softening expression, Liliana responded as though it were the most natural thing in the world.

Watching the exchange between the two of them, Subaru genuinely had no reaction left but stunned disbelief. Catching sight of his dumbfounded face out of the corner of her eye, Priscilla knit her brows in displeasure.

"What is it, mediocrity. What is that face meant to convey?"

"What is it, nothing — what else would it be but shock? Here I was bracing myself, sure you were gonna cleave Liliana in two for refusing you..."

"A foolish worry, that."

Priscilla snorted and spat the words out, but Subaru found that awfully hard to believe.

Until she'd heard Liliana's answer, Priscilla had surely fully intended to kill her. At least to Subaru's eyes, that stretch of suspended time had looked like nothing else but her genuinely unable to decide. Maybe Liliana's survival had simply been the luck of the scales happening not to tip the wrong way.

"But, honestly, I'm a little surprised too. I thought Priscilla was the type who wants to keep anything she wants for herself."

And there — the landmine Subaru had carefully stepped around — Emilia went and stomped right on it.

At Emilia's blunt, straightforward reading of Priscilla's character, Subaru instinctively straightened his back and watched Priscilla warily. But Priscilla merely closed one eye at Emilia's words.

"Spare me your foolish prattle, half-demon that you are. What could your clouded eyes possibly understand of me, to speak as though you knew? There are limits even to rudeness and insult."

"A girl with nothing but a sharp tongue, I suppose. If you've time to pick apart someone else's birth, something they had no say over, you'd do better to reflect on your own conduct — the very thing everyone already thinks of you. That would be far more productive for us both, I'll say."

"Beatrice..."

At Priscilla's merciless verbal assault, Beatrice took the troubled Emilia's hand. Priscilla, hearing Beatrice speak up in Emilia's place, raised an eyebrow as though noticing her for the first time.

"The little girl has quite a mouth on her. I'll have you know, age has no bearing on how far my generosity extends. If you're laboring under the delusion that your youth excuses your insolence, I suggest you correct your attitude at once."

"An unnecessary, uncalled-for kindness, I suppose. I'll say it plainly, little girl. You're the one who'll learn a painful lesson if you think Beatrice is only what she appears to be."

A sharp, crackling hostility sparked between Beatrice and Priscilla.

The two dressed girls apparently had, by all appearances, the worst possible chemistry. Subaru didn't think for a second Beatrice would lose, but the mere fact that this pairing could escalate into a physical scuffle was itself the problem — that was the kind of relationship candidates for the throne had with each other, after all.

"Cut it out, Bea-ko. Priscilla being irritating is a fact, but there's no point going at it with her."

"Don't stop me, Subaru. Doesn't it make you angry, Emilia being mocked like that? I'll rip out the proof of your manhood, I suppose."

"Please stop saying scary things!! And besides..."

Beatrice's anger stemmed from Emilia's worth being belittled. That she set aside the insults aimed at herself to be furious over this first struck Subaru with an oddly misplaced tenderness.

And that went for Emilia too, no one else.

"Beatrice. I'm fine, really."

Emilia pulled back the arm of the advancing Beatrice, gently stroking the girl's head. At that touch, Beatrice's face, for just a moment, looked on the verge of tears — visible only to Subaru's group. But it was only a moment; the expression that turned back to glare at Priscilla was Beatrice's usual one.

"Be grateful your life was spared, I suppose."

"That's a sentiment better directed at yourself. Be grateful for your own adorable face."

Beatrice, nostrils flaring, withdrew, and Priscilla gave a small snort in turn.

Honestly, Priscilla's final line had sounded suspiciously like a compliment on Beatrice's looks, but the indignant Beatrice didn't seem to notice. So — she'd let it go because she was cute? Was that the right read? Hard to say.

"You're a hard woman to figure out, one way or another..."

"Naturally. It's presumptuous in the extreme to think you could peer into a woman — especially me."

"Is this somehow my fault...? This whole thing started because you wanted Liliana in the first place."

In the end, Priscilla's true reasoning for letting Liliana go without keeping her remained a mystery. Almost involuntarily, Subaru put that very question into his gaze as he looked at her. Priscilla hid her mouth behind her fan.

"Everything in this world is mine. That being so, there's no need to keep beautiful things, noble things, at my side. They need only exist as they already do."

"............."

"If the whole world is my garden, it matters not where a singing bird chooses to sing. Caging it is crass. Guarding it from predators is crass. All of it, tiresome."

Priscilla's aesthetic, dismissing Subaru's reasoning as foolish.

Faced with her way of being — one that admitted nothing else close — Subaru lost his words.

The meaning, the logic — it wasn't as if he couldn't grasp it. But the scale of what she saw and the scale of what Subaru saw were worlds apart. Which meant Subaru would probably never, not in his whole life, understand her.

Frightening, he thought, plainly. And yet, he also thought this: that some might yearn to look up at that very fear.

Maybe that was the reason Al stayed at Priscilla's side, too.

"C-c-come now! Here's an idea! Now that everyone's settled down, why don't I, in the spirit of friendship, treat you all to a song!"

Liliana suddenly proposed this, cutting through the mood that had built up until then. Pulling the Lyulyre from its case, she plucked its strings in a blazing-fast riff, spinning in place as she drew every gaze onto herself.

"This time, Priscilla-sama, please, no joining in with dance — just purely enjoy the song! I'll show you my absolute full-power, no-holds-barred So-o-o-ngstress self!"

"Ho."

At Liliana's grand boast, Priscilla's face took on an interested look.

"And Emilia-sama, it seems you arrived right at the tail end of the last song. If possible, I'd love for you to understand me not merely as a singer who trades on her cuteness, but as a bard raking in coin through blessed vocal talent and masterful instrumental skill!"

"Wow, really?"

"That's a pretty unglamorous way to want to be understood — are you satisfied with that?"

Whatever Liliana's claims, there was no doubt Emilia was genuinely interested in her performance and singing. Even distracted by the exchange just now, Emilia's face showed she was eager to take Liliana up on her proposal.

Emilia and Priscilla stood at an awkward distance apart, and Liliana began preparing to perform. Before that, though, she waved Subaru over and, in a hushed voice, said:

"Natsuki-sama, Natsuki-sama. From what I can tell, are Emilia-sama and Priscilla-sama perhaps not on the best of terms?"

"'From what you can tell,' nothing — of course not, if you know their standing. Plus, Priscilla's basically got bad chemistry with everyone by default, so that's how it ends up looking like that with Emilia-tan."

"That is a grave matter indeed!"

Liliana gasped, her hair springing up like the alert tail of a dog. Did she have nerves running through it or something? He kind of wanted to grab it and yank it around.

"Well then! Allow me to bare a shoulder and dazzle the two of you with the world of song. Ah! Just now, at 'bare a shoulder,' did you imagine something lewd? Naughty, naughty, that won't do at all!"

"Could you not make me feel impressed and disgusted by the same sentence at the same time? It's exhausting."

Impressed despite himself at Liliana's brand of considerate lunacy, Subaru sighed. Clearing the bad air with a song — that much he understood. Both parties were interested in Liliana's singing; surely even Priscilla wouldn't pick a fight with Emilia in the middle of a song by an artist she liked.

"For the after-song chat, Natsuki-sama, why don't you go prepare some snacks? I'm sure if you bring along something sweet, it'll lift the mood and bring everyone closer together in an instant, don't you think? Don't you think?"

"Not really."

"For the after-song chat, Natsuki-sama, why don't you go prepare some snacks? I'm sure if you bring along something sweet, it'll lift the mood and bring everyone closer together in an instant, don't you think? Don't you think?"

"What is this, some kind of skit where nothing proceeds unless I say yes?"

At Liliana's pushiness — repeating herself word for word, tone for tone, without the slightest variation — Subaru gave up and picked "yes." Liliana's face lit right up, though he supposed she had a point.

He hadn't imagined that this level of consideration for others — a fairly advanced social skill — could even exist as common ground between humanity and Liliana-kind.

"So anyway, I'm gonna go buy something to snack on while Liliana sings. Emilia-tan, don't get into a fight, just wait here nicely, okay?"

"Oh, don't worry, I'll be fine. It's not like I want to fight with Priscilla either, so there's no need to worry."

Subaru said it to Emilia just in case, and she gave a light laugh in response. Not that he doubted Emilia, but there was no denying the possibility that Priscilla would pick the fight even if Emilia never provoked her.

"Beatrice. If anything happens, I'm counting on you to look after Emilia-tan."

"Understood, I suppose. If that girl says anything else pointless, Beatrice will reduce her to cinders, I suppose."

"Please don't get into a fight either, okay?"

Leaving the aftermath to Beatrice, whose blood was arguably running even hotter than Emilia's, Subaru turned to leave the park. But before that—

"Priscilla, is there anything you can't eat?"

"How surprising. Does a function called consideration exist even in a mediocrity like you? Very well. If you intend to offer something to me, prepare something befitting. Should you present me with something worthless, I'll return your head upon the very palm that offered it."

"It's not like I lost rock-paper-scissors and got stuck running errands — why do I gotta be slapped with such a brutal condition!?"

If some shop was selling some provocatively exotic delicacy, Subaru figured he'd buy it and hand it over out of spite. Priscilla, for her part, frowned in displeasure at his answer.

"Rock-paper-scissors... rock-paper-scissors?"

She tilted her head, murmuring the words.

Given that she'd forgotten Subaru entirely, it was entirely possible she'd forgotten they'd even played rock-paper-scissors at all. In more ways than one, she really was a girl not worth the effort of dealing with.

"Subaru, take care, okay?"

"Call Betty right away if anything happens, I suppose."

Seen off by Emilia and Beatrice, Subaru waved back. Then, with a perfunctory wave to Liliana too, who closed both eyes attempting a wink, he took off running out of the park.

A short distance away, the melody of the Lyulyre reached him faintly on the wind. Listening to it at his back, Subaru hurried onward, pouring strength into his kicking legs, determined to get back in time to be let in on at least a bit of the recital himself.

And then, ten minutes after Subaru left the park.

"Man, I really am spineless, huh."

Finished with his shopping and having left the store, Subaru looked at the contents of the paper bag and sighed.

Sent off on the errand under the pretext of "preparing snacks," Subaru had picked out a suitable shop and finished his shopping quickly. Along the way, he'd been drawn in by curiosity toward a bizarre Priestella specialty delicacy called "Guinagery," but he hadn't been able to muster the courage to buy it for Priscilla and bring it back.

Fearing it might worsen relations between their two camps would sound like a decent excuse, but honestly, the real reason was simply that Priscilla's reaction had seemed too terrifying to risk.

"Still, it did kinda resemble eel jelly... wonder if it tasted like it too. Pathetic of me not to have the nerve to check, but hey, I don't hate that about myself."

Muttering this complicated bit of self-reflection, Subaru retraced his steps toward the park at a light jog.

He'd only been away ten minutes, and there'd been no sign of trouble transmitted through the Pact-bound pathway from Beatrice's end. Even knowing that, a man's heart still wanted to hurry back sooner — but—

"Whoa, sorry 'bout that."

Turning a corner at a jog, he nearly collided with someone the instant he stepped into the plaza. He dodged in time, but turned back at once to call out.

"My bad. I don't think we actually touched, but you okay?"

"Hey now, buddy. Is that any way to apologize? You oughta show a little more sincer— ugh!"

The man he'd almost collided with responded to Subaru's apology in a coarse voice. He looked ready to start something, but the moment he actually saw Subaru, his expression changed.

At the same time, Subaru's own face turned exasperated.

"Oh, it's Chin. You're still working thug-lite for hire even after Felt hired you?"

"Shaddap! I told you, it's not Chin! Why the hell are you even wandering around here too!?"

Spitting as he shouted, this was none other than Rachins, who'd kept up his thug routine plenty yesterday too. According to Felt, he'd been given errands and was supposed to be staying at a different inn elsewhere in the city, but—

"Aren't Ton and Kan usually with you? Rare to see you alone."

"'Rare,' my ass — what do you even know about me? We ain't close enough for you to find it 'rare.' Ugh, whatever, get lost."

"Don't be so cold. We've been through a life-and-death ordeal together, haven't we?"

"No we haven't!?"

Rachins reacted with considerable reluctance to Subaru's overly familiar attitude.

Even Subaru wasn't sure why he felt this much fondness for the guy. Probably his inner "ordinary person" sensor recognized the bumbling duo as fellow ordinary folk. In a world where seemingly everyone he met was some kind of extraordinary person, running into someone like them occasionally brought blatant relief.

Kind of nerve of him, given the man had once actually killed him.

"Anyway! Don't bother me! I'm working right now!"

"You, working, instead of causing trouble without a steady job... I'm just so happy for you."

"Who talks like that!"

Clicking his tongue at Subaru's mock crying, Rachins shook him off and vanished into the crowd. Given the cold treatment, Subaru scratched his head in self-reproach. His bad habit of misjudging appropriate distance apparently still reared its head sometimes, if he wasn't careful.

Watching Rachins disappear into the throng, Subaru stepped forward again, meaning to head back to the park. His foot, however, suddenly stopped.

"Hm?"

Subaru cocked his head, a confused sound catching in his throat.

Before his eyes was the cause of everyone's stopped feet — the sight of people, all halted in place.

The crowd Rachins had melted into had, all together, come to a stop. Seeing that, Subaru too found himself stopping without meaning to. Meanwhile, Rachins, who'd supposedly vanished into the crowd, found his path blocked by the wall of stationary people and shoved his way back out, clicking his tongue.

"The hell — what's with everybody? What are you all staring at?"

Spitting the words out irritably, Rachins looked up in the direction the crowd had stopped and turned their gazes toward — up overhead, at the rooftop of a tall building.

It was an especially tall building, one that served a role like a clock tower, with a magic crystal embedded at the tip of its height. Such structures, standard fixtures in any large city or town, were called Time Towers, and it was typical for a single town to have several installed.

In the city of Priestella too, Time Towers for checking the time were scattered here and there throughout the city. The one before them now was one among that number.

But—

"—Well now, hello, everyone. Sorry for the disturbance. My apologies."

From the open window of the Time Tower, a figure had stepped out, standing at the perilous edge.

The figure, drawing the crowd's eyes with a bizarre appearance, spoke with a voice trembling as though moved by the sensation of being watched.

"Thanks. Just borrow a little of your time, if you would."

Even while voicing an apology, the voice prioritized its own will over any real sense of contrition — something oddly self-righteous about it. The trembling voice cracked and broke, and there was something deeply unpleasant about it, as though it clawed at the hearts of those who heard it with a reckless, feral hunger.

That strange sensation was probably owed, in large part, to the person's grotesque appearance.

—The figure's head was wrapped haphazardly in bandages, and from the small exposed portion, glittering eyes glared down over the world. Its body was tightly wrapped in a black coat, and long, misshapen chains were bound around both arms, their ends dragging along the floor as the figure paced restlessly back and forth atop the tower.

To the crowd, unable to tear their eyes from the grotesque sight, the figure smiled — or rather, twisted the portion of its mouth visible beneath the bandages into something meant, presumably, to resemble a smile, in a grim and dismal way.

"Sorry. I'm with the Witch Cult. I hold the position of Sin Archbishop of 'Wrath.'"

Uttering that fearsome title, the figure introduced itself.


"—My name is Sirius Romanée-Conti."


【Malice】said this, and laughed.
