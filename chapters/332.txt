Arc 5 Chapter 16: "The Uninvited Guest"

The finest of moments, ruined by the worst possible means.

The red-haired man's conduct was, if it must be named, a species of malice — no, of ugliness.
His ruddy face carried the faint reek of drink, and he stroked his stubble-shadowed cheek with a lecherous grin. Somewhere around forty, by the look of him. That the small gesture, and his slovenly grooming, provoked disgust well out of proportion to their scale was because the man's raw material — his features themselves — could hardly be called anything but fine.

Something beautiful, defiled into a shape it was never meant to take.
There was something about the tall man's figure that stirred, for whatever reason, that most fundamental revulsion.

"...Who the hell are you?"

"Ahn?"

While everyone else in the room fell silent in shock, it was — of all people — Subaru who spoke first. His hand went behind his back, fingers closing around the hilt at his waist.
Blood rushed to his head; a violent impulse swept over him.
Beneath it lay fury — fury that the fragile, hard-won reconciliation between two clumsy people had just been trampled.

Why Subaru was so consumed with rage, no one but Subaru could have said.
A reconciliation between a friend and a man he respected. No. Wrong.
A reconciliation between family. A family, finally, reaching for the bond they'd wanted.
And that —

"Answer me. Who are you?"

"...That's a hell of a hostile look you're giving me, brat. Freshly minted knight, and you don't even know whose fight you're picking?"

"Don't make me laugh, old man. You're the one who started this. I'm just the one buying it at your asking price."

Subaru's patience finally ran out, and he rose to his feet where he stood.
Beside him, he felt Beatrice shift her posture, ready to take his hand at a moment's notice, responding to the anger radiating off him. His dependable partner had caught, precisely, the flame of fury now rising in Subaru's heart.
Seeing him rise, the man scratched roughly at his head with an expression of distaste.

"Annoying brat. Oi. Sword Saint, Juukulius, whoever — hell, Argyle'll do too. — Cut this rude little brat down."

"——!"

Still pointing at Subaru with the hand he'd been scratching his head with, the man issued the order in an offhand, high-handed tone to the three of them — to Reinhard and the others in the room.
It could only be taken as an insult to his comrades, or at the very least people he could call that, and Subaru bared his teeth and started to move to slap the man across his face.

"If I may."

But the motion was cut off, at the last instant, by Julius's voice breaking in.
Julius had risen at some point without Subaru noticing, and now lightly gripped his shoulder from behind. Subaru turned; Julius gave him a small nod, then faced the man squarely.

"At present, myself, Ferris, and Reinhard — all three of us are on special assignment, detached from our regular duties. Which means that even as vice-captain, you should hold no authority to command us."

"Yup-yup. Feri-chan's currently serving Lady Crusch, in name and in fact. So we caaan't obey your orders."

Riding on Julius's words, Ferris — still seated — clung to Crusch's arm and offered his own flippant reply. Crusch, her arm suddenly claimed, looked mildly startled, but her eyes, filled with firm resolve, glared at the red-haired man all the same.
Looking around, the expressions of everyone else in the room were much the same, hostility toward the man plain on every face. Naturally so. The atmosphere of this gathering had belonged to all of them, shared — and this man had just smashed it to pieces.
And yet —

"Whoa, whoa, scary, scary. Obviously a joke. Even if I'm just a figurehead vice-captain, I know that much about the order's rules."

"...Figurehead... vice-captain?"

The man, reeking of drink, slapped his knee and laughed at their answer as if something about it amused him. Catching an unmissable word in that exchange, Subaru knitted his brow and voiced the question.
At that, the man skewered Subaru again with a mocking glance.

"That's right, figurehead. The Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's Order of Pegasus Knights' unwanted, universally-disliked figurehead vice-captain. 'Deadweight' Heinkel — that's me."

"Don't get all defiant about being deadweight and disliked."

"Kaha! Now that stings. Ow, ow, it stings so much I can't take it... maybe I should shut that mouth of yours, you little brat."

"——!"

A chill ran down Subaru's spine at the dark shadow that flickered across the man's narrowed eyes.
It wasn't like the feeling of facing a powerful opponent, or coming into contact with something overwhelming like the White Whale or a Witch. It was something else, harder to put into words.
Subaru felt he'd known it, somewhere close, before. But he couldn't recall, in the moment, what it had been.
Only an inescapable revulsion gripped him, conjuring an illusion like ringing in his ears.

"Calm yourself, Subaru. Don't let the vice-captain's air overwhelm you."

Julius spoke at his side, to a Subaru whose head was swimming.
Hearing this, the man — Heinkel — turned a gloomy smile on Julius.

"Hah. Just what you'd expect from the Best Knight. A model, well-mannered answer. Add a knight's actual skill to that, and no wonder you've collected all this orthodox respect."

"I'll take that as a compliment, Vice-Captain Heinkel. ...By the way, might I ask what brings you here this time? I recall your duty is the protection of the royal castle in the capital."

"Don't get snide with me. What difference does it make if I'm gone for a while? Captain Marcos will handle the castle guard just fine. And if that's not enough... ah, well, there's no royal family left to fall victim, is there."

"Heinkel!"

Coming from a man in his position, the remark was beyond disrespectful.
Hearing it, it was Wilhelm who roared Heinkel's name in fury.

The Sword Devil glared at the still-standing Heinkel with a terrible expression. His lips trembling, Wilhelm shook; Heinkel merely shrugged.

"Heinkel..."

"You only need to say it once. My hearing's not so far gone yet. Well, just take it as a drunk's nonsense and let it pass. — More importantly —"

Wilhelm's voice was bitter. Heinkel picked at his ear with a finger and closed one of his blue eyes. Then, looking at Wilhelm with the open one, he went on.

"Isn't it a bit cold of you? I wanted to offer my own congratulations for the White Whale's subjugation, and you just walked right past me. Fourteen years, that job took. I ought to have the right to celebrate too, to share in the joy. Don't you think so, Father dear?"

"Heinkel, I..."

"Reinhard! You feel the same, don't you?"

"——"

With an expression steeped in malice, Heinkel dug into Wilhelm's heart.
Pain like a blade cut across the old man's face, but Heinkel paid it no mind. Cutting off the protest rising in Wilhelm's throat, he turned the next edge of his malice toward Reinhard.
Reinhard, who had watched the situation unfold in silence all this while, slowly raised his eyes toward the man who had just called his name.

"Didn't a weight come off your shoulders too, thanks to Father dear? The great grandfather who avenged his wife, his mother, his grandmother. Surely you offered him a word or two of thanks? After all..."

"——"

"— he's the one who avenged the very predecessor that *you* let die, isn't he?"

— Let me retract what I said before.
I called Heinkel's expression something steeped in malice. That was wrong.

It was **malice** itself.
In Heinkel's words, in his expression, his bearing, his voice, his gestures, his gaze — in everything about this scene that could express his existence — there was nothing to call it but **malice**.
Pure, unadulterated malice was poured into every single thing Heinkel did.

"The predecessor... you let die...?"

The words Subaru managed to voice, in a murmur, were simply the ones that had struck his ear most vividly.
There had been several other threads he might have grasped. But Subaru, with no room left to sort things by reason, had instinctively seized on that one.
And malice never lets such an opening slip by.

"That's right, the predecessor you let die. I don't know how ignorant you are, kid, but even you must know the title 'Sword Saint,' at least. Our current Sword Saint here is called the strongest and greatest in history, but that position — she got it by letting the previous one die. Her own grandmother. It's all been kept nice and hidden, though."

"Stop it, Heinkel! You... just how far are you going to..."

"Don't give me that righteous act, Father dear. Of all people, you're the last one with the right to criticize me. You're the one who first accused Reinhard of killing the predecessor — nobody else."

"——!"

There was in Heinkel's words a viciousness like the concentrated essence of every hatred and curse in the world.
And the content itself was likewise nothing but slander too vile to bear hearing.

Reinhard, having killed the predecessor. Impossible.
Wilhelm, having filthily reviled Reinhard for it. Impossible.

Because to Reinhard, the predecessor had been —
And to Wilhelm, Reinhard was —
So there was no way such a thing could be true — and yet—

"——"

Neither Reinhard nor Wilhelm closed the accusation off with a denial.
Why. Just one word — say it isn't true. If they'd only dismiss it as nonsense, Subaru could believe it without hesitation.
A comrade-in-arms and a mentor he respected, against a drunken villain. There shouldn't even be a question of which to believe.
So he wanted, from the two of them, just one word — something to let him keep his faith.

"Silence, once it becomes inconvenient. That's how it's been for fourteen years. Neither of you has changed one bit. And if you haven't changed, there's no way you could ever really make up. Theresia van Astrea would never allow something so convenient."

In the silence that fell over the room, only Heinkel's blasphemy still echoed.
The former Sword Saint whose name he'd spoken — Wilhelm's wife, and Reinhard's grandmother. And, presumably, to Heinkel —

"My dead mother curses us. All three generations of us — none of us will ever be forgiven."

Reinhard's father. And Wilhelm's son.
From everything Heinkel had said and done up to this point, Subaru had correctly grasped exactly who he was.

"Heinkel van Astrea..."

Saying it aloud, he felt how well the name's ring fit.
Without question, the man before him was someone who bore the Astrea name. However far his character stood from the straightforwardness Subaru knew in House Astrea.

"Don't tack on the 'van,' brat. Unfortunately, I haven't earned that honor. When you address me, it's Heinkel Astrea."

"...?"

Catching Subaru's bitter mutter, Heinkel clicked his tongue.
Something flickered across his face just then — perhaps the first pain Heinkel had shown in this whole scene. In eyes that, while reviling his family, had held nothing but a dark pleasure, pain now flashed for the first time.
— Not that Subaru cared, and dismissed it at once.

"So... what did you come here to do?"

"Emilia?"

Everyone had been trembling in shock at the sheer excess of Heinkel's words and conduct.
But it was Emilia who rose to her feet in that moment, and was the first to condemn what he'd done.

Standing before Subaru, the girl with silver hair rippling down her back spoke with a quiet anger in her voice.
This was Emilia. She wasn't angry over the mood of the gathering being ruined, or over Heinkel's rudeness — nothing so simple as that.

Whenever she got truly angry, it was always for someone else's feelings.
She understood, too, that Reinhard and Wilhelm had been hurt.

"We were having a lovely meal together, just now. And you went out of your way to ruin it like this — what is it you're trying to do?"

"...Well, well. So you're the Lady Emilia. I've heard the rumors. The pitiable half-elf princess, dragged into a fight she can't win, or something like that."

"What you think of me is something I'd like to talk about someday, but that's not what we're discussing now. There's only one thing I want to know. What did you come here to do?"

"——"

Subaru caught the way Heinkel, having tried to provoke and unsettle Emilia, was thrown off by her steadiness. Emilia's composure seemed to catch not a few of the others in the room off guard as well.
A natural enough surprise, given how different this was from the innocent, guileless girl she'd shown herself to be from yesterday up until now. That she'd deliberately played the part of an artless maiden, precisely to invite this kind of surprise — that would be a lie. It was simply who she was.

"That we would all gather here was Anastasia-san's decision. That everyone happened to be able to gather at once was a coincidence, and I don't think you were lying in wait for a chance like that — not even if you're some important person in the Order of Pegasus Knights. Tell me what this is about."

"Tch. This isn't what I was told..."

"Answer me."

Clicking his tongue and roughly scratching his head, Heinkel found himself cowed by Emilia.
Emilia was angry, but she was by no means in a battle-ready stance. The pressure she gave off had nothing to do with mana — it came purely from the force of will Emilia herself possessed.

"Storming in here all confident and then wilting just 'cause a woman glares at ya — old man, you're lookin' pretty pathetic, y'know."

"That's the truth. Here Ah was, lookin' forward to hearin' somethin' interestin', and instead this — watchin' the songstress over there'd be far more outlandish and entertainin' than this."

"My, my, is that so? Well then, perhaps we ought to see this uncouth gentleman off, and spend some time with the rumored songstress ourselves."

"——!"

Following Emilia, Felt, Anastasia, and Crusch opened up with supporting fire.
Just as Emilia faced down Heinkel, the same degree of overwhelming presence radiated from the other three toward the uncouth intruder, and pressed by all four at once, his cheeks twitched faintly.

Different league entirely, one might say.
A rather paltry showing, set against his title and his standing as a party of interest.

"Have you satisfied yourself, Vice-Captain? If you have no other business here, it would be best for both our sakes if you took your leave."

Judging Heinkel's complexion and the mood of the women in the room to both have reached their limit, Julius offered Heinkel a way out. Subaru had thought it might be best to break Heinkel's spirit here and now, but seeing Reinhard's and Wilhelm's still-troubled faces, he held off.
Without a proper grasp of the circumstances, he cautioned himself against too hasty a decision.

"Ngh...!"

"Vice-Captain. Your decision, please. If possible, it would be best for both of us if you said nothing more..."

"— There's no need for that, mediocrity."

That voice was strangely lush, and filled with an arrogance that looked down on everything.
The magnificent voice, the kind that made a listener shudder, forced its own absolute sense of superiority upon everyone else without mercy.
A brazen act, trampling common sense and carving out new rules of its own — and that it could get away with such a thing was owed entirely to an outstanding presence that permitted neither objection nor rebuttal.

Everyone in the room looked up, eyes turning toward the sliding door behind which Heinkel stood.
Someone was approaching down the hallway, footsteps sounding on the floor, and everyone's attention converged on it. Heinkel was already gone from anyone's mind.
All that remained was wariness of a sun-like heat drawing closer down the corridor.

"So, the rabble has all gathered its faces here, has it. Well done, arranging a stage for me to grace with my presence. For that alone, I shall praise your efforts."

The hem of a blood-red dress, boldly cut low at the chest, swept as she turned; a fan hid her mouth in a gesture straight from the wicked-woman playbook. Her arms cradled a bosom too lush for modesty, lifting it, an alluring gesture that flaunted pale skin without the least shame.
Crimson eyes surveyed everything as though licking it with flame, and her bewitching air was the very incarnation of a demonic charm that captivated and enslaved anything male in this mortal world.

A face of violent beauty, unforgettable once seen.
Even beauty, carried past a certain point, becomes violence. Her very existence taught as much.

The girl's name was Priscilla Barielle.
The fifth Royal Selection candidate — one who, by rights, had not been invited to this banquet at all.

"Still, my, my. Today too, gloomy faces frozen stiff in a gloomy air. Do you lot enjoy this stagnant atmosphere? Or is it simply that whenever your faces gather together, this sort of air is bound to seep out regardless? If so, how pitiable."

Surveying the room, Priscilla wasted no time frowning and letting such words slip. She fanned her face with her fan, a provocative flourish thrown in for good measure.
Struck speechless by her abrupt appearance, Subaru and the others could do nothing but take her contempt full in the face, defenseless.

"Such poor reactions. I have gone to the trouble of coming here myself. Surely it is only proper courtesy for the lot of you to press your foreheads to the floor in unison and worship and praise me."

"...Whose religious organization do you think you're the head deity of? Even if you actually became queen, a sight like that would never happen."

"Hmm?"

At Priscilla's self-serving nonsense, Subaru couldn't help but cut in. Catching that mutter, Priscilla tilted her head, her red eyes snaring Subaru.

"What."

"...Who are you, wretch? I was told this room held only fools presumptuous enough, beyond their station, to compete with me for the throne. Setting those aside, the rest should be nothing but their tasteless followers, lacking any eye for worth — so why is a mediocrity such as yourself mixed in among them?"

"You're kidding me."

Faced with what was, genuinely, fairly dangerous hostility, Subaru muttered, all the fight drained out of him.
There was no hint of jest in it, no sense that she was mocking him. In other words — she meant it. Priscilla, in complete sincerity, had utterly forgotten Subaru's existence.
Despite a year's blank gap, granted, but he'd have thought their encounters and dealings had left at least some impression — and yet here she was, blithely...
Thoroughly Priscilla-like, if one wanted to call it that, but the situation and his own state of mind weren't in any shape to welcome it wholeheartedly.

"Now, Princess. Even for you, that's a bit much, don't you think? Might not amount to much to you, Princess, but far as I'm concerned, this brother of mine's a fairly interesting fella, y'know?"

Cutting through an atmosphere that was about to blend worst with worst, a glib voice broke in.
The voice was somehow muffled, and came with a clacking metallic sound besides. Tromping down the hallway, drawing level with Priscilla, was a one-armed man.

His head was hidden beneath a jet-black helmet concealing his face, his get-up as coarse and rough as a bandit's — an eccentric man. Priscilla's attendant, and, like Subaru, a summoned soul from another world: Al.
Naturally, having accompanied his mistress, he cut in between Priscilla and Subaru.

"C'mon, you remember, right? Might've slipped your mind, but back at the castle, at the declaration of candidacy, wasn't there some guy who made a huge fool of himself in front of everybody? That's the guy. Even you, Princess, laughed yourself silly over it back then."

"I have no memory of it. In the first place, Al, there is no possibility I would ever laugh myself silly over anything. Do not degrade a noble existence such as myself to the level of some casual village girl. Even you — next time, I shall take your head without mercy."

"Well, sorry, brother. My bad, guess I don't carry enough weight around here. Try and get friendly with her again sometime, yeah?"

"You should've spent this past year building up some pull, man!"

Abandoning any hope of reforming his mistress's attitude, Al turned promptly to apologize to Subaru instead. The sight of him going "my bad, my bad" showed not a trace of change over the past year, and Subaru could only sigh.
For a man who'd greeted middle age while still this glib, perhaps expecting a year to change him much at all was simply asking too much.

"You're late, Lady Priscilla. Just how long did you plan on leaving this to me alone, huh? I heard you'd be right along, so I..."

"Do not chirp, mediocrity. When I command a mediocrity to dance, it is that mediocrity's duty to dance until death, unless told to stop. To mistake that, and imagine yourself entitled to correct me, is a delusion worth dying for."

"Ngh..."

Meanwhile, Heinkel — sensing the mood of the room had shifted — turned to snap at Priscilla, standing just behind him. But his complaint too fell on entirely deaf ears with her.
Heinkel looked stymied, unable to get through to Priscilla, and Subaru, listening to their exchange, narrowed his eyes.

"Priscilla. Did you bring him here?"

"You there, mediocre one. Whose permission did you obtain to address me so familiarly? Even I, generous as a merciful mother, have limits to my tolerance for insolence."

"Princess."

Al spoke her name, short, to Priscilla, whose eyes had taken on a cruel light as she regarded Subaru. At that, Priscilla closed one eye and let out a small breath.

"For some reason, you seem to have oddly won my attendant's favor. That life of yours shall be spared, thanks to Al — no, there is no need to thank Al. Worship me instead. That shall settle the debt."

"...Much obliged for your generous consideration. Now, about that question."

"Whether it was I who brought this mediocrity along. If so, your suspicion is correct. Just so. I summoned him and sent him here."

"What for!"

At Priscilla's affirmation, Subaru raised his voice to demand her purpose.
An uninvited guest, and a guest that guest had, in turn, dragged along — uninvited to a degree that went beyond mere excess. He needed to know, at the very least, what intention lay behind it.
But at Subaru's question, Priscilla merely tilted her head.

"If I must say, it is because it seemed as though it would be amusing."

"...Amusing?"

"Just so. A twisted family portrait, and the tragicomedy born of human relationships. It quite thrills the heart, as a truly hideous spectacle. And indeed, was it not quite the show? A Sword Saint and a Sword Devil both baring a human face at once — that is not something one sees every day."

"Priscilla!"

At words more depraved than mere viciousness, Subaru flared with rage.
Just as Priscilla said, the inner turmoil Reinhard and the others had shown was a rare thing to witness. And by rights, it was nothing but a sea of suffering that need never have been witnessed at all.
If Heinkel simply hadn't interrupted, the two of them showing their human faces to each other would have been enough, and good. That alone would have let the two of them return to the ordinary bond of grandfather and grandson.
And that —

"Stop it, brother. There's no profit in you and me tanglin' here. The Princess's rotten personality, that's just how it always is. Chalk it up to bad luck — bad stars — and let it go, yeah?"

"If you know your mistress has a rotten personality, you're the one who should be holding the reins properly. Just letting a rampaging horse rampage is downright irresponsible."

Al, with his one arm, held Subaru back as he tried to step forward, slowly shaking his head. With his one arm using itself to restrain Subaru, it went without saying that Subaru couldn't draw his sword on the spot.
In other words, by that single act, he was declaring he had no intention of fighting.

Realizing this, Subaru let out a long breath and belatedly realized he was the only one in the room still spoiling for a decisive clash.
The candidates, of course, but Julius and Ferris too showed no sign of wanting to escalate things.
Naturally. Gathered in this place were the stars who might, awesomely, come to bear the weight of the kingdom's next generation upon the throne — no one wished to see them wound each other out of anger.

"But then does that mean it's fine for their hearts to be hurt as much as anybody likes...!"

"Subaru..."

Emilia called to Subaru, her eyes wavering faintly, as he let unbearable anger spill onto his tongue. Feeling a tug at his sleeve, he realized Beatrice too had taken hold of his hand.
Receiving that consideration from the two of them, Subaru let his head drop, shoulders slumping.

"It seems the mutt's howling has ended. I myself came here today purely to see your howling faces, in all sincerity. Now that purpose has been achieved, I have no further business here."

"Now that's quite the fine attitude. ...Ah wasn't under the impression we'd told you anythin' about this gatherin' — where'd you hear about it?"

Having stirred things up just enough, Priscilla made to leave the scene promptly. Anastasia struck first, bringing her to a halt.
Anastasia's tone was gentle, but a clear wariness rang through it.

"Ah don't think Ah've got anyone careless enough to let it slip runnin' around."

"Do not converse with me in pleasantries; it rots the ear. A woman with even a passably quick mind should understand well enough."

"Oh my. And here Ah thought you'd call me mediocre or common, same as the rest."

"If one is such a dullard as to have nothing worth looking at, then I lump them together with the rabble as a matter of course. Surely you would not have me believe that you, standing as my rivals, are quite so foolish?"

The two of them provoking one another — though for Priscilla's part, this might simply be business as usual, with no particular intent to dress up her words to provoke Anastasia especially.
Anastasia slowly stroked the fur of the stole about her neck.

"Ah thought Ah was bein' careful enough that information wouldn't leak..."

"Whatever matter reaches anyone's ear does so, inevitably, leaking from some loose mouth. It stands to reason that the more who know a thing, the more holes appear. You are not the only ones keeping eyes and ears on the movements of others."

"And here Ah'd assumed you weren't the type to bother with somethin' so painstakin' as intelligence-gatherin', keepin' an eye on the rest of us."

"A fly buzzing at one's ear is swatted. Though, without knowing its position, there is little to be done, is there? Tracking insects by eye and ear is the same for me as for anyone. So my response to you lot is likewise the same."

Treating each and every one of her rivals as no more than buzzing insects, Priscilla implied, without saying so outright, that she left nothing to chance.
Subaru, too, found himself in agreement with Anastasia. He'd never have imagined that Priscilla would actually devise proper countermeasures against a rival camp and see them through.
And it was the fruit of that carelessness that had summoned today's disaster.

"That old man — he's Reinhard's father, right?"

Ignoring the flow of conversation up to that point, a brash voice called up an entirely different topic.
Drawing everyone's gaze while still tearing into her steak-fried rice was Felt. Sauce smeared around her mouth, the girl picked out Priscilla's gaze from among those watching her and glared back.

"I remember him bein' all buddy-buddy back at the castle, and puttin' it together with what he just said, I got a rough idea. Don't care one bit about his family drama, that's none of my business. But him bein' with you — that's a different story."

"...Hoh. And what might a mere slum brat, of all people, have noticed?"

"Wish it weren't my business, but it is. The Astrea family headship — that's a lifeline for us. 'Cause it ain't been handed over to Reinhard yet. The real authority over the house is still sittin' in that old man's hands."

At what Felt had said, Reinhard's cheek beside her tensed, ever so slightly.
And Subaru and the others, too, correctly grasped the meaning behind Felt's concern.

Raised an orphan, possessing nothing, Felt had been operating on the foundation of Reinhard's family home — the Astrea territory — gradually producing results.
But that territory, however far things went, was never Felt's. It belonged, ultimately, to House Astrea, and was on loan from Reinhard.
But if even that thing recognized as Reinhard's own possession — the Astrea territory — turned out to be, in truth, nothing more than a borrowing of a borrowing, what then?

"Heh. Looks like you dimwits have finally caught on to the gravity of the situation."

Cutting into the conversation there, exactly as he'd hoped, was Heinkel with a grin.
He seemed to have been waiting for precisely this topic, and with an expression practically licking his lips, glared back and forth between Felt and Reinhard.

"That's exactly right. The Astrea family headship — I'm the one holding it. I've never handed it to Reinhard, and I've got no intention of ever doing so. Giving such a bothersome job to the Sword Saint, so busy with the nation's affairs? No, no, not a chance!"

"A lord's just a lord in name and that's supposed to hold up? When we got back to Astrea territory, the state of the place was awful. A handful of clerks and servants runnin' themselves ragged tryin' to keep it together. And you just left it to rot, and now you wanna show up and claim to be lord?"

"Name only or not, irresponsible or not, the crown of lordship sits on my head. And it's not collapsed, is it, even if it was close? The people, the servants — desperately trying to prop up their lord, weren't they. Hah! A lord loved so dearly by his own people — it really does bring tears to my eyes!"

Heinkel mocked Felt without limit as she spoke, forcing down her emotions as best she could.
The words were so vile, so vicious, that Subaru felt an illusion of the world whiting out with rage before his eyes. Fury and revulsion hung thick in the room, in every gaze.
And in that storm, drenched in a rain of contempt, Heinkel wore the face of a man basking in applause.
Seeing it clearly, Subaru understood. This man was completely out of his mind.

"Your sense of danger's spot on, Reinhard's dear master. The Astrea territory is my land. And I don't support you. Who I do support — well, you can see for yourself!"

As though contempt and revulsion themselves were worth a thunder of applause, Heinkel puffed out his chest and played his grand stage to the hilt. He gestured with a hand toward Priscilla beside him, and declared, loudly, that he would back neither his son nor his father for the throne, but her.

"I've heard about your results this past year. In my place as lord, working to restore the Astrea territory — genuinely, good work. Having acknowledged that your results were splendid, I'll say this. — That place is no home for you two, going forward. Once you understand that, hurry up and..."

"There, mediocrity."

"— Ah? What, Lady Priscilla? I'm in the middle of something important—"

"Quiet."

At the outrage that followed an instant later, everyone could do nothing but hold their breath.

Priscilla, having spoken those few short words, flicked her fan in one stroke toward Heinkel, whose eyes went wide. The folded fan cut through the air, striking the crown of his red-haired head directly, and slammed his tall frame down onto the floor with terrific force.
Heinkel's eyes rolled white at the impact, his consciousness reaped from him in a single blow. But Priscilla's motion did not stop there.

Heinkel lay sprawled face-down on the floor, and Priscilla kicked him up with the tip of her foot, sending him floating into the air. Then, drawing her arm back, she moved to smash that momentum straight into his torso—

"Princess, that's enough temper for one day. Any more and he'll actually die."

"——"

Priscilla's arm, an instant before it would have swung fully, stopped, her wrist caught. It was Al who had done it, having anticipated her violence, and Priscilla turned dangerous eyes on the helmeted man for his attendant's audacity.
In the arm Al had stopped, a crimson-bladed sword had, at some point, appeared in her grip.
A long Western-style blade, an exquisite piece with a wavering, flame-like temper line running through it. A sword whose extraordinary quality was obvious at a glance, born into Priscilla's hand between one blink and the next — and vanishing again just as fast.

Having seen it through, Al slowly released Priscilla's arm.

"Man, give me a break. Gettin' the Sun Sword pulled on me is bad for the heart... gah, oof!"

"Insolence, Al. Whose leave did you obtain to touch my flawless skin? That you should let your lust simmer, starved of women as you are, is your own affair — but that you should dream, even in your wildest fantasies, of defiling me — that is beyond even dreaming."

With the freed arm, Priscilla drove a blow straight into Al's gut, doubling her attendant over in agony. She gave a small snort through her nose, then looked down at Heinkel, pitifully sprawled once more upon the floor.
The sheer coldness of the emotion that flickered across her eyes — how terrifying it was.

"Still, Al's words have some merit. Perhaps killing him would have been a touch shortsighted."

"If you really feel that way... I kinda wish you'd been a little gentler, y'know?"

"Say nothing more. Even I am no demon. Later, as a reward, I shall let you lick my feet."

"Don't talk like I've got some kink where that'd make me happy!! It's gonna cause misunderstandings!"

Struck down, on his knees, Al protested desperately, but Priscilla paid him no mind. She gazed at Heinkel with those blood-colored eyes, seemingly hesitating for a moment over whether to simply discard him, before clapping her hands.

"Schult. Carry that mediocrity out. A dullard he may be, but he still has his uses. Considering the trouble of drawing him in, it would be a waste to spit him out just yet."

"Yes, Lady Priscilla."

Answering her call, yet another figure appeared in the room.
This person, who had apparently been waiting in the hallway the whole time, was a young boy whose height had not yet finished growing.

Fluffy, curling pink hair, a build so slender it could be mistaken for a girl's, and finely arranged features. The pitch of his voice, too, proved his voice hadn't broken yet, and there was something faintly illicit about seeing that still-undeveloped frame wrapped in a butler's uniform.
Schult, as he was called, looked to be perhaps still in his early teens. Judging purely by appearance, roughly the same age as Beatrice.

"Excuse me, Master Heinkel."

Dutifully addressing the unconscious Heinkel, Schult grabbed him by both feet and, dragging him with great effort, hauled him laboriously out of the room. It was, naturally, a stretch for a child to carry a man of Heinkel's height. But Schult didn't utter a single complaint at Priscilla's order, dragging Heinkel out with the attitude of one for whom obeying her was simply a matter of course.

"Schult-chan's always so admirable, isn't he? Gotta praise him later too, Princess."

"Naturally. That one always serves me with his entire being. And I am not so merciless as to treat a loyal retainer poorly. I shall let Schult lick my feet later as well."

"In Schult-chan's case, he'd probably do it with actual tears in his eyes, no joke, so don't. Give him somethin' a bit more sensibly rewarding."

"Hmm. Then the honor of being embraced by me and sleeping at my side, I suppose."

"...Well, guess that's fine, then. Almost wish I could trade places with him."

Watching Schult and Heinkel go, Priscilla and Al carried on their deflating little exchange.
And so, at last, with the outsiders removed, one could perhaps say that every camp of the Royal Selection had finally gathered face to face.

"So, what about what that old man just said? You lot seriously plannin' on kickin' us out of Astrea territory, tryin' to cut us down?"

"Do not take the words of such a mediocrity at face value. In the first place, was it not you lot who carried out the territory's reconstruction? If so, even should the nominal lord return to the manor and try to drive you out, where would his followers come from? The populace may be foolish and benighted, but they are not heartless pawns devoid of affection or gratitude. To move them requires waves of a certain magnitude. That one cannot raise such waves."

"...Then why the hell'd you drag him along?"

"I told you already, did I not. Because I thought doing so would prove more amusing. Everything, eventually, is destined to fall into my hands regardless. Given that, the only choice remaining is which path proves the more delightful. That is my decision. Bringing that one along, too, was nothing more than a plaything toward that end."

However self-serving, however unbending, her convictions never wavered.
Priscilla's absolute self-confidence trampled every common sense underfoot and forced its unreasonableness upon others. In the face of it, one could only give up and submit, or meet it with equal conviction.
And —

"——"

The four rivals gathered here did not hesitate to choose the latter.
Receiving that wave of gazes, Priscilla laughed, genuinely delighted from the bottom of her heart.

"That is as it should be. The outcome to come is already assured. Then let the path there be as delightful as possible. Excite me, you lot who are at least a cut above the mediocre. Grow fat and strong until you become my enemies... and then, at last, I shall meet you with my full strength."

Priscilla declared them not even worth recognizing as enemies yet.
No — she had already said as much. She still thought of Emilia and the others as no better than buzzing insects. Not even worthy of recognition as foes.
Then —

"That arrogance — I'm gonna make you cry and regret it."

Felt's words, spat out in an open challenge, were the shared sentiment of everyone in that room.
