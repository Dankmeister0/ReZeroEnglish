Arc 7 Chapter 95: "The Besieged Summit"

In that instant, everyone on the battlefield caught sight of the being that descended to the earth wrapped in white cloud.
Too majestic, too magnificent — a transcendent life-form existing on a plane utterly removed from the mediocrity of common dust down to the very root of its being. Everyone understood at a glance.

"...That's a Dragon."

Eyes of green opened wide, Garfiel murmured beside the crumbling ramparts.
Having finished his death-match with Kafma Irulux, breathing hard through his shoulders, the moment he swept his gaze across the battlefield in search of his next duty, it happened.
In the distance, a presence so colossal it gave the illusion that the sky itself had fallen appeared at one corner of the summit battle that was freezing white the fiercest-burning battlefield in this empire.

"Emilia-sama...!"

Possessing an absurdly vast Gate, and stripping the heat from the world with a use of mana utterly devoid of delicacy — this was, without question, Emilia in true earnest.
If she were fighting the enemy guarding one of the five ramparts' summits, then her opponent had to be one of the Nine Divine Generals or someone of comparable stature — at the very least, a match for Kafma.
That alone was more than enough reason not to want Emilia fighting. And now, on top of that, the additional force appearing there was a being out of myth — that alone was absurd.

"Did she call the 'Flying Dragon General'? Damn it, I've gotta pull her back right now...!"

Grinding his fangs, Garfiel resolved in his heart to swap places on the battlefield.
That Emilia *could* fight and that Emilia *should* fight were not simply the same thing. Emilia herself would argue otherwise, but letting her fight was, for the camp, a bitter decision made only in extremity.
By rights, Emilia ought to stand somewhere free of the risk of injury, in a position to see everything through to the end.

"I can't face the boss man if I let this happen."

That Emilia didn't want to leave the situation entirely to someone else was only natural of her.
That low-set line of sight of hers, meant in the best way, and her consideration for others — Garfiel liked those things about her. But he couldn't let her have her way in everything, couldn't simply permit it all.
Especially not when Garfiel was the Emilia Camp's man of arms, whose very role was to bat away every spark of fire raining down on the camp and cut down every enemy standing in its path.

"—"

Closing his eyes, Garfiel quietly took stock of his own condition.
The grave wounds he'd suffered fighting Kafma — his whole body sliced open, his insides forced apart, his flesh burned to kill the *insects* nesting within him — and yet Garfiel still stood. He was, in that sense, still in fighting shape.

Only just barely enough to claim as much.

"'Right as rain' might be pushing it, but... I can still go."

Even now, drawing power up from the earth beneath his soles, the healing magic he had activated at full strength was mending his flesh, inside and out, at a furious pace.
By all rights, the strain this placed on his body should have been shaving years off his life. But the events at the water-gate city had broken something open in Garfiel, both in body and in spirit.
Breaking through a thin membrane that couldn't be seen but had, without question, been there — Garfiel stood.

Blood rising off him in steam from his countless wounds, moving a body hot as if ablaze, Garfiel steeled himself for a distant reckoning with the Dragon.

"You lot! I brought the wall down! Get your asses moving, now!!"

Mouth open, Garfiel roared at the mass of rebels watching him from a distance.
It was the group that had challenged Kafma before Garfiel and been swept aside. Wounded, some propped up by comrades, but surely a considerable number of them still able to fight — yet all of them had, uniformly, kept their distance from Garfiel and Kafma's battlefield, and even after it was decided, none of them moved.
Some of that was surely being overwhelmed by the fight they'd just witnessed. But that wasn't all of it.

"You're the one who broke the wall! Then you should be the first through it!"

"—"

"Valiant warrior, we honor your martial might! No one — no one — has the right to defile it!"

One of the half-man, half-horse men answered Garfiel's words fiercely, so.
It was Garfiel, who had struck Kafma down, who had earned the right to be first over the wall. That was why they had halted, waiting patiently for him to cross it.
It wasn't just his opinion — it seemed to be the shared view of everyone who had witnessed the duel just now. Those who had started this fight for their own selfish reasons made the pride of the warrior hidden within them their own history.
And to their discerning eyes, Garfiel's way of fighting had, apparently, shone dazzling.
Their consideration was, in itself, something that warmed the chest — but—

"Sorry, but I got somewhere I gotta be. Ain't no 'returning Whipfrock' here. — It's that guy."

Jerking his chin, Garfiel indicated the white Dragon reigning in the distant sky.
At the Pleiades Watchtower, in the walled city of Guaral too — there had been chances to cross paths with it. Garfiel, who had kept missing it through sheer bad timing, finally had the Dragon within biting distance.
He didn't want to fight it. It was an opponent he *had* to fight.

"—"

Seeing Garfiel's gesture, the rebels too gasped at the sight of the white Dragon.
Naturally, they too had been aware of that same presence. That they didn't cower before it, didn't bow their heads and bend their knees, was the empire's people's peculiar brand of spirit — but whether to challenge it was another matter entirely.
Garfiel would challenge it. And so—

"Crossing the wall — I'm leaving that to you lot."

If the rebels swarmed over the fallen ramparts and poured into the capital, the situation would change.
If the empire's greatest forces were stationed at all five summit defenses, then the area around Emperor Vincent Vollachia, likely at the Crystal Palace, might well be lightly guarded.
There was even a chance that a literal decisive blow could be struck through the gaping hole in this wall.

"So—"

Yielding the vanguard of the assault on the capital to the rebels, Garfiel set out to begin his battle against the white threat.
Just as he was, in fact, about to step forward toward the summit where the Dragon waited.

—It was then that a chill ran through Garfiel's entire body, raising every hair on end.

"—"

Empty mind. His mind was empty.
Without hesitation, without question, obeying the sensation his brain screamed at him, an all-out blow was unleashed.
A backfist of steel that could shatter stone, punching the wind dead, driven toward wherever instinct led—

"Ohh, you saw through my concealment? Ain't that somethin'?"

A raspy voice came from beyond that clenched fist.

"Gah—"

An instant — the backfist should have struck the shadow behind him.
Without question, he'd felt something touch the surface of his gauntlet. And yet it was his own mouth that let out the pained groan, and Garfiel's eyes went wide even as shock struck through his thoughts.
The impact had punched clean through his back. Against Garfiel's back, someone's small foot — a toe — had been placed, just barely. An outrageous kick — no, wrong.

"Whole thing, that's your own punch, boy. Just passed it right through my body and sent it back."

A voice answering the question, and Garfiel's whole body creaked at the bone as his eyes bulged.
Lie or truth, his internal organs and brain were rattled with force equivalent to his own full-power blow, and Garfiel's vision swam violently. Cuts, bruises, fractures, ruptured organs — those he could heal instantly.
Even if not fully healed, he could force his body to move through it regardless.

But an attack that resonated in the very core of his body — that couldn't be cancelled out.

"—"

Grinding his back teeth against the numbness spreading through his whole body, Garfiel braced for a follow-up strike. Moving limbs slowed by the delayed reaction, he barely managed to guard the vital points of his neck and head.
But the follow-up he dreaded never came.
Instead, the time that could have been used for a follow-up strike was spent on something else — a warning shot.

"Truth is, it'd be a hassle if the wall got breached and they came pourin' in."

Mixed into the sigh-laced words came the groans of pain of a dozen or more men, overlapping.
Scolding his knees, which threatened to buckle, Garfiel raised his face, and what leapt into his field of view was the sight of the front row of rebels — the ones who, hearing Garfiel's call, had begun moving to push into the capital — collapsing.

Centaur people and beastfolk alike — their foreheads and chests pierced by black-iron throwing blades, kunai, their lives stolen in an instant.
Even had he rushed to their aid and cast healing magic, it would have been too late. Mortal wounds delivered in the span of a heartbeat.
They too, as warriors, should have possessed skill enough to dare challenge the capital. Even if they fell far short of a General like Kafma—

"Dead's dead, kid. Bein' a warrior don't mean squat then."

"—"

"Kakakakka! Look at that face — you don't like it, do ya, young'un? You're the one who blew the wall open, ain't ya. One after another, out come the scary ones — troublesome times, ain't they?"

Grinning with his mouth wide open, tracing his long white eyebrows with a finger — a small, slight shadow.
Finally moving his feet, kicking off the ground to gain distance, what came into Garfiel's field of view was an old man even shorter than the diminutively-built Garfiel himself.
But "small old man" was hardly a fitting description for such a monster.

"You bastard...!"

"Ohh, hold up, hold up, your turn's comin' a bit later. Er, right, there."

"Hah?"

Baring hostility, every hair on his body bristling, Garfiel glared. But the old man thrust out a hand toward him — the right hand, missing everything past the wrist — to hold him back.
The moment Garfiel's brow furrowed at the sight of that missing limb, the old man swung his leg.
In an instant, though what exactly he did wasn't clear, a single horizontal kick carved a deep line straight into the earth — splitting the ground precisely between Garfiel and the old man, and the rebels behind them.
Then, addressing the rebels on the far side of the line, the old man declared:

"You cross that line and come over here, every last one of ya dies."

"—!"

"Well well, good thing you're an obedient bunch. Wish the young'uns back home would take a lesson from ya. Lately they keep talkin' back to everything I say. I'm the village chief, y'know?"

Shrugging his thin shoulders, the old man grinned, showing white teeth.
An attitude utterly unconcerned with the threat he'd just delivered to the rebels a moment ago. But that the threat was no bluff whatsoever — everyone present understood that on instinct.
Garfiel understood too. — Just who this old man before him was.

"Olbart Dunkelkenn... 'the Vicious Elder,' is it?"

"That nickname — every time someone says it, I tell 'em, I don't much care for it, y'know. That's basically an insult, ain't it."

"—. What're you doin' here?"

At the old man — Olbart — tilting his head, Garfiel asked in a low voice.
Slack, casual, seemingly at ease — Olbart's manner did nothing to loosen Garfiel's guard, not one bit. Olbart had appeared behind him without a trace of presence.
Right here, in the middle of this wide-open, clear-sighted plain.

"—"

If he'd erased his presence and crept up hidden in a building's shadow, that Garfiel could have understood.
That alone would have been more than enough of a threat, but at least the events that occurred would make sense. But how was one supposed to grasp a being that could slip into this open space without any presence at all?

A being that could only be grasped as a threat, nothing more.

Before Garfiel, trembling at that thought, Olbart, his manner unchanged, raised an eyebrow with a "Hoh?" and said,

"What am I doin' here, ain't that obvious? Keepin' you traitors out is our job, and Kafma botched it, so I'm here cleanin' up the mess."

So saying, the old man indicated Kafma lying on the ground with his missing hand. Then, with an "oop," he withdrew the right hand and instead pointed properly with his left.

"Damn unlucky draw, this. Told 'em to send Chisha or call Groovy back or somethin', 'cause this'd get ugly, and sure enough, he goes and loses."

"Sure enough, huh? Old man, you got no right to laugh at him."

"Hoh?"

Closing one eye, Olbart looked put out. As if to block that gaze, Garfiel stepped between the old man and Kafma, breathing out a heated breath.
Kafma was an enemy — that hadn't changed. But no one besides Garfiel, who had traded blows with him face to face, had any right to talk about how Kafma fought, about who he was.

"This guy — he was strong. I just happened to be stronger."

"—? Well, I mean, that's obvious lookin' at it, ain't it? I ain't denyin' that either. What're you tryin' to say, boy?"

"I'm sayin' I don't like your damn attitude! Is that any way to talk about a guy on your own side, and a fellow 'General' at that!? Huh!?"

Mouth wide open, Garfiel roared like he was baring his fangs. At that ferocious growl, Olbart's brow furrowed, and he tilted his head.
Genuinely bewildered, the old man continued, "Y'know?"—

"I'm a First General, Kafma's a Second General. Ain't the same, not even close."

"—!"

Unashamed, delivering what he clearly took to be plain fact — Olbart's remark. Sensing an unbridgeable rift in that, Garfiel's pupils narrowed.
The look of a carnivorous beast the instant it fixes on its prey — that look trailed behind him as he leapt at the old man.
For Kafma, he had felt the respect due an opponent. For the enemy before him now, there was none of that.

Nor did the old man wish for it, or acknowledge it either.
Recognizing an opponent whose priorities in battle carried entirely different weight from his own—

"I'm gonna smash you!!"

Both arms raised high, closing on Olbart.
A brutal, merciless blow — no less, perhaps more, than the one that had smashed the rampart — carrying enough destructive force to punch clean through the old man's small frame with plenty to spare.
And it was driven, without holding anything back, straight into Olbart's head.

*Boom* — the impact tore through the earth, shaking the ground, sending up a cloud of dust as if the shattered ground had exploded, and grit rained down on the heads of the rebels whom Olbart had halted.
It was the kind of firepower that wouldn't have been strange to see Olbart come apart under, scattered in pieces.
And yet—

"Wha—"

At the sight before him, Garfiel's eyes went wide.
Right in front of him, Garfiel's fist had been driven in, pinching Olbart's head between them from either side. A definite impact and resistance had rebounded through Garfiel's own arms as well.
And yet the old man, head sandwiched between Garfiel's fists, showed his white teeth and grinned.

"Somethin', huh? Your attack's power — I just sent it straight into the ground."

"—ah"

"Well, the ground goin' and blowin' up like that is 'cause your arm strength is just that damn ridiculous, though."

The instant he finished speaking, the old man's face, pinched between the fists, slipped downward. Garfiel's eyes followed it—

"Guh?!"

Following Olbart, who should have slipped down, with his eyes, it was instead the crown of his own head that impact struck.
Olbart should have crouched low — yet something had struck him from above.

"Think it's low, it's high. Think it's high, it's low. Basics, boy, basics."

"Gaaaaaah—!!"

At the crouched Olbart's breezy words, Garfiel drove his fist down furiously. It plunged into Olbart's white-haired head.
The instant it did, a sharp impact tore through the back of Garfiel's head, his back, his backside.

Garfiel's downward-driven fist struck nothing but air, burying itself in the ground.
Instead, the voice of Olbart — who had vanished from sight in an instant — came from directly overhead.

"So I said, think it's low, it's high. Slow learners get left behind, y'know?"

Shaking himself, Garfiel swung his arm up toward the voice. The instant his fingers caught on something, he forced it down with all his strength, meaning to slam it into the ground.
Slam it down, pin it in place, and drive an all-out blow into it there. If its feet weren't planted, it couldn't pull off acrobatics like before—

"—"

That fighting instinct's demand ground to a halt as he realized: what his fingers had caught wasn't Olbart at all — it was Kafma's body, which the old man had thrown at Garfiel's head.
The motion to hurl it to the ground stalled, and Garfiel's lips trembled.
In that instant—

"Y'know, boy, why d'you think there's fifty-some Second Generals but only nine measly First Generals? — 'Cause we're damn strong, that's why."

The voice and the impact bored through both sides of Garfiel's skull at once.

—A colossal mass of falling flame, the wrath of heaven itself, seemingly bent on burning the entire world to ash.

Faced with a world dyed red by that sight, Yorna felt the illusion take hold.
That perhaps, if one drew enough anger, the world itself would hurl this much fury even at a single tiny individual.

Down onto ground already scorched to wasteland, onto rebels and vegetation already burned by that same method, came firepower falling with such catastrophic force that it would char even the corpses' innards until nothing remained but husks.
And yet, Yorna did not so much as flinch.

"— Yohkan."

"My, what a demanding way to use a girl."

The sound Yorna's lips formed was the signal, and a crimson jeweled sword swung down.
A beautiful sword that would seize any eye that beheld it, burn any heart, capture even the soul — but that bewitching quality was, for this sword, merely an ornament, not even a fragment of its true reason for being.
Which meant that when it fulfilled its true purpose, the sword's radiance blazed brighter still.

"—"

Rising from below to above, from earth to sky, the raised blade of the 'Yang Sword' met head-on the tremendous inferno plunging straight down.
In an instant, the mighty flame that had filled the entire field of vision vanished. — Not severed. Quite literally, it vanished as if it had never existed at all.

"The Yang Sword burns what I wish to burn, and cuts what I wish to cut."

An imposition of principle so violent, so unreasonable, so utterly irrational.
And yet its truth stood right there before them, the extinguishing of an inferno that had seemed capable of destroying the world itself.
A firepower unleashed with enough force poured into it to crush any first strike from the opposing side — and the sight of it wiped away with a single stroke of the sword should have left even that opponent unable to stay composed—

"Don't underestimate her. That's a 'Spirit Eater.'"

An unhelpful line, both as explanation and as warning, and with that, Priscilla's figure leapt to the right.
Likewise, Yorna leapt to the left, opposite the direction Priscilla had gone — and between the two of them, tearing through with tremendous force, passed a colossal mass of flash-flood water.

"—!"

Beholding a volume of water as if an entire well had been emptied out at once, even while succeeding in evading it, Yorna revised her assessment of her enemy — of Arakiya's — ability upward from her previous impression.

This was not the first time Yorna had fought Arakiya.
Once before, during what was called Yorna's "rebellion," they had crossed spears.
That time, though, with Vollachia's strongest glaring from behind Arakiya, and with Yorna herself able to meet the enemy at full strength on the stage of Chaosflame, the preconditions were, in various ways, already entirely different from now.

Of course, in the sense of fighting, Yorna had been in earnest then too — but in the sense of a fight to the death, if she was honest, she hadn't been. In that past rebellion, particularly the one that ended in a clash with Arakiya, Yorna's purpose had been retaliation against those who laid hands on the Magic City's residents, and to make known the legitimacy of doing so.
In other words, it had been, in some sense, an act of intimidation — a clash whose settlement Vincent, facing off across the board, had surely already understood the intent of.

However, Arakiya at that time had not known those circumstances, and had likely not held back at all in her fight against Yorna, either.
From that, Yorna had assumed that Arakiya's ability was, in the end, one excelling specifically against armies — a force whose role was to serve as a single body equal to an entire legion.
But that understanding was not just mistaken — it had been far too generous.

"Ranked 'Two' while passing over Elder Olbart — so this is what that meant, is it?"

Letting that slip out with a breath, Yorna glanced behind her — at the earth split lengthwise all the way to the horizon by the pressure of the released water.
She understood the logic of how pressurized water could become a threat.
But confronted with just how far beyond the norm it was, Yorna rolled up the sleeve of her kimono.

She had come dressed in her most treasured kimono, prepared for the decisive battle.
And still — could even that have withstood a single blow from Arakiya?

"Before, she only ever used fire — even burned half my city down once, she did."

Arakiya, who had burned half a city to ash in a single breath, and who this very day had burned rebels along with the ramparts themselves.
A defensive line of fire, well-suited against stone buildings, on a battlefield where there was no need to spare a thought for the surroundings — it had suited Arakiya well, and Yorna had assumed she wouldn't change her tactics now, of all times.

"This too is surely someone else's counsel put in her ear. If it weren't turned on me, I might even find it charming."

Muttering that, Priscilla kicked off the ground, closing the distance to Arakiya.
Placed to guard one corner of the star-shaped fortress's summit, Arakiya burned the air beneath her knees and flew through the sky.
Merciless firepower raining down from high altitude alone was more than enough of a threat.
First, they needed to drag Arakiya down to within arm's reach.

"Mother!"

"—! I know that much!"

At Priscilla's call as she dashed forward, a moment's unease ran through Yorna.
She still hadn't quite grasped a proper sense of distance with the daughter she'd only just been reunited with. If anything, it was Priscilla's composed attitude toward a mother whose very shape and form had changed that struck Yorna as the stranger thing.
Having parted from her when Priscilla was still a nursing infant, Yorna had no way of knowing how she'd been raised.

"Dance, won't you."

Taking her pipe from her mouth, Yorna swung its tip while stamping the ground with the thick sole of her sandal.
At that, the ground trembling slowly ahead of the advancing Priscilla peeled away and rose up, forming footing to support her path forward.

Yorna's 'Soul Marriage Technique' worked on inanimate matter as well.
But its efficacy scaled with the weight of feeling invested and the time spent together — and that was where her unease lay. Just how much could she love the earth she'd spent her time on with the man she'd loved?
But the result spoke for itself, as one could see.

"Well done."

Leaving behind words that showed no regard for her mother as a mother, Priscilla leapt onto the footing ahead.
Of course, the footing didn't stop there — more rose up one after another, forming a path toward Arakiya. Mindful that a single path would make an easy target, Yorna took care to shape several paths at once as well.
And with that—

"—In the way."

As Arakiya swung the branch-like arm she held, the great wind she unleashed tore the floating earth up by its roots, sweeping away every path entirely.
The force of that great wind was nothing short of being struck by a giant's palm — a direct hit could have crushed even Priscilla's whole body.

"Striking at me without mercy — have you no regard for my beauty?"

"Already decided. Even without arms or legs, the princess is still the princess."

"'Had it decided for you' would be the more accurate phrasing, surely."

Narrowing her eyes, Priscilla, falling from high in the sky, hurled her words down at Arakiya.
An instant before the great wind swept away the footing, Priscilla had already escaped into the air. That she'd evaded the wind's blow with such quick instinct was impressive enough, but that left her defenseless against a second strike.
And indeed, with all hesitation gone from her eyes, Arakiya, with nothing to stop her, fixed her aim on the helplessly falling Priscilla's limbs and raised her arm, intending to strip away her fighting power without mercy.
Into that—

"You mustn't forget about me."

Toward Arakiya, who had looked up at Priscilla overhead, Yorna's kick, launched from directly below, landed home.
Yorna's long leg, kicked up — the thick sole of her footwear, a treasured piece she'd taken great care of and mended over and over across the years, one she was deeply fond of — struck home, a blow poured with her whole heart and soul.

"Trying to outshine me, unbecoming of your age."

Timing it with Yorna's kick, Priscilla too brought her raised 'Yang Sword' straight down. Caught between attacks from above and below, mother and daughter, two generations.
Well-coordinated teamwork, a pair of strikes so gorgeous they might have congratulated themselves on it — that landed. — Yes, they landed.

"—?!"

The blow that should have struck Arakiya home instead delayed its impact's reaction, and with a jolt slightly out of time from what was expected, it rang out with a hard clang.
Looking closer, what Yorna's kicked-up sole had collided with was, of all things, the very blade of the Yang Sword Priscilla had swung down.
An attack meant to pincer their enemy had instead missed its intended target entirely, colliding with each other instead — a stunning turn of events.
More astonishing still was that Yorna's and Priscilla's attacks had collided with each other inside Arakiya's body, which appeared to shimmer and waver.

"Passed straight through, did she?"
"Insolent."

The instant Yorna's astonishment and Priscilla's irritation both spilled out at once, Arakiya's body, having absorbed the attacks within itself, glowed white, and the counterattack was unleashed.
Arakiya's entire body — as if it had burst apart. No — in fact, it had burst apart.
Scattered into fragments of white light, Arakiya's body became shrapnel carrying frightening dispersive force, scattering in all directions, striking Yorna and Priscilla like splashing water.

"Kh...!"

On instinct, Yorna gathered up the fine fragments of earth left by the great wind's earlier sweep and wrapped herself and Priscilla in a swirl of that dust, like grit.
Wrapping — though not in the sense of clinging to the body as a garment, but rather a minor technique, circulating it at high speed around them so as to deflect the attack.
If Arakiya's shrapnel carried only as much firepower as it appeared to, this should have sufficiently blunted it.
But—

"—!!"

Piercing easily through the wrapped robe of earth, Arakiya's fragments bored through both Yorna and Priscilla.
Blown back by the impact, Yorna fell to the ground with a pained groan. She stretched out her toes at the last instant to touch down, using every bit of strength to avoid the disgraceful sight of tumbling on the ground.
She could not afford to fall. Least of all could she afford the humiliation of rolling on her back.

"Those who love me... would be troubled by this..."

The weight of the soul Yorna carried was sustained by the existence of those who loved her.
Which meant Yorna had to answer that love with everything she had. Answering love was no half-hearted matter.
Her conduct, her words, even her emotions — all of it had to remain worthy.
That held true whether in daily life, in the bedchamber, or in the midst of battle — all the same.

—With a light *snap*, the hairpin thrust through Yorna's styled hair shattered. Along with it, the hair ornament hanging from that pin, made from layer upon layer of overlapping scales.

"A gift from someone, Mother?"

"—. A gift from my beloved children."

Receiving the shattered, dust-turned fragments of the hair ornament on her fingers, Yorna quietly lowered her eyes.
Most of the things Yorna wore were gifts. Her kimono, her footwear, even things like her hair ornaments — exquisite pieces the residents living in the Magic City had woven, shaped, or carved out of their own hands, wearing themselves down to make, each imbued with soul.
Things that, having received Yorna's love, held the right to shatter in her place, to protect her.

"And you, my lady?"

"Unfortunately, I am not quite so promiscuous as Mother. What I have is either originally mine, or a tribute from my late husband."

So saying, Priscilla quietly showed off her own ear. There, an earring set with a green jewel that should have been there was now gone.
She too had transferred the price of her own life to one who loved her, and thereby prolonged it.

"A husband, is it? Priscilla, you too—"

"You look rather solemn about it. The vanished earring, if I recall, was a gift from my fourth husband."

"...Fourth—!"

"I have taken eight husbands in my time. Though I daresay I fall short of Mother."

Stated flatly by Priscilla, that unexpected answer left Yorna shocked speechless, jaw agape. But even that shock was swept aside by the light of the Yang Sword Priscilla re-gripped.
That strange phenomenon from before, just now—

"Priscilla, the Yang Sword should be able to reach any opponent, should it not? Surely nothing but the Life Sword, capable of reaching the very soul, should be beyond its cutting reach..."

"Even without Mother telling me, I hold that same understanding. But there may be a misconception at play."

"A misconception?"

"My Yang Sword burns what I wish to burn, and cuts what I wish to cut."

So saying, Priscilla slowly raised the tip of the Yang Sword.
Following it with her eyes, Yorna saw, beyond the sword's tip, the scattered fragments of white light gathering together, gradually taking shape once more — Arakiya.

Reflecting the nature of the spirit she had consumed, and lodging that power within her own flesh — the 'Spirit Eater,' a rare power whose true nature remained little understood, but whose extensibility of function was breathtaking to behold.
Even now, by what principle had that body turned into light — and, pondering that far, Yorna realized.
The Yang Sword cut whatever it wished to cut, a jeweled blade capable of reaching all things — but—

"—Is that fire, or water, or perhaps wind? Or if it can't even be called light or shadow, then reaching it would surely prove quite the struggle."

"—"

Narrowing her crimson eyes, Priscilla crossed gazes with Arakiya, whose eyes shared that same color.
A confrontation between the wielder of a jeweled sword capable of reaching all things, and a transcendent being with the ability to transform into all things—

"I see. Quite the troublesome opponent she's grown into."

Were this Chaosflame, Yorna too might have had a fair chance of clashing head-on with Arakiya and grinding her down.
But this was not Chaosflame, and Yorna's power here could not be called full strength either.
Which meant that the one who held the decisive blow was not Yorna, but—

"—Whenever it may come, the center of the stage remains mine, does it not."

So said, with a bewitching smile, the crimson daughter, sun-like even in the hardship she'd been placed in, turning even that into fuel for herself.
At the dazzle of that smile, even Yorna, standing beside her, could not help but think.

If the only ones who could stand at her side were those who, burned by Priscilla's radiance, still did not burn away — then what a cruelly ironic turn of fate it was, that it should be Arakiya standing in their way now.


△▼△▼△▼△


—A shift in the direction of the wind. Otto Suwen sensed it.

At first, it was only a premonition; then he took it as a sign; and finally, it settled into certainty.
Gradually, gradually, the battle situation shifting, moving, tilting — Otto, immersed in the task of opening the channels he normally kept shut, gathering in every 'Voice' he could, drifted through a sense of omnipotence, as if merging with the world itself, even as it slipped away from him.
Something findable only within that state sank deep within a whirlpool of overwhelming information. To retrieve it, he had to dive deeper and deeper himself—

"—Otto-san!"

"—!"

A loud voice sounded right beside him, and at the same time a jarring shock shattered the whirlpool of 'Voices.'
Like a tank holding water breaking apart and spilling its contents out, like sand scooped up from a sandbox slipping through the gaps between fingers, the 'Voices' fled.
Part of him felt it as a waste, felt clingy regret over it — even as another part felt otherwise.

"Ah, that's dangerous... th-thank you, that helped, Petra-chan..."

"Your face just went a horrible color again. Wipe your nose, it's bleeding."

"Ahh, that's a lifesaver. ...Somehow I feel like I'm bleeding about as much as if I were actually fighting."

Taking the offered handkerchief, Otto pressed it firmly against his own nose.
The dripping blood had soaked the grass at his feet, and he keenly felt the weight of the strain placed on him by using his Divine Protection past its limits. In fact, the amount of blood lost didn't seem like something to joke about.
Though it wasn't quite as bad as when his leg had been cut open in Priestella, at least.

"Trying to dive deep and far — it really takes a toll..."

"Am I not helping?"

"No, without Petra-chan's support, this would probably be a lot worse. The exhaustion of what's inside my head... brain fatigue, I suppose you could call it. That part is pretty severe."

Even fellow holders of a Divine Protection couldn't fully understand each other on this, but the way the strain fell depended entirely on the nature of the effect. For instance, the Earth Dragon's 'Divine Protection of Wind Evasion' was a near-flawless Divine Protection, its only drawback the steep difference between when it was active and when it wasn't.
Garfiel's 'Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits,' too, granted good effects while his feet touched the ground, but on the other hand it also placed a continuous high strain on his body to keep it in constant peak condition.
It happened to do no harm because Garfiel was, physically, a hardy sort of race — but if an ordinary human were given it, they'd need to periodically lift their feet off the ground and the like, to ease the burden.

Viewed that way, Otto's 'Divine Protection of the Soul of Language' concentrated the bulk of its strain on the brain.
Since the 'Voices' of living creatures entering through the ear were converted by the brain and output as voices Otto could understand, it was only natural the strain concentrated there.
And what Otto was currently doing was picking up the 'Voices' of the part he normally kept the channel closed to, not taking in — trying to grasp hold of everything that could be heard.

To that end, Petra had learned Yang magic and was using it to physically strengthen Otto — specifically, focused entirely on boosting the function of his head.
It was, essentially, an artificial, externally-applied version of the physical enhancement through mana that a seasoned warrior would naturally perform.
Of course, strengthening the function of his head with Yang magic wasn't going to suddenly make Emilia as perceptive as Ram. What Otto expected from Petra's Yang magic wasn't so much an improvement in his brain's function as an improvement in its endurance.

Just as strengthening cardiopulmonary capacity allows one to dive underwater for longer, strengthening brain function let Otto keep the channel open longer as well.
Without Petra's help, without exaggeration, the results here would have been less than half what they were.

"Though that also means there are plenty of moments where I get tempted to push too deep, too far..."

"And that's when I get to smack you as hard as I want, if it looks even a little bit dangerous, right?"

Swinging her arm in a mock slap, Otto smiled wryly at Petra's reassuring resolve.
In fact, if Otto ever found himself in danger, having Petra pull him back was the wisest course of action. In that case, forcibly severing the channel's connection by brute force was the surest, fastest method.

Chasing the 'Voices' he could hear too deep risked losing his own initial footing.
Of course, the 'Voice' Otto was, as a matter of course, currently using to speak with Petra was the correct one — but if he ever lost track of that correct one, Otto would probably lose language altogether.
If he lost track of the 'Voice' closest to him, Otto would have to keep the channel open forever, just to be able to speak with anyone. — And if it came to that, he could easily picture a future where he mistook even the sound of wind or the rustle of clothing for 'Voices,' and lost his sanity entirely.

"In that case, I suppose I'd join the ranks of the many holders of the same Divine Protection who died because of it..."

Having luckily survived a childhood in which he might not have, this time it would be his own choice to open the channel that caused him to lose the self he'd established.
Truly, the path of a Divine Protection holder had no shortage of pitfalls.
Though, one could also say, that being made to walk such a difficult path came with a corresponding reward—

"So, how did it look?"

"...The First and Second are already hopeless. There's no one left there whose voice I could pick up. The First was burned to the ground, and the Second... this is Emilia-sama, isn't it."

"Ahh, Emilia-neesama..."

At Otto's mutter, handkerchief still pressed to his nose, Petra made a complicated face.
Guarding the First Summit was Arakiya, one of the topmost in ability even among the Nine Divine Generals — her power, wielded through spirits, had turned the whole area into a burned wasteland, wiping out the creatures Otto needed in order to hear any 'Voices' there at all, right down to the roots.

If birds or small animals, or even insects, hadn't survived, then no matter how wide Otto opened his channel, there was, to begin with, no 'Voice' being emitted for him to pick up.

"If it's a burned wasteland, wouldn't things underground be fine?"

"Even if they survived, creatures underground often don't converse much. And besides, as I told you before, Petra-chan, the 'Divine Protection of the Soul of Language' is..."

"Just because you understand what a creature you can hear is saying, doesn't mean your hearing itself got better."

"Right."

At Petra leaning forward to say it, Otto nodded weakly.
Just as Petra had said, Otto's 'Divine Protection of the Soul of Language' only ever allowed him to understand the 'Voices' he heard, matching language with a party who couldn't otherwise converse. It was a Divine Protection for aligning speech, nothing more.
Which meant, unless he was somewhere the 'Voice' could actually be heard in the first place, the Divine Protection had no way to take effect at all.

This was where Petra's Yang magic, strengthening his head, came in as well.
By boosting his hearing, his capacity to listen, he could pick up 'Voices' over a wider range than usual.
However—

"Hope's gone for the First and Second Summits. One was burned by General Arakiya, and the other got completely frozen over by Emilia-sama."

"Emilia-neesama didn't mean any harm by it, though!"

"I know. And even without Emilia-sama's doing, the response from the Second Summit was already pretty poor to begin with. — Probably because creatures are afraid of Dragonkin."

Petra's phrasing — not that there was no *malice*, but that malice didn't *exist* — was aptly put.
Malice simply didn't exist in Emilia, so she had no way to exercise ill will. That shared understanding aside, the defense Petra offered was also, without exaggeration, simple fact.
The guardian of the Second Summit was Madelyn Eschart, and the one who had left the walled city half-destroyed was Dragonkin — a demi-human race so rare that its very existence was often doubted.

Dragons stood atop the hierarchy of all beings, and Dragonkin, said to be connected to Dragons, inspired fear in animals regardless of size, leaving them no choice but to flee.
Emilia dropping the region's temperature to extreme lows was, in that sense, more like a finishing touch than anything else.
In any case—

"— The Fifth Summit, Garfiel should manage well enough. The Fourth Summit has a group of Glow People launching continuous attacks. As instructed, the surviving Weaponkin and Cyclops Tribe have joined them there."

"The Third has the Shudrak fighting the stone dolls alongside the people who were already there. What about that other route you were checking on, the one besides the summits?"

"Apparently there's a route running straight from the First Summit to the palace, but it looks like it's already been filled in ahead of time. Frustratingly, exactly as Abel-san predicted."

"But since that means no one had to go check it in person, we can move to the next step faster."

Sorting the distribution map of 'Voices' in his head, Otto cross-checked it against the map Petra spread out in her hands. Onto a map already marked with any number of notations, Otto added the freshly gathered information, and Petra further supplemented it with arrows and other marks.
Amid a brain fatigue so heavy it felt like warm water pooling inside his skull, having Petra there to draw out a diagram together that he couldn't visually share was, quite genuinely, invaluable.
For the Emilia Camp, she might well be its single greatest windfall. At the very least, Otto wanted, right now, to credit Petra's presence here as Subaru's single greatest achievement.

"Though, given that the reason we're doing all this is Natsuki-san to begin with, it's a wash..."

Exhaling deeply through his nose, a breath still smelling faintly of blood, Otto checked that his nasal passage was clear. Petra's handkerchief was already thoroughly soaked with his nosebleed.
Resolving to buy and gift her a new handkerchief later, Otto slowly rose to his feet, meaning to change position.
Just then—

"—Otto-chin! Petra-chan!"

"Ah, Medium-chan!"

Waving both arms wide, a small figure came running toward the two of them across the meadow.
Long blonde hair streaming behind her, it was Medium, a girl running restlessly about the battlefield. With her fleet feet, she made a beeline for Otto and the others and said,

"You're being super helpful, Otto-chin! Abel-chin says give him your next report!"

"I doubt he phrased it anything remotely that cute, but... if it's useful to him, I'm glad. No point handing something valuable to someone who can't tell its worth, after all."

"Otto-san, that's harsh. But I get it."

At Medium's report, eyes sparkling, Otto and Petra shared a wry smile.
Unintentionally, having Medium act as go-between softened the chain of command considerably — Otto could focus purely on gathering information and leave its application entirely to Abel and the others, which made things much easier to manage.
It was a structure, too, that likely wouldn't have worked without Medium in the picture.

"Even so, it'd probably still be way more awkward, for sure."

"Probably. Especially since Petra-cha... Petra-ojousama and I are on the side that's had to take a rather hard line with Abel-san."

"—? Am I being complimented? Yay!"

At the exchange between Otto and Petra, Medium threw both hands up in delight.
A moment behind her came four soldiers meant to relay messages to and from the main camp — likely a result of Abel weighing the value of the information Otto provided quite highly. By rights, it would probably be easier for Abel too if Otto were stationed at the main camp.

"Otto-chin can't hear his 'Voices' unless he's wandering around all over, right?"

"Otto-san's ears just aren't quite that big, you see."

"Both of those are the wrong way to put it, in different ways!"

Medium, without any malice, and Petra, with a hint of teasing, had each managed to phrase it in some snagging way. Both were, roughly speaking, factually accurate, so he couldn't even deny it.
In any case, he handed the map he'd just finished marking to the messengers, and received a new one in exchange, adapting to a battle situation that was changing in real time.

"Please make good use of this. The handwriting's a bit messy in places, but the arrows and other marks Ojou-sama added should make it clear enough symbolically."

"Understood. Analyst-dono, please take due care with your own scouting as well."

"Analyst..."

At the title handed to him along with the exchanged map, Otto made a sour face.
Merchant, then domestic administrator, then militant domestic administrator, and now analyst on top of it. Just how many more titles would he end up cycling through before the Royal Selection was settled?
Or perhaps, even after the Royal Selection was settled, this worry would never end either.

"That's a happy kind of worry that should probably wait its turn."

At the very least, unless Otto perfectly saw through what was in front of him right now, the tomorrow he imagined wouldn't come around at all.
Tomorrow was not something Otto could greet alone regardless.
So—

"Otto-san, let's move."

"Good grief, not even a moment's rest allowed, Ojou-sama?"

"You're one to talk, when you never rest even when I tell you to, normally. Once everything's settled, you can drink until you drop, so hang in there."

"That makes it sound like I'm some kind of hopeless drunkard!?"

As Otto raised his voice over the unfair reputation being pinned on him, Petra stuck her tongue out to cover for it.
Feeling that consideration being shown so things wouldn't turn too grave, Otto, separately from wiping away the blood, slapped his own cheek to bring back his focus.
Just how frightening, how lacking in composure, had his face looked to both Petra and Garfiel — that was something he both wanted and didn't want to know.
Pinning all the blame for it on Subaru, who wasn't present here—

"Might as well claim the victory bonus, seeing as we've come this far. And to that end—"

The instant Otto opened the channel further, for more information, more certainty.
A tremendous burst of noise shook his brain.

"—"

"—Otto-san?"

Noticing Otto's profile stiffen, Petra called his name.
But the call Petra voiced didn't reach Otto's ears. Because, with enough force to paint over it entirely, the world was screaming.

"Ugh, ah...!?"

Struck by a sensation as if his brain were boiling in an instant, Otto clutched his head against the shock. But he caught the collar of his consciousness just as it was about to be blown away, and just barely held his ground.
The tremendous scream still went on, swallowing the world whole.
The reason for it was—

"—ah"

Beside Otto, struck by the sudden shock, Petra and Medium, the two girls, looked up at the sky, mouths hanging open.
Within the girls' field of vision — plunging straight from sky to earth, and opening its wings just before impact, a colossal, majestic presence — even at a distance, its presence conveyed vividly the reason the world had screamed.
No wonder the world, every living creature in it, screamed.

"A Dragon..."

Whether it was Petra or Medium who said that, or perhaps one of the messenger soldiers, wasn't certain.
That it hadn't been himself, that much Otto could say for certain.
Why was that—

"—!"

The instant every being on the battlefield was gripped by the appearance of the white Dragon, there were only two whose consciousness wasn't seized by that presence — and Otto, one of the two, had, on instinct, already grabbed Petra's hand and shoved Medium's shoulder with his other hand.

"—Kyaa!"

Screaming, Petra fell, forced backward, into the chest of the falling Otto. Below, in his field of view, the shoved Medium landed on her backside.
That much was the extent of the evasive action Otto could manage in that single instant.

—Over the heads of the falling Otto and Petra, and the seated Medium, a mass of blazing red flame scorched the air as it passed straight through.

"~~!"

Following the shock of being pulled down, Petra's slender throat let out a scream.
But being able to scream was, in its own way, a sign of safety. If things had gone worse than that, there wouldn't even have been time to scream.
In fact, that was exactly what happened to the messenger soldiers whom Otto's hands hadn't been able to reach.

"—"

The mass of flame that passed over Otto and the others' heads struck the messenger soldier carrying the map instead.
The next instant, the soldier, clad in a uniform of red and black, had his entire body consumed by flame in an instant. Map and all, with no time even to stop it.
And he wasn't even granted the chance to raise a cry of shock at the horror of it.

"—! Look out!"

The instant a girl's high voice rang out, the clang of steel striking steel echoed.
The voice belonged to Medium, who should have been the one shoved down by Otto. From her seated position, she'd stretched her long legs, child though she was, and drawn the machete from the small of her back while keeping low.
And with it, she'd batted back with all her strength the murderous blade swung down at Otto.

"Get up, Otto-san!"

Pulled by the arm, hauled up by Petra, Otto stumbled forward.
Catching himself and looking back, he saw Medium, gripping with both hands a machete far too large for her small frame, facing down her attacker head-on, locked in a glare.
And then—

"—Failed, failed. Was gonna get all three of you in one go with that."

Saying so, the man facing off with Medium — someone who, amid the extraordinary circumstance of a white Dragon appearing on the battlefield, was, aside from Otto, the only other being who had consciously ignored the Dragon's presence — grumbled that.
It was an imperial soldier with a bandana wrapped around his head. In his hands was a long-handled axe manageable even one-handed, and judging by his armband, he wasn't of particularly high rank either.
A common soldier. If there was a problem, it was why such a man was here at all.

Otto had kept his channel narrowed, but hadn't let his guard down, even so.
How had this man managed to evade Otto's 'Voice'-based scouting?
Keeping the maximum possible vigilance, Otto shielded Petra behind him with an arm, and glared at the man alongside Medium.

"Even women and children, without mercy? That's quite savage of you, isn't it?"

"'There's women and children even on a battlefield,' is an easy line to say, but that'd make an awful lot of the capital's own residents collateral damage in this story. Besides, that's a pretty convenient excuse."

"Convenient?"

"If you were just non-combatants stranded on a battlefield, your logic just now might hold up some. But I don't call someone workin' a job on a battlefield a non-combatant."

Ambushing without mercy, targeting their lives from the start.
The chances of reasonable negotiation working from the outset had been low, but faced with that thoroughgoing attitude, one could say all hope of it was now completely cut off.
Even so, something didn't sit right.

"You think I, or these girls, are 'working a job'? Out here, away from any camp, what job would that be..."

"Beats me. But my gut's tellin' me. You lot are the biggest root of all the trouble bein' caused in this war. And my gut's also tellin' me somethin' else."

"...What's that?"

Flatly, as if appraising them, the man surveyed Otto and the others before cutting himself off there.
When Otto prompted him to continue, the man looked the three of them over in turn, and said,

"—That you three are also the kind it's best not to give any time to."

With that declaration, the axe swung back, flashing down without mercy to take the lives of all three of them.
