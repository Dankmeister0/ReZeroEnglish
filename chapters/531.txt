Arc 7 Chapter 33　"Now, Onward to the Journey to the Magic City"

—Use whatever can be used to reclaim the Vollachian imperial throne.

The fortress city of Guaral, of course, along with the People of Shudrak, two-star general Zikr Osman, and Natsuki Subaru himself besides — all of it, put to use.
That much confirmed, Abel had just nodded his assent to Subaru's question.

"—So it seems the talk has reached its conclusion."

Priscilla murmured, watching the meeting reach its verdict, and hid her mouth behind an open fan.
As she said, the meeting had, on the whole, come together. They'd shared their conditions for victory, decided their course going forward, and settled on their next destination and objective.
All that remained was—

"If it's going to be a small elite group heading to Chaosflame, what's next? Right now that's four of us — me, Abel, and Taritta-san and Medium-san..."

"—Mind if I put in a word on that?"

"Al?"

As Subaru moved to settle on the final roster for their next destination — the magic city of Chaosflame — Al cut in from the side.
Raising his one remaining arm, he scratched the back of his neck with it.

"Princess, mind if I slip the reins for a bit? Feel like sticking with my brother here."

"Wha—?!"

"Whoa there, your voice just cracked, brother. That surprising?"

At the unexpected proposal, Subaru's voice went up an octave, and Al's throat shook with laughter.
But no matter how offhandedly he said it, Subaru couldn't just nod along with a "sure, sounds good."

"W-why..."

"'Why' nothing, I already told you. That I'd help my brother out. Even a washed-up old man like me oughta be good for something, somewhere."

"You were serious about that?"

At Al's answer, shrugging his sturdy shoulders, Subaru cast his mind back to their exchange before the meeting, and found himself startled that Al meant to follow through on what they'd said.
That he'd been encouraged was a fact. That he'd been given courage was a fact. That he'd been pushed forward was a fact.
But he hadn't imagined Al meant it seriously when he'd said he'd "help."

"Heh, what's with the freeze-up? My offer move you that much?"

"...No, once I got over the initial shock, I just — I've never heard anyone say Al's strong, and with me and Abel added in, the male-to-female combat ratio's gonna be brutal."

"Sorry to disappoint if you were expecting something, but I'm way weaker than that Amazon-ing lady over there!"

Al pointed sharply at Taritta, proudly owning up to something so pathetic.
In truth, Taritta's skill probably ranked among the best of the People of Shudrak, but now that they'd witnessed a battle against one of the Nine Divine Generals, it made for a pretty unreliable reinforcement.
In any case—

"But I am glad you'd say that, at least. I'll take the sentiment."

"Heh, don't sweat it, brother... wait, huh? Just the sentiment? Don't tell me — is this a polite turn-down? Am I getting the 'it just wasn't meant to be this time' treatment?"

Al's questions were endless at Subaru's reply, but that reading of it wasn't wrong.
The offer itself really did make him happy, but this was the Vollachian Empire. To begin with, Al joining their ranks was even more a matter of happenstance than Flop or Medium's had been—

"—Al."

"Ah, Princess."

Just as Subaru reached that conclusion, a beautiful voice suddenly called Al's name.
The voice's owner was, of course, Priscilla Barielle, sitting regally at the round table. Narrowing her crimson eyes, she turned an unreadable gaze on Al.
At her own attendant, who had just declared his next course of action.

"Was it not you who offered to accompany me as far as the fortress city? And now you mean to leave me behind and go off on some jaunt with your fellow fools?"

"Don't figure it'll be cheerful enough to call a jaunt, but yeah, that's the plan. Or what, Princess gonna get lonely without me? If you wanna hug it out and beg me to stay..."

"Fool."

"Yeah, figured."

Cut off before he could even finish his joke, Al hung his head, looking like he hadn't expected much else.
Glaring at the top of Al's helmet, Priscilla let out a small sigh.

"—Dance well, then, for all it's worth."

"Oh, don't worry, I know. And you'd better not go slacking off just 'cause I'm not around, and neither's little Schult. Princess's good looks belong to the whole world, y'know."

"No need to tell me that. Who do you take me for?"

"Why, none other than the center of the world itself — my princess, Priscilla Barielle."

Delivering the flowery line with a playful flourish, Al gave Priscilla a glib little bow. Then he turned back around and raised a hand toward Subaru. "Count on me."

"Wait, that exchange just now was the final decision?! What about my opinion?!"

"What, you're awfully against this, that's a bummer. Or is it that I stink of old age or something and just don't notice? That rough to be around?"

"That's not what this is about. I mean, why does the right to decide belong to—"

"Nah, 'course you're free to refuse. But — can you refuse? It's the Princess we're talking about."

Al jerked his chin toward Priscilla, who was fanning her face.
Her sheer, imperious presence was overwhelming grounds enough for Al's claim — a testament to just how terrifying it would be to overturn something Priscilla had decided.
Still, if this would lead to trouble down the road, Subaru couldn't just let it slide.

"Priscilla, I—"

"—It matters not. Come along, if it's needed."

"Abel, you bastard, aren't you supposed to be treating me as your strategist?!"

But just as he tried to voice his opinion to Priscilla, he was shot down from behind yet again.
Subaru glared at Abel for butting in, and Abel faced him back just as boldly, arms crossed. Like Priscilla, he too intended to see his own will through, stubbornly.
But that wasn't the terms of employment they'd agreed on, and Subaru had no choice but to appeal directly.

"If my opinions never get taken into account, what's the point of being your strategist?"

"Do not grow conceited. If your opinion has worth, I will lend it my ear. But the authority to decide rests with me. That, I will not entrust to you."

"Grrgh, so you're the kind of nasty boss who takes credit for his subordinate's work...?"

"Do not make me repeat myself. —The credit is yours. It must be."

"—————"

—An emperor driven from his throne, with a man of formidable wisdom at his side.
That was the reputation Abel wanted attached to Subaru. He was saying, surely, that he wouldn't mind ceding credit, or even exaggerating things a little, to build it.
But that would be no different from piling up nothing but fiction.

"A fake won't do. It has to be real, or I can't get back what I've lost."

"—. Very well. Then convince both myself and everyone around us, with your own two hands, and no one else's. Until you manage that, it is nothing but the deluded ravings of a fool nursing empty ambitions."

"Damn you, fine, I'll do it. I'll put every rotten liability to good use, one way or another."

Gritting his back teeth, Subaru answered while staring straight into Abel's cold black eyes.
Even facing the same direction, theirs wasn't a relationship where either could let his guard down. That gulf didn't seem to come from standing on different ground — kingdom versus empire — but from something far larger.
As long as that remained, there was a chance he and Abel would come to a decisive parting of opinion somewhere down the line.
And when that happened, Natsuki Subaru would—

"...Don't tell me that 'rotten liability' crack was about me, Princess?"

"Fool."

To Al, who slumped his shoulders and muttered as much, Priscilla offered only that brief reply.

—With the expedition members to the magic city of Chaosflame decided, things began to move.

"Have the fortress city's troops in order by the time I return. Before long, I'll issue a proclamation."

"Even at the cost of my life, I shall see it accomplished."

At Abel's order, Zikr knelt on one knee and accepted the charge with reverence.
Zikr had unexpectedly found himself making an enemy of the mighty empire, yet there wasn't a shred of hesitation in his bearing — though naturally, not every soldier and officer in the city shared that resolve.
Unifying their will and forging them into a single army required, above all, the efforts of a general. In that sense too, Zikr had proven a real find.

"Zikr-san, I imagine this will be difficult, but..."

"No, this is simply the role I, as a general, ought to fulfill in the first place. Why, compared to the Bloodless Surrender, it's no trouble at all."

"—————"

At Zikr's words, spoken with a proud, hearty laugh, Subaru lowered his eyes.
Championing the Bloodless Surrender, boasting he could pull it off, and then failing spectacularly — that was Subaru's track record. As a result, he had betrayed Rem's expectations and brought about her disappointment, or so he believed.
But at the sight of Subaru like this, Zikr narrowed his eyes.

"Do not be so despondent. You proposed the Bloodless Surrender, and His Excellency sanctioned carrying it out. And as a matter of fact, myself and my staff officers did join your ranks without shedding blood."

"Without shedding blood, but that's..."

An exaggeration, Subaru's voice caught as he looked at the bandages wound around Zikr's wounds. But Zikr's cheeks eased into a smile, and then he forcibly tore off his own bandage.
The blood-stained bandage came away, laying bare the wound carved into his arm, but—

"A wound like this is a mere scratch to a soldier. More than that, you ought to be proud. Because of your efforts, the number of officers and men left to fight for His Excellency was not diminished."

"—————"

"All you need do is fulfill your own duty. —Miss Natsumi."

Subaru wasn't sure he'd managed to return an adequate answer to Zikr for saying so.
Still, he found himself thinking well once again of Zikr's character — a man who could find something worth praising even in an outcome that couldn't be wholeheartedly celebrated — and it struck him that not letting Zikr die had, itself, been an accomplishment.

And then—

"—Are you really going?"

"—————"

"No, never mind, that was a strange thing to say. Please forget it."

So said Rem, shaking her head, her eyes unable to hide her exhaustion.
Leaning on her cane, she stood at the entrance of the city hall. Her attire wasn't travel gear, but the light clothing of a healer who ran herself ragged tending to the wounded.

Rem was staying behind in Guaral.
She and Subaru, bound for Chaosflame, would be parting ways temporarily.
Ahead of that parting, Rem had come to see him off, and Subaru gave a wry smile at her remark. Even knowing it was said out of consideration, he couldn't do it.

"It's not like it's all good memories, but it's rough being told to forget something about you."

"Ah, that's not what I meant..."

"Getting my neck strangled, getting my fingers broken — everything you've given me is an irreplaceable, precious memory to me."

"Excuse me?"

He hadn't meant it as a jab, but Rem's cold gaze skewered him regardless.
It was only that, when he cast his mind back over the most shocking events since being flung to Vollachia, the memories that leapt to mind first tended to be the ones with the strongest impact of pain and suffering.
Of course, memories of hope and joy — of growing closer to Rem, of being shown her consideration — were also tucked away, in great abundance, deep in his chest.

—Though the most painful memory of all was stored away in that very same place.

"No sense of urgency at all... Are you really going to be all right?"

"Being on edge the whole time would wear a guy out too. ...If you ask whether I'll be all right, well, I've got a lot of thoughts on that. If I could, I'd rather stay by your side the whole time."

"Uh-huh."

"What an unenthusiastic response! Well, better than being ignored, I guess..."

Being brushed off like that stung, but the words he'd told Rem were his honest, unfeigned feelings.
Right up until the very last moment, he'd agonized deeply over leaving Rem behind in Guaral. Honestly, even in this very instant, he wanted to take back what he'd said, seize her slender arm, and drag her along with him.
How wonderful it would be if, within Natsuki Subaru's reach, he could shield Rem from every hardship, every stray spark, every single bad thing that might befall her.

"Honestly, I'd like to tie you and me together with a string that never breaks."

"Are you being serious...?"

"Pretty much."

"—————"

At last, he couldn't even get an "Excuse me?" out of her.
Of course, knowing full well he'd be turned down, he'd only floated the idea on the off chance. Tying himself to Rem permanently by a string, so he'd always know she was safe, would be the ideal.
Depending on the circumstances, he wouldn't hesitate to use the power he'd awakened at the Pleiades Watchtower, but—

"Trouble is, I still don't really get how to use Cor Leonis properly..."

Cor Leonis, Subaru's new Authority that had manifested at the Pleiades Watchtower — it was a power that let him vaguely sense the positions and conditions of his allies, centered on himself. On top of that, it also had a facet allowing Subaru to shoulder his companions' burdens, keeping them in top shape.
It occurred to him that, if he used that power, he might be able to take on the burden of Rem's crippled leg, letting her run freely across hill and field.
Although if he did that and she went somewhere beyond his reach, that would be a problem in itself—

"—————"

"W-why the sudden long face?"

"Ah, self-loathing."

Pointed out by Rem, Subaru pressed a hand to his own wilted expression and sighed.
He'd thought it, just now. That thanks to Rem's crippled leg, she'd been kept somewhere she couldn't get away from him. That her being unable to flee entirely had been one of the factors keeping his relationship with Rem from ending.
He'd thought himself lucky, that Rem wasn't at her full strength.

"No wonder Rem can't bring herself to trust me, thinking things like that."

Subaru was always thinking only of himself.
He wanted to be kinder. He wanted to become someone kind and wise and strong. He'd looked back on himself — someone who'd worked hard for everyone's sake — there at the tower, and it still wasn't enough.

—He still couldn't reclaim the Natsuki Subaru that Rem had once believed in.

"Um...?"

"Sorry. Got a little sentimental there."

"Senti-what, now?"

"Don't worry about it. It's a phrase that only makes sense back home... and to Al, more or less."

Subaru shrugged as he answered, and Rem furrowed her brow ever so slightly. But as if putting off whatever feeling had sprouted within her, she rubbed at her eyes and smoothed the crease away.
There was likely a good deal of conflict within Rem too. Even coming out here to see him off like this must have carried its own weight of feeling for her.

"I don't mean to be gone long. Without you around, I won't be able to stand it."

"...There you go again, saying things like that."

"Uh, it's how I really feel, but... if it creeps you out, I'll try to hold back."

"So you're not going to stop, then."

Given a flat, half-lidded stare, Subaru dropped his shoulders in a show of contrition.
Of course, he didn't want to sour Rem's mood, and he didn't want to make her uncomfortable. But that aside, what welled up regardless was the sheer strength of Subaru's feelings for Rem.
Though, granted, that was easy to say for a Subaru who had already disappointed Rem once.

"I'll do my best and come back soon. I mean to bring back good news."

"...Right. I'll look forward to it, from Abel-san, Medium-san, and Taritta-san."

"What about Al, and — what about me?"

"—————"

He'd asked just to check, but the temperature of Rem's gaze didn't change one bit.
But his expression at that answer must have been pathetic enough that, after a moment of silence, Rem let out a small, resigned sigh.

"If we're talking about whether or not I trust you, things have gotten a good deal better."

"We weren't even talking about trust..."

"The stench of wickedness hasn't faded one bit, as usual, but that's not the issue here."

"Not the issue...?"

In Rem's pale blue eyes as she went on, there remained a light of distrust that refused to fade.
They were about to be apart, even if only temporarily. Even knowing her distrust stemmed from Subaru's failures in Guaral, he wanted to remove even a little of it.
Not so much for Subaru's own sake, but for the sake of Rem's peace of mind while she waited in the city.

"Tell me, Rem. Whatever I can do, I'll do my best. What would it take to clear away your — your unease, or whatever it is?"

"...Then why are you still dressed like that?"

"Huh?!"

Told that by a flat-eyed Rem, Subaru looked down at himself.
A long black-haired wig, white powder applied to hide his scars, an outfit tailored to obscure his body's lines, and accessories elegant without being gaudy—

"Was something off...?"

"The strange part is that nothing's off about it at all — that's point one. Point two is that you're still dressed like that when you're about to leave the fortress city. How do you explain yourself?"

"Wait, I already explained this is necessary!"

Watching the temperature of Rem's eyes drop further, Subaru let out something close to a scream, still in full Natsumi Schwarz mode.
That this getup was contributing to her distrust was completely unexpected, but as he'd already told Rem, there was a proper reason behind it.

"Rem, like I told you yesterday, we're from the country next door to this one. And if my real name gets around, it'll cause trouble that crosses the border. That's why it's necessary. The existence of Natsumi Schwarz, who isn't the real me...!"

"Uh-huh. Is that so."

"That is not the response of someone whose distrust has been cleared away at all!!"

The temperature of Rem's flat stare didn't change; if anything, her distrust seemed to have deepened further.
But this was a countermeasure Subaru had devised in dead earnest, weighing his own circumstances against the role he'd been saddled with.

—Here in the Vollachian Empire, he couldn't afford to make the name "Natsuki Subaru" known.
Natsuki Subaru was already the knight of Emilia, an important figure of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica. Which meant that what he was doing now amounted to full-fledged interference in a neighboring country's internal affairs.

"Well, maybe there's no such thing as 'full-fledged' when it comes to interfering in internal affairs, but..."

Regardless, the important point was that the consequences of Subaru's actions wouldn't stop at Subaru alone. —Put simply, it might cause trouble for Emilia.
As her knight, sworn to help her along the path toward the throne, Subaru could never allow himself to drag her down like that.

"So that's where Natsumi Schwarz comes in. However famous the name Natsumi gets, there's no problem. Natsumi's just some black-haired beauty who suddenly turned up in the Vollachian Empire, nothing more."

"Are you finished?"

"Not yet! Besides, my calculation is — if it's the name Natsumi Schwarz, there's still a chance my people back in Lugunica will notice and figure it out."

In fact, that was the single biggest reason for sticking specifically to the alias "Natsumi Schwarz."
This wasn't the first time Subaru had crossdressed and gone by the name Natsumi Schwarz — the Vollachian Empire wasn't a first for that. Not exactly as entertainment back at Roswaal Manor, but there had been an occasion where circumstances forced him to crossdress, and everyone but Emilia knew his true identity from it.
The alias he'd used then was the same one. Which meant that, going forward, if Subaru's presence gained recognition within the empire as Abel intended, and the name that spread was "Natsumi Schwarz," there was a chance Emilia and the others might learn that Subaru and the rest had been flung into the empire.
And so—

"I'm wearing this because circumstances demand it."

"—. —. ——————. Understood."

It had taken quite a while, but he felt relieved to have gotten Rem's understanding.
In any case, for now, Subaru's crossdressing arrangement would continue a while longer. He didn't want it to drag on so long that it damaged Rem's trust, but that too would depend on circumstances.

"Rem, if something comes up, talk to Flop-san or Zikr-san. If it's something hard to bring up with a man, Mizelda-san and the others are there too. Don't try to carry it all alone."

"I'm not sure I like hearing that from you, of all people, but I'll keep it in mind. ...As for you, please try not to be a burden on Abel-san and Medium-san."

"I'll be careful about the second half of that, at least."

Medium and Taritta aside, he had no intention of holding back when it came to burdening Abel.
Every now and then, Abel ought to lose that cool composure of his and struggle with sweat on his brow too.

And so, once they'd gone through their round of cautions and lingered over the parting, the time to depart drew near.
Suppressing his reluctance to part, before setting off—

"Rem, what happened to that one?"

"Rui-chan? I imagine she's with Utakata-chan right about now..."

"I see."

"...You're not asking me to go fetch her, are you."

Rem's voice dropped, and she turned a probing look his way.
She'd guessed at his intent correctly, but her tone was anything but pleasant. If anything, it carried a thick undercurrent of irritation and bitterness.

Since the fight at the city hall, in the midst of the chaos, he'd had almost no contact with Rui, but Subaru's guard against her remained just as high as ever. If anything, his suspicions were unlikely to ever clear.
Even if she seemed attached to Rem and Utakata, and got along fine with the Shudrak, there was no telling when or where she might bare her true nature.
In that sense, there was unease in leaving Rui behind too.
But still—

"But you already left her out of your sight the entire time during the operation to bring down Guaral, didn't you?"

"—————"

"It's a bit late for this."

Told that by Rem, he had no answer to give.
If Rui were going to act, she'd already had chance after chance to do it. Which meant, perhaps, that going on guarding against her endlessly was pointless.

"I did at least tell Kuna and the others not to let their guard down."

"Stubborn..."

At that small, whispered word, Subaru thought it over for a moment.
At first, he hadn't told Rem about Rui's true nature or her dangerous Authority because there'd been no foundation for her to believe what he said. In that state, telling her about Rui's circumstances risked losing what little trust he had left with Rem, and being pushed further away — something he'd wanted to avoid.

But what about now?
Their relationship had improved; cold as she was, she'd at least hear him out. Maybe now, even telling her about Rui's true nature wouldn't be dismissed out of hand.

"...No, quit being an idiot."

He shook his head, rejecting the idea that had faintly surfaced in his mind.
She might believe him, but even if she did, nothing would actually change. Rui hadn't shown her tail up to now. It seemed unlikely that the moment he told Rem, Rui would suddenly reveal the true nature she'd kept hidden. The situation itself wouldn't change.
It would only serve to ease Subaru's own conscience while heaping more anxiety onto Rem.
There was no need to do something like that.

"...What's with that face?"

"Ah, just thinking I hope only happy things pile up in Rem's life from here on."

"Excuse me?"

Rem's gaze sharpened at Subaru, who was holding back something welling up slowly inside him.
That said, if he tried to put it into words, it would spill out endlessly, without end—

"—Brother! Let's get moving, time to head out."

So said Al, leaning against the carriage and waving.
Behind him stood the carriage, and a horse-like creature boasting a body nearly as massive as the carriage itself — a creature called a Galewind Horse, apparently the one that would haul them along their journey.

"I hear it's a rare animal even in Vollachia, one only granted to generals..."

"Zikr-san lent it to us. It's a proper mare too, so it's all consistent."

"Consistent...?"

Rem tilted her head, apparently not quite following Subaru's meaning.
Like this, Rem reacting to Subaru's words one way or another — that happy state of affairs, too, would be put on hold for a while now.

"Hey there, brother?"

"Um, Al-san is calling you."

"Yeah, right. I know that, but..."

"—?"

"The soles of my shoes just don't wanna leave the ground next to you, so they won't come off... ow, ow, ow, ow!"

Jabbed in the back by the tip of her cane, his supposedly stuck-fast soles came off the ground. He was made to take two, three more steps forward, the distance between him and Rem widening.
The gap between them, growing properly wide now.

"Rem, I've said it a bunch of times already, but—"

"I'll be careful. I'll stay on guard too. If something goes wrong, I'll rely on someone. Farewell."

"Ugh..."

Given such a perfunctory farewell, Subaru's shoulders slumped and he wilted. Watching him, Rem let out a deep sigh — "honestly" — and said,

"Please be careful, and go safely. I'll be waiting for you to come back."

"Ah..."

"I won't up and disappear on you. ...I trust everyone else, at least, besides you."

Added on as it was, Subaru savored those words slowly.
Then he nodded repeatedly, again and again, even as he watched Rem's face pucker in distaste.

"—I'm off!"

he said, waving broadly, and so Rem sent him on his way.

"—————"

The carriage receded into the distance, passing through the fortress city's main gate and vanishing into the far distance.
The carriage's destination lay southeast of the city — the magic city of Chaosflame, a stronghold of one of the empire's foremost powerhouses, and the whole matter hinged on whether they could talk that person around.
Honestly, if the point was recruiting someone, there remained doubts about the choice to bring Subaru along.

"...That it's a valiant effort, I'm sure everyone would grant."

Leaning on her cane, Rem narrowed her eyes at the trail left by the now-vanished carriage.
Right up to their parting, Subaru had never really risen above the level of foolish antics. He'd lined up all manner of excuses, but to Rem, it just looked like he didn't want to stop crossdressing.
Not that she thought every excuse he'd given was a lie, of course.

"So they've gone, have they. With that noisy lot out of the way, this city ought to breathe a little easier. Whether what they bring back is good news or Abel's own head, who's to say."

"—Priscilla-san."

The voice called out suddenly from behind Rem as she stood there.
Without even needing to turn, the woman who came gliding up beside her, leaving a vivid impression of red, was Priscilla — clad in an opulent dress, flaunting her violently beautiful looks without the slightest reserve.

Ever since being saved, her own life included, from the rampage of Arakiya's assault on the city hall, Rem hadn't exchanged anything resembling real conversation with her.
So, addressed so suddenly, her bewilderment and confusion only deepened.

"...Thank you, for what you did."

"Hm. Gratitude for what, exactly?"

"What happened at the city hall yesterday. You saved us from that woman, Arakiya. Not just me — everyone else too. Thanks to you—"

"Were you about to say a great many owe their lives to me? If you ask me, the credit for those lives belongs to you. It was you who used your own gift and training to save those on the brink of death. I had no intention of showing mercy. Do not go twisting my actions to suit yourself."

"That's not what I meant to—"

She'd been about to say "meant to do," but Rem caught herself.
Jumping to conclusions about someone else's thoughts and actions, their intent, and trying to force a judgment into her own mold — it was a bad habit. Not just with Priscilla; she'd caught herself doing it more than a few times before.
And this, despite having lived, as far as her lost memories were concerned, for barely two weeks.

"—————"

"What, nothing to say back? That too is a most tedious sort of judgment."

"...I'm sorry. I thought you were right. But — the fact that I'm grateful to you, that's not something even you can twist, is it?"

"Oh?"

Priscilla said this with interest, drawing her fan out from between her breasts. Snapping it open with a sharp sound, she gently hid her well-shaped lips behind it.
But her eyes, left uncovered, held a light of amusement and opportunity she couldn't quite hide.

"I had heard you'd lost yourself, but... my, my, talking back to me like this."

"'Lost myself' isn't quite the right way to put it. I've merely forgotten. I haven't vanished, or ceased to exist."

Pressing a hand firmly to her own chest, Rem argued back at Priscilla.
If she had truly vanished, that would be no different from some fleeting dream, beyond anyone's reach. But Rem's lost memories hadn't vanished. Even if they weren't within Rem, they existed within Subaru, who was desperately trying to bring them back.
If what he said was true, it seemed she had a twin sister.
Did that sister, too, still carry within her the existence of who Rem had been before she lost her memories? And within the companions and family Subaru had spoken of — people she had yet to meet — was she there too?

If only she could resign herself entirely to the idea that all of Rem had vanished, that everything was being built up from a place where nothing at all had existed before—

"—If I could resign myself to that, this chest of mine wouldn't have to hurt like this."

"—————"

"Constantly, always, every single second, I find myself thinking it. Every time I catch his eye, that I'm continuing to betray this person's expectations."

Of course, Subaru surely never meant it that way.
The stench had once grown so thick and heavy around him that it was hard even to make out his form — but enduring it to steal a glimpse of his face, it was always, simply, desperate.
And that desperation was directed toward the self that no longer remained within Rem — the self that had vanished.

"How selfish can I be. I'm appalled at myself..."

So she couldn't tell him — him, who found it so hard to part — that she'd felt relieved to finally be apart.
Nor could she tell him about this ugly feeling of hers, that went beyond mere relief.

"—What was your name, again?"

"Huh?"

"Your name, girl. Don't tell me you lost that along with yourself? And yet you're not nameless — you're plainly called by one. It was, let me see..."

Priscilla lifted her gaze lightly, searching the empty air for the answer to her own question.
In all likelihood, she remembered Rem's name perfectly well. Even in this short time, Rem could tell she possessed an exceptionally sharp intellect.
And, at the same time, that she was someone with a thoroughly wicked streak.
And so—

"—Rem."

Rem gave her own name before Priscilla could deliberately get it wrong.
Her memory was lost, with no sign of returning, and now she was parting even from the one who knew her past self — but at least this name, the one she had been called and had claimed as her own for these two weeks, was real.

"How amusing."

Priscilla murmured shortly, hearing that answer.
Then she snapped her fan shut and, with its closed tip, gently lifted Rem's chin. Pierced from the front by those crimson eyes, Rem felt her throat scorched by their heat.
But even unable to put it into words, she poured her resolve into her eyes and stared back.

"Rem. I shall keep you at my side for a while."

"...At your side?"

"You may have the aptitude and the will, but your skill doesn't measure up. That clumsy healing magic of yours — I'll knock it back into shape, in moderation. Do that, and it might become something worth watching, at least a little."

"—! You'll teach me healing magic?"

"Fool. I have no aptitude for the healing arts. I merely possess an eye for beauty. One look tells me exactly what you lack."

"—————"

The answer that came back wasn't what she'd expected — in a sense, it exceeded her expectations, or perhaps veered wildly off from them.
But if it meant putting to better use the one skill Rem currently had — healing magic, the power to mend others — then it was worth putting her trust in Priscilla's words.
After all, she'd only just been made to wish she had more strength, and to regret that she didn't.

"But weren't you going back to your own stronghold, Priscilla-san?"

"I've called that off. Partly because I've lent that fool Al to Abel. Whether he meets the conditions I've set him won't be long in showing itself. I'll wait here. There'll be inconveniences, I'm sure... but for that, I'll simply summon Schult and be done with it."

"R-right..."

Whatever had struck a chord in her, Priscilla had decided to stay behind in Guaral. Either way, if Rem was to study under her, having her stay nearby would be a help.
There were still plenty of people in this city who needed even Rem's unpolished healing arts. She couldn't leave the city while they remained.

"That settled, then — prepare a room for me. I'll leave the finer points to Schult, but I need somewhere to spend today and tomorrow at least."

"R-right, understood. I'll have it prepared right away."

Ordered around as though it were the most natural thing, Rem nonetheless found no urge to resist, and simply complied.
Part of it was Priscilla's sheer bearing, compelling obedience whether one liked it or not — but Rem herself also felt no resistance to being told, plainly, what needed doing.

Hurrying back to the city hall with quick, pattering steps despite her cane, she thought she'd need to consult Zikr about Priscilla's stay and preparing her a room.
The People of Shudrak weren't accustomed to operating within a city either, so she felt a twinge of guilt at relying on Zikr for so much of it.
Though, granted, he was the sort of man who'd never say no or refuse when a woman came to him for help.

"Ah, Re! There you are!"

"Utakata-chan."

On her way through the city hall toward the office where Zikr was likely to be, Rem crossed paths with a girl gazing out through a hallway window — Utakata.
The moment Utakata noticed Rem, she broke into a friendly, beaming smile. Unable to help herself, Rem's own cheeks loosened into a smile back.

"Sorry, things have been a bit hectic lately."

"U's fine, no worries! More than U, it's Ta you oughta worry about. She was all wobbly."

"Taritta-san, yes, well..."

Taritta, newly appointed as chief, had gone along on Subaru's journey.
Even to young Utakata's eyes, Taritta must have looked unsteady, nearly crushed under the sudden weight of her new role. And yet, in seeking her own way forward and taking a step to win back her confidence, she was admirable.

Even while Rem crouched down, whimpering that she had nothing to offer, the world around her kept moving forward, leaving her behind.
She never wanted to curse the world with complaints and drag everyone else down with her.

"Taritta-san will surely be all right. So let's — you and me and everyone — protect the place she'll come back to."

"Mm, got it! Re, you're reliable!"

"Do you think so? Hearing you say that gives me a little confidence."

At Utakata's unadorned words, Rem's cheeks eased into a faint smile.
Then, a sense of unease struck her, and Rem glanced around. Utakata, standing in the hallway gazing out the window, was entirely alone — no one else was in sight.
It still felt like an environment where a child wandering around alone was careless, but it wasn't that Rem meant to scold her for not being with an adult. —It was something more fundamental than that.

"Utakata-chan, are you alone? I mean, where's Rui-chan?"

She'd left it entirely to others up till now, but Rui had grown quite attached to Utakata.
As friends of about the same age, the two of them seemed to have built an easy, unguarded relationship — which made it all the stranger that Utakata was alone now.
Of course, she thought she was probably overthinking it, but—

"Isn't Rui-chan with you?"

"Yep! Ru? She went along!"

"...Huh?"

Still with her head tilted, Rem froze at Utakata's words.
For a moment her thoughts threatened to go blank, but she held them back, straining her mind to work out the true meaning behind Utakata's words.

Rui wasn't with her, and what Utakata's words meant was—

"R-Rui-chan is—"

"Mm-hm, she went along! Inside the carriage — U helped her climb in, too!"

So declared the bold and fearless Shudrak girl, chest puffed out, just as unabashed as ever, stating it to Rem plainly and without the slightest hint of guilt.
