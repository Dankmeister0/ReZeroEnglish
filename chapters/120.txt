Arc 3 Chapter 43: "The Pig's Desire"

"I may not look it, but I happen to be rather fond of books."

So spoke the girl seated in an extravagant chair, one hand resting on the armrest. In the hand opposite the one she'd propped up, she held an elaborately bound book, open to a passage already well into its latter half, and the way her eyes traced the page felt somehow different from the air she'd carried until then.

She wore a thin garment like a negligee, a white cloth of the same color draped over her shoulders. The voluptuous curves of her body were laid bare before him without the slightest reservation, and yet, even under a man's gaze, the girl showed no sign of being conscious of it. She was absorbed in the world of the book with a naturalness that made it hard to believe she was receiving a guest at all.

Sneakers planted on the carpeted floor, left to stand there, Subaru was at a loss.

He'd been shown inside well enough, but the very mistress of the room paid him no attention. When he tried to force his way into conversation, all he got back was that opening line. Could it be — was she telling him to wait until she finished the book, since she was almost done?

"Even for her, that's a bit much..."

He tried to dismiss the thought as soon as it sprouted in his chest, but watching her turn the pages with such languid grace, he couldn't quite shake the unease. And the fact was, Subaru knew perfectly well she was the sort of person who might say something that unreasonable.

Hair the orange of a captured sun, and crimson eyes like flames that seemed to burn away everything they reflected. Translucent white skin, a figure rich with feminine curves. Silent, her gaze bent quietly toward the book, she was beautiful beyond any words he had to describe it. And if that personality of hers had *not* been cultivated purely to offend everyone she met, one had to wonder just how unfair the gods had been to her.

The girl's name was Priscilla Barielle.

One of the candidates of the Royal Selection — and also the next person Subaru had come, hat in hand, to ask for help.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


Subaru learned that Reinhard was absent from the royal capital at the mansion Reinhard used as his residence while in the city.

It was an auxiliary residence belonging to House Astrea, Reinhard's own family, and according to the elderly couple entrusted with its upkeep,

"In preparation for this Royal Selection, he has accompanied our master, Felt-sama, and her family back to the main estate. It is possible to speak between here and the main house via a communication mirror, but..."

The crux of the matter was that the road between the royal capital and House Astrea's main estate took nearly four days by dragon carriage. Reinhard had left the capital two days ago, meaning he hadn't yet arrived at the main house. When Subaru further asked about the main house's location, it turned out that even rushing to the Mathers domain from there would take five days at the very least — however he twisted it, Reinhard joining the fight was hopeless.

"Roswaal and Reinhard both... useless right when it counts...!"

Having said his farewells to the old couple, Subaru clutched his head once the mansion had faded from sight.

Their timing this time was cursed on a divine scale. With a candidate who might have lent his strength unconditionally now removed from the board, Subaru had truly lost what little margin he had left. It wasn't even a matter of being refused — the fact that he hadn't accounted for Reinhard's absence at all was what stung. And with Reinhard having departed two days ago, even Return by Death couldn't undo it — he was already too late by the time he'd looped back.

If he wanted to rely on Reinhard's help, he would have had to spend those two days before Return by Death — something only possible, in effect, in the very first loop. But that had been a Subaru who knew nothing of the tragedy waiting for him, who had simply let the days pass idly by. There was no redoing that now.

"No use wishing for what I don't have... think, think think think think think think, I don't have the strength, the numbers, or the time. All I've got is my head to wrack."

Racking his brain desperately, Subaru struggled to work out a fallback plan.

With Crusch out and Reinhard out, Subaru — who knew few people in the capital to begin with — had almost no cards left to play. If the credibility of his report on the Witch Cult's appearance would be questioned just as Crusch had pointed out, then storming the knights' garrison seemed just as unlikely to win him any cooperation.

More than anything, Subaru's current standing put him nowhere near a position to negotiate directly with the Kingdom's Order of Knights — and worse, he could easily imagine that his reputation among the knights themselves was abysmal. He hadn't forgotten the ugliness of the knights cheering and egging things on as he was beaten to a pulp in his showdown with Julius at the training grounds. He couldn't imagine men like that lending him a hand, nor did he want to owe them one.

—Having been abandoned by Crusch, whom he'd believed treated him sincerely until now, a whirlpool of suspicion toward others had begun to churn inside Subaru.

In truth, by the latter half of that incident at the training grounds, there had been no knights cheering at all — if anything, most had frowned at Julius's conduct. But there was no way that could have reached Subaru at the time, and afterward, caught up in his exchange with Emilia, he'd had no room to reconsider it.

In Subaru's mind, the knights remained a pack of shameless cowards deserving nothing but scorn, people who — even if he swallowed his humiliation and begged — wouldn't listen to him in earnest. And so Subaru, pretending not to notice how his own past conduct had narrowed his own options, could only rack his mind bloodshot-eyed for a next-best plan.

If the Kingdom's knights were off the table, then the people Subaru knew in the capital numbered only two. And of those two, one was someone he absolutely could not bow his head to, for the same reason as the knights. Which left, in the end, only a single candidate.

"Subaru-kun, what will we do now? Rem..."

"It's fine. Leave it to me. Rem doesn't need to do anything. You don't need to. Just — stay behind me, always."

Unable to bear watching Subaru sink into thought, Rem started to say something. Cutting her off, Subaru kept thinking even as he turned a weak smile toward her.

Letting Rem stand in the line of fire was dangerous. He knew she wouldn't hesitate to throw her own body away, even sacrifice her life, if it meant protecting Subaru. That was the result of Subaru's own actions — having saved her, having let her grow so dependent on him — and so he had to accept it as his due. Having accepted it, the one thing he absolutely had to avoid was any outcome in which she was lost.

For that reason, Subaru stubbornly refused to let Rem step forward. He himself had to be the one to do this. Otherwise he couldn't protect Rem, and there'd be no point saving Emilia and the villagers, no way to settle his hatred for Petelgeuse—

"Huh, that's kind of..."

For a moment something disturbingly dark seemed to flicker through his mind, and Subaru touched his temple.

Just now, it had almost sounded as if killing Petelgeuse mattered more to him than saving Emilia and the others. And on top of that — the idea that protecting their lives *had* to be done by his own hands — something was wrong with that. It was a momentary lapse. Since he could recognize it as such, Crusch's words were beside the point.

"It's fine. It's fine. I'm doing the right thing. Properly."

As if convincing himself, as if grinding the words in through his teeth, as if pretending not to have seen what he'd seen, as if sealing a lid over the abyss, Subaru affirmed himself.

If he didn't affirm it, he wouldn't be able to hold on to his sanity.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


The next morning, having spent the night at an inn, the two of them visited Priscilla's mansion at the edge of the royal capital.

Built at the far end of the noble district, it was the Barielle residence — originally the home of Priscilla's late husband, an old man named Leip Barielle. He seemed to have held a fairly notable position at the royal court; even Rem knew his name, apparently, as someone now deceased.

"At the very least, I doubt this was the taste of that dead old man."

Even from a distance, the mansion's gaudiness, glinting wildly under the morning sun, burned itself into the eye. The roof looked to have been repainted only very recently — coated in gold so lavish it looked as though gold leaf had been scattered across it, gleaming fresh and vivid. If they meant to keep up that shine, they'd need to repaint it constantly.

Beyond the gate, the mansion's walls bore just as many showy ornaments as the roof, and every window facing them was a work of art carved with elaborate designs. Subaru had no firsthand experience with the term "nouveau riche taste," but something about this pressed the concept on him unmistakably, straight through his eyes.

"W-well, well, well, this is the Princess's taste, all right. Pretty rushed construction job too, y'know? It's only been about a month since she came out to the capital from her domain. I feel for the guys who got worked through the night, but hard to complain when you're getting slapped with a bag of gold coins."

"No, wait, getting hit in the cheek with a bag of gold coins isn't much different from just getting punched. Don't try to pass that off with the same vibe as a stack of cash."

There are mystery novels out there where people stuff sand into a sock to use as a blunt weapon. Getting beaten to death with a sack full of gold coins would be, cause of death: nouveau riche, and nothing more.

"That so?" the man said, tilting his head as he scratched the back of his neck through a gap in his helmet — this was Al. A mercenary Priscilla had hired, and, it seemed, a fellow soul summoned from Earth to this other world, just like Subaru. Given how often he dropped modern slang and his outlandish fashion sense, the consistency was high, though he'd apparently been summoned nearly twenty years ago, so there was no denying a generation gap.

In any case, oblivious to Subaru's inner thoughts, Al — who'd met the two of them at the gate — let out a yawn with an attitude of total unguardedness.

"So, what business you got showin' up this early? I mean, look at me, I've got low blood pressure, mornings are seriously rough on me. Give the King of Coi a run for his money."

"Save the 'level up and I shoot a crusher beam, super scary guy' talk. Sorry for barging in without an appointment, but I need to talk to your princess there."

"To the princess?"

Al, who had been swaying limp and boneless a moment ago, straightened up and stared intently at Subaru and Rem. Beyond the black helmet, the sharpness of his gaze was impossible to gauge, but the sense of being appraised was enough to stir discomfort regardless. After a good long while — apparently spent looking mostly at Rem — Al rattled the seam of his helmet.

"Ah, well, got my dose of maid-flavor, so, sure. I'll pass the word along."

"You've got maids in this mansion too, don't you? And that's a pretty light reaction."

"Hey now, you don't get the princess at all. A woman who thinks she's the cutest thing alive doesn't hire maids. Everyone in this house is a pretty-boy butler."

"Just hearing that makes it sound awful... my opinion of her keeps dropping, but go pass the word along anyway."

With a listless, muffled "heeey," Al vanished into the mansion.

Beside him, Rem stayed a step back, silent, her expression carefully blank. And yet, from the way she'd taken a small pinch of Subaru's hem, an unhideable anxiety leaked through — and though he wanted to comfort her, he was gripped by the exact same anxiety, so there was nothing to be done about it.

Even though he hadn't been able to think of anyone else to rely on, it wasn't easy to feel this unsettled just from an exchange with a retainer before the principal even appeared. For a Subaru with no room left to be picky about his methods, this was the last fortress standing.

"Still, maybe coming at this hour was a mistake. Knowing Priscilla, she'll probably just say something like 'You've disturbed my precious sleeping hou—' 'Hey, she says she'll see ya!'"

The words leaking out, carried along by his rising anxiety, were cut off by Al's loud voice, leaning halfway out of the mansion's entrance and waving.

Caught off guard for a moment, Subaru then pushed the gate open hesitantly and walked toward Al, who beckoned him on.

"Th-that was a surprisingly easy appointment to get?"

"You'd be surprised, but the princess is actually a morning person. In exchange, she turns in super early at night, though. Anyway, this way, this way."

Grinning — probably — from behind his helmet at Subaru's flustered reaction, Al guided them inside with an easy air. Following him into the mansion, sure enough, the interior had been just as heavily renovated as the exterior. The corridor, lined with an overwhelming number of art pieces, was more of an obstacle to walking than anything, and the way even the lighting fixtures and picture frames were decked out in precious metals bordered on outright madness.

"Might be a bit much on the eyes at first, but you get used to it. It's not so bad now 'cause it's morning, but the hallway at night's genuinely scary."

"You're not a kid, don't go saying a hallway at night is scary, a grown man like you."

"The statues' eyes glow at night, y'know?"

"That means your master's out of her mind."

Looking up, sure enough, the eyes of the stone statues lining either side of the corridor had something like jewels embedded in them. They probably glowed once it got dark. Madness.

Al tilted his head as if sensing something off about Subaru quietly letting this desecration of art pass without comment, but he didn't remark on it further. Subaru had been trying to make sure Rem, following behind, didn't feel uneasy, but Rem — still gripping his hem as she walked — had been sniffing repeatedly since a while ago, sending suspicious looks at the back leading them onward, as if seeing something dubious.

And then—

"The princess is up on the top floor of this mansion. Whole floor's a single room — luxury spec."

"Sounds like some hotel suite. We allowed up there?"

Reaching the staircase, Subaru asked as much, pointing his thumb up at Al's gesture. Al nodded and answered, with some meaning, "The Brother is, yeah."

At the suspicious look Subaru gave that answer,

"Nah, nah, nah, I'm not sayin' it to be mean. The princess said she'll only see the Brother. The little lady here gets shown to a guest room."

"You think I trust leaving her with the guy who just said he got his maid-flavor dose?"

"Relax, I've been told to wait outside the room too, so don't worry. Galling as it is, the little lady's escort's the pretty-boy butler."

Anticipating Subaru's concern, Al stepped elegantly aside, and from behind him appeared a boy with golden curls and blue eyes. A boy for whom the word "beautiful" was the only adjective that fit, his small frame wrapped in a butler's uniform sized just for him — there was something endearing about him, like a child trying to seem grown-up, that couldn't help but charm anyone watching.

"Alright, don't go being rude to our guest, yeah?"

At Al's easy instruction, delivered with a pat on the shoulder, the boy-butler said "This way, please," and moved to escort Rem with fluid, elegant motions. Rem glanced at Subaru for just an instant, uncertain, but —

"Sorry, wait until the talk's over. I'll bring all the dangerous people — including this guy here — up with me, so relax and make yourself comfortable."

"That's harsh, calling me a dangerous person, Brother. I get called a suspicious character plenty, but—"

"That one's actually a lower rank than dangerous person, if anything."

Turning a cold look on Al's unbothered reply, Subaru then faced Rem again and stroked her head to reassure her. Rem, being stroked, narrowed her eyes ticklishly at the sensation of her hair being mussed, and reluctantly accepted with a small "Understood." Then, leaning in just slightly, she whispered to Subaru,

"Please be especially careful of that man there."

The instant her gaze flicked toward Al, it was clear that the "danger" she'd just warned of was aimed at him as well. His appearance was certainly the most suspicious Subaru had encountered even among everyone he'd met in this other world, and yet, this was someone he trusted to a reasonable degree, which made the warning feel like a stretch. Still—

"Mm, got it."

In terms of trustworthiness, of course, it was Rem who ranked higher. Thinking back on the exchange at Crusch's mansion, he shouldn't extend full trust to someone just because they hailed from the same world as him. He needed to approach this place too as if it were enemy territory.

Having confirmed Subaru's nod, Rem was led off down the corridor by the boy-butler and vanished. To the very last, she left the room reluctantly, glancing back at Subaru with lingering attachment.

"Hyuuu, look at you, Brother. Somebody's loved."

"If you're gonna do it, whistle properly. Can't whistle either, like me?"

There aren't many things sadder than doing a whistle sound effect with your mouth. It was a gesture Subaru felt an odd kinship with, since he himself had never managed to learn to whistle no matter how much he practiced, but Al retracted the attempt with a fairly heavy reply: "Ah, my lips ain't exactly in one piece."

Anyway—

"Not great to keep the little lady waiting too long, but keeping the princess waiting too long's even worse. Let's get on up there."

"Glad you're quick on the uptake. ...By the way, how's Priscilla's mood?"

Going into an important negotiation, he wanted to avoid a situation where the other party was in a bad mood. For most people, this would probably be a minor factor, but when it came to Priscilla, that invisible stat called "mood" seemed likely to swing things unusually hard.

To Subaru's concern, Al tilted his head listlessly with an "hnnn,"

"I'd say neither good nor bad, but honestly, that's not much to go on either. The princess's mood flips around before, during, and after a conversation, up down left right, whatever. She doesn't even have a fixed favorite topic, so you'll just have to wing it and hope your improv skills pull you through."

"Winging it on the spot... that's my worst category."

Having received that heartening advice from Al Serving under her, Subaru headed upstairs, his face clouded with unease. Climbing the stairs, past the landing, at the top was the door to the room.

"Beyond here's the princess's ridiculously huge private room. I'm not invited in, so I'll wait here. Off you go."

Saying this with an easy air, Al plopped himself down on the step in front of the door. As he did, he unfastened the qīnglóng blade he kept holstered at his lower back and set it across his knees.

"Try not to piss her off too much, yeah? I don't wanna deal with her taking it out on me, and when she's in a bad mood she throws around some real unreasonable demands."

"...Sorry, but I actually came here to make a pretty unreasonable demand myself."

Responding brusquely to Al's plea, Subaru took a breath and pushed the door open.

And then—


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


And so, the timeline returns to the confrontation with Priscilla from the opening.

Having entered the room seen off by Al, Subaru, after walking some distance further, encountered her waiting in a hall beyond. She sat in a chair set on a raised platform, elegantly reading her book, not sparing Subaru so much as a glance.

Since he hadn't been thrown out, he was presumably being recognized as a guest, but with no action forthcoming from her side, he found himself unsure how to proceed.

But even while he fumbled over this, time kept ticking steadily onward. Deciding that hesitation would only breed stagnation, Subaru resolved himself and drew a breath to cut into the conversation—

"—Now then."

The instant he was about to preempt her with the first word, a dry sound of a book snapping shut echoed through the room.

Before him, Priscilla, having closed the book in her palm, shut both eyes and savored, palm still tingling from the impact of closing it, a brief silence as if relishing her after-reading impression. And then,

"A tedious tale, that was."

"You seemed pretty absorbed in it for something tedious..."

"While reading, the proper way to read is to bury oneself in the world of the book. Only after finishing the story does one voice one's impressions. How could I possibly call it tedious without having read it?"

To Subaru's feeble jab, Priscilla responded with unexpected legitimacy as a reader. Apparently there was no falsehood in calling herself a book lover. Having declared she would never criticize a book without reading it, she flung the finished book violently into the air, and—

"—Ah."

Before Subaru's stunned eyes, the moment the thrown book reached the peak of its arc, it was seared by a sudden burst of heat and burst into flame all at once. Consumed from binding to pages by a ferocious blaze, the book was reduced to ash in an instant, and after a single blink, all that remained was black ash fluttering down.

"Now then. You've stolen my precious morning reading time. — You'd best have brought me a tale more engaging than the one I just finished, at the very least."

Smiling with lascivious malice, crossing her slender legs anew, Priscilla pointed a white finger toward him. It felt as if heat were being pressed directly against his forehead from that fingertip, and Subaru felt cold sweat break out across his back as he spoke,

"—The candidate Emilia. The situation surrounding her right now, and — I need your help to break through it."


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"The Witch Cult, is it..."

Supporting her head with the arm propped on the armrest, Priscilla tapped her other hand's fingers against her knee. Having finished recounting almost the same content she'd given Crusch, she murmured that, seemingly deep in thought, eyes closed.

Sensing something — some feeling Priscilla apparently held toward the Witch Cult — Subaru pressed on.

"If we leave them be, they'll do harm to the people. I want to strike them down before that happens. To do that, I need your—"

"Kuku, fu."

"—?"

Before Subaru, pressing his case in an attempt to wring out her agreement, Priscilla's shoulders shook, her face lowered and hidden. What now, Subaru frowned — and before him, she snapped her face back up with explosive force.

"Hahahaha! Interesting! How interesting, you are. My, my — that's stirred my heart far more than the book from before. When a clown goes this far, it becomes a sort of art."

She laughed, and laughed, and mocked Subaru.

His instincts told him this was by no means a friendly sort of smile — it was the kind a savage carnivorous beast wears, the kind a cat shows when it tortures a mouse to death beneath its claws.

"What's so funny about it?"

"That you don't even understand it — that's the absurdity of it. Come now, you. Do you have any idea how utterly incoherent your conduct is right now?"

Threading her fingers into her own orange hair, winding a shoulder-length lock around her index finger, Priscilla asked this with genuine delight. There was something familiar about that manner of speaking, as if she could see straight through him. He'd heard it before, more than once, in Crusch's mansion — spoken to him as though implying he was slow-witted.

Unconsciously, Subaru's gaze sharpened, and Priscilla snorted at it.

"Whether you have no one else to turn to or what, I know not, but what you're doing is nothing more than going around informing every other faction of your own faction's weaknesses. 'This and that and so I don't have enough strength, boo-hoo...' You've got quite the blessed head on your shoulders, don't you."

Tapping her own temple with a finger, Priscilla sneered at Subaru's conduct.

Under that thoroughly condescending attitude, Subaru's thoughts went momentarily blank. He hadn't expected to be denounced this blatantly. And as Subaru fell silent, unable to process his emotions, Priscilla looked down on him and continued.

"Throwing caution to the wind, that's well and good, but you haven't thought it through. Not nearly enough. Trying to help and driving things into a crisis instead. Your conduct is the very picture of an incompetent hard worker. Beyond saving. You'd be better off dead."

Having said all she pleased, Priscilla rose from her chair, bent at the waist to lean toward him, and stared straight into Subaru's widened eyes. "Actually," she prefaced,

"I could simply lop that head clean off your shoulders."

The next instant, a fan drawn from Priscilla's bosom was lightly pressed against Subaru's throat.

A speed so overwhelming he never saw the step, never even registered when her arm had moved. The sensation of the fan's edge, warmed by Priscilla's body heat, touching his right carotid artery. It was by no means a sharp blade, and yet the instant it moved, it gave Subaru the illusion that his head had already fallen.

To Subaru, who couldn't help but hold his breath, Priscilla murmured, sounding bored, "Can't even follow it with your eyes?"

"Foolish, and dull-witted on top of it — truly beyond hope. ...And yet, even having received such harsh treatment, that you still act out of concern for your liege — that loyalty of yours, at least, is admirable."

As she spoke, Priscilla withdrew the fan from Subaru's throat, opened it to cover her own mouth, narrowed her eyes, and continued, "And so—"

"To simply mock your conduct and send you away would, even for me, leave something to be desired. So — I'll give you a chance."

"...A ch-chance?"

"Yes, a chance. A *chansu*, as it were."

Perhaps having picked it up from Al, wielding the modern loanword with uncertain pronunciation, Priscilla extended her folded fan toward him. Advancing straight and quiet, Subaru found himself, for some reason, unable to dodge it — pressed against his forehead, his upper body swayed and he fell backward onto his rear.

And so the two of them found their eye levels reversed. Subaru, now the one looking up, tried to raise his gaze to Priscilla with a mix of shock and protest, and—

"――――"

"Lick it."

Right before his eyes, he saw, up close, Priscilla's bare foot, freed of its shoe.

Not understanding the meaning of the words, Subaru's gaze wandered between her face and the foot before him. At the sight of Subaru looking so lost, Priscilla snorted, and, speaking as though scolding a poorly behaved child, with the malice of someone tormenting a slave,

"Crawl on the floor, savor your shame and humiliation, and, like some pathetic, ridiculous stray dog, lick my foot. — Do that, and I'll consider your proposal."

"Wha—!?"

"If you'd rather not, that's fine too. Prioritize your own petty pride, abandon the master who wags her tail for you — that's fine as well. That amuses me just the same."

Whichever way it falls, I'll enjoy it either way, Priscilla sneered, hiding her mouth behind the fan.

At that attitude of hers, dripping with nothing but malice, Subaru's guts boiled over with rage. But he barely managed to hold himself back from raising his voice and acting purely on emotion.

If he let emotion drive him and charged ahead here, the negotiation would collapse. More than anything, there was no denying he had drawn a concession-shaped posture out of his opponent.

He looked at the foot still held out before him, and at Priscilla's mocking face. When he closed his eyes, the faces of the silver-haired girl, and the pink-haired maid, and then the children and adults of the village rose up one after another, and the boiling magma slowly began to cool.

And then,

"I-I under...stand."

Swallowing his humiliation, Subaru took Priscilla's foot in his hand.

Compared to the agonizing deaths Emilia and the villagers would suffer, what was this humiliation Subaru tasted here now? If it meant averting that future and reaching the world he needed to reach, he'd become a dog, or anything at all.

His trembling lips drew close to the back of her white foot, on the verge of touching that skin that seemed to draw him in—

"Ah, truly, you—you really have sunk to being a tedious man, haven't you."

His nose was crushed flat by a kick straight to the face, and Subaru was sent flying easily through the air.

Spinning, his vision whirling around and around, Subaru had no idea what was happening. His head, rattled by the tremendous impact, couldn't hold a coherent thought, and the floating sensation that followed was cut short an instant later by the feel of something hard slamming into his entire body.

He realized, after a moment of silence, that he was sprawled spread-eagled on the ground. A thick, viscous liquid was dripping heavily from his nostrils.

"That thing of yours is neither loyalty nor devotion. It's something filthier — a dog's dependency, and a pig's desire. A lazy pig that does nothing but want. The pig's desire is the ugliest of all."

An unceasing ringing in his ears and a wave of nausea bounced around chaotically inside his skull. Priscilla's voice reached him from somewhere, but the content wouldn't register in his mind. Only—

"Even if you were to somehow beat back the Witch Cult, a faction that harbors scum like you — once you've all worn yourselves down, I'll crush it myself. Your reckless conduct just now has decided that for me."

A small hand grabbed the collar of his sprawled body and hauled him up roughly. As his upper body was pulled up, more blood streamed from Subaru's nose, and merciless words rained down further upon him as he coughed, struggling for breath.

"—Take pride in it. It is you who is inviting that woman — Emilia — toward ruin."

Shoved away with full force, Subaru's body slid across the floor and reached all the way to the entrance door. Trailing drops of blood marked the path behind where he'd rolled, but Priscilla's expression suggested she found even looking at Subaru himself more distasteful than the bloodstains.

"—Aldebaran!"

At Priscilla's sharp cry, the room's sole door to the outside opened from beyond it. Drawn by her call, the figure who appeared was the one in the jet-black helmet. He glanced around the room, and the moment he saw Subaru sprawled bloody before the door, he raised his single arm to his head.

"Whoa now, what the hell happened here..."

"Throw out that unpleasant piece of trash. If you like, you may cut him down instead, for all I care."

"Nah, I'd care, plenty... c'mon, let's go, Brother."

Without offering a word in his enraged master's defense, Al grabbed Subaru's fallen body, hoisted him effortlessly with one arm, and slung him over his shoulder.

"Don't get so mad, princess. That pretty face of yours loses value looking that vicious, y'know?"

"If you'd rather your own ruined face not be ruined further, take him away at once. I won't say it twice, Aldebaran."

"Don't call me that name."

Muttering that offhandedly, Al carried Subaru out of the room. Quietly closing the door, he broke into a brisk pace down the stairs.

"Anyway, better make yourself scarce sooner rather than later. Knowing the princess, she might change her mind any second and start saying to cut your head off after all. Better run while she's still not itching to cut."

"Ah, aah...?"

"Yeah, no, this ain't good. I'll go call your little lady, and you handle the rest yourself."

Seeing Subaru's mind still hazy and unfocused, Al shrugged his one-armed shoulder with a sigh, and picked up even more speed, all but flying down the remaining stairs.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"—Subaru-kun!"

At the sight of Subaru's ruined state, propped against the gate, Rem's face changed color instantly as she rushed over.

Touching Subaru, limp and motionless, she began checking the extent of his injuries, chanting "Water, O Mana," beginning her healing spell as she did,

"What happened?"

Touching Subaru's face with a faint glow, she directed the question to the one-armed suspicious man standing beside her. At the question, Al scuffed the ground with his toe, looking somewhat sheepish.

"Ah, well, that. Seems he went and pissed off our princess something fierce. Told him to watch out so that wouldn't happen, but... well, expecting a human to perfectly predict a cat's mood is asking a lot."

Though he looked sheepish, there wasn't a trace of guilt in Al's manner of speaking. There was sympathy for the beaten Subaru, but no sense of apology for his mistress's outrage. Noticing this, Rem was momentarily stunned, and then moved to raise her voice in protest, but—

"—Rem, it's fine."

"Subaru-kun. Are you conscious, all right?"

As his concussion began to heal, Subaru's mind starting to clear, he called out to Rem. Her face brightened at that, and she closed both eyes as if to concentrate further.

"Subaru-kun really is someone I can't take my eyes off. Barely an hour apart, and you come back with wounds like this."

"It's not like I'm doing this because I want to..."

As blood circulation began to normalize, blood started flowing from his nose again. Catching the dripping blood with a hastily raised hand, Rem gently pressed a handkerchief she'd taken from her pocket against his face.

"Please hold this in place. Once the wound heals, the bleeding will stop once what's already flowed runs its course."

"—Aye."

Pressing his nose as Rem instructed, Subaru accepted her healing, savoring the sensation of warmth slowly seeping in. Watching this, Al remarked "Looks like you'll be fine," then,

"No point stickin' around, so I'll head back too. Dunno what you two were talkin' about, but from how that went, doesn't seem like it panned out. If I take too long gettin' back, the princess might seriously start sayin' to go cut the Brother down."

"Cut Subaru-kun down—!?"

"Don't make that scary face, little lady! I said *might* say it! So go on somewhere before she does. I don't wanna do that either, y'know."

Waving a hand exaggeratedly to dismiss Rem's overreaction, Al then let the tension drop from his shoulders, and, with a slack posture, stepped back once.

"Well then, take care of yourself, Brother. And you too, little lady... ah, Ram, was it? Nice knowin' ya."

"—Ram is my sister's name. Rem's name is Rem, Al-sama."

With a casual farewell, as Al began to turn his back, Rem gave her name in correction.

At once, Al's feet stopped moving.

He stood still, tilted his face slightly upward, then slowly turned back around,

"Don't talk nonsense. It's Ram, right?"

"It's Rem. ...Forgive me, but, Al-sama, have you met my sister somewhere?"

Rem asked the question, having been mistaken for her identical twin sister, but no answer came from Al in response.

He touched the helmet covering his head with his raised, single arm, murmuring "What is going on..." and then,

"The little lady's Rem... and the older sister's Ram."

"Yes, that's right."

"Kinda a weird thing to ask, but... the little lady's sister — is she alive?"

"...? I don't understand the intent of your question, but of course my sister is alive."

The instant Rem answered that, Subaru — who had been listening to the exchange in silence — felt his skin prickle with gooseflesh.

At that unexplainable reaction rising on his skin, Subaru couldn't help but shudder, unable to hide his shock.

And then,

"—No damn way."

Low, cold, the words struck his eardrums with a heavy weight.

The murmur seemed wrung out from deep in Al's throat, his head bowed, hand still touching his forehead through the helmet.

At the same time, Subaru realized that the source of the chill that had assaulted his skin was some unknowable, murderous aura being emitted by Al. That presence, ringing an instinctive alarm that he must not be here — it seemed Rem felt the same thing, because,

"Subaru-kun, can you stand if I lend you my shoulder?"

Stopping her treatment, still bent at the waist, Rem asked this with open wariness. At her words, Subaru nodded, pulling his chin in, and waited for Rem to move. He needed her to get him out of here, timed with her own escape.

But the wariness of the two of them turned out to be needless worry when,

"Relax, I ain't gonna do anything."

Shaking his head, Al reined in the murderous air he'd been emitting.

At once, the chill that had gripped them vanished, and Subaru realized he'd forgotten even to breathe. Rem, too, softened her blank expression slightly in relief.

And then, the one responsible for both their reactions, Al, said,

"Sorry for killin' the mood, but get going, quick. I think you get it from just now, but seems like I ain't in that great a mood myself either."

"—Understood. Please pass along our thanks for your time."

"Yeah, got it. Take care."

Exchanging somewhat chilly pleasantries, Rem lent Subaru her shoulder and began walking.

Leaning his weight on her small frame, Subaru moved away from the Barielle residence. Descending the slope, putting distance between them and the mansion's gate, Subaru glanced back over his shoulder for just an instant.

—Al was still staring intently at the two of them, at Subaru's back.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


"Don't screw with me. That's really — is that really it... makes me sick."

The muffled murmur echoed only within the pitch-black helmet.
