Arc 2 Chapter 49: "A Story About the Future"

—Once again, Subaru's consciousness was summoned into a world where black haze drifted.

A world governed by nothing but pitch-black "nothingness."
Only consciousness floated in the void, and Subaru dimly became aware of his own existence.

No one was there. Nothing existed. Nothing happened.
No beginning. No end. A world where only inactivity existed.

Surrendering his body entirely to a vague, boundless sensation, like being cast adrift on a nighttime sea, Subaru's consciousness abandoned even thought and sank into the waves of that inactivity.

Then, suddenly, a change occurred in that world of darkness.

Directly ahead, someone stood before Subaru's presence—consciousness alone as it was.
From what seemed to be the ground, a shadow stretched upward, taking human form and letting its presence rise into view.

The face was invisible. The figure was hazy. Only vaguely could he tell it was, perhaps, the shadow of a woman.

The shadow wavered, then slowly reached a hand toward him.
Close enough that, if he stretched out a finger, he could touch it. And then Subaru's presence—until then nothing but consciousness—realized it now possessed a finger to answer that outstretched one.

Into a world that should have held nothing, a world where only consciousness had drifted, a body reflecting that consciousness had come into being. Though what had come into being here was only a right arm, and a half-formed left hand.

His awareness faltered, watching the finger reaching toward him.
The finger approached with a gesture gentle as if cherishing him, and for some reason he felt an inexplicable urge to burst into tears.

A strange, sentimental feeling, as though he had always been waiting for this to happen.
The shadow stirred further, and, on impulse, he tried to lay his own finger over that hand in turn—and stopped. No—he was stopped.

A white hand, reaching from behind, had enfolded the hand Subaru had tried to extend.
Soft, and warm almost to the point of heat.

He wanted to turn and see the owner of that palm. But unlike his arm, his field of vision would not obey his will, and no matter what, he could not turn around.

His movement was restrained as though forbidden, and gradually the grip of the palm touching him grew stronger, drawing Subaru's consciousness backward.

Which meant, in other words, separation from the shadow before him.

The outstretched finger moved as if pleading, as if clinging, in an agonized way that beckoned Subaru.
Against the hand held fast, he tried to reach with his free left hand to touch that finger, but his left hand, existing only halfway, uselessly clawed at the darkness and never reached.

His heart trembled. It screamed with raw feeling. —But there was no mouth to give that feeling sound.

The shadow grew distant, distant, and began to vanish.
Toward Subaru, who looked near tears, it stretched out its finger one last time, and—

"—...ove you."

The final words, which he could not make out, grew hazier still, and the world disappeared.

The first thing that entered Subaru's field of vision upon waking was the familiar, luxurious ceiling.

In a manor so lavishly given over to aristocratic taste, even the servants' private rooms fell within the territory of excessive decoration. And when it came to a guest room meant to entertain visitors, given what it had to convey about the master's authority and all the rest, an excess of ornamentation was practically unavoidable.

Whatever the truth of that fact, the discomfort it caused a commoner like Subaru was plain.
Using the shorter-than-average span of thought it took him to fully wake, Subaru mused vaguely on such things.
And then—

"—You're awake, then?"

Exactly five seconds after opening his eyes, once his consciousness had fully surfaced, a voice spoke to him.
The voice came from right beside the bed, at close range. Subaru tilted his head against the abnormally soft pillow to look that way, and—

"...Guten morgen, Rem."

"Gu...? A greeting from Subaru-kun's homeland?"

"Nah, by this world's standards it's more like some regional dialect. Other than that, all I know is 'danke.' 'Bono' was wrong, I think."

For a moment he grew anxious wondering whether this world, too, had different languages depending on the country. As things stood, he hadn't verified exactly how his linguistic comprehension here worked, so that was a matter that probably needed handling with some care.
In any case—

"Waking up with a maid right beside me—in a way, that's a boy's dearest wish, isn't it."

"...Considering Rem's failure this time, doing something like this hardly counts as atonement."

"Ah, that's heading somewhere negative, so let's not. Mo·re·o·ver—"

Sitting up, shaking his head with each syllable, Subaru lifted his right hand from beneath the covers. That hand was firmly entwined with the blue-haired girl's, and—

"Did I do this? Grabbed on and wouldn't let go, or something... if so, that's pretty embarrassing. Like a kid who won't give up his favorite towel."

"No, um, this is, well..."

At Subaru's question, Rem fidgeted, moving her still-clasped hand, then, cheeks faintly coloring, bowed her head slightly and said—

"It was Rem's doing."

"How come? I'll have you know, I sweat like crazy in my sleep. Especially around the hands and hips. Summer heat rash is a serious matter, seriously."

"Subaru-kun was..."

"Hm?"

Glancing repeatedly at their joined hands, Rem mumbled, trailing off. Subaru, feeling calm, waited for her answer without pressing.
Facing him like that, Rem took a few breaths, then looked up at him and said,

"Subaru-kun, while sleeping, looked like he was in pain... so, Rem held your hand."

"You held it for me?"

"Rem is ignorant, untalented, and full of shortcomings. So Rem doesn't know what she should do for someone at a time like this. Because she didn't know, Rem wanted to do the thing that made her happiest, when it was done to her."

Whether that led to an embarrassing memory or not, her halting words came out clumsily.
Even so, at Rem revealing her feelings, Subaru looked down at his still-held hand and let his lips curve faintly.

Subaru, who had been like a child, frightened as though by a bad dream—faced with him, this was the gentle method she'd chosen.
She, too, must have once had someone hold her hand like this on a night when she felt like crying. That she had done this for Subaru made him feel ticklish inside, and glad.

"That aside, you're really putting yourself down again, aren't you, Rem-rin. That's not good. It's easy to fall into a negative spiral, but hard to climb out."

"That... might be true. In Rem's case, she's lost sight of the exit entirely."

A weak smile. Something about it didn't sit right with him.
Feeling something simmering inside, Subaru nonetheless said,

"...Anyway, not an epilogue exactly, but I'd like to hear how things turned out."

"Yes. How much do you remember, Subaru-kun?"

"Up to Rozzy raining down fire, going full throttle and descending like a god, and then getting knocked out by an excited Rem-rin's bear hug, I think."

"...Then it's what happened after that."

Haltingly, Rem recounted the aftermath in a businesslike manner.

After Subaru had lost consciousness, Roswaal had carried out the extermination of the Ulgarm in the forest. Even unconsciously, Subaru's Witch's Lingering Scent apparently functioned as live bait potent enough to strip away any option of hiding for the Witchbeasts lurking in the woods.
The pack, which had been concealed in the darkness of Shamak, was wiped out with fire, and the scattered remnants throughout the forest were incinerated along with them.

"So, about my curse, then—"

"With the caster's death, there's no need to worry about it activating. Roswaal-sama, Beatrice-sama, and the Great Spirit-sama have all confirmed that the curse's effect has already been lost."

"Well, if it's been through a third opinion, guess I'm in the clear..."

Unable to recall specifically where the curse had been placed, Subaru rubbed at his chest and let out a sigh of relief.
At any rate, the time bomb that had been rigged directly into his body had been defused. Just how many times he'd nearly died because of it—he didn't even want to think about it, it made his chest tighten.

"Roswaal-sama personally settled the unrest in the village. The children's recovery is going well, and the village has mostly returned to normal by now."

"I see, I see. But I bet, right? Their beloved Subaru-nii-chan came back all bloody and beat to hell, so the brats must've been pretty sad. Some of them probably cried, huh? Ahh, well, can't be helped."

"—Yes, that's right."

As she spoke, Rem slowly, meaningfully, pulled back Subaru's blanket. Reflexively he tried to curl up, muttering "no fair," but the gesture cut off immediately.
The cause lay in the clothes hidden under the blanket, Subaru's clothes. It was the work-clothes-like outfit he'd been dressed in that first day at Roswaal Manor, when he'd been carried in wounded. But there was something odd about the lower half of that outfit. It was—

"Covered in scribbles... like a cast on someone with a broken bone!"

"Through Roswaal-sama's kindness, the children invited to the manor left writing on it."

"Ugh, those brats..."

Suppressing the urge to click his tongue, Subaru looked over the numerous scrawled messages. From his angle they were upside down, and the handwriting was messy to begin with, making them extremely hard to read.
But since they were all in I-script, which even Subaru could read, he was able to make out all of them, given enough time.

"Ugh, those brats."

Same words as before. But the feeling behind them was subtly different now.
With the bed pillow as a backrest, Subaru turned his gaze toward the window, wishing the moment he could go to the village would come as soon as possible.

'Thank you for bringing Rem-rin back' 'Thank you veeery much' 'Not cool but so cool' 'Promise we'll do radio calisthenics together' 'Love you'

"Hah, idiots. Obviously I'm the one who loves you guys more."

Saying "love you" so easily, throwing the word around so lightly, Subaru thought.
He didn't notice that it was a boomerang aimed straight back at himself.

Subaru cursed under his breath, the sort of curse likely to draw a warm, indulgent look. Rem, at this, suddenly straightened her expression, her lips trembling.

"Regarding your recovery, this time there's something to discuss about Subaru-kun's body."

"Ah, right, yeah. Curse aside, my body was pretty much an emergency situation on other fronts too."

Saying this, he belatedly noticed that the shoulder of the arm Rem was holding had been set back into place.
Testing it with a bit of force, there was no strangeness, no pain. Treatment must have been performed—truly, this world's healing magic was quite the miracle worker.
That was how Subaru was thinking about it, but—

"I'm sorry, Subaru-kun."

Before Subaru's optimistic judgment, Rem bent at the waist and bowed her head.
Unable to think of a reason for the apology, Subaru waved a hand at her, "hey, hey," and said,

"Lift your head, Rem-rin. Nothing's wrong with my condition, is it? My dislocated shoulder's back in place, nothing else hurts anywhere. I'm all better, all better."

"That's not true. It's certainly the case that treatment on the visible wounds is finished, and thankfully there's no worry of lasting after-effects that would interfere with daily life. But—"

She cut herself off, a shadow of anguish falling across her face.

"Scars will remain. On the body, of course, but on the heart as well. And, because treatment had to be repeated so many times, the mana inside Subaru-kun's body is nearly depleted."

"Ah, that explains it. I did feel kinda sluggish. ...But that's no big deal either. A man's wounds are medals, except on the back, and as for wounds on the heart, I'm a pretty tough guy, y'know?"

Pointing at himself with his thumb, Subaru smiled, trying to dispel her guilt.
It wasn't a lie in the slightest. If Subaru's heart were a weak, straightforwardly fragile thing, he could never have greeted a morning like this, hand in hand with her.
If it came down to talking about wounds on the heart, Subaru had been through experiences that would have made it perfectly reasonable if he could no longer even look Rem in the face.

Having thought that far, Subaru stared intently at Rem.
Blue, short hair. Features that were well-formed, more cute than beautiful. Her expression, which he'd initially thought showed little emotional change, now shifted constantly, spinning through moods. Not frightening. Not frightening at all. There was the her who had made Subaru loop again and again, and there was also this her, wholeheartedly delighted at Subaru's survival. It all came down to how the dice fell, he felt.

There was the Rem who went berserk out of concern for Roswaal, and there was also the Rem who leapt to hasty conclusions out of concern for Subaru, and there was, too, the fact that once she decided there was no risk of friendly fire, she'd charge headlong into a reason-abandoning berserker mode—

"You look calm, but you're actually not composed at all, are you, Rem-rin."

Judging by her usual demeanor around the manor, and her work as the senior maid instructing Subaru, Rem seemed like an extremely capable person with cool, collected judgment.
But once a situation stepped outside that domain, she seemed to have a tendency to suddenly go careening off in some wildly mistaken direction.
Subaru was hardly one to talk about jumping to conclusions himself, but in her case, it was made troublesome by the fact that it came paired with decisive action, and the actual ability to back that action up.

At Subaru's remark, Rem froze for just a moment, then bowed her head weakly.
Still with her face lowered, she said,

"Rem... does understand that."

She murmured, then continued, letting the thoughts in her chest spill out little by little.

"Rem is powerless, untalented, a failure of the Oni Tribe. So no matter what, however hard she runs, she often can't reach far enough. So, for Rem to catch up to her sister, she couldn't think of any way other than to start running earlier."

Covering her face with her free hand, Rem continued, wringing out something like a confession.

"Sister could have done it better. Sister wouldn't stumble at something like this. Sister surely wouldn't hesitate. Sister would surely manage it easily. Sister would surely never make a mistake. Sister, sister, sister—"

She cut herself off, and looked at Subaru with a weak light in her eyes.
What welled in her eyes was not tears. It was only emptiness, despair filled with resignation.

"Rem is Sister's substitute. And an inferior one at that, forever and ever—someone who can never catch up to the real Sister no matter how long, a failed copy."

—Suddenly, a faint droplet welled up in her eyes.

"Why did the horn end up remaining on Rem's side? Why didn't Sister's horn remain instead? Why was Sister born with only one horn from the start? Why—were Sister and Rem twins at all?"

As though seeking the meaning of her own existence, Rem's lips trembled.
The droplets gathering in her eyes traced down her cheeks, leaving a sorrowful glimmer on her pale skin.

Subaru fell silent. Unable to bear that silence, Rem hurriedly wiped away her tears, her expression crumbling into a clumsy smile, and said,

"I—I'm sorry. I said something strange. Please forget it. This is the first time I've ever told anyone this, so it came out odd..."

"Hey, Rem."

Rem tried, in a rush of words, to erase what she'd just said.
Cutting off her words, Subaru called her name. Breaking the silence—what words would Subaru form? Rem looked up, though she seemed to fear the answer.
To her, Subaru said,

"So what's the punchline?"

"—Huh?"

"C'mon, the punchline. The punchline. The part that sends the whole studio audience into hysterics. There's gotta be one, after all that."

Not understanding what Subaru was even getting at, Rem blinked in confusion, and said,

"What in the world are you..."

"I'm saying that was a story with absolutely nothing funny in it. —Rem, back to what we were talking about before. Remember?"

At his question, Rem's eyes clouded. Seeing her reaction, Subaru laughed, "well, can't be helped," and lifted his left hand, holding up one finger in front of her.

"Alright, let's start with a review. First idiot point: saying you couldn't save me. Can you see me right in front of you, perfectly fine? My legs are still attached and everything."

He waggled his legs for effect. Rem seemed to realize what topic Subaru had brought up, but still shook her head weakly, and said,

"That's just... a result. In hindsight."

"They say all's well that ends well, back where I'm from. If we're grading based on the process, honestly, I'm way worse off than you. That aside, second idiot point: trying to take everything on and handle it all alone."

Winking, Subaru held up a second finger, and said,

"Well, going berserk for my sake—I'm happy about that, sure, happy in a way. But there's a time and place for everything. More than anything, if you'd talked it over, a good method probably would've come up, right?"

As for the Witchbeast hunt, it was self-evident that Subaru had a point. Rem, as if ashamed of her own rashness, let her lips tremble, swallowing back any argument.
Though, admittedly, this was exactly the kind of after-the-fact theorizing that only worked because he already knew the outcome—Rem, too overwhelmed to notice, didn't catch it, and Subaru secretly stuck out his tongue, apologizing for the meanness.

Then Subaru leaned his own face down beneath hers, still lowered.
Laughing at her startled flinch, he held up a third finger on his left hand, and said,

"And now, the third idiot point I couldn't say before. Actually, the third one slipped out back then, so this is technically the fourth idiot point. Idiots all around."

"That's only fair, considering what Rem did."

"Yep, that's it. That's the fourth idiot point."

Pointing a finger at Rem, still unable to escape the cycle of self-deprecation, Subaru made his indication. Then, waving his four raised fingers about, he said,

"Constantly dwelling on the past and only looking backward—that's the one."

"————"

"Rem, y'know, you go on and on about how if it was Sister, if it was Sister, hyping Ram up like crazy while trashing yourself... but I really don't think things would've gone better with Ram-chi in your position? Less stamina than you, worse cook, skips out on work, and mouthy... maybe a touch more level-headed? That's about it."

Recalling Ram's specs, Subaru considered how far they diverged from the ideal image Rem described. A sister inferior to her younger sister in every regard. As they themselves were aware, Ram was inferior to Rem in every single ability. To the point that there was truly no need for Rem to belittle herself.

At Subaru's assessment, however, Rem shook her head as though rejecting it, and said,

"No, that's wrong. Sister—the real Sister is very different. If she had the horn, if Sister had the horn, this wouldn't..."

"But Ram doesn't have that 'if she had' horn. So I don't know anything about this high-spec Ram with a horn you're talking about."

Cutting off Rem as she tried to deny herself with that same predetermined answer, Subaru touched his chin with his left hand, and said,

"The Ram I know is exactly as I just described. Worse than you at cooking, sewing, cleaning, manners, and mouth. —Well, I think that's part of what's good about her, though."

Her attitude being far too haughty, occasionally clashing with her wasn't so bad either.
That kind of distance in their relationship was, to Subaru, comfortable. And turning his attention to the matter of the horn, or its absence—

"I bet you're probably the only one who cares about that, y'know?"

He recalled the brief conversation with Ram in the forest.
Attachment to being of the Oni Tribe didn't seem to linger much in Ram at all. If anything, Ram seemed to wish she could somehow do something about Rem's attachment to it.

Subaru didn't presume to think he could do something about it himself.
After all, he was, at best, a young man with painfully little life experience, a hikikomori even thinner in density than his peers.
How could half-baked preaching from someone like that possibly move anyone's heart?

He wasn't going to strain for it. He wasn't even going to try to make it resonate.
Things a person needed to reconcile within themselves ultimately had to be handled by themselves, not by clinging to someone else.
So what Subaru told Rem was, simply, an extremely straightforward imposition of his own feelings.

"If you weren't around, I'd probably be dead by now, mauled by dogs. I survived because you were there. I'm alive right now, like this. Not just thanks to Sister—thanks to you."

"...The real... Sister would have done it better."

"Maybe so. —But it was you who was there."

Speaking over her weak counterargument, Subaru laid his left hand over the right hand still held.
At Rem, who looked up with a start, Subaru smiled wryly, and said,

"I'm glad you were there, Rem. Thank you."

"—Ah!"

At the words Subaru spoke, Rem let out a strangled sound from her throat.
Then she turned her face away, hiding her expression from him, and said,

"Rem is... Rem has always been... Sister's substitute, always—"

"Cut that lonely self-definition out. To begin with, you and Ram aren't even the same genre. I mean, big-sister type versus little-sister type—depending on who you ask, that could start a war."

Preferences that could never be reconciled. Each type had its own good points—while Subaru thought that quietly to himself, Rem squeezed her eyes shut.

"Well, anyway. From now on, make better use of being twins. If sharp-minded Ram's body can't keep up, then have Rem take charge of the part it can't keep up with. Lovely sisterly love, covering each other's weaknesses, and that makes you unbeatable."

"So Rem's rashness really is a shortcoming, isn't it..."

"We were having a good flow, don't go and pick that part up. Oh, right. I just thought of something."

Clapping his hands, Subaru's face lit up as if he'd hit on a brilliant idea.
Then, leaning in toward Rem, who was still turned away, he said,

"Earlier, Rem-rin, you asked, right? Something like why Ram doesn't have a horn and you do."

"...Yes."

"Well, I haven't heard the details on why she lost hers, and since I'm not asking, I don't know. Since I don't know, let me talk like I do anyway, okay—"

Subaru tapped the top of his own forehead with his left hand—just about where Rem's horn used to grow—and said,

"You should be the one standing in for the horn Ram doesn't have. The two of you, together, being an 'oni' as a team. Has a heartfelt ring to it, doesn't it."

"—Ah..."

"And besides, you keep saying 'substitute,' but there's no substitute for Rem, as far as Ram's concerned, either, y'know? Can you imagine what state Ram would be in if Rem disappeared?"

Rem, at a loss for words, didn't know it, but Subaru knew that future.
He knew the sight of her screaming at her sister's death, spilling grief bitter enough to feel like the end of the world, driven half-mad, ready to avenge her with every ounce of strength she had.

"...But..."

Even so, Rem still wouldn't readily nod along to Subaru's words.
Convincing people was, in any case, difficult. In her case, she'd likely been carrying this particular feeling around, unable to manage it, for years—the knot in her heart had a stubborn, deep-rooted hardness to it.
At her obstinacy, Subaru scratched his head, "ah, geez," and said,

"Fine, got it. Then let's do this. Rem compares herself to some ideal Ram inside her head, and no matter how she struggles, she can't get anywhere with it, right? Then let's just erase that ideal-Ram idol you keep comparing yourself to."

"That's not something Rem can just do so easily. Rem has always, with Sister..."

"Then if you want an evaluation, ask me. I'll give you one grounded a lot more in reality than the Ram inside your head. Fair warning, I've got no ability to read the room, so I'll be blunt. No flattery, no mercy, none of that. Deal with it."

Smiling, Subaru rumpled Rem's blue hair with his left hand.
As she narrowed her eyes, ticklish, Subaru let out a small breath, and said,

"Back where I'm from, they say 'if you talk about next year, the oni laughs.' So, y'know—"

To Rem, who couldn't say anything, just letting it happen, Subaru tilted his head and continued,

"Laugh, Rem. Don't make that gloomy face, laugh. While we're laughing, let's talk about the future. All that time you spent looking backward, that was wasted time—let's spend from now on looking forward instead, and talk. Starting with tomorrow, for instance."

"...Tomorrow?"

"Yeah, tomorrow. Anything works, y'know? Like whether tomorrow's breakfast should be Japanese-style or Western-style, or whether you put your right sock on first or your left—something dumb like that's fine too. However pointless the topic, it's talk about tomorrow, and that's only possible because there is a tomorrow."

Spreading his hands, Subaru wrapped up his little speech that way, then shrugged, "so? What do you say," asking Rem for her answer.
She hesitated a moment in responding, then, brows drooping in a troubled way, said,

"Rem is very weak. So surely, she'll end up leaning on you."

"That's fine, isn't it? I'm weak too, and dumb, and I've got shifty eyes, and I can't read the room, and saying all that out loud is getting me down a bit myself, but on that front I've been living off relying on the people around me to pick up my slack, so. Let's just lean on each other and keep moving forward."

Trying to shoulder everything on your own meant your eyes stayed fixed on the weight of the burden, and you lost sight of the road ahead.
Someone like Subaru, walking along meaning to keep both hands empty—that was the easy way to do it. Even so, baggage piled up before you knew it—but if carrying it alone blocked your view forward, then you could just share it with someone else and keep walking. Something like that—how about it?

"Let's put our arms around each other's shoulders, laughing, and talk about tomorrow, about the future. Me, laughing with an oni while talking about next year—that was a dream of mine."

"...That's rather oni-like, isn't it."

"Right?"

Closing one eye and twisting the corner of his mouth, Subaru got Rem to laugh a little too, as if pulled along by it.
As she began to laugh, tears suddenly spilled from the corners of her laughing eyes. Falling in streams, tears that showed no sign of stopping poured out and flowed on and on, and still Rem kept laughing.

Laughing through tears, laughing through tears, Rem pressed her face into the blanket as if to muffle her sobs and laughter, and still her tearful laughter fell quietly into the room.

The whole while, Subaru kept gently stroking her hair.
His right hand remained, all the while, firmly held.

Endlessly gentle, gentle, he kept stroking it.

He looked back over the days at Roswaal Manor.
The first loop began, and he died on the night of the fourth day—cause of death, starvation.
He plunged into the second loop, and died on the night of the fourth day—cause of death, beaten to death by Rem.
In the third loop, despair, and he died on the evening of the third day—cause of death, brutally murdered by Ram.
In the fourth loop, he rallied, and died on the evening of the fifth day—cause of death, a fall from throwing himself down.

And then the fifth loop. Bitten all over by dogs, torn all over by claws, losing blood and something like willpower in great quantities, leaving wounds all over his body that made him want to look away—but he thought he'd finally managed to pick up everything.
His relationship with Rem and Ram, Subaru's standing at the manor. He'd been able to save the village's children, and the Witchbeasts in the forest had been wiped out, leaving no more danger. All told, some twenty-odd days of adventure, and it had ended in a total, resounding win.
Yes, it should have ended in a resounding win.

"—It's not that I'm angry, or anything. Yes, that's right, I'm not angry. It's just that the person I nursed so devotedly woke up and wasn't there, and when I thought about going to look for him, I found myself tied hand and foot to a chair, completely left behind—but I'm not the kind of narrow-minded person who'd get angry over something like that. Me."

Seated in the chair beside the bed, twirling a lock of her silver hair around a finger, was the girl whose foul mood was busy backing Subaru into a corner.

Sweating profusely, cold sweat streaming down, Subaru silently listened to her litany of grievances.
Ten minutes had already passed since she'd come into the room, and most of that time had been taken up by this stream of resentment wrapped in the guise of a lecture.
Though, admittedly, when she'd first come into the room, she had spent plenty of time worrying over Subaru's condition, checking that nothing was wrong with his body, letting out a sigh of relief—and only then, seeming to steel herself, sat down in the chair and shifted gears to start this. It showed, in its way, just how earnest she was.

Anyway,

"I'm not, angry, at all, me."

"Yes, Emilia-tan's anger is entirely justified. Yes, I'm sorry."

"So I said I'm not angry, honestly. But if Subaru thinks he was in the wrong, well, I suppose I can't help but accept that apology. —Please, really, don't make me worry like that again."

Swept up in Emilia's momentum, Subaru apologized. At this, Emilia smiled as though satisfied, then, at the last moment, laced that smile with a touch of tender affection, and added that.
Utterly unfair. Said with that face, how was he supposed to brush it aside?

Rem, having cried herself out, had left the room (with a strong warning that the crying was absolutely a secret, so he was currently considering how to spread the word without her finding out), and Emilia had come to visit the drowsy Subaru afterward.
Her behavior since arriving had been as described above. However, once all that had run its course, what shone in her eyes was purely a tender light of concern for Subaru's well-being, and that, somehow, was terribly ticklish.

"Still, Subaru, you really are a boy who's never without an injury. Even coming to this manor in the first place was because of an injury... and it's only been four days since then."

"It's not like I enjoy getting hurt, y'know? It's just that, honestly, the world seems built to be a little rough on me specifically... so at the very least, maybe Emilia-tan could spoil me rotten, just to balance things out!"

"You ran away when I tried to be nice to you. Never mind, then."

"Nuahh! I threw away my own chance! Damn it, Bea-ko, you could've handled that better! What's with the 'tied hand and foot,' huh!"

Shouting the name of the ringlet-haired girl he hadn't yet seen since waking, Subaru vented his frustration at this unreasonable turn of events.
At the phrase "tied hand and foot," Emilia's eyes grew distant, and she said,

"You fell asleep sitting in the chair, and when you woke up you were fixed to the backrest. I was completely astonished."

"'Completely astonished,' don't hear that phrase much these days."

"Don't joke around. ...Puck kept trying to keep me from chasing after you two, and if Roswaal hadn't come back, who knows what would have happened. Do you understand that?"

Emilia, lips pouting, looked a little angry.
Subaru could only respond with utter contrition to what she said.

Puck, true to his own intentions, had maneuvered to keep Emilia out of danger, and Beatrice, apparently giving up early on persuading her by talk, had shifted to forcibly restraining her actions instead.
Blocked by the two of them together, unable to do anything, her state of mind must have been fairly wretched, he imagined.

If it had been him left behind, he couldn't help but think the same.
Though, he could say with confidence that even if the same thing happened again, he still wouldn't take her along.

"But, you saved me again, huh."

"Huh?"

"I said, you saved me again. I invited you to the manor to thank you for saving my life, and here you go and do it again. Thaaank you, so much."

Clasping her hands together, Emilia smiled, her expression softening as if she couldn't help it.
At that, Subaru finally managed an "ah" in response, and said,

"Nah, it's fine, really. It's not like I did it for anyone but myself, and it's not like it has nothing to do with me. Yeah. I did it, huh."

Only as he spoke did the reality of it finally sink in.
Something that had sat hardened in his chest seemed to drop away, all at once.

Over twenty-odd days of repetition, looking back—Subaru had seen its endpoint.
After having his spirit scraped away, broken, crushed so many times, the thing he'd still gone on wanting had finally, at last, come within reach of his hand.
I did it, he could finally feel for himself.

"I figured you'd say something like that, but that doesn't sit right with me. Roswaal, and Ram and Rem too, I'm sure they'll all want to thank you."

"Is that right... well then, I'll take you up on that. Get Rozzy to sweeten my employment terms even more, and Ram and Rem can be my personal maids for a while, heh heh. And then—!"

Putting a hand to his mouth and grinning lecherously, Subaru swayed his body side to side, leaning toward Emilia, who pulled back slightly, and pointed a finger at her, and said,

"Does that mean Emilia-tan's giving me a reward too?"

"...You're so mercenary. I'll have you know, only things I can actually do. ...Come to think of it, last time you asked for my name when I said that."

"Heh, don't underestimate my greed. This time I'm having none of that restraint. Avarice, gluttony, a swirling libido driving me on!"

Unable to stand up from the bed, Subaru still struck a fierce pose, both arms raised at diagonal angles.
Seeing Subaru in such a spirited state, Emilia seemed unable to believe an anticlimactic request was coming, and straightened up, turning to face him properly.

At Emilia's attitude, as if to say "let's hear it," Subaru nodded, then quickly ran through the 'Emilia reward list' he wanted, searching his mind.
Amid a range of options spanning a little adventure or dramatically leaping up flights of stairs, Subaru mulled it over carefully and selected one.
And then—

"Then let's go on a date, Emilia-tan."

—and so he made, once again with her, the promise they'd exchanged twenty days before.

"A date?"

"The two of us going out somewhere together, seeing the same things, eating the same things, doing the same things, sharing the same memories."

"...That's all you want?"

"That's exactly what I want."

That was where the first step had begun.
How much trouble had Subaru gone through, just to get to have that long-cherished date with her.
Along the way, all sorts of feelings had piled up, and the bar Subaru sought as he looped kept climbing higher and higher, but the hurdle he'd wished to clear at the very end had always been right there.
So, as a close to these days of repetition, that promise was fitting.

"I wanna show you off to the village brats, and there's this amazing flower field and stuff. Even just wandering around aimlessly, it's special to me."

"I think the meaning of 'greed' might be different in Subaru's head than usual."

"Just you wait. Before long my shamelessness'll freeze even that smile of yours. Yes!"

Crossing both arms in some mysterious pose.
Watching Subaru, unable to hide his welling emotion, Emilia let a limp, relaxed smile spread across her face, and said,

"Alll right, understood. I'll go on a date with Subaru."

"Yessss! That's the spirit! E·M·B! (Emilia-tan Is Majorly Buddha-like!)"

The promise made, Subaru pumped his fist and proclaimed it loudly.
Hearing Emilia's small sigh, Subaru, buoyed with anticipation for his body's recovery, looked out the window.

Toward the village, the promised destination of their date.
Letting his thoughts wander there, Subaru suddenly thought of the Witchbeast forest.

The curse dwelling in Subaru's body had, apparently, entirely lost its power.
Which meant, in other words, the extermination of the Ulgarm that had been in that forest. The whole affair had begun with a single one slipping through a gap in the broken barrier and making its way in. In the end, it had all been settled in the form of the annihilation of one side.

There was a strange bitterness to it, one he couldn't quite make sense of.
He simply wasn't in a state of mind to unreservedly celebrate the elimination of some pest.

Suddenly, he remembered.
In the forest, lost in a desperate frenzy, the sensation of driving his sword into that Witchbeast's body.

The sensation of steel striking into flesh was distinctive, and it remained fresh in his palm, in his memory.
The sensation of taking a life.

That sensation, too, he thought, he would probably forget eventually.
As time passed, surely, this indescribable thing rising in his chest right now would fade away.
A sensation he hadn't known before coming to this other world.
Until the day came when he forgot it—what could he do...

"Subaru."

"Hm?"

Called to, he turned back.
What had she made of that distant, unfocused look in Subaru's eyes?

Emilia brushed back her silver hair, walked to the window, and drew the curtains open.
The sunlight, which had been blocked, spread all at once through the room, and her silver hair was swallowed in a riot of dancing light, gently drawing Subaru's eyes into the scene.
Then, to the silent Subaru, Emilia smiled softly, and said,

"On the day of our date, let's bring flowers along."

"—Yeah."

At that smile, unable to compete with it, Subaru covered his face with his palm.

Until the day comes when he forgets, he thought, let him not forget, let him carve it into his chest.
It was self-serving, a sentiment pushed on himself, but he thought it wasn't wrong.

Because Emilia's smile felt, somehow, like it had told him that was right.


Emilia and Subaru, the two of them, laughing together as time passed.
—The morning sun of the fifth day, finally arrived, went on gently shining down upon them both.
