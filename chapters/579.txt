Arc 7 Chapter 74: "Idra Missanga"

Footsteps — footsteps drawing closer, soft and relentless — made Subaru's heart quail.

He had to stop the massacre unleashed by Todd and Arakiya, the two who'd come from the capital.
So he'd struggled, desperately, with everything he had, trying to beat back the malice and unreason raining down on him. He struggled and struggled and kept struggling, and still, the worst had happened.

The restart point, anchored to "death," kept getting pushed back.
What Subaru feared most in that moment wasn't "death" itself. It was the reality that would be sealed the instant he met death and passed through it.

—What he feared most was a life spilling out of his hands, a life he could no longer save.

That, and the fact that someone's inescapable "death" kept drawing closer, soft and relentless. That, above all.

"AAAAAAAAAH—!!"

The sight before him — Weitz collapsed, drenched in blood — tore a scream from Subaru's throat.
He screamed, and lunged forward, but fingers caught his sleeve as it stretched taut, sealing his movement. It was Tanza who'd grabbed him; her round eyes brimming with tears, she shook her head at Subaru, desperate.
This too, he'd seen. Already seen it. Seen it, and it had already become something known, something familiar.

The same sight, the one he'd witnessed barely a minute ago, unfolding again before his eyes.

"——!!"

An instant later, an ugly, ear-splitting shriek crushed the corridor along with Weitz's fallen body, punching through the floor and dropping toward the island's midlevel.
The gray toad's massive bulk and the falling debris swallowed Weitz whole, vanishing from sight.

"No, no, no, no—!!"
"Schwartz-sama!?"

Subaru flailed his arm wildly, tearing free of Tanza's grip. Leaving the startled girl's voice behind him, he ran toward the falling Weitz.
If he could just get there now, scoop up the falling man, seal the wounds to his eye and left arm, wrap them in bandages back at the infirmary, treat everything — surely, surely, surely—

"Not yet—"

"Sturdier than I expected."

He ran without a shred of composure, convinced he could still save Weitz.
Something swung horizontally met Subaru head-on. He realized it was an axe blade, crusted black-red, only after his vision spun in a heavy, hard-edged blur.
Spinning, spinning, and thrown high up, Subaru's vision caught sight of a child's body running clumsily below. Caught sight of it pitching forward, and understood — that headless body was his own.

Then where was his head? No, more than that — Weitz, Weitz, Weitz, Weitz—


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　× × ×


His swirling vision snapped abruptly into focus, and his brain reeled from the jolt.
It felt like a camera spun into a frenzy and slammed to a halt — his inner ear, which hadn't actually moved at all, felt wrecked all the same.
Nausea surged, confusion surged, and beneath it all, the frantic thought that he had to save Weitz, that this was no time to stand still—

"First time..."

And there, restored to Subaru's returned vision, was Weitz, murmuring through the blood.
Blood poured in horrific quantity from his left arm and right eye — Weitz, who would collapse a second later.

"Someone told me... they believed in me..."

"AAAAAAAAAH—!!"

An irreversible reality trampled Natsuki Subaru's soul into ruin.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


The footsteps of ruin crept closer, soft and certain.

The massacre had begun, and Subaru had run to stop it.
But starting from the restart point's slide backward by a mere ten-odd seconds, the shaving-away of his margin, moment by moment, drove miracles further out of reach and wiped hope from Subaru's heart.

He'd snuck into Gustav's office, worked with Hiain to learn the truth behind the massacre.
Again and again, he was caught before the conversation between Todd, Gustav, and the rest reached its core, failure piling on failure, trial and error repeating, next time, next time — until there was no going back.

Because the restart point had been pushed back to the middle of hiding in the office with Hiain.

"——"

From that instant on, Subaru could hear ruin laughing uproariously inside his own heart.
Curled over Hiain, shielding him, listening to the terrified pounding of Hiain's heart — it was probably Subaru's own heart hammering louder still.

If he couldn't stop the massacre, and the restart point slid past it — what then?
If an irreversible scene became the new starting line, what could Natsuki Subaru possibly do? Despair filled him to the brim.
And then—

"Bishuu—"

When Gustav's head blew apart with that deflated little sound, Subaru understood.
He must never die again. Not ever. If he died and returned to find himself past the moment Gustav was killed, then Gustav's life would be lost for good, confirmed beyond saving.

Once someone's "death" was confirmed, it was the same as Subaru having killed them himself.
A world where salvation was foreclosed — no matter whose hand had dealt that "death" — was a murder Natsuki Subaru had committed.

So he fought desperately not to die.
Even if Gustav died, even if old man Null died, even if the sword slaves died, even if the guards died, even if anyone at all died — he struggled desperately not to die himself.
If Subaru died, then the people who'd been killed would truly, finally be dead.

He knew it made no logical sense. Still, it was the only way Subaru knew how to struggle.
As long as Subaru didn't die, he could keep alive the possibility of not letting them stay dead. That way, they wouldn't really die. That's why—

The illusion that if he just never died, ever, he could keep anyone from dying—

That illusion proved to be exactly that — an illusion — the moment Subaru's own life ended in a pathetic, ugly death.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


How many "deaths" had it been now?

"Why the hell are you vanishing right in front of me."

"Schwa—"

Slumped, on his knees before him, the voice trying to call that name cut off mid-syllable.
The axe swung without mercy, cleaving through Hiain's body, life and all, and he crashed to the floor with a heavy thud, eyes rolled back white.
Thick blood spread across the floor, mixing with the blood of Idra, who had already fallen.

Idra, his skull split open, had been killed before Hiain.
Because Subaru had failed to save him from Todd's very first strike. Idra died first, then Hiain, and in the end, Subaru was left standing again — alone.

Tanza had already been hurled out by Todd's hand, flung through the hole in the wall.
The world had slid back far enough now that he could still hear Tanza's scream as she fell.

Weitz — arm and face mangled, swallowed along with the toad witchbeast into the collapsing corridor — and Tanza — thrown outside trying to shield Subaru — both were "fixed" into a reality he could do nothing to touch.
And now—

"So, seriously, how is it?"

Todd asked this, axe slung over his shoulder, of Subaru, who sat collapsed on the spot, knees soaked in the blood of two men.
Subaru's numbed brain throbbed, feeding him the words that came next. The question from Todd he'd already heard so many times now, it was—

"Are you really the Emperor's kid?"

"...Don't put words in my mouth."

At Subaru's murmur, head still bowed, Todd made a sound of displeasure.
Subaru couldn't tell how Todd felt about being read so accurately, but it didn't seem to rattle him enough to fly into a rage and kill him outright.
It hadn't been meant to rattle him, anyway — it had just spilled out, a spasm of words.
But if it had thrown Todd's pace off even slightly—

"Don't move, if you don't want to die."

Saying this, Subaru pulled the black sphere from inside his coat — the curse tool that had been placed inside Gustav's body — and thrust it out at Todd like a threat.
Wiped clean of blood, the black orb had a glassy texture and feel, and though only about the size of a golf ball, it was far heavier than it looked.
He had no idea what it was made of, and honestly, the material didn't matter.
What mattered was that it held some vast, cruel power inside it.

If this was the key needed to trigger the "Curse Clause," and Todd wanted it too, then it could be used.
It could be the second card, the one that might finally make Todd listen.

"This is it, right? What's needed for the curse clause. If you don't want to die—"

"—. You, that's—"

"—I'm talking! Right now! Didn't you hear me say I don't want you to die!?"

Waving the hand holding the curse tool, spraying spit, Subaru roared.
His head, crammed full of regret and despair, had no room left to weigh Todd's words. Just obey, just do as he was told — a fierce fury boiled up in him.

"Yeah, yeah, all right, all right."

Seeing Subaru's fury, Todd let out a small breath, lowered his axe, and raised both hands.
Seeing Todd comply so easily, Subaru went blank with shock. At his reaction, Todd cocked an eyebrow and said,

"Weird guy. You're the one who told me to do this."

"Th-that's... because something so... simple..."

Unable to talk back, Subaru's eyes went wide, and he bitterly cursed his own stupidity.
It hadn't been about whether he was the Emperor's hidden child at all — he should have used the curse tool as a shield from the very start. Frightened, panicked, he'd leapt at the easy option and ruined everything.

Gustav, old man Null, Weitz, Tanza — all of it, Subaru's fault.
All because Subaru hadn't properly used his head to think it through—

"By the way, you know how to use that thing?"

"Eh...?"

While Subaru was busy blaming and cursing his own idiocy, the voice cut into his thoughts.
At Todd's question, hands still raised, Subaru let out a cracked sound and looked down at the curse tool in his hand. Asked how to use it — he didn't know. There was no obvious switch, no mechanism that told you how to use it just by holding it.

"Thought so."

And from that flicker in his gaze alone, Todd saw through the bluff instantly.

"If you knew, you wouldn't be threatening me with it."

"Ah."

An instant later, Todd — hands still raised — kicked out and struck Subaru's hand with his boot.
The hand holding out the curse tool was kicked, and the black orb bounced straight up into the air. Subaru's eyes followed it without thinking, and he made a foolish mistake all over again.
Looking up meant, effectively, offering his throat to his opponent.

"——"

His gaze, chasing the bounced curse tool, cut off abruptly.
Right before it cut off, he thought he felt a hard sound pass from his left side through to his right, but there was no way to confirm what it had been, and no reason to.
And so there was nothing left to do but admit it.

—There was no way to think of, to stop Todd Fang.

—A fool mocked for playing at being a good man, who lost everything.

That was how the people around him judged Idra Missanga: the man who lost the family trade, wandered destitute, and ended up a slave.

A small farming village in the northwest of the empire — that was Idra's hometown, the land where the Missanga family had run its mill for generations.
He'd heard that the mill was started by his grandfather's grandfather, and by all accounts that ancestor had been quite the capable operator.

Entrusted by the local lord with managing the waterwheel-driven mill built along the village river, the family oversaw the milling of every grain the village produced.
The reward was substantial, the labor light, and to the people who depended on the Missanga family's mill to grind their harvest, Idra's family looked like a privileged class.

And yet neither Idra himself nor his family ever lorded the trade over anyone.
Their life was undeniably more comfortable than most around them. But at the same time, in years of poor harvest, they shouldered the villagers' taxes to the lord, went personally to negotiate on their behalf, and took on duties that should rightfully have belonged to the village headman, acting as one member of the community among others.

—Deal honestly, and work to earn broad trust and confidence.

That was the Missanga family motto, the philosophy behind the milling trade.
Plenty of millers, using the waterwheel to grind the gathered grain, would skim a share into their own pockets along the way. The world's eyes on millers were harsh at the best of times, which was exactly why humility mattered so much — Idra, and Idra's father before him, had had that drilled into them by the parent who ran the trade before either of them.

Precisely because it was a role that invited suspicion, one had to always meet people with an open heart.
Never hoard prosperity for oneself; share both hardship and joy with those around you. That might not be the correct way of things for the Vollachia Empire, which prized strength above all — but in this remote, poor village, beyond the reach of the capital's iron-blooded law, it was honored as the right way to live.

Idra, too, had benefited from that ethos since childhood, and had grown up within it.
So even after his ailing father handed him the family trade early, Idra tried to uphold that tradition.

"That's an honest, good life you've got. I envy it."

The man who'd said this, envying Idra's life, had stopped by the village tavern.
Radiating the air of a battle-hardened veteran, he revealed himself as a mercenary, one who traveled from battlefield to battlefield wielding a great sword. Outsiders were rare, so Idra shared drinks with him and heard all manner of stories.

To Idra, who barely knew anything of the world outside, the world the man described was full of astonishment.
Unlike the peaceful, slow-flowing time of the farming village, the world the man lived in was dangerous, cruel — and yet, at the same time, filled with a heat one could never forget.

Even before that, Idra had harbored a vague longing.
As one person born into the Vollachia Empire, hadn't there perhaps been another path open to him besides inheriting the family trade — a wish he'd never seriously entertained, but had felt all the same.

To live as a warrior, prizing one's own strength and honor, never stooping to a base way of life.

"Idra, you've got a cousin, don't you? Actually, I've had my eye on her..."

The man visited the village every few months, and each time, he and Idra shared a drink and grew closer.
Then, on one of the nights they spent together, the man confided in Idra with a serious face. Idra's cousin, of marriageable age, was still unwed, and Idra liked the man well enough.
So without a moment's hesitation, Idra introduced them, arranged everything until the two were properly wed, and on their wedding night, raised a toast to the birth of a new family.

It was a month later that the man conspired with the cousin and stole the family trade out from under Idra.

The man claimed his new wife also had a right to run the Missanga trade, and further lied that Idra and his father had long been rigging the milling process, cheating the villagers of their due.
Of course, Idra protested that none of it was true, but the profession's inherent susceptibility to suspicion couldn't be shaken off, and the village split clean in two over the matter.

Before the dispute could be settled, his ailing father succumbed to illness, and his mother, worn down by the stress, followed soon after.

"I've had enough. I will protect my family's name, no matter what it takes...!"

Having buried his parents, Idra, cursing his own carelessness, resolved to fight back.
If the man and his cousin meant to scheme something so vile, then Idra would rightfully reclaim what was his. To that end, he sought to bring the man to trial before the village, and asked the villagers to stand witness.
He trusted that the honest dealings the Missanga family had cultivated over the years would see justice done.
But—

"—Why...?"

No one showed up at the appointed place. Instead, evidence of an armed uprising was found planted in Idra's own house.
Dragged before the local lord to face an unwanted trial, Idra pleaded his innocence with everything he had, but not a single villager stood up to defend him.

The man and the cousin had orchestrated all of it.
The villagers chose the man — a liar, but one who brought them profit — over honest Idra.

There wasn't a shred of a chance to win.
Stripped of the family trade, reduced to a slave, Idra was sold off to the island by a slave trader.

Nowhere left to go, with even the one life left to him made into a plaything — the worst of islands, Ginunhive.
In that place where life-and-death struggles were put on display as spectacle, Idra felt his heart stain black, his soul rot through, and considered simply surrendering to that despair.

Enough already. Hadn't he tried so hard, and gotten nothing for it? So why keep trying at all?

What he'd believed had been a lie. What he'd relied on had been a mistake.
Everyone, every single person, was thinking only of how to use someone else, of saving only themselves.

Then there was nothing wrong with him doing the same.
That was exactly it — there was nothing there at all. That was Idra.

Of course no one had chosen him.
No trained strength, no special skill worth anything. Who would ever choose Idra?

Even Idra wouldn't choose Idra.
And yet — who was it, who on earth was it, who would choose Idra, foolishly honest Idra and nothing more—

"—You wanted to be a warrior, didn't you, Idra! Then now's your chance! Now's the moment!"


—Living honestly was never supposed to bring a day of reward, was it.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


"——"

His head throbbing, pounding, Idra found himself unable to move.
His tongue was numb, his throat felt sealed shut so he couldn't breathe. His limbs felt distant, and terror gripped him — the fear that his own body had come apart.

Fear — there was no avoiding it now.
Considering everything that had just happened, even the sensation of every drop of blood in his body freezing solid, back when no one in his hometown had stood by him — that terror was nothing compared to this.

Weitz killed right before his eyes, brave Tanza thrown out through the corridor's opening.
Watching it happen, Idra had simply frozen, able to do nothing. If his cowardice was the reason he'd outlived Weitz and Tanza, that was too cruel an irony.

Could these few seconds, these ten-odd seconds, possibly balance out against a humiliation searing enough to consume his chest?

Unable to do anything, he'd let two people die who could have done something.
The despair and regret that fact brought Idra weighed heavier, even, than the past — the carelessness that had let his family trade be devoured out from under him.

"—Ha."

Thinking that far, Idra found himself laughing at how shallow his life's experience had been.
Setting aside the happy things, when it came to suffering, he could think of nothing to compare to the fall of his life when the trade was stolen from him. —He realized, keenly, just how blessed, how fortunate, he had been.
Perhaps it was exactly that quality in Idra that had enraged the man who'd stolen his life.
Even if that were true, it didn't make what the man had done forgivable.

"——"

Breath ragged beyond ragged, vision hazy, Idra turned it around him to take stock.
What on earth had happened? Was there any connection between the pain wracking his whole body and this nonsensical confusion? Was any of it even real, to begin with?

That the whole island of sword slaves might all be killed, that Schwartz was some illegitimate child of the Emperor, that Weitz and Tanza had been killed — wasn't all of it just a dream?
All a dream, and Idra was still living out some idle, luxurious hour, bored with his own comfortable life—

"Bold move you pulled there, weren't you."

A chillingly cold voice reached him, and Idra froze, forgetting even the pain in his body.
Every drop of blood in him went past cold into frozen, and Idra slowly understood that the footsteps of the voice's owner were drawing near.
And then—

"Even standing there like a post would've been better than jumping with no plan. Of course it ended like this."

The man's voice, tinged with exasperation, wasn't directed at Idra.
At the voice cast a short distance from himself, Idra savored a wave of relief. And then, though he knew better, he squinted his eyes open just slightly to see where the voice was aimed.
There—

"—Ah, uh."

He saw a black-haired boy, battered and bloodied, crawling across the ground.

"——"

Seeing the crawling boy, and realizing this place wasn't the upper corridor from before, Idra's mind recalled everything that had happened, the events before his consciousness had gone white.

Weitz killed, Tanza thrown out, Idra — screaming something incoherent, not understanding the man's murderous intent — on the verge of being killed himself, when Schwartz had moved.
Shrieking, Schwartz had grabbed both Idra and Hiain, frozen stiff, around the waist, and leapt through the hole in the wall opposite the one Tanza had fallen through.

The hole torn open by the great bird that had crashed through — Tanza had fallen on the far side of the island, and Schwartz had jumped with Idra and the others through the opposite side. Even so, the odds of surviving were slim.
Scraped along the wall, caught on jagged outcrops, slammed against the ground from a great height, dying on impact.

A reckless, desperate flight, the kind that killed you nine times out of ten — but the only option there was.
And that they'd seized the one surviving outcome could only be called a miracle. But the miracles stopped there.

The crawling Schwartz, the fallen Idra — neither could escape the pursuing man.
As for why Hiain was nowhere to be seen — whether he'd simply failed to land on this midlevel ledge, or was using his camouflage to hide himself, there was no telling.

Even if he was hiding, there was no counting on Hiain's courage.
He wasn't a bad man, but he wasn't brave either. Craven, cowardly, easily swept up in the moment — he doted on Schwartz, but beyond that, there was nothing more to expect from him.
Still, if there was any chance at all of not dying, holding his breath and staying hidden was the right call.

If the miracle Schwartz had desperately seized let Hiain live, then so be it.
That much of a reward, Schwartz deserved.

"——"

A thought crossed Idra's mind, unbidden.
Fallen from a height, battered and broken — with the man's attention fixed on Schwartz right now, maybe, just maybe, Idra too could be overlooked, taken for dead.

Terrifying as the man was, surely even he wouldn't go around crushing every corpse's skull just to be sure.
Depending on Idra's condition, if he could put on a convincing enough act of being dead, he might avoid dying after all. Just this once, even alone, he might be able to save himself.

"——"

It was time to choose.
For Idra Missanga to survive, what would he sacrifice, and what would he gain?

In this cruel world, in a place where the strong and the cunning could take whatever they pleased and the logic of it held — what, exactly, would Idra Missanga choose to become?

The man who, in foolish honesty, believed that honesty earned trust and confidence, and lost everything for it—

"—One, two—"

The back of the man swinging the axe he held in both hands, about to bring it down on the crawling boy's head.
If he held his breath, closed his eyes, hid his face from all of it—

—what, in the end, would that save?

"D-don't—!!"

Forcing his whole body to move, tearing himself free from the ground he felt fused to, Idra spat blood as he ran, pathetically.
He ran, and threw himself at the man's back. His left hand wouldn't move, so with the right alone, he clung to the man's body, desperate to sink his teeth in.

"I knew you were alive."

In that instant, as if mocking Idra's resolve, the man let the raised axe drop from over his shoulder. With his now-free hand, he drew a knife, and turning, in a single flashing stroke, severed Idra's right arm at the elbow.
A milling arm, thin and pale, that had never known heavy labor because the waterwheel did the work — flew off.
But even so—

"—Take him and gooooo!!"

Searing heat crawled up from the severed arm, and Idra's vision flooded crimson. But for that one instant, he forgot the pain and screamed with everything he had.
At the volume of that blood-spitting cry, the man before him raised an eyebrow, faintly.

He clearly couldn't grasp the point of Idra's scream, of his action.
But—

"Tch."

Clicking his tongue, the man turned and hurled the knife that had severed Idra's arm.
It was thrown behind him, at the ground where Schwartz had been crawling — no, Schwartz was no longer there. It was thrown at the stirring scenery, at whoever was carrying Schwartz away as they fled.

Hiain.
Hiain, who'd been holding his breath in hiding, had scooped up Schwartz and run. Seeing it, Idra gritted his teeth and threw himself again at the man's back.
His right arm was gone, his left wouldn't move, so he sank his teeth into the man's clothes and clung on.

"Guh."

Struck in the chest by an elbow swung back, he was kicked down as he reeled back. But the impact of the fall jolted his left arm — instead of agony, it began to move again. His dislocated shoulder had popped back into place.
And beside that left arm, by another string of miracles, lay the axe the man had dropped.

"Didn't think he'd gotten away?"

Having let go of the knife and now disarmed of the axe, the man tilted his head, looking at Idra.
His expression showed not the slightest sense of being at a disadvantage, weapons or no. And of course it wouldn't — the blood Idra had spilled, the blood still spilling from him, was endless.
That he was even alive was a miracle, and far too many miracles had already happened.
So, to the man's question, Idra shook his head.

"No, I thought he'd gotten away. But I believed something else, too."

"——"

"That he wouldn't leave, if he didn't have to."

A craven, cowardly man, easily carried away — but not a bad man.
That was all — Idra had simply believed, in his foolish honesty, that Hiain like that wouldn't run off if he could help it.

In the end, people didn't change so easily.
The lie he'd told, claiming to be some kind of warrior, meant to deceive and use everyone around him — even that had unraveled instantly.

Honesty. Broad trust and confidence.
Even if it meant being pointed at and laughed at for playing the good man.

Idra Missanga could be neither a warrior, nor a swindler.

Hefting the axe with his one working left hand, bleeding out, Idra screamed.

"I am Idra Missanga! Son of a miller!!"

"Don't know him."

Pouring every last ounce of his remaining strength into the charge, Idra threw himself at the man's coldly indifferent face.

—On that day, thanks to Schwartz, he'd been spared from becoming a liar. And he was glad of it.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


Carried on the back of a body running desperately, he was borne out of the death-ground.
Jostled violently, with no regard for the injuries or condition of the one he carried — but of course. Whoever carried him.

Subaru was battered too, but the state of the body carrying him was far worse.

"H-hi... a... Hiain...!"

Gripped painfully around the waist, Subaru dug his nails into the body carrying him. He couldn't even tell where he was clawing. The one he'd clawed was already a mess to look at.
It wasn't wounds exactly — it was the color, the pattern, everything about him, all scrambled together.
Camouflaging piecemeal off the surrounding scenery, until it was impossible to tell what was correct at all. And then the body, still running in that state, abruptly lost its strength and pitched forward.

"Gah!"

Of course, if the one carrying him fell, Subaru went down too.
Flung out in front of the falling body, Subaru rolled across the cold stone floor. He couldn't even brace the fall — the impact felt like it had broken his front teeth.
Lifting his throbbing face, he looked back from where he lay sprawled.
There—

"Heeh... heeh..."

Hiain lay collapsed, breathing quite literally like an insect's dying rasp.
Face-down and limp, a large knife buried deep in the middle of his back. There was no telling when he'd been stabbed.
Whether while running to escape, or even before that — however far back it had been, there was nothing Subaru could do about it now.

If Subaru died, he'd return to a point right before Idra's skull was split open.
Maybe even after it had already happened. —No, that wasn't right — he'd jumped out through the wall to keep it from happening, so it could be before he fell, or after, or even mid-air.
Getting all three of them to fall without dying was, no matter how he tried it, brutally hard — and just when it finally seemed to have worked, Todd showed up.

Idra's voice, Hiain's breathing — both felt so far away.
Everything, now, was somewhere beyond his reach.

"Funny... isn't it..."

As Subaru lay fallen, trying to crawl, Hiain's faint voice reached his ears.
It sounded like someone speaking with their face barely above water — surely because his throat was flooding with blood. Probably he needed the blood drained, some kind of treatment, something.
That — he had to do that. Even without being a doctor, he had to.

"L-life... they say, is like... you pick the pretty flowers first... heard that... once..."

"D-don't... talk... I'm coming, right now, right now—!"

"Then it's not... so strange, a rotten-hearted guy... like me... dying first, huh... ah."

He crawled. Kept crawling.
Across the ground, to pull Hiain up out of the blood he was drowning in.
And still, his body wouldn't move forward at all.

"But..."

It wouldn't move at all, wouldn't close the distance.

"...guess it's better... I go first, huh."

Slower than a turtle crawling. Far too slow, moving smaller than an ant, he crawled.
Crawled, and crawled, and when he finally, finally reached him — it was too late.

Hiain's color had already returned to its base gray.
A knife buried in his back, one arm broken, blood pouring from every part of his body — it wouldn't have been strange if he'd died somewhere along the way here, in the middle of running.
Maybe he had been dying the whole time he ran. That had to be it.

Now, everyone Subaru couldn't save had died.
It was the same as Subaru having killed them himself.

Because he couldn't save all of them, it was the same as killing all of them.

"Guh."

Was it blood staining his face now, or tears, or snot?
He couldn't tell anymore which was which, what was what, who was who, what the answer even was, anymore.

Anymore, anymore, anymore, anymore, anymore — none of it made sense anymore.

"—Oh, hey, is that Bassu I hear groaning over there?"

"——"

"Ah, it is, it's Bassu after all! Well now, what a coincidence to run into you in a scene like this. Quite the lively state of affairs we've got going — having fun yet?"

A light, breezy voice called out to Subaru, who sat dazed, feeling like he'd let go of everything.
He couldn't turn to face the voice behind him. Somehow he managed to lift his body, to look this person who'd said something so absurd in the eye, to say something back — pouring every last bit of strength in his body into it—

"No need to go turning around on pain of your life, you know."

"—Ah."

"After all, this isn't really the place to push yourself, is it?"

Saying this, circling around to face him from the front — Cecilus peered down into Subaru's face and spoke.
