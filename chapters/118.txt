Arc 3 Chapter 41: "Childish Negotiations"

"That my last visitor of the day should be you is an amusing turn of events."

Despite having her schedule thrown into disarray, Crusch laughed at that with an air of good humor, settling deep into her chair in the parlor.

Across from her, facing her, sat Subaru and Rem. Behind Crusch stood Wilhelm and Ferris, attending her as always—a scene Subaru felt he'd witnessed before.

The place, the faces present—the same as that first time, when he'd declared he would return to the mansion. And the subject Subaru meant to raise this time seemed likely to stir up even more trouble than that had.

After all, this was a matter that wouldn't be settled simply by Subaru and Rem returning to the mansion.

"Fortunately, the reason I had for dining out has vanished. I have time to spare for you until dinner is ready at the mansion."

"Showing up without any warning like that, you normally wouldn't get time like this, y'know. Subaru-kyun ought to be grateful for how deep and generous Crusch-sama's heart is~"

"Well, as a matter of fact, the time only opened up because my negotiating partner stood me up."

"Oh my, the way Crusch-sama doesn't even bother pretending otherwise for appearances' sake—so manly and wonderful...!"

While mistress and vassal carried on with their usual banter, Subaru turned over in his mind how best to broach the subject.

Given the time limit, he couldn't afford to fumble around too long. But if he handled it too clumsily, he wouldn't get what he was after either.

*Well, better to keep it simple, I guess.*

Given Crusch's temperament, dragging the conversation out with roundabout, long-winded talk would only earn her displeasure.

What he had to say was already decided—hesitating over it would get him nowhere.

Steeling himself, Subaru changed his expression, and Crusch straightened in her seat. "Well then," she prefaced, extending a hand toward him.

"This meeting was requested by you. I'll leave it to you to begin as you see fit. —What is it you want?"

*She really does cut to the chase,* Subaru thought.

He licked his dry lips to wet them and took a deep breath before Crusch. Then—

"The Witch Cult—those guys are planning to attack Roswaal's domain. I want to borrow your strength, Crusch-san, to crush them."

Straight to the point, Subaru laid out the condition necessary to achieve his goal.

The military strength essential to annihilating the Witch Cult—since it couldn't be expected from within Roswaal's own domain, it would have to be drawn from elsewhere. And Subaru knew, even from his short stay, that such strength existed under Crusch's command.

At Subaru's words, the four others present each showed their own reaction.

Wilhelm's reaction was no more than a slight twitch of his brow. Ferris's eyes went wide in surprise, glancing worriedly at Crusch. Beside Subaru, Rem pressed her lips tight, her stare boring into the side of his face.

And then—

"I see. The Witch Cult, is it."

Crusch laughed, resting her elbows on the parlor table and her chin atop her clasped hands.

Her captivating smile carried a certain allure, revealing a new side of her to Subaru, who until now had held a strong impression of her as dashing and vivid.

In any case, the opening shot had been fired.

Having plunged straight into the main issue, there was nothing to do now but push through without stopping. Soothing his quickening heartbeat, Subaru waited for Crusch's next reaction.

But she showed no sign of saying anything more than that first remark. Puzzled, Subaru furrowed his brow slightly, and—

"What's the matter? I told you, didn't I—this is the part where you speak."

"No, well... that's just what I said..."

"Don't tell me you think simply thrusting a demand at me is the whole of it. Why do you seek this thing you demand? What grounds do you have for raising it? What benefit would we gain by meeting your demand? Unless these are presented, it cannot be called a negotiation."

Crusch fixed her gaze on Subaru, who choked out a strangled sound, and closed one eye as if bored.

That gesture alone was enough to remind him what a different league she played in. Chastising himself for nearly falling into her pace, Subaru pressed on.

"Yeah, that's... fair. Sorry, my bad. I mean, I don't have much experience with this kind of negotiating stuff."

"Understanding one's own inexperience is necessary too. There's no need to fret over it. But the time is bounded until dinner. —Don't forget that."

Even while showing generosity, the way she brought up the time limit showed she understood the balance of carrot and stick well enough.

Subaru reorganized what he needed to say and continued.

"First, the reason I'm asking to borrow your strength is, well... simply put, we're short on manpower. Against the Witch Cult's numbers, ours fall completely short. As a result, we can't fend off an attack."

"A simple matter indeed. But wouldn't Lord Mathers suffice on that front? His capacity for mass annihilation is unrivaled even in Lugunica. Numbers shouldn't be an issue."

"That'd work if the enemy came as one group, but it's not that simple. Roswaal's only got one body, so if two places get attacked at once, we're done for."

At the very least, it was certain the Witch Cult would attack both the village and the mansion. And though it was mere conjecture, Subaru had a feeling they'd also assault any Dragon Carriage passing by. His grounds for this were the word "clear the area" that had come from Petelgeuse's mouth more than once.

"I see, that's understandable enough. But then, isn't that Lord Mathers' negligence as a landholder? Maintaining military strength to defend one's own domain is a lord's duty. If he neglected it out of overconfidence in his own power, I can hardly avoid lowering my estimation of him."

"On that point, I can only agree entirely. Anyway, that's why we can't withstand the Witch Cult's indiscriminate methods. That's why I need strength—numbers, specifically."

If the attack sites could be narrowed to two, then all that was needed was strength enough to defend both at once. Since there was a chance Roswaal would be absent, if one side were left to Rem and Ram, the other would need some other reliable presence.

Glancing briefly at Wilhelm, Subaru pleaded his case to Crusch. If it could be arranged, Wilhelm was exactly the strength he wanted to borrow.

Wilhelm, surely aware of the meaning behind Subaru's glance, gave no reaction to it at all. Watching Subaru lower his eyes slightly, Crusch waved a hand toward Ferris beside her.

"Still, the Witch Cult, is it. It seems they really have begun to move."

"Seems so, nya~. Well, the moment the half-elf Emilia-sama stepped into the public eye, that much was pretty much expected."

Ferris voiced agreement with Crusch's words, and Subaru frowned at the attitude behind it.

But rather than press the matter, what drew Subaru's attention was the aftershock of fierce emotion rising from beside him. Turning his gaze that way, he found Rem silent, lips pressed tight, head bowed.

Looking at that expression, deliberately drained of feeling, it was plain as day that inside, she was anything but calm.

The first time Subaru had ever heard the phrase "Witch Cult," it had been, if he recalled, from Rem's own mouth. To her, it seemed, they were something to be despised—and now they had become Subaru's greatest enemy as well. No doubt his own face wore an expression just as harsh as hers.

Seeing Rem's indignation cooled Subaru's own head. Watching this, Crusch swept her long green hair back and tilted her head. "And so?"

"I've grasped the circumstances. Next is your reason—and your grounds—for choosing my house as your collaborator."

"I'm choosing you and your people as collaborators because, given the current situation, it's the most feasible option. There's also the fact you're already looking after me and Rem like this, and compared to the other Royal Selection candidates, I think you're in a position that's easier to team up with."

This much he'd expected to be asked and had an answer ready.

Though, to be honest, there was someone else who would likely be even easier to deal with than Crusch. But prioritizing ease of access in the current circumstances, it simply worked out that he approached her first.

"Easier to deal with, is it."

Taking in Subaru's answer, Crusch murmured this quietly and smiled meaningfully. As Subaru struggled to read the intent behind that smile, she raised a finger. "Let me correct one thing."

"Perhaps treating you both as guests has invited some misunderstanding, but there's something I'd like you to reconsider."

"...What is it?"

"I do not treat you two as enemies. But the fact remains that Emilia is already a political adversary to me. Understand? I am already at odds with her."

Crusch emphasized these unexpectedly harsh words, stating her position clearly.

That attitude brought back for Subaru the memory of the first world, and—

"But, I mean, right now you're still letting us..."

"That is because a contract has been formed. Between Emilia and myself—through our respective vassals, but a contract nonetheless, price for price—and it is precisely because of that contract that I welcome you both. Should that agreement lapse, our positions become clear enough, and in matters unrelated to the agreement, we are nothing but rivals."

Crusch cut off Subaru's clinging voice sharply, refusing to yield an inch on that line.

In the first world too, she had declared her open hostility toward Subaru the moment he broke the contract. It could be called sincerity, or it could be called inflexibility.

In other words—

"So you're saying there's no chance at all of teaming up?"

"That and this are unrelated matters. I told you already, Natsuki Subaru. If this is to be a negotiation, present benefits sufficient for both parties to be satisfied and agree. Up to this point, we've only confirmed your reasons and the preconditions. I still need to hear your grounds, and purely the advantage to us in lending you our forces."

"Although," she added, continuing on,

"as for the grounds, one could say they're unnecessary. The moment Emilia's identity became known to the public, it was to be expected that the Witch Cult would begin to move. Whether the source of your information is reliable or purely conjecture, either way, it comes close enough to a certainty."

Regarding the precondition of this negotiation—that the Witch Cult had begun to move—Crusch apparently held no doubt whatsoever. This seemed to be common knowledge peculiar to this world, shared by those who lived in it, and for Subaru, an amateur muddling through this negotiation, it was a stroke of luck amid misfortune.

Though the fact that the Witch Cult was so widely known was, frankly speaking, on a level that made his skin crawl.

"In other words, what a negotiation requires is mutual benefit. In your case, by borrowing my house's strength, you can eliminate the threat that is the Witch Cult. Then what does my house gain by lending you a hand? Let me hear it."

"P-purely as an act of helping people, maybe...?"

"An amusing jest. So amusing, in fact, that it makes me want to jest right back by telling Wilhelm to draw his sword."

Of those present, only Wilhelm and Ferris wore swords, but the fact she'd name Wilhelm rather than her own foremost knight for such a command spoke volumes about the balance of power here.

In any case, this wasn't a scene that could be dodged with a joke. Subaru spun his mind at full tilt.

"Uh, well, that is. For example, by lending a hand in this crisis, you could build up a huge debt owed by our faction, or something like that..."

"—Should I accept that proposal, it would mean Emilia's withdrawal from the Royal Selection. Do you say this understanding that?"

"Eh?"

Cut off mid-word by the sharp interjection, Subaru's mouth fell open blankly. But the gaze Crusch turned on him carried an edge like a blade.

"Isn't it obvious? Dumping the crisis of one's own domain entirely onto another lord is a problem that comes before the question of a vessel fit to be king. If one cannot protect and command one's own people, how could one possibly bear the weight of a nation upon their shoulders? Natsuki Subaru—let me correct one more misunderstanding of yours."

Raising a finger, Crusch aimed it as though to pierce the now-silent Subaru.

"You are carrying out this negotiation bearing the weight of Emilia's entire faction. The rightness or wrongness of every word you speak falls upon her; your words carry Emilia's full weight. Judgments should not be made lightly, and what has been spoken cannot easily be taken back."

"Ah, ugh..."

"With that understood, I ask you again. —If you create such a debt through this matter, it means the defeat of Emilia's faction. Is that acceptable to you?"

It couldn't possibly be acceptable.

Only now did Subaru begin to understand just where he was standing.

Subaru was not in some carefree debating hall where he bore no responsibility. This was a scene where a single utterance could shake the standing of countless people, could even sway the fate of the kingdom.

The stage Subaru had chosen to stand on, of his own will, was precisely one where such crushing weight was inevitable.

"But, even so..."

Nearly crushed under the weight bearing down on both shoulders, Subaru bit his lip, his voice trembling.

Just as Crusch had said, if he were to borrow her strength under these terms, it would mean allowing Emilia a setback so great it could never be recovered from.

As for turning the tide of the Royal Selection—how much could really be done once such a gap had opened at this early stage of the contest?

If he pushed this proposal on Crusch, Emilia's Royal Selection would end right here.

But if he couldn't borrow her strength, all that awaited Roswaal's domain was devastation at the hands of the Witch Cult's fanatics.

The Balance swayed back and forth, and pain and grinding pressure came ceaselessly to Subaru's mind.

With noise battering every corner of his thoughts, the conclusion Subaru ground out through clenched teeth was—

"Even so, I want you to lend a hand."

"...Even knowing it means withdrawal from the Royal Selection?"

"Better alive, isn't it. —Because once you're dead, that's the end of it."

Shoulders slumped, Subaru answered so, unable to hide his despondency and disappointment.

Once dead, that's the end. Subaru had no courage left to watch, with his own eyes, that village's devastation again—or the utterly wretched death of the very Rem standing beside him now.

If bowing his head and enduring humiliation meant lives could be saved, then that was what he should do.

And Crusch, receiving Subaru's answer, drew her chin back. "Understood," she said.

"—In that case, House Karsten will lend you no strength whatsoever."

So she declared, flatly.

* * *

For a moment, unable to grasp what Crusch had just said, Subaru's sense of time froze solid.

"—Huh?"

The sound that leaked out carried a question mark, but it was little more than noise devoid of meaning, born of incomprehension. Crusch, receiving it, crossed her long, slender legs.

"I'll repeat myself. My house will not grant your request—aid to Mathers territory, and by extension, will not lend Emilia our strength."

Once more, spoken this time broken down more plainly.

That orderly manner of speaking felt, if anything, like it was mocking Subaru's poor comprehension.

"Are you kidd—!"

In the next instant, about to let the rising fury spill off his tongue, Subaru just barely reined in that rash impulse.

Raising his voice and shouting on pure emotion had never once made things better. Especially not against an opponent far more seasoned and composed than himself.

Tapping his knee, using the rhythm to loosen his rising temper, he managed—

"Why... does it come to that?"

He wrung out that obvious question.

Before appealing to emotion, first hear the other side's reasoning. Then search for common ground, reconcile it, and form an answer. That was the basic principle of negotiation, one he couldn't afford to throw away.

For it was Subaru's judgment that Petelgeuse's slaughter—the survival of an entire village—hinged upon.

To Subaru's low-voiced question, Crusch raised two fingers. "First, one," she prefaced.

"The withdrawal of Emilia from the Royal Selection, which you presented as our benefit and swallowed only after much bitter struggle—that cannot serve as a card in this negotiation. Do you understand why?"

"W-why not? Having one less rival is plenty of a payoff for your side..."

Precisely because it couldn't be traded for lives—precisely because of that—he had bet it, that heaviest of things, as the counterweight on the opposite side of the Balance, as his price.

Knowing full well Emilia was aiming for the throne, and yet weighing it against countless lives on the Balance.

To Subaru, gritting his teeth and pressing on regardless, Crusch merely shook her head.

"Do you not realize it even as you speak? As for Emilia's withdrawal, that is something that will occur regardless of my involvement."

"What are you—"

He meant to continue, *talking about,* but Subaru realized it then.

And Crusch nodded at Subaru, his face going rigid with shock at the realization.

"Just as you said yourself, without anyone's aid, Emilia cannot fully defend her own territory. That, as it stands, means Emilia's withdrawal from the Royal Selection. There's no need for me to lift a finger."

"If anything," Crusch said, waving the finger she'd kept raised,

"clumsily interfering and having it known to the other factions that my house had a hand in Emilia's downfall would carry far greater consequences. As you know, my house currently holds the most advantageous position in this Royal Selection. I'd rather avoid making a show of kicking down one other faction so early, only to become a target of scorn for all the rest at once."

By simply watching from the sidelines, she could obtain the exact benefit Subaru had presented, whole and intact. There was no need to deliberately court danger, to go pulling chestnuts out of the fire for it.

But then—

"Then are you saying you'll let Roswaal's territory—that village, those people—be slaughtered by the Witch Cult and just look away!?"

"Let me correct your misunderstanding. And do not shift the subject, Natsuki Subaru."

Piercing the shouting Subaru with a cold stare, Crusch let sharpness settle into her expression.

"It is Emilia who lacks the power to protect her territory, and it is Emilia whose incompetence will cost her people their lives. Certainly not I."

Powerless, incompetent—those words struck Subaru like a full blow to the head.

He wanted to refute Crusch's argument. But all that surfaced was nothing but childish, emotional reasoning, and no strength welled up in him to stand against her plain, correct logic.

"It seems you've said all you have to say."

Crusch murmured this, closing one eye at the sight of Subaru opening and closing his mouth over and over. With her remaining open right eye, she looked out through the window at the sky beyond.

"The Hour of Earth approaches soon—time for the evening meal. Right on schedule."

Cutting the conversation off there, Crusch made to rise from her seat.

Panicking at that attitude, which left him no foothold at all, Subaru cried out.

"W-wait!"

Holding out a hand to stop her as she began to rise, Subaru half-rose from his own seat, wetting his lips, racking his brain desperately as he tried to keep the negotiation with Crusch going.

"A-are you really going to abandon them? The people in that village haven't done anything wrong, there's no reason for this!"

But all that came from Subaru's mouth was nothing more than a string of unmanly words, clinging to the other's mercy to the last. Hearing this, Crusch let a faint disappointment surface in her eyes.

"I told you already. The one lacking in power is not I, but—"

"Knowing and still abandoning them! Isn't that evil!? If you have the power, if you could help, why don't you!? What's wrong with helping!? Just because it's another territory's problem, you'll play dumb since it doesn't affect your votes!?"

Cutting her off, he shouted his fury even knowing it was rude.

Crusch frowned at Subaru's ferocity, and Ferris, eyes flashing with anger at such an impertinent attitude, moved to step forward. "Hey, now—" But—

"Ferris, it's fine."

"But, Crusch-sama... even so, that was a bit much, nya."

"That's naked spirit, laid bare. If I don't answer it, it lowers my own standing."

Ferris grumbled discontentedly but obeyed Crusch's order, retreating quietly. Catching this at the edge of her vision, Crusch settled back into her chair once more.

"Whether overlooking it, letting them die, is evil, is it."

She let out a deep breath as she turned Subaru's words over in her mind.

Seizing on that reaction as if it were a handhold, a last possibility, Subaru pounced.

"That's right! You're aiming to be king, aren't you? You're going to bear the weight of the whole country, right? Then what kind of king abandons a single village!?"

Worked up, spittle flying, fists clenched, Subaru pressed the point with all his force.

At Subaru's relentless barrage of words, Crusch closed her eyes as if in thought, then slowly—

"Let me correct one thing."

So saying, she raised two fingers, glaring at Subaru with a piercingly clear gaze.

Flinching at the sharpness of that stare, yet frowning at the mismatch between her fingers and her words, Subaru watched as Crusch's lips loosened into a small, provocative smile.

"This is the second of my two earlier reasons. It should clear up most of your doubts."

The earlier reasons—that is, Crusch's reasons for not helping Emilia. Namely—

"The answer is—because your story lacks the credibility to move my house to act."

The statement struck Subaru like something that overturned everything—every word, every premise—that had come before.

With the single phrase "no credibility," everything exchanged so far was severed away.

"Huh?"

"The Witch Cult, is it. Indeed, it's plausible they'd act. That much is clear from their doctrine and their past activities. But the problem lies beyond that."

"Beyond that...?"

"It's a simple matter. Why is it that you can pin down exactly where they'll strike next?"

Still pointing the raised finger straight at Subaru, Crusch spoke with a voice and gaze like a blade.

"Their inscrutability is thorough to a baffling degree. The fact they've survived without being wiped out for close to four hundred years is proof enough of that. So how, exactly, could you have come to know their next act of folly?"

"That's... but you yourself just said something like that too—"

"History itself proves how murky and obscure their information network is. If you'd truly gotten hold of something, then show me your grounds for it. If not, there is only one reason you could possibly know."

In place of the silenced Subaru, Crusch spoke slowly, as though driving the point home.

"That you, too, are a member of the Witch Cult."

"Are you kidd—!"

This time, a scream born of pure rage surged up his throat, ready to explode. But once again, it was checked at the very last instant.

This time, it was not the result of his own conscience or self-restraint.

"――――"

It was because of the dense, truly demonic aura that Rem—who had watched the exchange between Subaru and Crusch in silence all this while—now emanated.

Beside Subaru, Rem sat properly upright in her chair, hands resting on her knees. Yet from her expressionless face poured a killing intent so intense the very air seemed to warp, and that it stemmed from the conversation just now was plainer than fire.

"Crusch-sama, please refrain from your jests."

Drawing every gaze in the room upon herself, Rem spoke in a tone no different from usual, tilting her head modestly toward Crusch.

"There's no way Subaru-kun could be with the Witch Cult, is there?"

"—Is that so? Reflecting on Natsuki Subaru's own statements, if he cannot voice his reason for knowing, there is no choice but to conclude as much. Do you have no idea yourself?"

"—None."

Whether Crusch noticed that instant's hesitation, who could say.

Only Rem, who could sense the Witch's scent on Subaru, felt something catch at Crusch's seemingly offhand probing just now, and clicked her tongue inwardly.

In any case—

"Either way. For those two reasons, my house cannot lend Emilia a hand. —To begin with, you haven't even been granted the authority to act as a negotiator, have you?"

"—Ngh."

"Earlier I threatened that Emilia's fate rested on your shoulders, but this is a problem prior even to that. There is nothing at all here for you to carry, in this place, right now."

Charging ahead on his own, trying to protect on his own, failing selfishly all on his own.

Crusch's words, coldly and dispassionately, gouged and tore into Subaru's bared heart.

His fury dispersed like mist, and what filled his chest began shifting from something hot to something cold, his mouth opening and closing pathetically like a fish gasping for oxygen.

Crusch looked at him with eyes tinged with pity.

"As you are now, you have no power to move me. Be still, and let yourself be protected."

"――――!"

Those were words that had been hurled at him, again and again, over and over, without end.

Words that forced Subaru to admit his own powerlessness, thrust his own ignorance upon him, compelled his own spinelessness, and jeered at his reckless, foolhardy, pathetic struggles—rotten pity, all of it.

Nothing at all went as he wished, and mortification came to dominate Subaru entirely.

Why, why did no one, not a single person, let Subaru accomplish what he wanted? What was so wrong about it? He was supposed to be trying to do the right thing. He believed it was right, believed he could save them, and was acting on that very belief.

"The Witch Cult is coming! They're going to slaughter every single person in that village...!"

Subaru cried out, his throat near to splitting with the fury and grief packed into it.

There were sights he had witnessed. There were deaths he had touched.

People dear to him, precious existences, everything—frozen solid, transformed into white crystal.

That was something that would truly happen. A merciless reality that would surely come to pass if left unchecked.

Why wouldn't anyone understand that, wouldn't let him stop it? Why couldn't everyone just get out of his way and let him bend fate to his will?

"Kill them! Just kill them! The Witch Cult, all of them, just wipe every single one of them out! Then everything works out fine! Don't you get it!? Bastards like them shouldn't even be allowed to live! Kill—lend me the strength—to kill them!"

He dropped to his knees right there, crawling flat against the ground, begging.

If grinding his head against the floor and pleading would work, rather than being looked down on with pity in her eyes, he'd rather play the clown, be anything at all, if only she'd lend him her strength.

He'd play the dog, take on the role of a beast, anything at all—if it meant this killing intent of his could be fulfilled—

"—So that is the wellspring of your actions."

But such conduct from Subaru, baring his own shame without hesitation,

"Hatred of the Witch Cult—is that the reason you drew near to Emilia?"

—stirred not even a shred of pity in a power-holder who let no personal feeling mix into her judgment.
