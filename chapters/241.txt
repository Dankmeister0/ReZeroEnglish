Arc 4 Chapter 74: "The Witch's Scheme and Proposal"

Gasping for breath, Subaru belatedly realized his hands were braced against a green meadow.
From the ground beneath his limbs, the thick scent of grass brushed past his nostrils. Like a meadow bathed in sunlight after rain, the almost overwhelming natural air enveloped Subaru's whole body in a faint haze.

Turning his head, Subaru looked at Echidna before him.
As always, she had arranged a table and chairs atop the small rise in the meadow, preparing for tea while she waited for her guest — for Subaru.
As always. — Yes. As always.

"I imagine you have all sorts of things you want to say, want to ask... but first, why don't you sit down and drink at least one cup of tea?"

"...Do you really think that after what you just did to me, I'd sit down at that table like we're friends? Much less agree to a tea party?"

"I do think you'll agree. You're a person who can prioritize reason, feigning calculating detachment, over an instinct raging out of control. Right now, you can judge, in your heart, that there's more benefit to having a meaningful discussion with me than to shutting me out... isn't that so?"

"――――"

Before Subaru, who choked back his voice in anger, Echidna's composure didn't waver.
Her words, spoken down at him, mocked Subaru as though he were fumbling some transparently obvious little trick — and stung by how close it hit, Subaru couldn't choose between affirming or denying it.
But what had been trampled on wasn't cheap enough to simply, meekly submit to her words.

"Echidna... say it wasn't what you intended."

"Hm?"

"Say that what just happened — with the Witch of Lust — wasn't what you intended. Say that, and say you're sorry, just once."

"…………"

"Say it couldn't be helped. That it was unexpected, that it wasn't supposed to turn out that way. Say that. If you say that... I won't hound you to the ends of the earth over it."

Echidna's reasoning was correct.
For Subaru to move forward, he needed her knowledge, her cooperation.
But what he couldn't forgive, he couldn't forgive. It was certain that Echidna had used the Witch of Lust to trample on a place Subaru could never allow to be violated — his "Sanctuary."
So, in order to forgive Echidna and agree to the meaningful discussion she spoke of, this was a demand Subaru needed to make.

"...Of all the things to say."

And in that instant, she must have understood the weakness and the stubbornness in Subaru's heart.
Echidna murmured, almost involuntarily, and then looked at Subaru, who was biting his lip, waiting for her answer. Slowly, twirling the tip of her white hair between her fingers, she said,

"Just as you wish, that was Carmilla the Witch of Lust running out of control. I tried to stop her, but she wouldn't listen to me. So, using the 'Trial' as a pretext, she tried to seduce you, exposing the part of you that you least wanted touched, and tried to drown you in it."

"――――"

"You nearly fell into the trap, but you escaped on your own strength. And in the moment Carmilla's guard slipped after failing to seduce you, you seized back the initiative and drew yourself to my castle. That we could face each other like this now — I'd call that a stroke of good fortune."

"――――"

"...Now, if I say things like that, will you be satisfied?"

Rattling off, in quick succession, the answers Subaru wanted to hear, only to betray them at the very end — that was Echidna.
Subaru silently lifted his gaze upward, taking Echidna's questioning face out of his field of view.

"...What were you trying to accomplish, setting the witch on me like that?"

"Didn't Carmilla tell you? That she wanted to save you — you, who were about to be worn down to nothing challenging the 'Trial.'"

"That wasn't — that wasn't the Witch of Lust's true feelings, was it. If what she said were true, that was nothing but the weakness I selfishly wished Rem would say to me. There's no reason for the Witch of Lust to feel goodwill toward me. ...That was your doing. Your instructions."

"To deduce that much from so little... Well, then, excuses won't work, will they."

Easily, Echidna dropped the pretense and shrugged. Then she lifted her own cup from the table and tilted it to her lips.

"Just as you worked out, it was on my instructions that I sent Carmilla, and that I had her pretend to be the girl inside your heart. As for the fact that her performance wasn't convincing enough and you saw through it — that's more Carmilla's problem than mine."

"...Why would you do something like that?"

"Put bluntly, you'll be angry. — Because it was the most efficient method, and above all, the one with the highest chance of success."

Without a hint of shame, Echidna continued before Subaru's expression, which had gone blank.

"That you'd be swallowed up by the second 'Trial' at this timing was unexpected even to me. And frankly, I'll admit that even the extent to which its content would pierce you so deeply — I hadn't anticipated that either, not until I actually saw the state you were in."

"――――"

"Oh, and I'd like you to overlook the fact that I was peeking in on the 'Trial.' I believe I mentioned this back at the first 'Trial' too, but this is a 'Trial' the witches prepared, isn't it? I don't much care to be scolded for the fact that it comes with such a mean-spirited punchline."

"...Keep going."

"In any case, watching you from the side as you faced the 'Trial,' I thought — if you kept challenging the 'Trial' alone, in this state, before long you'd wear yourself down to nothing... that's what I thought."

That wasn't an exaggerated opinion in the slightest — it was, in fact, likely true.
Subaru wasn't so blind to his own footing that he could deny it.
The second 'Trial' — the now that should never have been — being made to face it, being shown scene after scene, event after event, tragedy after tragedy. It had been more than enough to snap Subaru's bravado, his stubbornness, his misunderstandings, clean off at the root.

"So I intervened. Your wearing down to nothing is one possible outcome. I experiment, engaging in trial and error over everything. That's because the curiosity in me, seeking conclusions without ever growing tired, keeps crying out newborn. To satisfy that insatiable greed, I seek every possible outcome. — The outcome of you challenging the 'Trial' and breaking is no exception."

"Then why the hell did you intervene at all. If me breaking is one of the results you're after, you could've just left it alone. If the result you get is that I'm just that kind of guy, that's all I amount to — that'd satisfy you, wouldn't it?"

"There's certainly a perspective from which I'd accept that too, as one possible result. ...There is — but that doesn't mean I do nothing to produce the result I'm seeking. That's not what it means."

"What...?"

In response to Subaru's pressing, Echidna's voice dropped in tone.
Hearing it, for the first time in this scene, Subaru furrowed his brow for a reason other than anger.
If he swallowed the meaning of her words, if it took on a definite shape, then that meant—

"To reject the outcome of me wearing down and disappearing... you set up that whole situation? Is that what you're saying?"

"...As a result of that, there's no excuse for the fact that I violated your precious domain. So if you want to hurl abuse at me for it, I'll accept it and take it. Your anger is right, and my selfishness is wrong. That's all there is to it."

Setting her cup down on the table, Echidna looked straight at Subaru below the rise.
There was almost none of the playfulness or teasing emotion she'd shown until just a moment ago, or at any point before — the Witch of Greed faced Subaru with every last ounce of sincerity she could muster.
Her attitude, her posture, her words — they overwhelmed him.
The anger and distrust toward Echidna that had, until moments ago, filled his chest — words could hardly capture it — now began to feel dreadfully self-centered and selfish.

As a matter of fact, the resistance to putting into words that Echidna's help — the earlier situation — had truly been "help," still lingered stubbornly. But without it, what would have become of Subaru's heart?
It wasn't hard to imagine — on the cold floor of the crypt, his heart shattered, ground down, until even the faintest light vanished in the darkness, and he flickered out entirely.
He couldn't bring himself to voice thanks. But he couldn't think of her as someone deserving of his rage and abuse either. — That was where his feelings settled.

"――――"

Standing without a word, brushing the grass off his body, Subaru made his way up the rise.
Echidna, seated in her chair, watched Subaru approach, a faint trace of something pained crossing her expression. What words the approaching Subaru intended to throw at her — even a witch who had spent hundreds of years seemed unable to read it fully.
The very embodiment of the thirst for knowledge. The 'Witch of Greed.' That such an opponent could twist her expression like that, in front of him — it gave his heart, just now, a small measure of ease.

"—Ah."

Before Echidna, who let out a small sound of surprise, Subaru pulled out the chair and sat down across from her.
He made no move to bring the cup to his lips, but it was a declaration of intent — that he was taking a seat at the table for the discussion. To Echidna, watching him with uneasy eyes, Subaru propped his cheek on his hand, turning his face away, and said,

"I've got no intention of drinking Dona-tea. ...But I'll have that meaningful discussion with you."

And with that, swallowing down an unbearable emotion, he showed the magnanimity to comply.

"So, in the end, what exactly was that second 'Trial'?"

Still propping his cheek on his hand, Subaru asked without looking at Echidna.
In response, Echidna, across from him, shifted her chair to move diagonally in front of him, as if trying to enter his field of view, and said,

"What do you think it was?"

"You're not... trying to be evasive here, are you? Or is it just too convenient for me to expect a straight answer right off the bat?"

"I'm not thinking anything so mean-spirited. I did do something that made you angry, after all. Partly to confirm we can talk on friendly terms, I'd just like to hear a bit more of your voice."

That was a phrase with real force — the kind that ticklishly stirred the heart of whoever she was speaking with.
Had Subaru been in a normal state of mind, approaching this conversation with no particular tension, it surely would have flustered him, tangled his words. But given his current state of mind, there was no way he could give her the reaction she wanted, and Subaru answered only with a short sigh, briefly,

"The 'Trial' opened with 'Behold the now that should never have been.' That preface, and then I was shown that scene. ...The now that should never have been — that means the 'now' where I made a different choice somewhere along the path to where I stand now, right?"

As a way of thinking about it, it was the same as a game format like a visual novel.
A game where the story's content and possibilities branch based on the choices the player makes at key junctures. Speaking grandly, it wouldn't be wrong to say that life itself unfolds as that same kind of grand game.
A person facing some choice, choosing things of their own will — and as a result, the world beyond a branched possibility being wished for — that is, precisely, "life" itself.

"A world you should never be able to see. Perhaps it's a world far happier than the true 'now,' and you'd regret not having done things that way. Or perhaps it's a world far unhappier than the true 'now,' and you'd feel gratitude for the self you have here now. — The original purpose of the second 'Trial' was, by witnessing such a 'now' different from your own, to see whether you could affirm the 'now' that ought to be correct."

Picking up where Subaru left off, Echidna gave a brief outline of the second 'Trial.'
It matched almost exactly what Subaru had imagined the 'Trial' to be. Except for one thing — that its content had, for Subaru alone, pierced deep and sharp.

"—The different 'now' I saw... is that something that actually exists?"

"…………"

"When I die, I 'Return by Death' right there on the spot. So I've never once seen what happens after my own death. ...Before that, even — I'd never once thought the world might go on after I die. ...No, that's wrong. I made myself not think about it."

Well, of course.
The reason Subaru's 'Return by Death' happened was because the world had fallen into some hopeless dead end. He had endured the sensation of spending his life away by affirming that his 'Return by Death' existed in order to break that stalemate, to save the people precious to him, and for Subaru himself to reach the best possible future.

The existence of a world after death would overturn that premise of Subaru's from its very foundation.
For the sake of his own peace of mind, and above all by believing that "there is no world I've abandoned," Subaru had been able to save the people of the "abandoned world" that might exist.
So—

"Does the world go on after I die...? Does the world branch off with my choices, and in the world where I botched everything, dropped everything I couldn't hold onto — was everyone I couldn't protect still there...?"

"――――"

"Well, Echidna. ...Answer me."

Having lost the option of turning his face away, Subaru fixed a pleading gaze on Echidna, seated diagonally in front of him.
Echidna said nothing, and under Subaru's scrutiny, touched her own chin as if lost in thought, then closed her eyes once.

"There's one thing I have to explain about the 'Trial.'"

"…………"

"The second 'Trial,' the 'now' — is, at the end of the day, merely a phenomenon that shows you the scenery of a hypothetical world. The challenger undergoing the 'Trial'... in this case, that's you. It projects down to the finest details of your memory, and on top of that, the 'World's Memory' extracts the people, the world, the air, even the mana that form your surroundings, assembling the necessary information from the past, present, and future to give birth to that 'now.'"

"…………"

"In other words, that is, through and through, an extremely well-made 'unreality.' It's on a different level of fidelity from mere selfish imagination or delusion, and hypothetically, as a fact, 'such a thing' could occur. But it is, still, ultimately a 'fabricated unreality.' I can't nod along to the question of whether it truly happened."

"T-then..."

"However."

At Echidna's explanation, Subaru raised his head, finding a glimmer of hope. But Echidna held out her palm toward Subaru, who'd begun to see a light at the end, cutting off his words,

"The details of the mechanism behind your 'Return by Death' are unknown. It's beyond doubt that your 'Return by Death' is the work of the 'Witch of Envy's' hand, but exactly how the 'Witch of Envy' makes you 'Return by Death' remains an endless question. It might be a power that, triggered by your 'death,' rewinds the entire world. Or it might be that there's a parallel world — another world whose existence we can't confirm — and it takes the form of overwriting the 'you' there with 'you.'"

"Ah..."

"If it's operating on the latter principle, then a parallel world exists, and in that world, even after your death, a world without 'you' continues on, just as you saw."

"I-Is there any way to confirm..."

"—No."

To Subaru, his words trembling, Echidna declared, mercilessly, and shook her head.
Subaru's eyes went wide, his mouth hanging open, struck speechless. Echidna turned a gaze of something like sympathy on him, and tapped the edge of the table with her fingertip.

"If there's just one way to confirm it, it would be to hear it from the 'Witch of Envy' herself. But you yourself have already felt firsthand how difficult that is, haven't you?"

What Echidna meant was, presumably, the memory of when Subaru had actually come face to face with the 'Witch of Envy.' At the end of the tea party, the 'Witch of Envy' who had greeted Subaru when he stepped outside the crypt.
The genuine monster who had stolen Emilia's body, torn Garfiel limb from limb, and swallowed the 'Sanctuary' itself in shadow. — Suddenly, he recalled his doubts about the manifestation of that being's existence, and its circumstances.

"That's right... Echidna. Before, after this tea party ended... outside, in the 'Sanctuary,' I saw the witch. What was that? What was it?"

"It's obvious enough, but that was the 'Witch of Envy.' Though, it was a far cry from the genuine article — a mere imitation. The body it chose as a vessel was immature, and above all, not a single seal had been undone. With the Witch Factor incomplete, there's no way it could exercise the power of its prime."

"That, and it's still far from its prime...?!"

Even a monster that hadn't so much as spared a glance at a beastified Garfiel, killing everything without so much as a scratch, wasn't even comparable to the true 'Witch of Envy,' apparently.
Four hundred years ago, in the age when that witch had truly walked the earth — just how much of a hell had that been?

"As you imagined, the trigger for it stepping outside was this tea party. Here, even that thing can't bind you with the taboo. So, driven mad by envy, it tried to vent outside the frustration it couldn't release inside, throwing a tantrum and going on a rampage."

"You knew that would happen, didn't you?"

"I didn't know. This was a first. Only after it actually happened did I arrive at the hypothesis that, ah, that's probably what it was. Not being able to reach a conclusion until I've actually witnessed something with my own eyes — even I, the Witch of Greed, am no different from the rest of you in that respect."

"――――"

Subaru lost his words before Echidna, who never once let go of her stance as a bystander. Blaming her wouldn't accomplish anything. Even knowing that, there was something maddening about it.
If she felt like it, if she felt like cooperating with Subaru, maybe—

"As for choosing your beloved as the vessel, I don't think there was much of a reason there either. Being a fellow Half-Elf might have made it somewhat easier to blend in, but as a major reason, I'd say it's nothing other than 'envy.'"

"Envy...?"

"From the perspective of a witch who wants to monopolize your feelings, is it really so strange that someone you devote your heart to so fervently would be hated, would be something she'd want to destroy?"

To love someone with madness is to also wish to be loved by them in equal measure. And if that someone's love isn't directed at oneself, then turning to madness in order to redirect its aim toward oneself — that too is something the toxic substance called 'love' can bring about.
So is that why the 'Witch of Envy' keeps behaving as though she were the very embodiment of it?

"Everything you're agonizing over — only the 'Witch of Envy' knows the answer."

"――――"

"No matter how much you turn it over in your mind, honestly, you won't arrive at an answer. That scene that drove you to the brink, and above all, the matter of the 'now that may or may not exist' — no conclusion will come."

"That... that can't be..."

There was no other way to describe it, for Subaru, than as a cruel reality.
He'd wanted her to deny it outright. That the world after death Subaru had seen didn't exist.
If it was no good, he'd wanted her to state it plainly. That his self-righteousness had cost the lives of many.

Whichever answer she gave, Subaru surely could have taken the answer that emerged and turned it into a warning, a stake driven into the ground, something never to forget, and gritted his teeth, wept tears of blood, wailed with his very soul — and still, somehow, stepped forward.
—And yet, an answer that says no answer exists — wasn't that too cruel?

Was he being told to live without either affirmation or denial, leaving the matter of the world dangling in midair?
Without even knowing whether what he'd trampled had truly been trampled. Without even being allowed the awareness that what he'd abandoned had truly been abandoned. Was not letting him recognize a sin as a sin itself the punishment meted out to Subaru?
Had Natsuki Subaru committed a sin so unforgivable that no one could forgive it?

No one could judge Subaru. No one could condemn him. He'd known that much already.
—And yet, was he not to be allowed to do even that to himself?

"I do think it's cruel. But I think you have no choice but to accept it and move on."

To Subaru, beaten down, wordless, Echidna offered those words.
Sluggishly, Subaru lifted his head, looking at Echidna, who had spoken, with hollow eyes.
Receiving Subaru's gaze, Echidna drew a breath, and then, with a serious expression,

"To put the second 'Trial' in the most extreme terms — it's about accepting 'now' as the only thing there is, and resolving to treat any 'now' other than 'now' as belonging to some other world, forever out of reach."

"――――"

"In reality, for you — who has reason, stronger than any other challenger, to be conscious of the possibility that it might exist — this will be harsh. Even so, you have to shift your thinking."

"Shift...?"

"It's possible that your choices truly have come with a great many sacrifices. Among the things you've left behind up until now, there were probably many that can never be recovered. But isn't it a sad thing, an empty thing, a painful thing, to do nothing but count what you've left behind, what you've lost?"

"If it's just some spiritual pep talk, I'm not interested. ...Sorry to say it like this, but is this really something ordinary counseling can fix?"

Echidna's words were pleasant to the ear, something like comfort.
If the wound had been shallow, if the sin committed had been light, if it had been something that could be viewed with a bit more generosity — they might have had some effect.
Or he might have felt saved by them, might really have managed that "shift."
But—

"The reality that the debt for what I've done can't be settled — that doesn't change no matter what. The reality that everything I've spent could be built on sacrifices I mistakenly thought didn't exist, thought could be erased — that possible reality doesn't change."

"...That's true."

"In that situation, how am I supposed to affirm myself? What am I supposed to do to forgive myself? You tried to offer me salvation, and I threw it back in your face. I don't want to be saved by a fake Rem. I'll take back the real Rem myself, eventually — but—"

Catching his breath, Subaru's face twisted into a wreck, and,

"—The Rem I take back someday, someday — is she really the Rem I want to save?"

"――――"

"Until that answer comes, the walls closing in on my heart from every side don't change. ...Are you saying even that — I can just deal with, accept and move on from, that's what you're telling me?"

"――――"

"Count what I saved instead of what I couldn't save, and keep on living... is that what you're telling me to do?"

What Echidna wanted to say to Subaru, beyond that, was some form of hope.
Words that, for Subaru too, might once have become hope.
—Except that the darkness Subaru had sunk into wasn't shallow enough for him to see it as hope.

"With some ordinary spiritual platitude like that, you're telling me to... keep fighting, is that it...!"

"—Yes, that's what I'm telling you."

"――――"

"I am telling you that."

Brushing aside her own comforting words, Echidna said this to Subaru, raising his voice from the depths of despair.
Slowly, as though chewing each word to make sure it went down, Echidna looked straight at Subaru and said it.

"Rather than count the many things you may not have been able to save, you should count the many things you were able to save. I've been watching that, through the path that's brought you here, to this point, now."

"What... what have I... what of me have you..."

"To get here, I've watched you fight to live with everything you had, with all your heart, in your own way. So I can say it. I can say it."

"――――"

"There wasn't a single wasted thing in the path you've walked to get here. No one has the right to call your all-out effort 'not enough.' You threw everything you had, everything you could give, into walking to this very moment. — That's something to be proud of."

Echidna's earnest words struck Subaru's hollow chest. Inside the emptiness, something resounded. — But it wasn't enough. Words like that alone wouldn't let him stand back up.
Even being told it was something to be proud of, it was a fact that Subaru had dropped so much along the way. He should have been able to do something about it. Someone who wasn't Subaru, under the same conditions, would have done far better. And yet, because it was Subaru who'd been there, so much had gone unsaved.
That was Subaru's sin. Subaru's crime. A sin Subaru himself had to acknowledge, and atone for.

"No one can forgive me."

"I'll forgive you. I, who know it, will."

"No one can judge me."

"I'll judge you. I, who know your sin, will."

"—No one can affirm me."

"If you can't affirm yourself, then I'll deny that unforgivable self of yours."

"――――"

"If you affirm your own sin, then I will deny your sin."

Echidna, refusing to back down, tried to sweep away every single one of Subaru's words.
Why was this witch denying Subaru's sin so fiercely?
Why did this witch keep trying, over and over, to sweep away the darkness in Subaru's heart?

"Why do you... try so hard to do something about me, like that?"

"...Making a girl say that out loud herself is a little too cruel."

Echidna, who had never once faltered until then, hesitated slightly here for the first time.
And, her face faintly flushed, Echidna cleared her throat and said,

"—Won't you make a Contract with me, Natsuki Subaru?"

It was a quiet voice, yet one that made you feel a strong will behind it.
At those words, Subaru blinked, and it took time for the meaning to sink in, to permeate his mind.

"C-contract...?"

"Yes, a Contract. A formal Contract, with the 'Witch of Greed' — won't you make one with me?"

"And if I make it... if I make it, what happens?"

"It's simple. — From now on, whenever you run into some hopeless obstacle, I'll rack my brain over that wall together with you. When you want someone's words, I'll make the effort to give you back the words you want. When you feel like you're about to be crushed under the weight of your own sins, I'll help you push back against the sin that's about to crush you."

She said it all in one breath, and then Echidna wore something like a bashful smile.

"Won't you make that kind of Contract with me?"

"...Aren't you dead? Aren't you supposed to be unable to interfere with reality?"

"I would be overstepping the domain of the dead. It's a bit late to say so at this point, and even so — I think, now, it wouldn't be so bad. ...If you would allow it."

Her hand pressed to her own chest, Echidna's words, spoken with her head bowed, made Subaru's eardrums tremble. The tremor conducted through his body, gradually taking on heat, spreading through the flow of his blood to every corner of him.
Sensation returned to his fingertips, which had been complaining of numbness.
His parched, dried-up tongue regained its motion with a faint moisture, and his eyes, which had forgotten to blink, filled with something warm as if forgetting their thirst too.

He was at a loss over how to respond to the hand held out, to the offer, to the proposal, to the words of assistance.
He had vowed to keep struggling on, had nearly lost sight of the meaning of that vow — and the witch was telling him she would support him.

"Not to brag, but I have confidence in the sheer volume of my knowledge. I believe I can provide a countermeasure for most problems you'll face, and no matter how absurd the circumstances that befall you, unlike the people around you, I won't need to spend effort persuading you of anything. Above all, I can understand your 'Return by Death.'"

"Rattling that off so fast — are you seriously giving me your sales pitch right now?"

"If you make a Contract with me, I think it's only natural, as the one asking, to lay out the benefits beforehand. If this lets me buy even a little of your peace of mind, wouldn't that count as a benefit too?"

Taking Subaru's own words and turning even those into her own selling points — Echidna. At this side of the witch, one he'd never seen before, Subaru couldn't help but loosen his cheeks into a small smile.
He felt the air slip peacefully from his lungs, and let out an "ah" of a sigh.

Bathing the nape of his neck in the meadow breeze, he leaned back against his chair and looked up at the sky.
In the fabricated blue sky, he could see white clouds swimming.

When he hit a dead end, when he lost sight of the answer, when he faced hardship.
—If he could keep exchanging words like this, under a blue sky, in search of a conclusion—

"...Maybe that wouldn't be so bad, after all."

"—Does that mean?"

Echidna, rising from her chair with a clatter, her fists clenched as if without meaning to, looked down at Subaru. At Subaru's gaze, still resting against the chair back, looking up at her, Echidna's complexion changed as if embarrassed by her own reaction,

"Ah, no... well, yes, if you truly insist, then making that kind of Contract would..."

"Too late to cover it up now. I mean, it's not even me who asked, it's you who... no, wait, in this case, is it too uncool of me to point that out?"

Though it was Echidna's proposal, it was, in the end, meant to save Subaru's heart.
Put bluntly, this was a witch's mercy. That Subaru wasn't being made to cling to it as such was, without question, thanks to Echidna's own consideration toward him.
Was he to be saved like this, endlessly, by anyone at all?

Rocking forward with the momentum of pushing off the chair back, Subaru stood up.
Standing at a distance he could reach with an outstretched hand, Echidna, looking slightly up now that Subaru had risen a bit higher, wore an anxious expression.
Every little gesture — he thought, what a calculatedly charming witch.
And given how much he owed his salvation to her, there was no position from which he could say a word against it.

"How exactly does one go about making a Contract?"

"—If we're to form a formal Contract, I'll connect a path between you and me. I'll handle the finer arrangements on my end... for now, just your palm."

Echidna lifted her right hand, turning her white palm toward Subaru.
The meaning, presumably, was to press his own palm against hers in the same way.

Subaru looked at the witch before him, whose mouth was faintly, unable to hide her joy, twitching into a grin, and felt the fight go out of him. He let out a small "heh" of a breath, and,

"I wonder if this'll change the winds around here a little..."

With that, no small amount of hope for the future woven into the words, he brought his palm to meet hers—

An impact.

The sound of something splitting apart rang out, and right beside Subaru, the white table with the cup on it burst apart.
The shock that shattered the table traveled through the ground, carving a crater of destruction into the earth, the rumbling and tremors shaking violently beneath his feet — and Subaru couldn't help but cry out in shock.

"—I'll put a stop to that Contract."

Slamming her fist into the ground, a girl with golden hair and blue eyes declared, magnificent and imperious.
—The 'Witch of Wrath' glared at the two of them, her eyes burning with fierce anger.
