Arc 2 Chapter 22: "Words Can't Express It"

"――――!!"

He couldn't perceive the moment consciousness returned.
A ringing in his ears like a downpour crashing on and on beside him, his vision flickering between red and white in alternation, the world warped and tilted at a nauseating angle.

Sensation didn't reach his limbs; his throat, in agony as though his innards were being wrung out, was letting loose a scream too thick to even register as thought.
He twisted the body that wouldn't obey him, made it thrash, and with every part of himself that could still move he converted the incomprehensible surge of emotion welling up in his chest into destruction.

He had no idea what was happening.
Where had the pain gone? Why was his consciousness so unclear? Where were the wounds that had ravaged his whole body just now? Blood draining away, life draining away, himself dying.

He didn't want to die. Didn't want to die didn't want to die didn't want to die didn't want to die didn't want to die didn't want to die didn't want to die didn't want to die didn't want to die.
The pain, the agony, the suffering, the sorrow, the fear — he hated all of it, every bit.
He wanted to push it all away, but that was the only thing within his reach. So, giving himself over to the impulse, he struck at those negative feelings indiscriminately, crushed them, tore them apart, flung them away, cast them aside, trying to protect his own heart.

"――――!"

He heard something. Someone's voice.
Mixed in with his own beastlike howling, he heard someone's voice, clinging to him desperately.

The meaning didn't get through. He didn't understand the meaning. He didn't want to understand the meaning.
There was no point in listening. Listening would only get him hurt. Nothing would change even if he listened. Nothing changed no matter what he heard.

And yet, little by little, the color of the world grew clearer.
Blood returned to the tips of his hands and feet, and the sensations of his flailing body began properly reaching his nerves.
His arms, which had been thrashing wildly, seemed to have struck something hard again and again — his nails were split, the backs of his hands torn open, bleeding badly.

A sharp pain stabbed into his brain, and his wailing eased, if only slightly, in intensity.
And then he noticed. Something warm was wrapped around his aching arm, clinging to it just above the right elbow, as if to hold it still.
There was a similar sensation at his legs. Something small was pressing down from directly above, pinning down the legs he kept swinging up, again and again, to kick at the bed.

Color returned to his vision. Before his eyes spread a white ceiling he recognized from having seen it several times before.
From the blood flowing back through him and the weight pressing on his back, he realized he was lying on his back. The soft feel beneath him was the bed — he'd been lying there with his limbs thrown out.
And the ones who had been using their whole bodies to restrain his thrashing hands and feet in that position were—

"Guest, Guest. Have you calmed down now?"
"Guest, Guest. Are you completely done losing your mind?"

The instant those two familiar voices struck his eardrums, Subaru's brain stopped thinking entirely and settled on one single response: a scream.

For Subaru, this made the fourth "first morning" at Roswaal Manor overall, and it opened in the worst way it ever had.

Already six times now, Subaru had lost his life in this world and been dragged back.
None of those deaths had ever been easy, and each time, he'd tasted the agony of watching what he'd gained slip once more through his fingers.
Neither the pain nor the suffering ever grew any more bearable, and the loneliness and sense of loss that came crashing back with each return was nothing he could share with anyone.

Even so, gritting his teeth through it all, Subaru had meant to keep facing forward, doing his best to live. He'd resolved that his knees, at least, would not buckle — that his resolve, at least, would not lose.
But this time was beyond comparison to anything before.

The sense of loss, the loneliness, the pain, the suffering — all of it, in proportion to every bond he'd built across these days, carved into Subaru's heart deeper and sharper than ever.
There was no way he could stand. He didn't even have the will to try. He couldn't even summon a reason why he should have to. That was the state he was in.

"—There, done. The wound should be closed up, but don't be too rough, okay? If it starts bleeding again, call for me right away."

Gently stroking the back of Subaru's right hand, the girl seated in the chair beside the bed smiled at him.
Silver hair bundled into one, a faint blue light wrapped around her palm — it was Emilia.
Right after he'd woken up thrashing and torn his hand open, she had rushed over and healed it with her magic.

The only two people in the room were Subaru and Emilia.
The other two who had been present the moment he woke had, immediately after realizing what state he was in, bowed their heads and vanished from sight.

"I heard from Ram and Rem — you said something awful to them, didn't you?"

At the mention of a name he didn't want to hear, Subaru's head snapped up as if jerked by a string.
Emilia's eyes widened slightly at the overreaction, but seeing the confusion cross Subaru's face, she pursed her lips and continued,

"They were down about it, saying maybe they'd been rude to you or something — which is unusual for them. If you see them again, say something to smooth it over, okay?"

"Something rude, huh. Nah. There wasn't anything. ...There was nothing at all between me and those two."

His voice came out flat and hoarse, tossed off with a bitterness that made Emilia's fine eyebrows knit together slightly.
Even seeing that, no apology, no comfort came from Subaru's mouth.
Instead, what slipped out was a question that bordered on sarcasm.

"Hey, Emilia-tan... you don't think I'm in the way, do you?"

"Tan— you, seriously..."

For a moment Emilia showed her usual flicker of resistance to the nickname, but she noticed that Subaru's expression, as he asked, hadn't lost its seriousness for even a moment.
She thought it over in silence for a while, then said "Not at all," prefacing it before raising a finger.

"Why would I think you're in the way? Besides, Subaru, you're the one who saved my life, remember? If the person I owe my life to just up and disappeared without me ever paying that debt back, I'd have nightmares about it. So, out of my own stubborn pride if nothing else, you're not allowed to disappear until I've repaid you."

She said it quickly, as if trying to cover up some feeling or another.
Watching her intently, Subaru studied her every gesture. Was there any falseness in it? Any reaction, any motion, that suggested she was hiding something?

"...Hey, come on, seriously..."

Catching himself scrutinizing her reaction with suspicious eyes, Subaru muttered aloud, unable to hide his disappointment and disgust with himself.
And hadn't Emilia just said it herself, without meaning to? That if he couldn't even trust the person who'd saved his life, that was about as low as a person could sink.

Emilia was supposed to be the one oasis Subaru could rest his heart on, in this world with no one else to rely on.
The other possibilities — people who, even if not quite to her degree, might once have offered him the same — had already vanished from before Subaru's eyes, mercilessly, one after another.

If, even now, he lost his absolute trust in her too, then for Subaru that would mean nothing less than groping his way forward through total darkness.

A thought suddenly crossed his mind.
What if he confided in her — of all people — the despair occupying his chest, the situation he'd been challenging over and over, redoing again and again?

Thinking back, until now Subaru had struggled through every brush with death that had come his way, again and again, entirely on his own, trying to somehow manage it by himself.
But things had grown so dire that neither pressing forward nor retreating offered him any easy option anymore.
If so, then wasn't confiding in a third party — someone he could trust — and asking for their help exactly the best move he could make, the one clever stroke left to reach for?

As if fog were clearing, the anxiety and confusion that had been thick in Subaru's mind began to blow away.
Once he'd noticed it, it seemed so self-evidently right a course of action that he couldn't understand why he hadn't considered it before.

"—Emilia-tan, there's something I want you to hear."

Lowering his voice, Subaru turned to face her fully.
She must have sensed the change in atmosphere. Emilia, too, let tension rise on her troubled face, straightened herself in her chair, and looked straight at him.

Watching himself reflected in her violet eyes, Subaru began slowly, choosing his first words.
Where should he start? With Return by Death? Or should he lay it all bare from the very premise that he wasn't even a being of this world to begin with?

It was the kind of story that was likely to be laughed off, far more likely to be taken as a joke. And yet — wouldn't she, of all people, be the one not to dismiss his earnest plea?
That expectation, that hope, was everything holding Subaru up right now.

—Let's start with Return by Death. And then, if possible, I want to ask you to lend me your strength.

Ashamed of his own shamelessness — asking yet again of the person he already owed so much — Subaru held his breath and began forming his first word. From there, things would start to move.
From the depths of confusion, from a situation so hopeless he'd been on the verge of giving up entirely — that was where the light would finally break through.

—Or so he thought.

"――――"

The instant he meant to put it into words, meant to shape his voice, it happened.

A wrongness. Feeling it, Subaru hesitated over his first word in confusion.
Something's off, he thought, and immediately realized why. Sound. The sound had vanished. All at once, sound had disappeared from the world. The pounding of his own heartbeat, which he'd been hearing just moments ago. Emilia's faint breathing, right in front of him. The morning breeze slipping in through the slightly opened window, coloring the stillness that circled the room.

All of it had vanished completely from the world.
A world without sound — it was a space so utterly cut off that even the word "silence" felt too tepid for it. Even the ringing in his ears had been wiped away; even the sound of blood flowing through his own body was inaudible — an absolute, non-negotiable silence.
Just from that alone, a chill ran through his entire body, and an unbearable unease churned violently through his insides.
And that was only the beginning of the abnormality.

—What... is this?

From a world stripped of sound, next the motion of every single thing vanished.
Time stretched out; an instant became eternity; a single second's destination shifted vastly, far off into the distance of time.
Before his eyes, Emilia's expression remained fixed in its serious cast, unmoving. Her eyelids didn't so much as twitch; her lips stayed pressed firmly shut.
Emilia, poised and composed, had her next movement pushed eternally out of reach. And the same was true of Subaru — the feelings he had meant to voice, left as they were now, would never reach anyone, not for all eternity.

Sound vanished, time stopped, and Subaru's fervent wish was pushed further and further out of reach.
A phenomenon beyond comprehension that had arrived without warning. His body, its nerves unresponsive, couldn't move so much as a fraction — Subaru could do nothing but let his thoughts race on, carrying his confusion along with them.

Incomprehensible. Incomprehensible. Incomprehensible. Incomprehensible. Incomprehensible—.

What had caused this? He didn't even have the mental room to guess.
All he could do was scream out his grief inside his own head, battered down by this incomprehensible realm closing in on him wave after wave.

And then, that sensation's end revealed itself, abruptly.

—What is it?

A voice of suspicion rose in his mind, and Subaru tilted his head — an action that existed only as an image, in thought alone.
At the end of his gaze, within that fixed, unmoving field of vision, it could only be described as having appeared out of nowhere.

A black haze. To Subaru, who didn't even have the freedom to blink, its appearance was, in the truest sense of the word, sudden.
As if intruding upon his very consciousness, It hung there, drifting in place.

What made it especially strange was that only this black haze slowly, gradually changed shape.

In a world where everything else had come to a standstill, only the black haze shifted form at a gentle pace. About the size that could be cupped in both hands, the mist's outline grew clearer bit by bit, and after what felt, subjectively, like a dozen-odd seconds, it finally arrived at its intended shape.

—To Subaru, it looked like a black palm.

The shape-shifted haze had taken the form of an arm.
A floating black arm, equipped with five fingers, no longer than the length from elbow to fingertip.
And then, as though testing itself, It trembled its fingertips, and with unmistakable intent, closed in on Subaru.
Powerless to do anything but watch, Subaru, by sensation alone, involuntarily held his breath.

The black fingertips slipped into Subaru's chest as though nothing stood in their way. And then, unmistakably, Subaru felt those fingertips stroke his very organs.
They touched his innards, traced along his ribs, and the palm pressed further still, until at last its fingertips reached the single most vital organ in the human body.

—Hey, wait.

A voice that could not become sound. A body that could not resist. Unable to so much as flinch, with no idea what the black palm intended, terror tore through the whole of Subaru's being.

—This is seriously, no joking mat—

Before he could finish the thought inside his chest, an impact shook Subaru's very existence to its foundation.
Is there anyone who can explain why it hurts when your internal organs are injured? The answer is simple: there's no need to even think about it.
In that instant, the agonizing pain that assailed Subaru needed no explanation whatsoever.
Purely, simply, the pain of his heart being mercilessly crushed in a grip kept striking him down, again and again.

He couldn't raise his voice. He wasn't even permitted to shudder against the pain.
With no way to distract himself from it, with nothing to feel but the sheer pain of feeling nothing but pain — who else, besides Subaru in this very instant, could possibly understand it?
There was only agony there. And that agony, dragging along with it something more than mere agony, left behind for Subaru a warning so achingly kind it nearly brought him to tears.

Subaru's very existence was torn apart by the pain, his consciousness warped as if handled roughly and carelessly, his thoughts shredded to ribbons until he could no longer even recall their original shape—.


"—Subaru?"

"—Ah?"

"Subaru, are you okay? You suddenly went quiet — you're worrying me."

Her hand resting on his knee, that silver-haired beauty gazed at Subaru with concern welling in her eyes.
The moment he registered that, Subaru let out a long, deflating breath. Then he confirmed that his fingers moved the way he willed them to, and, belatedly, touched his own chest, confirming from the outside that his heart was keeping a quiet, steady beat.

His body moved. His voice worked. There was no pain in his heart at all.

But the fear had been carved into him.
His fingertips trembled, his voice refused to come out, and his body stayed hunched, small and cowering.

In proportion to the hope that had been rising in him, what It had brought was despair enough to crush Subaru utterly.
The moment he even thought, in some corner of his mind, of mustering his courage again to face It once more, the illusion of a black haze seemed to flicker at the edge of his vision.

And at last, Subaru had no choice but to accept it.
As the despair welling up inside him overwhelmed him, he covered his face with his hands and curled up small on the bed. Emilia, confusion showing on her face, opened her mouth to speak.

"W-what's wrong? You've really been acting strange since a while ago. If something's going on..."

"—I have a favor to ask."

Cutting off her worried voice, Subaru held up a hand toward her.
He couldn't lift his face. He was sure he looked awful. If he looked at her now, in this state of mind, he couldn't trust himself not to say something he'd regret.

Marshaling every last bit of self-control he had, Subaru forced out just a single line.
Throwing away every word he'd meant to say, every feeling he'd wanted her to hear, every surge of emotion he'd genuinely wished to confide — all of it, abandoned.

"Emilia-tan."

"Y-yeah. Tell me anything."

"...Don't worry about me."

He said only that, weakly, and without even waiting to see her reaction, collapsed back onto the bed.
Unconsciously, his hand came to rest on his chest, and as he felt the pulse beneath it through his thin skin, Subaru clearly, fully understood the reality now forced upon him.

—He was not permitted to confide in anyone.

—Subaru would have to keep struggling on, alone, all by himself.
