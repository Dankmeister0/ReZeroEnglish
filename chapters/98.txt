Arc 3 Chapter 22: "The Final Master and Servant"

"Help me, please! I don't wanna die in a place like this! Felt! Hurry up, save me! Tell them, tell these thickheaded fools!"

Raising his voice, spraying spittle, shoulders trembling, the old man's begging for his life went on and on.
The only sound echoing through the hushed hall was that old man's unsightly wailing, and it was rapidly dragging the atmosphere of the chamber down into something heavy and stagnant.

Most of the knights and civil officials turned eyes toward the old man that carried an indelible disgust, and even the members of the Council of Wise Men, who were probably close to him in age, mostly wore expressions of having witnessed something distasteful.
It had to be said that those who found any meaning beyond that in the old man's cries, in his pleading for his life, were a minority in this chamber.

"What's wrong with you all!? Who do you think kept you alive all this time!? Or did you forget all you owe just because you got a taste of the good life, huh, well!?"

Pressing on, the old man kept piling up his shameless, self-serving demands with a base sort of insistence. While everyone else avoided commenting on his attitude, Reinhard kept his eyes fixed on the profile of the girl he called his liege.
To her, the old man's transformation was drastic enough to be called a complete change of character. Their acquaintance was too shallow for Reinhard to fully grasp what she felt, but it wasn't hard to imagine that no small amount of shock had struck her.

Reinhard, having gotten some vague sense of the old man's true intentions, judged that he had to act to break the deadlock somehow, and moved to step forward—

"—Don't move, Reinhard. I'd advise against trying anything strange."

A girl's voice cut off his motion just as he was about to take that first step, checking him before he could begin.
*A blunder,* Reinhard thought, lifting his gaze toward the source—the red-haired youth found the girl who'd thrown those words at him wearing a smile of pure arrogance and disdain.
Holding a fan drawn from within her own bosom to her lips, her eyes lit with a sadistic gleam as she pierced Reinhard with her gaze, Priscilla tilted her head, both charming and lascivious, and said,

"That won't do at all, Reinhard. A knight of your caliber, flustered? With an attitude like that, it looks for all the world as though you're trying to silence that old wretch before he says something inconvenient."

Shrugging her shoulders with a mock "how frightening, how frightening," Priscilla's words made Reinhard inwardly gnash his teeth at his own blunder—*I've been had.*
What effect the words and conduct of the old man calling himself Cromwell would have on Felt, Reinhard didn't know. But he had been trying to preempt a situation that was difficult to interpret favorably, and in turn he'd been preempted himself, pinned down before he could move.

With that single line, Priscilla had reshaped how everyone present in the hall would interpret and weigh the old man's words. And now, even if Reinhard moved to deny the content of those claims, it would never again be judged without the preconception that he was "trying to conceal an inconvenient truth."

With a single utterance, Priscilla had completely remade the stage to her own advantage.
Without even fully knowing the background of the people she was facing, she had seen through their relationship from nothing more than the barest of exchanges just now, and had steered the situation accordingly.

Reinhard, now unable to move carelessly, stood before her. Priscilla, nodding in satisfaction, snapped her fan shut with a click and turned its tip back toward the old man.

"Now then, continue your begging. Dance yourself into a frenzy—unsightly, pitiful, ridiculous—until you drop, and amuse me. I permit it. I shall not stop you. Do your utmost, old wretch. Depending on how hard you try, I shall judge whether to grant that unbearable litany of yours."

"A detestable little chit, talking so high and mighty. ...Felt, you're different, ain'tcha? You've always been a kind child. You wouldn't abandon me, would you? We've gotten along, all this time, haven't we?"

Spitting at Priscilla's haughty manner, the old man still clung, with a fawning smile, to the mercy of a girl he claimed long acquaintance with.
Those around them shuddered at the old man's recklessness—not knowing what it meant to displease Priscilla—but Priscilla herself, ostensibly treated with such contempt, only loosened her lips in amusement, watching the continuation of what she'd called his unsightly pleading.

Having watched the exchange play out, the people around them seemed to come back to their senses, and began murmuring their impressions of the moment—that is, of the old man's pitiful pleading—in hushed voices.

"Did you see that, how unsightly he was?"
"Worse than that, the way he fawned. It kills any sympathy I might have felt. That's the very definition of a thief crying louder than his victim."
"Even if Lady Felt shields him, releasing him unpunished is out of the question."

The knights, one after another, condemned the shameless attitude of an old man who, having committed a crime, still begged to be let off—

"Is that the sort of person one finds in the slums... And Lady Felt was raised there?"
"Even supposing the story of her being a surviving royal relative is true, could someone who spent their life in a place like that possibly fulfill the duties of a monarch...?"
"Perhaps we ought to reconsider after all. Or else, in accordance with the Dragon History Stone, simply treat her as a candidate in name only, kept on merely to round out the numbers."

The civil officials began to raise questions about Felt's qualifications, given her apparently deep connection to a man like that.
Gradually the murmurs grew louder, spreading into a stir that filled the entire hall.

Watching the reaction around him, Reinhard bit his lip, his inward fears made real. He had foreseen this happening.
And on top of that, he had let the old man's outburst slip by unchecked and been neatly checked by Priscilla's warning. As a result, he'd failed to stop it, and the two of them—their interests aligned—had used the situation exactly as they pleased.

Cursing his own inadequacy, Reinhard gazed at Felt, who remained silent.
Head bowed, still holding her tongue—what was the girl thinking?

"——!"
"——!!"
"——!"

Even while the parties directly involved held their tongues, the voices from the sidelines kept swelling, forgetting all restraint.
Unable to bear the situation's clumsy deterioration any further, Marcos drew a deep breath, resolved that he really ought to shout them down. And then—

"—All of you, shut your damn mouths!!"

It wasn't Marcos's famous bellow, the one known for making his subordinates' spines quiver.
It was a shrill, foul-mouthed, unbearable shout from a young girl.

A hush fell first from sheer surprise, and then silence dropped over the hall.
At that, the girl, breathing hard, stepped forward into the center, sweeping her gaze over every face present, and said,

"People keep quiet for two seconds and you lot start chirping like a bunch of birds. Grown-ass men acting all whiny—pathetic. Bunch of gutless wonders."

"Lady Felt, that manner of speech is, however you look at it—"

"Shut it, blue-balls knight. You got put in your place by a pregnant lady and now you wanna lecture me? Actually, don't talk to me even when that's not the case. I don't like you."

Reinhard had tried to caution her on her unladylike language, but Felt's response was merciless and utterly without refinement.
Still, an order was an order, and Reinhard bowed deeply and withdrew from the spot. Watching him go, Felt swung the hem of her yellow dress and rolled her neck roughly, saying,

"All you overgrown-brain types whispering behind your hands—that's exactly the kinda thing that pisses me off. You paid money to study, paid money to buy your position, and that's why you're standing there, right? Then act like it. Standing there lookin' pale like you haven't had a proper meal in your life."

Sweeping her gaze over the faces of the civil officials, Felt cut them down flat with that assessment. Then her head turned the other way, and unease rippled through the Royal Guard knights under her stare.

"And then there's you lot, all dressed up so fine, scared outta your minds by some little runt like me. Don't even try that excuse about how it's not fear, just being on guard—same difference. The question is whether an attitude like that isn't pathetic."

Snorting at the guards' expense, Felt walked on and reached her destination: right in front of the old man who had spent this whole time making an unsightly spectacle of himself. There, the towering old man and the small girl, with him kneeling, finally stood at an average height difference for a confrontation.
The girl put both hands on her hips and swept her gaze once more over the people she'd just mocked, then said,

"Now then, take a look at this geezer's awful mug. On top of getting punched and having a bloody nose, there's like a mountain of botched shaving cuts on him. Mirrors are a luxury item, y'know, and decent sharp blades aren't exactly common either. And here he is, grinning all servile like this—one word for it: gross."

"Now hold on, that's a bit much, ain't it..."

Lowering his tone at the harsh assessment, the old man let out a somewhat deflated mutter. But he quickly seemed to shake himself and pasted the fawning smile back on, resuming the act.
Watching him do it, Felt let her head droop with a heavy sigh, and said,

"And on top of all that, there was that begging just now. Honestly, listening to it just made all the strength drain outta my shoulders. Pathetic, servile, couldn't stand to watch. ...Hey, Old Man Rom."

The old man raised his face at her call. The girl, her red eyes meeting his, held a trace of sorrow as she said,

"Us slum folk, yeah, we're hopelessly pathetic. Looked down on from above, of course we're gonna live like scum—me included, we were all rotten to the core. Really, it's an awful place."

Handing down that harsh assessment, herself included, Felt drew a breath and went on with a "but—"

"Sure, it's a fact the place was a dumping ground for hopeless bottom-of-the-barrel types. But we managed to hold onto our pride as human beings, at least, right? No matter how low people looked down on us, we never actually pressed our foreheads to the ground."

"Felt..."

"I wanna show Old Man Rom a mirror right now so he can see his own face. Servile, unsightly, wagging your tail and begging to be allowed to keep living—I ain't gonna call that living."

The old man called the girl's name like a sigh, and the girl shook her head and answered him so.
At that answer, Crusch, who had been standing quietly in the line of candidates, nodded gravely. Felt's words just now resonated with the principles she herself preached.

"If you're gonna beg me for your life, that's the wrong way to go about it. I'm not gonna give up my right to leave a place I don't wanna be, just to save someone like you."

Flatly, with her hand on her hip, Felt declared it so.
It meant abandoning a person she knew well—and, if the old man's words were true, it meant cutting off someone she had spent time with since childhood—

"...Lady Felt."

In front of the Knight among Knights—the red-haired youth—it meant declining participation in the Royal Selection.

At her declaration, Reinhard couldn't help but feel something bitter run through him inwardly.
He had foreseen this happening. Seeing the old man's behavior, he'd been able to predict how a proud girl like her would react to it.
And that very point had been used against her, expertly, by Priscilla and the old man—no, by the old man alone.

The old man, left in the shape of someone abandoned, slumped his shoulders and hung his head as if all strength had left him.
But Reinhard didn't miss it—the corners of the old man's mouth, faintly, weakly loosening. It wasn't the kind of slackness that came from regret or despair. It was the kind that dwelt in the satisfaction of having accomplished something.

Staking his life, the old man had gambled everything on a single move. And he had pulled it off magnificently, achieving exactly the goal he'd aimed for. It was an act worthy of praise.
Even now, it was necessary to expose the old man's scheme and set Felt's actions right. But that was something Reinhard could not do.
—The very thing that made him who he was, that quality itself, bound his actions.

Seeing the old man hanging his head and Felt standing her ground, Marcos must have sensed the conversation had reached its end. He pulled at the old man's shackled arm, the clank of chains ringing out, and bowed toward the hall.

"My deepest apologies for the disturbance. We shall remove this man at once—"
"Yeah, about that, see, I was actually waiting for someone to jump the gun like that."

Marcos, having offered his apology and moved to withdraw, was suddenly interrupted by Felt's words.
Marcos closed his mouth, looking, unusually for him, a bit taken aback. Felt eyed the shift in his rock-hard expression with a satisfied smirk, and turning her small frame in the still-stunned hall, said,

"So anyway, Captain, let go of that hand. Old Man Rom's got thick-ass arms and those shackles don't fit right—it's painful just to look at."

"As I have said many times, I must refuse. The reason I would follow your command is..."

"That I don't wanna take part in this Royal Selection thing, right? Well then, this is easy."

Cutting off Marcos again as he tried to refuse the order, Felt lightly tapped her own chest. Then, twisting the corner of her mouth into a wide grin that bared her sharp canine teeth in a predatory smile, she said,

"I'll do it. This Royal Selection thing. I just gotta aim to become king or whatever, right?"

"——!!"

That declaration, delivered with a smile, sent a violent shudder through the entire hall.
Something to be called a bombshell statement—it struck everyone who heard it with shock first, but what remained in each individual heart after that shock passed varied widely.

Some accepted it solemnly, some smiled with a lascivious curl of the lips, some touched their foreheads as if bearing a headache, some stiffened their cheeks trying not to let their expressions change.
And in the hearts of many, there swirled resentment toward a girl who spoke so lightly of a weighty decision—or anger at seeing a matter of grave importance to the kingdom treated so casually.
Leaving aside that host of reactions, the one who showed by far the biggest reaction was, of course, the old man who had taken her declaration full in the face.

"W-what are you saying, Felt. I—I've come to terms with it. What you said before was right. Losing your pride means you can't go on living. My conduct just now was exactly that, the lowest of the low. It can't be helped if you abandon me for it..."

"That's some third-rate theater, you damn geezer. Living this long and you still don't know you got no talent for the stage? Give up on the career change and go back to being a small-time crook."

"Don't be foolish...! In the first place, ain't you the type who doesn't do things you hate? It's only natural you'd abandon someone like me who's shown such an unsightly display. Even if it means enduring something unpleasant..."

"Yeah, yeah, you really do get me, Old Man Rom. That's right, I'm the type who doesn't do things I hate. And you know what, turns out I get you too."

A complete reversal from his earlier pleading for his life, the old man now scrambled desperately in the opposite direction. Felt pointed a finger at him, then turned that same finger toward her own face, saying,

"I know this from years of knowing you... Old Man Rom, whenever you're about to tell a lie, the veins on the bridge of your nose bulge right out."

"Wh-what did you say, that's a lie——!?"

At Felt's accusation, the old man was astonished, and in a fluster he lifted his shackled arm to rub at his own nose. Watching that gesture of his, Felt snorted and said,

"Yeah, that was a lie. And now we've found the dumbass. So, whatcha gonna do?"

"—Ah."

Old Man Rom, having fallen neatly for a leading question, stood dumbfounded.
Whatever she thought of an old man who'd fallen for such a low-level trick, Felt shrugged both shoulders and shook her head as if to say "good grief," and said,

"So anyway, take Old Man Rom's shackles off. Everything up to now, all of it—chalk it up to the ravings of a senile old geezer."

"To let such a pretext stand would not..."

"—That old man's my family. So let go of him. Right now."

To Marcos, who still tried to hold firm, Felt spoke with all emotion stripped from her voice.
Hearing those words, Marcos furrowed his brow for just an instant, but quickly wiped any sign of that unease from his expression, and said,

"As you wish."

—giving the salute reserved for someone of higher standing, and releasing his hand from Old Man Rom's shackles. Then he turned back and called to the knight waiting near the entrance, "The key to the shackles."
But Felt raised a hand to stop him, saying,

"Can't wait that long. —Reinhard!"

"Here."

Responding instantly to the girl's sharp call, Reinhard's tall frame stepped up onto the stage.
She didn't so much as glance at the red-haired youth standing beside her, only crossed her arms and jerked her chin, saying,

"Do it."

That was all she commanded.

"As you wish, my liege—"

And to that shortest command in the world, the knight responded with a wealth of feeling packed into it.
The arm he raised before his liege formed a hand-blade in the air, and it split the air as it swung down.

Severed—the metal shackles that had bound the old man's hands were sliced away as easily as paper before Reinhard's blade-hand.
The shackles, parted as if melted away, fell to the floor, and a sharp ringing note sounded through the hall.
Against that backdrop, the girl looked up at the tall youth with an expression that seemed to say how irritating this all was, and said,

"So this is all going exactly the way you wanted too, huh?"

"Not at all. This is something greater—the guidance of fate."

To Felt's displeased words, Reinhard answered with a hand over his chest. Hearing that, the girl let out a breath like a scoff of "hah!" and said,

"Fate again. Are you a slave to fate or something?"

"No. —I am a servant of what is right."

The youth answered even sarcasm earnestly and head-on. At that attitude, Felt closed one eye and pulled up the corner of her mouth, saying,

"Well, that job's outta business as of now. From here on, you're my servant."

"Yes. As my liege wishes."

"I-I'm gonna work you to the bone, so brace yourself."

"Yes. Entirely as my liege desires."

"Boring guy..."

At the momentum of what seemed likely to be total, unconditional agreement to everything, Felt let that slip out in a fed-up tone.
Before the two of them, carrying on this exchange, it was Old Man Rom who still couldn't hide his stunned expression. He shook his now-unshackled arm, his blood-streaked cheeks twisting in anguish, and said,

"Why, Felt. ...I, I was..."

"I kinda get what Old Man Rom's deal was, what he was aiming for, saying all that pathetic stuff. —You could tell I hated, hated being stuck standing in this place, right? You were trying to give me a push, weren't you."

To the old man drooping weakly, Felt raised a hand and apologized with a "my bad." Old Man Rom lifted his face to her, his lips trembling, and said,

"If you understood that much, then why...?"

"Told you already, myself. I don't do things I hate."

To the old man wearing a look of confusion, Felt smiled, a little embarrassed, and said,

"You want me to abandon my family and slink on back to the lower town? There's no way in hell I could pull off something that low."

"——"

Hearing that, Old Man Rom's expression shifted from anguish into something else entirely.
He turned his back to the girl and rubbed his face against his arm to hide his expression, saying,

"M-my defeat was..."

"Overwhelmingly clear, wouldn't you say, old man."

Sensing the emotion shaking through him, at Reinhard's prompting, the old man looked up at the ceiling, and in a hoarse voice, full of regret yet unable to hide a tremor that seemed to overflow with emotion, he said,

"I raised her too well——!"

Amid the cry ringing out—whether lamenting or rejoicing over his own methods of raising her—the representative of the Council of Wise Men on the dais, Miklotov, narrowed his eyes as he combed his beard, and said,

"Was this situation as you had intended it, Captain?"

"I would never presume so far. I am, after all, merely a knight. One who upholds the path—not one who forges it."

Miklotov nodded at Marcos's modest reply, then turned toward the three—no, the two now standing a step ahead as master and servant—in the center of the hall.

"Very well then, Lady Felt, Knight Reinhard. Shall we take it that both of you also intend to participate in the Royal Selection?"

"Yeah, sure."
"Yes. As my liege wishes."

Felt, still unreformed in her overbearing manner, and Reinhard, who followed her lead. The generous elder made no remark on their mismatch, only quietly nodding, "Understood."

"Well then, though there was a bit of commotion, I believe we may now consider the candidates all present. Lastly, if Lady Felt has anything to say..."

He was likely intending to offer Felt the same opportunity for a speech that the other candidates had been given.
At Miklotov's suggestion, Felt furrowed her brow for a moment as if in thought, then held up a single finger, saying "Just one thing," and stepped forward, standing in the path of everyone's gaze. And then—

"—I hate nobles."

With a bright, cheerful smile, she spread her hands as if to indicate the Council of Wise Men, and declared it so.

"—I hate knights."

Still holding that smile, she now pointed with her other hand at the Royal Guard.

"—I hate the kingdom."

And then, with both hands still spread, she wove words with venom hidden in the midst of her full, beaming smile.

"—I hate everyone in this room, I hate the ground I'm standing on, I hate every single thing about all of this. So I'm gonna smash it all to pieces."

Tilting her head as if to say "well?", Felt smiled on, enjoying herself.
At that attitude, in an instant, the emotions of those who had completely forgotten how to think exploded.

"Wh-what are you saying——!?"
"To speak of destroying the kingdom, here, at the very ceremony to choose its king!?"
"What do you think of all our time, all our history, up to now!!"

"You lot keep chirping like that, gotta slap a 'we' on everything just to talk, huh? Pride, history, don't make me laugh."

Before the crowd, raising their voices in a rush of fury, Felt cut them all down in a single breath. Then she pinched the hem of the yellow dress she wore, saying,

"Dressed up all frilly like this, wearing fancy clothes, jangling with expensive-looking jewels, your eyes all clouded over with blood and history. That's why not a single one of you can see how rotten, how falling-apart your own foundations really are."

So, she continued, drawing a breath, then looked up at Miklotov on the dais, and said,

"When I become king, I'm gonna smash it all to pieces. All you blind fools who can't even see the ground about to crumble under your own feet—I'll knock the lot of you down together and let some fresh air in for once."

At her declaring this with a bright, refreshed expression, the hall could do nothing but turn pale.
In the face of that unprecedented outrage, Miklotov, his expression still unchanged, nodded magnanimously, then turned his focus toward the knight standing beside her.

"Your liege is a fierce one indeed. Having heard those words just now, what do you make of it?"

"—Well now. Lady Felt's words are, regrettably, still the stuff of dreams for the time being."

Reinhard, with that, shook the very foundation of the girl he called his liege's declaration. At those words, Felt shot him a suspicious look, but Reinhard took it in stride with a cool expression, and said,

"But someday, Lady Felt's words will reach everyone. —It is my purpose to support her in every way, so that this may come to pass."

"And yet, among what Lady Felt has declared she will destroy, it would seem you yourself are included."

"This one shall also stand by her side through the rebuilding that follows the destruction. And should I remain at her side even then, there could be no greater fulfillment of my purpose."

Bowing deeply, Reinhard declared it without wavering before Miklotov. Watching the knight's conduct out of the corner of her eye, Felt roughly ruffled her golden hair, and said,

"In the end, are you my ally or my enemy? Which is it?"

"Your ally. —Yours alone."

"...Fine, then. I'll work you to the bone."

And thus, here, the final master and servant pair of the Royal Selection's candidates was born.
And then—

"Well then, this time we have truly heard from all the candidates. Now, let us once again put the question to our comrades of the Council."

Solemnly, in a low voice, Miklotov spoke thus.
Naturally, tension and silence descended over the hall, and as everyone held their breath waiting for the next words, Miklotov opened his narrow eyes just slightly wider, and said,

"For this Royal Selection, let these five persons be declared candidates, and let the beginning be proclaimed. I ask for the assent of my comrades."

"—By the authority of the Council of Wise Men, I assent."
"Likewise."
"Likewise, I assent."

The members of the Council answered Miklotov's proposal with nods, and having confirmed this, Miklotov rose from his seat.
Then he stepped forward and took his place beside the empty throne, saying,

"—Now then, I shall set forth the conditions of the Royal Selection!"

Head of House Karsten, Duke Crusch Karsten.
Crusch's First Knight, Felix Argyle of the Blue.

"The candidates shall be: Crusch Karsten. Priscilla Barielle. Anastasia Hoshin. Emilia. Felt. All of them persons possessing the qualification of Dragon's Priestess!"

The 'Bloodstained Bride,' Priscilla Barielle.
The one-armed man from another world, the mercenary Al.

"The deadline shall be three years hence, this very day—one month before the ceremony to confirm the Covenant with the Dragon, the Divine Dragon Ritual!"

The young merchant guild leader from a foreign land, Anastasia Hoshin.
Anastasia's First Knight, 'Knight of the Utmost,' Julius Juukulius.

"Selection shall be determined by the radiance of the Dragon Gem, before the Divine Dragon Volcanica!"

The lost royal bloodline (?), Felt.
Felt's First Knight, 'Sword Saint' Reinhard van Astrea.

"Each of you, in your effort to preserve the kingdom, shall make known your own path of kingship to the common people, to the citizens of this realm!"

The silver-haired Half-Elf, Emilia.
And, absent from this place, the self-proclaimed knight, Natsuki Subaru.

"With the above as the minimum terms of our covenant, I hereby declare the Royal Selection to have begun——!"

Miklotov cried out in a loud voice, and the hall was engulfed in tremendous heat.
There was no sound. But no one could quite suppress the cry welling in their heart.

Receiving the aftershock of that heat upon his back, Miklotov straightened his bent waist and opened his mouth wide.

"From this moment forth—the Royal Selection begins!!"
