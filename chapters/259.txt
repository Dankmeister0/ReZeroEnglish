Arc 4 Chapter 91: "The False Sleep"

Hearing Emilia's tear-choked words, a wave of regret stabbed clean through Subaru's entire body. Guilt riddled him through and through for having made Emilia dig up such sad memories, for having brought tears to her eyes.

From Emilia's halting, fragmentary words, what came through was affection and gratitude toward the people she'd spent her days with in the Elior Forest. Then, with that day of snow as the turning point, it all reversed — the memory shifting into one of hearing hatred and resentment.

What those people, sealed away in ice, had thought during that frozen span of time, he couldn't know. But the warm, happy time that had surely once existed for Emilia had been shut away, cold and pitiless, inside thick ice, showing no sign of melting even now.

"...Why did those people throw words like that at you? Thinking it over based on what you've just told me, it would make sense if... it was you who put that forest under the ice, Emilia. But did you, as a child, really have the power to cause something so outrageous?"

"—I don't know. Back then I was so much more naive about the world than I am now, not even knowing what I could or couldn't do, just relying on everyone's kindness. But... even now, I don't have the power to freeze a whole forest alone, without even Puck's help."

"Could you, if Puck helped?"

"——"

When Subaru pressed further on her uneasy words, Emilia dipped her chin faintly, without speaking. That reluctant affirmation was surely born of fear — fear that Subaru would misunderstand her as the very culprit who'd frozen the forest. But he wasn't thinking anything of the sort.

It wasn't favoritism. It was simply a matter of sequence.

"You don't need to look so anxious — I'm not going to get the wrong idea. You met Puck long after the Great Forest was frozen over, right? Like, a hundred years after, even. Ice, then Puck, then you — the order's backwards for that theory."

"Y-yeah... that's... right, but..."

When Subaru declared *I understand* ahead of time, Emilia nodded with an expression too stiff to be called relief. Suppressing the urge to furrow his brow at that reaction, Subaru told his facial muscles to hold steady and clasped his hands together in front of her.

—There had been small flickers of wrongness before this, in listening to Emilia's scattered words. But never, until this very moment, had the wrongness struck this hard.

Of course it hadn't. Until now, Natsuki Subaru had never once stepped into Emilia's inner self or her past — he'd only ever doted on the surface of her humanity, fawning over it like some flower to admire, and called himself content.

So this — this was where it had to start. This was, for Subaru, a Trial of his own.

Subaru, who'd lost the right to undergo the Trials of the Tomb, needed to confirm whether he had the right to stand before Emilia, beside her, close to her — and this was the Trial for that.

"I understand now what scene it is Emilia sees during the Trial of the Tomb. ...So conversely — what do you think you'd need to do, to get past it?"

"That's... um..."

Emilia's gaze wandered. It wasn't hesitation over how to answer — it was the reaction of someone unable to find a clear name or shape for an answer that still lay in vagueness.

Emilia had no clear vision for breaking through the Trial. This was still only her first attempt, and from her side, she'd suddenly had a years-long anguish thrust into the open, and was now being asked for a complete answer to it.

Echidna had said that, properly speaking, the first Trial of the Tomb could be broken through by presenting one's own answer to a past one had been avoiding facing.

Whether to affirm it or deny it — arriving at either answer was the way through.

Emilia had taken the reality of being denounced by people she'd once been close to and held it as a sad memory. Was letting go of that the condition for breaking the first Trial?

Letting go of what had been left behind in the past — how could that be cleared away?

Subaru couldn't offer Emilia a clear-cut answer. But there were things he understood precisely because he'd broken through the first Trial, however roughly, and had brushed against the second. Things he understood precisely because he'd touched, if only slightly, the nature of the being called Echidna.

—Likely, the Trial never presented a problem impossible for its challenger to overcome.

Considering Echidna's purpose in imposing the Trials, that much was only natural. What Echidna wanted was the treasure of a result that satisfied her curiosity, and that shone brightest precisely by the Trial being broken through. At least, that witch surely thought so.

Against that outcome, there was simply the challenger's answer — affirmation or denial, one or the other.

Which meant Emilia met the condition for clearing the Trial of the Tomb. If she could find that condition and drag it out against her past, that ought to become her answer.

Which meant, if anything, that the problem — if there was one — lay not with the Trial, but—

"And if you go in without finding some kind of answer to it, you'll probably just get the same result again."

"—Subaru, what do you think?"

"............"

"What I just told you... the Trial, and my past... hearing that, what do you think? Do you have any idea what I should do? What... should I do?"

For a long, long time — surely since last night, even after the Trial had ended and she'd returned here to their temporary lodging — she must have been repeating that same back-and-forth in her mind, even eating into her hours of sleep.

Wearing herself down in a whirlpool of thought so fierce it had, in the end, left her losing consciousness in something close to a faint.

"Earlier you said you wanted to melt the ice and thank everyone, right?"

"Yeah."

"Why do you feel that way?"

Emilia must have been dealt a cruel blow by people she'd once been close to. Why, then, did she find meaning in saving those men and women sunk beneath the ice?

"The last memory left inside you is one of being denounced by those people, isn't it? Cruel words thrown at you, resentment thrown at you... and yet, why do you want to help them?"

"—Subaru, if I said cruel things to you right now, here, would you stop wanting to help me?"

"——"

He found himself at a loss for words.

Emilia's violet eyes gazed back at him earnestly. The hesitation that had lived within her weakness was gone from those eyes now, as she delivered her answer.

"It's true that the last thing I felt, being with everyone, was painful. But that last thing doesn't erase all the time I spent with them before that. There were plenty of good memories between us too."

"............"

"I can't just forget that and decide the only memories I have of them are ones where I got hurt — I can't bring myself to deny everything like that. I want to save them, and laugh with them again... it's greedy, I know, but that's what I think."

Once she'd said it, Emilia pressed a hand over her own mouth and searched Subaru's face for his reaction. To Subaru, her expression looked as though she'd let slip some ugly ambition from deep inside her without meaning to, and now feared being despised for it.

Watching Emilia grow anxious like that, Subaru thought—

—Was a way of living that couldn't help but call such a wish *greedy* really her way of living?

"—Subaru?"

"No, I was just thinking. You're completely right, Emilia."

Even if the last encounter had felt like being killed, that didn't erase the bonds and memories built up before it.

Even after being killed, in a manner of speaking, by Rem and Ram, Subaru had run himself ragged trying to save them both. Even in the loop that had begun in the royal capital, he'd kept running with that same feeling. Just as Subaru felt that way, so did Emilia — that was all there was to it.

"——"

And then, the instant after that sense of relief settled in him, Subaru noticed the greatest wrongness he'd felt yet.

A wrongness so absurd it left him stunned that he hadn't noticed this oversight until now.

"—Subaru?"

Emilia's eyes wavered with confusion at Subaru, whose face had gone rigid and tense as he stared at her. Even knowing he was causing her that anxiety, Subaru couldn't easily recover his composure. Because—

—Within Emilia, an answer to the events of her past had already been reached.

"——"

In the ice of the Elior Forest, the tribe of elves she'd spent her days with slept on. Emilia had looked back on the past of the day snow covered the forest, and even as the people she'd trusted had turned the edge of their malice on her, she had declared, plainly, that she wanted to save them and tell them her thanks.

That was one decisive answer to a past one would rather look away from.

If Subaru's decision — acknowledging his own past foolishness and bidding his parents farewell — had been judged to meet the condition for breaking the first Trial, then this resolve of Emilia's ought to be judged just as precious a decision.

And yet, the Trial refused to acknowledge that she'd met the condition.

He wondered for a moment if it was because he'd shaken her awake and interrupted the Trial, but even across past loops, from the second day onward — even at times other than when Subaru himself had cut Emilia's Trial short — there had never once been a case where she'd broken through it.

Was Emilia's answer, against the Trial presented to her, simply not the right one?

"But that can't be..."

If it was Echidna who passed judgment on the Trial, did that mean whether she liked the answer given was purely up to the witch's whim? But Echidna's stance wasn't one that cared which answer came out — it was a style that valued an answer being reached at all.

Rejecting a challenger's answer, whatever form it took, wasn't like her. It wasn't like her — and yet, if he allowed for the possibility, just hypothetically, that Echidna alone was refusing to accept Emilia's answer specifically, Subaru had something that came to mind.

But acknowledging that weighed on his conscience.

Because acknowledging it meant nothing less than accepting the inference that Emilia — *only* Emilia — could never, absolutely, break through this Trial.

"No way am I accepting that... c'mon, Echidna."

"Subaru, what's wrong? Did I say something strange again..."

"No, it's not your problem, Emilia. If there's a problem here, it's on the one who set the question. ...You said you wanted to melt the ice and save everyone — but you couldn't melt it? You lived in the forest alone with Puck until Roswaal took you away, right? You must have had plenty of time to try."

Subaru knew it was a cruel question even as he threw it out.

Asking her to do that, after hearing her past, amounted to nothing less than choosing to have her, with her own hand, undo the ice's seal and let the hatred of those freed people rain down on her all over again.

Emilia herself must have wrestled with that anguish over and over. She lowered her eyes, nails digging crescent marks into the arm she gripped.

"I asked Puck for help, so many times... but the ice wouldn't melt."

"Couldn't melt it — was that a mental block, or... a physical problem?"

If it had been a mental block, Subaru had no intention of blaming her for it. No one could easily bring themselves to act, knowing full well it would wound their heart.

But Emilia answered Subaru's question with a weak, "A physical problem, I think."

"That ice was special ice... it wasn't the kind of thing that would melt no matter how hard you tried from outside. You'd have to do something about the person who'd cast it, or find some far greater means... or so I was told. So, I took Roswaal's offer, and..."

"Offer...?"

"Ah..."

At Subaru's furrowed brow, Emilia's face took on the look of someone who'd just said something she shouldn't have, and she covered her mouth with a hand. But before Subaru's continued silent gaze, her shoulders soon slumped.

"Roswaal... made me a promise."

"——"

"He brought me the crest, and had me hold it, and once he'd confirmed the gem was glowing red, he told me about the Royal Selection, and then... he said this."

Had the fact that a crest would glow in the forest — that there was an Emilia there qualified as a candidate for the Royal Selection — also been something recorded in the Gospel?

It was an exchange Subaru could almost picture, Roswaal's eerie smile and all — in his imagination, Roswaal, extending a hand to Emilia, said:

"—If you were to claim the throne, then melting the ice of this forest, too, might well be within reach."

"...And you believed that?"

"I clung to it, I suppose. I was never told the details of how it would work... but I accepted the offer, and left the forest with Roswaal. Puck... never objects to what I decide to do, so he came along without saying anything."

"That's why you decided to enter the Royal Selection... the selfish reason you mentioned before, for joining the Royal Selection — that's what it was."

Emilia had said, unlike the other candidates, that she'd decided to enter the Royal Selection for a selfish reason. He'd avoided asking for the details until now, but the meaning of those words finally connected here.

"...You must think less of me for it, right?"

While Subaru was still chewing over that one realization in his chest, Emilia suddenly murmured that. When he looked up, she was gazing at him timidly, her lips trembling.

"Everyone else... they've all decided on some noble goal, some resolve, before challenging the Royal Selection, but the reason I have is so, so personal..."

"I think wanting to help everyone in the village matters plenty too, you know. However many or few people get saved doesn't make what you're doing any less admirable. Besides... what you said in the Royal Selection hall — that wasn't a lie either, was it?"

"What I said, in the Royal Selection hall..."

"That you wanted to be seen equally. I don't think there was any lie in those words."

At the start, perhaps, it had been born from wanting some way to resolve an unsettled matter within herself. But Emilia had come to know the outside world, come to know the weight of a hundred years, come to know the shape of the present — and through that, she must have had the chance to reconsider.

To Subaru, the words she'd spoken in the Royal Selection hall hadn't felt like some hollow, insincere front.

If that feeling had been genuine, and the desire to win through the Royal Selection remained the same even now, then Subaru didn't think it was a reason worth condemning wholesale.

"So you don't need to look so anxious. I'm on your side, and the feeling that you can lean on me hasn't changed since last night. Even if you go and firmly refuse, telling me you're fine, when it comes to leaning on my shoulder."

"Ah... um, about yesterday..."

"Don't apologize, it'll just make you feel pathetic. Well — what I can tell you is that I'm always in a position where Emilia-tan can lean on me, so, please use responsibly. I think a strong Emilia who stands on her own is great and all, but it's fine if you let yourself be a little weak too."

He struck his chest and let his mouth loosen into an easy grin, and Emilia, in turn, let out a soft breath as if relieved. The instant she did, that relief seemed to spread through her whole body, and her upper body swayed unsteadily.

"Somehow, now that I'm relieved, all of a sudden..."

"You barely slept, with bad dreams and all. Don't push yourself — go ahead and sleep a little. I won't do anything, I'll just watch over you properly."

"That 'won't do anything' part worries me a lo-ot..."

Even as she fussed over that unnecessary remark, Emilia's silver hair swayed as she resisted the pull of sleep all the same. Subaru pressed a finger to her forehead and, with a light push, tipped her slender frame back.

"Ah—"

"Come on, just sleep already."

He pointed a finger at Emilia, now lying faceup on the bed, brooking no argument. Draping a light blanket over her slender body, Subaru pulled the chair he'd been sitting on closer to the bed and settled into a spot where he could watch her sleeping face.

"You talked it all out, got things straightened out in your head, and if my words gave you even a little peace of mind... then rest easy for a while. Once night falls, there'll be more time we have to push through again."

"...Is it really okay for me to be spoiled like this?"

"It's fine. Go ahead and be as spoiled as you want. Spoil yourself till your teeth rot from all the sugar I'm feeding you."

At Subaru's shrug, Emilia, tucked into the bed, let out a small laugh. Then she gazed steadily at Subaru, and slowly reached a hand out from beneath the blanket.

"—Hand."

"Hm?"

"If you're going to spoil me, then... will you hold my hand? Just until I fall asleep is fine. Can I ask that of you?"

"Sure thing, leave it to me."

He took the small, slender hand offered to him, smiling as he felt its delicate, smooth touch in his palm. Emilia smiled back at that same smile, and, as if obeying his words, gently closed her eyes.

It wasn't long before he could hear the small sound of her breathing settle into sleep.

"...Hope you get to see a good dream, at least."

Watching Emilia's quiet sleeping face in the bed, Subaru brushed aside the silver hair falling over her forehead with a finger, then let his gaze drop to the hand still joined with his.

If, by feeling the presence of another like this, she could be freed even a little from the loneliness within her dreams, that would be good. Being alone in a room, chased again and again by anguished nightmares — that was too cruel a fate to bear.

"Still, though... I got to hear a lot."

Still holding her hand, Subaru settled back into the chair and turned over in his mind the conversation they'd just had.

Emilia's past, and her reason for challenging the Royal Selection. The offer from Roswaal that had drawn her out of the forest, and how cornered Emilia must have been to have no choice but to accept it. And above all, the Trial bearing down on Emilia, and the true intent behind a Trial that refused to forgive her even though she'd surely already given her answer — leaving all of it half-resolved, Subaru had put Emilia to sleep like this, and now sat here.

"——"

Quietly, he gazed at Emilia's sleeping face.

It wasn't that he'd felt her utter exhaustion, found it too painful to bear, and simply put off the situation. There was a reason he'd set aside the answer that needed to be given, and all but forced her to rest like this instead.

The reason he'd arrived at that thought was simply too awful — he couldn't have done what came next with Emilia awake and beside him.

"It's just, thinking it through given the circumstances... I can't think of any other explanation."

The past loops, the details that had snagged in his mind, and the rest of the circumstantial evidence — all of it pointed Subaru toward this. There was only one way to confirm it, and it was something he could easily do right now.

And if this thought of his was correct, it would, without question, become a single ray of light to break open the situation—

He drew a breath, and held it.

Listening to the loudness of his own pulse pounding through his blood, Subaru reached out a hand to confirm whether his thinking was correct.

His left hand — the one not clasped with Emilia's right — reached toward the neck of the Emilia wearing that peaceful sleeping face, toward that pale, slender throat, and then—

"—You're not really asleep at all, are you."

A cold, hard sensation against his fingertips.

Feeling his own voice stiffen even as he spoke, Subaru wove those words together.

There was a moment of silence, and just as Subaru's heart began to scorch with impatience—suddenly,

*"Well noticed. —I'm glad, Subaru."*

From within the touched green crystal, an androgynous spirit's voice rang directly inside Subaru's skull.
