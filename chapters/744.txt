Arc 9 Chapter 48: "Apex Lifeform"

"――Well, Roswaal and Clind-san say they can do it. So they must be able to, right?"

That was the decisive remark from Emilia, spoken during a moment in the Aldebusters' war council, amid the discussion of who should face the Divine Dragon Volcanica.

Emilia's decision to entrust the battlefield to them without hearing a detailed explanation wasn't thoughtless rashness — it came from firm trust, and Roswaal felt both admiration and gratitude toward her for it.
Given the circumstances, he had half-resigned himself to a scene where, even if it threw his long-laid plans somewhat off course, he would have to reveal Clind's true nature — the truth that he was the Dragonkin of the Divine Dragon.

"Though if I were to speak of that, it would also mean touching on the matter of our Contract."

Naturally, Roswaal had taken Clind in as a servant of House Mathers fully aware of whose Dragonkin he was. Of course, that wasn't because he valued Clind's extraordinary abilities as a steward — it was because he judged his existence to be useful.
Useful, that is, to the long-cherished wish Roswaal was bound to fulfill — an indispensable asset to it.

"Even if it were told, I doubt anyone's attitude would change. A suggestion."

Knowing the full circumstances, Clind offered that suggestion to Roswaal, who kept them hidden.
There was no reason to stubbornly deny it. In fact, Roswaal agreed with him. Likely, even if Roswaal's circumstances were revealed, the attitude of the camp — of Emilia and the others — wouldn't change.

And yet, that Roswaal still couldn't bring himself to reveal it was, by now, simply a matter of nature.
Roswaal couldn't face another person unless he'd placed himself at an advantage. He needed to grasp the other party's intentions, hold their weaknesses, and ideally, keep a hostage in reserve.
Years upon years in which failure was not an option had shaped Roswaal into a cowardly villain.
So even knowing that Subaru, Emilia, and the others were an extraordinarily good-natured bunch, Roswaal simply couldn't bring himself to lay his true feelings bare.

"That said, I don't think it'll cause any trouble, no-o-o."

What mattered wasn't baring everything, but fully bringing to bear one's true worth, in proportion to one's ability.
On that point, Roswaal was confident he wouldn't fall behind his companions. If anything, precisely because he carried the debt of hiding his true intentions, he felt all the more that he had to show results.

*...A debt? I, disciple of the Witch?*

Even as that question rose in his own heart, Roswaal set it aside.
Whatever the case, without revealing Clind's circumstances, Roswaal would face the same battlefield as him — the fight against the enemy who had seized the Divine Dragon's Dragonhusk.
As for the rest—

"I'll use this chance to test it — the fruit of my long study, honed for killing a 'Dragon.'"

——In that battle, there was no such thing as feeling things out.

Clind removed his monocle and wrapped his body in violet lightning — with two black horns now sprouting from his head, he cast off the mask of the all-around steward he had long worn, becoming, simply, a Dragonkin.
The instant he kicked off, lightning sparked beneath the soles of his shoes, and the initial burst of speed matched the speed of sound.

"——"

Bursting the air apart, each stride sent cannon-shell-like footfalls shaking the world to its core.
Using the bursts of violet lightning as propulsion, Clind made straight for the towering presence seated at the zenith of the sky — the Divine Dragon Volcanica, its mighty dragon wings spread wide, sun at its back, opening its jaws.

'——!'

In that instant, the released breath became a white beam of heat and caught Clind head-on. A 'Dragon's' breath was a pure torrent of Mana, mere force given no directional intent as magic. And yet, just as a single drop of harmless water becomes, when gathered, a flood that sweeps a nation away, Mana given overwhelming momentum easily grinds away at the real world——

"Not much artistry for an opening greeting. How unrefined."

Clind split that city-leveling flash of white light clean in two with a downward blade-hand strike, cleaving it like splitting bamboo.
The breath was split down the middle and passed by behind him, as if avoiding a direct hit. In its place, the scorched earth peeled away, shattered rock turned to sand, and even that melted into a viscous, glowing red flow.
Leaving that behind, Clind's body, pressing forward, leapt high into the air, closing on the 'Dragon' in the heavens.

'Tch, cut the crap!'

The roaring 'Dragon' twisted its body and swung its thick, powerful tail. The whirling tail strike, punching the wind to death as it spun, caught Clind head-on with nowhere to flee — and rolling thunder echoed out.

An especially strong flash of violet lightning lit the sky, and the next instant, it was the 'Dragon's' tail that was sent flying.
The principle was the same as the acceleration from kicking off the ground — a shockwave from the burst of violet lightning wrapped around him. Clind generated it beneath the soles of his feet even in midair, using the impact as a foothold, and by detonating lightning in sequence through his shoulder, elbow, and finally his fist, he met the 'Dragon's' tail with a punch of unbelievable force.
And, terrifyingly, that violet lightning——

"Bear with me a little longer. Inexhaustible."

In the clear sky, its clouds blown away, flashes of lightning that ignored the weather itself flickered layer upon layer.

'RAAAAAAAAAGH——!!'

The 'Dragon,' raising its voice in fury, beat its wings and wheeled about. Around that 'Dragon,' Clind, accelerating with every burst of violet lightning, leapt about freely in every direction.
As before, the bursts of violet lightning generated beneath his shoe soles kept Clind aloft, granting a wingless Dragonkin the right to move freely through the sky. Of course, the diligent Dragonkin didn't just enjoy his aerial stroll — he never forgot to rain fierce blows upon the 'Dragon.'

"——"

Dodging the swiping dragon claws by a hair's breadth, he drove a lightning-wrapped strike into the face of an opponent whose size and reach both dwarfed his own, and there, at the peak, the lightning detonated. A one-two combination of blow plus lightning blast — not just once or twice, but ten, twenty times over in the blink of an eye.

'Damn it——'

With its airspace stolen away by the overlapping bursts of violet lightning, the 'Dragon' spat the words out and climbed still higher.
Not just the wasteland stretching as far as the eye could see, but the sky as well — a full three hundred sixty degrees, three-dimensionally unconstrained in choosing a battlefield, was the privilege of a winged 'Divine Dragon.'
However——

"——Al Duet."

——The 'Divine Dragon,' having climbed higher, plunged head-first into a massive mass of water and lost all sense of up and down.

'——!?'

The 'Divine Dragon,' letting out a great bubble of shock, had its enormous body enveloped by a water-sphere over a hundred feet across, formed by drawing the surrounding humidity and underground water streams and converging them into a single point.
With the water pressure creaking audibly, the 'Divine Dragon,' now trapped in a cage of water, opened its golden eyes wide.

"Getting so worked up you forget all about me — how co-o-old."

Below that 'Dragon's' gaze, Roswaal, who had sealed it within the water-sphere, showed a smile.
Standing on ground that had been licked by the heat of the breath Clind first split apart, now a glassy, black-glinting surface, Roswaal pointed up at the water-sphere in the sky and pressed the attack further.
A duet means a duo — this spell, too, wasn't finished with merely trapping it inside a water-sphere. An instant later, the water-sphere sealing the 'Dragon' began to bubble and boil under a searing heat that arose within it.
And into that, a violet flash of lightning sparked once more, fierce——

"Coming in a bit flashy now. Thunderclap."

Clind's launched fist, playing out a peal of thunder, drove straight into the water-sphere. In that instant, a flash of light ran through the entire mass of water, violet lightning coursing through its interior, scorching both the 'Dragon' and the sphere itself.
The next moment, heat and pressure reached their limit — and the water-sphere burst with a deafening roar.
Violet lightning with nowhere left to go tore through the battlefield, a storm of steam rivaling a blast of flame howled outward in every direction, and water that had survived the white-hot heat poured down from the clear sky like rain.

"——"

The violent detonation unleashed carried enough force to wipe out an entire small town.
Though it had the help of a Dragonkin, it was destructive force that no mage should have been able to unleash alone, and it had been driven home without any holding back — surely even the mighty 'Dragon' would——

'——Ahh, damn, that cooled my head off.'

Its dragon wings slicing through the white steam, the 'Divine Dragon' grumbled that at the very center of the sky that had become ground zero. — Yes, a grumble. Had it taken any wound, great or small, that grumble would have turned into a curse.
In other words, the 'Dragon' hovering in the sky was safe and sound. — No, it was unscathed.

"...Even knowing it beforehand, seeing it with my own eyes still gets to me, doesn't it, no-o-o."

Looking up from the ground at that unscathed survival, Roswaal closed one eye, leaving only his blue eye open.
The 'Divine Dragon' had taken not a single point of damage against firepower like that. It wasn't that Roswaal's magic was weak, nor that the 'Dragon' had instantly devised the best possible defense on the spot. — The truth was closer to the latter, but the reality was even more hopeless, overwhelmingly unreasonable.

"Master, please don't let it discourage you. Encouragement."

Beside that Roswaal, Clind, having escaped the blast, landed after falling.
Drying the water from the sphere off himself with violet lightning, Clind spoke without changing his expression or tone of voice, and yet there was, in it, a faint consideration that only their long acquaintance could convey. — Over that long acquaintance, Roswaal had also been told by him about the 'Divine Dragon's' unreasonable nature.

"As I thought, the Dragon Scale and Dragon Halo guarding the 'Heartcore' are a real pain, indee-e-ed."

That reply, which didn't actually answer Clind's encouragement, Roswaal voiced as proof that he hadn't lost his will to fight. — The Dragon Scale and Dragon Halo were the biggest reason the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica could be called the strongest being on land, and the unreasonable obstacle that had to be overcome.

"——"

—Where the difference lay between the two 'Dragons,' the 'Cloud Dragon' Mezoreia and the 'Blight Dragon' Valgren, and the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica — the answer lay in the Dragon Scale and the Dragon Halo.
Mezoreia, still young and midway through its growth as a 'Dragon,' and Valgren, who lost the balance of its mind and its future the moment the Dragonkin it had produced died — both of these two, unable to fully mature their Dragonhusks, had failed to forge the two kinds of armor that the 'Divine Dragon' possessed.
Namely——

"A Dragon Scale that lets no physical attack through, and a Dragon Halo that keeps every magical attack at bay."

"Unless those two suits of armor can be broken through, no attack will reach the 'Dragon's' Heartcore. Reconfirmation."

Clind's words, which stirred up something close to despair, described the single greatest obstacle to defeating the 'Divine Dragon.'
The Dragon Scale, an overwhelming physical defense, and the Dragon Halo, an absurd magical one — unless both of those two mighty defenses could be stripped away, no attack would ever reach the 'Heartcore,' its one weak point.
Not one or the other — both. Unless both suits of armor were stripped away, there was no outpacing the astonishing regenerative power of a 'Dragon,' a being of Mana Body, and no way to render it unable to fight.
That was why defeating the 'Divine Dragon' required two experts — one in close combat, one in magical combat.
Though, mind you——

"Before the Dragon Halo's even stripped away, taking that much firepower without a scratch — I have to hand it to it, ye-e-eah."

"It isn't called the strongest 'Dragon' on land for nothing, is it. A sigh."

"Wasn't that sigh a little proud of itself, no-o-ow? After all, in a manner of speaking, that used to be you. Feel free to quietly let me in on some weak point only you'd know about."

"There is one. The Dragon Sword. Immediate answer."

"...There's no tact in that at all."

The 'Dragon Sword,' answered so immediately, was an Enchanted Sword capable of cutting even that which could not be cut.
With the 'Dragon Sword's' abilities, one could quite literally ignore both the Dragon Scale and Dragon Halo and strike the 'Heartcore' directly. For that matter, simply bludgeoning the 'Divine Dragon' with the 'Dragon Sword' might even deal damage.
Of course, talking about a weapon that wasn't on hand was pointless, and it did nothing to improve the situation.

"For reference, as for the Dragon Scale, the reverse scale on its neck is the point to aim for. An old wound. As for the Dragon Halo... that will depend on Master's own exertions, I expect. Anticipation."

"Easy for you to say, right after my first trump card just failed to work, no-o-o."

"Is that so. ——Echidna-sama could have managed it, though. A soliloquy."

"——"

As expected — their long acquaintance cut both ways, it seemed.
Even knowing full well it was exactly what Clind intended, that shameless addendum lit a fire firmly in Roswaal's heart.
Of course, even now Roswaal was in no way arrogant enough to believe he had caught up to his teacher — but——

"A pupil can't very well go embarrassing his teacher, now ca-a-an he."

The karmic duty he had to fulfill, and the long-cherished wish he yearned to see through, breathed life into Roswaal's scar-ridden soul, lending strength to the eyes now fixed on the task before him.

The history of magic was one of endless trial and error, and Roswaal's life was no different.
Solve one problem, and the next stood in the way almost at once, and he kept searching for its solution, kept challenging it. But he was here now precisely because he had unraveled every single one of those problems.
Alone — just as it had always been, ever since he lost his teacher.

"To begin with, there's no human being with more possibility than I have."

Let him say it of himself, then: Roswaal was, without question, the strongest mage of the present age.
There existed no one with a greater possibility of breaking through the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica's Dragon Halo than that very Roswaal. — No, even that vanishingly small possibility was something he had spent these four hundred years cultivating.

In the field of magic, there could be no wall that had to be overcome which Roswaal could not overcome. There must not be.
That was the vow of Roswaal L. Mathers, and the thorned road he had resolved to walk alone.
And so——

"——I will stake my entire existence on overcoming this problem."

With that single word of resolve, Roswaal once more began tracing across the sky a formula so intense it would scorch an ordinary brain to cinders, all in pursuit of a breakthrough of the Dragon Halo that no one before him had ever achieved.

——Being a 'Dragonkin' truly was a troublesome position, Clind thought.

To begin with, a Dragonkin was, at most, the form of a 'Dragon's' next generation, and strictly speaking, was not identical to the 'Dragon' that served as its prototype. And yet, it inherited much of the 'Dragon's' memories, experience, and emotions, carrying within it a mingling of mind too difficult to cleanly divide into a separate existence.

That was no exception for Clind either, and the influence of his time as Volcanica was reflected strongly in the way he existed now. — To name a representative example, there was his strong envy of immature potential.
To Volcanica, born from the outset as an absolute species called 'Dragon,' the immaturity of humans — who determined their own way of being and grew toward their goals — had seemed dazzling and precious.
That nature carried over even now that he had become a Dragonkin, and at present, Clind could not help but respect, adore, revere, cherish, and love young, immature beings brimming with great potential — that is, children.
This nature of Clind's, one might call it a trait carried over from a previous life, was something Frederica, for one, seemed to have badly misunderstood. Still, since Frederica had her own separate Dragonkin circumstances she preferred kept quiet, he had deliberately avoided clearing up that misunderstanding, even with those around her.

"That said, being kept at arm's length from Annerose-sama, Petra, Beatrice-sama, Meili, and Emilia-sama is a little vexing. An objection."

Be that as it may, not just for Clind, but for 'Dragons' and Dragonkin in general, there existed a bond that could not be severed.
A Dragonkin's growth caused the 'Dragon' to grow, and a Dragonkin's death killed the 'Dragon's' spirit — the connection was plain. In fact, should the need arise, a Dragonkin could even lodge its own spirit within its empty Dragonhusk and wield its original power as a 'Dragon' to the fullest.

"In the Empire, Mezoreia and its Dragonkin girl are said to have done just that. Interest."

Clind, too, had been a Dragonkin for a long time, but his contact with other Dragonkin was next to nonexistent, and he hoped that someday he might exchange words with Madelyn, newly born in Vollachia.
As for that lodging of the soul into a Dragonhusk which she was said to have performed, Clind himself had done it several times in the past too, returning to being the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica in order to fulfill the Covenant with the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica.

"Though at present, control of the Dragonhusk has been seized by some means or other. A foul play."

Normally, for a Dragonkin to lodge its spirit within its own 'Dragon,' all it needed to do was touch that Dragonhusk. But this homecoming of Clind's soul, his first in roughly forty years, had failed, and the Dragonhusk remained in the usurper's hands.

"It's not as though the concern never crossed my mind... A bitter regret."

However mighty a species 'Dragons' might be, the means to hijack an empty vessel were not zero.
In fact, among the Malicious Spirits Clind knew of, there was one with the power to possess another's body, and it had once come up among his acquaintances that such a power could indeed seize a Dragonhusk. — He hadn't seen that Malicious Spirit, the one named Jjuse, in a very long time now.

"No use dwelling on it now. Sentimentality."

In any case, it was entirely possible for a 'Dragon' to have its Dragonhusk taken, and Clind knew that striking the 'Heartcore' was necessary to reclaim it. — The problem was that the Dragonhusk taken was the 'Divine Dragon's,' and the Dragon Scale and Dragon Halo stood as obstacles barring the way to the 'Heartcore.'

"As for the Dragon Scale, I'll pierce the reverse scale myself; but breaking through the Dragon Halo has to fall to Master. Full concentration."

The 'Heartcore,' guarded deep behind the Dragon Scale and Dragon Halo, the 'Divine Dragon's' absolute defenses — striking it was the victory condition of the battlefield entrusted to Clind and Roswaal.
Fortunately, there was already a prospect for breaking through the Dragon Scale. — The white old scar carved into its neck at the end of a fight to the death with the 'Sword Saint' Reid Astrea was the one hole in that absolute defense.

"It's infuriating that, of all things, that insolent man's blow should end up as our path forward. Indignation."

That dragon-flesh-eating lunatic had demanded he hand over his tail one moment, demanded to be fed his innards the next, and had even once tried to gouge out his heart. What exactly did he think a traveling companion was?
And in the end, it came down to a fight to the death — whatever the circumstances of the wound, a breach in the Dragon Scale already existed.
Therefore, the path to victory came down to whether or not Roswaal could clear the wall of the Dragon Halo.

"Carving out the time for that is my duty. Wholehearted devotion."

For four hundred years, Roswaal had honed himself. Now, that was being put to the test. — Standing witness to that ultimate trial, Clind once again reproached himself: being a 'Dragonkin' truly was troublesome.
Because the way that life called Roswaal burned, having started from something so immature, and the dazzling brightness of it, could not help but seem to Clind precious, dear, and something to be cherished.



'——!!'

A roar from the 'Divine Dragon,' one that frightened the heavens, made the earth cower, and threatened the world entire, rang out.
That mighty voice sent vibrations rippling through heaven and earth, and the thunderous roar tearing the sky apart surged forward like a torrent, swallowing Clind whole. Caught up in that shredding sound, Clind's own body was on the verge of being torn apart, of bursting — an instant before it did, he wrapped himself in violet lightning and dove into a mass of mist that had formed, and the heaven-shaking roar quite literally dispersed into mist.
The fine particles of water carved a path for the sound, greatly dampening the ruinous shockwave.

"——Al Trio."

Leaping past Clind as he raced through the mist on lightning-legs, three colors of light closed in on the 'Dragon.'
Red, blue, and green — three kinds of light resonated vividly together, and in an instant, the swelling light seared the sky. — A blast wind and a burst of flame on the scale of a hundred meters erupted, raging ice-fire whipped into a storm enveloped the 'Dragon,' and colors and scenery befitting the end of the world repainted the battlefield's clear sky into hell itself.

"Sh——!"

Into that hell, Clind, racing through the air trailing lightning, threw himself of his own will.
The end of the world, the sky over a battlefield turned to hell — surviving even a space of certain death like that, and wielding one's power as if to prove one's very existence, was the mark of a strong one who had lived through the age of the 'Witches.'

'——Welcome!!'

Gathering together the incoherent, mingled-colored patterns painted across the sky as proof it was still very much alive, the 'Divine Dragon' let out a resounding roar.
Behind the 'Dragon' with its dragon wings spread wide, giant arms formed of rock masses floated in the air — twelve of them in all — roaring fiercely as if in a shout of welcome, their mighty fists rushing out to greet Clind.

"——Seal-Shackle, Second Release."

An instant before colliding with those giant stone arms, the light given off by the black horns on Clind's head intensified by another stage.
Wrapped in the surging violet lightning, Clind temporarily gained protection equal to a 'Dragon's' own. He put that solidity to use in both attack and defense, resisting the 'Divine Dragon's' assaults with his entire body as an indestructible weapon.
A single flash of rolling thunder — an explosion of violet lightning that made the heavens themselves cry out — smashed apart the stone arms the 'Dragon' had conjured, bringing about a wild slugging match between two combatants whose difference in size was almost absurd.

"You fight without any regard for appearances. I can hardly believe you're a 'Dragon.' Resourcefulness."

'We're both well past the stage of picking a pretty way to win, aren't we! My personal trend for villains lately is a guy who's good and strong AND doesn't hesitate to pull dirty tricks!'

Smashing the giant arms one, two, three at a time, Clind nodded in agreement with that answer.

Self-praise wasn't to his taste, but the one who could handle the 'Divine Dragon' Volcanica's Dragonhusk best, and most powerfully, was indeed Clind. However powerful the vessel, if the contents inside it were different, its true worth could never be brought fully to bear.
The usurper had made up for that shortfall not by drawing out the Dragonhusk's true value, but by resorting heavily to petty tricks a 'Dragon' itself would never stoop to.
And the scope of those petty tricks had reached into thoroughly unlovable territory, driving Clind into a corner.

'——Gah.'

The 'Divine Dragon,' scattering a storm of twelve giant arms, dragon claws, and tail strikes, had light swirling with a roar deep in its throat.
It was a torrent of fire and water, both being kneaded together at once and compressed inside the 'Dragon's' mouth. This wasn't any ordinary cancellation of fire and water. An immense amount of heat and moisture, sealed together with no way to escape, sought an outlet for its force and would burst — in that instant, Clind generated a shockwave of violet lightning directly in front of himself, hoping to be flung straight backward and out of the 'Divine Dragon's' line of aim.

"——Ngh!"

That was stopped by a giant stone arm stretched out to enclose him. Gripped as if wrapped up inside a stone palm, he tried to blast it apart with a flurry of lightning detonations, but——

'——Get blown away!'

——The very next instant, along with an ear-splitting roar, a detonation struck the entire battlefield as if it had been physically punched.

The heat wave that arose, consuming everything, and the shockwave that tore the world apart, assailed Clind's whole body with nowhere to escape, meting out an excessive divine punishment upon the fool who had dared challenge a 'Dragon.'

"...Ngh."

Gritting his back teeth hard, Clind released the violet-lightning barrier that had covered his entire body. He had instantly excited his black horns and thrown up a barrier, at violet lightning's maximum output, rivaling a Dragon Halo. Even so, had he taken that attack full-on, even Clind would not have escaped a fatal wound.
Yes — had he taken that attack full-on——

"——Al Quartet."

The one who spoke those words was Roswaal, chanting a great spell from behind Clind, through the burnt-away, crumbling ruin of the giant stone arm that had lost all its original shape.
Roswaal, having taken to the air with flight magic and joined Clind and the 'Divine Dragon's' aerial battle, had cancelled out that tremendous detonation through the sheer feat of invoking four elements at once.

'Hey, hey, are you serious...'

It was no wonder the 'Divine Dragon' let slip a voice of astonishment at that fact.
That last blow from the usurper had carried destructive power on the scale of leveling a city. To cancel that out in a mere human body, and moreover to protect Clind through it all — that was control beyond all sane bounds.
Get even one thing wrong, and it might have amplified the 'Divine Dragon's' attack instead, reducing both of them to cinders together. It was, quite literally, a feat like threading a needle a kilometer away.
Still, it couldn't be called a complete victory.

"...Well, I'm done for. Hu-u-uh."

Having let that weak murmur slip, Roswaal swayed, lost control, and plummeted headfirst.

"Master! Hurrying!"

Kicking off through the air to catch up with the falling Roswaal, Clind's eyes went wide.
Roswaal's body had been torn ragged just from the aftershock of the detonation he'd canceled out, and his right arm in particular, which had suffered the worst of it, had become a pitiful, charred, blackish-red lump of scorched flesh.
Roswaal, who could wield every kind of magic, had exactly one exception: healing magic, which he could not use at all. Whether he could keep fighting with a wound like this was, all too——

'Don't take it personal — Origin's waiting on me!'

"Ngh——!"

But even against Clind, now carrying the wounded Roswaal, the 'Divine Dragon' showed no mercy.
As the wind-wrapped dragon wings closed in like blades of slashing light, Clind fled through the sky on lightning-detonated legs — fled, and fled again. Ashamed of the failure that had let Roswaal be wounded, he kicked apart blades of wind, took the full sweep of the dragon wings against his own body, and detonated lightning right there — the impact sending him flying backward.
Forcibly gaining distance that way, he righted his posture — and in that instant, the 'Dragon's' tail strike came crashing down.

"——!"

Converting the shock of the lightning detonation into rotational force, he took a direct hit from the 'Divine Dragon's' downward tail strike.
He instinctively shielded his head with his arm and avoided a fatal blow, but the force sent him plunging straight toward the ground. Even if he fell to earth as he was, the momentum alone seemed enough to dig his own grave — but——

'——Here comes the finisher.'

Saying that to the falling Clind and Roswaal, the 'Divine Dragon' drew in a great breath.
In a battle where attack and defense traded places at high speed, any action requiring a charge-up was a fatal liability. That was why the 'Dragon's' full-power breath, held in reserve as a forbidden move except as an opening gambit, was now — being unleashed.
Against a ruin he couldn't outrun even making full use of his lightning-legs, there was nothing left to do. — For Clind, that was.

"——Al Quintet."

Roswaal, whose whole body had gone limp and slack, having thrown the entirety of the battle's offense and defense onto Clind, now unleashed the maximum firepower he had been charging all along.

——The activation of the completed formula manifested a colossal Magic Circle overhead, five lights bundled together.

Fire and water, wind and earth, and further, light — principles that contradicted one another, layered and converged through a logic beyond human comprehension. What emerged was an overwhelming force of destruction permitted to exist only outside of order itself. It collided head-on with the 'Divine Dragon's' unleashed breath — and in that instant, sound and color vanished from the world.

"——"

The two unleashed forces tore, vividly, straight through the very boundary between heaven and earth.
Flames raged, the sky froze solid, winds spiraled, the earth surged violently upward, and all of it was amplified by light — the five contradictory forces became pillars of light, and the battlefield collapsed in a chain reaction.
The whole stretch of land that had, before the battle began, been a rocky wasteland, was filled with a riotous dance of light and thunder so intense it stripped away all sense of up and down, and everything was swallowed into a crucible of chaos.

"——"

In terms of upsetting the balance of the world, this was already a technique on par with the many Forbidden Arts that Od Lagna itself had erased from existence. The only reason this one hadn't been wiped out as a Forbidden Art too was, quite simply, that there had never even been anyone capable of using it, making the prohibition unnecessary.
In the past, the only two who had ever achieved this same great spell — no, super-spell — were the 'Witch of Greed' Echidna, and the 'First Magician' Myonmyon.
That super-spell, with one foot already over the taboo line, was not an arrow of 'destruction,' but one of 'denial.'

"Master, what a man you are... My hat's off to you."

Who could ever believe that this cataclysmic sight was proof of a single person's lifetime of study?
The details of the road that man had walked, and the footprints he had left behind, might well be reviled, cursed, and denied by many as deeds contrary to all ethics.
But Clind would not deny him. Nor would he hold him in contempt. He might well deserve contempt, but when one witnessed something that had gone beyond all measure, the shock that came and went through the heart at the sight was no different for a human than for a 'Dragon.'
He was simply, utterly overwhelmed. That a human being could accumulate this much.

——Clind loved human potential.

His interest never ran dry in what these weak, fragile, imperfectly-born creatures aimed for, what they sought. From that standpoint, Roswaal came close to holding the highest score of all.
As far as Clind knew, there was no one else who had clung so unsightly to a possibility so impossibly distant, and kept struggling toward it, as Roswaal had.

—Had he lacked talent, he could have given up so much sooner.
—Had he been a chosen being on the level of the 'Sword Saint,' he need not have suffered this much.

Being neither one nor the other, all Roswaal had ever possessed was an obsession beyond all sane bounds.
That was why Clind had come to respect the way he lived, to affirm every step he took, and to lend him his strength.

"That prayer still goes on shining within me, even now. ——A vow."

Voicing that vow, Clind slowly laid Roswaal down on the ground.
For a mage the likes of which came along once in an age, weeping tears of blood, his whole body convulsing from the extreme abuse of his Gate — a bare bed of sand was far too bleak a reward for his achievements, but for now, he would simply have to bear with it.

—Because up in the sky, the 'Divine Dragon,' having borne the very limits of what a mage could unleash, remained unscathed and very much alive still.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—And so, reality caught up at last with Roswaal, his life now a candle flickering in the wind.

"Five seconds, Master. A report."

What greeted the return of his scattered consciousness was a voice of unusual calm.
Still, that wasn't enough to make him want to blame Clind. In five whole seconds, the 'Divine Dragon' could have killed Roswaal fifty times over. — Given the talk that it was restricting itself to a 'no-kill' rule, the most it might have done was crush his limbs and freeze the wounds, just enough to leave him unable to fight.
If it had been Roswaal in its place, he'd have done at least that much to force an opponent out of the fight.
Clind had protected Roswaal from that harm to the very end, magnificently fulfilling his role on the front line.
The problem lay——

"...In my own sorry state, is it."

In the pathetic sight of himself, having butted into a battle between a 'Dragon' and its Dragonkin only to fall into a near-fatal state with such ease. It wasn't that he'd underestimated the enemy — he'd simply believed he could do more.

"——"

Thanks in part to the efforts of Petra, who had taken on a Witch Factor of her own accord, the battlefield onto which the 'Divine Dragon' had been drawn was thoroughly laid with Roswaal's advance preparations, and he must have put on very nearly the finest magical battle of his entire life.
A battle on this scale, one could say, hadn't happened since he'd fought the 'Warlock of Melancholy' alongside Clind. Though that wasn't to say, simply going by the outcome, that the 'Divine Dragon' was straightforwardly stronger than the 'Warlock of Melancholy.'
Either way——

"...I need — to change — the victory condition."

Even now, Clind might well be fighting on the belief that Roswaal would rise again, but no one knew his own limits better than he did himself. His stamina had bottomed out, the inside of his body was a wreck, and the violent depletion of Mana had eaten away significantly even at his very life force.
None of the many great spells he'd prepared had managed to break through the 'Divine Dragon's' Dragon Halo. Even simultaneously activating five kinds of Al-class magic had proven impossible. If there was any remaining possibility, it would be a simultaneous six-attribute Al-class casting — but a spell with no precedent of success even at full strength stood no chance of succeeding in his current state.
The sensation of his brain boiling over, and the pain of his blood seeming to flow backward through his entire body, were proof that the finest Gate of his line, selected and refined by Roswaal over four hundred years, was on the very verge of breaking.

There was no meaning in continuing beyond this point. If what he'd prepared hadn't worked, then he'd lower the bar on his goal and see through, at minimum, the bare quota required.
To begin with, the aim of this battle was to stop Aldebaran's plan, and for that, all he needed to do was keep holding the 'Divine Dragon' in place right here.
So here, wisely, adaptably, he would devote himself entirely to backing up Clind, who kept fighting on.
That was——


'Roswaal, you mustn't overdo it, but this is a really, really important job. So please, do your best!'

'Roswaal-sama, I'm sorry for all the trouble I've caused you up until now. At last, I can repay your kindness. Let's help him together... let's become Subaru-kun's strength!'

'It was Master who said he'd do it, so don't go back on your word now, okay? ...I dislike Master, but it's true that I really do think you won't lose to anyone.'

'Roswaal-sama, may fortune favor you in battle. ...And please, do exactly as you see fit.'


"...Oh?"

Wisely, adaptably, devoting himself entirely to backing up Clind — that had been the plan.
But by the time he noticed, Roswaal had already risen unsteadily to his feet on the spot, cradling his ruined right arm to his chest.

"——"

It made no sense. He'd meant to fight not in the way of the great mage Roswaal had once admired, sneaking about, but in the manner of a far more cowardly, mediocre mage, hungry only for victory.

Just why, exactly, had he ended up standing?

Surely he didn't mean to claim he hadn't given up? Not after everything he'd prepared had failed to work. Not after he'd never once, in his entire life, produced a result beyond his own ability.

"——Huh, so you stood up after all. I was planning to take the role off your hands."

Because he was already so busy being startled by his own actions, that sudden ambush of a voice, for once, didn't manage to startle Roswaal any further.

"——"

The owner of that voice walked slowly across ground so devastated it looked like a depiction of the world's end, and came to stand beside the unsteady, swaying Roswaal.
Only——

"Sorry, but I can't even spare the strength to turn my head. ...You're too short for me to tell who you are, but this is no place for a child, no-o-o."

"Who are you calling a child?! You really are an infuriating man...!"

Half joking, half in earnest — at Roswaal's words, the other party's voice flared with wholehearted fury, and the figure he hadn't been able to see gradually rose into the edge of his vision. It wasn't that the other party had actually grown taller. They had raised the ground itself with magic, matching the height of their gaze with an improvised platform.
It was a truly pointless waste of magic, and Roswaal didn't dislike it one bit.
Setting aside Roswaal's private thoughts, the black-robed, green-haired member of the Dwarf Tribe who had done it crossed their arms——

"Let me carve it into you all over again, so you won't forget who I am. ——I, Ezzo Cadner the 'Gray,' will overcome the 'Divine Dragon' before you do!"
