Arc 8 Chapter 68: "The Man She Fell For"

—Called the "Witch of Envy," Emilia's eyes went round with surprise.

There were two things that surprised her. One was that it had been a very long time since she'd been treated as the "Witch of Envy." The other was that being treated as the "Witch of Envy" didn't hurt her at all.

More than a year had passed since the Royal Selection began, and in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, there was hardly anyone left who didn't know that the half-elf with silver hair and violet eyes was one of the candidates.

To put it Subaru's way, Emilia's face had become known as Emilia's own.

Because of that, the ordinary Emilia had had fewer occasions to wear her perception-obstructing robe. The reason she'd gone back to wearing it properly again for the first time in a while, here in the Empire, was so no one would find out she was Emilia.

That was why it had been so long since she'd been called the "Witch of Envy." And even now, being called that, she wasn't shaken. She had a core inside her that let her say clearly: *that's not who I am.*

A core made of the fact that there were people who acknowledged Emilia as Emilia, and told her they loved her.

"I'm Emilia. Just Emilia. Not the 'Witch of Envy.'"

Supported by that core, Emilia stood up straight and shot the words right back at the woman before her.

Having said that, Emilia stared hard at the familiar white-haired woman in front of her.

"You... look reeeally similar, but you're not Echidna, are you? Could you maybe be a twin, like Ram and the others? Echidna's older sister? Younger sister?"

"—. You have quite the vivid imagination. However, I am a creation of the Creator, not a blood relation. Correction required."

"Zoubutsu, hizoku..."

Emilia furrowed her brow at the unfamiliar words, only understanding that the woman had denied being Echidna's sister.

Come to think of it calmly, Echidna was a child from four hundred years ago, so it would be very strange for either her or her sisters to be here. Of course, given that the dead were rising and walking around, she couldn't help but wonder if maybe Echidna and her sisters had too.

"But you seem perfectly lively — you don't look like you've died."

The woman who so closely resembled Echidna didn't have the sickly complexion of the Undead, and her eyes looked normal too. Compared to Echidna, her expression might be just a touch stiffer.

The Echidna Emilia knew had often worn a mischievous look on her face. —The tearful expression she'd shown at the very end still remained vividly etched in Emilia's heart.

"Um, I understand you're not Echidna, but can I ask your name?"

"Indeed. Being called by the Creator's name troubles even me. Please, call me the 'Witch of Greed.'"

"...Is there an easier name to call you by?"

"—. Accommodation required. Failing that, Sphinx."

"I see. ...Sphinx!?"

The moment she finally got something like a proper name, Emilia realized it was the same name as the central figure causing the "Great Disaster."

By Emilia's understanding, Sphinx was supposed to look exactly like Ryuzu.

"Ryuzu-san and Echidna — are you all lookalikes of people connected to the 'Sanctuary'? Then is it Garfiel or Frederica next...?"

"You know of the 'Sanctuary' as well? It seems you and I have more connection than I expected... Emilia — aren't you one of the Royal Selection candidates, the same as Priscilla Barielle?"

"Eh? Yes, that's right. I need to find Priscilla too, and bring her home soon—"

"—Elimination required."

In an instant, white light burst from the fingers of the woman who had tilted her head slightly — Sphinx — and Emilia, on pure reflex, threw up a mirror of ice at an angle to deflect it with an "eh."

If Emilia's specialty had been earth attribute instead of fire, a wall of earth or stone might have been punched clean through in a single shot, dealing her a fatal wound.

It was an attack so chillingly full of murderous intent that it made her think exactly that.

And what's more—

"Oh, come on! Starting things so suddenly!"

The white light released wasn't just that one shot — beams of light sliced through the world, chasing after Emilia as her silver hair danced, and the battle had begun.

Her opponent looked just like Echidna, and it seemed, like Echidna, she was skilled with magic.

But that was true of Emilia too.

"If that's how you want it, then I'll go all out too! I'll immobilize you and drag you off to Subaru and the others!"

"I have no wish to ever meet them again. So I will take your life here, and see what expression Priscilla Barielle makes."

"—! You know where Priscilla is?"

Cutting down the light attacks raining from every direction with an ice sword polished mirror-bright, Emilia's violet eyes lit up at Sphinx's words.

She had a vague feeling Priscilla was in the Crystal Palace, more out of hope than certainty, but if she could get a proper location out of Sphinx's mouth, that would be best.

Emilia grew all the more fired up, determined not to lose. At Emilia's sudden burst of spirit, Sphinx — wearing Echidna's same face — narrowed her eyes with a hint of irritation.

"It's also troublesome that I can't secure this position. —Repulsion required."

So she murmured, quietly.

The battle had begun inside the tower housing the gun emplacement of the Magic Crystal Cannon, installed at the top level of the Crystal Palace, in the chamber used to operate it.

The space was excessively large for its purpose, but even so, it had walls and a ceiling — a closed environment that was advantageous ground for the shinobi Olbart.

Shinobi techniques prided themselves on versatility and adaptability. That was a development meant to accomplish the mission in any environment, but ultimately, every technique's final purpose came down to one thing: sinking its teeth into the enemy's life.

As a battlefield for accomplishing that, it was an ideal space.

"In a cramped place like this, mages get all stiff and die easy, don't they?"

Combat was about forcing your strengths onto your opponent's weaknesses.

That was the foundation of Olbart's approach to fighting as a shinobi. In fact, precisely in order to counter every opponent's weaknesses, Olbart had deliberately avoided cultivating any single specialty of his own.

Before Olbart became head of the shinobi, the principle recommended was specialization in a single art.

But the "ideal" single-art specialization was a privilege reserved only for those like Cecilus or Arakiya, who possessed one skill that genuinely worked against anyone. Ordinary talents who lacked that just died for want of adaptability.

Olbart didn't particularly care how many people other than himself died, but he did have at least enough pity in him to mourn the shinobi who died having been taught all the wrong lessons.

So when Olbart became head, he discarded the teaching of single-art specialization immediately.

Rather than blindly polishing one's own specialty, he thoroughly trained the strategy of crushing an opponent's strengths, and drilled in the technique of maneuvering the battle into a situation where that could be made to happen. Even so, that rarely bore fruit. Only a handful had truly mastered it properly, the last being a genius girl who emerged from the village four or five years ago.

That genius girl, too, had apparently died, so raising talent was nothing but a losing proposition.

—Truly, his whole life had been nothing but regrets.

"So at least, give me a reason for having lived this long, until I'm an old husk."

Kicking off the floor, he sent fragments of shattered stone flying to smash the "Witch's" head. At the same time, Olbart swung his armless right arm and his armed left arm side to side, throwing kunai that arced in on a time delay from either direction, and on top of that, detonated the launch off his back foot.

Even if she dodged the flying debris, even if she evaded Olbart's hand-blade, even if she shot down the kunai closing from both sides — any one of those would whittle down the "Witch's" ability to act, letting him sink his teeth into her life.

That shinobi's surprise assault—

"—Consciousness reform required."

The white-haired "Witch," narrowing her eyes just slightly, took it all without doing anything at all.

"——"

Her forehead split by the flying debris, kunai driven into her neck and thigh, until Olbart's hand-blade pierced straight through the center of her chest — the "Witch" never so much as took a single step.

She hadn't been able to react to Olbart's movements. —No, the "Witch's" gaze had been tracking his movements. Olbart judged that she wasn't as sluggish a woman as she appeared.

In other words, the "Witch" could have done something, and simply hadn't. —No — she was about to, now.

The next instant, countless fist-sized orbs of light appeared floating around Olbart and the "Witch," pressed together at close range.

"Olbart!"

At Moguro's cry, sensing the same thing, Olbart raised one long eyebrow and looked at the "Witch." The "Witch," her beautiful expression not wavering in the slightest, was observing Olbart's next decision. Under that gaze, he felt as though his ninety years were being peered into, and it made him feel sick.

Even so, Olbart prioritized. Not the "Witch's" life, but crushing her objective.

Even with fatal wounds all over his body, even with his chest run through, he prioritized cutting off the mana still pouring from the "Witch's" hand into the pedestal it rested on — the Crystal Palace's mana core.

"Harassment's a shinobi's true specialty, y'know."

In that instant, his snapping toe blew off the wrist of the "Witch" still touching the pedestal — and the countless orbs of light surged forward to obliterate Olbart's diminutive frame, along with the "Witch" who had cast them.

"—Yorna Mishigure!"

The instant he sensed something was wrong, Vincent, stepping one pace back from the battlefield, shouted up at Yorna, who was surveying it from a distance.

Overhead, Olbart's voice, falling from the half-collapsed gun emplacement of the Magic Crystal Cannon, carried an urgency that even that "Wicked Old Man" couldn't mask with his usual breezy manner.

Glancing sideways, Vincent's eyes met those of Eugard, who stood beside him wielding the "Sunlight Sword," holding the surrounding Undead in check.

"Go."

Briefly, sensing Vincent's intent, Eugard took over the spot, and Vincent dashed toward Yorna. Receiving Vincent's glance, Yorna looked briefly stunned, then pressed her lips together, released the hand of the deer-girl beside her — Tanza — and said,

"Tanza, ya look after His Excellency Vincent, ya hear!"

"—! Yes, understood."

At Yorna's call, Tanza dipped her chin, clasped both hands together, and lowered her stance slightly. Sensing the intent behind Tanza's movement, Vincent leapt lightly and set his foot on the girl's small hands.

The next instant, with the momentum of swinging up her slender arms, Tanza flung Vincent skyward — straight up, toward the topmost level of the Crystal Palace.

"—!"

Making up for insufficient distance and momentum by kicking off the castle wall, Vincent reached the topmost level, more than fifty meters up. The thought flickered through his mind that he'd just done something like Cecilus cutting corners on stairs, but that thought dissolved instantly.

Because up close, Olbart was a sight Vincent had never seen before.

"Kah-kah-kah...! You think somethin' like that's gonna kill me? Ain't that a laugh?"

Against the wall of the half-collapsed emplacement, Olbart sat with one knee raised, laughing in a low voice. But the strange old man's whole body was drenched in blood, and his characteristically overlong eyebrows drooped limply. Worst of all, following his right arm, his left arm too had been blown clean off.

For Olbart to be pushed this hard — just how powerful was the enemy that had been here?

—No. Was here, present tense.

"Talk before you die. What is the 'Witch' scheming?"

"Kah, you really are one for workin' an old man hard.... Take a look at Moguro, ya'll see."

Turning at Olbart's words, spoken quite literally while coughing blood, Vincent looked to the pedestal set at the back of the ravaged emplacement, and the green jewel embedded there — the mana core.

The Crystal Palace, built with a lavish use of magic crystal — in other words, a castle that was an extraordinary condensation of mana — was practically an enormous bomb. The reason it could sit calmly at the heart of the Empire, in the capital, lay in the existence of the mana core that controlled it all.

The mana core — this, then, was the heart of the Crystal Palace. —And Vincent's black eyes could see that the green glow of that heart, the mana core, was abnormally intensifying.

What that meant was—

"—You intend to overload the mana core and blow the capital sky-high, along with the Crystal Palace!"

"Olbart. Stopped it. 'Witch.' Stopped, partway."

To Vincent, shuddering as he grasped the true intent behind the scheme Olbart had spoken of, the mana core — Moguro Hagane's voice — confirmed it.

As stated, it was the mana core's doing that miraculously stabilized the Crystal Palace's magic crystal. The "Witch" had poured a massive amount of mana into that core, placing a tremendous strain on its processing capacity. By doing so, she'd schemed to strip the core of its control function, destabilize the balance of the magic crystal, and blow the city sky-high.

Having grasped that much, something snagged in Vincent's mind for a moment.

"The 'Witch' was present here? Before the conflagration?"

"No. 'Witch.' Not burned. Olbart and self. Attacked together."

Moguro's account did nothing to resolve Vincent's doubts.

The one called the "Witch" on this battlefield — Sphinx — should have been caught in Chisha and Natsuki Subaru's scheme, her soul burned away until she vanished.

Did that mean there were two of the "Witch"? Or else—

"What must take priority is this matter here."

Cutting off the thought before it could stray further, Vincent looked down at the mana core.

Thanks to Olbart's hard fight, the capital had been spared collapse from the core's runaway state — no, merely had it postponed. Looking at the core's condition, it was clear it had already lost its stability.

The core had caught fire, so to speak. Its explosion could no longer be avoided.

"—Moguro Hagane. You have done great service, until now."

The moment he was certain of that fact, Vincent found himself speaking those words to Moguro.

As the "Steelfolk" Moguro Hagane, this "Meteor" that was the Crystal Palace itself had served Vincent, Emperor of Vollachia, well. Frankly, among the "Nine Divine Generals," who were nothing but trouble except in raw ability, how greatly he had valued Moguro and Groovy.

To that loyal retainer Moguro, there was only one way Vincent could repay him.

"Your wish — the peace and stability of the Vollachia Empire — I shall see it fulfilled, without fail."

"—. Your Excellency. Grateful. Your Excellency. Does not lie."

"Fool. If necessary, I would deceive anyone, without limit."

"Deceives people. Does not deceive me."

At Moguro's words, spoken as if certain of it, Vincent let out a faint breath, his lips loosening slightly.

This "Meteor," which should have been nothing but a bloodless stone doll, was, in an empire that was a swirling vortex of scheming and machination, a presence so precious it seemed almost a sham.

"...What do you intend to do, Your Excellency?"

"There is no means to remove the mana core from its pedestal and carry it out. Then there is no choice but to burn away both the collapse of the core and the collapse of the Crystal Palace itself. —'Sunlight Sword' Vollachia."

Asked by Olbart, Vincent looked to the "Sunlight Sword" gripped in his hand.

Summoning Eugard and using both "Sunlight Swords" together — that would be meaningless. What the coming event required wasn't a matter of numbers, but of output.

To burn away the mana core and the power it contained with the flame of the "Sunlight Sword." Before the power overflowing from the core could swallow the Crystal Palace, the capital, and blow it all sky-high.

Likely, it would all be settled in the span of a single instant.

"Or do you have another plan?"

"Hm? Plan? Hell, I don't even have hands left to surrender with. If Your Excellency can't think of anything, I doubt anyone in the whole world — forget the Empire — could either."

"—Hmph."

At the strange old man shrugging his armless shoulders, Vincent gave a small snort.

To think no one in the world but Vincent could come up with a countermeasure — quite the overestimation. If it had been Subaru, or Chisha, or Priscilla here instead of Vincent—

"Do not entertain useless thoughts, foolish Vincent Vollachia."

Spitting that condemnation of himself as a fool, Vincent gripped the "Sunlight Sword" with both hands.

Facing the mana core's intensifying glow head-on, he prayed to the "Sunlight Sword's" radiance — the crimson treasured sword of the Vollachia Empire — for it to unleash its power, as the reigning Emperor of Vollachia.

The next instant, the air around Vincent — no, around the "Sunlight Sword" — began to hold heat, and the world began to warp and shimmer like a mirage. Dust burned, the air scorched, the mana in the atmosphere forcibly overwritten into fire attribute, lending force to the "Sunlight Sword's" brilliance.

The blade, glowing deep red, began to whiten with the heat it emitted, the signs of rising flame.

"Well, this here's dangerous, ain't it."

Present in the same space, and taking the full brunt of the heat given off by the "Sunlight Sword," Olbart registered quiet astonishment at the rising power of that treasured blade.

To begin with, the "Sunlight Sword" was a thing rarely drawn at all. Even Olbart, who had likely witnessed three generations of emperors in his lifetime, was probably seeing the "Sunlight Sword" display this much power for the first time.

Vincent himself was releasing the "Sunlight Sword's" power to this extent for the first time as well.

However—

"—It is not enough."

Feeling a surge of power unlike any before, Vincent became certain of a shortfall in output.

Calculating from the ever-intensifying pressure of the mana core and the proportion of magic crystal used in the Crystal Palace's construction, the "Sunlight Sword's" current output could not burn away the full force of the coming explosion. It wasn't a matter of merely dampening it somewhat. What was required was complete annihilation.

And that was difficult to achieve with Vincent's incomplete "Sunlight Sword."

Vincent Vollachia's "Sunlight Sword" could not display its true worth.

The reason was simple and clear — Vincent had let his own younger sister, Priscilla Benedict, live, and had never properly completed the "Imperial Selection Ceremony." He was a false emperor, one who had deceived every citizen of the Empire to take the throne, never having earned true qualification as emperor.

Therefore, the "Sunlight Sword" Vollachia would not grant Vincent its true power.

As for the "Sunlight Sword's" true worth, the same held for Eugard, one of the past emperors as well. The "Sunlight Sword" lent its power, by virtue of imperial bloodline, even to Eugard the Undead, but the sword's true might could dwell only in the current, genuine emperor.

Was he to make his stand with an incomplete "Sunlight Sword"? Or else—

"—In exchange for my life."

He would offer that price, and demand the summoning of the "Sunlight Sword's" true flame.

It was a choice that risked breaking the promise he had just made with Moguro moments ago, but if it was necessary, then Vincent would do it.

Everything was the result of the paths Vincent had chosen. Piling up the results of every choice, he stood, now, here.

Therefore—

"The duty I must fulfill—"

"—There's no such thing, Abel-chin."

Praying to the "Sunlight Sword" gripped in his hands, ready to offer even his life as the price, Vincent — his hand was pressed down by a white hand belonging to someone who had come to stand beside him.

Into the gap in his awareness, undivided by anything else in his intense concentration, a tall woman had slipped close. At the blue eyes peering into the side of his face, Vincent's eyes went wide.

"Medium O'Connell..."

"Ehehe, I'm here."

"——"

At Medium's bashful reply, Vincent lost his words.

This place, where the heat of the "Sunlight Sword" kept climbing, was no longer even fit for an ordinary person to breathe in. And yet she had come barging suddenly into such a place, smiling all the while.

Smiling, she kept his hand pinned down, and said,

"I know Abel-chin's carrying some huge responsibility. But you can't just go and die. I hate that more than anything."

"Consider the gravity of the matter. To begin with, you have no standing to offer me your opinion."

"Whaaat! Yes I do! I'm gonna be Abel-chin's wife, aren't I!?"

"That is..."

"You said it was fine!"

"——"

"You said it!"

Pressed with that much force, Vincent was overwhelmed by Medium's intensity. It was different from pressure applied by those seeking his life, or by potential political rivals.

It was a kind of thing against which Vincent had prepared no defense within himself.

"Face reality. However much you regret losing your seat as empress, the Empire itself, that matters most, is—"

Just as he tried to push her aside like that—

A sudden impact struck his cheek, and Vincent, his face knocked aside, stared in shock. Still wide-eyed, he turned and looked at Medium. —At Medium, who had just slapped the Emperor's cheek.

"Don't you ever talk about my reason for worrying about Abel-chin like that again."

"You..."

Her cheeks tightening sharply, Medium declared that, and Vincent couldn't help but blink his eyes rapidly. At the sight, Medium gave an "ah" of surprise, and said,

"That's the first time I've seen Abel-chin close both eyes~"

Said with such a beaming smile, Vincent could say nothing.

His eyes, kept open. Should he ever close both eyes at once, it would endanger the Emperor's life. That was the Vollachian iron rule, one Vincent had upheld even while sleeping.

That rule, he'd been made to break. Not by someone trying to take his life, but by a woman slapping his cheek out of concern for him.

"Kah-kah-kah-kah! Oi oi, ain't really the time for that, but ain't that a sight?"

Noting Vincent's shaken state, the dying old man loudly stuck his oar in. That restored Vincent's power of thought and judgment, and he gritted his teeth.

It was true that, for a moment, the wind had been taken out of his sails, but nothing about the situation had changed. The capital, the Empire, the world, were all still on the verge of ending, unchanged. That—

"It's okay, Abel-chin. It's okay."

To Vincent, jaw clenched, Medium still smiled at him like that.

Baseless, purely emotional talk, nothing but hope spoken aloud. In the arena of argument, it was the very thing Vincent despised from the bottom of his heart as having the least developmental value.

—And yet, how many times had that very despised, emotional talk tormented him, and saved him, since he'd been driven from the throne?

"—So, how about it, Your Excellency? My little sister figure's quite something, wouldn't you say?"

It was at that moment.

That a voice spoke, one that seemed to see through the reason for Vincent's silence as if it were its own affair, as though he too had experienced the very same thing.

In the end, whatever he tried his hand at, he'd always done it halfway — Balleroy Temeglyph mocked himself with that thought.

Even while alive, he'd served the Empire, and defied the Empire.

And even after death, he'd defied the Empire again, and in the end—

"'Serving the Empire' ain't really my style, y'know."

In the chest of Balleroy, who let that thought spill out, there was a hole — proof of his defeat, punched through by the magic bullet of the worst mage around, and left gaping open still.

—The battle Balleroy had staked his wish on had ended in utter defeat, against an enemy who chose any means necessary.

The mage in question had said something like, "It was a coin toss — either of us coulda won, yeahhh," but that was nothing more than a comforting lie.

Likely, against that man, Balleroy would lose a hundred times out of a hundred tries. That was simply the nature of their match-up.

For the sake of a single purpose, that man could become coldly ruthless without limit.

In the end, though he'd been revived on account of unfinished business and lingering regrets, the very moment he'd failed to see it through, Balleroy had proven no match for someone with blood colder than even the Undead.

Having Medium tossed aside, he hadn't been able to abandon his little sister figure, knowing that to overlook her meant her death. That was Balleroy's weakness, and the entirety of the reason he'd lost again.

"Nuh-uh. It's 'cause Bal-nii's kind."

To the reason for his defeat that Balleroy himself had accepted, Medium, however, would not accept it.

Medium, who called weakness and softness "kindness," was his proud little sister figure, whose straightforward nature and bright smile hadn't faded a bit even as she'd grown taller. Yes — a little sister figure. Balleroy had truly been blessed in the people he'd met.

Serena, Flop, Medium, Madelyn — all of them were the same.

Not to mention his partner Carillon, and the many imperial soldiers who'd stood alongside Balleroy as he rose through the ranks as a "General," and the "Nine Divine Generals" too.

But — and yet — even grateful for every one of those encounters, he hadn't been able to let go.

That was—

"But still, Bal-nii, you just couldn't quit, huh."

He was struck by the sight of Medium smiling like that through tears.

The girl he'd thought hadn't changed a bit since they'd first met had, in fact, properly grown — from a girl into a woman — and had come to be able to smile in different ways like this, too.

Only he himself remained unchanged, forever, stamping his feet in the same place.

"...Seems the 'Witch' can only revive those who died within the Empire. To break that restriction, she'd have to destroy the Empire, and expand her range beyond it, to the Kingdom, the city-states, and so on. Only once that's done, finally, only then, at last..."

"—You could see Miles-nii again?"

"——"

"I get it. 'Cause I've been watching Bal-nii this whole time."

At Medium's words, Balleroy felt two piercing emotions.

One was self-mockery at having his inner heart seen through. The other was a faint jealousy. He understood the meaning behind the smile Medium was showing for the first time.

"Medi, got yourself a man you've fallen for?"

"Eh!? N-no I don't! ...Probably."

"The one whose smiling face comes to mind first when you close your eyes — that's the one, ain't it."

"That's just Onii-chan! Besides, Abel-chin never smiles at all... ah."

Medium clapped a hand over her mouth, her cheeks flushing red. At his little sister figure's reaction — another first — Balleroy laughed. Laughing, he slowly pushed himself up.

Balleroy really was, through and through, a halfway sort of man.

If he hated that about himself and could cut everything and everyone off cleanly, like that mage who'd defeated him, maybe he could have become a different man.

"A cold-blooded bastard like that, no thanks."

He couldn't be that. He wouldn't become that.

Halfway even while he lived, he remained, even in death, still halfway.

And it was precisely because he was a man so ambiguous even about living and dying, about wishes and grudges, that he could say this:

"Well then, guess I'll go help out Medi's fella she's fallen for."

"I still don't know that for sure!"

Her face turning bright red, wearing an expression that belonged to neither her childhood nor her girlhood, there wasn't a shred of persuasiveness in her insistence.

If anyone made Medium wear that face, and still made her sad, then Balleroy would kill that man with his own two hands. Setting aside all thought of himself, that was what he decided.

"—!!"

At Balleroy's self-serving, brotherly resolve, Carillon, one in body and soul with him, gave a spirited, pleased neigh.

—At that instant, without distinction between the living and the dead, everything in the capital looked up at the sky.

No one could explain why.

As for why they themselves had looked up, many could probably explain that much. Those with sharp senses felt a powerful pulse of mana; those with good hearing heard the ringing cry of a Flying Dragon. Some had had their eyes on the source of the Magic Crystal Cannon blast just before; others had had the castle as their destination.

But while each could explain their own reason, none could explain everyone's.

If anyone could, it would only be that leading man who decided this world's logic within himself, and to hear him tell it, the answer was simple and clear—

"—Well, of course, because it's someone's climax!"

He would surely have declared it like that, loudly, with a bright and beaming smile.

And there was not a single person in this Vollachia Empire who could deny it.

That was due to his sheer ability, and the one person who, for reasons other than ability alone, could have thrown a "no" back in that leading man's face, was, at this very moment, watching the reason to look up at the sky play out right before her eyes.

—Higher and higher, climbing and climbing, a dazzling line of green light traced itself into the sky directly above the Crystal Palace.

It traced on and on, and before anyone knew it, it plunged into what looked like a single vast sea of clouds, as though every cloud in the capital's sky had gathered into one—

"——"

—And in that instant, as though the sky itself blinked, the whole world flashed in a burst of dazzling light.
