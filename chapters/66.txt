Arc 2 Chapter 42: "Steamed Potato"

Far away, consciousness drifts on the waves.
Rippling wavelets, in the midst of drowsiness, consciousness rises and sinks in the gap between dream and reality.

"—Is there any other way to—"

"—shall we? As for the rest, do as you please, I suppose."

Far away—no, close by, at the border between there and here—someone's voices could be heard arguing.
A clinging voice, a rejecting voice, a tearful voice, a voice frozen of emotion. Voices.

Suddenly, his palm is wrapped in something soft.
From the memory of having been touched like this before, he's reminded that it's the feel of someone's hand.
He must have felt this warmth once before, too.

But the sensation of that palm suddenly grows distant.
It leaves his hand, far, far away, too high to reach, higher and higher.
And then—

"—I will save you. I promise."

Only that fierce resolve remains, left behind.
Everything fades away, is left behind, departs. Far, far away.

And then—

Forcibly cut off from consciousness, only to wake like this—how many times had this happened by now?

Gazing at the pattern on an unfamiliar ceiling, Subaru pondered that vaguely.
There was an old pillow-talk line about finishing before you finish counting the ceiling stains, but given the standard of civilization in this other world, the stain count was probably going to make for a pretty long haul—he thought, wryly amused at the pointlessness of the thought, when his side seized up and an involuntary groan escaped him.

"Ohh, owww..."

He reached to touch his aching side, and a burst of wrongness erupted from the arm he'd swung around. Following that sense of wrongness, he brought his left hand up in front of his face, and seeing how ugly it looked, understood at once.

From the fingertips of his left arm all the way to his wrist, pale scars rose up, packed densely together. Worse, those wounds were held closed with crooked, misshapen thread, in a job that would've been entirely believable as the work of a child with no talent for sewing gone berserk with a needle.

And that wasn't the only place with something wrong.
When he pulled up the hem of his clothes, he found similar stitching on his cramped right side. Not to mention both ankles, around his right upper arm, near his right shoulder, and even his backside—too many to count. Every one of them bore scars and stitches, enough to make him feel like a bit of a cyborg.

As he ran his hands over the transformed parts of his body, as if to confirm them, his memory began to return slowly, along with a faint ache.
Right, he'd gone deep into the forest to search for the children, and encountered a Witchbeast, and—

"I was sure I'd died... but I guess not."

He'd shielded Rem from the attack and taken the beast's fangs across his entire body.
The savage jaws should have easily crunched through Subaru's flesh, torn it apart, shredded him like a rag. He'd felt his life spilling out of him, half-certain it was over.

"Can't quite call it a mummy... Looking at how much needle and thread went into repairing me, maybe a jiangshi-type image is closer to the mark."

Or if they'd screwed a bolt into his head, he'd be more of a Frankenstein image.
And having thought that far, he suddenly became aware of his own carefree stupidity.

"—Right. No time to be joking around. Rem, and the kids—"

Springing up from the simple bed, Subaru looked around to get his bearings.
An unfamiliar ceiling, a shabby bed. A cramped room with worn-out furnishings—by no stretch of the imagination did this look like Roswaal Manor.
And then, right by the entrance—sitting on a wooden chair, her face bowed, asleep—he noticed the presence of a silver-haired girl.

"Emilia-tan."

There was no response to his call.
Her dozing sleep seemed to run deep, judging by the depth of her breathing. Her beautiful silver hair, rarely out of place, was slightly disheveled as if there'd been no time to tend to it, and worse, there were awful stains of blood and mud clinging all over her clothes.

Himself, injured. Himself, waking up on a bed. Emilia, sitting beside him in a chair, asleep. And her clothes stained with blood and the like.
Putting those facts together, even Subaru's slow head could grasp the situation.
In short—

"So I've gone and racked up another debt to Emilia-tan..."

"I wonder. I think you should be at least a little grateful to Lia too, but this time I think there's someone else you should be even more grateful to, personally."

What answered Subaru's self-reproachful mutter was a gray cat that emerged from within the silver hair. He gave sleeping Emilia's cheek a light pat with his paw pad, then floated up to hover in front of Subaru's face.

"Yo, morning. How's it feel to wake up?"

"Hard to call it tip-top shape. I'm a cyborg without even knowing it. Makes me want to escape before they mess with my brain and go get revenge on the organization as a masked hero."

"Modification... ah, you mean the thread. It couldn't be helped. Just healing you wasn't going to be enough with the mana you had in you. Even stitching you back together needed a tool's help. Without it, you'd probably be in pieces by now, you know?"

"That's a chilling thing to say! Even so, couldn't someone a little better at sewing have done it?"

Setting aside the stitches themselves, he had something to say about the poor craftsmanship. Especially as someone who held the title of S-rank in sewing, he wanted to drill the basics into whoever did this.
Puck waved a paw at Subaru's words, saying "now, now,"

"It's been a while since I've put a needle through a person's body. I ended up putting way too much strength into it here and there, it was rough going."

"Of all people, you!? More like, how'd you even manage to sew with that tiny body!?"

"It was easy since I don't move around. The bleeding was bad in a lot of spots, so I had to freeze the wounds while I worked—bear with that, okay?"

"So that's why the scars are so oddly white... Well, I'm a guy, so I'm not gonna whine about scarring or anything."

Looking over the various scars, Subaru turned to face Puck properly again. Puck blinked in surprise at Subaru's sudden posture, but Subaru bowed deeply to those black eyes.

"Thanks to you I'm alive. Thank you. I got to keep my life again."

"Yeah, yeah, it's good to be honest. And this time, you did enough to deserve it too. It'd be a real shame if you'd collapsed after all that. So it's fine."

Puck responded without embarrassment to Subaru's words of thanks. With the weight lifted off his chest by those words, Subaru said "well then," as a preface,

"'Did enough to deserve it'—nice words to hear, but... seriously, what happened after that? Honestly, my memory cuts off around when the dogs started chomping on me in the forest."

"Chomping is a cute way to put it. Judging from the state you were in when they carried you back, I'd say it was more like 'chomp-crunch-squelch-splat-crack-splurt.'"

"Whoa, hey, with sound effects like that I'm definitely dead. That's a missing-five-or-six-arms kind of scenario, one of me isn't enough for that."

"Yeah, well, that's why the blue-haired maid was in a pretty rough state too."

Said so casually and lightly, Subaru's throat froze up involuntarily. At his reaction, Puck added "though," before continuing,

"In her case, her wounds heal fast thanks to the oni transformation. By the time she'd carried Subaru back to the village, there wasn't any noticeable injury left on her. Didn't even need healing magic."

"Don't scare me for nothing. ...Anyway, so Rem's back in the village too. And the other one, the kid who got carried back with me?"

"Safe, so you can relax. Seven kids total, the six plus one more. And it's a good thing Subaru and the blue-haired girl were both strong enough to carry them. Your call turned out to be exactly right."

Puck clapped his paws together while saying so, though no sound actually came out—the softness of his paw pads got in the way of any clapping noise. It was a heartwarming sight that almost made Subaru's mouth curl into a smile, but he shook his head to chase the feeling off.

"Puck, I've got a few things I want to ask, that okay?"

"As long as it's quiet enough not to wake Lia. My own manifestation runs on mana I stockpile overnight, but when I was out during the night, I was using Lia's Od."

"Od?"

"Mana is what's drawn in from the outside world. Od, on the other hand, is what's originally stored within a living creature's body—literally, the power of the soul. Unlike mana, using Od means shaving away at oneself, so I'd rather not use it much."

As he spoke, Puck cast a glance toward Emilia, the way one might look at a troublesome daughter.
Drawn along, Subaru found himself gazing at her sleeping face too, and found himself readily accepting what Puck had just said. If it was Emilia, she wouldn't hesitate to shave away at herself if it meant she had to save someone. If Puck had needed it last night, and if it meant using herself to provide it, he could easily imagine there hadn't been a shred of hesitation in her decision.

"So then, does that mean I got saved at the cost of Emilia-tan shaving off part of herself?"

"That's part of it, yeah. But yesterday, my main role was mostly with the kids—the ones Subaru brought back from the forest. Breaking their curse was the main purpose."

"The curse—right! That too. Are the kids okay? The curse?"

"You can relax about that too."

Puck raised a paw to stop Subaru, who looked ready to press closer, and twisted his cat face into a charming grin,

"Healing magic held the weakening down pretty well, and the curse-breaking went smoothly, so there's no problem. The children's curse is definitely lifted."

Puck thumped his own chest as if stamping a seal of approval. At the sight, Subaru let out a deep breath, relieved that his actions hadn't ended up meaningless after all.
And then, in the way that a human body is a fickle thing once relief sets in,

"Oh."

The instant relief took over his chest, Subaru's stomach let out a great howl.
Touching his belly and feeling something like pain in its emptiness, he remembered that he'd missed dinner the night before.

"For reference, I don't get hungry as long as I'm taking in mana."

"Wasn't asking. Still, since mana's apparently everywhere, that's kind of like a plant getting full just breathing the air."

"Don't need water or sunlight either. All I need is love. That's an important thing never to forget when dealing with spirits."

"Yeah, yeah. Hey, mind if I go check outside?"

Pointing at the door as he asked, Puck, arms crossed, nodded several times.

"I think that's a good idea. Move your body a bit, check out your patchwork self. Oh, and the thread will absorb into your body on its own and disappear once it settles in, so don't worry about that."

"Oh, good. Trying to rock these earthworm-squiggle stitch marks as a fashion statement would've been a tall order. Like, born two hundred light-years too soon or something."

Puck tilted his head, still smiling, in response to the all-out pun.
Guess it didn't land. It was supposed to be the golden pattern of "a light-year measures distance, you know" leading into "that's a difference of sensibility from a whole different star"—but apparently it hadn't come across.

With Puck's permission, Subaru headed out of the room.
On the way, he gave a small bow to Emilia sleeping right beside the door. Catching sight of her peaceful sleeping face as he bowed made his breath go rough with the urge to do something mischievous, but the strict gaze of the guardian nearby made him give up on the idea. Reluctantly, he headed outside.
And then—

"Ah, well, I guess that's to be expected."

Stepping out of the room and through the entrance right in front of him to poke his head outside, Subaru muttered that at the sight of the village in an uproar.

It was still not long after sunrise, but a great many figures had already gathered in the plaza at the center of the village.
It was a small village. Given the number of people gathered there, it was probably safe to say most of the village's population was present. The elderly, women, and children huddled close together looking anxious, surrounded protectively by a group centered on sturdy young men.
The ones forming a protective ring around the villagers were the young men's group who had also worked hard searching for the children the night before. Subaru scanned the faces within that ring, failing to find the ones he was looking for, and cocked his head before turning toward another building with a lot of foot traffic.
But before that—

"—Barusu, you're awake."

Called from behind, Subaru stopped and turned around.
He could tell who it was from the way he'd been addressed, but seeing her face in person still brought a natural wash of relief.

Standing behind him was the peach-haired maid—Ram.
She had the sleeves of her familiar servant's uniform rolled up, carrying something like a basket piled high with a large quantity of what looked like steamed potatoes.

A faint scent close to salt drifted from the lightly steaming potatoes, and it made Subaru's empty stomach cheer with anticipation and excitement.
As Subaru's stomach let out a sound close to the onomatopoeia Puck had just used, Ram gave a "hmph" through her nose and said,

"Causing all that worry with such a grievous injury, and the moment you wake up you go begging for food. How pathetic. Did you catch something from being bitten by a dog?"

"What would 'catching dog' even look like—well, let's not get into that. Hmm, huh, so, you were worried about me?"

Stretching, bending, swaying side to side as he approached Ram, Subaru wore a smug grin.
At Subaru's teasing attitude, Ram narrowed her eyes,

"Eat this."

"Guhoph!"

A steamed potato was forcibly rammed into his mouth, literally crushing his throat.
On top of that, blazing heat ravaged the inside of his mouth, and Subaru tilted his head back, breathing roughly like an animal—"hah, hah!" And then,

"I thought I was gonna die! But it was good!"

"Wasn't it delicious. Fresh... no, freshly steamed."

"What's with that smug look, it's annoying! But it was good!"

"There, there, here's another one, so hush up and stuff your face."

Handed the potato, he received it, delighted like a child. Ram watched him for a moment with something like contempt, then,

"Well. As for last night's matter, Ram will honestly thank you. Good work."

"'Good work,' still talking down to me huh. Nah, it's fine, but... is that really something you'd thank me for?"

"If the villagers suffer harm, the responsibility falls on their lord. Thinking of what might have happened if the children had been left threatened by that pack of Ulgarm... Barusu's actions were correct."

"Ulgarm, huh..."

That must be the name of that black Witchbeast.
Quite a grand name for it. Just hearing it conveyed the sense that it was the kind of thing whose mere encounter threatened one's life.
At Subaru's nod, Ram turned her gaze toward the forest,

"Last night, Ram re-tied the barrier that had apparently frayed. After that, Ram spent the whole night checking the barrier for problems, so there shouldn't be any Witchbeasts slipping through to this side."

"As long as nothing slips through from this side, huh? If the brats cross the barrier and come back with a baby, that defeats the purpose."

"That stings. Ram let her guard down, since there hadn't even been minor skirmishes with Witchbeasts since the barrier was put up. —Ram will make sure they understand."

The slightly harsh way she put it was probably because she had her own irritation about the matter. For Subaru's part, his displeasure was about being dragged into it, but for her, it was probably directed at the villagers who hadn't properly heeded Roswaal's instructions.
Thinking of her excessive loyalty, Subaru couldn't help but feel a bit sorry for whichever villager was about to get lectured.

While silently offering a prayer of sympathy for that unfortunate villager, Subaru suddenly felt his head spin.
His body swayed involuntarily, and as he pressed a hand to his head, Ram lightly supported him with her hand,

"Don't push yourself. Honestly, if not for the Great Spirit-sama and Beatrice-sama, that was the kind of wound that should have killed you."

"If Bea-ko's name is coming up, that means I owe her a debt too... ouch, that stings!"

"Even at the Great Spirit-sama's request, it's rare for Beatrice-sama to leave the manor, let alone the Forbidden Library. Outside the manor, she isn't able to act at full capacity."

Steadying himself with Ram's support, Subaru shook his head lightly.
Something felt off, like he was low on blood. There was a sluggishness similar to his first morning at Roswaal Manor, and the heaviness in his fingers and toes was probably a serious mana shortage. According to Puck, they'd stitched together quite a severe wound.
Given how little mana Subaru had stored to begin with, it wouldn't be strange if he was running on empty.

"Ram will watch over the village's situation a while longer before returning to the manor. Ram is also concerned about the children whose curse was lifted... and about countermeasures for the Ulgarm."

"Countermeasures, huh—can't you just put up the barrier and call it good?"

"That might have been fine for the skirmishes up until now, perhaps."

To Subaru's modest question, Ram responded with her usual expressionless face.
But being met by the gaze of eyes that were supposed to hold no emotion, Subaru couldn't stop a chill from running down his spine, as if ice had been slipped down it.
She spoke to him, word by word, as if driving each one home,

"Causing harm to the village—worse, to Barusu, who is connected to Roswaal-sama's estate—after doing all this, to just leave it be without interfering? Unthinkable."

"S-so what are you planning to do...?"

Unconsciously slipping into polite speech, Subaru watched as Ram's intimidating air dissolved, and she lightly combed her fingers through her peach hair,

"Exterminate them. —Though that's a matter for once Roswaal-sama returns. Once Roswaal-sama is back, it shouldn't take much time at all."

Faced with such absolute trust, Subaru had no room for rebuttal.
Even without knowing the extent of Roswaal's actual strength, he could well appreciate the depth of Ram's overwhelming faith in him.

After that, Subaru pilfered two more steamed potatoes off Ram before parting ways.
After parting, she headed toward the group of villagers still huddled together anxiously. It was, in her own way, probably an act of consideration. That her way of expressing it was through steamed potatoes felt exactly like Ram's characteristically dry, understated way of doing things.

"But man, these steamed potatoes are good. Seriously, the salt's on point."

Munching on the steamed potato he'd received, Subaru continued wandering the village.
While checking on his own condition, he simultaneously checked on the safety of the children he'd recovered from the forest. The children themselves were still asleep, worn out from the previous night and the mana expended in breaking the curse, but their parents showered Subaru with an almost excessive amount of gratitude.
Honestly, since Subaru hadn't done it to be thanked, he panicked to death, and after being flustered more than he'd ever been, unable to pull off much of a joke, he fled the scene out of sheer embarrassment.

Wiping cold sweat at the unexpected welcome, Subaru judged that he'd accomplished his main objective. Thinking he'd head back to the room where he'd first woken and wait for Emilia to get up, he suddenly realized he still hadn't seen the blue-haired girl.

—A flash crossed his mind: the image of the oni, drenched in spattered blood, laughing wildly.

In that instant, what shook Subaru's body wasn't, he thought, an emotion like fear.
It was something more overwhelming, something hard to put into words—like standing before an existence so far above him as a living creature that he had to look up at it. Perhaps people called that awe.

With white horns growing from her forehead, wielding inhuman power to lay waste to the Witchbeasts—that figure had been the very image of the oni passed down in legend.
Facing her, would he be able to act the same as always? There was that anxiety.

"No, no, I was totally fine with Ram, the twin, right there. If Rem's an oni then Ram's an oni too... man, did I have some crazy rare experience or what!?"

Making light of his own cowardice, Subaru shook his head and tried to refresh his memory.
It was because he kept recalling only the most shocking scenes from last night that he felt this way. Something more vivid, some refreshing scene that would let him forget the harsh imagery—

An image surfaced: an iron ball crushing a Witchbeast's skull, blasting apart its torso, punching straight through the pack.

"Wait, even with the oni part left out, all I can think of is splatter. And ugh, remembering it's making me feel sick..."

The banquet of blood and viscera came suddenly back to mind, and Subaru pressed a hand over his mouth against a wave of nausea.
Stumbling toward the edge of the building, he crouched down in the underbrush, breathing roughly. Somehow, it seemed he might be able to hold it back, and he sighed in relief.
After all, this was the steamed potato he'd finally managed to stuff down after being famished. And more than that, a precious item infused with Ram's own brand of consideration. If he wasted it, he'd never be able to call himself a man again.

Somehow managing to hold back the urge to vomit, Subaru nodded to himself, praising his own restraint.
Then, raising his head to properly face Rem once more—

"—Perfect timing, I suppose."

From beyond the underbrush, a girl with cream-colored hair in vertical ringlets appeared. Recognizing Subaru, she beckoned him over,

"There's something to discuss, I suppose. Come with me a moment—" "BLEUGHHHHHHHHHH"

Overlapping the tail end of Beatrice's affected words, everything he'd supposedly held back came out all at once.

"KYAAAAAAAAAAAAA!!"

Surprisingly cute scream for her, Subaru thought, even as—having let it out once, there was no stopping now—he continued spewing up potato.
White, white, the steamed potato kept coming, without end.

Once the commotion had died down, Beatrice led Subaru to the back of a building right by the edge of the Witchbeast forest.

This area used to be lined with small privately-run shops, but naturally, the morning after all the Witchbeast commotion, there wasn't a single villager idly wandering around here.
Now that the row of houses stood empty, it was, indeed, an ideal spot for a private conversation within the village.

"So, what business could you possibly have calling me out to a place like this?"

"Not exactly the attitude of someone who was retching just a moment ago, I suppose. Perhaps some reflection is in order."

"Ah, right, thanks for rubbing my back, that helped a little. Oh, and come to think of it, I heard you helped stitch my body back together too."

"When you thank Betty the same way for both of those things, Betty starts to wonder how she's even supposed to deal with you, I suppose."

Either way, since he really was grateful, he'd like her to just accept it honestly.
But setting aside Subaru's musings, Beatrice, uncharacteristically, kept letting her gaze wander restlessly, fidgeting with the hem of her skirt with her fingers as though troubled.

It was the attitude of someone with something hard to say.
It struck Subaru as unlike her—she was usually fairly blunt with him, saying whatever came to mind. Perhaps it was also because that expression made her look, for once, age-appropriate—something like a "child afraid of being scolded by a parent."

Faced with that expression, even Subaru couldn't bring himself to roughly press her for more. Crossing his arms and leaning back against the building, he decided to wait for her words.
But that stance of waiting didn't need to hold for very long.

The fact that Subaru hadn't pushed for more seemed to have, conversely, pushed her toward a decision.
Beatrice closed her eyes, then, as if steeling her resolve, opened them again.
And then—

"Nii-cha has his own reasons for doing what he does, I suppose. But keeping quiet about it wouldn't be fair to you, would it, I suppose."

Nii-cha, the one she affectionately called by that name, could only be Puck.
Recalling the little gray cat he'd just been talking with, Subaru tilted his head, voicing his confusion at her tone.
Beatrice, facing that Subaru, said,

"Nii-cha favors that mixed-blood girl, so he prioritizes her. That's the right thing for him to do, as a spirit. But as for Betty, she has her complaints, I suppose."

"What are you..."

The words Subaru meant to say—"trying to say"—went unfinished.
Before he could continue, Beatrice spoke first, cutting him off.

She told him:

"—In less than half a day, you're going to die, I suppose."
