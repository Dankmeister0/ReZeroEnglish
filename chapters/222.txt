Arc 4 Chapter 56: "The Sanctuary's Reason for Being"

The place Ryuzu led Subaru to was the same detached little house where she'd once invited him for tea and conversation.

"Sit yourself down on the bed, wherever. I'll brew us somethin' to drink."

"Want me to make the tea? Ram trained me up some, so I figure I'm at least decent at it."

"I'd love nothin' more, but it don't look like that's gonna work out just now."

With a note of laughter in her voice, Ryuzu pointed—at Subaru seated on the bed, and at the girl who was the very image of Ryuzu, gripping the hem of his clothes and refusing to let go.

She was, in essence, a copy of Ryuzu—and after much agonizing over what to call her,

"Anyway, Pico here still won't let go of me, no matter how long it's been."

"I have my doubts about that name 'Pico,' but given how things turned out, I suppose there's no helping it—best just accept it. That's what comes of carelessly touching somethin' you don't understand."

"Well, that's easy for you to say..."

Ryuzu had a point to make, but blaming Subaru for carelessness when he'd been groping around blind at the time was more than a little unreasonable. Wearing a sullen look of protest, Subaru watched as Ryuzu returned, tea brewed, tray in hand.

"Here. It's hot, so let it cool before you drink."

"I'm not a kid. I'm not gonna go burnin' my mouth 'cause I drank it too fast."

"I've got someone close by who's forever restless and can't handle hot food, so warnin' folks has become a habit of mine."

At the teasing remark, the face that came to Subaru's mind as the prime suspect for a weak tolerance to hot food was Garfiel's.

A boy who turned into a giant tiger—sure enough, restlessness paired with an inability to handle hot food seemed to suit him perfectly. And the fact that he didn't seem like the type to learn from his mistakes matched expectations too, prejudiced as that assumption might be.

Just as Ryuzu had warned, the tea was brewed quite hot. Subaru took a sip anyway, wetting his parched tongue, and let out a breath. Now that he thought about it, this was the first time he'd had anything to drink since his Return by Death—since he'd woken up in the crypt.

"No wonder it tastes good, even if it does just taste like leaves."

"I feel like that's a rather unfair review."

"Just your imagination, just your imagination."

Subaru gulped down the tea he wasn't fond of, then set the cup back on the tray with a clatter. Then he turned to face Ryuzu, who had pulled up a chair opposite the bed and sat regarding him with an easy, relaxed posture.

"Alright, now that things have settled down some, mind if we get into it?"

"Mm. There's plenty I need to tell you too, on my end."

At her willingness to sit down and talk plainly, Subaru felt a swell of relief even as his mind kept working.

He'd had chances before this to sit face-to-face with people connected to the matter and talk things over. And yet he still hadn't reached the truth—partly because they deliberately withheld information, but there was a more fundamental problem than that. Namely:

He himself hadn't yet arrived at the real question he needed to ask.

The question that would connect every answer into a single thread—Subaru hadn't reached it yet. That was why he couldn't tell when he was being deflected, or when he was asking the wrong questions entirely. Asking the person who knew about the thing he needed to know—something that simple was, for Subaru, still impossibly far out of reach.

"That building... that facility, from before. What is it, exactly?"

"Hmm... shall we start with that question first, then?"

What had come to him on the spur of the moment was an innocuous question, more a probing feint than anything else. A question meant to gauge, before he got to the heart of the matter, just how willing Ryuzu was to face him head-on right now.

In response, Ryuzu ran a finger along her chin with a shrewdness that belied her appearance.

"If I'm to answer what that facility is, I'd say it's the core of this 'Sanctuary,' in a manner of speakin'. Put plainly, it's one piece of the Sanctuary's reason for bein'."

"The Sanctuary's reason for being...!?"

"To begin with, Su-boy. Who do you suppose needed this 'Sanctuary' badly enough to build it?"

"That'd be... Roswaal—"

Subaru started to answer reflexively, then caught himself—that wasn't right. Roswaal did indeed own this place called the Sanctuary and had managed it up to the present. But "built this place" was a different matter entirely.

"So the one who built this place is... the Witch of Greed. Echidna, is that it?"

"That's right. The one who built this was Echidna, that Witch. The Sanctuary was built because that Witch needed it. To put it in the extreme, that's all this place is."

"That's way too extreme. You're skipping the whole process... Come on, at least a little more detail."

"As for the contents of the experiment, you've already got the successful result sittin' right in front of you, haven't you, Su-boy?"

At Ryuzu's words, spoken with a twist of a smile at the corner of her mouth, Subaru felt his breath catch for a moment. He could grasp, however faintly, the meaning behind what she'd let slip. In other words, this was what she meant to say:

"The result of this place... is you, Ryuzu-san. And this girl too?"

"You're a kind boy, Su-boy. Or perhaps a soft one. You could just as well say plainly—we're experimental results."

Saying such a thing to her face was something even Subaru hesitated to do. Failing to read the room and being insensitive were two different things. And even Subaru could tell the mood here wasn't one he could joke his way through.

"There was a girl trapped inside that crystal, wasn't there, the spitting image of me?"

"...Yeah. Identical. You, and her too, includin' this girl. Should I think of you three as triplets or somethin'?"

"If lumpin' together similar-lookin' beings as kin is the standard, then 'triplets' falls a bit short for us, numbers-wise."

"A bit short, huh."

"A bit short."

Was the "bit" Ryuzu spoke of a difference of an entire order of magnitude? Having actually seen some twenty copies of Ryuzu with his own eyes, Subaru couldn't help but suspect exactly that.

He let out a breath, clearing away the stray thoughts. At this point, Ryuzu showed no sign of trying to deflect him. It was probably time to stop feeling each other out and push forward.

"—What's your relationship to the girl inside the crystal, Ryuzu-san?"

And so Subaru asked the question head-on. Ryuzu received it with the same unchanging, placid expression. Toying with the tip of her pale pink hair between her fingers, she cast a meaningful glance at Subaru—no, at the silent girl seated beside him.

"It ain't just my story to tell. That girl there stands in the same position I do."

"Including the girl in the crystal, you mean."

"No, that girl alone is different. The girl in the crystal is the real one."

The words were delivered so casually that for a moment Subaru couldn't take them in, and he furrowed his brow to show his incomprehension. Then, chewing over the meaning, he rose up off the bed.

"What d'you mean, the real one..."

"Don't rush me so. An old woman's stories need diggin' through memory first. You'll have to sit back nice and easy and wait."

"Don't go playin' the old-lady card on me now, not with somethin' besides your speech pattern. Given how bland and flavorless this girl next to me is, I already know that's nothin' but seasonin' on your part."

"Hmm, well, that's a sad misunderstandin' in its own right. To me, everything that makes up who I am now is a precious thing—somethin' I earned, a personality of my own, you might say."

"A personality... you earned?"

At a turn in the conversation too important to let pass, Subaru repeated the phrase back as a question. Ryuzu nodded, prefacing her reply with "That's right," and continued:

"Just as you figured, Su-boy, the original me was born just like that girl there—a vessel with nothin' poured into it. Over the years, I've lived on up to today by fillin' up that emptiness bit by bit."

"Wait, wait wait wait, you're gettin' ahead again. 'Born'? 'Empty'? What's that supposed to mean? It's gotta connect to what you said before, about the one in the crystal bein' the 'real' one, right?"

"What's inside that crystal is the real, original Ryuzu Meyer. Every other Ryuzu, myself included, is nothin' but an imitation of Ryuzu Meyer."

Ryuzu revealed her own origin so casually. Or rather—Subaru even found himself hesitating over whether he should still call her Ryuzu at all.

What Ryuzu had just described was exactly the hypothesis that had been vaguely forming in Subaru's mind ever since he'd laid eyes on the many Ryuzu duplicates. He'd half-suspected as much, and the only reason he hadn't been able to reach full conviction was nothing more than his own revulsion at the idea of a clone of someone he knew. To put it bluntly, even that was just a prejudice born of Subaru's own rigid, conventional way of thinking.

"Now that you've heard the word 'imitation'—does it change how you look at me?"

"...I dunno. Part of me wants to just say it don't matter, flat out. I do, I really do, but... if you're askin' whether I can say that with the person standin' right in front of me..."

—He couldn't say it with certainty.

Given that this was another world, strictly speaking, Ryuzu's existence couldn't really be called a clone. The way she'd been brought into being was probably different from the hazy image Subaru had in his head, too. There was no doubt that hers was a life created through a magical approach rather than a scientific one. And this seemed like exactly the moment he ought to say that there was no higher or lower nobility in how a life came to be born.

"I don't have the confidence to say it don't matter without my face givin' somethin' away. So I won't say it don't change anything."

"I'll correct what I said before. You're kind, Su-boy, and soft too... but more than that, it seems your roots run too honest for your own good."

It shouldn't have been words to be pleased about, and yet Ryuzu nodded at Subaru's answer looking satisfied. Chewing on his own frustration, Subaru looked at the girl seated beside him—the one he called Pico, who stood in the same position as Ryuzu.

—Pico, silently gripping the hem of Subaru's clothes, gazing vacantly around the room. Her eyes were surely taking in the same sights as his own, and yet with not a trace of emotion residing in them, they seemed almost like glass beads merely reflecting the scenery. Her expression never changed; in fact, he hadn't heard so much as a single word from her. And that—

"When you said this girl's empty..."

"It's because she's a duplicate only just born, only just given her role. She's got enough knowledge to carry out simple instructions, but beyond that, she's no different from a newborn. She don't cry or wail, and she don't need to eat, so I suppose that makes her less trouble, easier to manage."

"She doesn't need to eat...?"

"Duplicatin' a body ain't exactly a simple thing to pull off. Can't you guess at the principle behind how me and that girl are able to exist like this?"

At her testing tone, Subaru reined in his own impulse to demand the answer right away. He couldn't just be a child who only ever asked, only ever received. That wasn't what Ryuzu was asking of him.

Cowed somewhat by the earnest look in Ryuzu's eyes as she faced him, Subaru mulled over what she'd said, mustering all the knowledge he had—and what he arrived at was:

"Could it be... mana?"

What had spilled from his mouth had its roots in the great feline spirit that had flashed through his mind. As a spirit, Puck's body was formed using mana as a medium whenever he manifested. Apply that same principle, and might it not be possible to create a human-sized body as well?

At Subaru's line of thinking, Ryuzu raised her eyebrows and gave a small clap of her hands.

"Well done. To think you'd arrive at that answer on your own, without anyone tellin' you."

"You were leadin' me toward the answer the whole time, weren't you. It's just that I happened to have a spirit close by, so I could piece it together. ...So, is that the right answer?"

"Just about right. In truth, if you only use mana as a medium to build a body, the efficiency ends up terrible no matter what. That's the part the Witch of Greed forced her way around by constructin' a special formula."

"A forced-through formula, meanin' what, exactly?"

"She built, through the formula, a pseudo-organ for generatin' 'Od,' and had it store up a fixed amount of mana before manifestin' the body. That way, even though the body's made of mana, you end up producin' somethin' similar to a 'normal' living creature—one that actually possesses Od."

Od—unlike the mana that filled the atmosphere, this was an energy that served the same function as mana but was innately present within a living creature from the start. However, unlike mana, Od couldn't be drawn in from outside sources, and the total amount any single life could hold was fixed from birth. Wringing out Od meant directly shortening one's lifespan—it was the archetypal example of a trump card you couldn't afford to play, since depletion meant death. You might say it made it possible to cast magic using HP when MP ran short. Except that particular HP could never be recovered.

"You're sayin' this real casual, but... isn't that incredible? Reproducin' Od, even a pseudo version of it—that's basically creatin' life itself, isn't it?"

"Of course, it seems to be a phenomenon only made possible once quite a lot of particular conditions line up. Unfortunately, my head ain't good enough to understand the finer details. —But you can take it as fact that the Witch succeeded in creatin' life."

"That's a hell of a story... turns out she really was somethin', huh."

The white-haired Witch surfaced in his mind's eye, and he could almost picture her looking down at him with a somewhat smug expression. But right after, another thought occurred to him.

"Wait, but Daphne said somethin' about creatin' Witchbeasts too, didn't she? Maybe for Witches, creatin' life ain't actually all that hard? Lower rarity than I figured."

The white-haired Witch rose in his mind again, and he could picture her putting on a brave face at Subaru's less-than-generous assessment: "I—it's not like I'm doing it to be praised or anything."

"What's this, you've got the face of a man imaginin' somethin' awfully warm and cozy."

"Funny enough, whatever guard I had up around her seems to have melted clean away after a few exchanges. Anyway, I get where you come from now, Ryuzu-san. Echidna made copies of a girl named Ryuzu Meyer here, in this place—I get that much."

He understood both the principle behind Ryuzu's duplicates existing and the fact that Ryuzu herself had come to accept it. Building on that, if he were to pose another question—

"The next question is why Echidna was doing all this."

"Hmm..."

"I'm an amateur when it comes to magic and the formulas involved, so I only get the surface of just how impressive what Echidna pulled off really was. But even from that surface-level understanding, I can tell it's somethin' huge."

Subaru continued, addressing Ryuzu, who sat with her arms crossed, listening. He drew a breath, and went on:

"Where'd the motivation come from, to do somethin' that big? What was the reason? Why did Echidna need to make copies of Ryuzu Meyer in the first place?"

The position occupied by the girl named Ryuzu Meyer within the Sanctuary remained unclear. In the present-day Sanctuary, the Ryuzu before him now served as both representative and duplicate. If so, what position had the original Ryuzu Meyer occupied in the founding of the Sanctuary? Or, if she herself had been the very catalyst that brought the Sanctuary into being—

"There's one possibility I can think of, right off the top of my head."

"Oh?"

"It's the classic setup for this kind of story, and my top pick. The possibility that Ryuzu Meyer ended up losing her life for some reason, and someone tried to make a duplicate as a replacement for her."

In manga and novels, characters often searched for ways to reclaim a life that had been lost. The idea of cloning the dead, creating a being with identical DNA as a substitute, was practically a staple theme. More often than not, though, such attempts ended in failure, for reasons along the lines of "the body may be the same, but the soul won't be."

"Goin' by what you said, Ryuzu-san, and by how Pico is—seems pretty likely the attempt here at the Sanctuary ran aground for the same reason. Like, even if you can make the outside a perfect copy, you can't copy over what's inside."

And if, even so, someone kept churning out duplicate after duplicate without ever giving up, that could rightly be called sheer madness. If she'd kept going, repeating failure after failure until the count reached twenty, still chasing the mere possibility that a soul might take root in the next body—

"I don't wanna just write it off as an obsession, but..."

Wanting to reclaim someone's life that badly—he couldn't call that wish wrong. At the very least, he of all people could never say such a thing. Even now, in the ongoing present, Natsuki Subaru was acting in pursuit of a future where he'd managed to save everyone. Only the means and process differed—how far removed was that, really, from what the Witch had attempted?

As for how the Ryuzus who'd been brought into being as a result felt about it—that was something only their own hearts could answer, a domain no one but them could speak to.

Subaru trailed off, having wrapped up his theory. At the sight of him, Ryuzu let out a breath.

"Seems your head works better than I figured, Su-boy."

"With as much groundwork as you've laid out for me, I'm slower than I oughta be. And that's after makin' you say all sorts of things you probably didn't wanna say."

He wanted to click his tongue at how slow his own thinking had been. Subaru chewed on the regret. But Ryuzu, facing him, slowly shook her head. The gesture, though, wasn't meant to comfort him. A faint smile rose to her lips, and what dwelled within it was loneliness.

"It's just, you seem to be thinkin' a bit too much. You could call it dreamin' too big, maybe."

"Dreamin' too big... I didn't think it was that far off the mark..."

"It's dreamin' too big. What you're thinkin' is basically this, ain't it? That someone went to all this trouble just to give a life a second chance—that to the Witch of Greed, the girl named Ryuzu Meyer must've been worth all that, someone precious to her. Am I wrong?"

"............"

At the question, posed with her head tilted, Subaru found his words failing him—she'd hit the mark exactly. In fact, that was exactly what he'd thought. Someone the Witch had gone so far as to invent a new formula for, enduring such a tedious process just to keep her existence going—naturally, if she'd go that far, he'd conclude that person must have been precious to her.

Ryuzu denied Subaru's conclusion with a smile on her face—a smile terribly pained and dry, fixed in place.

"Ryuzu Meyer was just a village girl. She was never especially close to the Witch of Greed. No blood relation, no marriage tie, naturally. Ryuzu Meyer and the Witch were strangers through and through—they exchanged only the barest handful of words."

"That can't be... no, wait a second."

At Ryuzu's words, spoken as though she'd witnessed it firsthand, Subaru, still reeling, held up a palm to cut the conversation off. Slapping his free hand against his forehead, he said:

"Somethin's off here. You said it yourself, Ryuzu-san, just a minute ago—that you, same as Pico, were born with nothin' inside you. So how come you know all this about Ryuzu Meyer, the one in the crystal? It don't add up."

"That right there is the result of the other experiment carried out here in the Sanctuary."

Ryuzu received Subaru's objection gently, and laid a hand over her own chest. If what she'd said was true, then there was likely no heartbeat to feel there. So where did the warmth he'd felt when he touched her come from, then? Setting aside Subaru's musing, Ryuzu closed her eyes and continued:

"Ryuzu Meyer wasn't close to the Witch. But she offered up her body to the experiment. The Witch used Ryuzu Meyer's body, sealed her within the crystal, and granted her eternal time. On top of that, she built a formula and left behind a mechanism—one that, every time a fixed amount of mana had accumulated, would generate a pseudo-Od and produce a duplicate of Ryuzu Meyer."

"...To what end?"

"A duplicate of Ryuzu Meyer is born into the world in a state no different from a newborn, except for language and the barest sense of common knowledge. But that's already strange, ain't it? If it's truly no different from a newborn, it oughta be ignorant and innocent, doin' nothin' but cryin' and wailin'. So how come it comes equipped with enough knowledge to follow basic instructions?"

"That's... no way..."

Arriving at the worst possibility, Subaru lost his words. From Subaru's expression alone, Ryuzu seemed to understand he'd gotten there. She nodded, and said:

"The Witch built a means of selectin' which knowledge to give, and which to withhold, from each duplicate as it was born. On top of that, she only gave the bare minimum, leavin' everything else empty when she brought it into the world."

"So, bein' born knowin' nothin'—that's all part of the plan? But then, what the hell would be the point of that?"

It would just become a ritual for producing dolls that did nothing but follow orders. Of course, that angle wasn't entirely inconceivable. It wasn't—but it felt far too removed from the nature of Echidna, the Witch of Greed, as he understood her. He couldn't picture that white-haired girl going through such a roundabout scheme just to produce beings that moved like her own hands and feet.

"I don't even know if it's possible, but grabbin' some random person and brainwashin' 'em would be way faster and less trouble. It ain't that. There's gotta be somethin' else, some other reason."

An empty, blank-slate existence, brought into being out of nothing—

"—Ah."

For an instant, a possibility abruptly flashed through his mind. But it struck him as too absurd, and he quickly shook his head, trying to forget it. And yet, once that thought had taken hold, it refused to let go of him.

Supposing it were true—

*'It's not that I want you to hold me in contempt.'*

Yes—that would make sense of why the Witch had hidden the true intent behind her actions from Subaru. It would also explain why the Ryuzu before him now carried on no small part of Ryuzu Meyer's memories.

"If she could pick and choose which knowledge to give, why still make an empty duplicate?"

"............"

"What do you do, once you've prepared an empty vessel? What's an empty vessel set out on the table for?"

"............"

"—To pour somethin' into it. Obviously."

If you could prepare a duplicate as an empty vessel, and pour knowledge and memory into it—an original that would never be lost, preserved within the crystal. If you could produce as many duplicates of her as you liked, and pour in as much knowledge and memory afterward as you liked, into any one of them—

Then that would mean—

"Burnin' your own memory and knowledge into Ryuzu Meyer's body, over and over again. If that's possible, then it's—"

"—A form of immortality."

—That, precisely, was the true nature of the experiment that had been carried out in the Sanctuary.
