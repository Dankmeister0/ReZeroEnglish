Arc 6 Chapter 46: "Meili Portroute"

—Meili Portroute.

Staring at the book's spine, Emilia murmured the name, and Subaru went rigid with shock. His eyes went wide, glaring at the spine. The characters written there meant nothing to Subaru at all—he couldn't read them.

But there was no reason for Emilia to shake him with a meaningless jolt like that, not here, not now.

Which meant that the volume before his eyes—the *Book of the Dead* whose title bore Meili's name—was, beyond any doubt, exactly what it appeared to be.

"—"

Voiceless, his cheeks gone stiff, cold sweat soaked into Subaru's back. Inside his skull, his brain was screaming a single word, over and over: *Why.*

Why was Meili's name here. Why had the archive that kept the records of the dead prepared her book so soon. Why, among this vast, overwhelming number of books, had Meili's turned up so easily. Why, the very moment Subaru had wanted to trust Emilia—to trust the people in this tower—had it come to this. Why did fate show Natsuki Subaru no mercy at all. Why, why, why, why, why, why, why, why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why why—.

"—Beatrice, have you checked what's inside the book?"

"—!"

Buried under that overwhelming heap of *why*, the words nonetheless rang strangely clear against his eardrums. Snapping his head up, he saw it was Emilia who'd put the question to Beatrice.

She stared at the spine still seated in its shelf as she asked.

From what Subaru had gathered, the *Book of the Dead* found in this archive worked like this: if the reader had known the deceased in life, that person's memories from before death would flow into the reader's mind.

It was hardly a comforting sort of power for a cursed tome to have, but that fantastical aspect wasn't the problem here—something far more immediate was.

If Meili's memories from life could be seen, then her final memories could be seen too.

Which meant, in other words, that the answer to who had strangled her to death would be laid bare.

"—"

Given the circumstances, Subaru had no doubt that it was his own two hands that had killed Meili, and that it was "Natsuki Subaru" who had done it.

But he was the only one who could draw that distinction. A memory that functioned like recorded footage, like a security camera trained on the scene of the crime, would never make that distinction for anyone else. And this loop's Subaru had, on top of everything, been hiding his amnesia from Emilia and the others.

If his crime came to light through the memory, and only then did he confess to having lost his memories—such an opportunistic move would look, to anyone watching, like nothing but a shoddy excuse.

Naturally, Emilia and the others would think the same of Subaru.

If Beatrice had already gotten ahead of things and looked into the memories surrounding Meili's death—

"—I haven't confirmed it yet, I suppose."

"—! Is... is that true?"

"Of course it is. One has to be cautious in how it's handled. To begin with, we don't even know yet if it's really that Meili's book, the one Betty and the others know, I suppose. If it really is that girl's book, though..."

"Meili, inside this tower... oh no! We have to go look for her right away!"

Her color changing, Emilia moved to dash off at Beatrice's words. But Beatrice cut in front of her and shook her head.

"Wait, I suppose! If this really is that girl's book, searching in a hurry would be pointless. If a book's been added here, that's what it means, I suppose."

"...So that's why Ram is out looking for us, and for Julius and the others."

"If, partway through, Meili comes waltzing out looking perfectly normal, then it'll just be Betty's own adorable mistake, I suppose."

At Beatrice's cool-headed point, Emilia's momentum gradually drained away. Even so, her face still tight with unrelieved anxiety, she clasped her hands before her chest as if in prayer.

Praying, clinging to the hope that the book sitting on this shelf wasn't Meili's at all—just someone else who happened to share her name.

—And Natsuki Subaru already knew that prayer would go unanswered.

"—"

Watching Emilia and Beatrice out of the corner of his eye, Subaru was desperately turning his mind over.

His thoughts spun in circles, and most of that spinning was devoted to one question: how to extract himself from this situation and keep the truth for himself alone.

Just as he'd simulated earlier, Emilia and the others could not be allowed to see Meili's memories. It was a stroke of luck that Beatrice hadn't already looked, but that luck was only a matter of time.

Meili's death was a fact. She wasn't going to come strolling into this archive looking perfectly normal. So naturally, the moment would come to check the *Book of the Dead*. And not so far off, either.

If the book were seen, Subaru would face Emilia and the others' condemnation.

So then—if he could somehow dispose of Meili's book, wouldn't that solve it? No—he wanted to avoid that conclusion.

It was true the circumstances made that an extremely difficult thing to pull off. But more than that, the real problem was Subaru's own feelings—Subaru himself had a strong interest in what the book contained.

As things stood, Subaru believed without doubt that it was "Natsuki Subaru" who had killed Meili. If he could peer into Meili's record, he might be able to see that "Natsuki Subaru" for himself.

To see, with his own eyes, the shape of himself—or of the literal, potential "enemy" that might be lurking inside him.

If that were possible, Subaru's situation would change dramatically. At the very least, his resolve would finally be settled.

For that, too, he needed a way to keep Emilia and the others from reading the book here, a way for only Subaru to touch its contents—

"So, like, I got called out here real loud and stuff, what's goin' on?"

"Shaula, you came."

While Subaru was turning this over, Shaula appeared in the Taygeta archive.

Tilting her head, her black hair swaying, Shaula was greeted by Emilia and Beatrice, and then, noticing Subaru further back in the archive, waved a hand his way.

In that instant, the image overlapped with Meili walking into breakfast that morning, and Subaru turned his face away, fighting down a surge of nausea.

"Ohh, Master's bein' cold to me. And here we were havin' such a hot time together just a little while ago."

"What happened between you and Subaru is interesting, but it can wait, I suppose. If you've seen Meili anywhere, speak up. Were you two on good terms, I suppose?"

"The little squirt? Er—Number Two, ya mean? Hmm, ain't seen her in a while, now that ya mention it. Dunno nothin' past breakfast this mornin'."

Asked by Beatrice, Shaula waved both hands as she answered. Then she pressed the hand she'd been waving to her own cheek and tilted her head. "Somethin' up with Number Two?"

At Shaula's question, Emilia lowered her eyes anxiously.

"Actually, we found a book in the archive with Meili's name on it. We haven't been able to check inside yet, but before that, I want to make sure she's safe..."

"Ahh, I get it. Number Two kicked the bucket, huh. Kinda hard to picture, but there's a million ways to die, so I guess it could happen."

"—!"

"...You."

With zero regard for how painful this was for Emilia, Shaula stated her breezy view on mortality. At her words, Emilia's cheeks went rigid, and Beatrice glared at Shaula with something like real anger. But Shaula showed no particular reaction to either of them.

"So, where's the book that matters? Take a peek inside quick and we'll know how and where Number Two bit it, right?"

"W-will you knock it off already! You keep talking like that, over and over—"

Unable to stand it any longer, Subaru snapped at Shaula's tactlessness. There wasn't a shred of human feeling in anything she'd said just now.

Granted, Subaru's own thoughts up until a moment ago were nothing he could ever let Emilia hear either—but at least he had the decency not to say them out loud. Shaula apparently lacked even that much—

"Don't be mad, Master. I ain't got no bad intentions! But seriously, since the book's right there, ain't that what we oughta do?"

"That's... at least wait until everyone's gathered."

"No need for somethin' that roundabout—just read the book and it's settled, ain't it? If Number One's worried 'bout what happens when ya read it..."

Shaula pressed the advantage as Subaru trailed off, his counterargument losing steam. She sent a sidelong glance toward Beatrice, cut herself off there, and turned back to look at Subaru squarely.

Then, with an open, guileless face, she continued.

"—Master oughta be the one to read it."

"—"

"When we found this here archive, you and that other pretty boy already popped your cherries, didn't ya? And nothin' bad happened after, right? So..."

Her ample chest swaying, Shaula leaned in a step closer, close enough to feel her breath. At her proposal, Subaru caught his breath, and his mind began racing again.

Shaula's suggestion was, terrifyingly enough given who was making it, actually reasonable.

In truth, no one knew what kind of harm the *Book of the Dead* might do to a reader—but at the very least, two people had already experienced it. It was surely worth trying.

Of course, there was also the possibility that that very experience was what had stolen Subaru's memories, the trigger that had severed the current him from "Natsuki Subaru." But if that were the case, something ought to be wrong with Julius too. Suspecting that Julius was, like Subaru, hiding amnesia of his own—that was surely too great a leap.

There was no connection between the *Book of the Dead*'s effects and memory loss.

Given the circumstances, he could reasonably conclude that much. If so, this proposal was a gift falling right into his lap—

"—It's true, there might be something to what Shaula's saying."

"...Subaru, are you really going to do this? Someone you even shared meals with, I suppose?"

Riding on Shaula's words, he could at least clear this first hurdle.

The path was opening—to becoming the first reader of Meili's *Book of the Dead*, and its last reader too, all without revealing the thought hidden in his chest. Into Subaru's argument came Beatrice's plain, honest concern.

She was worried about the toll this might take on Subaru's mind, and speaking up because of it.

Until now, the dead that "Natsuki Subaru" had encountered here had, in the end, been people he barely knew. But with Meili, that would be entirely different.

Someone he'd traveled with, spoken to, shared meals with—a kind of comrade-in-arms.

Watching over her "death," Beatrice worried, would surely leave some wound on Subaru's heart. That was what she feared.

"...I'm fine, don't worry. Like Shaula said, I'm the safest bet."

At Beatrice's concern, Subaru tightened his cheeks to look serious, and nodded.

It was true—peering into the record of someone whose face you knew, someone dead, would very likely do damage to the mind. A decent, kindhearted person might not be able to bear it.

—But that wasn't him. That simply wasn't him.

To the Subaru of right now, Meili was in no way someone he'd traveled with, spoken to, shared meals with, some kind of comrade-in-arms.

For a mere few hours, he'd acted as though he knew her, shared breakfast with her two or three times, and yes, her presence had eased his heart a little—but that was the whole extent of it.

Just that much, a girl he barely knew at all. There was no wound to be taken from her death.

"I'm still against it. If it has to be done, then it shouldn't be Subaru—it should be me—"

"If Emilia's the one who's going to look, then Betty's against it, I suppose. If someone has to look... much as it galls me, Subaru or Julius is the safest option, I suppose. Though seeing how Julius was this morning, I want to strike him from the list too, I suppose."

"Even Beatrice..."

Beatrice silenced Emilia's emotional protest with reason.

At the very least, Beatrice seemed to have chosen to respect Subaru's will. Still, Emilia also knew how unstable Subaru had seemed just moments before.

So, to Emilia's violet eyes, still dark with worry, Subaru gave a nod.

"—I'll look. Who knows, maybe it's just a misunderstanding, and nothing at all will happen even after all this build-up, right?"

"...If anything happens, I'll pull you off the book right away. I'll pull your hair."

"I'd appreciate it if you'd go with something gentler there, like shaking my shoulder or something."

If Emilia's monstrous strength went to work ripping out his hair, the back of Subaru's head would become a permanent wasteland. With that hollow bit of banter, Subaru headed for the bookshelf.

Meili's book still sat there, radiating that same strange presence. The first time he'd seen it, it had looked like nothing more than an unremarkable volume—but the instant he'd realized the familiar name written on it, this atmosphere had settled in. People's perceptions really couldn't be trusted for anything.

And to probe himself—someone equally untrustworthy—Subaru picked up the book.

"—"

Behind him, he could sense Emilia and Beatrice holding their breath. Shaula watched Subaru's decision with easy indifference, hands laced behind her head.

Under the weight of all three gazes, Subaru took a light breath, laid his hand on the cover of the dictionary-thick book, and—

"—Here goes."

Murmuring to himself as if bracing his own resolve, Subaru opened the book—and his consciousness went dark.

—When the woman first became aware of her own beginning, she had nothing at all.

Dark, so dark, black, so black, alone in the forest, the woman simply was, alone.

"—"

Without knowing words, she did not know how to grieve.

Without knowing how to walk, she did not know how to resist.

At Beatrice's analysis, Emilia's cheeks went taut and she caught her breath. She flung herself toward ■■■ at once, grabbing his cheeks, forcing his eyes to meet hers.

"Subaru, remember. It's all right, you're Natsuki Subaru, my knight. World's greatest broke boy, gather 'round, everybody, gather 'round... and, and..."

Emilia groped through her own memory, stringing together some string of nonsense.

Listening to her, ■■■, ■■aru, Su■■aru—

—Subaru called himself back.

"I... ah, Emilia, Beatrice... I'm... me, right? Not her, me, and... Elsa's, not here, um..."

"It's okay, calm down. You're fine now... slowly, nice and slowly, okay?"

"Like a thorn, you're slowly pulling out the other memory stuck in there, I suppose. That should bring you back to the real Subaru, properly, I suppose."

"Ngh... uh..."

Emilia and Beatrice spoke to Subaru—Subaru, yes.

Listening to their words, he did his best to sort through what he'd just seen.

Right down to the very end—that final moment, the instant life had been cut off—he tried, somehow, to make sense of it.

"Lady Emilia, my apologies for the delay. We spoke with Lady Ram along the way, and—"

"Is it true that a book bearing that girl's name has been found?"

Just then, having climbed the stairs, Julius and the others—who'd been slow to catch up—arrived.

Julius, Echidna, and Ram bringing up the rear all caught up, and the absent Meili's name came up. Emilia and Beatrice began explaining the current situation to the three of them.

"—"

Indifferent to the book that had fallen at their feet, Shaula watched it all quietly.

Her black eyes narrowed, her gaze unreadable, giving nothing away, she simply—

—watched, quietly.
