Arc 5 Chapter 73: "Theresia van Astrea"

*—If you found out it was actually love at first sight, I wonder how surprised you'd have been?*


* * *


The Divine Protection of the Sword Saint came to dwell within Theresia van Astrea when she was twelve years old.

It happened abruptly, without a single sign of warning, an event that would shake her life to its foundations.
In the midst of an utterly ordinary day, it simply descended upon her, out of nowhere.

"—? Could it be... I've been chosen?"

That was, quite earnestly, how it felt to Theresia when the Divine Protection was bestowed upon her.
And that fact was something Theresia would go on keeping secret, locked away inside herself, for some time to come.

—House Astrea was a storied family of the sword, one that had produced the "Sword Saint" generation after generation.

As a result of the great feat accomplished centuries ago by the first Sword Saint, Reid Astrea, the blade of House Astrea had come to be recognized as indispensable to the Dragon-friendly Kingdom of Lugunica.
That tradition, even after the passage of hundreds of years, still flowed unbroken down into Theresia's own era.

For this reason, it was all but ordained that anyone born into House Astrea, whether or not they carried the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint, would walk a life bound up with the sword. Theresia's father, of course, and her two elder brothers and youngest brother besides, were no exception. Regardless of talent or blessing, growing up intimate with the blade from one's earliest memory was the Astrea tradition.

Born into a house where such an upbringing was simply taken for granted, how then did Theresia—a girl—grow up? The answer was: in days utterly unconnected to the sword.

Of course, being born into House Astrea meant a sword was placed in one's hands regardless of sex.
Theresia too, like her brothers, had her days of being made to swing a blade under strict instruction. She had them—but she showed not the faintest glimmer of swordsmanship talent.
Or rather, it should be said that she possessed not a shred of the disposition needed to face a blade at all.

Like so many girls, Theresia simply had no interest whatsoever in the sword.
It wasn't a question of skill or clumsiness. Swordsmanship undertaken only because she was made to, half-hearted training, a rebellious attitude—when these persisted, it was only natural that her parents would eventually find no reason to keep making her swing a blade.

—The Divine Protection of the Sword Saint could only be granted to one who proved themselves worthy of the Sword God's favor.

The Divine Protection of the Sword Saint, which only members of House Astrea could ever inherit, generation after generation—at least, that was how people understood it at the time, though its true details remained unrevealed even then.
Only by facing the sword in earnest, and having one's talent for it recognized, could the blessing be granted.

A girl, uninterested in the sword, forever turning her face away from it—the eldest daughter.
It was a foregone conclusion that Theresia would soon be written off as having no chance of becoming the Sword Saint, and would be allowed to live as selfishly and freely as she pleased.

Theresia had all but forced her freedom from swordsmanship training, but she had her own reasons for it, reasons that were hers alone.
Of course, having no interest whatsoever in the Sword Saint, and finding no meaning in devoting herself to the blade, was part of why her training never took—but that wasn't the greatest factor.

—Theresia was aware of the innate Divine Protection she had carried since birth: the Divine Protection of the Death God.

Any wound she inflicted upon another would never heal, and would bleed forever.
When young Theresia became conscious of this power—a power singularly, thoroughly specialized in taking the lives of others—what took root inside her was terror at what her own two hands might bring about.

Sword training was, of all things, the very thing most likely to feed that terror.
However much it was mere practice, mere drilling, the Divine Protection she could not control cared nothing for occasion. Even a scratch, if it would never heal for the rest of a person's life, was nothing to laugh about.
And a wound made by a blade, worse still, might take a life through the merest of accidents.

And so, freed from sword training, Theresia felt a relief that no one else knew of.

Living without ever hurting anyone is, if you actually think about it, an extraordinarily difficult thing to do.
Even without consciously, deliberately trying to hurt someone, unforeseen accidents and unconscious acts can happen to anyone, at any time. If she caused someone to cut a finger on the shard of a dropped plate, how could she ever be sure the Divine Protection's power wouldn't take hold there too?

Still a child, the girl named Theresia unconsciously began to avoid contact with others.
If she never touched anyone, never drew close to anyone, there was no risk of hurting them. Naturally, she came to avoid the eyes of others, and spent more and more of her time with flowers instead.
Having won the freedom to cast the sword aside, she built a flowerbed all her own in the mansion's garden, and became a girl who cherished raising and admiring the flowers of each season.

Her brothers swung their swords with desperate expressions, repeating training so grueling it was almost painful to watch.
It wasn't that she felt no sense of alienation, no guilt, at the sight of them. But she could tell no one of her Divine Protection, and so the only ones she ever spoke to were the flowers she herself raised.

"I wonder if someday, even someone like me, will be able to spend time with somebody else..."

The only ones she could confide her worries, her confusion, such feelings, to, were the flowers swaying their petals in the breeze.
Just as so many people are, Theresia too was an ordinary girl who wished to love others, and to be loved by someone in turn.
And yet, going against the aspirations of her brothers and parents, all too aware of a Divine Protection that might hurt and take from someone, she never stopped asking herself, again and again, whether she even had the right to stand beside anyone at all.

—It was in the midst of days like these that the Sword God's favor rained down upon Theresia.

"—? Could it be... I've been chosen?"

The awareness struck Theresia all at once.
It bore down on her as a sense of wrongness far greater than her awareness of the Divine Protection of the Death God had ever been.
Naturally so. Her innate Divine Protection was, to her, simply a function she'd had from birth, no different from being able to see or hear.
But being granted a new Divine Protection out of nowhere was an anomaly—no less strange than if wings had suddenly sprouted from her back. A new function that had never existed before suddenly taking root—what could that be but a source of pure wrongness?

—She must never grip a sword.

That was Theresia's thought, even as revulsion and nausea churned within her at this new blessing.
She recalled the times she had once been forced to swing a sword against her will. Now she understood, with painful clarity, just how futile, how meaningless, how idle, how utterly useless her every motion back then had been.
She understood, by instinct, the finest, the most optimal, the very best, the most supreme way to swing a blade.

A person specialized entirely in killing had now come to fully understand the technique of killing.

"—!"

It was terror. It was despair. It was the day the world ended.
The time in which she could wear the skin of an ordinary girl, could keep pretending to be an ordinary girl, had come to an end—and she was forced to grasp, with agonizing clarity, that she was a death god wearing the shape of a girl.

Theresia told no one about the Divine Protection she had been granted.
Both the Divine Protection of the Death God and the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint—she tried to hide them forever, to shut them away deep inside her heart, to seal away the fact that she was a monster made for slaughter.

Claiming to feel unwell, she shut herself away in her own room in the mansion.
She forgot even to tend her own flowerbed, and curled up inside her own shell. She did nothing but sleep, and whenever the suffocation forced her to lift her head, she indulged in the childish fantasy that someday, all of this would turn out to have been a dream, and kept herself locked away, hiding.
But that, in the end, was nothing more than a child's shallow trick of averting her eyes from something unpleasant.

—That Theresia had inherited the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint became clear almost at once.

"Brother. ...The next Sword Saint is your daughter. This girl."

The Astrea main house—Theresia's childhood home, and the headquarters of the Sword Saint's bloodline.
The man who came to that estate and, finding Theresia curled up in her bed, exposed her as the next Sword Saint, was her uncle, the previous Sword Saint.

The Divine Protection of the Sword Saint was a special blessing passed down, generation after generation, only within House Astrea.
One day, without warning, that Divine Protection would transfer from the current Sword Saint to the next. And once the transfer was complete, the current Sword Saint was released from their duty, and lost the blessing.
When the current Sword Saint lost the Divine Protection, it was only natural that the whole kingdom would mount a search to determine who the next Sword Saint was.
And the previous Sword Saint could tell, at a single glance, who the new Sword Saint was.

And so, Theresia's days of shutting herself away came to their end.

"Take up a sword, Theresia."

Gaunt, her hair and skin ragged and unkempt, Theresia was dragged out to the garden.
Barefoot, still in her nightclothes, her mind adrift somewhere between dream and waking, her uncle nonetheless hauled her roughly out into the garden and forced a wooden sword into her hand.

With her thinned, wasted fingers, Theresia shook her head again and again in refusal.
But no one listened to her protests, unwilling as she was. Her uncle made her grip the wooden sword over and over, and when she finally, despairingly, closed her fingers around the hilt, he shoved her by the back and turned her to face forward.

Standing before Theresia was her eldest brother, four years her senior.
Her gentle, kind-faced eldest brother wore a look of bewilderment. It was plain at a glance that he could not accept what was happening before him, that he was at a loss.

—He's wide open.

Theresia was horrified at herself for having that thought.
Shock tore straight through her heart at the realization, and she found herself speechless, eyes wide.

Ignoring Theresia's state entirely, her uncle flung a low, harsh command at her eldest brother.
He made him take up a wooden sword as well, ordered him to clash head-on with Theresia. To strike his sister down with that wooden blade and prove his own talent for the sword.

There's no way I can do that, her eldest brother cried out.
He was a gentle brother. Devoted heart and soul to his sword training, he had never once questioned the way of House Astrea—but toward his sister Theresia, he had only ever been gentle.
Too afraid of hurting her to reach out to her himself, she had nonetheless loved being lifted up in his big brother's arms. A kind, kind brother.

You coward, her uncle's voice rang out, hurling scorn at her brother.
At the contempt of the previous Sword Saint, the very man whose back her eldest brother had always looked up to in admiration, his face twisted with deep hurt. Theresia knew full well whom her eldest brother, her second brother, her youngest brother, had all looked up to as they kept swinging their swords.
To be scorned by that very man wounded her eldest brother terribly. Dragged out to witness this same spectacle at the edge of the garden, her second brother and youngest brother wore the same wounded expressions.

At last, still wearing that hurt expression, her eldest brother's eyes filled with grim resolve.
Pouring in the entirety of the swordsmanship he had honed all his life, he raised the wooden sword straight into a middle guard.

By the trembling tip of the blade, and the sharpness in her eldest brother's gaze, Theresia understood.
Her brother was trying to knock the wooden sword from her grip without hurting her. His stance, his eyes, the sword-intent radiating from his entire body, told her so.

With skill like his, doing that to an opponent so far beneath him would not be difficult.
It would be more than enough to prove his talent, and to take the sword from Theresia's hands.

"——"

At their uncle's signal, a duel that neither party wanted began.

Her eldest brother shouted a kiai, and honed sword-intent stabbed into Theresia.
It was sword-intent with enough force to make its target flinch, to still their movements. So long as he didn't even raise his blade, knocking the wooden sword from her hand would be effortless.

This fight was mistaken from the very start, mistaken in its very premise.
Theresia had no reason to fight, and her brother had no reason to hurt Theresia. With both their interests perfectly aligned, there was no way a real clash should ever occur.

And yet—

"—That's enough."

From a distance, a wooden sword sprang up and struck point-first into the garden earth, and a voice called out.
Startled back to herself by her uncle's grave voice, Theresia found the wooden sword in her own hand extended straight forward, its tip pressed to the throat of her stunned, dumbfounded brother.

—She remembered then: she had caught her brother's descending strike and wrenched the wooden sword from his hands.

All that was left was to hold the blade to his throat, showing that she could kill him at any moment, the difference in their strength made plain.
Her eldest brother, overwhelmed by what had happened, sank to his knees on the spot. Watching that happen, Theresia turned around, lips trembling, and looked at her second brother and youngest brother who had watched the duel, and at her parents.

Every one of them looked on the outcome in stunned, unbelieving silence.

"The next Sword Saint is Theresia. There's no mistake, after all."

Only her uncle's voice, saying that, rang hollow in her ears.

"Ah... a, ah..."

Theresia dropped the wooden sword, stared down at her own hand, and with that hand tore at her own red hair. She clawed until she bled, letting out a beastlike cry, and screamed.

She screamed, went half out of her mind, grieved until she nearly spat blood, and regretted, and regretted.
And Theresia became the Sword Saint.


* * *


The time her brothers had devoted to the sword, Theresia's talent trampled underfoot without mercy.
However much or little training one had put in—it meant nothing before overwhelming, innate talent.

To Theresia's eyes, now that she was Sword Saint, her brothers' flaws with the blade were laid bare, transparent. She was horrified at herself for wondering how they could pour so much time into it and still not see such obvious flaws.
And at the same time, she found it deeply, painfully pitiable that her brothers kept devoting themselves to the sword even after being shown, so unmistakably, the gap between themselves and her.

Neither her eldest brother, nor her second brother, nor her youngest brother had anything besides the sword to pour their passion into.
Born into House Astrea, raised in a family whose honor rested upon the blade, having devoted the greater part of their lives to the sword—even with its summit stolen from them by their little sister, their sister, they had no choice but to keep moving forward.
Even knowing they could never, ever reach that height.

—What fools, she couldn't help thinking.

She couldn't help but think it.
If only they would do what they loved instead. The sword didn't matter anymore, not really.
Since it was no longer something Theresia herself was even permitted, she wished they would go live in whatever world they truly loved.

"Lady Theresia, the preparations are complete. Shall we set off soon?"

The voice reached Theresia as she looked down from her window at the garden, watching her brothers swing their swords.
Turning around, she found a girl of about her own age, her beautiful blonde hair cut short.

This was Carol Remendis—a member of House Remendis, a family renowned for producing excellent knights, whose own remarkable swordsmanship had earned her, on account of their close ages, the role of Theresia's attendant.

In truth, her skill as a swordswoman was outstanding.
Theresia hardly wished to make loud comparisons, but her sword strength was probably a match even for her brothers.
Earnest to a fault, utterly devoted to swordsmanship—as a fellow young woman, it was almost unsettling.

"Yes, let's go. Today's the instruction for the castle staff, isn't it?"

"Yes. Everyone at the castle is looking forward to receiving your guidance, Lady Theresia. Of course, I feel the same way."

"...I do think Carol's already plenty strong, though."

"Not at all. I'm not even worthy to stand at your feet, Lady Theresia."

As if belittling herself, Carol assessed her own ability that way.
For Theresia, it was an evaluation she found deeply unwelcome. After all, Theresia had never once, not a single time, held a sword in front of Carol. No—it went further than that.

The last time Theresia had ever held a sword was that day she'd fought her eldest brother with wooden blades.
More than two years had passed since then, and Theresia hadn't so much as touched a sword.

Even so, her duties as Sword Saint kept coming around regardless.
The fact that she was the Sword Saint was simply undeniable. She couldn't bring shame upon the whole of House Astrea, and so, obediently, unquestioningly, she went on fulfilling that one role at least.

"Those who ought to see, see. Even without ever having watched it themselves, I'm certain that when you do take up a sword, Lady Theresia, you'll display power no one else could ever match."

Theresia smiled wryly at Carol's words, spoken with such conviction.
Accompanied by her, Theresia set off for her duty—instructing the soldiers, and the like. Not that "instruction" meant anything so grand.

All she did was point out the flaws in the soldiers as they held their weapons and drilled as usual.
The frightening thing about the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint was that it supplemented practically every combat instinct there was. And that wasn't limited to swordsmanship alone. Spear or axe, whatever the martial discipline, Theresia could tell at a glance what was right and what was wrong about it.

Pointing out each flaw one by one changed the soldiers' movements almost beyond recognition.
Even so, from Theresia's vantage, their motions remained riddled with flaws—but even such modest changes made an enormous difference for those without talent.
Being thanked, being respected for it, left Theresia feeling deeply uncomfortable.

She wanted to run away, right now.
Away from this place, away from wherever this unwanted power was wanted.

Just as she had when the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint was first bestowed upon her, Theresia despaired of her own circumstances.
She found herself wanting to shut herself in her room again, to curl into her shell again, and simply hope that the storm winds of fate would eventually settle on their own.

—Perhaps it was divine punishment for that reliance on others, that shirking of responsibility.


In the Dragon-friendly Kingdom of Lugunica, a large-scale clash erupted with a coalition of demi-humans.
In no time at all it became deeply entangled with the kingdom's long-persisting custom of demi-human discrimination, and intertwined with years of pent-up resentment among the demi-humans living within the kingdom, and burst into flame.

The kingdom's greatest and most terrible civil war, the Demi-Human War, had begun.


* * *


This civil war, which began in the eastern reaches of the kingdom, grew more severe by the day.
At first it was thought that such a small demi-human force would be easily suppressed—but the kingdom had underestimated just how deep the connections ran among the demi-humans beneath the surface.

What's more, it seemed there existed among the demi-humans some figure who had served as a go-between, bridging races that had otherwise had no ties to one another, and the flames of civil war spread rapidly to every corner of the land.
The whirlpool of raging conflict spread through the kingdom without pause, and only after a full year spent trying to put out the fire, with nothing to show for it, did the kingdom finally acknowledge that this was an unprecedented crisis.

"To Theresia van Astrea, the current Sword Saint. As with the knights and soldiers striving to suppress this civil war, results are expected of you as well."

Having thrown in unprecedented military force and come to regard this as a crisis for the entire kingdom, the high nobility were never going to keep the strongest existence, the Sword Saint, held in reserve.
As expected, word arrived demanding Theresia's participation in the civil war.

—The moment that had to come had finally come, Theresia thought, with a despair unlike any she had known before.

This was different from all those days when her own selfishness had let her avoid holding a sword.
What was demanded of her now was not the exercise of a Sword Saint's knowledge, but the exercise of a Sword Saint's blade.

There was no way around holding a sword.
It was then that Theresia was, for the first time, given the dragon sword Reid—the blade permitted only to the Sword Saint across the generations.

"But that sword cannot be drawn except at the time it must be drawn. You'll need another blade besides. Find something suited to you."

The one who gave her that knowing advice was her uncle, the previous Sword Saint.
Having once carried the dragon sword at his own hip, he knew of its capricious nature. Following her uncle's advice, Theresia took one look at a certain longsword—the very kind her attendant Carol favored—and knew instantly that it would suit her too, and chose it.

—For Theresia's first battle, Carol came along, and so did her uncle and her brothers.

She wouldn't go so far as to call this first battle of hers some glorious debut.
Even so, it was undeniably an event no one could afford to look away from. It was the perfect opportunity to make the current generation of House Astrea's blade known throughout the whole kingdom.

Entirely apart from Theresia's own state of mind, everyone around her grew excited on their own.
Everyone had simply decided, on their own, that with the Sword Saint present, there was no way they could lose.

Under the thoughtless trust heaped upon her from all sides, Theresia was afraid.
As always, unable to reveal any of it to anyone, keeping it all shut away deep in her heart, Theresia simply trembled, endlessly, before her first battle.
And then, to her—

"Are you scared, Theresia?"

That gentle voice belonged to none other than her eldest brother.
At her kind brother's words, Theresia, who had been waiting for her first battle inside the tent, went stiff with shock.

Deliberately, Theresia had avoided contact with her brother.
No—it wasn't only her eldest brother she'd avoided. Her second brother, her youngest brother, even her parents and her uncle—she had avoided them all.

This was very nearly the first time in two years that she had exchanged words with the kind brother she had loved so dearly.

Not knowing what to say, Theresia could only keep her head bowed.
But her brother sat down beside her, wearing that pitiful expression, and gently pulled her close, stroking her head.
At the touch of his hand, unchanged from before, Theresia was struck with disbelief.

"I understand you feel a debt to me, and to our brothers. It's not as if losing to you like that meant nothing to me either. But..."

He paused there, and smiled faintly at her.
It was the very same smile Theresia had looked up at so many times before.

"You're my precious little sister. And if that sister of mine says she hates this, says she's scared... then it's my job to protect you. Because I'm your big brother."

"B-brother..."

Tears overflowed. She mustn't spill her weakness, not like this.
More than anyone else, she had thought she must never let the very brother she had defeated hear it. And yet that brother had just denied her that belief entirely.

"I was frustrated, losing to you. There were times I thought about giving it up. But even so, in the end, I still loved the sword. Being born into this family, having my brothers, having you, my little sister—I'm grateful. Grateful to the sword."

"——"

"So I'm glad I kept swinging it."

What a fool I've been, Theresia realized, understanding her own foolishness.
Even after losing to her, watching her brothers throw themselves into sword training, she had assumed there was no other path for them, that they knew nothing else, that they had no choice but to swing their swords, that they were merely clinging to it—and she had looked down on them for it.
If only they'd do what they loved, she'd thought, measuring her brother by her own yardstick, selfishly.

The brother she should have looked up to, should have respected, the brother she had loved so much—she had belittled him, based on talent alone.
Who was the fool. She herself was, by far, the greater fool. And the Sword God, more than anyone, was the greatest fool of all.

Why pour such favor upon someone who loved him this much?
Why bless someone like her, who kept turning her face away from the sword, again and again?

That blessing should have rained down upon her brother, and people like him, instead.

"You don't need to fight. —Because you're such a kind girl, you couldn't even bring yourself to kill an insect."

Fearing the power of the Divine Protection of the Death God, she had lived her whole life trying not to hurt anyone, in any way.
Her brother's understanding of it was a little different, but he had known his sister's true nature all along.

That made her so happy—happier than her heart had trembled in years.
She let herself lean on him. She cried, clung to her brother, and left everything in his hands.


—And in that first battle of Theresia's, her brother died defending the main encampment.


Theresia never once swung her sword. Could not swing it.

And for several more years after that, Theresia never touched a sword again.


* * *


—Five years after the Demi-Human War began, Theresia turned nineteen.

The Divine Protection of the Sword Saint still dwelt quietly within her, unchanged.
But Theresia herself kept missing every chance to wield that power, and simply drifted through her days, uninvolved in a civil war that only worsened by the season.

The first battle in which Theresia had been unable to fight.
The front, which had counted on the Sword Saint's fierce effort, had collapsed entirely, and in the midst of it, her eldest brother had died in battle. Theresia, shattered by her brother's death, had been unable to touch a sword ever since.
The disgraceful truth of the current Sword Saint's first battle was kept entirely hidden from the public. The Sword Saint's existence served as a spiritual pillar for the kingdom. That she had wept and wailed in her first battle, let her brother die, and shut herself away in her shell—that could never be allowed to become known.

And so Theresia's dishonor was erased from the record, kept from ever becoming public knowledge.
And so, in place of Theresia, who could not fulfill her role as Sword Saint and remained shut away, her second brother and youngest brother threw themselves into the battlefield as well, determined to fight in a manner that would not shame the Astrea name, and died.

Her gentle eldest brother, who would hear out any request with a troubled little smile.
Her second brother, a little mean-spirited, but who always apologized first whenever they made up.
Her sweet, timid, crybaby youngest brother, who always used to toddle along behind her.

They all fought in place of a Theresia who could not fight, and every one of them lost their life.

"—I made you do too much. I'm sorry, Theresia."

Her uncle too, the previous Sword Saint, who had rallied the entire army by his mere presence, died in battle.
He fought campaign after campaign without rest, pushed through his wounds, and in the end took up the rearguard for the retreating allied forces, meeting a fierce and terrible death.

It wasn't as though Theresia held no resentment toward her uncle.
If he hadn't spoken up, perhaps no one would ever have known that Theresia had inherited the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint. If that had never come to light, her brothers might never have felt driven to such desperate resolve in this civil war, and might never have lost their lives.

Thinking of it that way, there was resentment. There was—but there were other feelings too.
Her uncle, in his own way, had surely understood the weight of the Sword Saint's title better than anyone. As the previous Sword Saint, he too had naturally been expected to bear the same burden Theresia now carried.
For the kingdom's sake, and for Theresia's own sake, he had surely acted, in his own way, believing it was for the best, so that the name of Sword Saint might function properly.

That it hadn't gone well was why there had been those parting words, the last time she saw him.
Because of that single remark, Theresia found she could no longer bring herself to resent her uncle.

So then, who was left to resent, if not herself?
Herself, who had inherited a title like "Sword Saint" and yet done nothing but weep and wail, weakly.

"Lady Theresia is someone who will surely rise, one day. It's simply not yet the time."

As Theresia was battered again and again by the deaths of her brothers, her uncle, one relative after another, her attendant Carol never once tried to abandon her.
Even after the disgrace of her defeat—or rather, her absence—in her first battle, even as she kept missing her chances with childish selfishness after that, and even now, as she remained shut away alone in her shell, Carol kept trying to believe in her.

Of late, it seemed Carol had even taken to secretly handling, in Theresia's stead, instructions sent down from the royal castle meant for Theresia, putting herself in danger to do so.
And yet, even knowing Carol was walking such a dangerous path, Theresia could not bring herself to answer that devotion.

Herself, blessed—cursed, really—with talent for killing beyond all measure.
She had never once imagined there would come a day when she agonized over her inability to kill. There was no reason anywhere for her to fight. She simply could not fight.



"——"

Whenever Carol's watch lapsed, Theresia would slip out of the mansion and wander the royal capital.
Five years into a civil war with no end, even the royal capital had lost much of its old vitality, and an indefinable gloom hung over it. Without any bright news, no cheer ever touched people's faces. Naturally, Theresia avoided crowded places and sought out solitude instead.

Of late, the place Theresia liked best to visit was an undeveloped stretch of land at the edge of the capital.
Development there had been abandoned partway through, right at the start of the civil war. She would make her way along what could barely be called a road, through gaps between buildings that were practically ruins, toward a certain place further within.

A faintly open space, hardly even a proper plaza, was Theresia's favorite spot.
It wasn't that it lifted her spirits, particularly. She simply felt, vaguely, that this empty space existing amid the ruin matched the state of her own heart, and that alone brought her a certain calm.

In the ironically clear, crisp air of morning, Theresia made her way toward the depths of the plaza.
Sitting down on a set of stone steps that led nowhere in particular, she peered over the far side, and found there, spread out before her, a field ablaze with yellow flowers in full bloom.

Sunlight filtering down, and soil unnecessarily well-suited to a flower's growth.
Taking advantage of this secret place no one else ever visited, Theresia had scattered flower seeds there. She hadn't had the will to tend the flowerbed at the mansion, which had already withered away completely.
But she'd had at least enough feeling left to want to see the result of the seeds she'd scattered, on a whim.

"I never even watered them... and yet they've grown so much."

Flowers are strong.
Even as Theresia went on staring at her own weakness, the flowers aimed only for the sky, spreading their petals, blooming in glorious beauty.
Once she had admired their beauty; now she found herself yearning for their strength.

Something welled up in her, and she nearly cried.
She pressed a hand to her eyes, which had gone unexpectedly hot, and fought desperately to hold back the tears.


—It was just then that a prickly presence drew near.


"Oh, sorry about that."

Intruding, tactlessly, upon Theresia's sanctuary that morning, came someone with a distinctly dangerous air about him.
Having nearly been caught crying, Theresia deliberately put on a tough front and forced out those words. Then she looked at the person standing in the plaza.

She looked, and was left stunned.
Short brown hair, features that were handsome yet edged with sharpness. A lithe, honed, slender body, and a fierce, bloodthirsty air radiating from his entire being, sharp enough to prickle the skin.
It was true that his manner, far from friendly, took her by surprise.
But that wasn't what actually stunned Theresia in that moment.

—To Theresia's eyes, the young man looked exactly like a naked, unsheathed blade.

A single length of tempered steel, holding heat, glaring at her.
At that illusion, Theresia's heartbeat faltered, ever so slightly. Pressing a hand to her chest, she wondered, bewildered, what on earth had just happened to her.

She only knew she didn't want the young man to notice that her heartbeat had faltered.
And so, to hide it, Theresia forced out some words.

"I didn't think anyone else came here this early in the morning. In a place like this—"

"——"

What a rude greeting that was.

At Theresia's friendly attempt to speak, the young man narrowed his eyes and slammed a sharp, piercing sword-intent at her. It was intense sword-intent, honed to a degree that felt like far more than a mere warning.
It was, no doubt, a display of his wish to drive off someone he found irritating, to be rid of her.

Immediately, she found it no longer amusing.
If that was how he wanted to play, then Theresia wasn't about to hold back either. That sword-intent of his, so full of confidence—she'd make sure he knew it hadn't worked on her.

"...Is something wrong? You've got such a scary face."

At Theresia's words, the young man's expression fell, as though the wind had been knocked from his sails.
From that point, he seemed to conclude that Theresia was a complete amateur when it came to sword-intent, or to combat in general. And in truth, that wasn't wrong.

Theresia had no actual combat experience, no track record of swinging a sword.
Though, if it ever came to a fight, she would surely prove stronger than anyone—a young girl who, all appearances aside, was no amateur at all.

"The hell's a woman doing out here this early in the morning?"

The young man replied in rough, ill-mannered language.
Hearing his voice for the first time, she found it—irritable as it was—easy on the ear.

—Again, faintly, she felt her heartbeat falter.


* * *


After that, Theresia and the young man's encounters repeated themselves, again and again.

It seemed the young man visited that plaza on fixed days off.
He seemed to find Theresia's presence a nuisance, but even so, he never tried to force her out. Perhaps he judged that approaching her would be more trouble than it was worth.

To see that field of yellow flowers, Theresia kept returning to the plaza.
Sometimes the young man arrived first; sometimes Theresia did. With Theresia seated on the step, gazing out at the flower field, while the young man swung his finely-made sword nearby, dedicating himself earnestly to his training—that became the unspoken routine the two of them shared in that place.

"——"

Out of the corner of her eye, she glanced at the young man's sword dance.
Without meaning to, she nearly let out a sigh of admiration. That Theresia should feel this way, watching someone else's swordsmanship, was extremely rare—no, it might well have been the first time.

At first, she had felt revulsion at the sight of the young man holding a sword.
That wasn't the young man's fault so much as a matter of Theresia's own state of mind. Having fled from her role as Sword Saint, only to run into yet another person holding a sword in the very place she'd fled to.
Being driven even from the comfortable hiding place she'd worked so hard to find—that pathetic anxiety vanished, from the very first day she watched the young man's sword dance.

The blade the young man wielded was, in all honesty, far from refined.
To Theresia, who possessed the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint, there were plenty of obvious flaws in it. Growing weary at the sheer number of flaws in other people's swordsmanship was one of Theresia's own bad habits—but even feeling those flaws in the young man's swordsmanship, there was a passion there more than enough to make up for them.

Surely her own brothers, too, had devoted everything they had to the sword.
And yet, even watching her brothers' swordsmanship, Theresia had never been able to help feeling some measure of distaste.

So why was it that she didn't feel the same thing, watching this young man's blade?
The answer to that was probably, embarrassingly, quite simple.

"Idiot..."

—There wasn't a single impurity in the young man's blade.

Pouring everything into the sword, devoting everything to it.
It sounded simple enough put into words, and until now Theresia had assumed her brothers had done just that too—but no, not at all.

Here, truly, was a passion with nothing but the sword in it, this young man's.
He had nothing but the sword. Loved nothing but the sword. A single length of steel that could love nothing but the sword.

"...Idiot."

Watching the young man's sword dance out of the corner of her eye, Theresia felt her cheeks growing hot.
Theresia was the Sword Saint. A being who had received the Sword God's favor entire unto herself, and been placed at the summit of the blade.

At the place he single-mindedly aimed for stood her own existence.
It had to be an illusion, but she felt as though he wanted her.

As Sword Saint, Theresia could understand everything about a sword just by looking at it.
Any treasured blade, any Enchanted Sword, any worthless scrap of steel, even a dragon sword—she could see its true nature. She could wield any of them freely. There was no steel that did not lay itself bare in Theresia's hands.

Only him.
The one length of steel that would not bend to Theresia's will was him, and him alone.

Even though he was a sword, even the Sword Saint herself couldn't see where his limits lay.
That, surely, was why she found herself so drawn to him.



"Wilhelm Trias."

It was three months after they'd met that Theresia and the young man—Wilhelm—finally exchanged names.

Despite having crossed paths so many times before that, neither of them had ever once asked for the other's name.
In truth, Theresia had waited for the opportunity many times, but Wilhelm never once picked up on her wish. It was only when Theresia, at the end of her patience, gave her own name in a natural flow of conversation, and prompted him to do the same, that names were finally exchanged.

"Been calling you Flower Girl in my head, up till now."

What a rude man this was.
He hadn't a shred of consideration, thought of nothing but himself, never tried to make conversation, and the moment he was satisfied with a bit of small talk, he'd just leave on his own—Theresia's heart was constantly being jerked around by him.

"Do you like flowers?"

"No, can't stand them."

Even when she showed him her prized flower field, that was the answer she got.
He seemed utterly incapable of saying anything meant to please someone, or to smooth things over.
Even while feeling irritated about it, Theresia found herself thinking, *but that's exactly why he's become someone like a sword...* and letting it go—which was hopeless of her, really.

At this point, Theresia hadn't yet noticed that she found a certain relief in the existence of a sword that wouldn't bend to her will, a relief in feeling that being "Sword Saint" wasn't such a big deal after all.


"—Why do you swing a sword?"

Ever since they'd exchanged names, the range of things they talked about, little by little, had grown.
Talk of flowers, and idle chatter, and then parting ways—it was probably the mood of that particular day that made her push a bit of dissonance into their usual routine.

Theresia had heard that the tide of the Demi-Human War had turned unfavorable again, that the fighting was fierce.
The core of demi-humans lurking throughout the kingdom, carrying out acts of sabotage, was said to be particularly formidable, and there were even reports that a "Witch," wielding supernatural power, had been confirmed among them.

She found herself, suddenly, anxious.
That Wilhelm was a soldier of the kingdom, he'd told her long ago. Even setting that aside, he was an exceptional swordsman. And on top of that, his eyes carried a terrible hunger for blood.
For a kingdom shaken by civil war, a soldier like him was, in a sense, exactly the right man for the role—but that didn't mean he was an invincible being either. Someday, he might simply stop coming to this morning plaza.
That anxiety was what made Theresia ask him that question.

Finishing his sword dance, drenched in sweat, Wilhelm looked her straight in the eye. Then, after thinking it over for a moment, he shrugged, as if to say what a stupid question that was, and said,

"Because it's all I've got."

That was, indeed, the answer of a single length of steel.
And that should have been exactly the answer Theresia had wanted from him.

Yet Theresia was well aware of the feeling of anxiety and loneliness clawing at her chest.


"Have you started to like flowers?"

"No, can't stand them."

"Why do you swing a sword?"

"Because it's all I've got."

Before long, that exchange, and that exchange alone, became a routine they never once let slip.
Theresia herself didn't know what answer she was hoping for, repeating that same question. Whether she should feel relieved that his answer never changed, or whether she should be hoping for change in a man who remained steel, unchanging—she didn't know either.

Leaving the things she didn't understand as they were, without taking responsibility for them—that was so very like Theresia.
Just as she'd entrusted the Sword Saint's title to herself and sent her brothers off to their deaths in battle, pushed her duties onto Carol, and gone on spending her days like this, uselessly.

And so change, as always, came for Theresia leaving her behind.


* * *


It was usually Theresia who arrived first at the plaza.
Wilhelm's visits, unpredictable at first, she now had entirely figured out, and she was fully prepared with what to say and what to talk about when they met.

Theresia was beginning to realize, by now, that she was leaning on his presence.
By exchanging words with Wilhelm, by watching him devote everything to the sword, Theresia could, if only for a while, forget the weight of being "Sword Saint."
She was drawn to him because she was the Sword Saint, and yet, in his company, being told—as it were—that being the Sword Saint was nothing much at all, she found a kind of salvation.

Whether she herself wanted to remain the Sword Saint or not—even that much she was dishonest with herself about.
The guilt of soaking, endlessly, in lukewarm water with no answer in sight.
Even that, his presence let her forget.

"—Wilhelm."

As always, Theresia turned around at the approach of a presence so obvious it was almost funny.
There, at the entrance to the plaza, stood the young man of the sword.

A smile rose to her lips before she knew it, and Theresia smiled at him.

"——"

It was then that his emotions broke, suddenly, without warning.

Eyes wide, lips trembling, hands shaking as they rose to cover his face. At his dramatic reaction, Theresia too was startled, struck with shock.
Wondering what she had done wrong, Theresia ran to him, to the man with his face covered. But she didn't know what words to say.

Theresia had always, until now, kept her distance from others as much as possible, to avoid ever hurting them.
So even when she had, apparently, hurt someone's heart, she knew nothing of how to handle it.

Despair welled up in her. Despair at herself, who knew nothing, who had never tried to learn.
Despair at herself, who could say nothing to the Wilhelm crumbling before her.

"Wilhelm..."

Not knowing what to say, Theresia's fingers stretched out toward him anyway. How long had it been, since she'd reached out to touch someone of her own will?
Too afraid of hurting people, she had never let herself touch anyone.

And yet, in that moment, the thought of him drawing further away without her touching him was far more frightening.
Theresia reached her fingers out and touched the hand that covered Wilhelm's face. And, trembling with an unbearable uncertainty, feeling an unbelievable heat, she understood.

Steel, when it is subjected to tremendous heat, is forged within it into something stronger.
Wilhelm was a sword, yes—but he was not yet a finished blade.

And right now, Wilhelm held that heat, was in the very midst of being forged, of changing into steel.
And the role of striking that steel, right now, was being asked of Theresia.

—If it concerned a blade, then surely she, as the Sword Saint, would understand.

If it was this person, if it was this sword, surely she could believe she wanted to understand.

"Have you started to like flowers?"

The usual question came out of her, naturally.
Had anyone else been watching them, they might have thought it a foolish, clumsy sort of comfort. But between just the two of them, that was enough.

"...Not... not anymore, I don't hate them."

And to her usual question, this time, came an answer unlike any before.
The old Theresia had once thought this:

That someday, when Wilhelm's answer finally changed, she would feel, once again, disappointment, and dismay, and the terror of being left behind.

None of that happened. She only found the him who was changing dear to her.
A blade of steel that was changing, growing stronger—that single length of steel—was simply, dearly, precious to her.

"Why do you swing a sword?"

So surely, this question too, would have a different answer.
And that answer might, perhaps, bring salvation to Theresia—

"It's all I could think of... the only way I knew how to protect anything."

The sword is all I have, Wilhelm answered.
Yes—the sword was all he had, this man.

And that was exactly why he was fine, just as he was.



—From then on, that fixed exchange was no longer necessary between the two of them.

But that didn't mean they lost the chance to exchange words in the plaza.
If anything, limited strictly to the act of exchanging words, their opportunities only increased from before.

Wilhelm, who had come to the plaza to swing his sword, now came to prioritize talking with Theresia over swordplay.
Seated on the step overlooking the flower field, she would listen to Wilhelm, a man with little conversational range, talk. His storytelling was rambling, clumsy—but pleasant, just to listen to his voice.

"There's talk of a commendation. I've been made a knight."

The way he brought up that topic that day, and the oddly intense look in his eyes.
Theresia, poor at socializing, had always kept her distance from others—but she wasn't foolish enough not to understand what such words, from a young man who'd had to summon his courage, meant.

For a mere commoner to be recognized as a knight through battlefield merit was an extraordinary thing.
Just how much the sword-demon Wilhelm Trias had accomplished in this Demi-Human War, an unworthy Sword Saint understood all too painfully well.
And on top of that, she understood why he, having received the honor of knighthood, had sought such a position in the first place.

"Oh, congratulations. You're one step closer to your dream, then, aren't you?"

Understanding his true meaning, Theresia deliberately responded with an air of indifference.
If she let her guard down, even for a moment, she'd flush red without fail. Pouring all her willpower into self-restraint, Theresia smiled at Wilhelm, whose expression showed he'd been caught off guard.

"Dream?"

"You took up the sword to protect something, didn't you? A knight is someone who protects others."

At Theresia's answer, Wilhelm nodded at once, with a suddenly serious expression.
Ordinarily so contrary, he sometimes had this side that was as honest as a child.

—If only she herself could be among the things he wanted to protect.

Half-certain of it as she was, she hated herself for hedging her bets like that.
Certain, surely, that their feelings were mutual, and yet doing nothing about it—that inaction of hers really was, truly, foolish, hateful, hopeless, and so, once again, Theresia made the wrong choice.

Thinking back on it, it felt as though she made her never once done the right thing, not a single time.


* * *


Wilhelm, his homeland burned, threw himself into the battlefield alone.

When a breathless Carol reported the sword-demon's unexpected, unauthorized action, all the heat drained from Theresia's blood, and she nearly collapsed to her knees on the spot.
She could sense Carol's panic at the sight of her face going pale. She could sense it, but no strength welled up in her at all. That was how thoroughly hopeless the situation was, she understood.

"——"

As Theresia stared down at the floor, an oddly out-of-place voice reached her ears.
Not Carol's. Not anyone else's in the household, either. And yet, it was somehow familiar, like the voice of someone who had always been close to her—and with that, Theresia understood.

It was the laughter of the Sword God, mocking her for the way she had spurned his favor.

"—I have to go."

Listening to the Sword God's laughter, Theresia slowly rose to her feet.
Even now, that mocking laughter kept echoing inside her small skull. But she must not let it go, must not surrender everything to the Sword God's derision.

She had entrusted everything to her eldest brother, and let him die.
She had pushed responsibility onto her second brother, her youngest brother, her uncle too, and let them die.

But him—Wilhelm alone—she would not let go.
That sword, that steel, that man—he alone belonged to her, and her alone.

"Carol, get me ready."

"Lady Theresia...? But, your health..."

"—Get me ready."

Carol, worried for Theresia's condition, straightened her posture at hearing the order a second time. She sprang into motion at once, quickly preparing everything Theresia needed.
Her battle attire, untouched since her first battle, and a longsword that had never yet tasted blood.

"This time, I won't make a mistake."

Swearing that to the longsword she had taken up, Theresia hurried Carol along and leapt onto a dragon carriage.
The number of dragon carriages headed to Wilhelm's rescue was far greater than Theresia had imagined. Men from his own unit, others who spoke of owing him their lives, a great many people gathered.

Nowhere was there any sign of that prickly, naked blade of a young man she'd first met.
Now, that young man had become a treasured sword, drawing so many to him with his brilliance and his edge.


—The front at Wilhelm's homeland had already, entirely, collapsed.

A battlefield filled with screams and roaring shouts, thick with the stench of blood and burning.
At the sheer wretchedness of it all, Theresia felt sick to her stomach. She had imagined, many times, herself standing on a battlefield. But reality painted over any imagined horror with astonishing ease.
On a battlefield teeming with mutual wounding, mutual killing, blood and death everywhere, resolve alone was worthless.

"Just find Wilhelm, whatever it takes!"

The one who shouted that was Bordeaux Zergev, leading a hard-bitten unit of his own. At his roaring command, a group clad in rock-like armor moved out all at once.

"Lady Theresia! We should..."

She heard Carol's voice, asking for instructions, but Theresia wasn't listening.
Bordeaux's men clashed with the enemy soldiers ravaging his homeland. In the midst of that furious collision, she felt him, faintly.

"Lady Theresia!?"

The instant she noticed it, her feet were already moving.
Across the battlefield, thick with clashing warriors, Theresia's feet ran without the slightest hesitation. She knew, without even needing to look, exactly which path to take.

Treading over earth, cutting across mountains of corpses, she made for the place where roaring cries and death throes overlapped most thickly.
And arriving at a spot where the stench of blood was especially heavy, Theresia saw it.

A green-skinned demi-human, right at that moment, raising a greatsword over a fallen Wilhelm.
Wilhelm, his face covered in blood, staring up at that greatsword. His lips moved. In a hoarse, faint voice, he murmured something.

"I don't... want to die..."

"——"


It's all right.


It's all right, so.


"——"

She could hear nothing else, now.
Theresia let only that final thing she'd heard, Wilhelm's whisper, echo through her.

She swung the longsword in her hand through to the end. It was light.
Without a sound, without even the faintest shock of impact, the demi-human's head was severed, effortlessly.

She kicked the massive body, still gripping its greatsword as it fell, so that it wouldn't land on Wilhelm. In that same instant, bullets of hostility and murderous intent came pouring in on Theresia's slender frame from every direction.
She could see every one of their trajectories. She could read them. She could feel them on her skin.

Dodging, Theresia traced with her sword the strange lines she could see with her eyes.
When had they appeared? Countless mysterious white lines floated in the air. Stranger still was that her instinct simply knew: trace those lines with the sword.

Trace, trace, sliding the sword along the white lines.
A gale of blade-wind howled, and the demi-humans caught along those lines were cut down, one after another.

Severing limbs, taking heads, piercing bellies, reaping lives.
The Divine Protection of the Sword Saint, the Divine Protection of the Death God, finally found their chance, and erupted.

Lose a hand, and the wound would never close.
Be pierced through the belly, and the blood would never stop.
Even a shallow scratch would ache, eternally, without end.

"——"

Out of the corner of her eye, she glimpsed Wilhelm being pulled up and carried away.
A young man holding a shield, and beside him, Carol. They lifted the stunned Wilhelm and began to withdraw from this stretch of the battlefield.

Yes, that was good.
Get Wilhelm out of this place, quickly.

"Lady Theresia...!"

Carol, watching Theresia swing her sword, clutched the pendant hanging from her neck. At that gesture, almost like prayer, Theresia nearly smiled, just faintly.

That's right, Carol. Just as you said, wasn't it.
I really am, after all, stronger than anyone, better than anyone at killing.

—If only I'd realized this sooner.

"——"

Struggling, still trying to remain there, Wilhelm was desperately hauled away by the young man. Wilhelm clawed at the earth in resistance, but battered and exhausted as he was, it was no use.

His presence grew more distant. That, she felt relief at.
Relieved, she went on swinging her sword, taking one, two, three lives. Easily. Effortlessly.

Cutting, cutting, cutting them down, drowning out the roars and death cries.
Drowning out that endless, nagging voice of the Sword God.

Let her hear only his voice—that voice desperately clinging to life.
Let it carve into her, please, the reason she fought like this.


Please, let me believe—that my sword is saving Wilhelm.


* * *


The battle ended.
The purpose that had brought Theresia to the battlefield was achieved; Wilhelm survived.

But Wilhelm's own purpose—fighting to protect his homeland—went unfulfilled.
His homeland was burned to the ground, even the house where he was born lost, and he was left entirely alone.

Fighting purely on rage, Wilhelm's kill count that day exceeded three hundred.
For a single swordsman on a single battlefield, that was an unprecedented, extraordinary figure.

—And that was precisely how far beyond the ordinary Theresia's own swordsmanship was, having lined up over a thousand heads.

The Sword Saint's presence, here, made manifest—
The disgraceful truth of her first battle hidden away, this was the battle recorded in history as Sword Saint Theresia's actual debut.

The battle itself had, without question, ended in defeat—but even so, it proved her overwhelming sword strength beyond doubt.
The name Theresia van Astrea spread far and wide throughout the kingdom, and gave powerful encouragement to the morale of the warriors still fighting on the battlefield.
Naturally, it must have reached the ears of that sword-demon youth as well.


"This is humiliating."

There had been no promise to meet again.
And yet, both of them held a certainty: that if they went to the plaza, they would find each other there.

And in fact, that certainty was proven true.
Arriving at the plaza, Wilhelm swung his sword straight at Theresia.

She slipped her hand into its trajectory and caught it between two fingers.
The optimal angle, the optimal amount of force—all of it, Theresia understood perfectly.
His strike halted, Wilhelm twisted his lips savagely and spat out words utterly unbefitting such a reunion, words steeped in raw fury.

"—So?"

"Were you laughing at me?"

"——"

"Answer me, Theresia... no, Sword Saint!!"

She had never meant any such thing.
But there was no point at all in offering such an excuse, either.

It was true that, in some way, Theresia had looked down on Wilhelm's swordplay. It was true she had seen through his flaws, and never told him of them, keeping them locked away instead.
Explaining what her true intentions had actually been would mean nothing to him now.

"——"

She swept the legs out from under Wilhelm as he lunged, sending him crashing violently to the ground. He came at her again, his face frantic, but Theresia evaded and countered every single strike.
Before long the fight became entirely one-sided, and in Theresia's hand was the treasured sword Wilhelm had used and worn down over the years. Literally so—she could see, in the hilt, traces of blood-soaked effort—and swinging it through, she slid a white line across Wilhelm's torso.
The flat of the blade swept across his body, and Wilhelm, the breath knocked from him, crumpled to the ground.

"I won't come here anymore."

Unable to bear being seen by him amid such hatred, such pity, such an unbroken swell of negative feeling, Theresia shook her head, and chose, as she had chosen so many times, to run away.

"Don't you... make that face... and go around carrying a sword."

Bitterly, his face pressed into the dirt, Wilhelm forced the words out.
He was the one who, more than anyone, had believed in and revered the beauty and nobility of the sword. And it was her own power that had kicked all of that aside, trampled it underfoot.

"I'm the Sword Saint. I didn't understand why, before, but now I do."

The Divine Protection of the Death God, which she'd carried since birth.
The Divine Protection of the Sword Saint, granted though she'd never asked for it.
She felt, at last, that she could come to terms with why these things belonged to her.

"...Why..."

"Swinging a sword to protect someone. I think that's a good thing."

Swinging a sword to protect someone.
If only she'd realized something so simple, sooner.
Even if she had realized it sooner, she might not have been able to choose who to protect.
But now, she could choose.

—She would protect Wilhelm.

With this power, this detestable power for slaughter, she would protect Wilhelm, for as long as she was able.
She would protect him, protect her family, protect Carol, protect countless others besides, and go on protecting even something as vast as the kingdom itself, and become a Sword Saint worthy of the name.

Because she was the strongest. Because the Sword Saint was the strongest of all.
Even trampling on his feelings, even betraying what he believed in—even so, she could not give up being the Sword Saint.

"Wait... for me, Theresia..."

There were no more words left to exchange.
Believing this would be the last time, a voice reached the back of Theresia as she began to walk away.

"——"

She nearly stopped. She forced herself, desperately, to hold back that feeling.
And even as she forced herself to hold back, Wilhelm's voice still reached her ears.

"I'll take the sword from you myself. I don't care about some blessing given to you, or some role assigned to you... swinging a sword... the beauty of a blade... don't you dare look down on it, Sword Saint."

"——"

He'd said he would take it from her.
The Sword God's laughter echoed once more inside her skull.

Mocking, as if scorning that reckless, green boy for his ambitions.
Mocking, as if scorning the beloved child whose heart had been so shaken by such a fleeting hope.


* * *


—It would take another two years before the civil war finally came to an end.

Ever since her parting from Wilhelm, Theresia had thrown herself into every battlefield.
And by relentlessly fighting with fierce, tireless effort, she contributed to sweeping away a great portion of the Demi-Human Alliance's forces all at once.

The alliance too, having lost the great figure who had been its backbone, found its foundations shaken.
It was hardly surprising that they finally accepted the peace offer extended by the kingdom.

And so the kingdom's greatest civil war, the Demi-Human War, in stark contrast to the sheer horror of its middle years, came to an end just as abruptly as it had begun.

"It's... over?"

She had steeled herself for the next battle, and the one after that, for an endless war with no end in sight.
So when word of its sudden end arrived, Theresia found the ground shifting under her feet.

"Yes, it's over. The civil war has ended. —And it's thanks to your accomplishments, Lady Theresia."

Catching Theresia as she swayed, Carol said this with an innocent sort of joy.
Carol, who had taken to showing such soft expressions more and more of late, held the bewildered Theresia steady, and gently rubbed her back.

"Accomplishments, hardly..."

Being told that didn't quite register.
Theresia had done nothing but cut, endlessly, believing it would somehow lead to protecting someone.
Believing that she could save the young man who had left the army on his own and vanished, somewhere.

Even so, Theresia's actions, taken without any such self-awareness, came to be celebrated, just as Carol had said, as the greatest contribution to the kingdom.
A ceremony was held in her honor, and even as she stood there in ceremonial dress, holding a sword of state, Theresia never quite shook the feeling, from start to finish, that she was walking through a dream.

No—if this was a dream, then Theresia had been walking through a dream all along.
Ever since being granted the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint, ever since first feeling the Sword God's presence so close to her, Theresia's heart had lived, all this time, inside the dream that great presence showed her.

So this too was a dream. A dream that would never clear, shown to her by the Sword God's love.
So, if there was ever to come a time when she was freed from this dream—

"——"

A stir cut through the crowd's excitement, gathered there for the ceremony.
Unsteadily, ignoring anyone's cries to stop, a figure stepped into the ceremonial grounds.

In his hand was a naked, unsheathed blade—rotted, battered, covered in rust.
He wore a dirty, brownish coat, and what skin was visible was smeared with unsanitary grime. But before anyone could so much as frown at his appearance, everyone present felt, instinctively, something else entirely.

—The overwhelming, ferocious air radiating from that presence.
—No. His sword-intent.

"——"

Facing that figure standing silently, ready, Theresia turned to meet him with her ceremonial sword.
The king, standing on the same dais, restrained the guards who moved to surround the intruder. For that, she was grateful. Now, nothing would interfere.

Her rendezvous with this "sword-demon" would go uninterrupted by anyone.

—There was no signal.

But, as though by silent agreement, both their blades flashed at the exact same instant, ringing out with a sharp, high sound.
Steel practically worthless clashed head-on with a blade that was, ceremonial or not, unmistakably a Holy Sword. Sparks danced, strikes tore through the air, and two shadows crossed one another like dancers upon the dais.

"——"

Swinging her sword, Theresia found herself in awe. Her heart soared, her pulse raced.
As always, Theresia's eyes could see the white lines floating amid the fight. Pass through this line, and you can kill—the Sword God's guidance, showing her the way.

The path to victory shown by that Sword God, by that summit of the blade—the sword-demon broke through it with nothing but sheer training and a passion bordering on madness.

The rising white lines were sliced apart by that rusted sword, as though he could see them just as clearly.
Countless white lines rose up, and the sword-demon met every single one of them, roaring a battle cry, pressing ever forward toward that summit of the sword he should never have been able to reach.

Her heart pounded. Again and again, with every clash.
Every time their swords crossed, every time a white line was severed, every time their eyes met.

She was falling in love, with the demon of the sword standing before her.
Again, again, over and over, the "Sword Saint" fell in love with this "sword-demon."
Dear. He was dear to her. So dear she could hardly bear it.

—This man was so dear to her, she could hardly bear it.

"—!"

Turning the ceremony into chaos, doing this in front of so many watching people—Theresia found what she was doing so absurd she nearly laughed.
Her cheeks burned. Her heart raced. With every exchange of blows, her affection for him only grew.

If she could, she wanted to throw down her sword this very instant, and leap into his arms.
There was nothing left to take from her. Long, long ago—from the very first moment they met, from the moment his figure first made her heart race—she had already, always, been—

"——"

The option to keep refusing to face him, to flee once again toward the easier path, was crushed away.
It wasn't the Sword God's scolding voice, but the eyes of the demon before her, that denied it with everything he had.

That he would take it, with his own strength; that he would borrow no one's hand, not even hers.
With his strength alone, with his own tenacity alone, with everything he had poured into the sword, he would steal the woman away from the Sword God himself.

Just how long, exactly, how much time had passed.
Just how many times, hundreds, tens of thousands, hundreds of millions of times, had he thought of her.

Blades clashed and crossed, guards locked, tips flashed, strike after strike exchanged.
And then, with the Sword God's furious roar, one final blow swung through to its full extent—

"——"

The rust-red blade snapped in two, its tip spinning off across the dais.
And what accomplished that was, without question, the "Sword Saint's" single blow, poured out with everything she had.

A decisive, all-or-nothing strike, carrying more of the Sword Saint's power than any blow before it.
But,

"Mine."

"——"

"It's mine. The win."

The treasured sword had been wrenched from Theresia's hand.
Her palm still numb from the impact, the treasured blade fell somewhere behind her with a resounding clang. And at Theresia's pale throat, the broken, half-shattered tip of a worthless blade was now pressed.

The beautifully adorned "Sword Saint" had lost to the roughly, doggedly honed "sword-demon."
It was the moment a treasured sword was defeated by a worthless one, the moment the illusion called "Sword Saint" was shattered.

"Someone weaker than me has no reason left to carry a sword."

His voice reached her.
Come to think of it, it had been a very long time since she'd last heard his blunt, curt way of speaking.
And yet, for that to be the very first thing he said—

"If I don't carry a sword... then who will?"

"The reason you swing a sword—I'll inherit it. All you have to do is become the reason I swing mine."

His reason for swinging a sword was to protect something.
That she should become that reason, he said, and as he said it, he pulled the hood off his coat.

His grimy, sullen face glared at her, and Theresia shook her head.
Coming here to say something cool about taking her sword, about protecting her, and yet he really didn't understand a woman's heart at all. Well, he was a sword, so it couldn't be helped.

"You're terrible. Wasting all of someone's resolve, all their determination."

"I'll inherit all of that too. You can forget you ever held a sword and just... live carefree. Yeah. Grow some flowers, and live safe and easy behind my back."

Oh, how—how—

"Protected by your sword?"

"That's right."

"Will you protect me?"

"That's right."

If she was counted among the things dear to him, and if he would answer this longing of hers—

Theresia smiled at the sword-demon's, at Wilhelm's, words.
Then she reached out and touched the blade pressed to her throat, and took a step forward.

Through the touch of the blade, she felt the two years Wilhelm had lived through.
If, all that time, he had been thinking of her the whole while, her chest grew warm at the thought.
An unbearable emotion welled up, and tears filled the corners of Theresia's eyes. They slowly overflowed, tracing down the cheeks of her smiling face.

"Have you started to like flowers?"

"I don't hate them anymore."

"Why do you swing a sword?"

"To protect you."

That was the limit of her patience.
The moment she let go of the sword, the Sword God's voice was already gone, no longer audible at all.

She saw nothing but Wilhelm.
She felt nothing but Wilhelm.
There was nothing, no one, but Wilhelm.

She leaned gently against his chest, and tilted her chin faintly upward.
As Theresia closed her eyes, Wilhelm's lips met hers. At the soft, warm sensation, affection welled up inside her, and Theresia's whole world changed in an instant.

Cheeks flushed, she looked at the dear man before her.
Wilhelm said nothing, only waited quietly for her words.

That attitude of his was almost funny. It was she who was waiting, actually. Since he didn't seem to understand that, she decided, once more, to take the initiative herself, just as before—

"Do you love me?"

"—Figure it out yourself."

He turned his face away, giving that curt answer.
Her eyes went wide at his response, and in that instant, sound came rushing back into a world that had, until then, belonged only to the two of them. The stillness that had held the audience frozen in time broke apart, and a crowd of guards came rushing toward them.
Among them were faces that clearly knew Wilhelm well.

"Sheesh."

At the sight of them, Wilhelm's face relaxed into something like relief, and Theresia puffed out her cheeks.
Looking somewhere else when she was standing right in front of him—what was the meaning of that?
And she hadn't even been given the words she wanted to hear yet.

"There are things I want you to actually say out loud, you know."

"Ahh—"

Scratching his cheek, Wilhelm turned his head as if to dodge the topic. But eventually, as though yielding to Theresia's gaze, he let out a breath, and suddenly pulled her slender waist close.
Then, leaning his face close to her startled ear, he murmured,

"Someday. Whenever I feel like it."

—It felt like it would take a very, very long time before "whenever he felt like it" ever actually came.

Even feeling that dissatisfaction, she found herself looking forward, impatiently, to whatever day that would eventually be.
Weak, as she was, to the man she loved, Theresia let herself forgive her beloved's words.


* * *


—After that too, looking back, there had been all sorts of things.

There'd been an uproar over taking in Wilhelm—who had committed the unheard-of outrage of crashing the ceremony and stealing away its guest of honor—as her husband.
There'd been an uproar when Wilhelm, taking Theresia's place after she stepped down as Sword Saint, joined the Order of Pryde Knights, thanks in part to a recommendation from Bordeaux and others.
There'd been an uproar when Carol, who had once sworn she would serve Theresia for the rest of her life, fell in love with one of Wilhelm's comrades-in-arms, with all the will-they-won't-they of getting married.
There'd been an uproar when Bordeaux, having earned far too much military glory on the battlefield, was given a seat in the council that oversaw important matters of state.

All sorts of things, truly all sorts of things—happy days, every one.


"I love you, Wilhelm. And you?"

"——"

In the end, he never once said those words back to her, not the rest of the way. Even so, he showed it, in place of words, through his actions.
Only a kind woman, or a woman utterly infatuated with a man, could be satisfied by that alone—and Theresia was both, so she let herself go on being satisfied, again and again.

Their time together as husband and wife passed peacefully, unhurried.
Just as he'd promised, Wilhelm never once let Theresia hold a sword again after that ceremony. And Theresia herself felt no lingering attachment to it. The Sword God's voice had long since fallen silent.

Even so, sometimes, the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint still showed its face.
When cooking, for instance, gripping a kitchen knife, she instinctively knew the optimal angle of attack. Though being skilled only at cutting only got her through the preparation stage; as she learned the steps that followed, she came to think being a housewife was, honestly, far more grueling than being a swordswoman.

"...Ah"

It was around this time, too, that she learned how to control the Divine Protection of the Death God. She accidentally cut her finger on a bit of shell while cooking.
Any wound she caused herself, on herself, was still subject to the Divine Protection. She went pale at the sight of the cut on her own skin, panicking about how to stop the bleeding—and the blood stopped almost at once.

—It had been that simple, all along, she thought, almost with disbelief.

Accepting the existence of the Divine Protection, and controlling its power.
For someone who had been given a title as grand as "Sword Saint," the reality was, she'd been a young girl who hadn't even understood the thing that was hers to begin with.
*If only it had been sooner*—that same old thought welled up again, thinking of her brothers, but—

"Theresia."

"—Hm."

At times like that, Wilhelm always seemed to come back at just the right moment. And he'd strip away the flimsy armor Theresia tried to hide her feelings behind, and force his way inside, without ceremony.
And in that, she found her salvation.

"Do you love me?"

"——"

Though he never once answered that particular question, no matter how many times she asked.


* * *


After that, too, there was more. A lot more.
Truly, so much more.

The son born to the two of them, Heinkel.
And the woman Heinkel took as his wife, and the child she bore—their first grandchild, Reinhard.

—There was surely no one to blame for any of it.

Not Heinkel, who had striven, with everything in him, to be sincerely devoted to the sword above all else, the son she had been so proud of.
Not the bride, afflicted by that illness called "Sleeping Beauty," who was robbed of the chance to explain herself to Heinkel, leaving young Reinhard alone.
Not Reinhard, blessed with a talent so immense it made one tremble at first sight, and burdened, in his young body, with a destiny far too heavy for a child to bear.

No one was to blame.
So the one to blame, as always, was herself.

Heinkel grew twisted; his bride was trapped in an endless dream; and Reinhard did his utmost to earn the love of parents like these.
That she'd been the first to notice all of it, and yet had done nothing—that was, more than anything, her own foolishness, her own helplessness.



"There's going to be a battle to bring down the White Whale... something called the Great Conquest. I'm going to be part of it..."

A knight in name only, a grave duty in name only.
Over the offer that had been extended, her son's trembling voice overlapped, and Theresia, alone, quietly made up her mind.


The longsword she had once favored had been kept, all this time, in pristine condition, tended to by Carol, exactly as it had been back then.



"I'm against it. What are you thinking!"

At Theresia's decision, just as she'd known he would, Wilhelm objected.
His gaze pierced her, sharp, unwavering.

His hair had gone white here and there, and his voice had lost the vigor of his youth, but even so, the core of Wilhelm hadn't changed.
His dignity, his passion, his clumsiness—all of it remained exactly as Theresia had loved, and gone on loving, all this time.

"—It's already decided."

"On your own?! Just who exactly would—wait."

At Theresia's stubborn attitude, Wilhelm realized whose idea this must have been.
His face reddened with fury, and sword-intent he couldn't suppress burst forth.

"That damn fool... he should be ashamed of himself...!"

"That's not something either of us has the right to say to him."

"——"

Over the matter of their son, Wilhelm grieved just as much as she did. The fury faded from his face, and he bit his lip.
The hot-tempered man in him hadn't changed, but he had grown adult enough, at least, to hold that heat in check and put on a mask of composure.

"I'll take that duty myself, in your—"

"You have your own duty. You understand that, don't you, Wilhelm? —I don't imagine you've forgotten how grief-stricken Lord Ford was."

"——"

The daughter of Prince Ford had been kidnapped by someone who had infiltrated the royal castle. As the captain of the Order of Pryde Knights, Wilhelm bore the urgent duty of recovering her.
Bringing the sword-demon along on the Great Conquest was simply impossible.

In his stead, the request had come for Theresia—who still, actively, carried the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint—to join the fight.
There was no way to refuse. All those days living peacefully, having set the sword aside, had only ever been possible thanks to her own selfishness.

She couldn't go on relying on that selfishness any longer.

"Theresia, something like this..."

"Wilhelm."

Theresia called to her husband, who was still trying to persuade her. At the sight of his face, faintly holding its breath, she smiled, and asked him a question.
A question it had been a very long time since she'd last asked.

"Do you love me?"

"Wh—"

That flustered look—because she saw, in it, the same feeling as always.
Still smiling, Theresia sent a hand-blade flashing at Wilhelm's shoulder. It cut through the air, slicing skin.
Caught unguarded in front of his wife, Wilhelm couldn't react in time to an attack from so completely outside his expectations, and the wound at his shoulder sprayed blood.

"Theresia... what are you—?"

The shallow gash on his shoulder was already under the effect of the Divine Protection of the Death God. It wasn't a deep wound, but the bleeding wouldn't stop. Not as long as Theresia remained close by his side, not as long as the distance between them didn't grow.

"Theresia?"

Gently, she leaned her body against his chest.
Feeling the strength of the arms that held her, Theresia pressed her lips to the wound on Wilhelm's shoulder.
Her lips were stained crimson with blood, and she tasted, for the first time, the taste of her husband's blood.

"This way, you won't be able to come after me. That wound won't close as long as you're close to me."

"You did something this foolish just for that...? I'll have you know, even if the bleeding never stops, I'd still—"

"If you did that, it would defeat the whole point, wouldn't it?"

Laughing softly, Theresia stepped back from him.
And, pointing at the wound on Wilhelm's shoulder, she said,

"I'm going to leave that wound just as it is. So you won't come after me. Once both our jobs are done, I'll close it for you."

"——"

"Don't worry, who do you think I am? The strongest swordswoman in this world, second only to you."

"You're pushing forty-five, competing with the young ones is..."

"No need for unnecessary comments."

She clapped a hand over his mouth before he could say anything more rude.
Honestly, more than twenty years together, and this was still how it was.

Steel remained, even now, unchanged. And so—

"I love you, Wilhelm."

"——"

"Yes, that's fine. I'll get that answer next time."

"Next time?"

At Wilhelm's furrowed brow, Theresia nodded.
And, swearing their reunion upon the wound at her husband's shoulder—

"When I get back, let me finally hear the words I never got to hear, that day."


* * *


Her memory blows apart.
Her vision blurs, as though caught in a sandstorm, and the sounds around her grow faint, difficult to make out.

"—!"

Somewhere, someone's roar; a scream; a shriek.
Her field of view was filled with green—no, this was the ground. The color of grassland. Looking around, a thick fog closed off the world entirely, so dense she could barely make out anything even ten meters ahead.

The subjugation army had been half-destroyed; her unit, wiped out.

There was a crush of bodies pushing against one another, and amid the thick fog, it wasn't even clear which way to flee.
Only a vague, overwhelming pressure could be felt, somewhere beyond the fog. Voices flew back and forth, urging everyone to flee in the opposite direction from it.

"——"

What had happened, she couldn't recall right away.
In the midst of fierce, brutal fighting, the tide of battle had been in their favor, she thought; she'd felt that even her own strength, retired as she was from the front lines, could still be of use, she thought—

"—?"

Thinking that far, she noticed a faint sense of wrongness.
She looked down at her own palm. Something felt off.

Her hands, her eyes, her legs—nothing wrong with any of them.
And yet, somewhere, there was a sensation like having lost a pair of wings—

"The Divine Protection..."

She realized it.
The sensation of the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint—she couldn't feel it anywhere, now. No matter how far she'd distanced herself from the sword, that presence of the Sword God who'd always lingered nearby—
Even that mocking laughter, now, was nowhere to be found.

"Reinhard—!"

At the same time, Theresia intuitively grasped exactly whose hands the Divine Protection lost from her own body had passed into.
Was it the same sense her uncle must have had, once, when he realized Theresia had inherited the Divine Protection from him? Or perhaps it was simply that Theresia had already sensed, all along, the unfathomable depth of Reinhard's innate talent.

Whichever it was, Theresia had no doubt whatsoever that the next Sword Saint, after her, was Reinhard.
That feeling of certainty might, perhaps, have been a kind of betrayal toward Heinkel, her own flesh and blood—but there was no one left to blame her for it, and no time left in which to be blamed.


"—My, my. For a woman to be left behind alone in a place like this—how brave of you."

A voice spoke then, graceful, and entirely out of place.
Turning, Theresia saw a small silhouette within the thick fog.

White robes, hair the color of platinum-white gold.
An intimate, affectionate gaze, singing of boundless goodwill and friendship toward a total stranger—a love so nauseatingly misplaced it was almost repulsive.

"——"

"Oh dear. It seems I've been disliked."

Raising her longsword, Theresia stepped forward.
In ordinary times, she might have worried about a girl like this. But this was the world of death ruled by the White Whale's thick fog.
For a girl to appear in a place like this went beyond mere strangeness.

Even with the Divine Protection of the Sword Saint gone, Theresia's body still retained a trace of her old swordsmanship talent. More than enough to display the very finest a swordsman could offer, her strike ran true and should have cleaved the girl's small body in two—

"—I want to understand you."

The girl's voice tickled at her eardrums, and her consciousness cut out.
With a small, popping sound.

Her awareness fell into darkness.
As though sinking, limbs bound, into a bottomless pool of lukewarm water, Theresia's body was slowly, steadily swallowed down.

Worries raced through her mind like a stampede—her grandson's future, her son's heart, the bride who bound the two of them together.
And, at the very last,

"Wilhelm."

She called the name of the man she loved, and her consciousness cut out entirely.
And then—


* * *


"...What a pathetic face..."

Beyond her slowly opening eyelids, she saw a face crumpled with grief.
His hair had gone entirely white, and the wrinkles on his face had deepened with the years, and she couldn't help thinking—well, that suited him too, in its own way.

There was no mistaking it.
It was her husband's face. Though it seemed a considerable amount of time had passed since that parting.

"——"

She let out a long breath.
Nearby, she sensed the presence of two others—Heinkel, and probably Reinhard.
Perhaps all three Astrea men had gathered, come all this way, to welcome her back—or to see her off.

They had all been such gentle children, all of them.

"Theresia, I—"

His face wrinkled and worn, Wilhelm's voice caught in his throat.
In front of his son and grandson, no less, what an embarrassing sight.
Where had his dignity, his composure gone? Though, thinking back on it, this soft, vulnerable side of him had always stood out surprisingly often, hadn't it.

"Hey, Wilhelm..."

Her own voice came out hoarse, and yet strangely young.
As though it didn't belong to her—no, it was certainly hers, and yet, she should have been thoroughly an old woman by now.
It sounded almost like the voice of someone falling in love for the very first time, and that embarrassed her.

"——"

Ticklish, this feeling, like falling in love for the first time all over again.
With so little time left, they were wasting it just gazing at each other.

But that was fine, too.

The words Theresia had needed to say, she had said, plenty of times already. Surely Wilhelm understood that too.
So it was time, and opportunity, and words, that were needed now—on his side.

Theresia only had to wait quietly for those words.
He would make her wait, but he would always, without fail, live up to her expectations. That was the kind of man Wilhelm Trias was.
And that was the kind of husband Wilhelm van Astrea was.

"There's something... I have to tell you."

"——"

"I— I've never been good with words... never been able to properly get my own thoughts across to anyone, and that's caused you so much trouble... which is why, for more than twenty years, I never once..."

"——"

"I may have left you uncertain, for twenty years. But I—"

"—You foolish man."

Watching him struggle, clumsy as ever, trying somehow to force out the words, she could no longer hold it back.
She laughed. Honestly, what on earth was this man even talking about.

"You really never noticed, did you?"

His face on the verge of tears, doing his utmost to put his heart into words—she reached a hand toward that cheek of his.
Her body felt terribly heavy. There was hardly any strength left in it at all—but pouring every last bit of strength she had left into her fingertips, she wiped away the tear tracing down his cheek.

"You'd been telling me, the whole time."

"——"

Had he thought he'd been hiding it?
Just because he'd never put it into words—had he really believed he'd managed to keep it hidden all this while?

"Your eyes, your voice, your manner, everything you've ever done—the whole time."

Everything Wilhelm had ever directed at Theresia.
More clearly than anything else could have, it had told her this man's heart—

"I— you—"
"You— I—"

So, that was enough.

"—I love you."


* * *


From beginning to end, it truly was a fortunate life, wasn't it.
She'd had brothers who loved her, a friend of the same sex who cared for her deeply, so many people who had helped her along the way, and she had met Wilhelm.

There were surely, still, all sorts of problems left unresolved.
But that would be all right—because she believed, in the people they had become, that it would.

But, in truth, there was just one thing.
Just one thing, at the very end, she regretted never having been able to ask.


—If you found out it was actually love at first sight, I wonder how surprised you'd have been?


* * *


Exchanging words of love with one another—that was the last thing they did.

Smiling in contentment, cheeks flushed with affection, eyes glistening with tears, the figure of Theresia van Astrea lost its shape and crumbled apart within the span of a single blink.
Within Wilhelm's arms, where he knelt, the bloodied woman who had, until moments ago, been gasping for breath left no trace of herself behind; all that remained was a pile of ash—the only evidence left to prove she had ever been there at all.

"——"

Her life burned entirely away within his own arms, leaving nothing but ash. Wilhelm lowered his eyes to that remnant of Theresia, and simply, silently, kept his head bowed.

"...Satisfied now?"

And then, in place of the silent, unmoving Wilhelm, a man's voice rose.
A red-haired middle-aged man—Heinkel—turned a gaze full of hatred on Reinhard, who stood quietly nearby.
Reinhard turned to meet that gaze slowly, and let out a breath.

"Satisfied... how do you mean?"

"Don't play dumb, you can see it plain as day! Satisfied? I bet you are! Now the title of Sword Saint is officially, completely yours, isn't it—congratulations! And now there's no denying the rumor that you let your predecessor die just to take it, either. Well? Satisfied, aren't you? Answer me!"

"I don't understand what you mean by that at all."

"Don't give me that composed look, you brat!"

Heinkel, breathing hard, lunged to grab hold of Reinhard. But Reinhard slipped past his grasping fingers and, with a single hand, restrained his own father as he stumbled off balance.
There wasn't the faintest trace, in the current Sword Saint's demeanor, of having just crossed blades with the previous one. In truth, it hadn't even been a contest.
As if driven home by that very fact, Heinkel's throat clicked with a faint tremor of unease.

"Don't get carried away, Reinhard...!"

As if to cover for that wavering in his own heart, Heinkel grew even more frantic, jabbing a finger at Reinhard, spittle flying.

"However pretty you make it sound, it doesn't change what I saw. You're the one who cut down my mother... Theresia van Astrea. That's a fact. I'll make it public. I'll spread it everywhere, make sure no one ever accepts you as 'Sword Saint' again!"

"——"

"However composed you act, you can't let go of that honor, 'Sword Saint,' can you? Maybe you could've kept it vague before, but not anymore. Killed your own flesh and blood, and you call yourself 'Sword Saint'? The kingdom's sword? Ha! Don't make me laugh! You murderer!"

"Vice Captain, no matter how many times you say that, I don't understand what you mean. —The idea that I cut down my predecessor is a misunderstanding on your part, Vice Captain."

"Wha—...?"

At Reinhard's quiet response, Heinkel's face reddened as he pressed his accusation, but his eyes went wide at what Reinhard said. There was no sign, in Reinhard, of evasion or excuse-making.
It wasn't a matter of choosing convenient words. Reinhard was simply stating a fact.

"The enemy you fought a moment ago was nothing but a corpse, animated by some secret art. There's no way it could have been the previous Sword Saint... our grandmother. Perhaps you're mistaken about something?"

"——"

At Reinhard's words, Heinkel's face went blank with shock.
Then he thrust his fingers into his own red hair, and clawed at it violently. A faintly cracked laugh escaped him, and Heinkel, smiling with something like madness, said,

"Then what the hell was that, at the end? What was that thing talking with the old man!? Glaring at me, at you, like it hated us... if that wasn't Mom, then what was it!?"

"—That's enough, Heinkel."

Baring his teeth, Heinkel burned with an emotion beyond hatred—and it was Wilhelm, who had stayed silent all this while, who finally stopped his son's fury.
Still crouched where he was, the old swordsman tore a strip from his own sleeve and used it to bind the wound on his right leg—the spot pierced through by a longsword.

The wound that should never have closed under the power of the Divine Protection of the Death God had lost its effect the instant Theresia's existence was gone. —No, in truth, it had already stopped the moment the sane Theresia had briefly returned to herself. What had begun to ache instead was the wound carved into his left shoulder, from that parting long ago.

The sane Theresia had marked his left shoulder; the corpse of Theresia, his right leg.
The wounds carved by the Divine Protection of the Death God had lost their power the moment both of those Theresias were gone.

"Stop it, huh... Dad! Are you really okay with this!? This guy...!"

"Enough, Heinkel. ...Enough."

Still trying to press his attack, Heinkel was stopped again by Wilhelm's repeated command.
Removing his sleeveless coat, Wilhelm spread the fabric out and wrapped it around the ashes that had been Theresia. To leave her to be carried off by the wind, just like that, was too lonely to bear.
At the very least, he thought, he had to return these ashes to her grave.

"—!"

At the sight of his own father like that, Heinkel bit down on his frustration and swallowed his words. Then Wilhelm, having gathered the ashes, rose to his feet, unsteady on them.
Even having stopped the bleeding, the amount of blood he'd lost was considerable. The wound at the base of his right leg ran deep, and there was real concern about letting him walk alone. Reinhard, instinctively, reached to support his swaying shoulder.
But—

"—Don't touch me!!"

"——"

Wilhelm's furious shout knocked away the fingers reaching toward him.
Reinhard froze his raised arm mid-motion, and Wilhelm made no move to turn and face him. Neither of their gazes met the other's, and the sword-demon simply let out a quiet breath.

"Reinhard..."

"—Yes."

Unlike Wilhelm's trembling voice, Reinhard's voice was steady, composed.
Closing his eyes for a moment at the sound of it, Wilhelm found the words to speak.

—It was a question.

"Do you regret it? Cutting down your grandmother... Theresia?"

"——"

There was a faint pause before the answer to that question came.
Perhaps, like the exchange with Heinkel moments before, it might have been dismissed as a meaningless question, not worth answering.
But Reinhard paused a single beat, and answered.

"No. —I did what was right. I don't regret it."

"...I see. Just as I thought."

"——"

"You're right. I'm the one who's wrong. —So there's nothing left for us to talk about."

Answering quietly, Wilhelm turned his back on Reinhard.
Grandfather and grandson, without ever looking each other in the eye, brought that decisive exchange to its end.
And then, raising a finger to point toward the center of the city, Wilhelm said,

"Sir Garfiel has headed that way, but I'm worried about the city hall. If you're able, please go lend your support. 'Sword Saint' Sir Reinhard."

"——"

At those words, so oddly formal, so distant, Reinhard looked in the direction the finger indicated.
Then he nodded, and finally turned his gaze to Heinkel.
Heinkel, still smoldering with hatred, caught his breath faintly at those blue eyes, but Reinhard let that small flicker of fear pass without comment, and said,

"It's dangerous outside. Vice Captain, please make your way to a shelter, if you can. —Together with Wilhelm-dono."

"L-like I need you to tell me that! Get lost already!"

Struck by those words, which sounded like nothing so much as sour grapes, Reinhard turned away. He ran toward the waterway, then kicked off the surface of the water and leapt, with a force that seemed to carry him clean over the buildings, and vanished toward the center of the city.

Watching that superhuman burst of motion disappear, Heinkel spat on the ground. Then, slowly, he hurried after Wilhelm, who was dragging his right leg as he walked.

"Father, going alone like this..."

"Leave me be. I don't want anyone looking at my face right now."

"Father..."

"Don't worry about me. Just think about your own safety. Hide in some building, or a shelter. ...That should be enough."

Leaving only that much of his own opinion behind, Wilhelm walked on, leaving Heinkel behind.
Clutching the coat that held his wife's ashes, he limped away, his back growing smaller in the distance.

"——"

Unable to call out to that retreating back, unable to walk alongside him, Heinkel was left behind.
Left behind, and eventually, even Wilhelm's figure vanished from sight, and Heinkel—

"What... the hell... what the hell, what the hell, what, what, damn it, what the hell is this!"

Alone now in the empty plaza, Heinkel glared down at the stone pavement and let his fury pour out. He clawed at his own head, screaming out a rage that had no words to it, and hurled the sword at his hip to the ground.
The beautiful knight's sword struck the earth with a hard clang and skidded, rolling away.

"Damn it, damn it, damn it, damn it, every single one of them...! Every single one of them can just die...! Just die, damn it, just diiiiiiiiie!!"

In the empty plaza, with no one left to hear, only Heinkel's blood-curdling scream echoed on and on.
On and on, endlessly, a scream tangled with resentment and grief rang out, high and far—




Grandfather, father, and grandson—the battlefield where all of House Astrea had gathered came, at last, to its end.

The grandmother, the mother, the wife.
Theresia van Astrea's final moments left, in the hearts of all three men, wounds each their own.


—And here, every battlefield of the Battle for the Watergate City of Priestella came to its final close.
