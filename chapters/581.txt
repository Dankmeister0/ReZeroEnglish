Arc 7 Chapter 76: "An Encounter in the Imperial Capital"

Rem happened to see the woman while wandering the mansion where she was being kept under house arrest.

In the midst of the battle for the fortress city of Guaral, Rem had been taken from the city along with Flop O'Connell in order to treat his wounds. Her person had since been transported to the imperial capital, where she was now confined within a mansion that could well have counted among the city's most stately residences.

That said, "confined" was a strong word for the relatively free circumstances Rem found herself in.
She wasn't locked in some cramped room or dungeon, nor was she subjected to excessive violence or verbal abuse. It could hardly be called peaceful, but it might fairly be called even-keeled treatment.
Meals were prepared for her at every hour, and she was even offered time to bathe. In some sense, it was undeniably more comfortable than life in the Shudrak settlement had been.
Still, not being locked up didn't mean she was free to come and go from the mansion as she pleased — her movements were watched by the soldiers on guard duty, the private troops of the mansion's master, Berstetz Fondalfon. It wasn't a situation that let her forget her lack of freedom entirely.

In any case, freedom or lack thereof aside, the situation Rem found herself in was not one she welcomed.
No doubt Kuna and Holly had been made to feel their own inadequacy on her account, and it wouldn't be surprising if Priscilla was furious that she'd simply vanished without a word.
And above all, she wondered how the others would react once those who'd journeyed east returned.
Rui, Medium, and that boy, Natsuki Subaru—

"—Ah."

It was while her heart ached over such thoughts that Rem heard that thin, faint voice.
Turning toward the sound, she saw an unfamiliar figure in the garden at the center of the mansion — a courtyard whose purpose seemed less to maintain lush greenery than to serve as a landing spot for Flying Dragons.
Rem hadn't met every soldier and servant working in the mansion, but she could tell at a glance that this person belonged to neither category.
Because this person wasn't walking on their own two feet — they were seated in a chair with wheels.

"Ngh... unh...!"

A pale-skinned woman with richly colored, wavy brown hair parted into two bunches on either side of her head. Her long-lashed blue eyes wavered as she threw her slight shoulders forward, straining with all her strength.
Her trembling arms were pushing against the very wheels of the wheeled chair she sat in. The wheels' frames were large, built so that the person seated could turn them by hand to move forward or backward.
But it seemed one of the wheels had caught in a groove at the edge of the passage, leaving her stuck fast, unable to move either way.

"——"

Biting her thin lips, the woman made repeated, desperate attempts to turn the wheel.
Even so, her slender arms couldn't muster the necessary strength, and the wheel only rocked uselessly back and forth in tiny motions. If she'd called out for help, someone surely would have come running — but she didn't.
She refused to rely on anyone. Rem felt something like kinship at that stubbornness, though of course she had no way of knowing what feelings lay behind it—

"—Let me help you."

"Ah..."

Unable to just leave her be, Rem walked up to the woman stuck in the groove and spoke to her from behind.
Startled, the woman's eyes went wide as she turned just her head, catching her breath at Rem's presence. But almost immediately her expression turned sheepish, her lips working wordlessly before falling silent.
At that reaction, Rem felt a fresh stirring of kinship as she set a hand on the back of the wheelchair.
The chair's backrest had handles on either side, meant for someone standing behind to push it. Rem gripped them and pushed forward.

"Here we go—"

With the force she applied, the wheels jolted with a clatter and the chair broke free of the groove it had been caught in.
Carried by the momentum, the chair rolled slightly forward before the woman stopped it with her hands. Then she spun the wheels in place, turning to face Rem.

And then—

"...Don't do unnecessary things."

—was the rather pointed greeting she offered.

"——"

Rem blinked her round eyes, momentarily taken aback by the woman's words.
Meanwhile, the woman looked away from Rem, her lips still working.

"I didn't need help with something like that. Besides, what about you, using a cane and all? W-worry about yourself first."

"Er... thank you for your concern."

"It's not concern! Are your ears rotten or something? If not, then the problem must be with your head instead."

Rem realized belatedly, from the halting way it was said, that this was apparently meant as an insult.
The tone was sharp, but there was a subtle awkwardness to it, as though she wasn't used to speaking this way — which robbed the insult of any real power to wound. Frankly, compared to dealing with Priscilla, this was about the difference in intensity between a puppy and Mizelda.

"J-just get lost already. I don't have time to bother with you... with someone like you."

"Does not having time mean you have some duty to attend to?"

"Duty...!? Th-that's right. I have a proper duty. Unlike you..."

Rem's question made the woman's cheeks stiffen as she answered. Partway through, she knit her thin brows and looked Rem up and down.
Then, bringing her right thumb to her mouth, she gave it a light bite.

"...You're not a face I recognize, and you don't look like you belong here either. Who are you?"

"—I'm called Rem. Circumstances brought me here — I was kidnapped and taken to this mansion."

"Kidnapped..."

"Yes. Um, may I ask your name?"

To the woman muttering while gnawing her nail, Rem stepped closer as she asked for a name.
She half expected to be coldly brushed off.

"—Katya."

But surprisingly readily, she — Katya — gave her name.
Whether there was some motive behind it, or whether she'd simply answered reflexively while lost in thought, Rem couldn't tell. Katya kept chewing her nail thoughtfully.

"Kidnapped, huh. Does that mean you're a hostage too?"

"A hostage... I suppose that would be the word. Though I don't think I'm really suited to that role..."

If anything, the only person likely to be desperately worried by Rem's disappearance was Subaru.
Of course, she imagined the Shudrak people and the O'Connell siblings would worry too, and Priscilla might so much as raise an eyebrow, but none of it would sway the larger course of events.
If Rem's presence were ever to affect the bigger picture, it would only be through Subaru's swaying it.
But—

"For him to do something like that..."

She didn't think he was capable of it, nor did she think he ought to be.
In their short time together, she'd seen him again and again, tormented by unreasonable circumstances beyond his strength, struggling on regardless. Carrying the burden of believing he had to do everything himself.
Rem found that somehow unbearable to watch. Not because she disliked him, or held any hatred for him.
Why that was, though, was something she still couldn't quite put into words.

"——"

"...Hey, could you not go quiet on me?"

"Ah, sorry. I was lost in thought for a moment... Katya-san, what is your relation to Berstetz-san?"

Called out by Katya, whose eyes had sharpened, Rem apologized for her silence and asked her question.
Since she was in this mansion, Katya was surely connected to Berstetz in some way. But Rem found it hard to imagine what that relationship might be.
If Katya had been a fighting force like Madelyn, or looked every bit an imperial soldier, the connection would have been obvious — but nothing about her appearance suggested either.
The one thing Rem could think of—

"Are you Berstetz-san's daughter, or granddaughter, perhaps?"

"The Chancellor's? Stop it, don't be ridiculous. For one thing, Berstetz-sama is unmarried and has no family. I'm nothing so grand as that."

"Is that so? That's surprising."

"Surprising?" Katya tilted her head, but Rem shook her own to brush off the real meaning behind it.
When Rem had once faced Berstetz directly, he'd explained to her his reason for rebelling against the Emperor — his distrust of an Emperor who refused to produce an heir. From the standpoint of Berstetz, who bore the burden of supporting the nation, the Emperor's choice must have looked like a dereliction of duty, a betrayal of sorts.
Rem, dragged into the affair as she'd been, found it hard to sympathize with that logic, but she could at least understand it.
And yet, for Berstetz — who had raised rebellion over such a reason — to himself have foregone starting a family just as the Emperor had, struck her as somehow not sitting right.
Of course, she supposed Berstetz had his own reasons for that too.

"But if you're neither of those, why are you at this mansion, Katya-san?"

"...You're slow on the uptake, aren't you. I already asked if you were a hostage too."

"—Then, Katya-san, are you here as a hostage as well?"

When Rem asked back, Katya nodded, grudgingly, with a sour look on her face.
Katya too was being held captive in this mansion, as someone's hostage. At that fact, Rem felt a small jolt of surprise as she looked around at the mansion once more.

A vast, stately imperial capital estate.
And yet within it, along with Rem and Katya, and the injured Flop, surely many other secrets lay sleeping as well.
Having faced Berstetz directly, Rem had come to see that the old man was a brilliant, calculating figure — a formidable wall standing in the way of Abel's attempt to reclaim the throne.
But the true extent of Berstetz's menace, a man who wouldn't hesitate at underhanded schemes or intrigue, was perhaps something Rem's own imagination and guesswork could never fully measure.

"With Katya-san taken hostage, your family must be worried sick."

"...Who knows. To him, I might just be some replaceable tool. If I- if I become a nuisance, he'd probably let go of me just as easily."

Looking away, Katya spat those words out in response to what Rem had said.
But Rem could tell, even setting aside the halting way it was said, that the venom wasn't Katya's true feeling. There was something familiar in Katya's manner.

"——"

Because it was, after all, the same shade of stubbornness as Rem's own.
Rem had no idea who Katya was trying to push away with that venom. But the words just now had been meant less for Rem's ears than to convince herself.
It was a lie told because she didn't want to hold excessive expectation or trust toward that person.

A lie told because she didn't want to admit that that person thought of her as irreplaceable, nor that that person was irreplaceable to her in turn.
It wasn't a lie meant to deceive someone else — it was, quite clearly, a lie meant to deceive herself.

"Katya-san, would you like to talk a little more?"

"Wh-what... doing something so presumptuous, the Chancellor will—"

"I don't think you'll be scolded. And if you are, just tell him I forced my way in."

"That's exactly what's happening, isn't it— ah, hey!"

Her lips trembling as she tried to refuse Rem, Katya found Rem quickly circling around behind her. Tucking her cane under her arm, Rem gently took hold of the wheelchair's handles.
By pushing the chair, Rem could also use it to support herself — she wouldn't need the cane to keep from stumbling. It wasn't a bad position to be in, all things considered.

"D-doing whatever you please... I don't even know you—"

"Which way is your room, Katya-san? Mine's on the west side."

"...The east side. The opposite."

"Understood. Let's head there, then."

Hearing Katya's feeble reply, Rem began walking, pushing the chair. There was a brief resistance at first, but Katya's fingers soon left the wheels, letting herself be pushed along.
Rem was aware she was pushing things along somewhat forcefully, but she didn't hesitate to press on.

It wasn't really out of any calculation — that Katya held some crucial secret about this mansion, or that becoming close with her might reveal a way out of this dead end.
Katya's existence probably wouldn't do anything to change the situation. Rem understood, however vaguely, that the reason Berstetz — who held both Rem and Katya hostage — had no family of his own was so that he himself would have no weaknesses.
Even so, there were a few reasons Rem wanted to reach out to Katya like this—

"—There's so much I need to understand."

She held the conviction and the resolve that she mustn't remain the weak version of herself who, taken hostage and confined, did nothing but sow seeds of anxiety and worry among the people who knew her.
And so Rem struggled on. Even without knowing the way, in her own way.

*"—No one can ever flee from themselves. Never forget my words, and apply yourself well."*

There were things she needed to do, rather than simply deny her own helplessness.
To put into practice the words of the one who had told her so, plainly and clearly — and not remain standing still.

—Sensing a presence in the room, Flop slowly opened his eyes.

"——"

Shaking off the clinging hand of drowsiness, Flop's mind returned to the present.
Beyond his open eyelids was a clean white ceiling, and understanding caught up with him at once: he was in a room of the mansion where he was being held captive.
By now, at least, he was well past the stage of panicking over the fact. He was also well past the stage of trying to flail his limbs about in sudden alarm and paying dearly for it in pain.
Still, calling this situation "familiar" didn't quite sit right either.

"Spending day after day laid up in bed like this — it does make a merchant like me a bit anxious."

He was, after all, a traveling merchant whose earnings depended directly on that day's work and travel plans.
He couldn't claim to be especially skilled at it, but careful planning was the surest shortcut to success in trade. Even setting aside success, one had to keep working just to earn even a little.
So this life of being tied down to a bed, however leisurely it might look from outside, left his heart bustling with restlessness on the inside.

All the more so when he thought about how his sister Medium would react, if she learned he'd become a captive.
In any case—

"I can hardly ask the missus to hurry up and heal me completely right away. It's a bit maddening."

Rem, who had likewise become a captive of this mansion, was here to treat Flop's injuries.
For Subaru's sake, and for the sake of the many people worried about her, she needed to be returned safely no matter what — but her position here was hardly secure.
As things stood, Rem's role was as Flop's healer, and once his wounds fully healed, her place here afterward would immediately become uncertain.
Surely they wouldn't simply dispose of Rem the moment she was no longer needed — or so he wanted to believe.

"That, I imagine, comes down to your thinking, and your mood, doesn't it, Lady Madelyn?"

Having wrapped up his organized train of thought, Flop tossed the remark toward the doorway.
He'd woken sensing someone's presence, after all. He hadn't heard the other's voice, but it was plain someone was in the room — and there were generally only two people who ever came to visit Flop.

Either Rem, coming for treatment, or Madelyn, who dropped by often.
Rem, given the unexpected freedom she'd been granted in the mansion, was one thing, but facing Madelyn required Flop to brace himself, more or less, every time.
After all, for all her youthful appearance, her arm strength was tremendous, and it would be all too easy for those claws of hers to tear Flop apart. On top of that, she was a proud member of the Dragonkin — even Flop, with all his experience navigating the world, couldn't quite gauge what the correct way to handle her was.

And besides — he'd probably be scolded for not reading the situation — Flop didn't much want to deceive Madelyn, or trick her with lies.
It wasn't as though Flop had lived his whole life without ever telling a lie. Lacking in physical strength as he was, he took some pride in having relied more than most on the power of words, here in the Empire.
Even so, he didn't want to pull the wool over Madelyn's eyes, purely because she sincerely cared about someone Flop knew well as "family."
Some other "family" of his would probably despair at him for living such a foolish way of life, though.

"But that's just the kind of man I am — can't be helped."

It was a resolve one could call stubbornness if one liked, and Flop spent his time in the mansion by it.
So today too, he expected to be called upon to wage the usual battle against Madelyn — telling no lies, yet using every ounce of conversational skill to keep her interest from running dry.
But that wasn't how it turned out. Because—

"Lady Madelyn? Have you come begging for the rest of yesterday's story? I left off right at the part where I asked what astonished remark Balleroy made the very first time Carillon took to the sky—"

"—An interesting topic, but not one for now."

"——"

Still lying on the bed, Flop had been speaking to the person he assumed was there, and the voice that answered — in a tone quite different from what he'd expected — silenced him.
He'd been certain it was Madelyn at the door, but the voice that came back was a man's. And more than that, it was a man's voice Flop recognized.

This was an honest boast, but Flop never forgot a voice once he'd heard it.
Even a voice heard from the edge of some bustling market, once heard, could be picked out and identified without fail. So there was no mistaking this voice either.
Though the feelings that voice stirred in Flop, upon hearing it, were about as complicated as they came.
After all, his relationship with its owner had — at least in Flop's own mind — changed drastically from before.

"Village Chief-kun... or perhaps I ought to say, His Imperial Majesty-kun?"

"You correct yourself, and that's what you land on? Either way, it remains a disrespectful form of address."

Calling out quietly, Flop strained his trembling arms and body, forcing himself upright in bed. A faint numbness and tightness still lingered in his upper body, but it wasn't unbearable.
Raising his eyes, the figure standing at the doorway finally came into view.

Standing there was a young man with black hair and pale skin, striking with his sharp gaze.
His attire, based on a deep, rich red, and his slender bearing matched precisely what Flop had seen countless times before. The features, the cast of his eyes and nose — all of it was familiar.

Abel — no, Vincent Vollachia.

That was the name of the current Emperor of the Sacred Vollachia Empire — the rebellious wolf who had been driven from the throne and now sought to reclaim it.
Called disrespectful by the very man himself, Flop gave a wry smile and bowed his head.

"My apologies, I suppose I ought to correct myself. Or, hm, perhaps I need to mind my manner of speech altogether. Oh dear, this is troubling. Would you mind if I started over from the beginning?"

"Unnecessary. Starting over does not erase what has already been done. More to the point — are you a subject with the resolve to swear loyalty and devotion to the Emperor of Vollachia?"

"I wonder. He is, after all, the Emperor of the country I live in. I suppose I do have some measure of patriotism and gratitude of my own. Under His Excellency Vincent Vollachia's reign, conflicts between races, including skirmishes, have dropped drastically. I'm grateful the country's become easier to live in."

"A loaded way of putting it. You manage to spin quite the empty words, standing before me."

Shrugging his slender shoulders, the Emperor advanced slowly into the room.
As his figure came into sharper focus up close, Flop's eyes widened slightly. However he looked at it, from any angle, the man's form and features were exactly the Abel he knew.
And yet, Flop understood full well that the man before him was not Abel himself.

"I'm woefully underinformed, and it's rather embarrassing to admit, but the resemblance really is remarkable. I suppose it has to be that convincing, to pull off a body double's role."

"Speak so carelessly and I may have to seal that mouth of yours shut. The only reason you're being sheltered here at all is because it is the wish of the 'Flying Dragon General,' Madelyn Eschart. But a single general's wish, weighed against my own whims — which way do you suppose the scale tips? It shouldn't be hard to guess."

"Your warning to mind my mouth is duly received. But I can't promise I'll actually manage to put it into practice! After all, my feelings on the matter are quite complicated! I've got plenty to say about that face of yours!"

"——"

At Flop pointing sharply and declaring this so loudly, the other man fell silent.
The other — in this case, someone who wasn't Abel, though Flop wasn't sure what else to call him — would, for the sake of convenience, be referred to as Vincent here.
That this Vincent was a false emperor scheming to usurp the throne was a fact Flop knew well. But Flop himself had no particular grounds to condemn him for it.
If Flop and Abel had been bound by an unshakable friendship, the kind of relationship where Flop would raise his voice on Abel's behalf over every matter — well, that wasn't the case here.
If anything, it was precisely in that sense that the relationship between Flop and Abel was a complicated one.

"Unfortunately, the person I really have things to say to is the other fellow wearing your same face. Even so, the resemblance really is uncanny... I used to help the mister with his makeup for playing Village Chief-kun, you know, and that's the spitting image of his bare face underneath."

"—I've heard of the affair at the fortress city. You were involved in the scheme too, then. All of you together — it seems nothing but a reckless outrage, trying to distort the Emperor's reign through such base methods. An outrage beyond forgiveness."

"You think so? It was an enjoyable venture, if I do say so. After all, no one was sacrificed."

"A matter of result, not intent."

Loosening his cheeks into a smile, Flop thought back on the fortress city scheme he'd carried out together with Subaru and Abel.
It had been a plan executed on an idea audacious enough to stun even Flop, and among all the schemes and plans Flop had ever been party to, it had been the most delightful of them all.
Everyone involved in that operation must surely have wished for its success.

"Lady Taritta, Lady Kuna, myself too. —Which is exactly why I can't make sense of it."

"——"

"Village Chief-kun, His Imperial Majesty-kun... whatever I should call him, since I can't quite settle on it — why was he driven from the throne and placed in the position he's in now?"

Pausing there, Flop said "more to the point" and fixed his eyes on Vincent.
On the usurper of the throne, whose face was the very image of the Emperor's, his expression unmoved.

"Why did you all have to shake the very foundations of a stable Empire, to set these events in motion at all?"

"—Quite the glib tongue you have."

"That's my stock in trade. I'm a merchant, after all. My sister often compliments me too — apparently I'm rather handsome. That's stock in trade as well."

Flop laughed, showing his teeth, and Vincent, nodding magnanimously at the remark, suddenly reached out and seized his face, pulling him close.
Forced forward against his will, Flop groaned "mgh" at the pain in his wounds. But Vincent paid the pained sound no mind, glaring at him from close range.

"That face, and that mouth — I could take them from you here and now, as atonement for your disrespect."

The voice that slid coldly into his ears carried an intimidating force that ran from his head down through his body, freezing his very heart.
It was directed at someone who wouldn't hold his tongue, at his disrespect toward the Emperor — cold and merciless, yet laced with a faint trace of genuine indignation.
Even as he considered what that might mean—

"...If the point is to teach me to watch my mouth, taking my voice might make more sense."

Once again offering up an answer sure to provoke further anger, Flop looked back at Vincent, his face still contorted from being gripped.
For an instant, Vincent's black eyes narrowed at the answer, but whatever emotion passed behind them was never let out into the open, and was shaken off.
Flop's face was released, and Vincent closed one eye. It was the same appraising look Abel, whom Vincent imitated, so often gave when observing someone.

"I told you plainly which weighs heavier — my own whim, or Madelyn Eschart's wish — yet her defection, or at least her rebellion against me, would be unavoidable in the end. Was your attitude calculated with that much foresight?"

"Eh? Ah — ah, I see. True enough, Lady Madelyn would likely be furious if I died. That would be inconvenient for you too... I hadn't thought that far ahead, but you're right."

"——"

"If I, a captive, were to risk my life trying to repay His Imperial Majesty-kun somehow, it could well be enough to push Lady Madelyn toward defection. —Unfortunately, that's not something I can manage right now, though."

Prompted by Vincent's words, Flop recalculated the value of his own life anew.
As things stood, with Madelyn's interest fixed on him, if Flop's life were carelessly taken, taming her — beast-like and volatile as she was — would become far more difficult.
If Madelyn turned against Vincent, the rebel side backing Abel would gain an advantage.
It made sense that Vincent might suspect this had been a deliberate provocation on Flop's part.

"I hear you don't bend to threats, and don't hesitate to stake your life. As Berstetz says, it seems you truly are a troublesome seed of calamity."

"Seems I've ended up in that position without meaning to. So, what will you do about it, fake emperor-kun? I'm quite the handful, I assure you!"

"I hear there is a healer's daughter who was brought here along with you. If you value that girl's safety, you'd best mind your excess words and attitude both, and stay quiet."

"Completely shut down!"

With Rem held over him as leverage, Flop suddenly found himself with no moves left to make.
But it couldn't be helped. Rem had to be returned safely, no matter what.
And besides—

"Even I haven't decided what I ought to do about all this."

In the end, one could say Flop's position had become the same as Madelyn's.
If so, then like Madelyn, should he join the rebellion she was siding with, unleash some vicious scheme to bring down the deposed — no, the usurping — Abel, and make him pay for his crimes bit by bit?
A mere merchant, whose only real virtue was being called handsome by his own sister.

"Even setting aside the matter of Madelyn Eschart, Berstetz will see that no harm comes to you. Know your place, and spend your time here without stirring up trouble, if you know what's good for you."

Fixing his eyes on the now-silent Flop, Vincent said this and turned to leave.
From the content, Flop understood he meant to bring this meeting to a close. Whether he'd judged there was nothing more to be gained from Flop, or whether he'd already accomplished whatever purpose brought him here — as Flop considered this—

"Fake emperor-kun, what did you come to this room to do?"

"——"

"It's beyond my imagination, never having staged a rebellion myself, but even so, doing my best to imagine it — the Emperor's position must be a busy one. Even without anyone else, when there are people like His Imperial Majesty-kun who refuse to give up and keep raising a ruckus, the workload surely never lets up."

His usual peddling wasn't exactly easy, but on days when a shipment happened to line up perfectly with what a buyer wanted, the business that resulted was on a completely different scale of hectic from the norm.
The false emperor's position, too, surely could never be secure so long as the real Emperor remained alive.
So under such circumstances, why had Vincent bothered to come all the way to Flop's bedside?
That was—

"Could it be that you wanted to hear from me what state His Imperial Majesty-kun was in?"

"Were I, as you so foolishly put it, truly a usurper, that would be a rather tasteless thing to do — to try and pry out, from a nearby source, what became of the one I myself cast down."

"The importance of gathering information doesn't really differ much between trade and war, I'd think. If that's the case, I don't think trying to learn the other party's situation is tasteless at all."

"—Even so, you're off the mark. I have no need to hear it from your mouth. What that man is doing now, I've seen with my own eyes. Words were exchanged as well."

"Is that... so."

An excuse born of desperation, after being caught out.
Flop took in Vincent's answer, sensing it wasn't the kind of flippant remark it might otherwise seem.
What kind of circumstances had led to a direct meeting, an exchange of words, with Abel — that was something he could only imagine, but it didn't seem like a lie at all.
He was worried, too, about Medium, Taritta, and Subaru, who ought to be at Abel's side, but the chances of getting any further answers by pressing on that point seemed slim.

At the very least, it seemed clear that whatever had happened in that direct meeting, Vincent hadn't been able to do anything decisive about Abel. Had he been able to, he surely would have declared victory already, and promptly. It seemed unlikely he'd be tasteless enough to come all this way just to gloat about it, but continuing the conversation while concealing the facts entirely would be equally tasteless — and Flop didn't think either was quite the case.

Which meant Vincent hadn't come to hear things from Flop at all.
If so, there was only one possibility that came to mind.

"If it wasn't something you wanted to ask, then maybe... there was something you wanted to tell me?"

"Berstetz called your position troublesome, if I recall. Looking at it with my own eyes, I'd say your very way of being falls squarely within the bounds of 'troublesome' as well."

"Should I take that as a compliment? I think I will, since believing so works out better for my state of mind."

At those words, Flop thought, Vincent's feet — which had been about to carry him away — came to a stop.

"——"

Honestly, Flop had no idea whether stopping Vincent here was, for himself, a good thing or a bad thing.
If Vincent's scheme succeeded, and the usurpation of the throne was fully completed, Abel would surely be driven not only from the Empire, but from this world entirely.

Was that what he himself wanted?
For the man connected to the death of Balleroy Temeglyph — someone who had once been dear "family" to Flop — to have everything stripped from him, to lose his life — was that what Flop wanted?

"Up until now, there's never been a time I didn't know what I wanted..."

He tried to think whether that had ever been true, before reconsidering almost immediately.
There was no need to even think back on it. Before he'd ever met Balleroy and the others, Flop himself had lived solely for the sake of protecting Medium, unable to wish for anything beyond that.
It was the same as it had been then. And if so, the answer, and the way to find it, would be the same as back then too.

*"You lot can choose whatever the hell you want."*

He remembered that gruff voice, how it had once pushed him forward like that.
So rather than simply be swept along knowing nothing, he wanted to choose to know something, and walk forward from there.

"Would you let me hear it, fake emperor-kun? What it is you came here to tell me."

Holding back the false emperor, busy as he was with the usurpation of the throne, Flop asked the question head-on.
At that question, Vincent — bearing an expression only very slightly different from the man Flop knew, a faint twist at the corner of his mouth — spoke.
And that was—

"—The 'Great Disaster.' The principle behind the ruin it brings. That is what I came to speak of."
