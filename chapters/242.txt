Arc 4 Chapter 75: "That Person"

Reeling from the impact, caught mid-stumble, Subaru's eyes went wide at the blonde girl glaring at him.
Her azure eyes were filled with a fury beyond measure, crimson rising to color her beautiful features — the Witch, Minerva.

After piercing the frozen Subaru with that intense stare, she turned her gaze to Echidna, who stood facing him without so much as a shift in posture.

"I'll say it again — I'm putting a stop to this. That contract, I won't allow it."

"...Hmm. This, I must say, is an unexpected turn of events for me."

An emotion too familiar to be called hostility, too savage to be called mere anger. Hurling that feeling at Echidna with her whole being, Minerva crossed her arms within the crater her own fist had carved, her ample chest heaving as she bit her lip.

"A Witch's Contract — surely you're not someone who fails to understand what that means. And yet you're interrupting anyway... could it be you want to make a contract with him too? Is that it — jealous, are we?"

"Do I look like my reasons are that peaceful to you? Can't you tell from how furious I am right now? I'm enraged. I'm indignant. My hair's practically standing on end from anger!"

Echidna's words, as if trying to deflect with a light joke, only deepened the flush on Minerva's face in response. The heat of her emotion welled up as tears at the corners of her eyes, lending her innocent profile something like a child's stubbornness.
Her voluptuous body and childlike profile were utterly out of balance — and yet, right now, Subaru had no choice but to accept that mismatch along with everything else that surprised him.
Because—

"Why are you here?"

"What, am I not allowed to be here like this?"

"That's not — that's not it. It's not that, but... Echidna's right there."

Subaru pointed at Echidna, speaking to Minerva, who puffed out her cheeks in displeasure. Minerva still tilted her head as if she couldn't grasp his meaning, but Echidna, apparently understanding what Subaru was getting at, gave a light clap of her hands and nodded.

"Ah, I see what's troubling you now. You find it strange that another Witch has manifested here while I'm still present, don't you?"

"Y-yeah. I mean, every other time I've met a Witch it's been one-on-one... it's always been someone appearing in your place. That's how it's worked, hasn't it? So this—"

"Nobody said we couldn't appear together, did they? Pulling that kind of pointless prank for no reason is exactly the sort of thing this ill-natured Witch does."

Minerva swatted down Subaru's protest without hesitation, shoulders bristling with anger. At her answer, Subaru muttered "no way" under his breath and looked at Echidna. But Echidna showed no particular sign of denial, neither at Subaru's gaze nor at Minerva's words.

"I'd rather you not misunderstand. Summoning a Witch other than myself carries a considerable burden and risk for me. Depending on the circumstances, another Witch might seize control of this place from me, and even setting that aside, forming a being as powerful as one of them requires a fair amount of effort."

"That's all well and — no, but, you—"

"I have never once told a lie. That much, I can state with certainty."

Echidna cut through Subaru's stammering words with a single sharp line.
It was true. Looking back through his memories, Echidna had never made a statement here that could be flatly called a lie regarding the current phenomenon.
Subaru had simply assumed "that's how it is" from the phenomena and facts presented to him.
So, to put it bluntly, even if Echidna had deceived him, technically nothing untrue had been said.

"I simply didn't want you taken by another Witch, once I realized other Witches could manifest so readily."

"Wha— huh?"

"You're a genuinely rare guest for me, you know. Talking with you, my heart hasn't raced like this in quite some time — not in life, and not since death either. Would you condemn me as contemptible for wanting to monopolize such a presence as yours, at least here?"

"——"

"I'll say it again and again — I find you agreeable. That's why I wanted to avoid your interest shifting to some other Witch, one more charming than me, or perhaps more cooperative, by bringing her out here instead. — You're welcome to laugh at that."

It's a terribly ugly possessiveness, Echidna explained, accounting for the circumstances of her concealment.
Wordless, listening to what amounted to Echidna's excuse, Subaru wondered — as he took in the attachment directed at him — just what about him could possibly warrant such devotion.
It was the same with the Witch of Envy. Why had Echidna, too, gone this far for Subaru—

"What are you letting yourself get talked into so easily, huh?"

"Gah!?"

A soft fist knocked against the back of Subaru's head just as he was about to sink into thought.
He clutched his head and turned around — right behind him stood Minerva. She caught the hand Subaru had raised to his head and, in one fluid motion, locked the joint and pulled him down to the ground.

"Ow, ah! W-wait, ow ow ow... doesn't hurt?"

"When I touch a living thing directly, whatever I do to it turns into a form of treatment. Punch someone at full strength and their wounds close up; try to choke someone out with a grapple and their chronic ailments get cured; keep this joint lock going and your stiff shoulders will loosen right up!"

"S-so that's why my whole body's been creaking... wait, that's not the point!"

Experiencing the true nature of the "Witch of Wrath" firsthand, Subaru desperately craned his neck to look up at Minerva, who had him locked in a joint hold.
His bones creaked, bent in a direction that should have screamed pain, and yet instead of hurting, a gentle warmth spread through his entire body — a bizarre sense of dissonance. A Witch who wielded some mysterious Authority that converted every action against a human body into a net positive — he'd never once held a negative impression of her, but even so—

"Just what do you think you're doing—"

"If I don't do something like this, you're going to get sweet-talked by Echidna and sign a contract before you know it! That rash decision-making, that shut-off-your-brain attitude of yours — it's really getting on my nerves!"

"'Sweet-talked' sounds rather unflattering. As for myself, I believe I properly explained the benefits of entering a contract with me and sought mutual agreement..."

"That's exactly the attitude that irritates me — acting like you've fulfilled your duty of explanation. Sure, you explained the benefits properly. You did. But — you haven't said a single word about the downsides that come with the contract, have you!"

Minerva brought her heel down in a fit of anger. Subaru's rear end, pulled down as he was, happened to be right beneath it, and he experienced the bizarre sensation of a heel driving into his backside while the ground beneath him caved in from the transmitted shock.
Feeling his bowels loosen from the blow to his rear, Subaru grasped the meaning of Minerva's words and went pale with shock.

— The fact that, in their conversation, Echidna had never once touched on the drawbacks. It was realizing his own carelessness in not even noticing that fact that struck him so hard.

"No, but... even if you call it a downside... it's not like it'd be something that serious—"

"You think it wouldn't be? You're taking a contract far too lightly. Especially when your partner is a Witch — among the seven Witches bearing the names of the deadly sins, the one who has forged the most contracts, touched the lives of the most humans, and intervened most forcefully in history: the Witch of Greed."

"It's true that not everything from that was crowned with glory in my past life... or rather, honor, I should say. It's a fact that not every being was saved by entering a contract with me."

Minerva confronted him with facts Subaru hadn't known. Echidna, continuing on as if to supplement those words, nonetheless insisted she bore Subaru no ill will.
Listening to both their claims, Subaru's mind, caught between them, sank into utter confusion.

He didn't know which side's words to believe.

Echidna was someone he'd come to regard as a kind of comrade-in-arms — someone he'd faced many times since becoming entangled with the Trials of the Tomb here, someone who had wracked her brain alongside him over the troubles weighing on him.
That was precisely why, when she proposed forming a cooperative bond in the shape of a contract, Subaru had felt something close to relief and had been about to accept.

Minerva, on the other hand, compared to Echidna, he'd interacted with far fewer times, for far less time, exchanged far fewer words with. And yet, each time, she had treated Subaru in his moments of crisis with the healing power unleashed from her mighty arms, a person of overwhelming, typhoon-like compassion who never even asked for a word of thanks.
Minerva had no reason to trap Subaru in anything, and the fact that she had gone so far as to manifest herself here just to interject deserved serious consideration.

No — before agonizing over any of this, there was something he should ask first.
It was this—

"Echidna. If we make a contract, there has to be a price."

"...Yes, that's right. A contract requires that. Just as I provide knowledge in answer to what you seek, you must offer a price in answer to what I seek."

"Right. That's right. — So then, what do you want from me? If I make a contract with you, what do I need to give you in exchange?"

Going forward, when Subaru hit a wall, when he found himself utterly stuck with no way forward, what would he have to pay for a being like Echidna to lend him her strength?
At Subaru's question, Echidna's cheeks relaxed into a faint smile.

"There's no need to be so wary. What I ask of you isn't so difficult a thing. In fact, since I won't be extracting some tangible object or intangible treasure as payment, you could even call it generous of me."

"— What do you want?"

"It's simple. — I want to keep tasting, forever, what you feel, what you think, what remains in your heart, what you come to know, what you accomplish, what is born from you, the fruit of 'the unknown' that springs from your very existence."

Echidna spoke with flushed cheeks, wearing an expression like a girl in love.
The fruit of "the unknown" — at that poetic turn of phrase, Subaru's brow furrowed.

"What is that supposed to mean? Are you saying you want to pull my emotions, my memories, that kind of thing, out of me and hand them over? Because if that's it—"

"Didn't I tell you? It's nothing so ominous. I simply want to watch, from the best seat in the house, the scenery you see, the melodies you hear, the story you weave. I simply want to feel that. I want to be in a position to know the 'unknown' born from you. That alone would satisfy me."

As if to dispel Subaru's misgivings, Echidna made clear exactly what she was after.
Simply to watch over the path Subaru walked. To see, alongside him, the scenery along the way. To know what Subaru felt, what he learned, the results of his actions.
The very incarnation of thirst for knowledge, the Witch who bore the name "Greed," wanted nothing more than that.

"That's not a lie, right?"

"To lie within a contract would be unthinkable. For my own sake, as much as anything, I swear I will never go back on this word. I'd stake my life on it."

Placing a hand over her own chest, Echidna finished with a wry, joking remark — "Though I suppose I'm already dead."
That attitude, those words, felt to Subaru like they held no lie. Or perhaps he simply wanted to believe that.

"Minerva. Echidna's said all that. If that's the case, then I—"

"E-everything... she said is true, but... that doesn't mean she's told you everything, okay?"

To Subaru, who was trying to demand his release from Minerva's hold, yet another voice now called out. It was one he'd heard just a bare few minutes ago — and one that, for Subaru, carried no pleasant memory at all.

"Carmilla... the Witch of Lust!"

"D-don't... look at me... with such scary eyes. I... I'm telling you I haven't done... anything... i-it's cruel..."

"I was born with unfriendly eyes. I'm not making any special effort to look mean."

Subaru, pulled down onto the ground, and Echidna standing before him. With Minerva behind him, the three of them lined up in a row — and a little way off, sitting in the grass, was a red-haired girl: Carmilla.
She timidly hid her face from Subaru's gaze while stealing glances his way now and then. As irritating an attitude as ever — but by consciously diverting his attention from her presence, he managed to avoid the risk of "losing his heart so completely his life would be in danger."
With that precaution in place, he pressed his question further.

"More importantly, what did you just say? At this point I've got no complaints about yet another Witch showing her face, but what's going on—"

"E-Echidna-chan... has, a lot of things, she's hiding... okay...? She's not... lying, but... she's hiding, a lot..."

"Hiding things...?"

Turning over the meaning of Carmilla's words, Subaru looked to Echidna as if clinging to her for an answer. Echidna, for her part, closed one eye while looking toward the suddenly-appeared Carmilla.

"To think you'd appear out of nowhere and line up such dishonorable accusations. In the first place, why would you go out of your way to alert him? Unlike Minerva, you shouldn't have any reason at all to take his side. If anything, you should dislike him."

"L-like, Minerva-chan...? A r-reason? I don't... really have... one. But, Echidna-chan... you... deceived me... didn't you?"

Against Echidna's composed manner of speaking, Carmilla, facing her, cast her eyes down and answered in a faltering, feeble voice. And yet, for all the feebleness of her voice, there was not a trace of weak-kneed compromise in what she said.
Carmilla let her uneasy gaze wander, glancing at Echidna again and again.

"I... don't, like this girl... but, I won't, take Echidna-chan's side either, since she... deceived me. People who... d-deceive me, or hate me... who do unpleasant things to me... 'I will never forgive them.'"

— Only that last line came through with startling clarity, unmistakably distinct.
Precisely because of that, it took Subaru a moment to accept that it was the girl beside him who had spoken it.
That single line was so utterly at odds with everything about the girl's demeanor up to that point. And yet—

"——"

Carmilla, silent now, but never once looking away, stared fixedly at Echidna.
In those twin eyes swirled some indescribable whirlpool of emotion — hostility toward herself, and something dark and grudge-like, a refusal to ever forgive anyone who directed such feelings at her.
A mass of self-love — the phrase flickered through his mind for an instant.

"I suppose it was a necessary measure, but acting against Carmilla's wishes was a misstep on my part. Making an enemy of you is nothing but trouble."

"E-everyone... is on my side... so, if you make me hate you, it's awful... you know? E-even if you apologize, I won't forgive you..."

Timidity and belligerence are not the same thing, bound together by some equal sign.
Carmilla was withdrawn, so faint-hearted she could barely manage to convey words to others properly — but that had nothing to do with her tolerance for hostility.

"Since a while ago... since a while ago, what have you two been talking about!"

And then, wedged between the hostile atmosphere of these Witches, Subaru — whose freedom of speech had until now been sealed off — raised his voice.
Feeling the gazes of all three Witches pour down upon him, Subaru strained to twist his neck around.

"Enough with talking like I'm not even here! This is my choice to make! Talk so I can understand! Echidna, what are you hiding!? What do the two of you know that makes you want to stop me?"

"Even if his heart's weakened, jumping to grab the first hand extended to him without even thinking is way too naive of him... though I guess getting him into that state is exactly what your careful planning was for!"

"That's an unkind way to put it. You might give him the wrong impression. If we form a contract, I will cooperate with him and, without fail, lead him to the best place he seeks. What I ask for, in that process, is simply to learn what he sees, hear the sounds he hears, come to know what he comes to know. I haven't said a single wrong word."

Against Subaru's raised, angry voice, Minerva shot back with a voice trembling with equally fierce anger, unwilling to be outdone. Against the two of them and their clumsy words, Echidna remained, to the last, composed.
At the thoroughly cold tone of Echidna's voice, Subaru too began to feel a growing sense of unease. Beyond the fevered state he'd been in until just now, he reconsidered Echidna's words afresh. Her attitude. And the reason the two Witches wanted to stop him.
What was wrong here? Nothing she'd said was wrong. Both Witches acknowledged she wasn't lying. So then, where was the problem—

"I'll say it again, Natsuki Subaru. If you choose me, and form a contract with me — I will, without fail, carry you to where you wish to go."

"— With 'in the end' always tacked on as a prefix to that promise, hm? *sigh*"

A weary voice overlapped with Echidna's declaration as she extended her hand toward him.
Looking over, across from Carmilla, this time — a reddish-purple-haired apparition. Sitting on the ground, seemingly buried in her own impossibly long hair, the Witch of Sloth had manifested.
Subaru no longer felt any surprise at another Witch appearing. What he picked up on, instead, was—

"In the end...?"

"Echidna, *whew*, will surely fulfill the contract, *sigh*. But, so long as she just, *whew*, keeps to the bare fact of having fulfilled the contract, *sigh*. Along the way there, *whew*, she can do whatever she likes."

"Whatever she likes—"

Sekhmet's words, laced with sighs, linked together with the unease Subaru had already been feeling, and a single hypothesis rose up in his mind.
But that hypothesis was one he could hardly bring himself to accept. Face stiff with shock, Subaru looked at Echidna — who had closed her eyes against the three Witches' attitudes — and spoke.

"Echidna, you said that if we made a contract... you'd definitely lead me to the best possible future. That's what you said, right?"

"Yes, I did say that. It's true. Without question, I will fulfill that contract. With my knowledge, and your ability, it can certainly be accomplished."

That's right, surely — that's exactly how a contract that gets properly fulfilled should look.
There was truly no lie in Echidna's words. If Subaru cooperated with her, he would surely be able to save everyone and reach the future. Except—

"This 'best possible future' I reach with your help — will it be by way of the best possible path?"

"——"

"Will you truly devote everything you have, with all your strength, to help me reach the place I want to reach?"

"——"

"Why are you silent? Answer me, Echidna... no, 'Witch of Greed'!"

Craning his neck upward, Subaru raised his voice until it threatened to tear his throat.
He was still pinned to the ground, the joint lock still in place. But paying that no mind at all, Subaru glared at Echidna with single-minded intensity.
And Echidna, on the receiving end of that sharp gaze, let out a small, sudden breath.

"— So long as you can reach the best possible future, you'll accept whatever sacrifices arise along the way. Wasn't that your resolve, Natsuki Subaru?"

"——!"

It was an answer that neither directly affirmed nor denied Subaru's question.
And yet, Subaru understood.

That Echidna's words just now were in no way meant to dispel his earlier suspicion.
Quite the opposite — she spread her arms, as if trying to make him understand her thinking.

"That trait you possess, Return by Death, is a tremendous Authority. You don't truly understand, in the deepest sense, how useful it is. To refuse an ending you don't want, to redo things as many times as it takes, to reach toward the future again and again no matter how many times you fail — that is close to the ultimate ideal for a seeker of truth. Because, think about it — normally, once a single outcome has occurred for any given matter, it cannot be moved from that point onward. During the process leading up to an outcome, you can construct all manner of hypotheses about what that outcome might become. Try this approach, or set these conditions instead — various hypotheses and verifications are possible. But the moment you actually step forward to produce that outcome through experiment, the outcome itself, and the hypothesis you can test, and the verification, are all inevitably narrowed down to just one. It is utterly, in the truest sense utterly impossible to recreate exactly identical conditions. No matter how carefully you arrange the conditions, you can never, ever recreate the exact same conditions as that moment. What result would have come about if you had done things differently back then — that is a fantasy lying beyond even the ideal, something we seekers of truth can never, ever reach with our own hands. I, who hold the 'Memory of the World,' certainly do have a means of 'knowing' that answer — I do, indeed. But I do not consider it acceptable to use that means, to employ it. I want to 'know' — I do not want to already 'be knowing.' It's a deeply contradictory thing, an object I find abhorrent, one might say. I'm getting off track, so let me return to the main point — yes, for us, who can only accept the outcome that ought to be as a single, fixed thing, who possess only a single means of observation, a being like you — your Authority — is something we want so badly it's almost unbearable. 'Under the same conditions,' being able to conduct 'a different verification,' being able to see 'a different outcome' from the 'original outcome' — an Authority so ultimate — how could we not want it? Faced with this, how could we not want to try everything imaginable? Of course, I have no intention whatsoever of forcing this upon you. You should, first and foremost, make full use of that Return by Death for your own purposes. I too will do my utmost to help you reach the future you seek. And, if possible, along the way, I would very much like you to contribute generously to satisfying my own curiosity as well. Surely it isn't too much to ask for at least that much. You get to see the answer. I get to satisfy my curiosity. Our interests align. I don't know the answer myself either, so there's no way I could deliberately steer you toward the wrong choice just to see the result, and then let you meet some horrific end on purpose. In the sense that neither of us starts out holding the correct answer to the problem we face, you and I are, to that extent, equals. In the sense that we struggle together over the same problem, flail together, strive together to arrive at an answer, we ought properly to be called comrades. On that point, I can state so plainly, without a shred of shame. In the sense that you give me more means of verification, I hold you in very high regard, so I swear I will never treat you carelessly. Of course, faced with a problem with no answer, there will surely be situations that even my cooperation cannot easily overcome. Even if I can lend you strength in terms of knowledge, I can never actually intervene in reality. If the obstacle standing in your way is one requiring physical, material force, I will be of no help to you at all. Time and time again, perhaps hundreds, thousands of times, your heart and body may be shattered. And if that happens, I sincerely wish, from the bottom of my heart, to tend to your wounded heart. I cannot swear that not a shred of that wish is mixed with the seeker's desire not to lose so useful a being as you. But it is also true that I hold genuine affection for you as a being, and genuinely wish to be of help to you. So I'd rather you not think ill of me for this. To repeat myself, I can proudly say I am a useful being for your purposes. Yes — just as I intend, in a sense, to make use of your existence to satisfy my own greed for curiosity, you too should make use of a being like me for your own purpose of 'reaching the best possible future.' To be used by you as a convenient woman in that way is, for my part, exactly what I want. If that is what will motivate you, I will gladly offer up my very existence for it. Whether you'd even want this poor body, already a corpse, is another matter, of course. Oh, but perhaps it's unkind of me to say such a thing, given the ones you love. The ones you love — the silver-haired Half-Elf, and the blue-haired demon girl. The girls you've sworn in your heart, and shown through your actions, that you will absolutely save, absolutely protect. I won't share my thoughts here about the nature of the strong feelings you hold for those two, but I will state, purely and simply, that the height of the wall standing before you is beyond imagining. Even limited to just the obstacles already known to you now, how many things utterly beyond your ability to handle are already piling up. Your resolve to try to overcome all of that alone is noble, and, at the same time, unbearably tragic. My wish to be of help along that path of yours, if I can, holds no falsehood whatsoever. And you ought to make use of that wish of mine. You must use everything you possess, everything at your disposal, to save the people with whom you've forged such bonds at such cost. Isn't that the vow you swore to yourself, the belief you settled on somewhere along that painful road, as something necessary? So I ask you, I press you again, I think of you. That you've been spending your life, over and over, discarding it, and walking this road — that, ironically, was just proven, in the form of the second Trial, only moments ago. It's almost enough to make one imagine that Trial existed precisely to make you understand the road you've walked thus far — it feels that necessary. It's true that it was a sight that need not have existed, the kind that wears down the heart simply from becoming aware of it. But between a state of not knowing and a state of knowing, I would rather hold the latter as precious, however tragic the fact may be. You have needed, up until now, and will need going forward, to offer up your own life as the price of Return by Death, and to draw the future toward you by that means. To keep in your heart the thought that there might exist, in some such form, a sacrifice, a world, paid for that purpose — that, too, was a necessary thing. Eventually, you would come to feel no sentiment at all about paying with your own life, your human feelings would grow thin, you would stop being moved even by the 'deaths' of the people dear to you, sinking into days devoid of emotion, devoid of feeling, devoid of will — and even if you reached the best possible future in that state, you would arrive there with the very 'you' who should be there missing from it — to avoid a future like that, one leaving nothing behind but a sense of futility, this too was necessary. Yes, nothing in this whole world is truly wasted; everything is a necessary step along the way, a necessary piece of the puzzle. The Trial existed for you to understand that. If you need some plausible meaning to make sense of the reason, the cause, of why you've come to a halt here now, then think of it this way. And I affirm that way of thinking of yours. If there are words I can give you, by which I might lend you the strength you need to move forward, I will offer any words at all. Whether comfort, or a spur to action, whispers of love, or something meant to stir up hatred — if it will become your strength, I will wield it without the slightest hesitation. You may find that distasteful, but on the path you walk from here, you will absolutely need the strength of a being like me. If you are to walk, from here on, a solitary road where getting hurt is unavoidable, you will absolutely need someone who can walk that road alongside you without ever looking away from it. And for that role, I — no one else, but I myself — can walk beside you without a single problem. Let me say it again, and again, and convey it to you as many times as it takes until it reaches you. — You need me, surely. And I need you. Your existence is necessary to me. My curiosity can no longer be satisfied without a being like you. Only your existence can satisfy me. To me, to my never-satisfiable 'Greed,' you will surely bring satisfaction. Your existence has become indispensable now to me, dwelling as I do in this closed-off world. If you wish to be someone's hope, if you intend to wield your strength to carve open the world, could you not spare a pitiable being like me some small share of that? If you would extend that mercy to me, I would not hesitate for even a moment to offer this body, this knowledge, this soul. So please. I beg you to trust me. The reason I never tried to convey my true feelings until now was never in order to deceive you, or to conceal something from you. I was simply waiting for the right moment. If I had appealed to you with even a fragment of my true feelings at this very instant, you would surely have walked away from me. That would be an unbearable loss for me. And of course, it would rightly be called a loss for you as well, in the sense that it would push the future you seek further out of reach. Eventually, given the nature of Return by Death, you will surely reach the future you seek. But it stands to reason that the price you pay for that reachable future should be as small as possible. I — I alone — can lighten that price. Please don't misunderstand me as some inhuman creature who neglects the smaller goal because the larger goal will eventually be reached anyway. It's true that I'm not restrained enough in my own desires to swear I would never, tempted by wanting to see the outcome of such a case, notice some element necessary to the best path and simply not mention it. That much I'll admit. But I won't deceive you about it. If I should ever slip into a betrayal of your trust like that, I will never, under any circumstance, try to hide it. I will always confess it to you. And I will strive to earn back the trust I lost, exerting myself on your behalf as many times as it takes. No matter what happens, I will absolutely deliver you to the best possible future you desire. Absolutely, without question. So won't you accept that as a necessary means to that end, and choose me? What I wish for, what I ask of you, is exactly as I stated when we discussed the contract. All that remains is how far you yourself are willing to cut into your own flesh for the wish you desire. My resolve is as I've just stated. Now, I want to hear your resolve. I want you to prove to me that you, in turn, have the resolve and the will, once you've entered a contract with me and gained my cooperation, to surely reach the future. Only once you can do that can you truly hold your head high and say you've overcome the second Trial. Advance to the third Trial, overcome that as well, and achieve the liberation of the Sanctuary. Considering the calamity that will befall the Sanctuary, your beloved, and the people dear to you going forward, this is truly a Trial that must be overcome. Show me that you have the strength, the resolve, to overcome it. And once you have, take me, use my knowledge, and go on to obtain what lies beyond. What I wish for, what I ask of you, and what I can offer you in return, is all of the above. I believe I have spoken sincerely, honestly, and told you everything. Now, what judgment will you make — that, I want you to tell me. If only to satisfy a small part of my own curiosity as a being."

— And with that, Echidna smiled sweetly.

Her hair, white as snow, swayed as her cheeks flushed faintly with fervor, and she gazed up at him from beneath lowered lashes, as if waiting for Subaru's answer.
Her trembling eyelashes betrayed a fear of his reaction, and her fingers, pressed to her chest as if unable to bear the anxiety, moved restlessly. Her lips, again and again, tried to spin more words only to hesitate, wetting them with the tip of her tongue and going no further.

He looked up. His eyes met Minerva's, still restraining him.
Minerva, meeting Subaru's gaze, let out a small sigh. And then, finally, she released the arm she had until then refused to let go of. Freed from the hold on his shoulder, Subaru rolled his arm and got to his feet.
Just as Minerva had said, the creak in his shoulder was gone. More than that, even the faint tension in his lower back and the fatigue in other parts of his body seemed to have drained away. The Authority of the "Witch of Wrath" — a terrifying healing effect, indeed.

"——"

Rotating each part of his body, checking his senses, Subaru gathered his thoughts.
The unvarnished truth — the true, true feelings of Echidna — that he had just been made to hear.

"Echidna."

"Yes?"

"Are you... going to use me?"

Use, be used. Those were words repeated over and over in what Echidna had just said. In response, Echidna nodded without hesitation.

"I will. And you should do the same to me. A contract is, in a sense, a safeguard so that neither of us strays from that principle. If you wish to condemn my thinking — that I'll use anything at my disposal so as not to let go of your existence — then I'll accept that condemnation without complaint."

"It's not like I never thought about it. To put it bluntly, I get that this is exactly what an interest-based relationship is like. I was prepared for the reality that you helping me out of one hundred percent pure goodwill — even if I hoped for that — wasn't going to be it. But—"

Before Echidna, Subaru covered his face with his palm and tilted his head back.

"Even so, that's just too much, isn't it..."

"'That' being?"

"Everything you've done up to this point looks faded to me now. Everything you've done that felt kind toward me, the feeling I was starting to trust that you weren't a bad person after all — all of it, faded."

Everything he'd built up from their very first meeting to this instant came crashing down with a sound.
The tea party where they first met, the scenes he'd faced in the Trial, and the time, again and again, when reality had blocked his path and he'd clung to her wisdom, her words. The time he'd thought that entering a contract, doing so, would be something he wouldn't regret.
— All of it, mercilessly, now mocked the foolishness of Natsuki Subaru.

"Was that your intention from the very start?"

"I'm not quite sure what you're taking issue with. Wasn't it settled that, so long as you reach the best outcome in the end, you'd make peace with whatever path leads there? You affirmed that yourself, and I only gave you a push, agreeing that it was fine—"

"You're saying that me coming to terms with it that way... I haven't come to terms with it, but — the fact that you steered me toward that conclusion, wasn't that exactly your plan all along? That's — that's what I'm saying."

"I'd rather you not misunderstand — that conclusion of yours was, to the very last, one you reached yourself. All I did was give the smallest possible nudge toward the conclusion you were going to reach anyway. To then turn around and lay the responsibility for the conclusion you yourself put into words onto someone else — frankly, I find that unadmirable. I find it unadmirable, and I am not so good-natured as to shoulder that blame."

Lips pursed, wearing an expression almost like a sulking child, Echidna protested. At the childishness — or rather, the sheer inappropriateness — of that display of emotion, Subaru felt, even more strongly than before, a sense of wrongness he'd been sensing all along.
What to call it — a mismatch, an incongruity, in the sheer quantity of emotion.

Echidna's emotional expressions weren't wrong, exactly. Indignant when doubted, smiling when pleased, showing a pained face at something sad. That much was correct, not wrong.
And yet — despite that — the sense of wrongness he felt, the distrust rising in him. The answer to that was—

"Everything about your attitude — none of it has any real weight behind it. It's all surface."

"——"

"Even when you're happy, even when you're supposedly angry, your emotional expressions are childish, shallow. Even now, instead of flying into a rage, all you did was pout. It's not a matter of you having a big heart or something. Your attitude — everything about your attitude up to now — is wrong. I thought you were easy to deal with, someone easy to get along with because you were so lighthearted, but—"

"——"

"But in reality it's different. You — you're someone who can't understand other people's emotions."

Everything about Echidna's conduct up to this point came back to him, and it all turned to sepia.
Every attitude of hers that he'd found endearing, once it felt like nothing more than the product of shallow emotional display, made everything they'd shared up to now feel faded and colorless.

And yet, even doused in such harsh words, Echidna's expression didn't change from its earlier pout. As if she knew no expression beyond that to show displeasure.

"This is exactly the kind of moment where you're supposed to get angry, you know."

"...I see. So here, I ought to have raised my voice and hurled abuse at you. I see, that's instructive. Should there be a next occasion, I'll be sure to do so."

At Subaru's words, all expression drained from Echidna's face.
Blankness — it was a face of the "Witch of Greed" that Subaru, who thought he knew Echidna, had never once seen before.

Before the silent Subaru, Echidna raised a finger and snapped it. At once, the hill that should have been destroyed restored itself, the shattered table and the blown-away chairs reforming into shape.
Echidna seated herself in one of the chairs and gestured with her hand toward the chair across from her.

"Why don't you sit down? I'd like to work out the finer details of the contract."

"...You really think, in this situation, that I'm still going to consider a contract with you favorably?"

"Surely you don't intend to reject me over a minor difference of opinion? What possible meaning would there be in doing that? Letting yourself be swept up by a moment's emotion and losing sight of the correct choice can hardly be called wise. I would recommend you look at reality and choose the rational path."

At Echidna's frozen words, Subaru closed his eyes and held his breath.
Echidna's words were correct. Even if it were said that Subaru was the one throwing a tantrum, there would be no arguing against it. Her logic was sound. She wasn't lying, either.
Echidna had only, to the very last, kept her true intentions hidden from Subaru. She had merely stayed silent about the benefit to herself that would arise along the way, over the course of Subaru's journey.
If they formed a contract, Subaru would likely be able to reach the correct path. That Echidna would spare no effort in cooperating toward that was, without question, also a fact.

"There's just one thing I wanted to ask you, if I ever got the chance to see you."

"— Hmm, what would that be?"

"If I can hear the answer to that, I feel like I'll be able to choose."

Echidna waited for the question Subaru was about to put forward.
To her, Subaru brought out a question as a touchstone — one of the answers he still hadn't found even a fragment of within this loop originating from the Sanctuary, and a question by no means unrelated to her.

"— You know Beatrice, don't you, Echidna?"

"...Yes, I know her. I was deeply involved in the process of that child's birth, after all. Has something happened with her?"

Echidna's innocent reply. An answer laced with no hidden meaning, only a genuine question.
Subaru closed his eyes and pictured, in his mind, the young girl with the drill curls.

For Subaru, the girl's final image was the instant she vanished, run through from behind.
The instant before that, too — the long, long, lonely stretch of time she had carried, and the darkness born of it that she had hurled at him — remained lodged, heavy, in his heart.
Shoving Subaru aside, protecting him from the murderous blade, and the expression she wore in that final moment — it was still burned into his eyes, refusing to leave. So—

"Beatrice, by contract, has been waiting all this time for 'that person' to come. Is that contract one you made with her? Are you the one keeping her chained to that mansion?"

"I don't recall specifying a location... but it's certainly true that I'm the one who made the promise with her, that she would guard the Forbidden Archive and wait until someone came to fetch it."

"If that's the case, then who is 'that person'? How do I free her?"

Through four hundred years of solitude, Beatrice had waited, all this while, for the existence of "someone."
Who that "someone" was, even Beatrice herself didn't know. Subaru couldn't find even a thread leading to an answer either.
But if he could get an answer from Echidna herself, the one who had made that promise with the "someone"—

"I wonder, just who could it be?"

"— Wha— huh?"

"No, I'm not joking, not in the slightest — I genuinely mean that. What do you think — who is this 'that person' Beatrice waits for?"

Echidna responded to Subaru as though he'd raised a question to which she herself didn't know the answer. At that attitude, Subaru went blank for a moment, then shook his head.

"You don't know either? Who Beatrice's person she's waiting for is?"

"That's right, I don't know. Who 'that person,' the one Beatrice waits for, is — I don't know."

"Why not? You're the one who told Beatrice to wait in the Forbidden Archive, aren't you? And you don't know... wait, don't tell me—"

Was it possible that the one who instructed Beatrice to wait in the Forbidden Archive, and the one who set the condition of waiting "until 'that person' comes," were two different people?
If so, then the one who knew the answer must be somewhere else entirely—

"No, that's mistaken."

"——"

"It was I who instructed Beatrice to wait for 'that person.' That much isn't wrong. What's mistaken is something far more fundamental."

"Fundamental... how?"

"You're mistaken about just why, exactly, I made such a contract with Beatrice in the first place. You believe, I think, that I have her guard the archive in order to hand its contents over to 'that person' — is that not what you think?"

Subaru didn't understand the meaning of Echidna's words.
It seemed the natural way to think of it. You instruct someone to guard something so as to hand it over. Naturally, that handover being the true intent of the instruction was a given.

But to Subaru, thinking in such an obvious way, Echidna shook her head and said:

"My instruction to Beatrice isn't that. I made a contract with that child to wait for 'that person'... and I am waiting for the result of who that child chooses as 'that person.'"

"——"

——.

————.

————————.

——————————————————what?

"That child, you see, was created for a certain purpose. But circumstances arose requiring her to be kept alive in a form different from her original purpose... and to that end, she was sent away from here. And in doing so, it became necessary to give her a purpose. To give a purpose to live to that child, empty and hollow as she was — that, too, was necessary. And so I made a contract with her."

"— That's what—"

"To maintain the Forbidden Archive, and eventually hand everything over to 'that person.' I set no restriction on that. To begin with, it's a condition with no correct answer. That child gets to live out her intended purpose, and I, in turn, get to see an answer to a separate inquiry of mine. Isn't that terribly rational?"

"——"

"Of course, spending four hundred years without choosing is itself one outcome. Not lightly choosing anyone she happened to meet along the way as 'that person' — that too is one. Or even agonizing over whether to break the contract, and wishing for her own 'death' — even that is one outcome."

"What do you think... about that?"

"—? I think it's wonderful, don't I?"

As if asked something perfectly obvious, Echidna tilted her head, utterly unashamed.
That answer, that attitude, together with the expression of the girl that rose in Subaru's mind, produced an answer.

He'd decided. He understood. He grasped it, clearly.
— Where he was, and just who exactly he was facing — the misunderstanding had been corrected.

"Echidna... you're a Witch."

"——"

"A monster beyond human comprehension, beyond understanding."

"——"

He conveyed it. The answer that had risen within him.
Withdrawing the hand he'd been about to extend, he decided, this time for certain, whose hand he would reach for instead.

"I... I can't take your hand. I've already decided whose hand I'll take."

"——"

"There's a girl who had four hundred years of her time stolen by words you tied her down with, innocently, without malice. — I've decided. I'll take that girl's hand. I can't go with you."

He declared their parting.
He shook off the hand of someone he had once thought he might be able to walk alongside.

He lifted his face. He looked ahead.
Behind Subaru's eyelids floated the image of the girl's face in her final moment.

— The girl who, even as she twisted her face as if about to cry, terrified of vanishing, of dying, still let relief show in her eyes at having been able to protect Subaru.

Subaru decided to take the hand of the girl who had mourned his "death."

"——"

Echidna narrowed her eyes.
Some thought raced behind those eyes, as if she intended to say something in response to Subaru's decision.

But before she could, a change came over the scene.

"——It's come."

"Oh no, come on... I... have nothing to do with this, okay...?"

"In such a bothersome place, a bothersome one has come to make things bothersome, hasn't she, *sigh*."

The three bystanding Witches each murmured their own thoughts.
And then, from behind — a sense of pressure.
Echidna, who had been facing forward and looking past Subaru, widened her eyes slightly. Following her surprise, Subaru turned and looked — and saw it.

"——"

There, standing, was the "Witch of Envy," her head above the neck shrouded in jet-black darkness.
