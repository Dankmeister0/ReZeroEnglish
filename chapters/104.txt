Arc 3 Chapter 27: "Voices of the Marketplace"

What had he done wrong—whenever Subaru had a spare moment to think, that was where his thoughts went.

Even as he told himself he didn't want to, his mind kept circling back to that evening's twilight, replaying again and again the sight of the silver-haired girl turning her back on him and walking away.

What had he done wrong—Subaru asked himself that every time the memory surfaced.

That he'd let his words run too far was something even Subaru himself had to admit.

Driven into a corner by her relentless, hammering words—and no doubt still reeling from the physical beating he'd taken before that—what came out of his mouth had diverged sharply from what he'd actually meant to say. And in the end, that divergence was what had driven a wedge between the two of them.

Since the words had slipped out on impulse, were they just empty things blurted out in the heat of the moment, meaning nothing beyond that instant?

Or was it precisely *because* they'd slipped out on impulse that they were the thoughts that had always been drifting somewhere in his heart?

Where his true feelings had actually stood in that moment—he no longer knew.

For Subaru, stunned into blankness right after that parting of ways, his memory didn't pick back up until much later—until Rem was leading him out of the waiting room in the royal castle, and he found himself riding along in the dragon carriage bound for Crusch's estate.

While Subaru sat there in his daze, he'd been dimly aware that Crusch and Ferris were speaking with Rem. None of the content had made it into his head, but the one saving grace was that Rem had kept holding his hand the whole time they talked.

Just being able to feel that warmth had let him know, at least, that the last thread hadn't been cut.

"—H-hey. Kid."

His consciousness, drifting from recollection into reverie, was yanked back to reality by the sudden call.

He registered a gruff voice coming from straight ahead, blinked, and pulled his awareness back to the present. When he focused his eyes on what was in front of him—

"C'mon now, kid. Don't go givin' my shop that thousand-yard stare."

A face crossed by a memorable scar, brows furrowed on that tough, weathered mug.

Having just returned to the present and been confronted with that face's considerable impact head-on, Subaru slowly rubbed at his eyelids with his fingers and let out a long breath.

"Hey, old man. Don't you think it's a bit much, intimidating your own customers like that?"

"I ain't intimidatin' nobody! If anything I'm worried about you! I oughta tell you just how much I panicked when I couldn't get in touch with you!"

The shopkeeper's furious bellow came with a slap of his thick arm against the counter, and the fruit piled on the display stand bounced hard, flying out of the basket. They arced through the air toward the crowded street, about to hit the ground and either get bruised or leave a stain on somebody's shoe sole, when—

"You mustn't treat food so carelessly."

With a flowing motion that sent the hem of her skirt flaring, Rem landed lightly in the space in front of the shop. Inside the apron she'd lifted with a light pinch, the falling fruit had been gently caught and gathered up.

Kadomon let out a breath, equal parts relief and admiration at the feat of dexterity. He raised a hand toward Rem, who held out the recovered fruit to him.

"Whew, you saved me there, missy. So listen here, I ain't sayin' this to be mean. You oughta quit hangin' around this shifty-eyed brat. He'll only bring you bad luck."

"Hey hey hey hey, what's that supposed to mean? *Supposed* to mean? It was my companion here who kept your merchandise from going bad, you know! C'mon, let's hear a word of thanks!"

"You're the reason it almost went bad in the first place. And besides, I don't think what I said was off the mark at all."

Rearranging the goods on the display shelf, the shopkeeper crossed his thick arms, snorted, and looked back and forth between Subaru and Rem standing quietly at his side.

"This ain't the same girl you brought around last time, is it? The other one... huh, for some reason I don't remember much about her, but I guess if I don't remember, that just means this one's cuter. You shameless bastard, go to hell."

"What, old man, you one of those types who thinks there's no such thing as friendship between a guy and a girl? Don't go tying everything back to romance. And besides, in the first place—"

*Why don't you remember Emilia?*—he almost said it, but the moment he felt the words bring a sharp ache to his chest, Subaru clamped his mouth shut. And with that pain, the reason for the shopkeeper's forgetfulness came back to him too, and he nodded to himself in understanding.

While Subaru fell silent, eyeing him with suspicion, Kadomon took the opportunity to lean in and murmur to Rem in a hushed voice.

"See that? Not a shred of shame on his face. Fall for a fella like that and you're in for a rough time."

"Thank you for your concern. ...But Rem does this because she wants to."

Blushing, Rem glanced sidelong at Subaru to check his reaction. At the heat in that glance, Kadomon shrugged, deciding any further words would be tactless, and drew back with an air of regret.

But almost immediately his eyes flew open again, and after prefacing with "that aside," he said—

"The romance stuff's one thing, but my opinion on him bein' a dangerous fool hasn't changed one bit. ...The hell were you thinkin', sneakin' into a shipment bound for the castle and forcin' your way in like that? Only a man with a death wish pulls a stunt like that."

Even at a normal volume, buried in the din of the crowded street, the words wouldn't have carried far. But Kadomon, weighing the gravity of the subject, had lowered his voice carefully as he muttered it.

Feeling something bitter rise in his chest at those words, Subaru scratched his head awkwardly and began stammering out an excuse.

"Ah, well, about that... I felt bad about it—or, uh, I might have felt bad about it, or something like that, is a thing I can't rule out not having thought, more or less."

"So did you feel bad about it or didn't you, pick one."

"Sorry 'bout that. And sorry for not comin' to report in right after, too."

"Damn right you should be."

Looking down at the back of Subaru's bowed head, Kadomon exhaled his anger along with his breath. Still wearing an expression of barely-contained fury, he went on—

"Even old man Rom went dark on me right along with you two. I figured somethin' was seriously wrong—damn near started packin' for a moonlight flit. Meanwhile I was rackin' my brain tryin' to figure out how to explain it to my wife and daughter without them catching on."

"You really should've just come clean and let yourself get looked down on for doing something wrong, smuggler."

"Shut it, ringleader."

At Subaru's sudden reversal from his contrite attitude of moments ago, the shopkeeper clicked his tongue. Then, tracing the scar running across his face with a finger, he said, "Well—"

"There was a pretty big to-do at the castle that day. Guess I oughta be grateful the fuss covered up your little small-time crime enough that it never went public."

"Ah, right, right. And thanks to that we found out how sloppy the security is over there. Next time let's aim bigger and go for the treasury, boss."

"I ain't never helpin' you again! Next time you get caught, may they cut your damn head off!"

At the shopkeeper drawing a hand like a blade across his throat and sticking out his tongue, Subaru shook his head, muttering "scary, scary" in reply.

Then, his mind snagging on the phrase "pretty big to-do" that Kadomon had just used, he let out a breath as if something had just clicked. "Ah, I see."

"So word about the Royal Selection's already spread through the whole capital, huh?"

"Course it has. It ain't some story the people of this kingdom can just laugh off as none of their business. They're decidin' who's gonna be king, after all. This ain't just a matter of the next few years—it's the swell of an era carryin' on four hundred years of the kingdom's history. Everywhere you go, it's all anybody's talkin' about."

Having caught Subaru's mutter, Kadomon spoke with a kind of feverish excitement of his own. Then, saying "look here," he turned his head to guide Subaru's gaze. In the direction he indicated stood something like a tall standing signboard, conspicuous even among the market street's other fixtures.

"Well, can't read it if it ain't written in something other than I-script, though."

"Tch, ignorant as ever. Then can you even read the sign on my own shop?"

"I feel like there's some pictograph on there kinda close to I-script, but the handwriting's too messy to make out."

"The hell you say. Writin' it all fancy and stylized like that is the trend nowadays."

"Bit rough on someone still learning the alphabet. Someday, once I've got the *i-ro-ha* of this world's writing system nailed down cold, I'll bring 'gal-script' into fashion as a spinoff."

Subaru's grand ambition to storm the history of the written word.

What it required was the ability to grasp letters not as letters, but as a single unified image, like a picture. Or perhaps the perceptive ability to blur the line between reality and fantasy. He was fairly confident in the latter.

In any case—

"So, what's it actually say on the sign?"

"I already told you the headline, like, three times. 'Royal Selection Commences.'"

Making a face at the unhelpful answer, Kadomon waved a hand and said, "Fine, fine."

"Guess I'll read it out for you real quick. —Missy, mind watchin' the shop a bit?"

"Understood."

At Kadomon's easy request as he stepped out from beside the shop and onto the street, Rem slipped inside to take his place without a single word of objection.

The seamlessness of it felt anything but seamless to Subaru, and he shook his head.

"Don't just hand the store over to an amateur like that. And Rem, don't just casually agree to it either."

"It's just makin' change based on the price tags and what the customer hands over. Ain't like anybody's actually gonna come in anyway."

"He said it himself, finally!"

Led by Kadomon, whose face had turned almost cheerfully resigned, Subaru stepped out toward the crowded street, leaving Rem behind at the shop. Crossing through the throng of people of every shape, size, and race, they came to stand beneath the tall standing sign, set higher than even the tall Kadomon by a good two heads.

A cluster of people had also stopped for the same purpose as Subaru and his companion, eyes tracing the characters on the sign. Craning his neck from behind them, Subaru followed the text as best he could even without being able to read it.

"What it says is basically the notice that the Royal Selection's begun, plus a summary. They're gonna decide the king before the Divine Dragon Ritual three years from now, then hold the ceremony after that. And then there's a little bit written about the candidates."

The content, already familiar, had begun to lose Subaru's interest—until the word "candidates" caught him.

Kadomon caught the twitch of Subaru's brow, the surprise flickering across his face, and, tilting his chin with a look of understanding in his eyes, said—

"Seems like the candidates got your attention. Five candidates total for the Royal Selection. Among 'em, the two names that stand out most are Duke Crusch Karsten and that girl Anastasia from the Hoshin Company."

"They're famous?"

"She's a duchess. Ain't hardly anybody livin' in the capital who don't know that name. And with the Hoshin Company, there ain't a merchant alive who doesn't know it either. That its head's a young woman was a famous rumor too, whether or not it was actually true."

Answering as he touched his scar, Kadomon's words naturally brought the images of the two women to Subaru's mind.

Crusch, whose sharp, sword-like bearing left such a striking, dignified impression.

And Anastasia, whose pale purple hair and distinctively soft Kansai dialect were unmistakable. As for whether the latter was really the woman the rumors described—having seen her firsthand, Subaru figured it was probably true. Though, given the precedent set by Ferris, there was no such thing as "absolutely" anymore in this world.

"Well, no point in there being a glut of that 'boy who's actually a girl' archetype—no character overlap, probably."

"No idea what you're noddin' along to over there, but—anyway, for now these two are the frontrunners. Personally, between an outsider like Anastasia and Lady Crusch of House Karsten, one of the kingdom's pillars, I'd say Crusch has the edge."

"'The favorite,' they called her."

Though, as it happened, that must have shifted considerably after her own declaration of intent later on.

Regardless, it was a fact that her standing and her family's lineage made for powerful backing. And for the common folk who hadn't heard her speech firsthand, it was an undeniable truth that her simply, smoothly ascending to the throne would be the outcome that stirred up the least trouble.

"Crusch-san's the favorite. Anastasia-jou is the runner-up... so where does the long shot come in?"

"Damn if you don't know some old-fashioned turn of phrase. As for a 'long shot'—that's a tough one. Aside from those two, the remaining three are about as nameless as it gets."

Reading off the names Priscilla, Felt, and Emilia one after another, Kadomon snorted and pointed at the sign.

"Even I don't recognize these names. This 'Priscilla' one, judging from the family name, seems to be nobility at least. But the other two, can't even find a family name for 'em. With the head of the Hoshin Company thrown into the mix as a candidate, I honestly gotta wonder how they even picked these people."

On that point, if he hadn't known the circumstances, Subaru figured he'd have shared that exact same opinion.

A duke's head of house on one side; on the other, a young up-and-coming company president; then some minor noble whose name nobody had heard of; and the rest, mysterious figures whose family names were even in question.

Just from this information alone, it was hardly a kind set of facts for the citizens watching the Royal Selection unfold.

Just as Subaru was thinking that—

"Only thing is... havin' a half-elf among the candidates, that's just plain out of its mind."

Narrowing his eyes, twisting his lips in distaste, Kadomon spat the words out roughly.

For a moment, at the sheer, naked displeasure in his manner, Subaru's thoughts came to a halt. But the instant the content of his words sank in—

"A half-elf... you mean—"

"There's a bit written about the candidates' backgrounds too. Apparently there's a half-elf named Emilia among the candidates. What a joke."

"Somethin' wrong with bein' a half-elf?"

"Hey now, save the joking for reckless living, not this."

Shrugging and shaking his head, Kadomon reacted to Subaru's question with exaggerated emphasis.

"It ain't exactly a secret, but half-elves get the cold shoulder wherever they go. You see demi-humans around the capital plenty, but unlike lizardmen or hobbits, elves wanderin' around are a rare sight. And when it comes to half-elves, well—naturally, they get shunned by both sides of that mixed blood."

Correcting Subaru's ignorance as though it were only natural, the shopkeeper, oblivious to Subaru falling silent, glared resentfully at the characters written on the standing sign.

"Dumb move, for somethin' the higher-ups decided. Of all people, a half-elf— no, a half-witch. How the hell can the country be entrusted to somethin' like that?"

"Half-witch..."

"That's what they call half-elves. Fittin' name for kin of the Witch, ain't it?"

Looking down at him as if seeking agreement, the shopkeeper got no reaction from Subaru. That was how much of a shock those words had just dealt him.

Subaru had, in no small measure, come to like this scarred shopkeeper.

Part of it was that he'd been nearly the first person Subaru had spoken with on the day he was summoned to this world; part of it came from the kind of man he'd shown himself to be in all their dealings since. He was someone who made conversation fun, a good man Subaru didn't mind being around at all.

That was exactly why it came as a shock that words belittling someone else could come so easily out of his mouth. And worse, they were slander against someone Subaru couldn't just let slide.

"It's not like everyone's got some connection to the Witch."

"Ah?"

"Don't just lump all half-elves together and write 'em off like that. That Emilia girl—she might, I dunno, actually care about the country and stuff. She might be a really good person, for all you know."

At Subaru's hurried, halting defense, Kadomon furrowed his brow in suspicion and held up a palm. "Hold on."

"No idea what's got you all worked up, but don't go around defendin' a half-witch. You never know who's listenin'."

"That's exactly the unnecessary worry here. Walls have ears, and paper screens have eyes—Iris, if you want to get cute about it—and for all you know that girl everyone's talking about could be standing right behind you this very second, ready to run you through. So take it back."

Garbling his Irises and his eyes even as the words tumbled out, Subaru pressed the point. Still, Kadomon let out a resigned sigh, a hand to his forehead.

"I'm tellin' you, quit it. I'm sorry I ran my mouth. There, satisfied?"

"...Tch."

It was hardly an apology with any real sincerity behind it, but Subaru let it serve as a stopping point and sheathed his sword. Yet as Subaru let his fighting spirit deflate, Kadomon drew a breath and added, "But still—"

"You're free to think whatever you want, but a half-elf becomin' queen—that's flat out impossible."

"There you go again...! Why? Is it because of the Witch of Envy? Just 'cause that Witch happened to be a half-elf, that makes every other half-elf dangerous too!?"

"—That's right."

To Subaru, venting his irritation at the argument starting back up, that voice rang out unexpectedly cold.

The single word affirming Subaru's outburst—it was Kadomon who had spoken it, and it snuffed out Subaru's momentum, leaving his voice caught in his throat as he looked up at the tough face before him.

And in the eyes meeting Subaru's own head-on, Kadomon's face held a clear, unmistakable trace of dread.

"Fearin' the Witch—that's just common sense, somethin' nobody needs explained to 'em. I don't know how much you don't know, but for most folks, that reason alone is more than enough to shun somethin'."

Listen, Kadomon said, as if explaining it to the silent Subaru.

"The Witch—the Witch of Envy—was a monster on a whole 'nother level, they say. Four hundred years ago, half the continent got swallowed up in the Witch's shadow. Countless named heroes, wyverns, all sank before her. If it weren't for the Divine Dragon's power and the Sword Saint of that age, the whole world woulda ended."

Subaru caught a few words in there he couldn't afford to let pass unnoticed, but he couldn't tear his eyes away from the shopkeeper's trembling pupils as he spoke. Kadomon's parched lips quivered as he went on.

"And yet, for all the Witch did, nobody's got the faintest clue what she really was. All anybody knows is that she was a silver-haired half-elf. No language could reach her, no communication was possible. She just went on a rampage like everything but herself was unbearably hateful to her, breakin' and killin' and swallowin' up everything, over and over."

That much, Subaru already knew from the summary in the picture book.

But in Kadomon's telling—tangled up as it was with his own turbulent emotions—there was raw feeling packed into it that no flat, even prose could ever fully capture. This was a tale of the Witch that had surely been passed down not just through picture books, but through generations of oral tradition from relatives. Each teller might change the path it took, but it always arrived at the same destination in the end.

Which was—

"Somethin' whose true nature you can't even grasp is terrifyin' enough as it is. So how could anybody not want to keep their distance, even from just the parts they do understand?"

"So that's why you discriminate against half-elves?"

"At the very least, it's true that half-witches tend to have twisted personalities more often than not. —Though whether that's somethin' rooted in 'em from the start, or just from the environment they're raised in, I couldn't tell you."

The bitter, as-if-chewing-something-sour look on Kadomon's face hinted at his own struggle, wrung out in response to Subaru's forced-out words.

Even in their short acquaintance, Subaru thought that at his core, Kadomon was a good man. He himself understood that what he'd just said didn't hold up logically. It was only that the feelings that welled up at the thought of "the Witch" wouldn't allow any argument against that logic to stand.

"Most folks'd agree with me. Ain't nobody gonna go outta their way to back the half-witch's side and shift their vote away from the other candidates. That's just how it goes, plain and simple."

"——"

At the way he tried to force the conversation to a close, Subaru felt in his own skin the truth of what Emilia had pleaded for at the Royal Selection.

Being a half-elf—it was a fate she could never cut herself free from, an iron chain that would never let her stand at the same starting line as anyone else.

So long as the Witch existed, Emilia would go on being shackled by her own birth.

And that was an unreasonable obstacle entirely beyond her own power to overcome.

"So long as that's how people see it, there ain't no chance for her from the start. Whoever went and put her up for this, I got no idea what they were thinkin'... pullin' some stupid stunt like that."

Crossing his arms, Kadomon showed his displeasure openly. His anger now seemed to have shifted from Emilia the candidate herself toward whoever had put her forward on a palanquin with no hope of winning.

That stance was, in its way, genuine proof of his goodness—but as long as the ill will toward the existence of half-elves was rooted so deep in him, it offered no comfort at all.

Kadomon had called Subaru ignorant. Said he didn't understand the history of how half-elves had been oppressed, or the tyranny of the Witch that had caused it.

But, Subaru thought.

*Don't give me that*, Subaru thought.

It was true that Subaru was a lump of ignorance when it came to this world's history. As for the atrocities the Witch had committed, he probably couldn't even begin to imagine anything beyond what the words on a page described.

There was no way he could empathize with just how much people feared half-elves, or, in turn, how half-elves—raised in that kind of environment—felt about humanity.

But even so—

*"—That's far enough, villain."*

That clear, silvery bell of a voice had shaken his eardrums fiercely in that moment.

Sprawled on the ground in that back alley, on the verge of being crushed under pain and humiliation, she'd reached out a hand to save him without a shred of doubt about what she was doing.

Subaru didn't know this world's history.

Subaru didn't know the history of half-elves.

Subaru didn't know just how terrifying a being the Witch of Envy truly was.

But he knew Emilia.

Subaru knew that there wasn't the slightest reason for that silver-haired girl—stubborn, endlessly good-hearted, someone who moved without any thought to her own gain or loss—to be lumped together with "the Witch of Envy."

He knew that a girl who must have lived through anything but a kind environment was still, at heart, someone capable of extending kindness to others.

No matter how harsh this world had been to her, still, Subaru alone would—

*"Is that really true, though?"*

That was the question that suddenly crept into his thoughts.

At the unsettling tone of that voice cutting through his mind, Subaru held his breath and blinked. The voice was questioning whether the one answer he'd been about to arrive at was really correct. He looked around, turning the question over, ready to shout back in anger at whatever had brought that unseen voice on. But—

*"Do you really know her? Her, truly?"*

To that pure, innocent question, Subaru meant to answer that of course he did, and found he couldn't.

A twisted sensation, his own inner voice getting dammed up—something that seemed simple but was, in fact, hard, carrying with it a kind of contradiction: the act of trying to deceive one's own heart.

The instant he couldn't answer the doubt clearly, Subaru realized whose voice it truly was—his own.

The voice repeated itself, in Subaru's own tone.

*"Do you really know her? Her, truly?"*

*I do*, he tried to answer, loud and clear—but his throat, unable to fully deceive his own heart, wouldn't let itself shake with the words.

Subaru didn't know what kind of life she'd lived.

Subaru didn't know what she was thinking, aiming for the throne.

Subaru didn't know how she felt about the people who equated her with the Witch.

Subaru didn't *want* to know what she thought of him.

"—Hey, you alright, kid?"

"Huh?"

Before he knew it, a scarred face peered at him from up close, looking startled. Reflexively recoiling, Subaru drew back a little to put some distance between them.

"Wh-what, don't come up in my face like that outta nowhere, you'll suck the life right outta me."

"That's a hell of a thing to say! You went and got that thousand-yard stare all of a sudden, that's what spooked me! What, you got some kinda condition?"

"Everyone's got a garden of delusions hidden away in the depths of their heart, you know. Most people forget how important that place is, in time, but every once in a great while, there's a grown-up who never forgets that garden. Guess I'm one of them."

"I get it, you got some nasty illness. Never mind, let's get back to the shop."

Unwilling to keep pace with the flippant remark meant to cover his own inner shock, the shopkeeper started walking back through the crowd toward his shop. Following that broad, sturdy back leading the way, Subaru noticed with a damp chill that his own back was soaked in cold sweat.

Just what emotion had been the source of that sweat—

Trying to dig into that felt uncomfortable enough that Subaru's steps grew heavy. And then—

"Oh, and—might be nothin', but."

Muttering as they walked, Kadomon didn't turn to look at him.

In a voice quiet enough that it barely reached Subaru's ears, he said—

"Don't go sayin' the word 'Witch' out loud in the open street. Me included—you never know who might be listenin', or where."

It wasn't, it seemed, simply rehashing what he'd said before.

At the gravity in the tone of that warning, Subaru let his silence stand as his acknowledgment.

Kadomon seemed to catch the meaning behind it, and didn't add anything further.

Just one line, left trailing off as though cut short—

"Whoever, wherever."

—which struck Subaru as strangely memorable.

A heavy atmosphere hung between Subaru and Kadomon as the two of them made their way back.

Honestly, Subaru hadn't managed to sort out the feelings knotted up in his chest, and Kadomon, for his part, seemed embarrassed at having gotten so worked up in his argument with Subaru.

And so the two of them pushed back through the crowd to Kadomon's shop in silence, only to be met with—

"—Welcome back. Your last customer just left, actually."

Kadomon stared, mouth hanging open in blank astonishment, as Rem said this after handing over merchandise and change and bowing politely to see off a customer.

There in front of him, wearing that dumbstruck expression, stood a row of display shelves picked clean, emptied of an enormous amount of stock.

That Rem, left in charge of the shop and gone off the rails, had shouted "take it, thieves!" and started flinging fruit for free at passersby—that clearly wasn't what had happened, given that the basket meant for collecting payment was overflowing with coins.

In other words—

"M-my shop's average daily sales, blown past in this short a time..."

Faced with a fact he didn't want to accept, Kadomon covered his face with his hands, trembling.

Handing back her post as shopkeep to that man, Rem slipped smoothly outside and flitted to Subaru's side as if flying, leaning in to look up at him.

"Well, what do you think, Subaru-kun? Rem's all-around competence, on full display. I heard you say he was someone who'd helped you out, so I tried my best to be at least a little useful. ...You're welcome to praise me, you know?"

Swaying the hem of her skirt, Rem glanced sidelong at Subaru with one eye. Her manner, so plainly at odds with her words, was easy to read. Comparing that to Kadomon still failing to accept reality in front of them, Subaru noticed his own chest had grown a little lighter.

Whether that was relief at seeing Rem's usual self, or the quiet satisfaction of watching Kadomon—who'd put him through a bit of an unpleasant time—get his comeuppance in shock, Subaru couldn't say.

"Either way, there's no doubt it's thanks to Rem."

Reaching a hand out to the blue hair offered to him, Subaru savored the familiar texture beneath his fingers and gently stroked her head.

Rem closed her eyes as if it felt good, drinking in the sensation of Subaru's palm stroking her, her cheeks flushing in a look of rapture, humming an oddly sultry little "mm" in her throat.

Behind the two of them lost in their own moment, Kadomon went on and on, tracing the scar on his face with a finger.

"Guess it really was my looks scarin' folks off, huh..."

—muttering, far too late, the reason for his ailing business.
