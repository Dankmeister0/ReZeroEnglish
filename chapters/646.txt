Arc 8 Chapter 30: "Grim News"

—The "Fortress City" of Garkla is known as one of the five great cities of the Vollachia Empire.

Sitting along the border with the neighboring Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica and the Kararagi City-States—effectively the border with both nations at once—Garkla carries a great mountain at its back, and within its ramparts stands a cluster of formidable fortresses, making it a mighty strategic bulwark of defense.
And yet the legend of that sturdiness was shattered a little over a decade ago, when the Witch Cult's Sin Archbishop of "Greed" assailed Garkla single-handedly.
The toll—several thousand of the fortress city's standing troops, the death of Kurgan the "Eight-Armed," who had been called the mightiest warrior in the Vollachia Empire, and the annihilation of multiple fortresses—stands as one of the worst catastrophes recorded in imperial history.

Ever since, deep scars have been carved into the Fortress City, and to this day the full restoration of the city and the recovery of its functions cannot be called complete. Tension between the two nations remains high—

"And so, that's the situation the kingdom believed the Fortress City to be in, but ye-e-es, well."

At the innermost part of the city, a great fortress carved into the flank of the neighboring Mount Gildray. Standing at the window of its conference room, Roswaal smiled as he surveyed the cityscape spread below.
The city held shelters ample in size and well-stocked with supplies, into which the refugees and soldiers who had fled the capital by the skin of their teeth were now being taken in, at last finding some measure of relief.
Below, the sight of it being realized depended on several fortresses built in neat, orderly rows—and not one of those structures looked half-finished.

"Quite different from what I'd heard... The rumor going around was that the Fortress City was, among all the Empire's holdings, still in the ve-e-ery middle of reconstruction."

Whatever foreigners might believe, even the imperial citizens themselves had not been let in on the city's true state.
If some nation had swallowed whole the notion that the Fortress City was incomplete and moved on that assumption, they would have found themselves greeted by an Empire arrayed in full readiness.
As expected of the Empire—willing to exploit even its own scandal for the sake of war. Roswaal's exasperation gave way to something closer to admiration.
And then—

"If that was the impression held even by the Kingdom's foremost mage, then it seems our information control functioned rather well."

"Not about to leave a visible, audible weakness lying around for all to see, is that it? Ye-e-es, well, my hat's off to the Empire's thoroughness."

A voice answered his admiration, and Roswaal turned with a shrug of his shoulders. Standing before him was a white-haired old man—Berstetz Fondalfon, chancellor of the Empire.
He was the man whose desperate fighting during the earlier attack on the Coupled Dragon Carriages had helped turn the tide.
He had once fallen from the carriage and his survival had seemed doubtful, but by a hair's breadth, Frederica—who had been pursuing the carriage—arrived in time to save him, and so their reunion had been achieved here in the Fortress City.
That said, given the circumstances, his return had not come without a cost.

"How are you feeling? The burns looked quite extensive."

"I'm grateful for your concern. Fortunately, thanks to the power of a certain healer, I kept my life. My limbs are somewhat impaired, but... that is more good fortune than a worthless old man like myself deserves."

"—...I see."

As he spoke, Berstetz leaned on a cane with one hand, dragging his right leg as he walked.
However potent the effects of healing magic might be, producing a result that vastly exceeds the limits of a person's own natural recovery requires nothing short of a miracle.
Unfortunately, it seemed Berstetz's miracle had run its course the moment his life was spared. Though the old man himself appeared to regard even that as an excessive miracle.
In any case—

"Casualties minimal, and we made it into the Fortress City without losing any notable strength worth speaking of—that's a stroke of luck. It's unfortunate about the chancellor's leg, but one can hardly hope for a better outcome than this."

The one saying so was Serena, who had entered the conference room together with Roswaal.
She sat in one of the seats at the round table in the center of the room, tracing the scar on her own face with a finger, and pulled out the chair beside her own for Berstetz.
At this consideration so unlike her, Berstetz gave a bow and took the seat.

"The Marquess of Dracroy's manner of speech is rude, but her gestures are considerate... I find myself at a loss as to how I should regard you."

"No need to think so hard about it. Both are just me. If this fortress were to come crashing down, I'd sling the withered old chancellor over my shoulder and run while giving my honest opinion of the effort involved."

"I see. That is beyond my comprehension."

That very bluntness only made Serena's charm more complicated, but Berstetz's manner of responding to it also carried the impression of a man who had shed a layer of himself.
The horde of undead Flying Dragons and their leader, the "Poison Princess," come to attack. On top of that, the appearance of the "Three-Headed" Valgren, held back only by Halibel the "Extoller." Honestly, it had been the worst of circumstances.
And yet, including Berstetz's survival, the overall outcome could be called a miraculous resolution.
The manner in which that miracle had been brought about, however, was a matter that invited controversy.

"By the way—is it certain? That Her Excellency Lamia will not rise again?"

At Berstetz's question from his seat at the round table, Roswaal noticed a faint heat in his voice unlike him, but unlike Serena, he did not press the point crudely.
Instead, Roswaal drew in his chin and said,

"It seems the other side found a method to unravel the technique—a hybrid of the 'Sacrament of the Immortal King' and restorative magic. Princess Lamia Godwin has, by that method, been cut off from any means of resurrection. ...No, in this case—"

"'Saved' might be a somewhat arrogant way of putting it, perhaps."

Serena pointed, without mercy, at the very part he had hesitated to voice aloud.
On that point, Roswaal himself had no clear answer. Whether a life resurrected as one of the undead would itself welcome that state was something Roswaal could not gauge.
Speaking his personal opinion, he would not condemn the resurrection of life itself as an evil. But that state of being reborn as one of the undead—

"—Saved. Or perhaps it may be said she was set free."

"Oh? I find it surprising to hear you say that, Chancellor."

At Berstetz's words, Serena raised an eyebrow and murmured with evident interest. Under that gaze, Berstetz, both hands resting on the head of his cane, let out a long breath and said,

"I do not believe Her Excellency Lamia would have approved of such a state. She, too, must surely have been aware that she had been defeated. ...That was an unwanted opportunity."

"So you're saying being struck down was her true wish? It seems a bit inconsistent, given that she came at us again, multiplied no less, after being struck down."

"...Even so, this worthless one believes Her Excellency's true intentions lay elsewhere."

Berstetz, his thread-thin eyes lowered, spoke his heavy murmur with an unconcealable wish behind it.
Roswaal felt he understood something of Berstetz's sentiment there. Unfortunately, it did not seem to reach Serena at all, resting her cheek on her hand as she was.
In any case, that too was a matter very much bound up in the focus of their discussion.

"The enemy is mighty, and the situation lies in the depths of chaos. It's a precarious state of affairs unless we stand united—no time for us to be squabbling amongst ourselves, wouldn't you say, Subaru-kun?"

"...I know that without being told."

Laughing lightly, Roswaal called out toward the entrance of the conference room.
Just then, the black-haired boy who had opened the door and peeked his head in answered with a bitter expression, exactly so.


△▼△▼△▼△


"Since it was an emergency we didn't get the chance to talk about it, but I'm sharing the information properly now. This girl, Rui—now going by Spica—was originally a Sin Archbishop of the Witch Cult."

"Aah-uu!"

In the conference room of the great fortress, at the very outset of the discussion that had just begun, Subaru laid bare, without concealment, the true identity of Spica, whom he had made stand beside him.
Those on the side who had already grasped the circumstances back at the Coupled Dragon Carriages naturally received the confession quietly. On the other hand, the reaction from the imperial side, who hadn't known, was exactly as expected.
Blank astonishment came first, followed—whether the confession was true or a jest made no difference to the nature of the reaction that followed—by fury.

"What—what are you saying?! A Sin Archbishop!? Impossible! Do you even realize where you're speaking such an outrage?! This is a city once brought low by a Sin Archbishop!!"

The one who shouted so, making his already loud voice even louder, was Goz Ralfon.
Having contributed greatly to the defense of the Coupled Dragon Carriages and joined the meeting with his wounds barely treated, Goz's outcry was surely the shared sentiment of everyone hearing this news for the first time.
But this was neither a jest nor a game.

"I already heard about that bastard Regulus rampaging through this city too. I don't know if it's any comfort, but we took care of that son of a bitch, so please just forget about him for now."

"That contemptible madman is of no concern whatsoever! What matters is the sin and guilt carried by the cursed title of 'Sin Archbishop'! Do you understand, boy!?"

"—I understand."

The very volume of his voice stirred a wind, giving the illusion it might blow him away, but Subaru did not retreat a single step from Goz's roar, answering back head-on, without hesitation.
Even the formidable Goz, met head-on by a mere boy's reply, widened his eyes with a "hm."

"May I?"

As Goz's momentum stumbled, the one who raised a hand in his place was Berstetz. The imperial chancellor looked at Subaru and, beside him, Spica, through eyes narrow as threads, and said,

"That you went out of your way to confess this must mean her existence and abilities are deemed necessary to the discussion ahead. Which is to say—might I assume this relates to the freeing of Her Excellency Lamia from the yoke of the undead?"

"Ah, yeah, that's right. Glad you're quick on the uptake. It really would've been a problem if you'd gone and died on us without permission, Berstetz-san."

Berstetz, having deduced the general circumstances from the flow of the discussion, nodded in confirmation, and Subaru found himself blurting that out.
During the battle at the Coupled Dragon Carriages, Berstetz had confronted Lamia single-handedly, and at the risk of his own life had excluded her and her divided selves together from the "Divine Protection of Wind Evasion." That said, the action itself had been entirely his own reckless decision, and he had very nearly lost his life over it.

"Please, don't let even the smart people get infected with this whole Imperial way of thinking. Accomplishing something in exchange for your life isn't admirable in the slightest."

"On that point, opinions may vary... but at the very least, this worthless one doubts there is much to be gained by exchanging my life for it. Understood."

Yield where yielding was called for, and stand firm where it was not—so Berstetz put it.
Subaru held the same view, so it seemed their difference in values regarding the worth of a life would remain a parallel line never quite meeting. Though Subaru had not the slightest intention of letting any of them die heroically in the line of duty, regardless.

"So then? Now that you've confirmed the chancellor's question, there's no doubting that this Sin Archbishop girl was immediately effective against the undead. What exactly did she do?"

"That's kind of hard to explain in concrete terms, but..."

Resting her cheek on her hand at the round table, prioritizing curiosity over hostility toward Spica, Serena asked the natural question. Subaru glanced at Spica.
The girl blinked her blue eyes at that gaze, then lifted her clenched hand to show it,

"Spica's Authority of 'Gluttony' lets her devour the special power of anyone whose name she knows... or something like that, I think. That's how she was able to nullify the technique behind the endlessly-reviving zombies."

"Now that's a real vague explanation, isn't it? If you don't explain it properly, all that's left is unease, and I can't feel the least bit reassured."

"I want to give a more convincing, more concrete explanation too, believe me!"

Anastasia, who was supposed to be an ally, shot down Subaru's vague argument without mercy.
But it was reasoning that deserved to be shot down, and the wound it left was, unavoidably, a fatal one. At the same time, it was also a fact that Subaru had no better way to explain it.

"Either way, Subaru's aim hit the mark, I suppose. That troublesome princess-zombie fled thanks to that girl's Authority, I suppose. That retreat wasn't a lie or a bluff."

"I too agree with Lady Beatrice's opinion. And the 'Pruning Corps' who held their ground there, striving to buy time for their lord's retreat... it seems unlikely their desperate fight was the product of a mere bluff."

"Both of you giving such sentimental answers, I see. But that's all circumstantial evidence... if you can't have confidence in the effect, using it is far too dangerous a poison, you understand that, right?"

"That's... of course, I think I understand that."

Even with Beatrice and Julius backing him up, the validity of Anastasia's plain reasoning did not waver.
The soundness of her argument was clear from the reaction of the imperial side, which had yet to be freshly persuaded. What must be protected was the Empire—but whether the risk they'd have to shoulder for that was worth relying on a Sin Archbishop was, frankly, a difficult judgment call to say the least.

The two lights the Stargazer Ubilk had spoken of, needed to resist the "Great Disaster"—with the survival of the other half deemed hopeless, in practice Spica was the only one they could rely on.

When the desperate investigation by Beatrice and the others had revealed, amid the onslaught of the attacking zombies, that the technique was an application combining the "Sacrament of the Immortal King" with restorative magic, Subaru had thought of Spica's potential.
Interference in the souls of the zombies through the Authority of "Gluttony"—specifically, how the exercise of Spica's Authority had been carried out and how it had connected to the result remained, as stated above, unclear.

Still, Subaru and the others had not forgotten the "name" of Lamia Godwin, the target of Spica's Authority—nor had Abel, her half-sibling.
As she departed, there had been no sign of memory loss in Lamia. So it wasn't that her "memory" had been devoured either. —Then what had Spica devoured?

As things stood, all they could do was line up every reckless argument available—fate, causality, roles, or whatever abnormal status she'd been afflicted with at the time.
Of course, Subaru understood Anastasia's point that such arguments lacked persuasive force.

"But this girl might be able to stop the other 'zombies' too, right? I think that's a re-e-eally important thing."

Beside Subaru as he struggled with this, Emilia, occupying one of the seats at the round table, spoke up with her brows drooping. At Emilia's opinion, Anastasia narrowed her pale blue-green eyes.

"I'm not saying I don't get how Emilia-san feels, y'know? I'm sick of this whole 'Great Disaster' commotion too, and I feel the same urge to jump at any plan that might let us win. But even so, throwing around a Sin Archbishop's power carelessly—"

"No, that's not it. I mean, that's true too, of course, but... what I really want to do something about is the people who died becoming 'zombies.'"

"You want to do something about the zombies?"

"I can't say it well, but... seeing people who are already gone made to move around like that, as 'zombies'—it's re-e-eally awful. Abel surely never wanted to meet his sister like that either. And Medium-chan too."

Lowering her eyes framed by long lashes, Emilia touched on the subject of Medium, who was absent from this gathering.
Her absence was less a matter of having been excluded from the meeting's participants and more a matter of her current condition. —The cause was the last of the undead they had encountered at the Coupled Dragon Carriages.

Struck by Spica's "Starving Feast," having lost her power to multiply and facing her end, Lamia had called out, and in answer to her call the undead Flying Dragon and its rider—a Flying Dragon Rider turned undead—had come to recover her body.
That person and the siblings Flop and Medium had apparently had a relationship in life.
Shocked by the fact that someone close to her had become one of the undead, now that the crisis had passed, Medium had shut herself in a room of the fortress and had not shown her face.

An event that had left even that ever-bright, ever-forward-looking Medium unable to help but become like this.
Subaru too had felt the ache of it, but he had not been able to be as considerate of her as Emilia was. So, belatedly, Emilia's words struck him with a jolt.

At himself, having had eyes only for the threat the zombies posed, unable to spare a thought for the grief they brought.

"...It's a bit different from Lady Emilia's way of thinking, but there's no doubt those zombies are troublesome things. To begin with, they're being resurrected in an unnatural form. It would be far too convenient if there were no malfunctions whatsoever."

While Subaru felt ashamed in the face of Emilia's opinion, it was Otto who cut in with that remark.
Having quietly observed the situation for a while, he leaned his back against the fortress wall, looking up at the ceiling, and said,

"The zombies aren't merely beings that have been resurrected, nothing more. This many of them appearing... though some of it is just one woman appearing many times over, but setting that aside, I've seen a great many zombies. I don't believe every single one of them held a grudge against His Excellency the Emperor in life. Surely not, even so."

"Right, surely not, even so."
"Even taking everything into account, that seems impossible, I suppose."

At the doubt and defense Otto had inserted, a bare minimum of trust nodded Subaru and the others into agreement.
However much of a tyrannical, coldblooded emperor Abel might appear to others, there was no doubt he had been an emperor both resented and respected.
Walking through the Empire up to now, Subaru himself had actually seen and heard that hard-to-believe fact firsthand.
In other words—

"The hostile awareness toward His Excellency Vincent and the Vollachia Empire should be considered something implanted, or amplified, during the process of being resurrected as a zombie."

"You mean brainwashing... I think that's a reasonable opinion."

"...I heard it from the soldiers fighting in the back. They said that when a soldier fighting alongside them was killed, that soldier sometimes turns into a zombie right away and attacks them, apparently, I suppose."

"Someone who, right up until that moment, was riding alongside them as an ally, and the instant they turn into a zombie they attack—Otto-dono's assessment seems likely to be correct."

"—...Yes."

Otto, having stated his personal view on the zombies, responded curtly to Julius's affirming opinion. Subaru frowned at the sense of a wall in that response, but Julius and Anastasia showed no sign of concern, accepting it as a matter of course.
In any case—

"As for Otto-kun's opinion—it happens to be something I was discussing with the chancellor and Countess Dracroy right before you all arrived. According to that... now, don't make such a sour face."

"The matter of deepening the relationship between Roswaal and an interior minister can wait for another day—but the chancellor held a similar opinion. Isn't that right?"

Prompted by Roswaal and Serena, the two of them steering the topic his way, Berstetz nodded. Both hands still resting on his cane, he surveyed the meeting's participants with those thread-thin eyes so hard to read, and said,

"Considering Her Excellency Lamia's character, she would not have wished for a second life. Much less would she have plotted, through it, to overturn the death of her first."

"So she really was brainwashed into attacking us all pumped up and gung-ho about it, huh."

"That is how it ought to be considered. ...Though, naturally, there remains the possibility that, resurrected and unable to resist as she was, she nonetheless held nothing back, deliberately testing whether the present Empire was worth preserving."

"If that's the case, then the Vollachian imperial family...!"
"Now really isn't the time for that kind of enthusiasm, I suppose..."

Prefacing it as a judgment she would not have made were she in her right mind, while insisting the possibility of an abnormal judgment made even with some remnant of a normal mind could not be ruled out, Berstetz sounded, for some reason, rather proud of himself.
In any case, it seemed they could agree, at least, that the zombies' thinking was not sound.

"Revived zombies are, without exception, hostile toward us. In that sense too, just as Emilia-tan said, I want to stop the enemy's zombie attacks even a second sooner. And for that, Spica's power is essential. As a practical matter, I don't think this is something I can compromise on."

"That's an awful lot of zombies, isn't it. Are you saying you intend to devote the effort of eating them one by one, all the way through? If we're talking practical matters, I think that can't be ignored either."

"I'll do it if I have to. I didn't take this girl's hand with the intention of just nibbling the tasty bits and throwing the rest away."

Squeezing—not just figuratively—Spica's hand, Subaru declared this to Otto.
Receiving that look from Subaru, Otto turned eyes that seemed to peer into her very heart toward Spica. Facing that merciless gaze of suspicion, Spica squeezed Subaru's hand back and faced him,

"Auu-aah!"

She let out a strong groan, as if to insist that she had resolved to walk a thorny path.
Hearing Spica's reply, Otto let out a breath through his nose and said,

"Just so you know, persuading me won't accomplish anything. I don't have that kind of influence over whether she actually gets worked into the operation."

"You might not have influence in the meeting, but your presence carries a lot of weight among us. At the very least, if I can't even fully convince someone on my own side, how could I convince anyone else?"

"I see. Perhaps I should have graded you more harshly, then."

Shrugging his shoulders with a sigh, Otto, answering so, nevertheless seemed to have no intention of retracting what he'd said before.
Likely, from his perspective, the moment he'd let Spica use her "Starving Feast" against Lamia back at the Coupled Dragon Carriages, he'd already resolved to pull the same rope alongside them.
Subaru had simply hated leaving that unconfirmed, letting it slide by unspoken.

"You seem awfully relieved about that, but Ah still haven't been properly convinced, y'know?"

"Ugh... okay, so what would convince you, Anastasia-san?"

With consensus reached among his own circle, Anastasia—someone half in that circle—stood in the way.
At the very least, in this Empire she was bound to them for better or worse. At Subaru's bitter question, she pressed a finger to her lips and said,

"Honestly, that's something Ah'd like ya to think through yerself, but that's a lot better than groanin' over an answer that never comes. —First off, we need to nail down what that girl can do, and if possible, check what happens after she does it."

"What she can do, and what happens after..."

"What she can do is stop the 'zombies' from getting back up, right? But what do you mean by what happens after?"

"Natsuki-kun, Emilia-san, you both know just how awful the Authority of 'Gluttony' is, don't ya?"

At Anastasia's quiet point, Subaru held his breath, and Emilia's eyes went wide.
That too was, of course, something that could not be brushed past untouched.

The Authority of "Gluttony"—its victims exist all over the world.
Even in this very conference room there was Emilia and Julius, and inside the fortress there was Rem as well. —No, in the sense that it affects those around as well, one could say everyone is a victim of "Gluttony" in some way.
Given that they intended to make deliberate use of that Authority, it was only natural to assess the risks.

"...So far, I suppose no one has forgotten about that girl who multiplied."

"That's right. But nobody thinks she's eaten nothin' at all either. Maybe it's not 'names' or 'memories,' but once whatever she's eaten piles up, what happens to that girl?"

"That's..."

"Natsuki-kun seemed real suspicious about it—there's no guarantee she won't go back to bein' the original her, is there?"

At Anastasia's merciless pressing, Subaru could only fall silent.
All he could do was pray, with no grounds, an emotional kind of belief. The girl who, rather than remaining Rui Arneb, had been given the new name Spica and chosen to live a different life.
In a sense, he could only hope she would be guided toward the side with more people willing to affirm as right this Authority she had been born bearing.

"Lady Anastasia, that is something impossible to confirm. Certainly, we shouldn't let our guard down, but..."

"Ah can't offer proof Ah'm not schemin' some scheme that doesn't exist either. So at the very least, Ah'd like some confidence that walkin' this dangerous road comes with a payoff worth it."

"Which is to say—confirmation of the results achieved when Lady Spica's Authority is wielded. Is that it?"

Julius touched the scar beneath his left eye, and Anastasia gave a short nod, "Yep."
Then she looked back and forth between Subaru and Spica together, and said,

"No merchant goes into a negotiation carryin' treasure whose worth isn't even clear. At the very least, if we don't know how it'll be useful, even the sales pitch ends up missin' the mark."

"Quite right."

From the fact that Lamia's assault had ceased, it was probably certain that she had been defeated.
But the only other grounds were the oracle of Ubilk, whose very existence and words as a "Stargazer" were dubious to begin with. With the concerns still unresolved, Anastasia was already conceding more than enough as it was.
At the very least, they needed everyone to be convinced, in some form, that Spica's "Starving Feast" was truly effective against the zombies—

"—Unfortunately, there's no time to spare for verification."

A low voice, cutting in without regard for anyone, stirred up the atmosphere of Subaru's disadvantage.
Every gaze in the room instantly turned toward the entrance of the conference room, where a slender black-haired figure had appeared—the one who was supposed to be the star of this meeting, arriving late: Abel.
Beside him, with Flop and, for some reason, Jamal in tow, he advanced unhurriedly, and without pausing, seated himself grandly in the empty seat at the round table.
And then—

"What, gaping like a fool."

"You showed up late with so much swagger it startled me, that's what. We were told to start without you, so what were you doing?"

"There was something that needed confirming with urgency. That was the greater part of the reason I hurried us to the Fortress City. —The true meaning behind the words that great fool left behind, to be verified."

Abel answered without the slightest sign of shame, and Subaru hesitated to press him further.
He could guess that the "great fool" Abel had spoken of was likely the vassal who had bested Abel through mutual deception, and had let him live. But at the same time, what caught Subaru's interest was the reason he'd rushed to this Fortress City.

"So, um, that thing Abel told us about—is that the reason we came rushing to this city?"

"Naturally, it's also true that it happened to be located in the most suitable position to receive the refugees."

"Can we skip the roundabout talk and just hear it? What did you mean earlier, that there's no time?"

Both Emilia and Anastasia directed their questions at Abel.
The same question was on the mind of everyone in that conference room. At the gazes questioning the true meaning of his statement, Abel closed one eye and struck the round table with his fingers.
A hard sound rang out, and Subaru and the others' attention was drawn, willy-nilly, to his hand.

"I'll state it plainly. What I wanted to confirm in this Fortress City was nothing other than the whereabouts and condition of the Sanctum that lies here—the great imperial Spirit, 'the Rockpile,' one of the Four Great [Spirits] beyond reason."

"Four Great, great Spirit... you mean the Four Great Spirits?"

"That is correct."

At this sudden, unexpected piece of information, Subaru blinked several times.
As for the Four Great Spirits, it was something he'd been told about back when he'd first come to this other world, and again when he'd formed a Contract with Beatrice and become a Spiritual Arts User.
The spirits of spirits, holding the greatest power in this world—though, given that a certain dim-witted, ash-gray cat was mixed in among that select company, the credibility of that claim felt rather shaky.

"You mean the Empire had already grasped the whereabouts of one of the Four Great, 'the Rockpile,' Muspel!?"

"W-wait, is that really something to be that surprised about?"

"Naturally. The City-States' 'Phantom Assailant,' the Holy Kingdom's 'Divine Beast'—both command unwavering faith and reverence in their respective nations. 'The Rockpile' is a being harboring power of the same order."

"If we could even form a cooperative relationship with one of the Four Great Spirits, that alone would be a matter of national importance... far more significant than that lie about the Fortress City still bein' under reconstruction."

Told one after another of the gravity of the matter by Julius and Anastasia, Subaru could only blink in honest bewilderment.
Still, given that no one corrected them, and that Emilia and Beatrice both wore somewhat smug expressions, it seemed that understanding was, indeed, correct.
The problem was—

"If you rushed to the Fortress City to confirm the whereabouts and condition of that 'Rockpile,' does that mean the great Spirit is here in this land? Certainly, if we could borrow that power, it would be a tremendous support, but..."

Against the endlessly welling tide of enemies, Spica's "Starving Feast" alone was not enough.
Naturally, the more strength available to hold back the zombies' onslaught until the "Starving Feast" could be unleashed, the better. Or perhaps, just as Puck had specialized in area attacks beyond what Emilia was currently capable of, "the Rockpile" too might hold some reliable power.

"Unfortunately, that's not how things are going to play out. Do you all recall His Imperial Majesty saying earlier that there's no time to spare?"

"Flop-san?"

Slowly shaking his head, Flop held back Subaru's forward-leaning line of thought.
Just like Medium, he who always maintained a bright, positive bearing now spoke with a tightened expression, in an earnest tone.
For whatever reason, having accompanied Abel, he had seen the same thing the emperor had. If Flop's expression, having shared in that conclusion a step ahead of Subaru and the others, looked like that, then—

"The aid you all are hoping for from 'the Rockpile' is not to be had. Worse than that—the very existence of 'the Rockpile' has become something that benefits the enemy... the 'Great Disaster' itself."

"What... do you mean? The great Spirit—"

"Is in the enemy's hands. Likely, it is that spirit's extraordinary mana being used by the 'Great Disaster' to raise the undead from their graves without limit. That is why they continue to well up without end."

"————"

"And furthermore—"

Subaru, his eyes wide at the worst possible report, turned a gaze of disbelief toward Abel as he continued speaking regardless. But that did nothing to deter the man from his words.
In the tense air of the conference room, Abel continued without stopping.
It was—

"I said 'without limit,' but that is not quite accurate. However extraordinary the mana held by 'the Rockpile' may be, it will eventually run dry. And when it does, the land of the Empire will meet its end. —Because the Sanctum that 'the Rockpile' guards, the power sustaining the very earth of Vollachia, will be lost, and Collapse will become unavoidable."

—It was, in truth, grim news itself: the revelation of the true nature of the "Great Disaster" fated to destroy the Empire.
