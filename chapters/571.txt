Arc 7 Chapter 66: "Hiain Yatz"

——Life on the Sword Slave Isle of Ginunhive, as it turned out, wasn't so bad for Subaru.

Subaru's performance in his "Sparka" bout had apparently gone over well with the sword slaves who vied against each other across the isle, and everywhere he went he received a welcome not unlike the one in the great hall.
Even in an environment where people were forced to fight, the customs of the Vollachian Empire didn't change.
So it seemed the principle of respecting the strong held true here as well, and to them, the "Sparka" that Subaru's "party" had put on had apparently met with their approval.

"No matter how you look at him, he's a scrawny little brat, but he's got guts."

That was, roughly speaking, Subaru's reputation among them.
Subaru was a child who looked like he could be wrung out with one hand if someone felt like it, and the fact that he'd put up a good fight against a gladiator lion was probably what made it so amusing to them.
Unlike the fake Cecilus, who was around the same age, the fact that Subaru acted like a kid his age might have helped that impression along. That's how bad the fake Cecilus's reputation was, by comparison.

"Honestly, I just live as myself, so what people around me think of me is beside the point. In the end, once everyone sees the results, they won't have any complaints left to make. Ah, and by that I mean in an emotional sense, not a physical one."

That was, more or less, his reaction when word of the surrounding opinion reached his ears, deliberately or not.
Subaru had somehow suspected as much, but the fake Cecilus had no notion whatsoever of bending himself to fit in, nor any consideration for smoothing things over by going along with everyone else.
An unshakeable will that refused to bend, and the strength to see it through—that was what justified the fake Cecilus's self-centered, unrestrained ego.
And on the Sword Slave Isle, just as in the Empire at large, that was considered correct by the rules of both.

"But it's not a good sign to be disliked for it. Just as with a 'party,' none of us can survive this isle alone."
"You're saying he has the strength to survive it regardless... That brat's high-handed behavior is backed by the confidence that he can get away with it..."
"Tch, I don't like it, but it's none of our business, is it. If it's an unsettling brat we're talking about, the two of us are plenty as it is."

That was the impression the other members of the "party" had of the fake Cecilus.
It could be called harsh, but it could also be called natural. To begin with, unless one was strong enough to be in the safe zone, every sword slave was desperately preoccupied with just surviving today and tomorrow.
It was only natural that there was hardly any time to spare worrying about others.

"Well, I've got a complaint about Hiain treating us like unsettling brats, but Tanza, what do you think? About Sessy?"

"...It doesn't matter to me. More importantly, Schwarz-sama."

"Hm?"

"——Do you truly intend to leave this place, Schwarz-sama?"

Asked suddenly, in a voice somewhere between exasperated and accusing, Subaru couldn't help blurting out "Eh?" and turning around, eyes wide.
In the shared quarters given to sword slaves—more of a communal cell, really—Tanza, who shared the room with Subaru, knit her round brows and let out a pointed sigh.

"'Eh' is not an answer. These past few days, Schwarz-sama seems to have been enjoying yourself with all the sword slaves, but do you remember our original plan?"

"Original plan...?"

"——Leaving this island and returning to Chaosflame. That plan."

For a moment, at the sound of Subaru's voice showing no recollection, Tanza's own voice went stiff.
The girl then averted her eyes from Subaru and, prefacing it with "I'm sorry," said,

"I intended to follow Schwarz-sama's plan, if possible. But if Schwarz-sama isn't going to act on it, that's a different matter. I need to return to Yorna-sama's side."

"————"

"Even now, I have no idea what's become of Chaosflame since we left. I do know Yorna-sama is safe, at least, from the fact that her favor still lingers on me, but..."

As she spoke, Tanza touched her own right eye.
That gesture referred to the same change Subaru himself had witnessed among the residents of the demon capital—people who had come under the influence of Yorna's "Soul Marriage Technique," their eyes burning with that mark.
The way she'd severed the lion's neck in a single stroke wasn't Tanza's own natural strength, either, probably. It meant the power of Yorna's favor bestowed upon her was still active.

"So that means Yorna-san is safe."

"...Her body, yes. As for her heart, I don't know."

"That's, yeah, that makes sense. Yorna-san cared so much about everyone in the city."

Too kind for her own good—that was why she couldn't bring herself to follow the Empire's policies, and had been branded a traitor time and again as a result.
Subaru felt he understood, at least a little, both Yorna's circumstances and the feelings of Tanza and the others who had been protected by Yorna's convictions.
So he understood, too, Tanza's desire to go back to Yorna's side as soon as possible.

"But rushing too much is no good either. Flailing around blindly won't get you what you want."

"But! I can't afford to be as leisurely about this as Schwarz-sama!"

"L-leisurely..."

"That's right. These past two days, I thought you'd be walking the island searching for a way to escape, but instead you've been chatting idly with the 'party' members, laughing it up with other sword slaves..."

Tanza counted off Subaru's actions of the past two days on her fingers.
It was true that for the past two days, what Subaru had been doing wasn't searching for secret passages or figuring out how to operate the single drawbridge that connected to the outside world—it was mostly just talking to people.
Sometimes he spent time with Weitz and the others of the "party"; other times he prioritized picking up the island's rules and amusing stories from people who'd been on the Sword Slave Isle a long time.
To Tanza's eyes, that must have looked terribly leisurely—perhaps it even looked like Subaru was preparing to lay his bones to rest on this island.
But if that was how it looked, then——

"That's completely wrong, Tanza. I've got someone I want to see just as badly as you want to see Yorna-san. I absolutely have to go back."

"...Do you mean the people who were with you?"

"Besides Abel, yeah, that too, and others... there's someone at that huge walled city, too. And in the country next door to the Empire. I'm a busy man, you know."

"————"

Tanza fell silent at Subaru's answer, that he had people he wanted to see here, there, and everywhere.
Perhaps she thought that her own feeling of cherishing Yorna alone outweighed Subaru's, spread as it was across multiple people—but she didn't say it aloud.
That alone was enough to reveal how earnest she was. If it was Yorna who had taught her so much, then Yorna's dedication as a teacher deserved admiration too.

"Even so, I think I'm probably better at using my head."

"——? What do you mean by that..."

Just as Tanza tilted her head at Subaru's words—

"——Bassy! The drawbridge is going up! New arrivals incoming!"

The fake Cecilus came dashing down the corridor, shouting so loudly. His momentum and volume made Tanza's shoulders jump, but Subaru's reaction was the exact opposite.
"It's here!" he said, springing up on the spot.

"Faster than I expected—they come at that kind of pace?"

"No, no, that's why I said last time's 'Sparka' with you Bassy folks was an exception. Everyone except the three of you in the same 'party' failed to escape and ended up at the bottom of the lake... the shortage of personnel had to be hastily made up for by you all."

"Oh, right."

Just as the fake Cecilus said with a shrug, the previous "Sparka" had been an irregular occurrence.
Originally, the sword-slave candidates who were supposed to enter the Sword Slave Isle together with Weitz and the others had attempted a mass escape before entering the island, but it failed—the carriage overturned. Everyone who fled into the lake from the drawbridge ended up as food for the aquatic Witchbeasts, apparently.
As a result, the "Sparka" for Weitz's group, short on numbers, was postponed until enough people could be gathered to form a full "party"—

"And then me and Tanza showed up, and Weitz and the others must've been super disappointed, huh."

Because their numbers had been made up, they'd had no choice but to hold the "Sparka," and on top of that, the additions were Subaru and Tanza—and Tanza had been unconscious at the time.
In effect, it was a "Sparka" fought by four, with one shifty-eyed kid tacked on.
Surely, all three of them had thought it was the unluckiest day of their lives.

"Though it turned out to be the luckiest day of their lives, actually."

"Oh my, quite a confident-looking expression you've got there. So, so, Bassy, what will you do?"

"I'm gonna go watch the drawbridge. Though I'd like to talk to the people who're coming in from outside too."

"That might be a bit difficult. Unlike last time, this time the 'Sparka' is likely to start fairly quickly, so you might not get the chance to make contact."

"I see... that's a pain, but if that's how it is, I've got a way around it."

At Subaru's answer, given with a hand to his chin, the fake Cecilus's eyebrows shot up.
It was an easy reaction to read—interest, or perhaps favor, toward Subaru's answer. A personality type incapable of hiding anything at all, and yet an opponent one couldn't afford to let his guard down around.
There were plenty of people in the world, after all, who could kill you even while liking you a great deal.

The fake Cecilus's personality, as rumored around the island, was best approached the way one approached a bomb.
Of course, treating him too fearfully would only shorten the fuse, so finding just the right balance was the wise course.
Anyway——

"Finally looks like we're getting somewhere. Tanza, what'll you do?"

"N-no, wait, what do you mean? Are you and Segmunt-sama plotting something?"

"Plotting, how outrageous! I haven't the faintest idea what's going through Bassy's head, and even if I did, I couldn't say anything of substance about it. I was simply asked to let him know if the drawbridge looked like it was about to go up. Everything from here is inside Bassy's own head!"

"...Schwarz-sama?"

Realizing no information could be gotten from the fake Cecilus, who cheerfully flaunted his own ignorance, Tanza turned her gaze to Subaru instead. It was a look as though she were accusing him of keeping secrets, but Subaru hadn't meant to keep anything from her.
Though he supposed he had been moving fairly carefully.

"Anyway, let's talk while we head to watch the drawbridge. It'd be too stupid to miss it."

Trailing a Tanza who clearly had more to say and a cheerful fake Cecilus behind him, Subaru left the shared quarters and headed for the upper reaches of the island, where the drawbridge could be seen. Within the island, sword slaves were granted a fair amount of freedom of movement; unless one's day-to-day conduct was particularly egregious, no one's actions were restricted outside of sleeping hours and death matches.
There were nominal rules about bathing and mealtimes that had to be followed too, apparently, but it was worlds apart from the slave life or prison life Subaru had imagined.

At the center of the Sword Slave Isle stood the arena, which took in spectators from outside and served as the stage for grand, showy events. Because of this, the arena and its surroundings, being the parts exposed to outsiders' eyes, were decorated in a rather flashy, eye-catching style.
On the other hand, the rest of the space used for the sword slaves' daily lives and free time was plain—aside from the bare minimum facilities needed for living, the whole place had a general feel of "gray."

"But there's a healing room, and a library too, right?"

"They say books only started being brought in after Governor Gustav became lord of the island. Apparently, they've unexpectedly become a source of relaxation for everyone."

"Yeah, there were a ton of books. Wish I could've read them."

Nodding along to Tanza's story, Subaru scratched at his temple with a finger.
The characters of this world, which he'd supposedly studied, seemed to have been evicted early on from his shrunken brain, leaving him able to make out only a vague, fuzzy shape of them.
He remembered some of it dimly, so it wasn't impossible to decipher, if he tried hard enough—but reading a single page required the kind of mental effort you'd put into a treasure hunt.

"If Neechan ever found out about this, she'd chew me out something fierce..."

For whatever it was worth, the one who'd kept Subaru company the most during his studies of the written language here had been Neechan—Ram. So if she ever learned of his current sorry state, she'd surely be furious.
Even without that, he'd left Rem all alone. Once Ram found out about that, too, she'd surely be angry. And that was something Subaru wanted to be scolded for, if he was honest.

"Schwarz-sama?"

"——There are two walls between us and getting off this island. One of them is the drawbridge connecting the island to the far shore."

"...Yes, I'm aware of that."

A stage built atop the isle in the middle of the lake, for the purpose of running the arena.
The drawbridge—half from the island's side, half from the shore's side, requiring both sides to extend their halves before it could be crossed—was, troublesomely enough, usually kept raised so it couldn't be crossed.

"Which is why I keep telling you, it's normally lowered. It rises when it's being used."

"That, no matter how many times you tell me, it doesn't click. That's why I want to see the real thing."

The fake Cecilus spoke in the tone of someone explaining something to an unreasonable child, but in Subaru's mind, the image of a drawbridge was inevitably one of a single bridge split down the middle, with each side raising and pulling back its own half.
Naturally, in that image, the drawbridge was something you "lowered."

"So, Schwarz-sama, the second wall would be..."

Waving away the mental diagram in Subaru's head, Tanza pressed to hear the rest. At her words, Subaru gave a small "mm" of a breath before continuing,

"Goes without saying—the curse rune. The one Gustav-san put on every sword slave."

"————"

The corners of her round brows drooping, Tanza's expression turned troubled.
Naturally, this was a problem she'd understood without being told—the curse rune.

Every sword slave on the Sword Slave Isle was branded with a curse mark, a curse rune; break the rule bound to it, and it would activate, taking that person's life. That was the primary reason Gustav, as governor, continued to reign as absolute ruler of the Sword Slave Isle.

No one could defy Gustav, and to defy him meant losing one's life.
Even without defying him, so long as the curse mark wasn't undone, one never knew when a violation of the curse rule might claim one's life.
And how to undo it, along with the details of the curse rule itself, were known only to Gustav himself.

"What's scary is not knowing the detailed rules. Even living perfectly normally, you might accidentally violate something without noticing and just... die."

"Well, apparently there was that kind of anxiety at first, right when Gustav-san became governor, but lately that worry seems to have mostly faded. Especially since Gustav-san doesn't seem to want to needlessly reduce the number of sword slaves either, so he's clearly stated the things you absolutely must not do, which is reassuring, isn't it."

"Clearly stated... don't defy the guards, no disputes between sword slaves outside of death matches, don't try to escape the island on your own, that kind of thing, right?"

"That's right."

"——The last one's the problem, as I expected."

The system of rules for living on the Sword Slave Isle—that was the curse rule Gustav had laid down.
Still, it was a fact that it stood in direct conflict with Subaru and Tanza's objective.

"Besides the secret passage, there's also a way to have the curse rule undone and retire as a sword slave, right?"

"Yes, there is indeed. The famous one there is the grand annual event—the reward given during the duel held before His Majesty the Emperor's honored presence. Aside from that opportunity, there's also apparently a method where a wealthy spectator at a match pays a large sum to purchase you outright. Which is why sword slaves also need to make themselves look attractive, and put on a flashy, beautiful show."

"...Somehow, that matches the gladiator vibe I had in mind."

It did seem close to the image of gladiators from, say, the Roman Empire and the like.
He didn't know the details very well, but it carried the nuance of slaves forced to fight with swords, which, going by the characters used, was a position similar to that of a sword slave here.
He'd also heard that among famous gladiators, there were figures who had risen up in rebellion together with other gladiators—

"What I want isn't a rebellion, it's an escape, so more like a great prison-break kind of thing, huh."

The hint to work from would probably be the pattern of prison-escape stories.
Though the knowledge he had from watching those kinds of movies and manga was fairly hazy in Subaru's mind too. It probably couldn't be applied as-is anyway, so he'd just have to find his own correct answer.

"Either way, to get off this island, I need to do something about both the drawbridge and the curse rule. Which is exactly why observing the drawbridge is absolutely essential. Got it?"

"...Understood. Even so, that still doesn't explain why Schwarz-sama has been spending the time until the drawbridge moves so leisurely."

"Ugh, holding a grudge, huh..."

Told off by Tanza's narrow-eyed look, Subaru scratched his head with a troubled expression.
While they'd been talking, the three of them arrived at their destination—a raised platform. Midway up the slope, which grew mountainous toward the center of the isle, was a balcony built at a position where half the lake could be seen.
It was less a terrace and more of a watchtower-like spot, but there were already a scattering of other early arrivals besides Subaru's group of three. Other sword slaves, apparently also here to gawk.
Amid that crowd of onlookers, a figure who happened to turn around waved at Subaru's group.

"If it isn't Schwarz. What're you doing here?"

"Oh, Hiain's here too. Just here to gawk?"

"As if I'd be here to gawk! ...I heard the next sacrifices were coming, so, that's why."

Averting his eyes, unable to continue the sentence, was the lizard human Hiain.
A fellow member of the same "party" as Subaru, quite the coward beneath his blustering attitude. Not a bad person, but not exactly a praiseworthy character either.
Even now, his excuse didn't fully deny the gawking angle.

"Did you want to get a look at the people arriving next, Hiain-sama?"

"Guh..."

"If you'd rather not say, it's fine either way."

At Tanza's reserved question, Hiain twisted his big mouth, hesitating over an answer. But unable to bear the silence for long, he blurted, voice trembling, "Yeah, that's right!"
He set his elbows on the railing and glared down at the lake below,

"I wanted to see the faces of the guys coming next. Got a problem with that?"

"No problem, but why? If it's because you survived 'Sparka' so you wanted to check whether the next people look like they can survive it too, that'd be a pretty nasty personality."

"Like hell I'd do something that twisted! ...Though I guess there're people who would, huh."

Hiain denied Subaru's suspicion, insisting he wasn't like that.
Subaru chose to believe that answer, for now. He understood well enough that Hiain was just a coward, not a bad person. Also the one who seemed least suited to the rules of the Sword Slave Isle.

"...Why're you guys here anyway?"

"We're not here to gawk or rubberneck. Sure, I'm curious about the people being brought in this time too, but I want to see the drawbridge."

"You wanna see the bridge? What are you, a kid... you are a kid!"

"Well, yeah, I am. You're being loud."

Perhaps to cover for his own feelings, Hiain's voice was oddly loud, and Subaru twisted his lips at it. Then he lined up beside him and looked out over the lake below.

Even though it was still broad daylight, the area around the Sword Slave Isle was overcast, dim beneath cloudy skies.
For some reason, the clouds apparently never cleared around here, staying overcast year-round. Judging by the sky, it was a place designed to darken people's moods.

"Well, there are plenty of people who enjoy that kind of atmosphere too, so, different strokes."

"Surprising. I figured Sessy loved sunny days... though I guess, going by the name Raiko, clouds don't bother you either."

"Hm, never really thought about it, but now that you mention it..."

With a hand to his chin, the fake Cecilus's eyes lit up at this unexpected discovery, and Subaru shrugged it off. The group lined up at the railing and waited for the troublesome drawbridge to appear.
And before too much time had passed——

"O-oh-h-h——!"

At first, there came the sound of gears or some mechanism grinding somewhere.
Probably a mechanism similar to the one that raised the iron bars of the arena, but the scale of the drawbridge's was on a completely different level. After all, they had to construct an absurdly long bridge, so it must be turning correspondingly huge gears, or a great many of them.
Right before Subaru's eyes, the drawbridge slowly began to "rise"——

"————"

What slowly, slowly came into view was the drawbridge that had been sunk deep in the lake.
Just as the fake Cecilus had corrected him time and again, it moved in accordance with the rotating gear mechanism, coming into view by "rising" up out of the lake.
It wasn't a matter of lowering a raised drawbridge—it was "raising" a sunken one.

To be precise, it wasn't a single bridge rising, but multiple connected bridge segments surfacing from the bottom of the lake and joining into one. The segmented sections lifted horizontally, then locked together into a single unit as they drained a massive amount of water, completing the structure.
The same mechanism was operating on the shore side as well, and the drawbridge rose there too. The two risen drawbridges then joined as one, freeing the Sword Slave Isle from its isolation, if only temporarily.

"So who's operating that bridge..."

"There's apparently a tower that controls the drawbridge, so it's likely operated from inside there. I've never actually been inside, so I couldn't tell you what awaits."

"——Inside the control tower."

With a bridge of that scale, quietly raising and lowering it in secret would be difficult.
Then again, crossing the lake in something like a small boat was blocked by the presence of Witchbeasts in the water. To prepare a realistic means of escape, there was clearly still more thinking to be done.

"...It looks like that's the carriage carrying the next arrivals."

Beside the pondering Subaru, Tanza, looking toward the far shore, murmured that.
A great black Galewind Horse pulled a single carriage, slowly crossing the drawbridge. The Galewind Horse looked like an armored war-horse, and appeared quite tough at a glance.
Soldiers mounted on creatures smaller than the Galewind Horse guarded the carriage's flanks. Presumably out of concern over the overturned carriage last time, security seemed to have been tightened.

"Oh, that's Gustav-san. Looks like a personal welcome."

Meanwhile, on the island's side of the drawbridge, a group waited for the carriage's arrival at the bridgehead. Among the guards clad in black uniforms, the one puffing out an especially large chest was Gustav.
This, too, might have been out of caution over the previous carriage incident—or perhaps Gustav simply made a habit of personally overseeing this every time.

"A little over a kilometer to the far shore, less than two...?"

Given the distance, he couldn't say anything with certainty, but that was roughly how far the drawbridge looked.
Even with the weight distributed by the mechanism, a drawbridge of this length was beyond imagining. Even accounting for Magic or some special magical tool in the foundation, it was quite the feat.
As Subaru continued observing the state of the drawbridge in question——

"Ah——!?"

The voice that cracked like that belonged to Hiain, who had likewise been watching the drawbridge alongside Subaru's group.
——No, what he'd actually been watching wasn't the drawbridge, but the approaching carriage. The carriage pulled by the massive Galewind Horse finished crossing the drawbridge, and the people riding inside were brought down.
People being handed over to Gustav as sword slaves—or more precisely, as sword slave candidates. Seeing their faces, Hiain's eyes went wide.

"Those idiots... they got caught...!"

Covering his face with both hands, Hiain glared down below through the gaps between his webbed fingers.
Subaru, seeing the new faces, understood the meaning behind his words. Everyone who'd been brought in were lizard humans, like Hiain—judging by the difference in scale color, they were probably different subgroups, but broadly speaking, they fell into the same category.
And Hiain's reaction was the reaction of someone who recognized them.

"...After they used me as a decoy to run, too."

At Hiain's bitter mutter, Subaru narrowed his eyes.
During "Sparka," when he'd redone the loop over and over trying to get to know Hiain and the others who'd joined him, he'd heard the circumstances of how Hiain came to be brought to the Sword Slave Isle.
Hiain had said he was made to serve as a decoy, using his ability to change his scale color to blend in, and was used as a stepping stone to buy his companions time to escape from the slave traders.
Which meant, judging from Hiain's reaction, that these people were——

"The ones who used Hiain as a decoy."

"Guh...!"

"Hiain-sama..."

At Subaru's words, Hiain's expression changed, and Tanza watched him with concern. Tanza probably didn't have any particular fondness for him either, but there was likely some sympathy for someone she'd exchanged words with.
But Hiain snorted "Heh" at the two children's gazes.

"S-serves 'em right! Use someone as a tool and you end up looking stupid yourself! After making me suffer, look at 'em now... ridiculous!"

"Hmm."

"W-what, got a complaint or something!?"

At Hiain's twisted, spat-out words, the fake Cecilus closed one eye. Hiain, recklessly, snapped back at the reaction, and the fake Cecilus shook his head, "No, no,"

"No complaints at all. I was simply struck—and impressed, honestly—by what an utterly mediocre line for a defeated extra to say."

"A defeated extra, you say...?"

"What else would I call it? This is something I've thought for a while now—why is it that people who are so obviously bit players all go and say something so fitting of the role? Watch even a single picture-story and you'd see how foolish that is."

Before the trembling-voiced Hiain, the fake Cecilus brought his own hands together high above him.
With a dry clap, drawing the surrounding attention to himself, the fake Cecilus continued,

"Look around and see for yourself. I haven't exactly devoured every story ever told across every era, but if you read through any story that catches your eye, one after another, you'll find a great many roles in it. And the named characters act in ways befitting them, while the unnamed ones act in ways befitting their foolishness. And, surprisingly enough, this applies to reality outside picture-stories as well."

"W-what are you even talking about...?"

"The weak say weak-sounding things! The strong say strong-sounding things! The star of the show says cool things, while the bit players who aren't mutter things in voices too quiet to hear! Ah, how truly bizarre, how mysterious—don't you agree?"

"————"

"Why is it that everyone so readily demotes themselves to a bit part? Everyone is an actor who must live out their own life, after all. Though, of course, the leading role can only ever be me."

Keeping everyone's gaze fixed on himself, the fake Cecilus made a light, ringing sound with his sandaled foot, drawing attention not just visually but audibly as well.
And then——

"Perhaps you should think carefully before saying anything else. That line of yours—doesn't it sound a bit too much like the kind of small fry who dies immediately after?"

"——Guh"

Leaning his face in close, looked up at from directly below by the fake Cecilus, Hiain's throat made a sound.
Still smiling at Hiain's reaction, the fake Cecilus drew smoothly back. But Hiain seemed to have been frightened by the fake Cecilus's eyes, and before he knew it, his breathing had gone ragged.
Just like that, as if fleeing from the fake Cecilus, Hiain turned his back and——

"Hiain."

"What! Just leave it alone already! I only deal with you people during 'party' business and—"

"What about the people down there? Weren't they your companions?"

Calling out to stop Hiain as he tried to leave, Subaru asked that. At Subaru's words, Hiain let out a "Hah!" of a breath.

"I told you already! Those guys used me as a decoy, and then screwed up afterward too! Who cares what happens to idiots like that!"

"——But when you dropped your bag off the cliff, they shared their food with you, didn't they?"

Hearing Subaru's interrupting words, Hiain's eyes flew open with an "Ah."
To the stunned Hiain, Subaru continued, "And more than that."

"They pulled you along when you were running from bandits, and did the fire-starting for you when you couldn't manage it... maybe the last memory was a bad one, but—"

"————"

"It's too sad to think that last look on their faces is the sum total of who they are."

There's a saying that a person's true nature comes out in extreme situations.
What a ridiculous notion, Subaru wanted to say—cut it out with that kind of logic.
It's an absurd idea, that in some out-of-the-ordinary, hopeless situation, the one action a person happened to take defines the entirety of who they are.

If that were true, then were the things Hiain, Weitz, and Idra had done during "Sparka" supposed to be their true natures?
Cowardly, underhanded, a con artist—was that supposed to be the sum of who each of them was?
Wasn't it because there'd been a moment where they hadn't run, hadn't tried to get ahead by betraying the others, hadn't lied, and had cooperated with Subaru, that all of them had gotten to keep their lives, together?
So——

"If you talked to them, you might hear a different story."

Even the people Subaru loved dearly now, he hadn't loved from the very start.
He'd seen their ugly sides too. And still, Subaru wanted to keep loving all of them. And he didn't think that was something special to him alone.

"...Creepy little brat. Acting like you know everything about everything."

At Subaru's appeal, Hiain muttered that with something like hatred.
From his perspective, being told a story he'd never told Subaru must have felt deeply unpleasant. And yet, there was something on his face that came before the unease—

"Those guys can't get past 'Sparka.' So it's already hopeless."

Leaving just that behind, Hiain walked away from the platform this time without stopping.
But did he realize it? That answer just now wasn't Hiain's true feelings—it was nothing more than an excuse born of the fact that the situation left him cornered.
And once that situation changed, that excuse would no longer hold——

"Schwarz-sama, I feel for Hiain-sama and his acquaintances, but..."

Watching Hiain's retreating back, Tanza gently touched Subaru's sleeve. In her round eyes, sympathy wavered together with a sense of urgency.
Having seen the drawbridge, one of the obstacles they had to clear, her desire to escape this island had probably only grown stronger.
Of course, for Tanza, leaving this island mattered far, far more than Hiain's feelings did. Naturally, the same was true for Subaru as well.

Subaru, too, had things that properly mattered to him, things he wanted to prioritize.
There was no comparing Hiain to the people Subaru had met up until now.
But still——

"——Bassy, that's what they call the thorny path."

"Sessy..."

To Subaru, wrestling with a feeling that burned hot and bubbling in the depths of his chest, the fake Cecilus said that.
