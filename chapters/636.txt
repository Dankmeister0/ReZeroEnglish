Arc 8 Chapter 20: "Two Lights"

"—I've learned the enemy's identity. A failed creation of the 'Witch' who once ran rampant in the kingdom... a monster called Sphinx, the worst of the worst."

At the emergency meeting reconvened aboard the Coupled Dragon Carriages, Roswaal, having hurried back from the battlefield, swept his gaze over the assembled faces and explained as much.

Sphinx—that was the name of the fake Ryuzu that Subaru and the others had faced.

And what's more—

"You said she ran rampant in the kingdom—when was that, exactly?"

"—Roughly forty years ago. In the thick of the 'Demi-Human War.'"

"—!"

At Roswaal's answer, Subaru's eyes widened in shock. The "Demi-Human War" came up as a topic often enough, but that was always in the context of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, the stage of that civil war. To hear it brought up all the way in the Vollachia Empire was another matter entirely.

"According to the records, Sphinx was considered one of the three most dangerous figures on the demi-human side during the 'Demi-Human War.' Her deep mastery of magic is said to have raised the tragedy of that war by several degrees."

"I think I saw that name too, when I was studying the kingdom's history. And the other name Subaru mentioned... Valga Cromwell, right? That's one too, isn't it?"

"—...Yes. That name also matches one of the ones we were warned about."

Emilia and Julius, who had actually come face to face with the fake Ryuzu—Sphinx—and tasted her threat firsthand, brought up the other name that had come up on the spot as well. To the two of them, it must have seemed like Subaru had suddenly pulled that name out of nowhere, but from Sphinx's reaction afterward, they understood that she and the man behind that name weren't unconnected.

Though Subaru himself hadn't known that name belonged to someone tied to the "Demi-Human War."

"So basically, the enemy is this Sphinx and some guy called Valga Cromwell, and the ones who ran wild in the 'Demi-Human War' are running wild in the Empire too? That makes no sense..."

"That 'Demi-Human War' was more than forty years ago, too. Ucchi's studied kingdom history, and I know what the demi-human side claimed during the civil war, but... it doesn't quite fit with the current situation, if you ask me."

"Right? Anyway, I'm just glad Bea-ko and the others made it out okay, but..."

Subaru and Anastasia both tilted their heads at the illogic of the situation—these two, Sphinx and Valga, who had once rampaged through the kingdom, now rampaging through the Empire.

At this rate, it seemed like the last of the three feared in the civil war might be among the enemy too.

"That's not possible, I suppose. The last one... Libre Fermi's name, I confirmed it in the archives of the Pleiades Watchtower. He's undoubtedly counted among the dead, I suppose."

Beatrice, pressed close at Subaru's side, denied that particular worry of his.

Having literally flown back together with Roswaal, Beatrice seemed to have resolved, ever since hearing about the desperate situation Subaru and the others had fallen into, not to leave his side for even a moment.

For his part, sending Beatrice into a battlefield would have torn him apart, so he was fully prepared to accept her adorable resolve.

In any case—

"If there's a 'Book of the Dead' involved, that should be reassuring... I guess? Though the enemy's a zombie, so honestly it feels like there being one is just to be expected."

"If that's the case, it's a frightening thought. What we're dealing with may not be mere zombies, but the possibility exists that we could face historical heroes, or villains, whose names live on in the annals of history."

"Heroes of history, huh. ...You're not getting excited about this, are you?"

"Unfortunately, I've already faced Reid Astrea. I'm disciplining myself not to hold excessive expectations."

Subaru shot a doubtful look at Julius, who shrugged.

In fact, Julius's inner history nerd had been on full display back at the tower when the topic of Reid came up. He had properly settled things with Reid, too. He wasn't a man who'd fall behind because of it, but his claim to be able to keep his composure in front of a great figure was only half believable.

"This isn't the time for that. ...For now, Natsuki-san and the others managed to head off one of the enemy's trump cards before it could be played, but we're far from safe. If there's any good news, it's that Sphinx, who was likely a formidable foe, dropped out early, but..."

"Sorry to break it to ya, Otto-anii. Truth is, I can't say that for sure."

Crossing his arms and clacking his fangs, Garfiel spoke haltingly, and Otto's eyes went wide. Fixing his gaze on Otto, Garfiel—fresh from the battlefield, his whole body caked with dirt—gestured with both hands toward Beatrice and Roswaal.

"That Sphinx bastard showed up in front of me and Beatrice and them too, before this. Same damn face. Made me pissed off, like, don't screw around with me—"

"Well, of course. She had the same face as Ryuzu-san, after all. It must have been really, really hard for Garfiel."

"But even if the face looks the same, somethin' that ain't Granny ain't Granny. So I didn't hesitate none to throw down with her. The problem's what came after."

"What came after?"

Emilia tilted her head, and Garfiel nodded deeply. His usually fierce expression clouded over, rare for him, and he spoke with a sigh.

"—We straight up finished Sphinx off. Watched her shatter to pieces right in front of us, made damn sure it was a 'clean kill, no doubt about it.' And yet..."

"That was Sphinx's aim all along, I suppose."

Picking up where Garfiel's conclusion left off, Beatrice gripped Subaru's hand tightly. The grip was fierce enough to turn Subaru's captured fingers white, showing just how much of a shock that "aim" of Sphinx's had been to Beatrice too.

Narrowing his eyes at Beatrice's state, Roswaal continued, "Let me state the conclusion," and said:

"Sphinx certainly died in front of us, and in quick succession, died again in front of you all as well, Subaru-kun. From this we can draw a simple, obvious inference—Sphinx can die multiple times, and revive as a zombie each time."

"Wha—!"

"To pile the worst atop the worst, one could say that the next zombie, reborn after dying, retains knowledge of its own immediately preceding death, and the cause that brought it about. In other words, she's aware of exactly what stopped her scheme to blow up the Coupled Dragon Carriages—that it was you, Subaru-kun, and Halibel-dono."

"—————"

Roswaal's conjecture, though he added that it was purely speculation, was a terrifying one. But there were plenty of details that made Subaru nod in agreement. In the midst of that torrent of destructive light raining down, even as she herself was exposed to a blow that would reduce her to dust, Sphinx hadn't been the least bit flustered.

There are, indeed, people who remain unshaken even facing their own imminent death. But if Sphinx's composure came from the fact that she didn't consider "death" to be "death" at all, then somehow, it clicked for Subaru.

"It's almost like..."

—Return by Death, he thought, though he didn't say it aloud.

A ruinous attack that didn't regard the extinguishing of one's own life as a disadvantage, but rather wielded it as a weapon. What made it structurally different was that the fact of having died didn't disappear.

Thinking about it that way, it might actually be closer in nature to Petelgeuse's "Possession," which Subaru had fought once before, than to his own Return by Death.

"No matter how many times you beat her down, huh."

For a moment, Julius murmured that, and his gaze crossed with Subaru's.

Julius had probably drawn the same parallel between Sphinx and Petelgeuse's troublesomeness that Subaru had. Unlike Petelgeuse, Sphinx's ability didn't involve "possessing" others—he'd go ahead and dub this one "Death Flight," but—

"If no matter how many times you knock her down, you can't finish her off, then there's a set way to deal with that kind of enemy. Keep knocking her down until she runs out of extra lives. That's it."

"Extra lives? Um, that's..."

"The number of times left that she can come back. No matter how scary the enemy, there's no way she can revive infinitely, without limit. There's absolutely a limit. Right?"

It felt a bit rich coming from him, given his own situation, but extra lives are the kind of thing that run out eventually.

If they kept killing Sphinx until hers ran out, then even her cool composure—confident she had nothing to do with "death"—would surely fail to hold.

"So there's no need to stay down in the dumps about it. Instead, let's celebrate the fact that we broke her nose for her. Rem! Thanks a ton!"

"—! I-I didn't do anything that impressive."

Suddenly put on the spot, Rem, who had been sitting there looking rather out of place, shook her head vigorously side to side. But claiming she hadn't done anything impressive was modesty so characteristically her that it was almost unbearable.

Right after Returning by Death, Subaru, taking Emilia and Julius with him, had gotten off the Coupled Dragon Carriages, leaving a message with Rem, who remained inside. —To request the strongest of Karagi make an appearance.

"If Rem hadn't called for Halibel-san, right about now, me, Emilia-tan, Julius, and the whole Coupled Dragon Carriage would've all been dust together. If that had happened, Bea-ko, my grieving widow, would've become a nun who mourned me for all eternity."

"Don't just casually bring up the worst-case scenario I didn't even want to think about, so I made myself not think about it! If I died and left Betty behind, would I really become that kind of 'nun,' I wonder!"

"Mm, that's right. It's thanks to Rem that Beatrice didn't have to become a 'nun,' and that we're all able to talk like this, cheerful and well. Thank you."

"...Understood. I'll... take that as it is, then."

Told so by both a half-crying Beatrice and a smiling Emilia, Rem accepted their gratitude with a somewhat bashful reserve.

Feeling something well up in his chest anew at the sight of Emilia, Beatrice, and Rem all gathered together like this, Subaru turned his gaze to the tall figure standing quietly nearby.

"And of course, thank you too, Halibel-san. Honestly, whether we could do anything about that whole mess depended entirely on your name value, and that was the whole plan..."

"Ha ha ha, ain't nothin' wrong with bein' honest about it. Truth is, I was surprised myself, gettin' called out of nowhere by the little blue ogre kid and comin' out to see that. If I hadn't known about it, I'd have died too, wouldn't I? Makes me feel like I'm the one who got to keep his life, thanks to bein' told about it."

Laughing heartily like that, Halibel maintained an attitude wildly out of proportion to the feat he'd pulled off.

His laid-back tone lacked any sense of urgency, but without him, Subaru and the others' total annihilation would have been unavoidable. Choosing to call him in on the second attempt had been a good call on Subaru's part, but just imagining what would've happened if Halibel hadn't been aboard the Coupled Dragon Carriages in the first place was chilling.

In that short span of time, they would've had to gather every warrior riding the dragon carriages together and search out the best possible countermeasure. And even then, there would have been no guarantee a solution even existed—truly, all praise to the strongest of the city-state.

"He's sayin' all that, but how'd it actually go?"

"Mm, Julius and I really, really tried hard too, but I still think we would've come up just a little short. It was great, really great, that Anastasia-san had won Halibel-san over to our side."

"Is that so, is that so. Then it was worth the big bucks Ucchi shelled out to drag him along."

Whispering together, Emilia and Anastasia reassessed Halibel, who so lacked any sense of tension, and in any case, it was precisely because everyone had made their best move that they could now hold this post-mortem.

That was Subaru's takeaway from the recent, outrageous life-or-death struggle—and:

"That's the whole story of that ruckus just now. Long story short, all of us pulling together saved our lives. Obviously, there's something you ought to say, right?"

"Well done."

"You son of a—...!"

At the gracious words of appreciation from the Emperor, Abel, who had been quietly listening to the report up to this point, Natsuki Subaru trembled with his fists clenched in fervent frustration.

Naturally, Sphinx's "Death Flight" scheme against the Coupled Dragon Carriages was shared, without their even realizing they'd been made into stakeholders, with the Empire's leadership.

The Coupled Dragon Carriages should have been an extremely important piece of confidential machinery for the Vollachia Empire, and since a portion of that valuable equipment had been destroyed out of necessity, a concrete explanation was in order.

That said—

"After putting a stop to something on that scale, and all we get is a 'well done'..."

"What are you saying, Natsuki-dono!? To demand a reward greater than words of appreciation from His Excellency himself! Know shame, as a subject of the Empire His Excellency governs!!"

"Goz-san! You've got it wrong! Subaru isn't a child of the Empire, he's a child of the kingdom, same as us!"

"Emilia, your voice is getting loud again, dragged along with him, I suppose."

Unlike the maximally loyal imperial soldier, Abel's words of appreciation were, for Subaru, sorely lacking in reward.

Though gratitude from Emilia—her voice rising, dragged along by Goz—might perhaps have made up for Subaru's dissatisfaction.

"At present, we are on the road, driven from the capital and rebuilding our position. However much reward is sought in proportion to the labor rendered, I cannot issue an empty check. Hence, I have nothing to offer besides words."

"Brazenly telling me your wallet's empty...! Waaah, Anastasia-saaan!"

"There, there, no need to be so bitter about it. I'll properly wring Julius and Emilia-san's fair share out of the Empire for you, don't you worry."

"Yes! Squeeze 'em dry!"

At the reassuring reliability of Anastasia, whom he'd run crying to, Subaru threw both hands up, and Abel made a sour face before sighing. Then, saying "Now then," he steered the topic back:

"This undead woman in question... Sphinx, was it—is she the center of this 'Great Disaster'?"

"At the very least, the formula reviving this enormous number of zombies was very likely one she constructed. Modifying and improving upon an existing formula—that's exactly the sort of thing that failed creation would do."

"Roswaal, I know she's someone you dislike, but don't overuse ugly words."

At Roswaal's reply to Abel, Emilia's brows lifted slightly at his remark. Her gentle violet eyes turned stern as she stared straight at Roswaal.

"We're not here to trade insults, are we? Even when we've done something good, if we keep saying nasty things the whole time, no one's going to want to listen to how we really feel anymore."

"—...Yes. I'll take that to heart."

At Emilia's smiling remonstration, Roswaal bowed his head obediently, then gave a wry smile.

Even as the kindness at the core of her stayed unchanged, Subaru felt that Emilia's way of thinking kept refining itself. He hoped Roswaal felt the same thing.

And, setting aside such thoughts of Subaru's for the moment—

"I hear Sphinx also ran rampant during the kingdom's civil war. What became of that affair?"

"According to the kingdom's records, all three—Sphinx, Valga Cromwell, and Libre Fermi—were struck down before the civil war reached its conclusion. The loss of these three is remembered as having sealed the demi-human side's decline in the war."

"I see. —But surely an undead does not simply sprout from the earth without cause. Is it not the fault of you people of the kingdom that they now, as undead, ravage the lands of the Empire?"

"My, my, for someone of Emperor Vincent's stature to say something so strange, hmmm."

At Abel's question, one eye closed, Roswaal broke into a smile and shrugged. With one hand, he gestured to the scenery outside the window.

"I cannot claim to plumb the depths of the schemes of the person in question, but the fact that she is bringing calamity upon the Empire rather than the kingdom, whose memory holds her defeat, makes plain that some different design is at work now than back then. Given the forty years of silence since, it is hardly realistic to suppose the kingdom's records are simply mistaken."

Having rambled on so, Roswaal drew back the hand he'd used to indicate outside, saying "Although," and raised one finger from that returned hand.

"If there is someone who took part directly in the 'Demi-Human War,' who had the chance to strike down Sphinx before their very eyes and yet failed to do so, then as Your Excellency says, I do think that person ought to bear responsibility, hmmm."

"How trivial. To question the propriety of an old soldier who took part in the kingdom's civil war forty years ago and there decided the course of the battle—it isn't even worth debating."

"Well, that's true enough. Anyway, I'm relieved you're not the type to demand the impossible on that front... hm? What's wrong, Bea-ko, making such an unlovely face?"

"...Betty is always lovable, I suppose."

Readily conceding that Roswaal's point was reasonable, Abel withdrew his jab without hesitation. Such needling had probably become a habit of his, but it was a bad habit, one he ought to break—so Subaru thought, while beside him, for some reason, Beatrice wore a sullen expression.

Her being lovable was simply fact, but something in the exchange just now seemed to have snagged on Beatrice's nerves. Probably her expression here had something to do with Roswaal.

"Now then, what else did you learn. Having leapt out with such bold talk, if all you found was the same as what remained on the dragon carriages, that hardly counts as an achievement."

"For the record, it wasn't Betty and the others who talked big, it was Subaru, I suppose. But taking responsibility for the big talk Subaru talked might also be Betty's duty, I suppose."

"Of course there was a harvest, indeeed. Nothing so grand as to call it the nature of zombies as a whole, but there were several things we learned, hmmm."

Pressed by Abel, Beatrice and Roswaal answered so reliably. Subaru snapped his fingers at the two of them, saying "As expected," and:

"So, so? You figured out the zombies' weakness or something? If we know that, we won't have to be scared even if Sphinx shows up again."

"Natsuki-san, please don't get your hopes up too high. Even so, an achievement of that magnitude..."

"You're underestimating Betty and the others too much, I suppose. We did find out properly, I suppose."

"Wheeh!?"
"Seriously!?"

At Beatrice's words, chest puffed out, wearing a smug face, Subaru and Otto were stunned. Pleased with their reaction, Beatrice laughed, "Of course, I suppose," and continued.

"The hint was Garfiel's instinct, I suppose. Garfiel, who was raising hell on the battlefield before Betty and the others got there, had properly noticed something was off, I suppose."

"Though, if Beatrice and them hadn't shown up, I mighta just forgotten about the damn thing that was buggin' me."

"For someone with such excellent instincts to not trust his own instincts truly is a waste, I think. ...Oh my? I meant that as a compliment, I believe..."

"Whether it's a compliment or a put-down, ain't makin' me happy either way."

At Garfiel's sticking his tongue out in protest, Roswaal closed one eye and shrugged. Setting aside the two of them and their usual bickering, Beatrice continued.

"The oddity Garfiel noticed was a difference in the zombies' durability, I suppose. Some zombies went down from a single arrow, while others stayed lively even with ten arrows stuck in them. That's the difference, I suppose."

"But couldn't that just be because that 'zombie's' individual strength was different? Like, I'm stronger than Subaru, so that kind of difference could happen."

"That wasn't it, I suppose. The difference was on the scale between Subaru and Anastasia, yet the difference in arrows came out to about one versus ten, I suppose."

"Comparing me to that is honestly kinda cruel to poor Natsuki-kun, don't you think, hmm."

Giving a soft, wry smile, Anastasia glanced over at Subaru. But with Subaru currently in his diminished state, there was no room for rebuttal, and he simply waved a hand in response to her consideration.

Still, the oddity Beatrice mentioned was indeed something worth being curious about. And Beatrice and Roswaal claimed to have solved the mystery of that oddity's true nature.

The answer to that was—

"State your conclusion. What produced the difference among the undead?"

"—Insects, I suppose."

"Insects...?"

At the answer to Abel's question, Subaru frowned at its content.

Then Beatrice turned her gaze toward Roswaal and gave a nod. At that cue, Roswaal produced something from within his robes.

"Everyone, please be sufficiently careful. We have frozen it to halt its activity, but should the ice break, it's presumed a zombie would be born right here, hmmm."

"What are you talk— hey, hey, hey, what is that thing!?"

"Beatrice told you, didn't she? Insects. If we must name it, I suppose we could call it a 'Core Insect.'"

In Roswaal's hand, pinched between two fingers, was a small lump of ice, and inside that ice, about the size of a coin, sat something small and round and red.

Looking closer, it became clear it was a small round insect, something like a caterpillar—.

"This core insect lurks within the body of every single one of the undead. And this insect is precisely the zombies' lifeline... one might just as well call it their core."

"An insect as the core, meaning..."

"—I see. In other words, the true nature of the oddity Garfiel noticed was the difference in exactly what stage the arrow killed the core insect within the zombie's body, is that it?"

While Subaru remained lost in his shock at the impact of "insects," Julius arrived at that understanding. Hearing Julius's words, the others too came to grasp the reasoning and arrived at comprehension. Of course, Subaru too finally sensed the significance of the insect's existence, but—

"So then, this core insect is basically the zombie's heart?"

"Elucidating the formula itself is a task for going forward, but this core insect acquires information about its intended zombie target, then fashions a vessel out of earth to reproduce the original form... that is our conclusion."

"I suppose so."

Beatrice nodded, expressing no objection to Roswaal's conclusion. But faced with the two of them's conclusion, Subaru's jaw simply hung open, unable to close.

If they'd said zombies were being created through magic, he could accept that with a certain fantastical sensibility. But magical insects creating zombies—at that, revulsion won out over acceptance.

"Me, who as a kid loved nothing more than catching bugs... how pathetic..."

"I don't think that's the reflection we're looking for in this scene, you know. —In any case, our personnel have produced results. What say you, Your Imperial Majesty?"

"At the very least, it is proven you are not incompetents who speak grandly and produce nothing. That much I will concede. Well done."

"...That one, I think it might honestly be better if you just stopped saying it altogether."

Regardless of what people of the Empire thought, among people of the kingdom, Subaru included, that praise had a bad reputation.

In any case, just as Otto had bothered to put into words and press the point, even the stubborn, eccentric Abel had no choice but to acknowledge the results Beatrice and the others had found.

Inwardly, he was surely groaning "grrrrgh" to himself.

"You did great, as expected of my Bea-ko."

"Naturally, I suppose. This is the strength of Subaru's partner, I suppose. Quite different from a certain deer girl who's only shared a little bit of hardship with Subaru, I suppose."

"Don't get all prickly, get along with Tanza too, will you!? Emilia-tan said we shouldn't say mean things, didn't she!?"

Soothing Beatrice, who showed her rivalrous streak in odd places, Subaru promised a hundred head-pats to smooth things over, then turned his gaze back toward Abel and the others.

To Abel, who returned his gaze, Subaru puffed out his chest, saying "How about that," and:

"These are my reliable companions. Worth the big talk, wasn't it."

"I also acknowledge the achievement of preventing the earlier attack on the dragon carriages. Why is it you insist so on gloating over me. The results speak sufficiently for themselves through what the eye can see."

"Heh, whatever you say, it just sounds like sour grapes to me. I'm in a good mood right now."

At Abel's displeased response, Subaru snorted in high spirits. Emilia scolded him with a "no-no," but he couldn't hide his true feelings.

Then, perhaps feeling pressed by Subaru and the others' achievements, the Empire's leadership—Berstetz and Serena—exchanged some words, after which Abel was appealed to.

"Your Excellency, given that the people of the kingdom—our friends, the Margrave Mathers and his companions—have shown results of that caliber, will we not lose face unless we, too, can offer some useful answer?"

"Upper Sergeant Dracroy, why is it you seem so amused?"

"Amused, am I? My apologies, Your Excellency. I myself was unaware of any such feeling, but perhaps, faced with a situation where I must demonstrate the Empire's dignity or lose face, I feel my pride as a noble of the Empire being tested."

"—————"

Placing a hand upon her ample bosom as she answered so, Serena's face, marked with its white scar, twisted. Though her expression itself was not smiling, the tone of her voice and the color of her eyes were considerably good-humored. To maintain that composure against an emperor, in a situation that could hardly be called advantageous, spoke to no ordinary strength of spirit.

"Could it be that Serena-san is actually a pretty scary person?"

"Dunno if she's scary or not, but I heard she's Roswaal's buddy."

"Ahh~"

Seeking information about Serena, with whom he'd had little contact, one dropped bomb alone was enough to demolish the wall of his questions.

However, it was hard to tell either Roswaal or Serena to choose their friends more carefully. After all, it was plain as day that the fact Emilia and the others had been able to come all the way to the Empire searching for Subaru and the others owed something to the two of them's cross-border friendship.

Come to think of it, looking back, Roswaal had been quite brazen even at the Royal Selection venue.

Hearing that Serena, who held that attitude toward Abel of all people, was his friend—well, that explained a thing or two.

"I can say it now, but Natsuki-kun, you were pretty something back then too, weren't you?"

"Can't hear you, can't hear you, can't hear you..."

To Subaru, covering his ears and fleeing from an unpleasant past, the very person who had lectured him that "a screwed-up past doesn't disappear" now turned a lukewarmly indulgent gaze upon him, and it stabbed right through him.

And, keeping that same lukewarm gaze turned, with a boldness bordering on impudence, upon the Emperor himself, Serena continued some manner of persuasion.

"In any case, surely Your Excellency finds them useful as well. Which is precisely why, I—a woman such as myself—can't help but suspect there's a side to it in keeping them close at hand."

"Enough. Even without going so far as to spell it out so maliciously, I understand well enough on my own."

Snorting, Abel waved a hand to block Serena's gaze. Then, having erased what remained of some deliberation, he revealed something Serena had been prompting him toward.

That was—

"—In confronting this 'Great Disaster,' there is one who holds information worth debating whether it is useful. It would be well to hear that person's account before we arrive at the fortress city."

"Oh my, my, has the flow of things finally brought us around to listening to what I have to say? If so, why, I'm quite pleased, Your Excellency."

In one compartment of the dragon carriage, sturdily locked, the figure who could be said to have been kept under house arrest behind that door broke into an ingratiating smile at the sight of Abel appearing.

However, judging by the treatment he'd received under that confinement, it was hardly something to smile about.

After all, his entire body was bound round and round in chains, tied to a chair in a manner clearly meant to ensure he could never escape.

"Hey now, Abel, this is a bit much even considering..."

"A necessary measure. For better or worse, losing this man would constitute a significant loss on our end. I'll have you know—release his restraints, and he will readily go seeking death."

"Going to seek death, how absolutely dreadful! I have merely, all this time, striven to put a stop to this 'Great Disaster'... it's simply that, to that end, I hold no attachment even to my own life, you see."

"Oh boy..."

Grinning vacantly, the effeminate man rocked his body back and forth on the chair he was bound to.

Sensing something touched with madness in his manner of speaking, even Subaru found himself unable to help but believe Abel's assessment wasn't exaggeration. Still, it was suspicious through and through, but—

"This 'Stargazer'... Ubilk, was it? This is the man you're relying on?"

"Ahh, dear me, being doubted so wounds me deeply... oh! Now that I look closely, aren't you the kingdom's 'Stargazer' I passed by in Chaosflame!"

"Not even close. Oh! Wait, was it you? The one who put weird ideas about me being a 'Stargazer' into this guy's head! Thanks to you I ended up in a fistfight with him!"

At the careless remark of the effeminate man—Ubilk—whose face had suddenly lit up, Subaru snapped back. As far as Subaru was concerned, being treated as holding some suspicious position like "Stargazer" was a massive nuisance. Of course, given that the "Stargazer" was known to bear a role something like a prophet, he understood the risk that his existence, as one who Returns by Death, might invite that suspicion—but it was a massive nuisance regardless.

In any case—

"You and I aren't the same kind of thing. Get that straight, would you."

"Eh~? How strange. Why, I've heard that you are indeed the same, you know..."

"From the stars, was it? If so, Abel, this guy's not all that reliable."

When it comes to a star that teaches the wrong answer, unfortunately, that's not something one wants to rely on right now.

What mattered at this point was a star that could give reliable information, however trivial. Vague, could-mean-anything Barnum-effect-style prophecies were not what was wanted here.

So, having decided this was a disappointment and starting to turn his back on the man already—

"Wait, wait, wa-a-a-it, please! I understaaand! Fine, you're not a 'Stargazer'! I don't mind that at all!"

"That's a suspicious way to phrase it, but correcting that part doesn't exactly restore my trust in you..."

"I don't mind remaining locked up like this, as is. Only, please, just listen to what the stars have foretold. That's all I ask!"

"—————"

Twisting his body forward with great effort, Ubilk pleaded so, his eyes gleaming intensely. The force of it was such that the chains wound around his body bit in hard enough to draw blood without mercy.

Setting aside the pain and everything else, he was trying to convey some piece of information he claimed to have heard from the stars as a "Stargazer." If he could manage that, the wounds meant nothing to him. It even made one wonder whether perhaps even his life meant nothing to him.

"Abel, these 'Stargazer' people are..."

"Set aside that sentimentality. Judge whether it merits consideration after you have heard it."

At that overly intense attachment to the stars, Subaru felt an unpleasant sense of déjà vu. But Abel paid no heed to Subaru's thoughts, and fixed his gaze on Ubilk, who leaned further forward. Seeing Abel's posture of listening, Ubilk too stopped twisting about, and smiled with a calm face.

"Your Excellency, rest assured. However mighty this 'Great Disaster' may be, the stars are with you, Your Excellencyyy."

"A cruel star, one that draws no distinction over which Vincent Vollachia remains, is it. Don't make me laugh. —Speak. What is it you have to convey?"

"There are two things."

To Abel's question, arms crossed, Ubilk answered briefly so. At that answer, Subaru murmured "two" under his breath, and Abel silently urged him on.

Whether it was something worth relying on remained deeply uncertain, but at least this "Stargazer," said to have correctly predicted the very coming of the "Great Disaster" itself, was now, for the sake of the trembling Empire, about to deliver information.

That was—

"Two lights, to overturn the fury of the 'Great Disaster.' One is a girl who speaks no known tongue, brought along by a boy from the kingdom who is... not the 'Stargazer.'"

"...What?"

Glanced at briefly, Subaru's eyes went wide, staring blankly at the words that followed.

He'd only just been thinking it was unwise to swallow Ubilk's claims wholesale, but the words that followed seemed to hold no factor worth believing, of all things.

Because the condition Ubilk had just described matched only one single person.

And, in the brief moment before that shock could even fade, Ubilk continued.

With the same lips that had revealed one half of what he called the two lights, he now spoke of the remaining one.

That light was—

"—A beastman, most versed in curses in all this Empire, one of the Nine Divine Generals."

△▼△▼△▼△

Kicking off the ground, breath ragged, forcing his way through a mountain path thick with bare rock.

"You damn, damn, damn bastards...!"

Even the force behind the spat-out words was weak, and he was aware of his own exhaustion.

Running across open country, fighting without sleep or rest—these were things he'd done ever since childhood, just to survive on this Empire's soil.

Even so, being forced to maintain this level of tension for over ten days without food or drink was on an entirely different plane.

"—!"

At the smell of dirt mixed with something foreign sliding into his nostrils, he swung his limbs furiously.

He didn't bother with the pretense of aiming by sight or by sound. What he caught with this nose was more than enough to grasp the world. The enemy's position, their numbers, roughly their armament, too.

Because of that, he also knew the enemy surrounded him in the hundreds, and faced with the fact that no matter how much he whittled them down it made no difference, he found himself wanting to believe his nose had simply gone numb.

"You goddamn bastards!"

But he immediately blew that thought away and slammed the weapon he carried into the scent of earth.

One, two, three—the five limbs of the enemies who took the direct hit went flying to pieces, and the impact punched through to those beyond them, forcibly breaking through the encirclement, and he burst out from within it.

No matter how many times he struck them down, an endless stream of sickly-pale earthen dolls kept welling up without end.

Like some badly-made nightmare, they were, without question, hunting his life.

The army he'd commanded had been wiped out, and by the time he'd realized it, everything was already too late; doubting his own qualities as a "General," he'd kept running desperately, east and further east.

However much he tried to link up with anyone, that path alone had been thoroughly, completely blocked, and beyond going without food or drink, his last contact with a living human being had been when he parted ways with his adjutant.

If only, at the very least, his subordinates had survived, even a few of them.

"Tch—!"

The instant his thoughts strayed sideways, his reaction to an enemy's slashing attack came a beat too late.

He blocked the blow that grazed his shoulder with his pauldron, and, letting the impact dissipate, kicked off the ground of the mountain path. Rather than going down a narrow path thick with obstacles, sliding down a cliff face would be the better option, if anything.

Judging so, he ran down a nearly vertical, sheer cliff, accelerating in an attempt to shake off the pursuing enemies.

Whether he'd be cornered by their human-wave tactics either way, if he could just catch one breath, it would count for something.

Believing so, he ran, and ran, and ran—

"—hn, this smell is..."

Mixed in with earth, plants, and the faint scent of flowers, what tickled his nostrils was the smell of everyday life.

Sensing that it differed from the iron stench of his pursuers' weapons, his thoughts scattered in a thousand directions for a moment, but he immediately weighed it against his own condition and followed the scent of daily life.

He felt hesitation at the thought of dragging other people into this fight against this enemy.

But if he avoided it, he himself, suffering from hunger and thirst, would simply collapse from exhaustion. That would be the stupidest, most idiotic, most goddamn awful ending of all.

"...Damn it, a hidden village?"

Following the scent of everyday life, he made his way into a settlement isolated deep in the mountains.

In the Empire, there were no small number of people who abandoned their land to escape taxation, becoming bandits or hiding away in the mountains and forests. This settlement, too, was surely one such hidden village.

Ordinarily, as a "General" of the Empire, the existence of such a hidden village was not something he could overlook, but—

"Damn emergency situation! Never mind that now! More importantly..."

Sweeping his gaze across the whole settlement to see if anyone was around, finding no one within sight, he snorted through his nose.

The source of the everyday smell was this settlement, but what tickled his nostrils strongly wasn't a lingering scent—it was the smell, in real time, of someone currently present in this settlement.

Heading that way, he'd request cooperation from anyone capable of fighting.

Even a little time bought would let him recover his strength with water and food in the meantime.

Spurring on his nearly-withered heart with that thought, he made for a large building in the settlement, guided by the scent.

Reaching its entrance, he tried to burst inside.

"Hey! Whoever you are, lend me a hand against these damn bas—"

—In that instant, two blasts of wind flashed violently to meet him.

"Damn close call!!"

Instinctively tilting his neck and hips, he dodged it, only belatedly recognizing that what had been unleashed was a sharp slashing blow.

Having dived in seeking help only to nearly be killed in return, he bared his fangs and glared at whoever had pulled such a damn ridiculous stunt.

Whereupon—

"Ohh? Well, well, how peculiar! To think, when I tried testing whether he was a dead man or a woman, a living person came leaping in instead...! Redhead! Might this wager be mine to win, I wonder!"

"Shut up, shut your mouth, drop dead. Winning, losing, who gives a damn..."

Within his glaring field of view, what he'd leapt into was the inside of a spacious building, where, gathered around a round table set within it, sat two middle-aged men—men, moreover, positively steeped in the reek of alcohol.

Each holding, respectively, a katana and an arming sword, as he took in their drunken state, a surge of rising fury swept away, for the moment, both hunger and thirst—

"You bastards, don't you dare be sittin' around drowning yourselves in damn booze while the Empire's in danger, you damn idiots!!"

And so it was that the "Curse Artisan" Groovy Gumlet let out a thoroughly righteous shout.
