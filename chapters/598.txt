Arc 7 Chapter 93: "The Army of Stone"

An arrow pierced a head that had no eyes, no nose, no ears, and the stone-made doll reeled sharply backward.
But a head with no human-like features apparently couldn't serve as a human-like vital point either — whether struck by an arrow or missing part of its skull, the advancing enemy's gait showed no hesitation.

"Just smashing the heads ain't enough! ...Holly!"

"I know that, noh!"

The stone dolls surged forward in a swarm, arms outstretched.
Figures no different in size from humans, closing in with movements utterly inhuman — the sight stirred up revulsion. Kuna poured that resistance into the fingers drawing back her bowstring, and the mighty bow let out a sound like thunder.
Three arrows loosed at once struck a stone doll's torso — not embedding, not piercing, but shattering the torso to pieces and halting the doll's movement.

Once a body was broken into fragments, even a stone doll couldn't move anymore.
But—

"There's no way to aim at 'em one by one, dammit!"

The target wall, the gap in the enemy's formation they'd been told was the Third Vertex — Kuna lifted her face, trying somehow to reach it, and felt despair at the sheer number and nature of the enemy troops arrayed there.
Word was that guarding the Third Vertex was one of the Nine Divine Generals, a man named Moguro Hagane.
Fortunately, perhaps, Moguro himself was nowhere to be seen on the wall. Whether that was because they hadn't yet reached him, or because he was stationed elsewhere, who could say.
Either way—

"It's just like ants swarming honey on a tree, noh! There's no way we can beat all of these, noh!"

"No time for whining! Shoot, shoot, keep shooting! You'll hit something no matter what!"

"Waaah! Kuna's so strict, noh!"

Letting out something like a scream, Holly pulled arrows from the quiver on Kuna's back, nocking them one after another and loosing them from the mighty bow. Each shot destroyed two or three stone dolls, but it wasn't nearly enough to keep pace with the enemy's numbers.
That was because the stone dolls numbered in the hundreds, the thousands — too many to count.

"Tch, what the hell are we supposed to do...!"

Clicking her tongue, Kuna put distance between herself and the enemy, cursing Abel for the orders he'd given.
She only knew it by rumor, but Moguro Hagane was a special kind of Demi-Human called a Steelfolk. Unlike the Weaponkin, who metalize only part of their bodies, this was supposedly a being whose entire body was made of mineral.
If that was so, were all these countless stone dolls before her eyes that very Steelfolk?

"None of them seem alive at all, noh! They really do look just like dolls, noh!"

"Same here! What the hell kind of trick is this?!"

Keeping a measured distance, Kuna glared around at the swarm of stone dolls. Perhaps helped by the fact that they had no eyes, the stone dolls gave off no sense of self-awareness at all.
They simply surrounded anything that approached, beat it down with their hard limbs and monstrous strength, and clubbed it to death. Those who couldn't escape, who were caught and exposed to the violence of numbers, died horribly.
The rebels who had challenged this Third Vertex and been trampled under the stone dolls' feet had, quite literally, had their corpses walked over — beyond recognition, somewhere behind the enemy's ranks. And that was not something Kuna and the others could laugh off as someone else's problem.

At this rate, left to the enemy's numbers, they'd be trampled and unable to fight back.
Before that happened—

"If it looks bad, we gotta pull back—"

"Ah! Kuna! Look, noh, over there!"

"Hah?"

Kicking through the grass of the farmland turned battlefield, the two of them were forced to shoot even as they retreated. In the midst of that, Kuna turned at Holly's cry, and something astonishing leapt into her sight.
A lone figure stood unmoving on the battlefield, and stone dolls swarmed toward it, slamming down arms made of rock. It looked certain the figure would take the blow, fall, and collapse into a pool of blood—

"—Dolls made of stone, don't get in my way!!"

The one who roared that was Mizelda, her fierce beauty twisted with fury.
Bellowing, she swung the clubs gripped in both hands, smashing the heads and upper bodies of the stone dolls that leapt at her, cutting them down in turn.

—No, she wasn't the only one who chose to attack the stone dolls rather than flee.

"Out of my way, out of my way, out of my way! You disgraces of the Empire, too blind to see the glory of the Emperor you ought to revere — don't you dare stand in my way!"

A slash launched alongside a rasping shout cleanly severed a stone doll's head from its torso, and split the flying head further in midair. If that wasn't enough, he'd cut a torso diagonally, sever an arm at the shoulder, and once a thing stopped moving, kick it down and move on to the next.
The rough-looking, eyepatch-wearing man threw himself at the stone dolls just as Mizelda did.

The merciless close-combat power of these two blunted the stone dolls' momentum.
But what truly left Kuna and Holly speechless was neither of them.

"——"

A mighty sword roared, and the stone doll it swept aside was sent flying with a thunderous crash.
It could hardly be called elegant — a rough, violent slash. But for dealing with stone dolls that needed to be broken to be stopped, it was, if anything, the most rational swordsmanship imaginable.
Wielding it, cutting down the surging swarm of stone dolls one after another, was a red-haired swordsman — the only thing Kuna knew about him was that he was the arrogant, haughty Priscilla's attendant. His name was Heinkel.

The sight of this ghost-faced man, who had joined their battle line at some point without anyone noticing, cutting down the voiceless stone dolls one after another had an unreality to it, like a nightmare glimpsed on a sleepless night.
Looking only at what was happening, it should have been something for Kuna and the others to welcome — and yet dark, swirling misgivings made her unsure whether she ought to welcome it gladly at all.
The reason, surely, lay in Heinkel's expression as he swung his sword — the look on his face.

"He looks so pained, noh."

Kuna couldn't agree with the way Holly, seeing the same thing, put it.
She understood what Holly meant well enough, but to Kuna's eyes it looked more direct than that — like the face of a man who wanted to die.

Sometimes there were warriors who went to the battlefield seeking a place to die.
Even among the Shudrak, someone whose years were running short might take up a bow and choose their end by challenging some great beast deep in the forest.
It wasn't an ending Kuna herself would choose, probably, but she could understand that kind of thinking.

But what Heinkel had was different from a warrior seeking a place to die.
Wanting to die, and yet afraid of death, and resisting death with everything he had — the sight of it stirred nothing but a mounting ache. Even knowing his presence was propping up their battle line to their advantage, Kuna found herself wishing he would just fall and die, right now.
That was the kind of man he was, swinging his sword wrapped in an aura dark enough to make those around him feel that way.

"Kuna! Holly!"

And then, heedless of what was passing through their minds, the battlefield's mood shifted once more.
Calling their two names, Taritta came dashing across the grassland as if flying. Quiver on her back, dressed once more in the garb befitting the People of Shudrak, she reached them and said,

"It's dangerous to stop moving here. The path Sister and Jamal carved—"

"Don't need to be told, chief. ...Just got a little distracted for a sec, is all."

"By Heinkel, was it?"

Taritta, narrowing her eyes, hit the mark precisely on Kuna's awkward unease.
Mizelda's younger sister, who had taken over as chief of the People of Shudrak. She had once been withdrawn and indecisive, a girl whose most notable trait was less caution than outright timidity.
But it seemed the journey to the cursed city of Chaosflame — the trial she'd undergone as a condition of inheriting the role of chief — had left a deep mark on her; the timidity had faded, replaced by a supple kind of strength.
Her remark just now was likely part of that too.

"Doesn't the chief find it unsettling, noh?"

"'Unsettling' feels too direct a word, but... it's true he fights desperately, and it's true he's useful in a fight. I have no objection to fighting alongside him."

"Now that's a bold answer. ...I think that's the right way to look at it too."

There were things that couldn't be neatly resolved, but there was no need to resolve them this instant.
To begin with, whatever she was like in everyday life, Taritta had always been quick to shift her mindset when it came to the hunt. Now, as chief, she was managing to make that shift smoothly no matter the situation.
As Kuna was appraising her that way, Taritta glanced at the quiver on Kuna's back.

"Your arrows have thinned out quite a bit, noh. When you get the chance, collect the ones that have fallen. It's best not to use too many arrows on a single one."

"That's the theory, sure."

"They just won't die in one shot, noh!"

"There's a trick to dropping one in a single shot. Aim for the heart. ——Like this."

Before the two of them, struggling, Taritta nocked an arrow to her own bow and fired three shots in quick succession. Far off, three stone dolls closing in on Mizelda's back had their backs, heads, and thighs pierced through and were shot dead.

"Wait, wait, waaait! Why'd that kill it in one shot?! Heart?"

"It's a doll made of stone, noh — we don't even know where a heart would be, noh."

"Er, is that so? If you look carefully, I think you can tell where the important-looking spot is, though..."

Pressed by Kuna and Holly, Taritta's brow drooped in a troubled expression.
From the look of it, she really hadn't done anything more than look carefully. Their personalities were opposites and hard to read side by side, but Taritta, who decided things by feel, was every bit Mizelda's sister after all.
If anything, as one of the People of Shudrak, Kuna felt a certain pride in how purely the chief sisters embodied their line.

"Ain't much use as a lesson, so we'll just do it our own way. Pick up arrows, use clubs or whatever else if we run short. Kill the enemy dead."

"No more playing with dolls, noh!"

"That's the spirit. Though — somewhere out there, the real deal, one of the Nine Divine Generals, is bound to show up. ...Yorna was like that too — the Nine Divine Generals are on another level entirely. Be very careful."

"Ain't that somethin' you oughta be tellin' Mizelda?"

At Taritta's grave-faced remark, Kuna jerked her chin toward Mizelda, rampaging on the front line.
The former chief, one leg now a prosthetic, had handed over her position to her sister and was tearing through the enemy with a freedom in her step; the way she was going, if one of the Nine Divine Generals did appear from the enemy ranks, she'd guaranteed be the first to clash with it.
Even missing a leg, Mizelda would surely put up a good fight against any great foe—

"Sister, don't overextend yourself! Alone, you'll die!"

"——"

Just in front of Mizelda, Taritta shot straight through the head of a stone doll blocking the path, shouting as she did. At Taritta's voice, Mizelda gave a broad wave of her hand in reply — message received.
At that judgment and that remark from Taritta, Kuna felt genuine surprise, genuine admiration.

Taritta really had stopped hiding behind her sister's back.

"Back when that whole thing with Mariuli happened, couldn't even watch her, noh..."

"We're so happy Taritta's grown so fine, noh."

"Is now really the time?! You two, fight as well!!"

As Holly clubbed away the stone dolls closing in around them with her bow, and Kuna smashed them with her short hatchet, Taritta's voice sharpened as she called out over their remarks.
At their dependable chief's order, Kuna and Holly nodded to each other and obeyed without hesitation.

An old covenant — a promise once exchanged between a bygone Vollachian Emperor and the People of Shudrak, one now distant to Kuna and the others living in the present day, a promise no one could blame them for having forgotten.
The battle that had begun to fulfill it had, nonetheless, become — for Kuna and Holly too, now, as members of the People of Shudrak — a battle they had to win.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


A doll of stone, charging straight ahead.
No face, no hostility, no killing intent. Swinging his sword against such an opponent, cursing the nerve of whatever doll-maker had so carelessly chosen a human shape for them, Heinkel Astrea faced the battlefield.

Why, on this foreign soil of the Empire, was he swinging a sword?
Why, after baring such shame, was he still able to hold a blade at all?
Why, in a land where the one person he needed to show himself being useful to wasn't even present, was he—

"—!"

He slashed upward through an outstretched arm at the elbow, the aftershock of the blow sending the rest of the torso flying.
His opponent was a stone doll; since severing a head or an arm did nothing to strip it of combat power, what was needed wasn't refined swordsmanship but a barbaric blow capable of driving the thing into total incapacitation.

He shifted his grip on the sword, planting his feet wide apart.
Swinging the blade roughly from a low stance, he sent the stone doll in his path flying apart in a way that would almost have been amusing. It wasn't amusing. Nothing here was amusing at all.

He couldn't remember a time swinging a sword had ever been fun.
The sword had always, for him, been nothing but a shackle heavier than it looked.

"Damn it."

Spitting curses, he struck down anything that came within reach of his blade.
His mind was crammed full of nothing but the question "why," and swinging the sword to blast that question out of the edges of his vision, he swung. Swung. Swung, swung, swung, swung, hacked away—

"Damn it."

*Go empty-minded* — that was what he'd always been taught, drilling with the sword.
Heighten your focus, empty your mind, become one with the blade, and your technique will sharpen. — No matter how many times he'd heard it, it was a teaching he'd never understood.

"Damn it."

Told not to think, and trying to put that into practice, the very act of thinking "don't think" meant he was thinking — so how could he possibly go empty-minded?
How was a human being even supposed to focus completely on nothing but the sword?

If you're alive, your stomach growls. You breathe. Somewhere on your body might itch, drowsiness might come over you. Worries never run dry, and somewhere in the back of your mind there's always your family. Not just worry about tomorrow, but worry about ten seconds from now, endlessly — and not just ten seconds ago, but yesterday's failures, and the ones before that, forever gnawed over. Piling up, on and on, endlessly, thought never runs out.

How was anyone supposed to become empty-minded?
Completely halting those ordinary thoughts that arise from simply being human — that itself seemed like no human feat at all. If that was so, then wasn't a swordsman capable of true emptiness not human?
Which meant — wasn't he, then, no swordsman at all?

"Damn it all!"

The endless flood of curses, no matter how much he let spill out, never emptied from his head, never vanished.
As if to sweep it all away in one stroke, Heinkel swung his sword recklessly, turning the stone dolls standing in his way into heaps of rubble.

What value could there possibly be in any of this?
Failing to do what should be done when it should be done, leaving someone else to clean up his mess just to curry their favor, and in the end having his life saved, leaving him an unpayable debt to another.
Repeating a thing like that, over and over — what could it possibly repay, and how much?

"'Fraid I dunno what's weighin' on you, old man."

Amid his own head full of curses, mixed in among them, he heard someone else's voice.
A voice still young, one he might even call green — he'd heard it while drunk up in the watchtower, hadn't he. A boy's voice, barging in without an ounce of tact, even as Heinkel had shut himself away as a clear sign he wanted to talk to no one.

"Dunno how bad you screwed up, or what. But screwin' up — yeah, I got experience with that too."

Even after he'd brushed the boy off once with some throwaway line, the boy kept showing up.
And despite having a face that made it clear he was carrying his own doubts, he had the gall to stick his nose into Heinkel's troubles as if he had any right to.
And what he said was some absurdly childish, cut-rate idealism, the kind of thing that made you want to sneer.

"Only you can settle the score for what you screwed up yourself. So if there's anythin' this old man can do—"

"Shut up, you damn brat!!"

Encouragement or comfort, pity or sympathy — whichever it was, he didn't care.
Everything aimed his way was just an irritation; there was nothing he wanted from it. Nothing at all he wanted from anyone else. There was nothing he wanted. There was only someone he wanted to be given something by.
Value only existed in what could be received from that one person. Everything else, everything from anyone else, was nothing but deadweight to be dragged along.

"—!"

A stone doll crept up soundlessly; he brought his blade down on it, splitting two of them clean down the middle in one stroke. Sensing what it must have thought was an opening in his overextended swing, one leapt in from the side — he smashed its face in with the tip of his scabbard, then skewered and killed the one behind it with his beloved sword, Astrea, which he'd drawn back to hand.

Faceless opponents, movements that were nothing but programmed motion — that kind of thing, he could cut down endlessly.
As long as his breath held out, he could take ten, twenty of them. But what did it mean?
Racking up a score like this, with no one worth showing it to watching — what was the point—

"—If there's anythin' this old man can do, it's makin' up for it with your own sword."

"God damn it..."

It was almost laughable, still clinging to some flame that might or might not still be flickering.
Once he started down that line of thought, he'd run headlong into the question of why he was even still alive.
What did he want to prove with this sword? What did he want to obtain? What was it he wanted?

"——"

With the main threat now a matter of sheer numbers, the others around him began breaking through one after another as well.
Standing out among them were Heinkel; the club-wielding woman of the hunting tribe; and the eyepatched dual-wielder whose elegant swordwork didn't match his face at all. But the hunting tribe, wielding mostly bows, pushed the battle line forward boldly under their chief's orders, and the other rebels, who had once fallen back, were each regaining their momentum.

It was, as they'd said, truly a gap in the enemy's defensive line.
Compared to the other four vertices, this one alone was clearly weaker in defense. Heinkel being able to cut his way through was proof enough of it.
If even one — just one — genuinely powerful fighter were holding this ground, there was no way Heinkel would be running out ahead like this.

"After him — chase that back!"
"Don't let the red-haired man take all the glory! We're right behind you!"
"Not bad swordsmanship. Shave that beard and you'd be worth looking at."

For so many voices to rise up, unwilling to fall behind the man running at the front — that shouldn't have been possible.
There was no time to spare, letting his heart be captured by some fleeting illusion.

"Damn it... come on, more of you! At this rate, it's not even enough to—"

*count for anything*, he thought, lifting his face to face the situation before him with harsher, more clear-eyed reckoning.
A row of stone dolls lined up directly ahead; he blew the formation apart with a single horizontal slash and put strength into his knees, ready to leap toward the wall now coming into view.
If he clung to that wall and wiped out the enemy atop it, would that count as taking the Third Vertex — not, clearly, having felled some named enemy commander himself, but at least having contributed?

Would that be enough to string together, however tenuously, a spider's thread toward what Heinkel wanted from Priscilla — some way to make up for his disgrace at that walled city?
If only a little, if only the smallest amount—

"—You. Kill most. Me."

"——"

Just as he was throwing himself recklessly at the wall, a voice struck Heinkel.
There was a sensation of his lungs seizing, and a rasping breath leaked from his throat. He hadn't been attacked, nor had an attack been blocked — he had simply been spoken to.
And that alone was enough to make Heinkel's entire body freeze.

Everything that had been passing through his mind until a moment before — the business about going empty-minded, about what he wanted, about making up for his failure — all of it was painted over white, vanishing from sight.
All he understood was that he had run into something. Something before which hands and feet were useless. A threat.
And that was—

"Most. Me. Kill. So. I. Kill. You. Too."

A voice betraying no emotion echoed out, and before Heinkel's eyes, the high, thick wall began to change.
Not the stone dolls lined up atop the wall.
Not the stone dolls deployed to guard the wall, either.
The change occurred in the wall itself — the thick wall at the star-shaped fortress's vertex, the one they called the Third Vertex.

"—Ah."

In Heinkel's field of vision, breath leaking from him, countless points of "light" bloomed across the wall, filling it left and right. —No, whether to call them "light" at all was a matter of some debate.
The "light" that had appeared on the wall were fist-sized spheres, glowing a bright green — at a glance, they seemed harmless, nothing to feel threatened by.

But they hadn't been part of the wall to begin with.
On a wall that had held nothing, countless spherical "lights" had suddenly sprouted. To Heinkel, they looked exactly like living eyes. It felt as though he had met the gaze of all of them at once.
It felt, in terror, as though every one of those "lights" had glared at him all at once.

"H—"

In that instant, Heinkel's whole body froze, the strength draining from his grip on his sword—

"Blown away."

In that same instant, at odds with the flat, toneless voice, an unbelievable roar and a violent gale erupted as a massive stone fist caught the airborne Heinkel and sent him hurtling upward.

"Gah—"

Struck evenly across his entire body from head to toe, Heinkel was blasted into the sky, utterly helpless.
Scattering blood as he flew, struck in a manner fitting the very word "kicked aside" — the same thing he himself had just been doing to the stone dolls — Heinkel saw it.
In his spinning field of view, the scene on the ground transformed entirely. It wasn't a hallucination born of his fading consciousness as he was blown through the air — it was real, undeniable change—

"—Nine Divine Generals' Eight, Moguro Hagane."

The proper etiquette of a warrior on the battlefield, a bold declaration of one's own name — but the scale of what accompanied it was far, far beyond the ordinary.

"——"

The star-shaped wall they were meant to conquer — the Third Vertex itself — was moving.
That was the true form of one of the Nine Divine Generals, the mighty being who had named himself Moguro Hagane.
