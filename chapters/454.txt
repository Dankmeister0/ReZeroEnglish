Arc 6 Chapter 49: "To the Failure That Is You"

Slowly, slowly, there's a sensation like numbness draining from his arm.
Averting his eyes from the healing beneath his sleeve, Subaru sensed that it was the claw marks Meili had left in her death throes, slowly but surely closing up and disappearing.

"...Pretty impressive, actually, this room."

He'd been told that the spirit living in this ivy-covered room could heal the wounds of any creature inside it. Even so, having never once visited the room with an external injury until now, Subaru had had his doubts about just how effective it really was.
Now, actually experiencing the sensation of a wound healing like this, he was forced to reflect that his doubts had been a petty, lowborn suspicion.

*What a shame about your dying message—how mean of you, mister.*

"――――"

*But it's a good thing Beatrice-chan wasn't sharp enough to catch it, isn't it? Thanks to that, the wound on your hand—the one piece of hard evidence—gets to just disappear~*

The girl's phantom voice prattling on endlessly inside his head was grating.
What galled Subaru was that he couldn't say for certain the girl's words *didn't* reflect his own true feelings—she cut straight to the heart of things with unsettling precision.

Whether this girl's phantom was a runaway part of Subaru himself, or a literal ghost that had latched onto him from reading the *Book of the Dead*, he had no way of knowing.

What he did know was this: whatever the truth of it, he must not listen to this girl's words. He should not listen. That was the one fact he clung to, tinged with a creeping sense of urgency.

So Subaru made a conscious effort to shut out the ceaseless auditory hallucination.
But the more he retreated into his own shell like that, the more gleefully the phantom multiplied her words, working her wiles to toy with him.
The worst of it being—

*—She's not with you right now, is she, Beatrice-chan? No one around to get in the way, so why not just finish off that sleeping girl while you can~?*

"――っ"

*Fufu... don't make such a face like you're about to cry. You try to ignore me and just can't quite manage it—you really are an adorable one, aren't you, mister.*

The unseen girl's voice sounded to Subaru as though it were whispered right into his ear.
If he closed his eyes, he could almost picture her leaning gently against his back, breathing sweet nothings into his ear along with a sweet exhale.

Brought to this spirit room for rest and peace, Subaru now found himself confronted, once again, with the same choice he'd faced while holding the *Book of the Dead*.
Beatrice, who had brought him here, had gone to tell Emilia and the others that she was stepping away from the Taygeta archive. And so, for this brief moment of freedom with no one watching, before Subaru lay a girl sleeping on, like an offering, on a bed of ivy—

"...Rem."

Even speaking the unconscious girl's name aloud, no particular feeling stirred within him.
She was a girl he knew only by name, without even a memory of exchanging words with her. What he knew of her: that she was Ram's twin younger sister, that she was one of Emilia's companions, and that they had come to this tower seeking a way to wake her from her long sleep.

—He wanted to know more than that.

*There's a way, isn't there. All that's left is whether you do it or not~*

The girl's sweet temptation, whether he liked it or not, called the *Book of the Dead* plan back to mind.
Even sunk now in deep sleep, this girl must have had some connection to Subaru back when she was awake. That connection—and what feelings she'd held toward him while interacting with him—could be known, if he borrowed the power of the *Book of the Dead*.

More than that, she was a being whose life could be plucked away even more easily than Meili's had been.
A girl in a comatose state—one could suffocate her just by laying a wet cloth over her face. Quickly, quickly, quickly, quickly, quickly—

"...Am I an idiot. No—I am an idiot."

Reining in the surging sense of urgency with his own hand, Subaru corrected his idiotic train of thought.
What good would it do to act so hastily? Even if he leapt at the situation dangling in front of him like a starving man drooling over food, it would mean nothing if nothing came after.

Killing was just the process. What Subaru wanted was the result—their true feelings.
To rush the process at the risk of jeopardizing that result would be putting the cart before the horse in the worst way.

Hadn't he thought this through already, back in the Taygeta archive?
If he were to actually carry out the *Book of the Dead* plan, he needed to carefully choose the order in which he whittled people down.

"Julius, Emilia, Shaula, Ram, Echidna, Beatrice, Rem..."

Counting off on his fingers, Subaru listed the people in the tower excluding Meili—that is, the targets from whom he'd want to obtain the *Book of the Dead* if possible, and the order of priority for doing so.
Presumably, this was the order that would cause the least interference with carrying out the *Book of the Dead* plan.
Conversely, unless he had a plan for whittling them down in this order, acting rashly out of impatience would never work out. —He refused to allow himself to fail.

*Otherwise it'll all be for nothing, won't it—killed for no reason. Scary~*

"――――"

He kept silent against the phantom's glib remark. He sensed the phantom's displeasure at being brushed off.
But no matter what she said, his attitude wouldn't change. It came down to the same thing.

Subaru didn't want to kill Emilia and the others.
And yet, if it came down to having no choice but to kill them, he wanted that occasion to happen only once.
Just once—once was enough. All he needed was a perfect plan to make that possible.

"――――"

As he let out a deep breath, a yellow gaze pierced toward him.
Looking over, he found it was the eyes of the jet-black Earth Dragon, Patrasche, present in this spirit room in a different capacity from the sleeping girl.

Patrasche's gaze, directed at the haggard Subaru, seemed—perhaps it was just his imagination—to carry a trace of worry and concern.
Perhaps this was the effect of Beatrice's parting words before stepping away for a moment: "Ronsuru's asked that you keep a good eye on Subaru, I suppose." No wait—she'd said, "I want you to watch over Subaru carefully, I suppose."
As long as Patrasche's eyes remained watchful, no matter how much the urgency drove him, he couldn't bring himself to commit violence against the sleeping girl. In that respect, he wanted to thank Patrasche's presence for sparing him from a rash decision.

And yet, even toward Patrasche—toward whom he should have felt an unrivaled, singular affection—Subaru, having seen the *Book of the Dead*, found himself less and less able to extend simple, honest trust.

"...Is this your doing too, all according to plan, 'Natsuki Subaru'?"

The more he wished to trust, and the more he thought about where that true intent lay, the more Natsuki Subaru found himself isolated in this alien world with nowhere to belong.
Was that itself the vicious game that wicked "Natsuki Subaru" had laid out for him?

"――――"

Slowly, the scratches Meili had left were healing.
Savoring the fading sensation of that pain, Subaru rolled up his sleeve and dug his nails into the scab-covered wound where "Natsuki Subaru Was Here" had been painfully carved.

The claw marks from his own—no, from the other him who wasn't quite himself—that grotesque crime, he didn't mind if those disappeared.
But the message hinting at the existence of that other, not-quite-himself self—that, he would not let disappear.

"This message too, over here."

Digging into the wound on his left arm, he now dug his nails into his right arm in the same way.
Along the arm, still unfamiliar with its black, mottled markings, was a wound that hadn't been there before. It was a wound Subaru himself had carved, like a reply to the message on his left arm.
Inscribed there, it read:

—"What are you?"—Natsuki Subaru's earnest, dead-serious question, carved as a wound.

"――――"

And so, while sparing no thought for his own flesh as he wounded it, Subaru failed to notice something else at the same time.

"――っ"

He failed to notice that Patrasche was watching his self-harm with a pained expression.
He failed to notice that even if the wound Meili had left disappeared, examining Meili's own hands would make it obvious she had desperately resisted the one who tried to kill her.
He failed to notice that the murder of Meili, which he believed he had perfectly concealed, rested on a foundation of appallingly sloppy cleanup.

Just one gear slipping out of alignment, and the entire foundation would collapse.
Standing atop such a clockwork tower built on thin ice, averting his eyes from the root of the problem while desperately flailing to keep his balance—that was the ridiculous clown Subaru had become.

But that ridiculous clown's performance would, for now, reach its curtain call undisturbed.
Because—

Fortunately, Meili Portroute's corpse was "nowhere to be found."

"――――"

At the dinner table, the air hanging between everyone gathered was terribly heavy.
The main cause of it was that Meili—whose condition was dangerous, whom nearly everyone had half-accepted was already beyond saving—had not been found despite a desperate search.

It was hardly enough people to call a human wave search, but even so, they'd mobilized every hand they could spare and spent nearly half a day on it, only to come up empty.
It was only natural that a sense of futility mounted, along with tremendous stress.

"Ever since we got to this tower, it's been one dead end after another."

"...Way to say out loud what everyone was thinking but keeping to themselves."

Gnawing on something like dry bread for dinner, Subaru glared at Ram, who'd voiced a comment that only deepened everyone's fatigue.
At his accusing look, Ram shrugged, her expression unreadable. But even from her composed manner, the color of exhaustion hadn't quite drained away.
Nor was that limited to Ram—everyone in the tower was in the same state.

"Just in case, I went and asked Reid too, but he said he hadn't seen her. Said no one's come by since yesterday and he's bored... I don't think he was lying. Meili had been watching Reid too, so I don't think she'd go see him alone."

"Even that unruly man—hard to imagine him hurting a girl that age... though the fact that I can't quite say that with certainty is the scary part, isn't it. But Betty agrees with Emilia's thinking, I suppose."

Emilia and Beatrice exchanged words about the second floor's guardian, the red-haired, eyepatch-wearing man.
For Subaru too, that man was nothing but a source of painful memories, but Emilia, who'd gone to question him, seemed to have made it back safely without being hurt.
Feeling a flicker of relief at that, Subaru then shook his head.

He had no reason—no right, even before that—to feel relief over something like this.
Even Emilia herself might not have the right to have her safety be a relief to Subaru.

"—May I say something? I'd like to propose something, fully prepared to be called cold-blooded for it."

Having drained her flavorless soup, filling her stomach just enough by the end of dinner, it was Echidna who raised her hand to speak.
Narrowing her eyes, she turned her pale blue-green gaze on everyone present.

"That girl, Meili's search... I think we should call it off as of today, and go back to focusing on conquering the tower starting tomorrow. What do you all think?"

"—っ! No way! We don't even know how Meili's feeling right now!"

"She no longer has a mind to think or a heart to feel—that much the *Book of the Dead* Natsuki-kun confirmed already proves. I'm opposed to any further searching."

Against Echidna's proposal—one that could be called either realistic or coldly detached—Emilia was the first to push back. But Emilia's objection was purely emotional, and Echidna's expression didn't waver.
However, in place of Emilia, who found herself at a loss for words, Beatrice cut in with a "Hold on a moment, I suppose."

"On the surface, your argument sounds reasonable enough, I suppose. But it also sounds awfully sudden, doesn't it. Why are you so eager to cut the search short?"

"—Is it really so strange? The food we've brought into this tower is limited, and the longer our stay drags on, the more of a burden it places on the people back home in both our camps. If the schedule stretches out significantly, people might well be pulled away to search for us instead."

"Naturally. Emilia-sama and Anastasia-sama—though the contents differ right now—are both persons of noble standing for the sake of the Royal Selection. They shouldn't be in a desert tower like this, should they."

Starting with Echidna's proposal, Beatrice and Ram began to display a somewhat chilly attitude.
A proposal grounded in rationality had become kindling for even colder opinions, and tension mounted steadily in the atmosphere of the dinner gathering.

"Man, this feels kinda nasty, doesn't it~. I don't really care either way, but if you're gonna fight, do it somewhere that's got nothing to do with me and Master. I'm gonna go build a happy little household with just me and Master. First a girl, then two boys, then a manservant, that's the dream."

Subaru had no leeway to respond to Shaula's glib remark as she stuck her tongue out at the tense atmosphere and slid her rear over next to him. Her attitude back in the archive, in that last scene, weighed on his mind too.
What had Shaula, forcing that choice on him in that final moment, wanted him to say? If she was the type to call him Master and show every sign of adoring him, then how far—
—How far would she go along with whatever Subaru ordered?

"That's enough arguing."

It was Julius, his fine features etched with anguish, who cut through the hostile atmosphere.
He put out an arm in front of Echidna, then gave a nod of acknowledgment to Beatrice and Ram.

"Beatrice-sama, Lady Ram. I apologize for Echidna's careless remark, on behalf of our camp. However, I ask that you not misunderstand—she didn't make that proposal for no reason, based on rationality alone."

"Julius, stop it. That story is—"

"Emilia-sama's group has already, in effect, lost the girl who traveled with you. We've been shown your wound. In that case, we owe you the courtesy of showing good faith in return."

At Julius's sincere manner of speaking, Echidna held back the rest of what she'd been about to say. In response, Julius turned once more to face Beatrice and the others.

"At present, Anastasia-sama's body is being used as an Anchor for the spirit Echidna. It's true that we've found no way for Anastasia-sama to wake while in that state... but there's another problem besides that. —Echidna is sustaining her activity by consuming Anastasia-sama's Od."

"Od, meaning... so this whole time, she's been draining that? Ever since Anastasia-san stopped being able to wake up, this whole time, it's been like that?"

"...I see. So that's why you're in such a hurry to conquer the tower and borrow the 'Sage's' wisdom."

At the secret about Echidna revealed by Julius, Emilia and the others all showed surprise in unison.
Having her inner circumstances exposed by one of her own, Echidna, the party concerned, shrugged as if to say "well, what can you do," and said,

"No use trying to dress it up now. Julius is right. I don't think it's a good thing that I'm draining Ana's none-too-abundant Od just by existing like this. If I could, I'd want to give Ana her body back even one second sooner."

"Even if it means giving up a human body you're free to use?"

"Even if I can freely use Ana's flesh, my own heart is bound, unfree. I don't know how much you'll believe me for saying this, but..."

There, Echidna paused, held the beat a moment, then continued.

"Properly speaking, the being that ought to occupy a Vessel should be the being that belongs there. Borrowing only the outer shell, without the substance to match, and the seams show. It becomes unnatural. —That's a hideously grotesque thing."

"—ッ!"

Eyes lowered, continuing as if cursing her own self, Echidna's remark struck an unexpected, piercing blow to the heart of Subaru, who had merely been listening idly to the conversation.
Borrowing only the outer shell, without the substance to match. —That pierced his heart with a terrible, crushing weight.

"Anyway, that's why I want to rush conquering this tower. If you still can't quite believe me, have Beatrice investigate this body herself. The extreme drain on Od, and just how distorted I am as a spirit—she should be able to tell right away."

Unaware of Subaru's inner turmoil, unaware of the wound her unwitting words had carved into him, Echidna entrusted the proof of her own position to someone else.
Indeed, when the named Beatrice took hold of Echidna's arm and, by whatever principle, probed her body, Echidna's words seemed to be confirmed as true.
And receiving that result—

"...Understood. I get it now, about Echidna and Anastasia-san's difficult situation. And that we have to hurry and climb the tower."

"I don't know if this will bring any comfort, but if this tower really does hold the secret to the wisdom of the so-called omniscient 'Sage,' then it might also reveal the whereabouts of the girl who's gone missing. I'm well aware how cowardly it is to put it that way."

"No, thank you. It's much better than having no hope at all. —You were thinking of me, and of Meili too, weren't you?"

"...I wonder. Maybe I just care about my own and Ana's well-being."

At Emilia showing a willingness to accept her argument, Echidna averted her eyes, looking rather sheepish.
Watching this, Emilia narrowed her violet eyes, then drew a breath and declared once more.

"I'm really worried about Meili. But I understand what Echidna's saying too. So starting tomorrow, let's do what we properly can to get up the tower again. Of course, I'm still going to keep looking for Meili as much as I can, but..."

"If that ends up interfering with conquering the tower, that would be putting the cart before the horse, Emilia-sama."

"I know. —I just have to think for myself, each time, about what matters most."

Hand to her cheek, Emilia held a strong resolve in her eyes as she chastened herself.
Then she turned to look at Subaru, who had been observing the situation from a distance. For a moment, he was cowed by the intensity of her gaze, but the words Emilia continued with weren't anything like an accusation.

"Is that okay with you too, Subaru?"

"Th-that's, I think that's fine. That way Meili can rest easy too... no, I mean, I think she'd want us to move forward... no, that's not it either—wait, why are you asking me specifically?"

"Because, Subaru, you read Meili's book, didn't you? Seeing how you reacted right after reading it... I figured you must be the one most worried about Meili, out of all of us."

At Emilia's gentle press for confirmation, Subaru's breath caught.
Looking around, he realized Emilia wasn't the only one watching him. Beatrice, Ram, Echidna, Julius—everyone was looking his way.

He couldn't puzzle out what intent lay behind those gazes.
Unable to work it out, Subaru moved his lips, following the cowardly instinct within him.

"—I am worried. But I don't think Meili would want us to stand still either."

Probably, the most hollow, glib words in the entire world, spoken for no other reason than to be spoken.


※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—Deep in the night, Subaru finally began to act, in the free time that had finally come his way.

"――――"

With the dinner gathering over and tomorrow's course of action settled, the group decided each of them would rack their brains for conquering the second floor, and set about preparing bedding to get through the night.

It was the night of the day one of their companions had met with an unforeseen tragedy.
Naturally, it was recommended that everyone gather their sleeping arrangements into one place and sleep together in a single spot. And so, though makeshift partitions were set up with blankets, the night arrived with men and women all gathered in one room.

Only Subaru, alone among them, insisted on sleeping in the spirit room.

The aftereffects of reading the *Book of the Dead*—that was his official justification.
In truth, since everyone had witnessed just how much Subaru's condition had changed right after reading the *Book of the Dead*, his request drew little pushback. Of course, Beatrice showed particularly strong reluctance about letting Subaru sleep alone, but the spirit room had a capacity limit.
Yielding to Subaru, who claimed his head felt heavy in a trembling voice, Beatrice withdrew her objection.

That was less a triumph of Subaru's acting skill than the simple fact that his complexion genuinely looked terrible.
Honestly, Subaru was aware himself that it was a wonder he hadn't collapsed already, plagued as he was by an absolutely rock-bottom condition.

*"I don't think Meili would want us to stand still either." —What an actor.*

"――――"

*Ufufu, don't be mad. It's not sarcasm. I mean it—I'm complimenting you for real.*

She'd been fairly quiet during the dinner gathering, but apparently Subaru's hollow declaration at the end had struck her funny bone just right, because the phantom, breaking her silence, gleefully replayed that scene over and over.
The trouble with an auditory hallucination as an opponent was that covering his ears did nothing, and even refusing to listen brought no relief—her sweet voice grated on him like nails on a chalkboard.

*So, tonight the blue-haired sister's neck is on hold, is it~?*

"――――"

Having waited patiently for the night to grow late before setting into motion, Subaru found the phantom overlapping her suggestion onto the presence of the sleeping beauty sharing his room.
It was the same temptation from the phantom as a few hours ago, but Subaru's answer to it was the same as it had been then, too.

"It's not this girl's turn yet."

So saying, Subaru left the room without so much as a glance at the sleeping beauty's face.
As he did, he briefly met eyes with Patrasche, who had opened her eyes just a crack, but he got past it with nothing more than a finger to his lips, gesturing for her to keep quiet.
He had no idea how much of his intent got through to a lizard, but if anything, Patrasche felt like a creature even smarter than a dog or a horse. He hoped she'd keep his secret.

"――――"

The reason Subaru, having arranged in advance to be alone, set out in the dead of night—it was not to take the lives of his sleeping companions and set the *Book of the Dead* plan into motion.
Of course, he had considered that option thoroughly as well. To realize the plan given Subaru's actual capabilities, a surprise attack would be ideal, and striking while they slept could well be called the optimal choice.
But it was still too soon to carry that out. That was a last resort, an act against human decency to be saved for when he had no other cards left to choose from.

So then, for what purpose had Subaru set out to act in the night?
It was—

*—Are you going to do something about my corpse~?*

Watching the path Subaru walked, the girl's voice asked as though standing right behind him.
Subaru didn't answer, but his footsteps themselves were as good as an affirmative answer.

—That he was acting in order to dispose of Meili's remains, which he had hidden.

"――――"

Whether to call it a strange twist of fate or a stroke of luck, that was simply all it was.
That Emilia and the others' search today had failed to find Meili's remains was not a matter of divine providence but rather a shift of fortune that should be called a devil's own scheming.

Looking back on it, the holes in his impromptu cover-up job for the sudden turn of events stood out starkly, and it was enough to make him want to curse his own poor wit.
It just happened not to be found. He'd been saved by that. But going forward, while the group focused on conquering the tower, there was nothing strange about something that just happened not to be found suddenly just happening to be found.

At the very least, Emilia surely hadn't given up on searching for Meili.
For a girl of such boundless, hardship-free optimism, it was easy enough to imagine her continuing to search for the missing girl until her heart was decisively broken.

That was why Subaru needed peace of mind.
Without peace of mind, there was no foundation to build upon. Without a foundation built, there was no framework for the castle of the future to be raised atop it. Without the framework raised, the future could never be completed.

For Natsuki Subaru's peace of mind, the existence of Meili Portroute was an obstacle.

*With your head being messed with the way it is right now, even more so~ Something like that, isn't it?*

He turned a deaf ear. He'd just clearly declared her an obstacle. He refused, flatly.

And so, evading the eyes of the night, Subaru arrived at the room where he'd hidden Meili's remains.
It was an area lined with similar-looking rooms, but discoloration on the stone wall right near the entrance served as an easy landmark for this particular one.

"Honestly, this leaves a bad taste in my mouth, but..."

Best would be to carry her out from the tower's entrance to the desert and bury her in the sand.
Leave a body in the building for several days and decomposition would set in. The temperature wasn't especially high or low, but a body whose life activities had already stopped couldn't avoid decaying.

If he truly wished to never lay eyes on it again, the best option would have been to push himself, however unreasonably, to carry it far from the tower and leave it as food for whatever Witchbeasts prowled outside.
But just imagining that pricked at his conscience. Even so, that was too unreasonable a judgment to make.

"――――"

Deep in the dim room sat a square block of stone, and behind it, the remains were hidden.
It was an appallingly childish way to hide something—impossible to miss if you deliberately looked. Feeling pathetic about his own state of panic at the time, Subaru circled around to the back of the stone block.

There, for the first time in roughly half a day, Meili's remains should have been—

"—Huh?"

A voice slipped out involuntarily.
His mind refused to process the sight before him.

Behind the stone block, there was nothing.
The girl's remains—hands folded over her chest and eyes closed, at least as much consideration as he could manage, the pale neck marred by a ghastly blue-black bruise, a wretched sight to behold—were nowhere to be found.

"Why... it should be, right here..."

It should have been there.
He couldn't have gotten the room wrong. He hadn't made such a careless mistake, coming here.
He hadn't. And if he hadn't, then where had the body—the one that had to be here—gone?

—Where had Meili Portroute's remains vanished to?



"—Sneaking around at this hour, looking for something, are you, Barusu?"

"—っ!？"

Stiffening in shock at the voice from behind him, Subaru turned around, trembling.
In the field of vision of his pale-faced self, in the entrance of the not-very-spacious room, stood a single silhouette.

Pink hair kept short, sharp and rational pale-pink eyes, a dignified yet lovely face gazing coldly back at him—the girl standing there with her arms wrapped around herself possessed an almost fiercely striking beauty.
Harboring that wildly out-of-place thought even as he stood frozen, the girl—Ram—narrowed her eyes and said, in a voice utterly devoid of warmth:


"Or perhaps I should call you a fake. A failure—of Barusu's, of Natsuki Subaru's."
