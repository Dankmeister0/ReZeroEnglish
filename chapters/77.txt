Arc 3 Chapter 1: "A Peaceful Early Morning"

"—This way, this way, it is! Hurry up, or they'll catch us!"

"I-I know, I know, just, hang on a sec—!"

Dragged along by the arm, they tore down a footpath of treacherous ground.
A narrow alley. Even in the mid-afternoon, sunlight failed to reach it, and the faintly dim road formed a tangled defile, as if refusing entry to the two fugitives fleeing into it.

For Subaru, with no sense of the local geography, this situation was less than ideal.
The least he could hope for was that the person leading him actually knew where they were going, but—

"We keep plunging deeper into the dark—do you even know the way!?"

"—I don't!"

The reply came back with a volume almost refreshing in its bluntness, and Subaru pressed a hand to his forehead in despair, muttering "Figured as much..."
It was the answer he'd expected. After all, the attire of the person pulling him along by the hand was so gaudy it seemed utterly unsuited to the air of a back alley like this.

A fluttering red dress of what looked like expensive material. A necklace that scattered the light in glittering refractions. The skin peeking through kept a white, lustrous beauty that could only belong to someone raised accordingly.
And above all, the vivid orange of her hair asserted, all on its own, both the nobility of her presence and a lively bearing utterly out of place in a slum like this.

Taking another good look at her appearance, Subaru reconfirmed that he'd saddled himself with a walking headache.
Behind them, he could hear multiple sets of footsteps in pursuit—there was no situation here that afforded him the luxury of stopping to catch his breath and think things through calmly.

How in the world had it come to this—

Gasping for air, wincing at the ache in his lungs, Subaru's mind was consumed entirely by that one question.

—The chain of events leading to Subaru's question traced back roughly two days.


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—For Natsuki Subaru, morning was, by definition, time for sleeping, and certainly not a time of day for vigorous activity.

Saying he'd *melt* under the gentle morning sun might be an exaggeration, but there was, as a matter of fact, a real sensation of the Natsuki Subaru DNA composing his body dying off little by little.
For a Subaru like that, the reality of standing in the early morning sunlight in front of a crowd, doing radio calisthenics, was a state of affairs he couldn't have imagined even a mere month ago.

—Though for that matter, being summoned to another world in the first place was already outside the realm of expectation.

It was the kind of transformation you'd only expect from a serious blow to the head. And in fact, he did have memories of taking hard enough hits to the skull that a bit of a personality shift wouldn't be so strange—so he couldn't entirely rule it out.
Still, even accounting for all that, the truth was he didn't feel all that unhappy about his current circumstances.

"Alright, last one—arms up to the sky and finish—VICTORY!"

"VICTORY!!"

As Subaru raised both hands to deliver the closing line, a chorus of voices followed suit, and radio calisthenics came to an end. Amid the rising cheers, Subaru gave a light wipe to the sweat beading on his forehead and looked out ahead.
Straight ahead—gathered around Subaru—were the residents of the village nearest to Roswaal Manor. Roughly half the village, by the look of it, had turned out.

He'd started radio calisthenics on a whim, but it had apparently struck an unexpectedly deep chord with the people of this world. It had since become something of a full-blown movement within the village, and Subaru—known for never sticking with anything for more than three days—now found himself unable to quit even if he wanted to.
Mind you—

"Subaru, we finished the exercises!" "Stamp, stamp it for us!" "P'tato! P'tato!"

"Gaah! Don't all swarm me first thing in the morning, you're too loud! Seriously, you kids have way too much energy, ain't youth grand! The stamps aren't going anywhere, so quit rushing—line up, line up!"

Confronted by the children crowding in on him with easy familiarity, Subaru's expression settled into a distinct wry grin as he humored them. But there wasn't a trace of genuine refusal in it.
Following his instructions, the children lined up neatly in a straight row in front of Subaru. Watching them settle into place, Subaru nodded in satisfaction, then turned around.

"So, Emilia-tan, if you would."

"—Yes, yes, here you go."

Emilia, who'd been standing right behind him, handed Subaru a small pouch, her lips curving into an expression somewhere between a wry smile and a genuine one.
Opening the handed-over bundle revealed a raw potato carved down to roughly the size of a maki roll. At the appearance of the slender potato, the children let out excited squeals, and Subaru raised the potato as if in answer to their high-pitched cheers.

"Alright, here we go—stamping time. Papers out, papers out!"

He dipped the tip of the potato into the ink container that had been packed in the pouch along with it, then pressed the tip against the paper each lined-up child held out. As the image that emerged brought delighted looks to the children's faces, Emilia spoke up.

"Carving the flat side of a potato—a potato stamp. You really do come up with the strangest things, Subaru."

"Scrape off the inked surface and it doubles as emergency rations, and it turns out these little pictures come out with more style than you'd think. This one's today's masterpiece, by the way—'Monday Puck.'"

What emerged pressed into the paper was a deformed little cat, somehow exuding a faint air of ennui. It captured the likeness well enough that anyone who knew the model would recognize it at a glance. Not that it was much of a challenge, given he'd just named it after the subject outright.

"...I'm genuinely stunned. That looks exactly like Puck. You're actually good at drawing, Subaru."

"Well, all that time I squandered practicing drawing back home is finally paying off. Though I gave up partway through, so it's basics only."

Every self-respecting otaku walks the road of artistic training at least once. Whether you go on to establish your own style and make a name for yourself as an illustrator depends on which way the wind blows. For the record, it hadn't worked out for Subaru.

"About the extent of what I'm good for is carving cat pictures into potatoes in a world that doesn't even have potato stamps as a custom, and getting fussed over by adorable little kids for it."

"That came out sounding weirdly bitter... Also, doesn't this Puck in the drawing seem like he's a little short on energy too?"

"Told you, didn't I—'Monday Puck.' I was going for that sense of melancholy, like, 'ugh, another week starting up...' This droopy ear here is the key detail."

Subaru expounded passionately on his labor of love in response to Emilia's comment. All the while, he kept pressing the potato stamp onto the papers held out by the pestering children, building up their proof of attendance for the radio calisthenics.
It was Subaru's own little playful twist on the summer stamp rally tradition. Since Subaru carved a new stamp every night before bed, plenty of the children looked forward to seeing what picture would show up each day.
He was a man who displayed his manual dexterity in the oddest of places, drawing out the childlike wonder in everyone around him.

After chatting a while longer with the giddily delighted children, he traded promises for tomorrow's radio calisthenics and waved his goodbyes.
Exchanging some light banter with the adults who'd participated as well, he headed back to the manor, Emilia in tow.

Lately, spending these early morning hours in the village like this had become part of Subaru's daily routine.
Part of the reason behind it was that, with his body still not fully recovered, he couldn't very well join in on the manor's morning chores—but rather than dwell on that pitiful state of affairs, Subaru, ever the opportunist, had decided to make the most of it as leisure time with Emilia instead.

"Ahh, tired, tired. Emilia-tan, sorry for having you tag along every single morning like this."

"It's fine. You're still not in perfect shape either, Subaru, and Ram and Rem have their work at the manor. Besides, I don't mind doing this."

"Don't mind—wait, you mean spending mornings with me!?"

"Pfft. I meant getting to know the villagers, since we never really had much contact before. I think I'd drawn kind of a line for myself, up until now."

Emilia brushed off Subaru's excited words without much ceremony, then broke into a faint, embarrassed smile. Beneath her hood, he caught a flush of red rising on her pale profile, and at its loveliness, Subaru couldn't help but feel the blood rush to his own cheeks.

Emilia's current outfit was close to what she'd worn the first time they met in the royal capital. Over a light outfit with a short skirt, she wore a white robe embroidered with a hawk-like motif, her beautiful silver hair tucked entirely away beneath the hood.
Subaru, standing beside her, was dressed in a grimy, cheap-looking gray tracksuit. It had been with him since the day he'd been thrown into this other world, and though it had been in rough shape, Rem's hands had mended it and brought it back. Though, admittedly, a few of the frayed spots had been stitched up by Subaru's own hand.

Comparing his own clothes, looks, and general specs against Emilia's, all that came to mind internally was a sigh.
He was fighting tooth and nail just to be worthy of standing beside her. Frankly, he had to start from the position of just clinging onto the ring in the first place.

"Still, though..."

It was Emilia who broke the silence, taking over from Subaru, who'd fallen quiet lost in thought.
She clasped both hands together, leaned her upper body forward, and looked up at Subaru from a slight angle below.

"You've really settled into the village, haven't you, Subaru. You might even be more famous than Ram and Rem at this point."

"Well, in a certain sense, I'm sort of like the hero who saved the kids, you know! Might even go down in legend as some kind of savior-hero type, who knows?"

It was said half-jokingly, but the truth was, Subaru had done enough to warrant that kind of talk without it being much of a joke at all.
Yet Emilia only responded to his teasing with an "Um..." falling silent, pressing a finger to her lips and tilting her head slightly.

"It's... maybe a little different from savior-hero."

"Ah, so it really has become a rumor, huh. ...But other than the forest incident, I can't think what else it'd be."

Given that the rescue hadn't exactly been the picture of dashing heroism, it made sense that the story circulating wouldn't be pure, unqualified praise. And yet, Subaru's assumption turned out to be off in a rather different direction, as Emilia's next words revealed.
Emilia gave a small burst of laughter, her pink lips curling.

"Your reputation in the village right now is partly about being the hero who saved the kids, sure, but it seems like the rumor about you being a 'man of learning' is even stronger."

"Man of learning...? I can't conduct an orchestra or anything. Zero sense of rhythm."

He was the proud holder of a legendary elementary school memory: volunteering for the bass drum in the school marching band, only to be given the explicit instruction to "just pretend to hit it." Handing a bass drum to someone with zero rhythm was asking for trouble. To this day it remained a mystery why nobody swapped him to a different part partway through.
In response to that patented brand of nonsense, Emilia shook her head. "That's not it."

"A man of learning, as in someone knowledgeable. Basically the village headman's exact words were, 'a fool posing as a sage.'"

"That doesn't sound like a compliment to me... sounds more like 'book-smart but acts like an idiot.' Also, wait, have I even done anything particularly intellectual...?"

"Mayonnaise."

Emilia pointed a finger at him and stated the single word, plainly.
At her words, Subaru understood at once. Ever since the mayonnaise incident the other day, the condiment's spread had been progressing at a remarkably favorable pace. On the opinion (mainly Emilia's) that it would be a waste to keep it confined to Roswaal Manor alone, both the physical product and the recipe had first been distributed to the nearby villages.
And in the span of just a few days, it had apparently sparked quite the reaction.

As a mayo-lover himself, Subaru found it gratifying that mayonnaise had taken a solid first step into this other world. Long live mayonnaise.

However—

"I mean, the spread of mayonnaise's greatness is the best thing ever as far as I'm concerned, but... my dramatic comeback from certain doom ranks below Hershey's kisses, huh..."

Being remembered as the mayonnaise evangelist rather than treated as a hero meant, essentially, that this was how the world at large actually valued him.
Feeling utterly unable to shake the sentiment, Subaru's shoulders slumped, and he sagged in dejection.
At his reaction, Emilia hurriedly patted his shoulder.

"Oh, um, of course everyone's grateful you saved the children too, you know? But... the rumor's spread that it was actually Rem who carried them back from the forest, and that Roswaal was the one who finished off the witchbeast in the end, so..."

Her attempt at reassurance trailed off weakly toward the end.
Her consideration stung all the more precisely because there wasn't a single point in her argument Subaru could refute—looking back on it himself, he could only be struck with astonishment.

Even with the matter of bringing the children back, Subaru had simply returned bitten all over and gravely wounded. As for mopping up the witchbeasts, aside from snapping Rem back to her senses, he'd left the rest entirely to Roswaal and had just been out cold, running on empty. He was astonished at himself.

"I—I didn't do practically anything, unexpectedly!"

He'd just been flailing around the whole time.
Feeling as though the effort he'd thought he'd poured into it, in his own way, had just been invalidated, Subaru shrank smaller and smaller.
Emilia, at a complete loss for what to say to him at this point, stammered through an "Um..." and a "Well, I mean, not that, but..." before finally settling on:

"That's enough, don't go worrying about silly things like that."

"But, Emilia-tan..."

"The people who understand how hard you worked, Subaru, really do understand. Roswaal does, Ram does—Rem especially, don't you think?"

She said it in a slightly teasing tone, poking Subaru's cheek with her index finger. At the sharp sensation, Subaru shot her a pitiful look, and Emilia said "Honestly," before breaking into a light trot ahead of him.
She turned to face him. The momentum knocked her hood back, and her long silver hair shimmered in the morning sun, cascading down her back.
At that dreamlike sight, Subaru stopped in his tracks. And there, in front of him, Emilia planted her hands on her hips, adopting the air of someone scolding a hopeless little brother.

"Because I do too."

"—Huh?"

"I understand how hard you've been working, Subaru. I really do. So no getting depressed about it, okay? Got it?"

Tilting her head, Emilia pressed him: "Well? Answer me."
At her words, Subaru, who'd been standing there dumbstruck, hurriedly snapped back to himself and nodded his head vigorously up and down. At that reaction, Emilia's smile burst into full bloom.

"Oh my, now what? Moving like some kind of broken toy. You're always like this."

"No, that wasn't on purpose or anything... I mean, if that wasn't on purpose, Emilia-tan, you're like a hundred times more unfair than me. There's no fighting it, I'm falling for you all over again, I swear..."

"Yes, yes. I think using flattery to dodge the subject like that is a bad habit of yours."

That last bit had been Subaru's genuine, unfiltered feelings, but the smiling Emilia wasn't in a listening mood. Walking beside her as she swept her flowing silver hair back with one hand, Subaru thought once again that he could never, ever hope to win against her.

He'd lost count of how many times they'd walked side by side like this now, and yet every single time, Subaru found a different kind of freshness in Emilia.
There was never the same Emilia twice. If he let today slip by, the chance to meet today's version of her would never come around again. He'd never once imagined he'd spend his days feeling like time was precious, looking forward to tomorrow, like this.

"Working vigorously, deepening a bond with the girl of my affections—what a wholesomely healthy lifestyle. No one could possibly call me a shut-in anymore!"

"It's fine that you act tough like that, boys will be boys, but you still need to take proper care of your body, okay? There's nothing good that comes from overdoing it right now. Just be honest and rely on Rem and Ram's—well, mostly Rem's—kindness."

At Subaru's burst of enthusiasm shooting off at a tangent, Emilia offered up some mild scolding, laced with a bit of a misunderstanding. In her mind, Subaru had apparently earned himself the position of a rather upstanding, well-behaved young man—upstanding enough that he might push himself too hard and throw himself into labor regardless.
Needless to say, the real Subaru was about as far from that kind of work ethic as could be; if he could get by without working, he'd happily spend his whole life eating, sleeping, and doing nothing else.

Still, he wasn't quite honest enough to bother correcting her favorable misunderstanding.

As they talked, the top of Roswaal Manor came into view down the road ahead. At this point, it would only be a few minutes more before they arrived—the end of their pleasant morning chat was already coming into sight.

"Talking with you, time just flies by... wait, why'd you suddenly slow down?"

"This is my pathetic little act of resistance against how much I hate to see this time end—the ox-crawl tactic. Wouldn't you rather enjoy this crisp early morning air a little longer, just the two of us, alone? With who? Me, of course!"

"If we're too late it'll upset the two of them, I think... I did say not to overdo it, but rules are rules, you have to keep them. I have the Oath with my Spirit to think about too, after all."

"Man, you hit me with the reasonable argument and I got nothing... Once Puck wakes up too, there's basically no window left for uninterrupted lovey-dovey talk with you, Emilia-tan."

Bring up her duties as a Spiritual Arts User, her Oath and all that, and he had no comeback.
Holding back what felt like tears of blood on the inside, Subaru masked it all behind his expression.

"Fine... let's head back. ...Damn it, I know I could've played that better...!"

"I'm not sure what exactly you're so frustrated about, but I'll listen when there's time, so don't sulk, okay, come on."

Emilia's gesture—by now practically her trademark whenever facing off with Subaru—of pressing a finger to her lips with a troubled smile, had him captivated all over again.
And then, right in front of Subaru, Emilia let out a small "Huh?"

"There's... a dragon carriage stopped in front of the manor."

"Hm?"

Drawn by her murmur, Subaru followed her gaze and, spotting it himself, narrowed his eyes.
In front of Roswaal Manor's main gate stood a vehicle that could be described as "carriage-like." He described it as "carriage-like" because, at a glance, he could tell it was something different from what his own knowledge understood a "carriage" to be.

After all, the creature pulling the vehicle wasn't a horse, but a lizard roughly the size of one.
Given that, it couldn't rightly be called a horse-drawn carriage—he hoped he'd be forgiven for settling on "carriage-like" instead.
And as outlandish as the vehicle's appearance was, Subaru actually recognized it.

"Come to think of it, I remember seeing these crossing the streets pretty casually back in the royal capital too."

The image of one of these tearing down the street facing the marketplace, kicking up a grand cloud of dust, on his very first day summoned to this world, was hard to forget. Given that the word "carriage" worked as a translation, he could confirm horses did exist here, but with how often he'd witnessed lizards pulling these things instead, it seemed like these creatures might actually be the standard.

"If I heard right, did you just call that thing a 'dragon carriage'?"

"...? Yes. Since an Earth Dragon is pulling the vehicle, 'dragon carriage' makes sense, doesn't it? Wait, don't tell me—is this another case of my common sense being wrong? Is there an actual proper term for it?"

In response to Subaru's question—born of zero prior knowledge—Emilia seemed to grow terrified at the vagueness of her own knowledge. Flustered, reading far too much into Subaru's words, she fretted, and Subaru waved a hand.

"Nah, nah, it's just that I'm the one lacking knowledge here. I'm pretty sure you're right, Emilia-tan—have some confidence."

"Really? You're not just teasing me? I can't go around saying half-remembered things in proper company and embarrassing myself, you know. If you're lying, I'll clobber you."

"'Clobber,' now that's a word you don't hear these days..."

As they talked, they arrived in front of the dragon carriage in question.
Getting closer, the lizard turned out to be startlingly large and lifelike. And on the wagon the lizard pulled—the driver's seat of a vehicle clearly meant to carry passengers—sat someone who appeared to be the driver. Noticing the approaching pair, he immediately stepped down onto the same ground and said:

"Welcome back. My apologies for leaving the carriage here in front of the gate."

At the old gentleman—for that was the fitting description of the driver's bearing and manner—Subaru found his own posture straightening reflexively. Given that he was accompanied by someone of this caliber, the carriage's passenger was surely someone of no small standing as well.
Subaru snuck a glance inside, but through the compartment's window, there was no sign of anyone within. In other words—

"The envoy has already gone inside. At present, I believe they are having an audience with the Margrave of Mathers."

"Well, well, how very courteous of you. Er, I mean, sorry for gawking so rudely just now. Not exactly used to such refined company, so, uh, please forgive me for that."

Flustered by the man's overly refined manner of speech, Subaru's own response came out wearing a thin veneer of politeness over utter chaos underneath. Beside him, Emilia gave him a look along the lines of "Oh, so you can actually speak properly when you want to," making Subaru painfully aware of just how low both their social graces ranked.

Taking over for the now-silenced Subaru, Emilia stepped forward and turned to face the driver.

"You said an envoy... could it possibly be—?"

At the question with no clear subject, the driver, seeming to understand exactly what she meant, bowed at the waist.

"As you have surmised, Lady Emilia, it concerns the Royal Selection."

At the appearance of the word "Royal Selection," Subaru looked up.
It was a keyword that, for some time now, he hadn't even so much as heard mentioned.

Emilia's expression naturally tightened, and Subaru's brow furrowed at the ominous turn of events. Facing their reactions, the old gentleman continued, still bent at the waist.

"I believe the formal details will be conveyed by the envoy. Please, do return to the manor."

"—A summons, is it?"

"For anything beyond that, please hear it from the envoy's own words."

At the driver's answer, measured and mindful of his place, Emilia said "I understand," her expression still tense, and gave a stiff nod. Then—

"—Let's go."

She said it briefly, and set off walking without so much as turning back toward Subaru.
Toward the manor's entrance.

Flustered, Subaru broke into a light run to follow after her back.
He glanced back once, at the very last moment.

—The old gentleman remained bowed toward them still, silently watching the two of them go.
