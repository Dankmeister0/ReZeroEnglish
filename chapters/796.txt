Arc 10 Chapter 36: "Utter Fool"

—Crusch Karsten's breast had become a crucible in which impatience and self-reproach swirled together.

That the impulse had been swollen to that size by outside interference, she did not realize. But even had she realized it, the black knot inside Crusch would not have changed. Swelling meant that nothing had been planted in her that wasn't already there. It only meant that something which ought to have grown large over time had been fattened up with that time skipped.

In other words, that "regret" was a sin that had been steadily growing inside Crusch all along.

"————"

Carrying that untameable impulse, Crusch ground her teeth in silence.

There was a reaction—a click of tooth against tooth inside her mouth. And yet the sound arose neither beyond her closed lips nor even within her own skull.

A world with something that ought to be there stripped out brings an awful, unsettling wrongness. And her opponent was driving his blade's edge into the seams of that wrongness with terrifying precision.

"————"

She turned the knight's sword in her hand and beat aside a slash that came at her from the side like a beast's fang. Soundlessly.

From there the exchange became a standing duel—one clash, two, then ten, steel colliding with steel, the shocks blooming countless fierce flowers of sparks in midair. Soundlessly.

Silence, silence, silence piling up. —Her breathing, the beat of her heart, the fall of her boots as she stepped in, the ring of crossed swords, even the lick of the lips from that belligerently grinning enemy: not one sound reached her.

This was the sudden, aberrant battle that had fallen upon Crusch while she moved alone through the castle.

"————"

—That Crusch had been able to respond to the aberration at all was owed entirely to the Dragon's Eye.

Thanks to that eye, which while its power was unleashed could distinguish even the finest motes of dust adrift in the air, Crusch had managed to block that "soundless" ambush from behind on reflex. And having blocked it, she realized: if this enemy was the culprit who had carried Ferris off, then his present plight was something she could have prevented.

If Crusch had mastered this Dragon's Eye, this situation could have been avoided.

"Then Ferris is still—"

A fragment of the welling regret spilled from her lips, and she widened her eyes at the fact that it had become sound.

In that instant, he caught up to the slight falter in her footwork, and the great blade he swung—a thing like one half of a pair of tailor's shears—cut a shallow line across Crusch's left shoulder as she failed to fully evade.

"Guh...!"

"Too slo-ow, Crusch Karsten. Ya gotta keep yer eyes on me pro-oper!"

Thrusting his face close and jeering, the speaker was a small man with short blue hair.

Shorter in stature than Crusch, he handled that half-shear—nearly as long as he was tall—with perfect ease, bounding freely through the narrow corridor in every direction, holding his own against Crusch and the Dragon's Eye she bore.

His aim, his intent, all the questions that boiled up in an instant—she opened her mouth to demand them sharply—

"————"

And again, Crusch's voice failed to become sound.

The ambient noise that had returned for a single moment was cut off once more, and Crusch reverted to an incomplete world, a finished painting with a portion scraped away. This had been repeated on her over and over and over.

If it were merely that there was no sound, she could lean on the sight of the Dragon's Eye and manage rather better. But with the vanished sound restored at irregular intervals, with the information of sound suddenly delivered out of silence like a downpour, continuing to fight while ignoring it entirely was, for her as she was now, impossible.

"There— there th—ere there— there— ereee!"

Sound came, didn't come, came, didn't come, came, didn't come, came. —Over and over.

There were crossings of blades that rang and acts of destruction that didn't, botched strikes that rang and telling blows that didn't, and a part of her consciousness was forever stolen away by the sorting of information, so that the hundred would not gather at her sword's tip.

The result was that the wounds she took multiplied, that she came nowhere near a chance to strike back—that Crusch, for the first time since the Dragon's Eye had come to dwell in her, was forced into a hard fight, into losing ground.

"This isn't—"

The thin, pitiful voice that leaked out had its remainder erased by her opponent's power, never becoming sound. Had it been "This isn't how it was supposed to be"? Or had it been "This isn't good enough"?

That was something neither her enemy nor—above all—Crusch herself knew.

"Tough as an old boot, ain't ya, woman! Yer just gonna lose to me an' go yappin' and bawlin' anyway, so quit makin' me work for it an' hurry up an' dro-op dead! Yer noisy, all of ya, every damn thing!"

In his irritation, the man whirled the half-shear about, carving up the surrounding floor and walls without mercy.

There was sound in the man's voice; there was no sound in his rampage. The discord composed by that fact stirred up Crusch's heart. It was too mismatched, unbearably so. —Herself included.

"Ferris—"

"—Nooo-oo-ope."

Her question, *where have you taken him*, was wiped away, and the soundless exchange of blades resumed.

She parried the swinging half-shear, struck back, beat it down—yet she could gauge nothing but the feedback through her hands. And all the while the man clicked his tongue, "Tch," in irritation.

"Noisy, noisy, you lot are noisy, endlessly noisy, shut up, noisy, don't breathe, noisy, don't blink, noisy, don't let yer hair sway, noisy, don't stamp yer feet, noisy, don't swing yer sword, noisy, don't let yer heart beat, noisy, don't feel anythin', noisy, life is noisy—"

"————"

"Don't go cacklin' away at me, noisy! Puttin' on that mother's face, noisy! Don't be born, don't be alive, noisy, noisy, noisyyyyy!!"

Eyes bloodshot, the man raged furiously through the silence with a grudging anger that had nothing to do with Crusch.

Handling that beast-like storm of steel with the aid of the Dragon Eye's power, drenched in the flashing malice of a grudge she had no part in, Crusch bit back her regret.

Hostility, malice—even killing intent was perfectly fine. *If it were aimed at her.*

Because it wasn't even that, because she wasn't even worth that much, she—

And Ferris—

"————"

It was the moment that torrent of emotion, that detonation, was on the verge of going white in her mind. —A sudden blue light swept over both Crusch and the man as they strained against each other in soundless deadlock.

"————" "Haah?"

Her fear that it was the enemy's next move vanished when the man before her wore the same question on his face.

That light had been outside his expectations too; the surprise was mutual. And so was the voice that came, an instant later, from beneath the feet of the two glaring at each other close enough to feel the other's breath.

What she heard was a scream from far off, as though torn up with blood, packed full of soul—

"—Don't you dare die, idiot!! What are you thinking, don't screw around!!"

"————"

The instant she heard that familiar voice, the one her ears knew so well, her whole body prickled.

What rose first was relief, then immediately a sense of duty, and trailing behind them, unease and fear—and crushing every last one of them down, Crusch shoved the man away with all her strength. At the man who leapt back on reflex, her sword flashed—a Hundred-Man Slash was loosed.

As the inescapable gale of the blade swept the corridor clean, the man clicked his tongue—"Tch!"—and swung his half-shear, cutting through the ceiling to escape upward. He had avoided it. —But an opening had been born.

"Ferris—!"

She turned the swung-through blade downward and hammered a furious series of blows into the floor.

The point of her sword became wind, and cuts like the tracing of stars were carved into the white stone. —And there, the ceiling directly above split open as the man, vanished from sight, brought down a straight overhead cleave. Soundlessly.

She caught that blow on the sword raised over her head, and the man's red eyes met the Dragon's Eye.

"Do not get in my—"

*—way*, she said, and the end of it was cut away, but it didn't matter. The result was the same.

Her raised foot came down hard on the floor, and an instant later the scored footing crumbled, and Crusch and the man, still locked blade to blade, fell into the underground she had cut open. Soundlessly.

"————"

—What spread out where she landed was a subterranean space beneath the castle, far larger than she'd expected.

The traces of the Warlock of Melancholy's rampage remained thick down here as well, and in that vast chamber—large enough to host an evening ball, or to serve as a drill ground—stood a scattering of figures. Among them: Ferris, his arms pinned to the wall, and Subaru, face down in a pool of blood.

"————"

—In that instant, no words but red and black came to her for the feeling that swelled up in her.

The explosive storm of emotion that welled up blew every rational thought and judgment clear out of reach. —And that gap was exploited; she took the full force of a spinning kick from the man. She caught it on a hastily raised knee, but her falling trajectory was wrenched aside and she was sent flying toward the wall.

She planted her outstretched foot against the wall, letting her sole slide to soften the impact, and landed.

Looking straight ahead, she saw the man with the half-shear standing on the far side of the circular chamber, and between them, in the very center of the room, a woman in a black dress and—for some reason—the White Sheep, hands clasped with her. The fallen Subaru was to her left, the wall-pinned Ferris to her right. —The situation was far too opaque.

The man must have felt the same confusion as Crusch. He struck the floor hard with his half-shear.

"Oi-i, what the hell's goin' o-on, Beralis! Whyyy'd the Detour come undone!"

"Reborn! Coming to me the instant the Detour broke... could it be you want me that badly? Do you love me? Because I love you!"

"Can't hold a damn conversation with you! Shut yer mouth an' answer what yer asked!"

"If I shut my mouth I couldn't answer at all, could I. A scatterbrain like you, Reborn, needs a sensible girl at his side. For instance... yes! This Melty here!"

"Mel...?"

Whether she had any intention of answering the question or not, the woman in the dress seized the White Sheep by the shoulders and shoved her forward toward the man. Caught between the two who had called each other Reborn and Beralis, the White Sheep's eyes widened faintly—but her expression at once became a cold, composed smile.

And then—

"A pleasure to meet you, Reborn. I'm Melty, one of the Beloved Children chosen by Mama."

"Beloved Children, my ass. Beralis, don't go gettin' taken in so eas—"

"—Mama, thank you as always. I love you more than anyone in the world."

At the low words the White Sheep spun out, Reborn caught his breath, and the anger drained from his face. What slid in to replace it was a powerful emotion of fear, and the shame of having felt it.

Just as she had heard, the relationship between the Beloved Children and their mother had been cultivated in a warped way.

She would admit to having thoughts about that mistaken way of weaving bonds. —But that gave no room for consideration toward them, not to a Crusch whose thoughts were painted over with red and black.

She kicked off the floor. The enemy was straight ahead—Reborn at the back, Beralis in front, and the White Sheep—

"Melty is our comrade! I think she and Reborn would be adorable together, so I'm rooting for it!"

Her back to Crusch, Beralis beamed at Reborn. —That one rang true; it was her honest heart.

Shaking the arm off her shoulder, the White Sheep whirled around and faced Crusch. In her hands she extended a short cylindrical implement, gripping it as a single striking staff.

And then the White Sheep, steeling her yellow eyes with crisp resolve—

"—I am one of the Beloved Children, and I am your enemy, 'Verdant Beauty'-san!"

So saying, she leveled the staff with a motion that spoke of real training, pointing its tip at the oncoming Crusch, taking the posture of one standing in her way. However—that one rang false.

"—A wind of lies is blowing, Melty."
"—Yes!"

In that instant the White Sheep—Melty—twisted at the waist and reversed direction, matching Crusch's step, and Crusch's flashing sword and Melty's staff-blow were unleashed together, tracing a slanting cross.

They drove straight at Beralis, standing there utterly defenseless—

"Oh my?"

—and the overlaid strike of sword-wind and staff blasted the woman in the dress away head-on.

—The Divine Protection of Wind Reading discerns truth from falsehood, and it had seen through the lie in Melty's words.

If the words were a lie, then her very positioning must have carried some intent. And indeed, matching that, Melty had joined Crusch in delivering a punishing blow to Beralis.

"—But I am surprised."

There had never been a lie in her everyday words and deeds, but she had seemed reserved—timid, even—as though she feared involvement with other people. —That assessment, however, had been wrong.

Melty was brave, a person of settled nerve. Far more so than someone like herself.

And on top of that—

"You are next, sir."

She turned her killing edge toward Reborn, standing beyond the blown-back, fallen Beralis. Shouldering his half-shear, Reborn clicked his tongue with a dangerous look in his eyes.

"Buncha idiots. Don't come cryin' to me, then."

For anger at a comrade being struck down, it was a statement that didn't quite make sense. But before she could confirm his true meaning, before she could begin the next fight—

"—Crusch-sama! Never mind the enemy, Subaru-kun first!"

The call snapped her back to herself, and Crusch looked to Ferris, pinned to the wall.

What skewered both of his arms above his head was the other half of Reborn's shears. Yet even in that pitiful posture, strung up with his arms run through, what Ferris cried out about was not himself.

"Natsuki—Subaru—sama, please—"

That was right: Subaru had collapsed. She had to treat him—no, shouldn't she prioritize eliminating the enemy? And in the first place, what could she accomplish by running to him? What was needed was Ferris's healing. In which case the order obviously began with freeing Ferris, and why could she not make even so obvious a judgment. Foolish and slow to grasp, so terribly insufficient. How had a creature like this ever been called a "Valkyrie"—

"—Kh."

A violent self-loathing, a pitch-black emotion, detonated abruptly inside her chest.

She was about to drop her sword. Her knees were about to shake. A sob was about to spill. What on earth had suddenly happened. Why had her head, the depths of her chest, some vital part of her heart, all at once come to the edge of breaking.

"Love does come with obstacles, doesn't it. —Being split into enemies and allies sets my heart on fire, too."

She heard a voice murmur that.

Lying on her back on the ground, hands folded over her breast, the woman—Beralis—spoke in a voice gone hoarse with the pain of her wounds, colored with a passion meant for something greater than pain.

And that voice awakened the black knot inside Crusch in a form she could not keep down.

—*I want to die.* *I want to simply vanish, to stop existing.* A ferocity unbefitting Crusch Karsten, cursing herself with her own voice.

"That's why I told ya so!"

"—Kh!"

Pressed beneath an impulse that threatened to fold her knees and break her spirit, Crusch was nearly crushed as Reborn's blade came down on her. The sword she reflexively raised cut into its path, but a stance that timid had no strength to block with, and the shock sent her flying sideways. Soundlessly.

"This is—the Breach is getting stronger—! Subaru-sa—"

Covering for the flung-aside Crusch, Melty cut in between her and the enemy, and the end of her words vanished. The wrongness of silence wedged into the middle of a shout was more than enough to bewilder someone encountering it for the first time.

And exploiting that bewilderment without mercy, the blade ran the brave girl's abdomen through.

"————"

She crumpled limply on the spot, and the striking staff tumbled from Melty's hand. Soundlessly.

Blood gushed out, and Melty's face twisted with pain as she cried and screamed. Soundlessly.

Beside the fallen Melty, Reborn raised the half-shear high. Soundlessly.

"————"

Heedless of everything, Crusch threw herself in front of the descending finishing blow. Soundlessly.

Tracing the trajectory the Dragon's Eye showed her, she swung her sword to protect a comrade. Soundlessly.

Flashes of steel bounded in all directions, the exchange with the scowling Reborn going on and on. Soundlessly.

"————"

Silence, silence, silence. Focus, focus, focus. Undistracted, undistracted, undistracted. Master yourself, master yourself, master yourself—.

"————"

There was a voice tormenting her. Again, it said, *you* had made a mistake; a voice that cursed.

She had heard Ferris's voice, and descended underground to save him. But before Crusch came rushing in, Melty had already burrowed her way into the confidence of the Beloved Children and won their trust. Some scheme had been in motion, and her actions had surely been part of a picture of success as they had drawn it.

Into that had come Crusch's unplanned intervention, forcing Melty to change course.

The result of that was the girl lying on the ground now. The boy face down a little distance away. Ferris pinned to the wall, and herself, doing nothing but defending.

"————"

*If I had not come, might it not have gone well?* These two months, the McMahon estate, the Watergate City of Priestella—every last thing Crusch Karsten had botched.

"————"

The black knot spread wide, and even in the midst of battle, a sense of helplessness was blanketing her heart.

When at last it covered her entirely, she would no longer be able to hold a sword. Or perhaps, before that, the Dragon's Eye would abandon her and that blade would sever the thread of her life first.

If the only difference there was late or early—

"—Lift your head! Crusch Karsten!!"

In that instant, a desperate voice came flying into the soundless world and set her stalling arm moving again.

She parried. Ungracefully, refusing to go quietly. She *made* herself parry, with nothing clean or resigned about it. What was it that made her do it?

It was—

"There's still time! Nobody's finished yet! I won't let anybody be finished!"

Bellowing that with his mouth thrown wide was Ferris, anger burning in his yellow eyes.

For some reason, his voice reached her. It shook her eardrums. It made her swing her sword—but the black knot flooding her whole body gained momentum from that voice as well.

"————"

No one else. He was the one she could least bear to face. She had wounded him with words that should never have been said, left the sin lying there with no means of atonement, and was being crushed by a guilt she could not dissolve.

And yet to such a weak, foolish creature, Ferris raised his voice.

"Subaru-kun's fighting and White Sheep-chan's fighting! Don't give up, fight! Fight and—please! Let me fight too!"

Held up by the voice pouring over her, she just barely resisted the violent blade hammering down on her. And as she resisted, a voice that would not become sound caught in the back of her throat at Ferris's plea.

*Let me fight*, Ferris had said. —His true meaning came through with perfect clarity.

And her heart at once screamed that doing such a thing was impossible.

"It's not impossible. It's nothing of the sort. You can do it. —If it's you, Crusch-sama!"

"————"

Perhaps he had read her weakness in her face or her eyes; it was encouragement meant to scoop up a heart on the verge of breaking.

Surely, if she had been anyone else, it would have been an exhortation to set the soul ablaze. And yet the helplessness of being unable to answer the expectation laid upon her only drained the strength out of her instead.

The right to answer Ferris's straightforward devotion belonged to no one but Crusch Karsten—

"—I've known for ages! Your memories never came back at all, did they!?"

—In that instant, her breath stopped.

△▼△▼△▼△

He'd said it. He'd finally gone and said it.

Even knowing that saying it would put on her face the very expression he least wanted to see.

"————"

Her eyes—two colors now, amber and gold—flew wide, and Crusch stared at him in stunned shock. Enduring the urge to look away from that gaze, Felix twisted his body.

Not to win back his freedom. To push his voice up from his belly.

"I knew! How long do you think I've been at your side!? Something like that, I saw through all of it from the very start!"

A lie. —He'd only realized because of these past two months.

At first, the sight of her heartbroken, grieving from the bottom of her soul at Felix's decision, had been too painful for him to think about anything properly. It was only after time had passed and he'd cooled down, only because of the two months spent moving with the Six Tongues, that he'd been able to hold that suspicion at all.

"But I'm a coward and a sneak and a weakling, so I was too scared to check."

That, too, was surely one of the countless sins Felix had committed.

"————"

Listening to Felix's outcry, Crusch beat aside the half-shear.

He couldn't hear any reply from her. In fact, for some reason, not one sound of the fight between Crusch and the shear-man reached Felix's ears at all. Silence.

But that his own voice was getting through, he could tell from Crusch's face, which clouded further the more he said.

He didn't want to hurt her. He'd never once wanted to corner her. —But if that harsh treatment was what was needed, then he'd accept that jamming his fingers into the wound counted as treatment too.

Because if he didn't go that far, he couldn't save the life slipping away from a certain utter fool—the utter fool who'd gambled on trusting a Felix like this, the utter fool beyond any medicine to apply or any magic to cast.

"'I can't, I can't'—your head's all in a jumble, isn't it? You're sure it's impossible for you, everything's gone pitch dark. —But let me correct one thing, Crusch Karsten."

It was the turn of phrase Crusch favored whenever she exchanged words with another person.

Speaking it deliberately, Felix fixed his eyes on those wavering amber-and-gold—

"—You can. You and I, together—we can."

Ah, what a foolish appeal to feeling, what an unreasoning argument from fate.

*I hate it, I hate it, I really hate it.* He didn't want to look at it straight. Didn't want to admit it. Couldn't accept it.

This was exactly, entirely, the same thing a certain utter fool did. —Believing and expecting all on his own, then using that trust as a shield to strong-arm you. No different at all.

But it couldn't be helped. There was nothing for it. He had no other move.

Because that very utter fool was standing, all on his own, on a cliff's edge where every second counted—so Felix had no choice but to adopt, exactly as it was, the method of the utter fool who had set his heart alight.

"—Crusch-sama!"

At Felix's cry, at his gaze, Crusch's expression twisted hugely.

Her lips moved, spinning out words that never became sound, trying to say it. —*I can't.*

"—You can!"

He couldn't hear it. Therefore she hadn't said it. He forced that absurd logic through and drove it home.

Her grief, her anger, her longing—he'd look after all of it later. Before a life, it was all trivia.

She can't? And the reason? —Because she isn't the Crusch Karsten of before?

"You're not the you of back then! I'm asking *this* Crusch-sama, the one who's here, right now!"

"————"

"Let's save it! You and I! This kingdom that His Highness loved!!"

At the voice, those two-colored eyes looked straight at Felix. —And in them, a fire lit.

The white blade was raised toward the heavens, and in that soundless world a whirling wind became an edge and took shape.

It was the signature of Crusch Karsten the "Valkyrie"—the single stroke of salvation that had once mown down the swarm of Great Rabbits that fell upon her own domain, turned aside their course, and saved a multitude of her people.

—A Hundred-Man Slash of everything she had was loosed into the underground of Kugelschreiber Castle.

△▼△▼△▼△

—Until the very last instant of swinging the blade all the way through, the hesitation kept trailing behind her.

"————"

Her breath did not become sound, her voice did not become sound, and the flash of her sword and its result did not become sound either.

Still, she watched the result of the loosed blade to the end. That much was the least duty owed by one who, having forgotten Crusch Karsten, had tried to pass herself off as the "Valkyrie."

—The black knot that tormented and cursed her writhed inside her breast even now.

That it made Crusch's hands and feet heavy did not change. The negative feelings would swell without limit, and surely, by now, they should have covered every last part of Crusch.

And yet—

"Son of a bitch! You an' that bastard both, yer outta yer damn mi-inds!"

Amid the driving steps and flashing blades, with every ambient sound withheld, Reborn's voice rang out.

As she cut aside the half-shear in his hand, she felt keenly that she had done a deed for which she had no words of defense. —Would the Crusch Karsten of before have done such a thing?

She didn't know. And right now, that had no meaning.

The one Ferris had put his faith and expectation in, the one who had answered—that was *this* Crusch Karsten.

And that recognition became a torrent of positive feeling, filling Crusch's body and heart with a vigor that would not lose to the black knot within her, and let her stand against Reborn.

And so, while Crusch held the enemy's attention—

"Seriously, you're such an utter fool, Subaru-kun. Maybe you ought to die once."

"Hey, hey, don't say stuff like that even as a joke. I don't even want to think about it—dying."

Beyond the steel she traded with Reborn, she could hear the two of them trading nasty remarks.

The boy, barely scooped up out of the brink of death, and the "Blue" who had scooped that life back—the two of them huddled close together atop a pool of blood, all but embracing.

Subaru with one knee raised, and Ferris behind him as if holding him from behind. —Except that this was no embrace of affection was obvious at a glance from the thoroughly unwilling looks on both their faces.

Indeed, they'd had no choice. —Ferris's healing magic required touching the other person's body to be invoked.

"Honestly, you're the one going way too far here."

"That goes both ways, doesn't it."

So said Ferris—who had made Crusch cut his arms off, leaving everything below both elbows still pinned to the wall—and snorted at Subaru, who had a knife sprouting from his flank.

—It was an argument between two utter fools, and no wonder anyone would call them insane.
