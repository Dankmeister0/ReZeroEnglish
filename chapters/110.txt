Arc 3 Chapter 33: "White"

The garden that had watched over the same morning again and again had transformed into a hell it had never once seen before.

The flowerbeds, small but vivid with color, had been blown apart and scorched black; the trees that had stood lining the mansion had been snapped in half and lay felled. Here and there, the gouged-out earth was the result of some tremendous mass exploding outward, and the violence that had raged there had scattered not only soil but scraps of flesh as well.

The green lawn was dyed with black-red blood, and the corpses in black garb lay sunk in eternal darkness. Scattered across the ground, most of them had lost all trace of their original shape—heads blown off, half a body crushed flat, torsos torn to pieces—a scene more gruesome even than the one in the village. It was surely proof of just how much rage the one who had turned these pitiful victims into corpses had carried while doing it.

The blood-soaked iron ball, meritorious creator of this carnage. Joined to its handle by chain, it had smashed apart and destroyed enemy after enemy—but partway through, it had been let go by its owner, and now it lay abandoned, rolling in a corner of the garden.

And the "oni" who seemed to have fought so fiercely, wielding it one-handed—

"—Rem."

Rem's corpse greeted Subaru, countless blades driven into every part of her body, her left arm severed.

One only had to look at the sheer number of corpses in this garden—hers aside—to understand.

She had fought. Against the malice that had slaughtered the villagers and turned its fangs even toward the mansion. She had fought fiercely, cutting down enemy after enemy, until she was covered in wounds, struck by blade after blade, until she lost her weapon and the arm that wielded it, and even then she had kept resisting—and died.

Her black-toned apron dress was torn in a dozen places, riddled with holes, discolored a blackish red by the blood that had gushed from everywhere on her body.

The weapons driven into her—so many that even counting them made him want to look away. Fashioned like crosses with blades at their tips, they were tasteless things, and there were more of them buried in her small frame than the fingers of both his hands could count.

And more contemptible still—despite all those weapons still lodged in her—the wounds left on her fallen body were far, far more numerous than that.

Again, and again, and again.
Blades had been driven into her fallen body, pulled out, driven in again while she still breathed, tormenting her, toying with her life, defiling her dignity, insulting the way she had lived—and then driven in once more, leaving wounds that would never fade.

What crime, anywhere in her, could have deserved being treated like that?

What were they thinking, the ones who killed her, when they killed her?
What did they know of Rem? That she was hardworking, diligent, that she looked after everyone, that jumping to conclusions was her one flaw, that she spoiled Subaru gently and yet was sometimes sharp with him, that she stood by his side when things were hard—but also left him behind—that she loved her sister dearly, that she hated herself, but had only just begun to like herself a little—

A life she had told herself again and again was only a substitute for someone else's—and she had only just begun to walk it as her own.

Rem did not move. Even when he shook her, her body, gone cold, was already stiff, and the soft blue hair he had stroked so many times was matted stiff with clotted blood. She lay face down, her face pressed into the earth, and Subaru had no courage to look at that face.

Whether it showed anguish, whether it was set in the grim determination of someone resolved to fight to the very end, or even if, by some chance, it wore a peaceful expression in death—he had no courage to face any of it.
Because Rem had died because of him.

Kneeling, shaking a Rem who no longer moved, Subaru raised his head in a daze. It was then that he noticed something unnatural about the place where she had collapsed.
Her corpse, which had resisted despair with the garden as its battlefield, lay far from the mansion, at the edge of the garden—apart even from the flowerbeds, discarded in a space that people rarely entered.
It didn't strike him as especially strange, in itself. She might have been outflanked by enemies while fighting, or driven back here while dodging attacks, and simply met her end in this spot.

And yet Subaru found the situation odd.
And as his sluggish mind turned it over, he realized what that oddness was.

Rem had fallen forward, her arms spread as she lay—no, her left arm was gone from the shoulder, so it was her right arm alone, outstretched, as she collapsed—and that posture looked, somehow, as if she had been fighting with something at her back, and had fallen only because she'd been cut down in the midst of it.

He raised his head, and beyond where Rem had fallen stood a wooden storehouse, where gardening shears and flower seeds were kept.
It also served as temporary storage for furnishings no longer in use, and though it was called a storehouse, it was sized more like a small shed.

Standing, then all but crawling, Subaru turned his steps toward the storehouse's entrance, and as he drew closer, he noticed it.
A sensation he had already tasted many times over. A clinging, sticky feeling crawling over his skin, drying the moisture in his mouth until it ached with thirst, dulling his thoughts into a stupor—the dense, oppressive presence of death.

Reaching the front of the entrance, catching that smell of death keenly in his nose, Subaru lowered his gaze. At his feet, from beneath the closed door of the storehouse, a great quantity of blood had flowed out, staining the young grass.

He held his breath, swallowed down his saliva, swallowed down the nausea rising in him, and reached for the door. The hand that trembled in small fits wasn't from caution. It was fear.

There was no sense of anyone alive inside. There was no need to worry about that, and besides, Subaru had no room left in his heart to spare thought for such things.

His fingertips brushed the door handle, missing it again and again as if refusing to grasp it. Irritated, tears welled up at how pathetic it was that his own body wouldn't obey him. Still, after repeating the attempt over and over, his little finger finally caught the handle, and with a creaking groan, the door opened.

In that instant, what spread out—thick enough to fill his nostrils and still not be done invading them—was the vast reek of blood that had saturated the inside of the storehouse.
Unconsciously covering his nose and mouth with his hand, holding down what rose in his throat, Subaru peered inside. He had given up even trying to imagine what sight awaited him there—he meant only to burn the unvarnished reality into his eyes as it was.

—Inside the storehouse were the village's children.

The instant he realized it, Subaru held back the stomach acid surging up into his mouth and staggered, stumbling, out of the storehouse.
He collapsed forward onto the grass and vomited up his burning stomach contents as much as his body would allow. He vomited. His stomach was empty, and all that came up was yellowish bile. Still he vomited. He kept on vomiting. Even as his insides screamed in pain, his throat burned by stomach acid, his mouth ravaged utterly by the sourness, Subaru kept vomiting.

He couldn't be allowed to throw up in there, inside that storehouse.
No one—no one at all—had the right to defile those children any further than they already had been.

"Rem, was…"

She had fought to protect the children, and died doing it.
The only child's body Subaru had found in the village had been the girl named Petra. He hadn't searched through every villager's corpse, so he had entirely failed to consider the possibility that some might have survived.
The adults had fought in the village, and sent only the children fleeing to the lord's mansion. And despair had come chasing after them even there, leaving behind the same conclusion regardless.

Mercilessly, pitilessly, cruelly, every life had been taken, utterly.

"Hh—"

A cracked, inverted voice suddenly slipped from Subaru's throat.
Nothing in particular had happened. He had simply, suddenly, grown terrified.

Seeking someone who knew him, Subaru had made his way back to the village, to the mansion. And yet not a single living soul remained—only the silent dead condemned him for his return.

He felt as though he were being spoken to. By those hollow eyes that reflected nothing.
He felt as though he were being accused. By those gaping mouths, dripping blood.
He felt hated. By the memories of days spent with them, laughing together.

"No... no, no, no, no, no..."

—Why are you the one who survived?
—Why did we have to die?

"No... it wasn't me... I never, ever wanted... anything like this..."

He'd had an ideal. A hope he'd fantasized about.
When he'd heard that Emilia was in danger, Subaru had thought it a revelation from heaven. He'd convinced himself it was a chance, freely given, to make the woman who'd given up on him reconsider.
As it had always gone before, Subaru believed he would save Emilia, and be thanked by her, and mend the small rift of misunderstanding between them, and they would walk on hand in hand together.

He had assumed, with easy confidence, that whatever crisis, whatever danger, whatever tragedy arose, would only be a stepping stone toward that. He had underestimated everything, certain that no matter what happened, he could make it right.

If this—this vast number of dead—was the price for that.

"It's not... my fault... I didn't, I..."

Shaking his head, standing up, Subaru turned his eyes from the storehouse, turned his back on Rem's corpse, and broke into a run toward the mansion.

"Someone... someone, someone, someone, someone, someone, someone, someone, someone, someone...!!"

He cut across the grounds, circled around to the front of the mansion, and tried to wrench open the front door as if slamming into it. But the door remained stubbornly shut, as though it had marked Subaru an outsider, and no matter how many times he threw his body against it, no matter how violently he kicked it, it showed no sign of giving.

Frustrated, he kicked the door one last time, then turned back toward the garden. Stepping on and leaping over the fallen corpses in black garb, he made for the thing he needed.
He picked it up—so heavy his arm creaked—and dragged it toward the terrace on the garden side of the mansion. Then, drawing a deep breath, tensing every muscle in his body—

"—Aaah!"

The iron ball spun on the centrifugal force of the gripped chain, and where it struck, the glass shattered into countless pieces that scattered throughout the room.
Scrambling through the entry he'd forced open, Subaru slipped his body inside the mansion. Shards of the half-broken window sliced thin cuts into his skin, carving several gashes across his hands and legs, but that sharp pain registered as nothing to Subaru now.

"Someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone someone..."

Clinging, as if possessed, Subaru's lips went on demanding the presence of another.
It was the same as when he'd rushed into the village, and yet it had shifted, by now, into something seeking a further, different thing than that had been.
Namely,

"It's not my fault... it's not my fault... it's not... my fault..."

He wanted someone alive to affirm that for him.
Or rather, the mere fact of someone having survived would itself be that affirmation.

So Subaru searched for the living. He craved it. He had to find them.
Otherwise, Subaru could not affirm himself, by himself.

If he let himself believe that this carnage had been caused by his own thoughtless judgment, there was no way his mind could hold its balance.
So that his heart would not shatter to pieces, so that he would not bear responsibility for the deaths of this vast number of dead, he needed some plausible logic to protect himself.

Staggering, his upper body swaying, Subaru ran through the mansion.
Unlike the garden, the interior showed no sign of having been ransacked or destroyed. The familiar sight of the mansion spread out unchanged, and somehow, the fact that this place had kept its ordinary calm, even while the outside world was so thick with death, struck him as horribly frightening.

He grabbed the door of the nearest room, flung it open roughly, checked inside, clicked his tongue, and slammed it shut with all his strength. For a mansion with so many rooms, far too few people actually used them.
There were four people connected to the mansion whose existence Subaru had not yet confirmed—until he found one of them, he would keep opening and slamming doors, one after another, at random.

"Damn it... damn it! Why... why is no one... it's always like this normally..."

Muttering in a strained, half-crying voice, Subaru clawed roughly at his own bangs.
Normally, Subaru could reach the library Beatrice managed so easily he wouldn't even have to aim for it. And yet now, when it mattered most, he simply couldn't find it.

He wanted that spiteful mouth of hers so badly right now it was like an ache in his throat.
He could bear being treated carelessly, like a bug, could bear the sighs of undisguised displeasure she breathed at him. If only she were alive, if only she would affirm him with her life.

"Come out... come out, please... please, I'm begging you... help me... help meee...!!"

No longer able to hold back the tears streaming out, Subaru ran through the mansion, his cheeks soaked with them. All that came from his mouth were sobs, and pathetic, weepy pleas for someone to pull him out of this despair and save him.

He passed by the guest room where he'd first woken in the mansion, trampled the carpet of the hallway that Beatrice's magic had once turned into an endless loop, crawled through the dining hall he'd passed through so many times, turned his back on the kitchen where he'd struggled to make mayonnaise, left behind the great bathhouse whose cleaning had been the heaviest labor among the chores assigned to him, and ran up to the floor where the servants' rooms gathered, throwing open every nearby room and peering inside—

—and in the seventh room, he found Ram's body.

That Ram, lying on the bed, was not simply sleeping—Subaru, who had seen far too much death in this short span of time, understood that at once.
Her translucent white skin had lost its color and turned pale, while her lips, in contrast, stood out an unusually vivid vermilion. Unlike her sister's death, whose looks were identical to hers, Ram's appearance—having even been given death makeup—was somehow lovely even in the countenance of death.
As he'd joked so often before, if she just kept quiet, she'd pass for an adorable maid.

The way she had been laid out on the bed, she surely hadn't died peacefully in her sleep like this. Someone had carried her dead body to the bed, and arranged her countenance in death this way.

Probably, it had been Rem. What had she thought, faced with her sister's death? How many times had she cried while applying that death makeup? And with what feelings had she left her sister behind, alone?

Now that Rem herself had been so mercilessly killed, there was no way to ever know what had been in her heart.

He touched a finger to Ram's cold lips, and a trace of the vermilion makeup came away on his fingertip. Rubbing finger against finger, toying with the color on his fingertips, Subaru found himself, before he knew it, sitting slumped in the corner of the room.
He raised his head in a daze. There before him was the figure of the dead, peaceful enough that, if someone told him she was only sleeping, he might just have believed it.

Her figure, having silently greeted Subaru's return, condemned him, the living Subaru.

Just like the hollow eyes, so many of them, that had looked up at him in the village.
Lips that would never again tremble cast an endless, silent curse upon him.

"Hh—"

Crawling, all but scrambling, Subaru fled from the bed where Ram slept. Forcing his trembling arms, his disobedient knees, to hold him up, he stood, and staggering, bracing a hand against the wall, put distance between himself and that room as fast as he could—even a single second sooner.

"Stop it... it's not... it wasn't me... I, I didn't do anything...!"

Covering his ears, shaking his head, weaving words to protect himself with his hoarse throat, Subaru tried to flee from that voice of resentment that seemed to whisper right beside his ear. But the voice, the despair, clung to him relentlessly, endlessly, as if mocking his flight.

He dragged his feet, running down the hall. He came out onto the landing with the stairs. Bracing his hands, going on all fours, he climbed. Along the way, he tripped again and again, stumbled again and again, blood dripping from his split lip, and challenged the upper hallway he finally reached once more.
Only the dead greeted Subaru. Ram was dead; three of the living remained. His feet naturally avoided Emilia's room, on the same floor. On the second floor, Ram had died in her own private room, the one assigned to the servants. The image of Ram, dead in her own room, flickered in his mind, and tears he'd thought he'd cried dry welled up once more. Even though he'd thought he'd expelled every drop of moisture in his body, even though the sobs and the bile should have run out—still, an inexhaustible grief, aching in the depths of his chest, drove him to run.

If not for that, he could have simply sat down, held his head, seen nothing, heard nothing, known nothing, sought nothing, been given nothing, had nothing taken from him, loved nothing, been loved by nothing, begun nothing, ended nothing, changed nothing—could have simply stayed still.

Streaming out his complaints and tears from a face crumpled with weeping, Subaru staggered his way to the top floor—Roswaal's study. The heavy double doors kept their silence, their solidity looking sturdy enough to repel even the malice that had descended upon this mansion.
But that faint hope vanished in an instant.
In this place where everything had already ended, there could be no safe place, no permitted place—there shouldn't be, there mustn't be.

He shoved the doors open, and a chill wind flowed out into the hallway.
The cold pricked at his arms, and Subaru suddenly remembered how lightly dressed he was, how defenseless against it. He thought—where was his coat?—and remembered he'd given it away to Petra in the village, and felt disgusted with himself, trying to protect himself with such useless thoughts.

He stepped into the room. There was, undeniably, a half-resigned part of him that thought Roswaal might be found dead, slumped over the study's desk.
With Rem dead, with Ram's life snuffed out, it was hard, in the midst of that, to keep holding on to hope for anyone else. He no longer even knew, himself, whether he was running through the mansion searching for the living, or dragging despair along behind him, trampling memories, just to lose sight of hope entirely.

But the world that had betrayed Subaru's hope three times now—in the village, in the garden, in the private room—did not, this time, turn a fourth betrayal against him.

There was no one in the study.

He swept his gaze across the empty room, crawled about searching under the desk's shadow, beneath the shelves where furnishings sat, even behind the reception table—and still he could find no trace.
A measure of relief took hold of Subaru.
It was relief at not having had to confirm whether Roswaal was alive or dead—and also, self-pitying relief that the dead had gained no further reason to torment him.

"—?"

Suddenly, he heard the sound of wind rattling a window, and Subaru turned around.
Looking, he saw that the moment he had opened the door connecting to the hallway, an oddly bone-deep chill of a wind had begun flowing through the room, striking the window, rustling the leaves of a potted plant set in the corner.

A current of wind was being generated.
Having gained a passage through the doorway, it swept, carrying its cold, in a circle around the room and out again. Feeling that fact against his skin, Subaru searched for the source of the wind, relying on its touch and his instinct—

"There's... a mechanism like this..."

The moment he fiddled with a device hidden beneath the ebony desk, a sound like gears meshing rang out. Then came a low grinding noise, like a millstone turning, and the bookshelf set against the far wall of the study slid nearly a meter to the side.

Before Subaru, stunned at a mechanism beyond his imagining, what appeared behind the moved bookshelf was a black hole, gaping open.
Approaching timidly, he peered his face gently inside, and saw a stone-built passage continuing for just a few steps, beyond which a staircase spiraled downward.

"A hidden... passage... huh?"

No doubt it existed to prepare for an emergency.
For someone of the rank of margrave, holding the role of lord, of course Roswaal would keep an escape route secured as a means of self-defense. Whether or not he'd ever actually have occasion to use it was another matter—but it was easy enough to imagine that a man like him would have prepared it gleefully.

Never had he imagined that a real occasion to use that passage would actually come.

The cold wind flowing through the room seemed to be coming from that hole. In other words, through the passage beyond that hole, the wind was blowing out from some point even deeper, even colder.

Holding his breath, taking several deep breaths in a row, waiting until his high, sharp heartbeat eased its pace even a little, Subaru threw himself into that hole.

The wall, cold to the touch, was made of some material he couldn't identify, giving off a faint, pale blue glow, securing his field of vision for a few meters ahead at least.
Relying on that light, keeping a hand against the wall, Subaru descended the spiral stone stairs downward, paying the closest attention not to miss a step.

Anxiety at stepping into a place he knew nothing about—how far it continued, where it led—tightened in his gut. But burning at his back even more fiercely than those negative feelings was rage at the existence of this hidden passage.

If wind was blowing through it, that meant it led out to an exit.
Which meant, in other words, that someone had used this hole, attempting to escape outside. Three survivors he had not yet seen in the mansion—given whose study this passage led from, there was no need to even wonder who it was that had abandoned the mansion's horrors and fled.

Had he left that behind—left behind that much carnage, let die a girl who adored him that much—and fled? Shamelessly, out of nothing but fear for his own life, had he run?

Subaru did not notice that the roots of his teeth were trembling—with rage, and with joy.

Rage at a person who had run to disloyal, contemptible, vile depravity.
Joy at having found someone onto whom he could shift, in his place, all the resentment, the curses, the contempt, the abuse—every silent accusation of the dead.

Wearing a manic smile in which every emotion was jumbled together, tears flowing endlessly without stopping, blood dripping from a lip he'd bitten through, the madman pressed on downward.

How many steps had he descended by now? In terms of count, it was a great many; in terms of height, it was well beyond the scale of simply going from the third floor down to ground level. The hole apparently led down into the mansion's basement, and at the bottom of the stairs, a straight passage seemed to continue once more. The wall's material remained the same glowing blue stone, sapping the heat from his fingertips at a touch, and with a rictus grin, Subaru pressed on, palm against it.

He was chasing the trace of the living. That single fact was, right now, a joy to Subaru.
It was a joy that whoever lay ahead would be a being who—no better, no worse than himself—had made a vile choice deserving of every curse.
To find the living in a world of nothing but the dead, and confirm whether he himself was truly alive right now, or whether he had already died—that he could do this was genuinely, deliriously joyful.

"Hn, ohh..."

Then, the palm he'd kept trailing along the wall suddenly lost the wall and swept through empty space. His body, swaying forward involuntarily, stepped out into a space that, unlike the passage until now, opened into a small chamber slightly widened on all sides.
In the dim, faintly lit space, a number of pillars stood scattered about. He felt something off about their uneven spacing, but if this hidden passage itself had been thrown together in a rush, he could accept such shoddy design without much question.

Satisfied with that reasoning, Subaru slipped past the bothersome pillars. By now, the movement of his hands and feet had grown terribly sluggish and heavy. A lassitude weighed on him as if lead had been packed into his fingertips, and even his thoughts, already growing hazy, began to dull further.

He struggled even to take a single step forward, pressing on regardless. His mouth was parched to the point of pain, and the blood that had been flowing from his lip had, at some point, stopped.
His eyelids grew heavy, his movement restricted as though weights pressed down on both his shoulders, and still, nothing but sheer obsession, sheer grudge, sheer madness drove Subaru onward.

Weaving past the pillars scattered through the hall, passing through the small chamber, Subaru spotted, in the blue light ahead, an iron door set into the passage. Passage, staircase, passage, chamber, and now a door—beyond this, would he find what he sought?

—What was it, again, that he'd been seeking?

Before his stalled thoughts could arrive at an answer, his bloodless fingertips reached out first. Standing before the door, gasping, mouth opening and closing for breath, Subaru abandoned the thought that refused to come to him, and reached for the door's handle on nothing but a sense of duty.

—In that instant, a searing, burning agony shot through the right hand that touched the handle.

"—Aghhh!"

His throat screamed with the agony, and Subaru wrenched his right hand back as if tearing it away. The burning pain had spread through the whole palm that had touched the knob, and groaning through the pain, Subaru lowered his eyes to the wreck that was his right hand.

—There was no index finger on his right hand.

"—Huh?"

Blankly, dumbfounded, Subaru raised his right hand before his eyes and spread it open.
Discolored white, the skin of the palm shredded to ribbons—of the five outstretched fingers, only the index finger was entirely gone, missing from the root.

Slowly, his gaze shifted to the door ahead. There, stuck to the handle he'd surely grasped, was what had been Subaru's right index finger.

—Have to reattach it, quick.

His thoughts, incoherent, produced only that, and Subaru reached his right hand toward the handle again to reclaim the severed finger. But moving his body was even harder now than before—his will failed to travel from shoulder to elbow, from elbow onward. Frustrated at his unresponsive arm, Subaru stepped forward to close the distance to the door further—and his right ankle shattered from the root, and he fell sideways to the ground.

"—Aaaaaaah"

A voice that could not become a voice leaked from his throat.
Whether it was a scream against the pain, or simply a meaningless outpouring of life itself, even he could not say. But amid what he did not know, there was one thing he understood. That the moment his open mouth, trying to release that voice, drew a breath, something white filled even the inside of his body, and it stopped moving.

His lungs spasmed, his breathing faltering. Taking short, shallow breaths, desperately sending oxygen to lungs that would not expand, Subaru's eyes alone darted restlessly around at this abnormal state.

He didn't know what had happened. Only that every sensation across his body had gone terribly vague.
There had been a pain, a sense of loss he'd never tasted before, as his right ankle shattered into fragments. And the moment he fell, he could tell that his right side, crushed beneath his own toppling body, had begun to lose parts of itself, piece by piece, the same as the ankle, collapsing.

From lips that no longer trembled, he exhaled, barely, the breath still tethering him to life. Only now did Subaru notice that the breath he exhaled was visibly white.

His face, pressed to the ground, had stuck fast to the floor, and he knew that if he moved his head, his cheek would tear away. He no longer felt any pain. He moved roughly anyway, and his cheek and the outer edge of his right eye fell away entirely. It didn't matter. In the field of vision he finally turned onto his back to see, he could make out, upside down, the small chamber he'd passed through.

And seeing it now, looking up from below, Subaru finally understood that the reason the pillars scattered through that chamber stood so unevenly was not the result of some slipshod, defective construction.

Every one of those things lined up in the chamber was an icicle—a person frozen and dead.

Victims, just like him, who had wandered into this white ending and become ice statues where they stood. And that fate was, before long, about to come for Subaru too.

His breathing had already stopped.
The limited oxygen circulated through his brain, but even his brain, sluggish enough that it was a wonder it retained consciousness at all, stood on the verge of vanishing in this world of extreme cold.

He understood nothing.
He could see nothing.

From the tips of his feet, his body was gradually becoming fragments of ice, ceasing to be Natsuki Subaru.
Though for that matter, perhaps what had been here was, by now, no longer Natsuki Subaru at all—only a madman wearing his skin.

The sensation in his lower body vanished. His arms, too, were nowhere to be found now. His right eye had just fallen away. It was strange that his remaining left eye could still see. Where did life reside—in the brain, or in the heart?

That answer was never going to be found within this frozen world—

"—It's already... too late."

A murmur, drained of all warmth, echoed through a world ruled by nothing but white,

—and Natsuki Subaru shattered into pieces, and vanished from the world as fragments.
