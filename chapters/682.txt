Arc 8 Chapter 63: "With a Miracle Upon the Lips"

The instant he took the diagonal blow of the upward-slashing blade, Eugard Vollachia resigned himself to the fact that he had tasted defeat for the first time in his life—no, in the whole of his life and death combined.

This was the first time anyone had crossed swords with him, contended with him as an equal, and even come out ahead.
Even as he praised the skill that had accomplished it, the fact that he had lost at a moment when losing was unacceptable made his chest ache—the chest that housed a heart which no longer beat.
He had barely exchanged words with Iris—the reunion he'd never dared hope for, yet had somehow been granted. He had not touched her. He had not been able to fully convey his love.

And yet, Eugard had achieved his reunion with Iris.
There must surely be others who, in order for Eugard and Iris to have their wish granted, wanted just as badly to exchange words with the ones they loved, to touch them, to convey their love—and could not.
The same held true for the turning of the stars that guided Eugard and Iris's fates.

—Or so he tried to tell himself.

"As a wielder of this sword, I'm a total amateur, and even so, having just one swing suck out this much of my energy—man, that's brutal."

So muttered Halibel, the jet-black Wolf Human who bore in his chest the brambles that marked the curse, having just finished swinging the ominously-powered Wicked Sword.
His expression called to mind another Wolf Human Eugard knew—one who shared nothing with Halibel but his race, and yet looked nothing alike.
In memory, that other one, too, had sometimes looked at Eugard with eyes like these.

"But it does its job properly, matching all the energy it takes—so I guess I can't hate it too much."

Unaware of Eugard's momentary reverie, Halibel went on.
It was clear enough that he was speaking of the sword in his hand—the Wicked Sword, Murasame. But something about the way he said it sounded different from a mere remark that the Wicked Sword's hidden power had helped him win.
It was almost as though the Wicked Sword had granted Halibel something beyond victory—

"—A dead man's obsession?"

Checking over his own body where the blow had landed, Eugard put into words a feeling that defied words.
Having been revived in an undead body, Eugard carried within him a kind of obsession—an instinctive hostility, a violent urge, directed at the living of the Empire.
This was likely not unique to Eugard. Moreover, Eugard had held that overpowering impulse in check with something stronger still: his feelings for Iris.
Even so, he could not defy the mastermind behind his revival, and had no choice but to comply, at minimum, with that one's designs—which was how he had come to cross blades here with the bearded Dog Human and Halibel.

That compulsion—the one he could neither defy nor resist—had vanished.
It had disappeared, as though severed clean away by the Wicked Sword's dull, baleful aura.

"Which is why nothing now remains within me but my feelings for my star."

With one hand resting against her cheek, Eugard spoke to her so gently that Iris=Yorna—the woman who was both Iris and Yorna—lost her words and could only stare, spellbound.
Not the heatless, pallid skin of the undead—and his eyes, too, were not the false black shot through with gold, but his true, original blue.
His deadpan face reminded her that he had always been a man whose facial muscles scarcely moved, quite apart from his having been revived as an undead.
—It was unmistakably the very same Eugard Vollachia that Iris=Yorna knew.

"Barging in without so much as a by-your-leave, and not even sparing me a glance—now that's a cruel gentleman."

A rowdy, hoarse voice broke into the two of them's reunion.
Despite what his words said, his tone lifted with an eager note, as if he himself delighted in his own sudden intrusion—the undead swordsman, the Undead Sword Saint, cast a look at Eugard like a man licking his lips.
At that stare, Eugard, predictably, did not let his expression waver in the slightest.

"Your particular art is one to marvel at, but unfortunately, the timing is poor. I wish to spend every moment of my limited time on my star."

"Now don't go saying such a cold thing—"

To Eugard, who had stated his wish flatly, the Undead Sword Saint turned a deaf ear. —No; he tried to answer that he'd turn a deaf ear, but could not finish saying so.

"Get back!"

So she cried sharply, and the small body in Iris=Yorna's arms spun—a foot in an icy geta sandal tore through the air with terrific force and drove a brutal kick into the Undead Sword Saint's torso.

"Not so fast!"

Against the girl's kick, the undead man brought up the scabbard at his hip, deflecting the direct blow and stepping back.
He hadn't fully absorbed the impact, but the Undead Sword Saint wore a maniacal grin, feeling more delight than anger at being struck back—

"Hyah!"

That maniacal grin was crushed beneath an ice hammer swung down with tremendous force.
Struck from behind, crushed from head to toe by the blow, that particular body of the Undead Sword Saint shattered into splinters. Even so, there was no room for relief.
If he could be felled this easily, he'd have been felled fifty times over by now. As always before, that undead man would surely come back to life again and again, without end.

But before dealing with the Undead Sword Saint any further—

"—Hm. A blow with more heart in it than one would expect from a child. In consideration of your promise, I shall forgive your insolence."

"Step away from Lady Yorna."

"T-Tanza! To His Excellency—how could you do such a thing to His Excellency!"

In the field of view of Iris=Yorna, who cried out, the hilt of Eugard's Sunlight Sword and Tanza's icy geta had locked together, grinding out a shrieking sound.
Tanza had kicked away the Undead Sword Saint to protect Iris=Yorna, but her target had included not only that undead man but also the unknown emperor who had abruptly cut in.
Given how sudden it all was, it was only natural that Tanza couldn't yet tell friend from foe where Eugard was concerned.

"But still, you mustn't just—not so suddenly...! Your Excellency, I do apologize for my Tanza. The child meant no harm..."

"Do not wear such a pained expression. Whatever face you make, I find it dear to me—but it troubles my conscience to go on adoring you while you plead so earnestly."

"H-he's... always been like this..."

Iris=Yorna spent every word she had defending Tanza, yet Eugard's replies never quite seemed to land on the point. This sort of thing had been an everyday occurrence in her days with Eugard.
Yes—in her time with Eugard, this had always been an everyday occurrence.

"...Lady Yorna."

Tightening her embrace with a squeeze, Tanza called out to Iris=Yorna.
Tanza had not lost her composure or her judgment so completely that she'd shake off that embrace and lunge at Eugard. Even without understanding the relationship between them, she must have grasped that Eugard held no hostility toward Iris=Yorna.
As for what remained—

"Hey! It might still be a little too soon to have a nice, relaxed chat!"

Running up like that was Emilia, who had been fighting the Undead Sword Saint alongside Tanza.
Having just mercilessly clobbered the undead man from behind with her ice hammer, she fixed her violet eyes on Eugard, standing near Iris=Yorna and Tanza, and said,

"Can I count you as being on our side?"

"That will do."

"Good! You look reeeally strong! Let me count on you!"

"Understood. Leave it to me."

At Eugard's immediate answer, Emilia let out a breath of relief, pressing a hand to her chest.
Iris=Yorna blinked at how instantly Emilia had settled the matter of friend or foe, but considering the circumstances of her life as a Half-Elf, it would be presumptuous to measure her by ordinary sensibilities.
In any case—

"Your Excellency, your body—"

"Regrettably, it is nothing more than embers, waiting to burn out."

"——"

Even as he said so, Eugard did not look away from Iris=Yorna, nor did he twist his expression into sorrow or loneliness.
Iris=Yorna, for her part, had hoped for exactly that—for him to say it without a shred of mercy.
And so, still gazing straight at Iris=Yorna, Eugard went on.

"But for the little time remaining before I burn out, all of me belongs to you."

"—Yes, Your Excellency."

So that he would not simply end things by leaving Iris=Yorna sad and lonely.
For this gentle King of Thorns, feared by everyone, had always kept his heart rippling in quiet stillness, so that he would never wound anyone by any means but his thorns.

"Not at all—not at all... this is happiness, through and through."

Even if the time was limited, she could touch Eugard, exchange words with him.
At the end of those days long ago, that was the one thing she—Iris—had failed to accomplish. And because she'd failed to accomplish it, an enormous number of sacrifices had been made.
To atone for that regret, that mistake, Iris had come to the Empire as Yorna—

"—That treasured blade in your hand, I take it to be the Sunlight Sword Vollachia."

A hoarse voice, its heat reined in, suddenly reached their ears, and Iris=Yorna and the others looked up.
A short distance from the gathered four, an undead figure rose slowly from the ground, as though climbing up out of the earth itself. And then, one after another, like insects swarming out from beneath a great stone, more and more Undead Sword Saints appeared, each possessed by that same ghastly obsession.
Every one of them was identical, without individuality, moving in perfect unison as their golden eyes gleamed and they grinned with murderous glee.

"Blades famed the world over as legends—forged beyond all reason, standing alongside the Dream Sword and the Wicked Sword that no-good son of his totes around... I've laid eyes on such things before, but never once have I been blessed with the chance to cross one."

"...And even now, would you have me believe a warrior's blood stirs in you still?"

"It's a shameful thing to admit... but that's exactly right."

He surely felt not a shred of shame—that was only words. It came through, painfully clear, in his eyes blazing ever brighter and in that maniacal grin radiating a cold sort of heat.
Compared even to other undead, the Undead Sword Saint's demonic aura and obsession were plainly abnormal, and Iris=Yorna found herself daunted by something beyond mere strength, clenching her own hand in frustration at her helplessness.
That tightly clenched hand was gently wrapped by Tanza's hand within her arms.

"Child, I permit you to go on holding my star's hand, and to stay close by her side."

"...I would do so regardless of whether you permitted it."

Tanza's sullen voice could not crack Eugard's cool, composed profile. But Iris=Yorna could tell that he had taken the reply in good spirits.
As Eugard advanced on foot toward the Undead Sword Saints, whose numbers kept climbing without end—ten, then twenty—Emilia tried to fall in step beside him with an eager look on her face.
Eugard held her back with a glance.

"Your will to lend aid is well taken. But leave this to me."

"But if it's just you alone..."

"—Eugard Vollachia. That is my name."

"Vollachia... isn't that..."

Emilia's eyes went wide at the family name Eugard had given, and she looked toward Iris=Yorna. Iris=Yorna answered with a nod, and watched Eugard's back as he went.
Eugard faced off against the Undead Sword Saints at a distance of some ten meters.

"Eugard Vollachia... could it be, are you the Thorned Emperor?"

"I have never called myself that, but there are those who do. It is an apt way to sum up my time upon the throne—one that should be easy enough for the history books of later ages to preserve."

"Ha, hahaha! Well, well, well! The strongest emperor in all the history of the Sacred Vollachia Empire! To think I'd get to meet such a man... I really do have the luck, don't I!"

His voice brimming with excitement, the Undead Sword Saint's murderous swordsman's aura pressed harder still.
Even knowing that his opponent was the Thorned Emperor, Eugard Vollachia, the Undead Sword Saint was far from cowed—instead, by some logic beyond the ordinary, he only spurred himself on further.
The golden eyes of the Undead Sword Saints all glinted, fixed on Eugard and on his Sunlight Sword.

"If I beat you, Your Imperial Excellency, do I get to keep that Sunlight Sword?"

"If the sword itself judges you worthy, then so it shall be. But I will not surrender it easily."

"Ohh."

"Many of my subjects staked their very lives so that this blade might become truly mine."

Of all who heard those words, only Iris=Yorna—who could share the memory of every one of those subjects Eugard spoke of—could truly understand the passion of that gentle King of Thorns, feared above all others.

—And before the watching eyes of that very Iris=Yorna, Eugard and the Undead Sword Saints moved as one.

"—Hh."

A short, sharp breath, and a lightning-quick step erased the distance between them.
The twenty identical men split off in four directions, flashing blade-light surging toward Eugard like a whirling gale.
It was the savage feat of a mediocrity trying to bundle together first-rate swordsmanship into something transcendent—a slashing assault that deserved to be called a sword-storm surpassing mere sword-wind. Tanza and Emilia both caught their breath at the sight.
But Iris=Yorna did not doubt him for even an instant.

Twenty strikes came raining down—but the clash was settled, precisely, in a single exchange.

The great storm of the Undead Sword Saints, blades given shape, aimed at Eugard from every direction.
But before those blades could reach the King of Thorns, the brilliantly blazing Sunlight Sword flashed once—burning away every one of the twenty approaching blades, its crimson tip sweeping across the Undead Sword Saint's throat.

"A blow like this—!"

Only the one directly in front took the fatal wound to his throat.
Even with his blade severed, the Undead Sword Saint twisted his cheek in a grin, certain that the other nineteen of himself—or the endless stream of still more selves—would still reach their mark.
But if he mistook having a next life even after death for his own strength—

"That's the end of it, I'd say."

—The next instant, the slashed Undead Sword Saint's entire body burst into flame, and the fire spread to the other nineteen as well, all of them swallowed at once by the blaze.

"—Wh—what—"

In Eugard Vollachia's grip, the Sunlight Sword Vollachia ignited its true flame, and fire rose up with the very soul at the root of the slashed Undead Sword Saint as its kindling.
By some strange twist, it was the same outcome as when Vincent Vollachia's Sunlight Sword had set ablaze the soul of the Witch Sphinx, the one who bore the mantle of the Great Disaster.

"Gah."
"Ngh—"
"—Aaagh!"

Wracked by that ferocious hellfire, the Undead Sword Saints turned one after another to ash. Each time a burning body perished, a new body rose to replace it—only to be swallowed by the same flames in turn.
Again and again, over and over, until he had rolled out more than ten charred husks of himself and understood there would be no exception, the Undead Sword Saint looked down at his own hands, still burning in flames that would not go out.
And then—

"Ahh, ahh, damn it all. As if I could end here, of all places...!"

The undead swordsman let a garbled voice spill from his flame-scorched throat, his burning cheeks twisting.
For a moment, it seemed to conjure the image of a man's last, futile struggle against a defeat he could not accept—but that worry was itself burned away, denied by the fate of the Undead Sword Saint, who crumbled into charred fragments.
Crumbling to pieces, he stretched his disintegrating hands toward the heavens, and the Undead Sword Saint's form faded away.
Before long—

"—He's... gone? Vanished?"

So spoke Tanza's voice, unable to feel certain even as she watched the same thing unfold.
Iris=Yorna, whose hand Tanza held, answered that unease by wrapping her free hand around the girl's small one in turn, telling her without words that all was well.

"...Did all of him just... burn up?"

"It is somewhat difficult to find the proper way to put it, but yes. The Sunlight Sword's flame burns away whatever its bearer wishes it to. Even a soul is no exception."

"I see... ...Thank you. You really, really helped."

Having offered that thanks, Emilia nonetheless turned a troubled gaze on the many charred fragments of the burned-out Undead Sword Saint.
Even if the opponent had been undead, even if he had made ominous use of an undead's nature, the fact that a life had been lost still shook the violet of Emilia's eyes.
That same feeling Emilia held had once belonged to Iris=Yorna too—one of the things, she thought, she'd had no choice but to let go of over the course of so many lives lived again and again.
And yet, there were also things she had never been able to let go of.

"—My star."

Called that once more, Iris=Yorna turned to face Eugard head-on.
Eugard, revived as an undead man and freed from that hateful yoke. His fine, elegant features, his voice that resonated in the chest far more than its volume warranted—both were a continuation of those days long ago.
And yet, there were things that could not be explained by that alone.

"Your Excellency, the thorns—"

The Curse of Thorns, which bound others and drove its barbs into their very hearts.
It was the curse that had isolated Eugard and had been the very reason he came to share a special time with Iris—a shackle that, even in death, had clung to his soul and permitted no escape.
And now, it could not be felt. Not by Iris=Yorna, nor by Tanza, nor by Emilia either.
Which meant, in other words, that Eugard had been freed from the Curse of Thorns.

"Has the curse... been lifted, then?"

"—Not quite. The Curse of Thorns has not been lifted; it remains. The curse was not undone within me—it was transferred to another."

"Transferred...? Can such a thing even be done?"

"That person made it possible. It was something that could not be accomplished in the era when I lived. Time has flowed on since then, and a great deal of wisdom has been born into the world. It is a splendid age."

Eugard Vollachia had been freed from the Curse of Thorns.
In Eugard's answer, given with a quiet dip of his chin, there was a deep note of respect for the one who had achieved it. Eugard had always been a man unafraid to praise and acknowledge others—but here there was an admiration that went beyond what an emperor or a member of the imperial family would feel, the admiration of one life for another.

"Your Excellency's curse..."

Meanwhile, Iris=Yorna found herself unable to speak at Eugard's answer.
She understood well enough Eugard's nature—his wish to describe things with strict accuracy, insisting that the Curse of Thorns had not, strictly speaking, vanished—but to her, this was still the fulfillment of a long-cherished wish.
If Eugard's soul could be parted from the Curse of Thorns, even after death, even now.

"Lady Yorna, who is this man?"

"Tanza, this is His Imperial Excellency, Emperor Eugard Vollachia... the man I love, my dear troublesome man."

At that introduction from Iris=Yorna, Tanza's eyes widened just slightly. Then, lifting her face, she met Eugard's gaze, and after a moment's hesitation,

"My name is Tanza. I serve as Lady Yorna's attendant."

"I see. Tanza. In my absence, my star was in your care. You have my thanks for your trouble."

Eugard nodded to Tanza, who had spoken so timidly.
Close enough to see each other's faces, close enough for voice to reach voice, close enough that a hand reaching out could touch another—at that distance, Eugard and Tanza exchanged words.
And yet, once, there had been no one besides Iris who could do that with Eugard without wincing in pain.

"I'm Emilia, just Emilia. Eugard, will you keep helping us?"

As if riding along on Iris=Yorna's own feelings, Emilia too spoke to Eugard without the slightest hesitation.
To her words, Eugard dipped his chin and said, "Of course," and then continued,

"The one who freed me from the thorns that tormented me for so long... Groovy Gumlet entrusted something to me. I must fulfill his role in his stead."

"Groovy... so that person was the one who lifted Your Excellency's curse."

"My star."

"Y-yes?"

Distracted by learning her benefactor's name, she let out a voice like some village girl's at the sudden address.
For an instant, the air around Eugard seemed to soften—proof, she thought, that he found her reaction endearing—but she chose not to remark on it.
Eugard simply kept Iris=Yorna caught in his blue eyes, and went on,

"I wish to spend the whole of myself upon you. There is no falsehood in those words. Which is why—"

"You needn't say it—I shall be with you regardless. —I no longer wish to be parted from you, not even for a moment."

Until the very instant you are lost to me, I want to keep this miracle held upon my lips.
That was Iris=Yorna's answer, honest and unfeigned, straight from her heart.

—The stench of a soul burning fills the nose.

There is no pain. Even the heat of a body wrapped whole in flame, scorching and blistering away, feels like nothing more than a light breeze.
Except this breeze cannot be shaken off, and it means to end his existence entirely, turning every newly made body into ash or cinder in turn.

Dousing it in water does not put it out. Trying to pass the fire on to something else does not work. Cutting away the part that starts to burn only makes the whole body ignite at once—an inescapable, final flame. Yes. Final.

There is a feeling. He understands. This flame will not go out. It cannot be extinguished, not until the very end.

"Ahh, ahh, ahh, what in the hell..."

The path of the sword he had sought to master even to the point of taking a blade to himself and falling into undeath.
The flame of the Sunlight Sword, encountered somewhere along that road's course, was now scorching and seeking to burn away entirely the soul of a man named Rowan Segmunt, a man consumed with longing for the Heavenly Sword.

Refusing to remain there any longer, Rowan left the battlefield and ran.
As the leg he planted crumbled to pieces, and if the next step cost him his lower body, he'd waste no more time than that—he'd lop off his own head and let the next self take that corpse's next step in turn.
Repeating that, over and over, Rowan ran. —Away from the Thorned Emperor, and toward the place he had to reach, so that he might arrive there before he burned out entirely.

"What in the hell... I really do have the luck after all."

Spouting flame from every inch of his body, Rowan wore a ghastly, magnificent grin.
By making himself into an undead man, Rowan had come to understand the shape of his own soul through direct experience, and had set the tip of his toe upon the stairway leading toward the Heavenly Sword. This, now, was the next trial, one meant to let him plant his foot surely upon the next step.

Rowan's soul would likely burn itself out before long. But a candle's flame burns brightest just before it goes out.

—For Rowan Segmunt, reaching the Heavenly Sword was the fervent wish of his entire life.

That he could throw his life away for that cause, without exaggeration, he had already proven.
In fact, by experiencing death and coming to know the shape of his own soul, Rowan had steadily drawn nearer to his wish.
And as for reaching the Heavenly Sword—even if it lasted only a single second, that would be enough for Rowan.

If he could only reach it for a single second, the briefest of instants, before his own soul burned away completely, that would be enough.
And what was needed for that was—

"—Well, well, well, here I thought someone was charging in like a bat out of hell, and he shows up on fire! Now that is quite the flashy entrance, wouldn't you say?"

The voice he heard—it belonged to the place he had wanted to reach before he burned out entirely.
Finding the owner of that voice beyond his burning field of vision, Rowan gave thanks for his own karma.
He had made it to the place he'd wanted to reach. All that remained now was to arrive.

Before Rowan, laughing as he burned, stood a boy holding a dark-skinned girl in his arms, and he laughed too.
Inevitably, and ironically, wearing a smile that had something of Rowan in it, the boy said,

"Just now, for the first time, I thought this about you, Dad. —I don't hate you. If anything, I like you."
