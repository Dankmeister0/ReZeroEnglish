Arc 6 Chapter 73: "'Natsuki Subaru'"

—Holding the Book of the Dead inscribed with the name 'Natsuki Subaru,' he stood frozen.

"—"

His black eyes wide with shock, Subaru felt his throat rapidly drying out.
Shock, astonishment—words that mild weren't enough. What struck Subaru in that moment was something far more massive, and far more localized in its damage.
A poison that worked on no one else, yet was lethally effective on Subaru alone—

That was the searing significance with which the Book of the Dead titled 'Natsuki Subaru' pierced through him.

"Wh-why..."

Why did a Book of the Dead that shouldn't exist here, exist here?

The third level of the Pleiades Watchtower, Taygeta, was supposed to hold only Books of the Dead recording the lives of those who had died. A book belonging to the living was a contradiction.
Or was he to believe he'd simply stumbled onto the book of some other person who happened to share the same name?

"If this weren't another world, that might've been convincing..."

The possibility that had crossed his mind was instantly dismissed.
Obviously—this was another world, a place where even the rules of naming differed drastically from what Subaru knew. How could a name like 'Natsuki Subaru' possibly arise here on its own?
Before that, though, there was a more decisive problem.

The title of this book—'Natsuki Subaru'—was written in kanji.

"—"

From everything Subaru had seen so far, even the writing system of this other world was different. So to anyone but Subaru—anyone unfamiliar with the script of that other world—this title would surely look like nothing more than some kind of pictograph.
The more he thought about it, the more finding the Book of the Dead titled 'Natsuki Subaru' here seemed like nothing short of miraculous luck, so overwhelming it could only have happened this way and no other—or—

"—someone's predetermined course."

He couldn't help but feel that was the only explanation.

"—"

Once more, Subaru fell silent, the Book of the Dead still in hand, and turned the matter over in his mind.
The reason Subaru had come across this Book of the Dead here was that he'd tracked it down relying on the faint reaction given off by the Kor Leonis.
He'd searched this archive for a specific book more than once or twice by now, so he knew well enough—finding a targeted book among this vast a collection was an extraordinarily difficult feat.
The kind of thing that was probably impossible without the invisible hand of some god at work.

"Or—if not a god, then someone's..."

—design. Subaru was certain of it.
What remained was the question of this Book of the Dead's authenticity—whether what was written within truly was the life of 'Natsuki Subaru.' And if it was, then what sort of mechanism made that possible?
As an undeniable fact, Subaru was alive, standing here right now.
If a Book of the Dead belonging to this very Subaru existed here—

"...maybe the moment I lost my memories, I got treated as dead. Taygeta's Books of the Dead—that Corridor of Memories—are they observing worlds where I died too...?"

If so, it wasn't inconceivable that his 'death' had been transcribed in some form and given shape as a Book of the Dead. But if that were the case, then what was Return by Death?

"I've wondered before whether it's rewinding time, or remaking the world itself from scratch..."

If he assumed a mechanism by which the Book of the Dead could generate a book for 'Natsuki Subaru,' the far more likely possibility seemed to be the latter. If that were true, then the phenomenon surrounding this Natsuki Subaru wasn't anything so cute as 'Return by Death'—

"Am I an idiot? No—I am an idiot. ...You coward."

Feeling his thoughts spiral off at some bizarre tangent, Subaru cursed himself.
He'd realized at once why his thinking had veered off course—the cause lay in his own cowardice. He was simply afraid of the Book of the Dead.

What would happen if he read 'Natsuki Subaru's' book?

He was afraid of that entirely unknowable, unforeseeable event. So he was chasing baseless hypotheses, putting off the inevitable again and again.
Putting off opening to the very first page of the book in his hands.

"—"

Not unlike the last-Thursdayism thought experiment, the fact that a Book of the Dead for 'Natsuki Subaru' existed here was—strange as it sounded—also a kind of salvation for Subaru.
It seemed like proof that he hadn't simply popped into existence out of nowhere in this very moment.
But at the same time, it also meant that a 'Natsuki Subaru' other than himself had genuinely existed, had walked his own path through this other world—and that Subaru was now trampling over those footprints from above, painting over them.
So all that was left was to confirm it.

"To begin with, even assuming this is 'Natsuki Subaru's' Book of the Dead...where does it start, and where does it end?"

In Meili's case, he'd been able to trace her life from her very early childhood—from around the time she first came to a childhood awareness [■], when memories first began sticking as memories.
But what would happen in Subaru's case? Assuming he could actually read the Book of the Dead—would it, like Meili's, begin with the budding of self-awareness, proceed through his growth, and then end where?

If it was 'Natsuki Subaru' who counted as having died—not the present, memory-lost Natsuki Subaru—then wouldn't it be natural to trace 'Natsuki Subaru's' memories right up until just before those memories were lost?

Or would it instead trace the memories of the present Subaru, who lost his memories and woke inside the tower? In that case, which 'death' would be chosen as the ending?
Or perhaps, like Reid's Book of the Dead, there would only be the blank remnants of memory left behind.
Just as Reid's memories, once recorded in his Book of the Dead, had vanished from within the book so he could be reproduced as this Watchtower's examiner, perhaps 'Natsuki Subaru's' memories had likewise vanished.
Which meant that what he'd find upon opening this book wouldn't be 'Natsuki Subaru's' memories at all, but—

"—in the end, what do you even want, you gutless coward?"

Did he want to see, or not?
Despising himself for being unable to even answer that easily, he berated his cowering heart [■] and let out a breath.
Having come this far, not looking simply wasn't an option. Even if it surfaced as a possibility, actually choosing it was unthinkable.

So, so, so, so, so—
And then, and then, and then, and then, and then—

"—!"

He exhaled hard, and opened the Book of the Dead.
And so began 'Natsuki Subaru's' journey—one whose beginning and end he could not yet know—

—This is seriously bad.

"—"

His face registered something hard against it, while his abdomen burned his brain with a searing sensation.
A ferocious heat that made even breathing difficult, his head hot as though magma had been poured into his veins. And yet his hands and feet had gone cold and heavy—the contradictory sensations shook his very existence, soul and all.

A thought crying out, *what happened?* A thought crying out, *I have to do something.*
The two intermingled, screaming.

But the thought of *I have to do something* was useless.
The moment he moved his hand to try to break the situation open, touching the belly that was the source of that searing heat, his palm came away drenched in blood, and he realized even more blood was soaking into the floor.

—Ah, is all this... my blood?

He couldn't gauge how much blood had spread across the wet floor, but it was well beyond anything on the scale of a blood donation. The source of his life, spilling out, couldn't hold on to the frail flame of that life.
In short, his body had already begun its countdown to death.

And yet his heart [■] hadn't died yet, because this life hurtling toward death still had one last mission left to carry out.

—Raise your voice.

He had to raise his voice.
Danger. It's dangerous. Don't come here. Run. Run, straight ahead, without looking anywhere else.

So he had to warn her of the danger.
He had to warn her it was dangerous, had to tell her not to come into this place.

And yet—

"—"

No voice came out.
What spilled out instead was a hot clot of blood welling up from within. His throat blocked by it, he could no longer get words out, pathetic as that was. He coughed, and the searing heat in his belly spread further.
Pain and suffering, wretchedness and confusion, and then—

"—...baru?"

A voice like a silver bell stepped into a space it must never enter.

"—"

He had to stop that.
And yet he hadn't made it in time.
It was something he'd needed to do even at the cost of his life—but he couldn't, because his own belly had been torn open.

"—!"

A scream rang out, and with a violent sound, someone collapsed to the floor.
That the fallen someone's hand should come to overlap with his own, as he lay collapsed there too, was fate's cruelest joke. Words could hardly do justice to how spiteful a thing fate could be.

"—"

Faintly trembling fingers gently gripped the hand overlapping his. Returning the grip of those clinging fingers, Subaru let out a long, deep breath, along with a clot of blood.

"...ait."

Blood clots spilling out, life draining away. There was no stopping that outflow.

There was nothing to be done anymore. This situation had reached a complete dead end.
Because of none other than this powerless, incompetent man, everything had been ruined.

So that blood-colored vow held no meaning at all.
The foolishness of trying to make a vow anyway, even knowing full well nothing could be done.

Even knowing it was already over.
Even knowing it was about to end.
As if none of it were ending at all, he swore his vow, pathetically.

"I will, without fail—"

—save you. I swear it.


In the next moment, he—Natsuki Subaru, 'Natsuki Subaru,' 'Natsuki Subaru'—lost his life.

—The instant the connection was severed, Subaru felt a hard pain at the back of his head.

"Gah—!"

An impact that reverberated through his skull—for a second he thought he'd been struck from behind, but he quickly realized that was a mistake. Because his body had fallen straight backward, and he could feel his entire back pressed against a cold floor.

"Wh-where..."

Not inside that cold, dim, shabby little building—he was inside Taygeta's archive.
Slowly pulling himself up, shaking his dazed head, Subaru turned it around—and then frantically felt at his own stomach.
There should have been a wound there severe enough to threaten his life.
But—

"It's... it's not there... not there, not there, there's no wound. My stomach—it's not cut...!"

He felt at his stomach again and again, confirming that whatever had caused that searing heat was gone. The most vivid sensation from the scene he'd just witnessed had vanished.
He'd heard that pain too intense could trick the brain into misreading it as 'heat.' That same phenomenon had been at work. In other words—

"—a memory of dying, cut open at the stomach."

An end where his belly had been slashed open by some sharp blade, and he'd drowned in bloody froth and helplessness as he died.
If dying like that had only cost his own life, he might still have forgiven it. But that wasn't the case.

"Satella..."

The name he murmured was that of the girl he hadn't been able to save, right to the very end—a false name.
In that memory, the silver-haired girl he'd come to know had been passing herself off under a false name the whole time she'd been with Subaru. Whatever her reasons had been, it was clear enough they hadn't come from malice.

"...still. What a pathetic way to die."

Pressing a hand to his own forehead, Subaru turned over in his mind everything that had happened to him.
Summoned to another world, he'd wandered the streets with idiotic optimism, gotten tangled up with thugs and nearly died, been saved by Satella, and then helped her search for something she'd lost—

"Not only was I no help at all..."

In the very last moment, he hadn't even managed to warn her of the danger she needed to know, and dragged her down with him.
It wasn't even in the same league as a wasted death—calling it a dog's death would be an insult to dogs.
That's how utterly beyond redemption Natsuki Subaru's—'Natsuki Subaru's'—death had been.

"But."

It wasn't as though he'd gained nothing at all.

"—This is 'Natsuki Subaru's' Book of the Dead."

That much, that much alone, was an absolutely undeniable fact.

"—"

A hopeless, foolish, weak, utterly unredeemable 'Natsuki Subaru.'
One who'd let his ridiculous self-consciousness swell out of proportion, who kept averting his eyes from the filial ingratitude he'd piled up. Who, in the end, had conveniently used his summoning to another world as an escape from reality, and by putting on a show of backward-facing "positivity," tried to deceive himself, everyone around him, everything.

The result of that foolishness had led straight to that tragedy at the Loot House.

What a shallow, naive, foolish mistake he'd made.
How could he go into such a dangerous place so unguarded? What gave a fool like him such baseless confidence? That kind of carelessness deserved blame even back home—hell, even traveling abroad, let alone another world entirely, could get you killed for less. And this was the result.

If he hadn't already died, it was pathetic enough to make him want to kill himself.

"What a hopeless idiot... but."

It had truly been a fatal kind of foolishness, but being able to confirm as much was a stroke of good fortune.
Reining in his anger, holding it back, he examined that fact dispassionately.

Certainly, the Book of the Dead had functioned. That book had recorded 'Natsuki Subaru's' death. And it had confronted him with any number of facts that, while obvious to 'Natsuki Subaru,' Natsuki Subaru himself had had no way of knowing.

—For instance, the circumstances behind how he'd obtained the power of Return by Death.
If this had been the standard, so-called otherworld-summoning template, then whatever had summoned Subaru to this other world should have been a god, or some supernatural existence wielding comparable power.
But that 'Natsuki Subaru' had no memory of contact with any such supernatural existence, nor any sign of having been granted power by someone and being aware of it.
That much Subaru could state with confidence, having witnessed that 'Natsuki Subaru's' final moments firsthand.

At the very last, he had blurted out some stubborn refusal to give up, but in that instant, 'Natsuki Subaru' could not have had any certainty that he'd leap over death and turn back time.
At least, that was how it had been for him. —Thinking that, he suddenly noticed something.

That he wasn't managing to view himself and 'Natsuki Subaru' with any real objectivity.
The cause was obvious.

"...I'm sunk deeper into this than I was with Meili's."

Even reading Meili's Book of the Dead and reliving her life had come with a fairly intense sensation of his mind being dragged along. His personality had half-melted together with hers, to the point that a hallucinatory Meili had appeared in his mind, saying whatever she pleased and toying with him at will.
Though he understood that, ultimately, that had been a false Meili conjured up by Subaru's own consciousness—a phantom born of inner discord, with no relation whatsoever to the real Meili.

"—"

But this time, the Book of the Dead was in a different league entirely from his experience with Meili's.
Whatever else you could say, the confusion right after reading Meili's book had still come with clues that let him tell himself apart from her. Differences in sex, in age, in values—all sorts of things.
Using those as handholds, he'd been able to gradually peel himself away from her, to convince himself they were separate beings.

This time, though, that wasn't going to work.
The opponent here was none other than himself. A self that wasn't himself—a situation that shouldn't even be able to exist—was unexpectedly shoving Natsuki Subaru into a battle against himself.

A battle against himself. —There was hardly a more clichéd phrase to hear, but it described, precisely and concisely, exactly the situation Subaru now found himself in.

And in truth, the odds weren't in his favor.
Overlapping with oneself was close to an act of painting oneself over. Like dipping a clean brush into water used to rinse off black paint labeled 'Natsuki Subaru,' he was being dyed through and through.

"—Emilia. Emilia, Emilia, Emilia, Emilia, Emilia."

Against the sensation of being painted over, Subaru chanted Emilia's name like an incantation.
The reason he chanted "Emilia" was simple. The 'Natsuki Subaru' within the Book of the Dead didn't recognize her as Emilia.
He had believed the false name he'd been given all the way to the bitter end, playing out his role as the fool to the very last.
That, then, was the difference between the present Subaru and 'Natsuki Subaru.'

"—"

Teeth chattering, quailing at the sensation of himself being whittled away, Subaru looked down at his feet. There lay the single book he had unconsciously let slip from his hands.

"—"

He had now witnessed, once, the point at which 'Natsuki Subaru' died.
Come to think of it, strangely enough, the Book of the Dead had begun from the very moment he'd arrived in this other world, from the moment he'd shouted about the change in his surroundings with an utterly idiotic look on his face. With Meili's, it had properly begun from around the time she'd first grown aware [■] of herself—but no, none of that mattered right now.

The problem was what came next in this Book of the Dead.

"You're the same way too, aren't you, 'Natsuki Subaru'..."

Thanks to the Book of the Dead, he had presumably just witnessed the "death" of the very first 'Natsuki Subaru' summoned to this other world.
But that was only the beginning.
This 'Natsuki Subaru' too must have made use of the power of Return by Death.
Or perhaps it hadn't been Return by Death at all, but some power that had manifested in an altogether different form. —No, that would be more natural.

Freely turning back time—that explanation carried far more weight.
If this was the 'Natsuki Subaru' that Emilia, Beatrice, Ram, Echidna, Julius, Meili, Shaula, Patrasche, Jaian, and surely a great many others he'd come to meet were counting on to accomplish great things, then that much should be within his reach.
If so, then—

"It has to be further ahead than this."

'Natsuki Subaru,' who had seemed no different at all from Natsuki Subaru.
But there had to be something decisive that had turned that 'Natsuki Subaru' into 'Natsuki Subaru.' In search of it, Subaru picked up the book once more.
And then, closing his eyes, he breathed deep.

"—"

Book still in hand, Subaru counted his heartbeats—one, two. Calming his heart [■] like that, he slowly began to walk.
His pace was slow, but without hesitation, and straight ahead.
Eventually, Subaru arrived in front of a bookshelf, and reaching out his hand—

"—Book two."

The next volume of 'Natsuki Subaru' was faintly asserting its presence to the Kor Leonis.

—'Natsuki Subaru's' path forward was clumsy, unplanned, and beyond saving.

"Utterly hopeless. An amateur through and through, sloppy in every movement. No Divine Protection, no skill, and just when you'd hope he could at least wring out some cleverness, there's none of that either. Whatever does he think he's doing, challenging this?"

Toyed with however an overwhelming enemy pleased, cut apart again and again without any proper chance to fight back.
And all around him as he was wounded lay a blood-soaked old man and a golden-haired girl, both fallen. He could save neither of them. He couldn't even move.

"Slowly, slowly, slowly, slowly, slowly—writhe."

Eventually, his stomach slashed, his eyes slashed, his sight stolen away, he spent the time remaining before death—a fixed limit placed on his own life—huddled close against terror in a world gone dark.
Right to the very last, frightened, trembling, terrified, pathetically—

"Oi, did you actually stab him?"

"Couldn't be helped. If he'd gotten out to the street, it'd be way more than just a hassle."

"Ahh, this one's done for. His guts are torn up, he's gonna die. ...His clothes are soaked through, too."

What a leisurely conversation to be having, right over the head of the man lying there.
If he didn't occupy his mind with such pointless thoughts, he'd have to face the shocking pain stabbing through his back.
The way he'd mastered methods of fleeing from pain, of protecting himself—he was thoroughly [■] disgusted with just how practiced he'd become at it.

A truly wasted death, a dog's death, beyond saving.
Calling a girl who wasn't Satella by the name Satella, getting cursed out for it by the girl herself, failing to grasp circumstances that should have been blindingly obvious, and then—having abandoned all thought—getting killed by an opponent he'd underestimated.
A fitting end. Why couldn't he, from start to finish, live out every single minute and second without holding back, pouring in everything he had?

Enough already. This world is over. He'd known that from the start.
He'd come here knowing full well from the outset that he'd witness "death." So, without something decisive, this world is over. It's over, it already ended, so move on to the next already. Next, move to the next, next next next next. Otherwise it hurts, it hurts, it hurts hurts hurts hurts, it hurts so much, it's unbearable, so painful he can't stand it, so even so he has to carry something forward into the next—

A death that came while sleeping, for all the simplicity of that ending, carried a cruelty like cold poison being poured straight into the veins.
One might think that a "death" arriving so quietly you couldn't even notice you'd died would surely be an easy thing, compared to the deaths he'd known until now, inseparable as they'd been from pain and suffering.
But that was never, ever the case.

Why had he died? Was he even really dead in the first place?
Among people who fear "death," no small number probably wish to die as if slipping into sleep—but having lived through it himself, Subaru could say: that's not a "death" worth wishing for.

"Death" has its own meaning as "death."

The close of a life, its final curtain, needs an ending you can actually recognize as one.
Amid confusion and despair, shock and desperate longing, Subaru reached for the next Book of the Dead. He had to know what had happened. He had to know what had killed him—

—Mystery calling forth mystery, he relived deaths that made no sense, that came without reason.

Endings piling atop endings, tragedies repeating over and over.
He had rejected dying as if slipping into sleep, but dying as if shattered, as if gouged apart, was hardly something to welcome either.

One after another, his life was targeted, taken, broken—and in the end, betrayed.

He stopped understanding why.
Why did he have to save the blue-haired girl who had killed him?

Why was he so desperate to save her, and why, even as she broke down and sank to her knees, did she still push so hard against Subaru's back?
Why had her words given him strength, made him feel he could keep moving forward?

Beyond the betrayal, he longed for something.
For a wish that, in truth, had never been betrayed at all. For a world where that wish would be affirmed as right.

That 'Natsuki Subaru' could actually save someone—an empty, sorrowful misconception—he forced open by brute strength, offering up his own life as the price.

He met Emilia, met Puck, met Felt, met old man Rom, met Reinhard, met Elsa, met Beatrice, met Ram, met Rem, met Roswaal, met Petra, met the people of Arlam Village—

"Guh, bl—...!"

Pressing a hand to his mouth, meeting yet another death bearing down on him, Subaru sank to his knees in Taygeta's archive. Unable to hold himself up, he pitched forward straight onto the ground.

"Haah, haah..."

His breath came ragged, and a torrent of cold sweat soaked his entire body.
Hot or cold, bitter or sweet, agonizing or pleasant—like or dislike, black or white, his feelings had all blurred together into one indistinguishable mass, with no telling which was the right answer.
It felt like being struck over and over, from every angle, without rest.

"—That makes eight books."

That was how many volumes he'd gotten through since finding 'Natsuki Subaru's' Book of the Dead and beginning to read.
Though calling them "volumes" was strange in itself—there was no numbering assigned to them, and by rights, a book placed in this archive shouldn't have anything like a "next volume in the same series."

Strangely enough, too, the mechanism seemed to be that he couldn't find the next 'Natsuki Subaru' book until he'd finished the one before it—even the loops were proceeding in proper order.
Without skipping over any of the "deaths" in between, he was managing to trace 'Natsuki Subaru's' footsteps.

And so, as he traced those footsteps, he thought: what an honest fool, what an incorrigible greenhorn 'Natsuki Subaru' was.

Seeing the fate awaiting the last book especially—the eighth Book of the Dead—he couldn't help but think exactly that.
After the start of the Royal Selection was declared, 'Natsuki Subaru' had quarreled with Emilia at the castle, done nothing but wound her heart [■], and died amid confusion without ever apologizing or reflecting on it.

As the one reading the book, his own consciousness sank nearly indistinguishable from the Book of the Dead's subjective viewpoint.
Being the very same person as its subject, he ought to have been affected all the more—and even so, it was unbearable.

Part of him thought: why couldn't she understand?
While another part of him thought: how could he possibly lament her not understanding, after that?

"Don't get caught up in the past..."

Because it was a tragedy he'd just witnessed with his own eyes, it hurt enough to feel like his body was being torn apart.
But that was the past. It was, after all, an event that had already happened.

Even if, for the 'Natsuki Subaru' meeting "death" in that instant, it was happening right in front of him.
Even if, for Subaru, synchronized as he was with the 'Natsuki Subaru' facing that moment, it was likewise happening right in front of him.

That was the past. A past that would remain as an unforgettable wound.
So, right now, in this moment, catch your breath and escape the shock. Forget it. Who killed Emilia and the others? Who caused that horrific scene in the village?

Otherwise, his heart [■] would break.

What would he do if Subaru's heart [■] broke here and now, and he could no longer stand back up?
Who would do what for him? 'Natsuki Subaru' wasn't here. So there was no one left but Subaru to somehow see this through.

"Not... yet..."

—He still hadn't found it.

"—"

There had to be something decisive, something given only to 'Natsuki Subaru.'
That would be the key that clearly separated Natsuki Subaru from 'Natsuki Subaru.' At the very least, this journey through the Books of the Dead wouldn't end until he obtained that key.

The decisive key by which Natsuki Subaru would emerge, transformed, as 'Natsuki Subaru.'
The key that could make him a savior capable of catching Emilia, Beatrice, Rem, Ram, Echidna, Julius, Meili, Shaula, Patrasche, Jaian, as they fell.

So far, there was no sign of it.
'Natsuki Subaru' hardly had the spirit or the capacity to fulfill such a grand role as savior or hero, someone who helped others.
All he had was an exceptional refusal to give up, and the good fortune of being surrounded by wonderful people.
But that couldn't be all there was.

Not some shapeless, vague "something" like that.
There had to be something more obvious, more clear-cut—an all-purpose key, plain to see at a glance, that everyone could accept as the reason he was able to do what he did.
It has to exist. So, in search of it—

"—Book nine."

Once more, he plunged into that swirling cycle of "death," churning with confusion and chaos, betrayal and despair.
If he could wish for anything—'Natsuki Subaru.' Hurry up and become you.

—Before my heart [■] can no longer bear your footsteps, your wounds, your "deaths."

—"Deaths" piling up, one atop another.
—Endings, accumulating, one after another.

Every time it hurt, every time he suffered, every time he lost something, every time something was taken from him, he heard the sound of his heart [■] cracking.

Not once but twice, not twice but three times, not three times but four, he was driven into a corner by circumstance and lost his life, and still he held his ground to break through the closed-off maze of confusion.

"There has to be something...!"

"Aaaaaugh—!!"

He slammed his head against the floor.
While he'd been watching, it had been bearable. But the instant he returned, his heart [■] was thrown into turmoil by shame.

"Dad... Mom...!"

There had been a 'Natsuki Subaru' who exchanged words with his father and mother, both of them, and apologized to them.
There had been a 'Natsuki Subaru' who, cast into another world and resolving to live on in this place, said his goodbyes to the two of them.

"Ugh, hnh, ehh..."

It made him sick. Tears spilled over.
What was agonizing, what was unbearable, was that he understood 'Natsuki Subaru's' feelings painfully well—and that he also understood, just as clearly, that his parents would forgive him for it.

Please don't forgive me. Curse me. He'd wanted them to call him an ungrateful son.
But they hadn't.
Neither his father nor his mother would go along with what Subaru wanted. To console himself, he'd wanted his parents to say something petty, something small—something human. It was impossible.
Subaru's father, Kenichi. Subaru's mother, Naoko. —They had been the best parents anyone could ask for.

Part of him was glad of that. Part of him agreed with 'Natsuki Subaru's' choice. And part of him, though he had no right to be saved, was trying to be saved anyway—and his heart [■] was ugly for it.

—Is this it? Is this the cause? Is this the reason he could become 'Natsuki Subaru'?

"No... no, no, no! That's not it! It's not something like this!"

Clawing at his own head, punching his throbbing forehead with his fist, Subaru cursed himself.
What Subaru was after wasn't some spiritual salvation. He was after something more clear-cut, a special key that functioned as a proper, tangible effect—a power.

A special power unknown to Subaru, known only to 'Natsuki Subaru.'

Just as Subaru had succeeded in manifesting the Kor Leonis, there had to be some special "something" that only 'Natsuki Subaru' had awakened to, and that—

"—that has to be the key to changing this situation. I have to believe that..."

Touching the spot where, moments ago, the fatal gash to his stomach should have been, Subaru murmured to himself.
The memory had begun to overlap with what he'd seen in Meili's Book of the Dead. The attack on the mansion. Meili's beloved, troublesome sister. Elsa's assault. 'Natsuki Subaru's' natural enemy.

But all of it belonged to a world that had already ended.
A world 'Natsuki Subaru' had already overcome.

What mattered wasn't empathy for 'Natsuki Subaru.' What mattered was exposing—and obtaining for himself—the trick that only 'Natsuki Subaru' should possess, the sleight of hand only he should be pulling off.
For that purpose, and that purpose alone, he read the Books of the Dead.


—Because that alone was why Natsuki Subaru kept "dying all over again."


"Tell me, 'Natsuki Subaru'! —Why are you special?! Why can only you get to be special?! There's a 'something,' isn't there?!"

"Some 'something' must have changed you! Some 'something' must have made you into something other than yourself, other than the hopeless wretch you were!"

"Change me—weak, pathetic, tiny, useless piece of shit that I am! I'm sick of this! I don't want to watch everyone suffer anymore, I can't stand it!"

"'Something'... 'something'! It's there, isn't it?! It'd be strange if there wasn't a 'something'... there's a 'something,' and that's why you're... different from me... otherwise..."

—Otherwise, he'd have no choice but to admit it.

"That you're just like me—weak, tiny, an utterly powerless nobody..."

—How many times he'd nearly sunk to his knees, and each time someone had struck him on the back to keep him going; because he wanted to repay everyone who had been good to him, because he'd been shown kindness and wanted to give something back.

"I'm begging you, 'Natsuki Subaru.' Please. I'm asking you, so just stop..."

—It would have been so much better if you'd simply been superhuman.
—He'd wanted you to be a being utterly unlike him, one who had transcended weakness in both heart and body [■].

—He'd wanted to be able to convince himself that was why you could do what he himself could not.

And yet—

Wrenching the book free as though tearing it out, struck over the head by death after repeated death, his heart [■] shattered a little more each time, he pressed on desperately, desperately, gnawing away at his own life like a man slurping mud.
And still, right down to the very end, he couldn't bring himself to abandon that one possibility to cling to—

"—"

He opened the book he had seized.
He opened it, braced for his brain to be churned, his heart [■] trampled, his very soul violated.
Because losing that last hope was far more agonizing than any pain or suffering could be.

"You understand, don't you, 'Natsuki Subaru'..."

As if seeking agreement, he called out to someone who wasn't there.
That his voice had lost nearly all its vigor by then couldn't be helped.

After all, this wasn't an opponent worth psyching himself up for.
Not that much of a man. —Even as he prayed it wouldn't be so, his fingers drew out the Book of the Dead as though hauling it in hand over hand.
And then, surely with the feeling of seeking a final blow—

"—Yeah, I understand."

"—"

It was a white, white world.
Before he knew it, Subaru had shifted—not into the archive, nor into some shapeless existence overlapping with a memory, but into a familiar place, one that wasn't here.
And there—

"I understand, Natsuki Subaru."

"—"

"After all, you're me, aren't you."

Standing motionless in that white world, a familiar pair of sanpaku eyes was waiting for Natsuki Subaru.
