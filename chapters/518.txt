Arc 7 Chapter 20: "Emperor / Merchant / Natsuki Subaru"

The fall of the fortress city of Guaral — that was the next move Abel had in mind.

Subaru's brain chewed on the content of that declaration, boldly stated as it was, and immediately arrived at a conclusion.

That it was a reckless plan.

"It's a walled city, for what it's worth—surrounded on all sides by big walls, with the gates always under watch. That's not the kind of place you casually talk about bringing down."

"Ho, spoken as if you'd seen it yourself... ah, but you have seen it, haven't you. Since nothing useful ever seems to come out of your mouth, I nearly forgot."

"Great, your sarcasm's razor-sharp, wonderful. But that edge only works on someone who'll sit down and talk it out with you like this. It doesn't work on someone holding a weapon."

At the center of the meeting hall, Subaru and Abel glared at each other across the fire. Abel, whose emotions never showed past that oni mask, was now someone whose thoughts Subaru couldn't fathom at all, and Subaru felt irritation mixed with bewilderment.

There was no way Abel hadn't already thought of the legitimate concern Subaru had just raised. And yet he'd said something like "the fall of the walled city" anyway—

"...Do you mean using a secret passage to force your way into the city?"

Beside the breath-holding Subaru, it was Rem who murmured that in a quiet voice. Sitting with her knees folded to one side, Rem stared fixedly at Abel with those pale blue eyes. Not so much a glare as a probing gaze.

Receiving it, Abel shrugged. "Needless to say."

"Just as Natsuki Subaru said, the checkpoint set up at the city's main gate is the first wall. Slipping past it and getting into the city is an essential first step."

"Even if we managed to get in, there seemed to be a great many Imperial soldiers inside the city. Even if we could ignore the checkpoint, I think there are too many enemies."

"Hmph."

As Abel laid out the obstacles to Guaral's fall, Rem countered him with logical precision. Abel clicked his tongue at her stance, but Subaru's bewilderment only deepened further. The cause, of course, was the sight of Rem arguing head-on with Abel.

Given the content of the argument, Rem's position could be called the same side as Subaru's, opposed to attacking Guaral. But her grasp of the situation was, if anything, too on-point—as if, in the short time she'd spent in Guaral, she'd carefully sized up exactly that point.

"—Natsuki Subaru, how do you understand the disparity in forces between attacker and defender in battle?"

"Huh? Disparity in forces between attack and defense... you mean like the three-to-one attacker's rule?"

"The three-to-one attacker's rule... indeed, aptly put."

Asked, Subaru answered reflexively, and Abel nodded as if impressed. The "three-to-one attacker's rule" was a way of thinking about troop strength in battle: the attacking side should ideally have three times the forces of the defending side. This comes down to a difference in objectives between attack and defense—while the attacker must defeat the enemy, the defender need not do more than repel them, drive them back, or simply block the attack.

In this case, the "People of Shudrak" led by Abel had to occupy the city to take Guaral, while the Imperial soldiers and guards garrisoned there could achieve their objective simply by repelling this attack, or ultimately just holing up in the town. Bridging this gap in the rate of achieving objectives was said to require that much more troop strength, but—

"There's no way we could ever muster three times the city's forces. Mizelda-san, how many people are in this settlement?"

"Eighty-two, all told. Counting Abel and that man—Flop—call it a hundred."

"I don't know how you're counting that pretty-faced guy, but that makes it a mere hundred. But that city, rough estimate, had more than three times that in fighting strength, didn't it?"

Setting aside Mizelda's arithmetic, Subaru estimated Guaral's strength that way. The city's scale fell just short of ten thousand people, but the guards protecting the city's peace should number accordingly. On top of that, the Imperial soldiers whose camp had been burned had joined them.

—Those Imperial soldiers, Todd included among them.

"――――"

"It seems the basic knowledge, at least, is in your head. But the reason you fell silent looks to be a fear other than concern over the disparity in forces."

"...The fact that I'm scared is true, but the fact that the disparity in forces is dire is also true."

Having his inner thoughts read aloud, Subaru clicked his tongue, feeling caught out. In truth, hearing that they'd attack Guaral, the first obstacle Subaru thought of was Todd. Just imagining facing him again made his insides twist. To begin with, judged by the "three-to-one attacker's rule," it wasn't even worth discussing.

"I can't think of any way to overturn this disparity in forces, other than a soldier worth a thousand joining our side, or the enemy commander being incompetent to a fatal degree."

"Unfortunately, I doubt either can be expected. At Shudrak's true strength, the rank and file could be scattered, but even so, being enveloped by sheer numbers would grind them down. On top of that, the enemy commander, I hear, is Zikr Osman. A steady, dull tactician, but without openings."

"Zikr... I've heard that name."

He must have heard that name from Todd's mouth, back before things went sour between them. The Imperial camp, already burned to the ground—Subaru recalled that the one who'd commanded that operation was someone called Zikr. But since the camp had been burned, he'd assumed that person had been struck down too.

"Those at the camp were rank-and-file troops. From the start, to conceal the true objective of the military operation, the information given to them was minimal... Facing a small tribe in the forest, there was no need for a Second General to go to the front lines."

"So the commander was holed up in the city from the very start?"

"In truth, had nothing gone wrong, that alone would have sufficed to achieve satisfactory results. That the plan was thrown out of joint was due to my own command and... ah, your existence as well, was it not, Natsuki Subaru."

"Ngh..."

Nastily, Abel tried to force Subaru to face the responsibility he wanted to look away from. Grinding his teeth at that, Subaru put a hand to his mouth.

In any case, it was highly likely that this Second General Zikr was stationed in Guaral. The Imperial ranks were said to run from First General down through Second General, Third General, and so on—being the second rank from the top suggested a considerable degree of skill.

"So what, exactly? On top of being outnumbered, the enemy commander is skilled enough to be counted from the top down. And on top of that, because we poked the hornet's nest, they're on alert too?"

"Just so. Do you now understand the weight of responsibility?"

"I'm saying it's not even worth discussing!"

Abel needled at Subaru's shallow thinking, but the real problem lay elsewhere. It was Abel's stance of refusing to abandon the fight even in a situation with no chance of victory whatsoever. Firmly, Subaru kept insisting that challenging it would be pointless. To begin with—

"I'm flatly against fighting itself. I said as much clearly when we left here before, didn't I. I just... I just want to go back with Rem."

"But that this is difficult has already been proven. Do you think your enemies are limited to the Imperial soldiers who entered Guaral? Can you convince yourself that some other village or town would be safe?"

"――――"

"Wherever you go, your peace can no longer be bought. I believe I've given you time enough to feel that keenly, to let it soak into your blood. Or is the poison still insufficient?"

Abel's mocking gaze pecked at Subaru's weak-willed spirit. Tasting a pain as if his flesh were being carved away, Subaru let out a long breath, and, infuriatingly, had no choice but to admit that Abel's words were true.

The hardship Subaru had tasted in the walled city had built a mental wall inside him. From now on, even if he tried to flee the country with Rem, wherever he went besides Guaral, the same unease and vigilance would not fade. Subaru's complacency had been lost, in exchange for five deaths.

"—Then how about this: I sell you out to the enemy instead."

Having had his escape routes blocked off, having been berated for his shallow thinking, Subaru twisted his cheek and asked that. For a moment, the air in the meeting hall grew taut at the words Subaru had thrown out. Clearly, at the edge of his vision, he saw Rem beside him widen her eyes.

But Abel, the very one being spoken of as sold out, gave a small laugh.

"Hmph. It seems your head has finally started working properly. But—"

"That's not a discussion we can have, Subaru."

Faster than the eye could follow, Mizelda drew her dagger and laid the blade against Subaru's throat.

At that swift move, Subaru caught his breath and looked up at the face of the tall chieftain who had moved in an instant. In Mizelda's striking, beautiful eyes dwelt the cold hunter she was at her core.

"We have already decided—we fight alongside Abel. That being the result of the 'Lifeblood Ritual,' there is nothing else, if it is the wish of one we've recognized as kin."

"...I know it's shameless of me to say this, given that I had all of you help me get Rem back. But is everyone really all right with this?"

Mizelda radiated firm resolve, but Subaru's question leapt past her. As chieftain, Mizelda had to embody the way of the "People of Shudrak," so she couldn't be persuaded. Still, the other Shudrak might have different opinions. Taritta, Kuna, Holly, and the others here might think differently.

"You heard what she said earlier too. The disparity in forces is stark, and the enemy is a battle-hardened general. It's plain from the start that fighting gives us no chance of winning..."

"I think, maybe, Subaru's got it wrong-o."

"Got it wrong...?"

Seeking other opinions, Subaru's question was answered first, surprisingly, by Holly. At the edge of the meeting hall, gnawing on dried meat as she listened to the conversation, Holly gazed at the immobilized Subaru with round eyes.

"If we're talking win or lose, backing down here is a loss for us too-o. If we can't fight for our kin, our souls get stained-o."

"Stained souls... you mean like pride, or facing your ancestral spirits, that kind of thing?"

"Yeah-yeah, Subaru gets it-o."

Even smiling, Holly affirmed Subaru's words. But that wasn't proof they understood each other—it was proof they didn't.

Subaru, too, understood that such thinking existed. Pride, family name—invisible proofs that didn't directly contribute to the body—that there existed an attitude of cherishing those, treating them as more important than anything, was certain. But that, however far it went, was a way of thinking that couldn't coexist with Subaru's own belief that nothing mattered more than life.

"Kuna! Taritta-san! Do you two feel the same way?!"

"...I ain't as far gone on it as Holly or the chief, but..."

"—. I follow Sister's decision. That is my will."

"I see, huh..."

The two others he'd turned to didn't give Subaru the answer he'd hoped for either. He'd thought maybe Kuna, being a bit off from the typical Shudrak mold, might be different, but that too proved to be Subaru's miscalculation. As for Taritta, absolutely obedient to Mizelda, it went without saying.

And so, with the dagger still held to the friendless Subaru's throat, it looked as though the standoff would drag on—

"...Mizelda-san, please withdraw your weapon. That person has no intention of handing you over to Abel-san as an enemy."

Of all people, it was Rem who told Mizelda to withdraw the dagger. Receiving her gaze, Mizelda narrowed her eyes.

"Are you giving me orders, Rem. You have not undergone the 'Lifeblood Ritual.' I permit you to stay in the settlement at Subaru's request, but you have no grounds to talk back."

"Then all the more reason to withdraw your weapon. That person underwent the ritual—the 'Lifeblood Ritual'—and was welcomed as kin. To injure him would not be a good thing."

"Mm..."

Mizelda had tried to intimidate Rem with a sharpened gaze, but instead found herself at a loss for words against Rem's resolute rebuttal. In the end, she swiftly sheathed the dagger at her waist. Releasing Subaru, she stared intently at Rem, and said,

"This time, your words were correct. But should Abel and Subaru's opinions split again, I will side with Abel. Do not forget that."

"Is that because that person's eyes suit someone of thoroughly wicked body odor?"

"A somewhat sharp look has its own charm. But I prefer a handsome face."

The touch-and-go scene calmed down, but for a final exchange, it was a rather deflating conversation. In any case, Subaru, freed from a situation that had nearly seen his throat slit, gently stroked his own neck where the blade had touched, and looked at Rem.

"—. What."

"...No, I was just confused about which to react to—the fact that you covered for me, or the fact that my face just got trashed."

"I didn't say anything about your face. I was talking about your eyes. And your body odor. It makes my nose bend. Sit further away."

"Now, of all times...!?"

Rem's attitude entered an ice age, and Subaru was wounded as she put distance between their seats. But inwardly, an even more complicated confusion tormented Subaru. In truth, he had no idea why she'd said something that covered for him. It might have simply been that she hated letting something illogical slide, or it might not have been that at all. Was thinking so merely Subaru's wishful bias?

Because, after all, she hadn't unpacked her luggage at the inn in Guaral—

"The conversation's been derailed. But even if you managed to slip past the Shudrak's watch, the idea of selling me out would be pointless."

"...Thanks so much for reconnecting that derailed conversation. I'll ask anyway, since you're offering. Why."

"You too spent time in the city, however briefly. Then surely word reached your ears of how the capital is currently being governed?"

"How the capital is... oh! Right, that's right! You..."

Slapping his knee on the spot, Subaru rose to his feet without meaning to. Then, drawing every eye in the room, he called out, "Flop-san!"

Unable to keep up with the shifting situation, Flop had been in a daze, and he turned with a "Hm?"

"Sorry to leave you behind, but let me confirm something. The Imperial decree Flop-san talked about in Guaral... or rather, the Emperor's proclamation."

"The Emperor's proclamation... you mean the trouble in the capital?"

Snapping his fingers, Flop showed his understanding, and Subaru nodded.

The impression of the fierce back-and-forth he'd repeated six times over with Todd right after was burned vividly into his brain, but that conversation with Flop had also taken place in Guaral. Recalling it, Flop smoothed back his long bangs and said,

"Our Empire, ever smoldering with sparks of unrest, apparently had some sort of disturbance break out in its capital. It's been quite a while since His Majesty the current Emperor took the throne, and there'd been no sign of internal strife—the Imperial subjects were enjoying peaceful days. But then, this happened."

"The trouble in the capital spilled outward, and the Emperor himself was stepping in personally to resolve it—that was the gist, right? And that was supposed to be an unprecedented announcement..."

"That's right. In fact, it's the first such thing since His current Majesty took the throne. Still, he has a proven track record of running the Empire up to now. No need to worry! Long live the Vollachian Empire!"

Throwing both hands up, Flop unwittingly dug at Subaru's old wound. It was the same kind of praise for the Empire Subaru had been forced to listen to endlessly at the Imperial camp, but now he had no room in his heart to innocently ride along with it. In any case, the issue wasn't the praise—it was the Emperor's movements described just before.

For the Emperor to personally act to quell trouble in the capital was an unusual situation. And moreover, if the Emperor were to give orders before a public audience—

"You, standing right here—what standing, what identity does that make you? Can you prove you're not some crazy lunatic?"

"Prove it, you say? What need is there for that?"

"What?"

Sitting with one knee raised, Abel scoffed at Subaru's suspicion. Then, placing a hand on his own chest as if to assert his own existence—

"Setting aside those thoughtless fools who've turned against me, if at this point you still treat me as some deluded fool spouting nonsense, how do you intend to explain the situation you find yourself in?"

"That's..."

"Stop grasping at the kind of convenient idea you couldn't even fool yourself with, just to gain peace of mind. Eliminate the possibilities that don't fit, one by one as fitting, and what remains is simply fact."

Abel's words were harsh, but Subaru himself was aware that his current logic was weak. That the oni-masked man before him was an impostor pretending to be Emperor, and that Subaru and all the "People of Shudrak" were being deceived by that lie—that was a hopeless scenario, and hard to credit besides. In fact, Abel had used Shudrak's strength and the information he'd drawn out of Subaru to win against the Imperial soldiers. That wasn't something explainable by mere habitual lying.

"But then what about the capital? If you're talking about personally taking command, there's no way the Emperor wouldn't appear in public."

"Then he shall appear, surely. A double closely resembling me... to keep the Emperor's absence from being noticed, the finest double available is used. —Chisha Gold."

"Chisha...?"

An unfamiliar name, but so that was the Emperor's body double, then. In a nation where an imperialist, survival-of-the-fittest mindset breathed life into everything, it was easy enough to imagine the Emperor using a body double as insurance against assassination. But for that body double to be used by the enemy, stealing the Emperor's own position—that was putting the cart before the horse.

"Silence. I'm well aware."

"Is that right. Well, this side's caught in a nasty bind because of it. If only the Empire were stable..."

The problem Subaru carried would have amounted to nothing more than getting the cold shoulder from a memory-less Rem. He should have been struggling through a journey to escape the Vollachian Empire in order to reunite with Emilia and the others. And yet, by some twist of fate, here they were in this situation instead.

"Um, um um, you see, milord?"

It was Flop who called out to the sour-faced Subaru. Furrowing his handsome brow, tilting his head at quite a steep angle, he said,

"Listening in on this for a while now, it strikes me you're having a rather strange conversation with the village chief, wouldn't you say. Frankly, I'm already surprised by the joke about bringing down Guaral, but..."

"Joke... no, never mind that. Um, Flop-san, I was going to explain."

"Ah, please, by all means, I'd love an explanation! Otherwise, I'll have no choice but to take what I've heard at reasonable face value and draw the following conclusion!"

Saying so, Flop jabbed a finger sharply at Abel. And then—

"That the masked village chief over there might just be His Imperial Majesty—however unlikely an idea that is!"

"――――"

"Oh dear, oh dear, oh dear? Falling silent at that leaves me feeling rather troubled, milord. Fortunately, I have quite a reputation for jumping to conclusions. So I also possess the flexibility to retract a thought right away, if needed..."

"—You, that was a display of dishonesty as if drawn from a textbook."

It had been a careless conversation to have, if concealment had been the intent. Naturally, then, Flop, listening in from nearby, arrived at the correct conclusion on his own. And Abel, of all people, showed displeasure that Flop had reached the truth by himself.

Abel loaded his gaze with contempt and spat "dishonesty" at none other than Subaru. It was a rebuke aimed at Subaru for never explaining any of the circumstances to Flop, whom he'd brought all the way to the Shudrak settlement. But being looked down on by Abel over that was something Subaru found hard to swallow.

"I'm the dishonest one? Like you have any room to talk! Besides, I know as well as anyone that this isn't something you go blabbing about carelessly!"

"Then you ought to have settled the matter before bringing him along. It seems you still fail to grasp the situation you're in. In Guaral, chased by the enemy, I gave you ample time to think."

"――――"

"You keep at your side something more important than your own life, so why compromise on your thinking? If you had no intention of placing him beside you as an equal, it was a mistake to bring him back at all."

Something more important than his own life—hearing that, Subaru thought of Rem beside him. What followed cut Subaru like a blade too. Abel's argument was simple and clear: in a position where he had to protect Rem, why was he cutting corners? Of course, Subaru had never intended to cut corners, never meant to compromise. But from the eyes of Abel, whose thinking ran far deeper and farther than Subaru's, Subaru's own thinking fell utterly short, failed to reach, wasn't even worth discussing.

He was being accused of bringing along someone he couldn't trust enough to explain the circumstances to. Abel coldly cut down the naivety of Subaru's thinking—that he couldn't drag Flop and Medium into this—declaring it a ridiculous notion.

"I..."

"—If I might interrupt the flow of conversation a little myself, village chief!"

Subaru fell silent, but in his place, it was Flop who raised a hand vigorously. He stepped forward with a "there, there" motion, plopped himself down cross-legged on the spot, and faced Abel's oni mask head-on.

"Or perhaps you're not the village chief. What ought I call you?"

"I hold no title at present. I go by Abel, but call me whatever you like."

"I see! Then, given the atmosphere, I'll continue calling you village chief. Would the ladies over there mind as well?"

"Ah, you're a handsome fellow. Anything's forgiven."

"Sister..."

Setting aside what had already become a routine exchange between the sisters, Flop laughed and said, "My thanks!" Still laughing, he struck both his knees firmly with his hands and said,

"It's a bit frightening not getting flatly denied, but I'd like to return to the topic from before. Village chief, you asked me about a secret passage into the city, but..."

"Ah, I asked. Do you have any idea, merchant?"

"I do! —though I nearly answered that way; forgive me. If it's to be used to attack Guaral, I'm afraid I cannot answer!"

Thrusting his palm forward, Flop declared this with a bright, clear expression. At that firm way of speaking, Subaru, listening beside him, widened his eyes. Beyond the oni mask, hadn't Abel's eyes narrowed slightly too? That was how much genuine seriousness Flop's words carried, seriousness that didn't show in his tone.

"――――"

Flop was conspicuous for his rather outlandish speech and behavior, but he wasn't stupid. He too had guessed that the true identity of the Abel before him was someone of exalted standing. Since it was neither confirmed nor denied, it could be called a guess close to certainty. In other words, he'd just said "NO" to the presumed Emperor.

"...Do you understand what kind of meaning that carries?"

"Of course I do, village chief who is no one in particular. If it comes to a fight, I refuse to take part in it. I'd rather avoid harming anyone with my own knowledge as well."

"A fantasy. In reality, malice comes at you without regard for your circumstances. Will you hold out your palm to every instance of it and beg it to back off?"

"If need be!"

"Even if you did, it would achieve nothing. —This is a land of wolves."

From Abel's whole body, as he declared this, leaked a cold, piercing killing intent. It was not a matter of raw power, but a terrifying pressure born from the sheer scale of his existence. Even Rem, who could probably overwhelm Abel in a fight, and the Shudrak besides, held their breath and stiffened. Naturally, Subaru too felt pressured to the point his very breathing was endangered. Flop was no exception.

And yet, even as he was struck by the Emperor's killing intent, Flop said—

"Even in a land of wolves, the sheep still live on. If a wolf comes biting at my backside, I'll hop in the wagon and run off with my sister. That's what we've done, time and again, up to today, village chief."

"――――"

His cheeks twitching, yet never losing his smile, Flop's rebuttal came. Hearing it, the killing intent Abel had been emitting suddenly unraveled. At once, the crushing pressure that had dominated the meeting hall vanished, and Subaru too regained the freedom to breathe.

But even with room to catch his breath, no peace of mind returned to Subaru.

"F-Flop-san..."

Called out to in a rasping breath, Flop glanced Subaru's way, his smile dazzling. It carried a nuance close to a wry laugh, but there was no regret on Flop's profile. Yet even if Flop himself held no regret, regret welled up all the more in the chest of Subaru, who had brought him here.

After all, Flop had argued back to the Vollachian Emperor head-on. If that earned his displeasure, Abel, who commanded the Shudrak, could do whatever he pleased with Flop's person. And yet, contrary to Subaru's anxiety—

"—. It seems, for all his soft way of speaking, he has a spine. A troublesome fellow, merchant."

"Is that so? I do pride myself on having a rather likable face, even so!"

"I get it-o."

Having withdrawn his killing intent, Abel didn't turn the edge of his anger on Flop. In response, Flop gave a reply that made it unclear whether or not he was aware he'd just walked a dangerous tightrope. No comment on Mizelda's remark.

"That praise from before is retracted, Natsuki Subaru."

"Eh?"

Abel's words fell on Subaru, who still hadn't regained his composure. Beyond the oni mask, the man radiating sharp eyes jerked his chin at the stubborn Flop.

"There is your own lack of self-awareness to blame as well, but if you were to bring someone along regardless, you ought to have brought someone shrewder about his own interests. That would have caused far less trouble afterward."

"...If he'd been that kind of guy, he wouldn't have been kind to us in the first place."

"Hmph, that assessment is likely correct. How infuriating."

If that "praise from before" referred to bringing Flop along, then Flop had certainly demonstrated stubbornness enough to make one want to retract that praise. Who would have imagined there existed a man who could bite back head-on against the Emperor's authority—no, rather, deflect it like a willow bending in the wind. And more surprising still was that Abel would allow such an attitude from Flop.

"I thought Abel-san was a man of much stronger will."

"Wai—Rem?!"

At Rem's remark, which seemed to reflect exactly what was in Subaru's own heart, his eyes went wide despite himself. It was true he'd found it surprising, but he'd decided saying so would gain nothing. And now it had come from Rem, and Abel's oni mask turned toward her. Oni and mask faced each other, and a moment's silence arose between the two.

"—A person of conviction is troublesome. They put down strong roots, and store power in their trunk."

"...What does that mean?"

"Doesn't it reach you? Never mind, it's a trifle."

At that quiet answer, rather unlike Abel, Rem furrowed her brow. Showing some measure of disappointment at Rem's reaction, Abel slowly shook his head side to side. No one could tell the reason for that disappointment. Only Subaru, dimly, sensed something.

"...Was that, maybe, a quote from something?"

"Ho, unexpected. You didn't strike me as having much cultivation."

"Just a feeling I had. That it sounded like borrowed words."

There was surprise in hearing Abel say something so unexpectedly worldly. But Abel himself paid no mind to being seen through that way, answering shortly, "Is that so," before continuing.

"As for the earlier question—do not underestimate me by calling it unexpected. To begin with, what would you have me do to him?"

"...I rather assumed you would at least resort to torturing the information out of him."

Asked by Abel, Rem answered too honestly for her own good. That said, it wasn't an option lost on Subaru either. If Abel intended to bend Flop to his will, it wouldn't be strange for him to order the Shudrak to do something torture-like. But Abel merely shrugged at her words and answered, "Pointless."

"Indeed, pain can at times serve as the finest means of negotiation, but information drawn out that way is terrifyingly fragile in its reliability. Humans will calmly lie if it means escaping immediate pain."

"――――"

"And in this case, those are the eyes of someone prepared to shield him with his own life, if need be."

Abel appraised the gaze Rem had fixed on him that way. Looking closer, on Rem's stiffened profile was a grim resolve severe enough to nearly paint over her loveliness. That profile spoke eloquently of the truth in Abel's assessment.

"I will not commit the folly of reducing my own forces to obtain information of low reliability. —Hence, negotiation."

"Negotiation?"

"Merchant, I will buy the knowledge you possess. Let us call it negotiation, toward that end."

Lowering his raised knee, Abel settled cross-legged and spoke. Receiving this, Flop, eyes widening, faced Abel while still smiling. And then—

"Hearing the word negotiation, it's unavoidable that a merchant's blood should boil. But! Let me say clearly before we begin negotiating: I am extremely stubborn. Even if my head were cracked open, what I cannot accept, I will not accept!"

So he declared.


△▼△▼△▼△


"'Even if my head were cracked open'—that's not the least bit funny. ...It's not funny at all, Flop-san."

Recalling Flop's bold declaration from just before, launching into the negotiation battle, Subaru made a bitter face. Flop surely hadn't meant it that way, but to Subaru, who had actually watched his head get cracked open more than once, it was a phrase that landed like an ambush.

"――――"

Narrowing his eyes, Subaru looked toward the meeting hall from a hill a little way off. Inside the meeting hall, even now, the "negotiation" called a "business deal" between Abel and Flop continued.

Abel wanting a foothold for bringing down Guaral, and Flop, possessing knowledge that could lead there but unwilling to reveal it—that was the shape of things.

The reason Subaru had left the meeting hall was none other than because the key figures in the negotiation had declared him useless as a fighting force. That is, Abel had. And Subaru himself hadn't been able to offer any reason of his own for staying there.

"Use the secret passage to get into the city, then go straight for the enemy commander's head in a lightning strike..."

Driven out, bathing in a wind that smelled of the forest, Subaru mused. As far as he'd been able to follow, that was the gist of the plan Abel was proposing. Simple, but arguably the only conceivable move to overturn the disparity in forces. If there was room for finesse, it would be things like a diversion outside the city to draw attention, or some trick to thin out the defenses around the enemy commander.

If there was a problem, it was—

"A simple plan is easy to read, I feel like. ...Especially if the enemy has Todd."

If there was no other option, naturally, the enemy would be wary of exactly that. And a plan the enemy is on guard against is, in other words, like throwing a fastball straight down the middle. From the perspective of someone waiting for it, that's a mouth-watering, hittable pitch. Subaru couldn't imagine, for the life of him, Todd letting that pitch slip by.

"Or is it just that I'm too scared? Blowing Todd way out of proportion... if the regular soldiers besides Todd are all like that too, there's no chance of winning."

He didn't know. It was too much of an unknown. But he wanted it not to be true. He didn't want to think that enemies like that Todd existed beyond just Todd himself. That his compatibility with "Return by Death" was the worst possible match—Subaru, who had piled up the fastest and greatest number of deaths, knew that all too keenly. The thought that this was the standard for the Vollachian Empire—

"—The negotiation seems to be dragging on."

"――――"

At some point, Subaru had covered his own face with his hands, shutting out his sight. Into the ears of Subaru staring into that darkness came a voice he knew well—a girl's voice he knew well, but one he could have kept listening to endlessly. But that was only true when Subaru could keep himself in an ordinary state of mind.

"Rem..."

Lowering the hand covering his face, Subaru timidly opened his eyes. Rem stood quietly on the gentle slope of the hill, leaning on her cane, and pierced by her gaze, Subaru's body went rigid. Right now, facing Rem like this weighed on his heart.

During the days it took to return to the "People of Shudrak" settlement, Subaru had been able to keep his focus on anger toward Abel, who had, in effect, laid a trap for them. In doing so, he could be said to have deliberately avoided talking things through with Rem. In fact, that wasn't wrong. He'd been afraid to exchange words with Rem, to learn her true intentions. So he'd averted his eyes from what he didn't want to face, tried to muddle through the moment. But there's a limit to how long you can keep running from something unpleasant that way. Because there is—

"――――"

Now, he had no choice but to face her earnest gaze like this.

"Abel-san seems to be attaching various conditions, but Flop-san seems set on not budging an inch."

"...Yeah, he's an admirable guy. He doesn't want to help hurt anyone with his own knowledge. And having the guts to say that surrounded by the Shudrak—that's amazing too."

He'd watched the negotiation's progress for a while, and Flop's stubbornness matched his own self-assessment exactly. The focal point of the discussion, as expected, was the damage that would be done in the city of Guaral—both to people and to property—and Flop showed no intention of yielding on either front. Abel had proposed reducing casualties as much as possible, but even that wasn't a decisive blow.

It was a standoff. And at the sight of Flop stubbornly holding his ground, Subaru found some relief too.

Hating to fight, arguing head-on against Abel. That someone besides himself existed here who could do that.

But—

"I don't think it's a situation where you can simply call him admirable."

"...What?"

Contrary to Subaru, who took Flop's attitude in a positive light, Rem murmured with a quiet, yet strongly reproachful tone. Startled by the intensity of those words, Subaru couldn't help but stare at Rem. In a gaze he'd been trying to avert, unable to face her directly, Rem was looking straight at Subaru. Still looking straight at him, she continued.

"Not wanting to hurt someone with his own knowledge—I understand that feeling. But... considering the circumstances everyone is in, including Flop-san himself, how do you regard the harm that arises from him not revealing what he knows? Couldn't that too be called knowledge hurting someone?"

"That's, that's just splitting hairs, isn't it. Blaming Flop-san for every bit of harm caused by him not talking—that's just an unfair accusation."

"That's true. But I don't think running away indefinitely is realistic either."

Against Abel, who had declared this a land of wolves, Flop had declared that the sheep would run. That this was a fantasy—Rem said so, pessimistically—no, realistically. She too was one who had gone to Guaral. She knew firsthand the tenacity of the Imperial soldiers. Could they really keep shaking off that brutal pursuit, going forward?

Under circumstances of clear hostility rather than vague malice, could Flop, together with Medium, really keep running forever and ever, evading capture? And Subaru and the others, bound to the same fate—how far could they too go?

"Wait, wait a second, Rem. What you're saying doesn't make sense. Didn't you say yourself you hate fighting? And yet the way you're talking now..."

"――――"

"It sounds like you're accepting that we have to fight."

His throat trembling, Subaru couldn't get the words out properly. But as the saying goes, the eyes speak as much as the mouth—at that moment, the precarious exchange between Subaru and Rem spoke of their feelings not just through words, but through their gazes as well. The grim color dwelling there looked to Subaru the same as it had when she'd argued back at Abel to shield Flop.

"...It hurts me, not understanding you."

Seeing that attitude of Rem's, Subaru let out the feeling welling in his heart just as it was. Saying something that criticized Flop for trying to follow his own convictions, and yet looking at Subaru with the same light in her eyes as when she'd shielded him after he'd shown his own conviction. Covering for Subaru against Abel, yet not unpacking her luggage at the inn in Guaral—the exchanges between the awakened Rem and Subaru were always mismatched, one-directional.

That she had awakened—he was genuinely glad of that, from the bottom of his heart. He believed some solution would be found for the sorrow of her lost memories. But in the Empire, where no one could be relied upon wholeheartedly, Rem's uncooperative attitude, when he had to bring her back to Emilia and the others, pierced Subaru's feet like a blade. Each time, it was enough to make him want to curl up from the pain.

"...You didn't unpack your bags at the inn in Guaral, did you. Thanks to that, we shortened the time it took to escape, but it's been nagging at me ever since."

"That was..."

"When you arrive at an inn, I think most people either rest their body or unpack their things. Sure, there's a chance you don't fall into that category. But..."

He didn't want to say what came next. If he could have kept averting his eyes, he would have wanted to. But now that he felt he couldn't avoid addressing Rem's inexplicable attitude, he couldn't keep looking away. So—

"But you were, again, trying to run from me, weren't you."

"――――"

Rem's silence stabbed into him, and Subaru felt his heart bleeding. But once a scab has been peeled back, it can no longer serve its purpose. He had no choice but to peel it all the way off, not caring that blood would flow and the wound would be exposed. Enduring the pain.

"You not trusting me... that's fine. It hurts, but I get it. With no memories at all, I probably smell to you like someone you can't forgive, and there's no basis for me to tell you to trust me. I understand the reason you can't trust me, the reason you want to keep your distance."

"――――"

"But it's true that there are people waiting for you to come back... people who treasure you. If you hate me, you don't have to talk to me. Even if you shake off my hand, I'll bear it. But please, stop trying to pull away."

"――――"

"Please, I'm begging you... don't shut me, don't shut me out of your life."

His pleading voice trembled that way, his eyes clouding as if about to well up with tears. The tears welling up bit by bit—whether they came from shame at himself, or from his plea to Rem, he himself couldn't tell for certain.

It was simply pathetic. Laughable. Even if he hadn't already been given up on, it wouldn't be strange if this plea alone were reason enough to be abandoned.

—How hopeless, how pathetic a figure he kept showing, again and again, in front of Rem.

It was a side of himself he could never show Emilia, Beatrice, or his other companions. At the very least, Subaru had made an effort to intend not to show it to his companions. Whether or not he'd actually managed that was another matter, but that had been his effort. Because he'd decided that showing weakness was something he would only do in front of Rem.

But that was never meant, in any way, to burden Rem—memoryless, without anyone to rely on, feeling helpless in a foreign land—with the weight of someone named Natsuki Subaru.

"――――"

Hearing Subaru's unsightly plea, Rem said nothing. Still, even as his vision threatened to blur with welling tears, Subaru didn't take his eyes off her, and she didn't try to look away from him either. And a brief silence wrapped around the two of them—

"—You."

"――――"

"I wasn't, trying to give up on you."

So Rem said, haltingly, choosing her words carefully. It was an attitude Rem had never once shown Subaru, up until now.

Words that were considerate of Subaru, someone she should regard with wariness, careful not to hurt him. And there was a ring to them that made him believe they weren't merely words tossed out in desperation. Though even that, perhaps, was nothing more than Subaru's own wishful hope.

"You..."

Clinging to that faint hope, that thread he shouldn't rely on, Subaru tried to choose his words. What Rem was thinking, what she hoped for, what she believed, in saying that— should he even know the answer to that, he didn't know even that much, but wanting only to hear her voice more, Subaru tried to keep speaking.

But—

"Aaah-uuh!!"
"Guohh?!"

Before he could, an impact leapt at his waist, and Subaru's body lurched sideways. Unable to brace himself at all, he went flying, and Subaru rolled magnificently atop the hill. The mood from just before shattered to pieces, and Subaru, eyes spinning, glared to see what had happened, only to find—

"Lui! Getting on top of Su not good-u! Uu stopped her. Lui did it on her own-u."

"U-Utakata...? Then that means, this is..."

Trotting in with comically light footsteps came Utakata, her tan-colored face faintly flushed. And pulled along by the sleeve by Utakata, straddling Subaru's chest, sat a girl with long blond hair bundled at the back of her head—

"Rui-chan, please get off. That person will be crushed."

"Uu?"

Her face beaming with delight, a question mark rising as she tilted her head at Rem's words—that was Rui. Still perched on Subaru's chest, she patted his cheeks with an innocent expression, over and over. That very lack of malice was, in itself, what irritated Subaru most.

"We're having an important talk here. Get off, now."

"Ah?"

"Not 'ah.' Utakata, please."

"Because it's Su's request, Uu will listen. Uu, good wife, wise mother."

Whether or not she understood the meaning of the words, Utakata pulled Lui's arm and hauled her off of Subaru's chest. With the pressure gone, Subaru sat himself up. Still, Lui's dissatisfied face right in front of him left him at a loss for words.

"And in the end, I still haven't figured out what you even are..."

Archbishop of Sin, wicked existence, "Gluttony," Rui Arneb. Words to describe the blond girl before him—Subaru could think of any number of vile insults. But the girl who had once embodied all of that didn't overlap with the girl in front of him now. That wicked girl had never helped Subaru, worried for him, or smiled at him so defenselessly.

But—

"Uu?"

As Lui tilted her head, her past actions flashed through Subaru's mind. Saving him when Rem had strangled him, and afterward, her voice trembling as she'd faced Subaru's crises time and again. Most recently, even against Todd wielding an axe. The sight of her taking his elbow strike and spilling a nosebleed was still fresh in his memory. That no wound remained, that Lui too looked as if nothing had affected her at all, seemed almost infuriating, but—

"Rui-chan."
"Uu!"

Called that way, Lui's face lit up brightly, and she ran to Rem. Perhaps having learned how to approach Rem, who leaned on a cane, without excessive force, Rem caught Lui's not-too-forceful leap and gently stroked her head. To Rem—Rui Arneb, part of the Archbishop of Sin who had played some part in the current state Rem was in.

"That look in your eyes when..."

"Huh?"

"The way you look at this child confuses me too. This child is so attached to you, and yet you..."

"――――"

Holding Lui's small body, Rem murmured that, and Subaru fell silent. Was this a continuation of the words she'd left unfinished before—the continuation of the true intention she'd been about to reveal, about not unpacking her bags in Guaral? But no matter how long he waited, Rem didn't try to spin out any more words, only smiling and holding the beaming Lui to her chest.

"...What do you want from me?"

Watching Rem like that, at last, Subaru chose words that felt almost like a last resort. It might have been a cowardly way of putting it, leaving the answer up to Rem. But if everything Subaru chose—words, actions, all of it—was being rejected by Rem regardless, then this was the only thing left. To ask her directly what she wanted, what she wished he'd do.

And with that, if the answer she came up with was something Subaru couldn't fulfill, then that, he feared as he asked, might truly be the moment their relationship broke apart.

At Subaru's words, she held her breath for a moment, and then,

"...I don't want it to come to a fight."

She said.

That much, Subaru could accept. In fact, when the Imperial camp had been burned, she'd assumed Subaru had orchestrated it and had come at him fiercely. Rem did not wish for a fight. Did not want one. That much was certain.

So he found it made sense that her position hadn't changed at all. But just as Subaru began to feel that understanding settle in, Rem continued further. And that was—

"But I don't think we can keep running forever either. That Flop-san's words aren't realistic. And..."

"You can't affirm Abel's opinion either?"

"...Right."

Dipping her chin slightly, Rem nodded at Subaru's question. That timid nod was proof that she herself was aware she was saying something unreasonable—naturally so.

Affirming Flop's opinion that he didn't want to fight, while also agreeing with Abel's opinion that fighting was unavoidable—that was a thought that might as well embody the very essence of fence-sitting. Honestly, Subaru himself thought how wonderful it would be if such a wishy-washy opinion could actually be made to work.

Not wanting to fight. But having to fight anyway. Not wanting to take a life. Yet facing a battle where taking one might be unavoidable. If he had to clash with that two-sidedness surrounding life itself—

"If it's you..."

"Huh?"

Making a bitter face, Subaru agreed with the conflicting thoughts Rem held within her. But to Subaru, wrestling with that anguish, Rem glanced his way. Still holding Lui's head to her chest, she turned her uncertain pale blue eyes toward Subaru. And to Subaru, holding his breath, once more, as if steeling herself with a small resolve—

"—If it's you, could you find some way to make it work?"

She asked, as if clinging to him.

"――――"

The moment he received that question, Subaru froze as though struck by lightning. It was, it was such an unreasonable question, an outrageous one.

A question worse even than the one Subaru had asked himself, having defined himself as a coward, a craven. Not wanting to fight, and knowing they had no choice but to fight—neither of those two options, but a third answer. That was what Rem was demanding of Subaru.

Why? Having forgotten everything, having lost her trust and affection for Natsuki Subaru, harboring wariness and revulsion because of the stench that clung to him—why would she ask this of Subaru, of all people?

Why would Rem turn to Subaru with a gaze that seemed to cling to him for salvation?

"――――"

That terribly one-sided, even selfish question set Natsuki Subaru's blood boiling. He could have felt anger, could have railed loudly, could have condemned her presumption, and surely would have been forgiven for it. That was how self-serving the choice Rem had made must have been.

But what welled up in Subaru at that moment was a sense of mission surging from the very depths of his soul.

"I..."

He had to fight. He had to resist. He had to smash apart the reality in front of him—that was what he felt.

Flop O'Connell, shunning battle, refusing to open up his knowledge. Vincent Abelux, preparing for an unavoidable battle, determined to win survival.

Two men who, given the circumstances they found themselves in, had each arrived at what might be called an inevitable choice. And Natsuki Subaru, who had to find an answer, a path, different from either of theirs. Why was that?

That was—

"—Because I'm the hero Rem believed in."

And because he was a man who had kept fighting in this other world, armed with nothing but sheer refusal to give up. It wasn't as though the philosophy that had carried Natsuki Subaru forward, up to this very day, would stop working just because he'd crossed a national border or two. That, he believed.

The days of meeting Emilia, being saved by her, running headlong to save her in turn. The vow made holding Beatrice's hand, leading her out of the forbidden archive, deciding to stay together. And now—having saved Rem, having been saved by her, having been loved by her, and defining himself as one who would answer that devotion.

—All of it set Natsuki Subaru's blood ablaze, demanding that he change this limited situation.

Something—there must be something, something. The days spent in the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, the days since being flung into the Vollachian Empire, the people he'd met, the enemies he'd faced, whoever stood beside him, whoever waited far ahead—all of it, material to draw from.

Think. Reason. Imagine, and seize hold of every possibility that exists. In this world where every single person surpassed him in every way, the only weapon left to Natsuki Subaru to fight with—was nothing but his tenacity to survive, and his cunning.

So then, so then, so then, so then—

"—Ah."

Struck by helplessness, dominated by a sense of defeat, having left the meeting hall with nothing to be done—into Subaru's mind, then, flashed an idea like lightning. Reeling in that possibility, one that seemed almost like a bad joke, Subaru seriously turned it over, trying to see whether he could refine it into a solid plan.

"――――"

Rem's eyes watched that silent, thoughtful Subaru intently. Holding a hand to the mouth of the fussing Lui, pressing down so that Utakata, tilting her head beside her, wouldn't let slip an unnecessary word, she kept watching Subaru.

With a gaze that, despite having no memories, was the same as it had been when she did have them. As if she believed Natsuki Subaru would arrive at an answer no one else could reach.

And then—

"—Rem."

Hand still at his mouth, at Subaru's murmur, Rem straightened her posture. There was no reply. Somehow, she understood that none was being asked for. But without noticing Rem's reaction, Subaru held his breath quietly and continued.

"I found the answer—what I'd do, if it were me."

Trudging forward, treading on the hard-packed earth, he pushed open the meeting hall's door. Seeing Subaru appear, the oni-masked man clicked his tongue in displeasure. As if to say, uninvited guest.

"What is it, Natsuki Subaru. There's no place here for you, unable to offer any constructive opinion."

In truth, he'd been told, in effect, that he wasn't wanted—and yet Subaru's feet didn't stop. Abel, who had allowed Flop's insolence but seemed unlikely to allow Subaru's insolence, a man with no convictions of his own—under the gazes of everyone remaining in the meeting hall, Subaru walked straight forward. And coming to stand right beside Abel, looking down at that oni mask up close, he said,

"Any progress in the talks, you arrogant bastard?"

"――――"

Reaching out, Subaru forcibly tore the oni mask from Abel's face. At that unhesitating gesture, Mizelda and the other Shudrak widened their eyes. Needless to say, so did Flop, seeing Abel's bare face for the first time.

But none of those reactions registered with Subaru. At his question, Abel twisted his demonic, spine-chillingly handsome face into a cruel expression and shook his head slightly, saying, "No."

"The negotiation is stalled. This merchant is, contrary to expectation, immovable."

"Is that right. Then, since you're clearly no good at wooing people, let me do the wooing for you."

"What?"

Furrowing his well-shaped brows, Abel murmured that, and Subaru looked down at him with a satisfying sense of triumph. Then Subaru turned to face Flop.

Since nothing had been confirmed outright, Flop, who had been going head-to-head with the presumed Emperor, was bewildered by the change in Subaru's expression and manner, calling out "Milord?" And to the two of them, each harboring a different kind of suspicion, Subaru declared it.

The content of which was—

"—There's a plan to make Guaral surrender without bloodshed. If it's a fight where no blood is spilled, couldn't we find some middle ground both sides could agree to?"
