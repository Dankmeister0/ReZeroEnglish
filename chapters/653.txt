Arc 8 Chapter 37: "Foreshadowing Time"

With a sword in one hand and baggage tucked under his other arm, he slowly pushed open the door in front of him.
He didn't kick down the door, or cut it down. He had to avoid making noise carelessly, which might provoke any Undead that could be lurking inside. Drawing attention from around was the same concern.
If it were some rank-and-file imperial soldier turned Undead, that would be one thing, but he wanted no part of clashing with a skilled Undead. All the more so in a situation where he was carrying baggage he couldn't just toss aside.

"――――"

Sniffing once, he scowled at the smell of iron and rust drifting from inside the room.
It was the smell of blood. Once you got used to it, you could tell roughly how fresh it was, give or take. This blood had only just been spilled — and it wasn't just one person's worth.
Sure enough, when he checked the back room, he found two bodies lying atop each other.

In this house facing the main street, what remained were the corpses of an old woman and a man.
The culprit was surely the Undead he'd just cut down in front of the house moments ago. The blood wetting the sword in its hand was proof enough that these bodies had only just been made.
The victims were likely mother and son. Unfortunately, their final expressions weren't calm enough to compare their features by. The old woman lay in the bed, and beside the man who had fallen as if shielding her, a broken sword lay on the floor.

"Too slow to flee... no, that's not it."

Not two people who'd failed to escape in time, but a son who had stayed behind to protect a mother who couldn't flee at all.
That was the natural reading of the scene — and it was a bitter irony, happening as it did in the imperial capital, the very heart of the Vollachia Empire, a nation that took the trampling of the weak for granted.
Still, he couldn't laugh at the irony. If only he'd arrived a few minutes sooner.

"――――"

He sheathed the sword he'd drawn, and silently regarded the corpse of the man who, caught in an unavoidable tragedy, had decided how to spend his own life.
If he himself were placed in the same situation, could he shield the bed where his beloved family lay behind him, and fight until his life ran out, just as this man had?

"Don't be stupid. You already know the answer."

He spat out bitter saliva, irritation still lingering, unable to shake it off.
Yes, a question whose answer he already knew. He was bound to turn tail and run, no matter what. — Just as he was mocking himself for it—

"Oy, Red! Where be you!? Yours truly's business here is done!"

An absurdly loud voice suddenly came from outside the building, and he snapped his head toward the window.
The ruddy face of the man who'd split off from him earlier rose behind his eyelids, and he felt his own drunkenness drain away in an instant at the recklessness of shouting so loud in a city crawling with Undead.
Drinking and drinking, trying and failing to drown out the weight of reality with intoxication, his head throbbed as if struck. Clicking his tongue, he stepped over and peered out the window.

Down below in the street, he saw a blue-haired man in Wasou robes waving and searching for him — no, beside him stood a small shadow dressed in a similar style.

"Where might you be, Father's companion!? We can hardly talk calmly around here, so how about somewhere a bit easier to converse in!? That is, assuming Father's talk of having a companion wasn't some drunken illusion or a load of nonsense!"

"Ohh, would you listen to this. Talking to your own father like that — what an unfilial child you've grown into. Wherever did yours truly go wrong in raising you...!"

"No, no, Father did nothing wrong! After all, I was hardly raised at all!"

Two noisy fools side by side, and the racket was far more than double.
Whether drunk on liquor or on himself, both voices were unmistakably those of drunkards, and the "companion" the two of them were searching for — Heinkel — clutched his head with his free hand.
His hangover-throbbing head now ached for reasons that had nothing to do with the drink, and as the emergency-rations bearded Dog Human tucked under his arm stirred, he readjusted his hold and let out a deep, liquor-scented sigh.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△

Two fools kept making a ruckus on the main street without a shred of concern for onlookers.
Leaving them be and letting them get swallowed by a swarming horde of Undead would've been their own fault, but Heinkel had no intention of getting dragged into that process himself, so he wasted no time calling the two of them over.
And when he brought the two of them up into the house, an unexpected extra came along with them.

"Didn't think you'd still be in the capital, Heinkel. Figured you'd have run off already, or else..."

"Dead, you mean? I could say the exact same thing right back at you, Aldebaran."

"Just Al'll do."

The iron-helmeted man Al, with whom he'd had this unexpected reunion, replied while fidgeting with the fittings of his helmet.
In the all-out battle between the regular army surrounding the capital and the rebels, Heinkel had been posted, as one of the rebels, to a different battlefield from both Priscilla and Al, and so he knew neither how that battle had ended nor whether Priscilla and the others were safe.
An answer he had no way of learning on his own had, unexpectedly, been delivered to him.
And what's more—

"That Lady Priscilla was captured, hm? Hard to believe on such short notice."

"Well, plunging headlong into the unexpected is one of the princess's more troublesome charms, but even I figure I can't just leave this one alone. So I stuck around the capital, and..."

"And that's how you ran into that?"

At Heinkel's words, Al nodded, "Yeah."
Al's face and expression were hidden behind his iron helmet, unreadable, but the exhaustion in his voice and bearing hinted at just how much hardship he'd been put through.
This was Al, a man who could keep up with Priscilla even at her most outrageously self-centered and unpredictable. If he'd been reduced to this state, one could well imagine just how fearsome the other party must be.
Still, it was quite the coincidence, just who it was that had worn Al down to exhaustion.

"Never in a million years did I think I'd be reunited with Father in a place like this! I was thinking it'd been a while since I'd seen him around, but could it be that Father was the one who tossed me onto Sword Slave Island to scheme up some kind of legendary island story arc for me?"

Chattering away at a furious clip while gnawing on jerky he'd helped himself to from the house's storeroom was the Wasou-clad boy — this child who called himself Cecilus was, astonishingly, the son of Rowan, the man who'd been traveling with Heinkel.
And as if it were some kind of joke, this Cecilus's name, family name included, was none other than—

— Cecilus Segmunt. Vollachia's "Blue Lightning."

The strongest swordsman in the Vollachia Empire, the unshakable "One" of the Empire's Nine Divine Generals.
Even Heinkel, for all his faults, was still vice-captain of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's royal guard. He knew at least the names of the notable figures of that untrustworthy neighboring nation. Above all, the "Blue Lightning" was simply too famous not to know.
Unmatched even in the blood-soaked history of the Empire, he was said to be the individual who had taken more human lives than anyone else in the world, a warrior strong enough to stand shoulder to shoulder with the Kingdom's Sword Saint.

"――――"

Narrowing his eyes at Cecilus, who looked roughly eleven or twelve, Heinkel mocked himself for trying to use the boy's youth as grounds to underestimate his strength.
When it came to true strength, talk of appearance or age was nothing but foolish, pathetic noise.
The first time Heinkel had lost to his own son Reinhard in a sword match, Reinhard hadn't even turned six yet. — There were, in this world, beings like that.

If anything, the one Heinkel ought to acknowledge as a rare case was Rowan.
If he too, like Heinkel, was the father of someone called the strongest, then surely he must have complicated feelings about his son Cecilus's swordsmanship and fame—

"Hah hah hah, don't make me laugh, indeed. The killing on Sword Slave Island is naught but a sideshow — however many folk you cut down there, there's no learning in it for your blade or your skill. Tossing you into a place like that would've been nothing but a detour on the road to the Heavenly Sword, yours truly reckons."

Utterly indifferent to the thought that had risen in Heinkel's mind, Rowan shrugged his ruddy-faced shoulders, put his lips to the gourd flask of liquor he carried, and chatted with his son in high spirits.
At Rowan's answer, Cecilus tilted his head, unbothered, saying, "Was that so," while tearing off another bite of jerky.

"Well, it did seem like a scheme without any clear direction for something Father would do, so I figured it probably wasn't him. But then it just makes it all the more baffling why on earth I was on that island in the first place — a real mystery of mysteries, you know!"

"Well now, that's a peculiar, mystifying way of putting it, indeed. In any case, it wasn't yours truly's doing... hm?"

Cutting himself off mid-sentence, Rowan suddenly looked Cecilus up and down, studying him closely.
Noticing his father's stare, Cecilus fluttered his Wasou sleeves and spun around in a circle, letting his long, unkempt blue hair fan out.

"What's the matter? Even after all this time, I'd think the brilliance of yours truly, the leading man, would be hard to forget."

"Never mind that, wait, wait, son! Where in blazes have you put Murasame and Masayume? Blades that fine, you don't just carelessly take off your hip!"

"Fine blades, meaning swords? What are you talking about? It's an unspoken agreement between you and me that I don't carry a sword, and you don't let me carry one, until a suitable one comes along. Unfortunately, I still haven't come across a famous blade that's the one, so my hip stays light and free — swish, swish."

"Hmmmmm?"

Watching Cecilus swing hips bearing nothing at all, Rowan's eyes clouded ever further with suspicion. But before long, as if something had clicked into place, his eyes went wide. "Ah."
And then—

"Something seemed strange, and now that I look closer — Cecilus, you've shrunk!"

"You're only noticing THAT now!?"

At Rowan's shocked face, Al, who was even more shocked, raised his voice.
Meanwhile, Cecilus himself only looked puzzled, saying, "Shrunk?" with no idea what they meant. Of course, Heinkel had no idea what was going on either.
Instead, Al, who seemed to have some idea, strode straight up to close the distance with Rowan.

"There's something I want to get straight — you're Cecilus's old man, and the Cecilus you know was a proper grown-up. That much's right, yeah?"

"Hold on now, Al, I have to wonder about that. In the first place, at just what point, and under what circumstances, can a person truly be said to have become an adult? For instance, if you don't count someone as full-fledged the first time they cut a person down, then the person they cut down was cut down by a half-fledged swordsman, which strikes me as a truly outrageous state of affairs..."

"Shut it for now! So which is it, old man?"

"No need to press so hard, mister helmet-man, indeed. In the first place, from a parent's eyes, a child stays a child no matter how much they grow taller — the category doesn't change. And besides, this Cecilus here, his words and manner have stayed just like a child's no matter how much time passes, not growing up one bit, so..."

"I've got plenty enough proof by now that you two are father and son, my ears are practically callused from it...!"

Buried under an avalanche of answers from both the closing-in Rowan and Cecilus beside him, Al stamped his feet as if to say his frustration had nowhere left to go.
Feeling a bit sorry for Al, Heinkel scratched roughly at his own red hair and said,

"So basically, Aldebaran, this is what you're saying? That Cecilus Segmunt over there has, for whatever reason, shrunk down into the form of a child."

"It's Al. ...Well, I know the reason, I know how to fix it, I know all of it. But yeah, that's right."

"You even know how to fix it, huh..."

Being told a whole person had shrunk and just nodding along with, "Is that so," felt ridiculous, but given the hellish scenes he'd witnessed in that decisive battle for the capital, he found he could accept most anything by now.
If there was a way back, it probably wasn't worth dwelling on much longer.

"Then why not just turn him back first, and talk after?"

"I'd love nothing more, but turning him back needs a certain old Shinobi geezer. So for now, we're stuck with this size, like it or not. ...And why is it you didn't notice your own son had shrunk on the first look?"

After shaking his head slowly, Al turned the point of the conversation back onto Rowan.
But in answer to that question, Rowan traced a finger along his stubbly chin and said,

"Well now, whether my son's big or small is a trifling matter to yours truly, indeed."

"Hey, hey, is this the shitty-dad finals or something? Is every nation's strongest guy's old man like this?"

"...What about you? Do you feel like you've shrunk at all?"

Utterly unashamed, Rowan's answer had been far from that of a model father, and Al grumbled in exasperation. Hesitant to react to that grumbling, Heinkel turned the question to Cecilus instead.
At that, Cecilus, now onto his third piece of jerky, laughed, "Let's see, now,"

"If you're asking whether I'm aware of it, I'm not, not at all! Whether I shrink or grow, I take the stage as myself, through and through!"

"Is that right..."

"But! But, listen! The things Boss and Gustav and everyone on the island and all those other bit-part characters were saying, it all lines up now! I figured, ah, this is a foreshadowing payoff!"

Cecilus slapped the nearest table, his voice bright with excitement.
He himself looked like something had clicked into place, but to Heinkel, who'd only heard the conversation secondhand, it was gibberish. About all he could tell was that neither father nor son was taking the momentous matter of a shrinking body all that seriously.

"But it all makes sense now, indeed. No wonder yours truly thought you seemed so unbothered facing me. After all, the way we parted was quite the way to part."

"Oh-ho? I haven't the faintest memory of it, but just what sort of parting did you and I have—"

Cecilus tilted his head at Rowan, who had said that while stroking his chin thoughtfully.
A parting with his father that the son had forgotten — but judging by the exchange so far, Heinkel guessed it hadn't been anything decent.
And then—

"— That damn guy there's a damn bastard who plotted to damn assassinate His Excellency and got cut down for it."

A sharp voice cut in, and Heinkel and the others' eyes turned that way.
There, the small, bearded Dog Human who'd been sound asleep until just a moment ago was sitting up on the floor where he'd been left. Sitting cross-legged, he glared at Rowan with an ill-tempered expression.

"A damn guy who was supposed to be dead, alive and kicking — I'm damn near as surprised as you are. Especially seeing as I can't imagine that damn idiot Cecilus screwing it up."

"Oh my, awake at last, are we, Mr. Dog. Glad to see you're barking and full of energy."

"You're being a damn idiot in your own damn way, you damn brat!"

At the bearded Dog Human's raised voice, Cecilus twisted his lips into a sulky pout.
Judging by that reaction, the bearded Dog Human and Cecilus apparently knew each other. Though it seemed Cecilus himself had forgotten. — No, going by the flow of the conversation so far, it might be more accurate to say he'd forgotten everything from before he shrank.
It was a strange thing, but presumably that was why his conversations with both Rowan and the bearded Dog Human kept failing to line up — because he'd forgotten his time as an adult.

"Come to think of it, who's this fellow you had laid out here? Awfully lively now..."

"Ah, that furball gentleman there is General Groovy, indeed. One of the Empire's Nine Divine Generals, and a colleague of Cecilus's, no less."

"Groovy — as in Groovy Gumlet!?"

At this unexpected identity being sprung on them, Al couldn't help raising his voice, and Heinkel felt much the same shock.
After all, along the way, Rowan had only told him this was an important personage, without giving him any more information, and Heinkel had been carrying him along like so much baggage.
Baggage that, depending on circumstances, he might well have carelessly used as a shield against an Undead's attack.

"So that means, one of 'em's shrunk, sure, but we've got two Generals gathered here. I'd like to say that's an unexpected ray of hope, but..."

"—? What is it?"

"Didn't the general just say something outrageous a second ago? Something about plotting to assassinate the Emperor?"

When Al pulled the topic back a step like that, attention gathered on Rowan once again.
For a moment, everyone's attention had been drawn away by the bearded Dog Human — Groovy — waking up and his exchange with Cecilus, but he had indeed said that.
At their suspicious looks, Rowan put a hand to his forehead with an "Ohh, dear,"

"There's a misunderstanding on that score, indeed. Yours truly never plotted to assassinate His Greatness the Emperor. Yours truly merely tried to have my son do it."

"...That's not much of a defense. So the two of you, father and son, are both attempted assassins of the Emperor, together?"

At this excuse that failed entirely to excuse anything, Heinkel stared at the blue-haired father and son in dumbfounded silence.
He hadn't heard any talk of the Emperor having been killed, so presumably the matter had been settled as a mere attempt — but even so, what an outrageous scandal to have on one's hands.
But at Heinkel's puzzlement, Groovy shook his head. "Nah, that ain't it."

"This damn kid told His Excellency his damn old man was plotting somethin' like that. Then, near as I heard, he went and settled the damn scheme himself, damn old man and all."

"But he's alive, so he must've botched it, huh?"

"That part I don't get either, damn it! Oy, you damn Cecilus! What the hell were you thinkin', lettin' your damn old man live!?"

"Who knows? Even if you say it was something I did, yesterday's me, today's me, and tomorrow's me all flash on different brilliant quips and grand stagings in the moment, you see. Though it does seem unlikely that I'd simply botch a job, so maybe I chose not to do it on purpose?"

Groovy hopped to his feet and closed in on Cecilus, but the boy — of a similar height now — waved a hand airily, unrepentant, as if to say the answer had vanished along with his lost height.
In truth, asking Cecilus, who surely didn't remember his own reasons, would only turn into a pointless back-and-forth. Rather than cling to that unknowable answer—

"Blue-hair, why'd you plot to assassinate the Emperor in the first place? Was overthrowing the state the goal?"

"Hah hah hah, Red, don't say such odd things, don't say such odd things. The goal was perfectly simple, indeed — if word got out that I'd laid hands on His Greatness the Emperor, the entire Empire would turn against my son."

"That's... yeah, I guess it would."

"That was the goal, indeed. A state where the Empire's soldiers come at you with no thought to eating or sleeping... there's no better environment for honing one's swordsmanship on the razor's edge between life and death, wouldn't you say!"

"――――"

With a sharp slap, Rowan struck his own knee in high spirits, as if to say this had been a brilliant idea.
Utterly unable to comprehend the logic he'd just voiced, Heinkel could only stare in frozen astonishment. Cecilus, on the other hand, upon hearing it, cried out, "Ah-ah-ah,"

"I see, I see, so that's it! I don't remember it myself, but sure enough, Father's just the sort to make me do that kind of thing! No means too extreme in reaching for the Heavenly Sword!"

"Your talent for the blade is the genuine article, indeed. ...And yet, here you are, stalled out with the Heavenly Sword right in front of you. So yours truly figured a somewhat forceful method might crack that shell open — a father's love, if you will."

"Hah hah hah, 'a father's love' — how unlike you! If Father still had any such human feeling left in him, he wouldn't have killed off five or six children before finally getting a talented one like me, now would he!"

"What! There's no room to argue with that, indeed!"

As if some great mystery had melted away, the eerie sound of father and son laughing together rang out.
While Groovy listened with a scowl and Al listened with a hand to his neck, Heinkel felt a dizziness like the world swaying before his eyes, and stumbled his shoulder against the wall.

It was a parent-child relationship beyond all comprehension.
He simply couldn't grasp what could drive someone to such lengths. In an instant, the faint hope he'd harbored toward Rowan just a moment before — something like sympathy for a man in the same position as himself, the father of someone said to be the strongest — thinned out all at once and vanished.

A man who had plotted to assassinate the Vollachian Emperor, been betrayed by his own child on top of it, failed in his aim, been treated as dead, and now carried the worst possible infamy about him.
Why, given all that, he could laugh so calmly, Heinkel simply couldn't understand.

Back home, Heinkel was in a somewhat similar position himself. And even so, he found it suffocating beyond bearing.
Merely being suspected of a crime he had no memory of committing — involvement in the kidnapping of royalty — was already unbearably stifling as it was—

"— Well, I get it, we're all people with our own baggage."

Indifferent to Heinkel's dizziness, Al spoke up quietly.
He too, no doubt, had all sorts of thoughts churning inside him about the father-and-son conversation just now, and Groovy's own position. But he swallowed those feelings whole and shut the lid on them.
That was because Al had already decided, here and now, what mattered more than the questions right in front of him.

"All of us here have something we're looking for, or some purpose, in this capital. Might be we're all skilled enough not to need anyone's help, but there's no particular reason to turn down allies, either. Am I wrong?"

Al posed the question and swept his gaze around the faces gathered in the room.
At Al's question, Groovy crossed his short arms and puffed out his chest.

"I only just woke up, and all I know is the damn capital smells like a damn grave. On top of that, I ain't keen on gettin' left behind with a damn idiot and his damn idiot old man. — And who the hell knows what that damn sharp-minded Excellency's schemin' up, neither."

"But at the very least, without worrying who's friend or foe anymore, driving off the dead's the first priority — you're with us on that much, right?"

"No need for damn worryin' about that, we're on the same page there."

Snorting hard, Groovy gave rough agreement to Al's opinion. Next, having gotten that answer, Al turned to look at Cecilus.

"I get that you and your old man have a messed-up relationship. I don't get how you two can laugh so friendly after all that, but hell, talk of parents makes me choke up too. I ain't diggin' any further. But instead—"

"No need to fret and pick your words so carefully, Al. In our fight against the archer earlier, there were moments your judgment saved us. So! For my part, I don't think there's any need to go off in a different direction from you for now."

"That's a relief. And while you're at it, cut it out with the whimsical attempts on my life. I've only got so many to spare."

"Hah hah, 'only got so many to spare'! Nice joke!"

"Wasn't a joke, though."

Cecilus laughed merrily, giving a thumbs-up, and Al sighed.
Then, last of all, he brought Heinkel and Rowan together into view and said,

"Old man, what about you? What'll it be?"

"Ohh, mister helmet-man! Compared to my son and the furball gentleman, how heavy and grudging your tone in inviting yours truly and this fellow here... why, that wounds, indeed."

"My impression of you right now's pretty rock bottom, that's why."

Shrugging his one-armed shoulder, Al conveyed his impression of Rowan bluntly, then turned his head toward Heinkel as well.

"Might be you didn't come off looking too good in that all-out battle, but helping the princess here can turn that around. Whatever it is you want, you're not getting it without her. Right, Heinkel?"

"I..."

At Al's words, Heinkel clenched his back teeth and hung his head.
As Al said, the reason Heinkel had joined Priscilla's camp was that he wanted something — a reward obtainable only through her winning the Royal Selection.
In truth, no one expected much from Heinkel's own contributions or ability. What was expected of him was, at most, the role of keeping Reinhard — who served another candidate — in check.
Thinking about it that way, however much Heinkel contributed here for Priscilla's sake, it would only amount to achieving a result outside his true role.
And besides—

"――――"

Once already, Heinkel had bent his knee on the battlefield and given up resisting.
When he'd witnessed a sight so unearthly it seemed as if heaven and earth had been turned upside down, his spirit had broken. — Heinkel had tried to give up.
So he'd left the battlefield, let Rowan drag him along, drowned himself in drink, and shut a lid over everything.
And yet, he found himself thinking.

"...Louanna."

Shamefully, being told there was still a chance to make up for it, lingering attachment showed its face in his heart.
If Priscilla hadn't seen the moment he fled, or the state of mind in which he'd given up — if he could still smooth it over — then, closing his eyes to a fear already past his throat, he wanted to reach out his hand once more.

"— But what can we actually do? Even with all our heads lined up, that's only five people. Are you telling me the five of us can save an Empire teetering on the edge of ruin or not?"

"What a thoroughly unwelcome opinion, that is. Sure, if you're just counting heads, it might look like only five, but the hidden power here goes well beyond a single one of me being worth a hundred men, so you could just as well call it a million and four, mmf mmf mmf—"

"Shut your damn mouth."

Wrestling with his own inner conflict, Heinkel asked about their odds of success and their outlook going forward.
Cecilus's useless quip in response was promptly stopped, true to form, by General Groovy's own hand clamping over his mouth. Even while doing so, Groovy said, "Still," and looked at Al.

"Damn middle-aged redhead's got a point. No matter how much me or this damn idiot are around, wipin' out every damn one of 'em out there ain't gonna be easy."

"By the way, I personally think it's better not to do that. Reason: leading man's intuition!"

"Shut your damn mouth, I said!"

Setting aside the two of them, who'd started squabbling once more over whether or not to shut a mouth, Heinkel's question still hung there unresolved.
Honestly, Heinkel's mind was, to some extent, already made up. If he could get away with not giving up, then he couldn't bring himself to give up. All that was left was that he simply wanted a reason.
To Heinkel, wanting that reason, Al clicked the fittings of his helmet with a finger and said,

"Like you said, Heinkel, the five of us turning this whole situation around is aiming way too high. So what we ought to be doing isn't some grand feat of saving the Empire. — It's laying down a path."

"A path, is it?"

At Rowan's question, after taking a sip from his gourd flask and wiping his mouth on his sleeve, Al nodded, "Yeah."
He turned his head and fixed his gaze on the closed window — and beyond it, the imperial capital, now transformed into a city of the dead.
And then—

"Saving the Empire's a hero's job. What we're gonna do is lay the groundwork for that. As much of it as we can, grabbing at whatever's in reach — 'cause there's no telling what'll end up being useful."

At Al's words, which carried some strange note of conviction, Heinkel blinked.
It was the sort of remark that sounded as though he had someone specific in mind who might pull it off, but of course Heinkel had no idea who that might be, and no doubt the same went for Rowan and Groovy.
The one exception to that, however, lit up at the remark, eyes shining—

"— In other words, foreshadowing!!"

Declaring this in high spirits and full of enthusiasm, the young "Blue Lightning" welcomed Al's policy.
Given the balance of personalities in the room, it didn't take long at all for that to be settled as the consensus of the five gathered there.
