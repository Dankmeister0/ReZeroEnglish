Arc 7 Chapter 89: "Kafma Irulux"

——Even in the imperial lands where such a diverse array of demi-human tribes live out their lives, there exist those regarded as heretical.

The 'Insect Cage Tribe' was precisely that. Even amid an empire teeming with demi-humans of every conceivable ecology, their way of being was so bizarre and unnatural that they could never escape the eyes that saw them as heretics.

In outward appearance, the Insect Cage Tribe bears no great difference from the human race.
At most, many of them are racially dark-skinned, and there is a cultural custom of tattooing — nothing so immediately obvious as the distinctive eyes of the 'Cyclops Tribe' or the 'Evil Eye Tribe,' or the distinctive limbs of the 'Multi-Arm Tribe' or the 'Long-Leg Tribe,' let alone the 'Beastfolk' or 'Half-Beast Tribe.'

And yet the Insect Cage Tribe is viewed with peculiar eyes by other tribes, and the reason lies in their ecology.
That is the trait unique to the Insect Cage Tribe: their way of living in symbiosis with an 'Insect' placed inside the body.

As mentioned above, compared to other demi-humans, the Insect Cage Tribe has little outward difference from the human race. If they never took an 'Insect' into themselves, they could probably live out their lives passing as human.
But they do not do so. The tribe takes 'Insects' into their bodies and inherits their traits.
This is, so to speak, the acquisition of demi-human characteristics after birth — a forbidden art that remakes the body one was born with. This is the foremost reason the other demi-human tribes keep their distance.

This sets them apart even from the Weaponkin, who are born with parts of their bodies already metallized and reforge those parts into whatever weapon they desire as they grow, or the Glow People, who are believed to increase the brilliance of the glintstone growing from their brow by absorbing the souls of those they kill.

Because they never mix with outsiders and never leave their homeland, much of the Insect Cage Tribe's ecology remains shrouded in mystery.
Mistaken knowledge often spreads hand in hand with prejudice, and if one ever had the chance to actually hear the rumors, there are some an Insect Cage Tribesman could not help but laugh at.
But there is rarely any opportunity to correct those rumors, and just as rarely any chance for the misunderstanding to be cleared up.

Among the more common misconceptions are those concerning when the Insect Cage Tribe takes the 'Insect' into their bodies, and the point of contact between the nature of the 'Insects' themselves and the tribe — in other words, its history, which touches on the very essence of the matter.

To begin with, taking an 'Insect' into one's body carries considerable danger.
In the southern reaches of the Vollachian Empire, deep within the settlements where the Insect Cage Tribe dwells, lies the home of the 'Insects' — a cavern teeming with grotesque creatures and poisonous air, called the 'Abyss.' The 'Insects' that live there are strange in form even by their own standard, utterly unlike what one would normally picture as an insect.
Who first thought to take such an unidentifiable creature into themselves, no one knows.

The learned consensus is that it was probably deviants seeking laws beyond the ordinary — Curse Arts Users, Shinobi, and the like — who devised this forbidden method to gain power unattainable through conventional arts.
In any case, from the perspective of such beings, the existence of the Insect Cage Tribe is nothing more than a byproduct.
The ancestors of the Insect Cage Tribe were those who took the unknowable 'Insect' into their bodies and desired symbiosis with the power it granted after the fact, and that madness has been passed down, generation after generation, to the present day.

Let us return to the matter at hand. — The Insect Cage Tribe's custom is to hold the rite of first taking in an 'Insect' upon waiting until the age of twelve.
Until that age, one trains body and mind to become a fitting Vessel for the 'Insect,' and upon actually undergoing the rite, if recognized by the host 'Insect,' one properly greets the moment of hatching.
After that, only once the symbiotic 'Insect' has been fully mastered and brought under complete control is one said to have truly emerged — molted — and only then is one permitted to call themselves a full-fledged member of the Insect Cage Tribe.

The rite is forbidden before the age of twelve because taking in an 'Insect' is a matter of life and death.
Attempt it before body and spirit are ready, and the 'Insect' one tries to take in will devour and kill the challenger. Twelve is merely the earliest one may attempt it — if the Vessel is not yet ready, the rite may be postponed until as late as fifteen. If one still cannot manage it by then, that person is deemed unfit to be welcomed as a member of the Insect Cage Tribe and is cast into the 'Abyss,' to become feed for the 'Insects.'

As for the rite of taking in the 'Insect' itself, the suffering involved defies description.
The blood that flows through a person's body comes in different types, and if one tries to make up for insufficient blood with another's blood of a different type, it becomes a threat to life. The agony of the rite of taking in an 'Insect' is close to that.
It feels as though all the blood coursing through one's body has turned to poison, rotting the organs and searing the brain.

The 'Insect' taken in becomes a chrysalis that tests whether the host is worthy of it, and over three days and three nights decides whether to make the person its Vessel, or simply dissolve and devour them whole.
When the chrysalis breaks, if the person still retains human form, the rite is a success — symbiosis with the 'Insect' is established.

Once the 'Insect' is taken into the body and one hatches from the chrysalis, the Insect Cage Tribesman undergoes their first physical transformation.
Some gain antennae or wings, some find their eyes becoming compound, some sprout extra limbs, and others find their hands, fingers, or torso covered in a carapace — the changes vary widely.
It is because these traits resemble those of actual insects that the Insect Cage Tribe came to be called what they are, even though the 'Insects' they take in are utterly unlike insects in the ordinary sense.

Of course, no matter how much one's outward form changes, the person themselves does not change.
And yet it is also true that there are those who view the Insect Cage Tribe — who undergo a rite that deliberately deforms themselves after birth — as deviants who desire to become mere vessels for 'Insects.'

Prejudice awaits at the end of the agony they endure, and yet the Insect Cage Tribe's way does not change.
To be recognized as having molted into a full member of the Insect Cage Tribe, taking in a single 'Insect' is the requirement. But the more 'Insects' one takes in, the greater one's power as a warrior becomes.
Hence, the tribe's elite warriors have taken in a minimum of three 'Insects.'
However, the more 'Insects' one takes in, the greater the risk of them devouring one another within the body, and the greater the danger to the host's life. For this reason, the number of symbiotic 'Insects' is directly tied to a warrior's quality.
The current chieftain of the Insect Cage Tribe is called a warrior among warriors, a towering figure feared and revered as the tribe's hero for having taken as many as eight 'Insects' into his body.

——And Kafma Irulux was a monster who had taken in thirty-two 'Insects.'

The birth of a monster that put even that hero's feat to shame — from the very start, it defied the laws of the Insect Cage Tribe.
The rite of taking in an 'Insect,' which was never supposed to be performed before one's twelfth birthday for fear of endangering the Vessel's life, was first undergone by Kafma only a few days after his birth.

The elder brother of the chieftain, a man who had spent his whole life outshone by his gifted younger sibling — his madness was turned upon his own child.

That was what Kafma was told of his father, once he came of an age to understand his own circumstances.
His actual father had been executed by the hand of his own younger brother, the chieftain, so his true intentions are unknown. What is known is that his father declared his newborn child dead and hid Kafma away, performing the rite of taking in an 'Insect' upon him every single year.
Ironically, it was at the age of twelve — the very year his fellow tribesmen would undergo the rite for the first time — that Kafma's existence was discovered and he was first brought out of hiding. By then he was a monster already living in symbiosis with thirteen 'Insects.'

An existence beyond the tribe's imagining — even among the Insect Cage Tribe, opinions on how to treat Kafma were divided.
His father, who had broken the law and, in a fit of emotion, been executed for supposedly cursing his own child, was already dead, and the reason Kafma had been able to take in more than ten 'Insects' remained entirely unknown. In the end, the chieftain — his uncle — declared that he would take responsibility for Kafma's existence, and Kafma was permitted to live.

This much is no lie: Kafma Irulux was truly grateful to the chieftain.
His uncle by blood treated him, for better or worse, with a certain measured distance and restraint, never heaping excessive blame upon him for his father's crimes, nor showing him excessive pity either.
His uncle's attitude — neither especially warm nor especially cold — was, in effect, a declaration that he would not treat Kafma as anything special within the Insect Cage Tribe, and for that, Kafma was grateful.

No matter how his uncle chose to treat him, it remained a fact that he himself was an anomaly even within the tribe.
Those of the generation who did not yet know the suffering of taking in an 'Insect' kept their distance from Kafma; those of the generation already recognized as having molted feared Kafma for the unimaginable number he had taken in.
The Insect Cage Tribe was already viewed as heretical by other races — and within that tribe, Kafma occupied a position viewed as heretical still further.

Of course, Kafma had done nothing to deserve blame, nor any reason to be persecuted.
He could have ignored the stares directed at him and continued, as before, a life entirely disconnected from the outside world. But despite having been raised differently from others, Kafma's nature was noble at its core.
He could not accept an environment in which his own kin held him in fear.

To become a member of the Insect Cage Tribe, one is required to take in an 'Insect.'
But Kafma had passed through that stage before his self-awareness had even awakened. So, in order to understand his fellow tribesmen in some other way, he spared no effort.

He actively sought out others, learned the way of the warrior from his uncle the chieftain, and through patient, persistent contact with every generation, demonstrated that he was no different from them.
And to be respected as a warrior of the Insect Cage Tribe, he challenged himself to undergo the rite of taking in a new 'Insect.'

There was opposition from those around him. Kafma, who had already taken in thirteen 'Insects,' was without precedent in the tribe's history, and there were even hopes for how much he might grow simply by maturing as he was.
For him to lose his life before molting, they said, would be an unforgivable mistake.

He understood the elders' reasoning, but Kafma could not accept standing still.
Taking an 'Insect' without permission, Kafma undertook the rite without any witness present. Only that reckless drive, he would later chide himself, had he truly inherited from his father.
But in that moment, Kafma recklessly took in his fourteenth 'Insect,' and endured the hellish agony of three days and three nights of coughing blood, and survived.

And so Kafma Irulux at last achieved the hatching that made him a member of the Insect Cage Tribe.

Despite the peculiar circumstances of his birth, Kafma carried within him a noble spirit, and he won a degree of respect within the tribe that set him apart from all others, becoming the strongest being in the Insect Cage Tribe's history.

In the Vollachian Empire, it is the strong who are honored, and glory is bestowed upon them.
Bearing the tribe's expectations and hopes upon his shoulders, Kafma too rose to become one of Vollachia's 'Generals,' and set out to make known the strength of the Insect Cage Tribe as a race.
Naturally, there will always be those with loose tongues, wherever one goes. There were those who, having swallowed the false rumors about the Insect Cage Tribe whole, subjected him to heartless abuse and harassment. But it was a trivial thing.
To Kafma, who had stepped out into the wider world, it was a trivial thing. Yes — a trivial thing.

——Kafma Irulux was a 'monster' born of the Insect Cage Tribe's history.

Possessed of a noble spirit and a sense of fellowship toward his kin, he had fought at the forefront, protecting the tribe.
But no matter how much effort he poured in, his fellow tribesmen continued to draw a line between themselves and Kafma. Precisely because they knew how difficult symbiosis with an 'Insect' truly was, they could not think of Kafma as the same kind of being as themselves.
And so, for Kafma Irulux, leaving his homeland was an act of hope.

He believed that only in a place without his kin, without the Insect Cage Tribe, could he see the light he sought.
The hatching, the molting, that every member of the Insect Cage Tribe was supposed to undergo naturally as they grew — the process by which one is recognized not as a monster, but simply as an individual — he sought at last to achieve it for himself, in his own way, at last——.

"——!!"

Facing him head-on, a golden tiger roared and lunged, and Kafma clenched his back teeth hard.
The enemy who had explosively bulked his upper body and raised his sharp beast claws — the warrior who had called himself Garfiel Tinzel — Kafma met him with everything he had.

"I apologize for underestimating you!"

Flapping the torn wings on his back, Kafma unleashed purple brambles from his outstretched arms.
Among the 'Insects' Kafma had taken in, this was one of the newer ones, but he relied on it heavily for its versatility. Yet even its suppressive force could not halt Garfiel's momentum.

The claw, swung as if scooping upward, tore through the brambles' heads along with the very floor of the rampart, and the sensation of the wounded 'Insect' screaming inside his body shook Kafma's brain.
The brambles that seemed to gush forth without limit were, too, part of the 'Insects' Kafma had taken in.
Naturally, when wounded, there was a corresponding backlash. He was simply suppressing it through sheer will, acting as though it had no effect at all.

"GAAAAAAAH!!"

As Garfiel closed in without slackening his momentum, his beast claw swinging down at the edge of his vision, Kafma used the acceleration of his wings to slide along the rampart, slipping past the tiger's side.
The impact and roar rang out right beside him, and Kafma shuddered at how what should have been an evasion with room to spare had, at the last instant, barely made it.

Compared to the exchange just before, this was even faster, and its power had increased.
Growing stronger in the midst of battle, or drawing out power he had been holding in reserve — neither explanation was realistic. Nearly every change in fighting strength that occurs on a battlefield is a decline.
Naturally so — the peak condition one prepares before a battle only erodes with every second once the fighting begins; reserves run dry, and one's best output becomes impossible to sustain.
That is precisely why, in a fight, it is crucial to unleash one's maximum firepower, one's finest technique, on the very first strike.

Of course, Kafma too was no exception to this, delivering his own maximum firepower and combat arts upon his enemy.
Garfiel should have been the same. — And precisely because he should have been, this made no sense.

Even granting that he had undergone Beastification, for his power and speed to increase in the middle of a fight was against all reason.

"And more than that, you bear grievous wounds——"

Kafma's earlier attack could fairly be called a surprise strike, or even a form of poison assassination.
Whatever one might think of such methods, Kafma himself felt no aversion to the act itself. In battle, if clinging to propriety could mean the difference between life and death, one ought to choose whatever means serve one's goal.
Though that principle might change, of course, if such scruples were themselves tied to whether one could perform at full strength.

"————"

Regardless, the wounds covering Garfiel's entire body were far from ordinary.
The greatest was the head wound inflicted by Kafma's earlier attack, but the damage wrought inside by the 'Insect' Kafma had forced in was taking a heavy toll as well. And yet the same method would not work twice.
Garfiel's body, having swallowed a fire magic stone, still burned red-hot even now.

Though his whole body was wreathed in flame, the inside of his body ought to be an even more unmanageable inferno.
Taking in an 'Insect' carries danger for the host, but an 'Insect' without a host is likewise extremely fragile, and would die easily if thrown into even a slightly harsh environment.
There was no way an 'Insect' could survive in a body that was continuously, actively burning.

"A fearsome notion."

Even with healing magic, one cannot kill an 'Insect' whose purpose is not to wound. And yet if the 'Insect' is not eliminated, magic cannot heal the wound.
This was the best possible solution to break that paradox — but it did not seem like something arrived at through thought.
Rather, it was likely the result of following instinct over reason.
If he had stopped to think it through, swallowing a magic stone and setting his own body ablaze would never be something one could actually go through with.

"——!"

The instant he slid into the wide-open flank, red antennae shot from both of Kafma's shoulders like cannon shells.
The tips of these 'Insect' horns, formed from his transformed shoulder bones, pierced through abdominal muscles like steel, sending Garfiel's body — letting out a pained groan — flying with a thunderous force off the rampart.
A telling blow — but Kafma did not escape unscathed either.

"Gh."

His two antennae snapped off at the root, and Kafma's cheek twitched at the pain that numbed him down to the bone. A victory bought with pain — with that, he could well have dropped to one knee.
But Kafma could not allow himself to be foolish enough to kneel here.
Because——

"Gaaah! Ohh! Grrrrraaaaahh!"

Garfiel, who should have been sent flying, was climbing back up the rampart, claws dug into the stone, and leaping high right before Kafma's eyes.
The flank that should have been pierced through by the antennae was gushing red steam as the wound closed. Kafma let out a breath at the outrageous sight of the healing magic's pale light shimmering violently as the wound rapidly mended.

"Ha."

And then, realizing that this was the impulse to laugh, Kafma raised a hand to his own mouth.
Then, as though giving up, he lowered his hand and slowly shook his head.

"Delightful."

Let him admit it.
Kafma Irulux was thoroughly enjoying this battle against Garfiel Tinzel.

"OOOOOOHHH!!"

Garfiel roared and brought both arms down, becoming a spinning golden disc as he fell. Judging he could not fully block it even with both arms raised, Kafma moved forward instead, slipping beneath Garfiel's legs to circle behind him, aiming at the wide-open back he glimpsed from the corner of his eye.

But the slashing blow Kafma unleashed with his wings without turning around was struck aside by a jagged slab of stone that leapt up between them. Just before Kafma's attack — having slipped beneath his legs — could land, Garfiel planted his extended forelimb on the ground and activated his own Divine Protection, blocking the strike.
Moreover, as his wing was struck against the hard stone and torn apart with a crack, from beyond it, Garfiel's back leg — with the forelimb planted — lashed out with tremendous force.

"——!?"

With both of them still facing away from each other, Kafma's body took the ferocious blow and went flying.
Bracing himself to hold his ground proved his undoing — his fully extended body could not disperse the impact, and coughing blood, he bounced across the floor, his tall frame skipping along the top of the wall.
Bouncing high once, twice, and beyond the momentum of his tumbling body, he glimpsed Garfiel's face turning to look at him.
And there——

"A second time——!"

His chest opened, ribs spread wide, and the reddened organ nestled within pulsed, unleashing a shockwave that shot straight toward Garfiel.
This, Kafma's trump card, was not the result of any newly taken 'Insect,' but a new organ born from the symbiotic coexistence of all thirty-two 'Insects' he had taken in over the years.

The functions of multiple 'Insects' combined, and the shockwave it unleashed swallowed and destroyed anything in its path with ferociously fine vibration, grinding it to powder — a devastating blow.
Any warrior who took that invisible force full across their body, in an instant, would be reduced to a mist of blood.

"——Buh."

That conviction did not waver, confirmed by the sight of Garfiel's golden fur stained with blood.
Any warrior would turn to a mist of blood. Therefore——

"——You monster."

Braking his tumbling body with an arm driven into the floor, Kafma raised his head.
And there, his blood-soaked upper body trembling, mouth wide open, Garfiel came charging straight at him. The 'monster' who had taken a blow that should have killed any warrior outright, charging straight ahead.

"————"

The massive fist that swung struck Kafma's face, and the fist he reflexively swung back caught his opponent's jaw and snapped it upward. From there, a ferocious exchange of blows raged on, blood blossoming red across the top of the wall.

A ferocious battle no one could dare step between — a collision of monster against monster.

"Ha."

Breathing out, peering past the pain, Kafma poured everything he had into it.
The peerless monster born of his singular origin — that was the reason Kafma had been kept at a distance by his own kin, and it was the fate he himself had cursed.
By leaving his closed-off homeland and stepping out into the wider world, Kafma had sought to know it —
The grounds on which he could proudly say that he was not a monster.

But reality had not been so kind.
Even out in the world, Kafma's extraordinary strength was regarded as beyond the norm, and viewed with suspicion. Many who had stood shoulder to shoulder with him as regular soldiers feared his abilities and kept the strange one at arm's length.
He had begun to think that no matter where he went, he would always be a monster — that it was a fate he could never escape.
And yet——

'——Get up here, General Kafma! Let us give our all together for His Excellency's sake! Now, no need to overthink it — like you, we too are all monsters through and through!'

Those words, spoken with a loud, valiant laugh by that giant of a man, had been a gift from heaven to Kafma.
Wanting to deny that he was a monster, Kafma had tried to fit in with his own kind. When that wish went unfulfilled, he had sought it outside, and failed there as well.
But look upward instead — what did he find?

Monsters that even Kafma Irulux, feared as a monster himself, could not measure up to, packed together in droves.
Kafma had not wanted to be told that he was not a monster.
What he had not wanted was to be alone — unable to share himself with anyone, unable to be understood by anyone.

Monster or not, the world had not left Kafma behind.
And so——

"——This battle against you, too, is the same."

At point-blank range, a barrage of antennae fired off, and Garfiel took them full across his body, healing each wound the instant it opened, forcing his way through by sheer endurance.
This extraordinary defensive power and vitality, this superhuman regenerative ability likely bolstered by his Divine Protection — this was the mechanism behind the 'monster' before him, and the very reason Kafma's blood was rising.

The brambles unleashed from his right arm wrapped around the great tiger's entire body, but were forcibly torn free even as the thorns sliced into flesh. The fist, covered in a carapace that repelled every slashing blow, collided head-on with Garfiel's silver-gleaming gauntlet and was gloriously shattered.
The 'Insect' eggs planted by the finger-flick of his left hand were burned away by flame, and the shockwave meant to churn the internal organs, released by a pressed-in knee, likewise failed to outpace his recovery and ended in nothing.

Delightful. Ah, how utterly delightful.
In the end, he was a warrior — no matter how much airs he put on, a monster — and in time with the cheers of the 'Insects' within him, a smile came to rest on Kafma's cheeks and would not be peeled away.

The thirty-two 'Insects,' beings closer to him than family, one with him — they rejoiced and clamored at having been given the chance to unleash their all.

He had to seize victory.
For the greater cause, for the sake of His Excellency the Emperor who leads the empire, to repay the benefactor who had raised him to this station, for the sake of his kin who wished for the Insect Cage Tribe's standing to rise.

"——Oi, where the hell are you lookin'."

Amid the pain and breathlessness and accelerating thoughts painting over his brain, a voice reached him.
Both of them, driving blows of a force that could kill the other into vital points with furious intensity, desperate, with no room left anywhere for proper conversation — and yet it reached him.

The green eyes before him bore straight into his own, bloodshot and piercing straight through to his soul.
Sharp fangs clacked as they sought to drink blood and flesh, and the creak of grinding bone drove away every other sense.
And even while using all of it as mere flourish for the battle, the monster before him roared.

"I'm right here."

"————"

"Right now, this instant — nothin' else gets to butt in."

In that instant, the colors of the world receded, the sound of wind and the ringing in his ears fell away, and only that great enemy before him became the whole of Kafma Irulux's world.
What a graceless thing to make him say, Kafma thought, ashamed of his own inattentiveness.

And then, casting aside even that shame at his own gracelessness, he nodded.

"——Yes, only you and I."

In that instant, colliding fists sank into each other's faces, and time accelerated.
His open palm seized Kafma's face, and Kafma's skull screamed under the inhuman grip strength. But Kafma too thrust his hand into his opponent's mouth, and there poured brambles straight into his body.

If he could not be destroyed from outside, then from within.
The overflowing brambles ran rampant inside him, closing in on a decisive end that would eat through him from the inside out. But even as the brambles poured into him, Garfiel lifted Kafma's body, raised it, and smashed it down against the rampart.

"——!"

His back was driven into the rampart, lifted, and dropped again. Up, down. Up, down. Up up up up up, and down down down down down, stomped upon.
Cracks spread through the rampart where his whole body was buried, and the tip of the star-shaped battlement split in two. His vision turned crimson, and blood spilled out in place of breath.

Even so, the brambles did not lose their strength, continuing to pour into Garfiel's body.
Until Kafma's strength gave out, the 'Insect' would greedily press for victory.

"——Guh."

The tiger's open jaws could not bite through the thick, bundled brambles. No matter how deeply the claws dug in, the writhing layer of brambles was too thick. Whatever happened, they would not let go.
But brambles were finite; there was a limit to how much could be released.
If he expended them all here, he would be throwing away, needlessly, an effective means of driving back the other rebel forces closing on this rampart. But this victory was worth that price.

——No, the monster called Garfiel Tinzel was worth that price.

"Ahh, ah, ahhhhhhhh——!!"

Ignoring the pain of every bone in his body being wrenched and cracked, a roar tore from Kafma's throat.
The overflowing brambles filled Garfiel's body entirely, and the pressure, with nowhere left to go, would burst and bring death.
No matter how he twisted, no matter what form of Beastification he underwent, Kafma would not let him escape.
Gathering the remaining strength of every 'Insect' in his body, Kafma pinned Garfiel down, leaning forward to wrench out his victory — and then he saw something unbelievable.

"——Wha—"

The golden great tiger, swollen to near-bursting from the brambles poured through his mouth. Just as his body was about to burst, the pressure of the brambles rapidly began to fail.
Why? — Because an escape route had opened up for the brambles that had forced their way inside.

Garfiel's sharp claws had torn open his own abdomen sideways, and from that wound, the brambles came gushing out.
This terrifyingly reckless, self-destructive tactic was an act of barbarism that drew death itself close. If the brambles rushed to fill this newly created wound, by the same logic that kept his jaws from closing, the wound in his belly would be forced open. Do that, and his body would split in two — an end.
Yes, it was an act of barbarism so foolish it could only invite death.

But the instant Kafma witnessed that act of barbarism, the momentary blank that formed in his mind granted Garfiel — on the verge of bursting — a single breath's reprieve.

The jaws closed. There came the sound of brambles being torn apart, and the great tiger's massive mouth shut.
Losing the decisive blow the brambles represented, and before Kafma could even register it, Garfiel's fist drove forward, the hard impact of the silver gauntlet blasting through Kafma's face.

Knocked down onto the top of the wall, lying flat on the floor, Kafma took a fist slammed into his face, and the impact driven deep into him this time dealt decisive damage to the rampart.
A thunderous roar rang out, and the rampart of the imperial capital Lupugana, once sung as an impregnable wall, began to collapse.

Feeling that collapse as sound and against his own back, Kafma looked up straight ahead at Garfiel, drawing back his fist.
The boy, slowly releasing his Beastification and returning to his original human form — the wound in his belly that he himself had torn open closed up, gushing bloody steam.
Watching that fatal wound erased at a terrifying speed, as though it had never happened, Kafma burst out laughing.

What a ridiculous sight.

"...You monster."

Right after he murmured that, letting out a breath, the collapse fully set in, and the rampart crumbled away.
Falling amid the collapsing wall and its mixed rubble, Kafma's consciousness slowly faded, faded, fading away with no strength left even to try to hold onto it——.

"Your Excellency, forgive me..."

Falling, disgusted with himself for playing the loyal retainer even in this, his very last moment.


——Even the voice of the 'Insects,' which he had heard without pause since the moment of his birth, seemed strangely silent now.

Forcibly grabbing hold of the man's body as it fell defenseless, Garfiel kicked off the rubble to escape the collapsing site.
Killing his momentum by scraping his heels against the ground, he turned back to see the rampart crumbling down with a thunderous roar, a great hole now torn open in what had been an impregnable stronghold.

"Blew it clean open, didn't I. A hole in the wind."

Recalling the words spoken before the battle began, Emilia's rallying call, he twisted his cheek into a grin — and the corner of his badly split mouth throbbed with the motion, making Garfiel yelp, "Guoh!"
Hastily pressing a hand to the wound, he activated healing magic.

"Aah, damn, that hurts...but."

Roughly healing the split in his mouth, Garfiel stared down at his own hand.
A sudden, fierce battle, and honestly, he'd taken enough damage that it wouldn't have been strange if he'd died — but all the countless wounds across his body had closed up, and the throbbing ache was already fading into afterglow.
Even while undergoing Beastification, he thought he'd managed to keep fighting with a certain degree of composure. That was probably why his wounds had healed so quickly. — Was that really all there was to it, though?

"...Am I gettin' stronger, huh."

Clenching his open hand into a fist, Garfiel muttered as much.
He had no real sense of it. Fortunately, he supposed, he hadn't run into an opponent that had forced him to go all-out like this — not until now.
Aside from the fight against Kurgan of the 'Eight Arms' at the water gate city, every battle since then had, for Garfiel, felt somehow unsatisfying, incomplete.

With that restraint removed, having fought once again with everything he had, there was a definite feeling now.
He'd broken through one more wall compared to before. And in this fight, he'd made it certain.
So——

"——That 'monster' label you gave me, I'll hand it right back to ya."

So saying, Garfiel lowered Kafma's body, which hung from his right arm, down onto the ground, and snorted.
Kafma's chest rose and fell faintly — still breathing. If this were truly war, and he wanted victory in the truest sense, he knew he shouldn't leave the enemy alive.

But Emilia had said she wanted to reduce the number of people who died by their own hands.
And Otto, too, had told him to beat Garfiel — no, to beat the enemy — down until they couldn't stand, and no further.
Emilia had said it from the heart, surely, and Otto out of concern for him. Words spoken for his sake.
He wanted to honor that.
So, here in this place——

"——This one's mine."

So saying, having brought down one corner of the star-shaped fortress's peak, Garfiel thrust his fist into the air.
