Arc 8 Chapter 51: "Tennōzan"

—Thanks to the strategy devised by Natsuki Subaru, the "Save the Vollachian Empire from Ruin Corps" had scattered across the imperial capital.
It was a plan meant to achieve a kind of coordination in which each person fulfilling their own role would avoid interfering with the others — but the linchpin of that plan wasn't actually any of the people fighting in the capital.
The linchpin of this operation was, in a word, the full might of the Empire that remained behind in the fortress city of Garkla.

A great city that had deployed a massive military force and sheltered within it the personnel who served as Vollachia's brain and heart.
How long could they hold back the army of the Undead surging forth to bring this city down? That was the time limit on the final battle that would decide whether the Vollachian Empire lived or died — and by extension, a critical question that would determine the fate not just of the Empire, but of the entire world.
The more of the Undead's strength was drawn to the fortress city, the easier it would be for the "Save It from Ruin Corps" in the capital to move. At the same time, if the fortress city fell, then even if the operation in the capital succeeded, rebuilding the Empire afterward would be impossible.
In other words—


"—This, precisely, is the 'Tennōzan' that will decide the outcome."

Swinging his knight's sword and voicing a word unfamiliar to the ear, Julius Juukulius's tall frame dashed along the fortress city's wall, cutting a dashing figure through the air.
The point of the elegant knight's blade was aimed at the horde of Undead clinging to the wall, attempting to force their way into the city. Against enemies who drove their weapons into the wall to fashion makeshift footholds and climb, Julius spun as he descended, his sword flashing without mercy.

"I won't let you!"

He rained cuts down on the backs and arms of the clinging Undead, slicing them down to the ground below.
Then, landing atop one of the very swords they had used as a foothold, Julius turned his eyes to the countless weapons driven into the wall.

"Aro! Lend me your wind!"

The instant he called out, a green wind rose up and wound around the weapons stuck in the wall, wrenching them free and undoing in a single stroke all the effort the Undead had put into building their footholds.
Finally, snapping the sword he stood on with a stomp, Julius leapt and returned lightly to the top of the wall.
Tickling the green light of his contracted spirit with his fingertips, Julius let out a faint breath.

He'd succeeded in stopping the enemies clinging to this stretch of wall, but he couldn't afford to let his guard down.
Even if the outermost wall of the city were breached, there was still a second and third defensive wall within the city, and beyond that a sturdy fortress—

"Them getting this far already — faster than we expected, this is."

"—Lady Taritta."

The one who called out to Julius as he stood atop the wall catching his breath was a dark-skinned woman with part of her black hair dyed blue — Taritta, the female warrior who led the People of Shudrak.
Every member of the tribe wielded a bow, capable of striking the Undead from afar, and the Shudrak warriors were the main fighting force in this fortress city's siege defense. Taritta, whose skill with the bow stood out even among the already bow-adept Shudrak, was one of the linchpins of the current defensive strength.
In fact, even now she was accomplishing, with arrows loosed from atop the wall, the very same thing Julius had just done in knocking down the weapons stuck in the wall — hindering the Undead from scaling over.
Her skill was genuinely admirable and reassuring, but her words carried a harsh reality that couldn't be ignored.

"—In this short span of time, the front line has already been pushed back that much."

So saying, Julius turned his eyes to beyond the wall and traced the scar beneath his left eye with a finger.
At the far end of Julius's keen gaze, the horizon seemed to shimmer — the heads of the Undead army surging toward them, visibly confirming that an army of unprecedented, monstrous scale was closing in.
The defensive line that had originally been set up outside the wall had already been breached, forcing the imperial soldiers fighting outside to fall back and contract their front. Of course, given the enemy's sheer numbers, it had no doubt been anticipated that this would happen eventually.

"The tough ones mixed in, now and then, those are the real trouble."

"I agree. The moment we spot one, we'll have to have whoever's skilled and nearby deal with it, but..."

That was the moment Julius had just finished speaking.

"—!!"

Below, on the battlefield, a cluster of soldiers fighting together as one group was sent flying all at once.
Looking closer, the ones letting out heavy screams as they were thrown were members of the Pleiades Battalion, the reinforcements Subaru had brought to the capital.
By whatever bizarre trick, this was a battalion in which every single member boasted extraordinary strength — and yet the one who'd sent its members flying in every direction was a female Undead of a terrifyingly vicious appearance, wielding greatswords in both arms — no, whose arms had been fused into greatswords themselves.

"Damn... you got us good..."
"Wait, wait, wait, wait! Don't charge in without a plan! You'll put the burden on your brothers!"
"Fall back! Surround her, everyone, and take her down together!"

Before that vicious Undead, the group that should have been struck down got back on its feet, tough as ever.
That they'd taken no fatalities and their fighting spirit hadn't broken was praiseworthy, but even to Julius's eye, that woman warrior's skill was unmistakably first-rate.
Thinking he had to rush there at once, Julius left the wall to Taritta and moved to leap down to the field below—

"—No need, the 'Lion Knight' already went."

What struck Julius's ears was a cheerful voice utterly out of place on a battlefield. The information that voice carried did, in fact, bring about a change on the field below.
Goz, wielding his golden warhammer, had charged straight at the dual-greatsword warrior.

"We never had the chance to meet face to face, but there's no mistaking you! So you've been brought back, 'Sword-Slave Empress'!!"

Goz's roaring warhammer and the woman warrior's greatswords collided, and a shockwave tore across the battlefield.
Both wielded weapons of such monstrous weight that no ordinary person could even lift them, and the violent force unleashed by their clash scattered the imperial soldiers and Undead around them like toys tossed by a storm.
Neither side gave an inch, but the advance had at least been halted. Leaving that spot to Goz was the best course.

"My thanks for stopping me, Sir Flop."

"Ha ha ha, think nothing of it! You and Miss Taritta need to keep your focus on the fight right in front of you, after all! This is about the extent of what a powerless fellow like me can do to help!"

The one laughing so unpretentiously was Flop, cheerful as ever even on the battlefield.
As a non-combatant, and moreover the brother of an imperial consort candidate, he should have been ordered to stand by inside the fortress. That he'd shown his face here at what could be called the front line left Taritta astonished.

"Flop!? Why is it you, here..."

"Oh, it's just that things have taken on the look of total war. Even those of us who can't fight have mountains of things we ought to be doing — treating the wounded, repairing equipment, and so on. Naturally, I'm one of them."

Thumping his own thin chest, Flop held out a quiver of arrows to Taritta.
Taritta accepted it reflexively — she carried her own quiver on her back, but her remaining arrows had grown worryingly few, and Flop's resupply could only be called perfectly timed.
He'd no doubt come walking along, handing out quivers to the other Shudrak on the wall as well.
Everyone fights, non-combatants included — his attitude embodied those very words.

"How goes the battle?"

"— Everyone's fighting hard, they are. If we stick to defense, hold the wall a while longer, we should. Only, that too..."

"If the enemy brings out a Flying Dragon Rider, that's another matter entirely."

Taritta had answered Flop's question, and Julius took up the conclusion in place of her hesitation. At that answer, Flop's expression, too, tightened into seriousness.
Precisely because he was a young man who gave the impression of always wearing a cheerful smile, the mere act carried an odd sort of intensity. But it was only natural that his expression would harden.
Anyone from the Vollachian Empire knew more than well enough just how formidable a Flying Dragon Rider could be.

"—"

Right after the battle began, the first wave of dead Flying Dragons — undead Flying Dragons — that had come surging in was repelled by a concentrated attack from Julius and the People of Shudrak. Since then, the Shudrak assigned the role of hunting undead Flying Dragons had been reliably handling the sporadic aerial threats that continued to appear.
But that response was only possible because the enemy had no undead Flying Dragon Rider.
A lone Flying Dragon with no rider and a Flying Dragon paired with a Flying Dragon Rider were simply not comparable in strength.
A single pair of Flying Dragon Rider and dragon, pitted against a hundred lone Flying Dragons, would likely overwhelm them outright. That was how thoroughly a Flying Dragon Rider, making tactical use of the sky, existed on a different level entirely.

—Once before, Julius himself had crossed blades with a Flying Dragon Rider.

That time, thanks to the help of Ferris, who'd been traveling with him, he'd miraculously come away with the win — but had Julius been alone, victor and vanquished would surely have traded places.
There likely weren't two Flying Dragon Riders of that caliber in the whole Empire, but if this front against the Undead saw not just undead Flying Dragons but an undead Flying Dragon Rider astride one appear, the situation would change entirely.

"Of course, it's precisely because cases of a Flying Dragon Rider and their beloved dragon both being brought back together as the dead are so exceedingly rare that the skies over this city haven't been taken from us yet, I'd think."

Flop's assessment was likely correct, judging from the current state of the siege.
A Flying Dragon Rider was a threat, but rider and dragon had to build trust over a long span of time and become truly one — man and dragon as a single being — before their true worth could be shown.
That rule couldn't be ignored even by the dead, brought back through some reasoning beyond the ordinary.
Only an undead Flying Dragon that had been together with its undead rider while both were still alive could give rise to what might be called an "undead Flying Dragon Rider."
That, surely, was the reason no undead Flying Dragon Rider had yet been seen in the skies over the fortress city.

"...Still, it's a bitter irony that the one rare example happens to be the most formidable Flying Dragon Rider of all."

What denied that slender relief was the existence of an undead Flying Dragon Rider Julius had confirmed with his own eyes.
The Undead who had appeared amid the battle over the Coupled Dragon Carriages — Balleroy Temeglyph — had ridden astride his beloved dragon, dead just as he was and together with him since before death, and broken through enemy lines with overwhelming mobility.
If he were thrown into this battle, he alone, a single rider, might well collapse the fortress city's entire front.
There was no guarantee he wouldn't appear at this very moment, even now.

"—I don't think you need to worry about that."

"Sir Flop?"

"If some other Flying Dragon Rider were brought back, that'd be different, but there's no need to worry about Balleroy showing up."

With an oddly confident tone and expression, Flop dismissed Julius's caution.
Julius frowned at the remark, and Taritta, who had just loosed an arrow at an Undead clinging to the wall, confirmed it had struck the creature's knee as she spoke,

"Why is it you can say that with such certainty?"

"Balleroy knew that Medium and I were aboard the Coupled Dragon Carriages. Naturally, he'll assume the two of us are here in this fortress city. That'd make it a hard place for him to come, wouldn't it?"

"That is..."

Flop winked one eye shut, and at his answer, Taritta found herself at a loss for words.
Julius shared Taritta's reaction. Flop's reasoning couldn't be called convincing. If he were pinning his hopes on some lingering compassion or mercy in Balleroy, now that he'd become an Undead—

"I'm afraid you can't count on that sort of pre-death humanity remaining. Zombies... the Undead's thinking has been twisted to serve another's will. Otherwise, there's no way this many of the dead would be cooperating in the destruction of their own nation."

In truth, an overwhelming number of Undead, enough to fill the horizon, had been mobilized.
It was hard to believe that every single one of these Undead had harbored a bone-deep grudge against the Vollachian Empire in life and had taken up arms the instant they were revived to see that grudge through.
It was far more natural to think that whoever had revived them was manipulating the Undead as they pleased.
At Julius's point, Flop scratched his cheek with a finger and nodded, saying, "True enough."

"I'm of the same opinion as you, young 'Finest.' To begin with, our friends among the dead have had their thinking altered. And yet, I believe there's a part of them that hasn't changed."

"Then are you saying Sir Balleroy alone has retained some feeling or mercy?"

"That would be terribly moving, if so! But that's not it, young 'Finest.' Actually, I think this is something you or Miss Taritta would notice more readily than I would."

"Us, more than you, is it?"

With nothing coming to mind, Julius and Taritta exchanged glances.
Something that Julius, who had crossed spears with him, and Taritta, who hadn't even met him face to face, would notice more readily than Flop, who'd had a relationship with Balleroy in life — what could that be—

"—His way of fighting."

"—"

"Even if their thinking can be changed from what it was in life, if you strip away their way of fighting too, there's no point in reviving that person in the first place. So our friends among the dead fight the way they fought in life. Am I wrong?"

"...I can't say for certain, but the possibility is high."

He didn't know the pre-death forms of the Undead he'd actually fought in any real depth, but Flop's guess had to be on the mark.
At Julius and Taritta's affirming reactions, Flop smiled.
A deeply mournful smile, tinged somewhere with nostalgia and grief.

"If Balleroy were coming here, he'd have come first, before anyone else, and shot straight through this city's vital point. Balleroy Temeglyph, the 'Magic Bullet Marksman,' didn't take the vanguard. That's my grounds for believing he isn't on this battlefield."

—Balleroy Temeglyph made it a point of principle to hate, above all else, letting his subordinates die pointlessly.

That said, this wasn't because he despised the Iron-Blooded Code that Vollachia upheld, or because he was some humanitarian who rejected imperialism.
The imperial way of thinking, in which the strong were revered and the weak trampled, had actually worked in Balleroy's favor, born as he was into a poor commoner household. It would hardly make sense to use that social system to claw his way up, only to turn his back on it the moment he'd gained status.
Though in truth, Balleroy himself hadn't thought that deeply about his relationship with imperialism. It was simply that the system already in place happened to suit his nature and talents well, so he'd gone along with it.

So, in the end, Balleroy's hatred of pointless deaths among his men had nothing to do with imperialism.
This was a way of thinking Balleroy had acquired later, taught to him by others.

—Two people, above all, had a major influence in shaping Balleroy's character.

One was Serena Dracroy, the benefactor who had recognized his talent, taken him in, and given him the chance at an education and his encounter with his beloved dragon.
The other was Miles, the big-brother figure he'd met in the most rotten way imaginable — a man who had used Balleroy back when he was still a boy too young to know better, tricking him into disposing of the pursuers chasing Miles down for a theft he'd committed.

His encounters and the days spent with these two, who between them held a complicated mix of what was and wasn't typically Vollachian, forged the nature of the man called Balleroy Temeglyph.

And the character thus forged in Balleroy came to deeply hate seeing his subordinates die for nothing.
His subordinates were people within his own reach, his own people. —He hated it when his own people were sacrificed.
That was why, even when he joined a rebellion whose chances of victory had been snuffed out beforehand, engineered entirely for the emperor's convenience, Balleroy hadn't brought his subordinates along — he'd joined it alone.
And in the end, it cost him his life — so Balleroy's judgment had been, in its own wrongheaded way, correct.

—If you fight, deaths among both enemy and ally are unavoidable.
He himself took the lives of others. He couldn't very well ask that the rule simply not apply to his own people. And yet, if he still meant to make that reasoning hold true, there was no way to do it but by his own strength, not another's.

And so the conclusion Balleroy Temeglyph arrived at was the fastest possible sniping.

Riding the sky on his beloved dragon's wings, creating the perfect moment himself, and shooting through the enemy's vital point, its very heart, in the blink of an eye — that was the best move for fulfilling his own wish.

Balleroy was a man of the Empire. However many enemies died, it wouldn't cost his heart so much as a scratch.
But he hated it when his own people died. So Balleroy's sniping sought the swiftest possible resolution. He was convinced that was the best way — not to spare enemy or ally, but to keep casualties from touching his own.
And so—


"—I hear you, Carillon."

Answering the call of his dead beloved dragon, Balleroy rose from beside the bed.
On the bed before him lay Madelyn, unconscious, resting quietly. —Her consciousness had, for now, returned within Mezoreia, her Dragonhusk.

Needless to say, the sheer violence of a "Dragon" was far too dangerous, too overwhelming a force to unleash within the capital.
But considering the enemy they faced, the terrifying thing was that it couldn't even be called overkill.

"I used to serve one of you myself, but you Empire folk sure are somethin' else."

That there was even a monster he could think of who might rival, let alone surpass, the "Dragon" — supposedly the mightiest living creature in the world — was itself an abnormal thing.
Balleroy himself had been placed among those ranks by title, but he didn't for a moment think his own skill stood shoulder to shoulder with such extraordinary beings.
It's only that strength and winning or losing don't correlate absolutely, that's all.

Brushing aside with a finger the sky-blue hair falling across Madelyn's forehead, Balleroy turned his steps toward the balcony where his beloved dragon waited. On the way, he grabbed the spear leaning nearby, and found Carillon, wings folded, standing on the white terrace with her back turned to him.
When he stroked the base of her wing, his beloved dragon rubbed her long neck against his shoulder.
That was a bond going all the way back to the moment she'd hatched from her egg. Carillon's habits hadn't changed one bit since the days when she'd still been small and weak, a baby Flying Dragon.

"Habits and preferences don't change even in death. ...Not that I'm one to talk, mind you."

He muttered it with a touch of self-mockery, then, with the same hand that had been stroking her, gave her back a flicking slap. At that signal, Carillon lowered herself, and Balleroy leapt smartly onto her back, facing forward.
Looking out from the Crystal Palace's balcony over the vast imperial capital below, he could see the once-beautiful, orderly streets ravaged and broken here and there, the air of a battlefield spreading everywhere.

This battlefield atmosphere surely hung not just over the capital but over the whole Empire — thick and heavy, above all, over the fortress city of Garkla, where the Undead army had been directed.
The fortress city had swallowed up the refugees fled from the capital and the imperial soldiers stationed there, and a fierce battle was raging within it. By rights, Balleroy should have sortied out to help take the city himself.
And yet—

"—I'm coming to understand the feeling of intimidation myself. Given the risk that you wouldn't be able to display your true capabilities there, it wouldn't be appropriate. This calls for careful allocation."

The sorcerer who had revived Balleroy — the Witch who called herself Sphinx — had said as much and decided to keep Balleroy stationed in the capital. It felt less like consideration for Balleroy, and more like she wanted to savor an unfamiliar flavor, a sensation newly sprouted within her, one she'd never known before.
Of course, there was no doubt Balleroy was also expected to play the role of controlling Madelyn, who'd defected to their side, and keeping the Cloud Dragon Mezoreia as an ally.
He had his own thoughts about that expected duty, but setting that aside, being left behind in the capital suited Balleroy just fine.

Not because it meant he wouldn't have to march on the fortress city and meet the siblings he knew who were surely there.
This, too, was Sphinx's assessment, and something Balleroy himself sensed by instinct as well —

"His Excellency, now — he'll come, won't he?"

The Vincent Vollachia Balleroy knew was a cold, fierce emperor.
In peacetime, he might never budge from his castle, but at the site of the final reckoning, he would always insist on standing there himself, on his own two feet. That wasn't unique to Vincent — it was the custom of Vollachian emperors.
This too could be called an influence of the so-called Vollachian way, the Iron-Blooded Code.

And because Vincent, who despised the Vollachian Empire more than anyone, was also determined to faithfully fulfill his role as its Emperor, he would come to the capital without fail.
To destroy Sphinx, the ringleader behind this Great Disaster — bearing the Sunlight Sword.

"—Chisha got one over on me, after all."

At the very outbreak of the Great Disaster, in a situation where neither the existence of the Undead nor of Balleroy himself had been grasped — there could have been no better opportunity than that, and yet Balleroy's surprise attack had failed to reach Vincent.
Chisha's self-sacrifice had saved Vincent, pushing the swiftest possible victory in the Great Disaster out of reach.
But—

"Next time, there won't be anyone left who can be His Excellency's shield."

He would never let the next chance slip by.
If Vincent came marching into the capital himself, Balleroy would put an arrow — surely and certainly — through his heart. In doing so, he would sever the last hope of the Vollachian Empire.
And from there, the threat of the Great Disaster would sweep on northward, even to the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica itself—

"—!!"

In that instant, his beloved dragon's cry snapped Balleroy's attention toward "it."

"—"

Feeling a prickle at the nape of his neck, Balleroy raised his face — and doubted his own eyes.
Riding atop Carillon, surveying the capital as it transformed into a battlefield below, searching for his target Vincent, Balleroy had been straining his eyes to catch every change on the field.
And from outside the bounds of Balleroy's vigilance, it had come brazenly, targeting the Crystal Palace itself.

—From directly above the most beautiful castle in the world, blazing fireballs came raining down, one after another.

"Carillon!"

At the sharp cry, his beloved dragon reacted instantly, spreading her wings — the lift from her beating wings lofted her massive body up into the capital's sky. With each powerful beat, once, twice, her speed and altitude surged higher all at once, and the undead Flying Dragon rose skyward with Balleroy clinging to her back.
As if to block their ascending flight path, fireballs the size of a grown man came falling without mercy. —Balleroy leveled his spear at them.

"That won't do."

In Balleroy's grip, the spearhead pointed skyward took on a faint glow, and in an instant, invisible bullets of light shot out, colliding with the falling flames — explosions burst in midair, staining the sky red.
Not just one. Two, three — in rapid succession, up to thirteen fireballs were shot down by bolts of light, a deafening roar enveloping the capital's sky, and Balleroy and his dragon punched straight through the swelling flames of the explosions.

"What kind of trick is this, sendin' it clear over the clouds like that."

Having batted away the falling rain of fire, Balleroy nonetheless found himself marveling at his opponent's skill.
Had the fireballs come flying straight at the castle, or arced through the air like an arrow or a thrown stone, the Crystal Palace should have easily spotted them coming. The reason even Balleroy's eyes had failed to catch it was plain: the fireballs had flown from far, far away, above the thick clouds hanging over the capital's sky, and only plunged straight down once they'd reached directly above the Crystal Palace.
Put into words, what had been done was simple and clear enough. But there was a gap as wide as heaven and earth between saying it and actually doing it — a godlike feat of hurling a stone over the clouds from kilometers away and landing it precisely on target, and doing so without even using one's hands, an even more terrifying difficulty.
Still, Balleroy himself, though rare within the Empire, was among those capable of using magic — even if only a single specialized art.

"Gotta return the favor now."

Having finished dealing with the fireballs that had plunged through the clouds, Carillon wheeled about high in the sky. Gripping her back firmly, Balleroy's attention turned toward far to the south of the capital.
Due south of the Crystal Palace, the area around the gate leading in and out of the capital was under Mezoreia's protection. From the distance and angle of the fireballs launched, Balleroy judged the enemy in question to be positioned slightly to the west — and around his left eye, the undead eye with its golden iris set in black, the air began to waver gently.
It wasn't a change occurring on Balleroy's face, but a change occurring in the air near his left eye.
Bending light through magic, Balleroy conjured a far-seeing lens for viewing places at a great distance. Peering through it with his left eye, he leveled his spearhead boldly in that direction.
Specialized in a single art — using his Flying Dragon's speed to secure the distance and angle for a shot, then firing bullets of light to achieve ultra-long-range sniping — and equipped with his lens of light to keep from missing his mark, that was how Balleroy fought.
Given an open plain with no obstacles, Balleroy could land a bullet of light on a moving target several kilometers away.

"—"

This time too, meaning to do just that, Balleroy closed his right eye and focused all his awareness into his left.
The nonexistent far-seeing lens conjured by bent light leapt clear over the capital's streets, farther and farther still, and caught the figure of the enemy who had hurled fireballs at the Crystal Palace—

"—Midi."

A rasping breath escaped him, and Balleroy Temeglyph's far-seeing lens showed her figure with perfect clarity.
Her golden hair, glittering like sunlight, danced as she rose into the sky, cradled in the arms of a man in long robes, and with her blue eyes she turned her face straight toward Balleroy — toward someone she had no way of seeing.
And then her lips, though no sound could reach him, rang the words clearly through Balleroy's heart.

"I'm not letting you run off wherever you please anymore. You're going to talk to me properly, Bal-nii."
