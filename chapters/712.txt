Arc 9 Chapter 17: "Please, Don't Forgive"

On a small hill amid a lush, verdant meadow, a parasol and a white table had been set out.

A sky blue enough to see clean through, and a faint breeze stirring black hair. In a chair I'd somehow been seated in without noticing, across the white table, a girl—white, and black—was savoring her tea.
Warm steam rose from the teacup, and yet no fragrance reached me. As if only the outward form of "tea" had been perfectly imitated—a papier-mâché construction.
She was the sort of person who left that unfair impression.

"What happened to me?"

A question.
One seeking the answer to a confusion that had, until a moment ago, been building.

"Don't you already understand that yourself?"

An answer.
Though not one that could be called an answer to that confusion.

Irritation at feeling brushed off, and a single-minded, rational effort to hold back the urge to hurl my dissatisfaction over what had happened at the girl in front of me.
And mixed into it, a pinch of something else: the viewpoint of a "self" watching as a bystander.

"Echidna! Get me out of here, right now!"

Impatience and impulse, deliberately mistaken for courage, drove the conversation forward.
Atop the green hill, I stood facing a girl who called herself a "Witch," assailed once again by that inexplicable sensation. —Yes, once again.

Again and again, over and over and over and over, it had been like this.
Always, every single time, no matter the circumstance, everything that happened was one incomprehensible, senseless thing after another. And against every bit of it, gritting my teeth, forcing my head up, I had kept on fighting.

*—No. He had kept on fighting, for me.*

"Why don't you try it and see."

*—Please, stop.*
*—Please, I'm begging you, stop. Don't make him say what comes next. Don't make him say it.*

"Acting in order to obtain the result you desire is a noble thing. That much, I will never change my mind about. And I believe it's precisely those who take that action who have worth in living."

*—Please, stop. Please, stop. Please, I'm begging you, stop.*
*—Stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop, stop.*
*—Stop.*

"Echidna. I have—the power to Return by Death."

*—Ah, ah, ah, please.*

*—Please, don't suffer.*
*—Please, don't cry.*
*—Please, don't be hurt.*
*—Please, don't be afraid.*
*—Please, don't laugh.*
*—Please, don't forgive.*

"Your question of 'why' was the qualification that opened the door to this tea party. And having tasted the tea the Witch offered you, you are now a full-fledged guest. As the hostess of this tea party, I have an obligation to entertain you properly. —Now, go on, tell me."

*—Please, please, please, please, please, please, please, I'm begging you.*
*—I'm begging you, stop.*

"Racking my brain to arrive at an answer—that too is a kind of bliss to me."

*—I'm begging you, please, don't you dare save this person.*

—The trouble with mental exhaustion, unlike physical fatigue, is that it doesn't show.

Of course, there are plainly visible symptoms too—your head not working right, your nerves so on edge you can't stand it, bags under your eyes darker than a bottomless swamp.
But those obvious symptoms are physical changes, and in the end, no different from ordinary physical fatigue—or so Aldebaran had concluded.

True mental exhaustion, in the real sense, is the kind you can't loosen up with a touch, can't heal with a spell.
And what's needed to rebuild a worn-down spirit isn't words, and it isn't bonds. —It's a blunt thing to say, but what's needed is time.
Only time offers the kindness of sanding a jagged, worn-out spirit smooth again.
So—

"—Domain Redeployment. Matrix Redefinition."

Muttering it under his breath, Aldebaran updated the matrix, finally satisfied he'd calmed down.
Roughly speaking, this was his first matrix update in six hours—real time hadn't advanced at all; being able to stretch his own subjective time out indefinitely was the single greatest benefit of Aldebaran's Authority.
Within a set domain, by draining poison he could repeat a fixed stretch of time as many times as he needed, carving out however much time it took to smooth his spirit back out. In the fight against Reinhard, too, he'd made full use of this benefit for the fine adjustments needed to draw things into the plan he'd prepared in advance.

"Well, pretty much all of it got nullified, though…"

In the end, he'd been forced to play a card he hadn't wanted to play to settle things, so from Aldebaran's own perspective it was hard to call it a win—a draw at best. Put more bluntly, they'd both lost.
Even so, his sluggish head and frayed nerves had both gotten somewhat better.
Unfortunately, he had no idea what state the bags under his eyes were in, but he couldn't spare the time to cover them with makeup, so he made do by coordinating his ragged mind and body with a nice, jagged black helmet that suited them perfectly.

Actually, "ragged mind and body" aside, his physical condition was excellent.
Calling it the bottomless reserves of the "Dragon," the mightiest superorganism alive, would be an exaggeration, but he was in a state where he could burn through vast quantities of Mana as freely as he liked. Water magic healing, sun magic buffs, all stacked to the brim—it was probably fair to say this was the strongest Aldebaran there had ever been since he first drew breath.

"Still, it's like an ant getting a hundred times stronger—no matter how many buffs I stack, I still can't beat a human…"

Much less when, by that same standard, his opponent wasn't a human at all, but an elephant or a lion.
Sadly, given that he was outclassed by the base specs of his own vessel to begin with, even the strongest Aldebaran in Aldebaran history couldn't be anything but the one who gets beaten in a head-on fight.
Well, setting aside that sorry self-assessment—

"—Seven days."

One knee raised, leaning against a grimy wooden plank wall, Aldebaran counted off the deadline.
The place he'd chosen to rest was a hunting cabin in the woods that Yae had found for them. Fortunately, whether from the wrong season or the owner simply being away, no one was using it, so they'd let themselves in and made use of it.
He couldn't hope for the luxury of a fluffy bed or an open-air bath, but not being seen by a single soul made this, for Aldebaran right now, practically a suite-class deal.
From here on, as much as possible, he had to avoid any fight that could be avoided and slip past every event untouched.

His conduct in Mirula, the town closest to the Augria Sand Dunes, was the exception.
It had been a meeting point with his allies, and also, honestly, he'd been dying for some milk. But more than that, he'd needed to make the townspeople there understand just how far outside the norm what was happening was, and that there was no one coming to save them from it.

After all, barely a dozen-odd kilometers away, the strongest battle on the face of the earth was taking place.
Reinhard would no doubt be fighting for his life to hold back the Witch of Envy that Aldebaran's scheme had lured out. But he had no certainty that the aftershocks wouldn't reach beyond the sand sea. There was no calming down with the sky flashing nonstop like that, but evacuation was essential regardless.
And the seven-day deadline Aldebaran had set wasn't unrelated to that battle either. —If anything, it was tied to it closely.

As far as Aldebaran knew, the Sword Saint and the Witch of Envy were evenly matched. Their fight was a stalemate—a back-and-forth struggle with no conclusion in sight.
But that only held true between the Sword Saint and the Witch of Envy alone.
Now that Aldebaran's machinations had actually brought that fight about, whether it would really go on for a thousand days—no, it wouldn't come to that.
Because—

"The world's gonna break before those two ever settle it. And if that happens, it's game over."

Before that ruin of the world could happen, that fight, too, needed to be brought to an end.
In that sense—even though he was the one who'd arranged the matchup in the first place—that clash at the summit was itself one of the potential causes of the world's end, and thus one of the things Aldebaran had declared he would remove.

"There's a lot… there's a lot to do…"

Pressing a hand to his forehead through the helmet, Aldebaran let out a long, heavy breath.
Thanks to his Authority his mental exhaustion was kept in check, and his physical fatigue had been forcibly restored with water and sun magic, but the sheer volume of things to think about, the sheer weight of what he was carrying, was about to overflow his capacity.
Of course, the moment he'd started all this, he'd forfeited any right to whine about it to anyone—

"—Instead of stubbornly gritting your teeth over something so boring, wouldn't going all limp and leaning on someone be an option~?"

"…Yae, huh."

"Yes, yes, yes, Yae here, Al-sama's all-purpose, super-useful Shinobi maid~"

Without so much as a presence or a footstep, appearing the way she always did—as though sprouting straight out of the shadows—the girl, Yae, tossed off that quip with a smile.
Hands clasped behind her back as she strolled over, Aldebaran shrugged at her.

"All-purpose and super-useful aside, isn't 'Shinobi maid' piling the attributes on a bit thick."

"You think so? And here I already cut 'beautiful girl,' 'lovely,' and 'adorable,' reluctantly, at that~. Well then, well then, how would Al-sama rate me?"

"…Submissive cat-type Shinobi maid?"

"Wo~w, that's the kind of idea that would never get you anywhere with girls. And you still snuck 'Shinobi maid' in there, too."

Given a look of what seemed like genuine contempt, Aldebaran clicked his tongue and muttered, "Shut up."
He was, at that moment, in the middle of grappling seriously with problems he genuinely needed to worry about. He didn't want to waste time on the idle chatter of an ally he had no need to flatter.

"Anyway, I told you to keep an eye on the old man. Plus check the trail through the woods, keep watch around the cabin, and get the food ready."

"Don't you think, saying all that yourself, 'wow, that's super sweatshop-y'? That's Al-sama-speak for overworking someone, right? This right now is super sweatshop-y."

"You're the all-purpose, super-useful Shinobi maid, aren't you. Live up to the name."

"Seriou~sly, Al-sama, you're a real devil-beast—devil and beast, all rolled into one~."

Sticking her tongue out, Yae shot back without hiding her displeasure in the slightest. When he shot her a sharp glare in return, she raised a finger and said, "I know, I know," then continued,

"Food's ready. Shinobi field rations, though. Perimeter watch around the cabin, done. Guess even woods like this have Witchbeasts~. As for the woods, I already found a route where the footing isn't bad. You really should train your legs a bit more, you know. And as for Heinkel-sama…"

"As for him?"

"Since keeping him alive is just dead weight, I thought I'd use him as live bait to keep the Witchbeasts away… geez, don't make that face, I'm joking."

"You can't even see my face."

"I can see your aura. You're pretty loud, Al-sama."

Saying something that half made sense and half didn't, Yae turned her face away with a huff. But Aldebaran didn't look away, keeping his glare fixed on her profile.
Eventually, as though giving up, she let out a deep sigh and said,

"I've got Heinkel-sama tied up. I'll know the moment he wakes up, and if he tries to force his way out and escape, his arms and legs come right off."

"Come right off, huh. You could actually do that, to that old man?"

"Tough as he is, he's not a Steelfolk, so he's got weak points. My techniques are the kind that go after exactly those points. Besides, even if I could, you'd get mad if I did, wouldn't you?"

"Well, yeah."

"Then I won't do it, so there~. There's no benefit to me in making Al-sama mad, is there?"

At Yae raising both hands to declare she wouldn't defy him, making a show of her harmlessness, he let the tension drop from his shoulders.
Her tone was light and she had a taste for teasing, but there was no lie in what Yae said. What Aldebaran had done to Yae wasn't gentle enough to leave room for her to plot something here.
As thoroughly, as mercilessly as he could manage, he had broken her will to resist—no, broken her spirit itself.
The fact that Yae Tenzen was cooperating with Aldebaran now was the result of exactly that.

—Yae Tenzen was an assassin sent to take Priscilla Barielle's life.

With her declaration of candidacy in the Royal Selection, Priscilla Barielle's existence had come to the attention of the Vollachian Empire as well, and it was only a matter of time before someone realized she was Priska Benedict—the one who had slipped away from the outcome of the Imperial Selection Ceremony over the Empire's throne and survived on in another country.
Yae was the assassin sent by Chisha Gold, one of the Empire's Nine Divine Generals, to douse the smoldering spark named Priscilla before that fire could catch in the kingdom.

In fact, as a Shinobi, her infiltration had been masterful: hired on as Priscilla's attendant, she'd made herself indispensable as a hardworking maid in the Barielle domain, earning Priscilla's favor and Schult's deep affection besides. Aldebaran, too, had thought of her as an easygoing colleague.
But Yae never forgot her original purpose, and made her attempt on Priscilla's life.
So—

"Al-sama on that day was terrifying~. I've experienced pretty much every awful thing this world has to offer, under the name of village training and all… but I'd never been that scared before in my life."

"…Ouch. That's a real dig at my chest, that story."

"The least you could do is feel a little heartache. If you put yourself in my shoes—forced to serve as the faithful servant of such a terrifying Al-sama ever since that day—you should be able to bear it just fine. Kept away from Okusama, kept away from Schult-chan… made to obey Al-sama's every word, this body used however he pleases…!"

"I haven't laid a single finger on you!?"

He shot that back at Yae's coquettish tone, angling for sympathy as she hugged her own slender shoulders.
It was a statement scandalous well past any reasonable limit; of course, the fact that she'd said it where no other ears could hear was, no doubt, either harassment or a warning shot aimed at Aldebaran.
But Yae, sending a sidelong glance at an Aldebaran thinking exactly that, said,

"…Shall we finally go beyond just one finger, then? You do look awfully worn out, and I wouldn't mind comforting a weakened Al-sama like that, you know?"

"—————"

"Oh? Oh my my~? Silence—does that mean it's crossed your mind as an option? Then, before I change my mind, let me go prepare the bed…"

"That's not it, I was just trying to figure out what to say to shut you up. Besides, you…"

"What about me?"

"You said yourself there'd be no point in that kind of relationship with me, didn't you?"

Pointing at Yae, who was radiating a seductive air, Aldebaran pressed her for her real meaning.
Unfortunately for her, no matter how many seductive lines Yae threw out, Aldebaran wasn't about to bite. Believe it or not, he'd spent over ten years as a Sword Slave on Sword Slave Island, the most lawless place there was. He'd heard that kind of sales pitch until he was sick of it.
Besides, this wasn't the first time Yae had tried to seduce him like this. So it wasn't the first time he'd turned her down either, but Yae, looking disappointed, smoothed a hand over her own body and said,

"For the record, this is technically one of a Shinobi's weapons too, so getting shot down like this does hurt, you know."

"Don't cling to a weapon that doesn't even work. Mix up your means and your ends and nothing good comes of it."

"Well, that's fair advice, I suppose. —Any hope that earning Al-sama's affection would keep my future safe and secure forever fell apart the moment Okusama passed away, after all."

"—————"

"I can see that scary face, you know?"

Tilting her head slightly, Yae's words cut into Aldebaran's heart like a sharp blade.
There was no way she meant nothing by it. Yae fully intended to wound Aldebaran, and Aldebaran, for his part, accepted the blade willingly. Neither of them had ever put it into words, but Aldebaran had meant to protect Priscilla's life, and Yae, too, had believed her Okusama—Priscilla—would be kept safe.
Now that that unspoken understanding had been broken, the torment Yae dealt out was only a fitting punishment.

"I won't say you went back on your word. We never confirmed it out loud, after all. It's just that Al-sama loved Okusama, so I naturally assumed he'd protect her to the end."

Touching her own lips as she spoke, Yae's woven words tore Aldebaran to shreds.
In silence, tasting the sharpness of that blade, Aldebaran listened for what came next, telling himself this was the clear, deserved punishment for him to receive—

"—Don't get me wrong, though. I'm not blaming Al-sama for failing to protect Okusama, or anything like that, you know?"

"Huh?"

"I liked Okusama. Even though I tried to kill her, since it was an order. Noble and clever and beautiful, and surprisingly kind for all that. But if she's died, well, that's that. The dead don't come back, after all. Though apparently, in the Empire, she was in the middle of coming back somehow~."

He hadn't shared the details of what had happened in the Empire with her in that much depth, yet somewhere Yae had picked up on it and brought up the Great Disaster. But Aldebaran's attention was less on where she'd gotten that information than on the unreadable direction her words were heading.
If Yae had no intention of blaming Aldebaran, then what was the point of harping on Priscilla's death and mounting this verbal assault on him like this?

"My words are just words~. Al-sama's just going and hurting himself with them on his own. I'm only fulfilling my duty to explain myself, that's all."

"Duty to explain yourself, huh?"

"Didn't you ask me yourself? What my purpose is in trying to comfort Al-sama… well~, maybe your phrasing was a bit rougher than that."

"—————"

"Before, it was to earn Al-sama's affection so he'd protect me. But now I know that's no guarantee either, so my purpose isn't the same as it was back then."

As she spoke, Yae slowly stepped right in front of Aldebaran and folded her knees. Kneeling like that, she brought her eyes level with Aldebaran, who remained seated on the grimy floor, and said,

"This offer to comfort Al-sama right now isn't seduction. If anything, call it insurance."

"Insurance…"

"Al-sama, you've already gone and started it, haven't you~. Betraying all the people who were good to you, even unleashing the Witch of Envy just to hold the Sword Saint down… you can't stop now, and I'd be in trouble if you did, and if you go and fail, that would be more than I could bear. So~"

There, she cut herself off, gently reaching out a pale finger to touch Aldebaran's neck. Tickling the back of his neck with that short-nailed fingertip, Yae brought her lips close to his ear, whispering,

"Please, Al-sama, see your purpose through to the end. For that, use my body, my abilities, my skills, anything you need. I'll be your comfort, whether for peace of mind or just somewhere to take it out on."

Her breath was hot, and there was no falsehood in the voice pleading to offer up her entire existence. Showing the faint glimpse of a fang at the corner of her mouth, Yae went on.
It was—

"—And once you've seen your purpose through, please, make sure you die properly. If Al-sama doesn't die, I don't think I'll ever be able to feel safe again, for the rest of my life."

Saying it in the most chillingly seductive tone she'd used yet, Yae smiled.
That was the reason Yae Tenzen would cooperate with Aldebaran even at the cost of making an enemy of the whole world. —And an ending Aldebaran himself had already accepted.

It was something he'd known from the very start.
Even if Aldebaran accomplished his purpose, that wouldn't mean everything he'd done up to now would be forgiven, or that he'd be let off the hook. Much less would it be approved of, or understood.
He didn't want to be understood. He didn't want to be forgiven. Please—please, don't forgive him.
Once everything was over, without fail, Aldebaran would burn at the stake.

That ending at the stake was Yae Tenzen's wish—no, it was Aldebaran's wish as well.
Once he'd fulfilled his purpose, he'd gladly let himself be burned. And just like the woman who once burned herself to save her homeland, Aldebaran, too, would become ash.

—Only then, at last, could Aldebaran release his domain and discard his matrix.

"—————"

Both fell silent, Aldebaran and Yae gazing at each other at a distance close enough to feel each other's breath.
There was nothing like romantic charge between man and woman in it; all there was, was the bone-dry misery of a pitiful girl with her life's chain held in a monster's grip, and a foolish clown eagerly playing the part of that monster.
Their gazes stayed locked like that for a while longer—

"—Guaaaah! Wh-what?! What's going on—guaaah?!"

Abruptly, a third voice, neither Aldebaran's nor Yae's, crudely split open the space between them.
What they heard was Heinkel's scream—apparently he'd just woken up. If Yae's earlier report was accurate, he was tied up in a way that, if he wasn't careful, could leave him in pieces.

"…The old man's gonna come apart. Go untie him before that happens."

"Are you deflecting? It'd be fine if Heinkel-sama came apart anyway, wouldn't it?"

"It's not fine. I made a promise to the old man. Once everything's settled, I'll get him Dragon's Blood. I keep my promises. …Promises matter."

"A promise… then will you make one with me too? A promise."

"—————"

"If you'll make a promise, if you'll keep the promise, then fine, I'll step aside. I won't let Heinkel-sama come apart either. Yae is your faithful servant."

Letting a red tongue peek out, hiding murderous intent behind narrowed eyes, Yae posed the question.
At her words, Aldebaran closed his eyes inside his helmet, then slowly raised his right hand and extended his pinky finger.
Seeing that, Yae blinked, her face for a moment looking like nothing more than a girl her own age, caught utterly off guard. —He remembered Priscilla once being taken by surprise the same way, when he'd shown her his raised pinky like this.
Remembering that, he said,

"Hooking each other's pinkies together and doing a pinky-swear—that's my ritual for making a promise."

"Hooking pinkies together… how naughty~"

"It's not naughty. Fine if you don't want to, then."

"I'll do it, I'll do it. There, there, Yae-chan's pinky and Al-sama's pinky are all hooked together now."

The way she said it was suggestive enough on its own, but Aldebaran no longer had anything to say about it. Just like that, he shook their pinky-linked hands up and down and said,

"Pinky swear—if I lie, a thousand needles down my throat. —Promise sealed."

So he finished the pinky-swear that promised his own end.
Watching Yae look so intensely pleased by it was complicated for an Aldebaran who'd just had his death wished for, but he thought that, sure enough, she really was a Shinobi maid who could pull off "beautiful girl," "lovely," and "adorable" too, even after cutting them from her introduction.

"S-somebody, somebody do something! Gh—gah! Guaaaaah!!"

Heinkel's pained groaning, still left unattended, echoed loud and high through the woods.

"Damn it, you'll remember this, maid…!"

"Please don't use such filthy language~, Heinkel-sama. As you can see, we're isolated with no backup and short-handed—we can't spare useless manpower just to babysit Heinkel-sama."

"Then you could've just let me sleep peacefully! Instead you go and tie me up and hang me for no reason…"

"I thought seeing a little blood might help calm you down~. It's just poor, frail little Yae's cute survival strategy to keep from wrecking this whole operation, isn't it?"

"You…!"

Heinkel, rubbing at the red line across his neck—the one seeping blood—bristled at Yae's endless mouth and reflexively reached for the sword at his hip.
But at the same moment, seeing the dangerous glint that lit up in Yae's eyes, Aldebaran had no choice but to cut into their exchange with a "Wait."

"Yae, don't provoke him. And old man, the fact that Yae's like this…"

"How would I know! By the time I showed up, this woman was already gone from the mansion."

"That's right, Heinkel-sama and I just missed each other at the mansion. Getting Heinkel-sama as a replacement for beautiful, capable little Yae here—Schult-chan must've been so~ shocked."

"…Surprisingly, Schult-chan got along just fine with the old man."

"Oh~, that really is surprising. He can't get along with his own son at all, though, can he?"

"You—!"

"Yae."

Once again, at Yae deliberately rubbing Heinkel's nerves the wrong way, Aldebaran shot her a stern look, but all she did in response was give a little bow with her hand and say, "So~rry."
Bringing the two of them face to face—when he'd first proposed this whole plan to Heinkel, sent him ahead out of the capital, and had him join up with Yae—had been the first time they'd met, but the two of them got along worse than he'd expected. Heinkel not liking Yae was one thing, but Yae seemed to genuinely despise Heinkel too.
Honestly, unlike her usual self, Yae's hostility toward Heinkel was plain as day.

"Sorry, old man. But you and Yae are both parts we can't do without from here on out. I won't ask you two to get along, so just make it work somehow."

So Aldebaran gave up on getting Yae to change her attitude and decided to ask Heinkel for compromise instead. He felt bad for Heinkel, but as long as he wanted Dragon's Blood, he couldn't oppose Aldebaran's policies or objections. He'd just have to quietly let himself be used.
Contrary to Aldebaran's expectation that this would naturally provoke pushback, Heinkel only said a brief "I know," and then,

"More importantly, it's really true, right? Wrapping this up in seven more days."

"—. Yeah. Well, 'true' might not be the word, but I'm serious about it. Dead serious about settling it in seven days."

"Then you'll hand over Volcanica's Dragon's Blood to me. Don't you dare break that promise. …And, the Witch of Envy—"

"I've got a plan worked out to pull her back too. Before your son ends up in trouble in that fight with the Witch of Envy. No need to worry."

"Worry, my ass…!"

"—————"

"Worry, my… ass…!"

His expression clouded over with something bitter, Heinkel couldn't squeeze out anything more than that. After watching him in silence for a moment, Aldebaran turned back to face forward.
Following Yae's lead, Aldebaran and the others were headed out of the woods where they'd been lying low.
As mentioned before, now that they'd made it past their unavoidable clash with the Sword Saint, Aldebaran and his group had to avoid as much trouble as possible while heading for their destination.

To that end, they had to keep pressing on and on, crossing the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica, continuing steadily westward.

"But~, is it really gonna go that smoothly, with no one getting in the way? I mean, there's a pretty high chance the people close to Sword Saint-sama already know about Al-sama's little screwup, right?"

"Don't call it a screwup. …Yeah, no doubt about it, my betrayal's already known. When Flam-chan contacted her little sister, I doubt the Sword Saint was alone, and there's no way he just charged out without telling anyone. So I'm turning that around and using it."

"Turning it around, meaning—that's the Divine Dragon-sama?"

"That's right."

Answering Yae's question, Aldebaran pointed up at the sky, blocked from view by the trees of the woods.
Aldebaran the "Dragon" was not traveling with them as they made their way through the woods like this. Of course, part of it was that that body of his stood out ridiculously, but it was also because he wanted to put that ridiculously conspicuous body and its fame to use for something.

"For now, I had the Divine Dragon fly off in a different direction from us—north. The plan is for him to draw as much attention as possible over there, and pull everyone's focus away from us."

He couldn't be certain how accurate Flam's Divine Protection of Telepathy really was, but if word had gotten out not just about Aldebaran's betrayal but also about the Divine Dragon Volcanica turning hostile, then a sighting of Volcanica would become a threat to the entire kingdom, and draw everyone's focus.
With Reinhard gone, the assumption would surely be that without mobilizing knight-order-level forces, they couldn't even get Volcanica to the negotiating table in the first place.
And—

"Obviously, the enemy's assumption is gonna be that their biggest threat is traveling together with me."

No one would think to go out of their way to separate from a Divine Dragon they'd gone to the trouble of winning to their side.
But from the standpoint of Aldebaran's actual objective, drawing more attention than necessary was pointless, and so "Aldebaran," who inevitably stood out, was someone who needed to be left out of the group's movements.
Having read Aldebaran's Book of the Dead and sharing his purpose, his plans, and his intentions alike, "Aldebaran" would surely see through the role assigned to him well enough.
Meanwhile, Aldebaran and the others would smoothly close the physical distance to their objective.
For that too, Yae's skills as a Shinobi were proving enormously useful—

"—Al-sama, we've got a problem."

At Yae's sudden stop and those words, Aldebaran held his breath.
Heinkel, walking at the back, stopped too and called out "Hey" to Yae. She didn't answer the call, instead quietly turning her gaze past the trees—toward the edge of the woods—and said,

"Putting the 'most' together with the 'awful' makes 'the worst,' but we're being ambushed."

"—!"

"Ha?! An ambush? What the hell happened to the Dragon's decoy!"

At the unexpected news, Aldebaran's cheeks stiffened while Heinkel's voice rose in anger. More flustered than angry, Heinkel voiced what Aldebaran, though silent, agreed with all the same.
There shouldn't have been any way for them to be ambushed. The existence of the Divine Dragon Volcanica wasn't something anyone could ignore. In fact, unless someone could focus on something other than the Divine Dragon Volcanica's raw strength—

"—Hey! You can hear me, can't you? I know you're there, helmet bastard!"

"—————"

A voice hurled at them with dead-on precision, as if it had been timed to catch exactly the moment they held their breath.
But no. That wasn't it. She hadn't been timing it at all. Unlike those without, those who possess never miss their timing in a moment like this.

The Sunlight Princess, Priscilla Barielle, had been just like that.
So it was only natural that the same miracle would dwell in another woman who shared that very title with Priscilla Barielle, one who had once vied with her for that same seat at the kingdom's summit.

Waiting for Aldebaran and the others outside the woods was the return of the "Golden Lion"—

"Sounds like my useless knight's been a handful for you. Guess I've got no choice but to come clean up after him. —He's not used to having someone else wipe his ass for him, is he? Well, that's gonna be me."
