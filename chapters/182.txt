Arc 4 Chapter 16: "Morning at the Natsuki Household"

Listening to a grating cackle of laughter, Subaru gave his half-awake head a light shake to chase off the drowsiness. He liked to think of himself as the type who woke instantly when rousing naturally — but when forced awake by outside interference, that was a different story.

Rubbing at his eyelids, feeling a faint throb as if too little blood were reaching his head, he turned his neck idly to look around — and his own familiar room leapt into view.

A room crammed to bursting with manga and light novels on the bookshelf, jeans and sweats scattered wherever they'd been shed. The study desk, no longer used for anything resembling its proper purpose, was stacked with half-read books, and the old TV-video combo unit — now purely a gaming device — gave off a certain melancholy.

Cracking his neck atop the futon that had long since become a permanent fixture, rarely if ever aired out, Subaru felt an odd sense of wrongness about this thoroughly ordinary scenery. *Why is my chest so unsettled?*

"Oi, oi, ignore me like that and this grown man's gonna start crying, you know? The morning air's so nice and refreshing — get up and dive in like you mean it!"

"You want me to greet a morning that started with a diving press cheerfully? Don't ask the impossible. Actually, I think my bones are creaking, so I'm sinking back into a second sleep right here."

Subaru answered sullenly the voice that had waited for him to wake, then rolled himself flat onto the futon once more. No room for negotiation — turning his back on the man standing right beside the futon, who let out a dissatisfied "wha-a-at" and continued,

"Rebellious phase! This is the rebellious phase! I knew it'd come eventually, but I never thought it'd hit THIS morning — even I wasn't mentally prepared! I should've spent less time on breakfast and more time preparing to properly talk with my son. Dammit, I hate how powerless I am...!"

"Saying that while grabbing a guy's leg — hey, wai— OW! OWOWOWOW!"

"Alright, today I've decided we're gonna talk this out properly. Starting with body language! Figure-four, figure-four! There we go, a joint lock right after waking up really works!"

Kenichi twisted Subaru's sprawled leg into a figure-four lock, lying down facing the opposite direction and bearing down on his knee and shin for maximum damage. Subaru let out a pained groan at the inescapable agony, and Kenichi cackled again in that voice of a man thoroughly enjoying life.

"There, there, what's wrong, what's wrong? All that size and all that daily training and you're struggling against one middle-aged man — don't you feel embarrassed, wahaha— ah, wait, hold on, OW! OWOWOWOW!"

"Idiot! Choosing the figure-four for a surprise attack when it's so easy to reverse — old age is getting to you, Dad! Just flip the body over and the damage bounces right back — teaching me the figure-four is coming back to bite you— ah, wait, no flip-reversal, that's not fair— OWOW! OW!"

Two grown men, limbs fully extended, tangled their legs together and rolled around trading offense and defense. Each time, victim and aggressor swapped places, and the screaming side alternated. Their rough movements sent a book tumbling off the desk and knocked over a game console that had been standing upright.

To this pair carrying on their not-quite-a-family-fight roughhousing so early in the morning—

"—Hey, you two. Mom's getting hungry, so let's have breakfast."

A leisurely voice, along with an off-rhythm knock, came flying into the room, and the two who'd been trading joint locks in a dizzying blur went still.

Through eyes blurred with half-tears from the pain, the figure standing in the doorway came into focus — a woman with a vague, drifting air about her and sharp, unfriendly-looking eyes. At a glance, her gaze suggested considerable irritation, but Subaru knew, from seventeen years of experience, that in truth she wasn't thinking anything in particular.

Her poor eyes made her parentage instantly obvious — this was Subaru's mother, Natsuki Naoko.

At his mother's appearance, Kenichi stuck out his tongue and said "Oops!" as he sprang up.

"My bad, my bad, got a little too into the father-son bonding time with Subaru. Might as well have just gone ahead and eaten without us."

"—Huh? But it's a morning where the whole family can eat together — why would you go and eat first? It's better when everyone eats facing each other."

Kenichi's well-meaning remark only earned him a puzzled tilt of the head from Naoko. There wasn't a trace of irony in it — she genuinely thought so, and said so.

Looking down at his wife's reaction, Kenichi nodded vigorously, over and over.

"Right, right, exactly. As expected of my wife — you really get it. Breakfast tastes better when everyone eats it face to face!"

"I don't think the taste changes whether it's morning, noon, or night, though? It's just that doing everyone's meal all at once means the dishes get done in one go."

"Ah, it was about the dishes. Huh, sorry. Got a little carried away on my own there."

His triumphant expression collapsed, undone by his wife's guilelessly literal remark. Kenichi's shoulders slumped, and Naoko regarded him with mild puzzlement before looking down at Subaru, who was still sprawled out.

"C'mon, Subaru, let's eat too. Mom worked hard on today's breakfast, just for you."

She said it with a faint, pleased smile — the kind of smile only someone close to her would even recognize as one.


※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


Reluctantly rubbing his sleep-fogged eyes, Subaru came down to the first-floor dining table — and there, felt his hazy consciousness snap fully awake from the sheer shock of it.

"Mom. You said you worked hard for me, but..."

"Yep. Mom worked hard for Subaru. It was a lot of trouble to get ready this morning."

Naoko gave a little "hmph," looking somewhat proud of herself. Without even needing to look closely, Subaru could tell there wasn't a shred of guilt in her attitude, and he sighed.

Following the trail of that sigh, Kenichi — having swung by the table via the bathroom — spotted the destination and let out an appreciative "Ohh."

"Whoa, Subaru. Your plate's on a whole different menu. It's like a green forest."

"Thanks for the concise summary. Yeah, that's basically it. ...What is this, though? Why is my plate the only one piled sky-high with green peas?"

At Kenichi's remark, Subaru nodded and pointed at his own place setting — the breakfast arranged in front of his usual seat, one plate in particular giving off a distinctly alien aura. Piled with peas as though they were a mortal enemy, whatever other ingredients might be underneath were either buried from sight or simply never there to begin with — all Subaru could see was green peas. For the record, Subaru hated green peas, but—

"Well, didn't you say sometime, ages ago, that you hate green peas? Mom thought that kind of picky eating wasn't good for you. So I figured this would be a good chance to have you eat a bunch and get over it."

"You're trying to fix my food preferences based on a memory so vague you don't even remember when it happened. And this 'chance' — it's not like today's some special occasion or anything, is it?"

"Heh, you're still green, Subaru. Listen — no matter when, no matter the circumstance, this very moment, today, can only be tasted here and now. You might think another day just like this one will come again, but in doing so, you're missing out on countless one-of-a-kind moments—"

"I'm good on that for now, thanks."

Shoving aside Kenichi, who'd cut into the conversation with a bit of a dance move, Subaru let out a deep sigh and made for his seat. He pushed the pea-piled plate away from himself first thing, then said,

"Anyway, I appreciate the thought behind making this for me, but I'll pass on the peas themselves. I don't want to fill up on something I hate first thing in the morning."

"Aw, you always say that. Then what would you do if the whole world ran out of everything to eat except green peas? You'd eat them then, wouldn't you?"

"If the world got to that point I'd be dead from malnutrition pretty soon anyway, so eating a few peas wouldn't do much good. So no, I will never eat them."

Meeting his mother's sophistry with sophistry of his own, Subaru crossed his arms and puffed out his chest.

"Even if Armageddon came raining down, I would never eat green peas."

"Geez, being that much of a picky eater is a loss for your life, I'm telling you. Oh, Mom, take the tomato out of my salad, I don't want it, you eat it instead."

"Truly my father's son... contradicting himself between the first half and second half of the same sentence."

He pushed the tomato from his own salad over to his wife with his hand, then in exchange plundered the boiled egg from her salad. It was a routine exchange, so neither complained — an unspoken understanding between husband and wife. Watching it out of the corner of his eye, Subaru put his hands together in front of his non-pea breakfast — steaming miso soup and honey toast slathered generously — and said,

"I always wonder, why this weird Japanese-Western mashup?"

"Mom likes wakame in her miso soup, and honey on her bread."

Not really an answer. But pointing that out felt like too much effort now. He could already picture exactly how she'd respond — a puzzled look and a tilt of the head.

Subaru murmured a small "Let's eat," and took a sip of the miso soup, at which point Kenichi and Naoko each took their own seats — facing Subaru across the table.

The two of them pressed their palms together, said "Let's eat" as well, and in perfect unison began sipping their own miso soup first. No one noticed, but all three of them had done the exact same thing in the exact same order.

"Hm, this miso soup... don't tell me Mom's skills have gone up while I wasn't looking?"

"Can you tell? Actually, I recorded yesterday's Three-Minute Cooking show at lunchtime."

*So what.* Subaru's cheek twitched at Naoko's off-kilter response and Kenichi's random change of subject. What's more, since Naoko's statements tended to be quite precisely accurate — having said "recorded," she'd probably only recorded it and never watched it. And probably never would.

Besides, in the first place—

"Dad's home this morning, so he must be the one who made the miso soup and toast."

"Whoa, sharp eyes, my son. Catching onto that... you're trying to shake loose the contradiction in what I said and slam down the evidence, aren't you?"

"You're way into an old game like this now! I mean, it's a classic, but still!"

Kenichi had probably just walked off with the whole game console from Subaru's desk. Great for killing time on the commute, sure, but the image of a grown middle-aged man hunched forward, absorbed in a handheld game on the train, made Subaru's skin crawl just imagining it.

While chewing on this, and on his sweet toast, Subaru brought up, "Oh, by the way,"

"Bit late to ask, but why are you dressed so light, Dad? It's starting to get warm out, sure, but running gear plus just your stetteko underwear is way too casual, isn't it?"

"You're wearing stetteko too, aren't you? Anyway, Dad's like — I managed to get up early for once, got all excited, and did some dry towel rubs out in the yard, that kind of thing."

"Dry towel rubs — isn't that supposed to be done in colder weather to actually mean anything?"

"This kind of thing is about willpower. If you're worrying about petty details before you even start running, are you ever gonna reach your destination? Mom, back me up here."

"That's right, Subaru. And even though it wasn't cold out, Mom did watch Dad's dry towel rubs with a very cold look in her eyes."

"Wha—!? No backup from my own wife!?"

"Huh... I did back you up. I told him it really was cold."

"That's not backup, that's a follow-up strike!"

Kenichi rattled his chair, venting his grievances, while Naoko, watching her husband, murmured the wildly off-topic, "Feels like there's dust flying around, I should clean later," in response.

Watching his parents, Subaru lowered his eyes and quietly worked through his breakfast. Meanwhile, the pea plate that had been pushed away from Subaru made its way to Kenichi, who — disliking it — shoved it toward Naoko, who then sent it right back to Subaru, in an endless loop.

"I mean, this is officially the pattern where nobody eats it. What do we do with this mountain of peas. Take responsibility and eat it, Mom."

"But Mom hates green peas, too..."

"You tried to fix MY picky eating and that's your excuse!?"

"Oh, but don't get the wrong idea. It's not just green peas Mom hates — it's, like, small round things in general. They feel gross in my mouth."

"I'm not getting any wrong idea, I'm just getting more and more suspicious of you!"

*Come to think of it, I've never once seen Mom eat beans,* Subaru thought, pushing the plate toward Kenichi this time.

"Well then, since the wife's the one responsible, the husband takes the fall — Dad, you eat it."

"Don't say something so lonely, Subaru. Aren't we a rare, close-knit family these days? Which means whatever you and Mom hate, I hate too."

"That's a consideration that makes literally nobody happy, this green plate!"

The peas sat abandoned, entering no one's mouth. In the end, Kenichi made the executive decision with the face of a mischievous kid: "Well, guess we'll just have to throw it into some pilaf. Pilaf assault until it's all gone, heh heh."

Subaru, figuring he could tolerate it mixed into something else, accepted this, while Naoko stubbornly shook her head, insisting hated things stayed hated no matter what they were mixed into. In the end, it fell to the two men to deal with it.

"Thanks for the meal."

"Yep, you're welcome. Alright, let's get these dishes done quick, then race to school to work off breakfast, Subaru!"

"I'm getting real tired of how smoothly that turns into 'go to school.' Also, I don't recall being raised into the kind of energetic brat who runs right after eating."

Watching Kenichi toss the dishes into the sink and turn around, teeth gleaming, Subaru shrugged and rose from the table. He muttered, "Sleeping till noon," under his breath and, scratching his head, headed for his room on the second floor — when his feet suddenly stopped.

"What, the...?"

A throb ran through his temples, and Subaru pressed lightly at his head, closing his eyes. Light flickered behind his eyelids, and he felt something hot chafe at the back of his throat.

Something was wrong. Something was off.

Turning back, Subaru looked at the faces of his parents, both watching him.

Kenichi, rebuffed in his invitation, pouted his lips in dissatisfaction; Naoko, wiping the table with a dishcloth, watched Subaru with eyes that seemed somehow lonely.

The gazes directed at him from his father and mother — becoming aware of the emotion held within them, Subaru's chest was assailed by a heat he couldn't ignore. Feeling his face flush hot, he hurriedly turned away so they wouldn't see his expression, and fled — no, retreated to his own room.

"What is this? Why, why am I feeling this weird?"

Placing a hand to his chest, Subaru let out a breath, startled by the speed of his own pulse. He sank down onto the futon as if collapsing, eyes darting restlessly around the room.

The room was unchanged from when he'd woken. No different from what he'd seen the night before, before falling asleep. A room stuck in perpetual stagnation, never once visited by change —

as if reflecting the stagnation of its occupant, Subaru himself.

Glancing at the clock, it was nearly eight in the morning. School started at eight-thirty, and it was about a twenty-minute bike ride from home. Not an impossible time to make it.

But Subaru made no move to change, only sat hugging his knees on the futon, staring fixedly at the moving hands of the clock. The second hand ticked steadily on, the minute hand quietly advanced ten times — and crossed the deadline.

*That's it — I've missed school again today too.*

"So it's fine. Yeah, it can't be helped."

If he'd had just a little more time before his resolve settled, maybe he could have made it to school. But reality had mercilessly declared his time limit.

So now, no choice remained to be forced on him today. And yet—

"...Usually, this is when I'd calm down, isn't it? What's wrong?"

His heart wouldn't settle, and there was no sign of his ragged breathing easing.

Bewildered by this abnormal change in his own body, Subaru flinched even at the chattering sound of his own teeth.

—For Subaru, this morning hour was the most terrifying time of the entire day.

"Calm down, calm down... time's up. It's fine to relax now. It's okay."

Pressing down on his trembling body, Subaru repeated the assurance to himself over and over.

The daily ritual of terror, now routine, was over for the day. Even knowing he'd collide with the exact same terror at the exact same time tomorrow morning, he had still gotten through today.

No one rushed Subaru, and no one cornered him. Which meant the only one making Subaru's heart impatient, the only one cornering him, was Subaru himself.

Going to school — just that single choice was the source of this agonizing stretch of time forced upon him.

Having refused to attend school, having spent a long stretch of time as a truant, he still could not bring himself to simply accept it — and so this was the time he spent, continually confronting his own weakness.

Tormented by self-loathing and inferiority, he waited only for time to pass, and once he confirmed the hour for attendance had gone by, he was freed from it all — an inertial existence, day after day.

Precisely because it was a daily agony, no one knew the peace of that release better than he did. Nor the weakness of clinging to it, nor the ugliness of continuing to make excuses that he had no other choice.

Having accepted all of that, he should have been simply passing through this hour as always — and yet—

"Why, of all days, today...?"

The guilt, the self-loathing, the clinging discomfort — none of it would go away.

Unable to identify the source of the restlessness that made him want to claw at his own chest, Subaru broke into an unpleasant sweat, his breath ragged, writhing in agony atop the futon.

The faces of his parents, as he'd seen them leaving the table, stayed burned into his mind and would not fade.

The usual manner, the usual conversation, the usual back-and-forth, the usual betrayal, the usual idleness — that's how it should have been.

And yet today of all days, that whole litany of the usual weighed strangely, painfully on his chest.

*—Now that I think of it, something's been off since the very moment I woke up this morning.*

It was routine for Kenichi, his father, to devise every trick in the book to harass Subaru awake. A father whose way of treating Subaru — who had stopped going to school and become, in both name and reality, a freeloader — never changed. Which meant this morning's exchange, too, must have simply been a continuation of what had gone on for seventeen years — and yet, this morning, for some reason, his chest had ached for a reason that had nothing to do with the diving press.

And his mother Naoko's misguided, off-the-mark consideration — even though it overwhelmingly failed to actually help most of the time — always put Subaru first regardless. Subaru, who never left the house, and Naoko, a stay-at-home housewife, inevitably spent much of their time together. And still, as if entrusting things to time itself, she kept watching over Subaru with gentle eyes. At mealtimes, for some reason, he'd found himself keenly aware of that gaze far more than usual.

And now, even past the hour for attending school, this abnormality in his own body — still restless, still scorched by anxiety — was the most inexplicable thing of all.

"Something's wrong. Why? Did something happen? Yesterday, let's see..."

Trying to trace back through yesterday's events, racking his brain over what might have happened between then and this morning — a searing, numbing sensation cut the thought short.

A sensation like sparks scattering behind his eyes flared with heat, as if refusing to let Subaru reach for the memory. Blinking in confusion at the sensation, Subaru tried once more to venture into the sea of memory — then gave up. Even if he tried, the answer would be the same.

Yesterday, and the day before that, and the day before that — Subaru had spent his days doing nothing at all.

This morning's — this very moment's — chest pain wasn't happening because anything special had occurred.

It just happened that today was one of those days he felt guilt, felt pain, for no particular reason. The fact that he couldn't look his parents properly in the face was surely just tied up with the same thing.

"—Hey, got a sec, Subaru?"

Just as Subaru, still unable to settle his pulse despite reaching this conclusion, was growing sick of himself, the voice came flying through the door.

Turning his eyes toward it, he saw Kenichi peek his upper body through, then glide smoothly into the room. His footwork, skillful enough to call to mind the King of Pop, was polished — but—

"Isn't the whole point of knocking kind of lost if you come in before I even answer whether it's okay?"

"Oi, oi, between you and me, bound by the unbreakable bond of parent and child, is there really a need to squabble over trivial things like knocking...? No, wait, there is, huh. Yeah, that's fair — a boy in the throes of puberty holed up alone in his room, sure, there's stuff he might wanna do. Got it. I'll come back in like ten minutes."

"Don't jump to your own conclusion and try to leave some 'realistic' gap of time! It's genuinely fine!"

Raising his voice at the unwanted consideration, Subaru inwardly breathed a sigh of relief that it gave him cover for his ragged breathing. Receiving Subaru's response, Kenichi said "Really-y-y?" with a dubious look, but re-entered the room walking backward with a moonwalk.

Facing his son, who looked up at him with narrowed eyes, Kenichi pointed a finger skyward and struck a pose right there.

"Now then, Subaru. As you can probably tell, I actually happen to be off work today."

"Yeah, kinda figured. Anybody'd notice if you're doing housework at this hour on a Monday morning. So, what about it?"

"Now, now, don't rush to conclusions. It's more like — there's some stuff I wanted to talk about, so I figured I'd use this chance to get it all out at once, or something like that."

"Talk about what? That I should wash my own dishes after I eat?"

"That too. Dad hates doing dishes. I like cooking, so the prep and the actual cooking part's fun, but once that's past, I've got no motivation left."

Kenichi swayed back and forth before crumpling dramatically. Watching his father's overacting up to this point, Subaru furrowed his brow, sensing his father was hesitating over something.

Not getting to the point, instead using a joking attitude to buy time for both himself and the other person to steel their resolve — he wasn't doing a great job of it, but Subaru recognized the tactic, since he had a similar habit himself.

Which was, naturally enough, only to be expected, given the resemblance.

"—Ow."

Just as that thought crossed his mind, another sharp pain struck Subaru's head once more.

A pain like a needle being driven into the inside of his temple. Feeling a grinding ache in his skull as if the bone itself were being scraped, Subaru bowed his head to keep his father from seeing his expression of pain.

"S-so? What'd you wanna talk to me about, now that you've lost your motivation?"

"Right, yeah. Subaru, is there a girl you like?"

"—Are we in middle school or something!?"

The attempt to change the subject to mask the pain provoked such an overreaction — from a topic dumb enough to make him forget the pain entirely.

Kenichi watched Subaru raise his head, cheeks twitching with irritation, and waved both hands, saying "There, there,"

"Overreacting like that is basically a confession that you do have a girl you like."

"The hell are you looking so smug about. I'm too exasperated, too grief-stricken, too sighing to even respond."

In truth, it was a completely off-base guess. Interest in things like a girl he liked — Subaru had none of that right now. Couldn't have it, and felt he shouldn't have it. Though maybe that was just something he'd convinced himself of.

"Aw, boring, what a letdown. Didn't Dad give you proper advice back when you were little? Girls are suckers for that whole 'promise made years ago' situation, so I told you — just go around making promises like 'in ten years, let's—' with every halfway decent girl you can find, plant those flags, and once you're a teenager you can just ride the resulting story route."

"My innocent self took that seriously and went around demanding pinky promises from girls all over the place, and this whole neighborhood ended up issuing a pinky-promise ban. The official reason given was 'multiple incidents of a scary-faced male child attempting to force female children to swallow a thousand needles'!"

"...If only you'd inherited Dad's smooth good looks along with everything else. Short legs, Mom's bad eyes, Dad's overexcitement, and Mom's obliviousness — you really got the worst possible stat distribution coming out of the womb."

"Tell that to me back when I still had my umbilical cord attached..."

Recalling this achingly bittersweet memory, father and son both deflated together for some reason, sighing in unison. Then, redirecting the conversation that had wandered off track with a "So?", Subaru brought it back to the point.

"So what was it you actually wanted to talk about. I've got important business after this — a second nap, then a third nap — so please leave your message after the beep and go gossip with Mom instead."

"Don't kick me out with that natural a flow. Besides, there's no way talking to Mom about it would even get through. My wife, your mother, is the single most oblivious creature on the face of the earth. Which is also what makes her cute."

Subaru's face fell into a weary look at the sudden, naturally-dropped lovesick remark.

In front of him, Kenichi hummed "Hmm" for a moment, looking up, then touched his own nose with the face of a mischievous kid.

"Well, anyway. It's nice weather out, so — how about we step outside for a little father-son talk."
