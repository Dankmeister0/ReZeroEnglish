Arc 3 Chapter 32: "The Sickness Called Despair"

Anxiety, an anxiety so thick it made him want to claw at his own throat, held his entire body in its grip.

His legs pressed forward, his heart aimed at the future, his will was fixed on its goal — and yet he felt as though some nameless terror were creeping up on him from behind, closing the gap inch by inch.

The nausea was severe. His ears rang with a violent clanging. His heart kept up its frantic roar as though it might spill out of his chest, and the legs kicking against the ground felt so weak and unreliable that if he stopped paying attention to them, they seemed like they might simply flicker out of existence from the toes up.

Why had it come to this?

Everything was supposed to work out. Everything was supposed to turn toward something good.

It was just bad luck. Just bad timing.

If he tried, he could do it. If what he had to do was clear, he could go forward without hesitation.

He knew what he had to do. All he had to do was save Emilia.

If she was cornered, driven into a corner, then it was Subaru's job to pull her out of it.

That was how it had always been. This time would be the same. And then everything would work out. She would look at him differently. She would think, *after all, it's no good without Subaru*, and she would keep him at her side.

He didn't want to be given up on. He didn't want to be abandoned. He didn't want to be looked down on. He didn't want to be written off.

With desperate resolve, with frantic will, with a determination born of near-death, Subaru chewed the thought over and over.

His breath was ragged. His lungs ached, his overworked body creaked, and he could feel every part of his not-yet-healed flesh screaming. — But there was no way he could stop.

He had to run, even a second sooner, even the barest fraction sooner.

If he didn't, it would catch him. That something incomprehensible closing in from behind — it would catch him, and devour him whole.

He didn't want that. That alone he could not stand. Right now, that was the only thing that terrified him, utterly and completely.

He wanted to see Emilia, wanted her to smile at him. Why had Rem left him behind? Where had the warmth he'd surely felt against him last night gone? Beatrice's spiteful tongue, Ram's smart mouth — how achingly dear and nostalgic they felt to him now. How much peace would Roswaal's crankiness bring him, Puck's easy languor?

If only he could have stayed there, in that place, forever.

If only, forever and ever, Subaru's time could have stayed buried in the days at that mansion.

Going to the royal capital had been the mistake.

Every moment spent in the capital was the cause of the misery he was in now.

Reinhard, Felt, old man Rom, Crusch, Ferris, Julius, Anastasia, Al, Priscilla, the faces of the Council of Wise Men, the knights of the order — one after another they rose in his mind, and every single one of them could now only be an object of hatred to him.

*May you all be cursed. May you all suffer. May you taste every last agony before you die.*

If only they didn't exist, Subaru could have kept from losing himself.

If he could go back to the mansion, make things right with Emilia, return to those heart-soaring days — he would gladly throw away everything else for it, and think nothing of the cost.

There was nothing left now. Nothing left to him.

Everything had spilled out of his hands. So now, all he could do was go gather it back up.

For that — for that, for that — he kept running.

Enduring the agony that felt like it would scorch his lungs, the regret that felt like it would crack his heart in two, the ringing that felt like it would bury his consciousness whole — enduring all of it, Subaru ran.

Over and over, as if on endless repeat, resentment and regret passed through his mind.

With such a backward-facing spirit, how long had he been running forward like this?

Around him, the trees that had grown thick and close began, without warning, to open up their ranks, and traces began to appear of nature carved back by human hands.

Bit by bit the ground beneath his kicking feet took on the packed-down feel of a path long trodden, and Subaru, snapping his head up, noticed the gentle upward slope beginning beneath him and felt his cheeks pull involuntarily upward.

Hazily, he had come out onto a road he recognized.

There was nothing that could be called a landmark; to anyone else it might have looked like nothing more than the same unbroken forest road as before. But to Subaru, who had run in single-minded pursuit of any sign of change, to Subaru who had walked this road several times before, he knew — this was the end of the endless green labyrinth.

He hadn't arrived at the mansion. Not yet.

Before that, right in front of him, beyond the slope, lay the village nearest the mansion.

Suddenly, into a mind that until now had held only the faces of the mansion's residents, there flowed the memory of the people he'd grown close to over the many times he'd passed through that place.

The overly familiar children. The village folk with their strange lack of wariness. That odd bunch who had never once laughed at his fragments of otherworldly knowledge as nonsense, who had simply accepted them as they were.

It was so dear to him he nearly wept. He couldn't even understand how he'd forgotten. The mansion wasn't the only place Subaru had to return to — there was still a place left that would take him in with warmth.

There too, there remained a place where Subaru's worth as Subaru still stood.

That village — Subaru had saved it. A village that might have vanished without him. His achievement. As a result of Subaru's own actions, there was nothing greater than that.

Remembering his own anchor, so close he could almost touch it, Subaru's feet quickened all the more.

Beyond the slope, past the screen of trees, trailing into the early-morning sky was white smoke rising — the symbol of daily life going on.

Cooking, maybe, or water being boiled. Or perhaps it was from a smithy or a metalworking shop. Whatever it was, it was proof that someone was there.

Right now, that alone was enough.

He wanted someone who knew him, someone who would accept him, to call his name. Warmly, with affection, to prove to him that it was all right for him to be here.

He tore forward. He scrambled up the slope. The top of the slope drew near, the base of the white smoke came into view. He crested it. Sweat trailing down his brow suddenly caught in his eyelid, and Subaru, annoyed, wiped it away roughly — and then, with a heart lifted in relief, looked out at the village.

— And there, at last, Subaru was caught by the nightmare that had been chasing him from behind.

When he ran into the village entrance, the first thing Subaru did was let his eyes wander in search of the first villager he could find — and then, noticing something wrong, furrow his brow.

The moment he stopped moving, all the strain he'd been holding off came crashing down on his heart and lungs at once.

Breathing in ragged gasps, spitting out saliva and phlegm, he planted his hands on his knees, bent at the waist, and tried to recover his strength right where he stood. Of course, even then, his eyes stayed fixed upward.

At first glance, nothing about the village seemed strange at all.

And yet something was clearly, unmistakably off.

The early-morning village air hung crisp and pleasantly taut, pouring the light of waking into a still-groggy head. And yet, for some reason, there was no sense of another living soul anywhere.

He crossed his arms, thought in silence for a moment, and then went "ah" — understanding.

Subaru, who had run through the night, barely registered it himself, but this was still the early hour of the morning, a time when few villagers had yet begun their day.

That was probably why there were no figures out of doors yet, no one who'd stepped out to begin their daily work.

Satisfied with that explanation for the emptiness, he set off instead to confirm the source of the white smoke curling into the sky. They say there's no smoke without fire, and surely no smoke rose without some hand behind it. If he headed that way, he'd surely run into someone.

But that hope, too, came up empty — Subaru met no one at all.

By the time he reached the edge of the village where the white smoke rose, there was already no one there.

Only the weak, smoldering source of the smoke burned on feebly, and though he looked all around, there was no sign anywhere of whoever had set it.

This time, it was not some vague unease but a clear, sharp dread that wrapped itself around Subaru.

His breath quickened for a reason that had nothing to do with fatigue, and his heartbeat began to pick up speed again. Driven by these reactions, Subaru, unable to hold back, pounded roughly on the door of the nearest house.

No response. Spurred by a bad feeling, he shoved the door open — it wasn't even locked — and forced his way inside. But there too, the place was empty. No one there.

*Maybe the whole family's out working the fields together*, he muttered, a useless little joke, and, wearing a dry, forced smile, went back outside. Without breaking stride, he moved on to the next house. This time he skipped even the formality of knocking and pushed straight in. A scream should have gone up, the resident should have shouted at Subaru for trespassing — it didn't happen. This house, too, was empty.

At last, understanding began to fail him entirely.

That nameless chill felt uncannily like the one the black-clad figures in the forest had given him, and Subaru, nearly beside himself, threw himself frantically into the search for another human figure.

But no matter how much he ran, no matter how hoarse his throat grew from calling out, no matter how thoroughly he searched every spot a child might hide in mischief, he could not find a single soul.

Silence had settled here too, in isolated pockets, and Subaru found himself left behind by the world.

He sank down heavily onto the ground, all the strength gone from his body, and let out one long, deep breath.

A situation that made no sense — no matter how many times you encountered that, you never got used to it. And of course, the same was true of situations that made all too much sense, in the most unreasonable way possible.

Subaru's present state, endlessly assailed by both at once, could not be captured even by phrases like "surrounded on all sides" or "a road fraught with hardship."

A sudden, sharp pain seemed to flash through his skull, and he reflexively pressed a hand to his forehead. But the instant he touched it, an unpleasant sensation smeared thickly across his skin.

Looking down, he saw that his right hand, which he'd set on the ground when he sat, had come away caked in the filth of the wet earth beneath him — and without realizing it, he'd just smeared it magnificently across his own face.

The sensation of reddish-brown mud running down the bridge of his nose was the height of unpleasantness, and Subaru wiped it away with his still-clean left arm, then turned a resentful glare on the ground that had done this to his hand.

He'd noticed it while running around — the ground this morning was unusually muddy, hard to run on. It was especially bad near the village entrance and around where the smoke rose; more than once, while dashing about looking for villagers, his foot had nearly gone out from under him.

He'd chosen a spot to sit that seemed relatively dry, but judging by the state of his right hand, the damage to the seat of his pants might well be considerable too. Once he started paying attention, he became aware of a faint dampness spreading at his lower back, and let out another great sigh into the open air.

*My head's gone pretty stupid,* he thought.

No wonder. More than a full day and night of brutal travel, almost no sleep. He'd put nothing into his stomach but the bland, flavorless emergency rations Otto had shared with him — hardly what you'd call a proper meal. On top of that, every waking moment his mind had been racing, endlessly worrying over Emilia's safety. If all that didn't exhaust him, Subaru would have to be not a human being but some kind of hyper-advanced artificial intelligence.

A useless joke crossed his mind, and once again a dry laugh rose to his lips.

He brushed off his backside, wiped the filth from his right hand against the wall — reluctantly — and, turning his body, went to search the village once more. One last, desperate flailing effort.

He had called out this earnestly, run around this much, and still not a single face had shown itself. Surely there was no one left in the village anymore. He couldn't grasp where they'd gone, or why — but if he accepted at least that much, there was nothing left to do but draw a line under it.

He made one full circuit of the village, and at last came once more to the edge where the white smoke trailed into the sky. Somehow, it felt as if time flowed only in that one spot swaying in the wind — as if it might soothe the loneliness of being left behind even by time itself.

By the time he reached the spot, the smoldering white smoke was on the very verge of losing its last heat and going out.

The feeble smoke was struck by the light breeze, swaying helplessly left and right before scattering into nothing. Feeling an odd sorrow at the sight, Subaru swept his gaze around the area without much hope left in his eyes.

And, sure enough, still no one in sight. The search seemed to have ended in nothing.

Letting out yet another sigh — he'd lost count how many by now — Subaru kicked irritably at the ground. His toe gouged into the mud, and the spray of dirt delivered the final blow to the dying white smoke.

A small death-rattle of extinguished fire tickled his eardrum, and the smoke, cut off at its root, vanished into the sky. The last of the white smoke was swept away on the wind, and when it was gone entirely, nothing remained.

Watching the vanishing smoke go, Subaru slowly lowered his gaze.

With the smoke gone, that spot too became just like everywhere else — a place where time had stopped, sealed off from the world. There was nothing left there to hold Subaru's attention.

There was only the village headman, lying there, who had been the one making the white smoke rise.

Scratching his head, he let his attention drift away and looked around him once more. Against the wall, cast aside like refuse, lay a young man with a close-cropped haircut. A weapon was buried in his chest as he lay face-up, and a great pool of blood had stained the earth red beneath him.

In the first house he'd entered, too, he now saw — in the corner of the room just past the entrance — a middle-aged man and woman collapsed together, one atop the other.

In the next house, and the one after that, there was no sign of the human figures Subaru had been searching for. There was only what remained after dignity had been stripped away like so many rags, after lives had been violated — wreckage, and nothing more.

The wreckage piled up beyond counting, and even as he took it all in, Subaru kept searching for a human face. Hoping, single-mindedly, that someone, anyone, would call out his name.

But no matter how many times he circled the village, that wish of his was never granted.

There was nothing here that Subaru sought. Understanding only that much, casting aside every other kind of understanding, Subaru abandoned his search of the village and turned toward the road leading to the mansion.

He'd wasted time on a needless detour. This was the result of not seeing his original purpose through. He'd squandered time for nothing, and still hadn't reached the peace that should already have been his. It was all pointless. Everything had been pointless. There was nothing here but waste. Subaru included — nothing but waste.

Muttering that to himself as if spitting it out, he moved forward on unsteady, dragging feet. Then, on the path out of the village, Subaru's foot caught on something without warning, and he fell.

By the time he registered his footing sliding out from under him, it was already too late. There was nothing around to break his fall, and with all his momentum he crashed shoulder-first into the ground.

Pain split through his skull; a groan tore itself out of the depths of his throat, and with reflexive tears welling at the corners of his eyes, he glared down at his feet to find the cause of his fall.

— And met the empty, hollow eyes of Petra, which no longer reflected anything at all.

"AAAAAAAAAAAAAHHHHHHH——!!"

— He hadn't managed to outrun it after all.

Subaru, his throat now raw and cracked from screaming himself hoarse, still shook with sobs, tears streaming down his face without end, as he gathered up Petra's discarded body from where it had been thrown to the ground.

The warmth had long since faded from Petra's body; her limbs had already begun to stiffen. An unconscious body should be far heavier than a conscious one — and yet Petra's body was too light, even accounting for how small she was.

No doubt, far too much blood had flowed from the wound that had pierced her chest.

Petra had died with her eyes wide open, her face frozen in an expression of surprise. That her face showed no trace of pain left behind was the one small mercy — proof that her heart had been destroyed, that death had come instantly. There was no reason a child like her should have had to taste pain on top of dying with a hole torn through her chest.

Her once-lovely face had lost all its color, her light-brown curls now so caked with blood and mud that they were barely recognizable. He wished, at least, that he could close those wide-open eyes for her — but her eyelids, already stiffening, wouldn't grant even that small mercy.

Laying Petra's body down on the ground, Subaru draped his jacket over her staring eyes as the least he could offer in atonement, and prayed that peace would come to her rest.

And then, having averted his eyes from it as long as he could, dreading to look, he raised his gaze — and finally let the reality sink in: the village he knew so well had become hell.

The one who had sent the smoldering white smoke up into the sky was the burned body of the village headman.

One of the young men of the village's youth corps must have fought with a sword in hand. The familiar blade now ran through his chest, sinking its owner into a pool of blood.

The man and woman he'd found collapsed together had been husband and wife. The husband had wrapped himself around his wife as if to shield her, and the two of them together had been run through by the same weapon and died as one.

There were bodies slaughtered. Bodies burned to death. Bodies torn apart. Bodies crushed. Bodies beaten to pulp. There was nothing here but bodies.

Death lay scattered everywhere throughout the village.

Over the village strewn with nothing but corpses hung a thick, choking silence, and everything here had already ended long, long before Subaru had arrived.

Now, alone, Subaru was the only one left to bear witness to the conclusion of the atrocity that had unfolded in this place — reaching out hands that had come far, far too late, gasping for breath with no one left to take hold of them.

What had happened here?

Something had happened. Something monstrous, something beyond comprehension had taken place.

Whatever it was, it had violated this village with merciless brutality, defiled the dignity of every living thing in it, and slaughtered every innocent villager to the last.

Not a single breath remained. Not one soul had survived.

The search had been relentless — every possible hiding place turned over and searched with meticulous care, so thoroughly that he even found a body that seemed to have been burned alive while hiding in concealment.

His mind wouldn't work. From every opening in his face, fluid poured out without end.

Tears, snot, saliva — they kept staining a face that had lost the will to hold anything back.

He understood what had happened. But he could not comprehend it.

He had no idea what had happened — not the first thing about it. He understood nothing. And yet there was one thing he did understand.

It was the fact that this tragedy could not possibly have ended here, in this place alone.

The moment that far-too-late understanding reached him, a chill unlike any he had ever felt swept through Subaru's entire body.

It brought him a terror greater than any he had known since falling into this world — greater than any he had faced across all the times he had overcome, or succumbed to, mortal danger.

His teeth chattered.

His eyes, aching now from too many tears, flickered; his unsteady vision lifted toward the sky.

Beneath a sky so blue it was almost hateful, the mansion stood waiting for him.

The place he had wanted so desperately to return to, the place he had longed for without end, the place now so close he could almost touch it — it now felt like the single most terrifying place in the world.

He didn't know what had happened.

But something, without question, had happened.

And whatever that something was, it surely hadn't spared that place either.

He was afraid. He was terrified.

He didn't even want to consider the possibility. If he let the thought take shape in his mind — worse, if he let it pass his lips — it felt as though the very act would make it real. That terrified him.

So Subaru shook his head, trying to drive the thought away.

But once it had crossed his mind, it slipped past the arms he raised to ward it off, and whispered at his ear, refusing to be forgotten.

So Subaru gave up trying to drive it away, and clung instead to a different means of escape.

Rather than speak that possibility aloud — rather than risk her safety by giving it voice —

"Rem... where is she? What happened... to Rem...?"

The girl who should have reached this place before him. Weaving together words of concern for her safety, Subaru tried, by the most contemptible means available to him, to deceive his own heart.

"If Rem had made it back, there's no way she'd have let the village end up like this..."

An excuse. In a place where he was utterly alone, he was making excuses that couldn't even fool himself.

"Did something... happen? Don't tell me — on the way, some kind of trouble—"

It was despicable. It was the worst thing he could have done. He didn't want to understand it, but he understood it all the same.

Rather than speak aloud the possibility that the girl he loved might be lost, rather than let his own heart break under the weight of it, he had chosen — deceiving his own heart — to offer up some other sacrifice in her place.

He had betrayed her trust.

"That's it... Rem... Rem... Rem is..."

Unsteadily, Subaru rose to his feet and began to move forward on legs drained of strength.

Leaving Petra's body behind, dragging his feet, he moved slowly toward the village exit — toward the mansion — advancing at the pace of a crawling tortoise.

Not knowing what waited ahead. Not wanting to know, even as some part of him understood that he had to know, still unable to summon the courage to break into a run.

Dragging himself forward, clinging to it like a lifeline, calling the name of the girl who was his one remaining anchor, Subaru walked on, slowly, slowly, up the slope, toward the mansion.
