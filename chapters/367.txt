Arc 5 Chapter 51: "Malice at Play"

"Now what's this, wearin' a face like that ain't like you. Somethin' botherin' ya?"

Just before reaching the control tower, Ricardo called out to the knight beside him, whose sidelong profile had turned grim.
Julius stopped walking and raised an eyebrow in surprise.

"I'm astonished, Ricardo. I never imagined you capable of such delicate consideration for another person's subtleties."

"No need to dodge with fancy words. It's just the two of us here. The young miss ain't around, so if ya wanna gripe a little, I'll keep it hush-hush."

"...You're impossible to beat."

Ricardo rarely showed it, but for all his roughness, he was a keen observer of people.
It made sense — one couldn't lead a group like the Iron Fang otherwise, and it fit with the fragments of his harrowing past that Julius had heard. A man who only watched himself couldn't have survived either life he'd lived, slave or mercenary. Naturally so, for both careers.

"Well, that's what they call the wisdom of age! Even so, I like to think of myself as the reliable old man figure in our camp. I'm always ready to hear out my son-in-law's troubles."

"Son-in-law is far too bold a term. I harbor no such indecent feelings toward Anastasia."

"Huh, who said anythin' about the young miss? Might be talkin' about Mimi. I got plenty of other daughters besides, so it's not exactly convincin' that the young miss is the first one that comes to mind."

"――"

Julius gave a wry smile. The gesture of shaking his head was, as ever, elegant, but even so, his choice of words and manner of speaking lacked their usual sharpness.
And the sign of that was—

"Ever since we retook the city hall, somethin's been off about ya. The young miss thinks so too. She didn't seem able to press ya on it, but I will."

"You won't show me any mercy, will you."

"Course not, our lives are on the line here. I ain't keen on entrustin' my back to someone who's hesitatin'. Got some plausible-soundin' excuse to talk your way outta this?"

"...No, you're right. I'm the one in the wrong. Indeed, I am at present beset by a hesitation I've been reluctant to put into words."

Julius nodded honestly at Ricardo's pressing and knit his well-formed brows together.
Yet no further words came from that troubled profile. Ricardo's patience finally ran out at this attitude, and he gave an irritated snort.

"Why're ya goin' quiet now? What's there to hesitate about? Just spit it out honestly, why don't ya. What is it, and about what, that's got ya hesitatin'?"

"――"

"Julius."

"Forgive me. I can't find the right words to say. —The cause of my hesitation, as you've likely already surmised, is the Sin Archbishop we encountered at the city hall. There's no doubt it was the person who called himself Roy Alphard of Gluttony. There's no doubt, but..."

Julius trailed off haltingly, uncertainty surfacing in his yellow eyes.

"Like the other Sin Archbishops, Gluttony no doubt possesses some manner of inexplicable Authority. The horror of it—devouring memories, devouring names—we've already gotten some sense of from the damage the White Whale caused. But..."

"Julius—!"

Just as he was on the verge of touching the heart of the matter, Ricardo's voice called out, tinged faintly with urgency. Julius understood at once what it meant.

—A tremor traveled up through the ground, the air shuddered, sound vanished from the world, and light rose into the sky.

The sight of blue aurora piercing the night heavens could belong to nothing else.
It had to be the afterglow of a slash unleashed by the strongest individual in this world.

"Puttin' on quite a show. That's the Sword Saint's one strike, no doubt about it?"

"Yes, it must be Reinhard. It seems Subaru's group has made contact with Greed. We can't afford to fall behind. We must hurry as well."

Once the attack on the other Sin Archbishops began, there was no telling whether the remaining Sin Archbishops would launch a coordinated retaliation—but neither could one rule out some kind of reaction.
Aiming for the control tower looming ahead, Julius and Ricardo quickened their pace.

"So, what's off about 'Gluttony'? Was it some monster on a whole different level!?"

Shouldering his great cleaver, Ricardo pressed Julius, walking ahead of him, to continue the conversation he'd interrupted. Julius turned back with just his head, denying it with his eyes alone.

"No. It didn't seem to be fighting at full strength, but at the very least, Gluttony's own skill wasn't so far beyond human comprehension. The two of us together could handle it well enough. —However, the enemy's unsettling nature lies elsewhere."

"――"

Julius's answer was vague precisely because he himself didn't understand the true nature of that eeriness. And the fact that he hadn't mentioned it during the earlier discussion of who would be assigned to the assault team was, for Julius, an unusually selfish omission.

Even while regarding Gluttony as an eerie, unfathomable enemy, Julius still believed he had to cross swords with it once more.
Where that reasoning came from, Ricardo didn't know.
Julius himself couldn't put it clearly into words either.

"――"

Kicking off the cobblestones, they turned a corner where the street bent. And just as the control tower—one of the four, standing out starkly from the other buildings—came into view before them—

"Aah, I knew you'd come. I knew you'd come, I did. That's right, that's right, that's right, isn't that right, isn't it, isn't it just, isn't it though, isn't it precisely because of that! It was worth the wait!"

—Before the entrance to the control tower, in the cobblestone plaza, stood a single boy.

He wore filthy rags, and his dark brown hair had grown out unchecked. His maddened, glinting eyes shone with delight, and from his mouth spilled sharp canines and a drool-dripping tongue.
His frame was small, both arms hanging limply at his sides. From every angle, he looked like nothing more than a street urchin with no particular strength to speak of—if not for the bone-chilling malevolence radiating from his entire body.

"Just to confirm. ...That's him for sure, right?"

He didn't ask "who." There was no doubt it was the one. That much was certainty.
To Ricardo's question, Julius answered only by silently dipping his chin.

Without a doubt, beyond any question, the one standing there was the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony.
The lowest of blasphemers, one who chews up others' memories and names.

"Roy Alphard—"

"Yep, correct-o! That's our name. So glad you remembered. Glad, glad. Glad, I tell ya. Glad, indeed. And because we're glad—it's worth the gluttony! It's worth the binge! Worth devouring, worth drinking dry, it is. And besides..."

Hearing his name called, Alphard grinned with brutal delight. His gaze then shifted to Ricardo, standing beside Julius.
He opened his fang-filled mouth and sniffed with ecstatic eyes.

"This time you even brought along a filling little mutt, huh. That consideration is unbearably delightful. After all, Julius Juukulius alone doesn't fill us up much. What's the word... bland."

"I was growing rather tired of your insults, so I thought I'd bring a friend along to settle things quickly this time. Ganging up on one man isn't exactly elegant, but..."

"Aah, that's fine, spare me that kind of preamble. It's just like you, Julius, to try and psych yourself up like that, but that feels bland too, doesn't it. We're gourmets, so we're picky about flavor, and you, Julius, rank among the least appetizing we've ever seen! Too neatly put together, y'know."

"How unkind of you to say, after such a warm welcome earlier."

"Can't be helped! It's not really our—my—true intention, or rather, I'm being dragged along by something a bit. I'd appreciate it if you overlooked a little inconsistency between word and deed—it's just my nature."

Waving his hand back and forth, Alphard refused to drop his mocking stance. Julius kept his composure against the provocation, but Ricardo, in his place, couldn't hide his displeasure. He clicked his tongue and cracked his neck.

"Oi oi, sayin' whatever ya please, ain'tcha, kid. If ya think bein' a brat'll get ya off the hook, you're dead wrong. What you lot are pullin' ain't cute in the least. Way past the point of just a spankin'. I'll crack that skull o' yours wide open."

"Ohh, scary, scary. Don't glare at me with such a scary face. If callin' you a mutt bothered you, I apologize, Ricardo Welkin. We were, believe it or not, a bit of an admirer of yours. That fearless way of talking with a big loud voice, so carefree!"

"—?"

Hearing his name called, Ricardo frowned and glanced sidelong at Julius. Julius shook his head at that look.
Something was wrong. There was something about Alphard's remarks that couldn't simply be dismissed as a madman's ravings. For instance—when had he learned Ricardo's name?

"Creepy little brat. ...Where'd ya dig up our names?"

"Digging things up, how tediously clever that sounds. We're just saying what we naturally already know. Isn't that right, Juliusss?"

"I can hardly answer when you ask for my agreement. I don't know you the way you seem to know me. So I'll simply resign myself to concluding that such tricks are simply your way of doing things."

"See, there you go again with that boring conclusion. You've got so much on your mind—anxiety, dissatisfaction, displeasure!—and you tuck it all away in your chest and put yourself last. That's a virtue in a knight, sure, but as a human being, it's dull."

Drawing his knight's sword, Julius quietly murmured something under his breath.
At once, light welled up around him, and a six-colored radiance encircled his tall frame.

The six semi-spirits Julius commands.
The fusion of swordsmanship and Spiritual Arts that makes the Spirit Knight Julius what he is—the "Most Superior Knight."

"The fragrant savor of inferiority, the rich palate of experienced failure, the sweet taste of fervent longing for something, the full satisfaction of a treasured secret held close—you've got none of that, none at all!"

"—Ricardo. I'm putting everything into this from the start. Match me."

"Ohh, leave it to me."

Swinging both arms, Alphard revealed a pair of daggers strapped to his wrists, peeking out from his sleeves. Wielding twin daggers was Gluttony's fighting style, but they were flimsy weapons against either Julius's magic or Ricardo's blow.

Barring some hidden trick, the outcome of this fight was already plain to see.
And yet, to Ricardo's eyes, Alphard's bearing showed nothing of a man facing near-certain defeat.

"Spirit Knight, Julius Juukulius."

Properly, before entering battle, Julius announced his own name.
Beside him, Ricardo shouldering his great cleaver had no such courtesy to spare in return. He simply kept his eyes sharp, ever intent on exposing the true source of Gluttony's composure.

Unbothered in the slightest by Ricardo's gaze, Alphard sneered.

"Sure, fine, why not, might as well, sounds good, sure thing, why not indeed, sure it is, precisely because it is! Gluttony! Binge! Gourmet, foul food, gorging, overeating! Bland, subtle, delicious, exotic! We'll devour every last bit of it! Even an uninteresting life is a flavor we've never known!"

"—El Krauzel."

The six-colored light traced a circle before Julius's eyes, and from the tip of the sword thrust into its center, an aurora blazed forth toward Alphard.
Multiple elements intermingling, the destructive force tracing a rainbow was a strike meant to swallow everything whole.

A beat behind the dazzling light, Ricardo also lunged forward with enough force to shatter the cobblestones. Brandishing his great cleaver, so that whatever way Alphard moved against the aurora, he would be crushed.
Before the wind-wrapped slash and the rainbow aurora carrying destruction, Alphard bared his fangs viciously.

"—Truly, big brother, you're just as delightful as I imagined. We're enraptured, absolutely."

—Beneath the moon, silver flashes cutting through the wind traded blows, scattering sparks as they sang out in clashing steel.

One half of that clash was the sharpened blade of a sword-demon wielding twin swords.
Facing him was the woman swordsman meeting the sword-demon's charge, wielding a sword-intent as soft and yielding as flowing water.

The gleam of blade against blade danced wildly through the air, and the clangor of steel, which should have been fierce, carried something fleeting and sorrowful. The high-pitched clash of strikes brought to mind the caresses of lovers yearning for one another.
Why such an illusion arose was surely because the swordplay of these two swordsmen meshed together more perfectly than anything could.

"Shiiiiii!"

Holding his breath, the sword-demon's twin swords lashed out in every direction, up and down, left and right, tracing paths without restraint.
The arcing trajectories of the slashes were almost artistic, the honed strikes a pinnacle that any swordsman might aspire to.
For a swordsman of some standing, such sharp strikes—strikes that might cause defeat merely from being enraptured by their beauty—were unleashed without any reservation.

"――"

Countless slashes, any one of which could easily prove fatal.
And yet, the long sword receiving that storm of an assault, and the skill of its wielder, were likewise beyond the human realm.

To begin with, the very existence of the long sword being wielded was an anomaly in itself.
Its blade length, so long it seemed unreasonable to wield as a weapon, matched the height of the very wielder swinging it. And yet the slender-armed woman swordsman brandished that immense blade as lightly as if it weighed nothing at all.

A hood covering her entire body, restricting her own line of sight and field of view, the wielder of the long sword made the tip of her blade waver with a smooth motion like paddling through water.
In speed, in sharpness, the long, unwieldy blade fell short of the boundless twin swords bearing down on her. And yet the sword-demon's slashes were, as if drawn in, blocked by the long blade's body.
Amid a rapid series of high-pitched clangs and sparks, before the sword-demon clicking his tongue in frustration, the woman swordsman leapt sharply backward. With no telltale motion beforehand, the sword-demon's reaction lagged, and the instant he stepped forward to close the distance—light plunged straight for the space between his brows.

"—Nguh!"

What flashed in that instant was a thrust of the long sword, unleashed to pierce straight through his skull.
A refined stance that gave no hint the blade had been drawn back, and a thrust specialized purely for killing that closed the distance in the blink of an eye—had long years of experience not warned him of death's approach, he would surely have died there, his brain scrambled by the light he glimpsed an instant before.
A tingling heat rose at his brow, from the vision of his own death he'd hallucinated. The sword-demon discarded that emotion in a single blink and moved to press the counterattack against the woman, still frozen in her thrusting stance—

"Gu, buh"

"――"

Just as he was about to move, the woman's toe drove into his torso.
It struck home in the gap between his hardened abdominal muscles, and her slender, long leg churned his innards. As the weight of the blow folded his body into a "く" shape, the silver flash traced a half-circle, raised high overhead.

The flash came falling straight down, as if to split the very moon.
A total reversal from the flowing-water stillness of her prior swordplay, the vertical cleaving strike came down with a sharpness so acute the air itself seemed unaware it had been cut, aiming to split the sword-demon clean in two.
The force of this single blow was incomparable to anything before it. The wielder's skill, and the blade's refinement—both met a standard more than sufficient to cleave a human body apart.
Truly, death approaching in the space of a blink—

"Don't you dare underestimate me!!"

Still doubled over, his snapping-upward arms crossed the twin swords above the sword-demon's own head.
The long sword slammed down at the center where the two blades overlapped, and the force of the impact shattered his clenched back teeth. His arms, unable to withstand it, gave way, and the long blade's edge shallowly slashed across the sword-demon's forehead.
Blood sprayed, mottling his vision red. But he did not fall to his knees. Nor did his twin swords break.

"Nguuuh!"

The upper arms gripping the twin swords bulged, pushing his lowered arms back up once more.
With a sweeping motion he flung off the long sword locked against his blades, and the shock of having her weapon thrown upward left the woman swordsman's body carelessly open before him. A retaliatory front kick was unleashed at her torso.

The force of the kick, powerful enough to bury itself in cobblestone, was absorbed by the sole of the shoe she'd raised to meet it. Using both the recoil of the long sword and the impact of the kick, the woman's body flipped backward through the air in a great somersault—and into that empty air, with no way to escape, the old sword-demon's body leapt.

—An opening.

At the woman swordsman spinning through inescapable open air, the sword-demon roared with his shoulders and unleashed a slash.
Closing in on her leaping body, catching up to her in a single bound, he struck with slashes from above and below. Two blades traced arcs simultaneously, biting into her slender frame like the jaws of a beast.

In midair, and with her back turned toward him no less, the woman had no way to defend.
And yet, that unclouded sword-light wavered.

"—!"

The woman swordsman twisted her body in midair, and the hood covering her head slipped off backward. Unable to withstand the pull of gravity on her upside-down body, what had been hidden beneath the hood was revealed.
What spilled free was long, beautiful hair the color of burning crimson flame.

"――"

The instant it flickered across his vision, a deviation of less than a blink's span arose in the sword-demon's slash.
A faint deviation at most, only the barest sliver off from perfection. Even losing that much, no strength possessed by any ordinary person could have blocked the slash unleashed.

But for the one facing the sword-demon, it was a fatal deviation.
To the sword's chosen child, one who had once received the favor of the Sword God, a clouded blade could not reach.

"――"

At the sight before him, the sword-demon's throat froze with dread.
The slash he'd been certain would land the decisive blow was cut off midway, halted just short of reaching the woman.

It was nothing complicated. The woman had simply drawn the long sword close to herself in midair and wedged it between the twin swords closing in from above and below—with the ease of jamming a prop stick into the jaws of a beast.
The tip and pommel of the long sword meshed perfectly with the blades of the twin swords. What sent a chill through the sword-demon wasn't just that alone, but also the fact that only a single clash of steel had rung out.

To receive two blades and, on top of that, reduce the resonant clang to just one, there was no way to achieve it except by precisely timing the moment when the blades closing from above and below matched exactly with the length of her own weapon.
What was truly to be feared was the eye that could judge such a thing, the skill to pull it off, and the mental fortitude to accomplish all of that without the slightest wavering.

"—Heh"

At such an utterly extraordinary feat, something close to admiration escaped the sword-demon's throat.
At that instant, the woman's legs—having caught the blow—spread wide up and down, kicking hard at the sword-demon's arms, still only halfway through their swing.
The blades, gripped under that impact, slipped from his hands, and in that instant, he was left in a state of unthinkable defenselessness.

Immediately after, the long sword was unleashed in a horizontal sweep, making the very air scream in its death throes.

The speed of the closing blade, and above all, its reach.
The unarmed sword-demon had no means to block, no time or distance to dodge.

The long sword tore through the thin skin of his right flank, and continuing on, severed his innards, his spine, and burst clean through his left flank, cleaving his body in two, top from bottom—spraying blood and viscera, the old body bisected without even a chance to groan. That, by rights, was the inescapable conclusion of fate.

In other words, an unavoidable end. A fitting close.
The final destination of a blade to which he'd devoted his entire life, everything slipping through his fingers, not even atonement within reach.

—Such an end, there was no way he could accept it.

"Ohhhhhhhh—!!"

In an instant, he rebelled against the bloody conclusion that had flashed through his mind.
The sword-demon's throat cast the hallucinated finale into flame, and crimson vitality burst forth like fire. His concentration, pushed to its limit, blurred the passage of time, and sound, color, everything but himself and his opponent vanished from the world.

The approaching blade traced the trajectory he'd envisioned, plunging into his own torso.
Slowly, feeling the heat and pain of blood as the blade tore through his thin skin, in a sensation as if gravity had increased tenfold over the ordinary world, he poured every ounce of strength into both legs.

His heel gouged into the cobblestones as he pushed off, and swinging both arms down to the right, he gained momentum from the motion.
Twisting his body along the shortest path and at the optimal angle, he performed a side-flip as if vaulting over the blade touching his torso. It was a form of evasion where he rolled his body over the blade sliding sideways across him.

"――"

Having dodged her all-out strike, even the formidable woman swordsman was slow to follow up with a reversed blade.
In that gap, the sword-demon leapt backward, catching the knocked-away twin swords in midair. Letting out a deep breath, he placed a palm against the gouged flank and checked the depth of the wound.

It was by no means a shallow wound.
He had let the blade penetrate his right flank, and in that state twisted his body to perform the side-flip. Rotating with a blade still driven into his body naturally left a circular slash carved across it.
Fortunately, it seemed he'd narrowly managed to evade just before the tip reached his innards, but the amount of blood dripping from the wound—cut deep enough to scrape the surface of his organs—was no small amount.
For an ordinary person, this would already be a serious wound, one that would warrant an order of complete rest, but—

"...I never expected to be able to fight long, to begin with."

The already short time limit he'd set for himself had simply grown shorter still.
The sword-demon—Wilhelm—wrapped the jacket he'd removed around his waist, roughly stanching the bleeding. Baring his sturdy body as he administered first aid, Wilhelm received no follow-up attack.
The woman before him quietly gazed at Wilhelm with those emotionless eyes.

At himself, hoping for some flicker there, some small change to arise, Wilhelm gave a wry smile. He gouged his own wound, waking his consciousness with the pain.

"Faintness of heart is unnecessary. Do not dream idle dreams. As for trysts, there will be time enough for those in the world above, whenever that may be."

"――"

"I don't believe you came out here from confusion. Nor do I hope for some arrangement of the heavens. My wife was a woman who disliked wielding a sword, but she never once pushed the responsibility of holding one onto another."

Emotionlessly, the dead wielding the skill of her living years.
Long, lustrous crimson hair, pale, translucent, smooth skin. Beautiful eyes set like jewels, a beloved face he could recall without tiring even with his eyes closed.
Everything was there before him, and everything was something that should not be there before him.

"Theresia, you are beautiful. —And so, you must not be here."

Spinning the twin swords in his hands, Wilhelm took up his stance once more.
The one standing here was not Theresia van Astrea's husband. The one called upon to be here was not Wilhelm van Astrea.
The one who ought to be here was the sword-demon, Wilhelm.

—Before the living image of his deceased wife, Wilhelm's heart sharpened, growing clear and cold.

His blood boiled, and his fury toward the vile being who had committed this act knew no end.
But in this one instant, this moment, this single clash, nothing extraneous was needed.

Once, a friend, a comrade-in-arms, a wife, had told Wilhelm:
Do not let heat cloud your blade. Do not act inflamed by blood. Love the coldness of steel.

Now, how was it? Was he burning hot?

"No, I am cold. —Like a blade."

Beneath the moon, the sword-demon fixed his enemy with a gaze like steel.
The superlative swordsman facing him likewise, without hesitation, made the tip of her long sword waver once more.

In an instant, sword-light was again unleashed toward one another.
The ringing of clashing steel resembled a scream, a plea, a courtship, all at once.

Wishing for it to end, yet wishing, too, that it would never end.
As if exchanging inexhaustible words, the clash of blades kept resounding, again and again.

"Ahh, damn it all! It ain't respondin', don't screw with me!"

Kicking off the ground, off walls, off rooftops, leaping.
Flying diagonally through the air, his short blond hair streaming in the wind he cut through, his face bared with fangs was the very picture of desperation.
Click of tongue upon click of tongue, the impatience scorching him from within stripped away any composure from his heart.

"Damn it! What the hell's goin' on, huh!?"

His clothes fluttering, he broke into a run the instant he landed, without pause.
It was a feat possible only through the combination of superhuman physical strength and physical ability. And yet, on the face of the one crossing the city with nothing but his own body, there was no color of pride in it.
Only desperation, relentlessly, howling at the unresponsive hand mirror.

The one running was Garfiel; the one being called out to was the Magic Vessel in his hand—a communication mirror.
The communication mirror, which should have allowed him to speak with its paired counterpart, gave no response. No one answered Garfiel's call. And that despite there being two candidate pairs that ought to respond.

"The city hall, and the folks facin' Wrath too! Why ain't anyone answerin'!?"

They had split the communication mirrors between them for the purpose of staying in contact, and each had gone into the operation to retake the tower with that understanding.
In fact, right after departing the city hall, they had been able to communicate with one another. But now, at the very moment contact was truly needed, the communication mirror had gone silent, its function dead.

—He had to convey it, right now.

"Gotta tell 'em to get outta the city hall, and here I can't do a damn thing—!!"

Spitting the words out, he leapt, clearing the street ahead of him in one bound as a shortcut.
The roof he landed on with a rough thud crumbled beneath him, but Garfiel spared it not a single glance. Right now, protecting the counterattack force took priority over damage to the city.

Garfiel was hurrying, heading for the city hall.
To the very place he'd departed from just a few dozen minutes ago, Garfiel was now returning alone. Leaving Wilhelm, his companion, behind, while frantically calling out into the communication mirror.

The reason was simple.
Danger was closing in on the city hall, their home base.

—The moment Wilhelm and Garfiel, the two of them, reached the control tower occupied by Lust, was nearly the same moment Reinhard's strike was delivered upon Greed.

Witnessing the aurora rising in the distance, the two warriors pressed into the control tower.
No Witch Cultists came out to greet them, no obstacles blocked their path. As expected, it seemed the Witch Cult's forces participating in the assault on the city had been limited to just its executive-class members venturing out.

Up to that point, things had proceeded smoothly. Inside the control tower, equipped only with the functions necessary to operate the city's floodgates, there were few rooms or places worth noting.
The two of them made straight for the upper floors, bracing for the decisive battle against Lust. Lust's camp was predicted to be the most dangerous in terms of combat strength ratio. In addition to Lust, there were two superlative-class warriors, three enemies in total that the two of them would have to face—naturally, tension filled their entire bodies.

"If possible, I would ask that you leave the woman swordsman to me."

"Heard from the boss there was somethin' up. But hey, I got business with that woman myself. Can't just go 'sure thing' and hand her over."

"—That one is my wife. Those wretches have toyed with my wife's remains, trampled her soul, and made her turn her sword upon those she once sought to protect."

"――"

"It is not, by any means, something I can forgive."

Along the way, Wilhelm's words, speaking of his own reason for fighting.
There was a force behind them that made even Garfiel, who surely held his own reasons he couldn't yield, fall silent without meaning to. The very fact that he couldn't immediately talk back—perhaps that alone had already decided which of them was the more fitting match for that woman swordsman.

"――"

Though it was never put into words, Garfiel yielded the enemy to Wilhelm. And Wilhelm, taking it as such, dipped his chin, if not offering thanks outright.
And so, when they stepped into the control tower, every fine hair on Garfiel's body stood on end.

If Wilhelm was to cross blades with that woman swordsman, then Garfiel had to take on the remaining two himself. The woman swordsman aside, the huge man standing beside her was no less formidable an opponent.
In terms of raw combat power, he judged Lust to be inferior, but Subaru had drilled into him time and again that what made a Sin Archbishop truly terrifying wasn't combat power—it was their very nature.

Quiet tension, and welling battle spirit.
The moment his sense of smell caught the scent of blood growing steadily stronger, Garfiel equipped the silver shields fastened to both his legs and kicked open the door to the target room with tremendous force.
And within, he saw it.

『Like hell we'd sit around waitin' for ya, dumbass.』

Those words written in blood, in a massive quantity of fresh blood, covering the entire room.

The instant he grasped their meaning, Garfiel's brain boiled over.
At the stance of fleeing as a matter of course in the face of an assault. No obligation to wait around. And at the mindset that could carry that out so brazenly.

"—I had forgotten. That these are the sort we're dealing with."

Suppressing his voice, Wilhelm drew the communication mirror from his coat. That he moved at once to contact the city hall was because Wilhelm had grasped the possibility first.

"If forces are allocated to the attack, then naturally, the defenses of the home base grow thin. This sort of scum wouldn't hesitate to target that kind of gap."

Before Garfiel, turning pale, Wilhelm clicked his tongue at the unresponsive communication mirror.
At the same moment arose a thick, dense hostility trailing down from the rooftop of the control tower.

At the sensation of a blade tracing down his spine, Garfiel understood the enemy's presence.
Wilhelm, too, grasped the true nature of that hostility.

"Sir Garfiel, please see to the city hall."

"If it's just runnin' back, I'm faster."

The exchange of intent took only an instant.
The enemy remaining behind to hold them there was, beyond doubt, a person radiating a sharp sword-intent. If they turned their backs to flee, that blade would cut them both down from behind in an instant.

One of them had to stay.
And one of them had to return to the city hall.

"Please keep calling out. —Please, look after my liege."

"Ya don't even gotta say it. 'A soldier's spirit rises at the sound of Libre's voice,' ain't that how it goes."

Catching the communication mirror tossed to him, Garfiel bolted from the control tower.
He leapt clear across the city, over the canals, continuing to call out ceaselessly into the unresponsive mirror. —Wilhelm's own battle, surely, had already begun.

"Damn it all! Got played for a fool, tricked by some cheap little trap...!"

If Lust had launched a surprise attack on the city hall, then the forces able to fight back were few.
Anastasia and Ferris had no combat ability, and Crusch, too, lay incapacitated by her wounds. A handful of Iron Fang members remained on guard, but even compared to Mimi, their skill fell short.

The moment he thought of Mimi, a painful throb struck Garfiel's chest.
The girl who, even now teetering at death's edge, had pulled him back, shielded him, saved him.

Keeping her life tethered, saving her—that should have been his role to fulfill.
And yet he had handed that role to someone else over a matter of feeling, pushing his chance for retribution further away. And now, even the task he'd taken on instead, he couldn't properly accomplish.

What was he even doing. In this sorry state, what was he doing.
He couldn't face Mimi, or Subaru, or his sister, or Ram, or anyone at all.

"I'm—again—!"

Was he to remain unable to do anything?
Reflected in the unresponsive communication mirror was his own pathetic face. In the very instant he cursed it—

"—!?"

An instant after crushing through a rooftop and leaping, his reaction to a shadow diving in from directly to the side came too late.
His body, airborne, took a direct hit from a mass far greater than his own, and was sent flying sideways.
He couldn't even cry out in pain—an elbow had hooked around his throat, choking him. Blood and oxygen stopped circulating to his brain, and maintaining consciousness rapidly grew difficult.

What kept his fading consciousness tethered was the impact slamming through his entire body.
His body, intercepted in midair, flew off at a diagonal, crashing straight into the building beside him. Smashing through the wall with his whole body, Garfiel's body was flung about, kicking up a cloud of dust.

Groaning at the dull pain and the agony of shattered bone, he simultaneously realized the restraint had been released. Using the spring-like power of his entire body, Garfiel punched at whatever was within reach and forced himself up.
His own body was inside a building with its lights extinguished. The billowing dust, lit up white by the moonlight, hung thick, and before him, spitting bloody phlegm, he sensed a presence.

That had to be the very one who'd shot him down and slammed him in here.

"You bastard, playin' around with me like that—"

Raising his fists, the instant he moved to lunge, a fist punched clean through his gut.
Struck across his entire abdominal muscles by the enormous opponent's fist, Garfiel's body lifted into the air. Immediately after, struck by a fist swung down from directly above, he was smashed through the already half-collapsed floor and sent plunging further down to the level below.

"Guh, wha—guoh!?"

His body, having fallen face-down, was struck from behind by the sole of a boot.
Both the momentum and the mass behind it dealt enough damage to make him spit blood, and as his body arched backward, he was kicked further, sent tumbling violently. He was sent crashing through the building's entrance and thrown out into the street.

Coughing at the tremendous force, Garfiel pulled himself up. At the same time, he applied simple healing magic to his body, mending only his broken bones as he lifted his face.
Pursuing Garfiel, emerging from the building, was a towering figure, big enough to have to look up at.

His entire body wrapped in a black robe, yet unable to fully hide his thick arms and legs. More than merely muscular, it would hardly be an exaggeration to say his entire body was clad in armor made of muscle.
For Garfiel, this was already the third encounter with this enemy.

He already knew the name, too.

"'Eight-Armed' Kurgan...!"

Once, one of the heroes said to have wielded a blade in the Vollachian Empire.
The story went that he had died in battle over a decade ago, defending one of the Empire's cities—but his current state, much like Wilhelm's wife, was likely due to his death being defiled.

"――"

The instant Garfiel spoke the name, the huge man—Kurgan—extended his arms.
At the same time, the fastenings of the robe covering his body came undone, revealing his true form. Which was to say, it meant being exposed, up close, to the full majesty of the hero Kurgan.

Just as he'd imagined, that sturdy body was wrapped in a thick armor of muscle.
A physique massive enough to rival that of the Giant race, and above that neck, a face of a war-god, brimming with dominance and battle-lust fierce enough to put even an ogre to shame.
And what made a war-god a war-god was the eight arms supporting that monstrous combat prowess.

In addition to the usual two arms extending from the shoulders, two more arms extended from the same points. Lowering one's gaze, two more arms extended from around the flanks below the shoulders, and the remaining pair extended from the back, their palms turned forward.
A body fitting the name "Eight-Armed" Kurgan—a form so singularly devoted to battle that merely facing it might sap an enemy's will to fight.

"――"

Before Garfiel, holding his breath, Kurgan silently took up his weapons.
Fastened to the outsides of his thick legs, in coincidentally the same manner as Garfiel's own shields, were thick, immense, distorted blades—the war-god's "Demon Cleavers."

The war-god drew out two more Demon Cleavers from his back as well. Four Demon Cleavers in total. The remaining four arms were empty-handed, but even so, Garfiel remained utterly overwhelmed by the sight.
There wasn't even room to snap back that he was being underestimated.

"――"

A shudder ran through him.
Before a true hero, Garfiel's body trembled quietly from within.

Garfiel, who had admired heroic tales, legendary figures, great men whose names endured.
There was no way he didn't know the name "Eight-Armed" Kurgan.
The countless legends that war-god had left behind were also firmly within the scope of Garfiel's genuine admiration.

And now, that very man stood before him, a puppet of the enemy.
A bad dream. This nightmare must surely have gone on unbroken since that single day called yesterday.

How else could this much malice keep raining down on him?

"...Haah, haah."

Breathing hard, Garfiel reached his hands toward his own legs.
Fastened there, just like Kurgan's Demon Cleavers, were silver shields. Touching their edges with his fingers, running his nails again and again across the surface, he finally managed to undo the fastenings.
He then equipped the shields onto both arms, covering his fists. To check the fit, he struck his fists together, and a sharp, ringing sound echoed into the night sky.

His equipment was ready. His wounds, too, no longer left him unable to fight.
Only his heart still, somewhere within, clung to hesitation.

"Ain't no time for bein' a damn fool—!"

Clenching his back teeth, Garfiel struck his own cheek with his own fist.
Shaking his head against the pain and shock sharp enough to blur his vision, he looked forward once more. Baring his fangs, he raised his fists toward the war-god standing before him.

"If I stop here, what the hell am I even for!? The boss is fightin'! Everyone else is fightin' too! Fightin's the only damn thing I'm good for, ain't no room to be draggin' my feet!"

"――"

Even at Garfiel's spirited roar, Kurgan remained silent still.
At the war-god who merely stared back at him quietly, Garfiel resolved to make him eat his words, and crushing the street beneath his feet, closed the distance at once.

Drawing up the power of the earth through the soles of his feet, via the Divine Protection of the Earth Spirits, he channeled it through his entire body, converting it into a single blow.
It was a punch carrying enough force to bring down a stone building in a single strike.

The impact of a fist reinforced by a silver shield was, with that hardened blow, enough to shatter even a hero.
Unleashed from a low, coiled stance, both arms drove in a straight line into Kurgan's breastbone—

"—damn."

"――"

Garfiel's all-out blow was blocked by a Demon Cleaver Kurgan thrust forward.
Against the flat side of a single Demon Cleaver, the full force of the strike was absorbed entirely.

It wasn't deflected, nor was it parried aside.
It was simply, in a straightforward contest of raw strength, that his all-out blow had been stopped.

By a single arm, out of the eight, belonging to the hero.

"—Gubah"

Into Garfiel's frozen face slammed a fist from over the shoulder. As his body reeled backward, one of the arms extending from the flank seized him, and unable to absorb the shock, he was showered with a barrage of blows.
In an instant his cheekbone shattered, his eye socket caved in. His vision in his right eye turned entirely red, and his sharp canine tooth broke in half and flew off. Still gripped by the torso, he was slammed into the ground, kicked by a thick leg, sent rolling and rolling and rolling down the street, bouncing, and falling into the canal.

"—Ah"

In an instant everything grew distant, and he stared hard up at the moon hanging above.
Right as he felt as though even the moon was mocking him, Garfiel's body sank beneath the water of the canal.


—The surface of the water slowly turned red.
