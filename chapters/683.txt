Arc 8 Chapter 64: "Parent and Child of the Empire"

—Rowan Segmunt was, as a person, a man utterly unsuited to fatherhood.

Even to Cecilus Segmunt — who believed that so-called ordinary sensibilities were unnecessary for a leading man — it was plain that Rowan was a father who strayed far from any conventional image of family.
An ideal father, after all, does not cut down his own child simply because the child "doesn't seem likely to grow up" the way he wanted.
A single-minded abnormality utterly divorced from anything resembling paternal love — that, in its entirety, was the reason Rowan had killed Cecilus's older brothers while they were still infants.

A swordsman possessed of a genuine, murderous obsession that would trample anything and everything underfoot for the sake of reaching the Heavenly Sword — Rowan Segmunt was nothing more and nothing less than that.
Rowan was, in all likelihood, one of the most spectacular failures as a father the world over. And yet, having been born the son of a father he could neither respect nor love as family, Cecilus believed, with his whole heart, that he was fortunate.

—The longing for the Heavenly Sword that burned in Rowan — Cecilus carried that same longing.

An undertaking like setting out on foot for some invisible point far above the clouds was never going to be accomplished by anyone who let his eyes wander or took detours along the way.
Had Cecilus been raised in honest poverty by some respectable, storied house, by a family that would hesitate to rob and kill for a single meal, there was no guarantee that his wish to reach the Heavenly Sword would have remained free of needless impurities.

That is why it had to be Rowan Segmunt.
Cutting down even blood relatives when they became inconvenient entanglements, removing without hesitation any obstacle to mastering the way of the sword with unwavering resolve, setting trial after trial before his son with the boy's very life treated as an afterthought—
—that pure, unadulterated madness poured into the sword, Rowan had lavished upon Cecilus without reservation.
And so Cecilus thought:

—Having been born the son of Rowan — he really did have it in him, after all.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


Drawn from its sheath of dreams, the flash of the Dream Sword tore across the waking sky—

It cleaved head-on through an explosion of light that seemed as though it might trigger the world's very collapse, and its point reached the beautiful girl weeping tears of blood, blue flame burning in one eye.
Struck by that transcendent stroke, the girl — Arakiya's slender body — was not, however, cleaved in two along with her life.
For an Enchanted Sword forged by a logic not of this world does not let even the outcome of its own cut conform to common sense.

—Muspel, the Stone Clod.

One of the Four Great Spirits, the great spirit that upholds the very earth of the Vollachian Empire.
That the thing he had just subdued with the Dream Sword, Masayume, was Arakiya and yet not Arakiya — some transcendent being that had been on the verge of painting over her very existence — Cecilus understood instinctively. But its true identity was beyond his knowing.

Not that knowing would have changed Cecilus's resolve or his actions in the least.
Even had he known that destroying Muspel would bring the Empire's earth crashing down along with the great spirit, Cecilus swinging the Dream Sword through to the end was inevitable.

Prioritizing the heroine's existence over the Empire's earth was, for a leading man, simply the obvious call.
To begin with, it was dramaturgically impossible for something Cecilus believed in and carried through to the end to lead to a bad ending.
And so—

"――――"

Arakiya, unable to contain the power she'd taken in, had been on the verge of bursting.
Before Cecilus, who had subdued with the Dream Sword the vast power threatening her, the countless bands of light encircling Arakiya — diamond light — turned to dazzling motes and dissolved into the red-stained capital.
As Arakiya drifted slowly down amid the dancing swirl of light, Cecilus sheathed the Dream Sword in the scabbard that had sprung, dreamlike, to his waist, and reached out a hand.

She was a wreck from head to toe. Countless gashes covered her whole body, her blood-soaked kimono torn and unraveling, its design now far too aggressive to still be called battle attire.
And yet, contrary to that pitiful sight, there was not a shadow of darkness in the light of Cecilus's eyes as he caught her in his arms.

"――――"

Limp and unconscious, she lay with both eyes closed, her sleeping face as still as death.
But he knew from the feel of the strike that she wasn't actually dead, so there was no need to panic. He merely gave a quiet shrug, as if to say, well, well.

"You really are a troublesome one, Anya."

Amid the battle, memories had dimly surfaced — most of them a conversation, in the dusk, with the friend who had compressed Before-Cecilus into After-Cecilus. But of what remained beyond that, it was she who had left the most footprints.

Cecilus was renowned, by his own admission as much as anyone else's, for being incapable of looking after other people. That he himself had been made to go to such lengths on her behalf made her about as high-maintenance as they came.
It might not even be an exaggeration to call her the single most high-maintenance person in the whole Empire.

"...I can take it this is settled, right?"

The voice, somewhat tense, came from behind Cecilus as he held Arakiya.
Approaching with wary footsteps was Al, who, no less than Cecilus, had been left a wreck by the aftershocks of the battle.
Not only had he survived the fringes of that summit battle without being reduced to cinders, but at the last he'd thrown Cecilus a weapon right at his big moment — a supporting performance that showed he well understood what a scene-stealing moment was.

"Quite the understated, workmanlike performance there, Al-san. The way you caught my intent and tossed that weapon in — I thought that scored you real points."

"Points for what, your affection meter? If so, I could not care less."

Al let his shoulders slump as if fed up, then said, "More importantly," and looked toward Arakiya. Behind his iron helm, his gaze carried an air of fear and trembling, of edging closer expecting the worst.

"Is Arakiya, the kid..."

"Not dead. If anything, I went and pulled off the reckless feat of blowing past her limits two, three layers deep, precisely so I wouldn't have to settle this by letting her die. Killing her to stop her? Out of the question! Answering expectations while overturning the boring predictions — that's the Cecilus Segmunt way."

"...If she doesn't have to die, I won't gripe about how you settled it."

Cecilus answered by puffing out his chest, and Al sighed, fiddling with the metal fittings of his helmet.
Al's concern was, of course, understandable. It came down to whether the risk of letting Arakiya live was really worth trading away the peace of mind that killing her would have bought.
But—

"If you want that kind of complicated, heart-unstirring discussion, go have it with some clever type who wears a scowl well, somewhere I'm not around. My answer is exactly what you're looking at, Al-san."

"...I already knew trying to talk sense into you or argue you down was reckless, impossible, and foolhardy, all three. You'd better be able to take responsibility for that girl."

"Seems that was already the plan, near as I can tell. Feels like I'd already resolved to shoulder that responsibility from the very first time I beat Anya."

He answered with a floaty sense of it being his own affair and yet somehow someone else's, and as he did, he saw, overlaid on Arakiya's sleeping face in his arms, the face of a younger her.
Just like with the friend he'd parted from in the dusk, it was a vision born of what must have been a fittingly long acquaintance. Maybe he ought to put a bit more effort into knocking loose the memories that worried too much about how the story would pull back from here.

"But it seems that before I dig into that episode, there's another responsibility of mine I need to see to."

"Huh?"

At Cecilus's sudden mutter, Al let out a startled sound. But Cecilus didn't answer in words — he simply turned his gaze elsewhere, and let that be his answer.
In the hellish, red-stained capital where buildings and streets and everything else had been melted away by the aftermath of the fight with Arakiya — standing amid that scene was a figure of madness perfectly suited to it.

"――――"

A blue-haired man, unquenchable flame wreathing his entire body, crackling and roaring.
His skin, scorched and charred black, hid one of his features, but the blazing presence of his gleaming golden eyes announced to anyone at a glance that the man was Undead.
And to Cecilus, it announced one further fact beyond what anyone else could see.

"—Well, well, well, here I thought someone was charging this way full-tilt, and they make their entrance on fire! My, my, now that is quite the flashy bit of staging."

He hadn't been running blindly — no, he must have been running with a clear purpose.
That his headlong, life-rushing stride had come to a stop here was because his purpose lay in this place — no, in none other than Cecilus Segmunt himself.

—Rowan Segmunt, the swordsman who had trampled everything and everyone underfoot in pursuit of the Heavenly Sword.

At the sight of his father, arrived at this place in so changed a form — had he finally decided even his own life was needless and cast it aside? — Cecilus nodded again and again, and laughed.
Rowan, too, laughed even as the flames swallowed him. Cecilus laughed as well, blood-soaked as he was.
Father and son, who could only live and die by the sword, laughed and grinned—

"Just now, for the first time, I've thought this of you, Father. —I don't dislike you. If anything, I like you."

So he said, and — ironic as it was — he thereby welcomed the chance to fulfill a promise made by Before-Cecilus, one that After-Cecilus had already forgotten.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—Rowan Segmunt thirsted for the Heavenly Sword.

Why, for what reason, to what end.
He was sick to death of such tedious questions and answers. Every bit of it was idle chatter that had nothing to do with him.

The trigger for it lay somewhere beyond memory; its beginning, at the bottom of the waters of forgetting.
From the moment he had once touched steel shaped into a sword, nothing had remained to Rowan Segmunt but the path of mastering how to wield it.

Even after being granted his heavenly mandate as a Stargazer, and after fathering a son as a Vessel likely to reach the Heavenly Sword, Rowan had continued, for none but himself, piling up training toward reaching the Heavenly Sword.
Even the fact that he had, with his own hands, brought into being the greatest obstacle to his own reaching the Heavenly Sword — he sought to turn that too into a foundation stone, honing training upon training upon training—

"Ahh... what a stage to make one's grand appearance on."

A burning soul, a life on the verge of ash, his own fate on the brink of running out.
Feeding all of it into the flame, Rowan let his self bleed out into the realm of the dead, entering a domain reachable by neither the living nor the Undead — a point that ought to be called the boundary between life and death, or perhaps beyond it.

That was equivalent to peeling back the very essence of what life and soul are.
Mere seconds remained, and yet it was reality: the final meaning of Rowan Segmunt's existence overflowed.

"――――"

Every time his Undead body was shattered, his beloved blade kept reviving alongside it; he leveled it now, and faced forward.
Beyond the wavering flames, a small silhouette came into view, and he saw it take up the exact same stance as his own—

"—Come, let us settle this fair and square."
"A duel—!!"

—Rowan Segmunt, challenging the Heavenly Sword, flashed in an instant's blink.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


"Please look after Anya."

To answer the challenge of the blazing Rowan who had appeared amid the remnants of a street that could no longer even be called a street, Cecilus forcibly shoved Arakiya, still in his arms, onto Al.
He heard Al's bewildered voice cry "Hey, hey!?" at the suddenness of it, but he cast that sound down from the edge of his awareness and focused every nerve on a single person.

Once again, color, sound, and smell vanished from Cecilus's world, and only the pulse of steel ruled.

"—Come, let us settle this fair and square."

He could tell, from the way Rowan's lips moved as he set a hand to the sword at his waist and took his stance, that this was what he'd said.
Though it made no sound he could hear, the bond between a flashy, vainglorious father and son let his soul hear it all the same.
And so Cecilus, too, answered in kind, magnificent and proud.

"A duel—!!"

In an instant, the distance between them vanished.
For Cecilus, the Blue Lightning, this was nothing unusual in a fight — but this particular instant hadn't been made by Cecilus alone. It was proof that Rowan had stepped across the boundary line of the human realm.

"Now that's a surprise."

This was honest admiration.
Some hours ago, when he'd parted from a still-living Rowan, his father had not, by Cecilus's reckoning, reached this realm. But now, meeting again after Rowan had become Undead, his swordsman's power had leapt forward, his talent for the blade blossoming only after death.

It wasn't speed, nor the polish of technique — it was a transformation drawn out by a change in awareness.
The influence of the mental on performance is nothing to scoff at, but breaking through a mental shell doesn't, in itself, dramatically boost physical ability. So whatever had happened to Rowan wasn't that kind of extreme physical change.

—Rowan had simply become able to step one pace deeper.

Not a physical pace — something hard to explain as more than a feeling. Just as there is a great gulf between amateur and professional, there remains a gulf even among professionals, between second-rate and first-rate.
And there is a gulf, too, between first-rate and super-first-rate, and beyond that again, the transcendent.
That invisible thing, impossible to fully explain in words — Cecilus chose here to call it simply "one pace," and acknowledged that Rowan had crossed that boundary.

That he should cross, by experiencing death, the single pace he could never cross in life.
People would call it foolish, call it far too ironic. But Cecilus, of all people—

"I don't dislike it. If anything, I like it."

His body, his very life, burning in unquenchable flame, Rowan unleashed a stroke from within the realm of death, and Cecilus found himself smitten by the flash of the blade racing through a world stripped of sound and color.
Unflagging training, effort never once spared — the light of a blade that held all of it made his eyes flicker and sting.

Rowan would surely be judged the worst, most terrible sort of father, and Cecilus wasn't about to deny it — but that spirit of pouring his entire soul, without end, into the role he'd cast for himself — that, Cecilus loved.

"――――"

Using his life, on the verge of burning out entirely, as fuel, Rowan unleashed the finest stroke of his sword, life and death combined.
That honed-to-perfection stroke carried within it a killing intent sharp enough to make even Cecilus think of death. —But the nightmare ending that such single-minded devotion might produce, the Dream Sword Masayume would not allow.

The Wicked Sword Murasame is an Enchanted Sword that severs the very concept of a "core" from anything at all.
By contrast, the true worth of the Dream Sword Masayume, sheathed in dreams, is this—

"—It devours dreams, and grants dreams."

An Enchanted Sword that, in exchange for the motivation behind a wish one longs to see fulfilled, houses the swordsman's power to fulfill that wish.
So it was that the Dream Sword was given the name Masayume — "the dream come true," the dream made real — and has been sung of ever since as a blade that, depending on its wielder, might be a dull, blunted thing or an Enchanted Sword capable of cutting down the world itself.
Passing through the hands of people who, as the price of having their wish granted, forgot even the reason they had longed for it in the first place, changing owners countless times across the years — the Dream Sword now rests in Cecilus's hand.
For both Cecilus and the Dream Sword alike, this was a miraculous convergence.
Because—

"There's no way my dreams or my motivation could ever run dry!"

That was the reason Cecilus Segmunt was the master of the Dream Sword.
An off-the-charts thirst for the dream he held drew out the full potential of the Dream Sword, Masayume.

—In that instant, the loosed flash of the blade cut through even the boundary between dream and waking, all in one stroke.

"――――"

Compared to the summit battle just before, between Cecilus and Arakiya, this was far too quiet a bout.
Its effect on the capital, the Empire, the world, was negligible — a one-on-one duel against an Undead who would have vanished on his own soon enough was, in the end, nothing but a trifling matter.
But for the father and son, Cecilus Segmunt and Rowan Segmunt, it held meaning.

"...Ahh, honestly, what an unfilial son you are."

With a single clash of blades, they passed each other in that instant, and father and son ended up with their backs to one another.
His father, Undead now and no longer able even to be looked in the face, let out a long, loathing sigh and said,

"Should've just cut you down clean while you were still in your birth-bath, if it was going to come to this."

"Ha ha ha ha ha! Yes, yes, quite right! That was your one and only chance to win, Father!"

At such a thoroughly graceless bit of sore-loser talk, Cecilus roared with laughter.
Listening to Cecilus's unrestrained laughter, Rowan tipped his face to the sky. His sword, its shape lost, crumbled to dust, and as his existence unraveled from his burning fingertips, his golden eyes narrowed.
And then—

"Next time. Next time. —I'm not the sort to give up just because I've died."

To the very last, without leaving behind a single word that sounded like a father, his body — his soul — turned to ash.
That was the end of Rowan Segmunt, the mad swordsman who had gone on chasing the Heavenly Sword, and who, even in death, refused to let go of that wish.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


Ahh, how loathsome, how loathsome, Rowan thought as he faded.

A lifetime spent walking on without ever stopping, and in the end, he himself had never reached the Heavenly Sword.
Not even the final brilliance of his burning, blazing soul had managed, in the end, to illuminate the invisible road to the summit of the sword. What a maddening, maddening state of affairs.

At this rate, would it be Cecilus who reached the Heavenly Sword?
His son instead of himself — truly a spiteful bit of scheming on the part of whatever sword god arranged such things. Regret and disappointment, discouragement and grief — once he started down that road, there was no end to such dark thoughts, and no wiping them away.
And yet—

"Well, better him than anyone else reaching it, I suppose."

In that last duel, had Rowan bested Cecilus, would he have reached the Heavenly Sword?
He hadn't bested him. In the end, there was no point dwelling on a hypothetical that never came to pass. Rowan did not dwell on things that were pointless. Least of all now, on the verge of vanishing.
Telling himself not to do what he shouldn't, he tried to think of something else, and then, all at once, he noticed.

For as long as he could remember, he had aimed for the Heavenly Sword, and by dreaming, waking and sleeping alike, of nothing but reaching it, he had carried his own existence forward to this very day.
And once that same Rowan concluded that he would never reach the Heavenly Sword, there was nothing left at all for him to think about.

"Hm."

For the first time since he had ever felt a thirst for the Heavenly Sword, he let it go.
The sensation left Rowan feeling less liberation than loss, and a certain discomfort besides. Amid a feeling that made even his soul itch, Rowan thought, for the first time since he was born:

—The single flash of soul he had spent his whole life reaching for — had it at least become enough of a candle's flame to light his son's way forward?


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


Leaving behind nothing but ash and dust, Rowan Segmunt's form vanished away entirely.
That this, precisely, meant his father's death — Cecilus Segmunt understood it in his very soul.

That he had, with these very hands, surely cut off the life of Rowan — a man already finished as a living being, who had chased the Heavenly Sword even reduced to Undead — right at the brink of that life burning itself out.

"Sorry for suddenly dumping Anya on you, Al-san. With just the one arm, holding someone isn't easy — let me take her back."

Sheathing Masayume, its duty done, Cecilus turned around and called out to Al.
But Al gave no answer to the call. He had sunk to his knees where he stood, supporting Arakiya's body with one hand as he shook his head weakly, and said,

"Why... are you fine with this?"

"Meaning?"

"You know damn well what I mean! That was your old man just now, wasn't it! He was alive and well when you left him, and then he died and got used by the enemy, and you—!"

"Hold on, hold on, stop right there, Al-san. You've got the wrong idea about one part, and let me set just that straight. Father was indeed dead, yes, but he wasn't being used. The reason he came at me with his sword wasn't because he'd lost his mind."

"Wha—"

"Ah, by the way, when I say 'his mind' I mean whatever passes for his usual state, so it's entirely possible Father is normally about as far from sane as it gets. No offense meant by that."

Cecilus added that about Rowan's mental state, waving a hand lightly.
Whatever the case, he wanted to clear up any misunderstanding on Al's part that Rowan had been made Undead and had his mind twisted before attacking, and that Cecilus had simply cut him down in self-defense.

"A child killing a parent is a major development, dramatically speaking — it even happened in Magrizza's Guillotine. —Though, mind you, Father and I ending up like this was something fate had reserved for us long ago."

"Fate... reserved it...?"

"So really, it just means the time finally came."

It was a reckoning both Cecilus and Rowan alike had known, each of them, would come eventually.
That was why Cecilus felt, to his own surprise, so little surprise at Rowan — parted from alive — returning to him dead. To Rowan, everything besides reaching the Heavenly Sword was trivial, and so it made sense that, if needed, he would throw away even his own life as a matter of course.

No one could understand Rowan. —No one but Cecilus.
A father who had been, as a cautionary example, superb beyond measure, was not some incomprehensible monster. That, in itself, was proof enough that the Segmunts, father and son, shared an unconventional but healthy relationship.

"I imagine most everyone would deny that Father and I had any such relationship, but—"

Unlike Rowan, Cecilus did not think everything in the world trivial. So long as one properly distinguished cheers from noise, the more one looked out at the world, the more it overflowed with Blessing.
Sunlight, a cool breeze, the fragrance of grass trodden with dripping raindrops, and Rowan Segmunt — every one of them a Blessing bestowed upon Cecilus.

"Father called me unfilial, but I don't think there's a more devoted son than me anywhere. After all, simply by being my father, he did something so enormous — bringing me into this world."

Of course, that would be no comfort to Rowan, whose lifelong wish had gone unfulfilled.
If he were called unfilial in that sense, Cecilus had no room to make excuses. Not that he had any intention, to begin with, of living a life that required excuses.

"――――"

Cecilus walked up close beside Al, who was struck dumb, and folded his arms, tilting his head.
His trouble now was what to do with Arakiya. Even if he wanted to carry her somewhere safe, where in the capital, as it stood now, could there be any safe ground?
If anything, was there perhaps no safe ground anywhere except right beside Cecilus himself?

"Then again, carrying Anya around everywhere while I run about is a strange notion too... I ought to head back soon, or Groovy-san — holding off that curse-caster for us — is liable to chew me out something fierce. Speaking of which, I wonder if it's fine by Groovy-san's standards that I'm bringing back the Dream Sword and not the Wicked Sword?"

This whole battle had, from the start, sprung up unplanned, beginning with the chance encounter with Arakiya.
Cecilus and Al had come here searching for the Wicked Sword Murasame, capable of cutting through curses, but unfortunately, that goal had gone unfulfilled. Right about now, Groovy, left behind to hold the line, was surely fighting the troublesome caster of the Thorn Curse.

"So for now, how about this, Al-san. You do your best to hold onto Anya while I clear a path for us, working together and—"

"—No, I can't get behind that."

"Al-san?"

Al shook his head and rejected Cecilus's proposal. He'd been kneeling on one knee; now he rose to his feet and pushed the Arakiya he'd been holding back onto Cecilus.
Cecilus, reflexively, found himself taking her back, and then—

"Told you already, didn't I. I've got my own thing I've got to do. Arakiya, the kid, was one heck of a detour. ...Sorry to Groovy, but there it is."

His voice dropped as he said it, and his gaze, behind the iron helm, went to the Dream Sword at Cecilus's waist.

"Even I can tell that sword at your hip is something else. Might not be the one Groovy was after, but go do your best with it."

"So you're going off on your own, then. It's rich coming from me, but is Al-san really going to come out fine walking around alone with no weapon? Isn't dying for nothing about the best you can hope for?"

"—. I won't die for nothing. That much, at least, I won't do."

"Yes, seems that way. I can sort of tell, actually."

At Al's tone, sounding so much like conviction, Cecilus nodded, chin drawn in, in agreement.
Having acted together with him this far, Cecilus had come to think that Al possessed some trait like the "Farsight" that Schwartz had — the kind that could see uncannily far ahead. With a "Farsight" like that, it might well be true that Al wouldn't die for nothing.

"But that's not the only reason you want to leave my side, is it, Al-san?"

"――――"

"Seems like the way things ended between me and Father really doesn't sit well with you. Could it be Al-san has his own complicated thoughts about parents and children?"

"...That's got nothing to do with it."

Even if it had nothing to do with it, the tone Al answered in made clear the shot hadn't missed its mark, and Cecilus gave a small shrug, shifting his hold on Arakiya.
Personally, Cecilus wasn't keen on parting ways with Al and leaving him alone.
Part of it was the judgment that having his hands tied up with Arakiya indefinitely wasn't good — but more than that, there was the matter of what he owed Al: the fact that he'd borrowed Al's strength to settle things with Arakiya without letting her die.

In short, Cecilus needed to repay the debt he owed Al.
If there was someone Al wanted to rescue, then helping with that rescue was surely the proper thing to do.
Worst case, he could knock Al out, carry him back to Groovy, sweep aside every last problem with the Dream Sword, and then have the whole group go together to fetch whoever this "Princess" of Al's was.

That was when it happened.

"—That you didn't try to take her life was unexpected. Reconsideration required."

A third voice suddenly cut into the conversation between Cecilus and Al.
Cecilus turned his face toward where it had come from and narrowed his eyes slightly. —The owner of the voice was in midair, not in the middle of leaping down, but simply floating there.
For a moment, the familiar verbal tic called to mind the Witch he had once killed, but the one there wasn't that Witch in question—

"—No, wait, same person, aren't you. Looks different, but the contents are the same... hmm? Or maybe the contents are also a bit different? Can that even happen?"

"If a soul's traits change, the outer form changes as well. And the reverse holds too... but by what principle did you see through it at a single glance? Answer required."

"Ha ha ha ha, now that you mention it, I wonder how I did know."

There was no intent to mock or provoke — he genuinely didn't know, so Cecilus simply laughed.
He'd simply, somehow, felt certain of it at a glance: that the woman floating overhead in midair, white hair streaming, was the same being as that Witch.

"But why here? Not learning your lesson after getting killed by me once, come back for revenge? An opponent with that much fighting spirit does score well on the charm meter, but..."

"To put it simply, it is to carry out a goal. I have achieved one of my two great objectives. Once I achieve the other, my expectation is that I will be able to devote myself to my original purpose of creation."

"Uh-huh, uh-huh, I see, no idea what that means!"

"I believe I explained that concisely. Explanation required."

"Well then, allow me to answer just as simply — rare for me, briefly, concisely, by my standards! —If you've come to my doorstep meaning to achieve that goal, you're not going to achieve it."

A simple, clear answer — perfectly obvious, about as easy to understand and unambiguous as it could possibly be.
Hearing that answer, the white-haired Witch narrowed her long-lashed eyes and nodded.
And then—

"Ah, I see. In that case, the answer is simple. You cannot kill me."

"――――"

"I have identified those in the Empire capable of killing me. I will approach none of them, not ever. Therefore, no one can kill me."

The Witch declared this in a flat, even tone, raising both hands to the sky.
In that instant, Cecilus felt on his skin an uncanny sensation, as if the space around the Witch and the sky itself were warping. Beside him, Al took one step forward.

Al, who had not said a single word since the Witch's appearance.
He looked up at the Witch, who was still generating that uncanny pressure, and thrust his single arm straight out to the side. —The next instant, the melted ground bulged upward, and a misshapen stone guandao took form, gripped in Al's outstretched hand.
Raising that stone guandao high, Al let his throat tremble toward the Witch—

"I am the Witch of Greed—"

"—Echidnaaaaaa!!"

With a voice of blood-spitting fury, he charged in, cutting, as though he'd forgotten himself entirely.
