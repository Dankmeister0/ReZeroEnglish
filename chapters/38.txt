Arc 2 Chapter 14: "Declaration of Rebellion"

—Far away, he hears something like the ringing of a bell.

A bell that surges violently, that recedes and returns like a wave, again and again. Subaru, raising a keening wail that trails on and on, doesn't notice that the pain accompanying it is akin to ringing in his own ears.

His temples throb violently, and he feels something hot welling up behind his nose. But by biting his tongue, splitting his lip, and tasting blood, he focuses his consciousness on that sharp point of pain.
The pain that gouges at the inside of his chest, the sense of loss that carries no weight of reality, the rage that can do nothing but howl at the unreasonableness of it all—he paints over all of it with the immediate taste of blood.

"Guest, Guest, you seem to be in pain—are you all right?"
"Guest, Guest, your stomach looks like it hurts—did you wet yourself?"

Two voices, close by, voicing their concern for him.
It had still been a short span of time, but these were voices he'd come to know well. Voices that had sometimes annoyed him, sometimes irritated him, sometimes brought him relief, and sometimes earned his trust.

—And now, wearing an utterly different resonance, they shake Subaru's eardrums with cruelty.

"—Ah, I'm fine."

Answering briefly while feeling their eyes on him, Subaru raises his face, eyes still closed.
The cause of him pressing his face into the futon, the surging swell of emotion, has somehow vanished into the waves. If he opened his eyes now, they might still be faintly red from its lingering trace—but as long as they stayed closed, no one would notice.

Breathing quietly, again and again, Subaru works to steady his own heart.
Having passed through the initial shock, having clambered over a despair that tightened around him slowly like silk floss, the sense of loss he still feels keenly is sobbing away inside his chest even now.
Even so, he ought to at least be able to grasp some foothold to start moving again.

"Sorry for worrying you, I—that was bad of me. What, I guess I was a little out of it, just waking up."

Once his mouth opens, his tongue moves on its own. Spinning out hollow banter with dry lips, Subaru forces a rasping "haha" of a laugh. —He still can't open his eyes.

While his eyelids stay shut on the darkness, he wishes the world would repaint itself into something else. Or that this was some fleeting dream shown by a doze—even an ending like that would do.
Ah, how wonderful, how infuriating, how welcome it would be if this were all just some scheme of Roswaal's, some plot to deceive him.

That stray thought feels so absurdly like a good idea, like it's brushing against some sliver of truth, that the knot in his chest seems to loosen all at once.
Feeling almost saved by his own heart's excuse to itself, Subaru lets out a small, escaping breath, and—

"—Ah, yeah. That's right."

He opens his trembling eyelids, and into the world that spreads out, blurred for an instant—reality is thrust upon him.

Standing on either side of the bed, hands braced on the mattress, looking down at him, the twins. Ram and Rem, familiar to him now, gazed at Subaru with their usual lack of expression.
There was nothing to be read in it, no emotion at all. Whatever it was that should have accumulated between them, however slightly, over these four days—it had vanished somewhere, like mist.

""Guest—?""

The confused voices came spun from both their lips at once.
Their gazes, in an instant, shifted to follow Subaru as he leapt up from the bed as if flung. Subaru himself, as though struck by a chill, obeyed the urgent dread driving him and put distance between himself and them.

"Guest, you mustn't move so suddenly. You still need to rest."
"Guest, moving suddenly is dangerous. You still need to lie down and rest a while longer."

Fingers reaching out toward him—and he reflexively twists his body away from them. For a moment their eyes narrow in something like pain, but Subaru is in no state of mind to notice the change.

An unbearable sensation.
The sensation of being treated as a stranger by people he knows.
Just the other day, he'd tasted that same feeling—in the crowded streets, in the back alleys, in that ruined house. But this is decisively different. The situation is different, the time is different, the experience is different.
This isn't starting over with people who barely knew each other to begin with. This is a one-sided do-over with people he'd surely, truly built trust with.

The wrongness of it, like someone he knows having become someone else entirely, turns his stomach and sends nausea surging up.
The twin maids, too, were starting to notice the change in Subaru—the fear-like emotion lodged in his trembling eyes as he stared at them.

A brief silence falls over the room; neither side able to act, each waiting to see what the other will do. And so—

"Sorry—I can't, not right now."

Grabbing the doorknob and all but tumbling out into the hallway, Subaru's move came just a beat faster than the twins' attempt to stop him.

Feeling the chill of the hallway floor against the soles of his bare feet, Subaru breaks into a run, breathing hard. Headlong, blindly, understanding nothing.
He doesn't know what he's fleeing from. He doesn't even know why he has to flee. All he can do is run, run for all he's worth, driven purely by the instinct screaming that if he stops, it will catch him.

He simply couldn't stay there, not like that.
An alarm bell was ringing wildly inside his head. His heart, his instincts, his very soul were screaming that he mustn't stay there. He didn't know why. Only that he had to run.

Racing past door after similar door, Subaru careens down the hallway in a stumbling half-crawl, on the verge of falling at every step.
And then, gasping for breath, as if led by something, he throws open the door his hand finds—

—and slips his body into the forbidden archive, lined with countless bookshelves.

Once the door swings fully shut, the archive is completely cut off from the outside world.
When that happens, opening this door from outside becomes tantamount to turning every single doorknob in the mansion in search of the right one.

Panting, back pressed against the door, Subaru sinks to the floor.
Even sitting down, his knees keep shaking, and the fingers he stretches out to try to steady that pathetic trembling refuse to stop shaking, like an alcoholic who's run dry.

"If I tried paper sumo right now, I bet I'd actually do pretty well. Haha."

The self-deprecating words have no edge to them; the dry laugh only sharpens the sense of emptiness.
The archive's still air carries the smell of old paper, and it pours a strangely calm sort of ease into Subaru's current, desperate state of mind.
Even knowing it's nothing more than a false comfort, clinging to it is the only peace available to Subaru right now.
Again and again, he repeats deep, heavy breaths. And then—

"—Barging in without even knocking. What an ill-mannered creature, I suppose."

Like a fish washed onto a hill gasping for air, Subaru's repeated gulps of breath draw a mocking voice from deep within the shelves.
In the dim depths of the room, seated before a small wooden desk, is a girl with cream-colored hair. Ever unchanging, ever unwavering, the keeper of the Roswaal Manor forbidden archive who has always kept her distance from Subaru—Beatrice.

She closes the book in her hands with a snap, and holding the volume—far too large for her small frame—against herself with one arm, she says,

"How did you break through the 'Door Crossing,' I wonder. ...Both just now, and earlier."

"Sorry, just let me stay a bit. Please."

Pressing his palms together in a pleading gesture, Subaru closes his eyes without waiting for her reply. —In this quiet, undisturbed space, he has to face reality, and himself.

He has to calm down. Accept reality. He is Natsuki Subaru. This is another world, Roswaal Manor. The twins' names are Ram and Rem. The one before him was Beatrice. This is the mansion's forbidden archive, which he can get in and out of fairly easily via the hunch he'd named "Door Breaking." There's no scar on his left hand. No proof of the effort he'd made. The promised morning won't come. The promise they'd exchanged. Tomorrow, with someone, together, somewhere—

"That's right, Emilia..."

He recalls silver hair glinting in moonlight, a shy, bashful smile.
The girl who, even beneath the moonlight, shone bright enough to rival a sky full of stars—and the promise made with her.

"Beatrice."

"...No honorific, I see."

"What's today's date...? Do you know what time it is right now?"

Fired at her one-sidedly by a Subaru who had fallen silent, the questions come at Beatrice, who looks somewhat put out. She regards Subaru's attitude with distaste, then snorts through her nose.

"To Betty, who spends her days in an archive where time itself is frozen, the date and hour outside hold no meaning whatsoever, I suppose."

"So in short?"

"It means I couldn't care less, I suppose."

"Damn it! Useless! I was an idiot for expecting anything at all!"

"Saying whatever he pleases, this one..." Beatrice mutters, wrinkling her nose at Subaru's blunt words, given with no room left for patience. At the girl's pretty features twisting, Subaru nonetheless adds, "But—"

"At least you're not treating me like some suspicious stranger. And besides, you just said 'earlier' and 'just now' about the 'Door Breaking.' Damn it, this is confusing!"

"What are you muttering to yourself about... I'd rather you not make a racket in the archive. It disturbs the tranquility, I suppose."

Responding with something like a sigh, Beatrice then looks thoroughly weary of Subaru glaring silently at her, and adds,

"It was only three or four hours ago that I teased you for your rudeness, I suppose."

"What happened to the whole 'archive where time is frozen' thing..."

Weak as he is, Subaru doesn't forget to seize on the contradiction, needling Beatrice into sulking.
Even as he goes through the motions of their usual back-and-forth, Subaru turns her words over in his mind.

—Subaru and Beatrice's encounter three or four hours ago.
That most likely refers to when he first woke up at Roswaal Manor. After the illusion of the endless hallway had been undone, and he'd gotten it right on the very first try.

Back then, Beatrice's hand had yanked something out of him, something spiritual, wholesale, and he'd collapsed with no means of resisting.
The next time he woke, he'd been laid out on a bed, and there, Ram and Rem, the two of them—

"So then, right now, I'm... at the point of waking up a second time at Roswaal's house?"

The scene, barely clinging to his memory, comes back to him, and Subaru understands.
That was the only day both maid twins had come together to wake Subaru. And it was only the first day, too, that Subaru had had the standing of a guest using the bedroom.
Which means—

"I've gone back... from five days later, to four days before? Is that it...?"

Correctly reassessing his current circumstances, Subaru defines his present state as such.
Led by some incomprehensible force, he's traveled back in time once again. And this time, in a form and to a point in the timeline he never wanted.

He can understand that reality. But understanding it and accepting it are two different things.
Holding his head, Subaru tries to think through what could have caused him to end up back here like this.

The only time Subaru had undergone "time regression" was the single day of his first summoning to this other world. He'd judged that by sacrificing three deaths, he'd saved Emilia and broken free of the loop.
In fact, the time regression he'd defined as "Return by Death" hadn't occurred since—the five days at Roswaal Manor should have passed in perfect peace.

And now, out of nowhere, this sudden time regression—with no warning sign whatsoever.

"Are the conditions different from last time? I just assumed I'd come back whenever I died, but what if it's actually rewound roughly a week later regardless... no, wait, if that were the case—"

That wouldn't explain why it rewound to this particular day, of all days.
The principle behind the time regression is unclear, but given how that repeated span of time played out, there should be some set of rules governing it. One of them concerns the location of his revival.
If he still hadn't escaped that loop, then Subaru waking up should once again mean waking up in front of the scarfaced greengrocer.

"But in reality, it's two maids who, compared to that middle-aged scarface, look like angels. Though the state of mind I received them in was heaven and hell, reversed."

As he speaks, Subaru pats himself down, checking that he's unharmed.
If the previous conditions hold, then the reason Subaru has traveled back in time is clear. Namely—he died.
Except, the problem is—

"If I died, how did I die? Everything was completely normal right up until I went to sleep. And even after I fell asleep, I never once found myself in a situation where I'd say I sensed 'death,' at the very least."

Even granting that whatever eased him into sleep worked exceptionally well, could there really be a "death" in which he retained not even a fragment of memory of the moment he was killed?
There's the possibility of poison, or gas—that he was put to sleep, so to speak, his consciousness sealed shut forever without ever waking. But that would mean assassination, and for that to make sense, there would have to be a reason for someone to target Subaru. As things stand, he can't imagine he's worth that much to anyone, not even a little.

"Then maybe it's a forced loop from failing to meet some clear condition."

Framing it as a game, it would be the consequence of not raising some necessary flag. But he has no idea whose flag it might be, let alone what kind of trigger sets it off.

A game where the flags' on/off states aren't clear is a hard one to clear. It's a bad side effect of the internet that you end up leaning on walkthrough sites without meaning to.

"Trading info with friends by word of mouth, getting fed some false information along the way, making progress bit by bit... that's how you're really supposed to enjoy these things, isn't it."

"Just when I thought you were muttering to yourself, it's turned into something pointless, I suppose."

Watching Subaru sink into his sea of thought, Beatrice mutters, sounding unamused. At her words he lifts his face, and the girl twists her lips, as though resentful of the time she'd spent being ignored by him.

"Dying, living—boring, worthless things measured by Human standards. And in the end what comes out of it is nothing but delusion and nonsense, not worth discussing, I suppose."

The girl shrugs and dismisses him coldly. At her unchanged hostility toward him, Subaru feels no small relief as he gets to his feet.
Brushing the dust off his backside, he reaches for the doorknob, and from behind him—

"Are you leaving, I wonder?"

"There's something I want to check on. I'll fall apart after that. Thanks for this."

"I haven't done anything, I suppose. ...Hurry up and get out. I have to reposition the door, I suppose."

A tone with no trace of kindness in it—and yet, somehow, it's a comfort to Subaru right now.
Surely she means nothing of the sort by it, but under the illusion of being pushed forward by her words, Subaru steps out.
Turning the doorknob, he steps outside into a cool breeze. The wind stirs his short bangs, and a faint sting in his eyes makes him cover his face with his arm.
Then the wind dies down, and beneath his bare feet he feels the texture of grass—and within that field of view,

"Ah, yeah. Still gorgeous."

He spots a silver-haired girl in the garden, her breath faintly quickened, and his heart leaps.
*What a stylish bit of staging,* he grumbles inwardly at the cheeky girl.
And then—

"—Subaru!"

Noticing him, the girl's violet eyes go wide, and she hurries over, flustered. From her lips spill, like the ring of a silver bell, three syllables that never fail to bring a fresh tremor to his heart no matter how many times he hears them.

Naturally, Subaru's own feet move toward the approaching girl.
Facing him, she looks him over from head to toe, and the corners of her eyes ease with relief. But almost at once, as if catching herself, she straightens her posture and sharpens her gaze.

"Honestly, you had me worried. Ram and Rem were running all over the mansion in a panic when you disappeared the moment you woke up."

"Yeah, sorry. Beatrice kind of got hold of me for a bit."

"Again? I heard she'd already pulled some prank on you a little while ago..."

At the sight of Emilia's beautiful, unguarded face leaning toward him in concern, Subaru almost reaches out—and reins in that weak impulse of his own heart.
Doing that here would be far too rash. It would mean the whole point of disturbing the silence of Beatrice's room had been for nothing. Framing her for a crime isn't the only goal here.

Subaru can only answer Emilia's worried expression with an ambiguous smile. At his uncharacteristic behavior, Emilia holds back, somewhat distant, and doesn't press further.
Of course she does. There's no way she could recognize what's "uncharacteristic" for Subaru now—not when she's spent barely an hour or so together with him.

Between him and her lies a four-day gap that can never be filled.
Four days that only he knows about—days that, for her, never truly happened.

"Feels like I've got some kind of radio signal jamming my brain, huh."

"Ra... dio? What—what does that mean?"

"Just getting drunk on my inner poet for a second there. Anyway, glad you're okay."

A skirt that ends above the knee, black knee-high socks. The overall black-based active outfit is the same one that had delighted Subaru back on the very first day.
Naturally, the body beneath those clothes should have made it through that Loot House unscathed as well.

"The body beneath the clothes, huh—that's getting a little lewd in my head."

"I was going to say 'thank you for saving me'... but somehow that look on your face is really unpleasant right now."

Glared at with narrowed eyes, Subaru manages a natural-looking wry smile.
Bit by bit, his usual rhythm is coming back. Time to shift gears, wet his lips with his tongue, and get Natsuki Subaru started.

Honestly, depending on how you look at it, this is a lucky break.
This time, he isn't starting from in front of the greengrocer's. There's no petty little death flag from getting beaten up over some misunderstanding, no scenario where he says something stupid to Emilia and starts at rock-bottom affection. Elsa's Intestine Festival ENDING is completely off the table.
Having made it through that fight in the Loot House, having shared life and death with each other, the two of them should have a passionate bond burning between them now, one no outsider could intrude on.
Right now it might be nothing more than a small spark, but with the right little push, it should soon burst into a full blaze and bloom into something magnificent.

"Me, thinking positive."

And looking back on it that way, this is also an opportunity.
Unlike last time, when he'd had to survive the Loot House's dead-certain brush with death, this time there's no crisis at all standing between him and confronting the direct cause of the loop.
Which means all Subaru has to do is calmly retrace the route he's already studied, making corrections along the way so it becomes an even better answer than before.
Even by following the exact same route, that moonlit promise still gets made.
—So then, if he follows an even better route, just how much more that moonlit promise could be elevated, how much more it could come to mean.

"Heh. This time, I'm starting from the climax..!"

"You seem awfully fired up about something, but your body's really okay?"

"Hm? Oh, top form, perfect condition, peak performance. I'm a little low on blood, my life force got sucked clean out of me, both my stamina and my willpower are scraped raw right from the moment I woke up, and it feels like my mental fortitude just took a full swing from a baseball bat, but I'm doing great!"

"Isn't that basically the definition of 'beaten half to death'?"

"Pinch is chance, baseball's won from two outs in the bottom of the ninth—that's the furigana reading on it."

He snaps his fingers and throws up a thumbs-up.
At Subaru's attitude, Emilia's shoulders slump as if all the fight has drained out of her.

"Well, if you're fine, that's fine. Um, are you heading back to the mansion? I've got some business here, so I'm going to stay a bit longer."

"Oh, spirit talk time, huh? Mind if I tag along, promise not to interrupt? Wake Puck up too, would you—I want some fluffy just-woke-up cuddles."

"I guess that's fine, but you really can't interrupt, okay? This isn't playtime."

Emilia tilts her head at Subaru wiggling his fingers, and tells him so the way you'd scold a child.
Something about her playing the big sister like that is unbearably endearing—and in Subaru's chest, a flame of resolve lights.

"Well then, let's go, let's go. Time is finite, the world is vast, and me and Emilia-tan's time together is only just getting started."

"Right... eh? Wait, what did you just say? Where did 'tan' come from?"

Steering the protesting Emilia along with an "It's fine, it's fine, don't worry about it," he guides her back to her usual spot beside him.
By the time he's kept up the nickname long enough, she'll have lost all will to correct him—something he already knows well by now. And that, too, is one more bond built over those four days that had otherwise been lost.

"—I'll take it all back."

He murmurs that quietly to himself, following behind her dissatisfied back as she heads deeper into the garden.
He stops for a moment, watching the retreating silver hair, then lifts his eyes to the sky.

—Still low in the eastern sky, he sees the sun rising, infuriating as ever.

Five more times. Let that happen five more times, and then let the promised hour come.
Let him see the sun come to meet the promise he'd made with the girl who suited the moon so well.

—There's time. And he already knows the answer.

"I don't know whose idea of a sick joke this is, but I'll take it all back, every bit of it, and make you eat those words. Don't go underestimating a short-tempered modern kid like me."

He clenches his fist toward the sky, a declaration of war to no one in particular.
It was the first clear declaration of rebellion Subaru had made, since coming to this world, against whatever it was that had imposed "Summoning" and "Looping" upon him.

The second fight against the loop begins.
To make it through this week at Roswaal Manor, and to find out what comes after those days.

To keep that promise made that night, the promise they exchanged—

"Bring it on, Fate. I'm more than ready."
