Arc 5 Chapter 56: "Sign the Letter of Separation"

—The identity of "Invincibility," the Authority of "Avarice" associated with a star's name.

The connection between the star names in Subaru's knowledge and the names of the Sin Archbishops.
"The Hand of Jauzar," the etymological origin of the name Betelgeuse, matched up in many respects with the "Unseen Hand" of the madman Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti.

That was why Subaru believed there was a close relationship between the stars' alternate names and the Sin Archbishops' Authorities.
And then there was Regulus Corneas, the Sin Archbishop of "Avarice." His name, Regulus, meant the constellation Leo, and its etymology traced back to the Latin for "little king"—and "the Lion's Heart."

He'd dismissed the thought with a laugh once already, but Subaru had shifted his thinking further, concluding that this wasn't a meaningless line of speculation after all, and arrived at a single hypothesis.

To begin with, what was a "king"?

The Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica was currently in the midst of the Royal Selection, its candidates each striving to demonstrate their own "kingly way." The shape each of their "kingly ways" would eventually take would surely be made plain in time, but what Subaru wanted to bring up here was the more universal meaning of "king."

Namely, that a "king" was a nation's representative, the one who stood at its summit.
If you didn't choose your words carefully, you might even call him the nation itself—but did that mean a nation could exist with him alone? It did not.
A nation only became a nation through the existence of both a "king" and the "subjects" who followed him.

Following that reasoning, then Regulus Corneas, who bore the name of "the little king," must also be a Vessel who could only call himself "king" because "subjects" existed.

"Then who and where are the 'subjects' of Regulus's 'little kingdom,' with him as its king?"

There was no need to think hard about it.
The Sin Archbishop who had launched his assault on the city of Priestella without bringing along a single Witch Cultist.
Each of the Sin Archbishops was, on his own, a Warlock steeped in a wickedness so twisted it made you want to look away—but Regulus alone had gone out of his way to drag along an unnecessary entourage.

Why would he have needed to do that?
Given Regulus's personality, the possibility that it was simple exhibitionism couldn't be ruled out entirely—but what if it wasn't that? What if he'd had no choice but to do it?

"To make Regulus a 'little king,' he needed the wives—his 'subjects.' I don't know if distance has anything to do with it, but that restriction is exactly why Regulus had to drag fifty brides along with him."

Was the condition for "Invincibility" the number of brides, or the distance from them?
If the condition was that Regulus be a "little king," then perhaps it was limited to something like the reach of his "authority"—the range over which that power extended.
Either way, the brides were far from irrelevant to what made Regulus "invincible."

"But that alone isn't quite enough to crack the whole thing open."

What Subaru's reasoning so far had revealed was, strictly speaking, only the "little king" half of it.
The other alternate name, "the Lion's Heart," remained unaccounted for, and beyond the overwhelming offensive and defensive power that came with "Invincibility," there was still no explanation for the effect that made him immune to outside influence.
If it were simple physical enhancement, there was no way Reinhard couldn't break through it. Regulus's "Invincibility" clearly transcended that dimension of mere enhancement entirely.

"It's not some super-powerful barrier either. I've tried just about every conceivable way of breaking an 'invincible' enemy. And I've confirmed his heart isn't beating, and that he has no body heat. So—"

There was only one piece left to complete, drawn from the alternate name "the Lion's Heart."

Regulus's "Avarice" Authority wasn't "Invincibility."
The true nature of the madman's overwhelming Authority was "the halting of an object's time."

Fulfilled. Satisfied. Nothing lacking.
The twisted personal philosophy Regulus blurted out at every opportunity—that was his very way of being, but it was, at the same time, an unwitting confession of his own ability that needed no concealment.

"Because his body's time is stopped, he doesn't just resist attacks—he doesn't even get wet from water. Because the time of the sand he threw was stopped, it doesn't get deflected by walls—it just passes straight through as if punching a hole."

There's a familiar sort of power in manga, something like "a rift in space."
A power that literally tears open a crack in space itself, severing anything that passes through it regardless of its durability—and Regulus's very existence was, in a sense, exactly that.

Regulus Corneas, with his time stopped, might as well be a distortion in space itself.
"A different dimension entirely" was exactly what his own words had said—"Invincibility" was nothing more than a byproduct of the Authority "halting an object's time."
In other words—


"—The frozen treasure of time. That's the true identity of your Authority!"

"You say that with such conviction, but I don't know what you're talking about! Are you that type? The type who assumes everyone around him must know whatever he happens to know? You should be aware there's a limit to how arrogant you can be, you selfish bastard!"

Pressing his back against a stone wall, hiding himself as he called out, Subaru drew Regulus's roar in response.

Smashing through walls, cutting straight across canals, crossing the cityscape without the slightest hesitation—that destructive march had finally caught up with Subaru.
Right now, Subaru was facing it down alone. Though "facing it down" conjured an image far more heroic than what he was actually managing.

"You're irritating, scurrying around like that. Did you really think I'd say 'good nerve, stopping your running around' or something? There's no comparison between you and me. You watched the Sword Saint get blown away, and you still don't get that? No matter how you look at it! That's basically you looking down on me, isn't it!?"

"Whatever someone you hate does, good or bad, it just pisses you off, doesn't it. I can't see any future where you don't find fault with me even if I run. Besides... this has to be the right choice."

"What's 'right' about it? The people you picked, the strategy—all of it! There's no way to describe the result except twisted. You'd have been better off keeping that unfaithful woman around, the one who could still fight, wouldn't you? Does the homewrecker have any trick up his sleeve besides stealing another man's brides?"

"Man, that's harsh."

Bathed in Regulus's sticky verbal assault, Subaru still didn't panic.
Without rushing, without hurrying, letting his revulsion show freely while dominating the exchange with words alone.

Right now, Subaru had lured Regulus into a district a short distance from the church, and having split off from Emilia, was clashing with the madman alone.
"Clashing" might be a misleading word for it. After all, Subaru was doing nothing but hiding himself and relentlessly repeating provocations, devoting himself entirely to buying time.

If Regulus decided to get serious and unleash destruction that swallowed the whole district, Subaru's whole scheme would collapse in an instant. But he was confident that wouldn't happen.
In their brief, and far from friendly, interactions, Subaru had come to read Regulus's character with considerable accuracy.

Put simply, Regulus was scum.

That was too simple to explain anything.
Put more precisely: Regulus was the sort of man who placed his own values above all else, yet who could never bring himself to ignore the existence of others.

In short, he was the very embodiment of a need for approval and a thirst for self-display.
Even while calling himself desireless, prattling on about how his own existence was complete unto itself, Regulus couldn't live without flaunting his own worth to others.

Forcing his sensibilities on people, painting over their values with his own, using fear and violence to compel the belief that he was supreme.
That wasn't just his stance toward the brides—it was his stance toward all creation.

And so Regulus, in a sense, met everything head-on with a kind of upright, straightforward honesty.
His clash with Reinhard was proof enough of that.

If Regulus had wanted to, he could have used the power of his "Invincibility" Authority to ignore every one of Reinhard's attacks entirely, and set about killing the nagging Subaru and Emilia in the meantime.
That he hadn't resorted to such a shortsighted move was for no other reason than that he'd been foolishly, honestly taking Reinhard's attacks head-on, straight-on.

This wasn't a fact that inspired any unexpected hope for nobility in Regulus's character.
Quite the opposite—it was a fact that proved something far more contemptible.

—Regulus was a man who couldn't be satisfied unless he crushed everything beneath his Authority.

That was why he crushed Reinhard when Reinhard came at him, and why he never made the tactically sound decision to simply hunt down and dispose of the provocative, ever-invisible Subaru.
Standing on the premise that he himself would never be hurt, never be defeated, he tried to crush everything about his opponent, to break their spirit—that was the only way this man knew how to fight.

That ugly humanity of his was hideous even to look at directly.
The reason it felt that way was surely because, to some degree, everyone carries around similarly wicked feelings. Even Subaru was aware of that ugliness in himself.
It felt like being forced to look directly at that—which was why Regulus's very existence was so repulsive.

And yet, it was precisely because of that, that a faint chance of victory could be glimpsed.

"Setting aside the fact that 'the frozen treasure of time' didn't land, is my guess completely off base? If you could at least confirm that much for me, I'd appreciate it."

"Why would I have to answer that? I owe you no such obligation or duty, and not revealing my own secrets isn't even a matter of rights—it goes without saying. How far do you intend to make a fool of me? Do you only understand once you've been blown to bits!?"

Rising to Subaru's provocation, Regulus kicked at the ground.
His toes drove into the paving stones, and the earth was scooped away as easily as if it were pudding. The scattershot he unleashed relied only on the direction of the voice, tearing indiscriminately at Subaru's hiding spot.
Before it could land, Subaru—having already read Regulus's move—had already left the wall. In the midst of his escape, he knocked over a stone pillar at the edge of the street.

Then, the rope tied to the pillar came loose, and a chain of light, tinkling sounds followed at once.
As Regulus, wondering what was happening, looked up, countless chunks of ice came raining down on his head. With Emilia's help, this street had been turned into an improvised Subaru-made trap zone.
Of course, even a direct hit would do zero damage to Regulus—but,

"This kind of thing!! They call it 'one-trick pony,' you know!!"

Not even bothering to dodge the falling ice, Regulus raised his arms and let it rain down over his whole body.
Naturally, unable to pierce through "Invincibility," the ice shattered and scattered, returning to mana. Including the chunks that hadn't even touched him, Regulus stomped and smashed the scattered ice around him as if showing it off.

"What is this supposed to be? If that long-winded theory of yours a minute ago was correct, don't you yourself think this is a completely meaningless attack? Even that girl could attack way more effectively than this. What are you trying to accomplish, going the long way around like this!?"

"I'll tell you what I'm trying to accomplish going the long way around, if you answer my question in exchange. That's a fair trade, isn't it?"

"Don't you dare flatter yourself thinking you and I are equals!"

Leaping back a great distance, Subaru put space between himself and Regulus.
Determined to close on him, Regulus bent his knees slightly and leapt all at once. Gaining explosive propulsion, he closed the distance between them in the blink of an eye.
Just as those deathly fingers were about to reach Subaru—right before, the ground beneath Regulus's feet vanished.

"Wha—!?"

"Pretty surprising, honestly, but a guy like you who only ever goes for a head-on fight is super weak against underhanded tricks."

Even the classic pitfall trap was a simple thing—just having Emilia dig a hole in the ground, cap it with ice, and cover it with dirt.
And yet Regulus, a man who by rights should never fall for such a simple trap if he had any real battle experience, fell for it completely. Ironically, that was proof that Regulus had never fought any way but head-on.

Fair and square, crushing everything from straight ahead with cheat-level power.
Nothing else, ever—proof of a man who couldn't fight any other way.

"Aside from figuring out your Authority, I can think of plenty of ways to get you, too. Keep doing this over and over, and I might start losing track of which one of us is the villain here."

A shortcut on the road to the church, and the trap zone he'd built with the time he'd bought.
Emilia had resisted leaving Subaru behind right to the last, but this kind of nasty, dirty fighting wasn't something the fundamentally honest Emilia could pull off. The right person for the right job—the correct division of labor.

"——"

While waiting for Regulus to leap out of the pit, Subaru glanced down at his own right leg. Just as it had been during the parkour of his earlier narrow escape, his right leg was in remarkably good shape. Good enough that he could almost forget it had nearly been torn off, or that it was covered in that strange, unidentifiable black pattern.

If this really was the influence of the "Dragon's Blood," then the blood was telling Subaru something.
To show the pride of the Dragon Kingdom's kin to the impudent fraud claiming to be "king" before him.

"In that case, mind your own business. Though I'll gladly take the benefits."

"You keep pestering me, it's so irritating!!"

The ground exploded up from beneath the surface, fragments of paving stone and clods of earth scattering wildly all around.
All of it lay under Regulus's Authority, tremendous destruction ravaging the street. Ravaging it—but Subaru was nowhere within its reach.

Regulus, springing up from the ground, spotted Subaru at a considerable distance and glared with irritation. Subaru gave him the middle finger where he could see it.

"You said something about a 'one-trick pony,' but which one of us are we talking about here? They say 'watch others to correct yourself,' but perhaps you should take a good long look in a mirror first?"

"Y-you dare... to make a fool of me...!"

Politely pointing out the flaw made Regulus's expression twist into something murderous.
His murderous intent toward Subaru had long since boiled over, surely filling the inside of that unchanging madman with a blazing fire.

Regulus couldn't sense, not even slightly, that this was exactly what Subaru wanted.
As long as he let his bloodlust fray and fester rather than honing it to a fine point, it would never reach its mark—Regulus didn't even have the means to realize that.

"Still, that doesn't mean I can afford to let my guard down at all, though."

Wiping the sweat trailing down his neck, Subaru too hid a resolve to fight to the death behind a flippant smile.
Regulus must never see through the intent to buy time. Even if the intent itself was seen through, the true purpose behind it must never be discovered.

That was the duty Subaru bore, having taken on the burden of winning this fight and sending Emilia off.
That was the resolve Subaru held, having sworn together with Emilia that each would fulfill their own role.
And so—

"I'm counting on you, Emilia. —Pull out the brides' true feelings for me."

When Emilia reached the church, the brides remained there, unchanged.

"Thank goodness. Everyone's still here—"

Gazing over the ranks of assembled brides, that feeling of relief nearly slipped from Emilia's lips.
But she hesitated just short of it, because the forms of those seated brides had remained unchanged in the truest sense.
As far as Emilia's memory served—since that final moment they'd left the church, they hadn't shifted so much as an inch from their seats.

"Because Regulus ordered them not to move...?"

Emilia already knew, from what Subaru had told her about the "Avarice" Authority, that this wasn't some binding compulsion of ability.
Subaru had qualified it again and again as "just a guess, but"—yet Emilia didn't doubt his answer for a moment.
Nor did she doubt what each of them, Subaru and Emilia, had to do in order to defeat the madman.

"If everyone's still here... then the first checkpoint is fine."

The most fearsome possibility had been that the brides might have left the church to hide, or scattered and fled in all directions.
Before it even came to having no options left, they'd have been forced to resort to a last measure. The one Subaru had bitterly proposed—Emilia didn't want to carry it out if she could help it.
And so,

"Everyone, let's talk."

There was no time.
A conversation that might not even be heard out had to happen here and now.

"—What happened to my husband?"

The first to respond to Emilia as she stepped forward into the center of the half-collapsed church was a blonde woman—Bride 184.
Unlike the other brides, who kept their formation in neat rows and maintained their silence, she alone sat before the ruined altar.

With the same cold eyes she'd had when she made Emilia change clothes, gave her warnings, and spoke of a hopeless future all at once, Bride 184 questioned the returned Emilia without a trace of emotion.

"Regulus is outside. ...I'm sorry. He's still mid-fight, and I haven't beaten him yet."

"I see. ...I suppose that's to be expected."

Bride 184's lips loosened, ever so faintly.
A smile so slight it could barely be called one. And Emilia could tell, at least, that it wasn't born of joy or sorrow, but something closer to mockery.
That kind of smile, those kinds of words meant only to wound others—Emilia had been on the receiving end of them more times than she could count in the past.
And so,

"You're smiling with such a lonely face. I don't think it suits you. A face like that."

"...My apologies. My husband forbids me to smile, so I've shown you an unsightly expression."

"Don't apologize. That's not what I wanted to say."

At the resigned words of Bride 184, Emilia shook her head.
Inside her chest, in some part apart from her heart, heat was gathering. Just as Subaru had said. She truly felt it.
A dramatic emotion, unbearably welling up, directed at something so maddeningly frustrating.

Closing her eyes, swallowing down the swirling heat, Emilia looked around the inside of the church and said,

"I'm going to defeat Regulus. To do that, I want all of you to help me."

"——"

"I don't know what you've all been put through by Regulus until now. But even in the short time I've spent with him, I can tell that much—that Regulus is wrong."

Abducted while unconscious, proposed to the moment she woke. Then, without so much as a chance to catch her breath, a wedding had been held, and she'd seen and heard firsthand the view of marriage Regulus held, and how he treated his brides.

That was far from the happy marriage Emilia had ever imagined.

"I don't want to lose to Regulus. I know that fighting, and who wins or loses, doesn't make anything right just because of that. But today, right now, I don't want to lose to Regulus. If I lose, then something important is going to get trampled on, I just know it."

"Something important... you say."

"——"

"If you didn't want to lose your life, you'd have had a much better chance following my husband's orders from the start, or fleeing for your life with everything you had. You should have done that."

Bride 184 responded to Emilia with dark eyes.

"What happened to the Sword Saint and your knight who were with you? They must have angered my husband, and lost. That's why you alone came running here like this, isn't it."

"That's not true. Subaru and Reinhard are still fighting Regulus. They're trusting me, waiting for me to come back."

"And what happens if you go back? Besides, asking us for help... it doesn't make any sense."

"Does it really not make any sense to you?"

"—?"

At Emilia's question, Bride 184 knit her brow in silence.
It was a natural reaction, not one that seemed to be scheming anything. She'd reached a kind of resignation bordering on detachment, but Emilia felt that up until now, Bride 184 had never once spun words with the intent to deceive her.
Which meant, at the very least, that she didn't know.

—That Regulus's "heart" had been entrusted to one of the brides, herself possibly included.

"What about everyone else? Does everyone really think it's fine like this? Is there no one who keeps thinking they wish someone would do something, that they wish someone would save them?"

"Stop it. If you're going to talk, I'll listen. If you're going to speak, speak to me. My answer is everyone's answer."

Cutting in with a somewhat rigid voice, Bride 184 interrupted Emilia as she tried to gauge the will of those around her.
Stubborn, or perhaps courageous—Emilia recalled how, for her sake, this woman had even spoken back to Regulus, putting her own life dangerously at risk.
That had certainly been a devoted act, but—

"It also feels like an indifference toward your own life, which should matter to you."

"——"

"Isn't it really you who hasn't come to terms with this, more than anyone?"

Come to think of it, from the very beginning, Bride 184 had kept speaking to Emilia without pause.
That wasn't only because Regulus had ordered her to serve as Emilia's caretaker. She'd spoken up to Regulus in Emilia's place, stepped forward on behalf of the other brides, and even now was trying to take on their words and bear the brunt of them herself.

That very posture could, in fact, be grounds for suspicion—that she was Regulus's true confidante, trying to steer Emilia and the other brides to some convenient end.

"But I don't think that's it. I want to believe that you're not Regulus's 'heart.'"

Emilia had had her life saved by Bride 184 more times than she could count.
Not in any visible way, being shielded or having her hand pulled to safety.
Just by being allowed to walk ahead, in a way that let her avoid hard-to-see malice.

Someone capable of that kind of consideration for another person—

"I don't want to believe you're really that man's bride."

"...Perhaps I've been treating you this way precisely to make you think that?"

"Maybe. I'm not all that smart, so if you really are trying to deceive me, maybe I'd fall for it easily. But—"

Whether or not she had good judgment of people, she didn't know.
The people supporting Emilia right now weren't supporting her because she'd chosen them herself, because she'd wished for them to be by her side and it had come true.
Everyone who supported her had chosen Emilia.

Being chosen didn't make her think she was amazing.
She was anxious all the time, on the verge of buckling under the weight of the expectations placed on her.
But she wished, too, to live up to those expectations, to be someone capable of meeting them.

"I want to believe in you. Because that's what I've chosen."

"——"

"Why do you step forward on behalf of everyone who stays silent? Why did you help me even with those resigned eyes of yours? Why do you—"

"So many questions."

Cutting off Emilia's questioning, the woman shook her head.
And then, for the first time in this exchange, she slowly raised her face and looked at Emilia.

A rigid expression, frozen with emotion.
Terribly parched eyes, and tightly pressed lips.
The sorrow only made this woman's taut beauty stand out all the more. And yet, Emilia thought.

"Please, hurry and leave. If my husband sees you, none of us will survive."

"Let me hear it."

"I have no obligation to answer your questions. You're not even my husband's bride anymore. You're not the same as us."

"—I'm a half-elf, you know."

"...What?"

At Emilia's confession, the woman was struck dumb.
Feeling that she'd caught the woman off guard for the first time, Emilia smiled faintly. Meanwhile, the woman too came to understand the meaning of Emilia's confession.

She understood that the one standing before her was the silver-haired Half-Elf.

"The silver-haired... half-elf..."

"You're right, I think you and I are different. Our circumstances are different, where we're from is different, and even at a much more fundamental level, we're different. But that doesn't mean that just because everything is different, nothing can get through."

"——"

"What you and I see is surely the same. We cry when we're sad, get angry at things we can't do anything about, and laugh when we're happy and glad. That's the same, isn't it?"

"What are you trying to say?"

At Emilia pressing on, Bride 184 let out a breath.
Asked back like that, Emilia found herself at a loss too. What she wanted to say, what she'd been trying to say, was losing its shape.
Proof that she was speaking straight from emotion. Losing the main thread in the process would defeat the whole purpose. She needed to be more like Subaru, able to convey what she wanted to convey, straightforwardly—

"Um, so what I mean is..."

There were things she wanted to know. Things she wanted to ask.
About Regulus's "heart." About why this woman kept trying to stand at the head of the brides. About how she'd protected Emilia—who looked ready to throw everything away, ready to slip through the cracks.

She wanted to ask about all of it, taken together.
But there was something she needed to know first.

That was, of course—

"Will you tell me your name?"

"——"

"I'm Emilia. Just Emilia. A half-elf who's different from you in a lot of ways, but I'm sure there are things we share too."

"Ha..."

"If we see the same things, feel the same things, and can wish for the same things... then I'm sure talking won't be pointless at all."

Once, long ago, someone had asked her name in just that way.

At a time when she'd felt helpless, when she'd believed she had no one to rely on, when she'd felt she might be swept away by the flow of everything happening around her.
At a time like that, the same words had seized her heart.

—Only now did she think of it.

That she had been happy, in that moment.
That, before a boy of unknown origin, she'd felt as though her very existence had simply been acknowledged, and that had made her happy.

To someone who'd been denied over and over, having words like that thrown at her—there was nothing to be done about it after that.

"——"

Once again, she'd borrowed strength from Subaru.
A self made up of nothing but borrowed things, patched together from pieces.
But that was fine, just as it was.

"D-don't mess with me... why, now, of all times..."

Before Emilia's eyes, Bride 184—the woman—clutched her head, exhaling hard, forcing the breath out in refusal.
Her expression was bitter, her voice full of loathing, and her eyes brimmed with an emotion glaring at something hateful.

That was, for the first time, the raw emotion Emilia had drawn out of her—

"Why now, of all times, are you trying to turn us back into human beings!"

As if surrendering herself to it, she cried out, letting loose the turbulent flood of emotions she'd kept bottled up until now.

"It's fine if we're not people. It's fine if we're dolls. That man is satisfied as long as we're obedient dolls. As long as we let him play his doll games, we can keep from losing our lives. That's what we've believed, that's how we've made it through until now... and yet!"

She lashed out at Emilia, accusing her of ruining all their efforts.
What could some outsider who understood nothing possibly know of the days they'd spent desperately clawing to survive?

"What do you know about us!"

"I know that you're kind."

"What do you know about us!"

"I know you've all worked so hard, with everything you had."

"What do you know, about us, what do you..."

"I know you've been screaming for someone to save you."

At Emilia's words, the woman looked up as if struck.
Her eyes stared wide, rigid, and her trembling lips gasped for breath.

Not once had she spoken those words aloud.
Because surely, in all these days, if she'd let herself say something like that, their hearts would have broken.

The despair of wanting to be saved is two sides of the same coin as the hope of seeking salvation.
Holding onto such hope had never been permitted to them until now. To keep their hearts from being destroyed, they'd had to keep their hearts crushed down.

And so, sealed away from speaking those plain words asking for help as they were—

"You wanted to be saved—everything about you was saying so. So I'm going to save you. I'm going to free you from Regulus. That's why, for that—"

"——"

"Please, lend me your strength. Let me save you... and let me save the person fighting for you right now."

She bowed her head.
Earnestly, with all her wish poured into it, Emilia bowed her head.

She stared fixedly at the ground.
Her own heart was pounding painfully, and the faint sound of breathing around her felt like a raging storm.
Feeling as though she might be knocked over at any moment, she clenched her back teeth and steeled her heart.

Fear wasn't something she alone felt.
Surely, far more than her, these women had been living side by side with a nightmare they could never wake from, for far longer.

"There's something I want to ask. Something I've never been able to ask everyone, until now."

The woman began, and the brides, their expressions frozen, said nothing.

"Is there anyone here who loves that man?"

"I wish his guts would rot." "Give me back my lover." "I want to go home, I want to go home..." "Forget saving me, just kill him." "Scum." "I'm done, I'm done forever!" "There's no woman who could ever love something like that, is there?" "Not a man either." "There's no one who could love that thing."

As if a dam had broken, the brides poured out the words they'd suppressed until now.
The flood of words was filled to overflowing with heartfelt hatred and resentment, with grudges built up from years of pain and suffering—words that, by no means, felt pleasant to listen to.

—And yet, the faces of the women speaking them were bright with a satisfaction so vivid it was almost pleasant to see.

"It was unanimous, but no one could ever say it, all this time."

"Do you have something you want to say too?"

"Yes, I do."

Having listened to the brides' confessions, the woman turned back to Emilia.
She smoothed back her long blonde hair with a swift motion, and then—breaking the command that forbade her to smile—showed, for the first time, a beautiful, beaming smile.

"I hated that man. —Please, save us."

And with that, she signed her smile to the letter of separation.
