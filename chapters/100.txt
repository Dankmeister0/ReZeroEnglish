Arc 3 Chapter 24: "The Outcome of the Duel"

"—Captain Marcos, a report—"

The guard who came rushing in delivered those words with a salute, just as the fine details of the treaty settling the Royal Selection had been hammered out, and that day's conference was about to reach a stopping point.

The young soldier, his shoulders heaving with his flustered breathing, paled the moment he registered just whose company he'd barged into, and his blood ran cold at the discourtesy he'd just committed.

The guard blanched so quickly it seemed almost audible, and Marcos moved to shield his subordinate's rudeness from the eyes of the room.

"My subordinate has been rude. The failing is in my training of him."

"His timing was good, and his own face already shows his contrition. With that, and with his superior speaking up as you have, there's nothing for us to reproach."

It was Crusch who, on behalf of everyone present, extended that magnanimity toward Marcos's apology. She smoothed a hand over her long, bundled green hair and, drawing a breath, said, "More to the point—"

"—for him to come barreling in forgetting the circumstances of this meeting, it must be something significant, no?"

Crusch tilted her head as she posed the question, and the guard nodded without hesitation. Then he began to speak, but his expression turned to one fearing the spread of what he was about to say.

"Captain, there's something I'd like to convey privately."

"...That's rather poor conduct to display in front of everyone."

"Even so, sir."

Pressed even after his mild rebuke, Marcos intuited from his subordinate's manner that something 'bad' had happened. Sensing that much, he resolved to excuse himself from the room and hear the report outside, but—

"You are not permitted to leave the room, Marcos. I will not allow it."

Before he could even form the words, Priscilla, seated in her sumptuous chair, blocked his path. Ignorant of—or indifferent to—Marcos's intentions, she wore a sadistic expression that seemed to say she was doing this simply because it amused her.

"I will forgive the discourtesy of disrupting this gathering. But I shan't feel settled until I hear the cause of it. So let us hear the report—spoken loudly enough for everyone here to hear."

"With respect, Priscilla-sama, there is a great deal that need not reach everyone's ears. This is likely to be one such matter—"

"A mere old bag of bones who snuck into the castle managed to lend a spineless little girl the spirit to put on a brave face. Given that, perhaps it's not so trivial a matter after all, hm?"

Covering her mouth with her fan, Priscilla shot a sidelong glance across the room toward Felt. Having been mocked, Felt twisted her lips into a scowl.

"Cut it out with the weird accusations, you smug cow. Old Man Rom was s'posed to come with me from the start, we just got split up in the castle. Some soldier went and found him and brought him to the hall. That's all there is to it, right?"

Arms crossed, turning her face away from Priscilla, Felt looked up at the bald giant standing beside her—Old Man Rom—and asked, "Right?" seeking his agreement.

The old man, eyes closed and his expression that of one enduring pain, answered her request with a weak "...Aye, that's so," and Felt, a faint trace of concern kindling in her crimson eyes, refused to say anything more, cutting off Priscilla's sardonic pursuit right there.

"Princess, princess. This has only just gotten started, so could you maybe not go making enemies quite so fast? I'm already short an arm as it is, so I gotta work twice as hard as anybody else just to break even, poor unfortunate me."

"Hmph. Very well, then."

Priscilla, who had still been of a mind to keep tormenting Felt, reluctantly relented at her retainer Al's suggestion. Then she turned once more to face Marcos.

"But my inquiry into your matter is not finished. Give your report loudly enough for the rest of us to hear as well. I permit it. No—I permit nothing else."

"...Captain."

"There's no help for it. Follow the instruction."

Faced with Priscilla's imperious command, the guard sought Marcos's judgment, but Marcos's answer, his voice tinged with reluctance, was to accept the demand.

Given orders from both his superior and someone who might well shoulder the nation's future, the guard hadn't the nerve to refuse either instruction.

His expression tensed, his eyes wavering with confusion and impatience, he straightened his posture.

"Reporting. After the conclusion of the discussion in the hall, Sir Julius requested use of the training grounds. Having been granted permission, he is currently at the training grounds with—"

In the middle of his report, the guard's gaze flicked toward a corner of the room—toward Emilia, who stood there looking rather out of place.

Catching that glance, Emilia blinked in surprise, feeling as though she'd abruptly been drawn into the conversation.

Before her surprise could turn into a question and take clear shape in words, the guard delivered the actual information.

"—Emilia-sama's attendant, Natsuki Subaru-dono, is currently engaged in a mock battle with wooden swords."

"...Huh."

Standing bolt upright, the guard laid bare exactly what he had just witnessed with his own eyes.

Hearing this, it was Al who put a hand to his chin and let out a breath of admiration. The others, too, betrayed emotions beyond mere confusion in their eyes or expressions, to greater or lesser degrees.

Amid all that, Emilia was the one who plainly had no room for any emotion but bewilderment.

"...Eh?"

Unable to grasp the meaning of what she'd just been told, Emilia let out a breathy, deflated sound, blinking her large violet eyes as her thoughts ground to a halt.

Calmly, steadily, she tried to sort through what had been reported, one piece at a time.

In other words, this: at a place called the training grounds, using wooden swords, Julius and Subaru were, for some reason, dueling each other. She'd sorted it out, and still it made no sense.

"W-why would something like that happen...!?"

Understanding refusing to surface, Emilia simply voiced the doubt that had risen in her.

Stepping forward, she approached the guard standing on the other side of the table.

"The training grounds—that's inside the Knights' Garrison, next to the castle, isn't it? Subaru and Julius are... fighting, there?"

"Begging your pardon, but it is a mock battle. If it were thought of as a 'fight,' as though it began out of personal grudge, that would be a matter of Sir Julius's honor."

To Emilia's trembling voice, the guard offered a clear correction—on that one point, at least, he would not yield.

Even as his words reached her ears, Emilia had no room to concern herself with such a trivial distinction. The collision between Subaru and Julius had shaken her that badly.

Emilia regretted that, having been too consumed with her own troubles, she hadn't properly watched what became of Subaru after he'd effectively been sent out of the hall.

Subaru, who in front of everyone had ended up humiliating himself in the heat of the moment. She couldn't deny that some part of her had felt a flicker of relief when he, head hanging, was led out of the hall.

Wasn't the bill for such a selfish thought coming due right now, in this very moment?

To begin with, Subaru surely hadn't held a very favorable impression of Julius.

It had been Julius who, in the hall, threw cold water on Subaru's one-man stage by confronting him with reality, and even during the incident at the guard station in the royal capital yesterday, there had been signs that Subaru resented the way Julius carried himself as a noble to the very core.

Subaru still hadn't told her clearly where he'd come from, but from the way he treated Julius and the like, Emilia had a vague notion that he might have some reason to bear hostility toward so-called upper-class people.

The fact that no such effect showed toward Roswaal was probably in part because Roswaal himself was such an unusual case among the nobility.

There was nothing to rule out the possibility that a problem which had never surfaced before, and which had therefore been left unaddressed, had become the spark for the current situation.

Once thoughts began rolling in a bad direction, all that remained was to tumble straight to the bottom.

Following that tendency, Emilia's imagination too spiraled downward, and clear worry rose on her lovely features.

"Anyway, I have to go stop this right away. Please take me to the training grounds—"

"Ahh, I dunno 'bout that."

Emilia, thinking to hurry to the scene and mediate between the two, had her words cut off this time by a girl with a distinctive way of speaking.

Ignoring Emilia, who scowled at having her momentum cut short, Anastasia, her long blue hair swaying, pointed a finger at the guard, who still stood at attention, and prefaced with, "There's just somethin' I wanna confirm—"

"—this 'mock battle,' d'ya happen to know which of 'em proposed it?"

"I have been told it was Sir Julius. As a result of Natsuki Subaru-dono accepting the challenge, the current situation at the training grounds—"

"Ah, that's fine, that's fine, no need to make excuses. If you're sayin' it was Julius who proposed it, that's all I needed to hear."

No matter how it was dressed up, it was a situation liable to be judged a private feud. The guard had tried, at least a little, to keep it from reflecting badly on Julius, but Anastasia waved that effort away with a gesture. That said, she wasn't disregarding the guard's intent entirely—

"—if the mock battle was Julius's proposal, then I'm against stoppin' it."

With that, Anastasia showed herself ready to affirm her own retainer's judgment, standing squarely opposed to Emilia, who was moving to put a stop to things.

Emilia caught her breath, and to Anastasia's small shrug, she managed to ask, "Why—"

"Subaru and Julius are... I mean, your knight and my friend are fighting each other. Doesn't that worry you at all?"

"Worry? 'Bout what? That Julius'll go too far and I'll end up havin' to pay for your boy's medical bills?"

At Anastasia's answer, tilting her head as though genuinely puzzled, Emilia was left speechless.

But as if mocking her astonishment, Priscilla, an actual sneer rising to her lips, spoke up.

"Indeed. From what I saw of him, that's a fool with the face of one who doesn't know when to quit. By now, having stubbornly overreached, his already unremarkable countenance may well have become something one cannot bear to look at twice."

"True enough. That nerve he showed spoutin' off in the hall was somethin' to admire, but he seemed like the type so light he'd go flyin' off just from bein' admired."

Anastasia followed Priscilla's sarcasm, and the two exchanged spiteful smiles.

At the two women's attitude, Emilia pressed a hand to her forehead, unease welling in her violet eyes.

"Y-you two... don't you have anything else to say? Anything more?"

"Anything to discuss... oh, how 'bout we make a wager? That Natsuki Subaru boy, was it? Let's bet on how long he can keep tradin' blows with Julius."

"Ordinarily one wagers on who wins and loses, I would think."

"That wouldn't be much of a bet. Though if you're fine with me, the bookie, takin' the whole pot, that works too."

Anastasia formed a circle with her fingers and flashed a sly grin, and Priscilla, with a look of boredom, dismissed the whole conversation with a flat "Not worth discussing."

Emilia was struck speechless by the two women's consistent commitment to mere 'spectating.' By her own way of thinking, their reasoning was, from its very premise, impossible to accept.

But as she caught her breath, at a loss for words, another voice pressed in on her, all the more insistent.

"If we're questioning the propriety of the mock battle, I too find it unwise to stop it partway through."

Arms crossed, even Crusch, who had been watching quietly until now, held Emilia back from going to mediate, with an opinion that ran contrary to her own.

"Why—" Emilia's eyes clouded at Crusch, of all people, saying such a thing—Crusch, whom she'd thought, compared to the other two, more likely to render a judgment grounded in common sense. To that wordless question, Crusch closed her eyes.

"If it were Emilia-dono's attendant who had proposed the duel, it would be right for Emilia-dono to offer to mediate. But since it was Sir Julius who proposed it, and it was your attendant who accepted, it would be improper for you to step in and stop it."

"Why? I mean, Subaru is my—"

"If you cannot see that on your own, no amount of explaining will make you see it. —And besides, hasty as it may be, it is necessary."

Cut off by that firm tone, Emilia could press Crusch no further. Crusch, for her part, pressed her lips shut, as though she had nothing more to say to Emilia.

She hadn't exactly been talked down, but with the other three, save herself, united in their intent to merely 'watch,' Emilia had no choice but to consider what reasoning lay behind their judgment. To open her mouth without thinking would only mean being written off by them as someone not standing on the same stage.

Her lips trembling, her complexion somewhat drained of color, Emilia sank into thought. Watching this whole exchange, herself included, from the sidelines, Felt murmured in a low voice to Old Man Rom beside her,

"I got no clue what's goin' on here. So, Old Man Rom, you get what it means?"

"I don't know what that boy got up to before I arrived in the hall. So all I can do is guess, based on what I've heard just now."

"What d'you think that guy did, then?"

At Felt's question, Rom put a hand to his chin and, murmuring "Let's see," fell briefly silent in thought.

"Recklessly clownin' about and carryin' on in front of everyone, mouthin' off, tit for tat, till he'd earned scowls all 'round, and finally, unable to talk his way back out, gettin' ordered from the room with his tail 'tween his legs—that's the sort of feel I get off it."

"Whoa, Old Man Rom. You sure you weren't watchin'?"

Felt praised Rom's guess, which had landed nearly dead-on. In response, Rom, touching his bald head, knit his brow. "I only offered up somethin' a touch worse than my worst guess..." Then the old man nodded as though something had clicked into place.

"If it's as I imagine, I see... this knight everyone's talkin' about must be someone thoroughly hardened in noble pride, and unpleasant with it. Or else..."

Rom was about to offer up the other possibility. But, betraying Felt's eager anticipation of what came next, someone made a sound that swept the mood of the room clean away.

A hard, sharp stamping sound cracked dryly against the conference room floor, jolting the hearts of everyone in the room, over which stillness had been hanging.

Surprise, or a faint hint of displeasure—such gazes fell upon the culprit who'd made the noise. In response to those looks, he gave a shrug and flicked the rim of his metal helm with his one arm to make it ring—Al.

"My bad, my bad. See, I only got the one arm, so even if I wanna get everybody's attention, I can't exactly clap. So I stomp instead."

"Sooorry, sorry," Al said, utterly unrepentant, offering a chopping-hand gesture of apology before spinning around on the spot, stepping forward, and jabbing a finger straight ahead.

"Now, now, now, this whole conversation's driftin' off-center bit by bit, y'all notice? Two guys who don't like each other endin' up in a brawl, that happens sometimes, y'know. Titles and standing and all that's complicated, sure, but before knight or self-proclaimed knight or whatever, they're both just guys."

"I don't think it's that simple a matter..."

"You're makin' a simple thing way too complicated, missy. And what really matters here ain't that they're duking it out—it's why he brought this hush-hush little story in here in the first place."

Wagging a finger tsk-tsk-tsk, Al answered Emilia, then once more singled out the one he'd pointed at—namely, the young guard who had brought the report into the room.

"If they're just goin' at it, a report after the fact woulda been fine. So how come you came runnin' in here to fetch the Captain, lookin' all panicked like that?"

At the question Al posed, the guard's complexion visibly worsened even to onlookers. Uncertain how to answer, his gaze wandered—and met Priscilla's, smiling alluringly beside Al.

Her lips split wide in an expression of mad delight, cruelty mingled into an angelically lovely smile.

Finally, the guard turned pleading eyes to Marcos for salvation, but Marcos's only answer to that plea was a merciless shake of the head.

"The reason I came to fetch the Captain was, um..."

"C'mon, clear and loud and enunciate, please."

"The mock battle between Sir Julius and Natsuki Subaru-dono... has become so one-sided that I came to seek instructions!"

At Al's mean-spirited demand, the guard straightened up and answered in a half-desperate voice. Hearing this, Marcos, unusually, let his expression shift to one of puzzlement.

"...One-sided, in what way?"

"I believe Sir Julius is holding back, but... it's, well, to the point where it's become hard to watch."

Looking reluctant to say it, the guard glanced toward Emilia, unwittingly conjuring for everyone present the gruesome scene he had just witnessed with his own eyes.

At the bitterness of his manner, Emilia realized, belatedly, that things had grown worse than she'd imagined, and she cast aside every last hesitation.

"I have to stop this...!"

Letting slip a murmur colored with impatience, Emilia darted past the guard stationed by the door and dashed straight out of the room, racing down the corridor toward the Knights' Garrison—toward the training grounds in question.

"Please wait, Emilia-sama! Hey, go after Emilia-sama!"

"—Y-yes, sir!"

Sending his subordinate after the fled Emilia, the guard too raced out after her down the corridor.

As a result, Marcos turned back to the people left behind in the room.

"My apologies for the disturbance. I shall see to settling the matter at once—"

"Nah, it's fine. More to the point, let's chase after missy and go watch the mock battle ourselves, yeah?"

Stopping Marcos as he moved to bow, Al declared this with easy nonchalance, then shrugged toward Priscilla beside him, saying "Right?" as though seeking her agreement.

"You like this kinda thing too, don't ya, Princess? Watchin' some weak little critter get toyed with by a wild beast."

"Do not misjudge me with your presumptuous imaginings, Al. ...Though, well, I do love it."

Affirming her retainer's words with that qualification, Priscilla rose from the chair she'd been leaning her weight into, arching her back lightly so that her ample bosom swayed.

"Very well. This tedious talk had been dragging on a bit long, and I was feeling rather cooped up. Looking down upon some common fool's disgrace and having a laugh at it isn't such a bad idea."

With a snap, Priscilla closed her fan and pointed its tip toward Marcos, standing by the door.

And, maintaining her ever-unchanging arrogance, she said:

"Lead us to this training ground of yours. —That is my command."

* * *

Blood dripping from the shallow gash on his forehead ran into his one good eye, staining his vision red; Subaru wiped it away roughly and spat out his irritation.

He had long since lost count of how many times he'd been struck down and sent rolling across the ground.

His swollen left eye was completely shut, and whether it was his lip that had split or the inside of his mouth, the taste of blood was too thick for him to tell anymore.

The pain, he didn't feel strongly.

It depended on the degree of the injury, but when wounds grew too severe, the brain would sometimes refuse to accept them, and the body would fail to register the pain—a phenomenon that occurred often enough in human beings.

In fact, ever since being summoned to this world, Subaru had grown deeply familiar with life-threatening wounds. Speaking as someone who had, in truth, suffered mortal injuries more than once, he could say for certain: there really was such a thing as pain too great to feel as pain.

It was a phenomenon he could just as well explain away by trotting out modern knowledge—an excess of adrenaline secreted in the brain, or some such—but if he were to describe his present state in cruder, more primal terms, what was making Subaru forget his pain was a purely and simply undiluted feeling of *rage*.

"—Shit on shit, drenched-in-shit, cesspool of shit."

Spitting out the foul words, Subaru found himself unable to master the oppressive emotions rampant in his chest.

It was rage at Julius, standing ready before him with that same cool, composed face; rage at his own worthlessness, unable even to land a single blow against such an opponent; and rage born of a childish tantrum against a situation that refused to bend to his will.

It was rage. Nothing but rage. Rage alone was everything Subaru had left.

Even now, having just been struck full in the stomach by the wooden sword and having sprayed vomit across the ground, Subaru wearily hauled himself up from where he'd sat crumpled.

His knees shook, his breath came ragged, and his vision was hazed red with the blood he hadn't managed to wipe away.

And yet, still driven by an inexhaustible rage, Subaru would not stop facing an opponent he had no hope of beating.

"Isn't it about time you admitted your own limits?"

To Subaru's deranged tenacity, Julius responded not with admiration but with exasperation.

He remained, as ever, unmarred by dust, without a single drop of sweat on his handsome, hardened features, merely letting the tip of the wooden sword—still bearing the traces of how relentlessly he'd struck Subaru—waver in the air.

"You should have felt, in your very body, the unbridgeable gap between you and me. And you should understand, by now, just what sort of thing 'knighthood'—the thing you insulted—truly is, and how far that gap runs."

His words weren't meant to appeal to Subaru's heart—they were meant to break it.

That was only natural. Some development where the two of them came to recognize each other's worth by the end of the duel couldn't happen unless something had genuinely stirred between them.

Julius was merely continuing to strike Subaru down to demonstrate what it meant to be a knight, and Subaru was merely demonstrating his powerless resistance to the reality Julius thrust at him by continuing to stand back up.

There was no room for anything to be born from that. Even after trading blows for this long, nothing was being born between the two of them.

"Any further, and I think it may become a matter of your life, don't you?"

"Ahh?"

Perhaps trying a different tack for breaking his spirit, Julius shifted the direction of his provocation. But Subaru answered him with unmistakable rage riding his tongue.

"Like hell I'm gonna die from somethin' like this. Don't act like you know."

"You speak as though you had experience with it."

"I'm the man who knows that better than anyone else in this whole world."

Not as a bluff, not as a joke, Subaru said it in earnest.

Seven times, in total—that was how many times, since coming to this world, Subaru had had his life trampled underfoot.

Between his former world and this one—no, across the whole span of three thousand worlds, wherever one looked—there was no being who had confronted his own death as much as Subaru had by now.

That feeling told him something.

It hurt enough to die, it was agonizing enough to die, it was maddening enough to die, it was death upon death upon death—but a human being didn't die from something like this.

"I don't understand you."

To Subaru's declaration, spat out as though coughing blood, Julius shook his head and let out a breath.

He cast a sidelong glance at Subaru's unsteady form and said,

"Why cling to your pride so stubbornly? You must already understand full well that you cannot match me. No—I know you're already aware that you cannot win against me."

"...What're you—"

"We don't need pointless exchanges. Your aim is no longer victory. No, that's not quite right—I should say that what you and I each consider 'victory' differs."

Keeping Subaru, now fallen silent, in view, Julius swept his arm as though giving a speech.

Lifting his chin slightly, pitching his voice, it seemed, not only to Subaru but somewhat to those around them as well, he said,

"Your victory condition isn't to defeat me—it's 'to land one blow on me,' isn't it? Whether that means so much as landing your sword on me once, or actually handing me a single defeat, I couldn't say."

"—!"

Biting down on his lip, Subaru desperately suppressed a scream that threatened to tear loose from the back of his throat.

It was dead on target.

There was nothing but the shame, plain and true, of having a sore spot poked at.

Humiliation, shame, dyed themselves across Subaru's chest, and though his trembling lips tried to spin some excuse, he barely managed, through sheer desperate pride, to hold that much back.

"Hmm, I thought some unbearable excuse might come tumbling out... but it seems you had at least that much sense. Although—"

So saying, watching Subaru wring out the very last dregs of his pride, Julius continued,

"—having misjudged your opponent's skill from the start, and then even lowering the standard you set for yourself in the middle of the fight, going so far to debase your own worth—I can only call it pride you don't deserve to have."

"—You bastard!!"

It was a gesture of exasperation from Julius that had already, by this point in the match, been repeated often enough to become familiar.

Seeing it, Subaru's mind flooded entirely with rage, and combined with the frustration of having his inner thoughts read aloud, it made him explode.

Ignoring the pain of every blow that had rained down on his body, ignoring his hazed, red-tinged vision, ignoring all of it, he lunged with everything he had, swinging his raised wooden sword at that composed face—

"Not very beautiful, is it."

An instant before the blow could fall, a thrust lanced out, piercing the wrist of the right hand with which Subaru gripped his sword.

So sharp was the blow that the wooden sword was knocked clean from his grasping hand, and the instant his eyes reflexively followed it—a second strike sank into his solar plexus, and Subaru's body was sent crashing down as lightly as anything.

His breath caught; unable even to break his fall, he rolled across the dirt, tumbling head over heels some five times before landing flat on his back, spread-eagled on the ground.

Subaru, retching up literal bloody vomit—right beside his face, the wooden sword that had been knocked away came spinning down and planted itself in the earth, as if mocking its owner's pathetic, headlong charge.

"Just admit it already."

Subaru coughed painfully, struggling to spit out blood and grit. Looking down at him, Julius made once more the call for surrender he'd already repeated so many times.

"You cannot reach me. No matter how many times you reset your own private definition of victory over me and challenge it anew, the outcome is the same. —I will never let you attain it."

"—"

"I will shatter your spirit utterly, break it beyond repair, and make you regret your own conduct. I will make you reflect on your own words and actions, ignorant as they were of your place. And upon that, I will make you decide—"

"—"

"—to withdraw of your own will from Emilia-sama's side—!"

"—!?"

Gasping, breathing in thin, ragged draws, Subaru hauled his upper body up with a sudden, furious force at those words from Julius.

His battered chest screamed with pain, agony wringing a shriek from every part of his body, but he had no time to spare for that. He twisted his body, bared his teeth, and said—

"Don't... screw with me... what right d'you have... to say somethin' like that...!"

"Naturally, I have no authority over what becomes of you—no power to decide your standing. So I have no right to interfere in whether you stay or go. That's why I merely say this."

Looking straight at Subaru, Julius paused for the space of a single breath, then said,

"—that you should, of your own accord, leave Emilia-sama's side."

To the speechless Subaru, Julius prefaced, as though explaining something, with "Listen carefully."

"Your conduct, your words—they do nothing but knowingly lead Emilia-sama into hardship. Watching the exchange earlier in the hall, and your bearing here in this place, I've become certain of it."

"Just what... are you..."

"You are selfish, self-centered, and above all, every thought you have is shallow, immature, and far too naive."

Counting off on his fingers, Julius went on shattering, one by one, Subaru's every careless notion.

"To Emilia-sama, you are nothing but a nuisance who forces a burden upon her. There is not a single thing you can do for her sake, nor will there ever be. Until now, you may have clung to her kindness and been permitted to remain at her side."

Subaru had tasted helplessness time and again.

Ever since coming to this world, Subaru had, again and again, ground his teeth against the frustration of an unalterable reality born of his own insufficient strength, and struggled on regardless.

"But from here on, she cannot afford to keep at her side someone who merely leans on her. From now, the path she walks is the path to the throne. The obstacles blocking that road are high and many, and it's easy to imagine every manner of hardship befalling her."

And yet, until now, Subaru had overcome every calamity that had befallen him.

In moments where his own strength fell short, he had borrowed the strength of those around him, or made use of the one advantage he alone possessed—knowledge of the future—to rearrange the situation to his favor.

"Whoever walks that road alongside her should be someone possessed of both the resolve and the strength to overcome that hardship together. Someone able to push, with real strength, at the back of a woman striving for such distant heights."

And every bit of it had begun from feelings for a single girl.

However agonizing, however painful, however sad, however frustrating, however lacking he'd been, however far short he'd fallen, however much it had hurt—the reason Subaru had made it this far without breaking was that feeling for a single girl.

And that—

"—must never, under any circumstances, be something that leans on and clings to her kindness, holding her back."

That—this man—

"You should not be at her—at Emilia-sama's—side."

Was this man trying to steal from him even the very meaning of his existence?

"RRRRRRRAAAAAAAAAHHHH—!!"

His emotions exploding, Subaru sprang to his feet as if launched upward, wrenched the planted wooden sword free, and, putting the momentum of a full body-turn behind it, aimed a blow with the sword at Julius's upper body.

The tip cut through the air; the blow, laden with centrifugal force, would easily shatter bone if it connected.

It was, in the truest sense, an all-or-nothing final blow, packed full of the pent-up fury accumulated through the whole exchange thus far—a strike meant to crush Julius's face flat—

—and the sound of his own struck wrist bone shattering rang, high and clear, through what felt to Subaru like an utterly soundless world.

His right arm, gripping the whirling wooden sword, met the tip of the wooden blade waiting to intercept it—and at the edge of his vision, he saw it crumple and snap, mangled beyond recognition.

His wrist now faced entirely the wrong way, a jutting bone having torn through the flesh to bare its white tip; within seconds, agonizing pain and bleeding would surely follow.

Calmly registering all this, Subaru's consciousness followed the slowly moving figure of Julius.

Slowly, in a world without sound, it was strange that only Julius seemed able to move.

Caught in the sensation of the world slowing down under conditions desperate enough to border on death, Subaru was thoroughly disgusted with his own idiocy for arriving at such a wildly out-of-place thought.

Before his eyes, Julius's movement as he prepared to deliver the finishing blow to the utterly disgraced Subaru—every bit of that fluid, graceful motion—brought Subaru a sensation of piercing, vivid poignancy.

"—Let me simply pity you."

The blow of the wooden sword, once loosed, drove countless impacts into every part of Subaru's body within a single instant.

His arm was broken. Several ribs were snapped as well—it was doubtful whether any part of them remained uncracked. The forehead that had merely been gashed before was now split wide open; the wooden sword's tip ravaged the inside of his mouth once more, scraping away several ofhis adult teeth. A single blow to his windpipe carved a mark impossible not to mention, and as a finishing touch, a swing—not a thrust—slammed directly into his stomach, producing a dull, wet sound and the sensation of something ruptured inside him.

He went spinning through the air, tumbling so wildly he lost all sense of up and down, spraying a tremendous quantity of blood, and the reddish-brown earth of the training grounds darkened further toward vermilion with the blood Subaru shed.

At a result so brutally violent, no one—Julius least of all—could raise their voice.

The training grounds remained as crowded as ever with the knights and guards who had gathered to watch Julius's public thrashing of Subaru. And yet, every single one of those onlookers had shut his mouth, keeping silent as he watched Subaru roll across the ground like a discarded rag.

Their bodies had been gripped by excitement, too—but only through the first few exchanges of blows.

An insolent upstart who had mocked the very rank of knighthood, insulted the Royal Selection itself that would decide the kingdom's future—being punished by Julius, foremost of the Royal Guard, made to taste his own conduct in his own pain, and offering an apology—that was the spectacle those gathered here had expected.

Indeed, for the first ten minutes or so after it began, they too had raised cheers, or worn sneers as they looked down upon Subaru's disgrace, and had lavished unstinting praise upon their fellow knight Julius.

But that scene had begun to change entirely, no doubt, the moment everyone realized this "public lynching" could, in the literal sense, be nothing else but a genuine public lynching.

An utterly insurmountable gap in skill lay between the two of them, Subaru and Julius.

Compared to Julius, who ranked among the knighthood's elite even in swordsmanship, Subaru's skill with a blade couldn't, even generously, be said to have risen above amateur level. And since Subaru possessed no special ability to compensate for that, his way of fighting amounted to little more than child's play by someone merely somewhat used to handling a long weapon.

His attacks parried, the gaps in his clumsy defense punished in turn, Subaru was struck down onto the ground again and again.

For the first few rounds, sneering laughter had dominated. Past ten, sighs of exasperation began to pile up. And by the time people had grown sick of even counting anymore—

"This is too much."
"Why doesn't that guy just give up already?"
"That's a stubbornness that's both worthless and dull. Damn it, forcing us to watch something this unpleasant."

Cursing foully, they still buried their faces in their palms at the sight of Subaru rising to his feet yet again.

This was what it meant to find something unbearable to watch. So weak, so pathetic—the disgraceful sight sharply provoked a sense of "shame" in those who watched it.

Better to simply stop it. Victory and defeat were already plain to every eye present; everyone had had the superiority of "knighthood" itself reaffirmed. Beyond this point, the fight was meaningless.

And yet Julius never once showed mercy with the wooden sword that kept striking Subaru down.

Ferris, who as referee held the right to call a halt to the fight, showed no sign of stopping it, no matter how badly Subaru was hurt.

And Subaru himself, brushing off the knights' pleas, kept rising back to his feet regardless.

What possible meaning could there be in a fight, a struggle, like this?

Wordless, merely looking down at the pathetic sight of Subaru being beaten down again and again, every spectator searched for some meaning in the scene below.

Everyone understood. There was no meaning, no significance, in this struggle.

There was only, purely and simply, a stubbornness that was pathetic, unsightly, and worthless.

And so, at the very least, they had to see through to the end—to witness what became of that stubbornness.

That the knights and guards gathered there, faced with a sight from which they wanted to avert their eyes, still refused to leave—that was because of the responsibility they bore, however involved only as spectators, for this event unfolding before them.

Blood from the shattered wrist, and the immense quantity of vomited blood, stained the earth of the training grounds red.

Subaru's small, trembling body lay face down, its once-mad fighting spirit now deflated, having strayed completely beyond any state in which he could be said to still be capable of fighting.

This time, surely, it's over—everyone thought so.

At the sight of Subaru, beaten down utterly and completely, everyone sensed the end of this one-sided brutality. And yet Julius's stance, his blood-soaked wooden sword still readied, did not relax.

Looking at Subaru, whose consciousness was surely no longer even clear, he kept his combat stance without a flicker of wavering in his will to fight.

At that unshakable stance, the spectators surrounding the two of them read the knight's intent and caught their breath.

It wasn't over yet. There was a reason this fight could not be considered finished simply because one side had been struck down. And that reason was—

"—That's enough."

It was a voice that had not been raised even once throughout the whole tragedy that had unfolded at the training grounds.

Amid the spectators' cheers, and then their steadily mounting sighs. Amid Julius, dispassionately—or perhaps with a calm sort of passion—demonstrating what it meant to be a knight. Amid Ferris, presiding over the scene as referee, occasionally letting slip a sigh that could have been admiration or could have been gloom.

Just one person, a young man who had kept his silence the whole while, now stood at the center of the training grounds—between the fallen Subaru and the readied Julius.

The red-haired young man had wiped his handsome features clean of expression, his blue eyes, driven purely by righteous indignation, boring straight through Julius.

"If you intend to continue this any further, I will be your opponent instead."

He had only murmured a single line; he hadn't done anything out of the ordinary.

And yet, merely hearing that one line, chills ran through the entire bodies of knights who weren't even party to the fight. Startled by their own bodies' shudders, their hearts, lagging behind their flesh, began to ache as though frozen by the aftershock.

Reinhard, the "Sword Saint"—the strongest in the Dragon-blessed Kingdom of Lugunica, or rather, a being possessed of power without equal across the whole continent—his aura of pressure enveloped the entire training grounds and would not let go.

This was merely the aftershock, and already it was this bad. To imagine actually standing directly in the path of that pressure, receiving Reinhard's blue gaze head-on as Julius now was—one could only fear for his state of mind.

But just as the spectators had underestimated Reinhard, so too had they underestimated Julius, far too much.

Reinhard's fighting spirit was enough to make even knights who had steeled themselves to lay down their lives in battle tremble—yet exposed to it, Julius did not, even so, break from his composed bearing. Ferris, standing beside him, was the same, and the two of them thereby proved plainly, right here, that they too were a cut above the rest.

Ignoring the spectators' thoughts, Reinhard, having merely turned his gaze toward him, narrowed his eyes faintly at the unmoving Julius.

"I'll say it again, Julius. Anything beyond this is nothing but an act of violence. As a Royal Guard knight... no, before that, as one human being, I cannot let it pass unchecked."

"—"

Julius's brow, which had shown no expression until then, twitched faintly in response to those words.

Reinhard knit his brow suspiciously at that reaction, but Julius, keeping his stance as it was, gave a light wave of his free hand and said,

"Reinhard, I consider you a very good friend."

—words that only further bewildered Reinhard.

To Reinhard, deepening the crease between his brows in confusion, Julius shook his head slightly and continued.

"Your integrity as a knight stands out even among the Royal Guard. There is no doubting your loyalty to the kingdom, and your skill and effort in fulfilling it leave nothing to be desired. If asked what a hero truly is, I would answer, without hesitation, that it is my friend Reinhard van Astrea."

"...That's more praise than I deserve."

Not understanding what Julius was driving at, Reinhard declined the praise with an expression that carried less modesty than genuine conviction, and said,

"I too think of you as a good friend before I think of you as a colleague. That's exactly why I can't let you, of all people, continue to demean yourself with your own actions—"

"No matter who denies it, no matter if you yourself cannot believe it—you are a hero, Reinhard."

Julius cut off Reinhard again, just as he tried to continue his persuasion.

Though the content of his words was one of praise, the tone in which he spoke them carried nothing that could be called a pleasant feeling.

"You are a hero to the point of being too much a hero. The Divine Protection granted to you from birth. House Astrea, which expressed your very way of being in the most fitting form possible. And a soul so noble and pure it has no reason, no cause, to ever doubt itself—that is precisely why you are a hero."

"What are you trying to say, Julius?"

"You are a hero. —You can be nothing but a hero."

Shaking his head weakly, Julius told this to the bewildered Reinhard.

Then he lifted the wavering tip of his wooden sword and gestured to indicate the space behind Reinhard, saying,

"To someone who can only live as a hero, the meaning of this fight here surely can't be seen. Nor can the reason I won't lower my sword, and..."

Sensing a stirring presence behind him, Reinhard turned around, a faint flicker of surprise kindling in his eyes.

There—

"—nor the reason he's still, even now, continuing to stand."

There stood Subaru, moving with the slow, ghostly motions of a specter, still dragging his utterly battered body upright, rising to his feet.

* * *

Before his eyes, he could see Julius and Reinhard exchanging some words.

Having spectacularly expelled every impurity from his body, his own body felt, if anything, lighter for it.

The pain came in intermittent waves, his limbs remained as heavy as if stuffed with lead, and above all, the right hand he'd tried to press to the ground was wrecked to a pitiful, unbearable degree.

Even so, the lethargy that had threatened to steal away his will to rise had vanished, and his body, however sluggishly, had begun moving in accordance with his own will.

Unable to use his right hand, he dragged his elbow and shoulder along the ground and made use of his left arm—not unhurt, but still usable—to lift his body up. He poured pure defiance into his trembling lower half to fuel it, and, taking a great deal of time, somehow managed to stand.

And then, at the very last, on his way up off the ground, his left hand brushed a wooden sword lying nearby. He picked it up, used it like a cane to raise his upper body, and just barely managed to stand. He stood.

"—Subaru."

Looking up, he found, close enough that a breath might reach him, a handsome, red-haired face watching him.

In those eyes was a clear, plain concern, and at the same time, Subaru could tell a flame of righteous indignation smoldered there too.

Reinhard, worried about Subaru, had placed himself in front of him and stood facing off against Julius.

His mind, which should have been sluggish, arrived at that answer smoothly. The moment he reached that conclusion, a slow, crescent-moon smile spread across Subaru's mouth.

The inside of his mouth was cut to pieces, and with several teeth missing, the smile came with pain. But how could he not smile at this?

"There's no need to push yourself further. I'll avenge you. This is far, far too unjust a treatment. It's absolutely not something that can be allowed."

Even while conscious of Subaru, Reinhard's gaze was fixed on the Julius facing him.

Julius merely looked at Reinhard with quiet, sorrowful eyes.

What sort of exchange had passed between the two of them, Subaru had no idea. He didn't know—but there was one thing he did understand.

"Subaru, in any case, leave this to me—"

"Reinhard."

Calling out the name of the young man standing before him, Subaru fixed his one remaining eye clearly on those eyes reflecting the blue sky.

This was the third time now that he'd been shielded like this, with Reinhard standing in front of him.

The first time, when he'd pathetically cried out for help; the second, when his life had nearly been saved by him; and now, this time.

By the third time, a certain optimism took root—that if he simply left it to Reinhard, things would work themselves out somehow. The relationship of trust, repeated over and over, between protector and protected—that bond, which certainly existed within Subaru—was, once again, offering him the best possible way through this moment.

All he had to do was take the offered hand and entrust everything to that strong back.

If he did, every bit of the pain Subaru had suffered, every bit of his mental anguish, would surely be resolved in the best possible way by the young man before him.

Hooray, all around. A grand finale. He could see it—a development where everyone was happy, a mammoth-sized happy ending.

So—

"—Get out of the way."

"...Eh?"

"...I get it, that you're a super-good guy, that there's no malice in this... no bad intent at all, that this whole, entire action of yours sprang from goodwill, pure-cultured, not a single impurity mixed in. ...That much, I get."

But—

"...that alone, I can't allow. This, here—I can't give this up... to anyone."

This was something Subaru had started. A fight Subaru himself had taken up.

There was a stubbornness only Subaru could push through, and an ending that could only be reached by Subaru himself.

It was Subaru's duty, and his alone, to see this through to its end.

He couldn't abandon that duty, pass the buck to someone else, and let himself sink into the ending that way.

To put it more simply, more plainly, in just a single word—

"A guy's gotta have his pride—"

With bloodshot eyes, with lips dripping blood, his whole face stained red with bleeding, Subaru, feeling as though he were coughing up blood with every word, simply, stubbornly clung to a pride that was beyond all hope of saving.

At an answer he hadn't expected in the least, Reinhard was struck speechless, blinking again and again, unable to hide the shock within him.

"Pride, you say? For... for something like that..."

"—El Dorna."

The trembling denial never had the chance to be fully spoken before the mana in the air responded to the incantation, faster still.

* * *

The mana filling the air responded to the incantation transforming it, and, following the caster's will, its effect activated.

"—!"

Suddenly, the ground beneath Reinhard's feet burst, and a clod of earth, its sharp tip peeking through the crack, surged upward. It thrust up from his feet toward Reinhard's torso, aiming for it, closing in to strike his body with tremendous force—

"—Hah!"

Against the earthen upheaval, comparable to the charge of some colossal beast, Reinhard's stomping step exploded into action—the shockwave rippled outward, shaking the air, giving even Subaru, standing nearby, the illusion of being struck by a sudden gale.

Before Subaru's eyes, wide with astonishment, the earth was crushed flat beneath a stomping sole before it could reach full eruption, its purpose never once fulfilled, returned to its ordinary state.

Having thus countered the art of magic through sheer physical force, Reinhard leapt lightly backward to soften the impact of the collision, and upon landing, brushed off the hem of his clothes, returning at once to his usual composed self, as though nothing at all had just happened.

Then he turned a gaze grown suddenly more serious toward somewhere diagonally above—toward a figure leaping through the air toward them—and his expression hardened.

"What do you think you're doing?"

"What am I doin', huh."

Speaking as he landed in a cloud of dust was the man in the jet-black helmet—Al.

Resting his one arm on the hilt of the greatsword at his hip, he cracked his neck loudly, letting out a lazy groan of "Uahh—"

"Same as you, buttin' in, pretty boy."

With a ring of steel, he drew the greatsword, its dully gleaming tip pointed at Reinhard.

The well-worn dao, carrying the strong sense of long use, was probably a fine weapon for Al despite his single arm, thick and muscular as it was. Even so, it seemed unlikely to pose any threat to Reinhard.

In fact, Al himself surely knew as much.

"You can't beat me. I don't think you're so inexperienced you can't see the gap between our abilities."

"Normally I wouldn't go challengin' someone I can't beat, y'know. 'When in doubt, don't'—that's my motto. ...But after seein' what just happened, I can't exactly stick to that."

Al let the tip of his dao sway idly back and forth, gesturing toward each of the parties present in turn.

"Read the room, pretty boy. Can't you see this whole flat, dead atmosphere?"

"Flat...?"

Told this, Reinhard, still looking puzzled, glanced around the surroundings. And at last, belatedly, he noticed.

In the eyes of the knights and guards watching—several dozen spectators—there was excitement, and the more rational feeling that came from that excitement having cooled, and beyond that, a faint, indistinct wave of some other emotion rising as well.

"Guess you really don't get it. I see, so knights are the type to—"

Al began to say, then cut himself off partway.

He glanced around, taking in the sight of Julius, Ferris, and the other knights watching the situation, then shook his head.

"Nah, that's not the right category. Ah, it's heroes that are the bad type."

"What are you trying to say?"

"Nothin' I'm tryin' to say. Ain't there just no topic between you and me that'd produce anything worth exchangin'? After all, our values are different."

Shrugging, Al declared, unilaterally, a break in the conversation to Reinhard's question—all while adopting the contradictory stance of reining in his fighting spirit without once dropping his combat posture.

Paying no mind to the suspicious Reinhard, he looked instead toward Julius and Subaru, who, now that the young man had moved, once again stood facing each other.

"Alright, go ahead and continue. I'll hold down this room-readin'-challenged superhuman over here."

"I thought you and he were already acquainted?"

To Al, whose intentions remained unclear, Julius raised an eyebrow faintly and, with a tilt of his head, indicated Subaru—battered from head to toe, unable to muster any decent reaction since the moment Al had appeared. Al, seeing this, cleverly scratched the back of his own neck with the tip of his dao and said,

"In a way, more than friends, forever less than lovers, I guess you could say."

"Then couldn't there also be the option of using that friendship to somehow resolve this situation?"

"I think what I'm doin' right now is about as friendship-savvy a call as they come, honestly. See, no matter how bad the heart and body get beat up, they heal eventually. What I'm savin' him from is somethin' gettin' hurt that don't ever heal."

"I see."

At Al's muffled reply, Julius drew his chin in as if in understanding, and said,

"You have my thanks."

"Don't need it. Trade in that gratitude for cold hard cash instead."

Keeping Reinhard in check, Al answered Julius curtly.

At that, Julius's attention had already left the two of them, who were, after all, outside parties.

"Sorry to keep you waiting."

"That whole... window when you let your guard down... I coulda killed you ten times over, for real..."

To that brief apology, Subaru, battered all over but with his mouth still running, answered.

As Julius readied his wooden sword once more, Subaru abandoned his broken, shattered right hand, switched his sword to his one good left hand, and re-gripped the hilt tightly, wringing it.

Seeing this, Julius quietly grew certain of something.

—That the next clash would be the final exchange of this pointless struggle.

* * *

—The next blow will probably be the last one, Subaru concluded inwardly.

He could feel his body's limits. To begin with, it was already a wonder he was even standing, given the severity of his injuries. He was keeping his knees from buckling on nothing but sheer, grubby willpower, but how much longer could even that hold out?

If he was struck, that levee held up by willpower alone would collapse without a fight, sweeping away his consciousness, his pride, everything.

Conversely, even if he managed to land a blow, there was nothing left inside him to carry it any further. Either way, the muddy torrent of agony and exhaustion would end up sinking him all the same.

So why keep challenging him, then?

If the result waiting at the end of the road was the same regardless, why press on and challenge him still?

No answer presented itself. Even now, all that drove Subaru onward was a feeling of rage, one not unlike simply lashing out at whoever was nearest.

He didn't like the man in front of him, watching him with that same composed face. He'd challenged him again and again, meaning to break that nose of his—but the result had been every part of himself, aside from his own nose, getting broken and shattered instead.

So he decided. He decided, right now.

Why he kept challenging him: because he wanted to break that nose of his.

Land one hit—he'd land one hit on him, no matter what. As long as he could manage that much, he didn't care what else happened to him.

Just drawing breath sent pain through his lungs. Letting it out sent agony blazing through his mouth.

Clearing his mind with the pain, Subaru scraped together every scrap of strength left in his body and waited for his chance.

So as not to miss the instant Julius's attention wavered—that single sliver of a heaven-sent chance to break his nose, that one fragment of a moment.

Julius's gaze wavered, just for an instant.

The moment he saw it, Subaru's body had already lunged forward.

No sound reached him. Everything else was left behind.

With not even the leeway to cry out, Subaru raised the wooden sword clutched in his grip and closed in on Julius. The knight, his gaze having drifted from Subaru for just a moment, had not yet reacted to him.

Whatever it was that had drawn his attention—Subaru poured even the brain cells that might have wondered about that into this single blow.

"—!!"

He thought he heard a voice.

In a world without sound, in a world that should have left every sight behind, in a world that should have contained nothing but himself and the man he had to strike.

He heard a voice.

Someone's voice reached him. Someone's voice reached Subaru's ears.

"—ru!"

It became sound. Became a definite sound.

His consciousness felt as though it were being dragged away. Let everything, everything, be painted over and forgotten in blazing fury.

Right now, the sole reason for Subaru's existence was to bear down on the one thing before him.

"—baru!"

It began to sharpen. Began to take on meaning.

If he heard it clearly, there would be no taking it back.

So, as if to shake it all off, to flee from the overwhelming terror closing in right beside him, Subaru wrung out every last ounce of himself—and screamed.

"—Subaru!!"
"—Shamak!!"

Betraying the silver-bell voice he'd heard so clearly, he chanted it out at the top of his lungs instead.

A black cloud smothered the reddish-brown earth of the training grounds—

A world beyond comprehension unfolded. Racing through it, Subaru, within that world his understanding couldn't reach, did nothing but act exactly as his brain commanded.

His vision was not secured.

His brain no longer even registered a purpose.

His heart already saw nothing at all.

—He simply swung his left hand, his raised left arm, down toward whatever lay before him.

It was an act beyond comprehension, but his left arm had already begun that motion before being swallowed by the black cloud. And so, whether or not consciousness remained, the action would be carried out regardless.

What outcome the judgment and action of a Subaru who had lost sight of everything would lead to—Subaru, having sealed off the world of his own accord, could no longer know. Shouldn't have been able to know.

But—

"So this is your trump card, then—"

In a world where nothing should have been audible, that voice rang clear against his eardrums.

The next instant, taking the blade that closed in from beyond the clearing black cloud full on his body, Subaru was slammed down onto the earth, violently, without mercy.

There was surprise, not pain.

The black cloud, having erupted in such vast quantity, dispersed completely, and what spread across the sky was the same infuriatingly clear, unchanged blue as before.

It was only then that Subaru realized he was lying flat on his back, spread-eagled.

"Using a spell from the Shadow school—that was unexpected. I'll admit you caught me off guard."

The voice was cast down at him from above.

Subaru, his field of vision still occupied entirely by the sky, listened to it in a daze, too overwhelmed just trying to catch the reality pouring down over him.

"But your proficiency is far too low. Above all, a low-tier Shadow spell only works on an opponent beneath you in rank, or on some mindless beast. It would never have worked on me—nor, for that matter, on any single one of the Royal Guard."

Words of negation rained down.

He couldn't move. He didn't move.

His consciousness was perfectly clear, and yet his body no longer obeyed him at all.

"Even playing your trump card, this is the result. Surely you understand by now."

A voice tinged with pity was cast down at him.

Give up on all of it—that voice, beating down on Subaru's heart, kept pouring over him.

He'd thought he could change the situation.

He'd clung to whatever he could cling to, thrown out whatever he could throw out, and thought he could pull it off.

And yet—

"You are powerless, beyond saving. —Unworthy of her."

Wanting to deny only that one thing, Subaru moved just his creaking neck, shifting his view away from the sky. Somehow, some way, he tried to glare at the man standing there at the end of it—

"—"

—and instead met the violet eyes of a silver-haired girl.

She leaned out over the narrow walkway connecting the wing adjacent to the garrison with the royal castle. Behind her stood a row of other familiar women, each of them looking down upon the state of the training grounds with her own particular emotion kindled in her eyes.

But none of that mattered anymore.

No matter what anyone else thought of him, none of it mattered to Subaru in the least.

Except that the one person, the single person, in this world, in this whole world, whom he least wanted to see him like this—was standing right there.

Subaru heard a sound, something like a thread snapping inside him.

With that as the final note, his consciousness began to recede all at once.

His consciousness, so clear until then, cut itself loose; the world began to blur rapidly, and this time, in the truest sense, leaving everything behind, Subaru's consciousness fell toward the bottom of the Abyss—

"—Subaru."

He thought he was called by a murmur he shouldn't have been able to hear, and then everything vanished.
