Arc 8 Chapter 21: "The Fate That Cannot Be Avoided"

A thick reek of liquor and the smell of earth hung heavy in the tavern.

Piled up near the entrance were empty liquor bottles and casks, along with the wreckage of what had once, presumably, been humanoid clay dolls.

Both piles had been stacked up by the two middle-aged men currently making drunken nuisances of themselves inside—

"Oh? This fine fellow, I feel I've seen him somewhere before... Say, redhead! Do you know him, this hairball of a gentleman?"

"Who the hell's a damn hairball! Don't spout damn nonsense at me! I've been running nonstop without a bite to eat or a drop to drink, and I'm damn exhausted...!"

Groovy snapped back at the ruddy-faced man who laughed good-naturedly, slapping his own thighs.

The blue-haired middle-aged man, his hair tied back, only laughed harder at Groovy's protest, murmuring "can't be helped, can't be helped" for some reason with growing delight.

He was clearly, thoroughly drunk in the worst way.

Deciding there was no point continuing, the blue-haired man turned his gaze to the other one, the red-haired man. That one sat quietly in his chair, glowering at the bottle clutched in his hand.

"Hey! You, damn redhead! Are you the same kind as this damn blue-hair?"

"――――"

"Don't ignore me! Quit playing damn silent and look this—"

"—Shut up."

The instant he tried to say "this way," a flash of silver answered him.

Still seated in his chair, the man sent a horizontal slash streaking toward Groovy's neck. Just as when he'd first burst in, it was precise swordsmanship aimed straight at a vital point.

Had the target been anyone but Groovy, it might well have proven a fatal blow.

"Who the hell do you think you're picking a damn fight with!!"

Groovy dodged the blade so it grazed past the tip of his nose, closed the distance in a single step, and let his fist roar—the gauntleted blow connected with the red-haired man's side, and the shockwave sent him flying into the wall.

With a strangled "gah," the man's body spun through the air. He slammed into the wall without even managing to break his fall, and while he did, Groovy plucked a bottle tumbling end over end out of midair.

It was the very liquor the red-haired man had been drinking, and Groovy poured it down his throat in one hearty gulp.

"Gwah! Damn, that burns...! What I actually want is water and food, though..."

"—Splendid, splendid! My my, quite the impressive skill, Master Hairball. Redhead here ought to be a formidable fighter in his own right, yet you didn't even give him a second thought—I'm most impressed!"

"Hah?"

With the red-haired man now upside-down against the wall, the blue-haired man dropped heavily to his knees right where he stood, sheathing his blade as if to say he'd surrendered, and spoke on.

Sitting formally with his ruddy face, the blue-haired man praised Groovy's prowess, bowing deeply to show through his posture that he had no intention of resisting.

"We've caused you a great deal of trouble, Master Hairball! We are but wanderers, and we happened to drift into this village on a whim. But by the time we arrived, the village was already, as you can see, overrun by a swarm of clay wretches, and not a single villager remained—that is the state of things."

"...So you damn bastards figured there was nothing else for it, and drank up all the booze the dead left behind?"

"Why yes, shamefully enough, that's exactly it! Not satisfied with that, we even took to wagering on whether the next clay wretch to come through the door would be a man or a woman."

Grinning foolishly, the blue-haired man revealed the details of a wager made by men who didn't even seem to register the emergency as an emergency.

It was a ridiculous thing to bet on, but if what they were wagering was ownerless liquor and their own lives, Groovy had nothing to say about it. Where and how men chose to live or die was their own business.

Except—

"—I don't like this one damn bit."

"Oh? And what's that, then?"

"You've sheathed your sword and folded your knees, putting on a face like you've got no damn intention of fighting, but you can't fool my nose. ...Quit sizing me up and hunting for a chance to kill me, damn it."

"――――"

Groovy sniffed once and glared, and the blue-haired man scratched his cheek with a troubled look, muttering "well now." But the fact that no denial came from him wasn't because the words failed to spring to mind in the moment—more likely, he understood that any convenient excuse would only strike Groovy's nerve.

This blue-haired man—since right after, or perhaps even before, the red-haired one had been taken down—had been concealing murderous intent toward Groovy the whole time, hidden behind his manner and his words.

In the place he'd dashed into while fleeing the clay dolls, instead of rest he'd found himself tangled up with troublesome company—just as he thought that, Groovy realized something.

"...Your damn stink, I know it from somewhere."

It was a smell he'd caught somewhere before, Groovy recalled, the fur all over his body bristling.

He wouldn't claim he never forgot a scent he'd smelled once, but a distinctive smell was hard to forget. Combining it with the damp reek of murderous intent seeping from the man, he tried to place who this was—

"—You, could you possibly be that damn Cecilus's damn father?"

Voicing the memory that had struck him, Groovy fixed the man with a sharp glare.

At that sharp look, the blue-haired man's face turned bitter, and he said,

"Uhi—so I've been remembered after all."

"Damn it! So you were trying to kill me just so that wouldn't get out! Don't damn mess with me! And more to the point, why the hell are you even alive! You were supposed to have been cut down by Cecilus!"

"Asked why, all this one can say is that he's alive because he's alive. That said, it would be most inconvenient indeed if this one's survival became known."

Shaking his head slowly, the blue-haired man answered with brazen ease—he was the father of Cecilus Segmunt, the "First" of the Nine Divine Generals.

He used to come and go from the Crystal Palace alongside Cecilus, so Groovy had crossed paths with him a few times before. However, years ago now, he was said to have committed some insolence within the imperial palace, and to have been cut down—by none other than his own son Cecilus—and pronounced dead.

"Given that a wanted notice is still circulating on this one, the report of his death must simply be a misremembering."

"I just don't figure that damn Cecilus would botch it. A bastard whose only damn sure thing is his swordsmanship, if he botched that, he'd be nothing but garbage. Don't tell me he went easy on you just because you're his damn father."

"—That, there is not. That one's swordsmanship has never once dulled for the sake of a father-son bond."

"――――"

Told so plainly and firmly, Groovy swallowed back the retort on his tongue.

He didn't know much about the relationship between the man before him and Cecilus. But this was the father who'd raised Cecilus into that Cecilus. It wasn't hard to imagine he was a worthless, inhuman piece of work.

If the boy's skill hadn't dulled even when cutting down his own father, well, that was believable enough.

Still—

"Damn it..."

Chased and chased, running for his life the whole while, and after all that he finally ran into living humans—only for them to turn out to be a pair of drunkards, one a wanted fugitive, the other a raving alcoholic.

Cursing his own rotten luck, Groovy almost wanted to give up on everything out of sheer spite.

"Ugh, ghh..."

"Oh, seems redhead's alive too. Master Hairball is quite the gentle soul."

"...I didn't go easy on him or anything. That damn redhead's just damn sturdy, is all."

The raving-drunk redhead, flipped over by the wall, had indeed withstood a blow from Groovy meant to scramble his insides—only now he was drowning in his own vomit spilling out of his upside-down mouth.

Even in his battered state, Groovy could still thoroughly beat down these two middle-aged men, red and blue—

"...It's getting rather noisy outside, wouldn't you say."

Just as the blue-haired man muttered, that hateful smell of earth was seeping into the village.

It seemed a considerable number of them had surrounded the hidden settlement, closing in on Groovy who had fled here. Sensing the encroaching encirclement with his nose, Groovy agonized over what to do.

Kill these two middle-aged men, break through the encirclement with everything he had, find someone better than these drunkards, and make time to get water and food into his stomach.

Was that ideal actually realistic? More than that—

"...Damn bastard. Let's make a deal."

"I shall listen most respectfully."

Still sitting formally, the blue-haired man set his sword aside and composed himself solemnly.

Just moments before, in that same posture and that same manner, he'd been trying to kill Groovy—Groovy wanted to call the shamelessness of it out, but the smell of murderous intent had, in fact, settled.

Feeling irritated by that all the same, Groovy declared his terms.

"No matter what, I have to get back to His Excellency and the others. Even if that means using you damn bastards to do it. So..."

"――――"

"That wanted notice on you and all the rest of it, I'll wipe it clean using my authority as a General. In exchange, you're going to cooperate with me and help me get back to the capital."

This was a desperate measure even for Groovy.

If he'd been in perfect condition, he'd never once consider relying on a man like this. But if he stubbornly held out only to collapse, who would that benefit?

"This damn bastard who created this whole incomprehensible situation, no less."

Honestly, Groovy sensed all sorts of things going on beneath the surface of this civil war shaking the Vollachian Empire.

As for the matter of himself being kept away from the capital, there had been the plausible-sounding reason of watching the west, but he'd had a vague sense that Vincent had other intentions at work.

That he'd obeyed without question regardless was because he'd decided he could trust Vincent's judgment.

And to hear the answer to what he'd chosen to believe, Groovy had to make it back.

So—

"Choose whether you're taking the deal or not, you damn bastard!"

Groovy bared his fangs and roared it out, and before him the blue-haired man closed one eye, looking thoughtful.

Before the unreadable man's deliberation could reach an answer, a loud crash sounded as the tavern door behind Groovy was smashed in from outside.

Carried by the force that broke the door, pale-faced clay dolls came pouring in. Just as their hands were about to reach the small Groovy's back—a flash of steel ran through.

The drawn blade's slash cut through the clay dolls' necks and torsos, reducing them to clods of earth.

The blue-haired man who'd done it sheathed his drawn sword in one smooth motion and rose to his feet. Still ruddy-faced, he stroked his stubbled jaw with his free hand, and said,

"In addition to clearing my wanted status, how much might the reward money come to?"

"You're a damn piece of work through and through, you damn bastard..."

Taking that shameless question as his answer, Groovy let out a long breath. And just as his vision seemed to spin—

"Damn it."

So it was that, swallowed by the unavoidable limit of his own stamina, his consciousness sank down into dark water.



"Well, well. Putting off even the fine print of the deal to take a nap—Master Hairball has quite the personality."

The small beastman had collapsed limply right there, and had begun letting out hearty snores.

Looking down at Groovy Gumlet, one of the peaks of martial prowess in the Vollachian Empire and a member of the Nine Divine Generals, the ruddy-faced Rowan Segmunt tilted his head.

The terms just offered—Groovy wasn't likely the sort to go back on a mere verbal promise.

It was a proposal well worth considering. Of course, if he thought purely in terms of continuing to run, he could simply pierce the sleeping Groovy's heart right here and go on living as a fugitive.

"And who, pray, would benefit from a stunt like that? Better, surely, to take this as this one's own luck finally turning—the third act of my life, opening at last!"

Stamping his feet hard against the ground, Rowan minded the slight stiffness pulling at his left leg, yet smirked to himself that there was no need to look backward.

That said, getting out of here alive came first and foremost—sensing the presence of clay wretches gathering around the tavern, he hoisted Groovy up onto his shoulder with ease.

Then, just like that, he kicked at his upside-down partner by the wall—Heinkel.

"There now, redhead, up and at 'em! The circumstances have changed!"

"Uh, ahh...?"

"Leaving you behind would sit poorly with me! If redhead's life has hit rock bottom too, then wagering everything on a single stroke with this one here has its own charm, wouldn't you say!"

When he pressed the point loudly like that, Heinkel's eyes opened with an air of utter reluctance.

He looked at Rowan filling his upside-down field of vision, and at Groovy slung over his shoulder, gave his legs a light kick to right himself from being upside down, and immediately swayed as if struck by dizziness.

"What... is this? I feel sick..."

"For an answer to a single blow from a General, that's rather too impressive a reaction. Come, come—now that we've drunk this place dry, it's about time this one and you moved on to the next place."

"...That beastman? Are we eating him?"

"Should we ever find ourselves truly desperate for food, perhaps, but for now this one has no such intention. And so—"

He wiped his sleeve across his mouth, dirtied with vomited liquor, and clapped the swaying Heinkel on the back. Then, hoisting Groovy's body up once more, Rowan turned around.

All at once, the clay wretches came pouring into the tavern in an avalanche. Facing them, thumb breaking his sword free of its sheath with one hand, Rowan laughed, and said,

"Live and wait for me, you good-for-nothing son. —Reaching the Heavenly Sword is still a ways off yet."

—Two lights to overturn the fury of the "Great Disaster."

That was the prophecy brought forth by Ubilk, the chain-bound "Stargazer"—no, what they themselves called it: the mandate of heaven.

Prized within the Vollachian Empire, the Stargazers were said to have foretold events time and again over the years, and the impact of such a figure's words struck Subaru hard.

Honestly, Subaru didn't put much faith in things like prophecy or precognition.

He rather suspected that sort of thing was some kind of con using various psychological tricks. Of course, in this other world, phenomena unbound by common sense could well occur.

For that matter, even the results brought about by Subaru's Return by Death, depending on how you looked at it, could arguably be called a way of avoiding the worst future through a kind of foreknowledge.

Though he'd be a lot more grateful if he could know the future without having to die for it.

In any case—

"...A girl whose words don't reach anyone."

It was a remark for which someone had taken care to be precise—one that even went so far as to correct itself, insisting it wasn't a case of Subaru himself being a "Stargazer," just so the information could be conveyed accurately.

Picturing the one person that phrase could only mean, Subaru caught his breath.

But before Subaru could press him on what he truly meant, Abel, standing beside him, turned a cold look on Ubilk and said,

"One of the two lights you spoke of—if it's Groovy Gumlet, he's dead."

"Eh?"

At that flat declaration, Ubilk's eyes went wide, his mouth falling open in blank astonishment.

It was only natural. Anyone would make that face on being told that the very person their solemnly delivered prophecy concerned had died. In fact, Subaru too was startled by Abel's declaration.

Ignoring Subaru and the others' surprise, Abel merely shrugged lightly, arms still crossed, and said,

"So that's what comes out of the mouth that so grandly declared the stars favor me. I must call it more than a little anticlimactic."

"Wait wait wait! Don't say it like it's confirmed fact! It's not even verified yet!"

"Fool. With no sign of him rejoining us after all this time, that man may as well be dead."

"Don't lump 'as good as dead' together with 'is dead'! You've gotten so used to assuming the worst that you keep killing people off in your head!"

Subaru bit back fiercely at Abel's remark, which sounded almost like a pointless bit of bait thrown at Ubilk. Since there was no particular reason to deceive Ubilk here and now, this should have been a fair objection to raise.

At the exchange between Subaru and Abel, Ubilk let out a breath, visibly relieved, and said,

"I- I really thought my heart would stop there. So it's fine to think General Groovy hasn't di-ied after all, yeah?"

"Contact has been severed for a good while now, and the army meant to be deployed west shows no movement at all. With the whereabouts of the man commanding it likewise unknown, treating him as dead ought to be the more constructive way to proceed."

"Constructive—constructive of what, exactly? A graveyard?"

Perhaps taking Subaru's remark as mere heckling, a sharp edge crept into Abel's gaze. But there was no reason for Subaru to back down from what he'd said, so he stuck out his tongue by way of reply and turned back to Ubilk.

His trust in the "Stargazer" remained, as ever, not especially high.

"At the point when the stars were speaking to you, Ubilk, was this Groovy guy definitely still alive? Because if so, that'd make it a lot easier to convince this guy."

"I'm afraid no-ot. What the stars brought me was only the key points about the people needed to stand against the 'Great Disaster.' Whether those people are living or de-ead isn't something I can tell."

"A countermeasure against the 'Great Disaster,' held by a dead Groovy Gumlet, is it."

Repeating over and over that his life or death was actually unknown had grown tiresome, so Subaru didn't bother correcting him anymore, but the remark Abel had let slip would carry great weight going forward.

If one accepted Ubilk's prophecy as a premise, then whatever special something only Groovy possessed had to be a necessary element for stopping this calamity.

And in addition to that—

"—Rui."

Of the two lights Ubilk had spoken of, if one was Groovy Gumlet, a General of the Empire, then the other could only be pointing to her.

A girl who had been swept into this Vollachian Empire alongside Subaru and Rem, who had since overcome countless hardships together with Subaru and the others, and who had now joined them aboard these Coupled Dragon Carriages as well—a girl with a reputation notorious even by this world's standards—Ubilk had said she was necessary to turn back the calamity.

Whatever it was that Rui and Groovy had in common, that something had to be effective against defeating the "enemy."

"Groovy Gumlet is what's known as a 'Curse Tool Artisan'—one versed in both Magic and Curse Arts, possessing the skill to craft equipment incorporating those techniques."

He had likely followed the same train of thought as Subaru.

What Abel revealed by way of explanation was the unique skill possessed by the man named Groovy—one that couldn't be substituted by anyone else, and precisely the reason he'd likely been chosen as one of the two lights.

"Being one of the Generals means the guy himself is strong too, right?"

"There's no doubting his strength. Even among beastmen, he's exceptional in the keenness of his senses, and in scent especially, there's no one in the whole Empire who surpasses him. But given the conditions this 'Stargazer' here has laid out, what warrants attention isn't his skill as a warrior."

"I know. ...Magic and Curse Arts, huh."

On the matter of Magic, Beatrice and Roswaal had only just produced results.

The presence of the core Insects lurking within the bodies of the Undead—there was no doubt that this lay at the center of the mechanism by which the Undead were born.

But neither of them was included among the lights Ubilk had spoken of.

In other words, what mattered wasn't being a skilled mage—

"—Curse Arts," they said together.

Subaru and Abel spoke the same word at the same moment, their black eyes meeting.

That Abel had arrived at the same conclusion, not just himself, bolstered Subaru's certainty. Almost without doubt, the reason Groovy had been chosen as a countermeasure against the Undead was the Curse Arts.

By adding a Curse Arts approach on top of the magical one, some effective countermeasure against those Undead could surely be devised.

"Setting aside Groovy Gumlet, about the only one with knowledge of the Curse Arts would be Olbart Dunkelkenn. Gathering those with expertise is an urgent matter. What of your side?"

"On our side, Bea-ko knows a bit about it, and I'm still cursed myself, that's about it. Not sure how much Roswaal or Onee-sama know..."

"—Setting aside part of that, we'll need to have their knowledge shared as well."

It was widely rumored that the lingering effects of being bitten and cursed by a pack of the Witchbeast known as Ulgarm still nested somewhere in Subaru's body.

Fortunately it caused no trouble in daily life, so he'd simply left it be, but it was as if some mysterious wart sat somewhere on his body—a curse he'd be lying if he said didn't bother him at all.

In any case, as Abel said, they needed to gather everyone with knowledge of curses, one by one, and work their way toward the insight the expert Groovy would presumably have provided.

As Subaru thought this over, Abel quietly added, "And on top of that—"

"—Do you have an answer for what advantage that girl's very existence brings to the Empire?"

Naturally, the topic spread to touch on Rui as well, and Abel's question drove straight into Subaru.

"――――"

Subaru shut his mouth, letting one beat pass before answering Abel's question.

But that one beat soon became two, then three, and still he couldn't arrive at a clear answer.

What was being asked was plain enough, and that Subaru himself lacked the answer to it was obvious without even needing to agonize over it.

"Back when we split the appa, I told you as much then. —That I had no intention of declaring that anyone who seemed to be a Sin Archbishop should be executed on the spot."

As if standing in for the silent Subaru, Abel pressed the topic of Rui forward.

At the end of that unseemly brawl, Subaru and Abel had shared an appa between them, and in the course of that conversation, Rui had come up as well.

There, Abel had indeed said he wouldn't punish her on the grounds of what she truly was—

"That view hasn't changed even now. —Because defining what that girl is meant to be is a task for you to carry out, not me."

"...I define Rui?"

"It is you, not I, who is deeply bound up with that girl's actions. Even should she entrust her fate to someone, it is you she would wish for, not me."

Beside Subaru, who felt his throat go dry, Abel stated this flatly, then turned his gaze from Subaru back to Ubilk, and said,

"There's no error in what you heard from the stars, is there? That to stand against the 'Great Disaster,' the presence of the girl this man here brought with him is the key."

"—Yes, my story hasn't cha-anged. Same as with General Groovy, just what the stars recognized in that girl, tha-at I couldn't say."

With a shrug that set his chains rattling, Ubilk replanted the same unchanging seed of trouble.

There was no telling how it would sprout, what buds it would bear, or what flower it would bloom into. Even not knowing, Subaru keenly realized that he couldn't simply keep carrying the seed around forever, letting it neither bloom nor wither.

And to Subaru, his heart aching over that reality, the emperor—who made no effort to behave as though he understood another's pain—declared, without a shred of mercy,

"Reach your conclusion swiftly. If the taste of that appa, and your own big words, were no lies."

"—Subaru, did you get to hear what you wanted to hear?"

Stepping out of the cabin where Ubilk was held captive, Subaru was met by Emilia, who had been waiting anxiously.

Apparently, the existence of the "Stargazer" counted as classified information for the Vollachian Empire, so none of the Kingdom's people besides Subaru had been permitted to sit in on the meeting.

Presumably it was because Subaru already had a prior acquaintance with Ubilk, and because Abel, for his part, had judged that Subaru ought to be told what the "Stargazer" had to say.

"If you ask me if I heard it, I did, and if you ask if I didn't, I guess I didn't, but..."

"...That's a rather pointless answer, I suppose. Does that mean it was simply a waste of time?"

"I don't think it was."

Beatrice, who'd come trotting over to take his hand, frowned as well at Subaru's unavoidably muddled reply.

But neither Beatrice nor Emilia, nor Otto or Garfiel who were also present, tried to hurry Subaru's answer along.

Feeling vaguely that such consideration was different from what he'd find among the Empire's people, Subaru found himself wanting to lean on their familiar, warm concern.

But—

"—That won't do."

He couldn't keep turning his face away forever.

Beyond mere dishonesty, matters had reached a point too urgent for that. More than anything, once he stepped away from the people who'd let him get by with vague half-answers, the one who'd suffer most for it wouldn't be Subaru.

So—

"Everyone, for the Empire's sake too, there's something I want us to properly talk through."

"Something you want all of us to talk through?"

"Yeah."

Nodding to Emilia's question, Subaru drew a deep breath.

A topic that could never be sidestepped—one that must not be sidestepped—for everyone here, and for those precious to Subaru who weren't present as well.

And that was—

"—Let's properly talk about Rui Arneb, the Sin Archbishop of Gluttony."
