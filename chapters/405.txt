Arc 6 Chapter 3: "The Girl's Cage"

"Did your talk with the master go well?"

"About as well as usual, which is to say if you told me it pissed me off more than usual, that'd track too. Read between the lines."

"I see. Then let's leave it at that, shall we."

Leaving the parlor and heading for the east wing of the manor, Subaru's group was now entrusted to Frederica's guidance.
Her beautiful blonde hair grown long, dressed neatly in her maid uniform, the stern-faced waitress exchanged polite greetings with Anastasia and Julius — finally getting to greet them properly, as one of the manor's decent maids should — and then spoke to Subaru, who had set off with a scowl on his face.

"Was Garf of proper use to you all, on the road and in the city? I told him quite a lot before he left, and yet he came back badly injured and unable to return with you all... I'm worried he might have caused trouble for Emilia-sama and the others over there, too."

"No, don't worry. Garfiel really did work suuuper hard for us. He's resting quietly now with Otto-kun... I hope he's being quiet, anyway. I'd like to think so, but in any case, I told him firmly to rest up."

"I apologize that my unworthy little brother has troubled you so."

Even Emilia couldn't quite follow through on reassuring her, half-convinced herself, and Frederica looked mortified.
Still, whatever misgivings Petra, Ram, Roswaal, and Frederica all held about Garfiel's conduct, there was no doubt that the events in Priestella had brought about some change of heart in him — growth, one might say.

To begin with, Garfiel's strength was already exceptional for his age — fifteen.
Judging from the accounts of his fierce battles against the Bowel Hunter and the Eight Arms, he was still far from the "strongest" he so boldly claimed to be, but he was undeniably among the top-ranked fighters not just in the Emilia Camp, but across the entire continent.
His greatest problem was probably his still-immature mentality. Once he overcame that emotional fragility, he'd rise to yet another stage.

Well, that said, it wasn't a road to be rushed. Growing strong slowly and surely, treading each step of the staircase firmly — that was the kind of growth befitting a fifteen-year-old.
And besides—

"This time around, there's probably a reason he can't come straight home, too."

"Is it about Mimi?"

"That too, but not just that."

Subaru gave a wry smile at Emilia's oddly sharp-eared interruption.
Even Emilia, who was supposedly clueless about romance between men and women, apparently had some curiosity when it came to other people's love lives. She seemed thoroughly intrigued by Garfiel and Mimi's relationship.
Of course, Garfiel too was surely growing attached to Mimi, who'd shielded him at the cost of her own life and openly declared her affection both before and after the fight.

Frankly, Subaru was no exception either — there wasn't a man alive who could stay steady when someone he didn't dislike came pounding affection into him.
On that score, speaking as someone with experience, Subaru had nothing more to say.

How Garfiel would respond to his feelings for Ram, and to Mimi's feelings for him, was something to watch unfold going forward.
But separate from that, Garfiel had left a problem behind in the city of Priestella.

"—Subaru-sama? Is there something you need from me?"

"Nah, nothing from me in particular. I don't think it'd be right for me to be the one to bring this up."

He'd been eyeing Frederica's profile with a meaningful look, and she'd caught him at it and asked what it meant.
Deflecting her question, Subaru pictured what must be going on in Garfiel's heart. The family Garfiel had been so concerned about in Priestella — golden hair, emerald eyes, features that called to mind Frederica and Garfiel as siblings.
The relationship between those people and Garfiel, and what that implied about his relationship with Frederica.
Surely that was something Garfiel would want to report himself, chest puffed out with pride, to his family — Frederica, Ryuzu, and the rest.

"So I'm not gonna say anything. Natsuki Subaru exits, ultra cool."

"Right, right, speaking of Garfiel, there were some kids in Priestella he got suuuper close with, and those kids—"

"Emilia-tan, Emilia-tan, you're wasting my whole monologue!"

Subaru clapped a hand over Emilia's mouth before her artless bridge-building could ruin everything.
Left unchecked, the synergy was too frightening to contemplate, so he resolved to make proper time later to fill Emilia in on the background of it all.
To Frederica, who was looking ever more suspicious, Subaru waved with an ingratiating smile.

"Sorry to interrupt this delightful bonding session, but where exactly are we headed?"

"Ah, the place we call the 'zashikirou' — the parlor cell. There's someone I've been talking to who's staying there."

"Hmm, the person in question. — Do you think the conversation will go well?"

"Won't know till we try. For now, it's just worth asking, as insurance."

At Subaru's explanation, Julius, walking behind him, fell into thought with a difficult expression.
In truth, Subaru himself was well aware of how many problems this proposal had. Still, if it went well, it could considerably reduce the risks of this particular journey.

"Besides, that kid's pretty attached to Subaru, isn't she? Should be fine, right?"

"The one she's really attached to is Garfiel more than me. Might be because of something cat-like about him that she took a liking to... oh, we're here."

Just as he answered Emilia's optimism with a wry smile, Frederica's steps came to a halt. At the same moment, a level of the manor with an unmistakably different atmosphere came into view ahead of them.
The rough layout was no different from the previous manor in this "true" Roswaal Manor, but the basement of the east wing — this was the one place where the change was unmistakable.

The main building was the manor's core, where the facilities essential to daily life were concentrated. The west wing housed the guest rooms for servants and visitors, along with various multi-purpose facilities. The east wing, meanwhile, served as a storehouse for House Mathers' historic heirlooms and books.
Except for the basement of the east wing — that one place was the exception. In that cold, stone-built space, an all-too-clear purpose attached itself to what one would expect from the basement of a noble's manor.

"My, what an unpleasant air hangin' around this place."

Anastasia gave a short sniff and offered a blunt assessment of the change in atmosphere.
And it was an assessment so obviously correct that no dissenting opinion could arise. The air drifting through this level could only be described as "unpleasant," full stop.

"It's not quite like miasma, but it sure doesn't feel good for the body."

"There's the structural issue of the building itself, and the very purpose of this facility to begin with... I doubt those are unrelated, but ultimately, I'd wager it comes down to whatever lies within."

Looking down the staircase leading into the dim basement, Anastasia and Julius exchanged words.
They'd already spoken of it as a "zashikirou." No doubt the two of them had a fairly accurate idea of what kind of facility existed down here.

"Well then, allow me to guide you. Please mind your step so you don't slip."

With that, Frederica led the way down the stairs. As Subaru and the others followed her tall figure down into the basement, Julius and the others too wore expressions of resolve.
Their footsteps echoed oddly loud on the stone steps. A cold wind blew up from below, stirring their bangs faintly — and for some inexplicable reason, it grated on the nerves.

"I'll open it."

Descending into the basement, an iron door stood squarely before them. Several sturdy locks were fitted to the door, and Frederica undid them one by one.
With a clatter, the locks and bolts came free, and the iron door creaked open. Beyond it stretched a stone-built corridor, at the far end of which yet another door was visible.

"The person in question is on the other side of that."

Stepping aside from the door, Frederica bowed her head, yielding the way. Subaru gave a small nod in return, and with him in the lead, the four of them walked straight toward that door.

—The door at the very back of the basement was not locked.

Reaching for the handle, pushing it open would bring an immediate face-to-face meeting with whoever was inside.
Subaru touched the door hesitantly, then held his breath and glanced back.

"——"

Emilia, Julius, and Anastasia were all watching Subaru's actions intently. He nodded to the three of them, took a deep breath, and put strength into the hand gripping the handle.
And with a clatter, the iron door was pushed open——

"Rawr, rawr! I'm gonna eat you up!"
"Eek, help, no—"
"Hehehehehe, no matter how much you cry for help, no one's coming for you."

As the door opened, bright light spilled into the corridor, and along with it came the sound of a girl's playful voice.
Beyond the flung-open door, inside the room, a girl sat with her back to them, surrounded by a great many stuffed animals, playing with two dolls held one in each hand.
Changing her voice for each, she seemed to be playing several roles at once, utterly absorbed.

"No, she'll come. Because, because, she promised she'd come whenever I was in trouble... hm?"

Clutching a small girl doll, the girl who had stood up suddenly noticed something. She turned around cautiously, and upon seeing Subaru and the others standing frozen in the doorway, her large round eyes went wide.
Brown hair done up in braids, an artless, adorable face — a girl.

"Yo. Been doing okay?"

For now, Subaru raised a hand and called out as though nothing were amiss.

"Y-you idiot, Onii-san! At least knock before you come in!"

Naturally, he got yelled at for his little prank.

"And that's the deal — this is Meili, the prisoner in our manor who's going to be giving us advice on the witchbeast front this time."

"I've been sullied, Onii-san hurt me... sniff, sniff..."

Slumped in a corner of the room, surrounded by stuffed animals and sulking, Meili was introduced by Subaru; Julius, pressing a finger to the bridge of his nose, shook his head.
Keeping the girl at the edge of his vision as if to reproach Subaru for his prank, he said,

"There's fault on our side too, for not asking for details beforehand... but you, for your part, have a remarkably bad character. Do you simply feel compelled to pull pranks the moment an opening presents itself?"

"I don't think it's really pulling a prank, exactly... well, no, I'll admit it's in poor taste. But she really is a dangerous person, for what it's worth."

At Subaru's excuse, Julius's gaze remained suspicious. Meanwhile, paying no mind to the two wary men, Emilia and Anastasia went side by side to speak to Meili.

"Sorry, Meili. I'll give Subaru a proper scolding later, okay?"

"My, what an adorable little thing. Between her and Beatrice-chan and Petra-chan, Natsuki-kun's place is reflectin' a bit too much of somebody's tastes, wouldn't you say?"

"Don't say something so outrageous like it's a reasonable opinion?! It's not like I'm out here collecting lolis on purpose!"

The nickname "Lolicon Handler" was starting to feel uncomfortably real, so he'd rather she not say scary things like that.
In any case, thoroughly sulking, Meili wasn't about to lend an ear to Emilia and the others' words. That, at least, was in balance with her otherwise childlike sensibilities.

"Meili."

"Can't hear you."

"Meili, c'mon."

"Don't know you."

There it was. Once she dug in and refused to listen, she was a real handful.
So Subaru decided to bring out his final measure early. Reaching a hand behind his back, he thrust out what he'd been hiding, toward Meili.

"Here, Meili. A present, take it. Brand-new stuffed animal — Droopy Panda."

"—! Wah, it's so cute!"

What Subaru held out to Meili was a stuffed animal he'd been stitching away at, bit by bit, in the dragon carriage on the road back from the city of Priestella.
This past year, Natsuki Subaru's sewing skills had leveled up considerably as part of his household-chore repertoire, a cut or two above where they used to be. He could make stuffed animals now, and honestly, at this rate he might even be able to tailor women's clothing.

In any case, the new creation took a panda as its motif, with the added flavor concept of "wilted from the heat" — an ambitious piece. It bore a passing resemblance to something, he felt, but that something wasn't going to reach across worlds to file a complaint, so he let it slide.
Presented with the new piece, Meili's eyes lit up as she took it.

"So cute! A new friend! What should I name him... okay, decided! I'm gonna name him Giant Panda!"

"That's, uh, a name that really cuts to the heart of the matter."

Naming sense aside, her eye for essential truths was rather impressive.
Meili hugged the panda stuffed animal to her chest with great care, and turned back to Subaru and the others, her mood restored.

"Oh right, welcome back, Onii-san, Onee-san. Sounds like you went really far away. Petra-chan was lonely, you know?"

"Was gone for a bit over a month. And now I'm about to head off again for a while, so I'm kinda dreading getting yelled at by Petra..."

"Petra-chan loves you a lot, Onii-san. ...Oh, new faces?"

Rearranging the scattered stuffed animals back onto the shelf, Meili only now seemed to notice Julius and the others. Julius looked surprised at how quickly her mood had shifted, but Anastasia nodded with a face that showed she was used to this sort of thing.

"Compared to Mimi, gettin' jerked around by her is small potatoes."

"...Now that you mention it, that's true. Let's be grateful to Mimi."

Master and servant found agreement in an odd place, and then Julius suddenly turned his gaze around the room. Taking in the underground space at a glance, he said,

"Even so, having heard it called a 'zashikirou,' I'd expected harsher conditions... this is a surprisingly comfortable environment, isn't it."

"It's a girl living in here, and I never wanted to make her suffer. ...But there are complicated reasons we can't really let her outside, either."

Emilia lowered her brows, looking unhappy with herself.
Just as she said, the "zashikirou" — Meili's living space, to put it plainly — was, frankly speaking, an extremely lax sort of place to serve as an environment for house arrest.

The stone corridor alone looked cold, but the room at its very end was papered and carpeted in bright colors, and since she wasn't restrained in any way, her movements were free. The shelves held numerous stuffed animals of Subaru's own making, along with books and toys of various kinds to pass the time.
She wasn't denied meals, and bathing and using the toilet posed no problem. In short, it could be called a leisurely shut-in's paradise. If anything, Subaru himself wouldn't have minded holing up in there.
However——

"That peculiar, near-miasma-like air seems to keep leaking out endlessly, though."

"Apparently that's also coming from... well, from her, it seems."

At Anastasia and the others' glances, Meili nodded, smiling brightly.
From that artless-faced girl seeped an aura of dread she couldn't fully hide — and that, precisely, was the biggest reason Meili remained confined here, unreleased.

"I mentioned it in the dragon carriage too, but originally this kid's background is that she came after Emilia and the rest of us... well, like an assassin, basically. That much's fine, right?"

"Already at that point it doesn't sound fine at all, but go on."

"That's a loaded way to put it... anyway, she's an assassin. And what kind of work does Meili do with that small body of hers, you ask? Simply put, she controls witchbeasts. A 'Witchbeast Handler.'"

"That's right, me and the bad animals get along really, really well."

Meili puffed out her chest as if to say "ta-da," but what she'd just described was, to put it mildly, astonishing.
To begin with, witchbeasts were never creatures that took to humans — they were enemies of mankind. The one exception, apparently, was that if a witchbeast had its horn broken, some mechanism made it obey whoever broke it, but——

"In Meili's case, it works regardless of whether the horn's broken or not. The logic's kind of iffy — even when it's explained to me, I don't really get it."

"According to Mama, I play the same role for witchbeasts that a horn does. So, I can get along with the witchbeasties real well."

Fulfilling the role of a witchbeast's horn — what exactly that meant remained thoroughly unclear.
After all, in this world, no research aimed at unraveling the ecology of witchbeasts had ever been conducted, apparently. Of course, there were people who made a living hunting witchbeasts, but even they, however well-versed in their habits, likely never viewed them from a research standpoint.

"Meili came at first together with one other person to attack us. Garfiel took down that other person, and this kid was captured. Ever since then, she's been sheltered here at the manor."

"Why'd ya go and do somethin' like that? I mean, if she was an enemy, wouldn't it be quicker to just deal with her... though, I guess doin' that to a little kid doesn't seem so easy."

"...I couldn't consider killing her. But letting her go is hard too. Because, according to her, if she's set free—"

"Mama'll get angry at me. Elsa died too, and I failed the job, so if I'm found, I'll definitely get killed. So, staying here is the safest."

Meili answered in a carefree tone, though a shadow fell over her expression as she spoke.
Having lost her partner Elsa and failed at her job, Meili's guardian — presumably something like the head of a ring of assassins — would surely never forgive that failure.
Setting her loose, letting her face punishment — that was, admittedly, a consequence of her own doing, and perhaps none of Subaru and the others' business, but—

"It'd sure give me nightmares, that much's for certain."

"That way of thinking, yours and Emilia-sama's, isn't something that started just now. Whatever we outsiders might say, it would be out of place for us to belabor the point with words. ...Incidentally, what about this mother figure?"

"Dunno. Other than 'mother,' 'Mama,' everything else is a secret. According to Meili, she doesn't even know her face. What kind of home life is that."

But then, given that her line of work was as an assassin, perhaps that kind of shadowed existence was unavoidable. In any case, it seemed difficult to pin down this so-called mother from Meili's own words and eliminate the future threat.

"That Roswaal bastard's useless too — says there's no lead now that Elsa's dead..."

"Did you say something?"

"Nothing, just talking to myself."

The medallion incident that broke out in the royal capital on the day of Subaru's very first summoning had been Roswaal's doing.
Naturally, Elsa too, who had targeted Emilia's life, had been acting on his commission, so tracing back the chain from when the job was commissioned should have made identifying the mastermind easy. And yet.
For whatever reason, that lead had gone cold — the person who'd served as the go-between had died, and contact was lost. The subsequent attack by Elsa and Meili, if Roswaal's account was to be believed, had nothing to do with his own scheming — which left this "mother" figure as the one thing that nagged at him.

"Anyway, that's the gist of the whole Meili situation. This kid is here because circumstances demand it. I'm not trying to spoil her, at least not on purpose."

"For someone who says that, you and everyone else treat her a little too well, though."

"Well, when it comes to that, sleeping soundly at night takes priority!"

I mean, a little girl in the prime of her playful years, kept under house arrest in a cold basement — it's heartbreaking. It's not something you can just decide to forget, and if you try to ignore it, it gives you nightmares.
So then, since there's no particular grudge against her anyway, might as well make sure she's comfortable.

"...Thinkin' about it normally, wouldn't there be a grudge?"

"...You think?"

"I mean, you said she was someone who nearly killed you, didn't you? If that's the case..."

"Well, sure, there's such a thing as malice with no reason behind it, and evil's evil regardless of age, but..."

At Anastasia's question, Subaru scratched his cheek and thought it over. Glancing sideways at Meili, he found her watching him with eyes he couldn't read.
Not that he was choosing his words for her benefit in particular, but he spoke his honest thoughts.

"If there's someone who ordered a kid with no judgment of her own to do something evil, I think the one at fault is whoever gave the order. Even more so if it's a child. If she had a mind of her own to think it through and still went ahead and did it, then the conditions would be the same, I guess. But an eye for an eye, Code of Hammurabi style — that's nothing but a net loss."

"That's a pretty way of puttin' it. You think whoever this kid's killed up till now would be satisfied with that?"

"I don't think they would, and it's their own business if they want revenge on Meili for it. If it were me and someone had died on my side, I wouldn't be saying I forgive her the way I am now, either."

In the end, Subaru's opinions and way of thinking shifted depending on how he'd come to meet someone.
If people wanted to call that lacking a firm center, or being swayed by emotion, fine, let them say it. In practice, that was probably just how it was.

"When I was a kid myself, I had my parents or other adults take responsibility for things I couldn't take responsibility for on my own. So when someone close to me — well, a kid I can get along with — does something she can't take responsibility for, I figure it's fine if I try to take it on in her place. That's all."

"...Thanks for the valuable opinion."

At Subaru's words, Anastasia, who'd been gradually losing interest partway through, wrapped things up with that.
Of course, he figured that was probably the normal reaction, so it didn't sting. In an organization that prized strict, clean-cut judgment, evil would be judged as evil regardless of age.
That there was room for leniency here was simply because this camp was a lax sort of camp. And Subaru didn't think that atmosphere, that environment, was a bad thing.

"I..."

"—Hm?"

"I don't think what Subaru's saying is all that strange, you know."

"...Thanks."

He didn't need to be fully vindicated, but being told something like that was a comfort all the same.
And so, turning back to Meili — who'd been left out of the conversation despite it being about her — Subaru brought his eyes level with hers and said,

"There's something I want to ask your help with. Would you be willing to come along?"

"...Fine. I'll go along with you, Onii-san."

To Subaru's request, Meili nodded quietly.
That the corners of her eyes, half-hidden behind the panda she hugged close, seemed to glisten faintly with tears — neither Subaru nor anyone else pointed it out.

"I've been to the Augria Sand Dunes twice before, you know?"

Having finished listening to Subaru and the others' explanation, Meili said this while fiddling with her braids.
She closed her eyes as if recalling a memory.

"Went there to stock up on witchbeast cards. That place has a ton of witchbeasts, so it went great, but..."

"But?"

"Are you guys really going? Probably everyone except me is gonna die, y'know...?"

Even from the eyes of a girl with no particular sense of ethics, Subaru and the others' plan apparently looked reckless. However rampant the witchbeasts had been for her to describe it that way, the opinion of the foremost authority on witchbeasts carried weight.

"I really want to take the testimony of someone with experience to heart, but the whole 'you'll die if you go' argument has already been hashed out to death, so it's a bit late for that now. And as far as the maze aspect of the desert goes, we've already got a way to get through prepared on our end."

"Yup, that'd be me."

Anastasia gave a little wave of her hand, advertising her role as guide.
Still, merely not getting lost only earned thirty points out of a hundred toward crossing the desert to reach the watchtower — a failing grade, practically guaranteed. Or rather, on this problem, unless they could produce a perfect answer, surviving the crossing at all was likely to prove difficult.

There were three problems: the "desert maze," the "witchbeast den," and the "Sage's Eye."
Of those, the "desert maze" could presumably be handled by Anastasia — Eridna. What Subaru wanted was a countermeasure for the second, the "witchbeast den."
That was why he'd wanted to hear the details from Meili, but——

"You don't happen to have any ideas — like, a good way to avoid witchbeasts, or the opposite, a way to lure them off somewhere?"

"If Onii-san just runs on ahead all by himself, they'll come flocking real thick, probably?"

"We've already done that a couple times and paid for it, thanks."

Once at the manor a year ago, and again a little after that with the whale on the plains — he'd rather not do it a third time.
Of course, if that was the only method available, he wasn't averse to doing it, but he'd prefer to avoid a situation where Subaru alone was left stranded in the middle of the desert, if at all possible.

"If Onii-san's out, maybe get a bunch of other decoy people ready instead? Witchbeasts all go for livin' things over regular food, so, somethin' like that, maybe."

"Absolutely not! I object! I object!"

"You don't need to get so worked up objecting — no one's picking you, don't worry."

Even so, no constructive idea seemed to be forthcoming.
When it came to witchbeast ecology, if even Meili — surely more knowledgeable than anyone else present — couldn't come up with an effective plan, there was nothing to be done about it.

"Guess it's the old tactic of taking down every witchbeast that comes at us, one by one, then. High hopes riding on Emilia-tan and Julius here."

"The fact that you don't include yourself in that list makes me curious just how you rate your own abilities... but, do you think that's feasible?"

At Subaru's suggestion, Julius pressed Meili on its practicality. Hearing this, Meili looked back and forth between Emilia and Julius, and said,

"Probably not gonna work. Onee-sans, can you use magic non-stop for like a week?"

"You're asking for a last-ditch trench-warfare situation like that?!"

"I-I'll try...?"

"No way! This whole line of talk is impossible! Emilia-tan's hair and skin will get all dry and ragged, let's not! Okay, rejected!"

The plan to force a straight breakthrough through the center was, to begin with, apparently unreasonable on its face.
There was no telling exactly how extreme the scene Meili had actually witnessed was, but assuming she wasn't exaggerating with a child's tendency toward hyperbole, this really wasn't something they could risk recklessly.

"What about smearing witchbeast blood on the dragon carriage, to throw off the other witchbeasts' sense of smell?"

"No, that won't fool earth dragons, and there's no guarantee it wouldn't attract even more of them, like with sharks. Hiring guards, or... at this point, something like the large force we used against the White Whale?"

"That's no good either. I told you about the challengers who previously aimed for the Pleiades Watchtower, didn't I? Short of an army, large-scale groups have made the attempt. The result, you can imagine. What about breaking the horn of a powerful witchbeast and using it as a wall against the others?"

"That'd need something as overwhelming as the whale, or the numbers would just push right through. What I really want is something simpler — like witchbeast repellent, bug-spray style."

Subaru's own body odor — or rather, the Witch's Lingering Scent — was more of a bug-attracting spray, so that was useless.
As for the barrier that had separated Arlam Village from witchbeast territory, that only functioned because Roswaal maintained it constantly; apparently something like creating a barrier around oneself and moving along together with it wasn't possible.

"Might as well just grab onto Roswaal and have him fly us all in from the sky."

"If we could slip past the Sage's Eye that way, that might've been an option."

"Ah, damn it, right. There's still the 'Sage' thing too."

Ideas with no basis in reality were shot down from a realistic standpoint, one after another.
Even setting aside the desert and the witchbeasts, the problem of the Sage still remained. On this front, frankly, since no one had ever successfully slipped past it before, there was no telling what might happen.

"If only we could narrow it down to just one problem..."

"—Oh, well, I guess it can't be helped."

"Hm?"

Sitting in a circle in the room, racking their brains over what to do, Meili finally spoke up like that. Standing, she gave a small shake of her head at Subaru and the others still seated before her, and said,

"I could come along with you, if you want. If I'm there, I can handle the witchbeasties however you like. Keep 'em away, make pets of 'em, have 'em fight each other, or feed the Sage person to 'em — whatever you want."

"No, don't do that last one at all?! I mean, wait..."

He blinked at the rather extreme suggestion, but even more than that, he was startled by the offer itself.
Not just her cooperative attitude, but the fact that Meili herself had voiced an opinion she'd never once stated before — that she didn't mind going outside.

"You're scared to go outside, aren't you? Are you gonna be okay?"

"It's not like the moment I step outside the manor, Mama's gonna find me right away, is it? Bein' found is scary, but I can't stay inside forever, either."

That Meili had been aware, all along, of the fact that she'd have to leave someday — that came as a surprise.
But perhaps it made sense. They'd made time to check in on her, taking turns, but even so, the time she spent alone in her room had always been far greater. There must have been plenty of time to think — enough that the silence itself would grow frightening.

Being shut alone in a room was, at once, comfortable and terrifying.
And even if that sensation eventually grew numb, it would return, as if remembering itself, to torment the heart.

"Subaru..."

At Subaru feeling an odd sense of empathy welling up for what must be going on in Meili's heart, Emilia gently tugged his sleeve. He understood what she was thinking. Because he felt the same way.

"It's not gonna be some fun little outing. There's a guide, but it's crossing a desert, and even with you there, we're going through a witchbeast den. On top of that, there's a scary Sage possibly watching the whole thing."

"Sounds like just the thing for a walk after bein' cooped up so long, doesn't it?"

Ever defiant, Meili said this to Subaru with a look down her nose at him. How much of that was bravado and how much was sincere, there was no telling, but——

"Our trump card against the witchbeast problem, right?"

"Yeah, that's right. Count on you, Meili."

"You didn't even have to ask. I was gonna do it anyway."

Emilia and Subaru exchanged glances, then nodded at Meili. Receiving that, the girl said so with a slight air of putting on airs, and hugged the panda in her arms tight.
And so, with this small step forward toward accomplishing this journey's goal, Julius, having watched the outcome, crossed his arms with an impressed look and offered one remark.

"I hate to say it this way, but you really do seem to have a talent for winning over young girls. ...Though it's not exactly a reputation-flattering skill to have."

"It's 'cause you guys keep treating me like some lolicon handler! I'll have you know Meili's not even that small for a 'young girl,' either! A young girl is—!"

At Julius's pointless remark, Subaru stamped his foot and pointed at the room's entrance. And right at that moment, the door swung open from the other side to reveal the face of—

"—I thought I heard a racket. What's Subaru making a fuss about this time?"

The little girl in question, having finished her secret talk with Roswaal, joined them, and another little uproar followed — but that particular episode, we'll set aside for now as not worth recounting.
