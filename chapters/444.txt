Arc 6 Chapter 39: "Wreckage"

Intermittently, endlessly, a searing, cataclysmic heat painted over existence.

The skull, the limbs, the torso, the organs, the flesh and blood.
Every single thing that made up the body was crushed and shattered by a hard impact, twisted and broken, and the agony that sprang from it seared the brain, plucked at the nerves like harp strings, scorched the soul, and made it burst.
At that unfathomable sense of loss and heat, a scream escaped.

Pain, pain, pain, pain, pain pain pain pain pain pain pain pain pain pain pain.
There was pain.

There was only pain. Only pain. The world was filled with pain. Even the thought that the world was filled with pain was itself painted over by pain, and it too began to hurt.

Thought, existence — all of it was being painted over by pain.
Anxiety, confusion, tension, grief, fury, despair — before pain, all of it was worthless.

Worthless.
Yes, worthless.

Thought, action, deliberation, opinion, hope, memory — all of it, equally worthless.
If that which was worthlessness itself were shattered, crushed, and finally lost — what was there to mourn?

Only the endless pain, and nothing else, carved the certainty of existence into the soul.
And then that pain, which should never have ended, abruptly let go of existence—

"—Uwaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaah!!"

A scream tore free, and consciousness returned.
That the throat raising this scream had once been crushed, drowning in surging blood, was now a memory long past.

"Aaaaaaaaaaah!! Uwaaaaaaaah!!"

Screaming, thrashing arms and legs, trying to protect a body being shattered. Joints that should have been crushed and rendered useless — the arms, the legs — moved.
They moved, but threw off his balance, and his body was wrapped in a brief sensation of floating before slamming into the floor.

"—!!"

He let out a voiceless cry and thrashed across the floor.
The feel of the floor was wrong. His whole body, rolling across it, tasted a texture like thick ropes laid side by side, and when his throat — capable only of screaming — swelled in its demand for air, a sharp stinging pain sent him into an immediate coughing fit.
Unable to hold it back, he vomited. There was nothing in his stomach. Yellow stomach acid passed through his parched mouth along with a sour stench, and spilled out.

"Ugh, bleh! Guh! Cough! Hack!"

Coughing, desperately spewing out a scant amount of stomach acid, his face grew a wreck of tears and snot. He curled up, covered his face, and again and again, weakly, knocked his forehead against the floor.
He repeated it, over and over, and only then finally noticed.

—That the searing, merciless pain that had pounded his entire body to pieces had vanished without a trace.

"—Ah."

Startled at the pain's disappearance, he noticed something else, even later than that.
His back — the back of his curled-up body — someone was gently stroking it with the palm of a hand.

"Have you calmed down?"

He turned, and into his vision, a wreck of tears, the face of the one stroking his back came into view.
Even through his blurred vision, it was vivid and clear — a girl with beautiful silver hair and violet eyes, her brow furrowed with worry as she stroked his back. At the sight of her, his throat made a sharp, hitching sound.

"Suba—"

"—Uwaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaah!!"

He twisted his body violently, and roughly batted away the white hand touching his back.
Caught off guard, astonishment flickered across the eyes of the girl whose hand had been struck away. But the one who suffered graver psychological damage than that was, none other than, himself.

His back.
His back had been touched.

Just now, and just before — he had been touched on the back, and in the next instant, that agony had—

"Hyih—"

A crawling sensation, like the fine hairs on his back standing on end, and he leapt backward as if fleeing. Of course, his legs couldn't hold him, and he tumbled gracelessly backward onto his rear.
His falling body was caught by something oddly rough and black,

"—————"

and what looked back at him, terrified, were the sharp-featured, reptilian eyes of something. The moment he saw the rows of fine, sharp fangs lining its mouth and understood that they were a weapon that could easily crush him to pieces,

"—Uwaaaaaaaaah!!"

he screamed again, and this time rolled forward. A pair of human feet appeared right in front of him, and without even looking up at their owner's face, he rolled sideways and shoved aside the small shadow blocking his way.

"Beatrice!"

A high, bell-like voice cried out something desperately, but it didn't reach him — not with his ears shut against it.
He fixed his eyes on a gap in the green wall, drove his trembling legs onward, and burst out of the room. He plunged into the stone-built corridor, unable to stop himself, and his body slammed into the wall ahead.
He collided, and pain shot through him. His vision dyed red, and he heard the phantom sound of bones shattering.

"Hiih!"

The right arm he'd struck moved freely. As if to confirm whether it truly moved freely, he struck it against the wall. Pain answered him. His shriek cracked and broke.
Then, tracing the wall with his hand, he took off running on unsteady, faltering legs.

"Hah, hih, ahih...!"

Out of breath, drooling, cold sweat beading on his skin, he ran desperately, desperately down the corridor.
He felt like someone was chasing him from behind, and again and again he fought down the urge to look back. If he looked back, if he confirmed that someone really was chasing him, his heart, already fit to burst, wouldn't be able to bear it.

He had a premonition that his heart would rupture, the blood in his whole body would rot, and his body would shatter from the feet up like a piece of glasswork.
Whether that was a premonition or the truth, one thing was clear regardless — the absolute, unshakeable fact that, at this rate, he would die.

A person is ruined from the moment of birth.
Aging with every second, heading toward death with every second, falling toward the end with every second.
But to him, right now, that kind of abstract, conceptual "death" was meaningless.
In a far more extreme form, "death" was drawing near, step by certain step, lapping at his heels. It was hunting him down, as if to mock him for his graceless, unsightly struggling to flee.

He ran. He fled. He simply fled.
"Death" was chasing him. He would be killed. He would be killed. At this rate, he would be killed.
He should already have been killed, and yet he was still about to be killed. Or perhaps, even after being dealt that much pain, a person simply couldn't die? Was that the mystery of life? If so, it was, if anything, appalling.
A pretty word like "mystery" didn't suit it. It was an appalling, ugly attachment.

He should have simply been dead already, shouldn't he—

"—!"

His heartbeat, like an explosion, rattled his eardrums, and his vision flickered and flashed. To think he could escape the terror of death with a grasp on living more incompetent than a newborn's — one who had even forgotten how to breathe — was laughably absurd.
Absurd as it was, no laughter came. Everything, everything, was an object of terror.

"—————"

Ahead of him, an unchanging stone corridor stretched on, so featureless he was nearly losing track of where he even was.
What he knew was this: he mustn't go back. And yet, if he kept fleeing with nothing to guide him, sooner or later he'd run out of road and lose his way.
If he lost his way, someone would catch him. If someone caught him, his life would be taken.
Why, how, was his own life — such questions were, right now, of no use whatsoever.

Right or left — there was no time to hesitate over which way to go.
He simply relied on the wall his right hand brushed against, fleeing from a "death" that might catch up to him at any moment, gasping desperately for breath.

It felt just like drowning.
On dry land, with no water anywhere, and yet he felt like he was thrashing toward the surface of the water.

Drowning, drowning, thrashing the way a drowning thing thrashes, aiming for a water's surface — flailing, flailing, flailing, struggling and struggling and struggling, until at last—

"—The hell're you doin' here this early in the mornin', huh."

"—————"

Right in front of him, he sensed a terrifyingly enormous presence, and his feet stopped.
—No, it wasn't only his feet that stopped.

His breath stopped, the heartbeat that had been so unbearably loud went quiet, the trembling of his knees, weak with exhaustion and fear, stopped, and it felt as if every last function of his living body had been seized by the scruff of the neck.

—There was an illusion, standing before him, that what stood there was a giant.
But after a few seconds passed, it slowly sank in that, tall as it was, this was no build that had leapt beyond the bounds of human proportion.
And at the same time came the realization that a human being, a mere living creature, could carry an aura this savage, this ferocious.

"Alone, huh, you? A fry showin' up alone ain't much of a story. 'Cept a fry ain't a 'person,' one o' you — it's a 'fish,' one of ya. Still ain't much of a story even as a fish. Go home, and come back with yesterday's flunkies and some fine women, ya hear. Oi, you listenin', huh? Oi, you. Oi. You."

Like water rushing off a tilted board, the presence standing before him hurled words at him without pause.
Struck by an air of terrible violence and words steeped in some kind of careless, offhand emotion, his breath, his heartbeat, the trembling of his knees — all of which should have stopped — started up again.

—He had wandered somewhere he should never have gone: into the cage of a ferocious beast.

He had only been fleeing, desperately, the whole time.
He had raced through the stone corridors, trying to put distance between himself and anywhere he recognized. As a result, in an unfamiliar room, he'd found a staircase leading upward, and clung to it.
Perhaps he'd even been told about that staircase — yesterday, or some day like yesterday, or a few hours ago, whenever it had been — but it was lost now, beyond recall.

He climbed the long, long, far too long staircase, out of breath, climbing, and then.
And at the top, where he arrived, he found himself glared down at by the most terrifying beast of all,

"Oi, you listenin'?"

"Hyih."

Before he knew it, the man's face was close enough that he could feel his breath.
Long red hair, a black eyepatch over his left eye, a kimono worn loose enough to bare one shoulder, white bindings visible beneath — and for whatever reason, he was pressing the tip of a thin wooden stick right up against Subaru's nose.
That tip, not sharp in the least, felt to him like "death" itself, leveled at him.

"—!"

"Oi, don't you go ignorin' me, you."

"Ha, ah, wha—?"

Without a second thought, obeying nothing but instinct, he tried to turn his back and flee.
And yet, the instant he tried to run backward, somehow he slammed face-first into the man's chest. His head snapped back, pushed by the wooden stick, and he fell over backward.
The back of his head struck the floor, and sparks scattered across his vision. Tears welled up from the pain.
The pain of the collision, the pain of impact — it brought the pain of being shattered flooding back with ease.

"Ah, ah, ahhh..."

"Oi, oi, you. Don't tell me you're cryin'. What's got you bawlin' like a baby, huh, you? Get in a fight with your buddies down there or somethin'? Got talked down and now you're cryin', that it, you?"

"Guh, hih, uu, uuuuu!"

"Tch, what a hopeless bastard, oi."

Still lying on his back, he began sobbing, fear rekindling within him. Watching this, the long-haired man scratched roughly at his own head.
Then, crouching down right beside the head of the boy lying there, he said,

"Oi, tell me what happened. If ya want someone to listen, I'll listen."

"...Uh, huh?"

"You went and ran all the way here. Must be somethin' pretty serious, huh, you."

He blinked over and over, unable to quite grasp the meaning of the words delivered with a sigh.
All at once, the presence that had seemed nothing but pure malice until then seemed to slowly take on a definite shape. Not merely human in form, but taking on, in real substance, human feeling and human bearing.
In his tear-blurred vision, the man's face looking down at him gradually came into focus—

"—Like hell I'd say that, idiot."

"Gih, gah, aaaaaaah!?"

—With the ferocious look of a blood-hungry shark, the man twisted the wooden stick straight into his chest.

In that instant, the tip of the stick slid between his ribs, and he could feel it poking, tickling the delicate organs that should have been protected by bone — gently, as if toying with them, viscerally, as if tending them with care.
That alone sent agony that could quite literally make him spit blood tearing through his entire body.

"What, you go runnin' off, you. And on top of that, you run straight to me — the hell d'you think you're doin', you. I ain't your guardian, ain't your buddy, ain't nothin' to you, you. You're the one who picks who ya run with, ain't ya, you. You wanna die?"

"Gih! Gah! Agih! Guhgyaaah!"

"You think this is the kinda relationship that gets settled nice and friendly, with a little talk, you? If that'd do it, they wouldn't've called me out here in the first place. Come at me meanin' to kill. I'll play along. That's the only way this ends right."

Even as clear irritation and dark emotion built with every word he spoke, his anger mounting line by line, the fingertips toying with his insides boasted a precision that could only be called delicate, or perhaps meticulous.
He understood this precisely because the searing pain was driving home, all at once, just how precious and vital his own organs were.

If the man had been even the slightest bit more prone to being swept away by emotion, or if his skill had fallen even the smallest degree short of matching his brutal conduct, Subaru's insides would already have burst apart.

That it hadn't happened was down to the man's terrifying talent.
An overwhelming gift, as a tormentor, for violence and the skill to wield it.
Only because of that gift could this savagery unfold, precisely, exactly like this.

—No. This place was too different, far too different, from any place he knew.

"Get lost, fry."

"Gah."

The sensation of his organs being prodded withdrew, and then the man's foot kicked roughly into his side.
It was less a kick than a way of scooping him up on the top of his foot and hurling him. His body floated up lightly, tumbled with no way to resist, and was ejected from the wide space.
But what awaited the body as it rolled and flew out of the room was,

"N-no, nooo...!"

A vision of himself tumbling down the stairs flashed before him, and without meaning to, he dug his nails into the floor.
A twisted sound, and the nails of his right hand, which had tried to grip the floor — the middle finger's and the ring finger's — tore away at the root. Exposed nerve and red blood soaked the floor, but somehow, his body escaped the fall.

"Guh, guhh...!"

His relief at somehow escaping the fall lasted only a moment before he was assailed by a searing sensation originating at his fingertips. When the sharp pain made him look at his hand, he saw two nails peeled back like the lid of a box thrown open, and beside them, the nail of his little finger half torn away as well — the visual horror of the pain spurring his brain into overdrive.

"It hurts... it hurts, it hurts, it huuurts..."

He gripped the fingers of his right hand, their nails peeled back, in his left, squeezing to ease the pain even the slightest bit. Blood dripped down, running along his wrist and falling to the floor, and, leaving a trail of blood behind, he got to his feet on the stairs.

He had no courage left to look back.
There was no sign that the man, that ferocious beast, had turned his attention toward him at all. If he had been so much as glanced at, surely this time he wouldn't have been able to bear it, and would have tumbled down the stairs.

Stopped one step short of the worst possible outcome, settled instead into the second-worst — now what.

"Why..."

Why was he in a place like this?
His feet forgot how to flee, his heartbeat, swallowed by terror, went numb, and only then did his mind finally turn to the meaninglessness, the sheer strangeness, of his own real existence in this place.

He should have been shattered to pieces, and yet, here he was.
He should have been swallowed by the searing heat and erased, and yet, still, here he was.

If only all of it had been a dream, an illusion — something like that.

"A premonition... dream..."

That was how he had assumed things that had happened to him.
Because scenes he had seen before, people he had met before, conversations he remembered having, events he should already have passed through — all of it had passed before his eyes again.
He tried, in his own way, to reason through why it had happened, and that was the conclusion he reached.
Surely, somewhere, he had thought of even that with the vague detachment of a fire on the far shore, as if it were someone else's problem.

Never knowing that he would pay for that shallow, thoughtless reasoning with agony beyond description.

"—————"

Before he knew it, he had crouched down right where he stood.
He could see the unstoppable drops of blood, falling one by one, soaking red into the stone steps.

Futility, loss, disappointment — nothing but bad thoughts, one after another, circled through his mind.
Those thoughts, going in circles, all boiled down, in the end, to the same thing — he didn't understand why this was happening to him.

"—————"

Just a few hours ago, he had been living in warm, languid, tedious days.
No danger of any kind, the worst of his worries at most his own hopeless future, no one threatening him, nothing he had to take seriously.

—A place where all he had to do was keep his head down under his father's and mother's gaze.

Had that been what was wrong?
He had gone on burdening his father and mother. Gone on disappointing them. He hadn't managed to be a good son.
So was that why he was tasting agony fit to kill him, and still, even so, driven into a situation where he couldn't even die — his nails torn off and aching, tortured by a strange man, crying alone on a staircase?

If he was going to go through something like this, he should have, if anything, done it properly, at least—

"...I should've said 'I'm off,' at least."

It had been a life of nothing but regrets.
Nothing but failures, nothing but things gone wrong — looking back, there were more things he wished he could redo than he had fingers and toes to count them on — but that was the very first thing that came to mind.

When he'd left the house, his mother had called out, "Have a good day."
He hadn't answered.
Why?

—Because there was a cup, soaking in water in the kitchen sink, that he hadn't washed.

"Guh, huh..."

He hadn't washed the cup.
He'd had cocoa, and washing off the brown residue caked onto the cup had seemed like a hassle.

If he'd answered his mother's voice, if it had turned into a conversation, she might have told him to wash the cup. So he hadn't answered. Because he hadn't wanted to wash the cup.

Because he hadn't wanted to wash the cup, he had ignored his mother's words.
He'd said nothing. He'd left the house without saying anything, headed to the convenience store, spent money he hadn't even earned himself, and then, before he knew it, he was in a place like this.

Without a word to his mother, or his father, without washing the cup, he was in a place like this.

Without even washing a single cup, without a word to his kind mother, in a place like this, he was about to die.
He had burdened them, given nothing back, and now, without even washing the cup, he was going to die.

He would die, he was going to die. He would surely die. Without a doubt, he would die.
Falling from a place that high, there was no surviving it. He would absolutely die.

"...I'm going to die."

He was going to die.
Every living thing eventually dies, but he — he was going to die here.
In a place like this, where there wasn't a single soul he knew.
With no father, no mother, surrounded by strangers he'd never met, he was going to die, a filthy lump of blood.

"I'm going to die. I'm going to die. I'm... going to die. Die, die, die..."

He murmured it. And murmuring it, strangely, seemed to push it just the slightest bit farther away.
It was a small comfort. A trick of the mind. Saying it aloud wasn't going to change anything dramatically.

"Death" still stood there unchanged, smiling as it waited to entangle his fate.
Now, it looked to him, clearly, to have taken on a human shape. A black shadow, standing up, seeming to laugh at him mockingly.
A face was affixed to that black shadow, and it was a face he knew — and wondering whose familiar face it could possibly be in a place like this, he realized at once.

"Death," wearing his own face, was pointing at him, laughing at him as he trembled.

"Don't laugh."

He glared at the shadow, and said it with black, seething hatred.
The shadow's grin didn't stop. It didn't stop pointing, either.

"Don't laugh. Don't you dare laugh. Don't you dare... laugh...!"

He got to his feet, gritted his teeth, and, tracing the wall with his hand, walked toward the shadow.
The shadow's grin didn't stop. It didn't stop pointing, either.

"Don't laugh. Stop it. I'm going to die. Not by you. I'm not going to be killed by you."

The embodiment of "death," for the first time, twisted its expression.
It looked as if it had grown angry that something that belonged to it wouldn't obey it.
That seemed to be the shadow's weakness, and he pressed the attack for all he was worth.

"I won't be killed by you. I'm going to die. Sure, I'm going to die! I'm going to die! I died! I already died! I died, and came back here, but I — I won't let you—"

—Kill me.

That was what he was about to say, clearly, plainly, the instant it happened.

"—————"

His lips stopped moving as he willed them to. Next he noticed his eyes, glaring at the shadow, losing all freedom of movement, and a sense of loss as his own body slipped entirely beyond his control.
Even the question of why was sealed away from him, and he could only wait, helplessly, for whatever abnormal change was coming.

It wouldn't move. His body. —No, it wasn't just his body that wouldn't move. The world itself had stopped.
Even the black shadow before him had stopped moving, frozen mid-snarl, its face twisted in fury.

And yet, in that stopped world, leaving behind him, unable to move, there was one single thing that still moved.
It was—

"—I love you."

—It was, he thought, probably, a woman in black.

A black woman, her entire body dyed one solid shade of black, with a slender, willowy figure.
Whether the black had taken the shape of a woman, or the woman was wearing the black, wasn't clear, and there didn't seem to be any point in deciding which.
In any case, it was a woman in black. She wore black robes like a bridal gown, and a black veil, through which her face could be seen from neither within nor without, hid her features.

"—I love you."

But what the black woman's lips wove together was an emotion of an intensity beyond all imagining.
Just how much feeling would have to be boiled down, reduced, to come anywhere near the words spilling from those lips?
It was quality, and quantity, and time, and weight, and worth, and concept, all at once.
There was no telling how many beings in the world had ever spoken the words "I love you" — but if you gathered every single one of those "I love you"s together, surely, the sum would be this woman's "I love you."

And then the woman whispering her love slowly extended her black arm toward his chest.
Her slender fingertips passed through his chest, through skin, through flesh, through bone, and drew close to the beating heart within.

"—————"

Just a few minutes, maybe ten-odd minutes, he had no way of telling the time, but again and again since waking he had been made aware of the existence of his own heart — and yet, never had he been so aware of it as in this very moment.
Never had he found its existence so infuriating.
Because—

"—I love you."

With the same passion as the whispered words of love, the woman's black fingers caressed his heart.
And at the same time, a shock that pierced clean through utterly, completely subjugated this body that feared pain. Having his whole body shattered by the impact of the fall, having his soul seared by an unquenchable heat, even the way his heart had been worn thin by guilt toward his mother — before this pain, all of it felt like nothing more than dust.

If he could have screamed, he wished he'd been allowed to.
If he could have screamed until his throat tore, he could have done something, at least a little, against the pain. Not by facing the pain head-on, but by turning his attention to something other than pain, he might have escaped it.
He couldn't do that. He was only made to face the pain, directly.

"—I love you."

The woman's love wouldn't let go of his heart.
It was as if an inexhaustible possessiveness was driving her, forbidding him to turn his interest toward anything but herself.
—As if jealousy toward every single thing in existence was what drove her to it.

"—Hah."

The release came abruptly.

"—————"

He let out a breath, and collapsed right where he stood.
Tears spilled, one after another, and at last, right there, he wet himself. A lukewarm sensation soaked his groin, and urine trickled down the stairs, flowing downward.

At that graceless, shameful sight, the black shadow, still frozen in place, pointed and laughed loudly.

Watching it laugh, he realized he'd been played.
He'd been tricked into thinking that if it showed a moment of weakness like that, he would leap at it without thinking, and step on the tiger's tail he should never have stepped on.

"Me..."

The words that should have followed never became words.
He held his head in his hands. Blood still flowed from the wound where his nails had been torn away. The tears, the urine flowing down the stairs, everything, felt like punishment for his own weakness and foolishness.

—Just kill me, then.

Those words never became a voice.
Even if he were killed, could he really, truly, "be killed"?


Until the sound of footsteps and a worried voice came running up the stairs—
Covered in filth and despair, he simply kept crying, like a foolish child.


That — the wreckage of Natsuki Subaru — kept on crying.
