Arc 7 Chapter 56B: "Flying Dragon Cataclysm"

——The instant he saw the girl standing in the billowing smoke, an alarm bell rang in Flop's head.

Not the real alarm bell echoing through Guaral, but the one rung by his own survival instinct as a living creature. It clanged so loudly his head ached.

To put it plainly, Flop was not a man who could fight, unlike Medium. Even when he'd tried to train his body a little, he'd given up partway through, and he'd never thought to steal away the role his sister had chosen for herself after she'd realized, stroking her brother's wounds, that she'd actually been hurting him. So he had none of a warrior's instincts, none of a martial artist's discerning eye.

And yet, even so—

"That girl is bad news."

He swallowed a bitter mouthful of spit at the sight of the girl pulling her leg free of the hole her fall had punched into the ground.

"――――"

A short girl, golden eyes narrowing their pupils as she surveyed Flop and the others with contempt.
She looked to be two or three years older than Schult or Utakata, but the atmosphere she wore about her was nothing like Medium's or Mizelda's—no, it was on par with something far more dangerous than that. Close, even, to the threat posed by the "Nine Divine Generals" who had taken Mizelda's leg.

"...Red-hair, can you manage to create an opening for us somehow? I'd like to get at least the young butler and Miss Utakata away from here, one way or another."

The one Flop addressed in a low voice was Heinkel, the only one present who could actually serve as fighting strength. Heinkel, who had cut down a flying dragon and proven his blade's worth—he could do far more to protect Utakata and the others than Flop ever could as a mere meat shield.
Ideally, of course, it would be best if Heinkel were strong enough to defeat that girl outright.

"――ッ"

"Red-hair?"

Still wary of the girl, Flop found himself puzzled by Heinkel's lack of response. He was reluctant to tear his eyes from the threat before him, but coordinating with Heinkel was the top priority. Steeling himself, Flop stole a glance at Heinkel's state.

——Heinkel's eyes were wide, his pale face beaded with a torrent of cold sweat.

"H-Heinkel-sama? Are you all right, sir?"

"Uh, ah..."

Flop noticed Heinkel's abnormal state at almost the same moment Schult did. Schult voiced his concern for Heinkel plainly, and at the small boy's words, the red-haired swordsman's cheeks went taut, a voiceless sound leaking from his throat.
Whether this was a reaction to recognizing the girl's identity, Flop couldn't tell from the side. But regardless of that, one glaringly obvious fact remained.
And that was—

"What's with you? Are you afraid?"

The girl's narrowed gaze fell on the feet of the frozen Heinkel—on legs trembling so badly it seemed a wonder he was even still standing.
What was there, plain for anyone to see, was fear and terror. Despair, in other words.

"Well now! Young lady, whom I'm meeting for the first time—might I trouble you to hear me out, just a little!"

The instant he glimpsed Heinkel's despair, Flop immediately raised his voice like that.
At once, Heinkel and Schult's shoulders jumped, and the suspicious eyes of the girl in front of them turned this way. It wasn't as if a look could kill, but the oppressive weight behind it made it seem like it might as well be able to.
Still, Flop screwed up his courage, loosened his cheeks, spread both hands, and forced a smile.

"I'm Flop O'Connell, a humble traveling merchant. I find myself rather swept up and bewildered by quite the predicament here. You seem to know more about the situation than I do, yes?"

"...Caught in the crossfire? Then you're unlucky."

"Oh ho, 'unlucky' isn't a word I'm used to hearing applied to me. I lack for plenty of things, but luck is the one thing I pride myself on having in spades. Why would you say I'm unlucky?"

"Everyone in this city can be killed. That's what the old fossil told me."

"Everyone... in the city."

Jutting her small chin, the girl answered while gesturing at the surroundings, and Flop gave a wry smile. Inwardly, though, "wry smile" didn't begin to cover it—he was in an absolute panic.
If wiping out the city's population was the objective, where was there room for negotiation? If only he could speak with the "old fossil" who'd given her that order.

"From what I've heard, that old fossil of yours apparently said wiping out the whole city was a mistake. Why not ask the friends and comrades you came here with, just to make sure?"

"I have no friends, no comrades. Among dragons there are only kin and enemies. ...You're mocking dragons, aren't you. You don't seem to know fear."

"Mocking you? Perish the thought! I'm hoping we can become friends, in fact. As a first step, I'd love to know your name. Wouldn't that be nice?"

"Y-yes it would! I want to know your name too! I'm Schult, ma'am!"

Flop clapped his hands and appealed to his ally as the girl's mood threatened to sour further. Schult seized on the opening, raising a hand and announcing his own name.
Having thus received self-introductions from both Flop and Schult, the girl's lovely brow furrowed, and—

"—Madelyn Eschart. This dragon is one of the 'Nine Divine Generals.'"

"Ni—nngh!"

Heinkel choked on his words at the flatly-delivered title of the girl who'd introduced herself in return—Madelyn.
His knees, already trembling from merely standing opposite her, only worsened at hearing the title backing up her strength. It was a miracle he hadn't fallen on his backside.
Flop, for his part, was in no position to laugh at Heinkel. The opponent he'd judged dangerous enough to be a "Nine Divine General" had turned out to actually be one.
And what's more—

"—Miss Utakata."

Quietly, having noticed a shift in the situation, Flop called the name of the one who'd caused it.
The girl he called to, Utakata, had been moving while staring straight at Madelyn before her. She'd lowered the bow from her back, nocked an arrow, and taken aim at Madelyn.
The distance between them was seven or eight meters—close enough that even her arm wouldn't miss.
That said, it didn't mean they now held the advantage. Not remotely.

"That enemy, top of flying dragon pack. Same as Mee before, same as Tar now. Beat that enemy, fighting ends."

"I-Is that so!?"

"Most likely, Miss Utakata's reasoning is correct. Assuming no other 'Nine Divine General' has come besides her, that is."

Flop glanced over, and Madelyn didn't interject on that point. Given her behavior so far, she was the type to react in some way if something plainly wrong were said.
Which meant Madelyn was the one leading this attack on the walled city. Utakata's read that defeating her would call off the flying dragons was likely correct.
The only problem was that defeating her seemed utterly impossible.

"You are warrior, you are warrior too, you too... not run. Warrior."

"Eh... me, ma'am?"

"Yes. You are warrior too."

Pointed at without warning and evaluated so, Schult's eyes went round.
Madelyn, having startled Schult, pointed her small hand at Utakata, Flop, and Schult in turn, calling all three "warriors."
Then Madelyn's finger swung toward the last person—Heinkel—and—

"You are not warrior. You killed dragon, yet you are coward."

"Guh...!"

"Draw sword. Dragon will strike you. As much as the weight of the blood you spilled."

As she spoke, Madelyn slowly advanced a step toward them.
And so, step by step, paying no mind to being targeted by Utakata's arrow, Madelyn closed the distance, heading straight for Heinkel, who had killed a flying dragon.

"—Hit him!"

Along with her resolve, Utakata's arrow was loosed, aimed at Madelyn's chest. But the arrow, flying straight and true, was caught between two fingers of Madelyn's raised hand.
Madelyn didn't even look toward Utakata. Her gaze was fixed straight on Heinkel.

"Red-hair!"
"Heinkel-sama!"

Sensing the danger, Flop shouted, and Schult tried to pull Heinkel's arm. But Schult's feeble strength couldn't budge Heinkel an inch.
If anything, Heinkel, throat clicking at Madelyn's approach, shook off the arm pulling at him and pushed Schult away.
Schult fell on his backside, and Heinkel ground his back teeth.

"O-o-oooohh...!"

Tremendous, tremendous force gathered in Heinkel's hand, the one gripping his sword. His clenched jaw reddened his cheeks, and the surge of blood meant to rouse his own courage tried head-on to wring out the cowardice making his legs shake.
Still, Madelyn didn't stop, closing the distance to Heinkel.

Compared to a flying dragon, the girl approaching him was incomparably small in size.
Against that approaching girl, Heinkel raised the blow he'd staked his swordsmanship on—

"—Ohh."

With a high ringing sound, the sword tumbled from Heinkel's trembling hand.
At the very moment his full-strength technique should have been unleashed, his arm hadn't lifted, and the sword had slipped free.
And then—

"Just as I thought—you are not a warrior."

Madelyn's utterly contemptuous words were driven into the side of Heinkel's face along with her fist.
The blow struck Heinkel's sturdy tall frame and sent the red-haired swordsman flying sideways with terrific force. He slammed full-body into a stone building, cracking the stone wall with the imprint of his form.

A punch wrapped in a fierce gale, powerful enough to roll Heinkel's eyes white in one blow. She had just delivered a strike rivaling the shock and roar of the flying dragons' repeated stone-throwing against the city with her fist alone—and yet Madelyn's fury did not subside.

"Kah."

Madelyn's other fist punched through the face of the toppling-forward Heinkel. Slammed back against the wall by the back of his head, he rebounded, and the girl's kick pierced through his torso as he bounced back.
The shock passed clean through, and behind Heinkel a house, already exposed to destruction, collapsed with a roar. Grabbing the leg of Heinkel, now engulfed in the smoke, Madelyn hurled him forcibly the other way.

Heinkel's body fell back-first onto the street and rolled with great momentum. The building across the street from the one that had just collapsed caught him hard, this time.
The impact shattered its windows to pieces, and the shards rained down on Heinkel like rain. As fine cuts bloomed all over his body, the fallen Heinkel did not so much as twitch.

"――――"

For Flop, that would have been a lethal attack. For most people besides Flop, too, it was an attack that would kill. Even whether Medium could withstand such a barrage was uncertain, and Heinkel's life or death now hung unknown.
But Madelyn, whose trade was war, would never allow so incomplete a conclusion.

"Pathetic coward."

Muttering without hiding her contempt, Madelyn's hand reached to her own back. The button of the pack she carried there came undone, and immediately after, what had been stowed inside opened with a clatter.
It was a folded, sharp blade—an enormous weapon shaped like a great pair of open shears. Flop had a memory of seeing something similar used in the hunting practices of nomadic peoples crossing the wide, vast grasslands.
Called a "Flying Winged Blade," it was a throwing weapon that, when thrown by a skilled hand, would arc through dozens of meters and spin its way back to the thrower's palm.

However, the one on Madelyn's back was as large as the small girl was tall herself, and fitted with a handle for uses other than throwing—a razor-edged weapon through and through.
She turned it without mercy toward Heinkel, sprawled on the ground. That was the state he was in from bare-handed blows. If struck by a weapon, surely nothing would remain of his original shape.

"Won't you stop, Miss Madelyn. I understand your anger. Your comrade was killed, and you must be burning with rage. But when attacked, the attacked party can't help but say counterattack was unavoidable."

Biting his lip hard to steel himself, Flop said this to Madelyn's back. Still facing away, holding the Flying Winged Blade, she answered Flop with "Don't misunderstand," and—

"Dragons are the greatest creatures in this world. If you humans bite at dragons, it's only natural you get exterminated for it. And on top of that—"

"On top of that?"

"That such a pathetic coward's handiwork could do this—unforgivable."

Madelyn's golden eyes wavered with anger, and Flop understood the reason for her fury.
Of course, she was angry that an allied flying dragon had been killed, but far greater was the fact that the one who'd done it—Heinkel—had dropped his sword and been unable to fight.
She was enraged that her comrade had been killed by such an unworthy opponent.
That, to her, was an insult not only to Heinkel but to the very life of the flying dragon he had killed—

"Uwah!"
"U!"

In that instant, before Flop could move to stop Madelyn's action, she kicked a pebble on the ground toward them first. It struck the leg of Flop, who'd begun to move, and the arm of Utakata, who'd been about to nock her next arrow, hindering both of their movements.
In that gap, Madelyn raised the Flying Winged Blade, intending to crush the Heinkel lying below.
But—

"N-no, you mustn't—!"

Unlike the two who'd been struck by the pebble, Schult, who'd been outside her watch, threw himself forward in desperation. The young boy spread both arms and inserted himself between Madelyn and Heinkel.
Schult's small body draped itself over Heinkel, a far too fragile shield standing against the storm.
But Madelyn was not gentle enough to stay her hand at the sight of the boy's recklessness.

"Either way, I won't let anyone live."

Madelyn's eyes narrowed, and the Flying Winged Blade came swinging down toward Schult's head.

"—Rem! Are you all right!?"

Throwing open the great door, a slender woman burst into the mansion and raised her voice.
At that familiar voice, Rem, who had been dashing about the hall, lifted her face and answered "Yes!"

"Kuna-san! Holly-san too!"

"Glad you're all right, nooo! The whole city's in an uproar, real bad, nooo!"

Before the wide-eyed Rem, two sets of footsteps—one light, one heavy—rushed up. It was the pair of "People of Shudrak" stationed in the city, Kuna and Holly.
Hearing their voices, Rem's eyes relaxed slightly in relief, and—

"I'm glad you two are safe as well. You're not hurt anywhere?"

"We're fine over here, no worries, at least for now. But..."

"...Everyone's gotten all beat up, nooo."

Their voices dropping, the two women surveyed the inside of the hall Rem had been running through and let the words slip.
Around the three of them, victims from the chaos beginning to grip the city were being carried in one after another, injured, and volunteers were helping to treat them—it had the look of an infirmary.
If the wounds were shallow, stitches or bandages could handle them. But if someone had suffered a wound severe enough to threaten their life—

"I'm the one who has to answer for that..."

Biting her lip hard, Rem clenched her own hand.
On her right hand—the one opposite the one gripping her staff—blood not her own had already dried and caked. Blood that no amount of wiping could remove, layered on thicker than she could wipe it away.

The moment Rem and Priscilla had spotted the flying dragon swarm from the bathhouse terrace, the black dots closing in from afar had turned into a threat in the blink of an eye and begun their assault on the city.
Some of the flying dragons dropped great boulders from the sky; others snatched up fleeing people from the ground, carrying out the cruel hunt of dropping them again from great heights.
Against either kind of attack, citizens with no power to fight were nothing but easy prey.

The people exposed to that devastation were now rushing here, to this mansion serving as a shelter and infirmary at once—that was the reason for its current state of chaos.
In such a state, surely Priscilla would be furious, Rem had thought—but—

"To begin with, we seized this mansion precisely so the whereabouts of the precious healer would be both clear and secure. I've also ordered Zikr and the others to send the wounded here."

"Th-that's what it was...!?"

"What, you—did you truly think I seized this mansion out of a genuine desire for a bath? Correctly speaking, I wanted the bath, and the mansion's spaciousness."

...and I'll add that such an exchange did occur.
In any case, Priscilla was permitting the wounded to be brought into the mansion. It seemed she'd conveyed as much to Zikr, Mizelda, and the other key figures in the city as well.

"Then I don't know why you couldn't have told me that, of all people, when it mattered!"

Sensing she wouldn't get a decent answer, Rem didn't pursue it further.
More than anything, Rem had no time to indulge Priscilla's childishness or mischief. After all, the wounded requiring her hands kept being carried in, one after another, without a moment's pause.

"You two, Kuna-san and Holly-san, are..."

"Chief... ah! Not anymore, nooo! We came 'cause the former chief, Mizelda, told us to, nooo!"

"More corpses, morale drops. Fewer able to fight, that's a problem too—so we're Rem's babysitters, we are. More important than that..."

There, Kuna cut herself off, narrowing her sharp-cut eyes. At that keen edge, Rem stiffened slightly, and Kuna jerked her chin at the mansion full of the wounded.

"Where's that princess you're tendin' to? Don't tell me, with all this ruckus goin' on, she's off relaxin' in her room somewhere."

"If that's true, she's a real sleepyhead, nooo! Even I'd wake up on a full stomach if it got this noisy, nooo!"

Asked with subtly different degrees of urgency, Rem shook her head at both their questions.
Certainly, Priscilla was a woman whose very existence embodied arrogance and self-centeredness, so Rem understood why the two of them worried about her whims.

"Believe it or not, Priscilla-san is actually someone you can rely on."

Just as Rem finished evaluating the nowhere-to-be-seen Priscilla like that—

"——!!"

A high shriek, like a strong wind cutting between buildings, plummeted straight down into the mansion's front garden.
Kicking up a tremendous cloud of dust as it gouged the lawn, it was the enormous body of a winged dragon that had failed to land from above. Spreading its wings, the flying dragon—some four meters across—botched its landing with a clumsiness as if it had only just learned to fly.

But that was hardly surprising.
The basics of landing, which it would normally have judged by experience and instinct, had likely been beyond its ability to properly execute with its head crushed as it was.
And then, right beside the corpse of the crashed flying dragon, a figure landed from the heavens. The hem of her red dress fluttering, a crimson sacred sword hanging from her hand—the beautiful woman, Priscilla.

"To use me for clearing the way ahead—this debt shall come at a steep price, Rem."

"Thank you so much. With deepest gratitude, I'll wash your hair for you."

"To think that would settle it—what audacity. I permit it. I'm pleased."

Answering magnanimously through the open door, Priscilla accepted Rem's response.
For some time now, Priscilla had been like this, wielding her preposterous physical abilities to cut down flying dragons swooping toward the mansion, turning their attacks right back on them.
Witnessing even a fragment of this, even Kuna and the others couldn't hide their astonishment.

Rem, seeing the two react this way, felt an oddly proud sensation. And yet, facing a flying dragon like this, Priscilla was not entirely free of concern.
The biggest factor being the beautiful crimson sacred sword in her hand—

"Priscilla-san, how is the Sunlight Sword's condition..."

"As you can see, the sun has waned. For a while yet, this is nothing but a dull blade unworthy of the name 'sword.' However—"

Revealing some unspecified malfunction of the "Sunlight Sword," Priscilla turned her back at that moment.
In that instant, a flapping flying dragon aimed for Priscilla's back, opening its mouth to sink its fangs in with ferocious intent. Into that Earth Dragon's open maw, Priscilla mercilessly thrust the Sunlight Sword, its edge dulled though it was.
Its fangs snapped, the tip of the Sunlight Sword pierced through the back of the flying dragon's throat, and the dragon died instantly from the momentum.

"In my hands, even a blade too dull to cut is more than enough for a winged dragon."

Swinging her sword, Priscilla flung the massive corpse of the slain flying dragon to the edge of the garden. Having so effortlessly repelled the threat, she then noticed Kuna and the others standing beside Rem.

"Shudrak, are you. No mistaking it—you've come to guard Rem."

"Priscilla-san, the two of them came to help the wounded..."

"Don't mistake the essence of the matter for pretty words. What use is one who's taken a wound, on a battlefield? Who here holds the greatest value? If not I, then you."

"—Ah!"

Cut off sharply by the retort, Rem's throat caught. But as if affirming Priscilla's cold-blooded assessment, Kuna nodded, "That's right, huh."

"Protecting Rem is our job. And Rem's job..."

"Making sure as few people die as possible, nooo!"

Kuna's quiet words were reinforced by Holly's cheerful ones.
Burned by their words and by Priscilla's gaze, Rem sternly disciplined herself. Feeling sorry for herself, believing her position wasn't so important—that had ended yesterday.
She could no longer coddle herself in her own position. More than anything, Rem herself had wished for this.

"I will fight my own fight. Kuna-san, Holly-san, I'm counting on you both!"

"Yeah."
"Leave it to us, nooo!"

At Rem's resolute answer, Kuna and the others nodded with reassuring smiles.
Her heart bolstered by the two of them, Rem turned to look at Priscilla. She had been defending the mansion until reinforcements capable of guarding Rem arrived—but—

"You're leaving, aren't you."

"It seems there are more scenes than I can spare where things won't proceed without me. Zikr should know how to make the most of what limited forces remain, but he lacks what's needed to stand against the enemy's main force."

"The enemy's main force, meaning..."

"—Naturally, one of the 'Nine Divine Generals,' I would think."

At Priscilla's answer, one eye closed, Rem's throat clicked audibly.
The existence of the "Nine Divine Generals" was a decisive factor that could sway the fate of this empire's war. To secure even one more of them, Subaru, Abel, and the others had set off for Chaosflame.
And yet, that one of the "Nine Divine Generals" would be attacking this city—

"Naturally, this too must be one who's cast their lot with the other side. Abel's position only looks worse and worse. What a shoddy foundation he's built, to be sure."

"On that point, I don't know enough about Abel-san's work to say anything. If he's been going around acting like that, I imagine things have been hard on those around him, but..."

Rem had plenty of complicated feelings about Subaru, but she didn't think Abel's conduct deserved much praise either. A man whose arrogance stood out, justified by his cleverness, foresight, and grasp of circumstances—Abel was the sort of person Rem, lacking memories to stand on, found difficult to deal with.
If he'd once stood at the pinnacle of a nation, surely a great many people had once surrounded him. She couldn't imagine every single one of them had understood his thinking and followed obediently.
Hence the rebellion that had been raised against him. And yet—

"To use that as an excuse for something this terrible to be allowed to happen...!"

This mustn't be allowed—Rem's gaze, building herself up on that fragile foundation, pleaded as much.
At that squeezed-out cry from Rem, Priscilla, for whatever reason, gave a small laugh—"Heh." At the sound, Rem's eyes widened, and Priscilla, as if that laugh had never happened, turned her back and said—

"This swarm of flying dragons can't be explained by mere 'Flying Dragon Handling.' In all likelihood, it's one of the Generals sprouted up while I was away. Going by Serena's account, it would be the one called the 'Flying Dragon General.'"

"That would be... the one who's 'Nine'... they're somewhere in this city too, aren't they..."

"—The municipal hall."

"――――"

"If the enemy isn't a fool, that's what they'd target. After all, it's the one place a straight line through the sky can reach directly. There's no reason to overlook the command post."

Said as if it were only natural, Rem felt for a moment a blank spot cut into her awareness.
But once information flooded into that blank space, the urgency of the situation struck her chest at once. What further threw Rem into a panic was Priscilla's leisurely, unhurried demeanor.

"T-the command post being attacked—that's terrible, isn't it! Priscilla-san!"

"Fool. As plump as he may be, Zikr is still a 'General' of the Empire in his own right. He won't die pointlessly for nothing. Nor is he so foolish as to keep only those unable to fight at his side."

"That's... ah. Speaking of unable to fight, Schult-san..."

Before starting her bath with Priscilla, the boy who had left the mansion crossed Rem's mind, and she grew anxious.
Earnest but clumsy, Schult was a fragile presence with less power than even those who needed Rem's help.
And that Schult was now cast out into this city, thrown into utter chaos.
If only he'd joined up with some reliable adult—but if he hadn't—

"Priscilla-san! Please, please go quickly!"

"The least you could do is pray dutifully for my safety. Besides, there's no need to panic so. Schult won't die that easily."

"Eh...?"

Flicking her nose dismissively at Rem's frantically waving hands, Priscilla answered with an air of indifference.
Unable to grasp the meaning of Priscilla's words, Rem furrowed her brow. Naturally, Kuna and Holly too wore faces with no clue what she meant, both picturing the same small child in confusion.
By what measure could Schult—whose only weapons were his cuteness and earnestness—be called "safe"?

Bathed in the questioning gazes of Rem and the others, Priscilla put her hand to the mansion's door, moving to shut out the flying dragon assault from here, and—

"—That one has won my favor through that very endearing charm and earnestness of his, you see."

So she answered, and the mansion's door swung shut.

A violent, dull sound rang out, and Flop tasted the sensation of his own soul being shattered by his own helplessness.

His legs had stopped from the pain of the stone; he hadn't even managed to reach out a hand. That was the tragedy of it. Even so, though he would rather have shut his eyes and turned his face away from an ending like this, Flop did not do so. To do that, he thought, would be to flee responsibility under cover of his helplessness.
At the very least, he had to witness the outcome of what he had and hadn't managed to do.
It was that small resolve that kept Flop from looking away.
And then—

"...What are you?"

Madelyn had swung the Flying Winged Blade down, meaning to cleave her prey in two with its sharp edge.
Without question, she had meant to bisect, together, the Heinkel she held in contempt and the Schult who had tried to shield him. There had been no hesitation, no stopping partway.
And yet—

"Uu, uuuu...!"

Draped tightly over the fallen Heinkel, Schult groaned through gritted teeth.
His body had neither been torn apart nor crushed. And yet Madelyn's blow had unmistakably caught the back of Schult's head—had been meant to smash it.
In fact, Flop had seen the direct hit himself, had heard with his own ears the dull thud of impact.
Except—what had rung out was, after all, only a dull thud.

"—?"

Tilting her head, Madelyn compared her weapon to the back of Schult's head with a puzzled look. Then, head still tilted, she brought the Flying Winged Blade down on Schult again.
Once, twice, three times, four—over and over in rapid succession—

"S-stop it! Can't you see he's in pain!"

"That's what's strange! I meant to kill it. Why won't it die?"

"That's... perhaps, maybe your own feelings—not wanting to kill a person—are loosening the strength in your hand right before the weapon lands... guahh."

"Don't mock the dragon!"

Protesting that it made no sense, Madelyn's fury turned on Flop.
She kicked a stone even larger than the one that had stopped Flop's movement earlier, striking it against his chest and sending him flying backward.
There was a grinding sensation in his breastbone, and Flop couldn't breathe properly.
But even so—

"It h-hurts, it hurts, ma'am...!"

As a matter of fact, Schult, struck repeatedly by the sharp blade, was groaning in pain but had not lost his life. That bizarre occurrence finally lit the fuse on Madelyn's temper.
Madelyn's teeth clicked, and her hand seized Schult's pink hair, yanking his body upright.
And then—

"You, what exactly are you hiding...!"
"~~ngh!"

Hauled up by force, Schult's throat let out an anguished cry. But Madelyn's reaction, her eyes burning with fury fixed on Schult, exceeded even that.
Madelyn's golden eyes went wide, her lips trembling. Flop, his eyes watering from pain, witnessed with his own eyes the reason for that reaction.

The hauled-up Schult—his appearance had undergone a dramatic change.
And that change was—

"—Shoo, eyes on fire."

The blunt description offered by Utakata, who'd seen the same thing, was exactly right.
At first glance, Utakata's remark made no sense, but no other words came to mind. Even to Flop's eyes, it was exactly as she'd said.

Of all things, both of Schult's red eyes were now lit with flickering flame.

"Eh, eh, eh..."

However, Schult himself, pointed out like this, seemed to have no awareness of it, blinking in confusion at the reaction around him. Schult turned his gaze about, and, trying to check for himself the face everyone was staring at, peeked into the curved blade of the Flying Winged Blade thrust before him.
His face was surely distorted in the curved reflection, but the flames burning in his own eyes—the thing in question—were apparently clearly visible.

"Wh-what is this?? Ah, it's hot! Ah! It's not hot at all!"

Putting his hands to his own face, Schult protested the lack of any sensation of the flame.
It seemed his face hadn't suddenly caught fire, but the cause was utterly unclear. Still, Flop and the others weren't the only ones bewildered by the incomprehensible sight.
Peering into Schult's face, her cheeks stiff with astonishment, Madelyn had some idea of what the flame burning in the boy's eyes meant.

Hence, she shouted that idea aloud.

"You—don't tell me you're kin to that Fox Human, are ya!?"

"Fox..."
"...Human?"
"—?"

The three of them, having no idea what Madelyn's shocked outburst meant, blinked in bewilderment.
With no room to spare to wonder at Madelyn's sudden shift in speech, she stared at Schult, struck by intense shock and confusion.
The hand gripping Schult let go, and the boy tumbled forward with a "Wah!" Once again draped over Heinkel, before the boy, Madelyn stamped hard on the ground.

"――――"

In an instant, Madelyn's figure leapt up into the sky above the street.
So sudden and forceful was it that to Flop it seemed as if she'd simply vanished. But the ground she'd kicked off from caved in violently, and the building used as a foothold for that acceleration collapsed.
Having thus risen into the sky, Madelyn swung the Flying Winged Blade back with both arms with all her might.

"Interfering with dragons again...! Disappear, ya pest——!!"

A cry of rejection rang out high overhead, and the swung-down Flying Winged Blade bore down on the ground with fearsome speed.
The mechanism was the same as the flying dragons' stone-throwing, but the force packed into it was on an entirely different order of magnitude. What's more, unlike the indiscriminately hurled boulders, the blade was aimed to bisect Schult's body precisely.

By whatever principle, Schult's body had become tough.
And yet, when struck repeatedly, Schult had said it hurt. His body hadn't become some invincible thing utterly unaffected by impact.
And besides, however sturdy his body might be, if a child cried "it hurts"—

"I have to go, or Medium will scold me!"

Enduring the grinding pain in his chest, Flop found himself instantly dashing toward Schult.
He had no idea how much of a shield his own body could be against that attack. But if it slowed the blow's momentum even a little and saved Schult, that would be more than enough.
Without even the leisure to think that far, Flop exposed himself to the threat of the Flying Winged Blade, and—

"—Well done, you have my thanks."

For some reason, that voice, not even especially loud, rang out with perfect clarity, and a fierce light born of colliding forces seared Flop's eyes.

"――――"

It was the radiance produced when a crimson sacred sword, colliding from the side, met the Flying Winged Blade as it fell spinning at high speed from directly overhead.
The crimson light burst silently, and Flop had the illusion of being bathed in wind from head to toe.
When it was over, what followed was the dull sound of flesh striking flesh.

"—っ"

Looking up, he saw a silhouette gliding diagonally through the air with a cry of pain, crashing into a building on the ground. It took Flop quite a moment to realize that the figure, which had spectacularly demolished a stone building with its own body, was Madelyn.
And the one who had done this landed before the breath-held Flop and the others—a crimson beauty—

"Princess-kun!"
"Priscilla-sama!!"
"Puu."

"You do understand, don't you. At a time like this, what one ought to cry out is praise for me."

So saying, Priscilla brushed the hem of her dress with leisurely composure.
Even amid the city's assault by flying dragons, even with a "Nine Divine General" deployed against them, her unchanging bearing and manner were more reassuring than anything else could possibly be.

In fact, she had struck down that very Madelyn in a single blow, rescuing Flop and the others from the brink of death.

"However, I hardly expected to find you held up in a place like this. Naturally, I assumed you'd have made straight for the municipal hall first."

"Ah, well, that's actually what I was torn about. Whether to go to the mansion where Princess-kun and the missus are, or the hall where curly-top is..."

"Not that. That general's aim, the one who attacked the city."

Jutting her chin, Priscilla cut off Flop's words and pointed toward the spot where Madelyn had crashed down.
At that, Flop's thoughts wavered for a moment, but quickly let go of it as unimportant right now, and called out "Princess-kun" to Priscilla.

"Thank you for saving us. I'll be taking the young butler and Miss Utakata to safety, but I'd like to know where's best to head. I have to bring red-hair along too."

"Oh ho, you can make a proper choice when it counts, can you. Then head for the mansion. It should be safer there than the hall, which will draw concentrated attacks. And—"

Priscilla's gaze briefly left Flop, falling on Heinkel lying on the ground behind him. Her crimson eyes narrowed, and Priscilla let out a small sigh.

"You may as well leave that one behind. If he's of no use, that's the end of the matter."

"Unfortunately, that won't do. Red-hair will be a fighting asset once he wakes up, and besides, your precious young butler protected him even to the point of getting hurt for it."

"――――"

At Flop's words, Priscilla's gaze flickered briefly toward Schult.
Saved by his master's appearance at the very last, razor-thin edge, Schult's eyes brimmed to overflowing with tears of overwhelming emotion. And since both his eyes were still burning even now, it made for quite the spectacle—wet and aflame at once.
Even so, Schult said nothing of his anxiety over the strange thing that had happened to him, and instead gripped tight to the leg of the fallen Heinkel, saying—

"H-Heinkel-sama, I'll carry him myself, ma'am...! And Priscilla-sama..."

"There is something I must do. You understand that too, don't you."

"Yes, ma'am. Something amazing that only Priscilla-sama can do...!"

How did Schult's earnest trust land on Priscilla, one wondered.
Her expression unchanging, Priscilla merely nodded quietly, accepting Schult's words. Then she turned her gaze toward the alley, and—

"Avoid the main streets, cut through the side paths behind the tall buildings. Schult, you remember the way, don't you."

"Yes, ma'am! I go on walks every day, so I remember it well!"

"I commend your effort."

With that brief praise for Schult's diligence, Priscilla turned her back to them.
Her manner made it plain there was no more time for talk. Flop's own instincts, too, warned him that lingering here was inadvisable.
Staying near Priscilla would surely keep them safe from the threat of the flying dragons, but—

"Young butler, I'd like you to guide us. I'll manage to carry red-hair somehow. And Miss Utakata, keep watch on the road ahead—you're used to that from hunting, I'd wager. We're counting on you!"

"I-I understand, ma'am! I'll guide you the best I can, ma'am!"

"...Umo, got it."

Utakata, who had hesitated for a moment at the idea of fleeing, nodded once given a role.
With that, the division of duties was complete. All that remained was—

"Princess-kun! Thank you again!"

"No thanks are needed. Only words in praise of me will do."

Still with her back turned, Priscilla answered Flop's thanks like that. At her decisive manner, Flop gave a wry smile and hoisted up the fallen Heinkel's body.
His trained, tall frame made him quite heavy, but the practice Flop had put in carrying Medium in emergencies paid off here. Slung over both shoulders like baggage, he managed to move, somehow.

"Mustn't forget this either, ma'am."

So saying, Schult picked up Heinkel's dropped sword, gripping it tightly with his thin hands.
Then, meeting each other's eyes and nodding, Flop and the others dashed into the alley, heading for the mansion.
And before they had fully drawn away—

"Priscilla-sama! Surely this fire too... thank you so much, ma'am!"

With his burning eyes opened wide, Schult's cry of gratitude was cast back toward Priscilla.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


——And so, after Schult and the others had noisily, hurriedly departed.

"Schult, and that one was called Flop, was it. To find oneself caught in dire straits alongside those of some use—that one, too, carries no small share of dumb luck."

In the now-empty street, Priscilla murmured this quietly.
It wasn't about Schult, whose eyes now bore flame, nor Flop, who'd made up for his lack of strength with quick wit, nor Utakata, who possessed a courage beyond her stature—it was an assessment of the last one.
He'd survived a situation that, without question, should have killed him. Nothing but dumb luck could explain it.
Although—

"Whether one who cannot die when he ought to die can truly be called blessed with luck—that, I find dubious."

Not pity, exactly, but something close to pity was woven into those words.
It dissolved into the blood-scented wind of the battlefield, vanishing unheard by anyone.
And at that moment—

"――――"

With a sound, the jewel ornament decorating Priscilla's orange hair shattered.
It was no mere fragile part breaking—the entire hair ornament, jewel and all, cracked at once, all across it, and shattered into pieces.
At once, Priscilla's long, gathered hair came loose, cascading in rippling waves down her back.
And then—

"—So you didn't die either, hm."

Slowly, with the sound of rubble being kicked aside, a shadow appeared in the street.
Its whole body caked in dust, and yet the body itself entirely unharmed—that presence, two black horns growing from its head. Priscilla narrowed her eyes and said—

"So it's a dragonkin. Such an antique thing—wherever did you drag that up from?"

"Mocking dragons, huh? Don't think you'll get off free for that, ya know."

Showing her teeth at Priscilla's words, the girl—Madelyn—narrowed her eyes, pupils gone reptilian.
Feeling the fell aura radiating from her small frame like a wind, Priscilla gently smoothed her own loosened hair with a hand.

"Does it displease you so, that I haven't died?"

"I'm sure I crushed those swollen tits of yours flat. If there were a bunch of things running around not dying even with their hearts crushed, that'd be no laughing matter, ya know."

"—Even with the heart crushed, was it?"

Sighing at Madelyn's manner of speech, Priscilla looked down at the chest that had been called out.
Owing to the cut of her waist-cinching dress, Priscilla's ample bosom was emphasized more than usual, but indeed, Madelyn's blow had landed squarely on that pride of hers.

Schult and the others likely hadn't seen it, but the clash in the sky had ended in a mutual blow, so to speak.
Just as Priscilla's strike had sent Madelyn flying, Madelyn's counterattack had also struck Priscilla's chest with tremendous force.
That Priscilla appeared, at this moment, as if nothing at all had happened was because of—

"It seems even my accessories are loath to see me lost from this world."

"—That's the 'Soul Marriage Technique,' huh."

Unexpectedly, at the word that flew from Madelyn's mouth, Priscilla's brow moved.
Madelyn, still glaring at Priscilla, pointed past the buildings of the street—no, further, far further, all the way off toward the far southeast, and said—

"Same as that Fox Human. You two acquainted, huh?"

"I know nothing of it. If there is something that ties into this power of mine that you have some notion of, then it is that which is my 'copy.' I am the 'original.'"

"—?"

"You don't understand?"

Thrown an unfamiliar word, confusion arose on Madelyn's face. So Priscilla decided to unravel that confusion, to make the matter simple for her.
Namely—

"It means that, same as with someone you don't know how to handle, you cannot defeat me."

"—Ah, mocking the dragon again, are ya!?"

"Fool. As if I'd choose anyone for that. —I am above; all else is below."

The instant she answered that with a smile to match, Madelyn's fury crossed its threshold.
Her face reddening, her golden eyes blazing, the dragonkin let out a roar, and that threat came surging toward Priscilla head-on.
Facing it head-on, Priscilla briefly glanced up at the sky.

At the sky—no, seeking the hilt of the crimson sacred sword whose sheath was that very sky, she said—

"The sunlight has ended, has it. This may prove a bit of a bother."


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


——Shock and destruction, beginning in one corner of the walled city, did not end there.

"――――"

At first, the fierce battle that had begun in the southern part of the city triggered a chain reaction of collapsing, densely packed buildings, and, billowing up clouds of smoke, seemed to tell of the terrible force of a flying dragon disaster.
But what had reduced the south of the walled city to ruin was not, in fact, a disaster wrought by flying dragons.

Of course, the flying dragons' hunting practice—dropping great boulders from above, trampling the ground below—was itself a calamity in its own right.
When people fled in terror from the falling stones, the swooping flying dragons' claws and fangs tore and crushed them mercilessly, and corpses piled up in the streets.

There were those who resisted, too—some managing to loose arrows at the dragons and bring them down in counterattack.
But most people were left exposed to the flying dragons' one-sided assault, able only to cry out for salvation.
So great, so boundless was the devastation wrought by the flying dragons.

But even the flying dragons themselves avoided approaching—so destructive was that fierce battle.

"Gi, aaaaaahhhh——!!"

The small frame leaping and howling gripped in both arms the massive Flying Winged Blade.
The bent blade sliced deftly through wind and air resistance, and when thrown, it would spin its way back to its original position. Though normally a throwing tool with which a master could even take the head of a distant quarry, this one was being hurled with tremendous force, mowing down everything in its path as it flew back and forth.

Its weight, sharpness, and destructive power alike were beyond comparison with the ordinary, small Flying Winged Blade.
If the conventional version was about the size of a one-handed sword, this one was the size of two greatswords fused together—and on top of that, its weight was heavier still than melting down ten greatswords into one.

Once, in this world, ten special sacred blades of extraordinary power had been forged—the Enchanted Swords, of that ilk.
The weapon, made of the metal said to have been used to forge those very blades, unleashed its power to the fullest as a weapon far more vicious than its appearance suggested.

With every throw, dozens of meters were leveled flat in its path, and anything in that path—buildings, living creatures, anything at all—was devoured indiscriminately.
It was the dragonkin's inhuman physical might combined with a disregard for the surrounding devastation that alone made possible so ruinous a tactic—and that was the reason the south of the walled city had been reduced to ruin.

Those elsewhere in the city, fighting the flying dragons and with no room to spare to look at the ground, couldn't notice it.
Of course, the roar and tremors reached even them, but caught up in looking to the skies and exchanging words with whoever was nearby, they paid the devastation no heed at all.

And that, in truth, was a stroke of luck amid misfortune.
Had they witnessed that overwhelming destruction with their own eyes, they'd surely have forgotten even to cling to the thin thread of hope for survival, and simply dropped to their knees.
That was how much of a different sort of calamity the rampaging dragonkin represented, distinct from the disaster brought by the flying dragons.

For a single city to be struck by two natural catastrophes at once was nothing short of a nightmare.
And more than that, one half of that pair of catastrophes was being unleashed for the sole purpose of destroying just one person.

"――――"

Dancing as she kicked off the ground again and again, Priscilla continued to dodge Madelyn's rampaging attacks.
The dragonkin's physical prowess was not to be underestimated, but there was no finesse in Madelyn's way of fighting. She had learned no martial art whatsoever—likely because she looked down on humans as lesser beings.

"Though I myself have never learned any martial art either."

If Priscilla had any experience to draw on, it was only that she had closely observed the movements of those who wielded swords. Beyond that, she simply had a perfect grasp of what her own body was and how it moved.
If there was any reason she could wield the Sunlight Sword like a master, that alone was surely it.

However, at this moment, unable to draw the Sunlight Sword, each and every one of Madelyn's blows was, in fact, a genuine threat.

"――――"

The sunlight had ended; the sun had waned.
Even wielding the "Sunlight Sword" required certain conditions to be met. The sun did not shine forever without pause. Half the day, it shared its role with the moon, needing time to store up its power.
If there existed a sun that shone endlessly without fail, that would be none other than Priscilla herself.
That said—

"The longer this drags on, the deeper the wounds will grow, will they not."

Priscilla was responding to Madelyn's attacks with caution. Naturally, the lack of the decisive Sunlight Sword was one reason she couldn't press the advantage, but the dragonkin's sheer vitality was also a cause for concern.
If she failed to kill in one blow, there was a high chance Madelyn's counterattack would reach Priscilla in turn.
A mutual exchange of blows, like the first time, wasn't something that could be repeated over and over.

And if the resolution were postponed like this, the damage to the walled city would only keep spreading.
Just as Madelyn's direct destruction had leveled the south of the city, elsewhere the flying dragons' attacks kept raining down one after another, and the dead and wounded kept climbing.
On top of that—

"――ッッ"

Catching the Flying Winged Blade as it flew back to her, Madelyn planted both feet on the ground, her fighting spirit still rising. The dragon's growing might carried on the wind, stirring up the flying dragons still in the city's skies.
Among humans, this would be called a boost to morale, but among a savage species that lived wild, that stirring produced an effect better described as a frenzy.

If the flying dragons' ferocity increased and their assaults multiplied, things would, quite literally, spiral out of control.
Before that happened, she wanted to set events in motion. To that end—

"One more move is needed."

Some great move to shift this situation.

"――――"

Dodging Madelyn's attack with a great leap backward, Priscilla stood amid the collapsed cityscape.
The city once celebrated for its walled fortitude, its very sturdiness once the pride of its residents and the pillar of their peace, now lay unrecognizable—and even after this calamity was repelled, reconstruction would not come easily.
But all of that was a problem for after the great disaster before her eyes had been driven off.

"Next, it will land...!"

Growling like a beast, Madelyn, having repeated her clumsy attacks only to be endlessly dodged, was gradually adjusting her tactics through sheer repetition. It would be well if the enemy had little will to learn, but if not, then the opponent would grow even in the midst of battle.
Before Priscilla, wishing to avoid any further growth on Madelyn's part, the girl let out a breath of white vapor.

Dragon's blood coursing through her whole body, the rise in temperature made steam rise from the small girl's frame. Combined with the white breath, her figure looked as though wrapped in white smoke—

"—No."

Something's off, Priscilla thought, closing one eye and staring intently at Madelyn.
That the battle had dragged on and the dragon's blood had grown active was understandable. That the budding of Madelyn's emotions, as a dragonkin, was inciting the flying dragons' frenzy was also plain.
But in this climate of the Vollachia Empire, for one's breath to cloud white—
And more than that—

"For snow to fall—that is something that simply should not be possible."

Flickering at the edge of her vision, Priscilla remarked on the white fragments drifting slowly downward.
Little by little, but unmistakably increasing in number, it was snow, falling upon the walled city where most of the shelter protecting its people had already been lost.
"Unusual" was far too weak a word for it—this was some kind of natural catastrophe in its own right.
In Vollachia, there were no small number of people who had never even seen snow.

She had said that for a single city to be struck by two natural catastrophes at once was nothing short of a nightmare.
But what if a new catastrophe, a third one, were to be layered atop that?
Was that a nightmare, or was it, perhaps—


"—That's quite enough."


Onto the ground where the cold gifts from heaven were slowly piling up, a voice rang out.
A resonance beautiful yet unwavering, like the ringing of a silver bell.

——The third natural catastrophe—if it were to be layered atop the others, would that be a nightmare?
——Or might it, instead, prove to be that one great move?

"I'm in an awful hurry, but I can't just look the other way. —I'm very, very angry, you know."

Onto that place where everything had been struck by great disaster, a silver-haired girl sounded her footsteps.
Searching for her missing knight, the "Witch" who had brought the snow with her had arrived at the walled city.
