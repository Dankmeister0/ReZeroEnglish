Arc 6 Chapter 28: "Julius Juukulius"

—Probably, no one would believe it.

"My name is Natsuki Subaru! Servant of Roswaal Manor, and the first knight of the royal candidate present here—Emilia-sama!"

In that instant, an outrageous bluff had turned every single person in the royal castle's great hall into an enemy.

A declaration that even the man who'd blurted it out couldn't quite hide was born of nothing more than giddy nerves and sheer momentum—and the fact that there had been, just one man among them all, who was moved by it.

* * *

The sword he'd picked up fit his palm so naturally it was almost enough to bring him to tears—so well that he could almost believe it was the sword that had chosen him, and not the other way around.

Even though there was nothing about him now that gave him the right to feel such pride.

"Sh—!!"

Compared to his beloved sword, now broken, this one's blade was slightly thicker, its tip a touch heavier. But accounting for that as he loosed each strike, the minor differences were easy enough to correct for.

Not every battlefield allowed for a weapon that fit the hand like an old friend. He'd trained for every contingency, prided himself on being able to fight with any blade put in his hand, not only the one he knew best.

"Boring, kid."

That pride rode a sharp thrust aimed straight for the space between the yawning man's brows. But the man dodged it with nothing more than a tilt of his head, let the blade comb lightly through the tips of his hair as though trimming it, scattered a few red strands, and leapt straight backward.

The distance opened; he closed it again with a step and quick footwork, refusing to let go.

If one judged swordsmanship as a whole in the middle of a fight, what mattered wasn't only the handling of the blade, but footwork—how one moved.

Naturally, a sword had its reach. To arrive at the optimal position in the least time, and make full use of every opening, required not just the arm but the legs—the entire body.

So when he'd first begun training with a sword, footwork had been the very first thing drilled into him.

He believed he'd been blessed with a good teacher. His teacher's swordsmanship, set beside his own now, fell somewhat short—but that was simply an unavoidable matter of age.

Still, there was no doubt the man had been better at teaching others than his own skill alone would suggest. He'd loved to talk not just about the practice of the sword, but about where its techniques had been born, and how they'd been passed down.

Naturally, he too had come to enjoy listening to such things, and had taken pride in being able to put them into practice.

"—"

He caught up to the leap and drove a strike at the landing point. Up, down, left, right—three feints to disturb, and the true blow an upward slash from directly beneath.

"Straight out of the textbook, kid."

The blade's path, meant to carve from between the legs up to the chest, was casually redirected by the man's stick. The moment that stick, cutting across the line of the strike, touched the sword's edge, the force was deflected with unbelievable ease. He tried to resist, but a single swing of a sword was a contest measured in less than a second—and the man's skill, threading that instant like a needle's eye, was simply beyond measure.

"—!"

A groan of shock rose in the back of his throat as the strike, carried by its own force, sailed on past overhead. He spun his body so as not to let the opening be exploited, willed his mind to summon a blade of wind—no, that option wasn't his to take now.

No magic for a feint would be loosed. This had only made an opening.

"Gah!"

A thrust kick drove straight into his flank. Bare toes gouged in as though carving between his organs, and the instant his body doubled over, unable to bear it, the foot still buried in him twisted, and every organ inside him screamed at once.

He was sent flying. In that instant, he leapt with the direction of the blow himself, so as not to be thrown helplessly by its momentum.

But that didn't cancel the kick's piercing force. His vision spun in circles, the shock in his torso lancing up into his brain with a wave of nausea—but even so, he strained his eyes not to lose sight of his enemy, slammed his feet against the floor rushing up to meet him, and kept his footing.

His breath came ragged. Deep breathing would take too long to recover.

He forced his lungs to wring themselves out, expelling every last bit of air left inside him. Once, he emptied himself completely, forcing his ragged breathing to remember what calm felt like.

"—"

Exhale. Exhale it all. He could still fight. He had to be able to still fight.

"—"

In his field of view, some ten meters off, stood the red-haired man, smiling.

Once more, he would close on him. Chase him down, drive his blade home, and at the very least strip that easy smile off his face. From there, the real fight would—

"Don't put on airs. There's no such thing as 'real' or 'fake' in a fight, kid. You reading picture books or something?"

"—ah"

In the blink of an eye the distance had closed, and he was left dumbstruck.

Quite literally, the instant after a blink. The man closed ten meters in a heartbeat and thrust his stick right up before Julius's eyes. He swung his sword up on reflex to knock it away, and into that careless, overlarge motion two arcing blows of the stick caught him at the temple and the chest.

A sharp impact—more than the pain, it was the sheer sharpness of the blow that nearly carried his consciousness off with it. He clenched his back teeth, clawed back the awareness he'd nearly let slip, and stamped down hard on the floor.

"Ohh—augh!"

He let out a low roar on reflex, driving a crescent-moon slash at the man.

The man evaded it as gracefully as if dancing, and drove an elbow into the side of his face. His consciousness reeled again.

He stamped his foot down, bracing his fighting spirit on the dull throb spreading through the sole of his shoe. But his instant, precise judgment had been compromised. So his body chose the strike it knew best.

A simultaneous incantation of fire and water, joined by a sword strike—three prongs, all at once. It misfired.

There was no support from fire or ice—all that was loosed was the single artful slash that, after countless rounds of training, had earned him the name *the Best*. Had his opponent been an ordinary man, that alone might well have been enough to finish him.

"Peh."

The pinnacle of knightly swordsmanship was flicked aside, effortlessly, by a stick swung as an idle diversion.

A rising knee drove into his solar plexus, and stomach acid was wrung out of him along with a pained groan. Before his crumpling body could even finish falling, a barrage of blows was driven straight into him from the front—he wasn't even allowed to go down.

"Ohh?"

The impact sent him toppling backward instead of forward, and he caught himself on a hand thrown out by reflex. Using the momentum to roll backward, he lashed out with a kick, and the man let out a surprised sound as he dodged it.

He rolled back and put distance between them. Blood spilled from his nose. He wiped it on his white sleeve. His uniform was stained a garish, vivid red, as if by paint.

It didn't matter. He let out a sharp breath and poured everything he had into the sword still gripped in his right hand.

It would reach. It had to reach. He had to be strong—had to be strong.

"Pathetic, kid. How long you been holding a sword, kid? Me, I've only had one in my hand for three months, kid. I can cut light. So what can you cut, kid?"

"Right now, here, I will cut you—"

"Don't talk nonsense, kid. Like you could. You can't, kid. Can't swing it till it reaches. Haven't swung it till it reaches. Can't swing it till you're able. Haven't swung it till you're able. Don't go running your mouth about what you wanna do when you haven't done what needs doing, kid."

Instead of a retort, he drove one strike home, as hard and fierce as he could.

As if in answer to that single strike, more than ten blows rained down and were pounded into him.

"—"

His consciousness swam; he didn't fall. Why didn't he fall? Because—

"You're lacking, kid. Not enough, kid. This ain't the place for you. Wrong field entirely. Wrong cast for the part. Nobody called for you."

He had to be strong. He had to prove it with the sword.

He had lost his name, his house, his family, his liege, his comrades-in-arms, his friends, the spirit bound to him by soul.

What remained was only this. What remained was only himself. The one shapeless thing he'd built up as himself, and himself alone—that was all that was left.

Because this alone was the proof that he existed.

"It's disgusting, kid. You got some pretty mask glued on your face, kid? Satisfied just aping other people, kid? Your sword's boring. So are you."

There had been a time he'd aimed for the summit of the sword.

There had been a time he'd thought that place, at least, might be within his reach.

He'd quickly let that go, telling himself it had been aiming too high.

The moment he'd looked into the eyes of a red-haired boy, a boy who held a sword, and abruptly realized just what a great burden that boy carried.

"Nobody's even looking at you, kid. Nobody's expecting anything. Don't get soft on me just 'cause you figure I'm playing around. Ain't no fun hitting or kicking you, kid."

He'd had admiration. Shining stories had overflowed in him.

He'd thought that to stand shoulder to shoulder with them, who he was now fell far too short.

So he'd worked desperately, struggled, thinking that someday he might reach even the dream he'd let go of back then.

"—"

The blue eye not covered by the eyepatch, the flame-colored hair grown out in a ragged mess—they overlapped with the boy who had once been the reason he let go of that dream, and with one of the many admirations he'd carried since.

He'd meant to spend every day hoping that someday he would reach it, never once neglecting the effort.

"Not enough, kid. Not nearly enough. —Don't go slacking off on your whole life."

Spat out by the very admiration he'd longed to reach, beaten down by nothing more than a stick.

Not even given the chance to properly swing his sword, unable to make the sword he did swing land—told that all the effort he'd piled up had meant nothing, that the blood and sweat he'd built up had been for naught, and in a life suddenly torn apart, even the one single thing he'd believed in trampled underfoot.

"—"

Something welled up inside him, slow and heavy.

Something rising up even stronger swept it away.

"Hah! You're still holding out? Even more boring, kid."

With a click of the tongue, light burst forth, and his limbs were pierced through.

He crumpled like a puppet with its strings cut. But the violence wouldn't allow even that.

His torso was struck. His breath caught. He was seized by the hair and swung left and right. Slammed down onto the floor, and as he rolled, kicked square in the face. He spun across the floor like a thrown disk, tumbled on and on through an endless white world.

He slapped the floor. Springing his body up, he looked toward the direction he'd been kicked from. The man's knee, diving in, struck his face dead on. In the instant of collision he met the knee with his own forehead, and even as his brow split open, he managed to knock the man back.

A gap opened up. He should have been able to right his stance—and yet his body wouldn't move.

"Hah, guh..."

His whole body was screaming. The damage to his head especially was severe. His consciousness swayed unsteadily, refusing to settle, and if he let his guard down for even a moment, it felt as though the contents of his skull might spill right out.

His sword—where had it gone? As if to check, he slowly put strength into his right hand. There, he felt the certain shape of the hilt. He felt relief.

He couldn't let go of it. Not this, at least. If he let go of even this, what would it mean he'd let go of?

—Or perhaps what he held in his hand now was something else entirely, merely shaped like a *sword*.

"—"

That there was no fault in the way he had chosen to be. That this, precisely this, was his own path—he had walked on, believing it.

That was still true now. He'd thought that belief could never waver, not in his whole life.

So the fact that it had slipped through his fingers and vanished had to be a matter separate from right or wrong.

—Or had it been wrong after all?

Was this because he'd damaged the way he ought to be, mistaken the path he'd chosen, misjudged the thing he believed in?

He had lost his name, his house, his family, his liege, his comrades-in-arms, his friends, the spirit bound to him by soul.

If even the one thing that was supposed to remain, the one thing he'd told himself was left to him, had itself been worthless, unworthy of clinging to, no support at all—a mere sham.

—He had sworn to his liege that he would be strong, and support her.

—The one friend who remained had told him she remembered his strength.

In a world where he'd lost everything, that *strength* alone was the sole thing holding him up.

That *strength* alone had been the one unshakable certainty in this fragile, feeble self of his.

"—Your doubt showed in your blade, kid."

"—!?"

How much time had he spent on that self-interrogation?

Probably less than a single second—a bare instant. But that instant's gap was, for the man—for the Sword Saint—as good as being handed infinite chances to cut down an enemy.

He hauled his sword up recklessly, trying to block the attack closing in.

A high, ringing sound echoed. When he opened his eyes wide, wondering what had happened, he saw his own sword rolling away across the floor.

Slipped from his hand, and at last, even his sword was gone.

Without a name, without pride, without even a sword—then what was it that stood here?

"No qualification to reach the Heavenly Sword. —Can't even give you a spot as my flunky, kid."

The dry voice declared it coldly, and the Sword Saint gripped the stick in a forward grip, dropping into a stance.

—The first moment the Sword Saint had ever taken a proper stance.

An instant later, the stick let out a roar—a swordstroke. It was, unmistakably, a swordstroke.

An absolute strike was loosed, and he was sent flying, buffeted by the shockwave it carried.

"—"

A blow unlike any of the violence before it—unlike any fist, any kick.

This wasn't violence. This was the summit of the sword, its highest peak, the swordsmanship loosed by true *strength*.

Swallowed in light, his consciousness flew away.

Was he seeing death? Or something that had transcended death? Even that, he couldn't tell.

But in the instant he was blown away, he heard, just barely, a voice.

"Julius—!!"

The raised voice was filled, somehow, with a kind of tragic desperation.

As though someone had run desperately up a long flight of stairs, only to stumble upon a decisive, fatal moment.

Because that was the voice crying out, a smile rose in him, wildly out of place.

The knight known as *the Best*. Of the Dragon Kingdom of Lugunica's Order of Royal Knights. Eldest son and heir of House Juukulius. First knight of royal candidate Anastasia Hoshin.

—Julius Juukulius.

"Hah."

Did he, as he was now, even have the right to be called by that name?

With that question as his last thought, Julius's consciousness was swallowed by the light, and snapped off into nothing.

- The Sword Saint's verbal tic "オメエ" (a rough/dialectal "you") is tacked onto nearly every sentence in the Japanese. I rendered it consistently as "kid" appended to his lines to preserve the repetitive, contemptuous rhythm, since a literal "you" repeated that often reads as broken English rather than a dialect quirk.
- "強さを覚えていると、ただ一つ残った友に言われた" — the referenced friend's identity/gender is not stated in this excerpt; I rendered it as "she" based on likely context (this appears to reference Ferris) but this should be checked against surrounding chapters.
- 選定の剣 ("the sword he'd picked up") is not a catalogued proper-noun term; translated contextually as an improvised replacement sword rather than a formal "Selection Sword" (distinct from 王選, Royal Selection).
- Sound effects/kiai (し――っ, かっ, おお、ぁ, ぺい, ふ、く) were adapted freely into English exclamations rather than transliterated, per natural-prose priority.
