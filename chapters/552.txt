Arc 7 Chapter 54: "Chaosflame, the Promised Land"

"U, ugh, ahh...!"

"Ah, ah, bawling your eyes out with snot running down your face, what a handful."

Sniffling wetly, unable to stop his sobbing, Subaru clung to Olbart's back. Olbart, with the boy still stuck there, shrugged his shoulders and scratched his cheek with a finger, at a loss for what to do.

—It had been an incredible, an utterly incredible experience.

The ten seconds of despair that had befallen Subaru, and what lay beyond them, past the eleventh second he'd overcome them to reach—how many "deaths" had he tasted before arriving there?

That his soul, worn down to nothing by despair and a shattered heart, hadn't simply vanished seemed like a miracle.

But at the end of that hellish experience, he had arrived.

"Uawoo—"

Clinging tightly to the arm of the sobbing Subaru, it was Rui who made that sound.

She hadn't gone through the exact same experience as Subaru, but through the uncountable repetitions of trial and error, Rui too had followed the same fate as him time and again, and lost her life.

Leaping onto Olbart's back and clawing their way to that almost absurd victory would never have been possible without her.

"I... really don't understand you at all."

"Uu."

"But without you, it... wouldn't have been possible. ...Thank you."

Subaru's heart was already a chaotic, jumbled mess.

He knew full well what Rui had done, and what kind of history lay between them. And yet, knowing that, gratitude welled up in him regardless.

And it wasn't only Subaru's gratitude that overflowed.

"Uah... u..."

"Rui?"

The collapse came suddenly.

Having heard Subaru's halting words of thanks, Rui's large, round eyes welled up with tears, which spilled over and streamed down her cheeks in an instant.

Tears poured down Rui's cheeks in a steady stream, falling with a force that seemed to know no way of stopping.

"Rui, you're... you're crying, you're crying so..."

"Uu, uu!"

Rui surely knew she was crying, and yet she made no move to stop the tears or wipe them away. All the while, she clung desperately to Subaru's arm, as if she thought he might vanish the moment she let go to wipe her face.

"Wipe your face, idiot, you idiot..."

Watching Rui's tears, Subaru—whose own wave of emotion had just started to settle—found himself teetering on the edge again. He'd already lost count of the tears he'd shed, the sobs he'd choked out.

He wondered if the two of them might just go on crying forever, endlessly, without ever stopping—

"—Well done, both of you."

"Ah..."

"Cry as much as you like. I, the ruler of this magical city, give you permission."

A pair of long arms reached out from behind and gently, tenderly embraced Subaru and Rui both.

Startled by the warmth and gentleness of the words spoken, Subaru's eyes went wide, and he found himself captivated by the beautiful profile so close beside him. Long lashes, lips painted vermillion, and shining blue eyes so clear they seemed magnanimous enough to absorb any worry, any suffering.

He already knew what kind of person those eyes belonged to.

So he knew that her words, her warmth, were kindness with no ulterior motive whatsoever.

Because he knew that he was allowed to entrust everything to her.

"Uh, guh... ah, uh, aaaahh"

"Uawoo... uawoo, uwah, u, uawoo...!"

"There, there. ...Children have the privilege of crying and clinging to a grown-up's chest. If mine will do, then go ahead and soak it through with your tears."

Subaru lifted his face, and the tears came flowing again. Rui clung tight to him all the while.

Yorna held the two of them gently, stroking their small backs. Such a moment, at the end of the battle, played out atop the keep of Kōruri Castle—

"With all this happenin' on my back, I got no idea what kinda face I'm supposed to be makin' here."

"Silence."

—and with that, the mysterious old man's tactless remark was silenced by the ruler of the magical city.

"Now then, Elder Olbart, do you have anything to say for yourself?"

"Somethin' to say? Is there somethin' I need to be makin' excuses for? It was you who came swingin' at me outta your own temper, fox girl, weren't it?"

"—"

Some time after Subaru's great crying fit, things were finally about to settle into a calm discussion—or so it seemed, before the keep was suddenly swallowed by a dangerous tension.

The cause, needless to say, was the breezy, utterly unrepentant Olbart.

He sat there with his rear planted firmly on the roof, scratching the inside of his ear with his pinky finger.

"I did keep to what I was told, more or less? Well, I did try usin' a loophole or two, but hey, that makes us even with the kid over there."

"Ngh... b-but that's..."

"Now, now, you'd best hear me out, kid. —'Cause if we make it an anything-goes kinda deal, I'm the one who comes out ahead. Kakakakka!"

Olbart roared with laughter, mouth wide open, and Subaru found himself at a loss for words.

In truth, Olbart was right. If Subaru objected to his abuse of the rules here, and the result was a situation where the rules were tossed out entirely in favor of anything goes, there was probably no way whatsoever to defeat a shinobi like Olbart.

Olbart had any number of hidden techniques up his sleeve, and no hesitation about dragging bystanders into the fight. Subaru had learned, more than enough since coming to the Empire, that opponents like that were the toughest of all.

"Somehow, Olbart-san, you kind of remind me of someone scary I know..."

"That so? Then you'd best go ahead and kill 'em, for sure. Anyone that's no good and a huge pain in the neck like that."

"In that case, it only stands to reason that you, Elder, should agree with the idea of my ending your life here and now."

"Hey now, seems I'm mighty disliked. Well, can't say I blame her."

A crackling tension filled the air, though it came entirely from the one-sided hostility Yorna was giving off.

Olbart, on the receiving end of it, had already dropped his combat stance entirely. Of course, that was surely just for show—he could flip the switch back in an instant if needed.

Still, Yorna's anger was only natural, and Subaru himself felt a very strong anger toward Olbart as well. But there was a significant reason keeping him from acting purely on that anger.

None other than these shrunken limbs of his, and a head that wasn't working quite right.

"Olbart-san, about the technique that's been cast on us..."

"Oh, that. Course, it don't undo itself if I die. Well, there might be some other way, but I ain't never seen anyone manage it."

"I knew it...!"

"Well, it's kinda like insurance, see. It's supposed to be a secret technique even back in my own village, so normally we dispose of the body without a trace, so nobody's the wiser."

Olbart said this chilling thing with breezy cheer.

At the same time, Subaru found himself praising his own self for having walked a truly precarious tightrope.

Even if Yorna had beaten Olbart, if Olbart had died, their "Infantilization" would have remained unresolved forever. He'd have been stuck as "Nachuki Shubaru" for good.

Just thinking about it sent a chill down his spine.

"I don't know the details of the situation, but I've heard you subjected these children to something monstrous."

Yorna held her pipe between her lips, drawing purple smoke into her lungs as she fixed her gaze on Olbart.

When he'd asked for her cooperation, Subaru had conveyed the information as honestly as he could, but he had left out the messier details. Among what he'd omitted was the fact that he'd been shrunk by the effect of Olbart's technique.

Once Olbart undid the "Infantilization" by his own hand, Subaru would return to his original body.

When that happened, he intended to apologize with everything he had and ask Yorna's forgiveness. —Surely it wouldn't turn her impression of him entirely on its head.

Because he wanted to believe Yorna was the type of person who'd understand if he explained things to her.

"Elder Olbart, you admit you lost the earlier contest, yes?"

"Oh, sure, I admit it, I admit it. Gettin' worked over like that, it's a clean loss. Lucky I ain't dead for losin', is what I'd call it. Still, it were an unexpected way to lose, I'll say."

"Unexpected, is it?"

"Figured the one who'd give me a good match would be the boss of these here kids, is all."

Setting aside Subaru's concerns, Yorna and Olbart pressed the conversation forward. At Olbart's words, Yorna murmured "boss" softly to herself, and gently placed a hand over the breast of her kimono.

"The sender of the letter, is it. I had been looking forward to meeting him..."

There, Yorna suddenly cut herself off, and her eyes—turned on Olbart—narrowed sharply.

At the sharpness of that gaze, Olbart tilted his head. "What's that now?"

"Ain't nothin' scarier than gettin' glared at by a beauty. What's up?"

"Nothing 'what's up' about it. I had Tanza pass word to the envoys to come to the castle. —Now then, where has Tanza gone?"

The air turned suddenly, sharply cold—cold enough that one's throat might catch.

Yorna's anger always surfaced when harm came to the city's residents or its children. And with Tanza meeting both conditions—a resident of the city and a child—all the more so.

That said, Tanza was suspected of having conspired with Olbart to keep Subaru and the others from meeting Yorna. Some care would be needed in how she was handled.

However—

"I also figured Tanza would be with Olbart-san. But she's not... right? Not hidden under a roof tile or something..."

"Within the castle, it is as good as my own innards. There is no chance I would overlook anything that entered or hid itself here."

"O-oh, I see. Amazing, Yorna-san's technique..."

Hearing Yorna's supremely confident answer, Subaru found himself reeling once again at just how far beyond the norm she was.

Once he'd finished reeling, he realized something: that he and Rui had teleported straight into Kōruri Castle as intruders, and been found by Yorna almost immediately after.

At the time, Yorna had made it sound as though she'd simply happened upon them while walking through the castle, but that must have been a convenient fiction. In truth, she'd noticed the moment Subaru and Rui suddenly appeared inside the castle, and moved to catch the intruders.

That she hadn't done so right away, and instead treated them as Tanza's friends, was perhaps because she too had been unable to decide how to handle the two of them, so terribly young as they were.

In the end, it meant that Yorna had been protecting them from the very first moment they crossed paths.

"—"

The more Subaru came into contact with Yorna's character, the more a certain feeling grew within him: was it really right to drag her into the great conflict of the Vollachian Empire?

The same went for the feelings of the people who lived in Chaosflame, the ones called the Horned Tribe, who had blocked their path along the way.

Having suffered and been cast out, they wanted to protect the place of refuge they had finally found.

Who could blame them for wishing to go on living their peaceful lives? All the more so when they had a ruler there who cherished them.

—How was that any different from the world the girl Subaru loved so dearly had wished for?

"—"

Silently, Subaru looked down from the keep at the city below, at the cluttered streets of the magical city.

Everything about it was jumbled, tangled, chaotic, without a shred of unity. When he'd first come to this city, he'd been baffled by the disorder of it all.

But now, having seen the people of this city and Yorna, even for such a short time, Subaru thought differently.

There was freedom here.

A freedom to be bound by no one, to never have to make oneself small. That was why this city overflowed with a diversity in which everyone could express themselves exactly as they wished, and no one would deny them for it.

Free, equal, and fair.

It felt, in a way, as though some ideal vision of the future were sketched out right here.

Was it truly right to take Yorna away from a place like that?

"Elder, answer me. That girl—where is Tanza?"

Setting aside Subaru's inner conflict, the interrogation of Olbart continued.

Tanza was not within the castle. Surely, Subaru wanted to believe, Olbart didn't possess some technique for tucking a shrunken person into his robes.

"No need for such a scary face, I'll tell you plain and simple. Teamin' up with that deer girl, that were just 'cause the conditions happened to line up nice for usin' in the contest."

"—Where is she?"

Making clear that idle chatter was unnecessary, Yorna's repeated question drove into Olbart like a blade.

In response, Olbart tilted his head and turned his gaze down toward the city below—the magical city spread out before them like a breathtaking view—

"Ain't nothin' hard about it. I'm an outsider round these parts, ain't I? Places where I could stash away one little girl, there's only so many of 'em."

—and that was how he answered.

—A little before Olbart broke under Yorna's interrogation.

It was at a certain inn within the magical city of Chaosflame.

An unremarkable inn, not especially large, nor decorated with any particular splendor. —And yet, in truth, its walls and interior were built to be sturdy, so much so that hardly any sound could pass through them.

Within Chaosflame, this place—differing in atmosphere from nearly everywhere else in the city—was a lodging facility prepared for VIPs, in readiness for the improbable one-in-a-million contingency.

The room that served as the stage for this scene, owing to how it had been laid out, had its walls removed to join three rooms together into one spacious space. Even so, only a handful of those who used this inn, by virtue of their standing, ever stayed in such a spacious room.

No elegant furnishings, no array of art to please the eye, no fine liquors to soothe the tongue had been prepared, because the majority of those who used this place were pragmatists.

In that sense too, the nature of this inn stood as something foreign within Chaosflame.

The magical city, known as a lawless zone and a symbol of "freedom." Yet contrary to that chaotic appearance, the residents of Chaosflame were, under the rule of Yorna, its master, astonishingly unified in will.

Among a people holding up a kind of shared illusion, there lived a spirit of showing no mercy to outside enemies, and mercy to internal troubles—which was precisely why preparations like this inn were unnecessary.

Precisely—

"—'Let the people of the Empire be strong,' is the ideal, and this is surely a demonic city that stands against it."

"Ah..."

"A certain degree of policy is entrusted to a city's leader. But even that has its limits. —Was it because of one who failed to know those limits that such an act as this was carried out?"

Asked so by the man with his arms folded, the small girl stiffened her cheeks and fell silent.

The man who had posed the question was Abel, a figure clad in black and red, his face hidden behind a demon mask. Facing him was Tanza, a Demi-Human girl bearing deer horns, dressed in a beautiful kimono.

It was a meeting between two parties presumed to stand as adversaries within this city.

—No, to be precise, it was not only Abel and Tanza who stood face to face.

"Do you people even understand what sort of place this is, that you would barge in here...!"

"—"

"Answer me! Depending on your response, think of it as though not even your bones will remain!"

So bellowed the man whose dark skin flushed red with fury—Kafma Irulux, one of the Empire's Two Generals—glaring at Abel, who had barged into the inn's room, along with the boy and girl accompanying him.

The hand he thrust out held nothing, but he had no need of a weapon. Every manner of arms nested, quite literally, within Kafma's own body.

That declaration of not leaving even bones behind was no mere threat—it was simple fact.

—For that was the true worth of the "Insect General," Kafma Irulux.

"Hey, Abel-chan, you serious?"

Before Kafma's fierce intensity, it was the masked boy, Al, who spoke in a voice taut with sharp wariness. A boy who bore on his back a green dragon saber he couldn't properly wield—Abel glanced briefly in his direction at his words.

"How many more times must you ask that before your mind is made up?"

"I'm asking so many times 'cause even after barging in here, it ain't made up! Besides, I'm sayin' it 'cause this is straight-up insane! Damn it, why the hell did I even come along...!"

"It should be obvious. —You are, as you are now, far too much of a coward to be alone."

"Guh...!"

Al's voice caught, and no further words came out. It was plain at a glance that the remark had struck home, and the fact that he couldn't even argue back was surely proof that he himself was aware of it.

For some reason, Al in his current state couldn't keep his composure even just walking down the street.

"Abel-chin."

In place of Al, whose words had been sealed away, it was their other companion, Medium, who called out to Abel as if in protest.

Medium, who had worked hard alongside Abel to make it this far while protecting the now-useless Al, nevertheless wore a fair amount of shadow on her own expression too.

All of it, every bit, stemmed from what had happened with Subaru and Rui, who had broken off along the way.

"I think... the way you just said that wasn't very nice."

"Do you imagine this is a scene for deepening mutual understanding through roundabout words? Both you and the clown ought to grasp the situation. One misstep, and it will be exactly as declared—not even bones will remain."

"That's what I'm saying! That way of talking, it's not good!"

"—"

"It's because Abel-chin talks like that, that Subaru-chin and Rui-chan too..."

Having argued back once in a strong voice, her words trailed off into a mumble after that.

That was unavoidable, given Medium's nature. Even wanting to lay the entire blame for what had happened on Abel, someone as considerate as her simply couldn't do it.

She understood, all too well, that her own attitude and gaze and such had played a part in cornering Subaru and the others too. That, no doubt, was a product of her brother's upbringing.

In any case, both Al and Medium were, each in their own way, malfunctioning.

"Therefore, we have no intention of fighting either. Lower your arm, Kafma Irulux."

"You... are you sane? You answer none of our questions, and try only to force your own will through with high-handed arrogance. Such conduct—do you imagine yourself some kind of king!?"

"—. Not quite right, but not far off, either."

"You—!"

His eyes bloodshot, Kafma—a man of towering loyalty to the Emperor—found his anger approaching its limit.

Without meaning to, Kafma had brushed against the true nature of Abel's identity, yet he fell just half a step short of the truth—such was the strength of the "cognitive obstruction" effect of the demon mask obtained in the Shudrak settlement.

Broadly speaking, no one could overcome the mask's effect except someone the wearer intended to reveal himself to, or someone who already had certainty as to the wearer's true identity.

In other words—

"I'll not tolerate this farce with children in tow any longer! Whatever happened yesterday..."

"—Be still, Kafma Irulux."

Power gathered in Kafma's arm, the tattooed patterns upon it beginning to writhe. That impulse of his came to a sudden, complete halt at a voice called from behind.

Kafma's eyes went wide as he turned, and there, deep in the room, sat a black-haired, handsome man of striking bearing, seated at his ease in an armchair.

Black hair, black eyes—his dignified and singularly well-formed features were as clear and penetrating as if he saw through everything entirely, unshaken even before an uninvited guest.

So well-made a "mask" was that, in its own right, that even Abel found himself impressed.

Setting aside Abel's admiration, the handsome man deep in the room looked over each of the three of them in turn, and said,

"Those people bear no hostility. At least, not in this situation."

"But, Your Excellency! This person is insolent, refusing even to show his face before you, and showing not the slightest trace of reverence—an outrageous scoundrel! And what's more, with children in tow..."

"What of the children?"

"No, it's just—the fact that he has children with him makes no sense to me at all...!"

Kafma, unable to conceal his deep bewilderment, rubbed at his eyes as he wrestled with his own anguish.

This man Kafma had, to begin with, a bad habit of taking things too seriously. Engaging with matters earnestly and increasing the room for needless worry were two different things.

It was precisely because of that earnestness that he had, at one point, even resigned the position of one of the "Nine Divine Generals" of his own accord, deeming himself unsuited to it. —Which meant that, setting aside his temperament, he possessed strength equal to that of a Nine Divine General.

Even supposing no one had fallen to Olbart's venomous fangs, and Taritta had been present here alongside them, fighting Kafma head-on would surely have been impossible.

On that point, this particular confrontation favored Abel's side. After all—

"I will apologize for disturbing your sleeping quarters, but our business here lies with that girl there. As things stand, we have no intention of making trouble with you."

"Wh—!"

"Oh? Speaking rather grandly, before me. You should recognize this face, this voice. All the more so, if you have some connection to the envoy who met with us at the keep yesterday."

"Hmph."

Snorting, Abel touched the chin of his demon mask as he glared at his opposite—the one Subaru had called the false Emperor, whose true identity Abel already knew: Vincent as disguised by Chisha Gold.

Right beside Abel, Al and Medium wore complicated, sour expressions—proof, surely, that to the two of them, standing outside the "cognitive obstruction" effect, the exchange with Vincent sounded like an exchange between two identical voices.

The disguise of Chisha, the "White Spider," was perfect.

That voice, that bearing—if it came to standing in as Vincent Vollachia's double, there could hardly be a second person in this world who could pull it off as well as Chisha Gold.

It even gave rise to the contradictory thought that he seemed more like Vincent than Abel himself did.

But—

"I've heard about the matter—that a greeting was exchanged with a subordinate. For so fierce a Vollachian Emperor, that's a remarkably generous pardon of an ill-mannered woman's insolence."

"Even I am not so eccentric as to bother with those who don't know their place. But when I recognize fitting value in one who stands opposed to me, it is only proper to treat them accordingly. After all—"

Vincent answered Abel's sarcasm, and after a single beat of silence following his words, the two men's gazes crossed in the empty air between them, and their lips moved as one.

"'—Let the people of the Empire be strong.'"

Yes, that was the ideal of the Sacred Vollachia Empire, its symbolic way of being.

As one who was the Vollachian Emperor, it was an answer one could only call truly admirable: that he had met, in fitting fashion, one who had shown such will to defy him.

Even if it had been Abel to whom the same thing was said, he would have given the same answer.

No matter what feelings he held within, regardless.

"Kafma, about that girl there."

"Yes. Elder Olbart asked that she be kept here at this inn... though, the Elder himself, the very one who matters, went out into the city and has not returned."

"I see."

At Kafma's answer, given with an awkward expression, Vincent gave an unhurried nod, and his gaze turned next toward Tanza.

The girl, curled small in the corner of the room, trying at least to devote herself to the duty of one being sheltered, shuddered at the gaze looking down on her from the very summit of the Empire, and let slip a breath. "Ah."

Vincent accepted Tanza's reaction—whether it was fright or fear, it was hard to say—as a matter of course, and said,

"Olbart's involvement, and yesterday's agreement with Yorna Mishigure. On top of that, given that someone connected to yesterday's envoy has now appeared searching for you, I can grasp the general shape of the situation."

"I-I..."

"There's no need to explain yourself. Though I will have Olbart Dunkelkenn speak the truth of it, your master is not me. And your enemy is not me, either."

"Ah..."

"If it is a battle you have begun, then struggle with all your might until it reaches its end. Decide for yourself whether that will burn away the fate you have chosen, or not."

Flatly, Vincent declared to Tanza that he would extend no further protection.

And yet, to say that this gave Tanza the despair of being abandoned would be a mistake.

"—"

What resided in Tanza's round eyes was, most likely, the same light, the same flame of resolve, that had kindled the moment she had first decided to take up this fight, this defiance.

That Tanza, a member of the Horned Tribe, had lived a life that was neither smooth nor bright was something Abel could easily imagine. All the more so given that, as one of the Deer Humans of Chaosflame, hers was a race that had once been the fundamental cause of the rebellion Yorna Mishigure had raised.

It would come as no surprise if Tanza herself had some connection to the victims of that.

"Vincent-sama, I apologize for the trouble I have caused. But this whole affair—all of it was arranged by me alone, kept secret from my mistress, Yorna-sama."

"As I said before, there's no need to explain yourself. As for the facts of the matter, I will question Yorna Mishigure on them directly. Still, I will take note of your will, that you wished to speak it with your own mouth."

"Yes. Thank you very much."

Straightening herself, Tanza knelt formally and bowed deeply to Vincent. Then she lifted her face, and her gaze turned back toward Abel and the others.

Unlike the look she'd given Vincent, this one carried unmistakable hostility.

"—!"

At the girl's sheer intensity, Al, standing back and waiting, gave a faint catch in his throat.

His cowardice had reached quite an extreme, but it was also true that a spirit powerful enough to overawe him had been packed into that look. And the background of just how far this girl had driven herself was something one could imagine, too.

It was all—

"—Please, I beg you, do not drag Yorna-sama into the chaos of war. She is such a kind person; she would surely fight for the sake of the many weak, and be wounded, and worn away. I cannot possibly overlook such a thing. Yorna-sama is my everything."

"Ah..."

"Please, please, I beg you. Please...!"

Medium's eyes went wide at Tanza's plea, her voice trembling faintly as she made it.

For Al and Medium, the motive Tanza held seemed somewhat unexpected. Broadly speaking, maintaining the standing of the Horned Tribe had been the objective of the residents who had attacked them along the way.

Losing Yorna Mishigure's protection would mean losing the safe haven that was the magical city of Chaosflame, and a resumption of the harsh days spent wandering the wilderness for so long. That fear, they'd assumed, was what drove it.

No doubt, that same feeling was held, at least to some degree, by the city's residents in general.

But on the other hand, that the feelings held by most of the magical city's residents would lean closer to Tanza's own was, for Abel, well within the bounds of what he'd imagined.

What had convinced him of this was the existence of the "Soul Marriage Technique," thoroughly permeated throughout the city's people.

That was a secret art that could not be achieved through one-sided feeling alone.

As for the mechanism by which it had been bound with such an overwhelming number of partners—so many that it ought, by rights, to have driven her to madness—Abel had not yet found certainty on that point himself, but in any case.

"It is Yorna Mishigure who decides, surely. Bowing your head to me will not move the situation."

"—! You—"

For some reason, Kafma's anger grew at Abel's answer, given with his arms still folded.

Though he had next to no relationship with Tanza, he extended a certain warmth simply because she was young. On that point, broadly speaking, he was a man of the same breed as Yorna.

And speaking only of the essential nature of the magical city, there was something visible to Tanza that was not visible to Kafma. Which was why, head still lowered, she continued on.

"No, that's not right. It is not Yorna-sama who decides—it is you."

"—"

"Yorna-sama's heart is vast and deep, and she is so very kind... and she is someone who dreams a dream too immensely distant for the likes of us to help her reach. You, I think, hold the means to grant it."

"...Have you learned the contents of Yorna Mishigure's letter?"

"No."

Shaking her head, Tanza denied Abel's suspicion. But she brought her shaking head to a stop, facing forward, and cast her eyes down with an expression of terrible fragility.

"But I understand. —Because it was the first time I had ever seen Yorna-sama wear such a face, so much like a young maiden's."

"A maiden's face, is it."

"Yorna-sama answers the affection directed at her. But for her to yearn for someone herself, that much... if the answer to that lies in that letter—"

"You judged that Yorna Mishigure would not refuse. And so."

"—Yes. I schemed all of it."

Bowing her head deeply, Tanza confessed to her own scheme.

"All of it" was not, strictly speaking, correct. Without doubt, Olbart's hand had been added to Tanza's scheme, twisting its content into something more vicious.

And yet Tanza was trying to take it all as her own responsibility—that it was something she herself had begun, that her own will had driven it—including even the portion where Olbart had gotten involved.

It was, altogether too much—

"—Beautiful."

So went a voice that never became a voice, shaped only within a mouth that no one could hear.

Naturally, it reached no one's ears—not the bowed Tanza, nor the braced Al and Medium, nor Kafma restraining his anger, nor Vincent with his chin propped on his hand.

"I understand the extent of your involvement. But—what will you do now?"

"—. What do you mean, what will I do."

"Even having laid your heart bare like this, you could, if you chose, keep this situation going. Even were your head to fall, you could go on having the magical city's residents target us. —No, rather, if your head fell, you would no longer be able to take Yorna Mishigure's hand."

"—"

"Or perhaps having your own head fall was itself part of your scheme."

Head still bowed, a faint tension ran through Tanza's neck. Catching sight of it, Abel became certain that Tanza had held even her own death in reserve as a last resort.

It was a fully conceivable thing. —Though few would drive themselves so far as to actually carry it out.

"So you mean to say you were prepared to die. ...What a hell of a kid."

"I won't let that happen! Absolutely not! We won't let that happen, right, Abel-chin?"

Learning of Tanza's resolve, Al was left speechless, and Medium looked to Abel for his response.

If Abel were to say something here that ran against her wishes, what attitude Medium might take was worth some consideration, but it was ultimately meaningless.

"I believe I said as much. If even her own death is part of her scheme, then taking her life would mean fulfilling this girl's plan. Unfortunately, I have no intention of playing into such a scheme."

"Abel-chin...!"

"People ought to die more efficiently than that."

"Abel-chin..."

A piece that would not follow one's intentions was troublesome, whether it stood in one's own camp or the enemy's.
