Do you happen to know what "The Dream of the Butterfly" means?

One day, a man dreamed that he had become a butterfly, fluttering gently through the sky. In and of itself, that has no deep meaning, but when he awoke from that dream, a thought occurred to him:

"Well now, did I just awaken from dreaming I was a butterfly? Or is the me standing here right now merely a dream being dreamed by the butterfly?"

Don't you think that's an intriguing way of looking at things?
This isn't a thought limited only to butterflies. What matters is: what proof is there that allows us to definitively define "the self that exists here and now"?

When you behold a scene that would be a "dream" to someone, can you really say that the self existing there isn't truly yourself? When you see a place that might well be the ideal world—could you truly give it up, resigning yourself to the idea that it was only a dream?

Or perhaps, when someone's logic swings so completely toward the dream, one might describe it as... having lost sight of the boundary between dream and reality.
