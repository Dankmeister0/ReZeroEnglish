Arc 8 Chapter 55: "Madelyn Eschart"

—Madelyn Eschart is the youngest Dragonkin currently in existence.

By nature, Dragonkin possess an origin unlike that of any other race, owing to the very mechanism of their emergence. To properly explain their nature, one must first understand the relationship between Dragonkin and the Dragons.

In the present day, the number of Dragons confirmed to exist on the earth has fallen drastically — no small part of this owes to the fact that, in ancient times, the favorite food of Reid Astrea, the Stick-Swinger, happened to be Dragon flesh.
Reid killed Dragons on a whim, and the Dragons who sought vengeance against him were killed in turn, and as this continued for years on end, the Dragons — whose total numbers were never great to begin with — dwindled in the blink of an eye and stood at the brink of extinction.

The ecology of the Dragons themselves played a major role in this near-extinction.
First, Dragons require neither mating nor breeding to increase their numbers. They are creatures capable of reproducing alone, without a male and female pairing off. The mechanism of their bodies, composed of vast quantities of mana, differs fundamentally from that of other races — if one were to force a comparison to find something similar, Spirits would be the closest match.
However, unlike Spirits, who exist in gradations such as minor spirits, quasi-spirits, and Great Spirits, a Dragon is a Dragon from the moment of its birth, and its unrivaled might undoubtedly places it at the pinnacle of all living things.

For this reason, Dragons had next to no instinct to protect other individuals of their kind, nor any drive toward preserving their species.
Because parthenogenesis was possible for them, their interest in other Dragons was inevitably thin. When they rose against Reid in the name of avenging their kind, it was not for the sake of the Dragon that had been slain, but out of a pride that refused to let the very race to which they belonged — the Dragons — be held in contempt.

As a result, it was only when they stood at the very brink of annihilation — when even the mighty Dragon who led their pack had ended up in Reid's belly, and elsewhere their conflict with the Witch of Sloth had turned fatal — that the Dragons finally realized how poorly their banner was faring, and were forced into a decision.

That decision came down to a single choice: whether or not to follow the pride of the Dragons.

Those Dragons who followed that pride abandoned the earth. Refusing to accept further attacks from hostile forces — Reid and the Witch of Sloth chief among them — they chose to fly away into the far beyond.
Those Dragons who did not follow that pride remained on the earth, making the aberrant choice to keep fighting on, never severing their ties with the arrogant, presumptuous creatures who dared treat Dragons as enemies.

By human sensibilities, this seems backward — one would expect the interpretations of following pride and not following it to be reversed — but by the Dragons' own sensibilities, this was correct.
To begin with, a Dragon is a transcendent being beyond comparison to any other race. There is no need to go out of one's way to clash with other races merely to prove as much. To a Dragon, battle is a contest of survival, and if victory or defeat is to be measured, then survival itself is the measure. Thus, the individual capable of surviving is the superior one.
By this reasoning, those who failed to grasp this and instead fought humans to prove a point that needed no proving, or who endangered their own lives by clinging to the earth, were the ones in the wrong, disgracing the Dragons as a whole.

Hence, most of the Dragons left the earth, and those who nonetheless remained were treated as oddities — or, to put it more bluntly, as aberrations among Dragons.
Foremost among these was the Divine Dragon Volcanica, who not only stayed behind but actively sided with humanity — though the subject of that particular Dragon strays from the matter at hand, and so will be omitted here.

And here, at last, the discussion returns to its true subject: the Dragonkin.

In defiance of the racial will of the Dragons, those who remained on the earth each acted according to their own designs.
Some Dragons challenged Reid or the Witch of Sloth and died. Some lived out their days like hermits in lands they had long called home. And some Dragons became human. — It is this last choice, made by that final group of Dragons, that forms the prototype of the Dragonkin.

Ironically enough, the existence of Reid Astrea played a large part in the birth of the Dragonkin.
It was not only his outrageous conduct, which drove the Dragons to the point of having to choose whether or not to follow their pride, but even more so his sheer strength, which left a deep impression on that very pride.

As stated earlier, Dragons take pride in standing at the pinnacle of all living things, and are conscious of their own nature as transcendent beings. This is an understanding felt on an instinctive level — the same way one knows how to move one's limbs, or how to see and hear, without ever having to learn it.
For this reason, what matters to a Dragon is being a transcendent being — and if that is so, there is no need to cling to the Dragon's innate form. If it is superior regardless, then even a form identical to a human's will do.

It was mentioned earlier that the bodily mechanism of Dragons resembles that of Spirits — and here is where that trait comes into play.
To begin with, the reason Dragons take on their valiant form — a colossal body with wings, sharp fangs and claws, and hard scales — is simply that this shape makes it easiest for them to exercise their abilities as transcendent beings. For so long a time, the Dragon's form saw no change, because no more fitting form for expressing a Dragon's power had yet existed.
But if the passage of the ages and the transformation of the world demand a change in order to adapt, then a Dragon will not hesitate to cast aside its majestic form and take on the same shape as a human.

That is the origin of the Dragonkin, and their relationship to the Dragons. — In other words, the Dragonkin are the evolved form of the next generation of Dragons, born through parthenogenesis.

This is why every Dragonkin, while possessing the same physical characteristics as a human, wields physical abilities beyond any comparison to a human's, and a strength that defies all biological standards.
That said, even across the long history of the Dragons, never before had they undergone so abrupt an evolution into so different a shape, and the birth of the Dragonkin brought with it a host of concurrent problems.

Chief among these, considered the most serious flaw, was the recurring phenomenon of "Dragonhusk" — in which the soul of the Dragon that could be called the Dragonkin's parent suffered severe damage, leaving it in a state as an empty shell of a mind.
A Dragon reduced to a Dragonhusk became little more than a living corpse, and — save for instinctive acts of self-defense — turned into a puppet obeying the will of the Dragonkin descended directly from it, with whom its bond ran deepest.
Even so, the Dragon's extraordinary power remained wholly intact, making it more than enough of a threat to an ordinary human — but it was by no means a desirable state to be in.

The Dragons who remained on the earth were few to begin with, and those who evolved into Dragonkin fewer still. From every angle, this evolution into Dragonkin was regarded by the Dragons as a ghastly example of failure.
And yet, even knowing of the other Dragons' failure, the Dragonkin continued to exist, small though their absolute numbers were.
Even knowing full well that, as a race, they would be despised as the floundering of foolish aberrations — those who cast aside the pride of the Dragons, remained on the earth, and in the end could not admit their choice had been a mistake.

—Dragonkin are born even now, and Madelyn Eschart was brought into the world as one of them.

A shock beyond comprehension battered Madelyn, ensconced within the Dragonhusk of the Cloud Dragon Mezoreia.

If it were Madelyn's original body — the body of a human-shaped girl — being thrown about would make sense.
Even that would be maddening enough, but at least it would be understandable. But for this colossal body of the Cloud Dragon to be thrown by something no bigger than the tip of its own tail was an event beyond all reason.
Absurd. Unthinkable. A thing that lay utterly outside the bounds of imagination.

"—Human!"

Slammed back-first into the outer wall, what filled her upside-down field of view was a blood-soaked boy beckoning her forward atop the shattered cobblestones.
His golden fur, sharp fangs, and the ferocious, savage air about him marked him as a beastly Demi-Human, but he was no less human for it. There was no reason for the flames of Madelyn's rage to die down.
To begin with, surviving a full blast of a Dragon's roar was no laughing matter.

—To store her own will within Mezoreia's Dragonhusk and move it as her own.
This was a privilege unique to Madelyn as a Dragonkin, a feat of brute force made possible only because so few years had passed since her birth as a Dragonkin, leaving the bond with Mezoreia, the parent of her individual self, still strong.
Ordinarily, the power and destructive impulse that her small body could not fully contain, this great body let her wield to her heart's content. This overwhelming power — this, she could declare at the top of her lungs, was the true Madelyn.

She had never wanted to be born into a body so small, so weak, so frail it seemed it might be crushed at any moment.
If not for that — if not for that body — Madelyn would never have been left behind during the last battle Balleroy fought.
If only Madelyn had been a Dragon, with a fearsome face and a strong, great body — and yet.

"—The Shield of the Sanctuary, Garfiel Tinzel."

Snapping his sharp fangs together, the boy — Garfiel — planted his feet hard against the shattered street and gave his name.
Not knowing the meaning behind a warrior's declaration before battle, the Dragonkin sharpened the Cloud Dragon's gaze and swung its great tail wide.
Tearing the upended body from the wall with that momentum, she planted all four limbs on the ground and glared the boy down.

She could not lose. Even against an opponent who, after being bathed in a breath she was certain had killed him, sprang back out from beneath the rubble within mere seconds — Madelyn could not lose.
This very Dragonhusk of the Cloud Dragon was the wedding dress Madelyn had desired for Balleroy's sake.

'You're not the special one, y'know...!'

The Cloud Dragon's long white whiskers trembled as a scream tore from its throat. Yet before that roar, one that overawed the very world of Dragons, the half-dead Garfiel did not waver.
He looked, for all the world, as though he had a mountain of dependable allies standing at his back.

"—Yeah, I know."

'—————'

"So I don't go gettin' the wrong idea, when the woman I'm sweet on goes runnin' ahead like a fool, they're the ones who pull me back!!"

Garfiel roared, bringing his fists together in front of his chest and slamming his silver gauntlets together with a resounding clang.
Toward this creature — one who should have been frail, yet showed not a trace of weakness — Madelyn felt anger, and something else besides. That something else, a thing a Dragon must never feel for a mere human, she denied outright.

'—Disappear, y'knooooow!!'

In that instant, answering Madelyn's outburst, the Cloud Dragon's roar shook the imperial capital once more.
Until just moments ago, Garfiel had struggled to keep Madelyn positioned within the city, working to ensure that whatever she unleashed would be aimed from the city's interior outward. It was a maneuver born of wariness toward the sheer breadth and reach of a Dragon's breath — but here, that struggle came to nothing.
Unleashed from the city's true south, it locked its range straight onto the Crystal Palace at the northernmost point. The beam of destructive heat woven within the Dragon's body would scorch the streets, reduce buildings to dust, obliterate everything lying along its line of fire, and burst clean through to the north — were it not for Garfiel.

"O-ohhhhhhhh——!!"

Bracing both feet as if driving them into the ground, raising both silver-gauntleted arms before him, Garfiel caught the Cloud Dragon's breath head-on.
Even the faintest moment of equilibrium would have been nothing short of a miracle, and yet the boy was not blown away, was not obliterated — he held on, shielding the imperial capital from ruin and enduring.

The trick that made this impossible clash of forces possible was the two legs he had driven into the earth.
The Cloud Dragon's own eyes — its Dragonhusk likewise composed of vast quantities of mana — caught sight of mana pouring into Garfiel from the earth with abnormal force.
Garfiel's abnormal recovery and endurance up to this point had likely come from drawing power up from the earth in just that way, but now the scale and momentum of that draw had leapt upward all at once.
As proof of it, Garfiel did the unthinkable: not content merely to hold his ground, he took a step forward, advancing his foot even as he stood bathed in the Cloud Dragon's breath.
Step by step, steadily, he closed the distance between himself and Madelyn.

"So I don't go turnin' soft, I got a whole crowd of people slappin' my back and tellin' me to bring it on!!"

Within Madelyn's bewildered field of vision, amid destruction so total it seemed to scorch away even sound itself, that voice reached her.
Impossible. And yet Garfiel had declared it. Declared it, and still advanced. Amid this Cloud Dragon's breath — a ruin that might well redraw the very map — he pressed forward.
One step, then another — steps that should never have been possible—

'——!!'

Witnessing that advance with her own eyes, Madelyn's breath grew badly ragged.
Roar or breath, whatever one called it, it was nothing other than a Dragon's destructive battle cry. If the mind was thrown into disorder and the breath cut short, the destruction would break off.
Garfiel's advance and defiance were enough to overcome the Cloud Dragon's breath.

"Gyaaaaaahh!!"

With a thunderous boom of an especially hard step, Garfiel wrenched both arms upward.
With that, the Cloud Dragon's breath — its final gasp — was launched skyward. In the Dragon's eyes, wide and frozen in place, stood Garfiel, white steam billowing from his entire body as it rapidly healed from burns so severe, so red, they made one want to look away.
Behind him lay the streets of the imperial capital, which his slender arms had protected to the very last, and among those streets, a red-haired man sitting flat on the ground.

'—————'

Struck fully blocked — Madelyn reeled with shock.
But the true shock, more terrible than the mere fact that a Dragon's breath had failed to work, pierced into Madelyn in a different form entirely.
The red-haired human swallowed hard, the sound loud in the silence, and looking at the streets that had not been blown away by the breath, he spoke.

"That's insane... he stopped a breath that would've blasted away even the castle...!"

'——ah'

The red-haired man's words, trembling in dread before the reality before him, carried a fear directed at Garfiel — who had defied the Dragon's threat and weathered it entirely. But for Madelyn, it was something else.
For Madelyn, it struck like a blade of madness, forcing her to look back on what she had done.

The red-haired man was right. Had Garfiel not stopped it, that roar would have blasted the imperial capital clean away.
And along that line of fire stood the Crystal Palace — where Madelyn's own body should have been, along with Balleroy and Carillon.

'N-no, that's wrong, y'know...!'

Slowly, feebly, she shook her head, denying her own actions in a gesture unbecoming of a Dragon.
Blood had rushed to her head, her thoughts had bleached white, and she had grown frantic in her need to somehow deal with the enemy before her — and as a result, Madelyn had very nearly blown everything away.
And to think she had been saved from that by a small, weak, frail, slender human.

'No, no, no no no no no no no no no nooooo...!'

"Ah? The hell're you..."

'The dragon! For Balleroy! Everything, everything, all of it was for Balleroy, y'know!!'

Garfiel eyed Madelyn with a puzzled look as she clutched her head, piling denial upon denial. As though to drown out that voice of his — the one she least wanted to hear — Madelyn screamed.
Letting loose a cry even she herself could not make sense of, she beat the Cloud Dragon's wings.

"—!"

A violent gale kicked up, whipping hard at the hair of Garfiel, who instinctively crouched low, as the Cloud Dragon Mezoreia's body shot upward in a single burst, plunging into the heavens.
Waiting there was the thick black cloud that covered the entirety of the imperial capital of Lupugana.

'You're not the special one, y'know...!'

Answering the Cloud Dragon Mezoreia's call, clouds had gathered from across the whole of the empire — a white destructive impulse in which the mana Mezoreia could not hold in reserve had been sealed away.
All of it, she gathered up at once. — It was, by any measure, far more than Madelyn could ever hope to control. And yet, she could at least drop it upon the earth as one incomprehensible mass of power.

'It's not the dragon who's special, y'know...!'

By dropping the clouds to the earth, she could blast away Garfiel and the red-haired human — could erase from the world the ones who knew what Madelyn had done.
By doing so, Madelyn—

'Balleroy is the only one who needs to be special, y'know...!!'

The Dragon's voice, near to weeping, rained down from high in the heavens, and the tiny human below looked up at the sky.
Gazing up, the small — yet neither frail nor weak — boy snapped his fangs, and—

"—You great, colossal idiot."

—Madelyn Eschart was born atop the summit of Mount Palzoa, where the Cloud Sea City of Mezoreia stood.

Named after the Cloud Dragon that had long dwelt within the empire, that city was a place many who aspired to become Flying Dragon Riders — making use of the empire's own unique techniques — made their way to.
Whether due to the power of the Dragon dwelling there or not, it was a tall mountain wreathed year-round in great clouds that clung to its slopes like a coiled band, and the city had been built high upon its elevation.
The mountain was home to wild Flying Dragons of fierce temperament, and the height at which the Cloud Sea City sat marked the very edge of the habitable zone for humans — anyone who aimed higher than that could expect only one of two outcomes: falling to a Flying Dragon's fangs, or bringing back a Flying Dragon egg and taking their first step down the path of becoming a Flying Dragon Rider.
As for the mountain's very summit, hidden beneath the thickest of those clouds, no one had ever laid eyes upon it.

That unclimbed summit of Mount Palzoa was Madelyn's very birthplace.
Wrapped in white cloud, in a place that, though it stood in the sky, could not even glimpse the sky's own blue, Madelyn was born into the world as a Dragonkin — one of the rarest beings on the planet. As a new generation of the transcendent Dragons, she came into instinctive understanding of her nature — and yet, a tragedy awaited her.
It was this—

'—I am Mezoreia. In obedience to the voice of my Beloved Child, I shall become the wind from the heavens.'

It was that the Cloud Dragon Mezoreia, her very birth parent, had become a Dragonhusk through the act of giving birth to a Dragonkin, entering a state in which communication was all but impossible — and it was in that state that she greeted Madelyn's birth.
At the side of a Dragon with whom she could barely exchange a word, one she understood only as being close to herself, the newborn Madelyn passed nothing but empty, idle time, taught nothing at all.

Why the Cloud Dragon, who had lived so long a life and surely knew both the cruelty and the unreasonableness of the world, chose so harsh a generational handover — even now, Madelyn does not know.
The one thing that can be said is that Madelyn spent an endless stretch of time within the clouds, a solitude that was not truly solitude.
And that it was Balleroy Temeglyph — who accomplished what no one else in the empire's history ever had, reaching the summit swallowed by the sea of clouds — who brought that solitude to an end.

"Well now, I figured I'd give it a shot just to test my chops, but... I never woulda guessed what's waitin' up at the top would be a cute little lady and a scary ol' Dragon."

"—!!"

Having found, in a world not of thick fog but thick cloud, Madelyn — the Dragonkin child who did not yet even have that name — Balleroy scratched his cheek in a troubled manner.
Beside him, the Flying Dragon Carillon, though daunted by the Cloud Dragon's majesty and the sheer presence of the Dragonkin child, still stood as if to protect his partner Balleroy.

'—I am Mezoreia. In obedience to the voice of my Beloved Child, I shall become the wind from the heavens.'

That the Cloud Dragon — one who spoke of nothing but that same phrase, and did nothing but offer clouds of condensed mana as sustenance — did not reject these sudden visitors was likely because they bore no hostility.
Even before Balleroy and Carillon, Flying Dragons that had strayed from their flock and carelessly wandered into the clouds had been eliminated by the Cloud Dragon's instincts, erased before ever entering the child's field of view.
For this reason, it was at this moment that the child laid eyes for the very first time on a life other than herself and the Cloud Dragon.

"—————"

The child fell silent. Or rather, she simply did not know what words to offer.
Faced with the sudden, unannounced appearance of a human and a Flying Dragon together, she could only shrink back and curl into herself. And of course, it was at just such a moment that the Cloud Dragon said nothing at all, its consciousness left sunk at the bottom of some hazy sea.
Of course, depending on what the visitors did next, the Cloud Dragon would likely have forcibly eliminated Balleroy and Carillon, just as it had done to every Flying Dragon that had ever peered upon the summit before them.
And yet, faced with the overwhelming presence of a Dragon and a Dragonkin, Balleroy showed little sign of tension at all. He simply removed the mantle he wore and said—

"Here now, put this on, miss. A young lady oughtn't go showin' her skin so carelessly."

So saying, he gave to the child — the one who would later be named Madelyn by Balleroy's own mouth — the very first kindness and warmth she had ever known in her life.

—Madelyn and Balleroy's strange trysts always took place within the sea of clouds.

"Madelyn, I've come again. Been a good girl while I was gone?"

"Balleroy!"

"Whoagh!?"

Breaking through the thick white clouds, Balleroy appeared, and the child — Madelyn — ran to him.
Catching her full-bodied leap, Balleroy — sent reeling head over heels along with Carillon, who had only just landed, by the force of the impact — dizzily patted Madelyn's head all the same.

Ever since their first meeting, Balleroy had come to show his face at the summit of Mount Palzoa often.
It was a summit that countless Flying Dragon Riders had staked their lives to reach, and still none but he ever had. At the time, Madelyn had not the faintest understanding of how much trouble Balleroy went to in order to keep coming to see her, nor how grateful she ought to have been for it.
But simply by making his way up the mountain again and again, bringing with him a connection to the outside world Madelyn had never known, Balleroy became an irreplaceable presence to her.

The very name Madelyn was something Balleroy had given her.
When he found himself at a loss for how to call out to her and asked her name, the child answered that she had none — and so he racked his brains and gave her one.

"It's the name of someone who was good to me, back when I was a kid not so different from you. My own namesake, in a manner o' speakin'... owe that person a debt, I do."

"Balleroy, was that human... precious to you, y'know?"

"Never got the chance to properly think of it that way before we parted ways. But seein' as they've stuck with me in here all the way to this age, I reckon that's what it amounts to."

He gave a sheepish laugh, and Madelyn gently pressed her cheek against his chest, somehow lonesome as it was.
He had given her, in this way, the name of something precious to him. That, she thought, must surely be proof that Balleroy held her dear.
To feel that way about Balleroy, and to be felt for in turn, brought Madelyn a warm sense of wonder.
She wanted more of it — more and more of it — and so she longed for the time she spent with Balleroy.

Wanting to talk with Balleroy more, she properly learned the human tongue.
Balleroy, for his part, was astonished at how quickly she learned — likely a trait of the Dragonkin, inherited from the nature of the Dragons.
For some reason, no matter what, she could never quite shake her accent — but it never got in the way of talking with Balleroy.

"Heh heh, my eye wasn't wrong after all. Suits you real well, Madelyn."

"I-is that so, y'know? Hehe..."

Spinning around in front of Balleroy, who nodded in satisfaction with a hand to his chin, Madelyn checked the feel of the fluttering cloth — no, the dress he had given her.
The gently colored dress was, according to Balleroy, something called sky-blue. The very same shade as Madelyn's own hair, it was something he had gone out of his way to prepare for her.
Honestly, as a Dragonkin, Madelyn found it bothersome to be hampered in movement by wrapping herself in such cloth — but that feeling melted away the moment she looked at Balleroy.

She had received a name, received words, received a dress — she had been given happiness.
Madelyn counted off, one by one, everything Balleroy had given her, and remembered every last bit of it. It was a Dragon's nature — a Dragon never forgets a treasure it has stored away.
Every object, every feeling Balleroy had given her, was a treasure to Madelyn.

"Well then, I'll come again before the moon wanes to half, Madelyn."

After spending a good while together — time as precious and fragile as something breakable — Balleroy mounted Carillon and flew off from the Cloud Dragon's nest.
Holding the promise of their next meeting close to her heart, Madelyn swallowed her sorrow and saw him off.

—Who could call Balleroy heartless, for leaving Madelyn behind atop the mountain and flying away?

It was not that Balleroy never once tried to take Madelyn outside — Madelyn, who, within a Dragon's nest wrapped in white cloud, had never even once laid eyes upon a sky the same color as her own hair.
It simply could not be done. The Cloud Dragon, dwelling within its hazy world, might overlook Balleroy and Carillon making their way into its nest, but it would never permit them to take Madelyn out of it.

'—I am Mezoreia. In obedience to the voice of my Beloved Child, I shall become the wind from the heavens.'

Mezoreia, having accepted the presence of Balleroy and Carillon for their lack of hostility, would nonetheless bare its fangs the instant anyone tried to take Madelyn outside, following its instincts to the letter.
In a Dragon's own nest, glared down upon by the Dragon that ruled it, no human could ever hope to stand a chance.
And so Balleroy could not take Madelyn away. And Madelyn did not want Balleroy to die. So she could never bring herself to ask him to take her outside.

Instead, just once, she let a plea slip out.

"Balleroy, won't you stay here forever... with the dragon, y'know?"

"—————"

Having made that plea, Madelyn regretted it the moment she saw Balleroy's cheeks stiffen in response.
The instant of silence that followed spoke plainly enough of Balleroy's answer to her plea. Balleroy would not stay here at this mountain's summit.
Outside this white, clouded world, Balleroy had left behind many things precious to him.
Just as this white world was all Madelyn had, the outside world was all Balleroy had. This place was not the world where Balleroy — where a human — belonged.

"—I'm sorry, Madelyn."

Apologizing to Madelyn, who understood as much, Balleroy reached out to pat her head as he always did. For the first time, Madelyn refused his hand, and drove him away.
With tears in her eyes, throwing a tantrum, raising her voice, she drove Balleroy and Carillon from the summit as though laying blame on them, and then Madelyn broke down sobbing.

For three days and three nights she wept without stopping, and Madelyn came to regret what she had done.
Giving in to a fleeting burst of emotion like that — what if Balleroy never showed his face again? She regretted it from the very bottom of her heart. Madelyn absolutely could not bear the thought of that sad look on Balleroy's face being the last she ever saw of him. She could do nothing but regret it, over and over.

But Madelyn was still a child, and her thoughts and imagination were naive and shallow.
The single greatest regret of Madelyn's life came after those three days and nights of sobbing.

—That day, Madelyn hardly gave a second thought to the Cloud Dragon Mezoreia leaving her side.

Even leaving her side, the Cloud Dragon's wings never carried it far from this cloud-wrapped mountain. It was, at most, a distance within which its thunderous roar could still reach her clearly enough.
She assumed it was one of two things — either hunting some Flying Dragon that had carelessly wandered into the Cloud Dragon's nest, or dealing with some reckless challenger who had set foot on the mountain path aiming for the summit.
Whatever the reason, there was no doubt Mezoreia would return before long.
Far more pressing than that, to her, was the regret that refused to fade no matter how many days passed, and the struggle to control her own emotions, still swollen from crying.

So when the Cloud Dragon Mezoreia returned, for the first time ever, bearing countless wounds across its scales, Madelyn paid it no mind at all — nor did she wonder what challenger might have inflicted them.

"—I'm sorry, Madelyn."

Madelyn learned the answer to that only when, some days after that worst and most miserable of partings, Balleroy visited the summit once more — and she saw with her own eyes the wounds covering his entire body, wounds so terrible she wanted to look away.

The apology Balleroy offered to Madelyn, struck speechless as she was, was not for having been unable to visit for a while, nor for having failed to grant her wish.

"...Wanted to beat the Cloud Dragon and take you out of here myself, I did."

Speaking so despite wounds that must surely have nearly killed him, stroking the back of his beloved dragon — battered just as badly — with a pathetic laugh, Balleroy left Madelyn weeping torrents of tears.
She regretted it. It was the greatest, most unwashable regret of Madelyn's entire draconic life.

—A Dragon fixates upon its treasures. That is a Dragon's nature.
And for the Cloud Dragon, reduced to a Dragonhusk and sunk into its hazy world, that nature was one it never forgot even in such a state — and Madelyn was the treasure it could not bear to let go of.
Balleroy had risked his very life trying to take that treasure away.

"What a fool, y'know..."

"Figured I could pull it off... but even I gotta shake my head at myself over this one."

"No! That's wrong, y'know! Not Balleroy — the dragon's the fool, y'know...!"

Denying Balleroy's pathetic self-mockery, Madelyn clenched her back teeth hard.
And she cursed herself, who up until this very moment had done nothing but receive from Balleroy, and at last, without needing to be taught, found for herself what she had to do.

This thick white cloud, which had existed since the very moment of Madelyn's birth, was a wall that Madelyn herself needed to tear open with her own claws.
And yet Madelyn, indulging in Balleroy's kindness, had let him keep breaking through it in her stead, never once even trying to realize as much — not until he had gone so far as to risk his own life on the brink of death.
How shameless could she be. Was such an unsightly way of being fitting for a Dragonkin — a transcendent being?

"The dragon will do it herself, y'know."

"Madelyn?"

"The dragon will go outside by herself, y'know. Not by being taken out — by herself... This time, the dragon will go meet Balleroy herself, y'know. And then..."

Gripping tight the hem of the dress Balleroy had given her, she spoke — in words she had learned from Balleroy, of feelings Balleroy had taught her — to Balleroy, who had given her everything.
As a Dragonkin, a transcendent being, she spoke to the one special human who had captured her heart.

"And then, will you make the dragon your bride, y'know?"

"—————"

At Madelyn's question, a moment of silence arose, just as it had before.
This time, though, Balleroy's silence was different from the one that had once tormented the foolish, ignorant Madelyn into three days and nights of sobbing — this time, she properly understood what lay hidden within it.
Back then, Madelyn had seen only what she wanted to see.
Even while speaking to Balleroy, all she'd had was the desire to be looked at herself.
And so—

"—Yeah, I reckon so. If only that could be, how good it'd be, for both me and you, Madelyn."

With that kindness of his, given with all he had, Madelyn was made to fully understand that she was not the first in his heart — and even so, upon that understanding, the promise was made.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


—Some might say that this feeling of Madelyn's was not true romantic love at all.

There may be those who would analyze it as nothing more than imprinting — the way a chick, freshly hatched from its egg, fixates upon the first thing it lays eyes on.
That she simply wanted to think of the one who had touched her life — the first being besides herself and one too close to herself, the first to show her kindness, the relationship that had given her so many firsts — as something special.

There may be those who would mock Balleroy's fleeting, faint smile in response to Madelyn's courtship, calling it nothing but a kind lie told to spare the feelings of a daughter who claims she'll marry her own father when she grows up.
There may be those who would pity it as nothing more than Balleroy, unable to speak of his own hidden feelings for someone else he loved, extending that same sympathy toward Madelyn, who found herself in much the same position.

But all of that, every last bit of it, is nothing more than a human's yardstick.
The values and ways of thinking of the Dragonkin — or of the Dragons — differ from those of humans. And if one were to insist that even among Dragonkin and Dragons this does not hold true, then Madelyn's own values differ from theirs as well.

Madelyn wanted Balleroy Temeglyph with her whole heart — truly, seriously, from the very depths of her soul.
If that was not love or romance, then Madelyn would never come to know either one, not for all eternity. That was how fiercely she burned her soul with a life-or-death passion.

Swearing an oath to Balleroy, who had nearly died for her sake, Madelyn tore through the white clouds.
She challenged the Cloud Dragon that stood in the way of her attempt to reach the outside, that sought with all its Dragon's might to crush her hopes at every turn — sinking her fangs into it, and at last subduing it entirely.

And then, flying out for the first time beneath a sky the same color as her own hair, having for the first time accomplished something she herself had decided to do, setting out for the first time to go to her beloved of her own will — she learned the truth.

—That Balleroy Temeglyph had lost his life defying the empire.


"—What should the dragon have done, y'know?"

Even now, Madelyn does not know the answer to that.
Confronted with something too vast to swallow, Madelyn's love found nowhere left to go.

Left to struggle with a love that no longer had anywhere to go, on the verge of losing even the meaning of her own life, Madelyn had grown desperate enough to consider becoming one of the Blight Dragons remembered in imperial history — and it was in that state that Berstetz, searching for someone to fill an empty seat among the Nine Divine Generals, found her.

Following his scent, she had found Balleroy's dwelling, left behind in the imperial capital.
Balleroy had been recorded as a traitor, and Berstetz, entrusted with disposing of his dwelling, was not killed by the Madelyn he found there for one reason: within Berstetz's thread-thin eyes lay the same light of something lost that Madelyn herself carried.
Berstetz patiently listened his way through Madelyn's halting story, and once he had drawn out both the truth that she was a Dragonkin and that she had some connection to Balleroy, he made her a certain proposal.

One path was to give in impulsively to her overflowing emotions and follow the same road as Balleroy, cut down as an enemy of the empire.
The other was to accept the recommendation of Berstetz, whom she had met here, and become a member of the Nine Divine Generals, as Balleroy had once been, following in the footsteps of the man who had died, and waiting for a chance at retribution.

She did not agonize over it long. Even Madelyn herself is not entirely sure why she did not agonize over it more.
Perhaps she simply wanted to gather up as many traces of Balleroy's footsteps as she could.

"—Madelyn Eschart. It's an old Vollachian family name, one that no longer exists, but allow me to bestow it upon you. Prestige, after all, is a matter of some importance."

"Prestige, y'know?"

"For a name to no longer exist means it was extinguished fighting against this very empire. It's only the sense of an old bag of bones like myself, but I thought it might suit your cherished wish."

Following that low-voiced suggestion of Berstetz's, Madelyn Eschart was born.
After that, she accepted Berstetz's recommendation, and things proceeded just as he had intended — Madelyn took up the very position Balleroy had once earned, and set out on a journey to come to know the sides of him she had never known.

It was a journey toward a destination she had to reach, one whose answer she did not know.
Even after stepping out from that mountain summit, from that narrow world wrapped in white cloud, into the outside world Balleroy had shown her, Madelyn's heart remained captive to the man who had come to her that day astride a Flying Dragon.

Even so, she carried out the duties expected of her as one of the Nine Divine Generals. In Balleroy's place.
Even so, she displayed the dignity a General of the empire was meant to display. In Balleroy's place.
Even so, she wielded her power to strike down the empire's enemies. In Balleroy's place.

Berstetz had likely recommended Madelyn's current position believing it for the best.
Or perhaps Berstetz, too, carried some wish that had not faded even with age, and had simply resolved to use Madelyn as a piece necessary to fulfill it.
Whichever it was, Madelyn has no wish to resent Berstetz for it.
Nor was that feeling limited to Berstetz alone. —She simply had no room left in her heart for such a thing.

The time spent holding the rank of one of the Nine Divine Generals, tracing Balleroy's footsteps as a General herself — for Madelyn, those were days of fathomless terror.

Every time she strove and struggled, thinking of Balleroy, trying to fill the hole he had left behind, it felt to her as though she herself were killing Balleroy all over again.
Every time she made up for his absence, she was stealing away his place in the world — the very traces that he had ever been there at all.
Could she, then, hand it off to someone else? No — she would have felt that whoever took it from her was killing Balleroy in her stead, and surely Madelyn would have turned her hand against them.

She understood well enough that Balleroy would never wish for that, and so Madelyn wore herself thin.
Since she could let no one else do it, she went on killing Balleroy with her own hands. And when, someday, she finally finished killing him off entirely — would her own heart die along with him, too?

Somehow, that felt like it would be the best way for it to end.
It was a heart, a set of feelings, given to her by Balleroy. By rights, it ought to have died when Balleroy died — when she failed to be at his side in the most important moment of his life.
And yet—

"Why do you have a dragon's fang... Carillon's fang hanging there, y'know!?"

In the midst of the upheaval shaking the empire, she encountered a man wearing, on his person, the fang of the Flying Dragon that had died alongside Balleroy.
Hearing from this man, who knew a side of Balleroy that Madelyn herself never had, stories of Balleroy from the times she had not been with him — Balleroy apart from being a General — the love in Madelyn's heart welled up once more.
And so, shaken and shaken again, endlessly rattled, it finally happened.

—The Great Disaster brought back, without question, the very thing Madelyn had lost.

He praised her sky-blue hair as his favorite color, calling it just that, and patted her head.
With him and that soft, gentle smile of his — the kind that tightened her chest just to look at — she wanted to fulfill the promise they never managed to keep: to hold each other beneath a clear, wide-open sky, somewhere with no clouds in the way.

In a place other than that mountain summit, Madelyn was finally able to touch Balleroy again.
And yet, even so, thick clouds still hung over the sky, and the world remained dim and shut away.

"—What should the dragon have done, y'know?"

Madelyn no longer knows what she ought to do.
She loves him so much, so very much, that beyond that, she can no longer understand anything else at all.

Nor does she wish to understand.

The murky black clouds swirled, gathering in around the Cloud Dragon that had risen into the sky.
Looking up at it from the ground, Garfiel felt every hair on his body stand on end, the blood in his veins seeming to boil, his mind struck by an instinctive impulse.

He had weathered the Dragon's breath entirely, and his whole body, scorched by the wave of heat, was rapidly healing.
But the black cloud of destruction, near enough to blanket the entire sky, held within it power enough to spare to undo Garfiel's recovery, and even the tilted, half-ruined streets of the imperial capital he had fought so desperately to protect.

"That huge damn cloud — every bit of it's made outta a Dragon's mana...!"

It was a hidden trick no one had noticed until Mezoreia stirred it up and changed its very nature.
Faced now with the Cloud Dragon's trump card — hidden so boldly it had gone unnoticed — bared and ready to strike, what shook Garfiel's heart was not the ruin that this colossal mass of power would bring, but rather the very manner of the Cloud Dragon trying to wield it.

The Cloud Dragon Mezoreia — no, he could somehow tell that what lay within it was a different being entirely.
For a Dragon that was supposed to have lived hundreds upon hundreds of years, flying across the skies of this world for so long a life, its words and actions carried far too little of the weight one would expect.
It was a sense entirely separate from mere strength — one that Garfiel, who knew Ryuzu, a figure whose youthful appearance belied a dignity earned by truly living through a long span of time, could recognize.

At the same time, Garfiel came to truly appreciate just how remarkable Subaru's tactical judgment had been.

"Boss told me to go bring down a flyin' Dragon, he did..."

Being pushed forward to go toe-to-toe with a Dragon — the strongest living thing in the world — and being trusted enough to be handed that very role, was a fact of staggering weight.
The strength that trust gave him had been enough to pull Garfiel, on the verge of death, back from whatever incomprehensible theater he had glimpsed and return him to reality.
But the true meaning behind Garfiel having been sent here lay elsewhere.

Whether Subaru had aimed for that on purpose, Garfiel did not know.
Either way, it made no difference. This Cloud Dragon Mezoreia was the very enemy Garfiel needed to face.
Because—

"—Bawlin' your eyes out, straining yourself to the limit, all just to protect your own world."

The sight of Mezoreia, raising a scream-like cry and scraping together power with reckless abandon, brought to Garfiel a bitter, shameful feeling.
Once, Garfiel had unleashed that same fury and grief upon the people around him, just as Mezoreia was doing now.

It was because his comrades had forcibly stopped him, back then, that Garfiel now had the standing and resolve he did, the two legs planted firm, the sturdy body he had.

Of course, Garfiel and Mezoreia differed in standing, in circumstance, even in race. So there was no guarantee the same solution that had worked for Garfiel would work for Mezoreia.
But then again, there was no proof it wouldn't, either.
To find out, there was nothing for it but to do as had been done for him — make her lower her fangs, and talk.
For that—

"—"

Casting his eyes dizzyingly about his surroundings, Garfiel snapped his fangs together hard.
The half-crumbled outer wall; the imperial capital, half-destroyed and scorched by the Dragon's breath just moments before. Every tall building had snapped in the middle from the tilting ground, none of them offering Garfiel the height he needed.
However much he wanted to reach Mezoreia, Garfiel could not reach up to the height of those clouds.
So Garfiel gave up on doing it alone.

"Old man!!"

"—. Ah?"

Garfiel whirled around, his expression changing color, and within his view, Heinkel — sitting flat on the ground, staring blankly up at the sky — turned to look his way.
Reflecting himself in those trembling, unfocused blue eyes, Garfiel seized him by both shoulders.

"Lend me a hand! I gotta get up there somehow, no matter what!"

"Fly...? Fly? Did you say fly? What — what the hell are you talkin' about!? There's no way in hell you can do somethin' like that! You have any idea how high that is!"

At Garfiel's near-shout of a request, Heinkel's eyes went wide before he, too, shouted right back.
Trying to shake off the hand gripping his shoulder, Heinkel pointed up overhead.

With a roar, the churning clouds shifted color, as if swallowing up even the color of the sky beyond the black clouds, the heavens changing into a bluish shade of purple.
Pointing at that sight — a cataclysm in and of itself — Heinkel, his face pale as death, said—

"It's over already!"

"I ain't lettin' it end!"

"—"

"Nothin's over! Not me, not you, old man — neither one of us has lost yet!"

Still gripping his shoulder tight to keep from being shaken off, Garfiel appealed to Heinkel with all his strength.
Heinkel caught his breath, his cheeks going stiff. Even as he pointed a trembling finger at the heavens, his other hand still clenched tight around his own sword — and Garfiel decided to believe in this man.

"Don't feel real, does it. I get it, old man. —Feels just like the end of the world."

Muttering that, Garfiel narrowed his eyes, and with that swirling, cloud-shaped ruin looming overhead, he nodded, fixing his gaze upon a distant sky far beyond the battlefield they now stood on.
It seemed that, quite literally, everywhere at once, this was what Subaru would call a Tennōzan.

To the north of the capital, an iceberg was falling, tearing through the clouds; to the northeast, a slash that seemed to sever the world itself ran out toward the walls and the mountains beyond them. To the east, sky and earth alike were composed of nothing but a hundred-fold red, and every battlefield across the land was on the verge of bringing a different sort of ending down upon the empire.
But Garfiel did not despair.

"Ain't that right, boss."

Subaru must have chosen the way to fight against an ending that wore so many different faces.
And to counter the ending unfolding in this particular place, the strongest card Subaru had chosen to play was none other than Garfiel Tinzel.

Not Emilia, not Beatrice, not Roswaal, not Spica, not Halibel, not Olbart, not Tanza, not Medium, not Jamal — Garfiel.

"—'The Sword Saint Reid draws his blade and laughs before a Dragon.'"

"...Don't you dare lump me in with that lunatic of an ancestor."

"—'There's no running from Reinhard.'"

"Don't you say that name! I'm— I'm...!"

"—————"

"I'm..."

In that place where heaven and earth alike were on the verge of ending, Heinkel covered his own face with the hand that had been pointing at the sky, and let those feeble words slip out.
What words would follow after that, Garfiel could not say. Perhaps even Heinkel himself had not yet found what came next.
If that were so—

"—Let's you and me pry open whatever comes after those words you can't get out, old man."


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


Could anyone imagine it?
A sky vast and endless, every last cloud filling it transformed into a hail of blades and bullets, raining down as though it were the very end of the world.

That was exactly what was about to happen.
Except that, contrary to the impression such a hail of blades and bullets might give, this cloud of world-ending ruin was not raining down evenly across the whole of the empire's land — it was gathering into a single point, about to bore straight through.

—The swirling black clouds twisted into a spiral, weaving the very heavens into a colossal cone.

Monstrous, it was warped and ominous — and yet, in the single respect of being purpose-built for destruction, it was even beautiful.
Given shape by the Cloud Dragon, its white wings spread wide against the sky, both hands raised — this form of the apocalypse was released from the hands of a Dragon that, for all its colossal body, wept and sobbed like a small child.

'—Disappear, y'know.'

Whether that tear-choked cry was directed at someone on the ground below, upon whom the ruin would fall; or at the very earth itself, which would surely be blown away, unable to withstand it; or whether it was neither aimed at the earth nor at anyone at all, but a form of self-punishment hurled at herself alone — no one could say.

Whichever it was, the result would be the same.
With no way to stop a trump card that a transcendent being like a Dragon had spent so long a time preparing, catastrophic damage would occur, blasting away roughly the southern half of the imperial capital, and the design of the Great Disaster would be fulfilled.

The imperial capital's collapse would mean the empire's total defeat against calamity, and the unstoppable army of death, having swallowed the land it had destroyed, would turn its fury upon the remaining nations as well.
And so the world would be swept up into an unending chain of tragedy.

That was the unshakeable outcome of a world upon which the apocalypse had fallen.
The only one who could deny that outcome — an outcome that no one but some Witch, a lump of curiosity somewhere out there, would ever welcome — was a diminutive Valiant, hurling himself into the falling apocalypse—

"—————"

Feeling the presence of the earth in his very soul before he pushed off, the Valiant gathered every last scrap of strength he could scrape together, and staked his entire body upon it.
The leg he drove out landed upon a sword still sheathed, and after an instant of balanced force, it was swung upward with ferocious, all-consuming might.

It was a great blow.
It was not swung against some fearsomely mighty Dragon, nor did it cut into the cone bent on destroying the world; not a single fragment of trained technique went into it.
He did not even meet his enemy's eyes. And yet, it was a great blow.

At least, that was the judgment of the Valiant who received it on the sole of his own foot.
All that remained was for him, entrusted with the outcome, to prove whether that great blow would go unrecorded by anything, unremembered by anyone.

'—————'

Beyond the falling apocalypse, his eyes met those of the Dragon rising up to meet the heavens from below.
Seeing those eyes waver, badly frightened, a smile rose to his face.

A smile out of place, almost troublingly so.
It was quickly overwritten by a bellicose, powerful, ferocious grin worthy of a raging tiger.

"Gonna give you a knuckle sandwich."

With everything he had, without a shred of restraint, he drove it home.
Drove it home, and having done that—

"Tell me your story. —Same as my boss and the others did for me."

The mighty silver arm, snapping upward, collided with the cone-shaped black cloud of ruin.
In that instant, the folded heavens and the twisted black cloud became a soundless explosion, swallowing the world whole.

"—————"

The outcome of that collision arrived in a world stripped of sound.
Whatever conclusion it reached would surely be drawn out after a flash of light.

But the sky that emerged beyond that collision — the sky, once thick with black cloud, now scoured clean — was blue.
It was nothing but a spreading sky-blue, the very same shade as the hair that the Flying Dragon Rider — who had once given a name to a lone, solitary Dragonkin — had touched again and again, so lovingly, so tenderly.
