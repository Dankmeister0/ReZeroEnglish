Arc 6 Chapter 48: "――Murder Becomes a Habit"

"――――"

Staring at the black cover of the *Book of the Dead* in his arms, Subaru had fallen silent.

Beatrice pressed close at his side, and Subaru could feel her worried, or perhaps suspicious, gaze stabbing into the side of his face — and yet he found himself unable to move.

Until just a moment ago, what had dominated Natsuki Subaru's mind was a self-directed dialogue as fruitless as a Zen koan, one with no answer.

In truth, what occupied Subaru's head was, more often than not, nothing but the shallow sort of thinking that deserved to be dismissed as a waste of time — a vague, childish doubt that rightly ought to be laughed off.

—That no matter who a person is, they hide a face other than the one they show to others.

That was simply an obvious truth, something everyone learns in the process of growing from child to adult.
Behind a smiling face, an expression of hatred. Behind a look of fury, the tears of grief. Human beings are creatures capable of concealing such true feelings and interacting with others as if nothing were amiss.

No one else can ever know a person's true heart, true feelings, true nature — not with perfect certainty.

Not siblings, not parent and child, not husband and wife — even family cannot fully understand one another.
Friends, childhood companions, best friends, lovers — no matter how deep the bond runs, the true heart remains a black box, one that can never be pried open.

Loving someone. Feeling fondness for them. Opening your heart to them. Opening your body to them. All these various bonds of mind and body are, in the end, an entirely separate matter from *that*.

Yes, he understood that much. But understanding it didn't mean he could abandon the thought.
To want to know, to want to understand, to want to ask, to want to expose — that was an arrogant wish, but a desperate one all the same. All the more so when it was a situation where lives — his own and others' — hung in the balance.
Even if it were a means that could never, by rights, exist.

—No, it was supposed to be a means that did not exist.

Because it was supposed to be a means that did not exist, groping after the thought had seemed like a fool's errand, worthy only of scorn.
And yet, here in this Tiegeta archive, Subaru had learned otherwise.

"――――"

—Meili Portroute.
By reading through the *Book of the Dead*, Subaru had relived Meili's life, had come to understand what schemes moved her, what she thought, and who it was she had hated in the end.

Of course, that vanishingly brief relived experience wasn't the entirety of the girl named Meili.
The concept of time itself was far too vast to be received whole into a brain as a single instant's event. Even the span of a young girl's short life could not be crammed, uncompressed, into a vessel in one sitting.

He had merely nibbled at the essentials, chewed on them however he pleased, and now felt as though he understood Meili.
That, Subaru understood — painfully — was the true state of Natsuki Subaru's heart right now.

And yet, even so, still.
By coming to understand the fact that he hadn't understood even a fragment of her, Subaru's understanding of Meili had deepened beyond any comparison to before.

—That there had been a person "I" had cherished. That, robbed of that person, her heart, having lost its anchor, had gone on wandering. That being together with Subaru and the others who had done that to her had left her struggling with her own increasingly uncertain place among them. That she had gone into the archive seeking the *Book of the Dead*, wanting to know her own true heart, and there, ashamed that her scheme had been discovered, had even fallen into despair.

All of it was a Meili that Subaru had never known.
And, without quite meaning to, having learned the feelings hidden deep in her chest, Subaru realized something.

That this — this *Book of the Dead* — might be the one true, unfalsified means of knowing another person's real heart.

"――――"

He had only just wished to know.
Had only just wished to know Emilia's, Beatrice's, and the rest of his companions' true intentions, their real feelings, here inside the tower. Why they trusted "Natsuki Subaru."

Why did they trust the "Natsuki Subaru" who had killed "me"?
Right now, playing the desperate, contemptible role of "Natsuki Subaru," what feelings did they actually hold as they dealt with him?

What if their affection were a disguise, and hatred the truth of it — what if anger, sorrow, and joy were all borrowed masks, and hostility, malice, ill will — things like that — were their real, genuine feelings?

He didn't know. He wanted to know. He wanted to believe. He couldn't believe.
Were Emilia and the others allies, or enemies — enemies who had killed him, or allies who would let him live?
Could he love them? Could he not? Could he hate them? Could he not?

—Might the answer to that be found by reading their *Book of the Dead*?

"...Subaru, you still don't look well. If staying here makes you uneasy, maybe it'd be better to go somewhere else and rest."

Touching Subaru's shoulder, Beatrice made the suggestion as he sank into thought.
Caught by her distinctive blue eyes, patterned like butterfly wings, Subaru's breath caught faintly. Glancing down, he saw the girl's small — too small — palm resting on his shoulder.

What would happen, he wondered, if he took that hand, pinned her head down with all his strength, and slammed her into the floor?

"So small..."

"Mm? What are you suddenly on about? This minimal size is precisely the wellspring of Betty's loveliness, I suppose. Even Subaru has said as much before."

At Beatrice's pouting declaration, Subaru's cheeks nearly loosened into a smile despite himself.
True, if he were in his usual condition, he might well have said exactly that. There, feeling some resemblance between himself and "Natsuki Subaru," the almost-smile quickly turned bitter.

Small. Beatrice really was small — a child.
Her neck was thin, her bones soft. The hand on his shoulder was small enough to be entirely swallowed by his own palm; if he really put his strength into an embrace, her whole body seemed fragile enough to shatter.
If he actually carried that through, with all his strength, he could probably kill her with ease.

—If he killed her, if her life ended, would her book too appear in the archive?

*"Just like you did to me, hm?"*

"――――"

Suddenly, as he sank into thought, a voice that couldn't possibly be Subaru's own echoed in his mind.
Somehow sweet, more sultry than her age should allow, and yet strangely "familiar" in tone — a voice that, in the space of such a brief time, had already become as intimate as his own inner voice.

The dead girl's voice, absorbed into himself, mocked Natsuki Subaru's resolve.

"――――"

But Subaru paid it no mind, focusing his attention on the matter at hand.
Namely — as a practical measure — was there a path to making use of the *Book of the Dead*, or wasn't there?

If there was, how could he give it form —

"You look pale. We really should move somewhere else. But before that..."

"—Ah."

"I'll just put this back where it belongs, I suppose. Betty can't afford to go hazy in the head right now too."

Saying so, Beatrice took the *Book of the Dead* from Subaru's frozen hands. With careful movements, cradling it so it wouldn't fall open, she carried it to the shelf where it had originally been lodged and returned it to the empty space that had been left there.

"Position-wise, this should be easy to remember, I suppose. Though the books in this archive might move on their own, so..."

"Not exactly a foolproof plan, huh. ...Yeah, that's true."

"We should consider taking it out of the archive rather risky too. Leaving it lying on the floor seemed to bother Shaula, so it's probably wisest to avoid that."

"Feels like a once-in-a-lifetime library encounter, huh."

Exchanging words in a parched tone, Subaru let the phrase slip out.
In truth, even in the world Subaru knew, meetings with old, long-out-of-print books were often described that way — as once-in-a-lifetime encounters. Like a secondhand bookstore stumbled upon during a trip: miss the chance, and you might never come across that book again. Perhaps some similar logic governed this archive too.

Then again, that was probably overthinking it. Better to assume it was simple harassment.

In any case, while watching Beatrice's back as she worked carefully, doing her best to commit the placement of the books to memory, Subaru sank ever deeper into the sea of his own thoughts.

With brutal practicality, he worked backward from the warmth he'd felt to calculate a life, groping for ways to kill the girl.
And it wasn't only Beatrice he needed to consider. Every member of the party who had come into the tower with him was, by the logic of his *Book of the Dead* plan, a potential target.

*"Wanting to kill people just to find out whether you can trust them or not — my, mister, you really are twisted, aren't you."*

At the girl's voice cutting in to tease him, Subaru clicked his tongue inwardly.

The first thing he wanted to make clear was that this was, at most, a thought experiment forced on him by necessity.
Subaru was in no way a psychopath ruled by murderous impulse, nor did he possess some perverse desire to find pleasure in such an act.
It was simply that, when he pursued cold practicality to its logical end, this option happened to be the first thing that came to mind.

*"Skip the excuses, hm? Why don't you just tell me what you're planning to do?"*

—Eliminating Beatrice would be simple.

Even now, the back she showed Subaru betrayed defenselessness, powerlessness, a total lack of guard.
Bluntly put, Beatrice's life was held together by nothing more than the flimsy, unreliable balance of "Subaru's sanity" — a single thin layer of skin. Should that scale tip, should practicality outweigh reason in some form, Subaru would surely succeed, easily, in taking her life.
With Beatrice, it seemed there was only the binary choice of doing it or not — the countless branching twigs before that point could be ignored wholesale.

*"And what about the half-elf lady?"*

Emilia would be even simpler, an easy win.
There had once been a time when, having just woken, Subaru had grabbed her slender neck, and she'd offered no resistance at all. If he'd poured his full strength into his arm right then, a neck that thin would have snapped easily.
True, she possessed an arm strength that would put a pro wrestler to shame, but if she never got the chance to use it, that hardly mattered. One instant, with all his strength, choking her throat —

*"Fufufu, that one's your specialty, isn't it?"*

—. Ram, too — sharp-tongued as she was, was at her core nothing but a frail girl.
He recalled the time she'd pressed her forehead to his in tears, begging him about the rightness of restoring her memories. The arm that had clung to him so desperately had been thin, its strength weak; that had been one fragile, fleeting girl.
Echidna was in the same position. Thinking it over this way, they were all frail girls, one after another — it struck him, almost painfully, what a poor choice of personnel it had been to bring them out here to the far edge of a desert.
Surely there had been far more suitable candidates available; the shortage of proper manpower was severe indeed.

Which meant Subaru had plenty of openings to exploit — but the final gate was another matter entirely.

"—Julius."

The one man besides himself, the one who even carried a sword, stood as an obstacle.
Him alone, Subaru couldn't say a frontal assault would be the right answer. —Though for that matter, challenging Emilia head-on would also be suicide; that was a separate issue.

In any case, if he were to actually carry out the *Book of the Dead* plan, Julius would surely be the greatest wall in his way.
Put another way, that also meant Julius was the one who ought to be dealt with first.

*"Considering how long it takes to find the right book, though, there's never enough time, is there?"*

Just as the girl's voice said, Subaru's goal wasn't simply "kill them, and that's that."
Rather, killing was merely a necessary waypoint he had to pass through — the true objective lay beyond it, in the *Book of the Dead* obtained in exchange for that death.

For that very reason, he needed to be certain of success.
"I killed them, but it failed" wasn't an ending he could accept. Because beyond that lay the place he was actually after.

"Subaru, let's leave the book as it is for now, I suppose. We should get away from here... maybe head to the Spirit room for a while. You should be able to settle down there."

"The Spirit... room..."

Led by the hand by the returning Beatrice, pulled up to his feet by her slight weight, Subaru — hearing "the room where the Spirit is" — pictured his own restart point.
That place was the Spirit's room, and in that place was —

"Patrasche..."

"...Speaking that dragon's name again. Honestly, what an unrewarding partner to worry over. Betty is the one worrying here, and yet — it hardly seems fair, I suppose."

"...S-sorry. It's nothing, really, no hidden meaning behind it."

For a moment, the image of the black Earth Dragon flitted through his mind, and Subaru's lips softened into the faintest smile. Caught in the act by Beatrice, he hastily corrected his expression.

Even so, in this very moment, the one being Subaru could trust without reservation was Patrasche.
The one who had truly, at the risk of her own life, run herself ragged to protect him — Patrasche alone stood by Natsuki Subaru's side without regard for true feelings or hidden hearts —

*"Really? If that girl found out you're not really 'Natsuki Subaru' — would she still do the same for you?"*

"――――"

*"In the end, doesn't it seem like there's no one on your side at all, mister?"*

The voice echoing in his mind, laced with a smile, mocked Natsuki Subaru.
The presence lodged within him, distinct from Natsuki Subaru, tried to shatter his optimism.

He should have been able to laugh it off as a ridiculous accusation. But in truth, Subaru had no such room to spare. His heart had already, half-consciously, agreed with the girl's words.

Even Patrasche's kindness, which had thrown away everything of herself for him — perhaps that too had only ever been directed at "Natsuki Subaru," and if she learned that the Subaru here now was a fake, perhaps she wouldn't even look his way again.

"Here, Subaru, let me help you."

"Ah, yeah..."

Tossed about by the girl's voice inside his head, Subaru clenched his back teeth against an unacceptable feeling.
Because of that, his focus had drifted entirely away from what was right in front of him. Obediently, he held out his hand, and Beatrice, reaching to take it, suddenly paused, her expression catching on something.

Her gaze had fixed on the area around Subaru's wrist — and then she noticed.

"There are scratch marks, I suppose. And such deep ones, too..."

Rolling up the sleeve of Subaru's right arm, Beatrice frowned at the painful-looking claw marks. She didn't seem to notice the deeper gouge above them, but even the marks in front were bad enough.

"――――"

Given the circumstances, Beatrice had no way of knowing how Meili had died.
So she wouldn't immediately connect these scratches to Meili. But even setting that aside, she would surely feel uneasy wondering just what had caused this wound.

If Meili's body were found on top of that, suspicion falling on him would be unavoidable.

"――――"

His unprepared heart began pounding like an alarm bell.
Before him, Beatrice's eyes were fixed on his hand; she hadn't noticed the look on his face. Right now, he could act on impulse and restrain Beatrice before she reacted.

*"So, what'll it be? Ohh, are you finally going to start?"*

As if toying with his resolve, the voice pressed Subaru toward a decision.
In time with his pounding, throbbing heart, he felt his temples ache with the rush of blood.
Depending on what Beatrice said next, he might just —

"...Looks like you've scratched your own arm again, I suppose. A bad habit, hm?"

"...Huh?"

"This spot isn't good. If Emilia found this, no telling how worried she'd be, I suppose. Even Betty can't turn a blind eye if it gets much worse than this."

Tracing a finger over the wound on Subaru's wrist, Beatrice lowered her eyes, looking pained.
At her words, Subaru was struck by an unexpected shock that left him unable to move.

Beatrice's manner suggested she was, in fact, quite used to seeing scratch marks on Subaru's arm.
And moreover, it wasn't for the horrifying reason Subaru had assumed she might notice — it was for an entirely different reason: she showed no confusion at all about the wounds being the result of self-harm.
Even hearing that this was something being hidden from Emilia — that alone was shocking.

"If we go to that room where the Spirit is, this should heal too, I suppose. Still, I can't just watch it like this, so let me fix it up a little, at least."

Saying so, Beatrice wrapped the wound on Subaru's wrist in a faint light.
The warm, tingling sensation that followed was, no doubt, the throb of a wound being healed. Healing magic — he was witnessing it now, with his own eyes.

"――――"

At the same time, the hostility toward Beatrice inside Subaru rapidly dissolved.
Along with that warmth, along with the healing of the wrist wound, it was as though the cracking humanity of Natsuki Subaru had received some genuine repair.

*"How boring."*

In his mind, Subaru heard the girl mutter, her tone that of someone whose expectations had been thwarted.
Hearing it directly inside his skull, Subaru swallowed down the wretched state he'd fallen into.

Certainly, it remained an option. But it was only that — an option.
There was no need to take the initiative, to steer himself toward the path that would let him claim the *Book of the Dead*.
Especially not in a situation as unprepared, as ill-arranged, as this one.

"――――"

Here, now, doing Beatrice harm was not the wise move.
The preparations weren't ready yet. Even if he were to resort to that worst of measures, it would have to wait until every preparation was complete.

For that very reason, for now, here —

"Come on, let's go, Subaru. I'll explain things to everyone else later, I suppose."

"—Got it. Sorry for the trouble, Beatrice."

"That's a promise we don't keep between us, I suppose."

And so, whether or not it reflected her true feelings, Natsuki Subaru decided he ought to defer to the girl's consideration before him.

As an aside, there's a saying that goes like this.

—"Murder becomes a habit."

It's one of the words left behind by that famous detective, Hercule Poirot.

—"Murder becomes a habit."

The meaning of that phrase isn't that a person who kills awakens to a taste for murder, repeating the act to satisfy their own cravings.

—"Murder becomes a habit."

It means that once someone has resolved a problem through murder, when the next problem arises, they will think, once again, to break through the situation the same way — through murder.

—"Murder becomes a habit."

The moment one considers murder as one option among others, when it isn't strictly necessary, something — some first and most precious thing — has already gone askew.

—"Murder becomes a habit."

Even if one has never, in fact, committed a single murder of one's own will; even while despising the act itself; even having glimpsed, firsthand, the memories of those harmed by such an act — the habit does not go away.

—"Murder becomes a habit."

The habit does not go away.


—"Murder becomes a habit."
