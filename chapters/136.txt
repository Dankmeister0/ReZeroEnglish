Arc 3 Chapter 59: "A Gamble Against Despair"

High and far, again and again, gales of laughter rang out, layering over one another.

In a world overrun by mist, three fish-shaped shadows swam, their massive bodies swaying as they went.
A misshapen existence, countless twisted mouths open across its entire body, ceaselessly loosing shrill cries from every one of them. A product of malice that had devoured countless travelers, reducing uncountable lives to nothing.

Even a single one of these creatures possessed threat enough to plunge people into despair — and now, having multiplied its fish-shadow into three, it mocked the humans who still dared to resist.

Looking up at the White Whale's massive form floating overhead, the faint sound of someone's knees hitting the ground reached the ears. Little by little it continued, one after another, followed by the clatter of weapons dropped to the ground.
Looking over, one of the knights who had joined the subjugation force sat slumped, shoulders sagging, crouched down with his face covered, head bowed. His shoulders trembled, and no one could stop the sobs climbing up his throat.

Not one of the comrades around that knight had any words to offer him.
They had brought flawless equipment, struck first to seize the initiative, poured on firepower and pressed the offensive with everything they had — and still, this was the absurd, unreasonable situation they faced.

The mental contamination that had already halved their forces was serious enough, and now the remaining main strength had been shattered by the surprise appearance of the newly-arrived White Whale.
Even gathering together what remained, it didn't amount to half their original strength. And on top of that, the number of Witchbeasts they had to face had tripled — there was no way they could win.

Everyone grasped it in an instant, forced to realize that their lives, their purpose, would come to an end right here.
The terror and horror of the Witchbeast. And the weight of the precious bonds it had stolen from them. Their own powerlessness to repay those bonds — it was hopeless, unbearable.

When everything they had built crumbled away, when the hearts that had kept them standing finally broke, who could blame them for sinking to their knees?
When an unreasonable, immovable reality closed in, who had the right to deny them their despair?

"—Don't let it swallow him!!"

Suddenly, a furious roar thundered across the plain, where silence had been on the verge of settling.
Startled by the voice, heads lifted — and there, kicking off the ground and leaping at one of the White Whales, was a figure: a girl, the hem of her maid uniform flaring, a vicious spiked iron ball clutched in her hand.

Wrapped in a fierce gale, the roaring iron ball struck the muzzle of the White Whale, which had stopped moving, dead on. It smashed through the tough hide with ease, gouging and piercing through the exposed bone and flesh beneath, the destruction still propagating onward.
A shriek rose, and the White Whale lifted its head, thrashing its tail as it tried to climb into the sky. Its tail was pierced by a blade of ice rising from the ground, and as it stalled there, the whirling iron ball struck it again. From a single blow of the small girl, the White Whale's massive body shook violently, blood splattering everywhere.

"If it's before he's swallowed into its stomach, there should still be something we can do—!"

It was a boy shouting, pressing a hand to his aching shoulder, blood streaming from his forehead.
Stepping forward, he gave directions to the girl swinging the iron ball, his face twisted with frustration at his own powerlessness to join the fight directly — and still, he pushed his feet forward.

The Earth Dragon that had been lying beside him raised itself up, and he mounted slowly onto its back, in a clumsy posture that was clearly unpracticed — but he gripped the reins firmly, with all his strength, and—

"Not yet!! —Nothing's over yet, not yet!!"

Before the knights ruled by despair, as if to rouse his own heart, the boy lifted his face, bared his teeth, opened his eyes wide, glared at the White Whale, and shouted.

"—Don't you dare think despair like this is enough to stop me!!"


* * *


—Subaru could feel it clearly: the footsteps of despair, drawing slowly closer.

One overhead, one behind, one right in front — three in total. This had to be a joke.
Did they have any idea how much of their strength they'd had to pour into just a single one of these things to inflict even that much damage? And now that things had turned against it, it called up two more companions and started the real fight? Don't screw around.

Just how much more would fate toy with them, unreasonably, before it was satisfied?

Thrown to the ground as Ricardo shielded him, Subaru gritted his teeth where he lay. If he didn't clench his back teeth like that, weak words, sobs, would spill out of him.
A brief illusion, as if the world before his eyes had darkened. A sense of despair, as though his brain couldn't bear the load of this unacceptable situation and his consciousness would simply slip away.

He could feel the familiar despair, wearing its mocking smile, draping an overly familiar arm around his shoulders.

—Well now, isn't it about time you gave up, same as always?

A dim shadow, its face unseen, opened its mouth in the shape of a crescent moon, urging him toward surrender in a voice he knew all too well. At those words, Subaru clearly accepted the full weight of the situation before him.

Around him, he could see the knights, here and there, sinking to their knees just as he was.
They too understood there was nothing to be done about the situation before them. Even the resolve to begin resisting had been stripped from them; the light had gone out of every pair of eyes, the will to grip a weapon blown out like a candle.

Idly, he let his gaze wander over them, sinking toward the voice of despair with its arm around his shoulders — and then he noticed.

Right beside him was Rem, thrown from her Earth Dragon just as he had been. Lying on her side, she had propped herself half up, her lovely profile marked with anguish.
Her cheeks were tense, her lips pale, her eyelids trembling. Looking at her closely like this, he found himself idly thinking — her eyelashes are long.

—And he also thought: she looks so much better smiling.

So—

"Your turn's not coming — not ever again."

He roughly shrugged off the overly familiar arm slung around his shoulders.
To the shadow, its mouth twisting downward in surprise at Subaru's action, he flashed a smile before throwing a full-force right straight — the black shadow shattered to pieces, and at the same moment, the trembling in his body stopped.

Ridiculous. Pathetic. There was no time to waver, no time to stand still.

So what if there were two more whales.

His arms and legs moved. He could lift his face, his eyes could still see. His voice still came out, still carried. Rem was here. Rem was alive. None of this — none of it — was a situation where he could give up on picking things back up.

—Stand up.

Again and again, over and over, his heart had been broken.

—Stand up.

Tossed around by unreasonable fate, forced each time to accept despair's finality.

—Stand up.

He had thrown everything away, telling himself it was hopeless, tried to flee to a place beyond where he'd abandoned it all — only to be made to face his own heart, told that even that wasn't permitted.

—Stand up.

For what?

—Stand up!

"For a moment like this one, that's what!!"

He slammed his fist into the ground, using the momentum to push his half-risen body the rest of the way up.
Rem looked over at Subaru, his face lifted as he roared, her expression startled. Looking down at her, he held out his hand, and glared at the White Whale before him.

"It's not over yet. —I won't let it be over."

"...Subaru-kun."

"Let's do this, Rem. This is our moment."

Hesitantly, she reached out, and he grasped her hand with a kind of impatience, pulling her up.
Catching the rising girl against his chest, Subaru faced her face, now so close, and—

"Giving up doesn't suit us. Not me, not you — not anyone!"


* * *


Roaring, Rem hurled herself furiously at the White Whale, driving her right fist into its rocky hide and hauling herself up its body. As she climbed, the iron ball she swung tore into the stone with a violent noise, and under her ferocious assault that sprayed blood in all directions, the White Whale writhed its enormous body, letting out cries of pain.

The one Rem had leapt at was the one that had swallowed Wilhelm whole from behind. She had confirmed its jaw moving as if to chew, but it shouldn't have gotten as far as swallowing him down its throat yet.
In the worst case, there was a chance he'd been crushed between those millstone-like teeth, but—

"As long as his head's not crushed, I'll pull him out somehow—!"

Subaru pulled the reins, entrusting his weight to the Earth Dragon's back with a feeling that was hopelessly unsteady.
With Rem no longer handling the reins, Subaru guiding the dragon himself was almost entirely improvised. The road to Flugel's Tree, and the mere two hours or so after arriving — that was the sum total of the time he'd had to practice handling an Earth Dragon on his own.

For Subaru, who had never so much as ridden a horse back in his original world, there was no way a few mere hours of practice could let him freely command an Earth Dragon.
He could indicate direction and speed, and beyond that, clinging on so as not to be thrown off was about all he could manage. Even so, he could tell that the highly intelligent Earth Dragon had grasped his intentions and his limited skill, and was taking care not to throw its inexperienced rider from its back.

A good Earth Dragon. Fast, plenty of stamina, and above all, smart. From now on, your name's Patrasche. He'd thought about it as a loyal companion, and that was the only name that came to mind.

"Let's go, Patrasche! Circle right around that whale's nose!"

He shouted loudly, snapping the reins to send the Earth Dragon running. Answering him, Patrasche charged forward, suppressing its own instincts to hurl itself at the mighty White Whale.

The White Whale, twisting its body desperately to shake off Rem clinging to it, nevertheless sensed Subaru's approach and instinctively turned its head this way. Into the side of that face—

"Smelling Subaru-kun's scent is Rem's privilege—!"

Leaping up, Rem drove home a kick with the force of a cannon shell.
The enormous face jolted slightly off-line, and into that opening the follow-up iron ball came flying, splitting the air aside — it punched through one cheek, ravaged the inside of the mouth, and burst out through the opposite cheek, taking several teeth along with it.

The White Whale let out a shriek, spraying great gouts of yellow fluid and bright blood. When its body finally fell to the ground, it thrashed about indiscriminately, like a fish stranded on land.

The earth was gouged, clumps of soil scattering violently. Its wildly thrashing tail split the ground, cut through the air, closing in almost as a surprise attack right beside Subaru — and just as it was about to strike home—

"Ba-baam, it's Mimi to the rescue!!"

The kitten Demi-human cut in right before the blow landed, and with a swing of the staff in her hand, a barrier of magical force unfurled.
A yellow glow deflected the strike, and through the gap it created, the Liger and the Earth Dragon dashed through together.

Letting out a breath, Subaru turned back to look at the kitten who had saved him just in time — Mimi.

"You saved me! I was about to say something cool about starting a counterattack and then just get finished off right there!"

"Heh-heh, you can praise me more if you want! But today I'll call it even, since big brother did his best too!"

"Did my best...?"

He tilted his head at Mimi, who puffed out her chest and then smiled at him. In response, the girl flicked lightly at the whiskers on her cheek with a finger.

"Everyone else was so scared they couldn't even stand, but you were the very first one to get back up, right? Good job, amazing job — second only to Mimi, though!"

"It's nothing special. I just can't afford to let something like this beat me into despair, that's all."

He answered Mimi's loud praise with those words, biting down on his lip.
That's right. This wasn't something worth praising. Did she have any idea how much bitterness, how much suffering, Subaru had already tasted to get here?

Compared to the despair he'd faced before, one he couldn't fight against at all, the current situation still had plenty of room to fight in — so how could he possibly have the luxury of wallowing in despair?

If he had time to spare toying with despair, he'd rather spend it coughing up blood while searching for hope.

Because resisting was so, so much easier than giving up.

"—!!"

Right in front of Patrasche, bounding forward in a straight run, a fish-shadow suddenly appeared, opening its enormous mouth.
At a distance close enough to see the dark red, grotesque innards deep in its throat, Subaru leaned his body, trying for an instant evasive maneuver. But the mist filling the White Whale's mouth cavity was a fraction faster to burst out—

"Close your mouth—!!"

An invisible blade, swung down from high overhead, sliced vertically through the White Whale's gaping mouth from the side. Forced to close its mouth from the force of the strike, the White Whale writhed and slid along the ground as Subaru and Mimi passed alongside it. Lifting his face after that hairsbreadth escape, he saw Crusch, running toward them from across the battlefield.

She drew up alongside the running Subaru, and, glaring at the White Whale with irritation, said—

"At a glance, the situation couldn't be worse. What of Wilhelm?"

"The fact that you still remember him means at least he hasn't been erased by the mist. ...It's up to how Rem's fight goes."

Subaru answered while turning his head, wary of the White Whale twisting around to try to pursue them. In response, Crusch let her gaze wander the same way. Where her eyes came to rest was on the White Whale, thrashing and shaking the ground, and Rem atop it, desperately swinging her iron ball, carving out a sea of blood.

"What do you make of it, Natsuki Subaru?"

"What do I make of it — what do you mean by that? If you mean our chances of winning, I'll say, with a bit of self-interest mixed in, that whether I live or die decides a lot of it."

"That's not it. Doesn't something strike you as strange?"

A White Whale trying to close in from behind took a slash from Crusch's swinging arm across its muzzle. A roar rose, and feeling the air itself shudder at his back, Subaru looked at her. "Strange?"

"The number of White Whales has increased to three. On the face of it, that puts us in a hopeless situation. But if the White Whale really were a Witchbeast that formed packs, is it conceivable that not a single soul would have passed that fact along, no matter how you look at it?"

"I'm not quite following what you're getting at."

"There must be some trick to it."

She declared it flatly, turning her dignified face toward Subaru.
Naturally, pierced by that strong gaze, Subaru straightened his back.

"You're saying I should find it out."

"Buying time will fall to your fleet feet, and to us, supporting you in that. Either way, this can't last much longer. We will make it work somehow — retreat is no longer an option, after all."

Having said her piece, Crusch's Earth Dragon changed direction and pulled away from Subaru.
She swept in a wide arc, circling around the domineering White Whale, moving from one scattered unit of the subjugation force to the next, raising her voice.

"Stand! Raise your heads! Take up your weapons! What did you all come this far for!"

"—"

The men, their faces bowed in despair and grief, lifted their eyes.
Before them, Crusch stood tall and unbowed, holding her drawn sacred sword aloft toward the heavens.

"Look at that man! He has no weapon, no strength — a weakling who'd blow away in a stiff wind. I saw it myself, with my own eyes, that powerless man being struck down!"

With her sword she indicated the retreating back — Subaru astride his Earth Dragon — and Crusch raised her voice higher still.

That man is the weakest of all of us.
He has no strength to fight. No ability even to survive on his own. A man of nothing but defeat, broken again and again, crushed each time.
And that weakest of all men is roaring that he can still do it, faster than anyone else.

The most powerless man in this entire place is gritting his teeth, enduring the pain, holding back his tears, coughing up blood — and still he looks up, still he tries to resist.

"So how could the rest of us keep our heads bowed?"

"—"

"Our strength is weak — even joined together, we cannot know if it will reach the Witchbeast's throat. Even so, when the weakest man among us has not given up, how could the rest of us be permitted to fold our knees!"

"O-oh..."

The men, their morale broken, looked at one another and rose to their feet, willing their trembling knees to hold.
They took up the weapons they had dropped, and the Earth Dragons, waiting for their masters to mount, drew close beside them.

Reaching out, gripping the reins, the knights who had bent their knees mounted their Earth Dragons' backs. The dragons whinnied, and atop their backs the knights, too, drew their swords and roared themselves hoarse.
A battle cry rose. As if to rouse their own hearts, to take pride in their own souls.

Behind the weak boy who was fighting, they roared and raged, driving away the folly of crouching down and hanging their heads.
That emotion is called "shame." Shame cut through fear, through despair, through every negative feeling, and gave the knights the strength to lift their faces and step forward.

"Let's go — all units, charge!!"

"Ohhhh—!!!"

Staking everything on souls that should have been broken, the knights advanced once more.
Clouds of dust rose from the Earth Dragons' feet kicking off the ground, and the subjugation force, now fewer than fifty in total, tore down the highway and fell upon the two White Whales swimming within reach of their blades.

Hearing of the subjugation force's swelling morale, and the single roar from Crusch that had achieved it, Subaru couldn't hold back a wry smile tugging at the corner of his mouth.

"Weakling, loser — say whatever you want, why don't you..."

The fact that he didn't even feel like denying it — that alone spoke to how serious a wound it was.
She could call him whatever she liked, use him however she liked. The fact that Subaru had gotten this far powerless, losing over and over, breaking over and over, throwing things away over and over — that was simply true.

And because he knew that, Subaru could roar, here, now.

He wouldn't end things still losing. He wouldn't leave himself still broken. It was time to stop throwing things away. Being powerless was no longer something he could allow himself.

"Come on, Patrasche. One more time — right up to its nose and straight back out!"

The Earth Dragon leaned at an angle, carving into the earth, cutting a sharp turn and charging once more straight at the White Whale.
Ahead, at the White Whale desperately trying to shake off Rem clinging to its body, the mixed squad that had split off from Crusch was landing supporting attacks. Knights' swords struck sparks as they scraped away at the White Whale's hide, while cavalry, keeping their distance as they ran alongside the massive body, added bombardments with magic-stones.

Screaming, the White Whale writhed across the ground. Even its convulsions of agony were an inescapable violence to the humans nearby. One Earth Dragon and its rider were sent flying by a single blow, crushed beneath its overwhelming weight, the sound of shattering bone ringing out.

Blood sprayed, and a single life was snuffed out. —Subaru burned that image into his eyes.

A chill ran down his spine. Not making it in time, failing to save them — that was the result of Subaru's decision.
The result of Subaru saying they should start this fight. He couldn't look away from it. The moment he refused to accept it, Subaru would lose to the feeling of "shame."
When he had lost to his own heart, when he had faced the most despicable weakness in himself, someone had once rejected that, deeply and gently — so he couldn't afford to lean on that crutch any further.

The rampaging White Whale, sensing Subaru's approach, opened every mouth across its body.
Feeling the blood drain from his face, he entrusted himself entirely to the Earth Dragon's full-speed sprint, cutting through the wind. —Right beside him, the "Mist of Erasure" unleashed from countless mouths grazed past.
If it so much as touched a single finger, he'd be erased from there, and Subaru's existence would end. The imagined sensation of his entire body being enveloped by a sense of loss unlike ordinary "death" made his very core shudder.

But—

"El Fūra!!" "Like hell I'll let you!!" "Where the hell do you think you're looking!"

Wind magic swept the mist aside, and blades and hammers, raised with furious roars, smashed the mist-spewing mouths.
With the knights' support, the barrage of mist thinned slightly. But it was still hopelessly overwhelming firepower. With the sensation of erasure closing in on his whole body, Subaru's nerves sharpened, sensing an ending.

Leaving the route to the Earth Dragon, Subaru's body went into evasive motion atop its back. He snapped his arms up into a push-up, dodging the mist closing in from behind almost as if handstanding on the dragon's back, completely losing his balance and beginning to fall from its back — but—

"Guuu—ts!!"

He barely held on, gripping the reins tightly, hooking his knees against the stirrups. The grip strength he'd built up back in his original world, endlessly swinging a wooden sword for no particular reason, caught his palm just before it slipped free from the shaking, jostling, and panic.
A flight that was half a stumble — he broke through the barrage.

His view cleared, and matching the Earth Dragon's slowing pace, Subaru remounted in what would have looked, from behind, like the most graceless motion imaginable. Cutting even further into his already-meager reserves of stamina, he turned to head, this time, toward the other White Whale — the one Crusch and the others were pressing their offensive against.

"Stir it up... haah. Damn it, I'm not just risking my life here — use your head too!"

Breathing hard, exposing himself once more to life-risking bait duty, Subaru turned his thoughts to the "trick" that had come up in his earlier conversation with Crusch.

About the three Witchbeasts — the White Whales — Subaru remained, through his own ignorance, largely uninformed. Because of that, when it came to the threat of the "mist," or the long history of this creature's existence, he was nowhere near caught up with the women who had lived in this world all their lives.

But even a Subaru like that could find something to question. Would Wilhelm — who had pursued the White Whale for fourteen years as his wife's killer — really overlook something as fatal as the fact that "multiple White Whales exist"? And if it truly hadn't been known, could there really never have been a situation before now where they appeared simultaneously?

"Why did it suddenly multiply... the idea that there were three of them from the start doesn't fit the premise...!"

He felt like he was about to grasp some kind of thread. But before he could, Patrasche's desperate sprint brought them into the White Whale's olfactory range.
The White Whale, its gaze on Crusch as she sliced its underbelly open with her sacred blade, swung its eyes around in a great arc toward Subaru instead. At the same moment, the dense mist pooled in its open mouth cavity was expelled as a massive burst of destruction, along with a roar that shattered the very air.

Patrasche cut sharply at an angle, driving forward, saving its body from the raging mist bearing down on them — but it was half a step short of escaping the mist's effective range entirely. —That missing half step for Subaru and the others—

"We'll cover that!"
"We won't let it happen!"

—was made up for by the intervention of Mimi and Hetaro.
Beside Subaru, who couldn't fully escape, the twin Demi-humans opened their mouths, unleashing overlapping roars of "Wa" and "Ha" — the meeting of the two high tones produced ripples, which at a distance converted into destruction; a massive shockwave rippled outward, plowing up the plain's earth in waves and blasting even the oncoming mist apart head-on.

"Whoaaaa!! That's so freaking amaaazing!!"

"Right? Right?! Praise me more!"

At Subaru's blunt praise, Mimi puffed out her chest, her cheeks loosening in satisfaction. Beside her, Hetaro, out of breath, murmured a small "Sis, honestly..." before, riding his Liger, moving to flank them on either side, said—

"We'll cover you. Without Subaru-san around... there's no path to victory in this fight."

"Can't we just do a whoosh, then a kaboom, then a ka-ba-ba-boom?"

"To even do the whoosh or the ka-ba-boom, we need Subaru-san's help, Sis."

"Huh."

—and so the twins carried on this thoroughly unhurried conversation, with Subaru sandwiched between them.
Setting aside Mimi, who didn't seem to grasp even the surface of how dire their situation was, Subaru turned toward Hetaro, who seemed like he might actually follow him, and said—

"That combo attack from earlier — the one you slammed into the White Whale partway through. Can you still pull that off?"

"I'll be squeezing out mana right to the edge, so one more time is my limit. —Until the Boss recovers, Sis and I will protect you, Subaru-san."

"Ricardo's alive!?"

At this unexpected good news, Subaru raised his voice, and Hetaro nodded, "Yes."
Relief spread through Subaru's chest at his manner. He'd assumed, seeing the Liger Ricardo had been riding cruelly killed and the sheer volume of blood, that Ricardo had probably been blown apart badly enough to leave nothing recognizable.

"There's also a message from the Boss, on the verge of death, for you, Subaru-san."

"A message... please don't tell me it's 'this is gonna cost you.'"

"I think he'll say that himself later in person... but it's this. Ahem: 'Well now, feels lighter, don't it. Fact that I ain't dead is proof enough.' That's it."

Hetaro dutifully imitated Ricardo's voice, even down to his Kansai-style — Kararagi-style — dialect. Without commenting on the quality of the impression, Subaru turned his thoughts to the meaning of the words conveyed to him.
Literally, this was a message Ricardo had risked his life to pass along. If he turned his mind to the meaning packed into it, to its true intent—

"That impression doesn't sound like him at all."

"Yeah, super not like him! No talent at all!"

"This isn't the time to be saying that!"

Mimi chimed in agreement with Subaru's tone-deaf remark, and Hetaro protested in a voice on the verge of tears, but Subaru let it wash over him and looked up at the sky.

The two White Whales, still locked in fierce combat, grappling with the subjugation force split into two groups.
The White Whale floating in the sky was the first of the three Witchbeasts to appear, the one that had fled skyward before the initial fierce assault. It still hadn't joined the fight, looking down on Subaru and the others from high above.

Something about its behavior struck Subaru as unnatural.
With their strength already reduced, and that reduced number split further in two to fight — that was the current state of the battle. Even accounting for the disruption Subaru was causing, if the White Whale floating in the sky lent its strength to either battlefield, the tide would tip decisively in an instant, just like that. If either side got torn apart, it would be over.
And yet, that White Whale wasn't doing anything — why—

"Ricardo's message..."

Lighter — that's what Ricardo had told Subaru.
That the reason he'd risked his life and not died was because of it. What meaning could be in that? What exactly had been lighter? His life? Certainly, on a battlefield, that was what got treated lightly. But it didn't feel like that was the meaning. Lighter, lighter — what did that mean?

"In this heavy, super-hard situation, what the hell is 'lighter' supposed to mean...!"

Entrusting his body to the Earth Dragon's rocking gait, he cut across the White Whale's muzzle once more. The White Whale, beset by Crusch and the others, turned its open mouth cavity toward them, but Crusch's invisible slash and hurled magic-stones flew into that open mouth, and a mist of blood scattered.

The knights' war cries rose. Even as their numbers were steadily whittled down, one by one, it was their inexhaustible morale alone that now held the front line together.

Could people, even with death staring them in the face, become this strong once they resolved to resist it?
After all, against the White Whale that had barely been matched by the subjugation force at full strength, this force — having lost its main strength, its numbers reduced — was now holding its own. —If this wasn't the power of will, then what—

"Still, calling willpower some kind of cure-all is going too far."

Thinking that far, Subaru's face jerked up. He looked back over his shoulder at the White Whale they'd left behind, straining his eyes to glare at the receding beast's face.
And then, he realized.

"If that's the case...!"

He clenched his teeth, and a tremor ran through Subaru's entire body at the torrent of possibility surging up within him.
He passed his intent through the reins, and Patrasche cut a sharp turn, charging fiercely for the other White Whale. Rem, fighting hard, had unleashed the power of an Oni, and astride one of the Ligers, was punching one hole after another through the White Whale's body with her iron ball. Her apron dress soaked filthy with the beast's spattered blood, she noticed Subaru approaching and smiled at him gamely all the same.

Her smile, painted in fresh blood, only made the horror of the scene stand out all the more sharply — but improperly, Subaru couldn't help finding Rem, like this, noble, and unbearably dear to him.

Even in this battle of pure disadvantage, she still believed in Subaru's reckless resolve.
He had to answer that trust, that affection.

"—!"

Without exchanging a single word, Subaru's Earth Dragon and Rem's Liger crossed paths, Subaru running his mount toward the White Whale's snout, Rem toward its tail.
There was no need to stop and talk it over. Subaru had his role, and Rem had hers — both of them already knew it.

Circling around toward the White Whale's head, the beast noticed Subaru's approach and turned its head this way. Right beside its head, near beneath its eye, several mouths appeared, baring fangs, drooling as they sprayed white mist. Too slow. It wasn't even enough to be a threat.

"Ka-doooom! Ba-baaam! Zun-ba-ra-baa!"

Crossing back and forth over Patrasche in a figure-eight, the Liger Mimi rode flew around in every direction. Striking a pose on the great canine's back, every time Mimi voiced one of her airheaded sound effects, the tip of the staff in her hand lit up, and the resulting magic barrier blocked the mist — buying time before it could reach Subaru.

"This is gonna cost you, big brother!"

"Once this is over I'll say thank you like a hundred times!"

"Then okay!"

Entrusting his back to Mimi's cheaply-bought reply, Subaru sped past the White Whale running alongside him, pulled ahead, and, out in front, tilted his head to glare at the beast. Confirming the White Whale turning its "single eye" toward him with a roll, Subaru's cheek twisted with the certainty that he wasn't wrong.

"Just as I thought. You lot aren't three separate whales — you split!"

The White Whale locked in battle with Crusch and the others also had no left eye.
The one Subaru had just now run alongside was the same. And naturally, the last one, floating in the sky, bearing the same wound, would be no different. —The missing left eye was, in other words, Wilhelm's blade.

The reason the other two shared the exact same wound as the first was obvious.
It could only be because the one floating in the sky had split, and given birth to the other two.

"Each blow landing lighter, being able to fight this well at all — that's exactly the trick behind it, isn't it!"

Despite the surprise attack, the tail strike that had failed to kill Ricardo.
The way a White Whale that should have matched their full strength was now being fought to something like a standstill by a subjugation force whose numbers had been drastically cut.
—Subaru, too twisted to believe in convenient explanations like miracles or the power of will, saw right there the root cause behind the appearance of multiple White Whales.

Because it possessed the one-hit-kill "Mist of Erasure," the White Whale had sacrificed its own durability in favor of numbers. Sheer violence of numbers — if the sight of its might multiplying had been enough to break the subjugation force's spirit, the fight itself would have ended right there.
It was hard to believe that a Witchbeast could understand human psychology well enough to execute such a strategy deliberately, but as a matter of fact, its "splitting" had produced exactly that effect.

What would have happened, he wondered, if he hadn't roared back there?
What might have happened if he hadn't — that, the current Subaru couldn't know. There might be some possibility of him seeing the future where he hadn't roared, but he had no wish to see a future like that at all.
Never again did he want to spend that much time looking at your faces—

"—What!?"

Before Subaru, who had arrived at a single conclusion, the White Whale that had been trying to pursue him showed a change in its movement. Rubbing its floating body against the ground, it glided along, scraping the earth with its belly, in a motion like it couldn't bear some foreign sensation any longer.

"Sis, now!"
"Man, it must be so hard having an itch and no hands to scratch it!"

Seeing his chance, Hetaro leapt out, while Mimi moved to encircle it, muttering her mistaken impression of what the White Whale's motion meant. Whatever the state of communication between them, the twins, perfectly in sync in their movements, sandwiched the White Whale's body from both sides, and opened their mouths at once—

"Wa—!"
"Ha—!!"

Under the resonant attack from both sides, the White Whale's body bent sharply inward, and inside it, at the point where the pinch had formed, several organs, unable to withstand the pressure, were crushed. Its hard outer hide warped, cracks ran through it, blood began flowing anew from every wound — the greatest damage the White Whale had yet taken. And then—

"—Guaaaaaaaaah!!"

Part of the belly that had been dragged along the ground swelled from within, and split open in a burst of flesh and blood. Amid a torrent of dark red fluid gushing out onto the ground, carried along by that flow and spat out to the outside, was—

"Wilhelm-san!?"

The return of the elderly swordsman who had been swallowed whole by the White Whale's jaws, and whose survival had been all but given up as hopeless.

As the subjugation force held down the rampaging White Whale, Subaru rushed back to the fallen Wilhelm. Wilhelm, his entire body soaked in a horrifying amount of blood, was down on one knee, propping himself half-upright with his sword driven into the ground for support, and said—

"...Unpracticed of me. I let my guard down..."

"You don't need to talk! Ah, damn it, I don't know what the hell to do, but the important thing is you're alive. Let's get you back to Ferris right now!"

Reaching out a hand, Subaru noticed that Wilhelm's left arm — the opposite of the right, which held his sword — was nearly torn off from the shoulder down, and dismounted from his Earth Dragon. He tried to lend a shoulder and lift Wilhelm onto Patrasche as he was, but—

"N-not yet. Not yet, I still..."

"This is no time to say that! You'll die before the whale does! And I'm not listening to any half-asleep talk about how you won't die from something like this! When it comes to living and dying, I'm the one with more experience here!"

"What are you..."

Even battered from head to toe, Wilhelm's eyes didn't let the flame of his fighting spirit go out. Subaru shouted him into silence over his recklessness and forcibly hauled his body up. Just then—

"The old man came out!"
"Wilhelm-san, you're alive!?"

The twins, rushing over, saw Subaru holding up the old man and came right to his side. The two of them exchanged a look, nodded, and—

"Subaru-san. Should we take Wilhelm-san to Felix-san?"

"Felic—ah, Ferris, right. Yeah. He's at death's door, could go any minute. Honestly I want to carry him there myself, but..."

Tilting his head, Subaru glared at the White Whale, which was beginning to stir back into action.
The wound in its belly was deep, and fluid was pouring endlessly from it, but from the way the beast opened and closed the mouths across its body, producing a faint mist, there was no sign of its fighting spirit fading.

There was no doubt that, at present, Subaru's disruption tactics were contributing significantly to keeping the fight even. If Subaru carried Wilhelm off now, that would mean a drop in fighting strength—

"And that'd be fine if that's all it was, but if I'm unlucky, I might drag the whale straight toward where all the wounded are gathered. Can I leave Wilhelm-san to you?"

"We'll handle that with our Ligers, but... did you think of something?"

Hetaro took Wilhelm from Subaru, struggling somewhat with the difference in their sizes, but somehow got him up onto the great canine's back. He then looked up at Subaru, and, taking the hand of his sister, who was clapping her hands and cheerfully saying "gotta do my best," said—

"If you have a chance of winning, I'll hear it. If it's a no, I have to take my sister's hand and get us both away from here."

"Ehh, why? We haven't beaten it yet..."

"Sis, hush."

At her brother's sharp reproof, Mimi pouted her lips discontentedly.
Watching this exchange between the twins, Subaru nodded in understanding, "Yeah, that's fair."

"You two are mercenaries. Unlike me, or Crusch, or the knights who have a grudge against the whale, you're only hired for money. ...You've got no obligation to bet your lives on this."

"It's just that we've got no obligation to throw our lives away. I don't want that misunderstood."

Despite his timid-looking face and manner, Hetaro said what needed saying, resolutely. Looking down at the small Demi-human who barely came up to his waist, Subaru prefaced with "Sorry," then said—

"No time to waste. I think there's a chance we can win. For now, send Wilhelm-san to the rear... and then, Rem and Crusch. I've gotta talk to the key people."

Leaping up onto Patrasche's back beside him, Subaru lifted his face.
Up at the sky he'd looked to, he glared with irritation at the fish-shadow swimming leisurely there.


* * *


"The White Whale is splitting itself, then."

"Yeah, I'm sure of it. The position of the wounds, and how strong they hit. Honestly, you guys, actually going toe-to-toe with it, probably felt it even more than me, right?"

"Rem was too caught up in the moment to notice... but, yes, that might well be true."

Rem and Crusch, having rejoined the group, nodded before Subaru even finished explaining, signaling their agreement.
They'd entrusted Wilhelm to the Liger Hetaro had been riding and sent him off to Ferris, and, with the twins riding double, had gone and picked the two women up.

The fight against the two remaining White Whales had been entrusted to the subjugation force and the Demi-human mercenary corps, and their high morale and skillful coordination were, just barely, making up for the lack of a main strike force.
A few minutes — if Subaru kept up his disruption tactics, prowling around its muzzle, he could at least buy enough time for a war council.

Running alongside on their Earth Dragons and Liger, Crusch nodded at Subaru's reasoning and lifted her face—

"I agree that it's weaker than the original single whale. But even understanding that, what do we do with it? Wounded and weakened though it is, its threat still exceeds our own. No matter how good Felix's treatment is, we can't hope for anyone who's fallen back to return to the front line."

"It hurts that Wilhelm-san and Ricardo aren't rejoining us, but I can't ask for the impossible. We just have to go for the win without them."

"Kill all three White Whales. Easy to say, but a tall wall to clear."

"We don't need to kill all three. —Just one should be enough."

At Subaru's words, Crusch's brow twitched upward.
Nodding back to her as she regarded him with interest, Subaru pointed a finger toward the sky.

"That bastard, letting its two copies do all the fighting while it sits up there watching from a safe distance — what do you think it's actually doing?"

"Healing its wounds, without helping either side...?"

Rem answered Subaru's question uncertainly, but Subaru shook his head.
From what he could tell, even calling it a Witchbeast, its biological functions weren't so far removed from those of ordinary creatures. High-speed automatic healing — some ridiculous ability like that at least didn't seem to be part of the White Whale's despair-inducing arsenal.

If so—

"It's the main body, then."

"That's my guess."

At Crusch's answer, her face lifting as if something had clicked, Subaru nodded in agreement.

Frankly, it was nothing more than speculation.
Still, there was no longer any doubt that the three White Whales were the products of a single one splitting apart. The question was how to defeat the White Whale now that it had become three, and what had contributed a piece to that reasoning was—

"I think the reason it doesn't come down, doesn't lend a hand to either copy of itself, is because it can't afford to be the one that gets taken down."

"That's reasonable. But conversely..."

"The two down here — even if we kill them, it might not hurt the main body at all."

Even if they went to all the trouble of bringing a White Whale down, there was no guarantee that its corpse wouldn't dissolve into mist and immediately be replicated into a new individual. If that happened, they'd be caught in an endless loop, and it was plain to see that with their own limit on how many times they could "continue," they'd be forced to throw in the towel sooner rather than later.

"So the reason it won't come down, and the way to defeat it, are connected. But then what do we do about it? With it flying that high up, we don't have any way to attack it."

Hetaro, who had been listening quietly, looked up at Subaru, his dark, wide eyes glistening as he asked the question. As Subaru found himself on the receiving end of that adorable feline gaze, Crusch looked up at the White Whale floating in the sky.

"Even with my Divine Protection, reaching all the way up there would be no small feat. I might manage a single strike, perhaps... but I doubt that alone could bring it down."

The altitude the White Whale had fled to reached roughly the same height as the clouds.
Its positioning, even higher than when it had first appeared, seemed to reveal the sheer nastiness of the White Whale's nature, and Subaru couldn't help a bitter expression.
At that height, even magic-stone bombardment using cannon tubes would see its hit rate drop drastically.

"Rem. What about floating a mountain of ice right next to that bastard..."

"I'm sorry. The farther mana gets from my hands, the harder it is to control. I think Roswaal-sama could manage it, but with Rem's skill..."

Rem's face showed genuine regret that, having glimpsed a possibility that might break the deadlock, she couldn't lend a hand to it. Subaru waved a hand at her, tilting his head to say it couldn't be helped.

He did have a plan he'd been thinking of.
He'd waited for Crusch's answer, Hetaro and Mimi's answer, and Rem's answer, hoping that between them a better plan might emerge — but it hadn't, leaving him with the second-best plan he hadn't wanted to use.

"I've got a plan, but it's got a bit too much gambling baked into it... you in?"

Closing one eye, Subaru asked after their resolve before revealing that second-best plan.
But that, one could say, was itself a rather graceless question to ask.

—Because Subaru already knew: the moment they'd rushed to this place, that made them the sort of grand fools who would never hesitate at a gamble like that.


* * *


—From high in the sky, the White Whale quietly looked down on the struggle below.

Observed as a battlefield split left and right, the fight dividing the plain in two was neatly bisected, with the sky-piercing great tree as its apex.
On both battlefields, tiny humans clung to the Witchbeasts' massive bodies, driving in the steel gripped in their hands, brandishing stones that birthed flame, resisting with their clever little tricks.

Every time flames rose and cries of pain from the Witchbeasts reached up from below, the White Whale swimming in the sky exhaled white mist.
The mist hanging over the battlefield aided its equivalent existences below, steadily driving the small struggling beings into a losing position.

The scurrying little shadows, as time passed, steadily decreased in number, one after another, swallowed into the "mist," their existences erased.

Devouring everything, the end of this futile struggle was not far off.
It was only a matter of time until the balance of power collapsed and everything fell apart.

If the White Whale possessed human intelligence, it would have thought exactly that, and been certain of its own victory.
But in truth, the White Whale had no such intelligence. It simply followed its instincts, acting only to avoid its own destruction, to utterly destroy its opponents.

Asking why it made such judgments would be a graceless question to put to a beast's instinct.
And so, entrusting its judgment to instinct, the White Whale, calm and precise, sought to torment its prey to death.

"—"

It breathed out mist, dyeing the ground below white.
Interference had interrupted it, but it had to spread the mist and cover the world below entirely. That, too, was a command of instinct, and doing so was the meaning of its existence.

And so, the White Whale, its consciousness detached from the scene below, suddenly rolled its enormous single eye, redirecting its attention downward.
It had sensed mana converging with tremendous force, and had located the source of that flow.

"Al, Huma."

At the center of that vast whirlpool of mana stood a girl with blue hair.
Kneeling, imparting direction to mana she had spent time refining, beside the girl there slowly took shape a long spear of ice, its sharp tip peeking into view.

The ten-meter-class frozen weapon aimed its sharp point at the White Whale's underbelly.
Its target was obvious, and carrying it out in a form visible to the eye was a fatal mistake.

"—Please!"

At the girl's prayer-like cry, the spear of ice was launched from the ground up toward the sky.
The tip aimed, naturally, at the very center of the White Whale's body as it moved through the air.

Accelerating rapidly, closing in with enough force to pierce the sky — that icy intent to kill — but it fell apart pathetically, thanks to the distance needed to build that acceleration, and the mistake of having its launch moment observed.

The White Whale swung its tail, cutting through the wind as it swam through the sky.
That alone took its massive body out of the ice spear's range, and the spear, missing the White Whale's body, passed right beside it, its aim slipping away with pathetic ease.

Twisting its huge body, the White Whale watched the ice spear go sailing past.
Finally, even the tail end of that pillar of ice passed by its side — and that the White Whale heard, faintly, the sound of something small shattering, was nothing short of a miracle.

—A miracle, demonic in a godlike way, meant to inform it that the sound was one of irreversible consequence.

"—Yo. Seeing you up close like this, you're seriously disgusting, you know that?"

An almost impossibly light presence pressed down onto the rocky hide of its enormous body.
At the very same moment it sensed the presence of whatever had landed on the tip of its head, the White Whale caught the scent of a wave of dispersing mana — from the ice pillar that should have simply passed by, now vanished without a trace.

—And then, too, of the source of an unbearable stench hovering right at its snout.

"Come along with me. —I'll have you know, I'm a man with a well-earned reputation for being too damn annoying to ignore."

The White Whale heard the stench, wearing a smile brimming with malice, murmur those words.


* * *


Clinging to the ice spear Rem had created, riding it up into the sky, then breaking the "Mist-Clearing Crystal Stone" there to disengage — and cling onto the White Whale instead — that was the opening move of the reckless plan Subaru had put together.

He'd faced fierce opposition from Rem, but had pushed through it by repeating "I believe in Rem!" over and over, and had received the transfer of that trump-card crystal stone — the "Demon-Warding Crystal" — from Crusch.
He'd predicted that the White Whale would dodge an obviously telegraphed finishing move with ease, and had set Subaru up as a trap riding along with it. Conversely, if the White Whale hadn't dodged, Subaru, clinging to the tail end of the ice pillar, might well have been smashed to pieces by the impact. In a sense, it had been razor-thin.

"And if we're talking razor-thin, this whole situation is razor-thin too... or rather, this is seriously terrifying!!"

Clinging desperately to the White Whale's snout, Subaru, feeling the texture of its rough skin and coarse hairs against his palms, showed open revulsion at the high-altitude wind and the raw stench of the massive creature.

With Subaru clinging to it — that is, a being giving off the Witch's Lingering Scent — the White Whale's demeanor transformed before his eyes. The Witchbeast, which had until then maintained a detached observation, clearly entered a state of excitement, drooling mist and saliva and gales of laughter from every mouth across its body, welcoming Subaru with savage fervor.

Of course, entrusting himself to the White Whale like this didn't mean Subaru could just unleash some finishing move to bring the massive body crashing down. Reality wasn't kind enough to let him awaken some new power through resolve alone, and even if he blasted it with Shamak at the cost of his own life right there, all that would happen is that some fool, senseless from the strain, would slip his grip and fall to his death.

So what Subaru intended to do, having latched onto the White Whale, was—

"So, that's the deal. Come along with me. —Steel yourself, all right?"

Letting go, before the White Whale's attack could even begin, Subaru's body slid off its rocky hide — entering a trajectory of free fall. A fool, but not one senseless from the strain, began his plunge toward the ground below.

The White Whale turned its head toward the sight of Subaru committing this grand act of suicide, moving its body slightly as if to chase after him, but then stopped the motion of its tail, as if hesitating over something.
It was probably sensing, through instinct, that if it let Subaru go now, the only actions left available against itself would be the same as before.

Quite the formidable instinct, indeed. But that wouldn't do. And so—

"At this height, there's no risk of anyone else hearing. Consider this a real bargain, so listen up! Because of you, Rem died once, and I've been carrying one hell of a trauma ever since, you hear me!!"

The instant he finished saying it, Subaru's body, buffeted by the wind, was severed from the world.
Every sensation in his body grew distant, and his consciousness, which had until then been dominated by the floating feeling of his organs being pulled upward, lost its grip on reality, drawn toward a place where the concept of time no longer existed.

*I love you.*

He thought he felt something whispered right by his ear.
The next instant — agonizing pain tore through Subaru's entire body, as if scorching him with lightning.

From somewhere unseen, from behind his back, a hand thrust in, passed straight through his breastbone, seized his heart, and squeezed it — roughly, and yet as if checking on something precious — tighter, and tighter still.
The unreality of having the vital organ that governed the circulation of his blood handled so roughly. The alien sensation of having something so fatal placed entirely at another's mercy — the end of the world, one where he couldn't even manage to scream, was announced to him only by the sound of the wind and his own groans of agony.

And then,

"You've come... baaaaack!!"

"—!!"

Right before him, the White Whale, jaws thrown open, dove furiously straight for Subaru.

Drawn by the Witch's Scent, magnified by his confession, the Witchbeast's instinct had been overwritten by a hatred that surpassed even that pull.
Roaring, its eyes gone mad as though it had forgotten the battle below entirely, the White Whale came at him as if intent on erasing only Subaru's existence.

Subaru felt terror at the White Whale boring through the wind, rapidly closing the distance between them.
With nothing to do but leave himself to free fall, Subaru had no means of dodging this charge. At this rate, he'd be chewed up by the White Whale's jaws before reaching the ground, hurtling straight toward BAD END 10: "Fish Food."

At this rate—

"—Rem!!"

"Yes, Subaru-kun!"

To Subaru's furious shout, nearly swallowed by the wind, a girl's voice nevertheless answered, unmistakably.
At the same moment, a pillar of ice, shot out from the side, slammed into the side of the White Whale's face, fixated as it was solely on Subaru. It ravaged the inside of its jaw, snapping off several teeth, and created a lag in its movement.

Seizing that opening, Rem, astride Patrasche, caught Subaru's body, still in the midst of free fall, with her Morningstar.

His waist bound by the iron chain, his trajectory forcibly redirected, Subaru let out a pained "Ghack!" at the sensation — a nostalgic one, in its way. This made twice now that Rem had saved him from a fall like this. The first time had been that bit of horseplay in the dragon carriage, on the way to the royal capital.

"Guess you really should try everything at least once..."

This time, at least, he hadn't passed out.
The chain reeled him in, and Subaru's body dropped, somewhat roughly, onto Patrasche's back. Rem, naturally, was already mounted there, and Subaru's body ended up diving straight into her arms.

"You saved me!"

"Thank you for the meal."

"What are you even saying!?"

Subaru shot back a blunt retort at Rem, who had pressed her hands together to give thanks, and lifted his face.
The White Whale's face passed right by them—

"—!!"

Unable to fully kill its momentum, the White Whale slammed headfirst into the ground. A thunderous roar and clouds of dust rose from the earth that shattered under the impact, and the ground itself heaved violently under the force.

Feeling that shockwave against his back, Subaru signaled Patrasche to sprint forward at full speed — and from behind them, the White Whale burst out through the cloud of dust.

Its head crushed into a mangled ruin by the sheer force of the impact, the White Whale still pursued Subaru, screaming in mindless fury.
That momentum had wrecked what had once been an unhurried, graceful swim, and the speed that had once seemed to outrun the wind itself was nowhere to be seen. But its sheer ferocity alone remained overwhelming.

Carving into the ground, lashing the earth with its tail, the White Whale bore down furiously from behind.
Leaning forward, entrusting his entire weight to it, Subaru staked his life on Patrasche's hidden reserves. This was a dragon that had served him this desperately, this devotedly, all this while. Even in the short time they'd had together, Subaru had come to hold enough trust in it to entrust his very life to its running.

"Come on, Patrasche! You're a dragon, aren't you!? Show me something cool—!!"

"—!!"

Patrasche whinnied, and Subaru felt, in the wind, its speed climb another level.
The White Whale's roar thundered, shaking his eardrums violently until the world blurred at the edges.

Straight ahead, straight ahead, running and running and tearing onward, purely and simply.
Swimming, swimming, the White Whale closing in furiously, intent on devouring Subaru whole.
And then—

"Eat this—!!"

"—!!"

A thunderous roar sounded twice in succession, followed immediately by a chain of sounds like something being torn away. The intervals between those impossible-to-ignore sounds shortened, drew closer, and finally—

—casting an immense shadow, falling straight toward the White Whale — Flugel's Tree came crashing down.

"—!!!"

Magic-stones, invisible blades, resonant fracturing — gouged at its roots by the combined force of destruction, the great tree the Sage had planted, after hundreds of years, crushed the massive body of the Witchbeast that had brought harm to humanity.

Under the weight of a tree over a thousand years old, the White Whale, having charged straight in, was smashed flat from directly above. Against power of a fundamentally different order from any destruction it had taken until now, even the tough hide covering its massive body offered no meaningful defense.

A scream rose, a tremendous shockwave tore through the Lifaus Highway, and the blast of wind swept the mist away.
Pinned beneath the great tree, its movement sealed, the White Whale's agonized howl trailed on and on. And yet, even after taking that much force, its life force refused to be extinguished.

Struggling, trying to escape that overwhelming weight, the White Whale — and at its very snout—


"—This, for my wife. For Theresia van Astrea."


Holding aloft the sacred sword borrowed from his liege, a lone Sword Devil descended.

To bring the curtain down on this life-and-death struggle, on fourteen years of obsession, on four hundred years of history in the war between humanity and the White Whale — for that purpose.
