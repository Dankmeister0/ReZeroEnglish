Arc 7 Chapter 19: "An Infuriating Reunion"

On the hard-packed earth, a man lay sprawled out, unmoving.

"——"

He was neither dead nor asleep.
He simply kept his eyes shut, carefully steadying his ragged breath as he quietly put his thoughts in order.
Building them up, building them up, building them up—

"Oi, you alive?"

"—Yeah, I'm alive."

Called to from directly above, he opened his eyes to find a familiar face looking down at him.
That the man's coarse impression didn't fade even upside down struck him as a rare talent. Still, that had nothing to do with solving the present situation.

"Where'd they go?"

"After they broke through us and dropped you, they hightailed it out of here. You... do something about that. It's painful just to look at."

"That? ...Oh, this?"

He scratched his head as he sat up, looking down at his own side where the other man was pointing. There, a thick arrow was buried deep.
Lodged in his left flank, it would have pierced his heart had it landed a little higher.
It was a wound that had come within a hair's breadth of costing him his life, and yet he felt little about it—not just at the sight of it, but at the fact that his own flesh had been pierced.

"Actually, it doesn't hurt as much as it looks. Though I probably won't move too well for a while..."

"Idiot. Who said anything about worrying over you? I'm saying it's painful for me just watching. Hurry up and pull it out."

"That's a rough thing to say to a wounded man. ...Right, then."

Told so by the scowling man, he grabbed the embedded arrow, resigned to it. The trouble with arrow wounds was that if you didn't pull them out before the flesh tightened around them, they'd become impossible to remove.
Fortunately, since it hadn't been in long, a firm pull was enough to yank it free.
As for the blood, he forced a torn strip of his own clothing into the wound to stanch the flow.

"So, there, the arrow's out, just as ordered. What now?"

"If it's a deep wound, fall back and rest. I'll take a few men and crush the ones who ran. Teach them they're the prey here..."

"—That'd be a bad move, Jamal."

"What?"

He raised a hand to stop the man who'd laid out their next course of action—Jamal.
He understood the urge to chase down the fleeing enemy in retaliation for being made fools of. But if they pressed the attack rashly, it would be their own side that got hurt.

"Think about it. Why would they march right into Guaral so brazenly? Given what they'd just done, it's obvious we'd be in this city."

"...Unless they were just witless fools."

"—They came in on purpose. And they even hid an ambush outside the city walls."

Jamal glanced at the freshly-pulled arrow and swallowed audibly.
For all his rough looks, Jamal was a warrior of some standing, with an eye sharp enough to gauge just how formidable the power and precision of the arrow that had struck his companion truly was.
Naturally, he would have arrived at the same conclusion.

"If that's so, then what they were aiming for was...!"

"To lure us Imperial soldiers stationed in Guaral out and hunt us down. If we chased with our full strength, maybe, but send out a small pursuit party, and we'd be playing right into their hands. —So then, which of us is the prey, I wonder?"

"——"

Grinding his teeth in frustration, Jamal glared in the direction the enemy had fled.
Inwardly, Jamal seemed consumed by a fury hot enough to make his guts boil, and the other man understood that feeling all too well. —Or so he would have said, if not for the fact that admiration, more than anger, was what filled his own chest.

To use oneself so boldly as bait, taking advantage of the enemy's carelessness to reel in the fools completely.
It was, without question, a formidable foe—a true child of war.

"...Failing to kill him, that was a real screwup, huh."

He murmured this heartfeltly, gazing in the same direction as Jamal.
Then, with a "well," he seemed to collect himself and took a breath, adding,

"There'll be a chance for payback, I'm sure. —They'll come again, one way or another."

"When they do, I'm showing them no mercy at all."

To that reply, spoken through suppressed anger, he nodded silently in agreement.
And then, for the time being, he chalked this skirmish up as a defeat. That settled, he said—

"Jamal."

"Yeah. ...What's with the hands?"

Jamal frowned at the sight of him, legs still sprawled on the ground, both arms outstretched.
Tilting his head at how slow Jamal was to catch on, he continued, "You can see for yourself, can't you?"

"Piggyback."

"Die alone!"

What an unhelpful thing to say to a friend, he thought, shrugging.

He strode forward with long steps, stamping firmly on the hard ground, cutting the wind with his shoulders.
As he neared his destination, the impatience that had made him lean out from the ox-cart grew impossible to hold back, and in the end, right before the moment arrived, his body burst forward on its own.

He thought he heard various voices around him trying to hold him back, but none of it registered.
Shaking them all off, he made straight for the building he was after. He burst into the especially large timber-framed structure at the center of the settlement, and the gazes of everyone inside converged on him at once.
And then—

"—So you're back. Sooner than expected."

The one who said this, so brazenly unbothered, was the arrogant man wearing the demon mask.
Saying nothing to that as-if-it-were-obvious attitude, Subaru pressed straight forward. Carried by his own momentum, he reached out and mercilessly tore the demon mask from the man looking down at him.
The mask came off with hardly any resistance, revealing the man's handsome features.
He grabbed that insufferable, bewitching man by the collar and hauled him to his feet. Then, winding back his fist to slam it into that face—

"Wait, Subaru."

An instant before the punch landed, his drawn-back right arm was forcibly seized from behind.
It was Mizelda, the tall chieftain of the settlement with red-tipped hair, who had stopped him. Even now, of all times—he opened his mouth to protest.
But before he could, Mizelda spoke first, prefacing with, "Listen up—"

"Not the face. Anywhere else, I allow."

"Take this—!!"

"—!"

Taking Mizelda's unwavering answer at face value, Subaru, after a beat, drove his fist into the man's exposed torso.
Whether he'd braced for a blow to the face or not, the man let out a pained groan and staggered back. That alone wasn't enough to clear away all the pent-up frustration, but even so—

"Don't think we're even now, you bastard...!"

"—Hmph. A greedy man, aren't you."

The man—Abel—picked up the demon mask that had fallen beside him and put it back on, his tongue as sharp as ever.
Having landed a blow on the very man responsible for all his built-up frustration, Subaru let out a ragged breath.

After all, he'd doubled back over three days' worth of travel for the sole purpose of landing that one punch.


　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　△▼△▼△▼△


"Behold, sister! This, apparently, is the settlement of the famed 'People of Shudrak'! Just as the rumors said, hidden deep in this secluded wilderness! What a discovery!"

"Oooh! Amazing, big bro! Look, look! Everyone's got rock-hard abs just like me, rock-hard! Rock-hard, big bro!"

"Rock-hard indeed!"

Such carefree remarks from the siblings, entirely indifferent to their surroundings, echoed up into the settlement's sky.
Trading such unreserved impressions were Flop and Medium O'Connell, the siblings who had, through the sheer momentum of events, ended up invited to the settlement of the People of Shudrak.
In the middle of the plaza, atop their ox-cart, the siblings were surrounded by curious Shudrak who'd gathered around them. Apparently their fearless dispositions cared nothing for place or circumstance—drawing the attention of so many, they showed not a trace of fright, wariness, or unease, every bit the picture of unshakable nerve.

In truth, given that they were surrounded by an unfamiliar tribe about whom almost nothing was known, feeling that their lives or bodies were in danger would have been the normal, appropriate reaction.

"You've brought back quite the rowdy pair. Are those two truly necessary for your journey? If so, then you and I hold very different values."

"I'm not gonna deny our values are different. What you and I see is worlds apart. But still, even so, that attitude of yours is a bit much."

"Oh?"

Seated on the floor of the meeting hall, Subaru and Abel faced each other head-on, just as they had a few days prior.
Abel, who had just taken Subaru's punishing blow to the gut, showed no sign of remorse in either word or manner. Not that Subaru had expected an apology, or intended to offer one himself.
But he couldn't let the remark about Flop and the others slide.
Because—

"Abel-san, we're the ones who dragged those two into this. They were kind enough to help us when we were stranded outside the city. ...That's the only reason."

"——"

It was Rem who explained the reason for Subaru's anger in his stead.
Unlike the previous time, no one had been cleared from the meeting hall, and faces besides Subaru's and Abel's were present for the discussion too.
Beside Subaru sat Rem, kneeling formally, along with Kuna and Holly, who'd come along as their companions; beside Abel sat the sisters Mizelda and Taritta.
As for Rui, incidentally, she was being looked after by Utakata, who was close to her in age. By now the two were probably out at the plaza, by the ox-cart, among the Shudrak crowding around Flop and the others.
In any case—

"Thanks to the two of them, we made it back safely. But in exchange, Flop-san and his sister are now targets of the city's soldiers too..."

"Wrong. Let me correct you. It isn't the city's soldiers hunting us and the ones outside. It's the Empire's soldiers. Those who serve this nation have become your enemies."

"——"

From behind the demon mask, Abel's cold words pierced Subaru and Rem alike.
Hearing such a dry statement, Rem could only lower her pale blue eyes weakly. Certainly, it was an undeniable truth—Subaru understood that much himself.
But understanding a thing and having one's feelings accept it were two entirely different matters.

"Getting on the bad side of the Imperial soldiers at the camp was my mistake. Making enemies of them was the result of the path I chose, too."

"Indeed. A grudge you forged before you even made contact with the People of Shudrak."

"I've got no excuse for that grudge. It was undoubtedly me who made those enemies. —But the bill for that grudge should've been mine alone to pay."

It was true that most of the fault lay with Subaru. But the issue here was Abel's conduct.
From the very start, Abel must have understood the danger of Subaru and the others entering Guaral. He must have realized the possibility of running into surviving Imperial soldiers and being attacked.
Which was precisely why—

"I told Kuna and Holly beforehand to wait for us outside the city. So that if we got chased out and had to flee, they'd be there to back us up."

"True, true, it was a close call-noh. If Kuna and I hadn't been there, Subaru's head would be split clean in two by an axe by now-noh."

Holly declared this leisurely, sitting cross-legged with her cheeks stuffed full of round dumplings. Unlike Kuna beside her, who wore an awkward expression, Holly seemed entirely oblivious to the mood in the room.
Of course, Subaru was grateful for Holly and Kuna's help. Without their cooperation, the injury of having his head split open would very likely have become a reality.

"Kuna's eyes kept watch, and Holly's skill saved us... a long-range bow shot, a combination of the two of their talents."

"...Though I wasn't told it'd get that dicey-na."

The lack of strength in Kuna's supplementary words was proof of the guilt she felt toward Subaru and the others.
Even so, the guilt Kuna carried was misplaced. Before entering Guaral, the words she'd given Subaru at their parting had given him a crucial hint.
Without that advice, he never would have received the help Holly spoke of.
That said—

"That reasoning applies to the two of you, Kuna and Holly, and no further. —As for you, who predicted this whole thing perfectly, I've got no intention of going easy on you."

Which was precisely why the first thing Subaru did upon returning was deliver that punishing blow to Abel.
Had Abel shown some sign of remorse after taking that hit, things might have gone differently. But, as largely expected, Abel showed no remorse—not even the slightest hint of guilt.
He remained just as arrogant as ever, toying with Subaru and the others' anger.
Even now, the sharp glint behind the demon mask showed not the faintest shadow of contrition.
He looked, in fact, as if he might say "So what?" at any moment.

"Oi, don't just sit there in silence, say something."

"—So what?"

"...He actually said it."

His irritating prediction having hit the mark, Subaru ground his teeth at Abel's words, his gaze sharpening.
But Abel deflected Subaru's accusing glare with cool indifference, and said,

"You come charging back and land a blow on me, and now all you've got to offer is petty grievances? To begin with, I warned you from the outset. —That this would be no easy road."

"Ngh...!"

"Know this as the price of leaping at momentary comfort without using your head. That defeated soldiers from a burned-out camp would most likely make for the nearest city was self-evident."

"Then... then you should've just said so from the start!"

Abel's words, spoken with one knee raised, cut into Subaru's every immaturity, one by one.
But to begin with, Subaru understood his own fault, and admitted it too—that his own lack of forethought, and the unfortunate chain of events born from his earlier actions, had come to a head.
But Abel, who had known of the danger and let it pass unmentioned—his conduct was, put simply, an act of aiding the enemy. And if the friend-or-foe framing missed the mark, then it amounted to nothing less than setting a trap.

"You knew everything from the start. The chance we'd run into surviving Imperial soldiers in Guaral, the chance we'd have to flee the city in a panic, the chance that in all that chaos Rem might..."

"——"

"Anyway! You knew all of it. And yet you kept your mouth shut."

Carried by his own momentum, words he shouldn't say nearly slipped out. Catching himself just in time, Subaru forced himself not to look at Rem beside him, focusing all his anger on Abel instead.
As Subaru's flames of anger blazed higher, Abel watched with cool indifference—no, with icy detachment.
With that penetrating gaze, just how much of the world did this man truly see through?
And seeing through it all as he did, to what end did he toy with Subaru and the others?

"Answer me, you bastard. Why the hell did you..."

"I merely spared us needless trouble."

"Needless trouble...?"

To Subaru's snarling intensity, Abel replied with a tone indistinguishable from a sigh.
Subaru blinked at the answer, and Abel slowly ran his hand along the floor of the meeting hall, scooping up a palmful of dry earth.

"A fool like you values what he sees with his own foolish eyes far more than any counsel from a sage. Whatever I might have told you with my own mouth, the harshness of the rain that falls on you speaks louder still."

"——"

"You've felt it keenly now, no doubt. —That there is nowhere for the likes of you to run."

As he spoke, dirt trickled from Abel's palm, falling in a light patter.
By that gesture alone, Subaru felt the eerily vivid illusion of being confronted, with terrible clarity, by the fact that he was hemmed in on every side.

As the Emperor of the Sacred Vollachia Empire, manipulating others with clever words was surely no difficult task for him.
Before an Emperor who was a battle-hardened veteran of every trick in the book, Subaru's protests amounted to little more than the shrieking of a caged monkey.
And indeed, all Subaru could do was hold his head in his hands.

"...In the end, what is it you actually want to do?"

"My aim, I've already told you. To reclaim what was taken from me. To that end, the Empire as it now stands is my enemy. And that makes it your enemy too."

"—So, what, you want me to cooperate with you?"

"For now, I've merely explained that I have no reason to do you harm."

Into Subaru, head still in his hands, Abel's words seeped in like poison.
Abel's way of speaking, which never let Subaru grasp a clear answer, seemed to carry the guile of a man deliberately clouding the issue—while at the same time, it also felt as though he were testing Subaru.
Again and again, Abel had never stated things plainly to Subaru, and yet he had kept saying it all the same.
Think for yourself, and choose.
Here too, Abel was provoking him—if you want to argue, come up onto my level first. As things stood, Subaru couldn't even reach the same footing to argue on.
And His Imperial Majesty was not so kind as to patiently wait for Subaru to climb obediently onto that stage.

"...So it's all playing out in the palm of your hand? I don't like that one bit."

"—Unfortunately, only a portion of men can be rolled about in my palm. And it's precisely because I failed to fully master the rest that I now sit here on the bare earth."

"——"

That reply to Subaru's spat-out words sounded, to Subaru's ears, almost like Abel mocking himself.
Subaru couldn't see his expression, and there was no change in the tone of his voice. And yet, it had sounded like self-mockery. That was rare—no, it was the first time Subaru had ever heard Abel mock himself.
And that, in turn, was proof that he was facing reality without illusion.

An uncrowned Emperor, driven from his throne after allowing his own subordinates to rebel against him.
Sitting here on the bare earth of the People of Shudrak's meeting hall, deep within the forest—this situation itself was surely, for him, undeniable proof of an inexcusable failure.

"——"

Staring Abel down head-on, Subaru quietly turned things over in his mind.
It concerned his own future path, and that of the others, but more than that, he couldn't grasp Abel's true intentions. Being swept up in the man's schemes again, only to meet with the same kind of misfortune, was something Subaru refused to stomach.
In truth, just what was Abel thinking?
Even Subaru didn't think Abel would go to such elaborate lengths just to scheme him into his own camp. Abel surely didn't see that much value in Subaru himself.
And yet, if there was a reason Abel wanted to keep Subaru close at hand, it likely lay not in Subaru himself, but in his interest in something else attached to him—

"—Young master! These people here are truly delightful and open-hearted! I'm impressed!"

"Waugh?!"

Right in the middle of Subaru's breath-held, deadly serious contemplation—
Flop appeared, slipping in through the entrance of the meeting hall, his resonant, handsome voice ringing out. He looked over the assembled faces within the hall, and said,

"Well now, my apologies for the delayed greeting! It seems these must be the representatives of this settlement. Oh my! And lookalikes of Miss Kuna and Miss Holly, too!"

"It's really us."

"It really is-noh."

"Was that so! How rude of me!"

Smoothing back his long forelocks with one hand, Flop strode briskly to the center of the meeting hall, and there, flashing an amiable smile, gave a bow.

"Allow me to properly introduce myself—I'm Flop O'Connell! I travel about as a merchant, together with my sister Medium and our dear Botecliff. Owing to a whole string of circumstances, it seems I've ended up accompanying young master here, his wife, and his little niece on their curious journey. I look forward to knowing you all!"

"He seems to know his manners-na, and yet not-na..."

At Flop's exuberant greeting, Taritta's face took on a pained look. Then she glanced over at Mizelda beside her, and said,

"Sister, what will you do? Shall we order the outsiders to withdraw..."

"Let's see-na... no, he's a handsome one-da. We'll keep him in the room."

"Sister..."

Arms crossed, Mizelda decided Flop's treatment based on his face alone.
It was an extremely simple, easy-to-follow standard for a chieftain's judgment, but for Taritta, supporting her sister in that role, it must have been a headache. Of course, if looks alone were truly Mizelda's only criterion, Subaru wouldn't have been allowed to stay in the settlement at all, so there had to be something more to it.

"Oh right, Mizelda-san, there's one thing I want to check. Flop-san is my guest, or, well, I brought him along on my own judgment. But making him go through the Lifeblood Ritual..."

"The Lifeblood Ritual? Could that be some sort of traditional welcoming ceremony passed down in this settlement!? I'd very much like to experience it myself!"

"It's a traditional ceremony, alright, but it's a bit rough for a welcome."

Flop was eager to take on the challenge, but even he would surely balk if told he might have to fight a Witchbeast. Or—and this was the frightening possibility—he might not change his attitude one bit even after hearing that. In any case, Subaru didn't want to put him through it.

"—I knew it from behind a single wall already, but he's a noisy sort."

And the one who did not welcome Flop's manner in the least was the ruthless, demon-masked Emperor.
Flop, warm as sunlight, and Abel, with ice running through his veins, made for a truly stark contrast. At the very least, there seemed to be no reason for Abel to take a liking to Flop, and that thought alone made Subaru's own blood run cold.

"Oh my! What an unusual mask... could you perhaps be the leader of this settlement? I once read somewhere that a person dressed so distinctively must hold a special position!"

Thus began the first meeting between the two of them, with Flop remarking on Abel's outlandish attire.
It was no wonder Flop drew that conclusion from the conspicuous demon mask, but it was difficult to take Abel for one of the Shudrak. After all, everything about him besides the mask was culturally worlds apart from the rest of the Shudrak. Looked at afresh, his getup was simply too mismatched.

"Your reasoning isn't bad, but you lack attentiveness and discernment. You mentioned earlier that peddling is your trade..."

"Ah, that's right! We load our wares onto the ox-cart pulled by my sister here, Botecliff, and travel about the Empire conducting business... We're a pair of wandering siblings, drifting with the wind!"

Flop answered with a hand over his heart, his voice almost song-like.
From outside the building, from the direction of the plaza, came the cry, "As expected of big bro—!" Even separated by a single wall, the bond between the siblings ran deep.
And yet, even such a heartwarming display of their bond apparently did nothing to thaw Abel's frozen heart.
Hearing Flop's answer, Abel gave a small "hmph" through his nose, and said,

"—Natsuki Subaru, you told me you picked these two up in the city."

"Don't talk about people like they're objects. Actually, if we're being accurate about it, it'd be more correct to say we were the ones picked up by Flop-san."

"What matters is the essence of the thing. There's no time to waste on trivialities. But I'll grant you your first bit of praise for having returned like this. Well done."

"Doesn't feel like I'm being praised... what are you scheming now?"

Honestly, Subaru's read on the situation was that Flop and Abel were a bad match.
For that reason, he'd meant to have Flop wait out in the plaza and introduce him properly when the timing was right, but Abel's reaction caught Subaru entirely off guard.
Then again, Abel's interest wasn't in Flop's character. And certainly not in Medium's existence either, of whom all one could tell so far was the volume of her voice. Which left the answer plain to see.

"Merchant, how well acquainted are you with Guaral?"

"Now that's a fine question, village chief! I do rather pride myself on being quite well-connected in Guaral. After all, it's plain to see that venturing too far afield tends to be fatal! Even peddling requires sticking to familiar routes between fixed locales to travel safely, you know!"

"Sounds like a fine line between careful and cowardly-na..."

At Flop's ever-optimistic, confidence-brimming answer, Kuna let out a sigh that sounded halfway to exasperated.
But hearing Flop's answer, Abel fell silent behind his demon mask. —No, in fact, Subaru's ears caught a faint sound that wasn't quite silence.
It was the small sound of Abel's throat, giving a quiet click.

"What good fortune. You've found a real prize, Natsuki Subaru. —A peddler well-acquainted with the city is sure to know of a secret passage or two."

"Oi, wait, Abel. A secret passage? What are you talking about now?"

"Seeking an answer from someone else again? It seems you still haven't grasped the meaning behind my repeated questioning. I have no words to spare for such ignorance."

"——"

It was an infuriating way of putting things, every single time, and yet Subaru had no answer for it.
To break off the discussion here and walk away would leave Natsuki Subaru far too isolated, without a single ally, for that to be a real option. And so, infuriating as it was, he turned his mind to thinking it through.
—No, in truth, he hadn't even needed to think it through; he already had a rough grasp of what Abel was thinking.
It was plain enough from the question Abel had just posed to Flop.

"Abel, you... don't tell me..."

"Even a sluggish mind can find the answer, if it deigns to use itself. Yes—it's just as you're thinking."

Looking back at Subaru, his lips trembling and cheeks tense, Abel's penetrating gaze, sharp even behind the demon mask, spoke of a ruthless resolve.
And then, as if to make it known to everyone present besides Subaru as well, Abel declared—

"—We will bring down the walled city of Guaral. As our next stronghold, that city is a necessity."
