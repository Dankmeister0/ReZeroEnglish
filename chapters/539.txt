Arc 7 Chapter 41: "Tea Talk"

"—Strategy meeting!"

The room fell hushed, still as water freshly sprinkled on the ground, as Subaru raised a short hand and made the proposal.
No one voiced objection to those words. Everyone felt the need to talk things through.
The topic, of course, was the matter of presenting themselves at Koururi Castle.

"The letter was written by Abel, and it's said that we who went to the castle yesterday are essential too, but..."

Subaru, mulling over Yorna's demand as relayed by Tanza—who'd come as her messenger—looked down at his own clothes, sleeves and hem cinched tight, and creased his brow into a scowl.

Setting aside, for now, the risk of sending Abel to the castle directly, the biggest sticking point was the condition that "yesterday's messengers" had to come along.
Would showing up with the shrunken trio of Subaru, Al, and Medium in tow actually pass muster with Yorna? At worst, there was a real chance it would be taken as a bad joke.
Or there was the option of laying their whole situation bare to Yorna and appealing to her heart with earnest sincerity—

"Showing your hand when you don't even know how the other side will react is no sane act."

"Yeah... Judging by yesterday's exchange, even I can't say for sure Yorna would hear us out."

Subaru agreed with Abel's refusal to lay all their cards on the table.
He'd avoided saying it outright yesterday, but if Subaru had to put into words the impression Yorna had made on him, "vixen" would be the accurate choice. Her courtesan-like speech and mannerisms struck him as those of a sultry woman gifted at toying with men in the palm of her hand.
Add to that her position as ruler of the Demon City and the strength befitting the title of one of the Nine Divine Generals, and Yorna's danger level went without saying.

Never mind that they'd marked her as the first Nine Divine General to win over as an ally—as Abel was wary of, she was not the type of person you could easily reveal your situation to.

"If we spill our whole situation and she comes at us over the brother thing, we're screwed. What if she goes, 'Didst thou go and tell lil' ol' me a lie about that cross-dressin', hmm?'"

"I don't want to think she'd pick a fight like that, but... should we just go ahead and play up Natsumi Schwartz's loli mode, just in case...?"

"W-wait, pleasE. That's no gooD. I fear I'll get confused as welL, too..."

The dilemma of piling lie upon lie to cover a lie was a familiar story, but taking on the karmic burden of having to keep cross-dressing even after being "Infantilized," just to keep the cross-dressing lie afloat, was probably without much precedent.
Unlike Al's helmet, a wig's fit could be adjusted, so cross-dressing itself was still possible—but whether it was necessary to go that far would depend on how the conversation unfolded from here.

"So, what are we gonna do? The Bell of the Hour of Fire is only, like, three hours away, right?"

"...There's no doubt we should go to Koururi Castle, but—"

Medium tilted her head as she asked, cradling Rui in her arms.
With the two of them now the same size, Rui tilted her head in time with Medium, and amid it all Subaru found himself consulting the Balance within him.

"First off, there's no option where we just don't accept Yorna's invitation, right?"

"Well, we went through all that trouble just to get her to read the letter. Otherwise all our hard work yesterday—and shrinkin' up like this—means nothin'."

"RatheR, onlY the damage would remaiN..."

Nodding along to Al's and Taritta's words, Subaru still considered retreat not entirely off the table.
"I can't go home until I win back what I bet" was the classic mindset of someone about to lose big at gambling. Depending on the situation, cutting your losses mattered too.
That said, what stood to be lost in this particular "loss-cutting" was their height.

"Is this something to take seriously, or brush off...?"

"If we'd aged, sure, but we got younger. Doesn't this seem more like some secret art of eternal youth?"

"Yeah, true enough—"

In a sense, "growing younger" meant an increase in opportunity, not unlike Return by Death.
If you could return to childhood while retaining an adult's knowledge and experience, you could train your growing body the right way this time, or take another shot at goals you'd once given up on.
Under different circumstances, there might well be ways to turn it to their advantage—

"—Reckon so, don'tcha? But it ain't so simple as all that, this here. It ain't as handy a trick as all that, y'know?"

In that instant, a third party's voice cut into the discussion, and Subaru's breath stopped.
—No, Subaru wasn't the only one. Everyone in the room froze, unable to react in the moment, locked in place by the presence behind that voice.
Yet the person in question was utterly unbothered by the tension rising in the room.

"I went ahead and made myself some tea, hope ya don't mind. Anyone else want some?"

So he said, lifting a steaming teacup and asking with easy familiarity.

"—!"

An instant later, Taritta moved with the snap of a spring-loaded doll.
In a single moment she'd drawn her bow, nocked an arrow, and leveled her aim at the man who'd appeared—Olbart Dunkelkenn.
The range was point-blank—not a distance you could miss at, nor one you could dodge from.
But Olbart, with an unruffled "Now, now," just shrugged his shoulders.

"Quit that, quit that. I ain't fond of havin' pointy things aimed at me, y'know. Old folks already gotta pee more'n most as it is—ya tryin' to scare me into wettin' myself? I'm shakin' in my boots here, ain't I?"

"Enough of your nonsensE...! Who exactly arE you..."

"—Olbart Dunkelkenn."

"——"

Taritta flushed at the diminutive old man who, despite having hostility leveled at him, refused to lose his composure. But it was the masked man—the one who'd spoken Olbart's name, reining her in as if answering her question—who cut in.
Abel alone had remained seated on the sofa without so much as a twitch. Meeting the gaze of the man in the demon mask, Olbart narrowed the eyes hidden beneath his thick brows.

"Well, I already confirmed yesterday that I'm somethin' of a celebrity, so I ain't too surprised. But that's quite the memorable face you got there too. Where'd ya pick up a souvenir like that?"

"Do not waste your breath on foolish words, old wood. What business brings you here?"

"These days the young'uns won't even humor an old man's idle chatter, and it's a lonely thing. Even the folks back home are gettin' better and better at tunin' me out by the day... kah-kah-kah!"

Olbart laughed heartily, mouth wide open, even as he spoke of the loneliness of old age.
Watching Abel and Olbart's exchange, the confusion of Subaru and the others watching on slowly began to ease—though their guard, of course, could never fully come down.

"But how'd you get in here, gramps? It's not like we weren't watching at all..."

"Oh, if it ain't the little missy who looked like yesterday's dancer, just shrunk down. Well, since gramps here's gonna answer ya—ya let that Deer Human girl through, right? I just came in along with her."

"Along with her? With Tanza?"

"Kah-kah-kah! How exactly I got in's a secret, y'know. Shinobi Techniques are the tools of my trade, after all."

Olbart laughed off Medium's innocent question, deflecting it like smoke.
How much of it was genuine was hard to say, but either way, the fact that he'd said the word "Shinobi" himself suggested he had no intention of hiding his identity.
If so, rather than fumbling around trying to guess his real reason for marching into their camp alone like this, it would be faster to simply ask him outright.

"So..."

"Hm?"

"So, what did you really come here to do?"

Olbart's gaze flicked toward Subaru, who'd asked after the purpose of his visit. The old man knit his brow for a moment as if in thought, then—

"Ah, you're that one, huh. The girl in the red outfit from yesterday. Couldn't mistake the dancer-lookin' girl or the one-armed fella for anyone else, so I was scratchin' my head somethin' fierce over you."

So he confessed—he'd had a hard time figuring out who Subaru really was.
Hearing this, Subaru's eyes went wide, while Al—who was casually repositioning himself—stifled a laugh. Through his mask, his voice came out muffled, more so than usual, as he chuckled.

"...As expected of ya, brother. Not even the head honcho of the Shinobi could see through your disguise."

"Oh-ho, well now, ain't that somethin'! I'd love for the folks back at my village to learn a thing or two from that transformation. How about it, wanna come teach a class? You'd be welcome."

"Unfortunately, Natsumi Schwartz's schedule is pretty packed right now. ...Though I might be able to squeeze in some time, maybe."

"Ho-ho, and what's this 'maybe' about?"

Subaru licked his lips at the unexpectedly enthusiastic bite on the cross-dressing bait, his throat going dry as he narrowed his eyes.
Setting aside how much of this nonsense to believe, he'd make full use of the chance to speak with Olbart directly. First on the agenda: the bizarre incident that had befallen them.
If Abel's guess was right, the culprit behind this phenomenon was none other than the old man standing right in front of them.

"If you've got any idea why we ended up like this, then be honest with—"

"Ah, that. That'd be me, y'know. Fun stuff, ain't it, Shinobi Techniques."

"——"

Olbart admitted it was his doing, plainly, without any pretense of maneuvering.
His declaration swallowed up whatever words Subaru had been about to say, and Subaru caught his breath faintly. Watching Subaru freeze up, the old man grinned, wicked and pleased with himself.

"If I go and kill somebody by accident, I can't hear a lick outta 'em, and it just leaves a mess behind, don't it? So I got all sorts of techniques for wringin' folks dry without killin' 'em. —Fun, ain't it?"

So he declared, and sipped his warm tea.


△▼△▼△▼△


—The "Infantilization" that had struck Subaru, Al, and Medium.
Based on Abel's testimony, given his knowledge of the Nine Divine Generals, it had been all but confirmed that Olbart was the culprit.
Still, having it confirmed directly from the man's own mouth carried a different kind of shock—all the more so given how utterly unashamed and brazen he was about it.

"——"

"Now, now, don't everybody go lookin' at me like that all at once. Didn't nobody ever teach ya old folks are s'posed to be cared for, not tormented?"

Tension mounted in the room once more. Taritta, who hadn't lowered her bow from the start, along with Al and Medium, edged their positions inch by inch.
Al in particular had planted himself before the entrance, resolved to block Olbart's escape route.
Though with his body shrunken as it was, he couldn't wield his beloved sword freely, so there wasn't much hope he could actually stop this elusive Shinobi chief.

"Grrr...!"

"Now that little missy over there's got nothin' to do with me, right? If so, bein' looked at like that hurts my feelings. Don't like the sweet old-man smell us geezers got, do ya?"

"Cease your idle chatter, Olbart. I ask again—what business brought you here?"

Olbart, trying to win over the growling Rui, had words thrown at his profile by Abel. As Olbart glanced back, Abel struck him with a sharp presence of authority that carried even through the demon mask.

"You will have heard the intentions of this Demon City's ruler, Yorna Mishigure. She has recognized these people as her messengers. She declared plainly that no harm was to come to them."

"T-that's right! No interference—that was the whole point of yesterday's contest! We staked our very lives on it! And yet, this treatment isn't what we agreed to at all!"

"Now, now, you're gettin' all worked up and mixin' yourself into this, ain'tcha."

Riding on Abel's offensive, Subaru pressed the point of what Yorna had said the day before as well. As Olbart pointed out, his feelings had regressed clear back to yesterday, but that didn't undo the facts.
Yorna had recognized Subaru and the others as messengers, and Tanza had declared outright that no one would be allowed to touch them.
To disregard that would be nothing less than a declaration of hostility toward Yorna.

"Naturally, that would matter not at all were you here intending to destroy the Demon City, I imagine."

"...Now that you mention it."

At Abel's added remark, the hot blood that had risen in Subaru drained away, cold.
In truth, the fake emperor's party, which included Olbart, had effectively ceded the right to negotiate with Yorna to Subaru's group. They could just as easily judge that a bad move and flip the table then and there.
In that case, a head-on collision with Yorna—someone even the Nine Divine Generals held in high regard, in more ways than one—would be unavoidable, but—

"Should they challenge her with destruction in mind, ruin would be the result. Naturally, they would suffer commensurate losses."

"...Well, handlin' that fox girl's a tricky business, sure enough. Still, ain't much fun havin' a masked young pup see through me so clean."

"Masked... young pup..."

Olbart scratched his chin, wearing the look of a man caught between two evils. Catching part of what he'd said, Subaru glanced sidelong at Abel.
Abel showed no change in demeanor whatsoever, but Subaru, for his part, had been on edge through every step of this exchange. —After all, Olbart was one of the Nine Divine Generals.
Naturally, he must have met Abel—that is, Vincent Vollachia—many times before.
Even with his face hidden behind a mask, if Olbart exchanged words with Abel—who hadn't altered his manner of speech or tone of voice at all—it wouldn't be strange in the least for him to notice who he really was.

"——"

And yet Abel, responding with his usual composed bearing, showed not a trace of such worry.
Nor did Olbart show any sign of having caught on to Abel's true identity. That being the case, Subaru couldn't very well point it out himself, and had no choice but to hide his inner unease and carry on.
Whatever the case—

"I still haven't gotten an answer, Olbart-san."

"—Hm. Beats bein' called 'old man,' I s'pose—makes me feel kinda cherished. I'll let it slide that you were pretendin' to be a girl, for that."

"Olbart-san!"

His patience worn thin, Subaru sharpened his gaze at the old man dodging his answer. Seeing that, Olbart raised both hands, saying "Alright, alright."

"Truth be told, it's just like you folks said. Both the fox girl and His Excellency told me not to lay a hand on ya."

"Then—!"

"But hold on now—when I poked at you folks, that was before yesterday's contest was decided, wasn't it? So it's like this: if nobody complains about dyin' from a sword wound taken mid-battle, then nobody's got grounds to complain about a shrinkin' technique slipped in mid-battle either."

"Th-that's sophistry...!"

"Well, it is sophistry, sure enough. —But can ya overturn it?"

Subaru's words caught in his throat before Olbart, who raised one eyebrow and smirked with all his cunning.
Olbart's argument was sophistry, just as he himself admitted. But at the same time, his argument was also, in its way, sound.
Even if someone died from a wound inflicted during the fighting, if that wound had been dealt before the ceasefire negotiations were concluded, it made no sense to hold anyone responsible for a post-ceasefire tragedy.
Though admittedly, a wound and "Infantilization" felt like very different matters.

"So the way I see it, whether or not I turn you folks back is a separate matter from the fox girl's orders. Ya follow?"

"——"

"Kah-kah-kah! Don't make such a sour face now. You'll ruin that cute mug of yours... though I s'pose that cute mug's just makeup and mimicry to begin with! Well, ya sure had me fooled."

Olbart twirled his teacup deftly with his fingertips, performing an almost acrobatic feat to keep from spilling a drop. His words themselves were steeped in a veteran cunning that wouldn't be easily overcome.
Yet even with Taritta growing more dangerous by the moment and Al straining to block the escape route, Subaru couldn't picture any scenario where they forced Olbart into submission by strength alone.
If there was any way to overcome this old cunning, it would be—

"Enough, Olbart."

—only the masked man, cutting through the old cunning with overflowing authority.
Without shifting his posture, Abel skewered Olbart with a gaze grown sharper and colder still, bringing his teacup-twirling to a halt.

"I believe I asked you what business brought you here. How many times must I ask before you see fit to answer?"

"Whenever there's some young pup around whose face I can't read, I just can't help wantin' to see the wrinkles form on his brow—bad habit of mine. Go easy on me, would ya."

Olbart winked one eye, answering Abel's authority with a bit of mischief. Still, the glare didn't ease, so the old man scratched his head, muttering, "No patience in this one," before continuing.

"Fox girl aside, I was told not to touch His Excellency either, so I ain't come here to do anything to you folks. But if you're all gonna end up enemies anyhow, weakenin' ya a bit ain't gonna earn me no divine punishment, is it?"

"...That might explain why you shrunk us, but it don't explain why you went outta your way to come drink tea here."

"Lookin' like your helmet don't fit no more's given you some trouble, eh, you. Anyhow, that read of yours is spot on too. Ahh, well, honestly, the simplest way to put why I came is that I'm here to hear you folks out."

"...Hear us out?"

Fiddling with his ear with the hand not holding his teacup, Olbart nodded at Subaru echoing the question back, saying, "That's right."
Still with the same finger he'd been using on his ear, he pointed out the window.

"That speech you gave at the castle yesterday, that fired me right up, y'know. Thing is, plenty of folk have tried pullin' somethin' on His Excellency, and every last one's failed so far. So I got to wonderin' why you'd go and cross such a dangerous bridge again—figured I'd like to hear what's in your heart on that."

"...And what do you do once you've heard it?"

"Huh? Obviously I decide that after I've heard it. And I rig things up beforehand so you can't lie to me, kah-kah-kah!"

Subaru grimaced at Olbart, who laughed uproariously, mouth wide, spit flying.
It would be easy to write off his attitude, exactly as it sounded, as simple bad taste—but it was clear this wasn't someone he could afford to dismiss on that basis alone.
Not only to get him to undo the Shinobi Technique that had "Infantilized" them, but because he too was, potentially, one of the Nine Divine Generals they needed to bring onside for Abel's bid to reclaim the throne.
If anything—

"...A chance, is it?"

The Nine Divine Generals occupied a position where even making contact, let alone winning them over as allies, was difficult to begin with.
Yet at present, by whatever twist of fate, Chaosflame held not only its ruler Yorna but also Olbart standing right here, and Chisha, seemingly disguised as the fake emperor.
Of course, winning over Chisha, who'd taken the lead in opposing them, was likely impossible—but—

"If Abel's reasoning from yesterday is right, then Olbart-san isn't confirmed as an enemy."

Compared to the Nine Divine Generals already claimed as the enemy's pieces, Olbart still had hope left, in Abel's estimation. Given how they'd managed to reach the brink of negotiations with Yorna as things currently stood, Abel's judgment had to be called correct.
As for Olbart's position, if his reasoning was to be trusted, there should be room to negotiate.
However, in order to win Olbart over here—

"——"

Subaru glanced sidelong, still silent, at Abel's profile.
Revealing the true identity of the man in the demon mask, who continued to carry himself with such composure—that, precisely, was the one piece of information that absolutely had to be disclosed in order to win Olbart over.
Honestly, they had no other piece of information that would land with Olbart harder than that.

"——"

Receiving Subaru's gaze on his profile, the masked Abel didn't so much as stir.
His profile was hidden by the mask, and none of his emotions could be read. But surely a mind as sharp as his had already arrived at the same thought Subaru had.
How far to open up to Olbart here.
That was the crux of it, in Subaru's thinking—but since Abel wasn't moving, was that thought premature? Or was he, too, simply watching to see how things played out?
Either way, if they could win Olbart over, a light would appear at the end of the tunnel for their "Infantilization" problem, their shortage of fighting strength, and the struggle to claim the Nine Divine Generals as pieces of their own.
So this did seem like the moment to make a move—

"—Funny thing is, a person's face here talks a surprisin' lot, y'know?"

"Huh...?"

"Where your eyes go, how stiff your face gets, just the tiniest tension in a muscle... Even without your mouth movin' at all, it tells me all sorts of things, really."

Olbart tapped two raised fingers against the corner of his own eye as he spoke. His gaze was fixed on Subaru, and the old man nodded at him as he caught his breath.

"For instance, the dancer-lookin' missy over there's relyin' on the archer missy. The young pup wrapped in bandages and the little one there, that's you. And you and the archer... you're both lookin' to the masked young pup."

"—!"

"You can see most everything just by followin' where each of ya looks. —And when folks are worn down, it shows even plainer, don't it?"

So he said, flashing white teeth in a grin, and his words pierced straight through Subaru's mind.
And Subaru understood, far too late, just how badly he'd misjudged the true character of this man who could pass for a kindly old grandfather.
Olbart had stated plainly that he was the one who'd caused the "Infantilization," and said clearly that he'd done it in order to hear Subaru and the others out.
—He had never once said it was to "negotiate" with them.

"You came here to talk to us..."

"Comin' to 'talk to' someone and comin' to 'hear' someone out are similar, but a bit different, ain't they? Chattin' away like this, I don't dislike it none. But—"

"——"

"See, I'm the head of the Shinobi, and I go about actin' like a representative for the folks back home. So... words from someone I ain't even tortured are just too risky to trust."

Without breaking from the air of a reasonable old man he'd carried up until then, Olbart voiced his own thinking, one that followed the unwritten code of the Shinobi—of ninja.
That bleak, merciless quality was indeed akin to the world of "ninja" that Subaru knew.
But the feeling of enjoying it purely as fiction, and the feeling of having it flaunted right in front of him like this, were worlds apart.
And then—

"So, big attitude, don't rely on nobody—the masked young pup's the boss here, ain't he? Let's hear it, what exactly you're plannin' by comin' to woo that fox girl."

"—. And should that fail, what then?"

"Then I'll just have to try somethin' else, won't I? Though, bein' bound not to lay a hand on ya, there ain't much I can actually do about it anyhow."

Olbart shrugged, his gaze and attention fixed on Abel. Of course, it was hard to imagine that Olbart, chief of the Shinobi, was neglecting to watch the others at all.
Even without facing them directly, he surely hadn't let his guard down toward Al or Taritta.
Still, even with a bow trained on him, it wasn't exactly wise for Subaru and the others, surrounding him as they were, to show any intent to attack Olbart.
Under Yorna's decree, they'd been welcomed into Chaosflame as messengers.
Whether that status would still apply after they'd caused an incident within Chaosflame, or how she'd treat ill-mannered guests, was a complete unknown.
In other words, the situation was a stalemate, both for Subaru's side and for Olbart.
Both sides could do nothing but glare at each other, each declaring themselves ready to do harm.
Such a directionless, extreme standoff—

"...But then again, where else could a situation like that even be arranged?"

Looking back over the situation they were in, Subaru posed the question anew to the blank space that had opened up in his mind.
And once again, the thought arose: Isn't this a chance?
Olbart, bound by both the fake emperor and Yorna, unable to take any further hostile action.
A chance to confirm which way his sympathies leaned, at a moment when he couldn't act on it even if he did lean toward the enemy—where else but here could such a situation ever be arranged?

"——"

Time crept by, second by second.
There was the tense standoff, yes, but also the deadline of the Bell of the Hour of Fire that Yorna had set.
Time was precious. —The more it was wasted, the more life was worn away.

In the silence, Taritta's fingers trembled around her bow, while Al and Medium held their breath. Even Rui, managing only a low growl from her throat, seemed to instinctively sense the danger Olbart posed.
Most likely, none of them could choose to move. If anyone could choose, it would be—

"—Abel."

"——"

Quietly, Subaru spoke Abel's name.
He could tell that Abel's attention, on hearing it, turned toward him from behind the demon mask. The mask blocked any view of his gaze, but his attention was fixed on Subaru.
He couldn't see the emotion behind it. —But he felt that his intent had been understood.

"He's Emperor Vincent Vollachia."

"...Ahh?"

Olbart raised his eyebrows at Subaru, who declared this while receiving Abel's gaze.
The old man's eyes, hidden beneath his long, bushy brows, went wide, and he regarded Subaru's words with suspicion. It was a natural enough reaction, but far from the one Subaru wanted.
So, to draw out the reaction he was after, he pushed in deeper still.

"You want to know the identity of the young man whose face you can't see, and what our goal is, right? The answer to why we came to meet Yorna..."

"Now, now, that ain't exactly somethin' to laugh about, is it?"

"I'm not trying to make it a joke. —Abel."

Olbart's words caught for a moment, a faint note of surprise mixing into his voice.
Seeing that clearly, Subaru called out to Abel at the back of the room. With things having come this far, Abel made no move to object to Subaru's words or actions.
Quietly, his slender fingers reached up toward his own face. And then—

"—Well now, this here's somethin' else."

Before Olbart, who let out a voice of pure astonishment, the hidden face was revealed.
Staring back at Olbart, whose face had gone rigid at the sight, was none other than the striking black-haired man—Emperor Vincent Vollachia himself.

"His Excellency, ya say? But that's too strange, ain't it. Then, the one I've been travelin' with—"

"Set your aged wits to work, Olbart Dunkelkenn. You already hold the answer. All that remains is to draw it out."

"With His Excellency's face, sayin' somethin' just like His Excellency would. ...But, I see."

Swallowed by astonishment for a moment, the elderly Shinobi nonetheless quickly regained his composure.
With his face bared, Abel had issued an order as emperor, and just as his words implied, it seemed the information Olbart already possessed was enough to explain the situation they'd found themselves in.
Olbart rested a hand on his chin.

"Then that'd make it Chisha, would it. Pulled a fast one on us, huh... But if that's how it is, then—not very like His Excellency, is it, gettin' walked all over like this?"

"You believe your eyes can see through to the bottom of my heart?"

"Oh-ho, scary, scary."

Olbart waved both hands, a dry chuckle rasping in his throat at Abel's answer.
In truth, just as Olbart said, Abel's situation really was one of being thoroughly beaten down and groggy—but this was an overwhelming bluff that betrayed none of it.
But seeing Olbart's expression soften, the tension drained from Subaru's shoulders as well.
Olbart had grasped the situation.
That Abel had been driven from the imperial throne, and that the Vincent he himself had been accompanying was a fake emperor.

"All sorts of things that were tangled up in my head just clicked into place. Yeah, I did find it strange, wonderin' why you'd suddenly come to meet the fox girl in person."

"That's... because it was all to get ahead of our moves and shut us down first."

"Ha-hah, an old man just can't keep up with the thinkin' of you smart folks, honestly. Young, clever, and handsome—ain't that just greedy of him? Don't you think so too? No? Well, you're young yourself, ain't ya. Ah, but that's my doin', ain't it! Kah-kah-kah!"

Olbart roared with laughter, trying to wring a laugh out of a remark that wasn't funny at all.
Subaru's cheek twitched at the sight, and letting out a long breath, he glanced over at Taritta.
The Shudrak warrior still had her wary gaze and bow trained on Olbart. He meant to get her to stand down and reset the situation.

"Taritta-san, you can lower your bow now. Olbart-san is—"

"Huh? Nah, no need for her to lower it, y'know? I mean, droppin' her guard in front of somebody who ain't confirmed as not-an-enemy—that ain't somethin' that missy could do anyhow."

"Huh?"

The one who stopped Subaru, as he moved to have her stand down and talk things through, was none other than Olbart himself—the very man Taritta's bow was aimed at.
The old man treated Taritta's wariness as only natural, and made no move to deny that "natural" thing Subaru found so hard to understand. —In other words, his own danger hadn't lessened one bit.
But that would mean—

"Didn't you hear us out..."

"I did, didn't I? Said I'd decide after hearin' ya out. And so I did."

"——"

Subaru's throat tightened, but Olbart's demeanor before him didn't change one bit.
Only a quiet click of the tongue sounded.

"You were a card whose play I could not predict, but so this is how you move."

"Not like you, Your Excellency. Nah, truth is, bein' plain outta cards was the fatal thing here. ...And on top of that, no luck at all that it's me who drew this hand. There's somethin' I always wanted to try, just once."

"Oh? And what is that?"

Olbart's course was set, and the Shinobi chief laughed at the words of the ousted emperor.
He sneered, true to his title of "the Wicked Elder."

"For an old geezer's last blaze of glory before he goes, I always wanted to try assassinatin' an emperor, just once."

"—!"

The instant that wicked wish was spoken, movement erupted out of the taut tension.
In that split second, trampling the rule of "no combat" each of them had been given, Taritta, Al, and Medium all moved as one to thwart Olbart's purpose.
But—

"Yesterday, you were the one whose trick I couldn't figure, one-arm. But shrunk down like this, bet it's rough havin' your body not move the way you want it to, ain't it."

"Guh...!"

"Techniques ya learn after gettin' on in years, ya can't use so well when you're young, y'know."

So he said, and Olbart, twisting his neck, flicked both hands.
Blood scattered from his fingertips, and before Subaru's widening eyes, a massacre unfolded.
Al, who'd lunged in to try to seize Olbart, and Medium—both of their throats were gouged open, and blood burst forth. It sprayed through the room like a fountain, so much of it that Subaru wanted to ask where a small child's body could possibly have held it all.

"Guh, ah...!"

"Looks like the one I was countin' on ain't gonna be much use like this. Still, doin' that before I explained the trick was a bit much—my bad, my bad."

Al pressed a hand to his gushing wound and crumpled where he stood. Medium's eyes rolled back white, and she toppled backward, going still.
It was plain that the wounds the two of them had suffered were fatal, beyond any hope of recovery.
And Taritta, the only one who'd truly been prepared to face Olbart head-on—

"—Ah."

It wasn't that Subaru wondered why the arrow hadn't been loosed.
But a nagging doubt, that an attack which should have come never did, turned his gaze in her direction.
Taritta had been pinned to the wall.
Taritta, tall for a woman, had her slender frame propped against the wall, her body held fast in place. The reason was a thrown weapon—a shuriken—buried between the twin peaks of her chest.
As if it were some cruel joke, the star-shaped throwing weapon had pierced clean through Taritta's chest, out her back, and into the wall, pinning her there. It had destroyed her heart—instant death, unmistakably.

"—So even the logic forbidding you to raise a hand does not reach you."

Suddenly, a man's voice rang out in the room now steeped in death.
It was Abel who spoke, standing in a room that had turned into a field of corpses with terrifying speed. Having risen from his seat, the man looked down on Olbart, who had built this death, with a piercing, clear-eyed gaze.
Under that gaze, Olbart wiped away the blood that had splashed onto his cheek with a finger.

"That's right. Before I die, I wanted to set off one big firework. Assassinatin' the emperor said to be the greatest in history, and gettin' executed for it after—that'd be plenty satisfyin' for me."

"That destructive taste of yours, I failed to foresee. A misstep on my part, perhaps."

"Kah-kah-kah! Nah, nah, if ya'd seen through an old man's dyin' dream like that, I'd have been too embarrassed to keep on livin' anyhow."

Al had gone still, and the breath of both Medium and Taritta had stopped as well.
The exchange between emperor and subject, carried on amid all this, felt to Subaru like something from another world. But it wasn't another world. It was reality. Reality, overwhelming reality.
It was the reality Subaru had arrived at, in the end, through a mistaken choice.

"Once I do His Excellency in, I'll head straight back to Chisha's side. That Chisha, well, he'll see to it my death's handled nice and proper, I reckon."

"W-wait—!"

"Hm? Oh, right, you were still here, weren't ya."

Olbart, who'd already slipped into the mood of finishing things off, turned at Subaru's far-too-late cry.
The old man cracked his neck at the sight of Subaru, face rigid, trying to stop him, then pointed in turn at Al and the others lying fallen.

"See, these two came at me first, so this was just me turnin' the tables. But you, there ain't really any need for you to die, is there? I ain't plannin' on makin' excuses or nothin', and there's no need to silence ya either."

"Th-uh..."

"Ah, one of them things ya just blurt out on impulse, huh. Figure out what you're gonna say before ya open your mouth, boy. Though, bein' an old geezer with not much time left, I can't wait around too long, can I?"

Olbart kept talking on, flatly, at his own pace, and Subaru's mind went white. —No, his mind was dyed a deep red. Whether that was rage, or the color of the blood Al and the others had shed, even Subaru himself couldn't say for certain.
But even so, whatever the case, in any event, never, not here—

"—Fool."

It was Abel who spat that out, contemptuously.
His gaze, surely, was fixed on the back of Subaru's head right now. Feeling the pressure of that sharp gaze, Subaru felt keenly, painfully, just how right those words were.
Standing in front of Abel now, as if to shield him, was utterly meaningless at this point.
If he'd been able to move like this before Al and the others fell, maybe something could have changed—but—

"Grrr...!"

"Now, now, even the little missy who's got nothin' to do with me's joinin' in? His Excellency's methods were always the type women and children hated, I thought."

Olbart put a hand to his forehead. Rui stood before him. —No, Rui hadn't taken her stand before Olbart, but before Subaru.
Subaru shielded Abel, and Rui shielded Subaru, the three of them lined up front to back.
It was an utterly ridiculous shield of flesh.

"I was sure His Excellency would die alone, y'know."

"—. No one is bound to fit within the limits of your estimation."

"Kah-kah-kah!"

Even at a moment like this, Abel's mouth showed no sign of slowing, and Olbart laughed loud and long at his reply.
In the old Shinobi's eyes, neither Subaru nor Rui truly registered as anything. But he wasn't kind enough to overlook someone who stood before him showing open hostility, either.
And so—

"—I will never forgive you."

Burning into his eyes the face of the Shinobi, sneering wickedly as he sought to satisfy his own ambition, Natsuki Subaru's small, childish body sprayed blood and sank into a pool that felt cold to the touch.
And then—


△▼△▼△▼△


Blood flowed, a hot sensation gushing from the wound.
The heat that poured out endlessly stole the warmth from his body in an instant, all the same.
And what flowed out like that swallowed him up in no time at all, drowned him, blinded him, and sank him down, down, all the way to the very bottom, and beyond that, beyond—

"——"

In an instant, the blood he'd lost coursed through his body again, roaring noisily in his ears.
His hearing honed to its very limit, he could sense, in the quiet space, the sound of blood coursing through him. Struck through his whole body by that sensation, Subaru's breathing turned ragged.
Ragged, his vision flickering with the receding pain and sense of loss, he clenched his back teeth.
To confirm just where it was he had returned to—

"I went ahead and made myself some tea, hope ya don't mind. Anyone else want some?"

In that instant, that unforgettable, wicked voice announced the start of Natsuki Subaru's new battle.
