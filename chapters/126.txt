Arc 3 Chapter 49: "Beast of the End"

—Blood, vomit, tears — hadn't he wrung every last drop of them from his body by now?

Just how much more was he supposed to cry?
Just how much more was he supposed to suffer?

Had he really done something so unforgivable? What about his conduct had displeased *them* so much? Why wouldn't they just put him out of his misery already?

He'd been wounded, had his heart trampled, had someone precious to him stolen away, had failed to save the people he was supposed to protect, and with these very hands had scattered the life of the person most important to him in a wretched, senseless waste.

—Whose punishment was this supposed to be, exactly?

"I... was..."

He'd been wrong. He'd misjudged everything. He'd let it go to his head.
He'd thought that because he'd managed to turn the Witch's Curse stuck fast to his soul to his advantage once, that made it something he could use. The reckless notion that he could die and come back had only encouraged that thinking, and it had led him to underestimate the Witch — that thing he should have loathed and feared.
The accumulation of all that had brought him to this: the wreckage in front of him now.

Collapsed on the floor, Emilia across his lap, Subaru let his hollow eyes drift.

How long had it been since Emilia — since her life — had been lost? The body he touched had gone cold at some point without his noticing, and the bright blood she had spat onto him had long since lost its heat, chilled through.

Everywhere on that soft body was beginning to stiffen, forcing an understanding onto Subaru that what lay before him was now a reality with nothing left to deny.

And still — even understanding that — no reason surfaced in him for his emotions to stir.

He was already so tired. He'd suffered this much already, so wasn't that enough? How many people in this world, and where, had ever suffered as much as Subaru?

Compared to who he used to be, he'd tried harder than he ever thought possible, worked himself to the bone trying to make things right. And still the worst had come to pass, disaster mocking him at every turn.

If nothing he did mattered, if it was all futile, all meaningless, then wasn't it fine to just end it here—


"—You look like you want to say you're the unluckiest man in the world, I suppose."


The voice rang out looking down on Subaru where he sat dumbstruck, its tone making no attempt to hide its contempt.

In a room that should have held no one else, Subaru lifted his head at what sounded like an auditory hallucination and turned his gaze toward the doorway.

Slowly, with maddening sluggishness, his neck turned. And there, once his eyes finally found their focus, a girl stood watching him.

A beautiful girl with long cream-colored hair done up in two great rolled curls, wearing an opulent dress fit for a Western porcelain doll.

She lived in this mansion — and through all the repeated days until now, she was the last one whose face he hadn't seen, despite her supposedly being somewhere in the house the whole time.

"Beatorisu..."

"In the little while I haven't seen you, your spineless face has gone and gotten even more spineless, I suppose."

Biting the words out sharply, the girl — Beatrice — crossed her slender arms, tilted her chin up slightly, and surveyed the carnage of the room. The blood-soaked floor, Subaru, and Emilia sunk in the sea of blood —

"Well, you've certainly made quite the spectacle of yourself, I suppose."

She appraised the scene with a sigh, terribly matter-of-fact about it.

Was that really all she felt, looking at unmoving Emilia, at Subaru holding her with hollow eyes?

Even that faint spark of resentment failed to rise in Subaru now.

He, who was the very author of this catastrophe, had no right to be seized by righteous anger, and giving himself over to a fit of rage would do nothing but add to his exhaustion.

In that sense, he was almost grateful that Beatrice's current attitude wasn't one of flushed, furious interrogation. And it would have been better still, gratitude aside, if she'd simply leave him crushed here alone.

"Niicha, can't come out, I take it?"

As she spoke, the girl who had walked right up beside him bent her knees. Small as she was, crouching down like that brought her head to almost the same height as Subaru's, seated as he was.

The girl looked at Subaru, unmoving, his eyes hollow, and pierced his profile with the same contemptuous gaze as before.

"Telling you to look for it wouldn't do any good, I suppose. ...Not that Ram really wants to get her hands dirty, in truth, I suppose."

Muttering it quietly, reluctantly, Beatrice reached her hand toward Emilia, lying faceup. *Was she going to do something for dead Emilia?* Subaru wondered dully, watching as her small hand slid smoothly into the folds of Emilia's clothing.

Her hand rummaged around inside, and from her motions there was no trace of anything magical about it. She was, quite simply, going through Emilia's body.

There was an unspeakable ugliness to the act, and Subaru opened his mouth to rebuke her for it. But his parched lips only let out a rasping breath, refusing to form anything meaningful. And heedless of Subaru's struggle,

"Found it, I suppose."

Beatrice withdrew her hand from the clothing and turned her palm upward, where a crystal glinted green.

Subaru recognized it — one of the crystal stones Emilia carried. The one used as an Anchor by the spirit under contract with her — Puck — the one Emilia never let leave her side.

That crystal stone, now—

"Broken..."

"Bold of the one who broke it to say that... though it seems you're not even aware you did, I suppose."

Beatrice looked down sadly at the stone, split clean in two on her palm, and let it drop to the floor. It made a light sound, sinking into the blood on the floor below. The green light that had once sparkled was drenched in the color of fresh blood, its dulled glow fading away before long.

—What happened to the broken crystal stone, and to the spirit that should have been inside it?

What had become of that spirit, who called the girl now sleeping in Subaru's arms his "daughter," who loved her more than anyone else in the world? What was he thinking, right now?

"No need to worry, niicha's not going to die, I suppose. He's just lost his Anchor, so it'll take him a little time to come over here, is all, I suppose. ...Though there isn't much time to spare, I suppose."

Answering Subaru's unspoken question as though it were only natural, Beatrice let her long hair fall down her back. Watching her drill curls bounce out of the corner of his eye, Subaru felt relief.

If that spirit was alive — if he said he was coming here — then surely...

"—Is there something you'd like to say, I suppose?"

Subaru let out a breath at that horribly out-of-place sense of relief. Keeping her eyes level with his, Beatrice asked him in a calm, gentle voice.

The composure in that voice left Subaru puzzled that she seemed to feel nothing about the situation at all. But he didn't feel like putting that into words.

Mumbling, moving his mouth, what Subaru's lips wove was not that inscrutable question about Beatrice, but something more purely, selfishly his own—

"Kill me."

Presuming on her kindness, clinging to it, trampling her goodwill underfoot — that was the sum of the words.

He wanted to be killed, right now.

He was sick of everything already. Exhausted by every last thing. Abandoned by all of it. There was no hope left in this world anymore.

He wanted, more than anything, to die. To die and put an end to everything. Even if he died and started over, surely he'd only meet the same end again. Whether he died and started over or couldn't start over at all, at the very least he no longer wanted to be in this world.

Emilia was dead. Rem had been erased from existence. Nothing Subaru did was ever rewarded.

So—

"Kill me... please..."

An ending was the only salvation left to Subaru now.

Not if hating what deserved to be hated, not if acting for the sake of someone he wanted to protect, could serve as an excuse to keep his own heart alive any longer.

If there was some request they'd grant him, then let them peck away at this hopeless life of his.

Let them burn away and destroy this pitiful, foolish body that had trampled the dignity of every life it touched, rendered every feeling meaningless, and been abandoned by everything.

The girl in front of him, with her preternatural power, ought to be able to do it.

She had probably hated Subaru all along, and if a girl like that were willing to grant his wish, surely she would give him a miserable end, one fitting for his folly.

The fool named Subaru hadn't changed a thing, dying nine times over.

This made ten. A nice round number. A fine time for gods, buddhas, goddesses, and witches alike to wash their hands of him. So—

"Kill me. Here."

Cradling Emilia's corpse, Subaru begged Beatrice.

If this was to be the end, he wanted it to be while still holding her body. To the very last, Subaru's selfishness, having had its way and brought about the worst outcome, remained selfish still.

Tightening his arms around her, he closed his eyes and waited for the end.

And then — how long did the silence fall, he wondered.

It rang out suddenly at Subaru, who had let himself sink, alone, selfishly, into that placid stretch of time.

"...I suppose."

"—Huh?"

It was a small, weak, hoarse voice.

Startled, Subaru opened his closed eyes and looked up at the girl. The girl who had been crouched beside him now stood, looking down at him.

Hugging her own small body with both arms, as though she were cold, she said—

"Asking Betty to kill you... that's just... too cruel, I suppose..."

Said with a face and voice on the verge of tears, Subaru found himself utterly at a loss.

He blinked, again and again, waiting for the girl's expression standing beside him to be repainted into something else. But no matter how many times he did, the deep, tragic cast of her face didn't change.

It was unlike any expression of hers Subaru had ever known.

Because she had always hated him, had always been curt with him, and though sometimes a soft-hearted streak that couldn't quite disregard his feelings had shown through, he'd thought of her, fundamentally, as someone who could be thoroughly cold-blooded.

Even if he hadn't expected her to accept it readily, even if he'd thought she might refuse — he'd imagined that refusal would come laced with scorn and mockery.

"You don't understand anything... you don't understand anything at all, I suppose...!"

He hadn't imagined that being asked to kill Subaru would be met with a rejection carrying this much sorrow.

"B-Beatrice...?"

"Betty's not granting a single one of your wishes, I suppose. If you want to die, go die on your own, I suppose... Betty declines, I suppose."

Shaking her head, Beatrice closed both eyes, killing her expression. The tears that had welled up didn't fall, hidden away instead deep behind her eyes, and then, as the girl raised her arms—

"The scenery... !?"

—began to warp.

Subaru saw the space around him distort, cracks spreading through it. It felt like some omen of the world crumbling apart, and out of a sudden helplessness, he found himself hugging the cold body in his arms tighter.

Looking down at him, Beatrice murmured "Pathetic," her voice drained of warmth, and—

"Everything's already hopeless anyway, but it'd be a problem if you stayed here, I suppose. —At the very least, let me keep this mansion safe, I suppose."

"What are you... no, Beatrice, you're—"

"—Betty's not like Roswaal, I suppose. Even to gain the future, pain, suffering, agony, sorrow, fear — Betty hates all of it, I suppose."

To a question that wasn't quite a question, an answer came that wasn't quite an answer.

But Subaru couldn't form any words for the raw grief packed into hers, and his body was swept up into the twisting distortion of warped space. —There was no pain.

"At the very least, Betty wants you to die somewhere she can't see, I suppose."

Her final murmur was cloaked in feigned indifference, but carried with it a terribly lonely smile.

He could say nothing, understand nothing. There was only one thing that got through to him.

—Beatrice was grieving over Subaru's decision, over what he had done.

The distortion reached its peak, and the crumpled space vanished as though it had burst.

A wrongness like static across his vision swept over the world for the barest instant, and immediately after, no trace of the warped air remained anywhere.

All that was left was a gouged-out patch, floor and blood alike, where Subaru and Emilia had been — the only remnant of the two who had disappeared.

Standing beside the spot where the two of them had vanished, Beatrice pressed a hand to her forehead as though exhausted, then covered her eyes with that same hand, hiding herself from the world.

"—Mother, how much longer must Betty..."

—What caught Subaru, flung out from the rift in space, was a heap of moss-covered brush and undergrowth.

"Bwauh!"

Face-first into it without warning, he tasted grass in his mouth and lifted his head. Hastily spitting out dirt, leaves, and an insect that had caught between his front teeth—

"Ugheh, bleh, haah... where is..."

Shaking off the revulsion of a bug crawling across his cheek, Subaru looked around, brow furrowing.

What filled his view was a cluster of dim trees, and turning his head in a full circle, he found no sign of a break in their ranks anywhere. Which meant—

"A forest... a forest at night..."

Fortunately the moonlight wasn't blocked, letting him barely make out his surroundings.

Wind stroking the leaves, a world ruled by the cries of insects — this could only be a forest steeped in twilight dark. Thinking that far, Subaru realized he must have used up a full half-day between then and waking up in the mansion.

At the same time—

"Spatial transference... is that the right way to think of it?"

The air had warped, and he'd been thrown into the resulting rift and cast out into the forest.

Unless this was some backyard garden stage hidden within the mansion itself, it was probably safe to assume he'd been transported somewhere far away. If Beatrice was the one who'd done it, then she was someone with the power to connect the door of her own library to any other room she pleased, as she saw fit.

But even understanding that much, her true intentions remained a mystery to him.

She had refused Subaru's plea to be killed, and instead forced him through spatial transference out of the mansion. —Even now, that face of hers on the verge of tears wouldn't leave his mind.

If she'd meant to refuse, he'd assumed she'd simply leave him be, unwilling even to look at him. And yet she had looked at him almost as though — disappointed.

"That's like..."

As though he'd been expected of something.

Too self-centered, too self-serving a way of thinking, Subaru shook his head to deny it.

He already understood, already accepted, that he was a plague god who could do nothing. The world was hopelessly merciless, and every person he'd tried to save met a wretched end, one after another.

Knowing that, unable even to stop it, what was there in Subaru worth expecting anything of?

If he couldn't expect anything of himself, there was no way anyone else could expect anything of him.

Much less demanding it of someone who was supposed to hate him — that was arrogance, plain and simple.

"There's just no helping me, is there..."

A ragged smile rose to his lips, and Subaru slowly drew his knees up. But it took a beat longer than expected for his body to respond, and he noticed, far too late, the absurdity of the situation.

—On his lap, just as in that room in the mansion, lay Emilia's corpse.

"Emi... ria..."

In the dark world, her pale face glowed faintly in the moonlight.

The death mask on her wasn't one of agony or peace — it was filled with nothing but bewilderment, as though she still didn't understand what had happened. She had, while still alive, in a world where time had stopped, had her heart crushed without warning. It was doubtful she'd even had time to register the pain.

And yet, even if she hadn't felt pain, that didn't feel like any kind of salvation.

Subaru had little experience with instant death, but he had far more experience with dying than any ordinary person — nine times over. He didn't want to believe any of those deaths had been peaceful ones, and he didn't think death could ever be a salvation, either. —Except, perhaps, for himself, right now.

"I'm sorry. I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I'm really... sorry."

As he looked down at her face, a drop of water fell onto her white cheek.

The tears he'd thought had run dry welled up again, once more forcing an endless torment onto Subaru.

Voices. He could hear voices. Voices condemning him. Everyone he'd ever met, hurling abuse at him, their anger cold as ice.

Among them was a girl with silver hair, and a girl with blue hair, too—

"Someone... anyone... please..."

—Kill me.

Bathed in unceasing curses, Subaru gathered Emilia into both arms and rose to his feet.

He began walking, treading through grass, pushing through branches, slowly into the night forest.

Far off, he heard the howl of a beast ring out.

Right now, he felt like he could welcome meeting that black, horned demon hound with a smile. He wanted it to devour his flesh and blood, his mana, down to the last scrap, and kill him.

A clean death like Emilia's — he didn't deserve anything like that now.

He wanted to be killed brutally, trampled until he lost even the shape of a human being, even his dignity.

Because if it wasn't like that, if it didn't happen that way, then Natsuki Subaru could never be saved.

As he walked through the forest, Subaru noticed something.

"Here... cross this ditch, and then..."

He carefully made his way down a slope, using a jutting root like a stairstep to descend. The handicap of not being able to use his arms ate into his stamina more than he expected, but for some reason, he didn't feel tired.

His body, pushed past the limits of its endurance, had stopped screaming, and his worn-down mind had honed itself into sharp focus. His trek through the night dark, using only his legs, was unbroken; his sight, which should have failed to make out anything more than a few meters ahead, was as clear as if a light had been cast on it.

That wasn't only because Subaru's body was spending its last strength like a flame just before it burns out. Simply put — this was a path he recognized.

There, up ahead—

"Ah. There you are."

A relieved smile, born of some misplaced sentiment, rose to his lips.

It was the kind of mad grin only a person caked in blood, their mind worn to nothing, could wear. A baleful smile that anyone shown it would have flinched from in disgust and chosen to flee.

But those it was shown to now were people well accustomed to that mad grin.

—Surrounding Subaru was a group cloaked head to toe in black garments that blended into the darkness.

Rising up as though welling out of the forest's shadows, they encircled Subaru, silent, soundless, their very presence dimmed to almost nothing, and simply stared at him.

Bathed in that gaze — devoid of hostility or goodwill, malice or benevolence, devoid of any will at all — Subaru recalled the world in which he'd first encountered them.

Yes, that time—

"...Same as before."

As though tracing the recollected image, the black-robed figures all lowered their heads at once.

It was "reverence" — something these people, devoid of any will of their own, showed to Subaru for the first time.

Why such an emotion was directed at him, Subaru didn't understand in detail. What was clear, at least, was that they were followers of the Witch Cult, and that the darkness Subaru wore had some connection to that Witch of theirs.

"—Out of my way."

There had been, truthfully, so much he wanted to ask, he thought.

Before he'd resigned himself to all of this, there had been mountains of things he wanted to demand answers to. But now, even that kind of sentiment was nothing but useless baggage.

At Subaru's brief command, the black-robed figures raised no objection, sliding back and sinking away into the darkness without a sound.

Once their presence vanished from view, the already-soundless world seemed to fill with an even deeper silence.

He realized, then, that he could no longer hear the cry of insects or the sound of the wind.

Was it that every living thing loathed them? Even in the world before, whenever the Witch Cult appeared, he recalled not hearing so much as a single insect's cry.

Or perhaps it meant that Subaru's presence alongside the Witch Cult was itself something to be despised. —That, he thought, felt like a more fitting assessment of him now.

The obstacle that wasn't really an obstacle vanished, but thanks to it, Subaru gained a certainty.

From his stride, already thin on hesitation, that hesitation vanished entirely, and Subaru's advance, crossing roots and treading earth, didn't stop.

His walk eventually opened out of the forest, arriving at a rocky outcrop where sheer cliffs continued on.

—There was a cave there, its path blocked by a great boulder,


"We have been awaiting you, dess. Beloved believer."

A gaunt man greeted him, wearing the same mad grin as Subaru's own kin.

"Oh-ho? And what's this what's this what's this what's this whatwhatwhatwhatwhat, could that possibly be, in your arms... a half-devil girl, dess?"

Treading over the grass, Petelgeuse tilted his head at the sight of Emilia in Subaru's arms. Tilting his head until it lay level with the ground, he stuck out his tongue in delight, drool spilling from it.

"My my, to lose her life even before receiving our trial... what dreadful misfortune! What terrible calamity! Ahh! And and and... how diligent of you! Before even we could act! The half-devil's body! Her life! You've already taken it from her, dess!"

Waving his arms, Petelgeuse shrieked out Emilia's death in an exaggerated display.

Before Subaru knew it, believers had come crawling out of the cave one after another, surrounding them, all listening intently, straining their ears toward Petelgeuse's mad ravings.

Insane. All of it.

"I'm... diligent?"

"Yes! That's right, dess! Diligent! Ahh, how wonderful a thing, dess! Unlike Myself, slow to judge, dull of wit, lacking the resolve to act — you were the very first to embody the Witch's will, dess!"

Catching Subaru's hoarse mutter, Petelgeuse laughed joyfully and rushed toward him, dropping to his knees in a smooth motion and slamming both hands into the dirt with all his might, shouting "And compare that to me!"—

"How slow, how foolish, how lacking Myself and these fingers of Mine are, dess! Ahh! Forgive me! Forgive this body unable to repay Love! This unfaithful body of Sloth! This deficient one unable to answer the Love bestowed upon him! Please, please forgive me...!"

Weeping, Petelgeuse slammed his own arms against the rock face, opening wounds.

Blood scattered from the violent self-harm, and even with bone visible through the torn flesh of his wrists, his mad act didn't stop. The believers around him, rather than stopping him, followed his lead and knelt as well, each beginning to show their gratitude to the being they revered in their own way.

Watching the madness of these lunatics, Subaru felt nothing.

Before — even just a day ago — Subaru would have thrown caution to the wind and charged to take Petelgeuse's life right then.

But now, seeing the man he should have hated standing right in front of him, no emotion rose in him at all, nor did it change what he wanted.

"Ahh, in place of this deficient self, unable to answer that Person's feelings, what can I do for you, who accomplished the trial? Please, tell me, dess. What can I do to prove to Love that I am not slothful, dess?"

"Kill me."

To Petelgeuse, closing in on him, weeping, pleading, Subaru said it quietly.

At the abrupt words thrown at him, even Petelgeuse, of all people, wore something like shock on his face for—

"Is that all, dess?"

—not even a moment, before shoving Subaru back without a second's hesitation.

Watching Subaru stumble and stagger backward, Petelgeuse's expression melted into rapture, and—

"Ahh, how splendid, how splendid splendid splendid splendid! To grant, without delay, salvation and Love to a believer who has completed the trial and seeks salvation — how diligent of Me, dess! Ahh, I need not become Sloth after all, dess! My thanks to you! Love, to My diligence!"

With no doubt whatsoever about Subaru's despairing answer, with not a shred of hesitation over his own actions, with not the slightest tremor in the order of this world, Petelgeuse unleashed his murderous intent.

Watching the lunatic before him, Subaru felt something stir uneasily within him and closed his eyes.

—This way, at least, in this moment, Subaru's wish would be granted. So—

"Still, though."

As Subaru felt the approach of death against his skin, Petelgeuse muttered something.

"Unable to overcome even a single trial, unable even to face one of the Great Sins, tripping over the very first stone despite harboring such great ambitions..."

That was—

"—Ahh, you truly are 'Sloth,' dess!"

—that was an insult hurled at Emilia's death.

"————"

The instant Subaru opened his eyes, he saw a palm-shaped black mist bearing down on him.

For a moment, the memory of that agony flashing through his mind made his heart shrivel in terror. But one thing was different from that time. His body could move. His legs could move. His arms could move. So he could dodge.

Slipping past the wavering black palm closing in on him, Subaru leapt to the side, Emilia still in his arms. The overshooting palm vanished as though bewildered, and Subaru, tracking it with his eyes, let out a ragged breath. And then—

"...Are you, by any chance, seeing the Unseen Hand right now, dess?"

In a trembling voice, prying his gleaming, glaring eyes open wide, Petelgeuse stared at Subaru.

He shoved his withered-branch-thin fingers into his mouth and bit down on each fingertip in turn. With each one, flesh burst and bone cracked with a sickening sound, blood dripping down as—

"No good. No good, dess. It's wrong, it's mistaken, it's an error, it's a fault. My Authority — the Authority of 'Sloth' — My 'Unseen Hand,' granted to me through Beloved's favor! For another to see it — that cannot be permitted, dess!!"

Spitting blood, chewing the fragments of nail and bone left in his mouth, Petelgeuse turned his bloodshot eyes on Subaru.

There, unmistakable murderous intent gleamed, drawing a clear line from the fanatical emotion he'd shown until now. What was contained in it now was purely, simply, murderous intent born of hatred for the existence that was Subaru.

—In an instant, black arms gushed forth, welling up from behind Petelgeuse.

Petelgeuse's shadow split apart, and the resulting palms numbered as many as seven. They looked exactly like the cursed arm that appeared whenever Subaru tried to reveal his Return by Death, and the sight of them sent a chill of terror down Subaru's spine.

But—

"If I can see them..."

—dodging them wasn't impossible.

The black arms weren't especially fast. Their reach and the sheer strength to tear a human body apart were the truly fearsome parts, but what was fundamentally terrifying about them was the part that "couldn't be seen."

And that part had no purchase on Subaru now — Subaru, who, as the burnt-out ash of a life spent to its last ember, was exhibiting abilities beyond the ordinary.

"Why why why why whywhywhywhywhy whywhywhy...! How are you dodging them, dess!? Can you see them, dess!? This is Mine, entirely Mine——!!"

"I just got sick of the idea of being killed by you."

Twisting his body to dodge a palm, leaping forward to evade another finger reaching up at him. He crouched immediately to avoid arms closing in from both sides, then rolled straight into close range with Petelgeuse.

Watching the man's madness twist into shock filled him with satisfaction, a dark pleasure roaring in triumph.

—He remembered. That he had once meant to kill this man.

"—Bugah!"

Leaping up with all his momentum, he drove his forehead into Petelgeuse's face in a headbutt. Petelgeuse, caught completely off guard, reeled back with blood scattering, and Subaru leapt backward too, a sharp pain lancing through his own forehead.

Watching the palms lose their precision and thrash wildly, his vision reddened where blood dripped from his forehead. His front teeth must have connected, splitting the skin. The gaudy bleeding filled his eyes, blinding his right eye for an instant.

—And in that gap, Subaru's leg was seized and he was thrown, using the grip as a pivot point.

In the moment he was slammed against a great tree, Subaru, rather than curling up to brace himself, instead held Emilia's body — still cradled in his arms — tightly.

Not clinging to her. Protecting her.

"—Goeh!"

He struck the tree square in the back with full force, feeling his spine give.

Several ribs shattered at once, and a number of his freshly-healed wounds tore open all at once, a chorus of agony rising in unison, and Subaru convulsed on the ground like an insect where he fell.

"How pathetic! How utterly pathetic, is it not, dess! Ahh, good. Truly good. If I had remained 'Sloth' as I was, all My deeds would have come to nothing, dess. Ahh, indeed I must remain diligent, in service of Love..."

"Shut... up. Idiot..."

To the lunatic trying to laugh loudly, proclaiming his own superiority, Subaru hurled a curse.

His breathing sounded wrong; he could feel the massive damage to his lungs. The pains in his gut all clamored to claim first place, and his consciousness flickered white.

Even so, blood trickling from the corner of his mouth, Subaru sneered.

"What Love, you idiot. That Love you're supposed to have gotten... I can see it too, can't I. ...Cheated on. Serves you right."

"What are... saying... saying, saying, saying sayingsayingsayingsayingsaying... my brain, my brain is shakingakingakingaking!"

Foam bubbling at the corners of his lips, Petelgeuse flew into a rage.

He strode up right beside Subaru and kicked the still-cradled Emilia, driving his foot into her corpse to send it flying as far from Subaru as possible.

His fingers wouldn't obey him, and Subaru couldn't hold on to Emilia as she was kicked away.

Rolling, coming to a stop against the roots of the trees — watching her body out of the corner of his eye, Petelgeuse laughed coldly and said,

"I will not permit an insult to My Love, dess! Ahh, I have decided, I have decided, dess! The half-devil who was to receive the trial has already died, but the sin remains of those who shamelessly harbored and elevated that half-devil, dess!"

Screaming, venting an anger that was nothing but a tantrum, Petelgeuse ground Subaru beneath him. Stamping through his palm with a heel, a black hand seized Subaru's throat just as he tried to scream.

His breath cut off, his upper body wrenched up by strength enough to tear his very neck from his body, Subaru, the core bones of his body already ruined, couldn't even cry out through the agony.

Looking at Subaru like that seemed to give Petelgeuse some measure of satisfaction, and he said,

"First, we shall exterminate all those connected to the mansion, then offer up the lives of the nearby villagers. Leaving nothing behind — any oversight is proof of 'Sloth.' Myself and these fingers, who hold diligence as the highest virtue, shall take everything, shall bring everything down. —The blockade of the highway, too, is already complete, dess! No one shall cross the mist to interfere, dess!"

Spraying spit in his excitement, Petelgeuse ranted on at Subaru.

Then he said, "But before that," breathing his rank breath onto Subaru, and,

"You seemed to be holding her rather preciously... I wonder, if I destroy that half-devil's body, how lovely a voice she'll cry out with, dess?"

Tilting his head, twisting his lips, his eyes gleaming with curiosity, Petelgeuse gestured. From behind him, five arms separate from the one choking Subaru rose up, each moving independently, converging on Emilia's corpse.

Each one seizing a limb, and her throat, in its palm—

"Can you see it, dess? Do you understand what's about to happen, dess?"

"...top... it...!"

The terror of *seeing* it was what struck Subaru now.

When he hadn't been able to see it, he vividly remembered what that man's black arm had done to Rem's body. And now, that same thing was being turned toward Emilia's body.

Subaru's view of life and death had always been a parched one. A body that had lost its life was just a lump of flesh; there was no chance of a soul still dwelling within it. Heaven and hell surely didn't exist; once you died, only darkness waited. Living with that parched view of death, thinking himself cool for holding it, he'd never once changed his thinking on it, not even after coming to this world.

He regretted that, now.

Even if her soul no longer dwelled within her body, that body was still Emilia. There was no way her soul wouldn't cry out in grief at its violation.

And yet now, Subaru had no power to stop it, and Petelgeuse's mad grin only deepened further, when—

『—What do you think you're doing.』

That voice suddenly rained down from the heavens, descending upon the eardrums of everyone present.

"—!"

Petelgeuse's expression changed, his eyes darting about in search of the voice's source.

That was how much overwhelming force and cold, honed fury that voice carried, enough to freeze the very blood.

Sure enough, as his gaze swept around, Petelgeuse found it.

A beat later, Subaru too, still straining against the pressure on his throat, saw it through his blurred vision.

『I'll say it again.』

—A cat with ash-gray fur, trailing behind it an overwhelming mass of icicles that seemed to blanket the sky.

Its ragged breath showed white, and a chill that would freeze the whole world swept across the forest.

The black-robed figures, still kneeling, and Petelgeuse, still wearing his mad grin, all lost their words.


『What are you doing to my daughter — you scum.』


—The beast of the end, painting the world white, had come now to bring the tenth iteration of the world to its close.
