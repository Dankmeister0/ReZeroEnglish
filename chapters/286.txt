Arc 4 Chapter 117: "Love Letter"

In front of the tomb, with the rift between them and Garfiel settled and the shock of his true age now out in the open, things had reached something of a lull.
Having overcome the Trial that forced him to face his past, and having put an end to the friction between himself and Subaru's group, Garfiel now sat surrounded by the others, everyone gathered face-to-face for a talk.

"Still... a man-to-man showdown looks a lot different once it turns out it was actually a bunch of us ganging up to beat down a middle schooler. So, uh, how do you feel about that?"

"No, I was surprised by Garfiel's real age too, but there's no need to twist it around like that. The fact that the opponent we could only beat by joining forces and ganging up on him turned out to be fourteen doesn't really change the substance of what happened."

"That's right. For a wet-behind-the-ears brat of fourteen, Garf had more than enough power to make it no laughing matter, so there's no reason anyone should have anything to say about Ram and the others ganging up on him."

"The lot of you keep going on and on about 'ganging up' — shut it already! Fine then, wanna go another round? Huh? I'll take you on anytime!"

Garfiel spat as Subaru, Otto, and Ram put their heads together and nodded in agreement.
In fact, looking back on everything he'd said and done, and watching him now, stubbornly raising his voice like this, the fact that he was fourteen suddenly seemed easy enough to believe.
If anything, it was undeniable that having treated him as a peer all this time had actually put a filter over their ability to see things clearly.

"Wait, so how old were you when you went into the tomb before and took the Trial? That means you've been carrying that stubbornness of yours since then, huh."

"Ain't like I remember it exact... but probably three or four. Ain't got much of anything I remember clearly, 'cept for the Trial."

"Well, I suppose that would be the case. Three or four years old... that's around when I still thought the world was hell."

"Why are you suddenly dropping something that heavy? Stop it. I don't want to dig into that."

At Garfiel's reminiscence, Otto's smile took on a shadowed edge.
Otto no doubt had plenty of his own baggage, but digging into that and getting to the bottom of it right now was well past capacity. Even as things stood, Subaru's hands were already so full of burdens he couldn't hold them that he was propping the overflow up with his head and knees just to keep it all from spilling.

"So, is it alright if I hear the details?"

Once the round of banter had run its course, Emilia, her face tense, spoke up first.
Her gaze was fixed on Garfiel, and the true meaning of her question was, of course, about the Trial of the Tomb. But at that, Garfiel snorted,

"Details, huh. The past I saw and how I got past it — that's gotta be different from yours, right? Don't think I got anything useful to tell you."

"No, I know that. My Trial is mine alone. Asking you about it wouldn't help anyway."

"—? Then what is it you wanna ask?"

"After getting through the Trial in there... no, after getting past your own past — do you feel like you changed? Have you accepted the person you changed into?"

"............"

At Emilia's question, Garfiel narrowed his eyes and fell silent.
With the air growing faintly taut, Subaru and the others watching held their breath, waiting for his answer.

A brief silence. Garfiel touched the tip of his own nose, then traced the white scar on his forehead with a finger. Walking his finger along it, tracing the scar that ran vertically down his brow, once he finished,

"Whether I oughta call this 'changed' or call it 'took back'... even I ain't got a clear answer on that."

"Mm."

"This scar on my forehead — I gave it to myself. To wipe out a memory I hated."

Flicking his own forehead with a finger, Garfiel turned his gaze aside — toward Ram, who sat watching him from where she knelt beside him. At his words, Ram blinked her pale red eyes,

"Garf."

"Shut it, don't say nothin'. Makes me feel pathetic. Pinning the blame on someone else just to bury a memory I hated, and calling that good enough... ...Finding out after the fact that the other person knew and just played along makes it feel about a hundred times worse."

Ram watched the grumbling Garfiel with eyes that seemed to say there was nothing to be done about it.
The true meaning behind their exchange didn't quite reach Subaru, but he could dimly tell it was something that only made sense between the two of them.
And that between Garfiel and Ram there existed a bond as warm and certain as family.

"Anyway, whether I went back or whether I changed, I ain't plannin' on stayin' the same as before. You lot went and changed me with your own hands. So now, how you all change from here on... show me it ain't just talk."

"Okay. Then I'm glad. ...I'll do my best to live up to that expectation too."

Garfiel's face twisted, while Emilia, smiling, showed her resolve.
Subaru suddenly realized that, at least in terms of mental age, these two were actually the same age.

Both fourteen.
A situation where the future here had to be entrusted to a boy and girl at that sensitive, adolescent age.
Subaru himself, seventeen and on the verge of eighteen, was in no position to talk, but still — it had turned into a situation that felt awfully like something out of a manga or anime.

"...If I linger too long, it'll only dull my resolve."

Saying so, Emilia stood and brushed the grass from her hips.
Letting out a deep breath, her eyes now lit with a fierce light, what Emilia fixed her gaze on was the tomb — the chamber of the Trial.

"Shall we go?"

"Yes, let's go. ...I'll follow after Garfiel, and then I'll overtake him."

"You think you can?"

"I'll do it. I've decided not to be afraid of changing anymore."

To both Subaru's question and Garfiel's, Emilia answered with a nod.
Subaru rose too, falling in beside Emilia as she stepped toward the front of the tomb. Even if he couldn't go inside and hold her hand beside her, he resolved to stay close until the moment she set off.

"Emilia-sama."

Ram suddenly called out from behind Emilia as she walked.
Emilia stopped and turned, and before her Ram bowed — pinching the edge of her skirt, solemnly, exactly as a servant would to show respect to a superior.

"Well, there's no 'exactly as' about it — Emilia-tan really is her mistress, after all."

"You're muttering too loud, Barusu. This isn't the moment for that — know your place."

Ram scolded Subaru's mutter sharply. Then, to Emilia, who was watching her with wide eyes, she composed herself once more and bowed again,

"Please forgive my rudeness. If I'm honest, Ram thought that Emilia-sama might not be able to stand."

"...Right. I'm sorry, for being so unreliable."

"Yes, you were unreliable, and it was hard to watch."

"Hey now."

Ram tore into the apologizing Emilia as if this were a golden opportunity.
Did she have any idea how much courage it had taken Subaru to say that to Emilia inside the tomb? Gritting his teeth at how easily Ram cleared a hurdle he had fought so desperately to get over, he watched to see where her words were headed.

"But Emilia-sama stood up, and decided to face the challenge. Whether that came from a fighting spirit, or whether deep down it was an escape, hardly matters."

"............"

"Ram had decided. Whether or not Emilia-sama showed us the sight of facing the Trial — Ram would entrust her own problem to that. If you fled into resignation, Ram too would simply follow the flow of the world. But if you chose to resist—"

Ram glanced briefly toward Subaru.
Where did Subaru fit into that pause in her words? Perhaps that was where the answer lay — to why she had sided with Subaru's group and fought against Garfiel this time.

"Please, go, Emilia-sama. We will be waiting for your safe return."

Ram, bowing solemnly, was the very picture of a perfect maid seeing off her mistress.
At the words sending her off, Emilia nodded as though drawing strength from them, her face bright and clear.

Seeing Emilia like that, Subaru crossed his arms and nodded,

"So, given the mood — got anything to say too, Otto?"

"You've made this an incredibly hard atmosphere to follow up on! Is this really a moment where it's okay for me to say something!? What do you think!?"

Subaru tossed the question to Otto, who had probably prepared some words of his own, but it seemed he hadn't readied anything with as much power as what Ram had just said.
Considering the flow of the moment, it seemed he thought it best to just let things end cleanly as they were—

"Yes. Go ahead."

"—!!"

Unaware of Otto's inner turmoil, Emilia — thinking she was about to be encouraged — was fully braced to receive it. Not noticing his fluster, she waited with a tense expression for his words.
At Emilia's utterly innocent manner, Otto finally put a hand to his forehead with a look of resignation.

"Um, well, Emilia-sama."

"Yes."

"Actually, with all this commotion, I've ended up paying out quite a lot of losses myself. Well, of course, this was meant as an up-front investment, so the cost itself was already accounted for, but..."

"Um?"

Once the topic turned to money, Emilia, who wasn't good with numbers, looked confused.
At her beautiful, furrowed brow, Otto bit his lip and raised a finger. "What I mean is!"

"I took on this loss betting that Emilia-sama would grow into something great and prosperous. So you need to succeed, so I can recoup my stake — otherwise I'm in trouble!"

"...I don't think I'll grow any taller anymore. Though I suppose I could put on some weight if I eat."

"Don't hit our sheltered angel with confusing figures of speech. Also, Emilia-tan, I think your current figure is the golden ratio, so you're fine just as you are. Right now you're at your most lovely."

From the top of her head to the tips of her toes, Subaru thought Emilia was at her best exactly as she was now.
Of course, he intended to love her even if she gained a ton of weight or lost it all, but still.

In any case, setting aside Subaru's private musings, Otto — his face indescribable at how badly his intended meaning had failed to land — flailed his arms and legs for a moment before saying,

"...Please come back safely. I'm rooting for you."

"Okay, got it. Thank you too, Otto-kun, for all your help."

Emilia answered Otto, who had wrapped things up in a thoroughly safe and unremarkable way, with a firm nod.
Watching Garfiel give the slump-shouldered Otto a consoling nudge, Subaru and Emilia then turned once more toward the front of the tomb.

Night had already fallen over the Sanctuary, and it was now a fitting hour for the Trial to proceed without issue.
For one last time, Emilia repeated deep breaths as if steeling her resolve. Watching her out of the corner of his eye, Subaru thought hard about what words would be best to send her off with. Then,

"Hey, Subaru."

"Hm?"

"About what happened inside the tomb..."

Did she have some anxiety about the Trial?
Thinking so, Subaru waited for the rest of her words. But Emilia, unable to string together her next words for a while, kept glancing at him with an anxious-looking face.
Her cheeks, for some reason, were faintly flushed.

"Emilia?"

"I— I said, it's about what happened inside the tomb!"

"What happened inside... oh, wait, you don't mean what's about to happen — you mean earlier?"

"That's right. Honestly."

Emilia puffed out her cheeks as if to say "obviously," but being scolded for it given the flow of things just now hardly seemed fair.
Who would have guessed that Emilia, about to face the Trial inside, would be worried about the past rather than the future? Though, thinking about how the Trial waiting inside was itself the "past," talk of "future" and "past" started to feel confusingly vague.

Besides, caught up in the momentum and the string of intense events that followed, he'd nearly forgotten — but looking back on it now, what Subaru had done in the tomb was, even by his own admission, the kind of thing that made his face burn.
Arguing with Emilia, spitting harsh words, thrusting his love at her with rough abandon, and stealing her lips as though biting into them — even if it was the pent-up frustration of a fifth loop finally exploding, that was no excuse.

What Emilia was worried about was probably that very incident.
Watching color rise on her pale skin was a pleasant sight, but Subaru had no room to spare himself, so this wasn't the time to be entranced.

"Inside, um, when Subaru and I... that, you know?"

"Ah, ah... yeah, that's right."

"So, um, I think it's going to turn into a big deal. But because it's important... let's talk about it properly once the Trial, and everything else, is all settled, okay?"

Even as he privately thought that his head was already in quite a state over it, Subaru had no choice but to nod at once to Emilia's proposal.
It had been a first for Subaru, and surely a first for Emilia too. They had thrown their feelings at each other, and there was a mountain of things they'd need to reconcile. And on Subaru's side, even as he made excuses to himself, there was still the matter of "Rem" that he had to somehow deal with.

Even so,

"Huh, being able to think about what comes after — you've got quite a bit of composure, Emilia-tan."

"Composure, is it? I wonder. Maybe I'm just putting up a brave front, you know?"

"But being able to put up a brave front means you're not at your limit. It'll go fine. I'll bet on it."

At Subaru, thumb raised and teeth gleaming, Emilia tilted her head curiously.

"Bet — on what?"

"The right to a date, between me and Emilia-tan."

"So what happens if Subaru wins, and what happens if I win?"

"If I win, I get to date Emilia-tan. If Emilia-tan wins, you get to date me."

Emilia burst out laughing at Subaru's joke, and the two of them shared a laugh for a moment.
It really did seem that there was no tension or anxiety left inside Emilia.

"I'll bet on me getting through the Trial."

"Then I'll bet on Emilia getting through the Trial too."

"What if we both win?"

"Two dates."

"Yeah, yeah."

As always, Emilia let Subaru's flirting slide right past her.
Emilia slipped forward. Watching her silver hair sway in the wind, shimmering in the starlight, Subaru raised a hand.

"Off you go. Watch out for cars, and men."

"Don't say silly things."

With a wry smile, Emilia's figure vanished into the tomb.
The stone passage with no light source swallowed Emilia and immediately hid her away in the dark, stealing her from Subaru's sight.

With that, there was nothing more Subaru could do for Emilia.
Everything from here on was a problem Emilia had to break through on her own.

"Don't make such a worried face, boss. Ain't manly."

"That distinctive way you talk — once I think of it as middle-school-edgelord, it goes down real easy somehow. I had a phase like that too, you know."

Garfiel, coming up right beside him, chided the restless Subaru who'd just seen Emilia off. Subaru shrugged at his words, and then Garfiel, as if remembering something, clapped his hands,

"Oh yeah, boss. That thing you blasted me with during the fight, what was that?"

"You mean Invisible Providence?"

"In... what now?"

"Invisible Providence. 'The Unseen Will of God.' Cool, right?"

"Ah, hell yeah, that's crazy cool."

Garfiel hit it off with Subaru's words at once.
Calling it the "Unseen Hand" outright would carry too bad a reputation, so going forward Subaru wanted to stick with "Invisible Providence." Still, the name of the technique probably wasn't the only thing Garfiel wanted to ask about.

"Ain't Magic... right? Just a feeling, but it had a different air to it."

"If you're forcing me to sort it into a category, even I don't really know what that is. But it's definitely something outside the proper way. Not something you could copy even if you tried."

"Wouldn't wanna. Punchin' people from somewhere they can't see — that reeks of cowardice."

"C-cowardice, you little...!"

Just when he'd thought they understood each other on how amazing Invisible Providence was, Subaru was indignant at having the rug pulled out from under him.
Garfiel answered Subaru's reaction with an unrepentant "my bad, my bad," and didn't press the topic any further after that. He'd probably sensed it — that this thing he'd called outside the proper way wasn't something a human body ought to be treading into.

"...Still, what kind of karma is this, I wonder."

Invisible Providence — this was, without question, Petelgeuse's Unseen Hand. There was a difference in power, and he could only produce a single arm's worth, but the sensation was exactly that.
Why had a power identical to that of the loathsome madman come to dwell in Subaru's body? The answer to that was surely connected to whatever this "Witch Factor" was that Echidna had spoken of.

Witch Factor — just from the sound of it, it wasn't anything good, but he did recall Petelgeuse using that very same word. And this wasn't even the first time Subaru had used something resembling the Unseen Hand. In a previous loop as well, he had a sense of having used it once, unconsciously, to dodge the charge of Garfiel transformed into the great tiger.

In other words, the Witch Factor was steadily putting down roots inside Subaru.
Subaru was aware that he could no longer use Shamak. Through repeated overuse, the Gate — already worn down — had completely lost its function. His connection to the magical world, which had been next to nothing to begin with, was now something he could feel nothing of at all.

Losing the power of Magic he'd once longed for, only to have the power of this outside-the-proper-way ability manifest in its place — what an ironic story.
Still, even so—

"Guess that's better than being flat broke of anything resembling a trump card. Not sure how useful it is, though..."

Either way, nothing changed about how thin his means of fighting were.
Racking his clever-enough head over and over, borrowing hands from those around him to find a path to survival amid mortal peril.
The height of the wall of the world Subaru had to challenge remained, as ever, formidably high.

"Ahh, oh yeah, boss."

"What. Also, I still ain't used to that 'boss' thing."

"You'll get used to it soon enough. More than that, there's somethin' I gotta apologize for."

He wasn't budging on the nickname, but Garfiel looked up at him with an unusually meek attitude. Just like Emilia earlier, it seemed things people wanted to say just kept coming, Subaru thought with a wry smile.
Shrugging, he prompted Garfiel to continue, and Garfiel traced the scar on his forehead,

"I went inside, right? So, I mean, I went into the Trial chamber."

"Oh, right, yeah."

"So, I saw it. —Boss, that, uh. The result of you goin' at it desperate-like."

At Garfiel's words, spoken as if reluctant to say them, Subaru's brow furrowed for just a moment, then his eyes flew wide as he realized what Garfiel meant.
And Subaru's ears, in his shock, went bright red.

He saw it. He saw it, he saw it, he saw it!

"I— I didn't mean nothin' by it, alright? But man, never figured it'd turn into somethin' like that..."

"S-shut up! Forget it! You— oh no... I forgot about that! I mean... I mean, I never imagined you'd end up going into the tomb! Of course it turned into that... aah, damn it!"

Clutching his head, Subaru shook his burning face wildly.
Right now he resented Garfiel, who was watching him with a pitying look. If anything, that face was more infuriating now than it had been while they were trading punches.

"Forget it!! I'm not asking for anything more! Okay, conversation over! That's it!"

"Yeah, alright. ...But, when I saw that, I thought — boss is apparently a bottomless idiot, but... good thing I didn't let him die."

"I said it's over, didn't I! Are you a kid who doesn't get told once!? Oh! You are a kid!"

Even being called a kid, Garfiel had the upper hand simply by holding this weakness of Subaru's over him. He laughed off Subaru's sore-loser shout, then went down the steps of the tomb.
Following the back of Garfiel returning to where the others waited in the plaza, Subaru prayed for Emilia's good fortune, while hoping that she'd never even notice the "cheer" he'd left behind.

That kind of thing, after all, if it doesn't reach the person it's meant for first, just ends up embarrassing on its own.


* * *


And Subaru's prayer, in vain, would not be answered.

"I'm here..."

Passing through the stone passage, Emilia arrived at the small chamber where the Trial would take place.
In the dark, damp air, Emilia walked relying on the faintly glowing walls, and turned her gaze to the door at the far end of the chamber. The tightly shut stone door was surely built to open once the Trial was overcome. Garfiel had come back without passing through it. Then, as for herself—

"I have to work hard and get past that door."

What waited beyond, she didn't know.
Only that the Trial would not end with just one — that there were multiple — had been told to her by the Witch's own mouth.

Picturing the Witch who set the Trial in motion, a prickling, itching sensation spread through Emilia's chest. That was because that white Witch had, toward Emilia—

"Huh...?"

Lost in thought, Emilia, letting her gaze wander uncertainly, noticed something off about the small chamber.
During the time she'd spent hugging her knees inside the tomb, waiting for night, Emilia had only stayed partway down the passage and hadn't set foot in this chamber. So this was the first time she'd laid eyes on the state of this room in — skipping a day — two days.
Only two days, but something had changed in that time.
And as she thought about what that something was, Emilia arrived at the answer to the strangeness.

"This is..."

Checking the wall her fingertips had brushed, Emilia murmured to herself.
Her violet eyes, beginning to adjust to the darkness, traced out the change clearly in the gloom.
It was—

"...Subaru, you idiot."

Unthinkingly, in a tone tinged with a smile, Emilia found herself saying it.
Well, of course. Seeing this, she couldn't help but think it, couldn't help but say it.

"You're really such an idiot."

Contrary to the content of her words, the expression on Emilia's face as she said it was soft and full of tenderness. The wall she'd touched, the wall before her eyes, the change that had come over the entire wall of the small chamber.

—It was carved there. Pictures, letters, gouged into the wall, huge and huge and crooked in shape.

The big cat drawn there was Puck, in the deformed style she'd seen so many times before. Several drawings of Puck were carved across the whole wall, and around them, surrounding them, were letters.
The messy I-script, scrawled like a child's scribbles, was undeniable proof of that boy having worked desperately, thinking of Emilia.

'Do your best, you can do it.' 'Puck and I are rooting for you too, it'll be fine.' 'The girl I love is amazing! So have confidence!' 'Let's go on a date once this is over, a date!' 'Let's do this, Emilia.' 'Nobody's expecting anything from us. Is there any situation more fun to flip on its head than this?' 'I love you, so I believe in you!'

"Idiot... idiot, idiot, idiot... Subaru, you dummy."

She still had to face the Trial ahead, painful and agonizing memories waiting for her, and yet here he was pretending to cheer her on while actually trying to make her cry — what an awful person.

Now she understood.
There was something she'd come to understand.

This was Emilia's first time here in two days. Whoever carved this picture and these letters had only had one day's chance to do it.
The time Subaru had been given, the time he'd been away from Emilia's side, and the one stretch of time during which he'd stubbornly refused to say what he'd been doing — it was all that same span.

"—Right. That's right. Let's do this, Subaru."

Touching the letters fondly with her fingers, Emilia answered the carved words.
In the next instant, she felt her consciousness being tugged toward a drowsy haze, and the outline of the world began to blur.

The Trial was coming.
The past she had feared so much was coming.

—And yet, Emilia's lips remained curved in a smile.


* * *


"Breaking my promise to write a love letter, and then having someone else see it first... I'm finished..."

"You're overreactin'..."

Surrounding Subaru, who couldn't quite recover from the shock, Garfiel and the others each wore their own look of exasperation.
Still, the group had no choice but to wait as they were until Emilia emerged. "Waiting with trust" sounded fine in theory, but for those left waiting, unable to do anything else, this too was a trial of its own.

"For Garfiel it took about an hour, give or take... guess I should figure Emilia'll take around the same."

"If it succeeds, then yes, I suppose... ow!? And ow again!?"

Otto, having tactlessly cut in, took the price for his careless remark from Ram's elbow. And seeing Otto get jabbed by Ram, Garfiel, his cheeks twisting with jealousy, delivered a forehead flick of his own.
Otto reeled back and rolled on the ground, but no one so much as remarked on it.

"Oh right, there was something I wanted to ask Garfiel and Ryuzu-san."

"Somethin' you wanna ask me and Gar-bou?"

Ryuzu, standing there looking uncomfortable, lifted her face at Subaru's words.
Having vanished from sight since the morning, Ryuzu had, inadvertently, ended up in the position of having cooperated with Subaru and the others' scheme to bring down Garfiel. In her own way, she still seemed uncertain how to deal with Garfiel, and the conversation between grandmother and grandson had felt somewhat awkward for a while now.
Though, it seemed it was only Ryuzu who was bothered by it — Garfiel, having shaken off the past, didn't appear to hold even a shred of ill feeling toward her.

"Right. Something I wanted to ask. Though, whether the Ryuzu-san here now would even know the answer is a bit iffy."

By "here now," he meant whether it was something Theta would know if asked.
Ryuzu, the representative of the Sanctuary, was a composite being of four — Alpha, Beta, Theta, and Sigma. With Garfiel's problem resolved, Subaru wanted to believe that the matter of Ryuzu's stance for or against the Sanctuary's release had been resolved all together as well, but confirming her intent was still necessary.
More than anything, there were still a few points that didn't sit right.

"Garfiel, right now you're in favor of releasing the Sanctuary, right?"

"Boss, 'in favor' ain't quite it. I lost to you lot. So, I ain't gonna get in the way of you tryin' to release the Sanctuary. And as a result, I'll act so the Sanctuary folks who end up changin' don't suffer for it... that's where I'm standin' now."

"Right, that stance."

"Huh?"

Raising a finger, Subaru put a stop to Garfiel's line of reasoning.
Garfiel looked puzzled, as did the others around them who'd been listening. Did no one else feel anything off about what he'd just said? Subaru himself started to feel uneasy.

"To begin with, when we first came to this Sanctuary, your position wasn't for or against — you said something like you were neutral... same kind of thing as now, right?"

"...Back then, it was 'cause I figured if you knew which way I leaned, you'd start bein' wary of me."

"But you got openly hostile pretty fast. Maybe we screwed up, maybe we literally stepped on the tiger's tail — but what caused that change of heart?"

It genuinely puzzled him.
At the very least, on the first day Emilia took the Trial — up until before and after she took it — Garfiel had, on the surface, treated Subaru's group in a friendly way.
The Garfiel who showed open hostility always did so on the night the Trial failed. Citing the Witch's Miasma drifting off Subaru's body as the reason, Garfiel would declare himself their enemy.

But it had already been proven that Garfiel's nose, in truth, could not actually smell any such Witch's Miasma on Subaru's body. It was someone else who noticed the Miasma surrounding Subaru, and Garfiel only chose hostility after being told about it.
And the one who told Garfiel about the Miasma, who set him against them—

"I've been figuring it was Ryuzu-san, the one opposed to the Sanctuary's release."

"............"

Looking down at the silent Ryuzu, Subaru raised a finger atop his crossed arms.
The Ryuzu here was Theta — meaning, as far as Subaru understood, the one Ryuzu copy who had opposed the Sanctuary's release.
Given that Alpha and Beta were in favor, and Sigma neutral, Theta was the one who, through the Trial, had learned the past of the real Ryuzu Meyer and come to regard the Sanctuary's release as dangerous. If this reasoning held, it was hard to imagine anyone other than Theta prompting Garfiel's change of heart.

Hearing Subaru's reasoning, Garfiel nodded with a sour look.

"Boss is right. Gran told me—"
"That's Suu-bou's misunderstanding. I never told Gar-bou anything like—"

Their voices, both trying to respond, overlapped — and yet each was trying to argue the exact opposite claim.
Before Subaru, his brow furrowed in confusion, Garfiel and Ryuzu looked at each other. Garfiel's mouth opened and closed uselessly as he pointed at the stunned Ryuzu.

"Wha— what're you talkin' about. The old lady told me, the first night that lady took the Trial. Said the boss reeked of the Witch. Said the half-demon girl too, both of 'em — that maybe those two were the Witch's servants... so, so I—"

"I said somethin' like that...? No, it's true I did notice the Miasma around Suu-bou, and it ain't like I have no thoughts about Emilia-sama's origins... but that's a separate matter from this. I only meant to make my judgment after tracing through the plan Roz-bou laid out—"

"Wait! Wait, stop! Just now, Ryuzu-san said she didn't do it."

Ryuzu had answered Garfiel's words with a denial.
Garfiel looked as if he found it hard to believe, but since Ryuzu's words had come from Ryuzu's own mouth, they had to be fact.
After all, the residents of this Sanctuary were bound by a Contract that made it impossible to "tell a lie" within the Sanctuary's bounds.

"Setting aside cases where the person doesn't even recognize it as a lie themselves — at the very least, if Ryuzu-san flatly states she 'didn't do it' regarding her own actions, that can't be a lie."

"But I sure as hell—!"

"I'm not doubting you. ...I'm accepting the fact that you're not capable of lying. Ryuzu-san. That statement just now — that was the consensus of all of Ryuzu, right?"

At Subaru's confirming words, Ryuzu, her face turning pale, nodded.
Affirming this meant that, regardless of which of Ryuzu's Alpha, Beta, Theta, or Sigma, it had not been "Ryuzu" who had appealed to Garfiel to change his heart.
And yet, Garfiel insisted flatly that it was "Ryuzu" who had prompted his change of heart.

"――――"

Subaru lifted his face and looked at Garfiel's.
Garfiel gritted his teeth with a click, shaking his head, and there was no trace of a lie in his expression. To begin with, his personality wasn't suited to lying anyway.
Now that the gilding of the "Sanctuary's Barrier" he'd been maintaining had peeled away, that was all the more apparent.

"Ram."

"...Just so you know, there's no such thing as magic that changes one's appearance. Not even Roswaal-sama could do something like that."

"Then what do you make of this?"

Ram had no answer to Subaru's question.
Even she didn't know the answer to this contradiction. Still, Subaru was half-convinced this was "a trap Roswaal had laid." Or rather, there was no other option left.

"I want to stay here and wait for Emilia to come out, but..."

It had only been ten minutes since Emilia entered the tomb. Subaru wanted to throw his arms wide and be the first to greet her when she came out having overcome the Trial. He wanted to bless her.
But—

"Let's go press Roswaal for answers. We need to find out just what that guy's up to, refusing to give up like this."


* * *


—Whether waking within a world of dreams could rightly be called an awakening of consciousness, Emilia didn't know.

The small stone chamber she had been in just moments before — released from the Trial's room, Emilia's body now found itself within a familiar, nostalgic forest.
Surrounded on all sides by tall trees, she felt a cool, refreshing wind and warm earth beneath the soles of her shoes.

Flashes of memory, one after another.
The snow-covered scenery of the white forest that she would come to see as the Trial's time passed.

That sight had not yet begun.
No snow fell in the forest; the green welcomed the return of Emilia's consciousness-only presence with gentle softness.

And then,

"Well now. These past few days have really been a flood of visitors, haven't they."

That voice reached Emilia as she held her breath, confirming her surroundings.
With Emilia's arrival, the dream world took shape. Within a landscape of memory that shouldn't have existed until this very moment, there stood a figure beneath the shade of a tree, as though her being there were the most natural thing in the world.

A girl clad head to toe in a single shade of black, her hair and skin as pure white as scattered snow.
A beautiful Witch who, with only these two colors, seemed to have pursued beauty with the utmost clarity.

The mistress of the tomb who presided over the Trial and showed Emilia her past — the Witch of Greed, Echidna.

The Witch rose, steadying herself against the tree trunk she'd been leaning on, and tilted her head toward Emilia.
Emilia, too, met the Witch's gaze head-on, and caught her breath.

"Truly, a flood of visitors. Both the welcome kind — and the unwelcome, uninvited kind."

"............"

"After exposing yourself to such a shameful display, I'm impressed you can show your face here so brazenly. Even 'I' am taken aback by that thick skin and stubborn refusal to give up."

The Witch, gazing at Emilia, hurled words mixed with malice and contempt without mercy.
Her cold, frozen black eyes bore no resemblance at all to the black eyes that always gazed on Emilia with kindness. Having weathered so much unreasonable malice before, Emilia could tell.

This was malice of an entirely different order from any Emilia had known before.
The malice Emilia had received up until now had been a baseless blade aimed at the insubstantial idol of "the silver-haired half-elf."
But the malice this Witch directed at her was different.

This was absolute hostility aimed not at "the silver-haired half-elf," but at "Emilia" herself.

"Breaking down, sobbing your eyes out, and then thinking it's all fine so long as you flatter the man willing to hold you — you harlot. Sullying, again and again, a world that belongs to 'me' alone — you blasphemer. Loving, over and over, the version of yourself that he forgives you for being — you shameless, immoral wretch. —Well, why don't you say something, daughter of the Witch."

"............"

Up until just days ago, this violence of accusing words would have thrown Emilia's heart into turmoil.
It wasn't that she'd knelt before this much malice and given up on the Trial, but there was no doubt that this was the starting point where her heart began to wear down, her resistance to the past whittled away.
The Witch wished for neither Emilia to face the Trial nor to overcome it.
The Witch had no expectation whatsoever that Emilia would overcome the Trial.

*'Nobody's expecting anything from us. Is there any situation more fun to flip on its head than this?'*

Indeed — Subaru had been exactly right.

So Emilia raised her hand and pointed a finger toward the heavens.
The way Natsuki Subaru did, whenever he threw down a challenge, whenever he rallied the courage inside his own heart.

"My name is simply Emilia. The Witch of Ice, born in the great Elior Forest."

As Emilia declared her name, she could see the Witch's composure falter.
Savoring the satisfaction of that, Emilia turned the finger she'd pointed at the sky toward the Witch instead.

"I won't kneel to the malice of a fellow Witch. After all, I'm a difficult woman."
