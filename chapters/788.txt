Arc 10 Interlude: "Brothers and Sisters"

—It was a room without windows.

A hall cut into a circular shape, with a round table taking up a large portion of its center.
To be precise, it wasn't a round table — it was an octagonal great table, its corners shallowly cut away. A single chair sat at each of its eight sides, eight seats in all.
And right now, four of those seats were occupied—

"—Hey, I always wonder about this, y'know. Where the hell do they even get these boxy tables from, huh?"

The voice tossed out the question with terrible manners, its owner sprawled with both feet thrown up onto the round table, hands laced behind his head and pressed against the back of his chair.
He was a young man with bright blue hair spiked up and a nasty look in his eyes. For all his bluster, he was small and high-voiced, with something about him that called to mind a hot-blooded little animal. The way he kept banging his heels down on the round table, one after another in a restless clatter, only reinforced the impression.

The one who answered the young man's question with a low, thoughtful "Hmm" was the old man seated at the side next to him.
His large frame was thick both in height and breadth — set beside the small young man, the difference was like that between an adult and a child. Dressed in work clothes reminiscent of Kararagi-style wasou, the old man turned to face the youth and said,

"Riboon, hypothetically speaking — what answer is it you actually want to that question of yours? Go on, say the answer you're hoping for. A hypothetical will do fine."

"Ain't nothin' hypothetical about it. It's well-made, fits the number of us just right, and I was just thinkin', how the hell'd they manage to prep such a convenient table, y'know?"

"Ah, so it was meant kindly. Good thing I braced myself all the same."

"Haaah? What's that supposed to mean?"

"Oh, simply that it was I who made this round table. Whether praise or a heartless review comes my way, one must be prepared beforehand for whatever is said, wouldn't you agree?"

"Roundabout as hell, old man! Guess I shouldn'ta praised it!"

Slamming his heel down hard on the table once, the young man called Riboon spat the words out. At the abuse, the old man frowned his thick eyebrows and stroked his own graying hair, muttering, "Now, now."

"This round table is made from timber several hundred years old. Show respect to things that have endured long years. More often than not, the passage of time does not lie. The same goes for me. Respect your elders."

"Respect, huh? What's that supposed to look like, specifically?"

"Now listen, don't take this seriously. Hear it as a hypothetical. Respecting your elders means this: if I tell you to die, you die, promptly."

"Guess I gotta kill you before you get the chance to say it, then, Albarest!"

"Wait—!!"

Riboon kicked his feet up off the table and spun around, dropping from his chair — and just as he was about to swing the large blade slung across his back toward the old man, Albarest, the clatter of a chair being kicked back overlapped it.
It came from the person seated on the opposite side of Riboon from Albarest — a young boy with chestnut hair who looked to be no more than his mid-to-early teens.
Eyes brimming with tears, the boy clutched a large lamp filled with Lagmite Ore to his chest with both hands, and said,

"I—I'm sorry, I'm sorry! I'm apologizing, so please don't be angry. When people get angry it makes my head go all dizzy. So please stop. I'm sorry. I said I'm sorry...!"

"Look, Riboon. Tornel, the youngest here and the one most looked down upon, is pleading so bravely. Being the elder here, if only nominally, don't you feel any shame?"

"Look, uh, so, this guy's over here sniveling out apologies. So, uh, why the hell should I let him off the hook for that...!"

Sandwiched between the boy and the old man — Tornel and Albarest — Riboon trembled with rage.
Just as it seemed his temper would get the better of him and blood was about to be spilled in the room—

"—Say, I just realized something. Just now, the way Tornel tried to protect Albarest — that was an outburst of emotion born of caring deeply for him... which means, aren't the two of them in love? Oh my! What an enormous age gap! It's a burning love!"

So said a woman's voice, pitched high with excitement, slicing through the room's mood without a hint of restraint.
Cheeks flushed, she stood with both hands braced on the table — a woman in a black dress, wearing a large hat, her long black hair streaked with purple nearly brushing the floor. Despite her mature looks, her eyes sparkled like those of a dreaming girl as she pressed both hands to her own cheeks.

"But wait — couldn't there be another possibility? Tornel wasn't protecting Albarest at all — he couldn't bear the thought of Riboon looking at anyone but him... so he deliberately stepped into the line of fire, pleading to be seen! That's it, that's exactly it!"

"It ain't 'that's exactly it'! Quit sayin' creepy stuff, Lycoris... you love-brained freak! Round the clock, your head's just goin' mush mush mush mush in there."

"Oh, oh, are you just embarrassed about being the one caught up in a romance? Keep that up and someone dear to you will turn their back on you... wait? Could it be — you don't want me to misunderstand you? Is that why you're acting like this? Do you like me? I like you too!"

"—Shuuut uuup."

Riboon leveled his blade at the woman as she squirmed, cheeks colored, and gave that order. As a result, she pressed her hands together in front of her chest and moved her lips silently in some mumble, but fell quiet. Even so, her expressions and movements were more than enough to broadcast just how noisy her presence still was.

"Riboon, this sort of thing is forbidden between brothers and sisters, is it not? Hypothetically speaking, if Mama were to learn what you just did to Lycoris, it would be you receiving the painful discipline, you know."

"If it comes to that, I'll just shut everyone's mouths for good. Is there anyone here who can even beat me?"

"Hypothetically speaking, no, I doubt it. Head-on, perhaps you'd stand a chance, but with no rules at all, even less so."

"Long as you know it, we're fine. ...How much longer are you gonna keep squirming around!!"

"—— — — ——"
"Eep! I'm sorry, I'm sorry!"

The woman squirmed on in silence, while Tornel clutched his head as though he himself were the one being shouted at. Irritated by both reactions, Riboon's forehead bulged with a vein. —It was then.

"—Ah, sorry, sorry, I've kept you all waiting! Truly my apologies, calling this meeting and then arriving late myself. What can I say, running a nation keeps a man busy!"

Saying this in a voice that carried well, a young man entered the room with the hurried clack of footsteps. He was a tall, slender, handsome young man with dark skin, dressed in a long robe of black trimmed with white and gold. Behind him he brought two girls of very different impressions.
One was a fair-skinned girl with wavy green hair, her eyes downcast as she kept her head bowed. The other was a beautiful girl with shining blonde hair cut straight at the shoulders, distinguished by dazzling, eye-catching eyes.
The young man, leading the two girls along, circled around the great table and tilted his head.

"Hm? What's the matter, Lycoris? For you, always so passionate, to greet me in silence isn't like you at all... ahh, I see, this'll be your doing, Riboon. No, no, you must keep to Mama's instructions! You wouldn't want to be branded a 'dropout' or a 'problem child,' now would you?"

At the sight of the woman — Lycoris — squirming and swaying in silence, the young man turned his prompting toward Riboon, who had twisted his lips and looked away. Riboon ignored him, and the young man gave a wry smile; in his place, the beautiful girl standing behind him piped up brightly, "That's right!"

"Yes, yes, exactly, just like Dandon says. Every bit of it. At this rate, Riboon's going to end up a dropout, a failure of a piece of work. Let's report that to Mama. That'd be best, wouldn't it? Right, Dandon, isn't that so?"

"No, no, Luchiereka, I have faith in Riboon. I believe he possesses the breadth of character to properly heed good advice — a true 'sage'!"

"That's right! Whatever Dandon says is always correct. Riboon's loud and cheeky and there are plenty of times I wish he'd just die, but he can still listen properly to important advice. Right, isn't that so, Riboon? Even if you're an idiot, you're not idiotic enough to die over it, are you?"

"Tch."

At the bright, beaming smile of the girl — Luchiereka — Riboon clicked his tongue with venom and let the point of the blade in his hand drop to the hard floor. A ringing sound that seemed to resonate in the bones struck through the room.

And then—

"—No, no, that just won't do, how shameless. But then, the greater the obstacle, the more fiercely the flames of love burn, don't they. Luchiereka and Clarissa — which of the two of you will win Dandon's love, I wonder? ...Or could it be — me!?"

"See? Can't even tell what she's on about, right? You two deal with her yourselves."

"That's right! Honestly, I have no idea what Lycoris is even saying. Everything, absolutely everything Riboon says is correct. I was thinking the very same thing. You too, aren't you, Dandon?"

"Ha ha ha, I'm no 'bigot' who'd favor Riboon over Lycoris, or vice versa. Both of you are equally dear to me as brother and sister! Though, Lycoris, I am not the sort of 'deviant' who harbors romantic feelings for his own siblings, so do keep that in mind!"

"That's right, Lycoris, you're abnormal! Your thinking is abnormal! You deviant!"

"Luchiereka's coming down so hard on me... does that mean you like me? I like you too!"

Breaking the brief silence, the woman — Lycoris — swept the room with a heated tone and gaze, and a bad atmosphere began spreading among the young man, the girls, and Riboon. But before it could spread fully, a high, loud crack drew everyone's attention to the center of the room.
The cause of the sound was the green-haired girl, who had hastily taken her seat; the instant after she crushed a piece of wood between her fingertips, a spiderweb of cracks ran out from the center of the octagonal great table.
Having done it, she met no one's eyes, keeping her head bowed as her lips trembled, and said,

"J-just, please, sit down... start already, and finish, or Mama will get angry. She will get angry. And that — I don't want that. I don't want it, so."

"—Right, everyone! Clarissa's told us just what to do! Come now, come now, take your seats! Let's have ourselves an important, important talk!"

"Hmm, but Dandon. This happened to be the great table that I made. Hypothetically speaking, a word beforehand might have been nice, before turning it into broken goods like this..."

"My brother, and dear Grandfather — I did say to take our seats. I'm the one Mama has charged with keeping everyone in line, after all. I'd hate to have to brand Grandfather with 'deadweight,' now."

"—. Oh, never mind, it was just a hypothetical. Just a hypothetical."

Raising his large hands, Albarest backed off, and the young man — Dandon — nodded deeply, then once more urged everyone present to sit with a "Come now!"
At that, the seven people in the room obediently took their respective seats. And once everyone was in their designated place, the only seat left empty was the one at the head of the table—

"Now then, let us greet Mama in her absence. Bow your heads, filled with daily gratitude and ever-growing love."

"—Mama, thank you always. I love you more than anyone in the world."

Solemnly, in earnest, the seven of them — who up until now had been out of step with one another, scattered in attitude, mismatched in gender, age, and conviction alike — came together, just this once, as though by a miracle.
Every word, every gesture, everything about it was a ritual directed at a single, sole object — and if one closed one's eyes to its true content, it might almost have felt like something sacred, something worth throwing away everything else for.

After holding that stillness a while, Dandon was the first to lift his head, clapping his hands lightly twice to announce the end of the ritual, and said,

"Now then, with our greetings to beloved Mama finished, shall we begin a pleasant, bright, fruitful discussion! —Our first order of business is the 'dangerous individual' who has vanished from sight, Royal Selection candidate Crusch Karsten."

So saying, Dandon set a sheet of paper down on the cracked great table.
Drawn upon it was a beautiful woman with long green hair tied up and a dignified face — the 'Battle Maiden,' Crusch Karsten, who for these past two months had continued to evade pursuit.
At present, the Kingdom had put out a warrant for her as the perpetrator behind the murder of Miklotov McMahon and continued to track her movements, but so far had failed to grasp so much as a shadow of her whereabouts.

"Not just entry and exit from the royal capital — we've had checkpoints along the highways, dragon carriage depots across the land, even peddlers and innkeepers questioned, and had the records combed through, but nothing. Natsuki Subaru, who vanished together with Duke Karsten, and Ferris Argyle, later confirmed to be traveling with the two of them — there's no doubt these three are acting together."

"And yet people can hardly vanish like smoke. Hypothetically speaking, even if they had turned to smoke, we surely have hands enough on our side to sniff them out even so. That they haven't been found means..."

"It means, I'd say, that they have a collaborator capable of doing exactly that. Someone we haven't yet identified is aiding their flight, providing them food and shelter, and hiding them from searching eyes. —Most likely, our old nemesis, the 'Six Tongues.'"

"...The 'Six Tongues.' Always, troublesome."

Biting at her thumbnail, the pale-faced girl — Clarissa — muttered gloomily.
To everyone present, the name 'Six Tongues' carried great, ominous weight. —Who could say how many times that hostile organization's interference had soured their mother's mood.
And their mother's mood, whether good or foul, was a thing terrifying beyond help either way.

"Say, I just realized — Crusch and Ferris were originally lord and vassal, weren't they? But Natsuki, who's butted in here, should belong to a different camp entirely, shouldn't he? And yet, for them to be together like this — don't you think it can only be a stolen love? This is the flame of love!"

"That's right! Just as Lycoris says, it's love! Natsuki Subaru loves Crusch Karsten! That's why they're fleeing together! Isn't it so?"

"No, no, that's wrong, Luchiereka! The one Natsuki loves is Ferris! Because that way the obstacle's so much greater, and it burns all the hotter!"

"No mistaking it! You may be out of your mind and hated by everyone, but everything Lycoris says must be absolutely correct! Everyone thinks so too, don't they?"

"I'm sorry, I'm sorry, it's my fault for letting the conversation stall because of you two, I'm sorry."

"Oh, there's no need for Tornel to apologize. In the sense of exploring possibilities, even Lycoris's sickness serves some purpose. Luchiereka, do take a lesson from Tornel and keep quiet."

"I'll do that! If Dandon says so!"

Laughing, Luchiereka rose from her seat and went over to Tornel, who sat almost directly across from her, and beside him pressed both hands over her own mouth, falling silent. Tornel grew flustered at the gesture, but eventually, just like Luchiereka, covered his own mouth with both hands and likewise fell silent.

"Now that the idiots have shut up, it's a little easier to talk. So? Have you finally figured out somethin' about those 'Six Tongues' bastards, or what?"

"No, no, no, not a clue! You see, their bodies bear a curse mark. Try to question them, and the instant even the tip of a fingernail brushes against the truth, they cut themselves down without hesitation. There's simply no drawing any information out of them that way. Thoroughly merciless orders — their leader must be a real 'cold-blooded' one!"

"Six tongues to their name, and not one of them free to speak. Hypothetically speaking, a formidable foe indeed."

Despite the talk of being unable to grab hold of their enemy's tail, Dandon's cheerful manner never wavered as he answered. While Riboon and Albarest, dealing with him, wore troubled expressions, Clarissa raised her hand hesitantly and asked, "Is it — all right?" before pressing on with another question.

"...Is there still a chance — that Duke Karsten is hiding within the royal capital?"

"There is. In fact, whenever we try to rule out that possibility, sightings within the capital crop up again. It's not just about erasing traces — they're deliberately leaving some behind to throw us into confusion. Nothing but capable enemies, giving us all the more reason to put our abilities to use! Don't you agree, brothers and sisters?"

"If it's a matter of nothing but capable enemies... hypothetically speaking, is that our second order of business?"

"Precisely! My brother and Grandfather understands well. The second matter, once again, concerns those of the Emilia Camp who have likewise slipped through our fingers."

Lumped together as the 'Emilia Camp,' the presence of Natsuki Subaru, who had vanished alongside Crusch, was admittedly a confusing outlier, but the rest of its members — setting aside Emilia's knight — had likewise slipped free before capture and remained on the run.
And, terrifyingly enough, they had managed to evade even the 'Sword Saint,' Reinhard van Astrea.

"The 'Sword Saint' — in the Water Gate City, cooperated with the other camps as well... that's what I've heard. And I've also heard he interfered with Mama's plans... could it be, he let them go on purpose?"

"If he did such a thing, he'd surely understand it would bring about a result he wouldn't want. I don't take him for that much of a 'fool.' Grandfather, what's your opinion?"

"Hmm, indeed. In preparation for the worst, we should have laid as many restraints on the 'Sword Saint' as we could manage, and yet he's proven a finer specimen than even rumor claimed. The number of restraints one can bear depends on the size of the vessel — but hypothetically speaking, if we laid the same restraints now on the 'Sword Saint' upon Riboon, both I and Riboon would already be dead."

"Hey, uh, so, why the hell are you bringin' me into this."

"Oh, oh, Riboon, don't you get it? There's only one reason Albarest would want to talk about you like this— —. —. — — — —. — — — —!"

"Riboon! Please don't go adding to the list of things to report to Mama! In any case, does that satisfy you, Clarissa? Ah, good. There, 'good girl.'"

Taking the quiet lowering of her raised hand as an affirmative, Dandon spread out, one after another across the great table, the wanted notices of those whose search continued.
Naturally, every face among them was one everyone present had already stared at until it might as well have worn a hole through the paper.

"After joining up with the 'Sword Devil,' Wilhelm van Astrea, the movements of the Emilia Camp's members have been impossible to trace. If the 'Sword Devil' has ties to the 'Six Tongues,' there's a risk the camp has linked up with Crusch and the others as well."

"Ain't diggin' that outta 'em your job? Get the old geezers to spill it already."

"Fitting them with restraints, or making a brand take hold — neither is a simple matter. If it were, hypothetically, that easy, the Kingdom would already have fallen into Mama's hands far more easily."

"...What about the one who was a candidate for leader of the 'Six Tongues'? The one — the western margrave."

"—Roswaal L. Mathers."

It was Tornel and Luchiereka whose voices overlapped in answering Clarissa's question.
As though horrified at having spoken out of turn, Tornel clutched his head, while Luchiereka's eyes lit up.

"I—I'm sorry, I'm sorry. I said something I shouldn't have."

"It's fine, Tornel, don't worry. Just now I only matched my voice to yours. I'm not thinking anything at all. I'm just an obedient little doll. But, but, if you want to be the useless one who said something he shouldn't have, I think that's fine too. You really are useless, aren't you!"

"Hyuu, I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I'm sorry."

"In any case, Margrave Mathers, whose name has just come up, connects directly to our third order of business. The suspicion that he himself may be the leader of the 'Six Tongues'... and indeed, he has ignored his summons to the royal castle and, together with his household, likewise vanished from sight!"

"So basically, there's just nobody left, huh!"

"Ha ha ha, precisely so! Margrave Mathers has been mocked as a 'demi-human enthusiast' and a 'court jester,' but it seems we're the ones being made fools of!"

Not to be outdone by the racket of Tornel, Luchiereka, and Riboon, Dandon laughed loudly, in a tone that seemed to ignore just how dire the situation truly was.
But this was by no means foolishness born of turning a blind eye to inconvenient facts.

"The castle, and the country too — we're the ones holding them, aren't we... am I wrong?"

"Not wrong at all. Hypothetically speaking, however skillfully they've managed to flee this far, they can hardly run forever. Even they have a deadline they can't ignore — much the same could be said of us, too."

"—Just so. The time to settle things once and for all with the 'traitors' draws near."

Bringing his palm down hard on the round table, Dandon crushed Crusch's wanted notice in his grip.
Still wearing a smile, he made that declaration, and the others filling the remaining six seats turned their eyes toward him — brothers and sisters bound together by the single thread of love for their one and only mother, at their peak.

"Well then, everyone, it's a fine thing that we've all come to share an understanding of the situation. Each of you, follow the instructions already given— hm? Ah, hold on a moment. A message has come in."

So saying, Dandon, who had been about to wrap up the discussion, drew a small mirror — a 'Dialogue Mirror' — from within his long robe and opened it in his hand.
Dandon met the eyes of the one who had sent the message, reflected on the mirror's surface, and said,

"Well, well, well, you seem to be keeping busy. Over here, all of us brothers and sisters were just having ourselves a nice, cozy gathering. A shame you couldn't be here, but is this urgent?"

Dandon smiled warmly at the figure reflected in the mirror, but his expression stiffened faintly the instant he heard the words that came back.
What he heard was this—

"—Emilia and Felt, who were being held in confinement, have escaped? And what's more, with the Divine Dragon Church's Filore guiding the way?"

"—— — — ——"

At Dandon's murmured confirmation, the air in the room where the round table stood went still.
Yet another unplanned occurrence — one that matched, or perhaps even exceeded, the incident two months prior when Crusch had slipped through their fingers, an unforeseen turn of events that could hardly be called trivial.

Under the weight of the gathered stares, Dandon fell silent for a moment.
At length, he shifted the stacked wanted notices on the round table over toward the one empty seat — the head of the table where no one sat — and covered his face with his hand as though in lament.

"—Let us set the 'Faithful' in motion. There now, no need to panic."

And so, still covering his face with his hand, he went on.
It was something he told himself, told all of them — the collective will of the brothers and sisters, the 'Beloved Children.'

"Reading the will of our beloved Mama — that's simply what it means to be a well-made 'beloved son' or 'beloved daughter,' after all."
