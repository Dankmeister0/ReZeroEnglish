Arc 8 Chapter 4: "A Warrior's Instinct"

"Ogre-Eye" Izmail was a hero of the Cyclops Tribe, and the hope of his people.

At the center of that valiant face, his large blue eye had been clear and limpid, gazing upon the future without a trace of hesitation. — No, had gazed. That was the past tense now.

Now, Izmail's single eye was an omen of ill fortune, like a golden moon floating in the dead of night.

"――――"

Having boldly taken up arms in the Battle for the Imperial Capital to make a name for his tribe, Izmail had joined the field as one of the rebels aiming for the head of Emperor Vincent Vollachia. True to his reputation, he'd broken through the regular army's defenses and been among the first to reach the castle walls.

But that was as far as Izmail's triumphant charge went.

The hellfire that had been unleashed burned "Ogre-Eye" Izmail and the Cyclops warriors who'd followed him to nothing, and even he, who survived it half-dead, had his life taken by the merciless pursuit of a murderous blade.

Yes, his life had been taken. — Or so it should have been.

"――――"

Blown apart by the bombardment, his flesh torn into a thousand pieces, he should have died for certain.

And yet Izmail, his charred and ruined body sprouting new limbs where they'd been blown away, had risen to his feet, hefted his beloved battle-axe onto his shoulder, and now walked through the war-ravaged imperial capital.

The only thing moving that cold, ice-cold body was a fiercely swirling emotion — hatred, and nothing else.

"—Where are you, you loathsome beast."

"Up to now it's all been rank-and-file — we haven't run into anyone who was a 'General' while they were alive. But whoever's up ahead is more than a match for one. That's the problem."

"When you say 'General'..."

"There are exceptions who get rated on tactics and command, sure, but in most cases it just means the person themselves can fight. This one's that type... nowhere near a match for a First General, but plenty good enough to have climbed as high as Second General."

"――――"

"If you're just comparing raw fighting power, he could probably wipe out all of us single-handed."

Todd concluded his hopeless report — the annihilation of their whole group — in the same flat, matter-of-fact tone as always.

A formidable zombie lay in wait ahead. Just how fatal an opponent that was for their party, and that the run of good luck that had carried Subaru and the others this far had finally run out — that was what his words amounted to.

"B-but you're the crafty one, you can figure something out, right? You've gotten us this far using these little ones' power and everything."

Katya, her voice cracking, turned pleading eyes on Todd.

The "little ones" she meant were Subaru and the others crystallizing zombies with magic, and Tanza, who mopped up any enemies they missed.

Fortunately, Rem's turn to protect the rear guard — Rem and the others following behind — hadn't come yet; up to now their path forward had been carved open by that dual set of aces combined with Todd's judgment.

However—

"Brute force works fine as long as the power gap isn't too big. Once the difference gets past a certain point, it's a whole different story. No matter how hard you blow, wind alone can't knock down a castle."

"Ugh..."

"So, soldier boy, your judgment is that pushing forward on this road is a lost cause?"

"That's right. — I'd like to say that, but I can't just change the route on a whim either."

Todd had been nodding along to Flop's words up to that point, but there he shook his head. Still shaking it, he turned to look back the way they'd come, and then toward the Crystal Palace.

"Looks like things have finally been settled over there too. Water's coming soon."

Just as Todd's flat, emotionless remark indicated, a roar came to dominate the sky far in the distance.

Looking in the same direction as him, they saw the enormous humanoid being that had been clashing with the "Dragon" beside the Crystal Palace have both its knees destroyed, its collapsing body crushing the area around the castle as it fell.

Beyond the billowing plume of smoke, the white dragon that had felled the giant god let out a triumphant roar.

Given that deafening sound together with that sight, there probably wasn't a living creature — zombies included — who couldn't understand it was a cry of victory.

"If our earlier guess was right..."

"Even if we go back the way we came and look for another route, there's no telling if we'd make it in time."

"I suppose."

It was a conclusion that could only drive them further into a corner, but putting into words the flow of the situation left no other option, and Beatrice, her hand joined with Subaru's, affirmed his thinking.

Receiving that, Todd gave a small nod, and Tanza, raising a hand tentatively, said, "Um."

"If that's the case, then isn't our situation deadlocked? A powerful enemy ahead, and no time to turn back either."

"Ha ha ha, Miss Tanza, that's a very frightening conclusion. Certainly, if we only pick up the facts as stated, it does sound like a deadlock that unsettles the heart, but I'm sure there are still moves left to make!"

"Such as?"

"That I don't know! But there's surely someone who might, right?"

Tanza, whose emotions rarely stirred, furrowed her round brows and turned her gaze this way at Flop's remark.

At a moment like this, having someone turn to think of Subaru when the topic of someone reliable came up was a happy thing, and a grateful one. There was pressure too, but it also stirred in him a feeling that he had to live up to it.

And what's more—

"—Do you think you can do something about it?"

Tanza wasn't the only one turning expectant eyes on Subaru like that.

"――――"

Rem, gripping the handles behind Katya's wheelchair so tightly her knuckles had gone white, asked that question, her pale blue eyes wavering.

That was something different from the full, unreserved trust Katya beside her placed in Todd, leaning on him as if clinging for support — something more fragile, like standing on thin ice or a castle built of sand.

Even so, Subaru's heart surged.

Enough that he could pull his attention back from the wariness he'd kept directed at Todd traveling alongside them, and put his mind to full use.

"If we're going forward or back, we need to decide now or we won't make it."

At that shift in Subaru's thinking, Todd added the condition once more, as if to press him.

If they went forward, what they'd need was fighting strength. If they turned back, what they'd need was luck and time. — On the premise of using Return by Death, the latter might have better odds.

"No, I can't say that for sure."

If he repeated Return by Death and eliminated the wrong routes one by one, he might eventually reach a different exit by the shortest path — but that was only true on the premise that a goal existed to reach.

If no route at all would get them there in time, the latter option couldn't be chosen.

On the other hand, if he took the option of fighting—

"Who can actually fight a strong enemy is..."

Among the fleeing group that was Subaru and company, those who could properly be counted as fighting strength were Subaru and Beatrice, Rui and Tanza, and then Todd, with Idra as a reserve force.

Of these, Subaru was more or less an appendage of Beatrice's, and he wanted Idra assigned to guard the non-combatants — Rem, Katya, Flop, and the fake crown prince.

In practical terms, the ones who could serve as combatants were Beatrice, Rui, and Tanza, plus Todd — four people. That the only ones he could trust turned out, magnificently, to be little girls wasn't even something he had the composure to laugh off.

Just as Subaru reached that thought—

"――――"

He put a hand to his chin, thinking, and when he raised his face, his eyes met Todd's, who had turned to look his way at that very same moment.

In that instant, lightning seemed to flash through Subaru's mind, and what he felt like an electric shock was terribly reassuring, and bitter.

Because—

"—Let's take down the zombie."

They said it together.

—Because he felt in his very soul that they'd both intuited the same conclusion at the same time: that what it would take to pull it off was combining strength and wits.

—The instant he heard a sound of a different quality from all the rest, Izmail had already spun his body around.

"――――"

Izmail, having swapped out his once-burned body for a fresh, cracked replacement, planted both feet wide and lowered his stance, catching the approach of a large shadow with his single eye.

Standing in the street, what came flying toward Izmail's head in an arc — of all things, as some kind of joke — was an entire building, one of the houses making up the imperial capital, ripped whole out of the thoroughfare.

Forcibly torn from the ground, spilling floorboards and wall fragments as it flew, it scattered debris like crumbs off a half-eaten loaf of bread — but the reality was nothing so charming; most humans would simply be crushed to death.

That said—

"—Too soft!"

"Ogre-Eye" Izmail was not the sort who fit within the bounds of "most humans."

The flying house was on a trajectory to land squarely in the middle of the street, and with both flanks neatly blocked off, the only escape was forward or back.

But to deliberately leave an escape route open was exactly what would play into the enemy's hands.

So Izmail fled neither forward nor back, and instead raised his battle-axe.

An axe so massive that two sturdy warriors together could barely lift it, Izmail wielded effortlessly with one hand, cleaving through any obstacle. This time too, the hurled house met that same fate.

At the moment of contact, the axe blade, swung upward from directly below, slid into the leading edge of the house, and like a slash splitting a huge man open from the crotch, the strike cleaved the house clean in two, vertically.

The severed house, scattering the furniture, dishware, and clothes its occupants had left behind across the street, ended its service as a house without inflicting any damage on Izmail's body.

However—

"—!"

The instant after unleashing that great feat, a thrown stone came flying straight at Izmail's face.

Naturally, that meant nothing against a Cyclops, whose eyesight was overwhelmingly superior compared to other races. Tilting his head, he dodged the incoming stone, and amid the swirling dust kicked up by the house, Izmail narrowed his eye and focused.

Both the house and the thrown stone had come from the same direction — the "enemy" was there.

—Through the Cyclops Tribe's eye-technique, the world Izmail saw shifted.

In Izmail's eye, which perceived a target's emotions as color, he saw multiple flickers of red in the direction the attacks had come from. — The color of fighting spirit.

That in itself was welcome, and strength surged through Izmail's whole body.

In this Battle for the Imperial Capital, or in battles against bandits or other races, nothing sapped his heart's vigor like fighting an opponent who bore no color of a warrior.

It was only in a battle between one man of arms and another, one warrior and another, that there was value in contesting skill.

The wellspring of hatred driving Izmail's supposedly dead body forward owed much to that universal honor of the martial way, and to the fact that it had been defiled.

"Come, then—"

Stepping onto the packed-earth street, Izmail's body advanced toward the enemy.

As if to meet him, the same kind of thrown stone came flying again, and using that stone as cover, the next mass projectile — a house — traced its arc through the air, but he thought it neither cowardly nor a weakling's tactic.

It was a fine tactic. He would simply crush it with respect.

"A warrior's death should come by a warrior's hand."

Advancing, the speed and precision of the stones closing in from the front were considerable, and while he dealt with them through minimal dodging and deflecting them with his raised battle-axe, taking one straight on would surely shatter bone.

And needless to say, the mass-projectile houses being hurled had the force to incapacitate him instantly.

However—

"Don't think hurling things is a privilege reserved for your side alone!"

Roaring, Izmail swung his battle-axe, blowing apart the second house-cannonball, and with that same momentum drove the blade into the street, tearing up the ground of the imperial capital.

Then, swinging his long leg into the upturned earth, Izmail delivered a kick that sent it flying forward like buckshot. Naturally, he didn't expect this to land as an effective blow against the enemy on the far side of the street.

"Just a smokescreen, but..."

Unlike the Cyclops Tribe, other races without special eyes couldn't see through a curtain of swirling dust. Hiding himself in the dust and shattered street debris, the accuracy of the stones thrown at Izmail dropped noticeably, buying him room for further advance.

"A grown man, and a girl—"

Peering through the veil of smoke, narrowing his eye, Izmail could now see the opponents more clearly for the distance he'd closed, and could tell it was a man and a girl.

The man bore the role of keeping Izmail in check with thrown stones, while the small girl beside him tore up houses on all four sides from the ground and hurled them as mass projectiles.

Their coordination was in sync, but what struck him as odd at the same time was the immaturity of their bearing.

Izmail, who had honed his skill on the battlefield, could tell.

Neither of these two, now on the offensive, was a born warrior. And yet, that a warrior's readiness had grown in them regardless was, to Izmail — their very target — genuinely admirable.

At times, the battlefield could transform someone who was not a warrior into one, in but a moment.

The same thing, he thought, was happening now to this man and this girl.

"Well done."

He would praise them, pay them respect, and must kill them no matter what.

Perfectly, flawlessly, without the slightest hesitation, without the smallest error, he must exterminate them utterly. Every last thread of their lives must be severed; their breath must be stopped.

Their limbs torn off, their heads wrenched free, and their extracted hearts' blood sipped from—

"――――"

He felt a ferocious craving for death and blood and life burning up inside his own head.

At the same moment, amid the third house-cannonball he'd smashed, Izmail saw his own reflection in a shattering window, its glass scattering with a high, piercing sound.

Izmail was grinning.

A malevolent heat crept through his cold, bloodless body, twisting the corners of his mouth as if in applause. It was a wicked smile he'd never once worn in life, one that reeked of nothing but blood.

"Ha, ha ha ha, hahahahaha!"

Far from disgust, a violent exhilaration welled up at that smile, and laughter spilled out.

Laughing, laughing, he knocked away a lucky hit from an incoming stone, then leapt himself into the next house-cannonball, smashing through it with his battle-axe as he charged.

Toward the two warriors — no, prey — hurling things while retreating backward, he lunged.

At the fifth house, what looked like their final act of resistance, Izmail brought his battle-axe down, meaning to bless the applause of his boiling blood with real blood, when—

"—Guess having just one eyeball narrows your field of vision after all, huh?"

"――――"

The house flying in its arc — Izmail smashed the front door, which had come squarely into view, with his axe, and let the force of destruction propagate through it; the whole warped structure burst apart like a bubble.

Just before that happened, someone's mockery struck Izmail's eardrum.

—No, no, no, no, no. Absolutely not.

That was not the mockery of "someone."

To Izmail, it was the voice of what he most despised — the symbol of evil, the object of his hatred.

"You—!!"

The elation of a moment before vanished instantly, replaced by cold murderous intent that seized Izmail's entire body.

What Izmail's wide, black-tinged golden eye caught, there in the wreckage of the shattered house, plastered flat against the floor, was an Imperial soldier with orange hair.

—That was, without question, the hated enemy who had brought about Izmail's "death."

"—Oooooooh!"

In that instant, twisting his body on the surge of erupting rage, Izmail's battle-axe changed course.

The strike that should have cleaved the house clean in two shifted from a vertical blow, deflecting at a sudden angle, bearing down to strike the prone Imperial soldier—

"—Oh—"

Just before he could land his full weight upon his hated enemy, from the field of view of Izmail — who'd put his whole spirit into a battle cry — that Imperial soldier suddenly vanished.

It wasn't something on the order of "moved at tremendous speed."

He had, quite literally, vanished.

"――――"

For an instant, Izmail's thoughts were painted over entirely in black and white.

The hatred that had surged toward his hated enemy lost its target, and his mind was seized by the blank space that bizarre phenomenon had created. But just before that instant, Izmail's "Ogre-Eye" had caught something amiss.

On the hateful figure of the prone enemy — a girl with long golden hair, gripping the belt at his waist.

"――――"

The intent and reason were unclear, but he didn't think it was information scattered without relevance either.

Even in their brief contact, he had a negative confidence that that Imperial soldier wouldn't needlessly bring in an unnecessary element without reason — and that thought robbed Izmail's mind of its bearings.

And then—

"—Guh!?"

A piercing impact bored into Izmail's left shoulder, and a groan of pain rose from him.

Amid the house whose destruction had been interrupted, punching through the wall and striking Izmail — whether by luck or by aim — was the blow of a stone thrown at high speed.

With the exact force he'd been certain would shatter bone on impact, it exploded against Izmail's left shoulder, and as a result, Izmail's body should have screamed — but it didn't.

Izmail's body, drained of blood and cracked in places, shattered with a startlingly hollow sound at the stone's impact.

Just like that windowpane he himself had smashed in the house.

"――――"

For an instant, Izmail's thoughts stopped at the sight of his own left arm, shattered.

But while there was surprise, the absence of the pain that should have accompanied it kept his consciousness from being torn to a thousand pieces. And so he didn't miss the next change.

"My arm—"

The arm, broken off at the shoulder and about to fall onto the tilted floor of the house, went through an inexplicable motion as if time itself were rewinding, and regenerated — the cracks closing, the shattered fragments returning, restoring itself.

As if to undo the very fact of it having been shattered, and in less than two seconds.

"Ha—!"

Izmail hastily switched the battle-axe from his right hand to his newly-restored left, and as if to test the sensation, he swung the axe, this time truly blasting apart the half-wrecked house-cannonball.

Bursting out through the structure, a mix of stone and timber, amid a shower of scattering debris, Izmail — reached his right hand out toward the sense of hostile intent closing in.

He caught, head-on, with his open right hand, an incoming fist-sized stone.

Naturally, if he didn't deflect the impact, the bones of his right hand would be brutally destroyed. But Izmail's body had departed from the way a human body breaks, and instead demonstrated the way an inanimate object breaks.

And on top of that, it restored itself to its original state with a recovery power exceeding that of any human body's healing.

"So this is this body's true nature...!"

Unconsciously averting his eyes from the unnatural situation he found himself in, Izmail moved on, fueled by burning hatred.

This ghastly body, brought about by an inexplicable resurrection, kept blood, pain, and even "death" itself — that thing inseparable from life — far from Izmail.

If so, then—

"—Hah."

Exhaling a breath, Izmail looked below and saw there the sight he had wished for.

Just behind the man and girl who had been boldly attacking Izmail all this while stood the cowardly hated enemy who had vanished into the distance just before Izmail's grasp closed on him.

The method was unclear, but the moment that man had vanished from right in front of Izmail's eyes, he had appeared like that behind the two of them — the warriors had joined hands with a coward.

At that moment, the praise he'd held for the warriors a moment ago reversed itself, turning into a thick, viscous hatred.

"All of you — pay for your mockery of war with your lives—!"

Kicking off the scattering fragments of the flying house, Izmail's body shot forward like an arrow.

Toward the body closing straight in on the impudent fools, the girl, judging that there wasn't time for another house, began throwing stones just as the bearded man beside her was doing.

But—

"—Useless, useless, useless, useless!"

Without even needing to swing his battle-axe to knock them away, the stones catching Izmail at high speed could not stop his advance, nor could they break his spirit.

Even where a stone's impact shattered part of him, it restored itself in the blink of an eye, and he took no damage.

With a new body that had let go of any sense of being alive, seeking to gain that sense of life from elsewhere by bathing in blood, Izmail reached toward the tense, hardened faces of his enemies—

"――――"

—and, reaching out his hand, he noticed.

The bearded man, the girl in the kimono, and even the Imperial soldier he'd marked as his hated great enemy — none of their expressions had gone dead.

It was the instant he made out their colors — red for the bearded man and the girl, and once again blue for the hated enemy.

—Izmail sensed a presence appear behind himself, even as he leapt at his enemies.

"—!"

Izmail's warrior instinct rang an alarm bell, and he twisted his body in midair to look back over his shoulder toward that presence.

What his wide-open "Ogre-Eye," its golden pupil, reflected was a figure who had somehow circled around behind him in that single instant — no, not alone. Three shadows.

A black-haired boy, and a girl in a dress held in his arms.

And the golden-haired girl who should have been beside the hated enemy just a moment ago, now clinging to the boy's back.

All of them small, yet all three burning with a bright red fighting spirit, raised their hands.

"El—"

At the murmured incantation, Izmail's warrior instinct reacted instantly, moving to kick off the debris.

But holding back that instinct, Izmail instead stretched out his arm to swing his battle-axe. Rather than dodging or stopping the enemy's attack, he would take the hit and counterattack through it.

An attack impossible for his former body, but a strike he could deliver now — he would cut down the enemy with it, he thought—

"Minya—!!"

A violet-and-navy radiance was unleashed, and Izmail deliberately took the blast that came flying straight at him.

He, who had schemed a counterattack from within it, did not realize.

—That the Cyclops Tribe's hero, "Ogre-Eye" Izmail, would never, under any circumstances, have made such a foolish choice.
