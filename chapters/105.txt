Feeling something that shook him to his very core, Subaru hid it and stood up.

"You're right... I've been, well, being pretty disrespectful about this whole thing. Me."

It was true — he genuinely felt something from that.
Shaking his head, not even bothering to brush the dirt off his body, Subaru re-gripped his wooden sword and turned to face Wilhelm again.

"Even knowing I'll lose, I go in to win. Sounds contradictory, but that's exactly what I have to do... I get that, not in my head, but in my gut."

"That is good to hear."

"If I can manage that, will I get even a little bit stronger?"

At Subaru's question, Wilhelm closed one eye and said, "Well now,"

"That's a separate matter entirely. Wanting to be strong, and becoming strong — those are two completely different things."

"Don't you think the story would be more beautiful if you just honestly agreed with me there!?"

"I do not permit myself to tell such cruel lies."

"Sometimes I think that very thing can itself become a cruel truth, you know!"

The conversation, which had been neatly wrapping up, started to unravel, and Subaru dropped his shoulders sulkily, casting his gaze to the ground. Then, quietly, he muttered,

"Do I have any talent for the sword, you think?"

"By my judgment, unfortunately, no. Subaru-dono's talent with the sword tops out at mediocre — much the same as my own."

Denying the question, Wilhelm let a self-mocking smile rise to the corner of his mouth. The words were so unexpected that Subaru raised an eyebrow in surprise.

"That's some strange false modesty. Saying Wilhelm-san has no talent for the sword..."

"It's the truth. I have no talent for the sword. If I did, I surely would not have gone on gripping a sword for this long. Which means, Subaru-dono, that if you set your mind to it, you too can reach the same place I have."

"...Just out of curiosity, how much effort would that take?"

"Nothing much. Simply devote the better part of your life to swinging a sword."

"'Simply'..."

Of course, it could never really be as simple as the word "simply" made it sound.
"Being able to keep making the effort is the true talent" — well said, indeed. In truth, even being told he could reach the same level as Wilhelm, Subaru couldn't muster the resolve, or even think of a reason, to devote as much time to the sword as Wilhelm had.
To begin with, the very reason Subaru was studying under Wilhelm like this was—

"Hyah!"

Letting out a breath and shifting his stance as if giving up — the tip of Subaru's wooden sword gouged into the dirt, flicking a spray of it up toward Wilhelm's face.
The instant the clump of earth covered the old man's vision, Subaru's strike aimed for his feet. It was a move he'd just received advice about, from the man's own mouth, only moments before.

—If you've decided to fight, use whatever dirty methods you must.

That his all-out attack ended in a complete miss was conveyed by the total lack of resistance from its target. But Subaru didn't stop there — riding the momentum of the spin, he unleashed a roundhouse kick, and, with a delay, followed up with a second sword strike as well.
Against that combination of attacks, Wilhelm, deftly dodging both the dirt-throw and the surprise assault, showed his teeth in a genuinely pleased grin for the first time.

"Well done."

The wooden sword slid in beneath the leg he'd kicked out with, and, canceling the attack, Wilhelm closed the distance instantly, turning his back and diving into Subaru's space. His free hand grabbed the collar of Subaru's shirt as the two ended up facing the same direction, overlapping — and by the time he noticed, his feet had already left the ground.
His vision spun in a circle, and before he even had time to register that he'd been thrown, his back was slammed into the ground at tremendous speed. The impact knocked the breath out of him and stopped his thoughts cold, but faster than the pain could tear through his whole body, he registered the tip of the wooden sword thrust in front of his face.

"Shall we call it finished for now, perhaps?"

Told this, Subaru let all the strength drain limply from his body and closed his eyes.
The coldness of the ground he lay sprawled on seeped up from his back, and naturally, a pain-laced numbness began to spread through his entire body. And then,

"Guess a head full of nothing but distracting thoughts like mine won't get me anywhere, huh."

"I don't believe that's so."

"But isn't getting better with the sword supposed to be about that? Like, only by devoting yourself to the sword with a clear, empty mind can you finally grasp something as a swordsman?"

"There's no such thing as suddenly becoming strong because you 'grasped' something. And whether your mind is empty or full of distracting thoughts, in the end, the one who cuts down the opponent wins — that fact doesn't change, I believe."

Stating this dry opinion, Wilhelm continued with, "Besides,"

"I hate to say this, but I myself have rarely swung a sword with an empty mind. Especially back when I first began swinging a sword, I hardly thought about the sword itself at all."

"Then what were you thinking about while you swung it?"

"Simply, endlessly, of my wife."

"Wilhelm-san, you really come out of nowhere sometimes with the wife stuff!"

He recalled that on their very first meeting, there had been a devoted-husband sort of remark, but even accounting for that, Wilhelm's tendency to dote on his wife in his remarks was really something to behold.
Sitting up, Subaru looked up at Wilhelm with a slightly narrowed gaze and said,

"Setting aside the heartwarming devoted-husband anecdote... being told I don't need to devote myself purely to the sword — that's unexpected, coming from you."

"Deciding to become strong, and then, in order to do so, devising ways to keep swinging the sword. In the end, becoming strong requires nothing but spending that time — there's no other way. Unless one is born naturally gifted with a Divine Protection, that is — and neither you nor I, Subaru-dono, are such people."

"Tahaa, that's harsh, that is."

Saying so, Subaru couldn't hide the wry smile carving itself into his cheeks.
Being flatly told he had no talent for the sword stung more than a little. He was, after all, someone who had, however half-heartedly, devoted himself to kendo back in his original world for a time. As someone who felt he'd achieved a certain level of proficiency, the pride he'd carried had been smashed to pieces.
And yet, watching Subaru form that bitter but genuine smile, Wilhelm ran his fingers through his own thick white hair to smooth it, and said,

"In any case, that is roughly the mindset needed for becoming stronger. Well, perhaps it wasn't a story of much use as a reference for you, Subaru-dono."

"What do you mean by that?"

Unable to grasp the true meaning behind those words, Subaru tilted his head, hand to his chin. Seeing that gesture, Wilhelm continued with, "No, well—"

"It occurred to me that explaining the mindset for becoming stronger to someone with no intention of becoming stronger would be of rather little use."

"————"

For an instant, Subaru's expression froze, unable to understand what had just been said to him.
But that stillness lasted only a moment. Subaru immediately thawed his frozen expression, shrugging his shoulders in an exaggerated, joking manner.

"H-hey now, hey now, hey now, Wilhelm-san, what's this all of a sudden? Don't go dropping some shocking twist on me out of nowhere. Me, what?"

"If you have the self-awareness for it, then it would be tasteless of me to spend any more words on the matter."

At the sight of him shaking his head, resignation held in his eyes, Subaru found himself unable to continue speaking, falling silent.
Just what was Wilhelm getting at — a mounting unease built within him. Recognizing that feeling, Subaru found himself questioning the anxiety welling up inside himself.

—Why should I feel any unease at all.

"—Subaru-dono, it seems that will be all for this morning."

"Eh?"

Wilhelm said this to Subaru, who broke into a cold sweat at that indefinable feeling. Startled, Subaru looked up, and found the old man's gaze directed not at him, but toward the mansion.
Following his gaze, Subaru saw a figure running from the mansion toward the two of them in the garden. — It was Rem.
She ran while wearing an expression consciously kept from showing her emotions, only a quiet tension surfacing on her face.

Something had happened.
And to Subaru, right now, that was something of a relief, too.

To Subaru, who felt an indefinable pain merely from facing Wilhelm, the sight of Rem's anxious, urgent expression was, if anything, a relief.

"Subaru-kun. —We need to talk."

And so, a period was placed on the ordinary, uneventful days that had been passing without incident.



The ordinary days ended, and the extraordinary days Subaru had been waiting for began — and there, Subaru would once again come face to face with despair.
