Arc 3 Chapter 23: "The Self-Proclaimed Knight and the Knight"

—How many times now had he been knocked down onto the ground like this?

Tasting the hard earth against his face, Subaru thought that with a kind of hazy detachment.
The inside of his mouth was a mess of blood and grit, and his whole body—struck so many times he'd already given up counting—burned as if it had caught fire.
His head, whatever state the blood flow inside it was in, couldn't hold a coherent thought together, and his swollen left eye had sealed shut completely, robbing him of anything like a proper field of view.

A wrung-out rag, battered from head to toe.
And yet, precisely because his opponent's skill was so overwhelming, he hadn't sustained any injury so far severe enough to fatally impair him.
His arms and legs moved. Discounting the pain, there wasn't a single part of him that refused to obey.
To call it "practiced at inflicting pain without crippling" sounds like malice, but the proof of exactly that was carved into his body from head to toe.

"—I think anything further would be a waste, don't you?"

The voice looking down on him came from far away, from somewhere high above.
Sprawled face-down on the ground in a spread-eagle heap, Subaru moved his head with weary reluctance to look toward the voice. Across his tilted, slanting field of view stood a purple-haired young man, the tip of the wooden sword in his hand swaying idly.
His ceremonial white attire was without a single stain, and naturally, not a scratch or even a bead of sweat showed on any visible skin. Only the wooden sword in his hand bore the blood of the man he'd struck—the one thing that stood out, garishly, amid all that elegant composure.

"Retract your earlier words, bow your head, and we'll end it here. What do you say?"

It was a question he'd been asked again and again since this had begun.
Battering this body mercilessly, striking it over and over without relent, knocking it down without mercy.
And after each repetition of that act came, without fail, the same repeated demand for surrender. The answer to that, of course, was already decided.

"...Not backin' down. Not bowin' my head."

His arms shook. He tried to push himself up off the ground, but his upper body wouldn't obey. He rolled, braced himself on a shoulder to raise himself up, then forcibly dragged his lower half in to bring his torso upright. Then, planting into the ground the wooden sword that seemed to have fused itself to his palm, he clung to it and hauled himself to his feet. All the while, his opponent made no move to press the advantage against the wide-open Subaru, only gave a small, weary shrug of his shoulders.

The difference in skill was plain as day. The outcome, obvious at a glance. His chances of victory were one in ten thousand—short of a miracle, he couldn't even land a single blow in return.
*As if that matters*, he thought. Everything else could be tossed aside here, and it wouldn't matter.
That he couldn't win was self-evident. That he couldn't reach his opponent, he already knew. He wasn't arrogant enough to think that someone as lacking as himself, inferior to others in every way, could produce a satisfying result.
So then, at the very least—at the very least, this much.

"...It's you who's takin' back what you said, not me...!"

His split lip stung, and biting down on the last word of his own defiant declaration, Subaru charged forward at a speed far too slow to matter.
Pouring everything into one reckless, all-or-nothing blow—the result of which was—

"There exist gaps that cannot be closed no matter what one wagers. That, too, is a matter of what one is born with. Even you, I'd think, understand that much."

Parried aside, the flicked tip of the wooden sword struck Subaru's forehead hard.
Red sparks filled his vision, his split brow began to bleed, and the sight in his barely-open right eye turned entirely red.

"How many times does that make it now?"

Gritting his teeth against the pain, he wiped at his blood-soaked vision with the back of his hand.
Through that gap, a strike caught Subaru in the solar plexus and sent his body flying backward.

Unable even to break his fall, Subaru rolled across the ground and once again lay spread-eagle on the earth.
The shock of the blow to his chest sent his lungs into spasms, and the agony of not being able to breathe assailed him. Even cornered by the combined torment of pain and oxygen deprivation, Subaru's consciousness, far from fading, kept burning on as if still hungry for the flame.

The blue sky he looked up at was high and distant, and beyond it there was nothing to see.
Facing that hatefully blue expanse, Subaru wrung every last ounce of strength from his body and stood up once more.

—However many times it took, however many times, he would do it again.

Feeding on nothing but an inexhaustible anger, enduring pain fit to make him vomit blood, he kept his eyes forward.
As if turning his gaze away from the simple truth of whether that anger's target was right or wrong.

—Subaru learned that the story had moved on without him when Reinhard and Ferris, done with the gathering in the great hall, came to visit him in the waiting room where he sat slumped, each wearing an expression of opposite emotion.

"So anyway, congrats, the Royal Selection's officially begun. Subaru-kyun, you're Emilia-sama's knight and all, right? Let's both do our best, 'kay?"

Ferris spoke as though he hadn't the faintest awareness of any part of what had just happened, despite having watched every bit of Subaru's conduct in the great hall and its aftermath.
Beside his carefree manner stood Reinhard, his eyes turned on Subaru with something like concern. Rather than pointing out Ferris's attitude, he lowered himself into a seat and smiled at Subaru, who sat there with his spirit visibly drained, and said,

"I don't think it's quite as simple a matter as Ferris makes it sound, but I do agree that I'd like for us to be in a relationship where we push each other to improve. Subaru, let's do this fair and square."

"...Ah, ah."

Faced with such a bright, straightforward declaration, Subaru had no choice but to mumble something noncommittal in reply. What their words pointed to was plain enough, and by rights Subaru ought to have been racking his brain furiously over the path ahead of him.
But right now, there was something Subaru needed to confirm that took precedence even over that weighty matter.

"——"

And yet, even knowing that himself, the crucial words wouldn't come.
He could feel his cowardly, contemptible, craven self holding him back, trying to trample over everything he needed to set right.
That he could sense this about himself was pathetic, and yet all he could do was bite his lip, his eyes unable to meet either Reinhard's or Ferris's, wandering instead.

"—Subaru, that old man is fine. Thanks to Felt-sama's intervention, his safety has been assured."

"—!"

As if he'd seen through Subaru's cowardly heart, the answer to the question Subaru had tried and failed to voice came from the red-haired young man.
Subaru's cheeks stiffened in shock, his eyes went wide, and Reinhard, meeting that gaze, gave a single nod.

"Given the layout of the corridors leading from the hall, it's hard to imagine you passed by without crossing paths with that old man. And knowing, as I do, that you and he are acquainted, it isn't difficult to guess what's clouding your expression right now."

Anticipating what Subaru meant to say before he could say it, Reinhard raised a finger and let the words spill out. But even he, sharp as he was, couldn't know the true source of the guilt weighing on Subaru.
The shamefulness of, in that moment, having given up on saving Rom-jii by his own strength, and of having let pass—even with a fallback plan in place—a scene where an acquaintance might come to harm.

No—to tell the truth, even that was only a pretext. The real truth in Subaru's heart, down in its depths, was far, far more beyond saving.

"Good for you."

To Subaru, head down and eyes averted, Ferris said this with a smile, peering up into his face. He looked up at Subaru from below with that lovely smile of his, hands clasped behind his back, and continued,

"You should thank Reinhard and Felt-sama for that. —This way, Subaru-kyun doesn't have to make any excuses at all, does he?"

"——!"

Subaru caught his breath; he'd been trying to look away, but now found himself facing Ferris despite himself. Ferris's cat-like eyes went wide, his chestnut cat ears on top of his head twitching, and his sadistic smile split even wider across his face.
The sight of him was nothing so much as a cat toying with a mouse under its claws.
Chilled to the bone by this creature who'd seen through his heart and now spoke his irony aloud, Subaru instinctively began to construct some excuse-like words—but caught himself, realizing that was exactly what Ferris wanted. In the end, all Subaru could manage was—

"Ah, yeah... good, good. No, seriously, exactly as I figured! I thought leaving it to Emilia-tan and her expert begging skills over there would go better than me pulling some stunt in that room. Well, in practice it seems things didn't go quite as calculated—turns out it was Felt, predictably enough, who reacted super hard, from what I hear."

Spreading both hands, shrugging his shoulders, loosening his cheeks into a lopsided grin, Subaru tapped out a jaunty little rhythm with his feet. Ferris's eyes went wide with astonishment at the sudden change, and even Reinhard's expression stiffened slightly. And Subaru, watching their reactions play out to the beat of his own footsteps, went on,

"Still, I'm guessing that's the reason Felt got her resolve locked in, huh? How'd that go, exactly, Sir Knight Reinhard?"

"Hm? Ah, that's right. The old man's appearance solidified Felt-sama's resolve in a good way. Though it does trouble me a little that the outcome ran counter to what he'd intended."

The handsome young man touched a hand to his chin, brow furrowing slightly in thought. Reinhard's words, delivered with a bearing already complete as a painting, meant little to Subaru, who hadn't witnessed the exchange in the hall.
All he could do was piece together, from the context of what Reinhard said before and after, an imagined picture of what had happened, and then, armed with that guess, precisely trace what reaction Natsuki Subaru ought to have.

"I see. So basically I went and set the stage for a powerful rival to show up, huh. Guess I might get an earful from Emilia-tan later for that one."

"I don't think it'll come to that. Emilia-sama, and surely the other candidates as well, would want to compete by fair means. If facing an opponent worthy of the competition is met with anything but welcome, it says something about the smallness of one's character."

"Nah, see, personally, if I'm gonna do a thing, I want to steamroll it easy and feel like the ultimate badass, so I don't really get that mindset. Even in RPGs I'm the type to overlevel early and just plow through. Basically, by the time I clear the first forest map, the first enemy's already in its second form."

Choosing the red-affiliated option first meant that if your early levels weren't high enough, you'd end up struggling abnormally hard at the first and second gates. The green affiliation was the easiest, but that was the cheap, self-restricted route.
In any case, setting Subaru's personal philosophy aside, judging from Reinhard's confidence, that camp's preparations seemed to be proceeding without a hitch.
Meanwhile, the Ferris camp, watching Subaru's frivolous attitude in silence, boasted the longest-established master-servant relationship of any of them, predating even the Royal Selection itself—about as gilt-edged a guarantee as one could ask for. Factoring in Ferris's apparent total devotion to his liege Crusch, there seemed to be no anxiety-inducing elements there either.

Weighing even that much of the relational dynamics, Subaru could only conclude that his and Emilia's camp were, indeed, falling behind. After all, right now Subaru couldn't even bring himself to have a proper conversation with the returned Emilia about the Royal Selection without flinching.

—Being afraid of being near her—that was something that had only happened once before, back when he'd been caught in the whirlpool of loops at Roswaal Manor.
What was distinctly different this time was the state of Subaru's own heart, the reason behind his fear of contact with her.

Back then, it had been terrifying because it felt as though Emilia had become someone he no longer knew.
But now, it was terrifying because, having come to know her far more deeply, he had no idea what she thought of him.

"Anyway, that aside—if the talks are over, where'd everyone else go? Just you two coming to fetch me feels like a bit much VIP treatment, doesn't it?"

"I think you know why and you're just asking, but the candidates still have a lil' bit more detailed talkin' to do, so they're staying behind in the hall. Us knights got told we're free to do as we please, so..."

"I said I wanted to come check on how Subaru was doing, and Ferris kept me company. I'm sorry we couldn't offer much support back there in the hall."

Picking up where Ferris left off to offer that apology, Reinhard drew an "ahh" and a sheepish scratch of the head from Subaru, who wore an awkward expression.

"Nah, it's not really your fault, and honestly, looking back, the whole thing's already turning into a black history I want to forget, so it's stinging my chest just thinking about it. Feel free to forget it too, yeah?"

"I appreciate your generosity. This debt, I shall never forget."

"You listened to a total different thing than what I just asked for!"

Interpreting Subaru's guilt in the most generously charitable way possible, Reinhard arrived at a conclusion diametrically opposed to what Subaru actually wanted.
His outcry ignored, Subaru turned back to Ferris with a troubled look still on his face.

"So, Reinhard aside—what about you... no, uh... damn it, that doesn't sound right either!"

"If you're strugglin' with what to call me, you could just call me 'Feri-chan ☆' with lots of love, y'know?"

"Shut it, cat-ears. Anyway, why'd you come here, cat-ears? Frankly, shouldn't you be by Crusch-san's side?"

He recalled their first meeting—that brief exchange at the front gate of Roswaal Manor. At the time, Ferris had said something along the lines of not wanting to be apart from Crusch even for a moment.
Thinking of that, it did seem odd that he'd come here at all.

"If you're worried about her safety, that's not a problem, mm-kay? I mean, Crusch-sama's way, way stronger than li'l ol' Feri-chan, y'know?"

"Don't say that so lightly... is that really okay, Order of the Royal Guard?"

"Feri-chan's selling point is somethin' else entirely, so it's fine. Besides, that selling point isn't unrelated to Subaru-kyun, y'know~?"

Irritated by the drawn-out way he spoke, Subaru nonetheless patiently crossed his arms and prompted, "And that would be?"
Faced with Subaru's curt manner, Ferris puffed out his cheeks, twisting his body coyly, and glanced sidelong at him with an adorable little gesture.

"Aww, still playin' dumb even though you already know~. Makin' me wait like this."

"Don't waver, my heart, don't flutter, my heart. What's in front of me right now is the same creature that's attached to my crotch...! Just say it more directly. What's your selling point supposed to be?"

"There's only one thing Feri-chan's sellin' point could be, silly~♪"

As he spoke, Ferris raised both hands in front of Subaru and held up two fingers. And then, the tips of those raised index fingers suddenly began to glow faintly.

"Ugh... somehow, is it just me, or does the fatigue in my elbows, shoulders, and lower back feel like it's fading...?"

"Guess you've got aches and pains all over like an old man, huh, Subaru-kyun."

Feeling, bit by bit, the fatigue drain away from every corner of his body as heat, Subaru shuddered as he registered his flesh being healed.
In short, that was surely the effect of the phosphorescent light being emitted right now from Ferris's fingertips.
And with that thought, Subaru finally recalled what "selling point" the beautiful boyish-looking young man standing before him had been referring to.

"Ah, right. Now that I think about it, aren't you supposed to be some amazing water magic user or something?"

"'Some amazing,' that description doesn't even come close to covering it, Subaru."

Recalling the setting information that surfaced in his mind, Subaru voiced it half-absently, and Reinhard offered a correction. Nodding toward the somewhat skeptical-looking Subaru, Reinhard raised a hand to indicate Ferris.

"Ferris's talent as a water-affiliated mage is unmatched—utterly without peer. Not just in Lugunica, but across the entire continent, there's no one who compares to him. He's been granted, at a young age, the 'Blue,' one of the Six Colors that represent the pinnacle of each magical attribute in Lugunica, and his skill is more than worthy of it."

"Another new bit of setting just dropped... my head's about to burst at this rate."

Subaru made no attempt to hide his weariness at Reinhard's fluent explanation. In front of him, Ferris, his talent praised so lavishly, stood with a hand on his hip, puffing out a chest that was, in fact, perfectly flat and unremarkable.

"Reinhard's explanation left out a lot, but basically, that's the gist. And so, on account of all that, Feri-chan's got herself a fancy title, and her schedule's always packed to the brim with people wanting the healing touch of water magic~."

Thrusting a clenched fist toward the sky, saying "Go get 'em, Feri-chan!" with a beaming grin, Ferris struck a pose.
From that explanation, Subaru guessed that Ferris probably occupied the role of "world-class virtuoso physician" in this world. With the kingdom's population at fifty million, he doubted Ferris's reach extended to every last corner—but even limited to those in a position to benefit from his care, it was easy enough to picture just how busy his days must be.

Sought after by everyone, pulled in every direction.
Needed by countless, uncountable people.

The moment he'd framed Ferris in those terms, Subaru couldn't help but compare that existence to his own. Himself, who hadn't been needed by the one person he most wanted—needed to be needed by. It made him feel wretchedly small.
And so—

"But honestly, since I don't even notice any symptoms myself, I don't really feel like relying on you for my body's issues. What do you think about that?"

Unable to bear the inferiority complex he felt toward someone treating him with such goodwill, Subaru deliberately trampled on it with a glib, offhand tone.
Knowing full well that the more he said, the more pathetic he'd feel about his own state of mind. Knowing full well that saying it wouldn't relieve even a shred of his frustration.

"My body not being at full strength is probably just some lingering aftereffects from my injuries, and I figure it'll sort itself out once those heal. And whatever's up with the Gate, I don't really feel it, anyway."

"I don't think the Gate problem is somethin' that oughta be brushed off like that, y'know? If you're thinkin' it just means you can't use magic, that's wrong."

"It's not?"

At Ferris's provocative tone, Subaru furrowed his brow and shot back the question.
In truth, even hearing about the Gate's malfunction, nothing clicked for Subaru. Having been forbidden from using "Shamak," he did feel, deep in his body, something like a lingering, murky, unshakeable lethargy.
But he'd attributed that to the same drained feeling as having every last drop of vitality wrung out of him, and figured a good meal and enough rest would set it right. It wasn't easy to believe that an organ he'd never even been conscious of in his old world could turn out to be fatally important.

"You really don't get it at all, do you? Lissen up. The common thinking is that the Gate's main job is to connect the inside and outside so mana can pass through when you use magic. But actually, the Gate isn't just active when you use magic—even in ordinary daily life, it circulates mana between the inside and the outside to keep you alive."

"So it's like breathing, or something?"

"You can think of it like that, yeah. Subaru-kyun, are you the kinda human who can live without breathing?"

"The humans I know can go about fifteen minutes, tops, worldwide record."

Apparently, by mastering some secret yogic technique to slow your heart rate to its limit, that kind of monstrous breath-holding became possible. Still, that probably wasn't what Ferris was getting at.
In other words, was Subaru's current condition supposed to be something like living without being able to breathe through his skin? Surely it wasn't quite that dire.

"So you're sayin' if this keeps up, that's what I'll end up like sooner or later?"

"So, about the state of Subaru-kyun's Gate—between all the strain you've put it through, it's already a total mess. Once it gets like that, your average water-user can't even treat it anymore. Best you can do is have someone stick by your side castin' magic constantly, and leave the important parts to heal on their own."

Along with underscoring the gravity of Subaru's situation, Ferris was implicitly emphasizing the point that he himself was no run-of-the-mill mage. Though perhaps that was only how it sounded to Subaru's envy-tinted ears.

"So basically, to get out of that kind of critical state fast..."

"Feri-chan's power is essential. Getttt it?"

Swaying his body side to side as he asked, Ferris got, in response, a wrinkled brow from Subaru signaling his reluctance. But Reinhard, watching the exchange, raised a finger.

"Sorry to interrupt, but an opportunity like this doesn't come around often, Subaru. Ferris's skill is known to everyone in the kingdom, and there's no shortage of people who'd want the benefit of his power. You shouldn't expect anyone to yield their place in line for you again anytime soon."

"Yeah, yeah. Feri-chan's got a whole line of customers waitin'. Buuut, just this once, I promised to prioritize Subaru-kyun. So just sit back, relax, and hand over your body and heart to Feri-chan, mkay?"

Riding on Reinhard's support, Ferris turned both palms toward Subaru, wiggling his fingers. Subaru eyed the gesture with distaste.

"I'm the kind of contrarian man who wants to say 'no' the second someone tells me that. Anyway, that aside—I don't remember bonding with you deep enough to deserve this kind of special treatment, y'know?"

Counting even their very first meeting—when they hadn't so much as properly exchanged names—today made only the second time Subaru had met Ferris. Even splitting the hall and the waiting room into separate encounters, that made three; and he couldn't think of anything in between that would have stirred the sympathies of the ever-in-demand Ferris.
At that question from Subaru, Ferris smirked mischievously and said, "Well, that's obvious, isn't it?"

"Feri-chan's got a real strict cat's temperament toward anyone who ain't kin, so honestly, under normal circumstances, there's no way I'd ever prioritize Subaru-kyun."

"Wow, you really say it straight, huh. Though that's easier on me, I guess."

For someone with Subaru's poor social skills, that was far easier to process than a bunch of hollow flattery or superficial small talk. Seeing Subaru didn't seem offended, Ferris twisted his body coyly as if drawing things out, saying "But, y'know—"

"There was that day Feri-chan went to deliver the summons notice at the margrave's mansion, right? Thaaat's the day of Feri-chan and Subaru-kyun's fated encounter."

"Ah, the day I should've just stayed in bed with a stomachache."

"Aw, sayin' stuff like that again. You were actually happy, weren't you?"

"Thanks to that gentlemanly old man you brought along mooching my expensive tea, I got chewed out half to death by the senior maid afterward. Got banned from morning and night mayo-chuchu."

Though he'd ignored that ban and secretly gone through with a mayo-chuchu the very next morning anyway.
Ignoring the incomprehensible term that had leapt from Subaru's mouth, Ferris pouted his lips, looking bored.

"Well, whatever, that's fine. What matters isn't meetin' Subaru-kyun—it's that Emilia-sama asked Feri-chan for a favor at the mansion."

"...Emilia-tan, huh. Figured as much."

"Yup, it was Emilia-sama, sure enough."

Since he'd more or less expected the answer, Subaru's tone came out bitter and heavy.
Thinking back to that first encounter with Ferris at the mansion, the content of their conversation then, and the original reason Roswaal had brought Subaru along to the royal capital, understanding came quickly enough.

And it was precisely because he understood that background that Subaru couldn't stop something heavy from coiling up in his chest.

The status ailment eating away at Subaru's body, this so-called mana depletion. The only one who could cure it was Ferris, one of the finest mages in the royal capital—and that same Ferris was an attendant of Crusch, his rival in the Royal Selection. To ask someone like that to treat one of his own would mean owing the Crusch camp a debt even before the Royal Selection had properly begun.
In other words, here too, Subaru had produced a result that dragged Emilia down.
Understanding that much, he said,

"Hey, do I really have to get treated, no matter what?"

Knowing full well it would be met with a lack of understanding from the other side, he'd said it anyway.
Sure enough, hearing that, Reinhard's brow furrowed in something like incomprehension. Ferris, on the other hand, gave a faint, wry smile as if he'd expected exactly this response from Subaru.

"The price has already been paid, y'know. If you don't let Feri-chan treat you now, it'll end up bein' Emilia-sama's effort wasted for nothin', y'know?"

"Price? What price? If it's something physical, you could just give it back and—"

"It's not somethin' physical, and it's the kinda price that can't be given back once you already know it, y'know? So, sorry, but Feri-chan can't grant that request of yours."

Coldly rebuffed in his plea, Subaru could only press a hand to his forehead and hang his head.
Ferris had read Subaru's feelings correctly, and had systematically closed off every escape route Subaru might have offered. That was, in his own way, a calculation meant to put his liege Crusch at an advantage—but it might also have contained some very human sense of duty and compassion, wanting to fulfill the wish of Emilia, who'd cared enough about Subaru's body to pay a price for it.

Both of those judgments together were enough to obstruct Subaru's shallow, inadequate thinking.

"Why am I always like this..."

He didn't want to be a shackle on Emilia's ankle.
Because he knew her—knew she wanted to become the ruler, and strove for it.
Because he knew her—knew she looked upward, aiming for that distant throne, striving to reach those heights.
Because he wanted to help a woman like that, because that was the only thing in which he could find meaning, because that alone was everything holding up Subaru's existence right now—he'd have thrown away anything and everything to make it happen.

So why was he so powerless, so ignorant, so incompetent, such a burden?

"—If you lament your own powerlessness that much, I'd think there's a better option available to you than this."

A tranquil voice rang through the air of the waiting room, and Subaru, who'd been hanging his head, looked up.
The voice belonged to neither Reinhard nor Ferris—it had come from the direction of the waiting room's door. Turning his gaze that way, he saw a slender, tall figure leaning his back against the open door, looking at him.

Receiving that gaze, the young man, smoothing back his purple hair, loosened his lips into an affected little smile.

"I'd rather you didn't make such a sour face at me. I never expected a warm welcome, but wearing that expression openly—"

"And if I do, what then?"

"It would call into question the character of the person keeping your company. Do be careful."

"Guh—!"

If it had come down to a simple exchange of insults, Subaru could have fired back with accusations of his own—but that was no longer a matter that could be settled between just the two of them, and that changed things.
Subaru twisted his lips, swallowing his objection, and glared with a dangerous look at Julius, who strode into the room with easy composure.

"Julius, are the candidates' talks over?"

Cutting across Subaru's tense line of sight, Reinhard stepped into the sightline between the two of them and asked Julius that question.
In response, Julius, one eye still shut, shook his head slightly. "No."

"The discussion looks set to run a bit long. Anastasia-sama is concerned that if the Royal Selection begins under the current conditions, things might devolve into an assassination free-for-all."

They were hammering out the relevant terms right now, Julius explained.
Turning his face away but still taking in the words, Subaru gave a small "I see" of understanding.

There were five participants in the Royal Selection, and however the method worked, it was a contest over the throne.
Under such circumstances, the simplest way to secure the crown was, by pure logic, to knock out every other candidate.

The candidates he'd seen in the hall had all struck him as people with a firm core of conviction—but whatever their personal character, that didn't necessarily hold true for the people around them.
And the Emilia camp, saddled with Subaru, already had a track record of having had blatant sabotage carried out against it.

—The theft of Emilia's insignia on the very day Subaru was summoned to this world was a case in point.

Reinhard, perhaps, had arrived at the same fact Subaru had just recalled, at the same moment. As Subaru looked up, his eyes met Reinhard's, who had also turned his face slightly this way.
Unsure what he ought to be feeling, Subaru hesitated—and Reinhard, in response, simply let a deep well of emotion swim quietly in his blue eyes and gave a small nod.
Then he turned back to face Julius.

"Assassination is hardly a pleasant word."

"Of course, resorting to such a method would be nothing less than an insult to the throne itself. But we ought to be cautious—cautious to the point of excess. Wouldn't you agree?"

"Well, yeah, that's true. After the huge incident half a year ago, you know how much effort it took just to rebuild things to where they are now. Nobody wants a repeat of that, y'know."

Julius calmly countered Reinhard's remark, and Ferris picked it up to close the loop. Watching the two tall figures nod at each other over his words, Subaru felt, once again, a sense of being left out, excluded even here.

"So in the end, what did you actually come here for?"

Because of that, Subaru turned a deliberately unfriendly attitude toward Julius.
Not only was he the wellspring of that feeling of exclusion, he was also someone who'd played no small part in the circumstances that led to Subaru leaving the hall. Subaru had already reflected that being shamed there was, in the end, the product of his own rashness—but even accounting for that, his opinions and Julius's simply didn't align.

Bathed in Subaru's no-longer-concealed hostility, Julius, his expression as cool as ever, advanced toward him. Reinhard casually tried to block his path, but nothing could stop that resolute stride toward Subaru.

Close enough that their breath might reach one another, Subaru and Julius stood face to face.
Compared to Subaru, of roughly average height for a Japanese man, the tall, slender Julius stood a good half-head taller. Naturally forced to look up at him from close range, Subaru said,

"Shouldn't the 'greatest of knights,' or whatever, be by his princess's side at a time like this? You never know, this castle's security might be a total sieve, and someone could just waltz right on in."

"—With the parties involved in the Royal Selection gathered as they are now, this castle is among the most crucial locations in the entire kingdom. Naturally, the guards approach their watch with commensurate diligence. There's no cause for you to worry on our behalf."

"...Nah, with that attitude, I think you're seriously in trouble. At the very least, I think I've got more cause than anyone else to worry about it, guaranteed."

Muttering that at the supremely confident-looking Julius, Subaru could only lament, once again, the laxity of the castle's security network.
Someone with no particular skills to speak of, who hadn't undergone any special training either, had managed to sneak in. When a real professional came along, well—it stood to reason.

"You've got a legendary mercenary walking around with a cardboard box, and you people wouldn't even notice till he's strapped a bomb to your back. Calling that 'thorough' security, frankly, my belly button's about to boil tea and evaporate from the absurdity."

"I don't understand the meaning, but I gather it's meant as an insult. —That makes twice, now."

Julius turned his face away and walked straight past Subaru, deeper into the room. Following his back with his gaze, Subaru watched Julius come to a stop by the window at the rear of the waiting room—positioned exactly where one could look down over the rear side of the castle.

"Now then. You asked me what I came here for, didn't you?"

Still gazing out the window, down at the grounds beyond the castle, Julius asked this in a voice devoid of any emotional tremor. With his back still turned, Subaru gave a silent nod of affirmation, saying nothing aloud.
Perhaps sensing that gesture by presence alone, Julius said "Then let me answer," as a preface, and turned around.

"The reason I came here is, of course, to see you. There's somewhere I'd like you to accompany me, if you would."

*How about it?*—Julius spread his hands, asking for Subaru's answer.
His stance seemed to be one of offering Subaru a choice. But given the state of prickly mutual feeling between them, it was hard to take this as a friendly proposal.

"'How about it,' nothing—I can't say yes or no or 'no thanks' without knowing the place and the purpose. Well, I could say it. But I won't."

"The place is the training grounds. The purpose is... let's see."

Faced with a remark from Subaru too laced with sarcasm to pass as mere banter, Julius answered directly, pausing briefly in thought with a finger to his chin.

"The purpose—shall we say, to teach you reality?"


※※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※　※


—Treading over the packed sand underfoot, Subaru tilted his head in bewilderment.

The location had shifted from the castle's waiting room to the knights' barracks, standing apart beside the castle.
Ground packed hard with reddish-brown earth, ringed by sturdy defensive walls that spoke of long years passed. Enclosed within it was a training facility meant for the guards to hone their bodies daily and sharpen their skill against one another—commonly known as the training grounds.

The grounds were about the size of an ordinary schoolyard, perhaps half of one. Whether for running about or crossing swords, it was a space of ample size either way.

Standing at the center of that place, Subaru moved through a slow set of stretches, bending and straightening his knees to loosen his muscles, sending heat into a body still lacking warmth.

While Subaru calmly went about his warm-up like this, Julius stood facing him at a distance of about fifteen meters—and the space around them was anything but quiet.
That was because—

"Julius, you should call this off. This isn't like you."

Reinhard, quietly going through his own preparations nearby, wouldn't stop pressing that argument.
There was no trace of impatience or anger in Reinhard's tone or expression as he pressed his case. What was there instead was nothing but a pure, genuine concern—something that deserved to be called anxiety.
Pouring that emotion abundantly into his blue eyes, Reinhard went on,

"I'll admit things went a bit too far, but that's the kind of matter that can be corrected through words alone. Under ordinary circumstances, wouldn't you reach the same conclusion?"

"Under ordinary circumstances. As it happens, that's exactly right, my friend Reinhard."

Carefully removing the excessive ornamentation attached here and there to his ceremonial white formal wear, Julius handed the pieces off one by one to a knight standing nearby.
Continuing that task, he turned an expressionless gaze toward Reinhard.

"Had the date fallen on some day other than today, or had we met somewhere else, I might have let him be. But that isn't how it went."

Cutting himself off as if severing the thread of conversation, Julius pulled his gaze away from Reinhard.
What his quiet eyes reflected now was only the figure of Subaru, still bending and straightening his knees, opposite him.

Then he unfastened the knight's sword at his hip, and with it still sheathed, pointed its tip skyward.

"In front of those connected to the throne, he insulted us who bear the title of knight, and made remarks belittling even the very way of chivalry itself. And rather than apologize for that, he heaped further insult upon me."

The training grounds, which had been filled with a faint stir up until then, fell hushed and silent.
All that echoed was Julius's resonant, sonorous voice, one that could enchant the ear.

Drawing the eyes of the crowd, the attention of every listener, to himself, Julius let his gaze sweep across them.

"—I shall now mete out judgment upon this insolent wretch who has defiled the pride of knighthood! Does anyone object?!"

"「—!!」"

A wordless gale seemed, all at once, to strike violently against the air of the training grounds.
The true source of that ear-splitting wind was the heat and voices erupting from the excited onlookers.

Their representative, Julius, and Subaru, the man who'd insulted them all at once—the intensity was the collective heat of the gathered Royal Guard and soldiers watching to see this confrontation through.

"Odds are a hundred to zero, nobody's betting on me. Well, well, even I've gotta admit, this is rough."

*Man, am I hated,* Subaru thought, giving a small shrug as he took in the hostile gazes bathing his entire body.
Having this much malice directed at him by so many people at once was a first in his life. Honestly, his heart had gone ice-cold, and he felt as though the malice pressing down on him from the knees down might crush and snap his legs at any moment.
And yet, despite that, his pulse hadn't quickened, and while his limbs felt heavy, they weren't unresponsive either.

It wasn't quite that his resolve was set—Subaru found himself in some indefinable state of mind he couldn't quite name.

"Now then, before we begin, let me ask you once more—do you intend to apologize for your earlier discourtesy and beg forgiveness? Even now, if you offer an apology fitting the insults you've compounded, I will forgive you."

"Can't think of what 'compounded insults' I'm supposed to have made, but... how am I supposed to apologize, exactly?"

"Press your forehead to the ground in tears. Or perhaps, like an obedient dog, lie on your back on the ground and show your belly, fawning for mercy—how does that sound?"

"Both options are lacking in elegance, so I'll pass, thanks."

Playing the clown was something Subaru excelled at, among the things he excelled at most—but that only had meaning when there was someone there he wanted to make laugh. Being laughed at by this bunch of insufferable people in front of him was something he absolutely could not accept.

He probably hadn't expected much from Subaru's answer anyway. Julius gave a short "I see," instantly discarding the exchange, and handed the knight's sword he'd been holding skyward to the knight beside him.
In its place, he received the two wooden swords offered by that same knight.

"Ordinarily, it would be no surprise if your discourtesy earned you a blade. But, however reluctantly, you serve as Emilia-sama's attendant. For that reason, I'll allow this to be settled with wooden swords."

*Any objection?*—Julius asked with his eyes, and Subaru answered curtly with a hand gesture: "no problem."
Judging the answer more from Subaru's expression than the gesture itself, Julius nodded.

"I'm glad you accept these terms. On my part as well, I will face you using nothing but the sword, purely. I swear I will make no use of magic whatsoever."

"Ah, so you're one of those magic-capable knight types too, huh."

"That, too, is part of what makes me 'Best Knight.' —Now then, let Ferris serve as our witness."

"Yes-yes~!"

Glancing to the side, in the direction Julius indicated, there was Ferris, waving a raised hand playfully. His inner thoughts, having accepted the role of witness so readily, were impossible to read. Unlike Reinhard, who'd tried to stop this to the very end, what was going through the mind of this man who'd come along so gleefully to witness the coming clash of violence?

"Okay, so, both of you do your best and give it your all with sincerity, kay? No matter how bad the injury, as long as you don't die, Feri-chan can fix just about anything, so do your best, Subaru-kyun."

"Why're you only saying that to me? Worry about him too."

"Whoa, feisty! Everyone hear that? Okay, on the count of three—"

Turning to face the crowd, Ferris waved both hands theatrically like a conductor, and on his cue, the whole venue erupted in laughter—mockery drawn by Subaru's recklessness.
Watching that wave of emotion play out, Ferris let his cat ears twitch atop his head.

"So, that's about the betting odds, but what'll you do, Subaru-kyun?"

Digging through his pocket, Subaru flicked, with his finger, the coin his fingertips had found, straight at Ferris.
Watching the coin trace a glittering arc and land in Ferris's hand, he said,

"Bet on me anyway. If I win, it's winner-take-all—I'll strip you all bare."

"No waaay, pervert! You're totally planning something lewd with Feri-chan. Like something out of a dirty book! Like something out of a dirty book!"

Ignoring the man-girl swaying his body suggestively, Subaru turned back to face Julius.
Sighing at his opponent, already brimming with fighting spirit, Subaru slowly closed the distance between them. He accepted one of the two wooden swords, and the fight began.
He'd been practicing sword swings back at Roswaal Manor for the sake of conditioning his body, but how far that would actually get him remained to be seen.

"To begin with, I feel like I've been sort of swept along by circumstances into this, but honestly, I don't have any memory of agreeing to this little pretend duel in the first place."

"But you came here. And there's no trace, in the way you look at me, of an aversion to crossing swords. Isn't that enough?"

"Hey now, Natsuki Subaru, famed as the modern-day Father Teresa of the three principles of non-violence, non-cooperation, and non-nuclear proliferation, would never get all fired up over such a violent approach, y'know."

Muttering vague words to buy himself time, Subaru drew up directly in front of Julius.
Whatever he might say, Subaru's body, thanks to his warm-up, had worked up genuine heat. Given the signal to start, he'd be able to move immediately.
Seeing Subaru's state, Julius narrowed his eyes with satisfaction.

"It's good to see you're getting into the spirit of things. Then, let's begin."

Offering one of the wooden swords to Subaru, Julius declared the start of their mock duel.
Feeling, quite naturally, the crowd's excitement climbing against his skin, Subaru gave the wooden sword he'd received a light swing to get the feel of it in hand, and stepped away from Julius—

"Ah, hold up. Something about this one feels off, like it doesn't quite mesh with me."

Twirling the wooden sword in hand, Subaru turned back and voiced that complaint.
Hearing this, Julius, looking somewhat thrown off his stride, said "Is that so?"

"I doubt it makes much difference, but would you like to try this one instead?"

"Ah, sorry about that. See, I'm kind of a high-maintenance, sheltered rich boy, so it's not good for me to hold onto items that don't feel right against my skin—"

As he spoke, he took the wooden sword Julius offered in one hand. Then, in exchange, he held out the sword he'd been given earlier toward Julius—

"Oops."

"——"

Before it could catch on Julius's fingers, the wooden sword slipped from Subaru's hand. Pulled by gravity, it entered a natural free-falling arc, and Julius's hand, instinctively leaning forward, reached to catch it.
Julius's straight, upright posture tilted forward, and the difference in their height vanished.

"—Hup!"

Stepping in, Subaru drew the wooden sword in his hand slightly back, then snapped it upward with a flick of the wrist, aiming for the tip of Julius's chin.
At the same instant, he thrust his free left hand forward, flinging the sand he'd secretly gathered up in his fist during his stretches straight at Julius's eyes—a two-stage combination of a blinding feint and a surprise strike.

*Got him,*—Subaru's mouth twisted into a wicked grin at his own masterful little trick.

The very next instant—

"It seems you truly have no sense of pride at all. How vulgar, and how easy that must make life for you. —I can't help but pity you."

The voice came right beside his ear. And at the same time came the impact—a hard, sharp sensation driving up into his solar plexus.
The air he'd been holding in his lungs exploded, and a short, pained "hah" escaped through his open mouth. With that hard impact against his chest as a fulcrum, his forward-leaning body lifted into the air. His feet left the ground, heaven and earth inverted, and just as his innards were being tossed about by the sensation of weightlessness, his face slammed into the ground.

His face scraped raw against the sandy earth, vomit spattering from the pain of the blow to his solar plexus, his brain taking a violent battering from pain and heat—and amid all that, the training grounds erupted with heat.

An overreach unbecoming of his own station, a recklessness beyond all measure—its retribution was, even now, about to come crashing down on Subaru without mercy.

Into the high sky above the training grounds, a delayed scream of agony rose and trailed off into the distance.
High, higher. Far, farther.
