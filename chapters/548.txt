Arc 7 Chapter 50: "The 'Hole' with a Nice View"

After parting ways with the suspicious Ubilk and turning down several side alleys, Subaru came to a stop.

Looking around warily, Subaru checked that no one was nearby. After also carefully confirming that Ubilk hadn't absentmindedly tagged along, he said,

"...The answer lies within me."

"Auu."

Rui tilted her head at Subaru, who was turning over the words Ubilk had told him.

Ubilk, who claimed to work as some sort of "counselor" whose duties remained a total mystery, still gave off the unmistakable air of a con man—but his remark had felt fairly on the mark.

Whether Ubilk had truly seen what Subaru was searching for, Subaru couldn't really say, but—

"...I think I might've figured out the 'abyss with a fine view.'"

Ubilk's words—that the answer lay within Subaru—had been the hint.

Surely it didn't mean that the vanished Olbart was secretly hiding somewhere inside Subaru's body. That had been a figure of speech, and Olbart's clue was itself a riddle.

The riddle "behind your eyelids" had led to the answer of the first room.

So naturally, "the abyss with a fine view" had to be a riddle too, its answer waiting somewhere.

And if the answer Ubilk spoke of lay within Subaru, then—

"I keep wanting to think it means somewhere I've actually been, but that's not it."

"Auau?"

"There aren't many places in this city I've actually visited. Besides the inn, it's basically just Yorna's castle. But that doesn't feel very abyss-like. No, that's not what I mean."

"Uu."

"It's about what 'abyss-like' even means."

Take Olbart's "abyss with a fine view"—to begin with, "having a fine view" and "abyss" were practically opposites.

Having a fine view meant a good outlook, a nice sight to see. An abyss was a deep pit. A pit with a nice view made no sense whatsoever.

So one half of it had to be a lie. And since you couldn't fake having a fine view, if anything was false, it had to be the "abyss" part.

"Something that feels like an abyss, but isn't actually one..."

No place Subaru had actually visited in Chaosflame fit that description.

The same was surely true for Abel and the others, who hadn't even left the inn. In fact, Subaru wasn't even sure how well Olbart himself knew the demon city.

But Subaru doubted Olbart got out and about much either—his job was to stick close and guard the fake Emperor, Vincent.

So the answer had to be somewhere that would hold true even for someone who barely knew the city.

A place matching the description "abyss with a fine view" that wasn't unique to Chaosflame.

And that was—

"—Rui, we're heading somewhere high up."

"Uau?"

Rui blinked in surprise at Subaru, who'd squeezed his eyes shut before saying that.

He didn't expect her to have actually understood. As long as she followed along obediently when he pulled her hand, that was all he needed.

Time was running short. It was unsettling to think he might be wrong, but there was nothing to do but try.

Toward the place closest to wherever Olbart's "abyss with a fine view" was hiding—

"—I have to get to the place closest to the sky."


△▼△▼△▼△


Ubilk's one-sided "advice" was what had led Subaru to that answer.

"The abyss with a fine view"—put another way, "the hole with a nice view."

An abyss wasn't just any hole—it was a pit so deep you'd fall and fall without end. Put more precisely, a pit so deep it seemed to have no bottom at all.

And just as Ubilk had said, Subaru had actually felt that sensation before.

In fact, just a little while ago, in real time.

"It was when that boy threw me down in the street."

The sheep-folk boy who'd grabbed Subaru's arm after he lunged out in a blind rage. Thrown down even as the boy apologized, sent flying high into the sky, Subaru had spun over and over, thinking the same thing again and again.

—That he was "falling" toward the blue sky.

Of course, looking at what had actually happened, that was backward.

In reality Subaru had been flung into the air, and from anyone else's perspective he'd been rising higher. But to Subaru himself, caught up in the moment and unable to tell up from down, it had felt like—

that he was falling toward the blue sky.

Falling into an endless, deep-blue, bottomless "hole."

"That's the true nature of the hole with a nice view... which means it's the place closest to the sky in this city."

That, Subaru figured, had to be the true identity of Olbart's "abyss with a fine view."

So he'd decided to aim for the highest point in the city—but,

"Ah, uh!"

Whether or not she understood how Subaru felt, Rui broke into a grin and jabbed a finger decisively at one spot.

While she swung their joined hands energetically and pointed off into the distance with her free hand, Subaru used his own free hand to cover his face.

Not because Rui, unable to follow the conversation, was just fooling around.

If anything, for once Rui had grasped exactly what Subaru meant. She was pointing straight at the building closest to the sky in this city, in Chaosflame.

It was just that the building in question was exactly what was troubling Subaru.

"U!"

"So you think it's that one too, huh..."

Tugged along by the hand, Subaru looked up at the building Rui was pointing to—Crimson Lazuli Castle.

Yorna Mishigure's castle, the tallest structure in this demon city. And, for a shrunken Subaru, without question a place he'd be turned away from at the gate.

The whole reason he'd ended up playing hide-and-seek with Olbart in the first place was to fulfill Yorna's demand, made while she waited at the castle. This way, the order of things was completely backward.

"But knowing how nasty Olbart-san's personality is, this feels exactly like something he'd rig up."

Olbart, too, knew why Subaru and the others wanted to return to their original forms.

Making it so that the path back required a trip to Crimson Lazuli Castle along the way seemed exactly like the kind of scheme a man called "the Vile Elder" would cook up.

The problem was—

"How am I even supposed to get into the castle...?"

He couldn't claim to be Natsumi Schwarz while shrunk down from "Infantilization." Actually, if that had been possible, he could've put off the hide-and-seek with Olbart until later.

He couldn't explain the situation to Yorna. Especially not when Tanza, Yorna's attendant, appeared to be working with Olbart.

Which meant even approaching the castle seemed fairly dangerous.

"Uau."

"Yeah, I know. ...If Tanza's cooperating with Olbart-san behind Yorna-san's back, acting on her own—"

Come to think of it, there seemed to be fewer horned demi-humans—Tanza's kin—around Crimson Lazuli Castle than he'd expect.

If a commotion broke out around the castle, surely even Yorna would notice something was up. That was presumably the last thing Tanza wanted.

Maybe it'd be simplest to just rat Tanza out.

"Wait, wait, that's way too hasty. There's a good chance Yorna-san would just take Tanza's side. Actually, that's a lot more likely."

If a child she doted on and two strange kids of unknown origin showed up, it wasn't even worth wondering whose word she'd believe.

Better to think it over and not go through with tattling on Tanza's scheme.

But it didn't seem wrong to figure that there were fewer enemies around Crimson Lazuli Castle.

"Sneaking into the castle really is the best option. After that, the top of the castle... I just have to pray Olbart-san's hiding up there. Rui, can I ask you something?"

"Ah?"

"That warp thing you do... you can take me along with you, right?"

Subaru asked, gazing into Rui's blue eyes.

Back when Rui had first gone on a rampage in the street, she'd used a short-range teleport to escape from beneath a collapsed tent, taking Subaru—who'd been sprawled over her—along to another spot.

In that moment there'd been a sensation like his stomach flipping inside out—not something he wanted to experience many more times—but there was no denying how useful it was.

Though there did seem to be a limit on the distance she could jump.

"If you can use that power, we could get inside the castle. Can you actually control it properly?"

"Au!"

"Really? 'Probably can' isn't going to cut it here. If you can't handle it properly, both of us are in danger. So—"

"Uu...!"

At Subaru pressing the point, Rui puffed out her cheeks in displeasure. Then, tightening her grip on his hand,

"Uau."

"—!"

In the blink of an eye, the scenery around them switched in an instant.

Since Subaru had been focused on Rui right in front of him, her appearance hadn't changed. But the scenery around her flickered rapidly, one after another—from the dim alley, to a bright street, to some room inside a building, and then to a rooftop.

As if to prove she really could control it properly.

"O-okay! I get it, I get it, that's enough! Stop, seriously, stop!"

"Auu."

"I lose! Fine, I lose, okay!"

Rui let the air out of her puffed cheeks, relaxing them into a triumphant look. Beside her, Subaru sank to his knees, a chill running down his spine from the harrowing experience.

Still holding hands with Subaru, Rui had performed at least five short-range warps in the blink of an eye. Each time, the scenery around them had changed—an absolutely absurd experience.

It had felt just like living through a nightmare.

"But, with this... uh."

"U?"

"Bleeeeergh!"

Just as he was about to pump his fist and think this could work, stomach acid surged up and scorched his throat. Still kneeling right there, Subaru spectacularly emptied the contents of his stomach onto the ground.

Short-range warping and the sensation of one's insides flipping over were apparently inseparable.

But even so—

"I-it's useful. With this... urk."

"Uuah."

Still fighting back the sour urge welling up again and again, Subaru glared at Crimson Lazuli Castle.

Beside him, Rui wrinkled her nose and, for the first time, let go of Subaru's hand on her own, waiting from a short distance away for his retching to finish.


△▼△▼△▼△


—Point in the direction to jump, then squeeze Rui's hand tight.

That was the signal Subaru had taught Rui for sneaking into Crimson Lazuli Castle.

It felt a bit like training a puppy to do tricks, but in reality it was nothing so tame.

He had no intention of treating Rui like a dog, and besides, a puppy would have been more endearing—at the very least, you wouldn't have to live in constant fear of it suddenly leaping at someone and inflicting serious injury.

Handling her required the utmost care.

"Stop."

He called out softly and pulled her arm back, bringing Rui to a halt.

When Subaru raised a finger to his lips and hissed "shh," Rui copied him, pressing a finger to her own mouth and going "uu" in imitation before falling silent.

Confirming this, Subaru left Rui behind him and quietly peeked around the corner ahead. —About ten meters off stood a tough-looking gate guard.

Arms crossed, planted firmly in front of the gate, the guard was a Lizard Human demi-human with a face shaped like a lizard's. His whole body was covered in blue scales, and he looked incredibly sturdy and cool.

From a distance, Subaru could see him yawning wide-mouthed, so he clearly wasn't taking his gate-guarding all that seriously. Still, there was no way to slip past him through the gate unnoticed.

So—

"Rui."

He called her name to draw her attention. Then, with her watching, Subaru pointed a finger at the wall—the stone rampart encircling Crimson Lazuli Castle.

And with that, he squeezed Rui's hand tightly.

"—————"

The next instant, Subaru and Rui's bodies had leapt clean over the castle wall and into the grounds within.

An infiltration that skipped clean past any exchange with the guard or any forced breakthrough—even to a child's mind, this felt like cheating, enough to make him pity the security staff.

No matter how tight the security or how vigilant the watch, none of it meant anything against Rui's teleportation.

Security cameras might have made a difference, but no such thing existed in this other world. In a world where guarding relied entirely on human effort, an ability to warp in and out unseen was a total cheat code.

Still—

"Can't afford to be seen, so I need to be careful..."

Outside the castle, off the grounds, he could've made up some excuse if caught. But once inside the grounds, there'd be no excuse that would work.

Rui's short-range warp could only be used twice in a row at most. A third time and beyond would definitely make her sick enough to vomit, and worse, she might even pass out.

If someone spotted them and Subaru passed out in the middle of fleeing—

"No one would be able to stop this girl anymore."

"U?"

Rui tilted her head innocently, clearly not understanding what worried him.

He wouldn't let her kill anyone else, ever again. —That was the vow Subaru had made when he fled with Rui in tow.

The shrunken, childlike Natsuki Subaru couldn't reach any proper conclusion about what to do with Rui. So at the very least, he wanted to carry through on the best conclusion he could manage right now.

And that was to make sure Rui never took another life.

"...Clinging to the outer wall and warping up in stages while resting probably isn't going to work."

Their goal was "the abyss with a fine view," and even if that meant the top of the castle, climbing there from outside relying on Rui's warp seemed difficult.

For one, he'd probably slip and fall somewhere along the way; for another, no matter how stealthily they tried to avoid notice, scaling the castle wall would surely get them spotted by someone.

Especially since this was Yorna Mishigure's castle.

"Getting caught by Yorna-san would be the absolute worst."

Along the way, they'd fought several times against pursuers who'd inherited part of Yorna's power through the Soul Marriage Technique.

Even with formidable power, those amateurs at fighting had been no match for Rui—but Yorna herself, one of the Nine Divine Generals, was surely in an entirely different league.

If it came to an actual fight, there was no telling how even Rui would fare.

Besides, if they were forced to fight Yorna, the whole purpose of coming to this city would fall apart. It would be a total failure. He wouldn't be able to face Rem after that.

"Guess all we can do is warp our way through the castle bit by bit, being careful."

Judging from having entered the castle once yesterday, there was almost no one patrolling inside.

Even a visitor had just been left waiting in a reception room unattended. Security when no one was expected was probably even more of a sieve. The guard's yawning was proof enough of that.

Though Subaru did wonder whether the castle of a city's ruler could really afford to be so slack.

"—Well, no point dithering about it. We're already inside anyway."

"Uu?"

"Yeah, let's go. ...Praying there's no one on the other side of the wall."

With walls as thick as a castle's, pressing an ear to them didn't let you hear anything on the other side. So the very first time they'd passed through the castle wall, whether they'd be spotted had been entirely a matter of luck.

Given his track record so far, Subaru couldn't claim to be lucky even if his life depended on it.

Even so—

"...Doesn't look like anyone's around."

In a castle with hardly any patrols, they hadn't run into the bad luck of being spotted right away.

"—————"

Glancing left and right, Subaru shielded Rui behind him and surveyed the corridor of Crimson Lazuli Castle they'd safely slipped into via her short-range warp. It hadn't bothered him yesterday, but walking on the wooden floorboards meant he had to be extremely careful about footsteps.

Also, if they ran into someone in the corridor, there was hardly anywhere to hide to either side.

If it came down to it, he'd need to warp straight into a room without hesitation.

"Should be fine as long as we don't use it back-to-back, probably."

He didn't know what the recoil was like for Rui herself, but as for Subaru, if they spaced the warps out one at a time, he could probably manage to hold back the nausea.

Depending on the situation, he should probably consider warping not just left and right but upward too. —No, in fact, maybe he should actively aim to jump upward.

"Would that actually cut down on the time spent wandering around inside, compared to carefully searching for stairs? But if I go up too carelessly, I might run into exactly the person I don't want to be seen by."

Obviously, if Yorna lived in Crimson Lazuli Castle, she'd always be somewhere in it. And since she owned the castle, her living quarters were probably in the upper part.

Aiming for the top of the castle meant climbing closer and closer to Yorna.

It didn't quite click for Subaru personally, but this world had masters capable of sensing another's presence.

Even with Rui's teleportation, no matter how much he suppressed his breathing or footsteps, there were some things he simply couldn't erase on his own. How much that would work against them was the real contest from here on.

"Anyway, we go as carefully as we can. If we do get spotted—"

"—If you're spotted, what will you do, I wonder?"

In that instant, a voice that froze his spine came from directly behind him, and Subaru's shoulders jerked.

The shock carried through their joined hands to Rui as well, and her eyes went wide with surprise. As if flung around, Subaru and Rui both spun to look behind them.

There—

"Two children holding hands—how very charming, I dare say. It makes even my own cheeks loosen into a smile, quite without meaning to."

Standing there was the one person they must never run into—the beautiful visage of Yorna Mishigure.


△▼△▼△▼△


"—Ah."

There shouldn't have been anyone there.

Just a moment before, there had been no one in the corridor besides Subaru and Rui.

And yet, despite that, Yorna had suddenly appeared.

People like her possessed a kind of power that trampled and disregarded entirely the caution, the care, the desperate preparation of an ordinary person like Subaru.

—Such were the transcendent beings of this other world.

"Neither of your faces are ones I know," Yorna murmured, letting smoke curl up from the pipe in her hand as she looked down at their faces.

The same lustrous, kimono-clad woman he'd seen atop the castle yesterday. But since he'd only really seen her seated before, being confronted with her standing figure head-on was overwhelming.

Part of it was because Subaru was shrunken, but Yorna was tall. Not quite as tall as Medium, but between that and her thick-soled footwear, her eye level was probably higher than Abel's.

Pinned by that blue gaze from such a height, his throat froze solid.

"There's no need to be so frightened. Now then, what business brings you to my castle?"

"Uh..."

Asked in a flat, even tone, Subaru cursed his own stupidity.

He'd meant to sneak in with the utmost care, and yet he hadn't prepared even a single excuse for if they got caught. If ever there was a word for shortsighted, this was it.

Sure enough, seeing Subaru falter without a word, Yorna narrowed her long, sharp eyes. His heart pounded so hard it felt about to burst, screaming that he had to say something or arouse suspicion—

"It's about Tanza, is it not?"

"Eh...!"

"That would be the reason you two are here in my castle like this."

At that remark, Subaru felt his frozen throat crack.

That she'd seen through him that thoroughly in the mere seconds he'd stood there speechless was completely beyond anything he'd expected. At the same time, Subaru realized his true identity had been exposed.

A single glance, and just like that, the long journey to the demon city and everything else had been for nothing. In his head, he thought he could hear Abel's voice coldly spitting out, "You fool."

The ground seemed to give way beneath him, and he felt the illusion of plunging headfirst into the depths of regret—Subaru's reckless attempt was going to end right here—

"Uau."

but his consciousness, sinking into that pitch-black darkness, was held back by the sensation of a hand gripping his tightly.

When Subaru glanced beside him without thinking, Rui, standing right there, was looking at his face. Her faintly trembling blue eyes were waiting for his signal—telling him she could warp them away at any moment.

That desperate look in Rui's eyes steadied Subaru's teetering resolve.

Looking up, he found Yorna quietly waiting for an answer to her earlier question, not even trying to hurry him. —Maybe it was still too early to give up.

Yorna had asked whether Tanza was the reason Subaru and the others were in the castle.

That much was correct. But only half of it. To fill in the other half, Subaru decided to gamble.

"Um, Yorna-san... Yorna-sama."

"Yes, what is it?"

"Where is Tanza-chan right now?"

Steeling himself to ask that, Subaru watched Yorna's eyes narrow slightly.

For less than a second, Subaru felt like a mouse caught by a fox. Realistically, the gap in power here was probably even greater than that between a fox and a mouse.

Facing an opponent like that, he spent a few seconds that felt like an eternity. In front of the silent Subaru and Rui, Yorna put the pipe to her lips, exhaled violet smoke from her lungs, and said,

"As it happens, I've sent Tanza out on an errand. She ought to be back by now, though..."

"—————"

At that answer, Subaru squeezed Rui's hand back just a bit harder. Rui looked over at him, her gaze darting around to check whether he was pointing anywhere.

He felt bad for Rui reacting like that, but it hadn't been a signal to jump—Subaru had just reflexively tightened his grip because he'd won his gamble.

He'd won. He'd really won the gamble.

Yorna's question a moment ago hadn't seen through their true purpose. Nor, of course, had it exposed their identities.

It had been something far simpler, a much more mild question.

In fact, after answering Subaru that way, Yorna gave a wry "honestly" and closed one eye,

"Tanza really is a handful. It's all well and good for her to devote herself to me, but if she goes forgetting promises to her friends, she'll never manage as an attendant."

"F-friends...?"

"She's a conscientious child, is she not? I keep telling her it won't do to point all that diligence only toward me."

Grumbling so, Yorna shrugged as she assessed Tanza's attitude.

At Yorna's words and gesture, Subaru felt a flicker of surprise and raised his eyebrows.

The malicious, unfathomable empress of the demon city he'd seen atop the castle yesterday.

That impression had flipped entirely—Yorna's current demeanor felt like something completely different. More familiar, more easily understood: the air of an adult worrying over a child.

As if to reinforce that impression, Yorna let out a small sigh and said,

"It's careless of Tanza, but I can't bring myself to turn you two away either. —Wait in a room until Tanza returns."

So saying, Yorna turned her back with a swirl and began walking slowly.

Despite her thick-soled footwear, the graceful way she walked silently across the wooden floor made him doubt his own eyes at how refined she was, down to her very toes.

And then, right in front of Subaru, who found himself transfixed by the mere sight of her walking, Yorna suddenly stopped and said,

"Come along. I'll show you the way myself."

"Wha—?! Yorna-sama, yourself?!"

"Au?"

Subaru was stunned by Yorna's unexpected kindness. Seeing how startled he was, Rui tilted her head too, and Yorna, watching the two of them, brought a hand gently to her lips and laughed.

It wasn't a spiteful laugh in the least—only a thoroughly gentle smile.

"This is my castle. Naturally, playing host falls to me. With Tanza away, there's no one else to attend to the two of you."

"Ah, um..."

"It's the first time that child's friends have ever come by, too. Now then, come along."

Jerking her chin, Yorna bade them follow. Tracking her movements with his eyes, Subaru agonized for a single second over what to do.

He suspected this might be a trap, but quickly let go of the thought.

After all, Yorna had no reason to trap Subaru and Rui. If she wanted to, she could pin Subaru down with a single finger, and take out Rui with maybe a few more.

But that meant she really was offering to guide them out of pure goodwill. Which, in its own way, was completely at odds with the impression he'd had of Yorna yesterday—

"Uau."

As Subaru's thoughts spun round and round, Rui tugged at his hand. Looking at her, it seemed Rui had no objection at all to following Yorna.

That reaction from Rui was the final push that got Subaru's feet moving.

Rui's sense for danger was probably sharper than his own. If Rui wasn't wary of Yorna, then there was probably no need to worry about anything happening to them right away.

"Little one?"

"W-we're coming!"

Answering Yorna's repeated call in a voice cracking with nerves, Subaru started walking too. Watching him toddle along with short, quick steps, Yorna's eyes softened and she resumed walking as well.

He wasn't sure if he should call this good fortune, but it was far better than being caught out.

"You two are children from elsewhere, are you not?"

"Eh..."

"I don't recognize your faces. Nor have you received my 'love letter.'"

Unknown face, unrecognized face—she'd said something similar before. The phrase "love letter" that followed wasn't something he had any clue about either, so Subaru just nodded with a "y-yes."

"We came from outside the city... with, um, a scary grown-up."

"A scary grown-up... my, that does sound like a great deal of trouble. Why travel with such a frightening adult?"

"I-it just sort of happened that way..."

Answering Yorna's question, Subaru replied with a fair measure of honesty, while still being careful.

In truth, traveling with the scary grown-up—Abel—had come about largely by happenstance. Though perhaps he should put that in the past tense: "had been traveling with."

It seemed unlikely that Abel would readily forgive Subaru for fleeing with Rui in tow.

In the first place—

"Is this even something I need to be forgiven for...?"

Subaru didn't think he deserved to be blamed for having been unable to decide how to deal with Rui.

Sure, hiding Rui's identity had been wrong of him. But if this was the result of not hiding it and telling the truth, then keeping it hidden had been the right call after all.

If there was anything to apologize for, it was failing to keep it hidden all the way through and letting it slip partway.

"...You seem to be in quite a complicated state of mind, I dare say."

Yorna suddenly slowed her pace, glancing back to tap a finger against her own brow. There was nothing on the skin her pale finger touched—she wasn't indicating herself, but pointing out Subaru's furrowed brow.

There, no doubt, deep creases had formed from thoughts with no answer in sight.

"A little one oughtn't wear such a difficult expression. —Shall I discipline that scary grown-up of yours for you?"

"Discipline... wait, you, Yorna-sama?"

"Fufu, I may not look it, but I'm quite strong, you know."

Yorna lifted her sleeve and gave it a soft wave. At her words, Subaru had a fleeting vision of Abel being disciplined, and immediately shook his head hard side to side.

Partly it was a hunch that Yorna's idea of "discipline" wouldn't end anywhere near as cute as the word sounded, and partly it was the gap he kept noticing between his impressions of Yorna. The latter surprise, in particular, was the bigger one.

"I'm glad you're worried, but it's fine! I mean, I think he's probably someone who'll understand if I just talk to him..."

"Is that so. If talking will do the trick, nothing could be better. In this world, there are far too many people one simply cannot reason with."

"...Yorna-sama, are you—"

"Hm?"

For a moment, Subaru hesitated over whether to say it, his words trailing off, and Yorna stopped walking at that.

Guilt at having made her stop so many times already, anxiety that saying what came next might earn Yorna's anger, and shame at his own thoughtlessness for blurting it out even knowing that—all of it churned together in his head.

He was well aware that shrinking down had dulled both his judgment and his ability to think things through.

But the real problem, he felt, was this lack of restraint—this inability to hold himself back. Both dragging Rui along, and sneaking into Crimson Lazuli Castle without adequate preparation.

Carried along by that same momentum, he hesitated to say what came next—but,

"Um, Yorna-sama, you really do listen properly, don't you. I just assumed—on my own, without really thinking—that important people were scary..."

"Hmm."

Aware he'd said something terribly rude, Subaru found himself unable to keep meeting Yorna's eyes partway through. But hearing him out, Yorna gave a small chuckle in her throat and said,

"From the look of you, you two aren't demi-humans, are you."

"Um, that's right."

"And through your eyes, how does this city appear?"

"This city..."

Caught off guard by the sudden question, Subaru turned his gaze toward a window in the corridor. Set high on the wall, the window was small, and at his current height there was no way to peer outside.

Still, he could make out a faint, hazy patch of blue sky, and knew it was close by.

"It's... an amazingly lively city, I think. There's all kinds of people, so many of them."

Choosing his words carefully, Subaru voiced his impression as honestly as he could.

The impression of a bustling city had been there from very early on—both in the jumbled, piled-up look of the streets and in the sheer variety of people living within them.

To both eye and ear, the strong impression had been of a hectic, busy city.

"An honest little one, you are. But it's a pleasant thing to hear, all the same."

Hearing Subaru's impression, Yorna's lips curved into a faint smile.

Then, gazing out the same window Subaru had looked to—though the view she saw was surely different from his—she said,

"This city draws together great numbers of those who find it hard to live anywhere else. Children who shrink themselves small outside its walls, with nowhere else to go... children whose voices go unheard even when they cry out."

"Even when they cry out..."

"In this final refuge those children have found their way to, if I do not lend an ear to their words, then who on earth would?"

As she spoke, putting the pipe back to her lips, a lustrous light passed through Yorna's eyes.

Feeling something pierce his chest at that, Subaru grew terribly embarrassed with himself. —Hearing Yorna's words just now, he could feel his impression of her shifting dramatically.

"She is a deeply loving person. She loves her allies and hates her enemies. —A lover to all who live in the demon city."

That was how the Deer Human girl had answered when Subaru asked Tanza about Yorna the day before.

At the time, Subaru hadn't understood at all what that meant. When he'd heard about the Soul Marriage Technique from Abel, he'd wondered if that was what it referred to.

But probably, that wasn't the real meaning Tanza had been trying to convey.

—It had been the answer to what Yorna Mishigure truly, genuinely held dear.

"I'm sorry, Yorna-san..."

The moment that thought struck him, Subaru's restraint failed him yet again, and the apology slipped out.

At Subaru's sudden apology, Yorna laughed as she exhaled violet smoke, and said,

"There's nothing at all for you to apologize for. Important people appear frightening because, without that, important people themselves would have something to fear. There are matters that go more smoothly when one is feared, after all."

"Important people... want to be feared too..."

Somehow, it felt like he'd just been told something very worth remembering.

And it seemed like a principle that wasn't just theoretical for Yorna herself, either.

"You needn't worry—I wouldn't stoop to something so unseemly as getting angry at a little one over a matter like this. As it happens, I'm in rather good spirits just now."

"Au?"

"Yes, indeed. —Signs have begun to appear that something I've long desired may finally be within my reach."

Yorna's eyes softened at the corners as she gave her sleeve another gentle wave.

There wasn't a trace of anger in her manner. As she herself had said, it likely came down both to her not being the sort to act childishly toward a child, and to the fact that she really was in good spirits.

Behind that good mood was, in all likelihood, the contents of the personal letter Abel had sent—

"—————"

Once he'd thought that far, Subaru held himself back this time and pondered carefully.

Whether the contents of that letter—which Yorna was taking positively—would actually be honored depended on whether Abel and the others could safely reunite at Crimson Lazuli Castle.

But the one obstructing Subaru and the others' efforts was Yorna's own attendant, Tanza.

Even from their exchange just now, it was clear how much Yorna cherished Tanza.

If Yorna's own wishes ever came into conflict with Tanza's, which would she choose? —This was, after all, a woman who had once defied the Emperor himself for someone else's sake.

Once she learned the truth of the situation she'd been placed in, which would Yorna choose—

"—There, you've gone and carved creases into your brow again."

Tapping her own brow with a finger, Yorna pointed out Subaru's distress. Following her lead, Rui was patting at her own forehead too.

Watching the two of them, Subaru let out a faint breath.

This, too, might well be a hasty decision.

But he didn't have a mind clear enough right now to properly judge whether it was right or wrong—

"Um, Yorna-san, there's something I'd like you to hear..."


△▼△▼△▼△


—Atop the roof tiles of Crimson Lazuli Castle's main keep, a figure sat with a blend of blue and red radiance beneath him.

Basking in the clear wind particular to high places, he brought a gourd flask tucked in his robes to his lips. Wetting his parched throat with deep gulps of liquor, he propped his chin on his hand and gazed at the scene below.

"Kah-kah-kah, what a view, what a view."

Looking down at the jumbled cityscape where every style imaginable mixed together in disorder, the old man's throat rumbled with a chuckle.

His raspy laughter was scattered by the wind, fading away without reaching anywhere in particular. That it seemed to be swallowed up and vanish into the demon city's clamor amused the strange old man again and again.

He loved chaotic things.

The chaotic streets, the tangle of different demi-human races, the schemes drifting this way and that.

So much so that the mere fact of their disorder made him want to root for them.

"Whatever it is, stirring it all up and gobbling it down—that's an old man's bad habit, that is. Still—"

Tipping the gourd back with a jerk, wiping away the liquor spilling from the corner of his mouth with his sleeve, the old man shrugged. Then, still sitting cross-legged, he swiveled around on his backside and raised one eyebrow.

It was quite the sight, one to make a chaos-loving old man's mouth water—

"Even setting aside that they made it all the way to me, this is one heckuva surprising lineup, seriously."

In front of the strange old man as he said this, three figures stood on the tiles of the main keep.

They were—

"Olbart-san, found you!"

A boy jabbing a finger straight at Olbart, and a blonde girl holding his hand.

And, standing behind the two of them like a guardian, the figure of the empress who ruled the demon city.
